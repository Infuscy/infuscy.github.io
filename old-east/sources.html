The great collections

  • Romania: Simion Florea Marian, Descântece poporane române (1886), Vrăji, farmece și desfaceri (1893) and Sărbătorile la români (1898–1901). Tudor Pamfile, Boli și leacuri (1911) and Mitologie românească (1916). Artur Gorovei, Descântecele românilor (1931), with more than 2,500 charms. I. A. Candrea, Iarba fiarelor (1928) and Folklorul medical român comparat (1944). Moses Gaster, Literatura populară română (1883) and “Scholomonar” (Archiv für slavische Philologie, 1884). Ion Ghinoiu, Obiceiuri populare de peste an (1997) and Panteonul românesc (2001).
  • Travellers and early witnesses: Anton Maria Del Chiaro, Istoria delle moderne rivoluzioni della Valachia (1718). Dimitrie Cantemir, Descriptio Moldaviae (1716). Emily Gerard, “Transylvanian Superstitions” (1885) and The Land Beyond the Forest (1888). Agnes Murgoci, “The Vampire in Roumania” (Folklore, 1926).
  • Bulgaria and Serbia: Sbornik za narodni umotvoreniya (Sofia, from 1889). Dimitar Marinov, Narodna vyara i religiozni narodni obichai (1914). Vuk Karadžić, Srpski rječnik (1818, 1852).
  • The East and West Slavs: L. N. Maikov, Velikorusskie zaklinaniya (1869). A. N. Afanasyev, Poeticheskie vozzreniya slavyan na prirodu (1865–69). Oskar Kolberg, Lud (1857–1890). W. F. Ryan, The Bathhouse at Midnight: Magic in Russia (1999), the best single book in English.
  • Comparative: Éva Pócs, Fairies and Witches at the Boundary of South-Eastern and Central Europe (1989) and Between the Living and the Dead (1999). Mircea Eliade, Zalmoxis, the Vanishing God (1972). Ronald Hutton, The Triumph of the Moon (1999) and The Witch: A History of Fear (2017). Alexander Carmichael, Carmina Gadelica (1900). Jonathan Roper (ed.), Charms, Charmers and Charming (2009).

Ancient and medieval witnesses cited

Plato, Charmides, Gorgias and Laws; Theophrastus, Enquiry into Plants 9.8.8 (trans. A. Hort, 1916); Theocritus, Idyll 3; Aristophanes, Clouds; Plutarch, Table Talk 5.7; the Atharvaveda 4.12; the Second Merseburg Charm; the Council in Trullo, canon 62 (692); the Indiculus superstitionum (8th century); the Word of St Gregory (11th–13th century); the Stoglav (1551); Visum et Repertum (1732).
