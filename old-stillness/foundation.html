The Spellbook of Old Stillness

The Foundation: the seat

Three of the oldest manuals agree on almost everything: a quiet place, a firm seat, a straight spine, nose over navel, and breath that makes no sound. They disagree about the eyes.

Zhiyi, Concise Śamatha-Vipaśyanā (6th c., China) Kamalaśīla, Stages of Meditation (late 8th c., India/Tibet) Dōgen, Universal Recommendation for Zazen (1227, Japan)
Before sittingModerate food and sleep; loosen your clothesA very soft, comfortable seatA quiet room; moderate food and drink; set all affairs aside
LegsHalf or full cross-leggedFull or half lotusFull or half lotus, on a round cushion over a thick mat
HandsLeft palm over the right, resting close to the bodyNot among his eight pointsRight hand on the left foot, left palm on the right, thumb tips touching
Spine and headStraight, neither hunched nor stiff; nose over navelStraight, neither leaning back nor bent; shoulders level; nose over navelUpright, leaning to no side; ears over shoulders, nose over navel
EyesClosed, just enough to shut out the lightNeither open nor shut, resting toward the tip of the noseAlways open
MouthLips and teeth lightly together, tongue to the palateTeeth and lips natural, tongue behind the upper teethLips and teeth closed, tongue against the palate
BreathSilent, unbroken and fine: not audible (“wind”), stuttering (“panting”) or coarse (“qi”)Not noisy, forced or agitated; soft and effortlessGentle, through the nose

Zhiyi and Dōgen also give an entry and an exit. Before settling, sway the body left and right a few times; Zhiyi adds breathing stale air out through the mouth, up to three times. Afterwards, rise slowly. Zhiyi’s exit is the most careful: move the shoulders, hands and neck, then the feet, rub the palms warm and cover the eyes with them, and get up only once the body has cooled.

Where the manuals disagree, on the eyes, choose one way and keep it. A low stool is also old: the Hesychast Gregory of Sinai (d. 1346) had beginners sit on one about a span (some 20 cm) high (Spell 28). The spells that follow assume this seat unless they say otherwise.

Sources: Kamalaśīla’s eight points, Zhiyi’s four kinds of breath, Gregory of Sinai’s span-high seat.
