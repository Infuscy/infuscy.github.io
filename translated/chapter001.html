Chapter 1: The Alliance Expedition

A furious banging on the door jolted Jace awake.

"Jace! Jace Sesso!"

"Coming."

Jace threw on his shirt and opened the door. Outside, crowds of people were heading toward the city gates in groups — some kind of grand assembly seemed to be going on there, loud enough to wake the dead.

In front of him, a brute of a man — one who barely came up to Jace's chest — reached out and grabbed him by the collar.

"What are you doing, huh? Sleeping in on a day this important? You're about to miss it!"

And "brute" wasn't even sarcasm. The guy — black beard, face like a slab of raw meat, a linen patch over his right eye — was a dwarf. Among his own kind, he must've been considered unusually tall.

"So, Grede, what's going on?" Jace asked, impatient.

"Don't you want to see Alleria Windrunner?" the dwarf said excitedly. "She's the Ranger-General of Quel'Thalas, General Turalyon's girl… if you don't see her now, when else are you going to get a chance to lay your eyes on an elven beauty like that?"

Hearing Alleria Windrunner's name, Jace felt his heart hammer twice, hard.

Not because he had any particular feelings toward the elven general, but because that familiar name had dredged up memories he'd buried deep — memories of his past life.

That's right. He was a transmigrator. He'd been in this world for seventeen years.

From the moment he'd been dragged out of his mother's womb, something had felt off. The memories of his previous world were carved in stone — not even the rapid brain development of ages three to five had been able to scrub them away.

One moment he'd been sitting at his computer, running a World of Warcraft raid with full focus. The next, a wave of drowsiness hit him, and everything in front of his eyes changed completely.

He'd been born in the capital city of Lordaeron. His father was a wall-guard of Lordaeron; his mother was the daughter of a Brill farmsteader.

His childhood had been smooth enough, but when he was ten, the Dark Portal had opened and the Orc Wars had broken out. Fortunately, the capital of Lordaeron hadn't been hit too hard during the war.

The refugees fleeing from the Kingdom of Stormwind had described the chaos and catastrophe happening in the far south, and that was when Jace understood: everything here matched the game world, down to the last detail.

Which meant that if he wanted to live out a peaceful life, he'd have to leave Lordaeron and settle in Stormwind.

Because according to the game's plot, in a little over a decade, the glorious kingdom of Lordaeron would be utterly destroyed by the Scourge. The entire kingdom's lands would be ravaged by plague.

And the dead wouldn't even find peace — they'd be raised from their graves by necromantic magic to fight for their undead masters until they were smashed to pieces and couldn't stand anymore.

Stormwind, on the other hand, though ruined at present, would have it relatively smooth for the next few decades. No civilization-ending catastrophes.

Naturally, Jace wasn't about to sit around waiting to die. But his parents had lived in Lordaeron their whole lives and refused to move no matter what. And there was no way Jace could convince them of something he'd learned from another world, something that wouldn't happen for another ten-plus years.

Fortunately, they didn't object to young Jace going out and seeking his fortune.

Stormwind had been destroyed in the war. To help the House of Wrynn rebuild, King Terenas II of Lordaeron had spent a fortune hiring craftsmen and young laborers from every kingdom — even from the dwarves.

At fifteen, Jace had ridden that wave, coming to this city of hope from the game — the future capital of the Alliance — together with the Stormwind refugees returning home.

Right now, Grede was shouldering through the crowd like an icebreaker, barreling a path forward with Jace in tow, laughing his head off the whole way.

He wasn't hiding his excitement in the slightest. After all, Alleria Windrunner, the legendary battle-master, was exactly his type of woman — a warrior who possessed both beauty and strength. And unlike those blowhard elves who loved to boast, she was a genuine hero who could take on a hundred enemies alone.

Jace was excited too, of course. This was basically the first time he'd get to see a famous World of Warcraft character in the flesh.

For the first seventeen years of his life, those familiar names had only reached him through his parents, or from other country folk and townsmen, or through battlefield dispatches.

He'd never actually known, or even seen, a single one of them.

Jace followed Grede out through the inner gate and into the Valley of Heroes — though of course, it wasn't called that yet.

The Valley of Heroes without its statues was a wreck. Years of war had left it scarred and ruined. The flames of demon warlocks and red dragons had burned the once-lush riverside shade into a wasteland.

Veterans of the Orc Wars marched in formation across the newly rebuilt bridge from the gate. Their armor still bore scorch marks and dented gouges from blade strikes. The weapons and shields they carried bore the sigils of different kingdoms.

Their destination lay a thousand miles to the southeast — the passage through which the orcs had come from another world: the Dark Portal.

Grand Marshal Turalyon was gathering the army at Nethergarde Keep, preparing to cross the Dark Portal the orcs had built, strike straight at the orcish homeland of Draenor, and put a permanent end to the Horde's threat to this planet — Azeroth.

Yet among the crowd seeing the army off, Jace was the only one who knew how this would end.

A few years from now, the orc leader Ner'zhul would channel the magic that destroyed Draenor, and the whole world would tear itself apart. To keep the catastrophe from reaching Azeroth, the expeditionary force would destroy the Dark Portal on the Draenor side.

From that moment on, every member of the expedition would be stranded in that dying world.

To honor the sacrifice of the expeditionary force, the King of Stormwind would order statues erected on this bridge for the heroes of the expedition — the "Sons of Lothar" — and name this place the Valley of Heroes.

"Where's Alleria? I want to see Alleria Windrunner!" Grede bellowed, completely ignoring the solemn atmosphere.

Every Stormwind citizen and expeditionary soldier there knew how dangerous this mission was. Everyone had come to see the soldiers off.

But to a dwarf like Grede, fighting to the death against an enemy that could destroy the world was the highest romance imaginable.

Gazing at the dwarf warriors who'd come from Aerie Peak, his eyes were full of envy, not worry.

"If that troll bat hadn't clawed out my eye, and I could still throw a hammer true, I'd have followed Kurdran Wildhammer straight through the Dark Portal," he said regretfully in Common with a thick dwarven accent. "Then I'd be General Alleria's comrade-in-arms, and who's to say whether me or your Grand Marshal Turalyon would win the fair lady?"

Hearing that, the slightly annoyed looks from the citizens around them quietly faded.

Before the Orc Wars, humans, dwarves, and elves had been divided by enormous prejudice and conflict — there had been clashes more than once.

But by now, every Stormwind resident knew that if the dwarves and elves hadn't stood with humanity in that desperate hour, fighting the invaders from another world to the death without hesitation, they might never have had the chance to return to their homeland.

Jace said, "Alleria has two younger sisters just as beautiful. You still have a chance, Grede."

"Hah!" Grede gave an awkward laugh. "I'm not interested in those childish girls. I like them mature."

"The second of the three Windrunner sisters, Lady Sylvanas, is said to be over six hundred years old."

"Six hundred?! Then her older sister must be—"

"Shh…"

Jace saw the blue-feathered golden phoenix banner of the high elves fluttering closer from a distance.

"They're here, they're here — help me up onto the railing… I can't see!"

Grede grumbled his complaints, and Jace used every ounce of his strength to lift the two-hundred-pound barrel an inch.

From the corner of his eye, a column of high elves was marching past. Clad in silver-white armor, holding carved lances and silver bows, their sea-blue cloaks snapping in the wind that swept through the valley — every one of them, male and female, elegant and composed, a stark contrast to the dwarves and humans.

The female elf riding the white horse at the front was probably Alleria Windrunner, Ranger-Captain of Silvermoon.

She wore a deep green cloak, and pale golden hair fell to her chest. Her arcane-infused eyes glowed with a blue light bright enough to be unmistakable even in sunlight, staring ahead with resolve.

Alleria's cheeks were gaunt to an extreme, to say nothing of the thin, pointed nose, the long ears that swept up high — by human standards, slightly alien.

Jace still remembered the first time he'd seen an elf from Quel'Thalas back in Lordaeron — he'd felt like he was looking at an extraterrestrial.

Though to be fair, their appearance was basically identical to the game, just more detailed and real. But Jace had completely lost the perspective he'd had when he used to play.

After more than ten years in this other world, he suspected he was getting further and further from that corporate drone he used to be, and more and more like a native of Lordaeron.

If not for his certainty about the Scourge invasion a decade from now, he never would have made the decision to cross the ocean, leave home, and come to Stormwind.

Maybe it was because he didn't have a cheat.

That's right — to this day, no old man in a ring, no suddenly awakened ancient bloodline, no arcane gem brimming with power, no whispers from gods or demon lords guiding his way.

He was just a laborer from the north. Aside from a Brill accent in his Common, he was no different from the locals.

Other people's transmigration, and mine.

If Jace had started with some kind of stat-allocation system like the protagonists in those other transmigration novels, maybe by now he'd have the power to stop all this, to take their place and save the world.

Instead, he could only earn a living by the work of his own hands, at best using his foreknowledge of future events to pick a safe, stable place to hole up and survive.

But at the very least, he needed to earn enough money to bring his aging parents from Lordaeron to Stormwind. If he couldn't save the world, he at least had to take care of the people who mattered most to him.

Grede watched the expeditionary column fade into the distance and sighed. "So, what are you up to today?"

"Heading to the job board to see what work's available," Jace answered. "Rent's due next month."

The two of them talked as they walked back toward the inner gate with the crowd.

"Mine's due too," Grede said. "Some Bronzebeard said Baron Revilgaz is raising rent next month — from five silver to six."

"Why not move somewhere else?"

"Move where? All of Stormwind's rents are going up, and the cheap places are all full. You want me to move to Goldshire? Don't want my head taken by an orc halfway there."

"Has the road to Goldshire not resumed regular patrols?"

"It has. Last month they found a patrolman's sword and intestines by the shores of Mirror Lake."

"His sword and… intestines?"

"Human intestines, not orc."

"Wolves, maybe."

"What's the difference, Jace? Wolves, orcs, murlocs — Elwynn Forest is more fucked up than the goddamn Hinterlands, I'm telling you. At least the Amani trolls understand a little Dwarvish. You yell 'spare me!' and they won't mistake it for 'stick my head on a spear!'"

"They'll stick your head on a spear either way," Jace said with a grin.

"Yeah, no difference there — but it feels more like dying in battle against an enemy than getting eaten by some brainless beast for no reason. You know what I mean?"

The two of them bantered their way to the job board at the Trade District crossroads. A few months ago, the board had been full of repair work for every district. Now a good portion of it was wanted notices for bandits and rogue orc chiefs in Elwynn Forest, plus labor recruitment for Redridge.

Stormwind's reconstruction was nearing its end. Most of the detailed, specialized work had been taken over by the local professional craftsmen's guild, the Stonemasons — which meant foreign laborers like them had to find another way to make a living.

Jace rented a place on the back street behind the Stormwind Trade Depot, one of the first buildings to go up in the new city. Most refugees lived in Old Town. The ones willing to live in the new city were all outsiders like them. Laborers.

But as the new district got more prosperous, Jace was now paying four silver a month in rent, and it'd probably go up soon. Dwarves always had good intelligence.

For someone who used to be a World of Warcraft player, four silver was a joke. After all, when he played, he'd always carried hundreds of thousands of gold in his bags and never thought much of it. A single trip to the Auction House meant tens of thousands in and out.

But here, plenty of people in Stormwind worked hard all month and couldn't earn a few silver.

Jace remembered there'd been a guy near his door last year who polished veterans' armor and weapons — charged only two or three copper a time. That was back when the silver mines of Westfall hadn't been retaken from the Blackrock orcs yet, and a single silver was worth nearly two hundred copper.

That guy was a Stormwind native, even had some connection to a noble family once, but he was living real tight now. Jace had heard his house had been occupied by ogres during the orc occupation, and he'd gone deep into debt to rebuild.

Jace was worse off than that guy. At least the man had a trade. All Jace had was manual labor, and King Terenas II's compensation payment was almost gone.

It was supposed to be one gold per person. When it actually reached their hands, it was forty silver.

The noble who'd distributed the wages claimed the kobolds in the gold mines around Goldshire had been driven out, and gold was cheap now — forty silver was enough to exchange for a gold.

But none of these laborers had seen either a gold coin or a kobold since arriving in the Kingdom of Stormwind. Only Jace knew that twenty years from now, the kobolds still wouldn't be cleared out of the mines — their boss would have gold teeth.

Not to mention, if gold production had resumed, why not pay in gold, or at least gold fragments of equivalent weight?

Finding work was getting harder by the day. Last week there was a job escorting lumber from Eastvale Logging Camp to Stormwind — a pack of gray wolves had followed the hired hands for two or three hours along the way, and they'd come back to find they were being paid thirty-five copper each. A joke.

And still a bunch of outsiders like him had fought over it.

Just as he was worrying about it, he spotted a rather unusual posting on the notice board.

"Seeking an assistant. Must have some knowledge of history and archaeology, and ideally some understanding of herbs. Must possess patience and other fine qualities. Work location: the Mage Quarter — Arcanist Malin."

Arcanist Malin?

Jace remembered there was a Master Malin in Stormwind's Mage Quarter. His daughter was an excellent mage too, and a decade or so later she'd even taken part in the Alliance and Horde's joint expedition to the north.

If this Arcanist Malin was that Master Malin, wouldn't applying for this assistant position be a perfect chance to become a mage?

History, archaeology, herbs… Jace had never systematically studied history or archaeology, but he figured his understanding of this world's lore should be more than enough to crush most ordinary people.

Since this mage had posted a notice on a board meant for foreign workers and ordinary citizens, he probably didn't have overly high expectations for applicants.

Once you became a mage, you probably wouldn't want for money, right?
