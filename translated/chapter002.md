## Chapter 2: A Migrant Worker's Understanding of Red Dragons

Around ten in the morning, the sun still hadn't fully risen, and the canal was packed with small boats hauling stone and timber. Workers were reinforcing the embankment, and Jace slipped across the makeshift wooden bridge they'd put up for the job.

Just ahead was the Mage Quarter, the district in the southwest corner of Stormwind City. Aside from the Dwarven District and Old Town, it was the best-preserved part of the city. From a distance you could already make out the spot where Stormwind's portal stood in the game — the Wizard's Sanctum.

Its tower thrust up past the treetops and rooftops, high into the sky, answering the spire of the Cathedral of Light to the north — still under construction — and the royal towers of Stormwind Keep off toward the docks.

The locals said the Mage Quarter had survived because the mages had left behind a web of terrible curses and magical traps that kept the orcs and ogres from burning and smashing their way in.

But as far as Jace was concerned, if those mages had enough power to throw up a barrier that could stop an entire ogre army, Stormwind would never have fallen in the first place.

The ogre tribe that had taken Stormwind was led by Cho'gall, and he wasn't the brutish ogre most people pictured. Even back on Draenor he'd shown a sharp mind, mastering magic and even the gift of prophecy.

A mind like that wouldn't have passed up a treasure house stuffed with centuries of history and magical artifacts. He must have given the order to keep the place as intact as possible — which was likely the only reason this district had survived more or less unscathed.

Jace wound his way through the shade and the eaves-shadowed alleys, past a few old-fashioned shops and houses, until he reached the lawn that led straight to the spiraling steps of the Wizard's Sanctum.

The smell of earth and old paper hung in the air the whole way, and the heavy, damp weather gave everything a campus-like feel.

Walking up, he kept catching young mages in light-purple and blue robes glancing his way. Jace looked down at himself — a linen shirt and a pair of trousers with the cuffs rolled up, no different from the bridge-repair crews outside — and realized he really did stick out in a place with this much academic atmosphere.

Then again, it didn't feel all that unnatural. Had everyone here walked in on their first day wearing silk robes with spellbooks and rune tomes in their arms?

After asking a few apprentices reading by the road, he found Eden Malin's place — only to discover a handful of young people about his age already standing at the door when he arrived.

These fellows were all dressed neatly, radiating that bookish air, the kind you could tell at a glance had never done a hard day's work.

By contrast, Jace had spent months laboring around the Trade District and Cathedral Square, or heading out into Elwynn Forest. Even in this relatively damp part of town, the sun had baked his skin red, and his hands were noticeably more calloused.

On top of that, he was lean — the kind of build that would've been more popular in the 21st century but didn't exactly win him points here.

Generally speaking, the sort of people who made it into a magic school came from comfortable families who'd never gone hungry.

Magic wasn't a path that bore fruit overnight. Ordinary families preferred to steer their children toward skills with more visible returns.

Let alone the fact that most magical institutions across the Alliance were tangled up in the connections and investments of nobles and merchants, which squeezed out the chances for ordinary families. Jace had learned that lesson deeply back in Lordaeron.

When he was little, he'd wanted to apprentice at the Royal Academy of the Arcane in Lordaeron's capital. His parents were willing to support him, but the name alone made it clear that an institution like that wasn't meant for a commoner like him. He'd been turned away again and again.

As for the magical city of Dalaran, far across the southern shore of Lordamere Lake… even if Dalaran was willing to take any human or elf with an interest in magic, by the time Jace reached school age, the Dark Portal had already opened.

Rumor had it that orc war ships had already closed in on the Hillsbrad Foothills, and scouts had slipped into the vast forests south of Dalaran.

His father had been recalled to duty, his mother needed to tend the farm, and a ten-year-old making the long trek alone from Tirisfal Glades straight across Silverpine Forest to Dalaran… he wouldn't have stood a chance.

By the time the war was over, he was nearly eighteen.

Approaching the mage's door, he could faintly make out voices from inside. He tried to listen in, hoping to get a sense of what they were discussing and prepare himself a little, but he couldn't make out a word.

The wooden door flew open and gave Jace a start. A young man came out wearing a look of disappointment — the interview clearly hadn't gone well.

"Next," came a middle-aged man's deep voice from inside.

Jace glanced back at the others. The rest of the applicants were staring at him, but nobody answered Malin's call.

"Where's the next one?"

"Here, Mr. Malin."

Jace shot a look at these cowards who'd been watching the show, answered, and turned to walk inside, closing the door behind him.

"Please, call me Master Malin."

Eden Malin looked up as he said it. He appeared to be in his forties or fifties, his brown-gray hair already showing signs of thinning, a deliberately grown beard hanging all the way to his chest — a bit unkempt, overall.

"Yes, Master Malin," Jace said respectfully, then dropped himself onto a stool nearby.

Malin shot him a glance, twirled the gryphon quill in his hand, and asked, "Your name?"

"Jace Sesso."

"I'm not familiar with that name."

"I'm from out of town. Lordaeron."

"But your accent doesn't sound like you're from the Menethil capital either."

"My mother is from Brill. I spent most of my time with her."

"What town?"

"Brill, a town in northern Lordaeron."

"Ah… Brill, Brill. Your pronunciation takes some getting used to. I know Brill. A lovely place. The town center is built like a small church, quite striking."

Jace could tell that Eden Malin couldn't care less about a town-center building that looked like a small church, or whether Brill was lovely at all. He'd said it all with a straight face, out of pure habit — the kind of automatic politeness that meant nothing.

"Where did you graduate? The Menethil Academy of the Arcane, or the University of Stratholme? Have you been to Dalaran?"

"I… ahem." Jace cleared his throat. "I only studied at a church school in Lordaeron."

"I see."

Malin set down his pen, placed both hands on the desk, and looked at Jace.

Jace knew that look well enough. This interview was as good as dead.

"So why are you applying for this assistant position? You don't quite meet my requirements."

"For the money," Jace said.

"Honest enough, Se—" Malin glanced at his notebook. "Mr. Sesso."

"But I would prefer to hire someone with a foundational level of learning. Communication would be simpler that way, because the work here inevitably involves rather complex matters… I am not disparaging your background. You have clearly given much to Stormwind's reconstruction, and for that I must offer my deepest respect."

"You could try asking me a few questions, Master," Jace said. "I think I can handle the work."

"Very well." The mage's eyes drifted toward a bookshelf not far away. "Tell me about the Kirin Tor. You know what the Kirin Tor is, I assume."

"What would you like to know?"

"Talk freely, or rather, introduce the concept of the Kirin Tor."

With that, Eden Malin leaned back in his chair, looking ready to enjoy the show.

"Understood." Jace took a breath. "The Kirin Tor is the mage council of Dalaran, governed by the Council of Six. It's one of the most powerful elite circles of sorcerers in the world. Historically, the Kirin Tor was once an organization removed from ordinary politics, but it later became the ruling body of Dalaran. The Kirin Tor maintained a friendly alliance with Silvermoon City of Quel'Thalas for over two thousand years. There have even been elven members on the Council of Six, such as Kael'thas Sunstrider, the blood elves'—"

"Wait."

Malin cut Jace's speech short. He furrowed his brow and mulled something over.

"Is Prince Kael'thas no longer a member of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six? When did that happen? And what are 'blood elves' — did you mean Sin'dorei? Sin does mean blood, lineage, in Thalassian. Sin'dorei literally means 'noble descendants of blood,' and of course it can also be translated as 'blood elves,' but we must not understand it that way. In fact, I'm not sure which translator's work you encountered this term in…"

Jace realized he'd accidentally let slip something that hadn't happened yet. Fortunately, the old mage had already talked his way around it on his own.

"Come to think of it, does the Kirin Tor have over two thousand years of history? I hadn't realized the Kirin Tor existed when Dalaran was first founded." Malin stood, walked to the bookshelf, and pulled out a purple-bound book marked with a Violet Eye, flipping through it.

"Hmm… the Kirin Tor was indeed an organization that took shape in the early days of Dalaran."

He nodded, waved the book in his hand, and asked, "Have you read this? *History of the Kirin Tor and Dalaran*?"

"I've read some of it," Jace said firmly, even though it was the first time he'd ever heard of the book.

Malin put the book back, cleared his throat, returned to his desk, sat down, thought for a moment, and immediately asked, "Talk to me about dragons. The dragon flights. I'm quite interested in dragons. For instance — do you think dragons are real, or just a legend the orcs put about to intimidate the Alliance armies?"

"Of course dragons are real," Jace said. "Plenty of people have mentioned red dragons burning and pillaging along the coast of the Hillsbrad Foothills."

"You're from Lordaeron, right? Have you seen a red dragon with your own eyes?" Malin asked. "Did you come here with the Stormwind refugees?"

Jace answered, "I haven't seen one myself. Only some shipwrecks burned by red dragons."

"Red dragons are evil and savage." Malin nodded. "Best not to see one in person."

As a transmigrator familiar with the lore, Jace of course knew that red dragons, for all being terrifying magical beasts, were also guardians of life — and that their leader, the queen Alexstrasza, was the protector of all living things in Azeroth.

The reason these proud red dragons had become the orcs' slaves and mounts was that Alexstrasza had been captured by the orcs with an ancient, evil artifact — the Demon Soul, which imprisoned the power of all the Dragon Aspects.

The orcs used the queen's suffering to drive all the red dragons into battle for them. To this day, the Dragonqueen was still imprisoned by this terrible artifact deep in the distant eastern Wetlands, in the cursed abandoned dwarven city of Grim Batol.

Jace knew all this clearly, of course, but he wasn't sure what Malin was driving at. Did the man genuinely believe red dragons were evil, or was he testing him — probing how much Jace actually knew about the dragon flights?

"Uh, Master."

Jace ventured carefully. Malin's eyes widened slightly, his lips parted, as if he were expecting something — and that confirmed Jace's read.

"The red dragons fighting for the orcs seems to have another cause. After all, before the Dark Portal there were very few cases of red dragons attacking human or elven towns on their own initiative… most of the time they kept to themselves."

"Then, Mr. Sesso, what do you think the red dragons' savagery is tied to?" Malin pressed eagerly. You could already hear the excitement in his words.

The Demon Soul wasn't something an ordinary citizen should know about. Even many of the Kirin Tor's elite mages firmly believed it was nothing more than an ancient legend.

Jace absolutely couldn't let slip knowledge that taboo and extreme. He could only shake his head.

The mage jotted something in his notebook, then said, "Mr. Sesso, thank you very much for coming. We've spoken for some time now. Let's leave it here."

All the way out of Eden Malin's door, Jace felt a little dazed.

If he'd told the mage what else he knew about the red dragons, landing the assistant position might have been a sure thing. But doing so would have meant running a considerable risk — letting others know he possessed knowledge that someone of his background and age had no business having.

If it had been an ordinary friend or some random passerby, it wouldn't have mattered — most people knew a few garbled country legends, and nobody would have thought much of it. But the person across from him was a mage.

Jace let out a long breath. Better not draw too much attention right now. If anyone found out he could foresee the future, it definitely wouldn't end well.

The thought even made him worry that the Bronze Dragonflight, the keepers of time, might suddenly appear at his side and crush him with some strange magic.

After confirming he hadn't triggered some kind of time-stream crisis, he wandered around the Mage Quarter for a while. The garden setting and the quiet atmosphere eased his mood a bit.

As for the interview — the conversation hadn't touched on pay, hadn't gone over the job duties, and hadn't probed much into Jace's abilities. It was probably a no.

All he could do was keep things in perspective and not long too hard for something that wasn't meant to be his.

While he was at it, he might as well ask around somewhere else if there was any work going. Throw your resume at enough places and something would stick, right? Even an hour-by-hour gig would do. In his current situation, he wasn't in a position to be picky.

Two tailor shops, the moment they heard he wasn't there to buy clothes but to look for work, immediately pulled a long face and shoved him out the door. One of the tailors was even from Darrowshire — and his attitude was worse than that Duncan fellow's, giving a fellow countryman no quarter at all.

Just then, a tavern sign in an alley caught his eye.

"The Slaughtered Lamb."
