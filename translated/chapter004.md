## Chapter 4: The Howl in the Deep Woods

"I wandered around the Mage Quarter a bit," Jace answered Grede's question, tearing off another chunk of bread.

The building where he rented was right in front of him now. If Grede tried to drag him out for drinks and an overnight, there was no way he was agreeing to that.

Honestly, he didn't want to agree to anything right now. He didn't even want to deal with Grede. He just wanted to charge into his shitty little room and pass out. After the hell he'd been through, he felt like he could collapse any second if he didn't lie down.

Grede gave him a once-over and muttered under his breath, "The Mage Quarter? Look at you, you look like you've been wrung out. Anyone'd think you'd been to Old Town."

Jace nudged Grede's chest with the baguette he'd been gnawing on. "I'm not you. Come on, what do you want?"

Grede glanced at the workers lying around on both sides and lowered his voice. "Got a job, of course."

"Can't. Can't do it." Jace waved a hand. "I can't do a damn thing today."

"Westfall Keep, tomorrow. One silver and thirty copper for the round trip. You in or not? No walking — we take a wagon. You can nap on the way."

"Escorting grain?"

"Military rations, yeah. Coming from Moonbrook, headed to Lakeshire — supplies for the expeditionary force at Nethergarde Keep. Things are getting complicated around the Dark Portal. Looks like a fight's brewing. But we only need to escort them as far as Goldshire this trip. With soldiers along, no worries about safety."

"What if orcs ambush us? Throwing your life away for a single silver?"

"What orcs at Westfall Keep? Let the next lot worry about that!" The dwarf climbed the steps. "Orcs come from the Redridge side. Westfall and Moonbrook have better security than we do here, lad. Plus we've got soldiers with us — this money's free money!"

"Not bad, then."

Jace let out an exhausted laugh. With this extra silver, rent was covered. Solid.

At noon the next day, the two of them signed in and joined the wagon train heading to Westfall Keep.

Just like Grede had said, they ran into nothing on the way there. No wolves, no murlocs, no kobolds, no orcs — nothing. Elwynn Forest was peaceful and calm.

The grain shipment, though — it should've reached the keep around three in the afternoon, but it got delayed by hours.

By evening, the red sunlight filtered through the trees onto the tent tops pitched beside Westfall Keep, making the riverside camp look like rows of freshly baked pudding cakes.

The wagons finally arrived. The quartermaster had a talk with the soldiers — sounded like they'd run into some trouble over at Sentinel Hill.

No more time to wait. The hired guards and soldiers strapped on their armor and grabbed their weapons. It was already late — they had to move out fast.

Six soldiers escorted the wagon train, fully armed and equipped with longswords and Stormwind lion-emblem shields, wearing intimidating blue-tasseled full nasal helmets that looked expensive just to look at.

Add in twenty-odd armed militia like Jace and Grede who'd been recruited to follow along, and any half-brained bandit would think twice about making a move on a few wagons of grain.

But the wagons were empty on the way back, and the soldiers had to walk. Everyone's guard was down a bit.

A few rookies on their first escort duty were talking about gray wolves and wild bears in the forest, going on about how these beasts might go mad with hunger and attack the wagon train. Jace had faced off against a few wolves back when he was hauling lumber at Eastvale Logging Camp, but he'd never been in a real fight either.

Listening to them, he felt a chill run through him as he looked toward that forest.

Before this, his mind had been completely full of that Curse of Agony he'd suffered in the basement of The Slaughtered Lamb. He hadn't even stopped to think about what the name "Westfall Keep" actually meant.

Then, from deep in the woods, came a lone howl. The quartermaster overseeing the grain transport blew his whistle, signaling everyone to prepare to move out.

That howl suddenly stirred a memory from over a decade ago in Jace.

Wasn't this Westfall Keep — the one overrun with gnolls?

Gnolls are humanoid creatures that live all across the Kingdom of Stormwind. A lot of young folks can't tell the difference between a gnoll and a kobold.

If kobolds are a bunch of rotten thieves, then gnolls are a pack of bloodthirsty bandits. The two can't be compared in size, fighting ability, or viciousness — not even close.

Several gnoll tribes had once united and clashed with the Kingdom of Stormwind in a full-scale war.

They'd ultimately been no match for human regulars and scattered, nearly vanishing from Elwynn Forest into the surrounding mountains and wilds. But after decades of recovery, these savage, semi-intelligent creatures had crept back, crossing rivers and bridges from every direction to infiltrate Elwynn Forest once more.

Right now, Westfall Keep was still in the post-Orc Wars reconstruction period. But a decade or so from now, it would become an outpost against the Riverpaw tribe living in Westfall.

Jace jogged a few steps to catch up with a Stormwind guard. "Have the grain wagons been attacked by gnolls before?"

The guard tilted his head. "Gnolls? A few years back, when the orc clans attacked Westfall, they killed one of the Riverpaw tribe's leaders. Now the Riverpaw don't have a chief — they can't organize enough to harass us anymore. Of course, you still don't want to go near the riverbank alone. That's still dangerous."

The Riverpaw tribe had no chief?

Jace nodded and slowly made his way back to his position. He watched the shadows darken under the trees and felt like something was off.

If the Riverpaw tribe had no chief, then when did Hogger show up?

This gnoll chieftain who terrified countless Alliance novices in World of Warcraft had repelled Stormwind's manhunt operations again and again.

Had he been born yet? If so, how old was he? Was he even an adult?

A cool breeze swept by. A rare bit of relief from the sweltering summer heat. Grede hissed comfortably and scratched his neck.

The breeze drifted through the trees, leaves rustling, the air a little humid.

Jace thought he heard the sound of metal scraping. Gnolls?

Unlike the rhythmic footsteps of the armored soldiers, those noises were chaotic and frantic, like something chasing after prey. But when the wind died down, everything went quiet again.

The aftereffects of the Curse of Agony — the ringing in his ears, the nausea — still lingered even after a full night. Jace didn't dare jump to conclusions about enemies being near.

Besides, gnolls hadn't really attacked humans since the war ended. It couldn't happen now, right when he was on a mission? What were the odds?

Right as he took a deep breath and tried to push the nausea down, he heard a BANG from the other side of the wagon.

Something had struck the wagon.

"What was that?" the quartermaster up front called back.

Whoosh...

Thunk!

Whoosh... thwump!

"Ahh!"

Whoosh...

"By the Holy Light... it's orc crossbow bolts! Orc attack!" Shouts erupted from the front, from the back, everywhere.

"Orcs?!" Grede gripped his woodcutter's axe with both hands, looking around. "Where?! I thought there were no orcs here!"

"Ha! Lalalala!"

"Alalala!"

"Wadadada!"

Bizarre howls exploded from the pitch-black woods. Twisted black shadows rolled out from behind trees, from the brush, from boulders and under railings.

A militia man was clutching the crossbow bolt in his neck and trying to flee when a shadow rushed at him head-on and took his head off with a blade. His body actually took a few more steps before it collapsed.

"Damn it, these aren't orcs — they're gnolls!" Grede muttered something in Dwarvish, leaning against the wagon. He turned and climbed up, standing on the sacks of grain with his axe raised high. "Come at me, you half-brained mutts!"

The gnolls seemed to understand Common. Hearing the dwarf's sudden barrage of insults, three or four of them shouted and converged this way.

They wore crudely made scrap armor and carried all sorts of weapons looted from orcs and humans. Their fighting style was a complete mess but absolutely berserk — no hesitation whatsoever. Except for the few Stormwind guards who fought back-to-back in pairs, most of the militia were scattered in an instant.

A gnoll climbed onto the wagon. Grede split its skull with one swing of his axe, and it shuddered and slid right off. But the dwarf hadn't kept a good grip — the axe was buried in the gnoll's skull and went down with it.

At the same time, another gnoll scrambled up and swung a small hammer at him. The dwarf caught its wrist bare-handed, and the two of them locked into a raw test of strength.

The berserk gnoll — fighting a dwarf that was actually shorter than itself — somehow held its ground. Grede was howling at the top of his lungs, completely unaware that a smaller gnoll with a meat cleaver had climbed onto the wagon behind him.

"Behind you, Grede!" Jace circled around and drove his sword into the small gnoll's side. It yelped and scrambled on all fours, trying to climb away from Jace's attack. That stab to the side barely slowed it down.

Jace swung again and only caught the gnoll's leg. It lost its footing and the dwarf kicked it off the wagon into the mud below.

By now the rain was coming down hard. Jace swept the hair from his forehead and eyes with his arm and walked over, pointing his sword at the small gnoll's chest.

"If I don't kill you, you'll kill me..." he muttered to himself, then drove the sword in hard.

The gnoll, run through the chest, suddenly lunged up like a rabid dog and bolted toward the river, coughing violently. It gave Jace a huge shock — he hadn't expected something stabbed through the chest to still have that much fight in it.

"Jace, hurry! Get my axe back — I can't hold him off much longer!"

Watching Grede still arm-wrestling the tall gnoll on the wagon, Jace stopped worrying about anything else. He ran to the gnoll that had fallen off with its skull split open, planted a foot on its neck, and pulled the axe out with all his strength. There was a crack — and the gnoll's head snapped clean off its neck.

"Catch!"

Jace hurled the axe up onto the wagon. Grede caught it without even looking and drove the butt of the axe into the gnoll's eye socket.

The gnoll howled and staggered back two steps before tumbling off the wagon. Grede roared and leaped down after it. A wet, metallic flurry of sounds — and then silence from that direction.

Jace gripped his sword, wanting to see if anyone on this side needed help. He'd barely glanced around when a Stormwind nasal helmet — still with a head inside it — came flying out of nowhere and smashed into the wagon wheel in front of him. Blood spattered across his face.

What the hell?

He looked in the direction the head had come from. Through the rain, a massive silhouette stood on top of a headless corpse, wielding an enormous iron axe, bringing it down again and again on a Stormwind guard. The guard's helmet was already full of blood. He probably couldn't hold on much longer.

Jace didn't think twice. He hurled his sword. The slightly bent piece of junk spun in the air and smacked right into the monster's absurdly huge orcish pauldron, bounced off, and stuck in the dirt.

The giant gnoll turned its head. Its features slowly came into focus.

Its expression was hard to call fierce — "idiotic" was closer to the mark. But the intimidation its size brought couldn't be offset by a pair of dim-witted eyes.

Jace felt his heart pounding so hard it was about to burst out of his chest. The lingering misery from the Curse of Agony had vanished in an instant.

Then the gnoll let out a cold snicker and muttered a single word: "Hogger."
