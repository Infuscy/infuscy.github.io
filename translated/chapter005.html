Chapter 5: "Former" Gryphon Rider

"H-Hogger? Did I hear that right?"

Jace swore under his breath in his native language, then turned and felt around for any weapon dropped by the gnolls or soldiers — all he came up with was the cleaver that small gnoll had been carrying.

But the blade was far too short, useless for blocking or thrusting. The gnoll's weapon appeared to a human-forged battle axe with a head bigger than his own skull; if that came swinging at him, wouldn't he be cleaved in two on the spot?

"Grede! Get over here, damn it!" Jace shouted. A rustle of movement behind him — he couldn't tell if it was Grede or someone else, but they were already at his side.

Jace grabbed him by the beard. "Look at that thing. How do we fight that, huh?! How?!"

Grede seized Jace by the collar and said in a low voice, "We can't take him. Run?"

"You run — you think you can outrun a gnoll? Where's your honor?!"

"What good is honor when you're dead?! …But you're right. Running… we wouldn't make it anyway."

Jace glanced around. Plenty of the militia were fighting exhausted, and the guards could barely stay upright. One who'd tried to flee into the forest had already been pinned to a distant tree by a spear…

He clenched his teeth and watched the massive gnoll bearing down on them; the guard who'd been holding it off was now sprawled on the ground, not moving.

Grede hefted his axe, ripped off his eye patch, and fully revealed the long scar that ran all the way from his forehead to the corner of his mouth.

He said in a suppressed voice, "There's a chance we can take the bastard down."

"Nothing else for it, then," Jace agreed.

"Dying alongside you wouldn't be the worst thing, pink-skin!"

"We might not die. Let's go."

The gnoll chieftain's pace quickened. He spun his axe and charged straight at the two of them. The dwarf and the human threw themselves apart in either direction. Hogger's axe smashed into the wagon railing and shook the whole carriage with the impact; a wheel bounced off with a clang. The wagon rocked and tipped sideways; sacks of grain spilled across the ground.

Hogger turned and swung his axe at Jace. Jace hugged a grain sack to himself; Hogger split it clean in two from the middle, wheat scattering everywhere. Even buffered by the sack, the impact went right through Jace's bones and left his whole body tingling. This monster's strength was on another level from a human's!

Just as Hogger drew back for a second strike, his whole body shuddered. He turned, trying to claw at something. He spun a full circle, and there was Grede gripping his axe with both hands, the blade buried in Hogger's backplate. In the light of the burning torch, the blood seeping out was as vivid as red ink!

"Can we kill him?!"

Jace muttered to himself. He picked up the fallen cleaver, stumbled forward, and drove it hard into the gnoll's flank… only to find that the cleaver's tip wasn't sharp enough and his strength, spent in the chaos, had fallen short. The blade punched through the gnoll's leather waist-guard, then slipped off the slick, wet coarse hide, deflected by the bristling fur. It only broke the skin — it didn't pierce the flesh at all!

Hogger snarled in pain and kicked Jace away with one foot. His whole body lurched and flung Grede swinging through the air. With his other hand he clutched and caught Grede by the braids!

"Ow!"

Grede yelped and was yanked off his feet. His axe came loose from his grip. Hogger, with the axe still embedded in his spine, moved a little strangely — but he was still fast enough, and more than strong enough.

He held Grede aloft with one hand and reached for the axe he'd tossed aside with the other. Grede sank his teeth into the gnoll's claw; Hogger growled in pain but still didn't let go.

Jace rubbed his kicked belly, gritted his teeth, and slipped over to the two-handed woodcutter's axe. He picked it up, gripped it with both hands, took a running start, and buried it with what strength he had left into Hogger's hind leg with a roar. The blow didn't sever the leg; it only gouged a bloody gash in the bone.

Hogger grunted and crashed to the ground. Grede wrenched himself free.

"While he's down, finish him!" Jace tossed the axe to Grede. Grede roared something in Dwarvish, caught it mid-swing, and brought it down hard — but Hogger lifted his head and rammed straight into the dwarf's chest, sending him flying onto the wagon. He lay there mumbling something, his mind seemingly gone. He tried to get up several times, only to slip on the rain and mud and fall again.

Somehow Hogger hauled himself back up, retrieved the dwarf's weapon, and now wielding a large axe and a small one in either hand, limped toward them, one leg dragging.

Jace's heroic resolve froze solid on his face. So this was how he died — for a single silver.

He grabbed a militia man's long staff off the ground and tried to hold a guarding stance. Hogger swayed his axe — and then, in a swirl of dust and smoke, turned into a sheep.

Polymorph?

Jace looked up the road. Through the torrential rain, a man in purple robes was riding toward them on a white horse, staff held high.

Was that Gandalf… or… Archmage Antonidas?

When the mage drew closer, Jace finally made out his features. It was Marin!

He shouted an incantation. The crystal head of his staff at the tip steadily blazed with arcane light as he chanted, illuminating the road and the woods on both sides.

The gnolls still fighting were caught in the light. When they looked to the center and saw their chieftain had turned into a sheep crouching by the roadside chewing grass, panic spread through their ranks. No small number of them covered their eyes and retreated into the forest.

Marin called out, "Polymorph won't hold for long, Mr. Sesso! Minutes — maybe even seconds… The gnolls have a strong resistance to my offensive magic. Once the spell wears off, I won't be able to handle him either."

"Then we need to get out of here fast. There might be more gnolls headed this way." As Jace said it, he suddenly remembered the guard who'd been knocked down by the gnoll chieftain. He pulled off the man's helmet — he was still breathing.

"Master Marin, take him!"

At Jace's shout, Marin dismounted and took one look at the guard's bloodied face. He gestured east with a wave. "Get him across the horse's back. Head east, quickly — you'll run into a sentry post soon. I'll come around from that direction."

"Grede! We need to move!" Jace shouted at the still-dazed dwarf, calling to the men and the guards who could still move. Together they stripped the wounded soldier of his armor and got him onto the horse.

Those who could still walk followed Marin's glowing magic light with the wounded. With their chieftain turned into a sheep, the gnolls had no will to pursue. The group marched briskly for ten minutes until they saw the torches of a sentry post, and only then did they slow down.

The militiamen who could no longer walk collapsed against the railing. Jace threw his head back and took a deep breath, and only then felt like he could finally breathe again.

Rain drummed against his forehead, slowly bringing him back to his senses.

The sentries who'd come from the post took hold of Marin's white horse and called for help carrying and tending the wounded. Marin, having a spare moment, looked over at them and said to Jace, "I need to help these lads. You'd best leave quickly too — there are still wolves in these woods."

"How did you know we were in danger?"

"I didn't," Marin said. "I was actually looking for you. I asked the labor-recruiting guards for your name. One of the workers said you'd headed to Westfall. I thought you'd gone to Moonbrook looking for work, so I was planning to ride out and talk you out of it. I didn't want to miss out on a helper like you. I just didn't expect a gnoll attack — as it turned out, it worked out perfectly to lend you a hand. What a coincidence."

"I don't know how to thank you, Master. So… you're hiring me?"

Marin nodded. "Come find me in the Mage Quarter once you've recovered to discuss compensation. Same place as before, Mr. Sesso. And regarding the red dragons — I'd still prefer you didn't hold anything back."

"Understood." Jace hadn't expected Marin to see that he was still hiding something. But when it came to the Demon Soul, he had no intention of spilling everything so soon. He'd need to come up with a reasonable explanation first.

The mage gradually faded into the rain. The dwarf lay sprawled on the ground and said quietly, "I can't grip my axe anymore, Jace."

"We're safe." Jace stared into the pitch-black forest, still unwilling to let his guard down — though he was so exhausted he could barely keep his eyes open.

"What I mean is I can't grip my axe, damn it!" Grede swore. Jace started at the shout and looked over to see that Grede's hands were still shaking.

Recalling the chaotic fight just now, Jace did remember Grede losing his grip on his axe several times, each time nearly getting himself killed.

But that didn't change the fact that Grede was a fine fighter. Without this dwarf, he and the soldiers who'd been saved might not have survived.

The dwarf said, his words slurred: "The human priest in Southshore told me those gods-damned bats the trolls raise got poison into my brain. I might never be able to fly again. Gotsrum was right — I'll never ride a gryphon again, never throw a war hammer again. I'm useless now, Jace."

Hearing the dwarf's words, Jace remembered the first time the two of them had met in Southshore — Grede lying motionless on the surface of the sea like a sack.

Not long before, a naval battle between orcs and the Alliance had taken place off the Hillsbrad Foothills. Floating debris often drifted close to shore, so the people on the dock hadn't even recognized it as a living person — they thought it was some waterlogged cargo or other.

Back then, Jace had been waiting near the dock for a refugee ship to pull in. He happened to notice that bulging thing let out a bubble, and only then did those tangled messes of rope-like things register as a dwarf's braids!

Jace had jumped into the water without hesitation and dragged Grede ashore — only for the bastard to punch and kick him, then break down sobbing.

Even though Grede later explained it as him thinking he'd run into a shark and fighting for his life, crying because he thought he was going to die — only now did Jace finally understand what had really happened that day.

The rain came down harder. Grede's disheveled hair was plastered to his scalp. He stared vacantly at the distant firelight, silent for a long while.

"That mage just now was willing to take me as an apprentice," Jace said. "A lot of the scholars in the Mage Quarter know herbalism. You think there's any chance they could cure your poison?"

"It's too late, brother," Grede said flatly. "The toxin has long since run its course. What's made me a cripple now are the aftereffects of the poison. It's permanent — there's no fixing it."

"But I'm still grateful, Jace. You've saved my life twice now, and you're still thinking about finding a cure for me."

"You saved my life too."

"A dwarf saving a human's life is the most ordinary thing there is. The other way around — that's something." Grede gave a half-laugh. "Besides, a true Wildhammer warrior would never let himself and his brother die at the hands of a two-legged dog."

"Even a dog that chases people with an axe."

"Ha… cough! cough!"
