## Chapter 7: The Difference Between an Apprentice and an Assistant

That morning, Jace arrived at the Mage Quarter. After resting for a few days, he figured he was presentable again.

He still had some bruises on his face, and his walk was still a bit awkward, but at least he'd taken the bandages off his arm. On the surface, he looked like a normal person — a man ready to re-enter the land of the living.

He came to the Mage Quarter and knocked on Eden Marin's door. Master Marin wasn't in; a young woman in her twenties was cleaning the room.

She glanced over. Her face was somewhat handsome, with clearly defined features, her dark-brown hair combed neatly back. The moment Jace saw her, she struck him as looking a lot like Marin.

"Hello — you're Mr. Jace Sesso, aren't you? Father's away in the Redridge Mountains. He might be gone for a week. He mentioned you might be coming by soon, though he didn't mention you were so young…"

"You're Master Marin's daughter?"

"That's right. I'm Amy Marin, an apprentice mage at the Wizard Sanctum. Come to think of it, you ran into gnolls near Westfall Keep a few days ago, at night — is that true?"

"It is. If it weren't for Master Marin, I might not be alive."

Amy Marin said with concern, "I didn't expect the gnolls to come back at a time like this. The orcs caused serious damage to their tribe when they invaded this land, and the gnolls benefited when the orcs were driven back. I never thought they'd turn around and attack us. Despicable."

"Maybe their intelligence doesn't even reach the level of gratitude or cooperation," Jace said.

"I'd like to treat them as pure beasts too, but gnolls have warlocks. Even if dark magic accepts anyone willing to accept it, you still need a certain level of intelligence to comprehend incantations. Sorry, I'm getting off track — that experience left me a little shaken. I was worried Father might get hurt. He told me to help you get familiar with the work when you arrived. Let's start here…"

Watching Amy set down what she was holding and wash her hands in the basin nearby, Jace said, "Actually, I came today to discuss compensation."

Amy dried her hands and asked, "You don't know the pay for a mage assistant at the Wizard Sanctum? Oh, right — you're from the north, aren't you? Your accent sounds like you're from the Kingdom of Lordaeron."

Jace hummed an acknowledgment. When he'd first arrived in this world, he'd learned Common carrying memories of Chinese and English, and he'd gone to some trouble to avoid a strange accent. Back then, he never would've expected that when he grew up, everyone could tell he was from Lordaeron just by his voice.

Amy said, "Then let me give you a brief rundown and see what you think. Six silver a month. For meals, you can eat in the Wizard Sanctum's dining hall alongside the mages. As for housing, the Mage Quarter has free dormitories for apprentices and assistants. They've been full this whole time, but because of the Expedition, some apprentices followed their mentors to Nethergarde Keep, so there might be openings. I'll keep an eye out for you. You could move in as early as next month."

"Thank you — I really appreciate it."

"Don't mention it. If anything, I should be thanking you for helping my father."

"Is food free too?"

"It is, but it might be very monotonous — just a few pastries and bread. If you want something nicer, you can go to a tavern outside. I wouldn't recommend the Slaughtered Lamb, though. I don't care for the atmosphere there — something feels off."

From Amy's description, this was more or less what Jace had expected. One more month in his rented room — he could handle paying one more month's rent. After all, he'd be living in a free dormitory from then on.

Still, he'd been expecting a bit higher pay, especially working in the Mage Quarter. But six silver was already better than a lot of places in the city.

Most importantly, he'd gotten a chance to become a mage.

"Next, the work." Amy picked up the mop. "Every morning, before my father arrives — around this time — his study needs to be cleaned. That includes mopping and dusting. After the ogres came through here, there's way more dust than when I was little. As for the books, you generally don't need to organize those — Father puts them away himself. Compared to organizing the library… this is much easier."

Cleaning a study and dusting — none of that was a problem for Jace. Back in middle school, he'd been the sanitation monitor for several years.

"The specifics of the work, my father will explain in detail when he's back. Today you can start by cleaning up here." She wiped the sweat from her forehead and breathed a sigh of relief. "Honestly, it's nice to finally have someone helping out. Ever since Father sent his apprentice to the Redridge Mountains, all this work's fallen on me."

"No problem at all, ma'am." Jace couldn't help but ask, "Actually, I wanted to know — when can I become an apprentice?"

"Apprentice?" Amy blinked. "You mean… a mage apprentice at the Wizard Sanctum? Becoming my father's apprentice?"

"Yes."

"An assistant and an apprentice aren't the same thing… An assistant just helps with the mages' day-to-day work. An apprentice is there to learn magic."

Hearing Amy say it so matter-of-factly, Jace felt like he'd been struck by lightning. How was it possible that becoming a mage's assistant didn't lead to becoming an apprentice? What kind of logic was that?

So that was how it was. So that was the reason he'd been hired — Marin just wanted someone to do manual labor. He wasn't looking for a disciple at all.

Then why ask all those mystic-sounding questions? Jace felt like he'd been cheated, though it was a weird thing to feel toward an old man.

Seeing the boy looked rather surprised, Amy asked, "How old are you?"

"Seventeen. Almost eighteen."

Amy shook her head. "To become a magic apprentice, training either starts at a very young age — you'd need to enroll around eleven or twelve. If you're your age now and don't have extraordinary talent, it might already be too late to train into a qualified mage. I started studying at the Wizard Sanctum at twelve, on my father's instructions. I'm twenty-six now and still haven't become a full mage. If I haven't earned my advancement by thirty, Father will probably be disappointed."

She sighed and went on. "But there are plenty of apprentice mages in their thirties at the Wizard Sanctum — the vast majority of them became apprentices before fifteen."

"What if," Jace said, "and I mean what if — I have very strong talent? Exceptionally strong."

Amy asked, "Have you never tried casting a spell? Archmage Medivh could already skillfully use multiple schools of magic by the age of ten…"

Oh, he's got a cheat, huh? Yeah? Got the Demon Lord, the Dark Titan Sargeras, possessing him — that's something, huh? Got an eight-hundred-year-old Guardian of Tirisfal for a mother — that's something, huh?

Jace clenched his fists, feeling the agony of having no cheat — getting utterly outclassed by some brainless bloodline prodigy native to this world.

He took a deep breath, calmed himself down, and said, "Someone like me… naturally can't compare to the most powerful mage in human history. But I'd still like to give it a try. Is there any way to test my talent?"

"Of course there is," Amy said. "The most fundamental foundation of all arcane ability: establishing a connection with the ley lines."

As she spoke, she pulled a book on arcane runecraft from the shelf and turned to a page with a rune. Jace recognized it — an arcane rune.

Amy traced the rune with practiced ease onto a blank scroll, and before long she'd completed a rather complex new rune.

Now Jace was starting to feel nervous. He had a feeling he couldn't practice for ten years and reach that level of proficiency.

As the female mage drew, Jace even glanced at the top of her head to see if she'd secretly cast Arcane Intellect or some other buff on herself — otherwise this apprentice mage was ridiculous.

When she finished, Amy handed the unfurled scroll to Jace. "Please make it glow."

"What?" Jace looked at the rune scroll in his hands. "Isn't there some kind of instruction?"

Amy said, "A gifted child will, in the course of growing up, intuit the ability to resonate with the ley lines and habitually practice that resonance — the way a child bites their nails. It's not a pain; it's an addiction… so it's very hard for a child to deliberately suppress it. The high elves could be said to be the extreme manifestation of this habit. If you don't know how to do it, it probably means you truly aren't suited to become a mage."

"Is that so?"

Jace was getting annoyed. He grabbed the scroll, tried to close his eyes, and attempted to sense the so-called "ley lines."

The ley lines could be understood as Azeroth's blood vessels.

Azeroth was actually an unborn, infant Titan. The blood flowing through a Titan's veins wasn't blood at all — it was extraordinarily pure arcane energy.

The Well of Eternity that the ancient elves guarded was a wound accidentally inflicted by Aman'Thul, leader of the Pantheon and Father of Gods, when he tried to remove a parasitic evil aberration from Azeroth's body.

It could be said that every living being born on Azeroth was affected by the ley lines to varying degrees, and those beings capable of harnessing the ley lines would in turn influence the ley lines — and Azeroth itself.

Jace absolutely refused to believe he couldn't resonate with the ley lines.

Maybe his cheat was right here — awaken, supreme magical talent!

He focused his mind so intensely that he even ignored the dull ache in his lower abdomen — right where Hogger had kicked him.

But after focusing for a long time, the scroll showed not the slightest change.

Did you maybe draw the rune wrong, apprentice mage?

He wanted to ask outright but couldn't bring himself to.

Amy took back the scroll, glanced at the rune on it, and the arcane rune immediately gave off a faint whitish arcane glow, casting a faint purple sheen over every wall and object in the room.

"Like that." Amy raised her eyebrows. "I'm sorry, Mr. Jace Sesso."

Jace's head buzzed. He couldn't believe this was the result.

What kind of transmigration was this?

Not only did he have no talent — he couldn't even compare to an ordinary apprentice?

Still, the frustration lasted only a flash. Over the past decade and more, he'd grown accustomed to this kind of disappointment.

Actually, when he was little, he'd done a similar arcane test — not as reliable as this one, but it had told him something. He'd just been hoping this time might bring some change.

In the end, there was no change.

He realized the mage path was most likely a dead end.

"Thank you, Miss Amy," Jace said. "Honestly, I didn't necessarily want to become an apprentice just to learn magic. I just wanted to broaden my horizons and learn things I didn't know."

Amy narrowed her eyes and smiled. "I'm glad to hear you say that. The library is open to everyone who works in the Mage Quarter. Come here this afternoon and I'll give you an identification badge. With this badge, you'll have free access to the Wizard Sanctum."

"Wonderful. That's exactly what I was hoping for."

Even as he said it, an idea had already surfaced in his mind. He needed to go to the Slaughtered Lamb again… no, not just once.

He'd go again and again and again — not just to earn money, but for something far more important.

Dark magic wasn't the Arcane. Just as Amy had said, "dark magic accepts anyone willing to accept it."
