Chapter 9: Discount Price

Stepping out the door, Jace watched the morning foot traffic ebb and flow and realized that the people in the Mage Quarter all seemed to share a similar aesthetic — or at least follow a particular trend: blue or purple robes or short jackets, light-colored trousers, and so on. Even those who dressed differently didn't stray too far from the formula.

And Jace's threadbare linen shirt stood out like a sore thumb among the people coming and going from the Wizard Sanctum.

If he was going to make a life in the Mage Quarter, he might as well go buy some new clothes. Being mistaken for a thief inside would be no good.

Near the entrance to the Mage Quarter, right at the base of the city wall, there was a tailor shop. An old oak tree by the door had nearly grown into the stone wall, its canopy blocking the windows on the second floor and obscuring part of the shop's sign.

But it was still legible: "Duncan's — Tailoring & Ready-Made."

Jace had been here once before when he was job-hunting. Sure enough, the moment he walked in, the red-haired owner frowned.

"You again. Didn't I tell you last time there's no work for you?"

"I'm here to look at clothes, sir."

"Oh. Well, do you have any requirements?"

"What kind of clothes does a typical mage assistant wear? Could you recommend something?"

The owner adjusted his glasses and glanced at the ring on Jace's hand. "That's a rather broad question. An assistant isn't a mage, and isn't a mage apprentice either — you're free to dress however you like."

All right, all right, I get it. An assistant isn't an apprentice and isn't a mage. Does every single person in the Mage Quarter need to carefully remind me?

Muttering this to himself, Jace glanced at the bolts of fabric spread across the counter, then looked over at a few tailor's apprentices tidying up some garments nearby, feeling somewhat hesitant.

Most of the clothes here were made of fine, high-quality fabric — probably not cheap. But if he was going to shop at stores outside the district, he might as well just keep wearing this outfit. Out on the construction streets, everyone dressed about the same anyway.

His hand brushed against a light-blue outer garment that felt decent to the touch but still appeared to be made of linen.

"How much for this one?"

"Three silver, sir. If you pay four, we can custom-make a set to your measurements — a fantastic deal. You can pick it up the day after tomorrow."

Hearing that price, Jace froze. But all he said was, "Sorry, I need it now."

"Then three silver and it's yours. A real bargain."

Three silver for a crappy linen shirt? Jace shot him a look, then glanced at the other clothes displayed around the shop. This was probably the cheapest thing in the place. The Mage Quarter's cost of living really didn't suit his current finances.

"Honestly, I'm here on my employer's orders to buy clothes. Whether I buy any myself doesn't matter." He spoke stiffly. "So I have some specific requirements."

"I'm listening."

"I need an enchanted outer garment. It needs to have a certain degree of shadow resistance, and preferably fire resistance too — otherwise linen clothes catch flame too easily, which is no good in a fight."

Jace paused, then leaned in closer to the owner, who was already stunned by the previous sentence.

"You've heard about the gnolls re-entering Elwynn Forest, haven't you? Gnoll warlock magic isn't something to trifle with. I'm responsible for the master's safety, so the quality requirements are very high. If that's a problem, no worries — I'll look elsewhere."

The owner nodded slowly. "An enchanted outer garment, needs shadow and fire resistance — high requirements. Silk would be best. Silk is an animal product and blends more readily with arcane magic, bringing out the enchantment's full power better than linen. I understand. Let me fetch one for you…"

You actually have one?! This time it was Jace's turn to freeze. He'd only meant to make an outrageous request so the owner could turn him down, allowing him to leave the shop with his dignity intact. What he hadn't counted on was that this shop catered specifically to mages.

Then what should he have asked for to guarantee they wouldn't have it? A full suit of plate armor emblazoned with the golden lion sigil, with pauldrons shaped like a metal dragon baring its fangs?

The owner turned and bolted down to the basement, the very picture of a merchant who'd landed a big client. The apprentices all looked over in their direction at once. Jace, who'd been preparing to slip out, was pinned to the spot by a dozen pairs of eyes.

Before long, the owner came running back carrying an exquisitely decorated wooden box. Just the box alone was probably worth more than Jace's weight in silver — if he were chopped up and sold by the pound.

The owner excitedly, tremblingly clutched the little copper key in his hand and unlocked the lid, lifting the cover bit by bit. A faint current of air actually rushed out of the box, making his hair quiver.

Wasn't this a bit too much magic?

Inside the box lay a thin outer garment, black with a hint of pale purple. It was genuinely silk, and the craftsmanship was exquisite — the first time in his life he'd seen a garment whose quality could go toe-to-toe with the synthetic-fiber clothes from his past life.

"Dead Man's Shirt." The owner lowered his voice and said conspiratorially, "The original maker of this garment was a mage of great renown in Stormwind — but he was secretly practicing necromancy. To keep the necrotic energy from harming himself, he magically stitched this piece, which naturally possesses shadow resistance."

Here he raised his tone slightly: "The famous Royal Court Mage Nielas Aran's distinguished disciple, Alfred Veblen, was its previous owner. To counter the fire magic of orcish warlocks, he personally enchanted this garment with fire resistance — making it a perfect match for your requirements."

By this point, the owner was narrating like a bard singing a ballad: "It's just that Master Veblen fell in the decisive battle in the Hillsbrad Foothills. His grandson sold this garment to us. It's said the damage to the hem of the robe was caused by the flames of a red dragon, so we converted it from a robe into a short jacket."

By now, Jace was completely dumbfounded.

It took him a long time before he somehow managed to ask: "Didn't you say it has fire resistance? Then how did it get burned by a red dragon? Isn't that false advertising?"

"Even the strongest armor can't withstand a mountain giant's fist, sir," the owner said with a wry smile.

Fair point. Jace pressed on: "But the name is too inauspicious. If I were its owner, wouldn't that make me a dead man?"

The owner sighed. "Honestly, that's exactly why this garment has been hard to sell. But I can see your employer's budget is probably limited, so for you, this is actually a blessing in disguise! Since the name isn't very appealing, we'll give you a discount."

"How much?"

"Seven gold, and it's yours."

Jace was genuinely dumbfounded now.

"It really… really is quite cheap, isn't it?"

"Isn't it?"

"But I'm not authorized to make a decision on something this valuable. I'll need to go back and ask my employer's opinion and get a sense of the price."

"No problem at all. We're always very patient with major clients." The owner smiled, packed the garment away, and saw Jace out.

Leaving the tailor shop, the sunlight was a bit blinding.

Seeing a garment that cost seven gold, Jace found it a little hard to accept — and a lot sobering.

Since he was tight on money right now, he'd better forget about things like changing clothes. Better to stay grounded and get the potions sorted first.
