## Chapter 10: Choosing a Gathering Site

Although during his two years in Stormwind, every time he looked toward the west side of the city he could see that tall, mysterious tower standing high — and sometimes catch the purple glow or the pale white arcane light rippling from its heights — this was actually the first time he'd walked up to the foot of its steps.

At the entrance, a mage in a short robe wore a wide-brimmed wizard's hat to shield from the sun, yet had a longsword hanging at his hip.

This man was checking the badges of everyone coming and going — presumably a mage guard of the Wizard Sanctum, a so-called battle mage.

It was said that in the early stages of the war, many veteran mages had been killed because they lacked combat experience against orcish warlocks, severely weakening the forces fighting the Horde's spellcasters.

Dalaran, together with the magical organizations of the various kingdoms, began recruiting people with magical aptitude from among ordinary soldiers and training them in a short time into battle mages who excelled at martial combat and used magic to support it.

These battle mages, by the war's end, had become a remarkably effective new school.

Still, Jace had been expecting something a bit more impressive for the screening process — like an arcane membrane that would block anyone without a badge, or with a forged one, on the outside.

He showed his ring, passed through smoothly, and began climbing the spiraling stairs that wound around the giant tower all the way to the top. Before long, he'd risen above the canopy of the dense greenery in the Mage Quarter.

Looking out, all that filled his vision now were the purple tiles of the Mage Quarter and the blue tiles beyond. The soaring spire of the Cathedral of Light at the city's center rose to the east, surrounded by layer upon layer of stacked residences and tall buildings. To the north, on the hillside above the docks, sat Stormwind Keep, encircled by the noble houses that surrounded it like a father holding his children's hands, overlooking the sea.

On the walkways that spread like cracks in a white wall, crowds of workers, merchants, and wagons hauling goods and building materials flowed between the new city and the old, the traffic stretching all the way to the roads beyond the walls.

Looking further out, past the walls lay the endless Elwynn plain, the River Neltharion cutting across it, the forest stretching all the way to the distant mountains — the Redridge range.

Eastvale, which had once taken half a day to reach on foot, now seemed close enough to touch.

The view here was completely different from the game. The orientation was roughly the same, but the impact of this real landscape, the light and shade, far surpassed the colored blocks and polygons of the game.

When he was in Lordaeron, Jace hadn't felt this as strongly, because the game had never given him a Lordaeron before its destruction — he'd lacked the contrast.

But here, the collision between reality and illusion was overpoweringly intense.

Stepping through the doorway, he entered the upper section of the Wizard Sanctum — where the library was located.

Following the directions of a library warden, Jace walked down two flights of the library's spiraling staircase to the alchemy-related section.

It was only upon arriving here that he realized his worries about clothing had been needless.

The apprentices and mages reading in the library probably didn't pay any attention to what was going on around them, and wouldn't mistake someone in rags for a thief. More likely, it was only those with nothing better to do, loitering outside, who liked to point and gossip about other people's clothes and appearances.

Thinking of this, he relaxed and began browsing the relevant books without self-consciousness.

Thankfully, his mother was the daughter of a farmer who'd received a proper education and had taught him to read and write. Combined with the education he'd received in his past life, which had given him a solid ability to absorb new knowledge, he was able to make sense of these specialized texts.

Still, matching up the complex herbs in these books with their in-game names required the help of illustrations.

"Shadow Protection Potion… Shadow Protection Potion…"

He searched through various indexes but couldn't find any relevant entries. The search engines he'd grown used to were so convenient — type in a keyword and a flood of related and unrelated results would come up. Returning to this classical method of finding things left him flustered.

After searching for a while, he realized he'd been looking in the wrong section. In fact, many things related to shadow magic were in the dark magic protection shelves, which also included many kinds of shadow-resistant potions and spells.

In a heavily annotated copy of *Laurel's Shadow Protection Techniques*, he found a relatively easy-to-make Shadow Protection Potion recipe and brewing process.

— *Laurel's Lesser Shadow Protection Potion*, improved by Master Laurel Hagar from the standard Shadow Protection Potion formula, designed for emergencies.

Its characteristics: simple to make, materials easy to obtain, short brewing time, yet still capable of providing the user with a degree of shadow protection and reducing the shadow damage the user takes.

Recipe:
1. Grave Moss — the base material of Shadow Protection Potion. The fastest way to obtain shadow energy is through this terrifying plant that absorbs necrotic power in graveyards. Somewhat toxic.
2. Kingsblood — an important medium that transforms harmful effects into enhancing effects. Kingsblood can convert the shadow energy of Grave Moss so that it instead absorbs shadow damage.
3. Evil Claw — the claws of beasts that have attacked and killed humanoids. Generally, taking the fangs of an older predatory beast near a humanoid settlement will do. Can weaken the potency while shortening the brewing time.
4. Clean spring water, far from any magical source.

Brewing steps:
1. Set up the crucible, do not light the fire. Pour in the clean spring water.
2. Add the Grave Moss to the water.
3. Light the fire and bring to a boil.
4. When the water turns pale purple and you smell a fishy odor, begin gently stirring for twenty minutes.
5. As the water gradually turns deep purple, add a spoonful of sun-dried, ground Kingsblood powder to the pot.
6. Stop the fire. Wait until the cloudy shadow energy becomes translucent and stable.
7. Light the fire again and bring to a boil.
8. Add Evil Claw powder, ground as fine as dust — no particles larger than a grain of sand allowed.
9. Gently stir for more than ten minutes, then extinguish the fire and let it cool.

Note: The potion must be stored in a lead bottle or a specially made magic crystal bottle. A small bottle is taken in two doses — one dose lasts about an hour, or fails immediately after taking a sufficient degree of shadow damage. In the last ten minutes before the effect wears off, the potency will gradually weaken.

Note 2: The finished product should be a reddish purple, more transparent than a standard Shadow Protection Potion. The taste is harsh and acidic; the initial sip may cause dizziness. As the effect fades, you may feel heat in your face, tearing up, etc. — all normal.

After reading it, Jace could only think what a hassle. In the game, a standard Shadow Protection Potion required just two materials plus a bottle bought from a vendor and you could make it directly — but here, it took so many steps.

Actually, he'd already looked at the standard Shadow Protection Potion method. It was like reading the instructions in a medicine box — several times more complicated than this recipe.

As for gathering materials: Grave Moss, while it grew in most places with dead bodies, absolutely would not be found in graveyards near Stormwind — they'd have been picked clean by herbalists and mages long ago.

And the graveyards inside Stormwind were regularly cleared by dedicated staff, because this medicine was toxic — its toxicity could even affect innocent mourners who didn't notice it.

And to allow room for trial and error, all the materials had to be prepared in extra quantity, in case a failed brew meant starting over from scratch. So he needed a place where he could find Grave Moss in large quantities.

That left only one ideal gathering site — Duskwood.

The moment Jace thought of Duskwood, the back of his neck crawled.

This was back when he'd first arrived in Stormwind. Once, he'd gone with some men to a small dock on a tributary of the River Neltharion to help move cargo. They'd worked until evening, and he'd seen someone standing on the far bank, in Duskwood.

That person stood there for about an hour straight. By the time they were preparing to head back to Stormwind, the figure still hadn't left. And the whole time, it hadn't moved at all — only occasionally swaying a little to keep its balance.

When he came back, he asked some guards who regularly patrolled near the River Neltharion. They said it was a zombie that had wandered down from Southshire along the river — and they were already used to seeing them.

A few years ago, a massive explosion in the direction of Stranglethorn had a terrible impact on the southern part of Elwynn Forest. Enormous shadow energy had swallowed up vast stretches of woodland, enveloping two towns — Grandshire and Raven Hill — in darkness, and even affecting a stretch of Westfall.

Many people in Stormwind still spoke of the incident. Those who had fled from Grandshire and Raven Hill to Stormwind tended to stay silent. In any case, the event had cast a shadow over all the residents of the Elwynn region.

Some believed it was dark energy blowing in from Draenor, the orcs' homeland, through the Dark Portal. This guess had some basis — after all, relative to Stormwind, the direction of Stranglethorn was roughly the direction of the Dark Portal, and the timing coincided with the orcs' massive invasion.

But the problem was that the orcs, when marching, had also tried their best to avoid entering Duskwood. If this dark energy came from their homeland, wasn't that a bit contradictory?

The real reason, Jace knew crystal clear: it was the evil reverberation caused by Karazhan, an ancient magic tower standing in the valley between the great swamp where the Dark Portal was located and Duskwood.

Karazhan's master — the great mage Medivh that Amy had spoken of — had been controlled by Sargeras and had guided the orcs in opening the Dark Portal.

After several heroes — Lothar, the mage Khadgar, and the orcish assassin Garona — learned the truth, they planned and carried out the assassination of Medivh and succeeded. The terrifying power within Medivh's body had spiraled out of control at the moment of his death, causing this explosion that completely transformed the landscape for nearly a hundred miles around Karazhan.

And this was also the main plot of the Warcraft movie.

Since then, Southshire, which had once been as sunny and warm as Northshire, had become a dreadful land covered in evil brambles and black, twisted trees. Only two human towns could offer meager protection to the civilians living within.

And now, many people in Stormwind had taken to calling that region Duskwood, and Grandshire Darkshire.

Near Raven Hill there was a large cemetery — the largest graveyard in the entire Kingdom of Stormwind, where many unnamed soldiers and nobles who had fallen in the Gnoll Wars and the Troll Wars were buried.

If he could get in there, he'd definitely be able to find Grave Moss. But the problem was — how to get in alive and get out alive.

Entering Duskwood would also solve the Evil Claw problem along the way. Countless beasts there had gnawed on human corpses, and many had gone mad from absorbing too much necrotic energy, frenziedly attacking anything alive. What could be more evil than that?

As for Kingsblood — Kingsblood grew in flat areas. It was found around Lakeshire in the Redridge Mountains, or in Westfall. There should be some in the Elwynn region too, but the herb was relatively rare. He could only hope to stumble across it on the road.

Thinking of this, Jace even felt it might be better to just go straight to the Slaughtered Lamb and tank the shadow spells with his bare flesh. Honestly, it was hard to say whether venturing into Duskwood or tanking warlock magic was more dangerous.

The important difference was that the consequences of tanking warlock magic were impossible to avoid and hard to estimate, whereas on an outing, some risks could at least be mitigated.

He recorded the general travel plan and the relevant materials on the borrowed scroll with a griffin quill, feeling he needed to ask Grede's opinion — after all, he was the professional survival expert from the Hinterlands.

But first he'd go to "Alchemist's Needs" and ask the Mage Quarter's herbalist whether they bought herbs and at what rates. He'd work out a proper price before talking to Grede — that way, at least there'd be a tangible benefit on the table, and the dwarf would be more enthusiastic.

The alchemy shop "Alchemist's Needs" was very close to the Wizard Sanctum. Many people thought it was just an alchemy lab, but for most mages, it was a small shop that combined herb trading, identification, grinding, efficacy research, and a range of other work.

Most veteran mages had their own alchemy laboratories, or at least a small pharmacy, and their apprentices used their mentors' alchemy rooms. So the alchemy shop, which had been intended as a public lab from the start, had its main purpose overlooked — instead, its herb-related business grew busier and busier.

The moment he walked into the alchemy shop he was hit by a thick wave of herbal-medicine smell. A blonde middle-aged woman behind the counter turned and glanced at Jace. "New to the Mage Quarter, young man? I'm Ruth Lumere, the owner of this shop. If you're here to buy herbs, head on in — my daughter will help you. I'm a bit busy with work right now."

"I'm not here to buy herbs, miss," Jace said. "Do you buy herbs here?"

"Of course." Ruth Lumere asked, "Are you an herbalist?"

"I… yes." Jace said. "I'm from Lordaeron. I just got here and don't know the market rates yet, but I already have a team that can go out and gather herbs, so I came to see your buying prices."

"For common herbs in the Elwynn region, the general buying rates per pound are: Peacebloom 2 silver, Silverleaf 2 silver 50 copper, Earthroot 1 silver each, Briarthorn 2 silver each."

"Does Elwynn have Kingsblood?"

"If you count Southshire, yes — but very little, and hard to spot. Besides, herbalists don't go there anymore. They call it Duskwood — anyone who goes there is asking for trouble. Because of this, Grave Moss has been out of supply."

"If I could gather Grave Moss, what would you pay?"

"Seven silver a pound — how's that? And other rarer herbs, like Wild Steelbloom, Liferoot… we can negotiate on those too."

After thanking her, Jace walked out of "Alchemist's Needs" and found himself torn.

Sell the Grave Moss, or use it to make Shadow Protection Potion?

Find a way to learn magic, or make some money to live?

Standing at the foot of the Wizard Sanctum's tower, watching the mages come and go, Jace gradually felt that it'd be better to quickly master something that could save his life — or even deal damage. A signature move, so to speak.

After all, if he had greater strength, the safety of his outings would be higher too. In the future, he might be able to gather rarer herbs, or other scarce things.

If he gave up the chance to grow for the sake of a little immediate profit, he'd probably spend the rest of his life trembling every time he left the city.

Seven silver — a little profit?

He gave a bitter laugh. He hadn't expected his appetite to grow this big.

He was already thinking this much before he even knew how difficult gathering Grave Moss would be. Judging from the seven silver price, it was definitely no easy task.

But if he didn't try, he'd never know.
