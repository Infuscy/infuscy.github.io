Chapter 11: Two Young Men

Jace couldn't find Grede at the dwarf's rented room that evening, so he headed to the Pig and Whistle Inn over in Old Town.

He knew Grede drank here often — and not just Grede. Plenty of Dark Portal War veterans, plus immigrants who'd come to Stormwind from all over the world, favored this place.

Old Town — or the East Quarter, same thing — was one of the districts that had come through the war in the best shape, which made it the base of operations for the whole reconstruction effort.

Countless laborers and merchants had lived here during the early days of rebuilding, and that was what had given the Pig and Whistle its lofty reputation today.

Sure enough, the moment he walked in he spotted Grede sitting in his usual corner, drinking his usual tankard of ale. Same corner every time, same enormous mug. Jace was used to it by now.

Grede was still staring into his empty tankard, his expression grave and solemn, as if his eye had been put out by that very mug.

Jace leaned in. "Grede, I need to talk to you about something."

"Talk away, talk away." Grede belched, loud enough to make you wonder if there was a half-dead whelp living in his gut. A couple of lads and lads at the next table, who'd been laughing it up a moment ago, suddenly lowered their voices and kept sneaking glances over.

Jace frowned and sat down a little further away.

"I've got something pretty important to tell you. It's about tomorrow or the day after… are you sober right now?"

"Of course I'm sober, listen up." Grede's eyes slowly swiveled over and locked onto Jace's.

Jace felt increasingly uneasy under that stare. He had a feeling something unpleasant was about to happen.

Grede drew a slow breath, stiffened his jaw, and suddenly bellowed out in a loud, stiff, completely unnatural voice — like a man reciting lines:

"Wildhammer lads, the best you'll find! Trolls and elves, we beat 'em blind! Can't find an elf? Then trolls will do — we beat 'em black and beat 'em blue! One to the left, one to the right, two in every bloody fight! Two trolls got four eyes, four tusks a-reachin' for the skies! Knock one down, then one more, knock 'em all out and head home for sure!"

When the "opening chant" was done, Grede had become the center of attention for the entire inn.

It took Jace a long while to recover. He coughed awkwardly and waited for Grede to refocus his attention on his tankard again, sinking back into that dazed stare from before.

Only when the tavern thawed out from its frozen silence and returned to its earlier bustle did Jace finally breathe again.

Grede muttered something under his breath. Jace hadn't caught a word of it.

"What?"

"I said it went perfect."

"Perfect how?"

"That chant. I recited it perfectly."

"You call that a chant? You made that up on the spot, didn't you?"

"Come off it, I haven't got that kind of talent."

"What, talent?" Jace blinked, only realizing then that the conversation had been hijacked entirely.

He rapped his knuckles on the table to drag the dwarf's attention back. "Let's talk business. I've got a money-making job. Interested?"

"Honestly? Not really. I'm planning to join the Fighters Guild." Grede said, "Their people came to me. There's a steady wage, and you can take on jobs. Probably rounding up wanted men, clearing wolves out of farms, that sort of thing. Stormwind's guards are stretched thin these days — bandits, robbers, orcs, gnolls. I heard the murlocs over by Crystal Lake even got themselves a new leader. Everything's gone to hell. I think I could give the Fighters Guild a go."

Seeing Grede ready to reignite his fighting spirit, Jace suddenly felt awkward bringing up his own business.

But the dwarf went on: "Then again, most of the Fighters Guild's current members are middle-aged and old-timers, plus a few Bronzebeard pups. A lot of them have families to feed, livelihoods to maintain. They can't force everyone into it full-time. Even if they took me in, I could still find work on the side. So go on, tell me about it, Jace. Let's see if your job's got any appeal."

Seeing how he put it, Jace said, "I'm planning to head out of the city and gather herbs. Sell them for coin. What do you think?"

"Gather herbs?" Grede frowned. "Isn't that women's and elves' work?"

"Your women up north aren't all that different from our men down here, are they?" Jace kept his voice low, making sure there were no female dwarves nearby, then said, "So our men ought to gather herbs too. Nobody'll say a word about it."

He then ran through the approximate prices the alchemy merchants in the Mage Quarter paid for herbs. When he was done, the dwarf slammed the table and stood up, glaring across the table at Jace. "You're serious?"

"Forget the Fighters Guild — that's a bloody retirement club!" Grede roared toward the bar. "Barmaid! Bring me another, and make it a dark one! Ironforge stout if you've got it!"

He roared it, then slowly sat back down. After thinking it over, he lowered his voice and asked, "But it's probably pretty dangerous, right? For that kind of money. And do you even know anything about gathering herbs? You're so young — don't tell me you sat in on herbalism classes back in Lordaeron? It's not that I don't trust you, it's just… well, mainly I don't trust you."

Was this guy actually sober? How was he spouting nothing but nonsense?

Jace stared helplessly at the dwarf, who was barely managing to keep his eyes open, and whispered, "Look — even the herbalism shop owner trusts me. She's willing to buy my herbs, and she shared her purchase prices with me. So why wouldn't you trust me, Grede? Isn't herbalism women's work where you're from? You're an old man — you're a complete outsider on this."

"That's fair. But I'm not old."

"Right, young man. Once we're out there, the two of us'll work together. I'll find the herbs, and we'll deal with any trouble that comes our way. Well — mainly you, of course. A former Gryphon Rider like you's got plenty of clout. When we sell the herbs, we split it seventy-thirty. How about that — seventy for you, thirty for me."

"That doesn't seem right." Grede was barely holding back a grin, looking sheepish. "When it comes to herbs, you know more, and you've got the buyer lined up. You'd give me the bigger cut? Let's just go fifty-fifty."

"I've got my own interests at heart too," Jace explained. "Kingsblood, and the pricey Grave Moss — I've got uses for those myself. I can't sell them."

"Ah, that's only fair then," the dwarf said.

"And if we manage to find any Evil Claws, I'd like to keep those too," Jace added.

The dwarf asked, "Evil Claws — you mean the claws of those cursed beasts wreathed in deathly black aura?"

"You know about them?"

"Of course. Trolls love using that stuff for alchemy. Smear it on a weapon and it inflicts a terrible curse. One scratch from one of those vicious Shadow Hunters' cursed blades on a dwarf's skin, and it can fester into wounds that won't heal. Ends up maiming you, killing you. Nasty business."

"Learned something new."

"That I did." Grede chuckled, then asked, "So where are you planning to go?"

"Raven Hill."

"Raven Hill?" Hearing the name, the dwarf hesitated. "Isn't that a bit far? The round trip could take a week."

Jace said, "If we want Grave Moss, that's the only place to find it. We go fast, don't waste time on the road — if we're lucky, four or five days is enough. Don't forget I've still got to get back and work for my mage boss. He's out on business, might be back within the week. I need to report in when he returns. I won't be gone long."

"So the question is — do we go through Redridge to Lakeshire, or take the western route to Raven Hill? What a pain. Why can't we just cut straight across Elwynn Forest and the Nefiri River to Raven Hill?"

The dwarf traced an arc in the air. "Unless you can fly across."

Jace thought about it — Stormwind didn't have a gryphon service yet. They didn't even have enough roosts for the people, let alone gryphon aeries.

He'd never seen a real gryphon up close since coming to this world, not even back in Lordaeron. He had no idea whether the gryphon masters in the game had just been "fast travel points" that bowed to gameplay convenience.

"Or we could take the long way round to Sentinel Hill," Grede said. "I hear the canyon road between Redridge and Lakeshire gets taken over by gnoll tribes all the time. It's been cut off for days at a time. The Westfall route's safer — even the Church people have stopped using that road."

"Then Sentinel Hill it is. I'll follow your lead," Jace said.

The dwarf swirled the ale in his tankard. "Want to make a detour to Moonbrook on the way? We could stock up on supplies. I hear their liver pie's twice as good as Stormwind's."

Jace said helplessly, "That'll waste a whole day. Cart fare, road tolls, and the pie on top of it… Grede, that's way too expensive."

"Fair enough. Anything else? Pre-departure preparations, that sort of thing?"

"A big bag. Sturdy, ideally waterproof." Jace said, "The bags they sell in the city are too small and too expensive. Got anything good?"

Grede said, "My travel sack's a hemp weave from back home. Tough enough to resist cuts. I carried it all the way from my hometown to here with weapons inside and it didn't tear. It's somewhat waterproof too — as long as you don't dunk it in water for too long, it'll hold up. Most importantly, it's big. I reckon it could hold several dozen pounds of herbs."

"That's plenty. More than enough," Jace said. "And don't overfill it — that'll slow us down. Besides, this is our first time heading out. We might not identify everything accurately, and we might not find that much anyway."

"If it looks like an herb, stuff it in and let the experts sort it out!" Grede asked, "So when do we leave?"

"Tomorrow morning?"

"Afternoon. Tomorrow morning I've got a date at the Guild — a fight, to show them what I've got."

"I thought you weren't interested in the Fighters Guild anymore?"

"Whether I join the Fighters Guild is secondary. The fight's happening no matter what, Jace, me lad. You coming to watch your old man show off his skills?"

Jace watched Grede clench his fists, as if suppressing a tremor in his wrists.

"Sure, I'll be there. Come to think of it, you always call yourself my elder brother and me your lad. How old are you, exactly? I've never actually asked."

"I'm about sixty. Sixty-four, probably. Calling you my lad's no insult, eh?"

"You call that young? I… you…"

"You what, I what? Don't measure us by your human years. Muradin of Ironforge was over two hundred and he still fought at the Battle of Blackrock Mountain — and I'd say he was in his prime. Those Bronzebeards can't do anything except live forever. Course, we're not bad ourselves."
