Chapter 12: The Prince

The next morning, before dawn, Jace went over to the Mage Quarter and dusted off the study. Amy Marin said things were complicated over in Redridge and her father might not be back for the next few days, so when Jace mentioned he needed to head out on errand business, she took it upon herself to give him the go-ahead.

Once the work was done, he hurried straight to Old Town.

The trip practically took him from one end of Stormwind to the other. By the time he reached the Pig and Whistle, it was already packed.

The crowd was chanting in rhythm: "Baintor! Baintor!" Jace felt like he'd heard the name somewhere before — familiar, but he couldn't place it.

He pushed his way to the front, to the corner of the inn, and saw two bare-chested men standing face to face in the middle of the crowd — one tall, one short.

The tall one was a man with light brown hair falling to his shoulders, gripping a wooden staff aimed at his opponent. His features were handsome, strikingly so — a classic northern face, with sharp, clean-cut bones. He'd grown a bit of a mustache, but it was clear he was still young, probably only around thirty. Both his handwork and his footwork were rock-solid.

Grede, by comparison, was in a sorry state. The fight had been going on for a while now, and his face and body were covered in bruises. The other man didn't have a scratch on him — aside from a sheen of sweat, he looked as fresh as if he'd just stepped into the ring.

"Take that dwarf down, Baintor!"

"Hit him!"

"Thrust! Use the thrust! The dwarf's arms are short!"

The shouting grated on Jace's ears. Not that he blamed them — he was human too, and if he started cheering for Grede he'd probably get himself beaten up.

The man they called Baintor stood a full head taller than Jace. Down in that cramped space at the foot of the ground-floor stairs, his head nearly scraped the overhead beam.

From the state of his hair and beard, his skin, his muscled frame and slightly rounded belly, this was most likely a nobleman — definitely no ordinary middle-aged veteran.

A nobleman who could fight, here in Stormwind… could this be His Majesty Varian Wrynn?

No — Varian was only about fifteen or sixteen right now, still a kid. Besides, he had dark brown hair.

Grede suddenly roared and surged forward, both hands on his staff, attacking furiously. The man reacted with a flick of his staff, deflecting Grede's strike off its mark. His whole body slipped lightly to the side, cleanly dodging the blow.

For a big, powerfully built man, he moved without the slightest wasted motion. His leg muscles tensed and carried his whole body in a lateral shift — like a well-oiled machine. It was almost beautiful to watch.

Grede was in trouble, no question. Yesterday, when he'd heard Grede was going to fight some old-timers from that retirement club, he'd figured it'd be a quick bout with some gray-haired codger and done. He hadn't expected anything this intense.

Wait a moment… Baintor. Trollbane. Could the name have been deliberately reversed?

If it was Trollbane, then Jace knew exactly who this might be.

Could this man be one of the Sons of Lothar, one of the leaders of the Alliance Expedition, the strongest human warrior of this era — Danath Trollbane?

Trollbane was the royal house of Stromgarde. Danath was the nephew of King Thoras Trollbane.

But the Alliance Expedition was currently stationed at Nethergarde Keep, facing off against the orcish tribes at the Dark Portal — the fighting might have already spilled over onto Draenor by now.

Danath should be in the thick of battle against the orcs. There was no way he'd be here, squaring off against some unknown Wildhammer dwarf in a tavern while his comrades were still in the field.

"Kill that mustachioed pretty boy, you Aerie Peak bastard! Don't make us look down on you!"

"This guy… if it was me up there I'd be ten times the fighter he is!"

Jace glanced toward the shouting. Two dwarves — probably from Ironforge. The Ironforge dwarves in Stormwind had been growing in number over the years, enough that they'd even set aside a whole district for them.

Just as Jace was worrying that Grede might lose and get hurt, Grede suddenly whirled around with a sweeping strike after his thrust was dodged. The man clearly hadn't expected the sweep — he barely dodged it, nearly losing his balance.

The dwarf seized the press, attacking wildly while the man's footing was in chaos. He threw everything into a flurry of strikes from impossible angles, slashing and chopping with his absurd upper-body strength. The tide turned in an instant — the man was suddenly struggling to cope in a panic, retreating nonstop until his back hit the wall!

This guy didn't seem accustomed to a dwarf's strength. He lacked experience fighting dwarves?

If this were Danath Trollbane, that shouldn't be the case.

Danath was a master fighter recognized across the board — from Quel'Thalas to the three dwarven clans to the seven human kingdoms. It wasn't just that his technique was polished and his martial skill superb; it was that across his fifty-odd years of life, he'd fought elves, dwarves, and trolls alike, and accumulated battle experience with all of them.

Right — Danath Trollbane was already past fifty. This man was clearly much younger.

Even pinned against the wall, the man had no intention of yielding. His reach was far longer than the dwarf's, and Grede didn't dare thrust his staff at the man's neck or chest — because he'd get countered and hit where it counted first.

In that moment of hesitation, the man suddenly shot out a hand and grabbed Grede's staff, then kicked the dwarf square in the chest!

The blow landed with a dull thud. The staff went flying, and Grede's whole body was launched backward, crashing onto the floor. A collective gasp went up from the dwarves and humans watching.

"What the hell?!"

"An absolute disgrace!"

"Baintor!"

Gritting his teeth at the crowd's jeers, Grede hauled himself up, spread his arms, and charged with a howl. The man tried the same trick again, lifting his foot — but the instant it extended, Grede cleanly caught his ankle!

"Die!"

The man's face went white. Grede hurled him upward, slamming him straight into the ceiling.

Grede closed the two steps in a rush, pinned the man's back to the nape of his neck with his knee, wrenched his arm tight, and bellowed: "Yield?!"

The crowd erupted into a thunderous cheer. Ale and milk splashed everywhere. Even the humans who'd been chanting Baintor's name thought this was magnificent.

"I yield!"

Only after hearing the surrender did Grede's eyelids droop. He let go and slowly stood up.

The man rubbed his arms as he got to his feet, leaning against the wall. He looked at Grede's face and couldn't decide whether to laugh or wince at the ache from being thrown around — in the end his expression was something between a grin and a grimace.

"They'll let you into the Fighters Guild, Mr. Grede," the man said.

"That's a given." Grede sat down on a nearby step. "Not bad yourself, Baintor."

The man limped over to a spot not far off and picked up a straight sword of medium length. "That was the best fight I've had in ages. This is yours — a bet's a bet."

Grede beamed as he took the sword. "You're a man of your word, I'll give you that."

After the crowd dispersed, Jace came over. Grede glanced at him and his face immediately darkened.

"Tell me — I told you to come watch first thing in the morning. Why didn't you show up until we were halfway through?"

"I had to work in the Mage Quarter, Grede. And you said morning — how was I supposed to know you meant that early? The sun had barely come up and you were already at it. I got up while it was still dark just to clean the study first so I could make it."

"Fair enough. At least the ending wasn't bad, and you didn't miss the best part." Grede waved the sword. "I was mainly after this. That Baintor's sword was a proper new blade — a far cry from the broken swords and axes we pick up off the battlefield. Once we head out, we'll need decent weapons on us too."

Jace hadn't expected Grede to be thinking about arming the both of them.

He took the sword and turned it over in his hands, inspecting it. Finally, on the counterweight ball at the end of the dark red grip, he spotted an emblem of a clenched fist.

"The sigil of the Kingdom of Stromgarde. This is a Stromgarde blade."

"Probably some Arathi mercenary," Grede said. "But he missed the war, so the sword looks practically unused. I spotted it earlier — this is a weapon of the Stromgarde royal guard, not some common soldier's sword."

Jace asked, "How much could we sell it for?"

"Probably not too much. Twenty silver at most, and that's riding on the 'royal guard weapon' angle. A high-status owner doesn't mean the sword itself is top-quality. Stromgarde isn't what it used to be — they can't produce weapons of real quality anymore. This blade's decent, sure, but it can't hold a candle to our gear. It's not even as good as what you'd get in Lordaeron."

Jace asked again, "Did you not notice anything odd about that man's name?"

"Odd how?"

"What's the royal house of Stromgarde?"

"Don't they bear the Arathi name? No, wait — Arathi is their ancestor's house title. The King of Stromgarde is Thoras Trollbane… Trollbane! Could that fellow be Danath's kinsman, Gaine Trollbane? No wonder he could fight. He must have been sent here on his father's orders to see Danath Trollbane off, and he's been staying in Stormwind this whole time. And he spends his brief stop here causing trouble at the Fighters Guild?"

Jace agreed. "Quite possible. After all, the Trollbanes are born fighters. If that's really the case, then you just defeated the brother of the greatest fighter in the world."

Grede shook his head with a laugh. "Whether he's a real Trollbane is another matter. Gaine's nowhere near Danath's level — beating him's nothing to brag about. Still, he's a name. If I could beat Danath in a duel someday, that'd be a feat to brag about for a lifetime. Hope he doesn't die over there in Draenor. The sooner he comes back, the better."

At that, Grede seemed to suddenly remember something, and the excitement faded from his face quickly.

"Let's talk about our plans for this afternoon."

"Aren't you going to rest a bit?"

"What's there to rest? I just thrashed some spoiled rich princeling."

Gaine Trollbane's reputation was indeed nothing to write home about. As an outstanding fighter, he'd done virtually nothing of note during the earlier Orc Wars. It stood in sharp contrast to Danath Trollbane, who'd led the Stromgarde army in a head-on clash with the orc legions in Khaz Modan and served as Turalyon's tactical advisor.

Plenty of people suspected he was a coward who'd hidden behind his father and brother, unwilling to lead from the front.

As the king's only son, Thoras had spoiled him somewhat — unwilling to let him join the Expedition for seasoning, even reluctant to let him near Nethergarde Keep where the orcish forces guarding the Dark Portal were.

Yet this controversial heir harbored ambitions that far outstripped his own abilities and his kingdom's strength. He was obsessed with the delusion of restoring the ancient Arathi empire and reuniting the Seven Kingdoms into a human empire.

It was a dream that would sow the seeds of tragedy — for him, and for the entire Kingdom of Stromgarde.
