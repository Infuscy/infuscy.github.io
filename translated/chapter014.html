Chapter 14: The Holy Light Is With Me Right Now

The next morning, the streets were empty — only a few merchants were getting ready to set out. The workers didn't seem to be up yet.

Jace asked around after several caravans, then came back to Grede with a grimace. "Bad news. These merchants are either heading to Goldshire and Stormwind or to Moonbrook. If we want to get to Raven Hill next, we'll probably have to walk it ourselves."

"Walk? Are you out of your mind?" Grede said, displeased. "You want to swim across the Nefiri River with your own two legs?"

"We could find a bridge to cross."

"And if we're walking anyway, we'd have to go south just to find a bridge?" Grede howled in agony. "That's even more walking — we won't reach Raven Hill until the day after tomorrow! I regret coming out here with you. I want to go back to Stormwind!"

"Are you two headed to Raven Hill?"

A brand-new covered carriage pulled up beside them. Jace glanced at the four horses up front — all fine, handsome animals. The freight wagons they'd been riding used bony old nags that looked more like wild donkeys.

The man speaking was in his fifties, wearing a gray robe and holding a long staff, its head wrapped in wood around a handsome gemstone.

Pinned to his chest was the half-ring cross emblem of the Holy Light. A priest of the Church.

"Yes, sir," Jace said. "Could you give us a lift?"

"Of course." The priest said, "The two of you can take the seats in the back — there's no more room up here."

Jace peered inside. A paladin in a blue shirt was squeezed in beside the priest, leaning against a shield, a bundle of chainmail in his hands resting on the floor. Two more priests sitting across from him were tucking their feet in on account of that bundle. The priests were each clutching stacks of books, and the carriage roof was lashed down with all manner of large crates — the load was considerable.

More seats in the back were occupied by a male priest, and beside him a handful of travelers like Jace and Grede. The remaining empty seats couldn't fit many more.

"Praise the Holy Light!" Grede excitedly clambered aboard. A young priest opposite him beamed at the sight of the dwarf planting himself down, as if the teachings of the Light had just achieved a perfect conversion.

"Excuse me — how much is the fare for this trip?" Jace asked once he'd sat down.

"We don't charge," the young priest said. "We're heading to Gelande anyway — Raven Hill's right on the way."

"I love the Holy Light," Grede said. "I loved it back in Aerie Peak too."

"The Holy Light loves too every soul who carries hope like you, sir," the young priest said with a smile.

Jace had never had any particular feelings about the Holy Light. When he was little, he'd tried praying to it once, just to see — since the Light genuinely existed in this world, there was no point clinging to his old atheist notions and picking a fight with a fantasy universe.

Unsurprisingly, the Holy Light had never once answered his call.

It hadn't healed him when he was wounded. It hadn't soothed his heart when he was grieving. It hadn't stopped the near-fatal bout of diarrhea after he'd eaten roast river crab.

And yet he had personally seen priests call upon the Holy Light to cure other people's ailments. All he could say was that the Light probably didn't much care for an "outsider" like him.

"What are you headed to Gelande for?" Jace asked, curious.

"Healing the sick," the priest said. "Haven't you heard? Plague and deathly corruption are raging across South Elwynn. Many in Gelande have fallen ill, and their medical resources are severely stretched. We were sent by Archbishop Alonsus Faol to support our brothers there."

Jace glanced up at the crates lashed to the carriage roof. "So those are all medicine?"

"Some of them," the priest answered. "The rest is food, medical books, that sort of thing. If you'd like, when we stop to rest at Raven Hill I can give you a little."

"Ah — thank you, really." Jace said. "But I think the people of Gelande need it more than we do."

The priest smiled. "You're young and look full of drive. Would you be interested in helping out at the Cathedral of Light in Stormwind?"

Jace had thought about joining the clergy before. But as he'd just said, he and the Light weren't exactly a good match, so he'd never bothered approaching the Church.

This was the first time he'd had a real conversation with a member of the Holy Light clergy, and the man's friendliness and warmth had caught him off guard — even someone like him, who instinctively kept evangelists at arm's length, felt his guard softening a little.

Of course, he knew it was mainly because the man was willing to give them a ride to Raven Hill.

But then, in a dangerous world like Azeroth, wasn't this kind of timely aid exactly what ordinary people living hard lives needed?

Perhaps that was precisely why, no matter how many disasters followed, the Holy Light remained the most widespread and deeply rooted faith on this land.

"I'll think about it," Jace said.

"Wonderful. Even the fact that you're considering it is an honor to me," the priest said.

"I'd like some," Grede said. "I know the fruit from the cathedral's especially delicious. You wouldn't happen to have some, would you?"

"That would be a Sunfruit." The priest pulled a small cloth bag from behind him and took out a golden, orange-like fruit. "This the one?"

Grede took one and bit into it immediately, golden juice running all over his lips.

"That's the one, that's the taste — sweet!"

Watching Grede enjoy himself so thoroughly, Jace licked his own lips. The priest had already handed a fruit to his front. "Try one?"

Jace bit into it gently. It was a bit sour, with a texture like a plum but more fragrant — just not as sweet.

Considering this world probably didn't have twenty-first-century cultivation technology, the fact that fruit this good existed at all was impressive. It was leagues better than the crooked, misshapen produce he'd eaten back in Lordaeron.

The priest told many stories of the Holy Light Church fighting the orcish invasion in Redridge and the Hillsbrad Foothills, plus the heroic deeds of the recently founded Order of the Silver Hand — some of which Jace had never even heard.

To his surprise, the priest also mentioned one of the leaders of the Silver Hand, a well-known figure from the World of Warcraft game and a future leader of the Alliance — Tirion Fordring.

Listening to the stories and swapping what they knew of the years-long Orc Wars, Grede gesticulated wildly the whole way, and even the other travelers who'd been quiet at first joined in.

Time flew. Just past noon, after everyone had finished the food they'd brought, the carriage had already rolled onto the bridge over the Nefiri River.

After crossing, the carriage kept following the riverbank. Ahead, they were slowly approaching Duskwood. Grede kept his eyes on the carriage's heading, wanting to see exactly when the sky started to darken, while the priest and the other travelers napped.

Jace gazed out over the broad surface of the Nefiri River — the longest river on the continent, flowing from the mountains where Karazhan stood, through all of Elwynn, all the way down to empty into the sea at Stranglethorn in the far south.

The midday sun scattered across the water, glittering and magnificent.

As he watched, Jace suddenly spotted something white floating among the water weeds not far off.

He patted Grede and pointed at it. "What's that?"
