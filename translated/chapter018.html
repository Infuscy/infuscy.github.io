Chapter 18: The Dark Underground

The dwarf sorted through their gear to see if anything had been lost in the chaos. As he did, his gaze suddenly locked onto something to Jace's right.

Jace glanced over and found a skeleton buried in weeds up to its chest, lying there. The bones were a jumbled mess, but each piece was more or less intact. Its right leg was currently under Jace's ass.

Jace closed his eyes in exasperation. "I'm not gonna get poisoned, am I? Damn it."

"At least you brought a change of clothes. I've got to say, that was foresight, brother," Grede said with a half-smirk.

"I brought clothes, but I didn't bring pants," Jace said slowly standing up. The skeleton was missing an arm — probably the owner of that arm from earlier.

"His body's rotted down to bare bones, but there's still flesh on the arm. How's that possible?"

"My guess?" Grede said. "His arm might've been grabbed by a zombie and poisoned. To keep from turning into a zombie himself, he chopped his own arm off."

Jace stared at the dusty, hollow skull. "So did that work or not?"

"Chopping off your arm hurts like hell," Grede said. "If I'm right, I've got to hand it to this human — that took guts and decisiveness. He probably passed out from the pain, bled out, and got eaten by some small critter. If it'd been a big animal like a bear, it wouldn't have left such a complete skeleton."

Jace asked skeptically, "You're describing that in a lot of detail."

"Stuff like this happens all the time. I saw an elf die like that in the Hinterlands," Grede said. "Except it wasn't a zombie that bit him — it was a spider."

Spiders of the Hinterlands. Jace's impression of those was very vivid.

He used to have a bit of a phobia about spiders. When he'd done the Spider God quest in the Hinterlands and reached the final step — killing the boss — the sudden appearance of the massive spider mother had him fighting the boss while his skin crawled from head to toe.

That Spider God had a really badass name too, but it slipped his mind for a moment. The more he tried to remember, the more it became an obsession — he had to recall it.

"Sha… Shad…" He tried sounding it out.

"Sha'dra," the dwarf finished. "The spider god the Amani trolls worship. You really do know your stuff."

"Right, that's the one," Jace said, satisfied. "There are some troll ruins in Lordaeron too, around Hillsbrad. Doesn't strike me as strange that I'd know that."

Without the metal railing in the way, without the low wall blocking them, the cemetery shrouded in the morning's lingering mist felt even colder than before.

Now Jace felt like the cemetery was standing before him completely exposed, with no protection or gap between them. If anything dangerous suddenly appeared, there'd be nowhere to hide.

The two followed the railing, tracing the cemetery's edge. Grede watched for anything unusual in the middle, while Jace held his scroll and scanned the surroundings for suspicious plants.

After an hour or two, as the mist thinned a little, they'd found three clusters of Silverleaf of varying sizes — and one of those was hard to confirm as Silverleaf rather than some similar plant of the same family.

All in all, not a great haul.

More importantly, they'd searched several headstones near the cemetery's edge without finding a single trace of Grave Moss.

This wasn't a game. Real herbs didn't glow, the mouse cursor didn't show an interact prompt when you hovered over them — and of course, there was no mouse cursor to move around here. Everything came down to eyeballs and bare hands.

After two hours of intense focus, Jace's eyes ached. The bag had barely anything in it, and it was all cheap Silverleaf.

"We have to go deeper," he admitted. "We're not finding anything good on the edge."

He pointed at a stone building guarded by animal statues not far off. "See those mausoleums? There might be a whole bunch of Grave Moss waiting for us in there, and we're just going in circles out here, passing up a fortune."

"Lead the way," Grede said.

Jace looked at him, took a deep breath, and said, "Fine. I'll lead."

With that, he walked straight toward one of the mausoleums.

Grede licked his rough, dry lips, seemingly a little surprised by Jace's nerve. But since the human was already out front, no dwarf was going to hang back.

The mausoleums here had stone-brick walls, but the doors were mostly wood.

With no one maintaining them for ages, the door of this one had split open — looked like one axe blow could smash it in.

Jace gripped his axe tighter, but the extreme silence around him kept him from swinging recklessly. One strike and the whole cemetery would hear it.

"Let me handle this." Grede stepped up, peered through the gap in the door first to make sure no zombie was lurking behind it for an ambush, then grabbed the door seam with both hands and pulled.

"Nngh…"

The dwarf threw his weight into it, face turning red, veins bulging, spit flying — and with a sharp crack, he tore off a big chunk of wood.

The chain dangled loosely, swaying back and forth. Grede casually snapped off the remaining boards and stepped inside.

Jace thought, This is basically bringing a siege engine.

The mausoleum's interior was surprisingly spacious, with a tunnel leading straight to the back. From the inscribed stone tablets around the walls, it was clear a noble of Stormwind was buried here — though his family name was long forgotten.

This noble had died decades ago during the Gurubashi Empire's invasion, and he'd been a war hero.

For thousands of years, the Gurubashi trolls had occupied all of Elwynn down south to Stranglethorn Vale. But as the Kingdom of Stormwind grew in power, centuries of friction between the two had slowly pushed the troll empire's effective borders southward, until the once continent-spanning empire was confined to Stranglethorn.

Twenty years before the Dark Portal opened, Stormwind's farmers clashed with the Stranglethorn trolls again, and the Gurubashi War broke out.

The Gurubashi trolls' chief and his strongest warriors marched out in force — they could no longer tolerate their already-shrinking territory being whittled down further, and intended to teach humanity a permanent lesson.

The might of a ten-thousand-year empire, once roused, was unstoppable. Stormwind was routed, and in less than a year the troll army had reached the walls of Stormwind itself.

But this time, what awaited them was Medivh.

A Medivh barely in his twenties unleashed terrifying magic of unknown origin that wiped out the entire troll army beneath the walls in a single stroke. The Gurubashi chief and all the empire's elite were obliterated in an instant. Only a small remnant fled back to Stranglethorn, holing up in the capital of Zul'Gurub and the troll cities further south, never daring to venture out again.

It wasn't just the trolls who trembled at Medivh's magic — the humans were terrified too. The House of Wrynn deliberately downplayed the power of that spell, claiming it was the advantage of weather and the soldiers' bravery that had ultimately driven back the trolls.

Of course, though humanity had turned the tide and won in the end, they'd paid a terrible price. Based on the time marker in the epitaph's inscription, this noble had died in the trolls' first wave of invasion.

"Didn't expect the owner of this tomb to be a war hero," Jace said.

"Might've been a knight," Grede said. "Then his burial goods might include a fine sword and armor…"

"We're herb-gatherers, not grave robbers," Jace said. "And do you really want to be chased by ghouls carrying a full suit of metal armor weighing dozens of pounds?"

Grede said smugly, "If I had a full suit of Stormwind knight's plate, why would I run from ghouls?"

Jace said, "They'll spit rot right in your face. Unless you plug every gap on your face with mud, you're just as screwed."

"Fuck off," Grede said, tossing a chunk of stone at him. Jace dodged it easily.

They took the torches from the rack by the mausoleum entrance, lit them, and headed underground together. They soon found that though the mausoleum was fairly grand, most of the coffins were empty, and there weren't many burial goods.

As it got darker and quieter around them, their footsteps slowed.

A faint, damp, unidentifiable stench hung in the air and never quite dissipated — which meant there should be corpses in the mausoleum.

And where there were corpses, there might be Grave Moss. After all, according to the books, Grave Moss grew by feeding on the necrotic energy of dead bodies.

Something that should've been creepy had instead become a reason to look forward to what came next.

The only sounds in the mausoleum were their footsteps, Grede in front and Jace behind. Not long after, Jace suddenly heard the sound of water, right beside him.

"What?"

"I stepped in water." Grede held the torch down and realized he'd stepped below a stair — the area underneath was completely flooded.

He shook the torch and realized the firelight couldn't illuminate anything more than a few meters away. That was why they hadn't noticed the water not far off.

Grede asked, "What the hell? Why is it so dark that even firelight gets swallowed?"

Jace countered, "Isn't it the same outside?"

He said that, but down here, truly touching the shadow that blanketed Duskwood, he could finally feel just how terrifying the darkness in Medivh's heart had been.

It had seeped into every corner of the forest, gathering in every shadowy place hidden underground — pooling more and more, growing darker and darker, making the living wish they were dead and denying the dead their rest.

"We go in fast and come out fast," Jace said. "If this darkness is toxic, we've got no cure for it."

"You're only remembering to warn me now?" Grede raised his torch and started wading, his pace quickening.

The mausoleum seemed to have collapsed from some geological pressure, with cracks running through many of the walls.

The floor had gone from stone tiles to dirt, and strange cave algae grew here — completely unidentifiable, and not any herb species Jace had seen in the books.

After a few more steps they reached the deepest point. All that remained ahead was a single stone coffin, half-submerged.

Jace held the torch close to the water's surface and searched the bottom. Aside from some water weeds, there was nothing resembling Grave Moss.

Grede felt along the wall for a long time, then turned back to Jace and shook his head.

Now, the entire mausoleum had been searched except for the last sealed stone coffin.

Jace felt his scalp tingle. The idea of prying open a stone coffin was unpleasant for anyone — especially not knowing whether the corpse inside was dead or alive.

"Whatever the owner's wearing or holding, don't touch it," he emphasized.

"You think I'd take something off a dead man?" Grede muttered as he approached the coffin.

He gritted his teeth, pushed the coffin lid open a crack, then immediately raised his sword and waited a moment. When he was sure the corpse inside wasn't reaching out to claw him, he kept pushing.

Jace held the torch close and looked inside — when suddenly the darkness inside began to churn violently.

"What the fuck?!" Grede recoiled as if shot out of a cannon, falling backward into the water. Jace backed up several steps and hit the wall.

A mass of blackness poured from the half-opened coffin like boiling mud. In the pitch-black mausoleum, it was impossible to make out clearly.

"If we don't make a gold out of this trip, I'm tying you to Old Town and selling your ass!" Grede roared as he stepped forward again.

"That… would require someone willing to buy," Jace said, moving the torch closer. Only then did the two see what all the black things were.

Countless beetles, packed tight!

A tide of beetles poured out of the mausoleum and into the water. Watching this, Grede could barely keep hold of his sword.

But the beetles didn't seem interested in swarming them — they were just fleeing the firelight, scattering into the water.

Grede said, as if realizing something, "These beetles aren't living insects anymore — they're mutated creatures sustained by necrotic energy. A lot of the torches in these mausoleums are made by the Church of the Holy Light, especially in noble family tombs. They have a slight evil-repelling property — that's why the beetles are running."

"If one of these bites us…" Jace asked hesitantly.

Grede said, "Might get sick — vomiting, dizziness, that sort of thing. But one or two bites wouldn't have enough poison to turn you into a zombie. Quick — check if there's Grave Moss inside."

Jace moved the torch close again. Most of the beetles had fled. Inside, a rotted corpse lay flat in the mud, and all around the exposed bones grew clusters of dark green, eerie plants — clinging to the surface of the leg bones and the blackened clothes like a layer of spores.

"This is Grave Moss," Jace said, fighting the urge to vomit.

Grede sighed. "I really, really wished you'd said it wasn't."

Though Jace had his arm pressed hard over his nose, he knew it didn't really help.

It was just that the corpse before him had rotted to the absolute limit of his imagination, yet the stench in the air wasn't actually that strong.

"Why doesn't it smell much?"

Grede said, pinching his nose, "All corpses affected by necromantic magic don't have the foul stench of natural decay — more of a weird, fishy smell, like this one. Being underwater probably makes it a bit lighter… Let's stop talking about this and get to work! The sooner we finish, the sooner we get out!"
