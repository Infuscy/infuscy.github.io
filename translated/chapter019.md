## Chapter 19: The Dead Awakens

"Hand me that." Jace passed Grede a small copper spoon.

Grede took it, saw that Jace had one too, and frowned. "What the hell is this thing?"

"For digging up Grave Moss," Jace said, already using the little spoon to scrape the leg bones. He scraped a bit and deposited it into a small pouch.

Now it was Grede's turn to want to puke. He bolted to the wall and started heaving, but since he hadn't eaten anything all morning, all that came up was some stomach acid.

"7 silver a pound, Grede," Jace said, pressing his lips together. Then he went back to scraping.

"7 silver?!" Grede roared, then thought again and caught himself. "Fuck you — I thought you weren't selling the Grave Moss?! What good's 7 gold, even?!"

He cursed, but wiped his mouth, walked back to the coffin, pushed the lid open a bit more, and started scraping.

Though there was only a thin layer on the surface of the corpse, once they'd scraped enough, they'd actually collected quite a bit of Grave Moss. It probably wasn't a full pound, but by feel it was about as heavy as a bottle of cola — half a pound at least.

After working at it for a while, it didn't feel so disgusting anymore. The two scraped, pushed the lid open a bit further, and kept scraping.

When they'd pushed it all the way open, Grede saw the corpse's face.

"Jace, hey." Grede elbowed Jace, who was still absorbed in scraping Grave Moss beside him.

"Look — I think this guy's looking at me. I'm afraid I'm hallucinating."

"Hmm?" Jace glanced over and saw that the corpse's face was actually still fairly intact. Eyes open, soaking in the filthy water — it was a remarkably lifelike sight.

Just then, the corpse's eyeballs shifted and looked straight at Jace.

"He's not looking at you — he's looking at me," Jace said, and went back to scraping.

Grede smacked Jace on the back of the head and shouted, "He's *alive*, you ass!"

"What?!"

Jace was jolted awake by the slap. He'd been so focused on scraping Grave Moss that his brain had automatically filtered out anything scary.

The corpse suddenly grabbed Grede's arm. Grede howled. Jace reacted on instinct and hacked the zombie's arm off with his axe. The dwarf hopped aside, flung the arm away, grabbed the pouches and tools, and bolted for the exit.

Jace snatched up his axe and followed Grede, but his foot slipped and he nearly fell. The corpse sat bolt upright in the coffin, let out a feeble hiss, and lurched to its feet to give chase.

The two didn't dare look back — they fled with everything they had. As they neared the mausoleum entrance, they found a zombie blocking the door!

"Get out of the way!"

Grede roared and charged, cleanly lopping the zombie's head off with one swing and kicking the headless body to the ground.

Jace followed him outside — only to find the cemetery already full of standing zombies.

As they headed toward the hole they'd come in from, they heard a chorus of guttural moans from that direction.

Several hunched, rotting humanoid monsters were pushing through the undergrowth, weaving between headstones and rushing toward them. Some alternated between running on all fours and on two feet, looking less like people and more like a pack of rabid beasts.

"Ghouls… run, the other way!" Grede shoved Jace and pointed behind them.

Jace turned and ran, but he knew there was only one way out for the two of them — the hole behind the ghouls.

Everywhere else was blocked by the tall railing, topped with decorative spikes to keep people out. No way they could climb over.

But the ghouls were fast — darting through the mindless zombie crowd like a pack of wild dogs, bearing down on them. Jace barely had room to think of a solution.

They reached the railing. The dwarf hurled the herb bag and all their luggage over, then sized Jace up. "Come here."

"What are you doing?"

The dwarf didn't answer. He grabbed Jace and, before Jace could protest, hurled him over too.

Jace's mind went blank. He felt himself flung into the air by some monstrous force, flying over the iron spikes. His clothes got torn in several places, and his back was in agony.

He hit the ground in a daze, gritted his teeth, and felt his back. No blood — probably just scraped skin.

Realizing Grede was still inside, Jace grabbed the railing. "Are you out of your mind?! How are you getting out?!"

"Run! Get back to Raven Hill! If I can't handle these few ghouls…" The dwarf brandished his sword and said nothing more.

"Shut up… let me think." Jace looked around, then at the zombies and ghouls closing in from the other side, and suddenly a scene from a similar TV show popped into his head.

*The Walking Dead*, right?

He picked up his Woodcutter's Axe and smashed it hard against the iron railing. The deafening clang of metal on metal made Grede jump, and even the zombies and ghouls hesitated for a moment at the noise.

"Now, run… Grede, try to make it to that hole on the other side."

"Got it — you be careful!" Grede used the moment the zombies and ghouls were confused by the noise to roll into the undergrowth beside him.

Once the dwarf had disappeared, Jace stood alone in front of the railing, gripping his rusty Woodcutter's Axe, facing the swaying figures standing upright in the mist-shrouded cemetery. A sudden, intense loneliness gripped his heart.

He swung the axe again and smashed the railing. The zombies seemed to rouse at the sound, starting to shuffle toward him.

Before he could swing again, a ghoul suddenly leaped right up to his face. Its unnaturally long arm reached through the gap in the railing, nearly grabbing Jace's neck!

Jace's heart skipped a beat. He backed up two steps, staring at the monster with swollen eyes, rotting flesh, and teeth sharp as a wild wolf's, and unconsciously swallowed.

Then a second, third, and fourth ghoul piled up against the railing, growling and reaching through. One even spat a glob of green pus that nearly splattered on Jace.

The ghouls were like a troop of starving baboons gone berserk, and Jace was a juicy piece of fruit in their eyes.

Jace hesitated for a long time, afraid to go forward. Then one of the ghouls seemed to hear something, paused, and glanced behind it.

"Fuck you," Jace muttered in his mother tongue and charged forward, smashing the axe against the railing again. This time he caught every ghoul's attention. They went wild again, reaching for Jace's arms, his collar. Jace backed up several steps and tripped over a stone, falling to the ground. He checked his arms and body — no wounds — and breathed a sigh of relief.

"You brainless bastards, come on!" he shouted, feeling his voice was three or four notches weaker than the axe clang. He found a chance to switch positions and strike again, dodging the ghouls' lunges once more.

This time, he felt like he'd found the trick of it.

More and more zombies gathered, each pressing against the railing and staring straight at Jace. But Jace was used to it now — each strike was safer than the last.

Until one of the ghouls looked up at the very top of the railing.

Seeing that, Jace felt his throat go dry.

He coughed to wet his throat. The ghoul suddenly leaped, nearly grabbing the pointed tip at the top of the railing.

"I…" He muttered, frantically grabbing the pouch to run — when someone suddenly grabbed his collar.

"Get off!" Jace wrenched free and saw Grede standing right behind him.

"It's me, it's me!" Grede, seeing Jace drenched in sweat, shook him hard by the shoulders. "Now, go!"

"Go… go."

The two gathered all their luggage and sprinted into the forest, only slowing down when they could no longer hear the zombies' howls.

Jace threw his yellowed, filthy shirt into the undergrowth, put on a new brown linen shirt, sat on the ground, and stared blankly, panting for all he was worth.

"You know, Jace," Grede swallowed. "Even the bravest warrior among the Wildhammer couldn't stay calm in a situation like that — I guarantee it. You are absolutely the most goddamn brave human I've ever met. If you don't end up a knight or a member of the Royal Guard, then that Wrynn kid's blind, or the whole kingdom's got something wrong with its brain."

A long time passed before Jace murmured, "I'm never coming back here again."

Grede couldn't help but laugh. Jace looked at him and laughed too.

After they'd laughed for a while, Jace asked, "What time is it?"

Grede's smile slowly faded. He said coldly, "If I could afford a pocket watch, would I be risking my life here with you?"
