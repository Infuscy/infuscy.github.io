Chapter 21: Two on One

Grede said something in Thalassian, slowly straightening up. Though his head didn't clear the grass by much, his big face twisted with anger was on full display.

The elf couldn't have cared less. He pronounced a phrase in Dwarvish, one syllable at a time, then burst out laughing.

Grede bellowed through gritted teeth and launched himself forward. The elf smiled and nocked an arrow — his arms were slender but held terrifying strength. He drew the bow with ease, released, and the arrow streaked toward the charging dwarf like a bullet.

In the split second it took Jace to even process what was happening, Grede had already drawn his small round shield from his back and knocked the arrow clean away.

The dwarf kept barreling across the meadow. The elf's smile had vanished. He grabbed a second arrow, took a quick aim downrange, then let his fingers relax.

Grede was closing too fast — there was no room to draw the bow.

The elf crossed his arms, then fluidly drew a long and a short scimitar from his belt. He calmly sidestepped Grede's sword thrust, then spun and brought both blades down in a twin slash — the dwarf met them head-on with his shield, battering them aside. But the short blade's strike was a feint; it flicked past the shield and stabbed hard for the dwarf's flank… Grede was quick too, he brought the shield crashing down and deflected the blade. The elf used the momentum to spin back two paces. When they faced off again, both blades were set — one high, one low.

Grede tossed the small round shield aside with one hand, cursed in Dwarvish, and switched to a two-handed grip on his sword. This time the elf's expression was far more serious than before, braced to meet the assault with everything he had.

Watching those two or three exchanges from behind, Jace's mind had gone blank. At that level of combat, if he charged in, wouldn't a single graze punch a couple of holes clean through him?

But Grede didn't have the advantage. If Jace didn't help and the dwarf lost, then Jace was dead anyway. So he drew his axe and circled around.

The elf noticed the movement from the corner of his eye. Grede roared and raised his sword, charging in. The elf frowned and caught the heavy blow on both blades — but the dwarf's strength was simply too much, and he forced the sword edge down onto his own shoulder.

The elf gritted his teeth and glanced at the Stromgarde fist emblem on the sword hilt. Behind him, Jace had arrived!

Jace swung his axe in a brutal horizontal arc at the elf's midsection. The elf snorted, twisted his neck to shed the downward pressure of the dwarf's blade, and at the same time sidestepped Jace's sideways chop, slipping free like a slick mongoose.

He backed up in a series of quick steps, keeping both Jace and Grede in his line of sight. His gaze followed that lock of golden hair of his, sliding down the dwarf's blade.

"Just for a single herb, hmm?" The elf looked between the two of them. "Not worth dying for."

"Aren't you?" Jace said. "You're the one outnumbered two to one."

Grede said, "The herb doesn't matter anymore, pointy-eared rat. Time to die."

"That's right, that's right." The elf looked at Jace. "The herb doesn't matter anymore. The mister dwarf here wants to reclaim his meaningless honor and dignity, and me? I'd rather he didn't."

"Die!" Grede roared and charged with his sword. The elf just gave a cold snort, deflected the dwarf's diagonal thrust with his long blade, took two steps back, and hurled the short sword in his other hand. It grazed Grede's calf, opening a clean gash, and buried itself in the dirt. Grede grunted and hit the ground.

"Grede!"

Jace clearly couldn't keep pace with the two of them. Seeing Grede about to eat it, he hurled his axe instead. The woodcutter's axe spun through the air on a dead-accurate line for the elf — who dodged it without breaking a sweat.

"Human, are you out of your mind?"

Jace looked at Grede struggling to get up and the elf eyeing him sideways, and a terrible idea suddenly popped into his head.

He started muttering the English dialogue passages he'd memorized back in the day — "How are you" and the like — disguising it as an incantation. At the same time he mimicked the way Sulenna cast spells in the basement, weaving his hands in front of his chest.

The elf started, immediately panicked, and waved a hand in Jace's direction. His lips moved slightly, and in the next instant Jace felt something snap inside his head. A vicious ringing swallowed every thought.

For a moment it felt like the whole space was pressing in on him. His mouth opened but no sound came out. He tried to scream but couldn't make a sound. The world spun and he couldn't stand steady at all.

He blacked out for a bit and collapsed face-first in the dirt. From the corner of his eye he saw Grede get up and plant a fist on the elf's jaw, sending him flying several meters before he hit the ground and skidded another stretch.

Jace couldn't help but let out a dumb grin. The elf had actually fallen for it. He'd thought Jace was about to cast a spell, so he'd done everything he could to shut him down.

Honestly, Jace had expected the guy to throw a knife or shoot an arrow to interrupt him.

He'd never intended to cast anything real. All his focus had been on the elf, so he was confident he could dodge whatever came — or at least keep a wild hit from landing on anything vital, buying Grede time to counter. He just hadn't expected the elf to nail him with a spell and choke him off.

So this was what getting counterspelled felt like? It was like getting walloped on the back of the head.

"Enough!"

Just when Jace thought Grede had won, the elf roared to his feet, spoke an incantation, and Grede was frozen solid to the spot by a sheet of ice that erupted straight out of the ground. He kicked and pulled but couldn't budge his legs.

"You two idiots… I've had it up to here." The elf backed up as he pointed at the two of them. "I'm taking this herb, you hear me? What are you going to do about it, you two lunatics… I've had the worst luck today. Let's see what else you've got. How else are you going to stop me!"

He was limping now, and his shouting had gone hoarse. He touched his jaw where the punch had landed, cursing as he stumbled toward the kingsblood — when a sudden metallic scrape cut him off mid-rant.

"Argh!!! Just kill me! Damn dwarf, long-haired freak, blind dog, and that cursed short-lived monkey — I'm going to burn Goldshire to the ground, no, I'm going to blow the snow off Ironforge to bury your homeland, I'm going to wipe every last one of you lowborn mongrels off the face of the—"

The elf's cursing quickly dissolved into sobs. Grede waited until he was done before saying, "If you don't undo the ice on me, I can't get the trap off you either."

"Get it off? Get it off?! Curse this piece-of-junk dwarf-made contraption, it must be broken, that's why it won't open!" the elf shouted through tears.

"Brother." Grede glanced over at Jace, who'd been dazed to hell by the counterspell. "Go see what goodies are in his pack."

Hearing that, Jace immediately perked up.
