## Chapter 22: Allies? Bah!

The elf had given up. He sat on the ground, staring without blinking at the animal trap clamped tight around his foot.

By rights, he should have had the strength to pry it open, but he didn't even try.

The humiliating fight, on top of the disgrace of getting caught in a bear trap, had short-circuited his brain.

It was the height of summer. Even if Duskwood was cooler than elsewhere, the ice wouldn't last long against a dwarf's scorching skin.

Grede broke free of the magic soon enough. He hitched up his sword, pulled the short blade from the ground, picked up his small shield and strapped it to his arm, then swaggered over to the elf.

The elf looked up at the dwarf with a vacant stare. "Cut off your head and mount it above your pathetic dirt fireplace as a decoration. Maybe that'll earn you some standing in your Bronzebeard hole in Loch Modan."

"I'm a Wildhammer dwarf, pretty boy." Grede couldn't help but snicker at the sight of him as he knelt down to cut herbs. "Though I'll give it to you — your description of the Bronzebeards is spot on. That deserves a compliment."

"Whatever. Bronzebeard, Wildhammer, who cares? I suppose only you lot keep track of those distinctions, hmm?" the elf shouted, then lowered his head and muttered, "Damn, that hurts."

"Hand me your sword." Grede held up the bundle of cut kingsblood.

"It's already in your hand."

"The other one."

The elf reluctantly handed over his long blade. The dwarf hefted it in his palm. "Could sell this for ten-silver-or-so."

Jace picked up the travel bag the elf had dropped, not far off. He opened it and was a little surprised.

The herbs inside weren't much in quantity, but they were valuable — some kingsblood, briarthorn, a small pouch of grave moss, and even a vine-like herb he'd completely never seen before. Might be earthroot; Jace couldn't say for sure. He'd have to check it against his own scroll.

There were also four glass vials of potions in different colors. He took them out and was about to open one and sniff it, but figured with his alchemy knowledge he probably wouldn't be able to tell anything anyway. Better not to open them — there might be volatility issues.

The travel bag also had a small cloth pouch with some biscuits and cheese inside. Jace couldn't help but break off a piece of biscuit and try it. Slightly salty, with a rich spice flavor. After eating it he felt refreshed, like the energy he'd burned through had come right back.

The cheese was raspberry-flavored. Since transmigrating, he'd never had anything this close to ice cream.

So this was elven pastry? They'd hooked him by the gut.

He couldn't bring himself to finish it all at once. He picked up the pouch and walked over to Grede. "Could sell for a fair bit. We'll probably walk away with forty or fifty silver from this trip."

Grede shook the two swords in his hands. "These'll fetch something too."

"So you were bandits all along? Humanity's retaken Stormwind — you could head north and find honest work." the elf said sideways.

"This isn't banditry, this is self-defense. Don't forget you started it," Jace said. "Taking your stuff is to compensate us for our injuries. Taking your weapons is to make sure you don't come after us."

With that, he glanced at the bow on the elf's back. "Grede, shouldn't we take that too?"

"Can't believe I forgot." Grede said. "But if we take it, how's he going to deal with the wild wolves?"

"Leave him two arrows." Jace said. "Elven arrows are sturdy enough — should work as a makeshift dagger."

Grede chuckled and pulled the bow off the elf.

The elf stared at the dwarf without a word, just silently taking it.

Jace kept rummaging through the bag and suddenly found a box wrapped in a small ornate pouch.

When he took out the box, the elf blinked involuntarily, clearly fighting not to look.

He opened the box. Inside was a necklace set with a beautiful gem that was hard to call either gold or green.

"What's this?" he asked the elf.

"Cat's eye." the elf said. "You two clearly aren't rich. Willing to fight for a few coins — take it and go."

Jace asked again, "Is this necklace yours, or did you steal it?"

"Why would I steal someone else's necklace?" the elf said. "Do I look like I'm hard up for money?"

Grede glanced at Jace. "This lad really has a death wish."

"This was a talisman given to me by a lady." the elf said. "Take it. Spare my life, and the talisman will have done its job. Then I won't have let her down."

Hearing that, Jace suddenly lost all interest in robbing him of it.

"This…" he hesitated for a moment.

"Leave it to him." Grede clicked his tongue. "Let him have something to hold onto in the dark. At least he won't die alone and pitiful."

Jace tossed the necklace back by the elf's feet. "We'll keep the box. You keep the necklace."

The elf picked it up and clutched it in his hand. After a moment, he looked up at Jace. "Human, you're a warlock, aren't you?"

"Huh?" Jace said. "Why do you say that?"

The elf said, "You were about to cast a spell at me earlier — like a warlock. Don't worry, we don't have that much hostility toward shadow practitioners. I just wanted to let you know — yesterday I found a hidden orc warlock in an abandoned human farm to the south. I killed him and left his things untouched. The farm has a tall mill; the windmill only has one blade left. You can see it from far away, very distinctive. If you have time, you can go take a look, see if there's anything you need. This necklace means a great deal to me, so consider this information my thanks for leaving it."

"It's probably around three in the afternoon now. If you set off now and move fast, you should make it to the farm and probably get back to Raven Hill before nightfall. If you came here from Raven Hill, that is."

Grede whispered, "That warlock might have grave moss, kingsblood — herbs related to shadow magic. Worth checking out."

Jace nodded, his head already full of grimoires and dark magic and the like. If that warlock had some kind of spell relic, wouldn't that be a massive leap forward?

The elf said, "Go on. After I rest a bit and recover my strength, I can pry this trap open myself."

"Still, we should leave you a weapon for self-defense." Grede looked at the two elven blades in his hands, about to set one down — but the elf had already pulled three daggers and short swords of varying sizes from his belt. "No need."

"Right then." Grede tucked the weapons into his belt, slung the quiver over his back, and slung the bow over his shoulder. "Let's move."

"Oh, right." the elf said. "There's a pink potion in the bag. Leave that one for me."

"Pink?" Grede looked at Jace, who pulled out the vial. "Is this a revival potion?"

The elf said, "That's right. You don't want your Quel'Thalas ally limping along and getting torn apart by wolves, do you?"

"Allies? Bah!" Grede spat, and the elf laughed.
