Chapter 23: Farm at Dusk

Following the elf's directions, the two headed south. Crossing the main road, not far off was a farm.

Jace remembered that among the many abandoned farms south of the Duskwood road, one was occupied by werewolves. So before approaching, he'd been keeping a sharp watch on their surroundings.

Although they'd been going for a full day and night without catching a single glimpse of a werewolf, both of them had started to have their doubts about what Morgan had said. But better safe than sorry — if they didn't care at all and actually ran into one, they'd be the ones holding the short end of the stick.

After all, Grede didn't even know what a werewolf was. Once a fight started, he'd definitely be at a disadvantage.

The two of them hid behind a dark old oak trunk that looked like it was about to die, peering toward the farm. Grede pointed at the windmill standing alone across from the barn. "Is that the one he meant? The windmill?"

"One blade left. That's probably it." Jace pointed to the wild growth at the edge of the farm — low trees and shrubs that had gone crazy, covering the fences. "We'll go in from over there."

Grede's gaze followed the fence line. He rubbed his calf, which the elf had slashed, and sighed from exhaustion.

He glanced at the flat, weed-choked abandoned fields. "Doesn't look like anything alive out there."

"Remember the graveyard? This morning?" Jace said.

The peaceful graveyard had suddenly turned into an undead zombie stronghold the moment the two of them climbed out of the crypt. That was the kind of thing nobody would forget for the rest of their lives.

The dwarf clearly understood what Jace meant. He stared at Jace's eyes for a moment, then silently slid into the ditch on the right — a sunken depression lower than the flat ground.

Feeling their way along the edge of the farm fence toward the windmill, the sky was darker than before. It looked like it was about to black out.

"After we're done with this farm, we're heading back no matter what, Grede." Jace said. "The people in Raven Hill have been living here all these years. Werewolf or not, whatever they said at least proves the danger at night is real."

"Maybe, but we've come this far — going back empty-handed would be pathetic," the dwarf said, displeased. "If we can't find anything, we'll at least find one more. We're so close to the main road, it's way safer than when we were hunting for kingsblood."

"You think it's possible that elf tricked us into coming here to screw us over? Given how badly we roughed him up." Jace asked quietly.

Grede, who was feeling his way forward, turned his head. "I've thought about it too. But what danger could there be here? The elf's buddies lying in ambush? No need for that. Or the farm's surrounded by a bunch of ghouls? Doesn't look like it. But I can't say for sure either. We'll just have to be careful."

Jace said, "You're being careful by cutting straight across the middle of the field?"

"Who said anything about cutting across the middle?!" the dwarf snapped back, then turned and kept moving.

Jace sighed softly. Before he could say anything, Grede suddenly turned back and put a finger to his lips in a "shush."

What's wrong?

He mouthed the words: "Wolves."

Jace raised his head slightly and looked around. Nothing.

With the eyesight he'd trained up over the past day, he could spot a silverleaf in a bush. If there were wolves around, they'd be impossible to miss.

"You sure?" he whispered right against Grede's ear.

Grede wrinkled his nose. "Smell."

What, so even wolves — animals that were careful and good at covering their tracks — could be smelled by him? Did dwarves have some kind of superpower?

Jace tried sniffing. Naturally, he didn't catch a thing.

But Grede wasn't the type to cry wolf over nothing. Jace slowed his pace too, crouching low and following him forward bit by bit.

The windmill was less than twenty meters ahead now, and still no sign of any wolves.

Jace looked up. The tall windmill's lone remaining blade hung low, like a giant knight in gray plate armor standing in the dark with his hands resting on the hilt of a broadsword.

Grede crouched and watched for a long time, waiting for any hidden beast to show itself. But after a long wait there was no clear movement.

"Strange." He twitched his nose and whispered, "Let's go in."

Jace nodded and felt his way to the windmill wall. The moment he got close to the door, he heard a series of faint, slick, strange noises.

This time it was his turn to shush. Grede was stopped in his tracks by him, and after listening quietly for a while, his eyes slowly widened.

Just as the two of them were listening closely, the sound of a bone snapping broke through the rustling noise.

That basically settled it — the sounds of a carnivore feeding.

Jace gripped his axe a notch higher, making sure he had a firm hold. Grede drew an arrow and nocked it.

The two nodded at each other and slowly crept through the door.

The moment they stepped in, the pitch-black windmill was impossible to see in. As his eyes slowly adjusted to the dark, Jace caught sight of a massive silver-gray shape swaying constantly behind the cobwebs of abandoned tools and collapsed partition walls.

He glanced at Grede. The dwarf's eyes were even wider than his.

The dwarf raised his bow and aimed at the beast, drawing it to full draw. The bowstring creaked — and the beast suddenly stopped moving.

The moment it turned its head, Grede — whether from decisiveness or from fright — loosed. The arrow whistled through the cobwebs and the cracks in the partition, and buried itself in the beast's neck. A mad roar exploded in the confined space of the windmill, pounding Jace's ears.

The beast leaped out, smashing through the wall and a pile of broken farming tools on the ground. Its fur, even in the faint light leaking in from outside, still gleamed with a terrifying silver sheen.

Jace brushed the dust and wood chips from his body and pressed against the wall, gripping his axe and staring at the monster. This wasn't a werewolf — it was a giant wolf, almost as tall as his shoulder. Nothing like those wild dog-like forest wolves up north.

The giant wolf's charge split the two of them onto opposite sides. Grede scrambled to his feet and raised his sword, then lunged and drove it into the beast's flank — so deep his arm disappeared into the fur.

But the wolf, maddened with pain, didn't weaken from the wound. It twisted around and swatted Grede to the ground, about to pounce on top of him.

Jace had heard that a dog's waist was the thinnest and most fragile part — wolves probably weren't much different. He raised his axe and brought it down right on the wolf's waist. The giant wolf was just about to bite the dwarf pinned under it, but the axe came crashing down and it let out a whine. Its entire lower body convulsed out of control.

Grede shouted and scrambled out from under the wolf. The monstrous wolf kept convulsing and whining, but couldn't mount any kind of counterattack. Neither of them dared to get too close. All they could do was wait for it to burn through its strength.

A long time passed before the giant wolf finally stopped moving. The blood that had pooled had soaked the entire floor of the windmill.

When things went quiet, Grede muttered, "If that elf hadn't taken a slice at my leg, I'd have finished this wolf off! How'd it get the drop on me?!"

Jace looked at him. He was drenched in blood from head to toe, completely red.

"You hurt?"

"Huh? Ah?" Grede looked down, saw the blood on his body and flinched. He patted himself all over. "No, not my blood."
