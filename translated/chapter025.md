## Chapter 25: A Chance to Prove Himself

On the way back to Raven Hill, Jace and Grede took the main road. They could sense wolves or some other predator following them in the grass for a long time, almost all the way to Raven Hill — probably having caught the scent of the wolf meat and bones.

Of course, they never actually attacked. A dwarf carrying a long staff, with a longsword and a hunting bow on his back, still had some deterrent power.

Still, the two of them had been worried the whole way. This day had drained them of every last bit of energy. Having to fight a pack of starving mad wolves would really be asking too much.

In the evening, Raven Hill was as desolate as yesterday, even the old man who usually sat outside the inn was gone.

But the inn was still brightly lit. A short man sat by the window, watching the new customers come in. He glanced with disinterest at the various luggage they were carrying, then turned his attention back to the cheese in front of him.

"Boss." Grede put the cut wolf meat on the counter and set the bones on the table. "Could you grill this meat up, sprinkle some salt and soothing spice on it… everyone can come eat together."

The boss was a bald man, looked a bit on in years, a bit slow in his movements and speech. He weighed the meat in his hand. "Add some bread and pickles, and you could throw a proper little party, dwarf sir. The folks in Raven Hill haven't gathered together in a long time. With this food, plenty of people will come. What do you think?"

"That'd be wonderful." Grede rubbed his hands together. "Think there's any wine?"

The boss hoisted the meat and bones onto his shoulder. "There's still some red wine in the cellar, from before the explosion. A lot of people say those bottles have been contaminated. Still interested?"

"Why wouldn't I be?" Grede said. "If the contamination was really that bad, we'd be dirtier than the wine by now."

Jace cut in. "You forget those black things we saw in the crypt? Unholy energy might have seeped up from the south and settled underground."

"Ah, to hell with it — that's pre-war red!" Grede said, annoyed. "Today I almost died more than once. Without wine, I'm not getting through this day!"

"Then as you wish." The boss smiled and headed for the kitchen.

And so, a small bonfire party got going in the middle of town. One giant wolf bone, a few slabs of fatty sizzling wolf meat, several plates of dry bread and sour cream, a few jars of pickles — that was the whole feast.

A lot of townsfolk were too scared of the "werewolves" at night to come out, so only about a dozen people showed up. Most of them were old men who weren't particularly afraid of dying anymore.

Grede drank and sang and danced with an old human woman about his age, singing and jumping around the bonfire in the center of town as big as a campfire, as if the day of running around hadn't tired him out in the slightest.

Jace clutched his wooden cup, staring at the red wine inside without daring to drink. But the distinctive aroma was so enticingly alluring. After a few bites of the cloying wolf meat, he really did want to wash it down with a gulp — but he was afraid of poisoning.

Just then, Morgan Ladimore walked over with his cup and clinked his against Jace's.

"To still being alive today." he said with a smile.

"To still being alive." Jace replied, and took a small sip.

After realizing that Morgan Ladimore was the legendary Moradin, Jace couldn't quite look at the young man the same way anymore.

Especially watching Liz Ladimore in the distance — his young, shy wife in a pale green dress, holding the child in her arms as she sat on a log, smiling this way — it made his heart ache.

The baby girl lying in her mother's arms, staring blankly at the fire, must be the future leader of Darkshire's watch, Sarah Ladimore.

According to how the game went, a decade or so later her whole family would die in agony and despair, and even years after death find no peace, wandering the dark woods, threatening and harming innocent passersby.

And he himself was powerless to change any of it, only able to let the mad rumors about her parents spread unchecked between Raven Hill and Darkshire.

"I heard you two went hunting today. Looks like you did well." Morgan stared at the wolf leg bone glistening with oil. "A wolf this big — I'm guessing Mister Grede was the one who killed it, right?"

"Why couldn't it have been me?" Jace asked, half-joking.

"Even you have to admit, a dwarf warrior fighting a giant white wolf to the death is more like a scene out of a poem." Morgan smiled. "I hope someday I can be written into a story like that, passed down for posterity. I'd bring that kind of victory to my wife, Liz, and to Sarah, and let them see their husband and father become a true hero… not a weakling who couldn't even protect his homeland."

Jace looked toward a window that had just gone dark. "The people here are still living normal lives because of your efforts."

"You call this normal? People are leaving, and the ones staying are thinking about leaving too." Morgan said. "They don't trust me. The other young people and I who want to protect this place all feel helpless. And now even they're slowly giving up. I think they'll choose to move to Glen, or somewhere farther, soon. It's not because of any werewolf curse — it's because they don't think this place is worth protecting anymore."

He took a drink and shook his head. "I don't think I'm doing enough. Maybe it's a lack of ability. I'm young, I haven't trained enough. I'm too far from a real warrior who can bear this responsibility on his own — not enough to stand on my own yet. It's no wonder they all see me as a kid, even though I'm married and have a child of my own. So sometimes, I really envy adventurers like you who can leave your homeland without shame and have the chance to prove yourselves. I hope I get a chance like that too — to let everyone know I have what it takes to make Raven Hill prosper again, so that when people see me here, they won't have to worry about this town facing any threat."

He'd drunk too much. He was drunk, Jace could tell — drunker than Grede, who hadn't stopped drinking since the start.

"Put your family first." Jace said. "Your family is your most important treasure. No… no treasure compares to them, Mister Ladimore."

The dwarf, who'd been dancing, seemed to overhear the conversation. He was still shouting and singing songs that were completely unintelligible, but his gaze flickered this way.

"Of course." Morgan smiled. "I love my wife and daughter. They're the most important people in the world to me. I'll protect them with my life. Take care of them."

"I don't doubt it." Jace said without thinking. "But what I'm trying to say is — never leave them."

Morgan let out a long breath. "Stay in this little town forever? Then…"

Jace wanted to say: the Scourge invasion a decade from now, the outcome of the entire war, the fate of the entire continent of Lordaeron — none of that would change whether Morgan participated or not. But the moment he left here, his family would lose all protection.

But he held back in the end. He didn't want to influence the course of this world too much. Because if things followed the set timeline, this world would eventually be saved by all kinds of people and be spared from destruction.

But if he intervened and changed even one tiny variable, where would that steer the future?

Maybe his words had already influenced the other man. His existence had already changed the world permanently — it just wasn't obvious yet.

Jace didn't say anything more. Morgan seemed to be thinking something over, staring in the direction of Liz and Sarah.

And Liz was looking at her husband the same way, her eyes brimming with overflowing love and expectation. The child in her mother's arms slowly closed her eyes to the gentle rocking, opened them again, then slowly closed them once more.

The night in Raven Hill was full of laughter and warmth. The firelight shone on Jace's face. Sitting among them, he felt like an outsider — as if he'd never truly entered Azeroth. He didn't like this feeling.

In the middle of the night, Jace lay in bed, watching his clothes drying outside the window drift back and forth in the evening breeze, like a harmless ghost.

His whole body ached from the day. Several places where he'd been hurt throbbed in waves. Now that he'd finally let his guard down, the pain hit even harder.

Though he was dead tired, exhausted to the extreme, he couldn't fall peacefully asleep no matter what — as if he didn't want this night to end.

What was even stranger was that Grede wasn't snoring either.

"You're not asleep, right?"

"You're not asleep either." the dwarf said low.

"What's wrong."

"Sometimes I really don't think you're a seventeen-or-eighteen-year-old man, lad. I'll admit — I heard what you said to Morgan."

Grede rolled over and looked at Jace with one eye.

"I'm guessing you had a thrilling love affair back in Lordaeron that ended in a dramatic, tragic finale? I get the feeling you envy that kid Morgan Ladimore."

"No way. What kind of love affair could a poor kid have?" Jace said.

"Listen to yourself." Grede clicked his tongue. "A boy your age ought to be full of dreams for the future, full of beautiful longing for love, full of drive, full of passion. And instead? You're like a self-righteous cynic who thinks he's seen through all the hypocrisy in the world, making up random excuses to cover for your cowardice."

Yeah — this so-called not wanting to interfere with the course of this world was actually just cowardice, wasn't it?

Jace didn't argue. Grede seemed to feel the atmosphere had gotten a little strange, so he added, "Tell me, what type do you like? When we get back to Stormwind, I can set you up. Young, mature, skinny, fat, rich, poor, human, dwarf, gnome."

"I just want an elf girl." Jace said.

Grede rolled over with his back to Jace and started snoring away.
