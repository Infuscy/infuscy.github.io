Chapter 26: Back to Life

The carriage ride back to Stormwind took another two days. When they got off at the trade station by the little square in the Trade District, Jace watched the crowds flowing through the streets and felt, for the first time, that he'd finally returned to the civilized world.

Between the bone-weary travel and the collection of wounds — some healed, some not — neither of them had any intention of hauling around to sell their gathered goods right then.

They agreed to rest up for the morning, meet outside the Gilded Rose that afternoon, and then head home separately. Grede took the sack of weapons; Jace took the herbs and the warlock's little leather pouch.

Back in his tiny rented room — after five or six nights in an inn — Jace almost couldn't readjust to the cramped space.

The room was maybe four or five square meters, probably only a little bigger than the bathroom in his old-life home. A sloping eaves ate into a big chunk of the ceiling, and the bed was so small that at just under a meter eighty, his feet hung off the edge if he stretched out his legs.

But for an era that resembled the Middle Ages or the Renaissance, a regular city worker having a place of his own — even one this small — already far exceeded anything Jace had imagined.

He'd expected that coming to Stormwind meant sleeping in a bunkroom packed with a dozen people for years. In reality, only that first brutal stretch camped in tents outside the city gates had been like that. Once Old Town had been swept and laid out, it could house plenty of migrant laborers, and after the new districts were mostly rebuilt, the housing situation loosened up considerably.

And in the end, he'd even gotten the chance to rent a little room like this.

So before now, he'd never once complained about how cramped it was.

Soon, he'd be moving into the Mage Quarter — the most beautiful and most expensive district in the city aside from the Park where the nobles lived. Right next to it was the University of Stormwind, a place Jace had never actually seen in the game. The surroundings were genuinely flawless.

Thinking of that, he started feeling genuinely excited about his future life... Right, and he still had to go see Master Marin!

He wondered if his boss had made it back from Redridge yet. While resting in Goldshire, he and Grede had heard that the roads between Elwynn Forest and the Redridge Mountains had been harassed by gnolls lately. Could his teacher still be stuck out there?

He went to the canal and gave himself a proper scrub. Back at the pond near Goldshire, he'd been nervous about kobolds or something else ambushing them, so he and Grede had rushed through it — just a quick rinse to wash off the stink of days without a bath and rolling around in the dirt. Only now did he finally have time to clean himself up properly.

The canal bridge still hadn't been repaired. On the makeshift wooden bridge, a group of young women passing by covered their mouths and giggled at the boys bathing naked over here.

Jace was used to this kind of attention — though, of course, he was completely unremarkable among these young men eagerly showing off their muscles.

He was thinking about that warlock's pouch. When he went to the Mage Quarter later, should he hand the scroll over to Marin after all?

What if Marin asked where he'd gotten it? Should he spill the whole story? Who would believe him anyway?

And if what was written on the scroll was too evil, what if it dragged him into trouble?

He had to learn Orcish. Then he could figure out what the scroll actually said.

The library should have reference books on Orcish. After all, the war was over, and the study of orcs had moved to the table — orc research wasn't dark magic.

In particular, the stance of the Alliance's leader, King Terenas II of Lordaeron, was already well known: he believed orcs should be regarded as sapient beings rather than invading beasts, and that their culture and customs should be studied to prepare for future crises.

Dalaran, as the vanguard of knowledge among the human kingdoms, had not publicly opposed Terenas II's view. As far as Jace could tell, the leader of the Kirin Tor, Archmage Antonidas, was also a supporter of this stance, and the Dalaran he led must have already begun researching orcs long ago.

The Wizard Sanctum modeled itself on Dalaran in many respects. If the mages here had any grasp of Dalaran's recent movements, they shouldn't be falling behind in this area either.

As for those two books, there was absolutely no way he was handing them over. If they truly contained Demonic, or even dark magic, not only might they be confiscated — he'd land himself in trouble.

After bathing and changing into fresh clothes, Jace hurried toward the Mage Quarter.

Master Marin's door was half open. Amy might have already come and gone — it was already around eleven in the morning, so she was probably off to class.

He'd left the work aside for several days, and dust had already settled back over no few spots in the room. Even in a clean place like the Mage Quarter, there was no escaping the curse of dust.

Jace rolled up his sleeves with practiced ease, set out his tools, and started wiping down the table. He hadn't been cleaning long when someone suddenly pushed the door open.

He turned to see Master Marin standing in the doorway with a cookie in his mouth.

"Ah... Master Marin, you're back."

"I was delayed a bit. Why are you so late today?"

Marin walked behind his desk and sat down, set a cup of hot tea at his side, took a small lump of sugar from a pouch in the drawer and dropped it in, turned his book to a certain page and pressed it flat with a ruler, and stirred the tea with his other hand.

Jace asked, a little dazed, "Didn't Amy Marin mention it? I've been out these past couple of days."

"Ah! So you only came back today? What a coincidence." Marin settled into his seat. "I myself only returned to Stormwind last night. The situation in Redridge is not encouraging. The gnoll tribes are stirring up trouble not only in Elwynn Forest — though of course I'm hardly surprised, since Redridge is their home turf. I'm told the dwarves have found traces of gnoll activity around Loch Modan and Menethil Harbor. I suspect that after the Orc Wars, with dwarves and humans reeling from the devastation, neither could reclaim their former lands in time, and that gave the gnolls an opportunity to surge back."

Jace asked, "Will the kingdom send troops to clear out these gnolls? The Redridge gnoll tribes seem to be causing major problems for the trade routes between the three towns."

Marin replied, "The Redridge garrison is already planning to deal with those creatures, but I don't know what the kingdom's view is on the new gnoll situation here in Elwynn. The approach of hiring mercenaries for bounties will probably continue for some time — after all, the threat here is far less immediate than the one in Redridge."

"What about the orcs?" Jace asked while wiping down the bookshelf. "Haven't the orcs in Redridge come into conflict with the gnolls?"

"Ah, the orcs." Marin lifted his cup and blew on it. "Guess what the Blackrock orcs are up to right now?"

What were the Blackrock orcs up to? Since coming to Stormwind, Jace had never actually given that question serious thought.
