## Chapter 27: A Young Assistant's Understanding of Orcs

Jace had never planned to go anywhere near Redridge or the shores of Lake Everstill since coming to Stormwind.

Three or four gnoll tribes, countless naga lurking in the lakes, the Black Dragonflight liable to show up at any moment, spider monsters the size of cattle, and a few orc legions occupying the wild hills — one wrong step and you could die without even knowing what hit you.

Then again, Redridge probably wouldn't get that bad for many years yet. At this point in time, the Horde had just been defeated and was holed up in Blackrock Mountain, having seized part of the Dark Iron dwarves' surface city — Blackrock Spire. Right now they should be in the middle of a war with the Dark Iron dwarves for control of Blackrock Mountain.

Soon the Blackrock orcs would gain the upper hand — not because they were more numerous or stronger, but precisely because the Dark Iron dwarves, backed by their underground metropolis of Shadowforge City, would launch furious sustained assaults against the surface invaders, driving the Horde to the brink of annihilation in just a few years and on the verge of total victory.

And then a group of uninvited guests would arrive. The Black Dragonflight, led by Nefarian — eldest son of the Dragon Aspect Deathwing — would descend on Blackrock Mountain, strike a deal with the orcs, and swiftly drive the dwarves back underground, seizing full control of Blackrock Mountain's surface and turning it into an orcish stronghold.

Nefarian's goal was to find a new dragon rookery to grow the Black Dragonflight's power. Through this deal, he would enslave the orcs completely, turning the Horde in Blackrock Spire into his dragon-guard sentinels.

The dwarves of the Burning Steppes, now cut off from their underground reinforcements, would also be beaten back again and again by the orcish offensive. The arrival of the black dragons meant that Nefarian's Blackrock Horde would soon seize control of vast stretches of the mountains and scorched earth north of Redridge, forming a line of confrontation with the Kingdom of Stormwind.

When would the black dragons arrive? Now? A few years from now? Or had it already happened?

It was the seventh year since the Dark Portal. The orcs' current warchief, Orgrim Doomhammer, had been captured beneath Blackrock Spire barely a year ago. The conflict between orcs and dwarves atop the spire shouldn't have reached its most intense point yet, so the dragons' arrival probably hadn't happened.

Jace answered conservatively. "The Blackrock orcs are probably mustering their forces. They took a heavy blow from the Alliance armies on the Burning Steppes — they won't recover anytime soon."

"Hah, they're far more aggressive than you think." Marin took a small sip of tea. "The Redridge garrison has already spotted them south of the region. The Blackrock Horde's scouts... have reached all the way here. According to our informants, the Blackrock orcs are fighting the dwarves everywhere across the Burning Steppes, fighting for control. So tell me — should we just let the Blackrock orcs into Redridge and let them tear into the gnolls, while the kingdom sits back and reaps the benefit?"

"The Blackrock orcs are more likely to cooperate with the gnolls," Jace said. "Those kinds of black-magic-loving races probably have more in common than you'd think. And neither of them is stupid enough to slaughter each other to please us when they share a single, far more powerful common enemy."

Marin nodded. "That's certainly possible."

Then he twitched his nose. "Why do I sense an aura of grim decay in this room? Did you bring it in with you?"

"What..." Jace sniffed at himself. "I don't smell anything."

Marin came around the desk and leaned in for a closer whiff. "Have you been near a graveyard or something?"

Since Marin had caught it, Jace couldn't very well deny it. "Yes..."

"What were you doing at a graveyard? Do you have loved ones buried in Stormwind?" Marin asked, suspicious. "Though if it were a family member, I doubt you'd need to pry open the coffin just to say hello."

"No," Jace said. "I was looking for Grave Moss."

"Ah, that explains it." Marin's expression eased. He picked up his teacup again and took a sip. "Trying to earn a little extra coin? Is there still Grave Moss to be harvested around here? I thought you'd have to go all the way down to Southshire to find any. So that's what you've been up to these past few days?"

"Nothing much to show for it," Jace said.

"For Grave Moss to grow, an ordinary grave won't cut it." Marin said. "A knowledgeable herbalist wouldn't go poking into just any cemetery — it would have to be a tomb heavily saturated with necrotic energy... So my guess is that the big cemetery in the south, the one touched by that dark-magic explosion, might have some. But whatever you do, don't try going there. Infecting yourself with corpse toxin is no small matter."

"I understand. Thank you for the warning." Jace nodded and started wiping down the lower, grimier shelves.

"You work efficiently, Mr. Sesso." Marin pulled out his pocket watch and peered at it through his glasses. "I'd like you to finish your cleaning work by eight o'clock every day from now on. I'll be here around half past eight. Don't leave in the morning — I want you at my beck and call. You can knock off at half past eleven at noon. How does that sound? In the meantime, you're welcome to read any of my books. There are more in the next room. If you find yourself truly bored, I can fetch a few of Amy's novels for you."

"No problem, Master Marin." Jace glanced up at the bookshelves, which were filled mostly for the most part with history and natural history, covering an incredibly broad range. Taking the chance to learn about the world of Azeroth in detail wasn't a bad idea at all.

"I generally have classes in the afternoon. If you need to find me at other times, come look for me at the Green Crystal building on the south side of the Mage Quarter. I live on the second floor — my name's on the doorplate."

Jace had seen the place they called the Green Crystal. Its walls were covered in gorgeous flowers and plants drawn by arcane magic, with violet-tinted eaves, the whole thing like a villa. Downstairs was a lovely little square that even had a stone fountain in the center.

The people living there had to be the highest-ranking professors and scholars in the Mage Quarter.

If he ever got to live in a building like that, he'd probably have quite a high standing in Stormwind already, wouldn't he?

"Is there anything else you'd like to ask?" Marin said.

"I'd like to use my spare time to learn a language," Jace said. "Something like Elvish, Dwarvish, or Orcish."

At the mention of Orcish, Marin looked intrigued. "Elvish and Dwarvish I can understand, but why would you want to learn Orcish?"

Jace stared at the old mage, his brain racing to come up with a reasonable explanation within the next few seconds. He didn't have the energy to berate himself for not thinking up a good reason before he came.

"I'm thinking... we might have to accept the fact that we share this world with the orcs eventually. I want to learn their language ahead of time — it might open up more opportunities down the line. Basically, I just want to be more useful."

"Ah..." Marin raised his eyebrows, nodding nonstop. "You're thinking further ahead than I expected. Have you heard the contents of the letters King Terenas II wrote to the other kings? Lordaeron seems to be softening its stance on the orcs. They've set up orc internment camps, giving the orcs work to do within the camps and so on... I suspect King Terenas II might share your view of the future. Maybe it has something to do with the fact that you're both from Lordaeron."

He flipped through a stack of documents beside him and pulled out a sheet covered in dense entries, pressing a finger to one line. "If I remember correctly, the Wizard Sanctum actually began preparing an Orcish course several years ago. The class has been running for half a year now, with very few students. Would you be interested in sitting in and seeing how it goes?"

Jace asked, "The class has already been running for half a year — won't I be behind?"

"You don't need to worry about that." Marin tapped the words on the entry with his finger. "The current Orcish course is completely different from other language classes. The teacher isn't much better off than the students, honestly. They're all out scouring for Orcish writing, talking to captured orcs and slaves who survived the Horde, collecting the correct pronunciations of characters, the meanings of words... and then doing a bit of guesswork. Calling it an Orcish class is really just a process of students and teacher figuring it out together."

Jace said, "I see. What about the Elvish class?"

"As for Elvish, I wouldn't recommend studying it here at the Wizard Sanctum. We don't have any elven teachers, and the human teachers aren't up to much either — after all, the courses here are really just meant to train diplomatic personnel to serve the crown, the goal being basic communication and mutual understanding. But in practice, most diplomatic occasions are conducted in Common anyway. So-called diplomatic Elvish is basically just there to make a good impression on the elves — saying hello at the start, complimenting their clothes, remarking on the nice weather, that sort of toothless small talk. You can imagine the level. So if you want to learn real Elvish, you can go to Dalaran in the future. And of course, the best place is undoubtedly Quel'Thalas — study there and you can become the first human to settle in Silvermoon City while you're at it."

Marin smiled as he finished. "Oh, would you like me to put you in touch with the Orcish teacher, Mr. Carro Dushan?"

Jace quickly said, "If it's not too much trouble, I'd very much appreciate it."

"Don't hold your breath waiting for results." Marin said. "I don't see him often, and I certainly won't write him a letter — that would look as if I were begging him to let you into his class."

Jace nodded. He hadn't expected finding a chance to learn Orcish to be this simple, or for Marin to be so willing to help. If he could start learning soon, then his progress in studying warlock magic would speed up by a lot — and then he'd be able to crush those back-alley amateurs in the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb.

All he could do now was pray that the book written in two scripts was genuinely a Demonic-language textbook translated into Orcish — that would practically open up a shortcut. One that stayed under the Wizard Sanctum's radar, of course.
