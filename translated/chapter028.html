Chapter 28: The Swordsmanship Teacher

That afternoon, Jace and Grede met up near the Gilded Rose with their respective keepsakes, borrowed a scale at a trade station, and saw how much herb they'd actually gathered on this trip.

Grede pulled the bundled herbs out of the sack one at a time while Jace weighed them beside him.

The final tally: seven pounds of Silverleaf, six-tenths of a pound of Kingsblood, two pounds of Peacebloom, five stalks of Briarthorn, two pounds of Wild Steelbloom, one plant that looked like Earthroot, and about one-point-one pounds of Grave Moss.

"Feels a little less than what we estimated." Jace hefted the whole bag in his hand and gave it an appraising shake. "Did we lose some on the way?"

"More likely dried out a bit." Grede picked up a bundle of Silverleaf and gave it a waggle. "Look — the leaves have already gone stiff."

"Stop shaking it," Jace warned. "Every shake probably costs us a few more copper."

"You know how terrifying that sounds once you say it out loud." Grede set the herbs back down. "So what's the total?"

Jace pulled out the scroll and read aloud. "Peacebloom, two silver. Silverleaf, two silver fifty copper. Earthroot, one silver per stalk. Briarthorn, two silver per stalk. Grave Moss, seven silver. Those are the listed prices. She didn't tell me what she pays for Kingsblood, and she didn't mention the price for Wild Steelbloom or anything like that."

The dwarf held up seven fingers. "Grave Moss and Kingsblood don't count — leave those prices out, they're yours. I'll take thirty percent."

"Fine." Jace started muttering under his breath. "Two sevens are fourteen, five sevens are thirty-five..."

"What are you mumbling?"

"Quiet... Thirty-two silver fifty copper. There might be some variance — we'll know for sure once we get to the alchemy lab."

"How are you doing that so fast?" the dwarf said. "You sure you're right?"

"Close enough anyway — it's just a ballpark figure." Jace said. "Don't they teach arithmetic up at Aerie Peak?"

"Of course we do." The dwarf said quickly. "Well then, let's get moving — we can't let these herbs sit here baking in the sun. The Harvest Festival sun is brutal, I'm telling you."

The two of them shouldered their bags and headed toward the canal. Jace asked, "Did you ask about the weapons? Will the smiths in the Dwarf District buy elven weapons?"

Grede said, "Six silver for the long one, twelve for the short one. You planning to sell, or keep them for yourself?"

"I can barely use a sword — what would I do with a short blade? What about you, want to keep one?"

"I'm not great with them either — elven stuff." Grede said. "So let's just sell them."

The two of them walked out onto the makeshift wooden bridge. Jace asked, "Hold on — why is the short one more expensive than the long one?"

Grede drew the short blade from behind his back. "Take a look at these patterns. This isn't a standard-issue weapon — it was custom-made by a craftsman. That dwarf was only willing to pay extra because he wanted it for his collection."

Jace asked, a little skeptically, "Twelve silver for a collection piece?"

Grede explained, "What did you expect to get for it? You didn't think this dagger was mithril, did you? Honestly, the craftsmanship isn't anything extraordinary — it only costs a bit more because elven weapons are rare. The long one isn't even elven-made — the Bronzebeard over there said it was made by a Dalaran craftsman. I'd believe it. Dalaran things love copying elven styles. I told you it'd sell for ten silver at most, didn't I? My eye doesn't lie."

"Fine, I'll take your word for it." Jace said. "Did you ask about that black rod?"

Grede shook his head. "They couldn't identify the material. They offered three silver. I turned them down — it's not some pine stick! I have a feeling it might be some otherworldly material from Draenor, something that came through the Dark Portal. I'm not selling it for half a month's rent. Are we hard up for that kind of money right now?"

He shifted his load to the other shoulder. "Oh, right — I'm planning to move soon. Moving to the Dwarf District. A Wildhammer dwarf who's settled in Stormwind wants to rent me a room at a really cheap price. I'll bring that rod over to your place tonight. You hold onto it. Since it belonged to that orc warlock, if you get a chance, ask someone in the Mage Quarter whether anyone recognizes what it is."

"I wouldn't dare ask," Jace said. "If it really is some kind of profane object, I'd get myself arrested."

"It can't be that dangerous, can it?" Grede gave a dry laugh, then glanced toward the Wizard Sanctum tower in the distance. "Well, keep it for now either way. Check their library, see if there's any record of it. Until we figure out what that thing actually is, let's not sell it."

"It's probably just an orcish staff," Jace said. "When you bring it over, wrap it in cloth — don't let anyone outside see it." As he said it, he suddenly remembered that he might be moving soon too, into the Mage Quarter.

If he moved in, wouldn't all that orc warlock stuff he was hiding be way too easy to expose?

The Mage Quarter was full of things that could detect dark magic. You never know what might suddenly set one off, and then you'd be found out. Thinking about it, if he wanted to study shadow magic further, or any other warlock knowledge, it might be better to just keep living out here. It was only half an hour's run each way, after all.

Besides, he was earning a bit more now — a few silver a month in rent wasn't that heavy a burden. He might as well tell Amy Marin tomorrow that she didn't need to keep an eye out for a Mage Quarter apartment for him.

It was the dwarf's first time in the Mage Quarter, and the whole way he kept staring at the beautiful houses on either side, completely mesmerized.

"Did you sell that bow?" Jace said. "The elven bow."

"No." Grede said. "I'm going to keep it. I've taken a real liking to it."

Jace asked, "Did you get a price for it?"

Grede said, "A hunter offered seven silver. It is an old bow, honestly. I figured I'd rather use it myself — go hunting, relax, and improve my meals while I'm at it."

Jace grinned. "Then bring me along when you go. I need to learn how to hunt too."

"Sure, no problem." Grede said suddenly, "But that day I was wondering — would you be interested in learning the sword? I think you've got talent."

"Talent?" Jace said. "You mean with the sword?"

"I've never seen you use a sword. I mean in a fight."

"Uh..."

Grede said, "What, you think I'm no good with a blade? I'm a seasoned swordsman, lad. Humans all think dwarves prefer hammers because most of the dwarves they've met are blacksmiths hired by human nobles."

He actually had a point. Dwarven warriors were all highly skilled with the sword. The sword instructor of Prince Arthas Menethil of Lordaeron was Muradin of the Bronzebeard clan. Whether Arthas turned out well was another matter, but at least Muradin's swordsmanship had certainly been recognized by the royal house of Lordaeron.

"If you've got the time, I'd love to," Jace said. "When do we start?"

"We can start tomorrow night," Grede said. "Tuition's two silver a month."

"You're making money off me now?"

"Hah, you expecting it to be free? I'm not your father!"

Jokes aside, after that trip through Duskwood, Jace felt it would be hard to find a second teacher on Grede's level who'd be willing to teach him the sword for that kind of money.

Hiring a proper former Gryphon Rider to train you in swordsmanship for two silver a month — anyone who heard that would think it was absurd.
