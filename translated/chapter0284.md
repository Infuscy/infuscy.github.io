## Chapter 284: The Morning Meeting

Even though he'd eliminated the escaped succubus, Jace still hadn't slept a wink all night.

The next morning, he sat in the sitting room of the Slaughtered Lamb's basement, staring fixedly at the door, until Erland, Sulenna, and Galders all arrived, and Ursula came out of the alchemy lab.

Everyone sat in a circle around the burning campfire. Tharle poured the wine, and Galders spoke first. "How was the Jagin situation handled? Do we need to evacuate the Slaughtered Lamb?"

"I haven't heard anything," Sulenna said. "Erland's already taken care of everything on Jagin's end."

"Relax, no outsiders were involved," Erland said. "Including the demon's corpse — it's already been sent out. Nobody in Stormwind knew Jagin besides us, so even if something happened to him, no one would know. If anything, there are people who know him back in Ironforge. If the dwarves there don't see him for a long time, they might get suspicious."

"Ironforge, I can handle that. They'll understand," Galders said.

Hearing this, Sulenna let out a breath. She lowered her head and choked out a sob. "Goddamn it. It's just… it makes no sense."

Galders said, "Since we chose to come here, we should have been prepared to make sacrifices. Jagin understood that perfectly well."

"Jace." He looked over. "I don't want you to blame yourself over this. After all, I was the one who found the incantation, and I was the one who gathered all the ritual materials for him. I urged him to find an opportunity to attempt the summoning together with you and me, but he was clearly too impatient."

Jace said nothing. Erland asked, "Why didn't I hear about any of this from the very beginning?"

"We invited you to try this incantation too, at the start, didn't we?" Galders said to Erland. "We all knew how dangerous this incantation was, so we didn't participate in the follow-up research…"

"And now Jagin is dead," Sulenna said. "What more do you have to accuse Galders of? If it had been you, would your ending have been any better than Jagin's?"

Erland picked up the beer beside him, took a long drink, then nodded and leaned back in his chair, saying nothing more.

"Isn't there a counter-summoning incantation?" Ursula suddenly asked. "Why was he killed by the demon? Did the summoned demon servant not even give Jagin enough time to speak the counter-summoning incantation and protect himself?"

"I… I don't know," Sulenna said quietly.

"Because he never intended to speak it in the first place," Jace said, watching the campfire. "Erland, Sulenna — don't tell me you don't know what that demon was."

"We know now."

Galders nodded. Hearing this, Ursula said nothing. Clearly they'd all known what Jagin had summoned — Erland especially.

"When I killed that succubus, I heard what she said," Jace said. "It might be hard to accept, but Jagin turned himself into that of his own free will."

He still added, "Though I don't know if that was under the influence of the succubus's evil magic or what."

"At least he looked like he was enjoying himself," Erland said.

"Damn it, Erland!" Sulenna shouted. "Are you out of your mind?!"

Erland slammed the table beside him. "What do you want from me, Sulenna Keldont? Cry a river over that guy's death? Kneel at his grave and lay him a bouquet? Don't forget how he used to treat me!"

"And don't forget who helped you clean up this mess! Who was the one who tidied up that room? What I went through last night is enough to give me nightmares for days. That self-righteous piece of filth made himself more revolting than a pile of orc vomit!"

"Don't talk like that, Erland," Galders said. "We all appreciate what you've done."

"You bastards!" Sulenna stood up furiously and headed for the door. Jace said, "Sulenna… don't be rash. At the very least we should discuss what to do next."

"Sorry, Jace. I'm not mad at you. But I don't want to stay here anymore."

Sulenna spoke in a cold tone, turned around, and walked toward the door without looking back.

"There she goes again." Erland swore a few times in a row, then sighed. "All right. Whatever we said, I'll let her know."

Galders nodded. "So that incantation shouldn't be attempted again. From now on, any summoning incantation needs to be handled with care. We can't just try things out so recklessly. The price we pay for recklessness ends here."

"At the very least, we should wait until everyone is present before attempting anything as a group," Erland said. "Stop running these little cliques, you bastards who think you're so gifted. I've really had enough. I hated that old black dog Jagin, but I didn't want to see him die that horribly either. Damn it, I can't get the sight of him out of my head even now — it's right there in front of me, and the moment I close my eyes I see it."

"But how are we supposed to know which incantations are summoning spells and which are other kinds of magic?" Ursula asked.

"Summoning incantations have certain things in common," Jace said. "My guess is they're verbal pacts made with demons in the Void. By looking for patterns within them, we should be able to tell some things. I'll do my best to find records related to these dark-magic incantations in the Wizard Sanctum's library — whether it's language, script, or rules — to help us more easily distinguish whether the incantations we obtain are real or fake, effective or ineffective, and what category they belong to…"

"Why didn't you do this before?" Erland asked.

Jace gave him a cold look. "Maybe because this is the first year I've finally been able to get into the Wizard Sanctum for classes, Mr. Dudmore."

"Sorry," Erland said. "I keep forgetting, you little…"

"Do you actually have a way to do this?" Galders looked at Jace. "I've heard that even among the official mages at the Wizard Sanctum, not many understand the orcish language, let alone the demons' tongue."

"Exploring language is surely a lot safer than exploring incantations, don't you think?" Jace said.

"That's true," Galders said. "In that case, we'll leave it to you!"

"Still, I do know some things already, and Erland wasn't entirely wrong," Jace said. "It's just that I was worried about saying something wrong, or misjudging something, or causing some kind of misguidance… so I've been reluctant to share my guesses about these incantations with you all."

Erland rubbed his face vigorously upon hearing this.

"No need to doubt yourself, Jace," Ursula said.

"Ursula's right," Galders said. "All of us are feeling our way forward in the dark. Before, Jagin and I would also share whatever ideas we had with each other as much as possible for judgment."

"I understand," Jace said. "From now on I'll try to share what I know as much as possible."

Jace wasn't just paying lip service. It was time for the warlocks here to understand more about what they were learning.

He knew how to consciously resist the Shadow's influence, but not every warlock here had that awareness.

If he just relied on the imp's knowledge of Demonic to translate things and then passed them on to everyone here without context, sooner or later something like this would happen again.
