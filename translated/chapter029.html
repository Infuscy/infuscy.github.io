Chapter 29: Herbalist, or Bounty Hunter?

Jace and Grede walked into the alchemy lab one after the other, each carrying a bag of herbs. The moment the owner, Ruth Lumere, laid eyes on Jace, her face lit up.

"You didn't happen to dig up some Grave Moss, did you, Mr. Sesso... I didn't get your name wrong, did I?"

"You didn't, ma'am," Jace said. "But unfortunately, no Grave Moss. Just some Silverleaf and a few other herbs. Would you like to weigh them? We're selling everything in the bag."

"Help me carry them over to the counter, handsome dwarf gentleman." Ruth Lumere said with a smile. Grede's eyes instantly brightened and he cheerfully snatched the bag away by himself, hauling it over.

"However." Ruth Lumere turned back to Jace. "I smell something rather unpleasant on you. You must have searched through quite a few graveyards, didn't you? It's a real shame you didn't find any — it's been so long since my last shipment that I'm planning to raise my price. Naturally, the purchase price will go up too. If you get good news, you be sure to let me know first. Grave Moss is an important ingredient in anti-dark-magic medicines, as you know. Quite a few orc warlocks are still scattered through the forests and mountains, and any soldier who runs into them and gets injured relies on Grave Moss for treatment."

"I heard some trolls actually 'cultivate' Grave Moss." Ruth Lumere said in a low voice, glancing at the dwarf. "You know it's very hard to grow using animal bodies, and the trolls wouldn't use their own people's corpses either — you know what I mean, don't you?"

"Oh, I know exactly what you mean." The dwarf nodded. "I'm from the Hinterlands."

"A warrior of the Wildhammer clan." Ruth Lumere said. "Then you must feel it deeply. We might end up having to use that method too, actually. To be honest, if any politician or noble were willing to advise the king to make that decision, I'd be quite gratified. It would show that at least some people still have their wits about them. A dead man's dignity matters less than a living man's life."

"You're absolutely right." Grede agreed. "If all the living are dead, who's going to respect the dead?"

The middle-aged shopkeeper and Grede seemed to hit it off like old friends, but Jace wasn't in the mood to join their idle chat. He secretly sniffed at himself and couldn't smell a thing. Why did both the boss and Ruth say he reeked of death?

And why didn't Grede have it on him?

Grede handed the plant that looked like Earthroot to the shopkeeper. She examined it and asked, "Where did you harvest this? This is Living Root."

"What's Living Root?" Grede asked.

"A remarkably rare medicinal herb — very hard to find within the kingdom." Ruth Lumere explained. "It's used to make anti-venom potions, and its efficacy is extremely strong, so demand is huge. Strangely enough, Booty Bay always has goblins buying this up — I can't figure out why — which drives the price up too. This one looks in pretty good shape. I'd be willing to buy it for twenty silver. How about it?"

"Twenty silver?" Grede's exclamation startled Jace, who'd been lost in thought.

Jace stared at the plant. "Living Root's that expensive? Where does it grow in quantity?"

Ruth Lumere said, "Up north — the Alterac Mountains to the Arathi region. Khaz Modan has it too."

"That really is too far." Jace said helplessly.

"Then where did you get it?" Ruth Lumere asked at once.

Naturally, he couldn't say they'd robbed it off an elf.

"An herbalist's secret." Jace said mysteriously.

Ruth Lumere asked with a smile, "You weren't camped next to some precious herb for several days, waiting to knock out some innocent herbalist who came to gather it and steal their bag, were you?!"

This herb-buyer really did know her stuff, but Jace said immediately, "Do I look like the sort of person who'd do something like that? I'm always the victim!"

Ruth Lumere pointed at the dwarf. "You don't look like it. He does."

The dwarf roared, "What does it have to do with me?! A moment ago I was still a brave Wildhammer warrior!"

Realizing Ruth Lumere had already caught on that their herbs were of questionable origin, Jace didn't dare ask about the potion bottles they'd taken from the elf either.

He simply set the bottles on the counter. "There are three potions we have no use for. We're planning to sell them too."

"We generally don't buy finished potions here, Mr. Sesso..."

Even as she said it, Ruth Lumere picked up each of the three bottles one by one, pulled out the stoppers, and sniffed them. "Elixir of Agility, Frost Affinity Potion... Light above, you've even got a Detect Lesser Invisibility. Are you herbalists, or bounty hunters?"

Grede and Jace exchanged a glance. Grede said immediately, "None of your business, ma'am. If you want them, name a price."

"Marya! Marya!" Ruth Lumere called toward the back of the shop. "Is your Uncle Kordil still buying potions over at Caudil's?"

"He is, Mama — he was asking about it just the day before yesterday."

"Got it!"

Ruth Lumere answered, then shook the bottles again. "The craftsmanship is excellent, not much impurity, and these three bottles are quite nice-looking too. Call it fourteen silver for the three."

"Take them — we have no use for them anyway," Jace said.

The three of them chatted a bit about herb distribution and prices. Ruth Lumere arranged for two alchemy-lab apprentices to help pick out the weeds from the bag and separate them from the other plants that didn't quite qualify as medicinal herbs. With the Living Root and the three potions included, she finally offered a price of sixty-two silver — which already far exceeded both their expectations.

The two of them barely kept their excited faces in check as they left the alchemy lab, then hurriedly opened the money pouch once outside.

"That's a whole pouch full of silver, that is." Grede said. "Gotta thank that elf properly — without him, this bag would be half-empty."

"Yeah, if we hadn't run into him, this whole trip would've felt like a losing proposition." Jace said.

Grede said, "Don't forget your Grave Moss, Kingsblood, and Evil Claw — we kept those too. Those together should come to another ten or twenty silver or so. What are you planning to use them for?"

"Keeping it a secret for now," Jace said. "I don't even know if it'll work yet. If it fails, I'll lose face big time."

"Like I care that much." Grede said. "Well then, let's settle up."

Jace counted out forty-four silver and handed it over. "Yours."

"You're just handing it over without calculating?" Grede said. "I don't feel like that's seventy percent. Give it here — let me count."

Jace said, "I even gave you half a silver extra. You're getting a bargain and you still doubt me?"

Grede's brows knotted together. "What?!"

"Look." Jace said. "Sixty-two silver — that's six-point-two silver per tenth. Six sevens are forty-two, two sevens are fourteen, comes to forty-three point four..."

Grede cut him off. "Hold on... what is all that? I need to write it down to work it out."

Jace shoved the purse straight into his hands and kept eighteen silver for himself. "Do the math on your own time — I'm not going anywhere. If you think I calculated wrong, come find me in the Mage Quarter and take a swing at me!"
