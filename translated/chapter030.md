## Chapter 30: Necrotic Poison

After Grede sold the elf's two weapons, he gave Jace six silver. All told, the Duskwood trip had netted twenty-four silver.

Travel expenses plus lodging for the two of them came to less than five silver, so there was roughly twenty silver of net income.

On the way back, he stopped by the corner where the rent collector was and handed over four silver for rent. Back home, he lay in bed counting the remaining silver coins and bits of copper.

He didn't want to think about weapon depreciation or any of that — when something broke or wore out, he'd count the loss then.

This herb-gathering run had turned out far more terrifying than Jace had anticipated. He'd even run with some dangerously competitive rivals and landed in life-threatening situations more than once.

The problem was that in Azeroth, if you wanted to make money, either you had to be well-born — the offspring of some noble — or you had to trade your life for it.

So was it feasible to search for herbs only in the forests of Northshire, focusing on common, high-demand herbs like Peacebloom?

If you stuck close to settled areas, you couldn't find any herbs even in a dangerous environment like Raven Hill — never mind here.

But go farther out, and was the unsettled parts of Elwynn Forest really any safer?

Gnolls and murlocs were both deadly, and when it came to herb-gathering, both gnoll and murloc tribes were formidable competitors. Ruth Lumere had told them that plenty of gnoll and murloc herbalists were far more professional than the ones in Stormwind.

He had to find a way to improve himself, and fast. It was urgent.

He thought for a long time before finally falling asleep, then the next morning he jolted awake before dawn.

With nothing else to do, Jace arrived early at Marin's study, cleaned the room, and picked up a book on alchemy — *Alchemy and Common Ailments* — to read.

This afternoon he'd be starting to make medicine. He'd need to master the grinding technique for Evil Claw, plus the ins and outs of drying herbs and powdering them.

When Marin walked in, he caught Jace reading with total focus.

"You have an interest in alchemy, Mr. Sesso?"

"Yeah," Jace said. "When I was little I actually dreamed of becoming an apothecary or a doctor, though I never had the chance to study under a teacher. It's too late now — I can only read about it to scratch the itch."

"I have an alchemy lab right next door." Marin said. "It might not be as fully equipped as the one in the Wizard Sanctum, but it can handle some simpler recipes. You can do experiments in there, but you'll need to supply your own materials — don't touch anything of mine on the shelves."

"Really? That's amazing!"

Jace was genuinely thrilled. On his way here this morning he'd been debating whether to go buy a crucible and things at the alchemy supply shop, and then he'd started worrying about whether boiling things in his rented room would start a fire.

Now everything was solved — a ready-made alchemy lab. The pay for this job might not be high, but the perks were first-rate.

"Brew me a pot of tea, Jace." Marin beckoned. "Go to the alchemy lab. Boil the water from the waterskin in that brown pot. Once it's boiled, let it cool for five minutes. While it's cooling, you can wash the teapot and put in a teaspoon of Goldthorn — by then it should be ready to steep. Steep it, then bring the teapot to me. That'll be all."

Jace walked out with the book tucked under his arm and entered the alchemy lab. The dazzling array of pots, kettles, and bowls nearly overwhelmed him all at once.

He found the pouch of Goldthorn, the teaspoon, and the little brown pot on a small shelf, then started a fire and boiled the water just as Marin had told him.

He glanced at the label on the waterskin — it read "Spring Water." This must be the spring water the Shadow Protection Potion called for: water with no magical contamination whatsoever.

After the water boiled, he put out the fire, quickly washed the teapot, and put in the Goldthorn. He checked his pocket watch — still had four minutes to go — so he sat down and went back to reading the alchemy book.

After a while, he suddenly came across a passage describing the early symptoms of Necrotic Poison: difficulty falling asleep no matter how exhausted you were at night, the smell of decay on your body, sensitivity to sunlight during the day, a bone-deep weariness with no apparent cause...

His heart sank. He hadn't been poisoned by corpse toxin, had he?

He flipped the page and saw the other symptoms: vomiting green fluid, veins on the arms turning purple.

Jace rolled up his sleeve rather unwillingly and caught sight of some faint purplish lines on his arm.

No way. Had he actually gotten it?

Below this page it said that in severe cases it could be fatal within three to five months, but if caught early and treated properly, a full recovery was possible.

Thinking back to the fight with those zombies in the Raven Hill cemetery, he couldn't remember getting injured at any point. Even when the ghoul had sprayed venom at him at the end, he'd dodged it.

The book said you could only contract Necrotic Poison if necrotic fluid or ghoul venom came into contact with an open wound. If he hadn't been injured, how could he have been infected?

He started searching his whole body, looking for any wound he might have missed, and finally spotted several small blackened holes on his lower leg.

What was this... a snake bite?

He remembered the countless black beetles that had poured out of the final stone sarcophagus in that tomb. Grede had sworn up and down that even if you were bitten by these bugs it wouldn't be a problem... and those beetles had a pair of big mandibles!

If it wasn't a problem, why did the book say corpse toxin could kill you within a few months?!

Jace suddenly realized — this had been a dwarf talking. Dwarves were beings who'd evolved from the Earthen, and their resistance to poison was far greater than that of most other races. Why on earth had he taken a dwarf's word on the subject of toxins?!

The water was getting cold!

He touched the pot and got himself badly burned. Flustered, he steeped the tea, then carried the teapot back to the study — but his mind was full of nothing but corpse toxin... corpse toxin...

"You look distracted." Marin sniffed the tea. "Not bad. You brewed it just as I said. You won't need me to tell you again from now on."

When noon came and his shift ended, Jace sprinted all the way to the library and started hunting for a potion to treat corpse toxin.

Soon he found a recipe — Restorative Potion. It was one of the most effective and fastest cures for corpse toxin. Not only could it improve symptoms immediately, but over the following days it could prevent the toxin from spreading while further clearing the residual poison, and it could even repair the bodily damage the toxin had caused. Most importantly, a single dose was enough to do the job permanently.

And the potion was simple to make. It only required Goldthorn... which was Master Marin's tea leaf... simmered together with one other single ingredient.

That ingredient was — Jace turned the page — Elemental Earth?!
