## Chapter 31: A Price Too Heavy to Bear

Elemental Earth wasn't just any old dirt you could scoop up off the ground. It was a special substance close to the very essence of earth elementals, found almost exclusively in the Elemental Plane, within elemental creatures, and in certain minerals suffused with magic.

The dwarves' ancestors, the Earthen, had been composed almost entirely of this pure substance.

So that was the recipe — using dwarven physiology to fight off poison. Brutal in its simplicity.

As far as Jace could recall, Elemental Earth had a small chance of dropping only from slain earth elementals, and those elementals wandered some of the most dangerous places imaginable. With his abilities, there was no point even thinking about going out to find some himself.

Back when he'd played the game, Jace had used Elemental Earth to craft Elemental Sharpening Stones and made his first bucket of gold at the start of a patch. Even in the game — a world crawling with terrifying champions who could solo a whelp — Elemental Earth was pretty damn expensive.

He flipped back and forth through a few more pages, but the book said nothing about how to obtain Elemental Earth in this world or what it cost. He'd have to go ask at "Alchemist's Needs."

He walked through the door. Ruth wasn't in this time; a young blonde girl was minding the shop.

"Hello, can I help you?"

Jace asked, "You're Maria Lumere?"

"That, I am. My mother's out, so I'm watching the shop. I recognize your voice — you're the herbalist, right?"

"Right." Jace relaxed a little. If Ruth were here, she'd probably guess he'd been poisoned with corpse poison, and then he'd have to endure a round of mockery on top of it.

"I'd like to know the price of Elemental Earth."

"Elemental Earth?" The girl's eyes went wide. "You… want to place an order? I could tell my mother to write to Dalaran, or Lordaeron, and ask the alchemists there if they have any stock they're looking to sell… It might take two or three months. I can't give you a definite date."

Jace had a very familiar feeling — the same one he'd gotten standing in Duncan's tailor shop, listening to the shopkeeper explain how that seven-gold outfit had come together.

He wasn't all that interested in the price of Elemental Earth anymore. After all, the cost of writing a letter, contacting alchemists across multiple cities, and then waiting months on end was already well beyond anything he could imagine.

"Never mind." Jace waved a hand and turned to leave, then stopped and turned back.

"Do you know of any good way to purge corpse poison?"

"Purge corpse poison?" Maria said. "You could try asking at the Cathedral of Light."

Right… Why not just go see a priest?

Jace suddenly felt like an idiot. He thanked her and bolted out of the alchemy shop.

A little past noon, walking fast along the canal-side streets of Stormwind. Though the sky was overcast, Jace still felt like everything around him had a halo draped over it.

Maybe it was the shimmer of the canal's surface. Or maybe, after learning about the symptoms of corpse poison, he'd started paying more attention to these unusual sensations.

Now he felt uncomfortable all over, as if he was about to turn into a zombie any second. The thought alone made his legs feel sluggish.

The sunlight grew harsher and harsher, beating down on his skin until it hurt. Was he going to transform into a vampire right here? Single-handedly creating the San'layin a decade and a half ahead of schedule…

Would moving fast speed the poison's spread through his body? Jace seemed to remember hearing that if you were bitten by a venomous snake, you weren't supposed to exert yourself, or you'd die faster.

Thinking that, the world in front of him seemed even blurrier.

The church square had originally sat at the very north end of Stormwind. After all the surrounding expansion and rebuilding, the city had pushed northward a bit, and the church square had become the city center, hemmed in by a mess of construction sites.

During the war, the great cathedral had served as a shelter for refugees for a long stretch of time, and even now quite a few citizens whose homes hadn't been rebuilt were still living on the church square.

Even unfinished — scaffolding and ladders still stacked thick over its tall towers and white walls — the Cathedral of Light was still magnificent enough.

Admiring the cathedral's towering form as he went, Jace crossed the newly built canal bridge. Before he'd even reached the church-square gate, a guard stopped him.

"The King and Queen are visiting the sick and suffering refugees in the church square. No unauthorized persons allowed inside."

"I'm sick too," Jace said. "And pretty badly… I need a priest's help."

"What's the matter?"

"I've been poisoned. Might be corpse poison."

The guard tilted Jace's chin up and glanced at his neck.

"I can't say for sure what it is, but something's definitely off with you… You can go in, but don't head for the square — they won't let you in there either. Some priests are tending to patients in the building on the left. Go try your luck."

"Thank you so much."

After being let through, Jace had been about to make a beeline for the building the guard pointed out, but he was suddenly curious about what the young King Varian Wrynn actually looked like.

Passing the church-square entrance, he glanced inside and saw row upon row of guards and expensively dressed nobles blocking the door.

Behind the crowd, a shifting blue lion banner moved slowly along, punctuated now and then by cheers and words of praise. That was probably where the king was, but from here he couldn't see a thing.

A few of the guards blocking the street stared at Jace for peering in. Jace didn't dare linger and headed into the building.

What a shame — he hadn't gotten to see the king.

Jace felt like he was developing a fanboy mentality. But he'd transmigrated without a cheat, without some powerful bloodline — and now he wasn't even allowed to fangirl over a celebrity?

Inside the building the church had set up for temporarily housing sick citizens, there were loads of patients waiting to be seen. Many priests were surrounded by crowds. A tall man — Jace couldn't tell if he was a paladin or a guard — was trying to keep order, but it didn't seem to be working.

Just then a female priest finished talking with a wounded man who was being supported by someone else, and Jace squeezed right over.

"Excuse me… Priest, do you have a moment?"

"Of course. How can I help you?"

Jace was reminded of his pre-transmigration trips to public hospitals. Faced with a sea of patients of every kind asking all sorts of bizarre questions, it couldn't have been easy for the doctors to keep their composure.

The priests here went even further — after saying that, she actually smiled at Jace. Though the smile was tired and sweat-stained, and it flickered and vanished after only a moment.

"I think I've been poisoned with corpse poison…"

"Corpse poison? Are you sure, sir? Where have you been that might have exposed you to corpse poison? You'd probably have to be attacked by a ghoul to get it…"

"I was at the graveyard. I cut my hand."

The priest looked down skeptically and rolled up her sleeve to examine the veins on Jace's neck and arms, her gaze flickering slightly.

"Come with me."
