## Chapter 32: The Light's Keen Eye

The two of them made their way through the crowd into the basement. The priest pulled Jace to a deserted spot, held out her hand, and conjured a golden speck of light in front of him.

The moment the light hit him, Jace felt his chest tighten violently. A flash of white blinded him. He instinctively backed up a few steps until he hit the wall, panting hard. After the glow died down, it took him a long time to come back to himself.

"These are indeed the early signs of necrotic poison, sir. What's your name?"

"Jace Sesso."

"Mr. Sesso," the priest said. "Next I'm going to purify you. The process may be very painful — you'll feel like your body is being scorched by flames — but it's a necessary part of purging the corpse poison from your body. You've come a bit late. If things don't go smoothly, it may take several purifications…"

"Let's try one first," Jace said.

The priest nodded. "Take off your shirt."

Jace took off his shirt and hung it to the side. The priest placed her hand on his chest and slowly closed her eyes. Jace suddenly asked, "This isn't going to cost anything, is it?"

"The only requirement is that you keep hope throughout the whole process and trust us, sir."

"Does it help the Holy Light's effect on me if I think more positively?"

"It certainly does." The priest slowly opened her eyes. "Many people don't believe in the Holy Light — they don't think a warm, positive attitude can bring about a stronger response from the Light… and yet they believe that negative, depressed thoughts, or feelings of fear and despair, can stir the Shadow and give birth to dark power. Isn't that a contradiction?"

Even after living in Azeroth for all these years, Jace hadn't the faintest idea how the Holy Light healed injuries, so he had every reason to be suspicious of whatever the priest was about to do.

But his past experience told him that no matter what, you trusted the professionals. Since the priest's reasoning sounded fairly sound, he might as well give it a try.

What's more, Jace had no money to buy Elemental Earth, and he couldn't get any even if he did. The Restorative Potion was out of his reach. The fastest, most effective, and cheapest way to get treated right now was to throw himself on the Church of the Holy Light's mercy.

And he really was full of hope.

After all, this time he wasn't petitioning the Holy Light alone — there was a priest right here helping him. The Holy Light couldn't possibly fail to answer.

Thinking that, he closed his eyes and gradually felt heat building in his chest. Then burning.

The priest chanted softly: "Merciful Light, I beseech you to release this brave young man from the suffering that has befallen him, to free him from the clutches of the dead. I will be with him through this trial. Thank you, Light, for enabling me to conquer his fear and pain. Thank you, Light, for enabling him to conquer his own fear and pain. You have seen him come to your sanctuary seeking help — does it not mean he has set foot upon the right path? I trust him, knowing he shall see the light, shall witness…"

She chanted for God knows how long before she started repeating from the beginning, and Jace's pain slowly deepened.

Jace of course knew that priests and paladins had the power to cure necrotic poison, so he had no doubts or resistance. But after a while, the burning in his chest felt more and more wrong — like it was being heated gradually with no end in sight.

The heat spread bit by bit through his whole body, into his limbs. He started to feel like he was being tortured and punished, tied to some chair or board and subjected to endless cruelty, and the Holy Light was the whip, the fire-tongs, the spikes.

"It hurts…" he called out deliriously. "I don't think I can take it, Priest."

"I will be with you through this trial." The priest opened her eyes. In his peripheral vision, Jace saw her gaze was firm and blazing.

A burst of flame suddenly erupted from his chest and rushed to his head. Jace cried out, losing control, practically leaping up and slamming against the wall, gasping for breath.

The walls, the air — they turned cold and damp, cold enough to make him shiver. He grabbed the shirt he'd just taken off and wrapped it around himself, but it was a drop in the bucket.

But very soon he realized his senses weren't as overstimulated anymore. The dizzy, light-headed feeling had been swept away.

He looked at his skin. The veins that had been turning black and purple had faded.

"I feel a lot better."

Amid his joy, Jace noticed the female priest's expression had grown more serious than before.

Had something gone wrong with the treatment?

"Is something wrong?"

The priest said gloomily, "It went much better than expected. It means you've found favor with the Holy Light… It's just that while the Light was willing to cure your illness, it struggled to illuminate your path ahead, Mr. Sesso."

"I don't really understand."

Jace didn't know what the priest was trying to say, but from those two sentences he could more or less guess what had happened.

Maybe he hadn't yet touched warlock magic, hadn't yet come into contact with the Shadow or fel energy, but the Holy Light had already sensed his inclination.

If that was true, the Holy Light really did have something going for it.

The female priest admonished him softly: "My abilities are limited — I cannot interpret what the Holy Light has shown me. But I want to try to express its wish: no matter what choices you make in the future, always remember to hold on to your true self and never stray from your original intentions."

"I'll remember that," Jace promised, but he didn't take it to heart.

After all, he'd heard similar words countless times. In his past life, from childhood on, teachers, parents, bosses — they'd all said the same things to him. But he didn't know what his true self was, nor what his original intentions were, so he had no mind to carefully ponder what they were trying to tell him.

He thanked the priest and left the building. It had started to rain outside.

The king's procession had dispersed. The nobles and guards had left the church square, and the crowds were flowing in and out again. Some people were sheltering from the rain in the archway leading into the church square, chatting as they did.

Walking back, Jace watched the canal split before him — one branch heading toward Stormwind Keep and the Stormwind Harbor, the other rushing toward the sewers that led outside the city. For no reason, he thought of the priest's words just now.

The energies of World of Warcraft — whether Holy Light, Shadow, fel, or Arcane, death magic or Nature power — all influenced the user's mind to some degree, even their personality.

And among them, Shadow and fel had the greatest influence. Users inevitably grew prone to paranoia, irritability, and suspicion — doubting everything around them, hating everything around them, until they dragged themselves and everyone close to them into an abyss of suffering.

And yet how many people had set out on the path of these powers with the original intention of becoming saviors — to defeat evils they couldn't defeat on their own, to make the world a better place.

Including him. He'd wanted to walk the path of dark magic to prove his own ability, to gain greater power, to survive in this city and live a better life.

But wasn't that the very first step in the life of every tragic figure in World of Warcraft?

He'd never thought about the consequences of wielding these powers, about how to maintain himself after possessing such extraordinary energy, or how to find his way back if he really strayed somewhere unforeseen.

Jace looked back at the small building beside the church square. Through the rain, its outline had grown blurred and indistinct. The female priest had just walked out the door with a group of priests. She noticed him standing there and stopped to gaze back.

The air had turned cool — autumn was close, it seemed. Jace pulled his worn shirt tighter around himself.

Thinking about corruption by power seemed too premature right now, since he couldn't cast a single spell yet.

But realizing the problem was waiting for him now was far better than waking up in irreversible agony after everything had already spiraled out of control.

Thinking this, Jace nodded to the priest. The priest nodded back and left with her colleagues.
