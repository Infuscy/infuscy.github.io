## Chapter 321: Booty Bay

Back at the Stormwind City harbor, Jace had still felt his outfit might be a little too eye-catching.

Plenty of people wore cloaks when they traveled. Plenty wore masks. Quite a few carried staffs, walking sticks, or long poles. No shortage of folks with swords at their waists.

But someone who had all of those at once? You might not find more than a handful among hundreds of people. Still, it turned out that all the way until the ship set sail, only a single goblin sailor had noticed something slightly off about him.

And now, standing right at the dock in Booty Bay, Jace discovered he was utterly, completely unremarkable — especially compared to the troll squatting nearby in a full suit of blood-red spiked armor.

This troll was the spitting image of a warrior geared up and waiting to walk into a level-60 raid. He had four or five short blades and axes of varying lengths and bizarre shapes hanging all over him, plus a long spear made from the bone of God-knows-what creature on his back, and his face was covered in a mess of tattoos and tribal markings.

Even squatting down like that, Jace could tell he was enormous — far taller than any human. His arms, covered in dark-red armor scales, were long and solid, with proportions like a gibbon's.

Jace stood not far from him and couldn't help stealing glances at him again and again, but the troll seemed completely uninterested in the stares from the people around him, as if he were standing there specifically to be looked up at.

This was the first time Jace had been this close to a living male troll. After all, everyone in Stormwind turned pale at the mention of trolls. Most people couldn't even imagine surviving an encounter this close to one.

Strange to think that for twenty or thirty silver, plus a dozen or so dull days at sea, you could arrive in a completely different place and meet people and experience things you'd never encountered in your whole life. It all felt pretty unreal.

Of course, he still remembered that half a year ago, he couldn't have scraped together two or three silver, let alone twenty. And back then, he wouldn't have had the guts to come to a town like Booty Bay.

The reason he found this place quite interesting now was partly because he'd already become a part of the chaos himself.

Thinking of that, he observed the troll more closely — and only then noticed that the exposed skin beneath his facial tattoos, which wasn't easy to see, was actually light brown.

The troll was lowering his head, wrapping the broad two-toed soles of his feet with coarse cloth strips. From the constant movement of his hands and feet, Jace could see layers upon layers of dry, cracked skin. The fangs protruding from the corners of his mouth weren't the usual white of a troll, but a limestone-like pale gray.

Jace realized this wasn't a Gurubashi jungle troll. This was a sand troll.

The sand trolls originally belonged to the same branch as the Gurubashi jungle trolls. But after the explosion of the Well of Eternity ten thousand years ago, ancient Kalimdor had been split apart by the Great Sea. They'd been left on another continent, separated from their kin, and their long life in the desert had already caused obvious changes in their appearance. Their largest settlement was called Zul'Farrak, deep in the Tanaris Desert, among the dry mountains.

Seeing this sand troll who had crossed from the other side of the Great Sea to get here, Jace became even more certain that Noggenfogger Elixir might actually be sold here — and Ursula's words seemed a lot more reliable.

After all, the creator of Noggenfogger Elixir lived in the goblin city of Gadgetzan in the Tanaris Desert. Since this troll had been able to cross the ocean to get here, why couldn't a potion?

Booty Bay was a place saturated with a salty, fishy smell. At first Jace thought it was coming from the giant barracuda down at the dock, but after he left the dock and came out onto the streets, the smell still lingered, refusing to disperse.

Though every time he passed a doorway — especially those of the strange, eccentric shops — all kinds of thick, bizarre, and wildly different smells would drift out. Most of them were spices, with the occasional medicinal tang.

Every shop sign was written in several languages. It took some real effort to pick out the Common among them.

Here, Common wasn't the most widely used "common tongue." Every now and then he'd hear someone ask a question in Common and get an answer in Dwarvish — a chaotic mess of communication.

Without a doubt, anyone who'd lived here long enough probably spoke several languages, including even the Zandalari tongue used by trolls. Jace walked along, craning his neck, looking for something that could be called an inn. Since he'd come here, he should probably find a place to sleep first. The time had already reached the afternoon and the sunlight was getting dim. If he got lost a little longer it'd be evening, and any further delay would definitely cut into his rest.

And after… he didn't even know how many days of travel — probably around ten — Jace was genuinely feeling rather drained.

Just as he was staring up at a giant sign trying to figure out what language it was written in, he suddenly felt the wooden planks beneath his feet vibrating in a steady rhythm.

What was that?

The vibration grew closer and more rhythmic. A few people sitting on the edge of the planks chatting and gazing at the sea stopped their conversations and looked toward the south, in the same direction. Jace followed their gaze.

Then, from the shadows where the sunlight didn't reach, an enormous beast stepped up from below, one step at a time.

It was covered in black hair, with two massive horns curving toward the sky. Jace nearly thought some demon of the Burning Legion had walked into Booty Bay, and was still wondering why the goblins would let a monster like that in.

But as its whole body came up from the steps, Jace noticed the creature was wearing a simple set of leather clothes. Beneath its heavy skull hung thick braids bound with red and green cords — it even had a bit of a Wildhammer dwarf feel to it. When it stepped into the sunlight and that enormous bovine snout gave a heavy snort, Jace realized this was actually a tauren.

This was the first time he'd seen a living, breathing tauren outside of a book. And he was huge!

Jace felt he was no shorter than those ogres he'd encountered in Duskwood — at least three meters tall. Those weighty hooves were as broad as a black round shield, and every step he took on the wooden walkway shook the planks so hard Jace could barely keep his footing.

This tauren had no weapons of any kind, no arms of any sort on him at all, but Jace had the feeling this fellow was the most lethal monster in all of Booty Bay right now.

Clearly he wasn't the only one who felt that way.

No matter how vicious they looked, no matter how many knives, swords, or blades the pirates and mercenaries carried, they all instinctively made way for the giant when he walked past. After all, setting weapons aside, those thick fingers could probably crush their skulls with a single squeeze.

Seeing this creature, Jace's first realization was that the game had genuinely made the tauren too weak for the sake of balance. He felt that even after draining the power of two black dragons, he'd have no way of going toe-to-toe with this guy.

Jace had a feeling that if this fellow lost his charge and rushed at those fishing boats in the harbor, he'd smash clean through them.

"Nice hat, human."

A deep, heavy sound came from above his head, along with a thick wave of cow-dung stench. After saying that, the tauren walked off toward the dock without a backward glance, a thick hemp-rope-like tail swishing back and forth behind him, driving off the swarm of flies trailing him.

Jace stood there alone in a daze, replaying the tauren's Common — which carried only the faintest trace of an accent.
