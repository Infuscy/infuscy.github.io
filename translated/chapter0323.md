## Chapter 323: A Fellow Practitioner

When he'd first walked into the tavern, Jace had seen this dwarf and his succubus minion eyeing him and had gotten a bit of a bad feeling.

He didn't know whether the succubus had sensed something, or whether his own aura was just that easy to notice in Booty Bay, but either way it had drawn this warlock's attention. Jace wasn't too happy about it.

It was just that afterward, the goblin had told him the rooms were full, plus the bizarre taste of that fish had been so memorable that he'd gotten distracted and forgotten all about the warlock.

The Dark Iron dwarf in front of him wasn't all that dark-skinned — more of a pale gray — but his bright red beard was plenty obvious.

His beard covered his mouth so you couldn't read his expression, but Jace could still tell from his wrinkled eyes that he seemed to be sneering at the pathetic sight of him choking down the food.

"Nobody likes the taste of that except trolls. Even the orcs don't," the dwarf added.

"Something new. Haven't tried it before," Jace admitted.

"This beer too?" the dwarf said. "I don't see many young humans who like drinking dark ale."

"Get to the point," Jace said.

That's when he felt that cloying, nauseating fragrance drifting over from beside him. The succubus had to be behind him already.

"I noticed you the moment you walked in," the dwarf said from across the table. "You like her?"

He beckoned with a hand. The succubus walked over step by step and leaned against the dwarf, pressing her face tightly against his hairy one with a soft smile.

As the succubus's gaze turned this way, Jace keenly sensed an unpleasant feeling.

The dwarf said proudly, "No one refuses her."

"She is quite beautiful."

Jace offered the pleasantry offhandedly, then picked up another slice of fish and stuffed it in his mouth, chewing symbolically twice before swallowing it whole. His esophagus was violently pried open, nearly bringing tears to his eyes.

"What if I told you that you could have a lover like this too?" the dwarf asked.

Jace raised his eyes. What was this dwarf playing at?

Was he trying to recruit a new warlock too?

More likely, he was looking for a fresh test subject — just like the folks at the Slaughtered Lamb who tested their magic on people. Whatever the goal, a living, talking person was always more suitable for testing incantations than any inanimate target.

Jace looked at the succubus. She was staring unblinkingly right at him.

"No thank you, Mr. Dwarf. I'm not interested in ladies with wings."

As he said it, Jace took a sip of beer, swallowed twice to wash the taste out of his mouth before gulping it down, and stared at the fish on his plate in hesitation. He reached for another piece — and noticed the dwarf's expression wasn't so friendly anymore.

"You're a warlock too, aren't you?" the other suddenly asked.

Jace said nothing and picked up a slice of fish to stuff his mouth shut.

"Where are you from, Moonbrook or Stormwind?" the dwarf pressed.

"I'm not looking for trouble, dwarf." Jace stared at him.

The dwarf suddenly reached out to grab Jace's mask. Jace seized his wrist and wrenched it aside.

"No need to take it that far, warlock. If a fight breaks out here, those goblins will throw us both into the sea. You really want to get physical?"

The dwarf clearly hadn't expected this young human to be so strong. He shook his arm but couldn't break free, and could only grit his teeth and say, "What method are you using to resist seduction? Come to think of it, I actually don't know you — how strange. Tell me your name, human. Let's get acquainted."

"Or what?" Jace asked.

"If you ask it like that, it just goes to show you don't know what a succubus can do." The dwarf gave a cold, threatening laugh. "You have no idea the situation you're in."

Jace glanced at the succubus, then back at the dwarf. "Morophil!"

"Is that your name?" the dwarf asked. "Sounds like a demon's name. You trying to mess with me using a fake name…"

Before he could finish, the dwarf's eyes suddenly jerked to one side and his expression froze.

He stared fixedly behind Jace, his gaze following Morophil as she emerged from the shadows and moved step by step into the space between them.

The succubus across from them had eyes that showed almost no light left in them. It reminded Jace of the succubus he'd stabbed to death — the one Jagin had summoned — as she lay on the ground. The hint of allure and confidence from before had gone completely blank. Aside from staring straight at Morophil in front of her, she didn't dare shift her gaze anywhere else.

Only now did Jace feel a wave of lightness course through his body. From the moment the Dark Iron dwarf had sat down, he'd been under the continuous influence of seduction magic.

Morophil didn't care about the succubus or the dwarf at all. She simply moved closer to Jace and said, "I like it when people pay attention to me, master. I like showing up in crowded places. I like… you calling my name in front of others."

"Don't try to seduce me," the dwarf said, enunciating every word.

"Master didn't give the order, so why would I waste mana on you? After all, every bit of my mana is…" Morophil gently wrapped her arms around Jace's neck. "Given to me by him. Every drop is worth more than your wretched soul."

"Master… I… I can't…" the succubus was clearly terrified. She must have recognized that this was a flayer-huntress who specialized in hunting other succubi. She muttered under her breath, not knowing what to say. The dwarf gaped in shock at his servant's sudden surrender — and this time not even his beard could cover it.

"Don't be scared. I won't kill you." Morophil leaned against Jace without even looking at her. "Unless he tells me to."

"Fighting isn't allowed here," Jace said. "Otherwise those goblin soldiers outside will rush in and drag you away."

As he said it, he recalled the terror of being dominated by Booty Bay's infinitely respawning goblin guards — their capture nets, their endless knockback bullets, their ceaseless knockdowns, and a whole suite of horrifying tricks.

"If they hit me, you'll protect me, won't you?" Morophil asked.

Jace glanced at her. "How the hell am I supposed to protect you? You didn't see those hammers they're carrying? I don't want a beating either. Once you've killed your man, just turn invisible and run. They can't see you anyway. I can't turn invisible."

"I'd still rather have my master fight those green-skinned little runts to the death to protect me. What fun is turning invisible?" Morophil smiled. "Come to think of it, you're always so cold and heartless. I ought to have you flayed and gutted, just like that other warlock, nailed up alone in a freezing, pitch-black, deserted room to rot and stink."

The dwarf shuddered all of a sudden, then leaped out of his seat in one motion and went scurrying out of the tavern at a run. Seeing his master flee, the succubus ran even faster.

"Sit across from me, Morophil," Jace said. "Help me finish this meat. I really can't eat any more."

Morophil smiled and walked around to sit across from him elegantly, carefully rubbing her fingers together before reaching for a slice of fish.

"Can I taste this thing?"

The imp suddenly poked his head out from under Jace's cloak and asked.

Jace glanced at the chaotic, dimly lit tavern. No one had even glanced this way.

"Go ahead."

The imp grabbed a small broken piece of fish and stuffed it in his mouth to try, then stuck out his tongue. "Ugh. When are we going to find the merchant from the other continent?"

"Let me eat my fill and find a place to rest for the night," Jace said. "We'll talk tomorrow morning."
