## Chapter 324: The Trade House

When you actually walked into the nooks and crannies of Booty Bay, you'd realize it wasn't hard to find a place to spend the night with at least some peace of mind in a town this complicated.

Add in the protection of two demons, and he felt he could drop the qualifier "at least some."

Deep in the town were abandoned shanties everywhere, and some places even had people sleeping in them. Jace randomly searched for half an hour and found a spot that wasn't particularly filthy or foul, and sitting down beneath the shack, drowsiness came right on cue.

For Jace, this definitely wasn't the worst place he'd ever stayed.

When he opened his eyes in the morning, all of Booty Bay was already lively. Even from here he could see the tips of colorful ship sails moving back and forth across the bay.

Jace still remembered that in the game, heading down from the inn would take you toward the neutral Auction House and the bank.

But looking down from here, all he could see was an area too chaotic to make out any function — though it was far more prosperous than the dock area on the other side. It looked like it should be a marketplace of some sort.

There were goblins everywhere, chattering and shouting in swarms. Many of the shops and stalls were built from ship planks. Thinking back to the strange walls and floors inside the Sailor's Home Inn earlier, quite a bit of it seemed to be made from salvaged ship materials too.

Stopping in front of a small stall that was practically half-hanging off the street, Jace looked at the jumble of Gurubashi coins and troll voodoo dolls on display and asked the fat goblin inside, "Excuse me, sir. I'd like to know if there's a place in Booty Bay that sells potions?"

The goblin squinted and replied in halting Common, "Go look for yourself up ahead."

Jace picked up a rectangular copper coin carved with a sharp-fanged human face. "How much is this Gurubashi coin?"

The goblin looked at him. "You know your stuff?"

"There are plenty of them in the troll ruins in Stranglethorn Vale," Jace said. "Ancient coins discontinued by the jungle trolls."

"They haven't been discontinued, kid." The fat goblin sat up. "They're just used differently now than in the ancient days. This one's two silver, and you can take it. String a few together to make a small necklace if you like! You won't find these anywhere outside of Booty Bay!"

"They have them in Duskwood," Jace said. "Still, I figure buying them from Booty Bay is the real deal, right?"

"Of course!" the goblin said with a grin.

Jace put down two silver, took the Gurubashi coin the shopkeeper handed him, and tucked it into his pack.

"Handsome lad, are you an antiques dealer just starting out, or some kind of alchemy apprentice?" The goblin smacked his lips, picked up a white, skull-shaped coin, and toyed with it in his hand with a smile. "There are quite a few alchemists in town — goblin, human… I even hear there's a troll shaman from the north over there. What kind of medicine are you after? Something to cure an illness, save a life, or just for fun?"

Jace said, "I'm after potions that come from Kalimdor. Something like special brews made by centaur or tauren, or one of your goblin secret formulas."

The goblin cleared his throat. "Seeing as you know your way around, walk down this road, young man. Soon you'll see that tauren and his employer — the Trade House's director, Razzlovitz. The rarities coming in from Kalimdor are all sold through him."

Hearing "Trade House," Jace felt his stomach sink a little.

Of course he'd heard plenty of rumors about Booty Bay's Auction House — every one of them involving outrageous prices in the tens, hundreds, or even several hundred gold.

Ursula hadn't specified a clear price for Noggenfogger Elixir, but for a potion that could let you cheat a death curse, who could say where the upper limit on price even was?

"Have you heard of Noggenfogger Elixir, boss?" Jace asked casually. "I heard this potion from Kalimdor can make a person fake their death. Is that real or not?"

The boss shook his head. "Noggenfogger? That wealthy fellow from Gadgetzan? He's an alchemist, I think, but I honestly don't know much about him."

Jace thanked him and walked all the way down the road the boss had pointed toward, passing clusters of street vendors and shops one after another, until he reached the end of the road reeking of salted fish — the Trade House.

There were goblin guards everywhere here, watching over the cargo crates piled high on a dedicated dock and a tall stone-walled vault. From a long way off he could see a tall tauren covered in brown leather, wearing a bridle — or some kind of special "face-guard helmet" — sitting there, while a goblin wearing an expensive turban argued with a high elf standing below over a treasure chest.

"You can't go in!" A goblin guard blocked Jace, who was about to move forward, and waved his fish spear. "Back off, mage. Don't even think about casting any spells here!"

"I'm here to buy something," Jace explained.

"Don't be so rude to our customers. That said, sir, there's no auction today — what would you like to buy?" asked a goblin standing beside the guard.

Jace looked at him. This goblin was wearing a dark-purple embroidered outer robe and looked like a pretty wealthy fellow — probably some kind of assistant to the Auction House director inside.

"I'm looking for a potion. One that comes from Kalimdor," Jace said.

"Ah… another one looking for those wondrous Night Elf potions." The goblin nodded. "Then you'll want to talk to those short elves from Quel'Thalas. They're here to buy Dream Potion too, but in any case they'll have to wait for the next auction."

"Not Dream Potion," Jace said. "One called Noggenfogger Elixir."

"The special potion from that wealthy Noggenfogger fellow in Gadgetzan." The goblin looked up. "You mean the stuff that keeps turning people dead and alive?"

"That's the one," Jace said.

"What do you want that for? Ah, just asking — it's none of my business." The goblin laughed. "Truth is, the last time we sold that potion here was before the orcs ever set foot on Azeroth. We haven't heard any news of stock coming from Gadgetzan in this time. I know the wealthy Noggenfogger fellow himself isn't fully satisfied with his potion's effects. He's been planning to update the formula and manufacture a new generation of product. Maybe he's run into some problems with his material selection."

"You're an alchemist? But also a mage, I see. And a warlock too?" the goblin asked.

"A mage," Jace said.

The goblin nodded. "Before you leave, wouldn't you like to see if you've got anything good to sell? Our boss loves young mages — they always have strange and wondrous things on them, and they're usually short on money."

He glanced at the cluster of elves standing at a distance. "See those? Those are our big clients. Of course, trolls sometimes throw out outrageous prices for magical items too, after all — Gurubashi is the last place short on gold, am I right?"

"How much could you give me for this string?" Jace took the naga pearl bracelet off his wrist. The goblin took it and glanced at the pearls. "About forty to sixty silver. I'm no jewelry appraiser, but I can tell this is naga stuff. It doesn't look harmful, but naga things are always a bit off. I doubt it'd sell for much more than that, kid."

That was about the same as the estimate from the grocery merchant in Stormwind who'd reported him to the Watch, but Jace had been wearing this string for a while now and had grown a bit attached to it. If it was only a few tens of silver, he wasn't willing to sell it outright.

After all, this was the trophy from the first magical creature he'd killed on his own. Every time he looked at it, he'd remember just how absurd that night had been.

"I'll think about it more. How about this hat — how much for it?" Jace took off his red cap and asked.

"Is this an orc hat, or what?" The goblin turned it over a few times. "Got some magic in it, but not much. Those elves probably wouldn't look twice. But if you're set to sell, how about fourteen silver?"

"I'll keep wearing it." Jace took it back, then pulled the dragonkin fire staff off his back and handed it to the goblin. "What about this?"

"Ah… a staff. There's a flame stone on it, decent quality — still, someone would want this. I can give you one gold for it. The staff's material's not bad either. Where'd you get it?"

"It's a dragonkin's staff. I want two gold," Jace said.

"Dragonkin's staff?" The goblin hesitated, looked the staff over again, and leaned in to sniff it. "It does have the scent of black dragon fire. Give me a moment."

He carried the staff over to another goblin nearby, handed it to him, and the two discussed it for a while. The goblin came back and said, "One gold and eighty silver, then? On account of it genuinely looking like something a black dragonkin used."

"Two gold," Jace said.

"Oh, you." The goblin said, "Fine. Two gold it is."

The goblin took out a piece of paper, scribbled a dense line of text as a receipt, then handed Jace two Gurubashi gold coins and passed the staff to the goblin behind him.

"That's that. Got anything else you want to sell? I knew you'd have something."

"Nothing else," Jace said. "Do you have anything good for a mage here? Some kind of minor artifact, magical item, that sort of thing…"

"I've got an ice spike dagger forged from the ice of Winterspring," the goblin said. "Using this dagger as a conduit raises frost magic by a full tier. Perfect for you human mages. Most importantly, it's very small — easy to use covertly, enough to hit enemies with devastating magic they never see coming!"

"It went up to twelve gold at a previous auction. If you want it, I can let you have it for seven gold, mage."

"Something that only boosts frost magic won't do," Jace said.

The goblin nodded. "In that case, there's a very powerful dagger — Theranisha's Shadow, used by Theranisha, the ancient legendary harpy witch-queen herself. She relied on this mighty artifact to help her daughters rule over a stretch of mountains bigger than all of Stranglethorn Vale for decades!"
