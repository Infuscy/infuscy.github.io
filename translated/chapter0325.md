## Chapter 325: The Harpy's Relic

Theranisha — Jace had never heard that name in the game.

But he did know that harpies had once ruled a very large part of Kalimodor in ancient times. Legend held them to be descendants of Aviana, a Wild God, and they were an ancient magical species.

And if you wanted to become a leader among harpies, you didn't just have to kill enough enemies to earn respect — you had to kill enough of your own kind to command fear. So a harpy leader was, in herself, a statement of power.

In the game, harpy factions weren't that strong anymore, because in the future, when the orcs and trolls led by Thrall traveled to Kalimdor, they joined forces with the tauren to wage a series of campaigns against the harpies of the Barrens and Durotar. In the end, they brought those vast wild mountain ranges under the Horde's control.

From that point on, the harpies never recovered. They could no longer contend with the orcs and tauren for dominance over the Barrens and Stonetalon. But that didn't mean ancient harpies couldn't have produced terrifying artifacts.

"How much is this one?" Jace asked.

"I can't sell it to you right now. You'll have to wait for next month's auction. The starting bid is a hundred and twenty gold, with a deposit of seventeen gold."

"The starting bid is that much? I still don't quite understand exactly what kind of casting boost this weapon provides," Jace said. "This legendary Theranisha's Shadow — how does it compare to Azurewrath? Which one's more powerful?"

"You know about Azurewrath? I bet your knowledge of that weapon stops right at the name." The goblin smiled. "First of all, Azurewrath isn't a dagger — it's a magical sword. And it's not with the blue dragons anymore. It was sent to the elves of Quel'Thalas a long time ago, and the elves would never sell that sword to us. Legend has it that a member of the Order of Tirisfal once intended to buy that legendary artifact from the Windrunner general for several tens of gold, but nothing came of it in the end. I'm guessing the elves aren't very interested in metal that has no magic imbued in it."

"Of course, I'm not saying that sword could still be sold for that outrageous price today — that's just a legend that's been nearly forgotten, especially now that the Order of Tirisfal has vanished without a trace. But at least it shows these aren't things that can be discussed in the same breath, young mage. Don't expect treasures like that to be sitting here for sale. And if that sword ever showed up here, this little town would already be packed to bursting with sorcerers and collectors from every corner."

Hearing this, Jace realized it was a good thing he'd had a scabbard sewn by a leatherworker — something plain and unremarkable. After all, there was no way he could've left the sword back in Stormwind with no one to watch it.

Although the Marins — father and daughter — had promised to keep the matter strictly confidential, and he trusted both of their characters, there was no way he could've set his mind at ease leaving that sword behind when he left the city.

Come to think of it, Vereesa had really been generous… Maybe it really was as this goblin said — elves didn't like measuring the value of such items in gold.

But the one thing the world never lacked was people willing to measure the value of everything in gold. Forget thousands of gold… a few months ago, he himself had been willing to fight a black panther in a ship's hold for a few tens of silver.

Still, from the moment he'd first obtained it, Jace had understood clearly that this sword couldn't be shown to just anyone, and couldn't be used casually.

It could greatly boost his combat power, but he also needed to work hard to grow strong enough to protect this sword. Maybe this was what they called a "required level."

Then again, Jace still remembered when he'd taken Azurewrath out of its silk wrapping — Marin hadn't sensed its power until the blade was exposed. So as long as it was stored properly, the sword itself shouldn't attract extra attention either.

What's more, these goblin merchants in front of him hadn't noticed anything either.

"So, interested?" The goblin asked, a little too eagerly. "In that dagger?"

"A little interested," Jace said. "I'll come take a look next month."

"That'll do then! The Trade House always welcomes mages like you to come back!" The goblin pounded his chest. "Oh right, human — about that Noggenfogger Elixir, go have a look at that Gadgetzan specialty shop over there. It's run by Ganozkade from the Steamwheedle port. They might have some news."

As he spoke, the goblin pointed toward a storefront hung up high with a bunch of withered palm leaves. Jace could see from a distance that the shop seemed to have walls built from tawny-colored bricks, standing out conspicuously among the wood and stone buildings.

After saying goodbye to the goblin at the Trade House, Jace climbed the stairs up to the second-level street and came to this shop, where he saw that absurdly long, entirely unnecessary sign — "The Honest Ganozkade Couple's Gadgetzan Specialty Goods Trading Center."

Add in the various languages, and the sign looked less like a shop sign and more like some kind of public notice.

Jace glanced inside. "Is anyone there? Is Mr. Ganozkade in?"

"Mrs.!" A female goblin turned around. "Nobody told you the proprietor of Ganozkade's is a woman?!"

"My apologies, ma'am," Jace said.

"I should've changed the shop name long ago, seeing as good-for-nothing husband of mine is basically as good as dead." The female goblin was wearing a silk robe. She looked a bit taller than the average goblin and didn't wobble quite so much when she walked.

"What do you want, human? You know what we sell here, don't you?"

"Noggenfogger Elixir," Jace said, getting straight to the point.

"Bad luck — the last bottle was sold last year," the goblin said. "But I know the wealthy Noggenfogger fellow very well! Very well! I know he's been seeking a more interesting formula for his potion, so there hasn't been any finished product for a long time. Right now he's got his eye on the mayor of Gadgetzan — now that's a lot more profitable than making these strange potions. Though I figure he's not doing it for the money. Pure hobby, more like. Come to think of it, didn't you go check out the Trade House down below? They might have stock from a few years ago."

"I just came from there, ma'am," Jace said. "They told me to come find you."

"Then there's nothing I can do." The shopkeeper finished, then her eyes darted around. "Oh, right — do you know an alchemist named Bacchus Mithershield?"

"Who's that?" Jace said. "I don't know many goblins."

"He lives in Booty Bay, on the top floor of the buildings on the west side. I know he's on good terms with Noggenfogger, and I've heard he even participated in the development of that potion, human. You could go ask him."

"Thanks, lady," Jace said.

"You're welcome, young man." The female shopkeeper gave him a sidelong glance. "If you need any news about Gadgetzan or the Steamwheedle port, come ask me! I know lots of people, and all of them big shots. So I'm not going to be stingy like those paupers down below — the kind who'd squeeze two copper out of every single word they spit out!"
