## Chapter 34: Marin's Investigator

Early morning. Jace had packed the dried herbs and raw materials into the alchemy shop ahead of time, cleaned up the study, brewed the boss's tea as requested, and settled in by the bookshelf to wait for Marin to arrive.

As soon as his shift ended and the boss left, he'd start on his Shadow Protection Potion.

To kill time, he picked up a pirate novel — The Golden Shark — and started reading. It was basically all about the waters around Booty Bay.

Just as he was getting into it, Marin suddenly pushed the door open. He looked up and instinctively shoved the book straight into the nearby bookshelf.

The two of them exchanged an awkward look. Only then did Jace remember he'd already graduated high school in his past life — there was no need to hide a leisure book just because the teacher walked in.

"I'm sorry, Mr. Sesso. I should have knocked first." Marin smiled. "I'll be more careful next time."

"It's all right, Master Marin." Jace slowly pulled the book back out and set it on his lap.

Marin sat on the desk and poured himself a cup of tea, glancing curiously at the book in Jace's hands as he poured. "The Golden Shark isn't some forbidden text. I put it on the bookshelf precisely so anyone can read it freely. Why so nervous?"

"Ah… uh." Jace recalled that in this life he'd never really gone to school properly — he'd just learned to read and write from his mother. If he started talking about school experiences here, it'd be way too jarring.

"It reminds me of my days studying at Northshire Abbey," Marin said. "Back then the monks wouldn't let us kids read anything unrelated to our studies. If they caught us, they'd rap our knuckles — a bunch of heartless old bastards. But when I grew up and had my own daughter, hoping with all my heart she'd make something of herself, I'd get angry too when I saw her reading novels and junk unrelated to her studies. Only then did I come to understand what those old monks felt. Come to think of it, if I hadn't been so strict with Amy back then, would her studies have gone more smoothly?"

"Um…" Jace felt completely out of his depth evaluating something like this.

"Hah!" Marin sighed. "Am I talking too much? Right — about that question we discussed last time, the reason the red dragons were willing to serve the orcs. I felt like you were holding back on me. Got time to chat now? Reading novels, chatting — they're both pretty much the same way to pass the time, aren't they?"

"Of course." Jace answered lightly, but his brain was already racing to find an answer.

Between Westfall and Duskwood these past few weeks, between gathering herbs and selling them, learning the sword and brewing potions, he hadn't given a single thought to how he'd answer the old mage's questions about the red dragons.

Seeing that Jace wasn't taking the initiative, Marin said, "Let's start with this question: why, before the Dark Portal opened, didn't red dragons attack humans or other mortals on their own? Logically, as native magical creatures of Azeroth, red dragons shouldn't have had any connection to the orcs on the other side of the Dark Portal. So why, the moment the orcs arrived on this land, were they able to quickly form an alliance with the reclusive red dragonflight?"

Looking at Marin's expression, Jace had a feeling he already knew about the Demon Soul and was guiding him toward the answer.

"The orcs must have found some leverage over the red dragons," Jace said. "But that's just my guess."

"Actually, Dalaran thinks the same thing," Marin said, almost as if he'd been waiting for exactly that answer. "And they've already identified the orcs' likely accomplice."

"Black dragons," Jace said.

Marin nodded slowly. "Remarkable, Mr. Sesso."

Jace gave an awkward laugh. The praise was something he certainly didn't deserve.

If a seventeen-year-old native of this world had pieced this together on his own from all sorts of news and rumors, that really would be remarkable. But he was just a transmigrator who already knew the lore. Right now he felt exactly like a plagiarist.

Marin continued: "Archmage Krasus has been devoted to finding the missing Dragonqueen Alexstrasza. He believes the black dragons are involved. He's one of the Council of Six, you know? Not long ago, the Kingdom of Alterac was raided by the Horde. Those orcs were led by a death knight, and the mounts they rode weren't red dragons — they were black dragons. That proves Archmage Krasus's conjecture even further. The ones truly helping the orcs are the black dragons, not the red dragons. The Dragonqueen has most likely been captured by the black dragons as a prisoner. But the problem right now is, we have no idea where she is."

Krasus was a genuine red dragon. He'd taken the form of an elf, entered Dalaran, become one of the Council of Six, and concealed his identity to make it easier to communicate with mortals and find ways to help them deal with potential disasters — terrifying crises like the opening of the Dark Portal.

Most importantly, he was the most treasured consort of Queen Alexstrasza, so of course he was moving heaven and earth to find the queen.

Except he didn't know yet that the Dragonqueen was imprisoned in Grim Batol, the ancient old capital of the Wildhammer dwarves. When he found out, he'd surely rush around the world rallying people to tear that place apart — which was the suicide mission later carried out by the young mage Rhonin, the elf ranger Vereesa Windrunner, and the Wildhammer chief Kurdran: the rescue of the Dragonqueen.

On that mission, the protagonist-blessed Rhonin not only failed to keel over and die in the face of Deathwing as one might expect, but even got to know the beautiful white-haired elf lass Vereesa and married her — the outrageously lucky, cheat-possessing bastard.

Thinking of this, Jace couldn't help the anger surging up, clenching the book in his hand.

Krasus couldn't compare to those ultimate monsters with god-like power, but he was still one of the most powerful arcanists in Azeroth.

His thousands of years of life had given him the chance to learn countless strange spells, and Jace knew he'd use every means at his disposal to find his queen.

This was exactly why he couldn't reveal too much of what he knew in advance. If Krasus found out that some random Stormwind laborer knew the exact location where the Dragonmaw orc clan was holding the Dragonqueen prisoner, he probably wouldn't even know how he died.

However kind and amiable Krasus was, he was still a male red dragon who'd lived for thousands of years.

At that moment, Marin took a sip of tea, swished it around his throat — probably from talking too much and straining his voice.

After swallowing, he went on: "Earlier, a guard reported that the sentries at Stonewatch had spotted a dragon's tracks north of Redridge. He identified it as a young black dragon. That was also the reason I went to Redridge, but unfortunately I found nothing in the end. Perhaps it was just a stray wild black dragon, or perhaps the sentries simply misidentified some giant vulture as a dragon — and this wouldn't be the first time that's happened."

Jace hadn't expected a Wizard Sanctum mage to know so much about Dalaran's affairs. But thinking about it, it made sense — after all, the black dragons' next target was Blackrock Mountain, or the whole Redridge mountain range. The Wizard Sanctum was the closest human magical institution to the area.

If Dalaran wanted to learn more about dragon activity around here, they'd inevitably maintain close cooperation with the Wizard Sanctum and the Stormwind royal house.

"Mr. Jace Sesso." Marin set down his teacup. "Let me be direct — would you be willing to help me… handle matters concerning the orcs, the dragons, and Dalaran? Actually, when I first recruited an assistant, I had this partly in mind. I'm just one man — I really can't be in two places at once. But to help me with this work, you'd need to possess a certain level of knowledge, sufficient courage and responsibility. Most importantly, I think you're a good person, Mr. Sesso. I believe I can trust you."

Jace paused for a moment. "But, you know…"

"I understand," Marin cut in. "It would very likely mean traveling to some extremely dangerous places, or long journeys — a major change to your life. If you refuse, I understand. I won't fire you over something like that, you needn't worry. You're already an ideal assistant."

"No no, Master Marin, I mean." Jace set down The Golden Shark. "You'll have to pay more."

"Six silver a month right now — if I'm adding what I'm hoping you'll do, that is on the low side. And I'd expect travel expenses to be covered. If you ever have to make a trip to Dalaran, I can't very well expect you to pay for your own ship ticket, can you?"

Marin nodded. "Ten silver — how does that sound? If I have a task sending you to Dalaran, then it's certainly the Wizard Sanctum's directive, and someone will cover your passage — and that someone isn't necessarily me."

Hearing this, Jace felt a surge of excitement — not because ten silver was all that tempting, since his appetite had changed by now.

It was because ship tickets from Stormwind Harbor to Southshore fluctuated constantly, and even at the cheapest they were a dozen or so silver. Having to cough up that money would be a massive hit.

But he hadn't been home to visit in over two years. Saying he didn't miss his family would be a lie.

Although, as a transmigrator, his feelings toward his parents in this life might be subtly different from a real native's — they were still the flesh and blood who'd raised him for over ten years, changing his diapers and feeding him.

"As for going to other places." Marin stroked his beard. "Can you ride?"

"Yes."

Jace really wasn't bragging here. As the son of a city guard and a farmer's daughter, he'd had way more contact with horses than the average person.

When he was a kid, he'd figured that since he'd transmigrated, he couldn't waste time, so he'd learned a whole bunch of random things — including the most basic herbalism knowledge, which he'd picked up this way. Riding was naturally one of them.

He certainly hadn't learned it systematically. He'd just snuck rides here and there while running wild — a gang of bad kids from the city sneaking off to steal other people's horses, and some meddling adults secretly teaching them. Then when they got caught, their parents would beat them black and blue. Over time, he'd gradually picked it up.

Back then, his level was basically just riding a little faster without falling off — nowhere near actual horsemanship.

Even so, he'd still been one of the better riders among the kids his age in the Lordaeron capital. A lot of the city kids who snuck off to play with horses had initially thought knights were some kind of centaur-like creature born fused to a horse's back. They'd even get spooked seeing a horse with no one on it.

But in this world, the cost of keeping a horse was damn high — not much different from Earth, actually. So Jace had never thought about buying a horse either.

"The Wizard Sanctum's stable is just outside the western city wall. Walk out the small gate and it's a few minutes away," Marin said. "If there's ever a need to send you to Redridge or somewhere a bit farther, you can rent a horse for free with a note from me. But if you're gone for many days, you'll have to take good care of it."

Jace said, "I'll go take a look in a bit."

Marin asked again, "By the way, I heard you told Amy you didn't want to move into the Mage Quarter. Why? That way you wouldn't have to walk so far to get here every morning."

That stumped Jace. Who'd turn down a nice house and insist on living in some cramped rental instead?

"I'm not used to living in the Mage Quarter yet," Jace said. "Once I've settled into the work here, I'll figure out a way to move in."

Marin nodded slowly. "You have your reasons."
