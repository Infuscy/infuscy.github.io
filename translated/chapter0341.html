Chapter 341: Not Much Stronger

That evening, Jace hauled an armload of bags and bundles and followed Grede out through the city gates into Elwynn Forest.

Grede glanced back at Jace in the distance, gave him an extra look, and said, "I was wondering why you seem different — have you gotten taller?"

"Everyone keeps saying that." Jace stretched. "Guess I have."

"Feels like you swapped in a pair of platform boots." Grede eyed the bags he was carrying. "What's all this stuff? You skip dinner?"

"Had it, sure." Jace tossed one of the cloth bags onto the ground. "This one's full of dragon scales, plus a few claws and teeth — all from whelps. Do your dwarves back home have any use for materials like these? For making armor or weapons, that sort of thing?"

"What?!"

Grede dropped what he was doing, rushed over, and opened the bag for a look. He gave it a sniff. "These are the real deal... Let me ask around for you."

"Thanks, Grede." Jace said. "If they actually sell for something, you can keep a cut for yourself."

"You're planning to risk your neck in Dark Iron territory for me, and you think I'd take your money?" Grede cinched the bag shut and walked off. "Besides, we didn't get these together. I don't make money off your back, lad!"

Jace chuckled. "You think there's enough here to forge a suit of armor?"

"Not a chance, damn it." Grede said. "Unless you're just doing decoration — a breastplate with a few dragon scales inlaid for flair. Might be a bit better than ordinary plating when it comes to resisting fire magic."

"What about a helmet?" Jace asked.

The dwarf stared over for a moment, thinking. "That might actually work. Some kind of dragon-horned helm, maybe. I can ask. But you're a mage — won't wearing that thing get in your way?"

"It's for you, obviously." Jace said. "You can have one made to fit that oversized dwarven skull of yours."

"A gift for me?"

"Dream on. You're paying."

"Then I'll just sell the whole lot for you." Grede rapped his own head. "Do I need a helmet? This skull's harder than dragon bone."

Jace was about to crack a joke about how a skull couldn't possibly be tougher than dragon bone, but then he remembered the guy was a Wildhammer dwarf, and the words died in his throat.

"Enough chatter. It's getting late — time to train." Grede drew a gleaming longsword. "Take a look at this Loch Modan Mountaineer officer's blade. That beautiful dark guard, that broad, straight edge — perfect for a dwarf to swing."

Finished, he stared at the leather sheath at Jace's side and grinned. "What've you got there? Show me."

Jace gripped the blue-ran runed hilt and drew the sword in one smooth motion, accompanied by a sudden, ringing chord. The pale arcane glow shimmered all the more brilliantly under the moonlight.

Honestly, it was a little too brilliant. Jace was starting to worry that the blue radiance pouring off the blade would be visible from miles away.

But the very moment he thought it, the light drifting along the edge dimmed rapidly, as if the sword had read his mind.

"What... what kind of enchantment is that?" the dwarf asked. "Does it boost your mana?"

"This sword has no enchantment." Jace swung it back and forth a couple of times. "It's just like that."

"Hah." Grede snorted. "A sword that gives off arcane light on its own — what'd you think it was, the Song of the Blue Sky?"

"You know about the Song of the Blue Sky?" Jace asked.

"Of course." Grede said. "That elf told me about Quel'Thalas's magic sword, the Song of the Blue Sky. She said it was the kind of blade that could turn an entire garden silver-blue at night."

"And when something crosses its edge, it makes a sound like a musical instrument—" Grede suddenly stopped. "Wait... when you pulled that sword out just now, did I hear something?"

"This is the Song of the Blue Sky, Grede." Jace said. "A gift from that elf you're so convinced is trying to scheme against me."

"Wh—why?" Grede set his own sword down against his leg. "Why would she give you something like that?"

"Maybe because I killed Teron Gorefiend." Jace raised the sword. "Come on, Grede."

"What's the fucking point?" Grede looked at the blade in his own hand. "How am I supposed to fight that?"

"At the end of the day, it's just a magic sword." Jace waggled the edge. "If I'm not using it to cast, it shouldn't be much stronger than an ordinary blade."

"Fine." The dwarf groaned, fixing his eyes on Jace's arcane sword, and hefted his own blade again. "Then come on — let me see what you've learned these past few months."

Jace stepped forward. "Here I come!"

Grede nodded. Jace lunged straight at Grede's chest; Grede sidestepped out of the way.

"Shit—" Before he could finish, Jace followed up with a vertical slash. The blade cut through the air with a sudden, tightening chord. Grede parried lightly with his sword and deflected the strike, stepping back. He stared at Jace. "You've gotten plenty strong, I'll give you that. Growing taller came with a side of muscle, huh?"

Jace rolled his shoulders. "Never mind strength — what about technique? Any better?"

"Hard to say, Jace Sesso. When you're strong enough, you can cover for a lot of technical gaps. Still, I'm confident I can take you." Grede grinned.

"Then again." Jace said.

"My turn to attack." Grede gripped his sword with both hands, and his stubby legs suddenly drove forward in a rapid rush — so fast it made Jace's heart skip.

He should have expected Grede to have that kind of power. Back in the forest where Hogger had been hiding, the dwarf could get moving so fast even gnolls couldn't react in time.

Jace read the incoming slash and deflected it to the side and rear. Grede pressed the attack with a flurry of chops and thrusts. Jace parried a few of the strikes in turn, but just as Grede tried to close in for another thrust, his sword snapped clean off with the force of the swing.

Grede froze, staring at the half-broken blade in his hand. The fracture was still laced with scorch marks from arcane heat.

"Not much stronger, huh? You lying little shit." He sighed and set the broken sword on the ground. "That blade cost me thirty silver, I—"

Jace looked at the mangled edge on the ground, then at the silvery straight sword glowing softly in his own hand — not a single scratch on it.

"All right, I haven't actually used it before."

"Pay up!"

"Fine!"

"Don't you dare bring this thing out for practice again! You've used it — you're just pretending you haven't!"

"I really haven't..."
