Chapter 342: Retaliatory Practice

"No more sparring — next thing, next thing!"

The dwarf gathered up his blade and hilt, wrapped them together with the sheath, stuffed them into his bag, and looked up. "Let's talk about that magic business. What was it — a shield?"

As he spoke, he hefted the war hammer from his luggage. "Now I'm itching to help you practice your new spell, Jace! Look at me — I'm a real brother to you, dead set on making you stronger."

"I thought we agreed no hammers," Jace said.

"Who agreed to that?"

The dwarf swung the hammer and hurled it before Jace could blink. Jace caught it from the corner of his eye and immediately started chanting, "Tomuso... suowhat..."

Before he could recall the next word, the hammer was already spinning toward him!

Clearly the dwarf hadn't thrown it full force. The hammer carried no lightning, and it wasn't bearing down on him with the same vicious momentum it would have against a gnoll.

But it was still a hammer. Jace ducked. He'd barely lifted his head when the hammer came shrieking back with a crackle of electricity, grazing past him and slamming hard back into Grede's waiting hand.

For a moment he could hear the wind howling around him, the dark green forest swaying under the moonlight in the afterglow of the hammer's lightning.

Tomusogas, Veholissia... Jace muttered the incantation to himself. Grede's hammer was already whirling again in his grip.

"Wait, Grede, slow down!"

The dwarf completely ignored his protest and flung the hammer sideways. It flew in a flat spin, carving a curve through the air, the shock wave bending the tall grass flat in its path...

Jace still had a sword in his hand. No time for a chant, let alone a gesture — he could only leap aside. He watched the hammer sail past him, another burst of lightning flashing as it grazed his waist and rocketed back toward the dwarf like a cannonball.

Grede was trying to force him, through round after round of this kind of sparring, to drill the Shadow Shield into muscle memory — the kind that could block an incoming attack.

"Don't tell me you haven't even memorized the incantation yet, Jace Sesso!" he said sternly, though it was hard to tell whether he was still sore about the sword and wanted to settle the score right away, or whether he was genuinely trying to help Jace master the new spell.

Then again, the two weren't mutually exclusive.

The dwarf raised his hand. Jace quickly rattled off the incantation. A black ring of energy unfurled from his palm and spun outward, expanding until it encircled his whole body.

But Grede just stood there watching, never swinging the hammer. Jace watched the ring fade — and the dwarf suddenly hurled the hammer!

Jace tried to chant again, but this time he was too late. He threw himself aside in a clumsy dive. The hammer flew past his arm, the wake of air snapping his shirt violently. His feet had barely touched down when the hammer came flying back, forcing him to dodge again — until a sharp shout from Grede recalled the hammer to his hand.

Grede burst out laughing. "Barely lasted a second!"

Jace shot a sidelong glare at him. The dwarf kept summoning the hammer back with his thunderous energy as if it cost him nothing. Even now his breathing was even, his eyes steady, not a trace of fatigue.

No doubt about it — Grede hadn't spent the last two months just sightseeing in Ironforge. He'd trekked the shores of Loch Modan and the snowcapped peaks of Dun Morogh, and he must gone through battle after battle, ordeal after ordeal, all while gradually readjusting to his old rhythm and getting back to his former self.

But Jace had his own handicap.

When he'd practiced this Shadow Shield spell before, he'd always used both hands to cast. Now, with a sword in one hand — even if it was the Song of the Blue Sky — he couldn't quite get the gestures right.

"From the look on your face, you about to run dry?" Grede called out.

"What a joke."

Jace said it as he hurled the Song of the Blue Sky to the side. The blade plunged into the earth with a dull, low hum, its arcane glow dimming bit by bit until it merged with the moonlight.

"Again!" he shouted.

"All right!"

Grede swung the hammer without warning. Jace watched the spinning hammer and calmly began his incantation — but he still had to dodge sideways before the spell finished, and by the time the shield finally came up, it was a beat too late.

To buy the longest possible casting time, he had to keep his eyes locked on Grede's hand and start the incantation as early as possible.

But if he started chanting before the dwarf released, Grede — who knew exactly how a mage operated — would simply hold the throw, just like he'd done a moment ago.

Except that even though the dwarf had deliberately pulled his throws, the hammer still flew too fast. The window was so narrow that Jace could barely get a handle on it.

But he knew that was the whole point of the drill.

Jace silently ran through the incantation once more in his head, watching the gryphon rider swing his hammer round after round. Everything outside his field of vision slowly faded as his focus narrowed to a point.

The dwarf's movements shifted. Even in the dark, even at this distance, Jace caught the change in the muscles of his arm and neck. He was about to throw—

The instant the hammer left his hand, Jace began chanting!

This time he was ready, and he rattled off the words fast. By the time the hammer reached the halfway point, he felt no fear at all.

The incantation completed smoothly. He thrust both hands forward and raised the ring of energy around his body. The incoming hammer slammed hard into the shield, splashing off an oily, iridescent shimmer like oil floating on water!

The ring burst apart suddenly. The grass around them blackened and curled inward in an instant. The hammer wobbled uselessly in midair a few times before dropping next to a tree.

"So you said you couldn't chant in time, Jace Sesso." The dwarf reached out and recalled the crackling hammer to his hand. "Seemed fast enough to me!"

Jace said, "If that hadn't worked, my brains would have been splattered."

"You think I'd let the hammer hit you in the face, Jace?" Grede said. "You underestimate my skill. But if you don't get serious, how are you ever going to perform under real combat conditions?"

"I know." Jace nodded.

"But this is a flying hammer we're talking about." Grede said. "If it were a sword instead, you still wouldn't make it in time. And I think you ought to figure out how to cast that protection spell while holding the pretty toy Vereesa gave you."

"I still need to practice. One step at a time." Jace glanced at the Song of the Blue Sky. The sword flew straight back into his hand and settled firmly into his grip. He slid it back into its sheath without thinking.

Grede stared at that, dumbfounded.

"Can you pull any weapon back to your hand with that magic — besides a staff?"

Jace hadn't spoken a single word of incantation. It was as if he'd simply... wanted the sword to come to his hand, so naturally that he hadn't even realized what spell he'd used.
