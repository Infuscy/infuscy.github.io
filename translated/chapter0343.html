Chapter 343: Gorefiend's "Legacy"

"I didn't do that on purpose, Grede. Let me try again."

Jace drew the sword and tossed it a few meters away, then reached out, trying to pull it back with some kind of mental force. He tried several times and nothing happened.

Grede just stood there watching him flail through all sorts of strange gestures at a motionless straight sword, and couldn't help saying, "Are you pretending to be confused, or do you genuinely have no idea what's going on..."

"I really can't find that feeling from before," Jace said.

But even as he said it, recalling his staff had been perfectly natural. So why not try treating this sword like his staff and pulling it back the same way?

With that, he fixed his eyes on the sword again, imagined it as his staff lying in the distance, and reached out once more.

This time the Song of the Blue Sky shot toward him as if something had grabbed it and yanked it over. Jace tried to catch the hilt and nearly got his hand sliced by the blade.

"I don't have very good control over the force," Jace said.

"Try something else," the dwarf said.

Jace nodded, looked around, and finally settled his gaze on the hammer in the dwarf's hand.

"Toss your hammer out. I'll try to drag it over and catch it."

Grede nodded and hurled the war hammer high into the air. Jace locked onto the spinning hammer and reached out. A blood-red tendril punched out of his palm — a blurry, indistinct thing like a cobra striking, latching onto the hammer!

Jace yanked it back and caught the hammer in his hand.

This was definitely Gorefiend's magic. He was sure of it — after all, back in Dalaran he might have witnessed Teron Gorefiend use this very trick on a fleeing Kafell.

And by now, he could feel the toll it had taken on his energy.

"Where'd you learn that move?" Grede asked.

"Where else? That orc warlock, obviously." Jace tossed the hammer back to the dwarf.

Grede caught it. "That's got some real kick to it, Jace. Wonder what it'd be like using it on a person."

"You remember those rooftops we saw smashed in, back in Dalaran?" Jace said. "Those chunks torn out of the roofs and all — that was Teron Gorefiend's magic, ripping them apart."

Grede nodded slowly. "So if I'm locked in a fight with someone and you try to grab them with this magic... aim carefully. Don't grab me."

Jace said, "Until I understand exactly how much power this spell has and how to use it proficiently, I'm definitely not going to deploy it in such a dangerous way."

"So you want to keep practicing?" Grede asked.

"It's too late today." Jace said. "And I've burned through a lot of energy with all these drills. We'll do it another time."

Grede strapped the hammer to his waist, leaned against a tree, and sat down. "Anyway, sparring with you tonight has given me a lot more confidence about our trip to the Dark Iron dig site, Jace."

"Say, have you thought about how we're actually getting there?" Jace asked.

Grede looked up. "You said at lunch you went to the Blasted Lands with Vereesa Windrunner, right? How'd you get there — straight through Redridge?"

"Right." Jace said. "But going that way... on paper it looks smooth, except halfway through you've got to cross the Burning Steppes, Grede. And there are black dragons on the Burning Steppes now."

When it came to the journey, Jace suddenly remembered Alna, the mare Varian had given him. He'd have to check on her at the Mage Quarter stables first thing tomorrow morning.

He patted the bag at his side containing the black dragon teeth and scales. "Not just dragons — there are dragonkin roaming plenty of places in between."

Grede leaned against the tree and bared his teeth. "After the crazed fire elementals, the bloodthirsty Blackrock orcs, a third kind of monster has shown up around Shadowforge City — black dragons, the one thing nobody wants to run into. The Dark Iron clan sure got themselves blessed, didn't they? A hundred years of chaos, and they managed to carve out themselves a real paradise. Hope the next few hundred years treat them real well."

Jace couldn't help laughing. "Anyway, I'm not going through there again, Grede."

"Then take the route I took, through the Wetlands?" Grede said. "Swamp full of crocs and marsh beasts, plus gnolls and murlocs..."

"So you really crossed the entire Wetlands alone, through the Dun Algaz passage to Loch Modan?" Jace said admiringly. "You've got some nerve."

Grede said, "The mountains of Loch Modan aren't like Redridge. They're snowcapped year-round, and there aren't nearly as many mountain paths as in the Alterac range. Trying to force your way over is next to impossible. And even once you reach Loch Modan, you've still got to get past a mountain road overrun by kobolds. I really..."

He muttered something in Dwarvish. Jace was sure it wasn't anything pleasant.

"But if we don't take a ship to Menethil Harbor, our only option is to head north from Stonewatch into the forest." he said. "Dealing with a pack of gnolls in the forest is still a damn sight better than getting chased across an open plain by flying black devils. You know the ruins of Thaurissan? Vereesa and I got chased by a black dragon into those ruins. God knows how long we wandered in there before we finally found a way out. Teron Gorefiend was hiding in there."

"Hah, I know exactly why he was hiding in there." Grede said. "He and the ghosts of the Dark Iron clan must've had plenty to talk about."

At that, the dwarf laughed. "Now I understand why Vereesa Windrunner gave you that sword, Jace Sesso. If someone had dragged me out of Thaurissan, I'd marry them on the spot. I'm not joking."

"It's a place that makes living Dark Iron dwarves break into a cold sweat just mentioning it. Think about it — tens of thousands of poor souls burned alive by the flames their most beloved king summoned. How much resentment and anguish do you think that left behind? There aren't many worse fates in this world."

"All that said, I still think we should take the route I took, Jace. Compared to a whole army of gnolls in the forest and black dragons blanketing the hills, I'd say swamp crocs and marsh beasts that barely pay you any mind are a lot friendlier."

Truth be told, Jace also favored going through the Wetlands to Loch Modan, then entering the Blasted Lands from the south of Loch Modan.

After all, the dwarven town of Thelsamar sat on the southern shore of the Loch, not far from the Blasted Lands and close to the ruins of Uldaman — it could serve as a resupply point. Thelsamar was already famous far and wide as a food lover's paradise.

If they went through Redridge instead, they'd have to push south across the entire wasteland to reach the Dark Iron dig site, and God knew how many more troubles they'd run into along the way. It might end up taking even longer than detouring to the north.

"Then let's settle on that for now," Jace said.
