Chapter 344: Alna in the Rain

The next morning, Jace arrived at the Mage Quarter. No sooner had he caught sight of the Wizard Sanctum's spire than it started to rain.

He sprinted all the way up to the top floor and ducked into the tower doorway, where a hallway already held a number of apprentice mages sheltering from the rain.

Jace shook the water from his hair, exchanged glances with the apprentices nearby, and traded good mornings. Then he clearly heard one apprentice across the hall turn to his companion and mutter, "That's him."

"That person... right."

"Mmm."

Hearing that, lingering here was going to get uncomfortable.

His hair was still wet, but he didn't care anymore. He headed upstairs to Andormas's office. After squeezing in through the door with a few others, he found Andormas sitting behind his desk, sorting through stacks of yellowed documents.

"Jace? What are you doing here?"

"Master Andormas, I've come to collect my Apprentice Mage token."

"Right — what took you so long?"

Andormas set down what he was working on, pulled a small purple box from a shelf behind him, and handed it to Jace. He glanced at Jace's dripping hair. "Is it raining outside?"

"Yes, Master." Jace said. "Not long after I set out."

"I was about to remind you to go check on Alna." Andormas said. "With rain this heavy, that mare probably isn't keen on going out for a walk with you."

"I doubt she'd want to even if it wasn't raining at all," Jace joked.

Andormas smiled. "King Varian said she had a temper, but she's been quite well-behaved at the Mage Quarter stables. Probably the change of scenery."

Jace remembered the whispers he'd heard from the students in the Wizard Sanctum and couldn't resist asking quietly, "Master Andormas, do the students here all know what I did by now?"

Andormas said, "Even if word from the palace hadn't gotten out, news from Ironforge would have reached them by now — that a young mage of the Wizard Sanctum assisted the Windrunner in hunting down Teron Gorefiend. But I recall warning you to be prepared for this."

Jace looked at the inscription reading "Sesso" on the ring inside the box. "I understand, Master."

Leaving the magic tower, the rain outside was coming down even harder, with muffled thunder rumbling overhead. Behind the curtain of rain, he could barely make out the little door to Marin's study from the tower, and even the garden hedges were only dim blurs.

Jace put the ring on, took shelter under the eaves along the road, and headed toward the wall on the other side of the Mage Quarter. Rain or not, he intended to go see that horse.

Carefully picking his way down the rain-slicked stone steps that ran along the wall, he reached the small gate at the base of the wall. He followed the wall over to the stablemaster's quarters and knocked on the door. The portly stablemaster poked his head through the gap and said, "What can I do for you, sir?"

"Hello. I'd like to visit the stables, sir. Could you take me there?"

The stablemaster asked, "Are you planning to ride out? Let me see your credentials, mage."

"There's a horse in the stables that's mine." Jace said. "I'd like to see how she's doing."

The stablemaster gave Jace a once-over. "We've met before, I think, but I don't remember you boarding a horse here. I know the owners of every horse here by face. When did you put your horse here?"

"Just a few days ago," Jace said.

"A few days ago?" The stablemaster nodded as if something had clicked. "The black one? Brought over from the palace?"

"Right."

"You're Jace Sesso?" he asked, squinting. "You're the one who killed that Riverpaw gnoll chieftain who went 'Hogger, Hogger, Hogger' every time he opened his mouth? Heard he was a black monster over two meters tall!"

"That's me, sir."

"Let me see the ring, Mr. Sesso." The stablemaster beamed as Jace handed over the Apprentice Mage token. "My, you're younger than I expected. Honestly, I was a little worried the Riverpaw gnolls might come this way someday — I don't live behind that tall wall back there, after all. By the way, are gnolls and orcs in cahoots?"

"They certainly could become one, if it meant getting at us," Jace said.

The stablemaster nodded, threw on a black-gray linen cloak, locked up, and headed toward the stables, peppering Jace the whole way with questions about how he'd fought Hogger in that forest.

Once inside the stables, Jace wiped the rain from his face and looked at the beautiful black mare at the end of the row. Compared to all the horses here of every shape and size, she looked sturdier and more powerful.

The stablemaster pulled the stall door open. "This lady's upkeep is paid for by the palace. Is it because you brought back Hogger's head with your own hands? The king must have rewarded your bravery with this fine mare."

"Claws, sir. My companion and I brought back Hogger's claws. His head was too heavy."

Even as he said it, Jace watched Alna trot out from inside with bright, eager steps. When the stablemaster threw a saddle over her, she kept her eyes fixed on the yard outside — not looking the least bit frightened by the thunder or the rain.

"Can I take her out for a ride?"

"It's raining pretty hard out there."

"Will she get sick?"

"She's a highland warhorse's descendant, Mr. Sesso. I'm worried about you." The stablemaster handed the reins to Jace.

Jace took the reins. "It's fine. Me getting sick doesn't cost much."

Watching Jace lead Alna out into the yard, the stablemaster called out, "She's been much more obedient these past few days, sir. Have you met her before?"

"I saw her at Stormwind Keep," Jace shouted.

"Well then she's found someone she knows!" the stablemaster yelled.

Jace put his hand on the horse's back and swung up easily. Alna couldn't help prancing forward a small step, catching him off guard and nearly throwing him.

Alna happily trotted circles around the yard. Feeling the powerful mare's steady stride, Jace nearly forgot the cold, clammy discomfort of the rain soaking into his clothes.

It was just that these horses weren't like the ones in the game, where you just cast a spell to summon them and they vanished into thin air the moment you dismissed the mount.

Varian was right — once you rode out into the wild, you'd have to take care of her yourself.

Alna certainly couldn't carry him and Grede all the way from Stormwind to the Blasted Lands, to say nothing of whether she could even cross the mountains of Redridge. Even making it through that forest overrun by gnolls would be a tall order.

And if they went to Menethil Harbor, they'd need to take a ship along the way.

Taking a ship was miserable enough as it was. Who knew what trouble a horse might cause on board? And he still wasn't used to her temperament — better not push his luck. For now, he'd ride her around Stormwind or the area near Goldshire, build some rapport and get in sync.
