## Chapter 345: The Summoning Gathering

The rain had let up quite a bit by the afternoon. Jace said goodbye to the caretaker and headed back to the Mage Quarter.

He planned to stop by the Slaughtered Lamb and see if there were any potions that could help him and Grede on their trip to Khaz Modan.

Even if invisibility potions could help them slip past the Dark Iron dwarves, Jace knew there was another problem lurking in Uldaman: the troggs.

Brutish as they were, the troggs' affinity with earth and soil had also produced a handful of shamans capable of manipulating the elements of nature.

To deal with those things, he'd need to stock up on potions related to nature protection.

The moment Jace arrived at the basement, he saw Zardeth deep in discussion with Surena and Erlan.

"You're all here. Is Ursula around?" he asked.

"Of course, Jace. She's inside packing things up — she'll be out shortly," Zardeth said. "You've come at just the right time. I was actually about to have Surena come find you."

Jace flicked the rainwater clinging to the tips of his hair. "What's up?"

"I'm going to try summoning a succubus," Zardeth said. "I think it's time we took that step. Are you free this afternoon and evening?"

"Sure," Jace nodded.

"Excellent," Zardeth said. "Honestly, I've been wanting to finish what Gakin started for a while now. But right around then Ursula said you'd gone south on business and might be gone for a long time. So now that you're back and we've all got time, let's get together and get it done."

Erlan said, "From Gakin's notes, summoning a succubus requires sacrificing a soul, plus a magical object that proves the existence of love."

"A magical object that proves the existence of love?" Jace asked. "What does that actually mean?"

"I don't know what he meant," Erlan said. "Gakin had so much stuff lying around, I have no idea what he actually used."

But what Jace really cared about was why he himself had managed to summon a succubus without using any so-called love-proving magical object.

This was also exactly why he'd been completely caught off guard when a succubus first showed up — after all, in the game, summoning a succubus also required an object representing the power of love to attract the creature's attention.

Erlan sighed. "How did he figure all this stuff out?"

"I suspect he probably went to see Mor'zul," Zardeth said. "I've always suspected that even after Mor'zul left us, Gakin never broke off contact with that guy."

"Well, however he figured it out, it indirectly helped us out. Today, let's try summoning a succubus!"

"So what love token are you planning to use?" Jace asked.

"A wedding ring — that should work, right?" Erlan said. "I bought it. Cost me a pretty penny, too. The seller told me its previous owner had died of grief after her husband passed away."

"And who sold you this ring?" Ursula asked, walking over.

"Who else? Their kid," Surena said with a grin.

Ursula clicked her tongue and turned to Erlan. "The seller probably made up that story just to scam you out of your money, and in the end it won't work at all."

"Why would someone selling me a wedding ring make up an unlucky story like that just to put me off?" Erlan shot Surena a look. "Wouldn't you say?"

"Fuck off," Surena said, glaring at him sideways, clearly annoyed.

"Anyway, we'll find out if it works once we try it," Zardeth said.

"What about the soul?" Jace asked.

"There are a few small soul shards in Gakin's room." Zardeth pointed to a black box on the table.

Jace picked it up. "Can I open it?"

"Go ahead," Zardeth said.

"The soul's trapped inside the soul shard, not inside the box," Ursula said.

Jace opened the box and saw a tiny, dark, glass-like fragment inside. He could feel a soul trembling within it, filled with fear — and this wasn't a human soul.

Still, even summoning a special succubus like Morophil hadn't required a human soul, so this one should do the trick.

After looking it over, Jace closed the box. Zardeth took the soul shard and the ring and asked, "Do you want to do the summoning?"

Jace said, "You go ahead first."

Zardeth nodded. "If anything unexpected happens, I'm counting on all of you to help me out as much as you can. After all, this is a real demon, and we've already lost one of our number."

The warlocks around them all nodded. Jace noticed Erlan was already holding those iron tongs. He grabbed a fruit-cutting knife off the table himself.

Everyone moved to the practice room. Zardeth placed the soul shard and the ring in the center of the shadow circle, stood up, and exhaled a few times — clearly nervous.

The others were even more tense than he was. Surena and Ursula stood right by the door, looking ready to bolt at any second. Erlan pressed himself against the wall, gripping the iron tongs tightly, his eyes locked on Jace.

Zardeth glanced over the incantation on the slip of paper, then looked up at Jace. "I know you've killed a succubus before, Jace, but give me a moment..."

"The demon's mental magic," Jace reminded him.

Zardeth nodded, turned back, and fixed his eyes on the paper as he began casting. "Inhajet, Tulioxenoni..."

He spoke slowly and carefully. Jace could see the soul shard beginning to react.

"Tayatansayidini, Binodoxis, Tarvisayt, Elamun, Ulhoxkis."

By the time he finished the incantation, the soul shard had already dimmed, and even the shadow circle had lost its color. The ring gave a faint, barely perceptible tremor.

Then, a sudden gust of wind with no apparent cause blew out the room's only light — the brazier.

"What the hell!"

"I..."

A flurry of murmurs in the darkness. Jace said nothing and immediately cast a Scorch spell to relight the brazier. The room flared back to life, and there — a pale-skinned female demon was leaning against Zardeth with a smile.

Zardeth turned his head to look at her, the picture of someone drained of mana and strength, barely kept from collapsing by the demon's arm around his waist.

This demon wasn't looking at Zardeth. She was staring straight at them. "Hmm. You're a bit different from the rest."

Jace had just drawn his knife when those words sent a chill down the back of his neck. He glanced sideways and noticed that everyone in the practice room seemed off.

"You should kill him, master," the succubus whispered, leaning against Zardeth's neck.

"No, I shouldn't..." Zardeth forced the words out.

"Master won't obey. He must be very tired," the succubus said, a note of helplessness in her voice.

"Then master's companions — help master eliminate this disobedient outsider."
