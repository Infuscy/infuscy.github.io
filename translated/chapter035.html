Chapter 35: Not a Drop Wasted

After they'd more or less settled on the new terms, the old mage went on with his work, and Jace had already started envisioning his future.

Logically, Marin knew he was an ordinary person who couldn't use magic and didn't have any particularly outstanding combat ability, so he probably wouldn't send him on anything too dangerous.

Ridiculous tasks like sneaking into an orc camp or capturing a black dragon whelp would presumably still be handed to other mages, apprentice mages, or mercenaries.

And him? At most delivering letters, carrying things — otherwise Marin wouldn't be paying only ten silver a month.

At noon, when he should have started brewing right away, Jace's curiosity dragged him out to go see the stable instead.

Outside the western city gate, the Wizard Sanctum's stable leaned against the wall. Looking north from here, past the paddock, you could see the sea beyond the cliff top.

A few young people who looked like mages were gathered at the foot of the wizard tower on the cliff top, talking and laughing — their voices faintly audible even from far away.

The horses in the stable looked remarkably healthy and beautiful, mostly chestnut and black, with a few pintos. Each one was kept with a glossy coat, broad chests, and leg muscles tensed and bulging outward.

What can you say — the Mage Quarter had money. Even the horses they kept reeked of wealth.

Jace remembered when he'd gone to Eastvale, he'd made a special trip to the stable there. The paddock was much bigger than here, but the horses there always seemed more listless than these.

Eastvale Logging Camp was where Stormwind's knights got their warhorses. Logically, those horses should have been stronger and more disciplined than the Wizard Sanctum's.

Jace guessed it was because the war had only just ended. Plenty of sturdy warhorses had died on the battlefield, or been injured and couldn't stay, so many of the ones left were inferior pack horses.

As he approached, a few horses unfamiliar with his scent snorted uneasily — full of life.

When would he ever own a horse of his own? Buying a horse took money, but you also needed enough steady income to cover the ongoing costs of feed and upkeep. The house you bought needed a stable too. The more he thought about it, the more of a headache it got.

Anyway, a ten-silver-a-month job wasn't going to cut it.

But then, thinking of Grede, who'd once ridden a griffon, the horse didn't seem so appealing anymore.

And then, thinking of the top-tier characters in the game who rode kodos, dinosaurs, the heads of Keepers, whelps, and phoenixes — what was a horse even worth against that?!

Jace trudged back to the alchemy shop in a foul mood, opened his pack, and looked at the stuff he'd fought like hell to haul back. The contrast hit him hard.

He sat in a chair and zoned out for a while. He felt like he'd been getting unhappy about not having a cheat way too often lately.

Probably because he'd been through a lot of hardship recently and seen too many people more powerful than him — his mentality was a little unbalanced.

But all in all, he still had a unique advantage: foreknowledge of the future… his only advantage, and he had to make good use of it.

Thinking that, his drive came back.

He readied the cauldron, poured in spring water, and sprinkled Grave Moss into the water bit by bit. The revolting, nausea-inducing stench brought back the miserable experience in the crypt.

He took out the small pouch of ground Evil Claw powder and poured it into the boss's mortar. While waiting for the water to boil, he started grinding.

After all, the recipe's instructions said the Evil Claw powder needed to be ground as fine as possible, so he planned to spend the entire brewing process grinding that stuff — Evil Claw was the last ingredient to go in, anyway.

Before long, he glanced at the water's surface — it had started rolling, and the water had turned a light purple. He had no idea why green Grave Moss turned purple after a few minutes of boiling, but the recipe said so, so it must be right.

A strange, fishy smell rose up — quite different from the smell in the crypt. This smell… he seemed to smell it somewhere before.

Right — in the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb. This was the smell of Shadow energy, no doubt about it. And this purple — was this the purple of Shadow energy too?

He picked up the stirring rod and began stirring gently, keeping an eye on his pocket watch to time it. Twenty minutes later, as the water darkened, he poured in the Kingsblood powder.

Grinding Kingsblood was way easier than Evil Claw — the herb dried on its own and crumbled to nothing the moment you ground it.

After killing the fire, he watched the hot liquid in the cauldron already starting to turn a little transparent.

Smoother than expected. Jace even found a bit of that feeling of following a recipe in a short video to cook a dish — a weird thrill when every step of what he made matched up.

While the potion liquid stabilized, he stepped outside the alchemy shop for some air.

By now he was drenched in sweat, his shirt soaked through — he looked like he'd just jumped out of the water. Passersby stole sideways glances at him.

At this point he couldn't care less about his appearance. These nobodies walking by were all going to be minor characters in the future. Once he made the Shadow Protection Potion, once he learned warlock magic…

Had it been too long…? He turned back to the alchemy shop. The potion liquid was already semi-transparent. He hurriedly reheated it to a boil.

The final step: add the Evil Claw powder.

The slightly nauseating smell from earlier had mostly faded. The potion gave off a fishy smell that wasn't too offensive. After killing the fire, though the liquid was still steaming, it already looked about what the recipe described.

He dipped the stirring rod and tasted a little. Somewhat sour — it should be done.

Jace used the lead vials he'd bought earlier and filled seven bottles in one go, not stopping until the very last drop was poured out, then sealed them one by one and packed them into a bag.

He couldn't wait until the next day. That evening he brought a bottle to the door of the Slaughtered Lamb.

The barbarian-like bartender, Tharle Mor, was still standing there. The tavern still had that same dead-inside atmosphere.

Jace downed the bottle he'd brought in one gulp. Maybe because he drank it too fast, the punch of sour and spicy raced down his throat and into his gut, and he had to lean against the wall for a while before the potion's fierce dizziness passed.

At least it matched what the recipe said — he guessed his first attempt at potion-making had turned out all right. Good thing it was a simplified recipe, suitable for alchemy beginners.

So the people inside wouldn't notice he'd brought a potion, he hid the vial in the flower bed under the tavern steps, then walked in and up to the bartender.

"I'm back, Mr. Mor."

Tharle glanced over, wiping the table as he asked, "What'll it be? Mead, malt, Dwarven Stout, Dark Wine?"

"You don't remember me?"

"Ah… you're that." Tharle couldn't help but laugh. "The kid who passed out on my bar — Jace, right? I remember you now. I even gave you an extra silver."

"I passed out here?" Jace tried to recall that day. The process from the basement to the outdoor garden — he really couldn't remember any of it.

Tharle said, "I found two guys to throw you out. Don't blame me — you lying there was bad for business."

Is this what you call business? Jace muttered in his head.

"It's just, you've come at an unlucky time," Tharle Mor said. "They've already left."

Jace could hardly believe his ears. "What? Your people aren't in the basement?"

Tharle frowned. "Keep your voice down."

"Sorry." Jace leaned closer to the bar. "I really need two silver."

"That's not something I can help with," Tharle said. "They don't live in my basement. Of course they go home at night."

Jace said in a low voice, "Tell me their addresses — Mr. Erland, or Ms. Sulenna, either one. Someone willing to use me as a test subject. I can take less money if I have to."

Tharle shook his head helplessly. "Erland lives at a farm outside Goldshire. Sulenna… in Old Town. You sure you want to go?"

Jace felt like he might actually have a shot. "Give me Ms. Sulenna's address. I can't make it to Goldshire tonight."

"It might be none of my business, but I'm warning you." Tharle took out a slip of paper, writing as he spoke. "Sulenna isn't a woman someone like you should get close to. If she's willing to pay you to test her spells, take the money and leave. Don't stick your nose where it doesn't belong."

Jace snatched the note and bolted out the tavern's main door, heading straight for outside the Mage Quarter. This was hardly the time to be thinking about that sort of thing.
