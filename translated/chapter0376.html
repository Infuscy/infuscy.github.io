Chapter 376: Finally Getting Some Rest

After collecting the vial of spores from the herbalist in Thelsamar, Jace and Grede restocked their food and set off on the journey back. By the time they returned to Stormwind, it was nearly June, and the weather was getting hotter by the day. Jace and Grede found a small inn run by a gnome near the harbor, sat down, and ordered two grilled fish and two mugs of small beer with hardly any alcohol in it. Jace looked at the scrawny little fish the boss had brought over — the kind that looked like it had never had a full meal in its life — then glanced at the gnome proprietor and thought: is everything smaller at a gnome-run establishment? "I never want to set foot on a ship again, Grede," he sighed with feeling. "First a trip to Booty Bay, and before I'd even recovered from that, off on another boat with you to Khaz Modan." "I went to Khaz Modan once and then went to Khaz Modan a second time, Jace," Grede laughed. "Though of course you went with me — you didn't have to make this trip at all." Jace said, "The only thing I really regret is, we agreed that everything we found in that temple would be mine, and I didn't end up bringing a single thing out..." "At least we brought back an axe." Grede patted the stone short-axe hanging outside his pack. "One of the stone dwarf statues in that great hall. Honestly, it doesn't handle quite right for me, whether it's the weight or something else. We could ask how much we could sell it for — might count as an artifact." Jace shook his head. "Forget it. I'll keep it as a souvenir of this whole adventure. I wasn't in it for the money anyway. Still, I've got thirty-something gold coins on me now. If I save up a little more, I might actually be able to buy a house in Old Town." "Thirty gold won't get you much of a decent place over there right now, Jace," Grede said. "Fine for just you, or you could find a wife and squeeze in together, probably. But if you're bringing your parents to live with you — in that rough-and-tumble old district, dirty and run-down, crammed into a tiny room like that — could you live with yourself? Whatever new place you buy should at least be better than where they're living now." "Besides, maintenance on a cheap house is a fortune all on its own. You can't imagine what it costs to renovate and repair those old places... Even with a new house, you can't just blow all your savings at once. The ongoing costs add up no matter where you are, even in Goldshire. You don't want them arriving here only to start going into debt on your account, do you?" Hearing Grede say that, Jace thought about his parents having lived half their lives in the old craftsman district of the capital city, on those muddy roads. Bringing them to Stormwind only to stick them in a similar environment — an even smaller room — really did feel wrong. "You've been in Stormwind less than three years, and we've only really been making money for less than a year," Grede went on. "In a few more months, go back and show your parents the gold you've earned, then buy a nice new place near the Trade District — wouldn't that be better? I used to have a neighbor, a merchant from Ironforge, who bought a small house on the canal by the Trade District for seventy gold, big enough for him, his wife, and their son. Two years ago, when we first arrived in Stormwind, seventy gold seemed completely out of reach. But now, the two of us aren't far off at all." Jace nodded. "You've got a point." "Or you could ask at the registration office by the city gate," Grede said, sucking on a leftover fish bone. "People register their houses for sale there, and they'll take you to see the place." Jace had actually been meaning to go ask about housing. Before, he'd had too little money, and later he'd been too busy running around earning and spending, and he'd completely forgotten about going to look. After splitting up with Grede and heading home, Jace pushed open the door to find Morophil flipping through the demonic language vocabulary materials the imp had translated. When she saw Jace, her eyes widened. "Master... you're finally back!" "Master! I—" The imp lunged out from under the bed, only to get kicked back by the succubus' hoof. Morophil leaned in. "In the month you were gone, I felt myself withering away, dying. If you hadn't come back soon, I wouldn't have been able to hold together this body that walks the lands of Azeroth. I was starting to hear the echoes of the Twisting Nether..." "All right, all right." Jace caught her reaching arms. "Cut the act." Morophil's face went flat and she sat back down. "No fun." Jace set the vial of spores on the table and picked out a clean cloth. "I've still got to go out tonight, Morophil. But I do appreciate Sazeno and you watching the house for me." Hearing his name, the imp poked its head out from under the bed and dusted the dirt off its head. "Hmm? What's this?" Morophil picked up the vial, opened the lid, and sniffed. "Ah, a life elemental. Interesting. It smells like something unique to this world." Jace snatched the vial back and closed it. "What are you doing? If whatever's in here gets out, I can't keep this house." "There are two demons in your house, master," Morophil said, looking at the spores in the vial. "It won't grow big here. All it can absorb in this place is my presence and that imp's fel energy. It'll die quickly." She laughed. The imp finally crawled out from under the bed with a long face. "Master, don't make me live with her anymore. I'm going crazy, this demon..." "Keep helping me with that scroll." Jace pulled out the scroll containing the dialogue he'd memorized from Gorefiend's memories. "During this trip, aside from continuing to familiarize myself with that one incantation, I couldn't make heads or tails of the rest. You two need to keep helping me." "We can't make heads or tails of the rest either, master," the imp said helplessly. "I don't know if what you heard was too obscure, or if your notes are too vague..." "Then write down every other word of demonic you can think of that might be related, and see if you can piece together something coherent. That includes you too, Morophil — you're doing this as well!" Morophil's claw pointed at her own nose. "Then promise me — for every day I spend sorting through this language, every night I get to..." Jace said, "So if you don't figure it out for a few decades, that's it then? How about this — until you actually produce something, don't touch me." The succubus tilted her head. "Ah well. Who calls you my master, if not pitiful me..." "I'm the most pitiful one here! I don't get anything!" the imp shouted. "So what do you want?" Jace asked, curious. "Huh? I can make a request?" Sazeno asked. "Go ahead, try." "Then... then start with a little soul, master!" Jace thought about it. "Let me see if I can get you a soul shard. Work hard on sorting out that incantation. I want results!"
