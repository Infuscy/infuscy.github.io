Chapter 377: An Alchemy Family

That evening, Jace carried the cloth-wrapped vial down to The Slaughtered Lamb's basement. Sulenna was sitting in the main room drinking. When she saw Jace, she gave a lazy greeting, squinting. "Back, Jace."

"How's Galders doing? He and that succubus getting along all right?" Jace asked.

"Like a house on fire," Sulenna said with a smile. "I've only seen him once or twice these past two weeks. Cold as you please. Erland's been dying to find him, absolutely itching to summon a succubus of his own..."

"He summons a succubus and you're not jealous?" Jace asked.

That only made Sulenna laugh harder. "Why on earth would I be jealous of a demon servant?"

Jace asked, "So, did Erland manage to summon an imp?"

"No. But Ursula did."

"Really?" Jace asked. "She summoned an imp?"

"Yep." Sulenna nodded. "That demon does the same kind of work — helping her sort herbs, brew potions, keep track of time. What a hard worker..."

After saying goodbye to Sulenna, Jace walked down the passage to the alchemy lab. Before he even reached it, he could feel waves of medicinal-smelling steam drifting over.

"Ursula." He looked at the alchemist's back, holding up the glass vial. "Take a look at what I brought you."

Ursula turned around, leaning on her waist and catching her breath. "When did you get back?"

"Around this afternoon," Jace said, setting the vial on the side table. "Came to find you the moment I arrived."

"What is this?" Ursula leaned in curiously. "Weren't you supposed to bring me a marsh beast organ... my goodness, this isn't marsh beast spores, is it?"

"What about it?" Jace asked.

"I wouldn't know how to raise it..." Ursula examined the vial. "And there's no way my little alchemy lab could grow something like this."

"Then I'll have to take it to Alchemist's Needs and ask," Jace said.

"You mean that mother-and-daughter place?"

"Right."

"Hmm, that makes sense. Their alchemy lab's pretty big, and I hear they've got a garden out back. They might actually have a way to grow one," Ursula said.

Jace pulled out the cloth-wrapped stone. "Take a look at this."

"An Elemental Stone?" Ursula's eyes lit up. "How much did you pay for it?"

"We killed a couple of earth elementals and found it. Didn't cost a thing."

Ursula said in surprise, "Jace, I honestly can't imagine what you went through on this trip. Earth elementals are something I've only ever read about in books, and you went and killed several of them with your own hands?"

"You're really underestimating me," Jace said with a smile. "Keep it, Ursula. Last time I drank half a bottle of your Restorative Potion. I know one Elemental Stone won't refine into enough Elemental Earth to make a whole bottle that size."

"Suddenly being so generous?" Ursula took the wrapped package and set it on the shelf. "You're not going to tack on some extra demand, are you."

"A few gold coins would be appreciated," Jace said.

"I don't have a few gold coins..." Ursula patted the package. "But that Restorative Potion — when I said it was a gift, it was a gift. Refining Elemental Earth from a beautiful Elemental Stone like this is a rare opportunity. I'm not going to pocket your stuff. Let me think... come find me in two days, and I'll give you the refined Elemental Earth."

"If you say so," Jace said. "I'll be waiting for the good news."

"No problem," Ursula said. "Oh, come take a look, Jace."

"What?!"

"Look at my new achievement!"

Ursula led Jace into the apothecary's room, where they saw an imp scurrying back and forth hauling herb pouches. Jace nodded. "So that's the imp you summoned?"

"Ah, Sulenna ran her mouth and told you, didn't she?" Ursula said, looking a little disappointed. "What do you think of this guy?"

"Does it obey?" Jace asked.

Ursula said, "Very obedient, though it grumbles a little sometimes. I finally understand why Jagin was always urging me to learn to summon an imp as soon as possible. With its help, I can put more energy into studying and brewing instead of spending all that time in the storage room..."

"Weren't you worried the imp would burn your herbs?" Jace asked.

Ursula looked at the imp. "I specifically told it to be careful. At least it hasn't caused any trouble yet."

The alchemist fell silent for a moment. "My father always wanted to summon a little spirit like this to help him organize the storage room. I think he actually meant an imp."

Jace asked, "Your father was an alchemist too?"

"That should be obvious," Ursula said. "Not only my father — my grandfather was too."

"Were they Lordaeronians as well?" Jace asked curiously.

"No." Ursula's expression suddenly grew solemn. She watched the imp bustling up and down and said, "Actually, my whole family were not Lordaeronians. My grandfather wasn't, and neither my father nor I were ever accepted by Lordaeron. We only ever lived there."

"Because they were all half-elves."

"What?"

"Are you surprised?"

"It's just... I'd heard half-elves have a hard time producing offspring..."

"Most half-elves aren't willing to marry and have children. They're very conflicted about their own bloodline," Ursula explained. "It's true that half-elves, like elves, have great difficulty bearing children. But with a lifespan of two or three hundred years, if you try a few more times, something's bound to come of it, isn't it?"

She lifted her red hair, revealing her ears. "Look. Even though my mother and grandmother were both human, my ears are still a little different."

Jace had to admit, Ursula's ears were indeed a bit higher than a normal human's, and slightly pointed. But her appearance, her eyes, and everything else — if she hadn't said anything, it would have been very hard to tell she had any elven blood at all.

"Do the others know about this?" Jace asked.

"I've never told anyone," Ursula said. "Though I think Galders can probably tell, but he doesn't care, so I feel at ease here. It wasn't like that in Lordaeron — a lot of people there could tell you had elven blood from nothing more than a snippet of your trimmed fingernails. After my father died, I couldn't stand that place anymore!"

"I'm a Lordaeronian too," Jace said. "But I honestly couldn't tell."

"Of course I know you're a Lordaeronian," Ursula said. "Because I also know not every Lordaeronian looks the same — just like not every half-elf is a thief, a bandit, and a murderer."

With that, she glanced over at him. "You're not going to develop some kind of prejudice against me just because my ears are a little pointed, are you?"

Jace said, "Maybe. If you're a half-elf, you should be better at using the Arcane, right? Or at least live a little longer than everyone else?"

"You're actually curious about that," Ursula said, unable to hold back a laugh. "Those few benefits got bred out over the generations. All that's left is the bad reputation."
