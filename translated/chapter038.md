## Chapter 38: The Forgotten Suspect

Truth be told, Jace didn't much want to go. He'd been planning to hit the library that afternoon, find some books on orcish, and get a head start on deciphering whatever was on that mysterious scroll.

This was something he'd definitely have to handle on his own. Setting aside the fact that the orcish lessons with Marin hadn't even been arranged yet, even if they had — waiting for class, learning enough vocabulary and grammar and all that — who knew how long that would take.

Not to mention, the teachers themselves didn't have a complete grasp of orcish. The progress would definitely be slow.

He said reluctantly, "I've got things to do this afternoon."

Grede, hearing Jace talk like this, said with a hint of displeasure, "When you needed my help, I agreed right on the spot."

What "agreed on the spot" — wasn't it only after the soft-soaping and the seventy-thirty split that he'd agreed?

Of course, Jace also knew what Grede had put on the line for him in Duskwood.

"Fine. Wait till I'm off work."

Having agreed, Jace bought a small crab cake, tore off a tiny bit of the crab meat on top and tried it — sweet and salty, a weird combination.

Crab meat was cheap as dirt in the Kingdom of Stormwind. Over in Westfall, at Sentinel Hill, crab meat was something you could eat till you were sick of it.

Coastal crabs were running rampant, and the crabs hauled in at Stormwind Harbor were so plentiful they were practically worthless.

When Jace had worked on a harbor-repair project, he'd often seen fishermen hauling ashore giant sea crabs bigger than calves, then right there on the dock splitting them open and eating them raw — quite a spectacle.

Back in Marin's study, Marin took the crab cake, closed his eyes, and took a deep, lingering sip of it in his mouth, like he'd scratched a mighty itch.

After swallowing a bite, he set the cake beside his teacup. "What took you so long?"

"Line," Jace said. "Too many people buying snacks. Master, the Blue Hermit was really packed this morning."

"It certainly was," Marin said. "Perhaps that place should be expanded. Quite a few children discovered their magical potential during the war — a lot of new apprentices have joined here."

Jace cleared his throat and sat down at the table. "Right, Master — I'd like to ask for tomorrow morning off."

"Granted."

"That easy?"

"Docked pay."

"I'll… try my best to make it in."

Marin gave Jace a sidelong look, flipped through his book, and said, "Picking up where we left off — about the Raven Hill werewolf matter. We have a few theories. One: they mistook some oversized ghoul for a werewolf. Two: a hidden orc killer. You know many orcs carry weapons enchanted by warlocks — people killed by them show severe signs of shadow corruption. A third possibility we hadn't considered before, but now seems quite likely: the gnoll chieftain."

"The gnoll chieftain?" Jace said. "You mean the giant gnoll that attacked the grain wagon I was escorting?"

Marin said, "I think it's very possible. The Riverpaw gnolls have already begun invading Elwynn Forest. If they can push north, why not south?"

Though a giant gnoll was indeed the most likely thing to be mistaken for a werewolf, something about it still didn't sit right.

Jace said, "Stormwind never noticed a giant gnoll before, but the wave of emigration from Raven Hill started years ago."

Marin closed the book he'd been reading, leaned back in his chair, and looked over. "You can't use the timeline of when Stormwind noticed the gnolls to rule out the possibility that the Raven Hill townsfolk might have mistaken a gnoll for a werewolf. After all, the Raven Hill townsfolk could have come under attack by a giant gnoll before the Stormwind guards did."

Marin had a point, but Jace recalled his conversation with Morgan — how Morgan felt the Raven Hill townsfolk could tell the difference between a ghoul attack and a werewolf attack.

The inhabitants of Elwynn Forest had been fighting gnolls for centuries. Logically, they should be more familiar with gnoll attacks.

And gnolls always struck in groups, creating massive chaos. Would a single gnoll chieftain really be lurking around a settlement, ambushing lone stragglers? That went completely against gnoll nature — and against Hogger's nature most of all.

If you believed the townsfolk, that left only one answer: orcs causing trouble.

Was there another possibility? Anything else that could be mistaken for a werewolf?

Jace said, "Gnolls… I find that a stretch."

"There's another possibility," Marin said. "It's simply that the south Elwynn region has become unfit for human habitation due to the corruption of dark energy. Migrating away is the natural choice. It's just that many people don't want to admit they're leaving because the sky turned dark, so they pin it on the so-called werewolf legend to save face before their neighbors and friends. For my part, I'd rather the people of the whole south Elwynn region faced reality sooner and moved elsewhere as quickly as possible. If the Dark Portal erupts again, that place will become a front line once more. Maybe one day officially renaming it Duskwood might actually spur those stubborn locals to leave a little sooner."

This really didn't convince Jace. Recalling what he'd discussed with Morgan at the time, he said, "I don't deny that could be the case, but the murdered corpses are real. Didn't the Wizard Sanctum's mages examine those bodies killed by werewolves when they went to Raven Hill?"

"That's another serious problem," Marin said, taking another small bite of cake and washing it down with tea. "The people there couldn't produce a single body killed by a werewolf. They all claimed these corpses had been infected with the werewolf curse and vanished by the next day. Our mages never encountered a suitable incident at the right time, and without any solid evidence, they could only come back empty-handed."

Corpses vanishing into thin air, shadow energy… Jace traced circles on the tabletop with his finger, feeling like he was on the verge of grasping some key word, but unable to call it to mind all at once.

Corporse… shadow… a creature that walked like a werewolf, taller than a normal person.

Wait — there was one existence that had been overlooked, one that roughly fit that image.

The Death Knights created by Gul'dan.

Marin had mentioned Death Knights before, when he'd brought up the Dragonqueen's disappearance and the black dragons aiding the orcs in war. He'd cited the recent Battle of Alterac Valley as an example — in that battle, the orc legions rode black dragons into Alterac Valley, crushing the Alliance defenders.

And leading those orc legions was Teron Gorefiend, the world's first Death Knight.

Unlike the Death Knights most players knew — those loyal to the Lich King — the first batch of Death Knights created by Gul'dan were rather more "dead."

Many of the Lich King's Death Knights were fallen mortals. They wielded runeforged blades, wielded necrotic power, and commanded the dead to fight, but they themselves hadn't necessarily truly died.

For them, death wasn't a prerequisite.

But the Death Knights Gul'dan created were truly resurrected dead. He channeled the dark souls of the orc warlocks loyal to him into the corpses of the most valiant human knights and brought them back to life.

Dark magic combined with powerful dead produced an effect far greater than the sum of its parts, ultimately forging a host of terrifying meat grinders.

During the Orc Wars, these Death Knights made enormous contributions to Gul'dan's conquest of the human territories north of Khaz Modan, and became the nightmare of countless survivors.

Had it not been for the Silver Hand's desperate resistance using the power of the Holy Light, holding the Death Knight army's advance at Hillsbrute Foothills, they very likely would have entered the city of Lordaeron itself — the Scourge calamity might have arrived twenty years early.

Because these Death Knights had orc souls inhabiting them, their behavior and way of thinking followed orc patterns entirely. The human souls had already been stripped away, leaving only a shell with muscle memory to carry another soul.

So though they had human bodies, they walked and fought hunched over like orcs, with heavy strides, wielding savage, crude combat techniques and magic.

And because of the dark energy's catalysis, these human shells had grown abnormally tall, making them tower over normal people — like giants rotting inside their own skin. Even the warhorses carrying them had to be resurrected, terrifying undead beasts grown dark and monstrous through necromancy. Because an ordinary warhorse or warg simply couldn't bear the weight of these heavily armored warriors.

Because the Death Knights had been fighting north of Khaz Modan, and down south — in the Kingdom of Stormwind — there were few Death Knights running rampant, because this place had already fallen under orc control. There was no need for these most terrifying warriors to charge across the battlefields, no need for them to enslave humans or maintain order.

So many people in Stormwind had never seen a Death Knight. By the time they came back under human rule, the orcish Horde had been defeated, most of them fleeing into the Dark Portal — and they'd lost the chance to witness the Death Knights' fearsome power firsthand.

After the war, there was even less reason to learn about them. All that remained were the tales of those black death-gods told by a handful of veterans and refugees who'd come down from the north.

Stolen corpses, shadow attacks, tall hunched figures moving like werewolves, appearing only at night…

Jace could no longer rule out the possibility of a Death Knight.

This theory wasn't without its flaws. Gul'dan's Death Knights had always been officers or elite vanguard in the Horde. How could one have shown up alone at Raven Hill, and gone so far as to harm the common folk who crossed his path?

Then Jace remembered the scroll he'd hidden at home.

An orc warlock appearing in Duskwood. A Death Knight appearing at Raven Hill…

It felt like all of this was starting to connect in the same direction.

He couldn't wait any longer. He wanted to find a way to translate everything on that scroll right now.

But Grede still expected him to help clear out wild wolves at some farm that afternoon.

Thinking of this, Jace anxiously drummed his fingers on the table one after another, until Marin said, "There are still ten minutes before your shift ends, Mr. Sesso. Have a little patience."
