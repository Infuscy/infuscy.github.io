## Chapter 391: Preparing a New Spell

That noon, Jace finished his ward school class and headed to The Slaughtered Lamb tavern.

He planned to set out for Goldshire that afternoon, spend the night there, and then head to Duskwood the next day. Before setting out, he had to get all the potions for testing the incantation prepared.

He walked in and headed down to the basement. Something felt off about the dim room.

He focused his senses and saw a snoring shadow lunge right up to him. He was about to dodge when he saw the felhound stop right in front of him, growling threateningly twice.

"Bosadim..."

A shout came from the passage. The felhound turned and darted back into the corridor. Jace followed it all the way to the alchemy lab, where he found Ursula staring helplessly at Jace, who'd just walked in.

"This dog is really a handful," Ursula said. "Every time someone comes in, I have to call it off for fear it'll hurt you lot. It's a real headache."

"But I have to admit, it's made this place a lot safer," Jace said.

Ursula propped her cheek on her hand, watching the felhound eye Jace warily. "Well, it is a bit more reassuring. At least when a mage walks past the door, I know immediately. This fellow is extremely sensitive."

"Only mages?" Jace asked.

"That's right," Ursula said. "Even when Tharle Mor comes down, it couldn't care less. It seems to be interested only in people who know a little magic."

Jace said, "It's probably because in the Twisting Nether, it was a hunter that tracked mages. But the ones we need to be careful about are precisely those mages."

Ursula said, "Without your encouragement and the succubi's help, I don't think I could have completed this summoning ritual. Thank you, Jace."

"If it weren't for your potions, I wouldn't be alive today, Ursula," Jace said with a smile. "I remember in the beginning you were always telling me that everyone in the Dagger Circle had to help each other — and now here you are, specifically thanking me."

Ursula smiled. "Well, you've come today to ask for my help too, haven't you."

"Right," Jace said. "I need three mana potions, and if possible, one Shadow Protection Potion."

Ursula filled his potion bag with the concoctions. After Jace took the bag, he asked, "How much do you know about that female warlock Mosul was involved with?"

"That woman was a warlock?" Ursula shook her head. "Looks like I know even less than you do."

Jace nodded. Looked like he'd have to ask Galders after all.

But right after producing a felhound summoning incantation, going to ask Galders about Mosul would seem a bit too obvious.

And Galders had been going back and forth to Duskwood lately, contacting that tailor willing to make robes for the warlocks, so he wouldn't be easy to catch for a while.

"Are you asking for potions because you're heading out on another trip?" Ursula asked.

"Not exactly," Jace said. "It's just that I got hold of an incantation and want to test what it does. At most I'll just go to the south bank of the Nafeltari River. I won't be gone long."

Ursula nodded, turned, and dug a small round bottle of pale green liquid out of a cabinet, tucking it into the pouch at Jace's waist. "Elixir of Wisdom, Jace. According to those mages, it makes it easier to sense the presence of mana when you cast. I'm sure it'll help you test the new incantation."

"I didn't even think to prepare something like that. Thank you so much." Jace patted his potion bag.

At noon when he got home, he found Grede squatting by the canal at the door, skipping stones. Spotting Jace from a distance, he immediately kicked all the flat stones he'd gathered into the river.

"What are you doing here?" Jace asked, playing dumb.

"Waiting for you to go eat, aren't I?" Grede called out.

Jace asked, "How was the mine trip?"

"I thought this money would be easy to earn," Grede said, looking thoroughly annoyed. "Turns out those kobolds were one craftier than the other, running all over the place. Not only did I not make any money, I ended up paying quite a bit in medical costs and burned a coat."

Jace gestured an enormous outline with his hands. "Why didn't you Stoneform and smash those kobolds' heads in?"

"The little holes those kobolds dug in the mine — I could barely fit through them even with my head down, and you want me to turn into an even bigger stone man?" Grede sighed. "If getting angry and losing my temper could temper my ancestral bloodline, my strength would probably have surpassed the real Mountain King by now!"

Jace burst out laughing. "I think if you want to build up your strength, you still need a new temple... You could wait for your Dark Iron cousins to dig a little deeper into that temple, or look for another one."

"I'm coming around to that idea too," Grede said.

The two of them went to The Gilded Rose. The dwarf made his way to the very back of the tavern, found a corner seat, and plopped himself down. "After the commotion we caused, those troggs shouldn't have the nerve to stop the Dark Iron dwarves from digging any further, right? So how long until we go back and take another look — four years? five?"

"It's not tomorrow, that's for sure," Jace said. "I'm heading south tomorrow."

"Goldshire?" Grede asked.

"Right," Jace said. "I want to cross the river and head to Duskwood to test an incantation."

Grede leaned in and lowered his voice. "That shadow magic?"

"No," Jace said. "But speaking of that, I felt like at that stone door last time, the shield seemed to hold a little longer than before."

"Really? It's not some kind of illusion, is it?" Grede asked.

"It's not an illusion," Jace said. "And, do you remember those Nathrezim I mentioned before — the Dreadlords? Those cunning demons love using this kind of shield. They generally don't like getting into close combat with people. If this shield could only last a second or two, I have a hard time imagining a Dreadlord would like it."

"So I still need to keep practicing, or look for what's wrong with this incantation and figure out where the problem is."

Grede nodded. "So if it's not that spell, what are you going to test?"

"I don't know, that's why I have to test it," Jace said.

"I'm coming too," Grede said. "I've got nothing going on these couple of days anyway. Getting out will clear my head. And if you get blown up by your own magic, I can collect your body."

The dwarf burst out laughing. Jace tapped the table with his finger, helpless. "Since you just got back from the mine, sleep in for a few days in Stormwind. I've got that imp to help me. I know nothing about this incantation right now — testing it might take a long time. If you watch me mutter the same sentence over and over, it'll be nothing but annoying."

"Well, if you put it that way... I can only wish you luck." Grede slapped his thigh. "Get the boss to whip us up some food — let's eat our fill before you go."
