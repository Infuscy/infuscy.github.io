Chapter 393: The Key Factor

After resting for the night in Duskwood, the next day Jace crawled out of the gloomy forest. He looked at the deep woods, then at his pocket watch — it was already around eleven in the morning. He opened his cloth pack, dug out the prepared biscuits, dipped them in the honey he'd brought in a little bottle, and popped them in his mouth. He took a few deep breaths, feeling out his mana. After a night's sleep and a little sugar, his spirits had recovered quite a bit. Thinking back to the last attempt from the previous night — without a doubt, it had been his closest to success. Those dancing shadow serpents had been utterly mesmerizing. Jace had even thought it was some kind of hallucination. It wasn't a hallucination — both the imp and the succubus had seen it. That was shadow power truly summoned by the incantation. He hadn't completed it, but going down this path, there was no problem. It was just that before he could finish casting, his energy was exhausted... During the last incantation attempt the previous night, although he hadn't been at full energy, at least he'd felt he'd recovered fairly well. And this magic — it was like it had only just taken its first step. In his current state, could he actually release the whole thing? There was no way to know without trying. This time, Jace planned to find ways to optimize it further. In addition to strengthening the magic of wisdom, he also needed to reflect on the casting process from the previous night. According to Marin's explanation, efficient casting... came down to nothing more than adjusting the incantation and the casting motions. The incantation — he probably didn't have much room to adjust it anymore, given that it was the result of two demons adjusting it hand over hand. Then the casting motions... Jace had always cast by following the recipe — when he encountered a new incantation whose motions he didn't know, he'd reference an old incantation with a similar effect and go from there. But this wouldn't work now. Clearly, this incantation was very different from the others. When those energies floated up into the sky, he had to try to direct those forces too. But when those shadows drifted through the sky, should he have them attack a target immediately, or continue to spiral and gather into a greater energy, or did they have to trace out a specific shape? After all, Jace didn't know what magic he was learning. Shadow Rain and Shadow Meteor were completely different things. Wait... Shadow Meteor. Gul'dan. He recalled a skill from the game — Hand of Gul'dan. This was a spell created by Gul'dan that could summon a shadow fire meteor from the sky to crash into the ground, blasting all enemies within a specific range with astonishing power. When Gul'dan himself cast it, the shadow meteor even carried a vicious soul within it, and after impact it immediately transformed into a demon lord in the flames. Jace knew there was no way he could use this magic to summon some demon lord — after all, he wasn't Gul'dan. But it seemed that even in his last attempt, he'd seen a glimmer of hope for success. If it was a Shadow Meteor, then he should find a way to make those shadows drifting in the sky converge together. Then the casting motions should follow a similar logic. Beyond the incantation and casting motions, there was another factor that had to be considered. That was the emotion Morophil had mentioned. During that last attempt, Jace's recollection of the emotion of "loneliness" seemed to have had a rather good effect — going from only feeling his energy drain with no visible manifestation of mana, to seeing the shadow surge and spiral... this was enormous progress. Then what about the second emotion. Hatred. Muttering this word, a scene suddenly flashed through Jace's mind. Burning... That day in Southshore, watching those burned-down houses, that girl who'd nearly been suffocated by smoke, those innocent townsfolk fleeing everywhere with flames all over them. That willful black dragon whelp. And that house that had been smashed through, the griffin with its insides spilling out, the girl fleeing in panic, the Gryphon Rider with limbs twisted from a heavy fall. As his mind replayed those scenes from that time, Jace tried to steady his breathing. He still remembered watching Grede charge into the billowing black smoke with that hammer — that angry, resolute back of his. "I can sense that master seems a bit unhappy. Is it because last night's incantation attempt didn't go well?" Morophil asked softly at his ear. "It went extremely well!" the imp jumped onto the succubus's shoulder. "Master's a human. For a human to achieve that much in their first few attempts, that's really something!" Morophil grabbed the imp's tail, peeled it off her shoulder, and tossed it aside. "Sometimes I almost forget that master is a human." "It's not because of that," Jace said. "I'm looking for the feeling, preparing for the next attempt. This time I want to be fully prepared." He picked up his staff, unwrapped the yellow cloth covering the gem, and stroked Gorefiend's gem. "Time for our second round of attempts, you two. Remember what I said last night — same goes for today." "Understood, master!" The imp rubbed its hands together in anticipation, and the succubus nodded as well. Jace took another strip of dried meat from his pack, dipped it in a little honey, and ate it, chewing for a long time before swallowing. He looked at the dead tree he'd chosen earlier as a target, recalled loneliness... hatred... the two complex, bitter emotions, and swung his staff again to begin the incantation. Gorefiend's gem radiated an arcane glow. As Jace spoke the incantation, he tried to use his energy to make that shadow power converge. This time, the shadow that should have been chaotic and disordered seemed to be influenced by Jace. Those black-purple demon serpents in the sky began to spin, revolving around the center pointed to by Jace's staff... Closer, closer! But it needed a stronger force to guide it... A fleeting sensation flashed through Jace's mind, but he quickly cast it aside and continued to finish the incantation. But although those demon serpents gathered in the center as he wished, they failed to merge together. They collided with each other, tore each other apart, and gradually fell apart. Jace also felt himself gradually losing control of the shadows. The incantation ended, and the final summoning force was exhausted. The shadow energy scattered once more like a pile of sand and vanished... What happened? This time, although Jace hadn't completely exhausted his energy, he felt he was nearing his limit. Maintaining it any longer and he might have passed out from mana exhaustion. But clearly, this magic wasn't finished yet... Sazeno gazed at the shattered shadows in the sky. "Master needs a casting material that can converge this magic. Relying on willpower alone, it's temporarily very hard to achieve." If this really was Hand of Gul'dan, Jace had a feeling he was still missing something. Soul shards... Yes. In the game, Hand of Gul'dan required soul shards to cast. Soul shards were a vital material for warlocks to connect with the Twisting Nether. Perhaps with the help of soul shards, he could stabilize this void shadow power.
