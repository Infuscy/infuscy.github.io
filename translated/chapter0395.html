Chapter 395: Finally Some Progress

After leaving the spider-infested woodland and walking back for several hours, night had fallen again. He'd spent most of the day searching the forest, and by evening he was still a bit worn out.

Still, Jace felt he was just about ready this time. One pure soul shard — and if that wasn't enough, he could always find another unlucky bastard in the forest with Mortipher.

Next step: rest up properly, then try one more time.

He found a quiet low spot and got some sleep. The next morning he woke up, stuffed himself with the remaining food in his pack, and figured if this attempt failed too, he'd head back to Goldshire for a while to recuperate.

Rested and recharged, he picked out an old tree that had snapped and fallen for some reason in a forest clearing as his casting target. One hand gripping the soul shard, the other raising his staff high, he focused his mind and reached for that feeling of loneliness… and hatred.

He had to admit, the time it took him to build up the emotion this go-around was considerably longer than his first attempt that first night.

He recalled more of the details from back then, searched for more of the feelings, before he finally landed on something close enough.

After all, he'd been dredging up these memories far too many times over the past two days.

When he finally felt it was about right, he began chanting the incantation once more.

"Savius…" This incantation was already extremely familiar to him — he could recite it almost subconsciously, which allowed him to keep stewing in the emotion, keep savoring the feelings in those memories, all while closing his eyes and sensing the shadow magic gathering at the call of the spell, slowly rising into the air. And then came the moment to offer up the soul shard.

"Avarus…" he continued chanting, raising the soul shard high. Shadow serpents began to manifest, swirling far around wherever the staff pointed. Smooth. Couldn't have gone smoother.

And the mana expenditure was clearly still within what Jace could handle. He could keep going.

But he wasn't going to think about mana anymore. He was going to focus everything on completing this incantation. Plenty of people and demons had told him he had considerable talent for shadow, but this time, even he felt it slipping beyond his control.

"Anteus…"

Jace kept chanting and gently swept the staff. Teron Gorefiend's gem flickered with light, and the shadow serpents began gathering toward where it pointed, tangling together in the middle. This was the most critical stage — it was where he'd failed last time.

By now, the soul shard had already dimmed.

It was working!

"Minuelle…"

Jace continued the incantation. The shadow serpents in the sky were clearly bound by some force, and then all the shadows churned as if set ablaze, releasing billowing waves of violet smoke. Was this the power of a soul?

But as the burning grew fiercer, Jace felt his strength waning. The shadow power was gathering more and more frantically, more and more chaotically.

Jace forced himself to concentrate and shouted the final phrase: "Wilma!"

The shadow didn't come crashing down like a meteor the way he'd imagined. Instead, it slowly disintegrated in the air…

Something must have gone wrong…

Not enough soul power?

Or his energy had run out…

Jace didn't dwell on it. He'd finally gotten the shadow to take form — he couldn't afford to lose focus now.

The soul shard in his hand had already shattered completely, reduced to powerless powder. He drew Azuresong without hesitation and pointed it skyward. Arcane light instantly blazed from the staff, the gem at its crown, illuminating the whole forest!

Then he laid Azuresong across the staff, and the shadow mass that had been disintegrating in the sky suddenly erupted in a ball of black fire, roaring and rolling as it blasted jagged shards of shadow in every direction. Where they struck the ground, they set off bursts of flame.

Jace kept his eyes locked on the ferocious shadow and shouted once more: "Wilma!"

Azuresong seemed to pour yet another measure of fuel onto the spell. The shadow mass let out a piercing shriek, spinning and surging forward in the direction Jace's staff pointed, constantly struggling and warping in the air — like a swarm of black flies thick as paste, like the thick claws of a fel dragon, like rampaging, frenzied spirits!

He quickly aimed the staff at that old tree he'd picked out. The shadow mass arced across the sky, smashed through the canopy, and struck the tree's broken trunk dead center!

The violet-black flames exploded violently. A shockwave of shadow rippled outward. The grass within ten meters visibly yellowed and withered; the ground nearby decayed straight into violet, scorched earth.

Black fire kept surging from the ground skyward. Flames and shadow smoke completely swallowed the tree's silhouette. From within the smoke came bursts of inhuman shrieks and howls, serpentine shadows writhing and struggling inside, before finally collapsing.

Jace didn't know how long he stood there, watching the smoke grow blacker and blacker, the violet flames spreading for a while before slowly fading away.

The ground had been blown open like torn flesh, everywhere stained violet, cracks still glowing with violet embers. All that remained of the tree before were a few violet remnants, tipped with faint bright-violet sparks, collapsed on the ground — utterly unrecognizable from what it had been.

Only then did he feel an itch on his cheeks, his neck, every bit of exposed skin, and it was getting worse.

He drank a Shadow Protection Potion to slow the shadow corrosion, but it was still pretty miserable.

On top of that, he felt his energy just about completely spent. He could barely stand. He backed up a few steps, leaned against a tree trunk, and sat down.

That tree had basically been half-killed by the shadow shockwave too. The moment he leaned on it, leaves began rustling down as if autumn had arrived.

After that, he drank a Mana Potion to restore a bit of energy, so he'd at least have enough mana to stay conscious, sense magical energy, and keep his wits about him.

"Master, those shadow flames were summoned from the Twisting Nether," the imp said. "So scary! It must have been an Eredar who came up with something this terrifying!"

"That means the shadow energy in the worlds beyond the Twisting Nether can no longer satisfy their appetite for destruction," Mortipher said. "After the Eredar joined the Burning Legion, they lost interest in pretty much everything that wasn't plunder and violence."

This was indeed the Hand of Gul'dan — though the two demons were certain this incantation bore the fingerprints of the Eredar. Perhaps Gul'dan had only completed this spell under the guidance of the Deceiver himself, Kil'jaeden.

Then again, Gul'dan had his own reasons not to care about mana costs.

He had once seized the power of nearly all of Draenor, held the souls of thousands of Draenei captives hostage, and squandered nearly endless dark power with reckless abandon — far more confidently than any ordinary Eredar warlock.

But in the aftermath of the explosion, no new demon had been born.

Perhaps no demon had been drawn in by such a fragile soul shard. Perhaps his mana still wasn't enough to tear open a large enough passage between the Twisting Nether and Azeroth for a demon's soul to pass through. Perhaps demons were too fragile, and coming into this world meant dying in the shadow bombardment.

He supposed… he still had some distance to go before this spell could be called a true success.

He stared at the shadow scorch marks everywhere. Forget Stormwind — this absolutely could not be replicated anywhere in Elwynn Forest. His decision to switch locations once he'd gotten familiar with the incantation had been the right call.
