Chapter 406: Meeting a New Teacher

After returning to Stonard that evening and seeing the wounded delivered to the priests, Jace had been helping the priests and paladins in this orc den tend to the injured and sick.

He didn't know many support spells, and even when he used Azuresong to conjure water, he'd just end up shredding the container. With plenty of mages who could summon clean water smoothly already working hard, there really wasn't any need for him to make a mess of things.

At least he still had the strength to carry the wounded and haul supplies the priests needed — potions and bandages, for instance. That much was useful.

To make himself useful, he'd taken off his robe and was just wearing a white vest nobody else had claimed, moving things alongside a bunch of soldiers with Arathi accents.

While he worked, his mind kept drifting to what the elven ranger had told him. Jace couldn't help wondering how the blue dragon Mazegosa's sword had ended up at the bottom of Blackrock Mountain…

In the game, Azuresong was loot from the Molten Core raid dungeon beneath Blackrock Mountain. Such a beautiful magic sword seemed completely out of place among a loot table full of items tied to fire and lava.

Perhaps when Quel'Thalas was invaded by the Scourge, Windrunner Village was utterly destroyed by the undead, and Sylvanas was killed… the Windrunner treasuries were also looted clean by trolls or other scavengers, and somehow it had ended up drifting south.

Or maybe the loot table was just a game mechanic, and the equipment in it was actually "rewards for killing this target"? Either way, the sword was in his hands now, so there was no way to find out what had happened to it in another timeline.

Not long after, Barry Fred — the full mage Master Marin had left here to coordinate the mages' work — came over calling, "Jace Sesso… Is Jace Sesso here?"

"Here!"

Jace raised his hand in response and quickly threw his robe back on. Fred glanced at Jace and raised an eyebrow. "I hardly recognized you, Mr. Sesso…"

"Is there work, Mr. Fred?" Jace said, running a hand through his sweat-damp hair.

"Not about work," Fred said. "Captain Quenton told me about you. He said you performed quite bravely. I'll report your aid to the Order of the Silver Hand to Archmaster Andormas and Master Marin truthfully — it'll certainly help your advancement to full mage!"

"Thank you very much, Mr. Fred," Jace said.

"There's one more thing," Fred said. "The other mages have all seen their quarters. Let me show you yours too. Of course it can't compare to the apartments at the Wizard Sanctum, but here in the Black Swamp, having anywhere to sleep at all isn't bad, right?"

Jace followed Fred deeper into Stonard into a large orc den. Orcs liked building their structures deeper underground, carving out the lowest level like a semi-basement, so even entering from ground level meant walking down a stretch of stairs. It was pitch black — even with the braziers lit, it was hard to see clearly.

He entered a dark passage of the orc den and peered into the narrow little cave inside. The entire floor was covered in straw bedding. It looked rougher than Stormwind's underground dungeons by an order of magnitude.

Still, Jace could sense the trace of arcane magic that had passed through here. There wasn't much smell, and no dust to speak of beyond the dirt itself.

"Three mages stay in this room, Mr. Sesso," Fred said. "I'm in the chamber right across this passage — just keep going straight and you'll find me. If you run into any trouble, come tell me and I'll speak to the knight-captain."

After Fred left, Jace lay down on the bedding for a while, then left the den and went back to help the priests again.

That evening, after eating, he returned to the den and found a mage already sitting there.

The figure had his back to the entrance, apparently organizing something. Jace poked his head in and said, "Hello."

"Hello, my roommate." The mage glanced back, and Jace saw he was about his own age. He seemed to have been bandaging a wound.

"You're hurt?" Jace asked.

"Yeah." The young mage nodded. "Damn felhounds."

Jace pulled open his shirt. "Look, got bit by a felhound too."

"Hah!" The mage laughed. "So what is this, a felhound victims' ward?"

Jace couldn't help but laugh too. He sat down nearby, then realized the mage's entire leg was covered in bloody gouges from a felhound's tentacles — a pretty horrifying sight. He instinctively flinched his eyes away.

"Pretty scary, right?" The young mage sighed. "Ferby Golobo. Nice to meet you…"

"Jace," Jace said. "Jace Sesso."

"Jace Sesso?" The mage glanced at Jace's ring. "You're that Sesso?"

Jace sat down on the straw bedding beside him. "There should only be one Sesso in the Wizard Sanctum."

The mage asked, "I heard someone say you killed that death knight, Teron Gorefiend… is that true? I figured you were some thirty-something battle mage or something, but you don't look much older than me."

Jace realized that the news of him killing Teron Gorefiend was starting to bear fruit — more and more people were hearing about it. Maybe some didn't know his name, and some didn't know the specifics…

"It's true," he said, leaning against the wall.

The mage pressed on. "Tell me — how did you beat him?"

Jace said, "It definitely wasn't a fair one-on-one duel that I won… you're making me sound like some archmage who could take on an orcish warchief. But in reality, I can't even cast Fireball. If I could, I'd have ridden a griffin straight to Nethergarde Keep by now."

"Ah, Nethergarde Keep…" Ferby pursed his lips. "When I first got here today I was hoping to go to Nethergarde Keep too, but after a day of fighting, I'm having second thoughts."

Jace asked, "What mission were you on today?"

Ferby said, "A bunch of demons had occupied the high ground on the hills between here and Nethergarde Keep. I went with Commander Abbendis's force to take that high ground… hoping to reopen the safe passage from here to Nethergarde Keep."

Commander Abbendis?

Jace jolted at the name. Years later, after Lordaeron fell to the Scourge, the fanatical organization known as the Scarlet Crusade — dedicated to reclaiming Lordaeron and purging the Scourge — had been founded by General Abbendis. During the Orc Wars, he had served as Marshal Alexandros Mograine's lieutenant.

Ferby said, "We won, but a lot of people died. A lot were wounded. Those demons… hell, I don't want to think about that scene again."

He applied some herbs to his leg and winced with a sharp intake of breath, then went on: "I really don't know what things are like over at Nethergarde Keep. I've heard the Dark Portal's energy has turned the south into a blighted wasteland — every living thing dead… and even some that are half-alive, corpses driven by fel energy."

Corpses driven by fel energy — Jace could picture that well enough.

In the game, after Brutallus was killed by the army that retook the Sunwell, the fel blood that poured from his body corrupted the blue dragon Madrigosa, whom he had killed, transforming her corpse into an undead fel dragon.

"I probably won't sleep tonight from the pain," Ferby glanced at the bandages on Jace's abdomen. "You got stabbed in the stomach and you don't even feel it? Are you even human?"

"It hurts," Jace frowned. "Why'd you bring it up…"

Ferby laughed. "Then let's talk about something else. Jace, you must be pretty close with Lady Sylvanas Windrunner, right? You killed Teron Gorefiend together…"

"Yeah." Jace, wrapped in bandages, breathed out and tilted his head back, the back of his skull pressing against the cold wall.

"Is she pretty?" Ferby said. "By your standards, I mean."

"Saying she's not would be a lie," Jace said after a moment's thought. "I've never seen a more beautiful elf."

Ferby raised his eyebrows. "I envy you — going into a dangerous demon lair with a beautiful Quel'dorei to assassinate an enemy leader. You literally made real a scene I've imagined a million times. Though honestly, if Lady Sylvanas Windrunner really invited me to hunt down that undead monster, I might not have the guts to go."

"Who says that? You came to Stonard, didn't you?" Jace thought about it. "Besides, when she first asked me to come along, it wasn't to kill Teron Gorefiend…"

Ferby cleared his throat. "A lot of historical figures who achieved great deeds didn't originally want to walk the path they eventually took. But they went anyway, for better or worse."

"Maybe I'm talking to a future hero right now. Actually, you killing Gorefiend already makes you a hero. More and more people are going to know about you. Poets are going to write your story and teach it to bards to sing in taverns across the world…"

Listening to Ferby's string of flattery, Jace's thoughts gradually drifted off somewhere.

He thought of that rainy day when he and Grede had been ambushed by Hogger's gnolls, waking up in Goldshire and hearing that song about the fallen Alliance commander, Anduin Lothar. He wondered how it would feel if Lothar in that song were replaced with himself.

The Legend of Jace Sesso… just thinking about it felt awkward, like his name was hard to connect with any great tale.

"Oh right, Jace," Ferby said. "You said you can't cast Fireball — isn't your teacher Master Marin? He's the most powerful fire mage in the Wizard Sanctum."

"The curriculum hasn't quite caught up…" Jace said helplessly.

"I'll teach you the incantation," Ferby said. "I know the Fireball incantation. We'll get up early tomorrow morning and go outside to practice the chanting and casting. What do you think?"

"Really?" Jace asked.

"If it wasn't real, what would I be babbling about?" Ferby said excitedly. "I'm actually going to teach Jace Sesso Fireball! My goodness! Don't forget me in the future!"
