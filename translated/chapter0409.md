## Chapter 409: A Place That Dies by Degrees

After saying goodbye to Ferby, Jace joined the party preparing to head to Nethergarde Keep.

The group was being led by a band of Stromgarde cavalry in red who'd come from Nethergarde Keep. Those fellows rode horses a full head taller than Stormwind's cavalry — crimson cloaks, bright red armor, lances tipped with the Arathi fist banner, silver pointed helmets on their heads. They looked proud and imposing, like a flock of red whelps.

Even though Stormwind was far closer to here than the Kingdom of Stromgarde, it was clear the people of Arathi held considerable influence here at the moment — no doubt tied to the leadership of Danath Trollbane.

Jace still felt a little regretful that he couldn't ride a griffin to Nethergarde Keep like the mages had on that first day. But back then the sky had been total chaos — riding a griffin would have been dangerous, to say nothing of traveling overland the whole way.

Now that there was a cavalry escort, it meant the road from here to Nethergarde Keep had been reopened.

The party set out in the morning — roughly two hundred people, hauling supplies shipped in from Duskwood, heading south.

In the game, the divide between this swamp and Nethergarde Keep to the south — the Blasted Lands — was extremely stark.

The Blasted Lands were like a vast chasm split open around the Dark Portal as its center, nothing but barren wasteland everywhere. Only scorpions, vultures, boars — scavengers, creatures that could even feed on demon flesh — could survive here.

From the northern half of the black swamp that would later be called the Swamp of Sorrows, heading south, crossing a stretch…

But here on the ground, the boundary between the two wasn't nearly so clear-cut.

Jace could feel the fel energy growing thicker. Streams, bogs, and the like grew sparser. Dead trees multiplied around them. The climate gradually dried out, but the red wasteland didn't appear all at once.

But just as Ferby had said, this place really did increasingly look like a dead land. The clouds overhead grew thicker and thicker. Green fel lightning foreboded ill. Even at three or four in the afternoon, it was as dark as nightfall was near.

Jace could sense the mages traveling with him growing more and more tense. After all, plenty of full mages who'd fought in the Orc Wars had never seen a scene like this.

Of course, Jace was absolutely not calm about it either — especially when some unknown creature's shriek suddenly tore through the sky.

As they marched along the road, they occasionally felt something in the woods along the roadside watching them.

He tried casting Sense Demons to look around, but saw nothing at all.

In a place like the Black Swamp, bandits and thieves were unlikely. Could some beast be sizing up a military party of over a hundred?

If it were beasts, they'd have fled at the sound of the horses' hooves from a distance. Just then, he noticed a low shadow flitting through the trees — not human, and certainly not orcish. At a quick glance, it rather resembled the silhouette of a gnoll.

He wasn't the only one who'd noticed. Before long, someone behind him asked, "Did you all see that too?"

"Pay those folk no mind, mage," one of the cavalrymen said. "There are some monsters in this swamp that fled from the other world. They look like a bunch of toads that learned to stand up. Just don't provoke them. Those creatures may look crude, but they have a bare minimum of sense. As long as they don't feel threatened, they won't attack our people."

Another cavalryman added, "It's said they were driven here as slaves by the orcs and demons, poor wretches who escaped after the Horde's defeat."

Listening to the cavalrymen's description, a possibility occurred to Jace.

These low shadows might be the so-called Lost Ones.

The refugees of Argus who lived on Draenor, the Eredar who called themselves "Draenei" — after suffering defeat after defeat in the war against the orcs, many captives endured long-term corruption by fel and shadow. Some of the survivors had become exactly this sort of malformed monstrosity, severely degraded in both intellect and power.

The Eredar had all been tall and imposing, standing proud and formidable even among the Burning Legion's many demons. The Draenei who refused fel energy weren't much different.

He could hardly imagine what sort of inner transformation those Draenei must have endured to degenerate into creatures like this under torment.

Under the gaze of this dark dead land, the party marched to a gloomy low-lying hollow. Jace could tell this had once been a small lake or pond of some sort — the whole depression reeked of an extremely foul stench.

The stench wasn't quite the same as fel, but it wasn't much better. Just as Jace was searching for the source of the smell, a nearby mage whispered to another mage, "Look… that thing."

Quite a few people in the party were looking over there. Jace followed their gazes and saw a tattered demon corpse impaled atop a tree trunk, head tilted toward the sky, arms, legs, and wings all hanging downward.

The demon corpse was dark brown, its wing membranes dark red. Jace could tell this was a doomguard.

Though he'd never actually seen a doomguard in the flesh, this corpse's features were distinctive enough.

Doomguards were the fiercest flying demons in the Burning Legion. They came from a demon race called the Ered'ruin, whose numbers within the Legion were second only to the Mo'arg.

The Ered'ruin were the very image that ordinary people on Azeroth pictured when they thought of a "demon" — covered in horns and black down, a massive spiked head, enormous demonic wings, wielding sharp blades, filling the sky, possessing fearsome strength, and breathing wildfire.

A thousand years ago, when demons had infiltrated Tirisfal and Dalaran, succubi and felhounds had hidden in the shadows, while these terrifying creatures had revealed themselves openly, leaving a deeply dreadful impression on the humans of that era.

"Move through quickly!" a knight-captain up ahead shouted in Common with a thick highland accent. "Don't linger near this demon corpse for long, unless you want to wake up tomorrow morning with boils on your skin!"

By the time evening came, though the forest hadn't grown any darker, just as Jace was wondering when they'd finally reach Nethergarde Keep, a stretch of tall enough walls suddenly appeared on the high ground ahead.

So they'd already reached the Blasted Lands?

This was nothing like it was in the game. After all, in the game, the area around Nethergarde Keep was already a field of red wasteland.

Though of course this place could hardly be called thriving either, it wasn't completely barren of life — there was at least a sparse forest nearby.

Which meant the Blasted Lands had gradually become that monstrous sight over the decade and a half to come. Countless demons had poured unceasingly through the twisted rifts created by the Dark Portal's detonation. Years of warfare and the dark energy the demons had spread had never fully dissipated in the time since.

The sentinels on the tower spotted the approaching party from afar and blew a few bright horn blasts. The red-cloaked cavalry at the front urged their horses faster, and Jace's mood grew more and more exhilarated.

Hanging on those walls were the violet banners of the Alliance and the Lordaeron banners representing the Alliance, fluttering slightly in the fel wind blowing from the Dark Portal.

Jace could tell these walls were more in the Stormwind style. They were quite different from the walls of the Lordaeron capital, or the Arathi style, or even Durnholde Castle. Perhaps the Stonemasons had taken part in designing and building this castle.

At any rate, Nethergarde Keep… this place he'd always wanted to see for himself was right there before his eyes.
