## Chapter 41: The Wolves at the Riverbank

They were surrounded by wolves.

Jace and Grede scanned the area and spotted three or four of them watching from not far off. There were probably more lurking in the forest.

Life had been hard for the wolves of Elwynn Forest lately too. The gnolls' invasion had squeezed their territory and competed with them for food, forcing them to venture close to human settlements to scrape by — which put them on a collision course with farms and herders.

Jace unslung the pole from his back and gripped it. Facing actual beasts, he realized just how much more secure a bladed weapon would make him feel. At the very least, one touch meant a gash — no great strength or skill required. And besides being long… what other advantages did a stick even have?

"Grede, your knife." Jace beckoned. "Lend it here."

Grede tossed the dagger over. Jace quickly wound some cloth and thin cord around it, lashed it tight to one end of the pole, gave it a few shakes and tugs to make sure it wouldn't come loose, and only then felt a fraction more at ease.

"You think they'll come at us?" Jace asked.

Grede answered, "If they showed themselves, they will. If they weren't willing, they'd have caught our scent and bolted. These cunning bastards are no stupider than dogs — they wouldn't have come this far if they weren't confident."

Even as he said it, he stayed put on the ground, perfectly at ease, as if the idea of wolves lunging at him was of no concern whatsoever.

Jace had his battle stance ready, both hands on the makeshift spear he'd just fashioned, keeping a constant eye on the surroundings.

"These wolves are small." Grede tossed a biscuit into his mouth, stood up and dusted the dirt off his backside. "They can't have seen many dwarves. Probably took me for a human cub. And with you looking all nervous, they definitely think we're easy marks. Well then — let's give them a lesson they won't forget."

The moment Grede rose to his feet, the wolves shifted.

Jace raised his spear to meet them. Grede held up a hand. "Hold on. They're testing how dangerous you are. If you act too fierce, they'll decide you're not afraid of them — and if you're not afraid, there must be something backing that up. Magic, maybe. A proper weapon. Once they figure that, they'll back off — either run, or circle around burning your energy and patience until you slip up. So let's play it steady. Don't show too much aggression. Wait until they close in and start baring their teeth to threaten us, then strike."

Sure enough, the wolves pressed closer but didn't attack right away. Some hung back watching; others drew near. Counting them now, there were seven or eight — big and small. More than the man had mentioned.

Jace lowered his stance a little, but his nerves wound tighter. He glanced sideways at Grede. The dwarf had his sword gripped tight, his hands even shaking a little. Was that nerves too?

A wolf on their flank suddenly edged closer and let out a growl. Jace whirled around and thrust the spear at it — only to hear a rustling behind him.

Grede's sword fell in a flash. A wolf that had been sneaking up on Jace from behind had its front leg hacked clean off; it howled and tumbled to the ground. Grede moved terrifyingly fast — one bounding step and he buried his sword halfway into another wolf's skull, then kicked the convulsing half-dead animal to wrench his blade free.

Jace drove his spear hard into the wolf in front of him — and the dagger popped right off.

The wolf, the dagger buried in its back, let out a low whimper and shuddered backward into the treeline. More wolves that had been lying in wait were already charging up to them. Jace swung the pole into another wolf's ear — a solid hit that sent it rolling on the ground.

"They're running!"

Grede's furious roar came from behind. Jace looked back — plenty of wolves had already vanished.

"After them! They might head toward the den… and if they do, we might find their food there."

And with that, the dwarf was already crashing into the treeline.

Jace found the wolf he'd stabbed with the dagger, half-dead and gasping, pulled the knife free, and re-lashed it as he ran.

Only then did he realize what Grede had meant by "food" — the place where the Sturmil family's child might be.

Jace sprinted after Grede's figure, but how were two sets of legs supposed to match a pack of forest wolves? They lost the trail in no time.

Grede panted hard, tracking the wolves' chaotic footprints on the ground. "All these brainless idiots fleeing every which way… couldn't be more obvious. Today I'll find where the lot of you call home."

Tracking them as they went, before he knew it Jace could hear running water. They'd reached the river.

The wolves' trail followed the river downstream. After a while longer, Jace suddenly spotted a wolf on the far side of a stream that branched off from the main river, standing there watching them, its tail wagging nonstop.

Grede hitched up his trousers and splashed across the stream toward the wolf without hesitation — then stopped dead halfway over.

The wolf hadn't moved. It was still standing there, watching them both.

The dwarf turned and scrambled back even more urgently, splashing through the water. "Get away from here, Jace!"

Jace instantly understood why that lone wolf had been standing there — but when he turned around, the grass in front of him was already full of dark shapes.

From the direction of the trees, under the moonlight, one massive adult forest wolf after another regarded the two of them — like a pack of triumphant hunters gloating over their prey.

Grede walked up to Jace's side, sized up the pack, and snorted. "See now why I had to bring you along? This is one mess I couldn't handle alone."

Jace said wearily, "Could you at least not act like you saw this coming? We got played, that's what happened."

Grede didn't argue. "Well, if we want to solve that farm's problem, this pack had to be dealt with sooner or later. Now I know why the locals haven't taken that warrant for so long — it's a pain in the ass. But now it's simple. We kill them!"

This time they weren't facing seven or eight small wolves. This was a big pack — well over ten, quite likely, and several full-grown adults had joined in.

The dwarf pointed at that mass of dark shapes. "The lead wolf should be among the biggest ones there. Can't see clearly from here — focus on those when the time comes."

Compared to the pups from before, this pack had a completely different air about them. They closed in fast. Jace could already make out the fangs of the vanguard wolf nearest to him, mouth peeled back.

Grede was far more tense than before too, taking steady, deep breaths. This was going to be one hell of a fight.

Jace raised his makeshift spear. The back of his head burned.
