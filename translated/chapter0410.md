## Chapter 410: Nethergarde Keep

Nethergarde Keep was enormous. After all, this castle was also part of the Alliance's reconstruction plan — many nations, including the elves and the dwarves, had contributed funds to build this "last bastion" meant to "defend Azeroth and guard the Dark Portal."

Walking up the vast slope, Jace could already feel his own insignificance. Nethergarde Keep's walls stood like a grey cliff face high above, and atop the walls rose a towering mage's tower. The Kirin Tor banner flew from it, the Eye of the Arcane on the banner gazing southward like a true watchman overseeing this land.

Jace really couldn't fathom how such a terrifyingly massive fortress had been thrown together in the span of two or three years.

The mages of Quel'Thalas and the mages of Dalaran had all taken part in building this castle, just so it could take shape faster and protect the entire world for who knew how many years to come.

Clearly, its first trial was about to arrive.

When the cavalry party reached the gate of Nethergarde Keep, listening to the iron portcullis being hauled up by its chains, Jace looked back toward the Dark Portal. Through the dead trees still standing, across the endless hills, he could see all the way to the pitch-black shadow on the horizon, where a tiny green speck of light glowed.

"That's the Dark Portal!" a Stromgarde soldier riding near Jace said. "See that light? That's the Dark Portal. You can see the gate directly from here…"

Another soldier said, "That gate sits very low. This wretched land can barely endure the power it's releasing. I feel like it's sunk even lower than when we first started building this castle."

"But that's a good thing too," the soldier said. "It lets us always maintain the high ground advantage over the demons crawling out of the gate…"

Even as he said it, even he sounded a little short on conviction.

After the wagons arrived at the square inside Nethergarde Keep, plenty of soldiers came over to help unload.

Nethergarde Keep was busy everywhere. The square was packed with wagons, nearly all from the Kingdom of Stormwind — after all, the other kingdoms' supplies were also shipped through Stormwind's ports to get here.

Dust flew everywhere in the square. There was a pervasive smell of something scorched, and of course the never-fully-dissipating stench of fel and shadow. Still, it wasn't as bad as he'd imagined.

Looking around, massive keeps, towers, stables, and quarters leaned against one another. Further off, the barracks were each like small castles in their own right — not a bit smaller in scale than the fortresses Jace had seen elsewhere.

The place was the size of a genuine small city — a small city wrapped in impossibly thick walls, surrounded by towers that could fire ballistae and cannons. A true doomsday fortress.

Jace could hear the distant shouts of soldiers drilling. He didn't know where those soldiers were training — perhaps there was a training ground behind the walls too. Which meant Nethergarde Keep was even bigger than it appeared.

Looking up, he could see gryphon nests lined along the walls, with quite a few griffins dozing inside them.

It was the first time Jace had seen so many griffins at once. He stared hard at the dwarves, trying to pick Grede out from among them.

Of course that was impossible. Those dwarves wore goggles and big cloaks; all that showed was either hair or beards. Even if they were lined up right in front of him, he probably couldn't have told them apart.

Not to mention, Grede probably hadn't found his unit yet — he might be in the middle of negotiating with the Ironforge ambassador in Stormwind about how to join the Ironforge army or something.

"Mages, follow me."

A young full mage raised his emerald staff and called out.

Jace gathered his pack and followed the mage along with a few faces he'd found familiar back at the Wizard Sanctum. The direction they were heading was, naturally, that beautiful mage's tower.

A good number of mages were already standing on the open ground at the tower's base, waiting. Jace spotted the woman standing at the very front right away.

"Amy! Amy Marin!"

Jace called out as he approached. Amy whipped her head toward him and her eyes went wide.

"Jace?" Amy squeezed through several mages and came up to Jace. "You're finally here!"

She looked a lot thinner than when they'd eaten together two months ago. She'd never been what you'd call fat to begin with, but now she looked more like a beanpole.

In the ceaseless howling wind of this dark land, she seemed as if she might topple over at any moment.

But seeing the state Jace was in, looking him up and down, her initially delighted expression suddenly wilted considerably.

"What happened to your clothes? They look like they've been through a fire. Did you run into the enemy?"

"Demons," Jace said. "But it's fine. I'm all right. How about you?"

Amy said, "When the demons first appeared a week ago we ran into quite a bit of trouble. I fought the demons alongside the Kirin Tor mages a few times, but I wasn't hurt. The fighters here have taken good care of us."

"But my father took part in the operation to break the siege of the Giant Hammer Fortress the day before yesterday…"

"Is he hurt?" Jace couldn't help but ask directly.

"No," Amy said. "He's just been channeling mana too frequently and hasn't recovered yet. The Kirin Tor had hoped he could help oversee the defense work of this mage's tower, but he keeps forgetting how old he is and has no regard for his own body."

"I'd like to see him," Jace said.

"He's asleep — don't disturb him," Amy said. "By tomorrow morning, he'll be very happy to see you. For now, let me get you something to eat. Now that you've arrived at Nethergarde Keep, you've got to try the local specialty…"

"What kind of specialty could there be in a place like this?" Jace asked, stunned.

Amy said in a mysterious tone, "Bugs! And infused with the tender warmth of the Holy Light. Where else do you think you'd get to eat such holy cooking? Food roasted by sacred fire — you wouldn't even see that at the Cathedral of Light!"

Jace asked, "The priests have all become cooks?"

Amy smiled. "More or less. Even if they're not literally cooking, they're at least involved in the process. After all, the animals here are all tainted by fel contamination. Without the priests handling it, where would we get that much meat to eat? Stormwind's supply lines can't keep up as it is, let alone with a war on. Carrion worms, evil giant scorpions — the taste's actually not bad…"

"My god…" Jace sighed.

"Stop complaining!" Amy glared at him. "Otherwise you can eat the delicious food conjured by the mages…"

Jace glanced at Amy's arms, thin as sticks with skin stretched over bone, and could only nod.
