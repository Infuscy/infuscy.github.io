Chapter 411: A Short Rest

Following Amy into the mage tower, Jace noticed the ground floor hall was quite beautifully decorated — it reminded him of the cozy little taverns back in Dalaran.

Of course, the furnishings here were nowhere near as spacious as those in Dalaran. It was as if all the expensive and elaborate decor had been crammed together, leaving the passageways between them especially narrow.

Then again, this was a tower.

Amy had Jace find a seat first, then ran off toward the back stairs. A moment later she came back carrying something wrapped in paper.

She set the paper down in front of her, carefully unwrapped it, and gazed at the chunks of broken meat inside. "Try some."

"So this is the bug meat?" Jace asked, eyeing what looked like a pile of shredded chicken breast.

"That's right," Amy said. "What, did you think I'd put a pig-sized scorpion on the table for you?"

"Not exactly," Jace said. He had to admit, even though these few chunks of meat were supposedly insect meat... they looked and smelled pretty good.

Amy picked up a piece, then caught Jace's eager expression and said quickly, "Go on, eat."

Jace nodded. "Thanks, Amy."

"What are you thanking me for? Isn't taking care of my little brother the least I can do?" Amy said with a smile. "Remember when I told you I was coming to Nethergarde Keep and you said you wanted to come too? I thought it'd be ages before that day came. Who knew time would fly like that."

"A few days ago, during an operation against a demon air raid, a Kirin Tor master of frost magic named Amani recognized my performance. He wants to recommend me for full mage status, and he's planning to have me go to Dalaran to study under him after the war against the demons is won."

Jace glanced at her. Man, you really love tempting fate, huh?

"I'm jealous. Might not be long before I see you again as a Kirin Tor mage," he said.

Amy grinned proudly. Jace added, "Tell me about the demons you've been running into. I'm new here — I want to know what kind of trouble I'm walking into."

Amy nodded. "The biggest threat Nethergarde Keep and the nearby outposts face is a kind of bat-like demon. Now, Jace, don't hear 'bat' and think of those little bats that come out at night. Each one is the size of a mountain lion, some even as big as a horse. They've got wide bat wings on their backs, and fel fire pours from their mouths and eyes."

"They're a huge threat to the gryphon riders, the crossbowmen on the walls, and the mages. A few days ago I joined a rescue for some downed gryphon riders nearby and ran into a pack of those fel bats. One mage got snatched up — fortunately he reacted fast and broke free with Blink. Broke his leg, but at least he kept his life."

"Damn," Jace said. "I don't know Blink at all!"

"Relax, I'll protect you," Amy said. "The frost power in my hands won't let a single demon touch you."

Jace realized that after a few days here, Amy really had become more confident than before.

The two talked late into the night — from Jace's trip through the Wetlands to everything Amy had seen since arriving at Nethergarde Keep — until the hall had pretty much emptied out, and only then did they think about getting some sleep.

Getting a private room in the keep was out of the question. Every room housed a bunch of mages. Jace kept climbing the spiral stairs to a very high floor before he found a spot to sleep.

Passing a window, Jace gazed out toward the Dark Portal.

No matter the weather — dust, storms, overcast skies, thick fog — the glow of the Dark Portal was always there. As long as you looked in that direction, you could see that green curtain leading to another world.

It seemed right in front of you, but you could never reach it. The road between here and the gate was probably already packed with thousands of demons, and even more demons were still pouring out of that green curtain.

Early the next morning, a mage came to the door and shouted inside, "Mages! The gryphon-rider scouts have spotted a demon army advancing toward Rainmead Mine. Everyone here, downstairs immediately — we have to move out now and reinforce that position. That mine produces the raw materials for making runestones. If the demons take it, we might even have to ship the materials for opening portals all the way from the Kingdom of Stromgarde."

The mages immediately gathered their things, got dressed, grabbed their staves, spell components, and notebooks full of incantations. By the time they reached the ground floor, the square ahead was already filled with squads of soldiers in Lordaeron white, fully armed and ready to move.

They'd only just learned upon arrival that a group of Stromgarde mages who'd reached the keep last night had already been sent to a southern watchtower. So Jace and the other young mages, who were uninjured and had rested well through the night, got picked to reinforce the mine's defenders.

Besides these young mages, a number of Wizard Sanctum mages and battle mages were also assigned to the force. Jace spotted Amy among them — judging by her expression, she was clearly looking forward to this fight.

After all, she couldn't let down that Kirin Tor frost mage's trust. If she wanted to prove her command over frost, there was no more convincing argument than a battlefield.

Two gryphon riders had already set out from the walls. From where Jace stood, the soldiers preparing to reinforce the mine looked to be about three hundred strong, maybe more — especially with the cavalry up front.

The road to the mine was in fairly good shape. Marching along the blackened dirt path through the completely dead forest, Jace even had the feeling something might jump out at them in the middle of the night... Of course, day and night didn't really matter here. Black clouds blotted out everything, sun and moon alike.

But the world outside the keep wasn't totally dark. Jace almost felt like the glow of the Dark Portal illuminated the entire Blasted Lands, or maybe it was the evil lightning constantly crackling in the clouds. The mages trailing behind the soldiers didn't look too good. Most of them were fresh recruits who'd just arrived from Stonard. Jace even recognized a few faces who'd only gotten here yesterday.

Just when Jace thought they'd be marching in silence all the way to the mine at the foot of the northern hills, one of the mages beside him said, "From what I know, there are quite a few defenders there. By the time we arrive, the demons might give up the attack and turn back..."

"Don't count on it," another mage said. "The demons don't care about strength ratios. Hundreds of them will throw their lives away charging at you — if they can trade one of their cannon fodder for a single one of yours, they've achieved their goal..."

A knight-lieutenant nearby barked in annoyance, "You'd all better save your breath for casting! Demons feel fear too, demons know dread. When they charge, just hit them with your strongest magic right in the faces. As for whether we win or lose — leave that to the Holy Light to decide!"
