## Chapter 42: Darker Than the Night

Late at night, the two moons — one large, one small — hung in the sky. Moonlight spilled across the river's surface, shimmering silver, but the riverside reeds only made the deep thickets beyond look all the more unnerving.

The forest canopy blocked out the moonlight. Grede stared at the rows of dull yellow eyes watching them from the dark, straining to make out which wolf held dominance over the pack.

Take it down in the first strike and the whole pack would lose its backbone and scatter… and he and Jace's chances of surviving would multiply several times over.

A few smaller, younger wolves pressed closest, growling and baring their teeth, trying to goad Jace and Grede into making the first move. Grede ignored the lot of them, eyes locked on the older wolves watching from higher ground.

"I'm thinking it's probably that one on the edge… the big one. You see him?" Grede said.

"How can you tell?" Jace asked.

"That one on the edge is directing the young ones. If you watch carefully, you can catch the eye contact between them. Anyway, keep an eye on that one — when you get a chance, take him down. Don't waste time on the others."

Just as Grede was talking him through it, a young wolf on the vanguard suddenly lunged in from the side, going for his calf. Grede kicked it flat on its back and raised his sword to stab — but several wolves around them erupted in barks that made him hesitate a beat, and the kicked wolf was already retreating.

Seeing the two of them react, two more wolves charged Jace simultaneously from other directions. Jace swung the pole hard at one of them; it tried to dodge, caught the blow on its shoulder, and let out a low whine.

The other wolf was already on him. Before it could get its jaws in, Grede grabbed it, yanked it off, and hurled it to the ground, stomping its spine to a snap.

One wolf wounded, one half-dead and twitching on the ground. The wolves that had tested the attack slowly pulled back, and the older wolves up on the high ground began to shift.

"They're testing us too?" Jace said.

"That's right," Grede said. "Careful, Jace. They've probably decided you're the weaker target. You're the one they're marking."

Once the older wolves came down among them, the young ones visibly grew more confident, barking with twice the energy. When the old wolves joined in with their howls, the young ones surged forward in a scrambling rush — and sure enough, they were all going after Jace!

Grede had been meaning to push toward the older wolves, but seeing Jace suddenly swarmed by a pack of them, he could only swing his sword over to help fend off a few of the smaller ones.

That was when Jace discovered the advantage of a long pole. One hard sweep in front of him and the wolves before him didn't dare rush in. When he sensed wolves closing in from behind, he'd swing it back the other way, and those ones hesitated too.

When a wolf reckless enough to charge through came at them anyway, Grede would cut it down or open it up with a single stroke. But every so often he'd glance at the older wolves' positions — only to find them cunningly hanging behind the young ones, clearly waiting for a single opening to strike.

The older wolves must have changed their tactics after the first group came back hurt. After a spell of chaos, both sides had actually settled back into a standoff.

The night wore on. Jace felt a wave of drowsiness creep over him, and his hands slowed. Grede was the same.

"They're burning us out," Jace said. "Should we end this fast?"

"You're right," Grede said. "Jace… watch yourself. I'm going for it."

"Go," Jace said.

Jace had no confidence whatsoever that he could handle several wolves attacking him at once, but dragging this out meant certain death. Grede gripped his sword with both hands, his gaze already fixed on the older wolf he'd singled out before.

Jace screwed up his courage and let out a loud roar. As the wolves in front of him snapped to attention, Grede kicked aside a young wolf and charged straight at the old one.

The sudden move threw the whole pack into disarray. A few young wolves went after Grede but couldn't keep up. The wolves that had been crowding Jace were strung out, unable to close ranks. Jace seized the pole and thrust hard at a young wolf in front of him, driving the knife deep into its flank — that was enough to finish it. He wrenched the blade free and swept it sideways, opening a gash on another wolf and sending the young ones around it into a yelping frenzy.

Meanwhile, Grede came screaming toward the pack of older wolves — but the one he'd been targeting backed up a step, letting two other old wolves step into his path.

Grede swung his blade at one of their necks on the follow-through. His sword was blindingly fast; the old wolf tried to retreat and still took the blow across the mouth. Another wolf, seeing Grede off-balance with his sword still extended, sank its teeth hard into the back of his leg.

The dwarf's shout made Jace's heart lurch, and he looked over. He watched what seemed to be the lead wolf circle around behind Grede, about to deliver a killing blow. Grede sensed the danger behind him and split the wolf biting his leg clean in half — head and body — but there was no shaking the skull clamped onto his leg.

He cursed in Dwarvish. And as Jace looked over, a searing pain flared across his own back.

A wolf had used his moment of distraction to leap onto his back, raking at him and going for the back of his neck. Jace threw it off with a hard jerk — but seeing him wounded, the other wolves that had been hanging back erupted in howls and charged.

Jace was knocked to the ground by the impact. He used the pole to block the wolf gnashing in his face, shoved it with everything he had, and was trying to get up when another wolf seized his arm and dragged him down again. As he reached for that wolf's eyes, his leg was being torn at furiously by yet another young wolf.

His limbs burned with a searing pain, and he couldn't summon strength anywhere. On Grede's side, the dwarf was already surrounded by several older wolves. His height was completely swallowed up in the pack — Jace could only see a straight sword swinging back and forth, catching the occasional pained yelp as a wolf was cut, but those older wolves showed no sign of backing off.

If he didn't go all in now, he was done for.

Jace kicked out hard through the pain, stomping the young wolf clamped onto his right leg flat on its back. He grabbed the wolf dragging his sleeve and twisted its neck until it snapped, snatched up the pole, and brought it squarely onto the skull of another wolf lunging at him — that one dropped stiff on the spot.

The rest of the pack had gone berserk, not caring how many of their own had fallen. Before Jace could finish off one of the whimpering wolves on the ground with a follow-up strike, another wolf bit into his clothes from behind and dragged him several yards back, nearly pulling him off his feet.

A tangled mess of fury, tension, and madness clamped down on Jace's mind.

Now. It was time. It was time.

— *Fergt.*

The long pole in his hand trembled faintly.

The tremor snapped Jace back to his senses. The near-uncontrolled fury cooled considerably. Then the searing pain in his limbs, his back, and his neck hit him all at once, making him squeeze his eyes shut.

He bore with it for a few seconds, until the worst of it dulled to something he could stand.

But when he opened his eyes again and took in the scene, not a single wolf was still standing.

The forest rang with the agonized howls of wolves. Several wolf figures fled with their tails tucked, bolting as if they'd seen some terrible devil, never daring to look back.

The older wolves around Grede had vanished too, leaving only a few bodies on the ground and some too badly hurt to move.

The lead wolf wasn't dead yet. It lay there trembling, eyes locked on Jace, its bowels having let go — and still it refused to retreat.

But Grede wasn't looking at that wolf. He was looking over here too.

"…Did you do this, Jace?"

Jace didn't know how to explain it for a moment. He glanced at the pole in his hand — purple arcane light bled through the gaps in the cloth wrapping, vicious and wrong, and the air around him felt deathly cold. The chill and the pain came in waves, nearly buckling his knees.

The lead wolf toppled over, eyes still fixed on them, unblinking to the last. Grede stood frozen, staring over. His sword had fallen to the ground.

"Your hands, Grede." Jace pointed at the dwarf's hands. Grede's gaze drifted slowly downward and found both his hands shaking violently, beyond his control.

A long while passed without either of them speaking.

Grede crouched by the river, washing the blood off his body and clothes over and over, until his hands finally stopped trembling.

Jace checked his wounds — far lighter than he'd expected. Nothing too gruesome, no major blood vessels hit, it seemed.

He tore up his shirt, which the wolves had already reduced to rags, into makeshift bandages, then sat down on a large rock by the river.

The dwarf gathered his things and walked over to Jace with a small liquor flask in hand.

He leaned back against the rock, let out a long breath, and asked, "A spell that powerful — you learned it from those two books?"

Jace shook his head. "I learned it from another warlock."

Even through all that beard, Jace could tell the dwarf's expression was dead serious.

Jace said nothing. Grede put a hand on Jace's shoulder. "While we rest, let me tell you a story."

"A long time ago, Ironforge was the shared home of all dwarves. Since the earliest days recorded in history, we lived in the high mountains of Dun Morogh. Ironforge was the grandest city all dwarves built together, and it was our pride."

"But nothing is eternal. Ironforge had too many dwarves. Some were forced into the mountains and caves of Dun Morogh. That's when the three greatest clans of dwarves — Wildhammer, Bronzebeard, and Dark Iron — all wanted to stay in Ironforge and rule the city. Two hundred years ago, the last High King died, and everything finally went to hell."

"I won't judge who was right or who was wrong, who struck first, who killed the most dwarves, or who dealt Ironforge the heaviest blow. All of that's been buried in history beyond sorting out."

"In the end, Bronzebeard won. My ancestors headed north and carved out another vast underground city in those mountains to rival Ironforge — the great Grim Batol."

"But not many years later, the Dark Iron king who had fled to settle in Redridge — Thaurissan, who styled himself the 'Warcaster King' — used the rich ore and timber there to forge a golem army. Then he and his wife Modgud each led a legion, attacking Dun Morogh and Grim Batol at the same time, determined to wipe out the shame of defeat and conquer all dwarves once and for all."

"The one who attacked Grim Batol was Modgud. The witch besieged Grim Batol, but the siege dragged on and she couldn't take it. In a fit of rage, she used some evil artifact she'd gotten from who-knows-where to curse the entire city. Even though she died in battle, the artifact's power was far greater than Modgud herself — it was what had seduced that woman, what had seduced the once-brilliant mage-king Thaurissan. But once the truth was clear, it was all too late. The dwarves could never return to their former glory."

"The artifact sank into the depths of Grim Batol. No one ever saw it again, but the whole city became a fallen abyss teeming with wicked spirits and shadow curses because of it. Over a thousand dwarves died in agony. We had no choice but to leave and push further north, all the way to the Hinterlands."

"Dark magic is no good thing. It makes you lose your way, robs you of reason, pushes you further and further toward extremes — until eventually you don't even recognize yourself, my friend."

"But I won't try to dictate your choices either."

"Every person has their own path. My people love freedom, and they know how hard it was to win — so we don't like being told what to do and what not to do. The same goes for me with others, and even more so with you."

By this point the dwarf was already slurring a little. Even though he took a swig after every sentence, Jace knew he wouldn't get drunk that easily. He just wanted to talk Jace out of it, but couldn't bring himself to say it straight — so he was tangled up in a deep struggle.

"Still, I've got to admit your magic is something else," Grede said, looking at the toppled wolf corpses strewn across the riverbank. "I was in the thick of it with them, and then all at once I felt a fear like nothing I'd ever known. Even facing a troll hunter in the dark, I'd never been that scared. I thought it was over — knew I probably couldn't win, that I was about to get torn apart — and that's what terrified me. But then I saw that wolf was even more afraid than me. It was shaking like it had seen a living lion. That was actually kind of funny."

"Only then did it hit me — ah. We were afraid of the same thing." The dwarf chuckled at that.

"If there ever comes a day when I'm not myself anymore, kill me," Jace said.

Grede shook his right hand, which was still trembling faintly, and the smile on his face slowly faded.

"You know I couldn't do it, Jace. Even if you became a different person — even if you turned into an orc — I still couldn't do it. But if that day really comes, I'll go far away. As far from you as it takes, until I've forgotten you completely."

## Flags

- **弗艾格特 (Fergt):** The incantation Jace speaks to trigger the shadow spell. Rendered phonetically. Not in glossary — needs a consistent canonical rendering if one is established later.
- **格瑞姆巴托 (Grim Batol):** In the glossary as "Grim Batol." Grede's retelling of the War of the Three Hammers and Modgud's curse follows canon lore; kept the canonical names (Ironforge, Dun Morogh, Wildhammer, Bronzebeard, Dark Iron, Thaurissan, Modgud, Redridge, the Hinterlands).
- **"Warcaster King":** Grede's rendering of 索瑞森's self-styled title "巫王." Canonically Thaurissan is the "Warcaster King," so used that.
- **"my friend":** Grede says 我的老弟 — rendered as "my friend" to keep the dwarf's register natural without overdoing the dialect.
- **Dwarvish curse:** Grede "cursed in Dwarvish" — left as a narrative beat, no translation needed.
