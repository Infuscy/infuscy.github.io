## Chapter 43: The Wolf Den

The two of them walked single file along the edge of the riverbank. Grede eyed the raised ground near the treeline, waving a torch back and forth to illuminate the earthen hollows overshadowed by jutting tree roots along the low cliffs.

"What if the den isn't here?" Jace was so exhausted he could barely keep walking, but Grede was strangely alert — completely unlike his usual self.

"Let's make a bet," Grede said. "Five coppers says we find the den within half an hour. I can already smell it…"

"Who's betting with you," Jace said. "Why not just follow the wolves that ran off?"

Grede shone the torch in Jace's face. "If those wolves had run off normally, sure, we could track them to the den. But those wolves have been scared out of their minds by you, and their leaders are dead — what den is there left to speak of? And if you'd been paying attention, every single one of them bolted in a different direction. Which one are you going to follow?"

Jace tried to recall the scene, but his mind was a complete blank — he had no idea which direction any of them had gone.

And while he was busy not remembering, Grede had already pulled far ahead.

Without the sound of footsteps, without the crackle of the torch burning, there was only the river flowing, the wind passing through the trees, and the rustle of trembling leaves.

Jace took a deep breath and looked toward the river. The quiet, for once, seemed to give him a little room to breathe.

What Grede had said earlier sat heavy with him. He'd finally found a path forward, and his closest friend couldn't even approve of it.

But honestly, the dwarf wasn't to blame. As a veteran player who knew Warcraft's history inside and out, Jace should have remembered the ancient grudge between the Wildhammer clan and the forces of Shadow.

But shadow magic… seemed to be the only road to growth that had opened up for him since coming to this world. If he gave it up now, he'd probably spend the rest of his days as nobody more than a commoner scraping by in the dark.

He was about to turn eighteen, after all. If he didn't seize this opportunity, how many paths would even be left to him?

The heroes of Azeroth had mostly revealed their talents and made their mark by eighteen, and here he was — a day laborer knocking around the Elwynn region.

Suddenly, Jace heard a thin cry coming from somewhere.

In the still of the night, the sound sent a chill down his back — and only then did it hit him: that might be the Lakeview Farm child!

"Grede!" Jace shouted. The dwarf called back from far ahead.

"You hear that? A child — there's a child crying!"

"What?! You hear something? I can't hear a thing…"

"Over there! The other bank! Right over there!"

The two of them waded across the tributary together — from stepping through the shallows to paddling, then fighting the current with everything they had to swim across. Once they reached the far side, the crying came through clearer still.

Following the child's cries deeper into the forest, it wasn't long before they reached a low slope hidden by thick brush, where a dug-out earthen hole lay concealed.

Jace didn't give danger a second thought and crawled straight in. Grede had been about to warn him there might still be something inside, but before he could get a word out, Jace had already squeezed his lean frame through.

"We're here to save you, little Forayre!" Jace crawled forward until he reached the very back — only to realize he couldn't see a thing. Pitch black.

The child's voice had gone quiet. But it hit him now: the baby was only a few months old, of course she couldn't talk. The silence probably meant she'd been frightened into quiet.

Grede was shouting his name from outside. Jace called back and kept pushing deeper.

The further in he went, the stronger the stench of rot and the musky reeling of wolves. His stomach was already churning.

He forced himself to keep going, groping around in the dark. He felt what seemed to be the carcasses of small dead animals — not prey that had been killed and brought back, more like dead wolf pups.

This was the den, wasn't it?

Why would wolf pups die in their own den?

Following the faint, intermittent breathing deeper in, his hand finally landed on something wet and cloth-like.

Jace's scalp went numb. The only thing that could be wet in a place like this was — what, if not blood?

He rubbed his fingers together. No sticky pull, like blood.

He touched the wet cloth again and felt upward — and when his fingers met smooth, slippery skin, the baby suddenly cried out. He realized he'd been feeling the girl's lips and teeth. It had been drool.

In that cramped space, the child's sudden wail was piercing, but it didn't bother him in the slightest. He just held her tight.

"You find her?" Grede called from outside.

"Found her! I'm coming out!"

Jace carried the girl back out through the hole. When the dwarf brought the torch up to Jace's face, he found him covered head to toe in blood and wolf fur.

"How is there so much blood?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "The whole place reeks of blood in there. Probably other meat or animal remains — I couldn't see a thing."

"Were there wolves in there? Pups?" Grede asked.

"Seemed like it, but they were all dead," Jace said. "Could've been other prey too. I'm not sure."

Jace looked at the girl and let out a relieved breath. "Think how happy your mother's going to be."

The girl stopped her intermittent sobbing and just stared at Jace's face, reaching out to touch the mud caked on his neck and forehead.

Grede looked at this evil warlock who'd just terrorized a dozen wolves — and himself — half to death with a Fear spell, and couldn't bring himself to be angry.

He glanced at the den again. "Should we drag one of the wolf bodies out and have a look? Don't tell me the kid did that."

Jace looked at the baby girl, who wasn't even half a year old, and figured that seemed unlikely — but his curiosity got the better of him, and he decided to crawl back in and check.

He handed the girl to Grede, crouched down, and crawled back in to grab the carcass nearest the hole and drag it out.

The pup's death had been an ugly one. No obvious external wounds, but its whole body was covered in swollen, oozing pustules. Definitely not starvation, or any normal way of dying.

"Reminds you of those zombies in Duskwood?" Grede said.

It did. Jace thought suddenly of that graveyard and glanced at his bandaged hand. Please don't tell me I've caught corpse poison again. He'd have to go back to the Cathedral of Light to get it checked.

"Why would the pups die of corpse poison?"

"Simple," Grede said. "They probably ate some toxic carcass the adult wolves brought back from the forest to the south. They don't cook their meat like those folks in Raven Hill do. Strange thing is, how come the kid didn't get poisoned?"

"Picky eater. Lucky her," Jace said, wiping the dirt and ash off the girl's face. "But whether she ate any or not, we should tell her parents to have her checked."

"That's true," the dwarf nodded.

Jace said, "Maybe that's also what sent this pack over the edge."

Grede agreed. "Could be. Those wolves might've been infected too, like those sickly wolves down south. There might be other dens around here — a pack that big couldn't be holed up in just one little hole. And this den doesn't look like something wolves dug. More like a bear dug it first and the wolves moved in."

Jace asked, "A pack that fled here from the south?"

"Who knows," Grede said. "Anyway, we found the child, and we've about finished off the wolf pack. Even if those wolves can regroup, they won't come back around here. Let's head back."

By the time they returned to the farm, the sky had turned a deep blue. The moons had vanished without a trace.

The girl had fallen asleep on Jace's back. Grede could barely keep his eyes open.

Spotting the man sprinting toward them from a distance, Jace — in his half-conscious state — momentarily thought another wolf was about to pounce on them and broke out in a full-body flinch.

"The baby, the baby…" The man clutched at the girl's clothes and mumbled incoherently, then wiped away his tears with a trembling hand and looked back toward the dark forest. "You're hurt. Are there wolves chasing you? Should you…"

"No wolves. They're all dead. In the forest to the south, not far — a short walk and you'll be there," Grede said with a laugh, mumbling as he went. "The ones still alive probably won't dare come back. Their grandmother's dead!"

Seemingly drawn by the voices, the woman poked her head out too. When she saw her child being carried back in the man's arms, her legs nearly gave out beneath her.

The girl opened her eyes as if she had no idea what had been happening, looking at her parents — and the couple couldn't string a coherent sentence together anymore, just held on and cried.

Jace sat down on the skirt wall along the house's outer found and realized he couldn't make out what the couple was saying anymore. All he wanted to do was sleep.

He glanced over. Grede was already passed out on the ground at his feet, snoring away.

Watching the dwarf sleep so soundly, Jace couldn't hold out any longer either. He tilted his head and lost consciousness.

## Flags

- **巨湖农场 (Lakeview Farm):** Glossary has this as "Lakeview Farm." The child they're searching for belongs to this farm's family.
- **福莱尔 (Forayre):** Family name of the farm couple (Matt and Emily Forayre). Rendered as "Forayre" — not in glossary, flagged for confirmation. The baby girl is "little Forayre" (小福莱尔).
- **小福莱尔 / 女孩:** The baby is referred to as 小福莱尔 and later 小萝丝 (little Rose). The girl's name appears to be Rose Forayre.
- **尸毒 (corpse poison):** Glossary lists 尸毒 as "Corpse Poison" (Jace's informal term) and 亡灵腐毒 as "Necrotic Poison." Used "corpse poison" here to match Jace's informal register.
- **乌鸦岭 (Raven Hill):** Glossary has this as "Raven Hill." Grede references the people there cooking their meat.
- **"their grandmother's dead":** Grede's colorful way of saying the pack's matriarch/lead wolf is dead. Kept the humor.
- **"skirt wall":** 墙裙 — the low wall running along the base of the house exterior. Rendered as "skirt wall" (a low perimeter wall).
