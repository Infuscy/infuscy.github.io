## Chapter 431: The Mages

Jace said, "Teron Gorefiend's spirit tried to take over my body, but it didn't succeed. But some of his memories lingered in my head, and I've been practicing in private. I learned some dark magic."

"I'm not surprised. But why didn't you go to the Wizard Sanctum for help at the time?" Marin asked.

As soon as he'd asked, the old mage shook his head. "It nearly dragged you into enormous trouble just from using a little shadow power against me. Of course you wouldn't have wanted to explain." He paused, then added gently, "How is Amy? Was she hurt?"

"She only suffered some superficial injuries at the time," Marin said. Then he paused. "I'm truly grateful to you, Jace."

Hearing Marin's words, Jace felt as if he'd been pulled right back onto that battlefield.

Though three weeks had already passed in the outside world, to him it felt like something that had happened before he'd even opened his eyes.

That demon...

Azgoth's fel eye still seemed to be right in front of him, and his laughter still echoed in Jace's ears.

"As long as you're all alright, Master. I just couldn't stand by and watch that demon hurt you!"

Jace took a breath, trying to calm himself as much as possible, then asked the question he'd always wanted to ask but hadn't quite dared to.

"Is Grede Thunderfist still alive?"

"He's very much alive," Marin said, rising to his feet. "Though he's been taking part in operations to mop up the remaining demons, so he might not be back here right away."

Hearing that Grede still had the energy to go out and fight, Jace set his mind at ease.

"Rest well — I'll let you know when he's back," Marin said.

Jace nodded. "Then... do I still have a chance of leaving Nethergarde Keep?"

"Of course, once you've recovered," Marin said.

"I'm not being kept here?" Jace asked in surprise.

"Not exactly," Marin said. "Once you're ready to live under a different identity, once you've healed enough to ensure you won't be snatched up by some gnoll or the like the moment you step outside, you can leave Nethergarde Keep."

Jace nodded. Right now, all he wanted was to make a trip back to Lordaeron.

"Amy wants to see you. Would you like to see her now?" Marin asked.

Jace gritted his teeth and sat up, catching his breath. "Of course I would, Master."

Marin got up to the door, said something to someone outside, then nodded. Amy walked in and saw Jace. She opened her mouth to speak, but tears spilled out first.

She rushed over and hugged Jace, crying without restraint. Old Marin quietly pulled the door shut.

Amy cried for a long enough time to make Jace's own nose sting a little.

"You're not thanking me?" he asked suddenly.

Amy looked up, laughed, and wiped her tears. "Thank you. Thank you, Jace."

"I'm not Jace anymore," Jace said with a show of helplessness. "I'll need a new name."

"What are you thinking of calling yourself?" Amy asked.

"Still thinking about it," Jace scratched his head. "Give me some time. Can't pick something too careless."

Amy looked at Jace softly. "You're the entire Alliance's hero now."

"Aren't you?" Jace said. "If it weren't for the mages' mana crystals, who knows how it would have turned out... I was just afraid that demon was going to smash through the mage tower and finish us all, so I fought him for dear life."

Amy said, "At the time I was channeling power into the crystal. I didn't have much mana left. The mana potions, the mana crystals in the tower were pretty much all used up. A lot of wounded soldiers were helping move supplies."

"Channeling power into that crystal really hurt, Jace. Every time a fire meteor struck it, I could feel it — like something knocking against my forehead, bang, bang... Even now I can still hear it sometimes, and every time I can't help but feel nauseous, get a headache."

"But at that point we were about to win, weren't we? A lot of mages in the tower were saying we were close to victory, that we just had to hold on a little longer."

"We held on a little longer, and then that demon appeared."

"A lot of mages broke down crying — even some of the Dalaran mages. They were shouting, screaming, like everything was over. I think I probably cried too. I seem to remember tears on my face."

"The whole tower shook. I thought that was the end of everything. But the Kirin Tor mages ordered us to keep holding. I knew I had no other choice."

"Then they said that demon had been stopped by a Mountain King. But what puzzled me was... why this dwarf kept yelling your name."

"I thought I was hearing things, Jace, but I quickly realized other people could hear it too. He really was yelling your name — it was like he had to shout it with every swing to pump himself up."

Hearing that, Jace couldn't help but laugh.

"Because I nearly got killed by that demon," he explained. "Grede saved me."

"I figured it was him," Amy nodded. "Then that thing appeared — the meteor you summoned."

"I watched it slam into that demon with my own eyes. Everyone was running. The Kirin Tor archmages ordered every mage to evacuate the tower... At first I thought we were fleeing from those black fel flames. Later I found out that demon was going to detonate his own body."

The smile on Jace's face faded bit by bit.

"Did many people die?" Amy asked. "Those behind the walls were alright. The tower survived the blast because of the crystal's protection. But a lot of people on the battlefield really didn't make it out."

Jace's eyes trembled involuntarily. Amy said, "No one blames you, Jace. And Lady Vereesa Windrunner told everyone it was you who told them to run. Without your warning, the number of people who died in that final explosion would have been far greater than it was now — after all, aside from a few Kirin Tor demon experts, no one knew what he actually was."

"Sigh... am I getting a bit hoarse..."

"No," Jace said. "I want to know what happened, Amy. Thank you for telling me."

Amy nodded and asked, "Does it hurt?"

"What?"

"I mean..." Amy glanced at Jace's arm. "I saw your wounds, Jace... That day when I saw you carried back, you were drenched in blood, and you were coughing up black blood."

"Of course it hurts," Jace widened his eyes. "Let's just say it's a good thing I was unconscious."

Amy carefully touched the bandage on Jace's arm. "I can't even imagine."

Jace sighed. "It's all in the past. The only pity is, we probably won't be able to meet in Dalaran."

Amy said softly, "I'll find a way to clear your name someday."

"I'm dead," Jace said.

"You'll come back someday, Jace Sesso."

With that, she slowly leaned toward Jace's forehead, looked into his eyes, and gently kissed his brow.

Jace was taken aback. Amy immediately stood up. "Father says your injuries are still quite severe and you still need rest — he didn't want me to take up too much of your time. I won't disturb you. I'll come see you again."

"Oh, almost forgot." Amy took Jace's pocket watch out from her robe and set it on the table. "This watch..."

"Is it the same gnome mage who helped last time?"

"Yes. He came here too, though he's been in Stonard the whole time."

"Thank him for me, Amy." Jace picked up the repaired pocket watch.

Amy nodded and left the room.

Jace touched his forehead and let out a long, slow breath.

Everyone was alright. What more could he ask for?
