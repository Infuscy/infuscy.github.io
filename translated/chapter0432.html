Chapter 432: Dawn in the Blasted Lands

The next morning, Jace tried getting up and putting on the silk jacket from the wardrobe before heading outside.

The Kirin Tor guard in the corridor looked like he wanted to say something but held back. Jace said, "I'm not leaving Nethergarde Keep. I just… want to find a friend."

The guard nodded and stepped aside. "Go all the way down this stairs and you'll be outside."

"Thank you, sir."

Jace nodded and, supporting himself along the wall, made his way all the way down. He pushed open the wooden door at the bottom and stepped out to find he was at the very top of the tallest tower in Nethergarde Keep.

And the sight before him was one he hardly recognized.

Nethergarde Keep was cold and quiet. Far fewer people than before. The mage's tower stood solitary and alone, scaffolding erected all over it, looking far more weathered and worn under the sun than it had before. Of course, the arcane crystal that had been on top was gone too.

The thick clouds in the sky had cleared away. The Blasted Lands stretched endlessly in every direction — wasteland and dead forest as far as the eye could see. And the Dark Portal was no longer visible.

Right… at that distance it shouldn't have been visible in the first place. What Jace had always been able to see was that curtain of green, not the gate's frame.

Marin hadn't said where Grede could usually be found, but Jace automatically headed in the direction of the tavern. In the middle of the square, a group of craftsmen had set up scaffolding and were carving something. Jace glanced at the stone block in the middle and couldn't make out what it was supposed to be.

Coming to the entrance of the tavern, Jace tried taking a deep breath and then walked in. The main hall was completely empty. Not a single person inside.

He remembered the last time he'd been here — packed so tight you couldn't get in or out, couldn't even buy a drink. Now it all vacant at once. He even felt a little hollow inside.

He knew this should be a good thing. Who'd want a place like Nethergarde Keep to need to be packed with people?

Grede probably wasn't back yet, but Jace no longer had the strength to go back. He found a nearby seat and sat down to catch his breath. The walk alone had made his whole body ache.

"Are you a soldier who took part in the fighting?"

At some point, a head popped up from behind the bar and asked.

Jace nodded. The man stood up. "Want something to eat? We don't have much left, but cheese and bread are in good supply, plus some dried meat."

"I'd like some milk — cow's or goat's, either's fine. And do you have any salt crackers?"

"Yes." The man nodded and went into the back room. Before long he came out with a glass of milk and a plate of crackers, setting them on the table.

After drinking a sip of milk, Jace realized he didn't have a single copper on him.

"Boss… I didn't bring any money. Could I…"

"This is Nethergarde Keep, young man," the boss said with a laugh. "This food was always meant for you lot. If you don't eat it, it'll just sit on the shelf. Especially the milk — if you don't drink it, it goes to waste. Look how thin you are. You should be eating more."

"Thank you." Jace nodded.

"We should be thanking you, sir," the boss said. "Fighting the monsters that crawled out of the Dark Portal — I can't even imagine it. Just seeing those corpses gives me nightmares."

"Let me know if you need anything else, sir."

With that he ducked back into the inner room. Jace watched his back, took a sip of milk, and let out a long, deep breath.

After eating his fill and coming out of the tavern, he saw a familiar silhouette standing at the edge of the square, staring at the craftsmen and their carving, lost in thought.

Jace shuffled toward her bit by bit. The figure glanced over from a distance and then stopped watching the craftsmen.

"Jace…" the elf said softly.

Jace nodded and came to her side. "They took your sword, Vereesa."

"It's your sword," Vereesa said. "And they didn't take it — they put it into storage. They were worried the magical energy of those weapons would affect your recovery, especially… that staff of Teron Gorefiend's."

"So they'll give it back?" Jace asked.

"You haven't asked for it?" Vereesa asked.

"Who would I ask?"

"I can ask for you, if you need it right now."

"Let's wait a while… in my condition, I can barely walk on my own. And I'm supposed to lug around two big items on top of that?"

Vereesa smiled and looked at the statue in the middle of the square. "What do you think of that?"

"What is that thing?" Jace glanced at it. "I can't even make it out. Who is it supposed to be?"

"That's you, Jace Sesso," Vereesa said.

Jace stared blankly at the statue, but he really couldn't see anything it had in common with him.

"Why are both hands raised?"

It looked like it was surrendering to the Dark Portal. Of course, Jace didn't say that last part out loud.

"A sword and a staff were supposed to be placed in them," Vereesa explained. "The idea is that you used two magic implements to unleash a powerful fire meteor that killed the pit lord Azgoth — well, a pure fire meteor, not the thing you actually summoned."

She looked over and asked, "So, that night, Teron Gorefiend's soul did leave some mark on you after all, didn't it?"

Jace nodded. "Sorry. I didn't tell you everything."

"You didn't become him. That's enough for me," Vereesa said. "I trust my own eyes. I remember what you looked like at the foot of the mage's tower."

"I still haven't thanked you," Jace said. "You saved my life there. If you hadn't stepped in, those demons would have hacked me to pieces."

Vereesa lowered her head. "That was so long ago. Killing a few demon grunts — it's nothing at all."

"To me it feels like yesterday," Jace said.

The two of them fell silent for a while. Vereesa said, "Besides this statue, the Kirin Tor has another gift for you — it may not have arrived yet. For safety, that item can't be sent here by teleportation magic. You know how the demons have made a mess of the ley lines here."

"What is it?" Jace asked.

"Are you sure you want me to tell you, rather than waiting and seeing for yourself?"

"I'm curious…"

"Well, I don't know the exact details either," Vereesa said. "But my sister said it should be an ancient artifact of the Order of Tirisfal. Though the Order is no longer around, it left behind many precious things. Members of the Order who fought demons in the past used it to resist the corruption of shadow power and the mental interference of demons."

Jace smiled. "At least this shows the Kirin Tor don't think of me as a monster?"

Vereesa said, "They're grateful to you, if anything."

"Then I'll go back and wait," Jace said, bending down to rub his legs. "See you, Vereesa. My body can't handle standing for too long right now."

As he said it, Vereesa moved closer and gently hugged him, apparently careful not to squeeze too hard for fear of hurting him.

"I'm happy you're getting better, Jace."
