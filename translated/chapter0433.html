Chapter 433: Still a Teacher

Though he'd said he didn't want Vereesa to help him retrieve those two items, after sleeping at home he went, and by evening Jace was honestly trotting off to Marin to ask about his things.

By night, Nethergarde Keep came alive again. Most of the soldiers stationed here had finished a day of patrols or training and returned to their barracks, or hurried off to the tavern. The square was full of the sound of people talking.

But Jace could feel a gloom hanging over the place all the while. After all, nearly a month had passed. The celebrations, the cheers, the relief — all of that had come and gone.

What lingered over Nethergarde Keep now was nothing but grief for fallen comrades and friends, and longing for the Expedition stranded in another world.

Yet to find them, they would have to reconnect Draenor and Azeroth — which meant another Dark Portal, which meant possibly facing orcs, demons, or other, worse threats all over again.

The fel disaster Stormwind had endured was still fresh in everyone's minds. If even faraway Stormwind had been in that state, places like Goldshire, Duskwood, and Lakeshire would have been even worse. The Expedition's final efforts would have been completely in vain.

This unresolvable contradiction only deepened the complicated emotions within Nethergarde Keep.

According to what the Kirin Tor guard had said, the mage's tower had been contaminated by the pit lord's demon blood. Though it had been purified several times, as a precaution all the mages had been moved to a barracks closer to the elves' side. Marin was staying in a small room on the third floor.

For Jace, just walking from this high slope down to the bottom and then climbing up another slope had already cost him half his life.

Arriving at the barracks flying the Kirin Tor banner, Jace barely made it up to the third floor and knocked on Marin's door.

Marin opened the door and, seeing Jace, asked in surprise, "How did you get here by yourself? If you wanted to find me, you could have said something to the man watching over you."

"I still wanted to get some exercise, Master," Jace said. "You know I always ran to your place for work back in Stormwind. I'm used to it."

Marin nodded helplessly and said softly, "Come in then, Mr. Sesso."

Jace nodded and shuffled into the room, pointing to a small stool. "May I sit?"

"By all means." Marin leaned against the side cabinet as he spoke.

Jace sat down, caught his breath, and said, "Master, I've come to get my things back."

"You're leaving Nethergarde Keep?" Marin asked.

"No," Jace said. "Clearly I'm in no shape to leave either. But having them in my hands would put my mind at ease."

Marin said, "Then wait here."

With that he left the room. Jace was so tired he half-closed his eyes, thinking back to what Vereesa had told him that morning, and actually dozed off. In the end it was the sound of the door opening that woke him.

It felt to him like Marin had walked out the door and walked right back in.

Fortunately, he already looked half-dead, so bleary-eyed and drowsy for a moment didn't seem the least bit strange to Marin.

The old mage was carrying a staff wrapped in beautiful rune silk, while Azuresong was housed in a gold-trimmed green elven scabbard. Jace figured they must have thrown away his old filthy broken scabbard straight away.

"These two items are still quite recognizable," Marin said. "Be careful not to be too conspicuous."

"I can't even make it clear who I am inside Nethergarde Keep?" Jace asked.

Marin looked at him for a moment, then said slowly, "I know this outcome might make you uncomfortable, especially given your age. I wasn't young once either. I also yearned to become a hero, even dreamed of defeating unimaginably terrible enemies like the legendary Guardians of Tirisfal and basking in the adoration of countless people. So I imagine I can understand a little of what you're feeling right now."

"But right now, the vast majority of people in Nethergarde Keep believe we ultimately couldn't save you. So they'll gradually forget what magic you used to kill Azgoth, and only remember that you are the hero who saved Nethergarde Keep… because of your actions, they were able to return home to their families and live out their lives in peace."

The old mage sighed and continued, "But if they learn you're alive, their fear of you will grow stronger and stronger, gradually overwhelming their gratitude and respect for you. Many years from now, they might even forget how you became a hero in the first place, but they'll always remember the danger you could bring them."

"You can't blame them. I'll be frank with you — even I can't be completely at ease around you. After all, that man's betrayal is still fresh in my mind. I'm talking about Medivh."

At this point, Marin seemed unable to speak the name calmly, as if uttering it would trigger some curse.

He went on, "Of course, I'm not saying you'll commit the same litany of unforgivable crimes Medivh did. At the very least, you're far more normal than he was, up to this point."

Jace nodded. He couldn't make any promises either — any promise right now would feel hollow.

"I understand all this, Master!"

"Maybe I'm worrying too much," Marin went on. "In addition, the Kirin Tor will be paying closer attention to your movements from now on, Mr. Sesso. From what I know, from here on out they may link any unusual shadow disturbances in the Kingdom of Stormwind or Lordaeron to you first. So life is going to get a lot more troublesome."

"Of course, that's assuming you're innocent. If you really intend to use your magic to do something inexplicable, then it won't count as trouble — it'll simply be 'what you deserve.'"

With that, Marin handed the staff and sword to Jace. Jace took them, hung the sword at his waist, and held the staff in his arms. Suddenly he seemed to have a bit more spirit again.

"Teacher, thank you!"

"Clearly, as a teacher, I haven't taught you much that's useful," Marin said with a hint of humor.

"Absolutely not," Jace looked at him. "I was never much good with arcane magic, Master. I'm already grateful you taught me so many incantations. And you don't know how many times I almost died and was saved by the Mage Armor and Scorch incantations you taught me."

"Speaking of which, can I keep learning more magic from you in the future?"

"Come back to the Wizard Sanctum with your new name, Mr. Sesso," Marin said. "I'll have Archmaster Andormas get you a new ring, and then we can talk about new incantations."

Jace nodded. "I understand, Master."

"Take care of yourself then, Mr. Sesso," Marin said.
