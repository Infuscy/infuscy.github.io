Chapter 434: A New Name

After bidding farewell to Marin, Jace leaned on his staff and headed out the door.

The night was later now. Though it was probably already August — the hottest time of year back in Stormwind — the Blasted Lands was swept by chilling winds.

Of course it wasn't as cold as a Lordaeron winter, but it was still a bit nippy given the clothes Jace was wearing right now.

Still, cold was a damn sight better than the sweltering heat the fel infestation had brought before.

Jace tried walking step by step toward his quarters on the other high ground, thinking all the while about what name he should give himself.

Lost in thought, he suddenly stepped on what must have been a rock or a pothole. His leg gave way, his arm strained and a bolt of pain shot through it, and he couldn't catch himself. He tumbled to the ground and his staff went rolling away with a rattle.

He cursed under his breath, patted the dust off his face, and opened his eyes to look at his staff — just in time to see a dwarf pick it up.

Jace looked up at the dwarf. In the moonlight his features were hard to make out.

"Strange. Slept a few days and you don't recognize me anymore, lad," the dwarf said.

Jace couldn't help but laugh. He propped himself up and sat on the nearby earthen slope. "Give that thing back!"

Grede grinned and handed the staff over, then sat down beside him, leaning against the rock behind him. "And they let you out like this?"

Jace cleared his throat. "Maybe they figure a mage who can blow up a demon lord with a single spell probably isn't going to run into too much trouble inside Nethergarde Keep."

"You bastard…" the dwarf laughed.

He sighed and looked up at the moon. "Honestly, at the start I really thought you were going to die. I'm not like Vereesa Windrunner, watching over you every single day. I couldn't do it. Those few days, it was hard for me to stay that optimistic. If I didn't go out and kill a few demons, I felt like I wouldn't make it through, Jace."

"I thought you used to be pretty optimistic," Jace said. "Every time it was basically a suicide mission, you always acted like it was nothing."

"This time was different," Grede said. "I don't know why, but I had this gut feeling I wasn't strong enough to protect my own brother."

"How strong do you want to be?" Jace said, giving him a sidelong look. "Do you have any idea what kind of monster you were fighting?"

"They didn't go into much detail," Grede said. "I just know he was insanely strong. I've never seen a monster that powerful."

Jace said, "Probably because they themselves didn't know exactly what that thing was. If the elven records are right, only one pit lord has ever come into this world since it was created, Grede — and this one was the second. Sending him could only mean the demons were dead serious about holding the Dark Portal."

"Think about it — you went head-to-head with a commander the Burning Legion was too stingy to casually send onto the battlefield. And you still think you're not strong enough."

"But clearly I couldn't beat him at all." Grede rolled up his sleeve, revealing the gruesome scars on his arm. "Look. This is what happened after I took that blow. He split my skin open without any effort, just a casual swing. After that, I knew that if I failed to dodge his attack even once, I'd immediately turn into a pile of useless rubble."

Jace stared at the scars on his arm, wanting to look away but unable to, until Grede pulled his sleeve back down.

"Don't put too much pressure on yourself," Grede said with a smile. "After all, I know you were in a hurry to blow up that demon with that spell because you were afraid I'd die. I understand the sentiment, brother. I'll remember this."

Jace coughed. "I'm already starting to regret it."

Hearing that, Grede laughed even harder, and Jace couldn't hold back either.

The two of them laughed for a while, until Grede sighed and said, "It was pretty miserable, but it's been a long time since I fought like that. A real opponent ought to be like that too, even if he was a bit uglier than I imagined. By the way, did you actually get a look at him?"

"Of course I did, I'm not blind," Jace said. "And I heard a lot of people really think you're the Mountain King!"

The dwarf said, "They'd better not think that right now. If it had been Muradin Bronzebeard standing in front of that demon back then, he would have performed God-knows-how-many times better than me. So I have to work my way up to that level. I don't want to be a Mountain King who's worse than everyone else."

"There's plenty of time. No need to rush," Jace said. "I'll definitely help you, Grede."

"You focus on recovering first," Grede said, and clapped Jace on the shoulder.

"Gently, you bastard…" Jace coughed twice.

"Sorry…" the dwarf said quickly. "I didn't think you were this weak. Hah, my apologies."

Jace glared at him. "Now help me think of a name, Grede. I can't go around telling people I'm Jace Sesso anymore!"

"Hmm, true enough. After all, you're already dead." The dwarf stroked his beard. "Let me give you a Dwarvish name — Kaeldgael. How about that?"

"What does it mean?"

"Trust," Grede said. "I think you're a thoroughly trustworthy human, and every other dwarf would think so too."

Jace's expression slowly shifted from curiosity to speechlessness.

"What's the name of the archmage in the Sons of Lothar?"

"Khadgar," Grede answered readily.

"Ah… damn it, so that's a Bronzebeard accent," he said, suddenly catching on. "The point is, there are already too many dwarves and humans in this world named Kaedgar, Kaeldgael, Khadgar, and Khardhag. What's the harm in sharing a name…"

"No!" Jace said. "Think of another one!"

Grede lowered his head and started counting on his fingers, muttering to himself. After a while he said, "Yurk, then?"

"What does it mean?" Jace asked.

Grede explained, "It's a word in Dwarvish for a color between purple and pink. The Dark Iron dwarves used to use it to describe flames produced by magic. What do you think? Like arcane and shadow — I think it's a pretty good fit, very suited to a little bastard who likes burning things."

Jace nodded. "It's not complicated, that's for sure. So how many dwarves among your kind have that name?"

"Probably not many among the Wildhammer dwarves. I don't know about Ironforge," Grede explained. "There might be some in Shadowforge City, I suppose — those dark little bastards are more interested in fire and arcane and all that. But what you need is a name that's unremarkable and ordinary, right?"

Jace said, "Yurk it is, then. Just doesn't sound like the name of a good person."

"As long as you're a good person, this name will grow into a good person's name, Jace — ah, no, Mr. Yurk," Grede said, unable to hold back a snicker.

"You just keep calling me whatever you've been calling me," Jace reminded him.
