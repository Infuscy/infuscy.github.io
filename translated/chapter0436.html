Chapter 436: The Artifact's Effect

Jace honestly didn't have much of an impression of the Crystal of Flowing Ice.

He seemed to remember it being one of the treasures the Dragonqueen rewarded to players after they stopped the blue dragon king's ambition to destroy the world.

Since he couldn't recall it clearly, it probably hadn't been all that important in the game.

But he hadn't expected this thing to have such a pedigree.

An artifact that Aegwynn herself had used?

A decade and a half down the line, in another timeline, the blue dragon king Malygos — driven mad — intended to solve the problem of arcane abuse once and for all by stripping every spellcaster in this world of their access to the ley lines.

And his plan was to use the blue dragons' magical artifact, the Focusing Iris, to gather the world's entire ley-line network to himself.

That act would have torn the world apart and caused all of Azeroth to collapse. So it was quite likely that the Kirin Tor, to thank the players for saving Azeroth from Malygos, had brought out this treasure as part of the reward.

Just holding it, Jace could already feel the magical energy within continuously replenishing his spirit and soothing the damage his body had taken from overusing Shadow power.

He'd set the thing on his pillow that afternoon and taken a nap, and by the time he woke up that evening, he could clearly feel his mana was brimming — nothing like the past few days, when he'd felt like his energy had never quite topped off.

Sure, his whole body still ached, and the wounds under his bandages hadn't improved noticeably, but that exhausted, drained feeling was gone.

This thing was so effective — why hadn't anyone used it in the game?

Then again, nobody was racing for DPS here. In the game, gear that didn't improve your performance basically didn't interest anyone.

But this place was a bit different.

As long as Jace kept it on him, would he never run short of energy?

No wonder the great archmages were all so powerful — wouldn't you be, carrying a relic of this caliber around all the time?

When he went out that evening, even the guard watching over him thought he was recovering well. Jace was pretty sure that wasn't his imagination.

He came down the spiral stairs and stepped outside, looking out over Nethergarde Keep on a fairly lively night. His vision suddenly felt completely different from the past few days.

Whether or not this gem could resist Shadow — or even disrupt demonic mental interference — at the very least, it was already a huge help.

Last night, when that staff had fallen to the ground, he hadn't even had the strength to drag it back to himself. It was like his mind and his magic had completely disconnected.

Or rather, his body, ravaged by excessive Shadow damage at that point, had actually started resisting the intrusion of Shadow power.

But now, he felt like he'd regained a good chunk of his condition.

Last night he'd arranged to meet Grede at the tavern for dinner. He made his way there, walked in, and glanced around — still quite a few people inside.

Grede was sitting by the window, and Vereesa was right there beside him.

When Grede saw Jace, he immediately stood up and waved.

"You two are both here," Jace said, sitting down across from them.

"I ran into her and told her," Grede said. "Might as well all get together."

Vereesa beside him didn't look too happy. Jace looked at her and asked, "What's wrong?"

"This elf thinks drinking is bad for your recovery," Grede said with a laugh. "Isn't that a joke? How could drinking possibly be bad for you, right? Our dwarven ancestors—"

"Stop." Jace immediately held up a hand. "Don't start with that, I can't take it anymore."

"Don't listen to him, Jace," Vereesa warned.

"Relax, I'm not drinking," Jace said. "I'm not him."

"Then how boring — the only one drinking at this whole table is me?" Grede scratched his neck.

"Then I'll drink with you, dwarf," Vereesa said, fixing him with a stare.

"You little white-furred rabbit, how do you talk so rough?" Grede slapped the table. "Then today I'll teach you a lesson and show you the ancient traditions of Thunderfist Hall. Barkeep! Four of the strongest drinks you've got — we'll see if I don't drink this little girl half to death!!!"

Vereesa rapped the table with her knuckles, expressionless. "I'd like some of the cheese crumble biscuits from here."

"Right — biscuits, bread, cheese!" Grede looked at Jace. "What do you want?"

"Meat," Jace said. "I want smoked jerky."

"Jerky!" Grede waved the barmaid over, then said, "I'm feeling your energy, lad — you're doing great. So I'm telling you, your body's solid. How'd you get so messed up from one hit last night?"

"Because today I got my hands on something interesting. Let me show you two." Jace said it mysteriously, reaching into his coat as he spoke.

"Did the Kirin Tor package arrive?" Vereesa asked, curious.

"Yes." Jace pinched the water-blue gem and set it on the table, immediately bringing a faint cool breeze to the three of them.

"This thing'd probably work great dropped in a drink," Grede commented immediately.

Vereesa shot him a look. "That's a treasure of the Order of Tirisfal, and you want to chill your ale with it?"

"Fine, fine, a treasure, the Order of Tirisfal." Grede propped his chin on his hand, glancing at Jace, then at Vereesa. "Didn't that council go quiet a long time ago?"

"But the artifacts are still around." Vereesa looked at Jace. "May I hold it?"

"Of course. I brought it here to show you." Jace said.

Vereesa nodded and carefully cupped the gem in her palm.

She stared at it for a while, and Jace watched the arcane glow in her eyes slowly radiate outward — then the icy light the gem emitted seemed to be suppressed, as if pushed down.

"Oh, goodness." She suddenly shook her head as if waking up. "I almost activated it… damn. Sorry, Jace. I couldn't quite help myself. I've been away from the Sunwell for far too long."

"What do you mean?" Grede asked. "Activate? Activate what?"

So this thing could actually be used?

Jace was stunned for a moment. He hadn't expected that accessories here could be activated too… Why hadn't that mage mentioned this to him earlier?

Or maybe this was just so normal that he couldn't be bothered to bring it up…

Vereesa looked at Grede. "There are far too many treasures that can help restore mana. Even a vial of Sunwell water carried on your person can have that kind of power. If it only helped the wearer recover mana, it wouldn't count as a particularly precious treasure."

Jace nodded, and Vereesa continued, "Besides, trying to activate the power stored in an artifact is a technique every mage should learn, isn't it?"

"But you're not a mage, elf," Grede said.

"I am a mage," Jace said. "But I don't know how."

Vereesa looked at him. "How would I know…"

Jace sighed. "Blame myself…"
