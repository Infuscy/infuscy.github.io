## Chapter 437: Activating the Power

"You keep it, Jace." Vereesa stared at the gem. "When I hold it in my hand, my mind just itches."

"You elves really do have your problems," Grede said.

The elf shot Grede a glare. Jace asked, "So how do I trigger it?"

Vereesa said, "Honestly, I can't say for certain. You could try drawing mana from within it…"

"Draw mana from within it?" Jace repeated her words one at a time, then said, "You think that's something a human like us could manage?"

"I imagine it's about creating an arcane resonance inside, and then letting it release its power onto its wearer," Vereesa tried to explain. "But certainly not the aggressive kind — you wouldn't want to destroy it, right?"

Jace thought for a moment. "Something like casting Arcane Intellect?"

"I… wouldn't say for sure either," Vereesa said, looking at the gem.

Jace instinctively felt this would be difficult. He frowned. After all, casting other arcane spells all had incantations to help. And if not incantations, then there were casting gestures, casting materials, and a whole series of aids to help him connect to the ley lines. Without them, he had absolutely nothing to do with arcane energy.

"So is there some kind of incantation for activating an artifact like this?" he asked, glancing at the elf.

"I'm not a mage either. How would I know all that?" Vereesa said helplessly. "Probably… you could try touching it, feeling the mana within, and seeing if you can resonate with it. It's like… it's like…"

At this point Vereesa was already running out of words. She thought for a while before finally saying, "It's like dancing with the mana inside."

"I can't dance," Jace said.

Grede slapped his own face.

Vereesa looked at Jace and sighed, then suddenly snapped her fingers. "It's like riding a chocobo — ah, riding a horse! You know how to ride a horse, don't you, Jace."

"Isn't that a bit of a stretch? Still, let me try."

Jace cupped the gem in his hands, closed his eyes, and tried to feel the mana within.

He had to admit, the afternoon he'd spent with the Crystal of Flowing Ice had given him quite a feel for it. The mana it helped restore also seemed to resonate with the gem more easily.

Riding a horse… what did riding a horse feel like?

Feeling the rise and fall of a horse's back, Arna's pace and temperament…

Slowly, Jace seemed to get a bit of a feel for it. The arcane energy spinning within was like the mare he was trying to control — sometimes it moved with his intent, and sometimes it didn't quite obey… So, how was he supposed to "activate" it?

Jace tried to redirect the flow of mana within. As his consciousness grew more and more in harmony with the mana, more and more coordinated, he actually began to experience a hint of what she called "dancing."

"This is it, Jace."

Jace heard Vereesa's voice and suddenly tightened his consciousness. Immediately, he felt a warm wave of magical energy wrap around his palm and spread across his whole body. This didn't feel like the arcane at all — it was far more like the Holy Light healing the priests in the Cathedral had tried to use on him!

What a miraculous thing…

Jace opened his eyes and watched the gem radiate a golden glow right in his palm. This was indeed golden arcane energy, not Holy Light. Both Vereesa and Grede stared in shock.

When the golden light faded, the gem had dimmed a little, but Jace felt a fullness he hadn't experienced in a long time.

The places on his body that had still been swollen and aching, including the wounds under the bandages that couldn't be removed yet — they all seemed far less painful! Including those corrosive scars left by overusing Shadow power…

So this gem was relying on this very force to counter Shadow damage?

Jace finally understood. He looked at Vereesa and asked, "This thing can't be one-time use, can it?"

"Impossible." Vereesa propped her chin on her hand and eyed the gem. "If this really is a charm Aegwynn wore, then it's existed for at least eight hundred years. You're telling me that in eight hundred years, not a single person has ever tried to activate its power?"

"Then when would it be usable next?" Jace asked, looking at the gem that had dimmed somewhat. "I feel like its power is much weaker than before."

"I don't know," Vereesa said. "Just in this short while… I can't sense how quickly it recovers its power. Maybe tomorrow, maybe the day after. Let me look at it again then — maybe I can work out a rough timeframe."

"Definitely an arcane race," Jace marveled.

Vereesa smiled. "If I really had that much arcane talent, maybe Alleria would have sent me to Falarion to study magic back then."

As she said this, her expression suddenly dimmed, and the smile faded with it.

The three of them fell silent for a while. Just then, the barmaid came over and set down four drinks one by one. The dwarf sniffed his glass and said, "Hey, miss, what is this?"

"You can call it Nethergarde Keep Bitter," the barmaid said with a smile. "The stuff for warriors facing demons and orcs certainly isn't ordinary either."

Grede nodded. "Haven't tried it before."

"Probably because you were arriving late those days," the barmaid replied.

Grede looked across at Vereesa. "This smells like a fine drink — perfect for teaching you a lesson, little elf."

The elf eyed Grede. "Then let's go."

By now Jace glanced at the two of them, then at the drinks, then at the barmaid carrying plates of food over. He casually grabbed a piece of broken jerky from one and popped it in his mouth to test the flavor.

Grede grinned sideways through his beard, staring at Vereesa. "Might as well have never seen the world."

With that, he downed a whole mug in one go, coughed, and set the empty mug down. "Drink!"

Vereesa gritted her teeth, slammed the entire mug down in one go, then smashed the mug on the table and started frantically grabbing everything on the table to eat.

Jace immediately reached in and snatched a biscuit, popping it in his mouth. With this kind of energy, if your hands were slow you'd miss out.

Grede ate a piece of bread, then downed half a mug in one go and waved the barmaid over. "Two more!"

Vereesa stared fixedly at the dwarf, picked up the other mug and downed it in one go. Grede looked at her, stunned, immediately finished his remaining half mug, belched, took the mug the barmaid handed him, and pushed it across to the elf whose hands and mouth never stopped.

Jace looked at Vereesa, then at Grede. The latter picked up his mug and gulped down the whole thing — this time he was clearly struggling. He set the mug down and took a deep breath. But this time Vereesa didn't follow. She just propped herself on the table and forced her eyes open, staring at Grede.

A moment later she looked back at Jace. "Don't you drink what's left of mine either."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

The words had barely left his mouth when Vereesa face-planted onto the table with a thud.

Grede looked at her and sneered. "See? This elf's no match. Come on, Jace, I'll… I'll walk you back to the canal!"
