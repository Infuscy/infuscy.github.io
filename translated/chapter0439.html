Chapter 439: Returning from Nethergarde

It wasn't just Vereesa looking forward to having a chance to eat at Thelsamar again someday — Jace felt the same way.

By then, once he'd recovered, maybe he could even compete with those two idiots to see who could really hold their liquor…

I wonder if there's a status-boosting spell in Shadow magic that increases alcohol tolerance.

Everyone had their own wishes. Grede wanted to grow stronger, advancing toward his goal of becoming a "true Mountain King." Vereesa hoped to become a respected Farstrider ranger like her sister — maybe even Silvermoon's Ranger-General one day… or at least a Ranger-Lord like Lor'themar Theron.

Of course, Jace hoped he could always be around those two, whether it was chatting, drinking, eating, or going on adventures together.

Even facing life and death… didn't seem so terrifying that way.

But he wanted the two of them to achieve their goals sooner, just like he had his own goals.

Buying a house.

Buying a house soon.

Only now, he couldn't buy a house under the name Jace Sesso anymore.

But since it was for his parents, he could just register it under Mom and Dad's names. He himself could act as a "purchasing agent" and deal with the Stormwind registry office.

The next morning, after saying goodbye to Amy, Master Marin, and Grede, Jace set out on his journey back to Stormwind.

Riding with the supply wagons heading to Nethergarde Keep, cutting through the Black Swamp into southern Elwynn and reaching the town of Duskwood took upwards of a week.

While they were in the swamp, Jace had hoped that with the Dark Portal closed, the shadow hanging over all of Duskwood might disperse a little — just like the Blasted Lands, suppressed by the darkness.

But when it came to Deadwind Pass, he realized that Duskwood's shadow really had nothing to do with the Dark Portal at all.

Deadwind Pass was a valley that had to be crossed when entering the Elwynn region from the swamp — the unremarkable little path among the hundreds of miles of mountains stretching southward that Jace couldn't make out clearly when he looked south from the Redridge Mountains.

Back at the orcish-language meeting in Dalaran, Jace had lied and said he'd found that orc warlock in Deadwind Pass, and that he'd gotten lost here and suspected his mind had been disturbed by Karazhan's dark power. None of the mages present had directly questioned it — they'd believed him quickly.

At the time, Jace had felt his story was quite convincing.

But only after actually arriving here did he understand why those mages had believed him without hesitation.

Looking south from this path into the lowlands, dead black forests covered everything. Through the haze, there were even the shadows of town ruins.

This valley was rugged and complex. Go deeper in and it was either dense forest or deep hollows, and fog was everywhere. Even without Karazhan's magical interference, getting lost here would be perfectly normal.

If this place weren't blocked by mountains, and Medivh's Tower leaked its dark energy without any obstruction, Elwynn Forest wouldn't be much better off than here — Stormwind itself might have been affected.

Coming out of the Deadwind Pass valley, passing through a slope of dead, withered wasteland all the way down, you'd soon see an abandoned Stormwind Kingdom watchtower, still hanging a lion banner that had been corroded by Shadow energy to a grayish hue. This one had probably been hung there back in King Llane's era.

When they spotted the watchtower, the caravan leader reminded everyone that the town of Duskwood was almost there. Jace tried to stand up on the wagon and look out toward the lowlands in the distance — he could indeed see what seemed like the edge of a pitch-black forest, with a few flickering lights.

They arrived in Duskwood right at dusk. Jace only knew the time thanks to his pocket watch. He familiarly made his way to the inn, ordered some food and drink from the owner, and booked a room for the night — but the owner wouldn't take his money no matter what.

Jace initially thought he'd been inexplicably recognized again, since he'd rested at this inn a few times before and he remembered the owner. But after asking out of curiosity, he learned it was because of the clothes he was wearing.

That Dalaran battle-mage robe had, unsurprisingly, been burned to nothing by his own Shadow magic. It had only lasted a few days, nearly breaking the record set by that elven mage robe he'd worn out with Vereesa last time for shortest survival.

Now, aside from the everyday clothes he'd prepared for himself back in that little room, everything he wore and carried had been scrounged from the quartermaster's store — unwanted, worn-out military uniforms that were easily recognizable as belonging to a veteran back from Nethergarde Keep.

In the owner's own words, every veteran carried a "reassuring stink of demons" on them.

That being the case, Jace had no choice but to accept.

After eating his fill, that evening Jace sat in his inn room and looked at the Crystal of Flowing Ice. By now, its power seemed to have recovered quite a bit.

The day before leaving Nethergarde Keep, Vereesa had mentioned that this thing might take about two weeks to recover.

Judging by the energy contained in the gem at this point, her estimate had been pretty accurate.

Of course, Jace hadn't expected this thing to activate every couple of minutes like an accessory in the game. Even a two-week cooldown was enough for emergencies.

Time to test its mana recovery capability.

Jace hadn't cast any spells or expended any mana during this period, so naturally he couldn't feel the mana recovery rate it provided. Still, the cool, refreshing sensation it constantly gave off was quite pleasant in the summer heat.

I wonder if in winter he'd need to wrap it in a special pouch that blocked magical effects to carry it on his person.

No time like the present. Jace tried casting a Mage Armor with its arcane glow dimmed — the safest spell he could cast right now that he could clearly feel the mana consumption from.

After casting it, he felt he'd expended about a third of his energy. Compared to back when he'd just learned Mage Armor and would pass out before finishing the cast, things were much better now.

Part of it was that he was casting more skillfully, and of course part of it was that as he used magic more and more, his own energy ceiling was continuously rising.

I wonder if that vial of Sunwell water had given him an extra boost?

According to Marin, even after drinking that vial he hadn't been out of mortal danger. Presumably most of the water's energy had gone toward easing his condition and neutralizing the Shadow corrosion.

Holding the gem in his hand, he felt the magical aura it released seep into his body, slowly replenishing his lost mana bit by bit… Though he could feel the recovery, it still fell far short of just drinking one of Ursula's mana potions directly.

But even so, the significance was huge enough. If Jace ever faced a full-on demon legion assault again, as long as he wore this magical crystal, he wouldn't end up getting drier and more exhausted toward the end like he had before.

As for its effect against demonic mental disruption, he could test that by wearing it to find Molofiel once he got back. For tonight, he'd just go to sleep.
