Chapter 44: The Young Mother

The next day, Jace was jolted awake by the chattering of birds. He sat up and found himself lying on an absurdly large bed.

Of course, "absurdly large" was only by the standards of every bed he'd slept in before.

He glanced out the window. They were at Lakeview Farm. He noticed the cloth strips on his wounds had been removed and carefully re-bandaged, with no blood seeping through.

Thinking back to last night, he'd been bitten up pretty badly.

He was about to get out of bed when a wave of soreness hit his whole body. His ankles and wrists ached dully, and his footing gave out — he stumbled and crashed onto the table at his side, barely catching himself on its edge to keep from hitting the floor entirely.

"Grede?" he called. No one answered.

Judging by the direction of the sunlight, it was just past noon.

He craned his neck and peered out the window. In the farmyard, a woman sat on the steps with the sleeping little girl in her arms. Over by the granary, a few young people were bustling about doing who-knows-what, and the old woman stood not far off, watching them motionless.

The girl seemed to have come to no harm. Last night, Jace and Grede hadn't had the energy to examine her properly.

After watching for a while, the girl's mother lifted her head, as if she'd noticed Jace peering out from the window.

She hurried inside and carried the girl into another room. When she came back out, she caught Jace trying to haul himself upright using the table.

"Sir!" The girl's mother steadied him. "You'd better not get up just yet. I'm Emily Forayre — the wife of the man you met yesterday. You saved our daughter."

"I remember you," Jace said. His head hurt a little. The woman quickly helped him back onto the bed.

He looked around. His pole and his bag were both on the cabinet under the window, and only then did he finally relax.

"Where's the dwarf?" Jace asked. "How's his injuries?"

The woman said, "The dwarf gentleman has some minor bite wounds on his leg. He's very strong — he should recover in a few days."

Jace recalled the scene from last night of Grede being swarmed by several older wolves. So he'd actually had strength to spare.

Then again, Grede was in the habit of wearing leather vambraces and leather boots whenever he went out, no matter how hot it was — important protection against getting torn up by beasts. They might've played a big part.

Jace looked out at the bright sky and remembered he'd promised to go to work this morning.

It was far too late for that now, but he couldn't stay here forever either.

He still had unfinished business before he left — that scroll, the one written in Orcish. Even after the chaos of this whole mess, it kept scratching at his curiosity.

"Hail me a carriage, Mrs. Forayre," Jace said. "As fast as you can. I need to get back to Stormwind right away. You can pay the dwarf my share — I've got urgent business."

"Are you sure that's all right?" Forayre said, worried. "And we haven't even had a chance to thank you properly. Matt rounded up some hands from the neighboring farms this morning and found some wolf carcasses. He's prepared a wolf-meat meal, and they're in the skins now… Several of the pelts are still intact. They could be sold for a bit of money, or made into winter clothes. All of it's for you, as thanks."

Hearing there was wolf meat to eat, Jace's stomach growled. He swallowed involuntarily, embarrassed.

And there were wolf pelts to take home on top of that. Add in the full-body soreness, and he probably couldn't drag himself anywhere anyway. Might as well lie down a while longer.

He let his head drop back and sighed. "I want fruit. Do you have any fruit? Anything'll do."

"Ah…" Emily nodded and hurried into another room, then came back carrying a wooden tray.

"We have grapes from Northshire Abbey. I don't know if you…"

Jace plucked a grape from the tray and popped it in his mouth. Damn, it was sour enough to make his teeth ache — but there was a faint trace of sweetness in there too, and even a hint of wine. Probably sitting a while.

But that sour, juicy hit was something he hadn't felt in a long time. One bite and he felt a surge of energy. Grapes were wonderful.

"If I may ask, sir — you're quite young, aren't you?" Emily Forayre seemed to be searching for a topic to break the awkwardness.

Jace said, "I'm about to turn eighteen."

"I was so scared something would happen to you last night," she said. "You're so young, doing such dangerous work out there. Your parents must be worried too."

Jace took another grape. "My parents aren't here. They think I'm doing manual labor hauling bricks in Stormwind. They won't worry."

"But they…" Emily's words cut off as tears suddenly spilled out.

"I don't know, but if we'd lost little Rose, I don't know how I'd face Matt. I don't know how I could go on."

Watching her head hang, Jace felt a twist in his chest. If his parents knew what he was doing out here, they'd probably move heaven and earth to drag him back to Lordaeron.

"All right, all right, don't try to play the sympathy card. Want another kid? That'll cost extra. You have any idea how disgusting it is crawling into a wolf den? Your husband and I agreed on two silver, and that's two silver. The wolf meat and pelts you made don't count — I didn't ask for those. And this bunch of grapes doesn't count either."

"No, no, they don't count." The woman wiped her tears and forced out a smile. "The money's ready. We'll send it with you when you head back."

"Right," Jace said. "Remember to take your daughter to get checked — especially by a priest. You know about the undead down south? We're worried the wolf trouble might be connected to those walking dead."

The woman nodded gravely. "We know. We'll take her to town tomorrow afternoon to have her looked at."

"Good." Jace lay back on the bed and groaned. "I'm going to lie here a bit longer. Everything hurts."

The young Mrs. Forayre left the room and gently closed the door. Only then did Jace finally feel his mood lighten.

At least he didn't have to think about his parents' maddening, unsolvable problems right now.

This brought his mind back to last night — the wolves that had surrounded him and Grede, and the Fear spell he'd managed to cast.

He wasn't even sure it was Fear anymore… Because by the game's rules, Fear was only supposed to target one person. Besides, when Jin had cast it on the street in Old Town, it hadn't scared Sulenna Kel'don half as badly.

And the Fear Jace had cast in return hadn't affected Sulenna as a bystander either.

So then what was this?

Thinking of that, Jace's eyes fell on the pole leaning against the wall — the one he'd looted from that orc warlock.

He still remembered that night: the moment Jace had shouted the incantation, everything had gone blurry. Only the evil glow this pole had given off when the spell released had left a deep impression. Thinking back on it now, it was almost like an illusion.

He tore back some of the cloth wrapping the pole. No arcane inscriptions that could emit an evil light, no gemstones or anything of the sort.

Could this staff really amplify dark spells?

He didn't remember any ordinary staff in the game with a special effect like this. But Jace also knew that copying game mechanics and data one-to-one onto the real Azeroth wasn't quite right.

And besides, an ordinary orc warlock couldn't possibly own a legendary artifact that surpassed the power of countless others — not even if he was a member of the Shadow Council.

Come to think of it, he still hadn't looked deeply into how exactly staves influenced magical power in this world.

"Jace." Grede, who knew when, had appeared and was standing in the doorway of the other room, watching him. "I just thought of a brilliant idea."
