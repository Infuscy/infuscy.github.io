## Chapter 442: The Golden Conch

On a rainy night at sea, the Golden Conch sailed steadily over the waves.

It was a sizable merchant vessel, loaded with goods and grain shipped from Stormwind to Hillsbrad — and, of course, several dozen passengers.

Because there was enough space, passengers willing to pay an extra ten silver could get a small private cabin. Jace paid up too, given that he was traveling with two stowaways.

"I want you to think about... the look of despair on that bloated bastard's face as he's about to die... mmm... Master..."

"Then... think about how you felt when you killed him... ah..."

Morfell clung tightly to her master's neck, rising and falling forcefully on his lap, her two burning demon eyes locked onto Jace's.

"That's it, darling, that's it..."

Before long, her demon eyes began flickering — now dying out, now flaring back. One hoof tried to brace against the edge of the bed to steady her rhythm, but several times it failed to find purchase... A while later, she could not even get words out anymore.

One hand gripped Jace's arm fiercely. Her eyes squeezed shut, her lips parted uncontrollably, her head tilting back...

Jace watched the taut curve of her neck and caught his breath. One hand gripped her demon horn and he drove deeper, feeling the swallowing below his waist, his heart hammering, a distinct tingling growing clearer by the second.

A sudden knock — two sharp raps — on the cabin door. Morfell's eyes snapped open, baring her fangs in fury as she whipped toward the door. The killing intent radiating from the demon killed Jace's mood in an instant.

"Hide," Jace whispered.

The succubus pulled herself off him, bracing a hand on his shoulder. Jace frowned in discomfort, which actually made her drop that bloodthirsty air and smirk.

The demon melted into the shadows. Jace threw on a cloak, wrapped himself up, and went to the door. He opened it to find the knocker already walking away.

"Ah, what are you doing?"

"Someone was in after all." The man turned his head. "Watch yourself — there's trouble on the ship. Also, the captain sent me to find anyone who can fight and send them up to the deck. You..."

He looked at Jace's gaunt arms and hollowed cheeks, hesitating. "You up to it?"

"Depends on what we're fighting," Jace said.

"Don't know yet."

Jace frowned. "Don't know? Then how do you know there's trouble...?"

"The captain has a mage advisor from Kul Tiras. He sensed something unclean approaching." The sailor explained, then said, "Never mind if you don't believe me. Just keep your door shut!"

"Thanks." Jace nodded.

The sailor said nothing more and turned deeper into the corridor. Jace braced a hand against the doorframe, feeling the roll of the hull. The wind was not strong, the waves were not high.

He swept the area with Detect Demons. Aside from the two demons in his cabin, what else was out there?

"The captain's mage didn't sniff you two out, did he?"

Jace asked the succubus in the cabin.

Morfell sat with her legs tightly crossed, pouting. Sazeno sat on the other side, holding one of Jace's scrolls and pretending to read.

Jace pulled on his clothes and said again, "Hey, is it?"

The imp set down the scroll. "If he could sense us, he'd have sensed us the moment we boarded, Master. That mage isn't that strong! He's just an enchanter..."

At this it lifted its nose. "Now that I think of it, a succubus's scent is really strange! Wait — or is that Master's smell?"

"Skip the nonsense," Jace said. "Can you sense anything around us?"

"At least no demons." Morfell sat on the table, propping her chin in both hands. "Master won't take me anywhere there are demons, but he's happy to take me anywhere there are humans. How much use am I, really..."

Her expression suddenly shifted. "Wait... there seems to be a scent of shadow."

Jace sniffed, glanced toward the door. He did seem to catch a familiar smell.

"Watch the money. Watch the luggage." He pointed at the two demons. "Don't leave this cabin... don't reveal yourselves in front of the other passengers."

"You've said that a million times, Master," Morfell said, rolling her eyes.

Jace nodded and went back into the corridor, looking both ways. Rain and spray blew in through the side windows. He carefully observed the surroundings outside, then leaned out to gaze at the sea.

The black water was impossible to see through.

Just then, a door opened nearby. A young female passenger emerged from a nearby cabin. She wore plain clothes, but Jace noticed she wore an apprentice's ring from the Wizard Sanctum.

She glanced over. "What's going on, sir? Did the knocking wake you too?"

Jace nodded. "Our ship's probably run into trouble."

"A storm?" the passenger sighed. "I heard the Baradin Bay is never calm..."

"We're not in the Baradin Bay yet," Jace said. "It's something else."

The words had barely left his mouth when Jace watched, in broad daylight, as a pitch-black shadow suddenly burst through the window right beside the girl. A slick tail wrapped tight around her neck!

Jace reacted instantly, thrusting out a hand and chanting Immolate. A burst of blood-red flame exploded and severed the tail. Outside, a woman's piercing shriek tore through the air, and whatever it was splashed into the sea.

Jace quickly leaned out to look. After a flash of lightning, shadowy forms rippled beneath the surface.

"Naga," he said, looking at the girl. "There are naga."

"Dark magic... it's dark magic..." The girl clutched her neck, staring at Jace in terror. "You use dark magic!"

Jace looked at her, a flare of anger rising in his chest.

"Sorry you spotted that, miss. I am indeed an evil warlock. Even if these goddamn naga climbed aboard and saw me, they'd probably piss themselves in fear from their cloacas."

"So go ahead and tell everyone else on the ship — tell them I'm a bastard who commands dark magic and summons demons."

The girl stared at Jace, then hurried to the end of the corridor. "I'm sorry, sir... I didn't mean to say that."

Jace turned his head. "Go back to your cabin, lock the door, and don't come out looking to get yourself killed, apprentice."

The girl nodded frantically and darted into her cabin, slamming the door shut with a bang.

With that, Jace climbed the stairs step by step, drawing Azure Song, which shimmered with pale blue arcane light.

Right now he was not Jace Sesso. He was Urk — a warlock with a perfectly stupid-sounding dwarven name.

No ring. No Wizard Sanctum robes. Almost no one knew him. Even people who'd met him before would hesitate on seeing him again.

Felt pretty good, actually.
