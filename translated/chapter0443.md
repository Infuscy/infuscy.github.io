## Chapter 443: The Mysterious Target

By the time Jace reached the upper corridor, he could already hear screams breaking out from time to time on the ship.

No telling how many naga had climbed aboard.

But there seemed to be sounds of resistance too. Before boarding, he'd noticed the ship had some armed men — mercenaries and guards, perhaps, or passengers. In any case, quite a few people on board had some ability to defend themselves.

Not to mention that many of the sailors on this ship were tall, strapping natives of Kul Tiras. These fellows generally stood a head taller than your average mainlander — reaching a meter ninety or even two meters was nothing unusual.

He made his way toward the deck, following the sailor's directions, when suddenly a dark figure burst in through a window halfway there.

The figure was enormously tall and powerfully built — it nearly brushed the ceiling.

This thing charged straight at him, swinging a blade and hacking viciously at Jace. Jace raised his magical sword, deflected the blow at an angle, and stepped aside — and barely kept it from grazing his chest.

The shadow wasted no time and swung again. This time Jace met it head-on with his magical sword. The impact felt like getting hammered by a sledge; his feet nearly gave way. But this time Azure Song left a huge, blue-gouged notch in the shadow's blade, which clearly made the creature far more wary. It drew back a step.

By the light of Azure Song, the monster before him reared its huge lizard-like head high, muscles coiled tight beneath layer after layer of black, barbed scales. The fin on its head stood upright like a kind of tall crown.

A male naga. In their matriarchal world, they were born to be pure warriors and shock troops. Compared to the females, they were far more reckless, far more savage and bloodthirsty.

The naga gripped a chopper and stared at the Jace before him, perhaps sensing that Jace's strength was no ordinary human's. It was starting to think twice.

"That sword will be mine," the naga murmured with a grin. The Common forced between its heavy lips sounded like sawing wood — deeply unpleasant.

"Are you immune to fear?" Jace asked.

"What?" the naga asked.

"Fear..."

The moment it heard the incantation, the naga flinched and shrank back — then turned to flee!

Jace gave him no chance. He rushed forward and drove his sword through the naga's lower jaw, the point punching out through the top of its skull. Then he cleanly wrenched the blade free.

The naga howled and thrashed on the floor, its tail whipping wildly, blood spraying everywhere. Its massive body smashed through the corridor, sending splinters flying.

After fighting a fel guard of the Burning Legion head-on, a single male naga was nothing to him anymore — at least it couldn't intimidate him. If he hadn't been to the Blightlands, seeing a monster this size on his own might have given him pause.

Of course, the thing's strength was still considerable. Meeting its slashes head-on took real effort.

Jace flicked the blade, shaking off the filthy blood mixed with seawater, and pressed on. He climbed out of the hatch and onto the deck, which had already descended into chaos — a crowd of sailors and passengers wielding weapons and fighting the naga. Two or three men ganging up on one still struggled.

Right in front of him, a sailor had been knocked down by a naga, which was about to drive a harpoon into his chest!

Jace stepped in and deflected the thrust with his sword. The naga hefted the harpoon and lunged again. Jace shifted a step aside, dodged it, then chopped the harpoon's tip in half. Seizing the opponent's moment of shock, he stepped in and brought his full strength down in a single stroke from shoulder to chest, then wrenched the blade free. The naga let out a fit of wet, rasping coughs and toppled to the deck.

Jace raised Azure Song and climbed the steps to the high point of the deck, rain streaming down his face. A naga was pressed between two men and had no time to watch its back. Jace ran it through from behind, then hacked downward and lopped off its giant lizard head.

The two men were stunned stiff by Jace's strength, but he knew the truth — it was just that Azure Song's magical damage was too savage.

After cutting down another naga that had been skirmishing with two sailors while wielding a blade, Jace wiped the rain from his face — and suddenly felt a sharp streak of arcane power streak toward his head at nearly imperceptible speed. Having fought alongside Vereesa, he knew this feeling all too well — the arcing approach of an enchanted arrow!

He almost instinctively stepped aside. A razor-sharp arrow wreathed in arcane energy whistled past his hair and stuck in the deck beside his foot!

He pinched out the flame on the tip of his hair and looked up. All he could see was a dark figure standing high on the mast, drawing a bow and aiming straight at him.

A naga siren...

How did she climb up there?

The naga drew again, arcane energy gathering on the arrowhead, locking right onto him!

Jace thrust out a hand and willed that dark power he knew so well...

And then — the last time he'd released that power, the full-body agony, the feeling of his innards being torn apart — it was still fresh in his memory...

In the dark of night, a blood-red line flickered. The naga siren cried out and was yanked off the mast. The arcane arrow in her hand went wild and flew into the high sky...

Jace raised his sword, ready to impale the woman mid-swing — but instead, just as she was pulled before him, she vanished in a flash of light without a trace.

That wasn't Vanish... so it could only be...

Blink.

Jace spun around with practiced ease and hurled his sword. Azure Song flashed a blue arcane streak and buried itself in the face of the naga siren.

Arcane light still shimmered around her, the energy of Blink not yet faded. She stood at the bow, grinning triumphantly as she drew her bow on Jace — the smile still on her face when the blade pierced her skull.

She toppled backward toward the sea. Jace reached out a hand, and a blood-red fel line yanked Azure Song back into his grip.

He looked out at the vast, boundless dark ocean and thought to himself: next time, he was absolutely never throwing this sword again.

Back in the middle of the deck, the naga had already begun to withdraw. Only two male naga who couldn't disengage were still skirmishing in a corner, hemmed in and cut down by the sailors closing in.

The captain, gripping a bloodied longsword, came before the crowd, panting hard. "Thanks to everyone's effort and sacrifice... all of you who came up to the deck and helped us drive back the naga — please leave your names in a bit. When you disembark, I'll refund half your fare as thanks!"

Half the fare?

Twenty silver...

Jace looked at the naga corpses on the deck and thought just scraping the scales off these things had to be worth a fair bit of money.

Many of the naga here were males. Their scales were broader, tougher, and more resistant to magic. Better suited for making armor than female naga scales — more practical, and more expensive.

Even so, Jace still registered the name "Urk" down in the cabin, and rolled up his sleeve to show the scars torn into his arm by shadow power, as the registrar requested.

The man stared for a moment, then wrote it down in his book.

Leaving the cabin, Jace noticed a KulTiras man in a gray robe watching him.

On his hand was a ring bearing a sigil Jace didn't recognize. He seemed to have seen it before, at the orc meeting in Dalaran — on the hands of those Kul Tiras mages.

Of course, he couldn't be sure his memory wasn't playing tricks. But the sailor who'd come to alert him earlier had mentioned the captain had a mage advisor, and that man was standing right behind the captain. That had to be him.

Had he detected his shadow magic?

Whether he had or not, Jace couldn't care less.

Go ahead and report me — to Lordaeron, or the Kingdom of Stormwind, or Dalaran, report me to whomever you like... Tell them I'm Urk — a skinny, sinister, scarred weirdo of a warlock who reeks of demons besides.

Jace looked at him, smiled politely, turned, and left the deck, heading back down the corridor.

He pushed open the cabin door. Morfell sat there, tilting her head with a smile, watching him.

"Master... hurry..."

Jace shut the door — and saw, beside the succubus, the pale purple box that held the Frostflow Crystal.

Why had the naga attacked this ship?

Would they be interested in ordinary cargo? Or the gold and silver coins carried by these passengers?

Naga attacking ships directly at sea was rare. In all his voyages, Jace had only heard rumors now and then — and most of those were about snatching magical treasures aboard.

So compared to ordinary merchant vessels, it was the ships belonging to magical organizations like the Kirin Tor or the Wizard Sanctum that the naga watched more carefully.

Could his Frostflow Crystal have drawn the naga?

No way. The pendant box the Kirin Tor had given him had a special effect that isolated arcane energy. At this distance, he could not sense the magic emanating from the Frostflow Crystal at all.

Could the naga sense a power he himself could not?

"Master?" Morfell rubbed against his leg. "What are you hesitating about..."

"Can you sense the power of that treasure, Morfell?" Jace asked.

"You mean this?" Morfell glanced sidelong at the box. Clearly, she was not fond of the thing. Demons loved to devour arcane power — but evidently not all arcane power was to their taste. From the very beginning, both Morfell and Sazeno had been quite repelled by the energy the Frostflow Crystal gave off.

Sazeno sighed from the side. "What kind of shameless mage twisted such wonderful arcane energy into this stupid shape... what a waste, Master!"

Jace said impatiently, "I'm not asking you how the arcane energy is. I just want to know — inside this box, can you still sense its presence?"

"Not at all," the imp said. "The Kirin Tor at least knows how to make decent things. This power-isolating box must be worth a fortune!"

Jace nodded. He didn't think his senses of the arcane could be that far off.

Then there had to be something else on the ship attracting the naga.

But what?

Just then, Morfell reached an arm around his neck and guided Jace's other hand beneath her.

"Master... can you not feel how much I'm suffering?"

Jace drew a sharp breath and let it out slowly. Whatever... he'd think about it tomorrow. After all, they hadn't actually finished what they'd started earlier.
