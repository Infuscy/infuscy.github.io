## Chapter 444: You Can't Expect Me to Do This for Free, Can You?

The next day, before Jace had given the matter any real thought, the captain gathered all the passengers on deck for a meeting.

The Kul Tiras folk knew the sea inside and out. If Jace could guess what he'd guessed, that islander mage probably could too. So this was most likely an investigation into exactly that.

"Last night, naga attacked our ship. We tried to alert all the passengers, but in the end, two brave sailors died of their wounds. Many more were injured. Had it not been for the many passengers willing to lend a helping hand, I fear those numbers would have been far worse."

The captain cleared his throat and continued, "But the naga did not attack our ship without reason. As I understand it, naga are usually drawn to magical treasures... So I ask those passengers carrying enchanted items or enchanted weapons to voluntarily present them for inspection by my advisor, Mr. Harold."

"I ask the mages on board not to worry — not every enchanted item attracts the naga. It must be something..."

At this, he leaned over and conferred in low tones with the Kul Tiras mage beside him, then continued, "That item must hold a special significance for the naga."

"If it remains on the ship, the rest of the voyage will surely still carry danger — after all, we estimate it will be another seven days before we reach Southshore. If that thing cannot be found, we will have to stop at Menethil Harbor and request help from the mages ashore."

Jace looked around. The other mages and apprentices he'd noticed were doing nothing.

He scratched his neck impatiently, thinking why every time he took a ship to Lordaeron he had to run into some goddamn trouble. After all, even sailing to a shithole like Booty Bay had been smooth sailing.

The mage, seeing no one willing to own up, frowned and spoke to the captain again. The captain's expression seemed complicated, but in the end he nodded.

"Since no one is willing to cooperate..." the captain said, "then from now on I will allow ten more minutes for you to decide. If no one steps forward to admit they're the one bringing trouble upon us all, then Mr. Harold and I, together with the sailors, will begin screening all magical cargo on board."

"Once an item belonging to the naga is found, we will throw the uncooperative passenger into the sea. And trust me — Mr. Harold can certainly identify something from the deep."

"I am sorry to announce this decision," the captain said. "But it is also for the safety of all the other innocent souls aboard. We have already lost two people, ladies and gentlemen."

Hearing this, Jace touched the naga bead bracelet on his wrist.

They wouldn't throw him overboard just because of this thing, would they?

Right now, he noticed the other mages checking the enchanted items they carried — even their staves.

Most of the passengers were getting agitated, urging whoever was carrying the ill-omened object to fess up and stop wasting everyone's time. After all, nobody wanted the ship to stop at Menethil Harbor for days on end, and nobody wanted to see anyone actually thrown overboard over this.

Suddenly, a shout came from a distance.

"I'm sorry, sorry — I'll take it out! Don't throw me into the sea!"

A Bronzebeard dwarf pushed his way out of the crowd. He was quite well dressed and carried an elegant cloth-wrapped parcel.

"It... it should be this thing."

As he spoke, he peeled away the layers of cloth, revealing a beautiful little pearl inside.

Quite a few people in the crowd drew sharp breaths, their mouths falling slightly open, dazzled by the play of iridescent light the bead gave off in the sunlight.

Beautiful as it was, the bead radiated a thread of arcane energy that felt distinctly uncomfortable. Jace noticed the Kul Tiras mage's gaze was wary too.

"It should be returned to the sea," the mage said abruptly. "Throw this pearl into the sea yourself, dwarf."

The dwarf's face darkened. "Throw it down?! Is that your settled course of action?! Do you have any idea how valuable this thing is?"

"Valuable? Is it worth two lives?" the mage snapped. "Throw it down now! Don't waste any more time! Don't force us to take action. If this drags on until evening, the whole ship will be risking death for your greed!"

"Don't make me say it a third time!"

"No! Absolutely not — you might as well throw me in. I'll swim to Menethil Harbor myself!" the dwarf roared.

"Seize that pearl," the mage said to the sailors beside him.

Several men immediately pushed through the crowd toward him — but the dwarf produced a small handgun from his jacket!

"Goddamn it, don't come any closer! Are you fucking serious?!" the dwarf bellowed, jabbing the weapon at everyone. The crowd scattered to the sides.

Jace could tell that while this mage was a man of considerable learning, he wasn't much of a fighter. Just as Sazeno had said, he was an enchanter. So his own expression was flustered at the moment — there was nothing he could do against a dwarf pointing a gun at him.

"Nobody do anything rash," Jace called out. "I've got an idea. Dwarf, Mr. Harold."

The moment he spoke, everyone looked over.

"I have a box here that can shield arcane energy," he said, looking at the mage. "I'll use the box to help the dwarf carry that pearl to Southshore. That should be fine, right?"

The mage hesitated a moment. "Let me see your box."

"I'll go get it."

The moment Jace said this, a murmur broke out among the onlookers.

"A treasure box that powerful, just sitting in the cabin?"

"No way..."

Back in the cabin, Jace took the crystal out of the box and handed it to Morfell. "Hold on to it. Protect it with your life."

"Then stab me to death, Master," Morfell said flatly.

Jace glared. Morfell let out a pained "ow" and pinched the blue ice crystal between two fingers.

"Thanks." Jace stroked her horn, and only then did the succubus manage a reluctant nod.

Sazeno beside them couldn't help but snicker. Morfell suddenly whipped her head toward him.

Jace came out and closed the door. A shriek came from inside — it nearly overpowered the sound of the waves outside.

Back on deck, Jace brought out the box. "Here it is."

The mage took it and immediately spotted the Violet Eye on its lid.

"Kirin Tor?"

"A Kirin Tor item," Jace nodded. Then he looked at the dwarf, who was now holding the pistol with both hands.

"Come here and test it with your pearl, dwarf."

The dwarf looked at the mage. The mage nodded, signaling the sailors to stand aside. The dwarf took the pearl back out and placed in the box. He shot Jace a suspicious look. "You two aren't in cahoots, are you?"

"Or you could have a firefight with them, shoot some unlucky bastard, and get tossed into the sea," Jace said. "Your call."

"Fine. I'll trust you."

The dwarf let out a breath and nodded. Jace closed the lid. The mage took the box and examined it from every angle. "Indeed... there's nothing. So this really is a Kirin Tor treasure box? What was it meant to hold originally?"

"You're asking too much," Jace said.

"All right, I don't mean any offense, sir." The mage looked toward the captain.

The captain waved a hand. "No need to take more lives. If this works... then let it be."

The Kul Tiras mage nodded too and thanked Jace. The captain urged everyone to disperse. The dwarf's eyes lit up with excitement. "So it's really fine? I'm really sorry... I didn't know this thing would actually attract the naga, I didn't expect..."

"So someone warned you?" Jace asked.

The dwarf grumbled, "Too many people were jealous of this treasure, that's how many! How was I supposed to know who was telling the truth and who was spouting lies? Some even said the naga queen would marry the owner of this treasure — was I supposed to listen to that?!"

"So how much is this thing actually worth?" Jace asked.

The question made the dwarf go guarded at once. He hemmed and hawed, mumbling, "Actually... it's not that much. About five gold, give or take."

"Then I'll charge five gold as a storage fee," Jace said.

"What?!" the dwarf jumped. "That's daylight robbery! A five-gold item and you want five gold as a storage fee?!"

Jace drew an inch of Azure Song. "Since you put it that way — yes, it's robbery."

The arcane light from the blade illuminated the dwarf's face. He blinked several times. "Five gold... fine, five gold."

"Now you've made me unhappy with your attitude, so it's six," Jace said. "And pay up now."

"Six... all right, six. Sorry, young sir." The nodded, fumbled out his purse, and counted out several gold coins, handing them over.

Jace knew damn well that pearl couldn't be worth only that much.

This dwarf seemed like an Explorers' League man, or a treasure hunter connected to them. The leather armor he wore was already quite valuable — probably worth two or three gold. A man who would guard something with his life like that — and it was only worth five gold?

He even felt he'd undercharged on the storage fee.

Jace pocketed the gold coins and said, "Pleasing doing business. Come find me when we make landfall, dwarf — ask for Mr. Urk."

"Urk?" the dwarf asked.

"Right. Your good friend Urk," Jace said.

The dwarf nodded, aggrieved.
