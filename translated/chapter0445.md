## Chapter 445: Hillsbrad in Summer

Southshore. Jace had almost forgotten what Southshore summers were like.

A deep green blanket covered the entire Hillsbrad Foothills. He gazed at the rolling hills and forests stretching into the distance, all the way to the far-off chain of mountains shrouded in thick mist and capped with snow, and drew in a deep breath of the salty, humid sea breeze.

Standing on the crowded dock, Jace swept the whole town with Detect Demons. Aside from the two right beside him, there was not a single demon in the place. It was profoundly reassuring.

I guess I'm the worst thing here, he thought.

"Mr. Urk!" The dwarf came up to Jace's side, speaking gingerly. "That... that..."

Jace nodded, took out the box, opened it, and held out the pearl. "Can you tell me where this thing came from?"

The dwarf took the pearl and immediately broke into a beaming grin. "Stranglethorn Vale. I bought it off a bunch of Booty Bay pirates. They said they'd gotten it off the southern coast of Stranglethorn. Said it'd fetch a good price in Dalaran."

Jace nodded. He had a rough idea where it was from.

Off the cape and coves near Booty Bay, there was an island occupied by naga. On it stood a naga altar — perhaps that was where the pearl had been found.

He couldn't detect any naga shadow-aura from it, but the arcane energy within had indeed been twisted by some force. That said, if he had to call the pearl outright evil... that was a bit of a stretch.

After all, compared to that Yogg-Saron statue he'd encountered back in Loch Modan... this thing was considerably more normal, at the very least. It didn't have any direct mental corruption.

Jace said, "Why didn't you sell it to the Explorers' League? It's right there in Ironforge — wouldn't that be much safer? And you're a Bronzebeard to boot."

The dwarf smiled. "Sell it to those penny-pinchers in the Explorers' League? I'd rather throw it in the sea! The elves in Dalaran are much easier to talk to about gold. If you've ever met them, you'll know I'm right!"

"I don't know much about the Explorers' League, but the elves, yeah, true." Jace nodded. "I'd suggest you take it and leave Southshore as soon as you can, find the elves in Dalaran and sell it. Keeping this thing on you is not a good idea."

"I know that now," the dwarf said. "And thanks for getting me out of that jam on the ship. Otherwise those Kul Tiras folk might really have..."

Jace laughed. "I don't think the captain would have actually thrown you overboard. Of course, if you'd pulled the trigger, that would've been a different story."

"So it's all thanks to you! Hope we meet again someday, Mr. Urk!"

The dwarf stuffed the pearl into his pocket and darted back into the street. Jace watched his gaze, then looked at his little box, confirmed it hadn't been contaminated by the pearl, and placed the frost crystal back inside from his robe.

When the Frostflow Crystal settled into the box, the chill it radiated instantly swept away any lingering doubt Jace had about the box's cleanliness. Jace let out a long sigh. The work of a real master was something else.

Southshore was even livelier than when Jace had passed through half a year ago on his way back to Stormwind. Probably because the weather was much warmer this time of year. More importantly, the damage the whelps had done here was almost impossible to detect now.

The burned ruins had been cleared away, the destroyed houses rebuilt. Aside from a few houses near the far edge of the forest that still showed scorch marks, the rest of the town looked completely untouched — no sign at all that black dragons had ever paid a visit.

At the gate of the town hall, Jace saw a snake-like dried head hanging from the belfry.

That was Mafral. Yes, Jace remembered his name — remembered it perfectly clearly.

Even when he'd witnessed that adult dragon of unknown origin in the Blightlands, even when he'd encountered that whelp in the Thaurissan ruins — killed together with Vereesa — neither had come close to the shock Mafral had given him.

After all, this was the first real dragon he had ever seen, and taken part in hunting.

Jace walked into the inn, glanced at a few Dalaran mages sitting in a corner, and went to the front counter.

The proprietor behind the counter was a strapping young man about Jace's age — probably the son of the real owner.

Jace looked at him. "Sir, are there many caravans heading to Lordaeron?"

"Plenty," the proprietor said. "There should be one leaving tomorrow morning. The caravan leader's staying right here. Are you looking for a room, sir?"

Jace nodded. "One night. Something out of the way, against a wall if possible. And a pack of smoked meat and a mug of ale."

"Fifty-five copper, sir."

Jace set down a silver coin. "No need to make change. But could you have the food and drink sent to my room? I'll bring the mug and plate back before I leave."

"No problem, sir." The proprietor smiled.

Jace nodded and went to the room the proprietor indicated. He pushed open the door and collapsed onto the bed with a thud. A bed that didn't rock back and forth was bliss. Now that the ground was solid, he still felt like his brains were sloshing around inside his skull.

"Shut the door, Morfell... I can't stand up to close it."

"At your service, Master." The succubus shut the door with a smile and sat on the bed. "This is Master's hometown? Beautiful."

"Don't say things you don't mean," Jace said, staring at the ceiling. "You only like worlds full of fel lava, fields of purple shadow, and iron chains and hooks hanging up innocent people everywhere."

Morfell licked her lips. "I've always been good at finding beauty in all sorts of places... Master!"

"Actually, there's something I want your help with." Jace took the box from his robes. "The mage who gave me this thing told me it can resist demonic mental corruption. I'll hold it first — you try seducing me. Let's see if it works."

"Just looking at this thing is already messing with my head," Morfell said unhappily.

Then she glanced at the crystal again. "But since you're asking me to seduce you... ah, it might be fun to play along with you."

Hearing this, the imp poked his head up from the side, biting his fingers, wearing a half-curious, half-amused expression as he watched the human and the demon.

"Remember to use your strongest power, banshee-slayer," Jace instructed.

"Since it's Master's order... then be careful."

As she spoke, the magical flames in Morfell's eyes surged and roared.

Her right hand waved in front of Jace's eyes. He watched the demon-fire in her eyes waver, looking somehow less like a demon's eyes... more like human eyes.

Not only did they look like it — for a moment, Jace even felt as though this wasn't a succubus at all. As though he'd been mistaking a real human girl for a demon all along...

Wait — was this Morfell's seduction taking effect?

Looking closer, Jace had never seen a woman this beautiful. She was as unreal as some kind of painting that could never exist in real life... hitting his exact aesthetic in some indescribable way...

So did that count as getting tricked, or not?

Was this thing in his hand even working?

He looked at the crystal in his hand with some disappointment, and to his surprise, the cold mist it gave off was already reflecting a sunlight-like golden glow.

Then the girl before him frowned and coughed. "No... I can't do it, Master. I could never fully seduce you to begin with, and this thing... it won't even let me maintain the spell."

Jace was starting to understand. When a demon's mental magic took effect on him, this thing would backlash against the demon's mind and disrupt their casting — rather than boosting the victim's will to resist the corruption.

This reminded him of the night Grede had ridden his griffin Gorrjeth straight into a formation of doomguards. Jace had tried to cast Fear on one of them and felt his own mind seared by demonic fire, agony flooding through him.

That had probably been the demon countering his mental magic through some unknown fel will. After all, Fear was a form of mental corruption too.

He looked back at the succubus. She had returned to her original appearance, but the blue demon-fire in her eyes was flickering — she looked terribly weak. Could demons burn out too?

Asking Morfell to go head-to-head with Aegwynn's gem using her own power was really pushing her.
