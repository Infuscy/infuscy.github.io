## Chapter 45: Just Deduct the Pay

"What are you on about? What idea?" Jace tilted his head toward Grede, doing his best to look too badly hurt to move.

Grede leaned against the doorframe, right hand on his hip. "Listen. We step up sword training. Step it up, and up, and up again — until we've forged you into a master swordsman. And I'll be training harder too, because I haven't reached that level myself yet. That way, you can feel the pure thrill of combat, enjoy the deep satisfaction that comes from tempering your body and your spirit — which is far more seductive than any degenerate dark energy! Tell me yourself: is it those ugly, bloated orc warlocks you admire, or is it Danath Trollbane, who led his legions through the Dark Portal? As a human, I'd think the answer's self-evident."

"You're right, it's definitely heroes like Danath I admire," Jace said teasingly. "But less than Danath's martial prowess, I envy his noble birth. Don't have to learn the sword, don't have to study magic — lie back and people take care of you and earn your money for you."

Grede said, "Noble? Haven't you heard the old saying — there's no such thing as an eternal crown, let alone noble blood. In the end, only the sword in your hand stays loyal to you!"

The words had barely left him before he leaned back in the chair beside him. "Then again… it really is enviable. Can only blame my own ancestors for not amounting to much."

Jace let out a grunt of a laugh that made every bitten spot on his body throb.

After eating at Lakeview Farm — it had been a long time since he'd had a meal with this much meat — he'd figured the wolf meat would be tough and gamey, like the warg meat in Duskwood. But it wasn't that strange, actually. More like donkey meat he'd had before, and pretty tasty at that.

Then again, when he thought about it, wasn't this basically dog meat?

After a night's rest, the next morning Grede and Jace set off for Stormwind with several rolled-up wolf pelts in tow.

The two of them looked like wounded soldiers retreating from a lost battle against the gnolls in Redridge. Traders and travelers along the road kept asking them about the state of the trade route between Lakeshire and Gelande.

Between the bounty for this job and the reward for finding the child, the farmer had thrown in an extra two silver as a generous thank-you — nine silver total. Grede handed Jace six silver on the spot, as danger pay for his outstanding contribution and a "injury subsidy."

But since the farmer had only been able to give them four silver on the spot, the remaining five-silver bounty had to be collected in Old Town, so Grede had reached into his own pocket to make up the two-silver difference for Jace.

After the Duskwood job that had paid the dwarf so handsomely, he could afford to be a little generous this time.

With these six silver in hand, Jace's savings had reached thirty silver.

This wolf-hunting expedition had destroyed one of his only three shirts — and it was one of the two thin summer shirts, which meant he now had nothing left to change into. It was time to buy new clothes.

Stormwind's established clothing shops were all in Old Town. The clothes in the Trade District were mostly imported, and the prices weren't cheap either. But with his body still a mess of wounds, walking to Old Town was out of the question.

Jace used the orc staff to support himself as he hobbled to a ready-made clothing shop by the canal — right in the heart of the Trade District. From here you could see the fountain in the square and the accounting firm the gnomes had set up, so it was the liveliest spot in Stormwind, buzzing with people.

After the experience of facing down a pack of wolves the other night, the staff in his hand had drawn more and more attention. Walking down the road, he kept feeling people's eyes flick over toward him.

The shop in front of him, "Lionheart Outfitters," sold not only outerwear but also armor — though only the leather coats travelers wore on the road, or the more elaborate studded leather sets. And since it was summer, none of it seemed to be moving; the stuff was all gathering dust in the corner. The half-plate and full forged armor worn by the Stormwind royal guards and knights definitely wasn't something you'd find here.

After buying a Lordaeron-style light-linen shirt, Jace felt like a new man.

But the gray trousers he'd been wearing suddenly looked out of place, and they'd been torn in a few places too. So on the shopkeeper's recommendation he added a pair of dark-brown loose leather pants — sturdy and durable. The only downside was they'd be a bit hot in summer, but summer was almost over.

But Jace's policy for trousers, to save money, was to wear one pair the whole year. These pants wouldn't leave his legs swollen in a Stormwind winter, wouldn't have him sweating buckets in summer, and out in the wilds they'd protect against thorns and brambles. Perfect.

He haggled the whole outfit down to four silver total. The trousers were the pricier piece, but given how sturdy and good-looking they were, he let it go.

He went home, slept, put the staff aside, and in the afternoon hobbled bit by bit to the Wizard Sanctum.

The apprentices were just about to finish class. Jace sat on the entrance to the spiral staircase and waited until Eden Marin came over.

"Mr. Sesso?"

Jace struggled to his feet. "Master, I had some urgent business these past two days. I'm really sorry — I thought I'd be back yesterday."

"Went out to help someone fight?" Marin looked at Jace's slightly raised right leg. "Or got careless and a dog bit you?"

"Both," Jace said.

Marin adjusted his spectacles. "It's no trouble. Resting your injuries is more important. There hasn't been much to do these past two days anyway. Just deduct the pay."

Jace sighed and was about to turn and head home when Marin suddenly called after him. "Right, Mr. Sesso — one more thing. About the Orcish lessons. I've already spoken with Master Dushan. You're on the list. He's very willing to take on new students — especially gifted and studious ones."

Jace's eyes lit up. "When do classes start?"

Marin said, "Two to three sessions a week. Tuesday afternoon, Wednesday morning, and Thursday afternoon. You'll have to miss the Wednesday morning session, of course — you still have to work, after all. You can go to the language section of the library and look through the Orcish-language documents collected by the various scholars and masters, as advance preparation and to make up for missed lessons. It's one floor below the top-floor library — you can ask the librarian."

Perfect. As long as he could look through those Orcish documents and find the word correspondences, he might be able to crack the contents of that scroll soon enough.

From here, as long as he studied hard, he'd be able to decipher the intact book and the damaged one before long, unlock the arcane secret to mastery, and set himself on the path to the pinnacle of success. Jace could hardly wait.

"Aren't you going to ask why Master Dushan thinks you're gifted and studious?" Marin asked suddenly.

"Huh?" Jace thought, isn't that obvious — but asked anyway: "How would he know? I don't believe I've met him…"

Marin smiled. "Naturally because I only introduce students who are diligent, studious, and exceptionally gifted."
