Chapter 452: Parting Comes at Last

On the way back, Hennie seemed to sense something. She didn't drag Jace to the market or anywhere else again.

The two of them walked the whole way in silence. Jace wanted to say something to put his mother at ease, but not a single word came out.

Once home, his mother immediately started preparing dinner, waiting for his father to return.

At the dinner table, Jace chatted with his parents about Nethergarde Keep and Stonard, about the demons he'd encountered on the battlefield, about the different people he'd met over there. His father was deeply moved and told many stories of his own — about their dealings with Stromgarde cavalry on the border before the Orc Wars.

Back then, the Kingdom of Lordaeron and Stromgarde had always had poor relations, with frequent border frictions. But after the Dark Portal, everything changed. Even those red-robed cavalrymen who had once looked so menacing had become amiable and friendly.

That night, Jace lay in bed and couldn't fall asleep until very late. Sensing the light from inside, his mother knocked softly on the door. "Jace, are you still awake?"

"I'm fine, Mom."

"Can I come in?"

"Of course."

Jace sat up and watched his mother carry a candleholder in and set it on the table. She sat on the edge of the bed. "That man who came to see you this morning — he was a mage, wasn't he?"

"You saw him," Jace said.

Hennie nodded, sighed. "They don't want you to come home."

Jace said, "Yes. Once people know I'm still alive, it'll be troublesome."

Hennie opened her mouth as if to speak, then seemed reluctant to say it.

She appeared to hesitate for a moment, but in the end couldn't help asking, "There's one thing I've never understood, Jace. That elven envoy... she didn't make it clear to us either. Since your injuries were healed, why did they announce you'd died on the battlefield? I asked your father — he didn't know either. I thought he was going to ask you at the dinner table, but in the end he never brought it up."

Actually, Jace had a pretty good idea why his father hadn't wanted to ask. As a guard captain of Lordaeron, he'd probably already heard some rumors.

He looked at his mother, weighing whether or not to say it.

But what good would continuing to hide it do?

Hennie was no fool.

And he might not be able to come back to the royal city for a long time after this, just as Biso Ershad had said.

Better to tell his mother and keep her from guessing and worrying even more.

"To kill that demon commander, I used some... not-so-good methods. A lot of people saw it," he said quietly. "They said it that way to protect me and you."

"I more or less understand, son," Hennie nodded.

Jace lay back. "Mom, tell me a story."

Hennie smiled and leaned against the headboard. "Then I'll keep telling you about the vile pirate of Cunning Bay. Last time I left off with Greg Arkstone boarding a ship full of naga snake-folk..."

"Right!" Jace's eyes widened. "Those naga could even speak Common!"

"You actually remember." Hennie stroked Jace's hair. "They could speak Common, so they demanded that Mr. Arkstone hand over all the treasures on his person and offer them to their queen..."

Even though Hennie told it just as captivatingly as when he was little, as if it had really happened, Jace still drifted off to sleep before he knew it — a deep, peaceful sleep.

In the days that followed, Jace helped his parents fix quite a few things around the house that weren't working well, and sorted out a storage room that hadn't been cleaned in years. Considering Biso Ershad's warning, he didn't go out with his parents again.

But no matter what, he had to leave.

And he knew the longer he stayed at home, the harder it would be to tear himself away.

That morning, Jace packed his things and threw on his cloak. His father took the longsword — a gift from the House of Menethil — off the wall and handed it to Jace. "Take this with you, Jace."

Jace said, "Mm. You keep it. This sword was the King's gift to you, not to me. After all, they've already built my grave."

Viktor shook his head. "I have my own weapon, Jace, and it suits me just fine. Take this sword for self-defense."

"I have my own sword too." Jace patted beneath his cloak. "It's probably been sitting in my room these past two days where you haven't seen it. But rest assured, mine works great. I know you love this sword — you keep it!"

"Besides, I'm a mage. What am I doing carrying two swords... you know what I mean, Dad?"

Viktor smiled. "A mage can run out of time to cast a spell too. But since you insist, I'll take it as a gift you're leaving me."

Jace nodded and looked at his mother. He could tell Hennie was struggling, but to keep her son from being sad too, she just held it in and said nothing.

"Mom, I actually do have something for you."

"Huh? What?"

Hennie clearly hadn't expected that.

Jace took the dragon-fang dagger from his robe. "Take a look at this."

"This... is a dagger?" Hennie's eyes lit up, then she shook her head. "Your mother's long past the age of running wild. With Viktor around... I have absolutely no use for this, Jace."

"But you still like these little things, don't you?" Jace said. "And most importantly — take a guess what this is made of."

"Some kind of beast's fang." Hennie took the knife and turned it in her hand. "Very light, son, and beautifully made, but I can't guess what exactly. A highland raptor's claw?"

"This is a black dragon's fang, Mom," Jace said. "And from a black dragon I killed with my own hands."

Hearing that, Hennie went stiff. Viktor's face changed. "You... killed a black dragon?"

Perhaps the concept of a demon commander was still too abstract for a guard captain living in the far north. But a black dragon... for the people of Lordaeron today, that was no longer some unimaginable monster.

"Though it was a whelp, and I had help," Jace said. "But don't worry, Dad — I guarantee this black dragon's fang has no residual toxicity."

He gripped his mother's hand holding the dagger and said softly, "If old Viktor gives you any trouble, you stab him with this."

His mother burst out laughing, then nodded. Her father dissolved into laughter beside her and patted Jace's head lightly. "Whose side are you on now, siding with your mother?"

"Naturally... but you two don't need to worry about my safety out there anymore, do you?" Jace said, hugging his mother softly. "Even a black dragon couldn't do a thing to me. It tried to play chicken with me and ended up losing its own life — and I'm standing here safe and sound. I'm not bragging. And Dad, Mom — I will come back to see you."
