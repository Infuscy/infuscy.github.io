Chapter 453: The Newcomer in the Basement

That afternoon, Jace went to Brill, checked out of the inn, picked up his two demons, and set off back to Southshore.

The scenery along the way seemed to have taken on a grayish cast. The clouds in the sky sat low, pressing down on people's chests — at least not as high and open as the skies over Stormwind.

The seawater had turned somewhat black, nowhere near as blue as it had been on the way in. The white caps looked more like the foam left behind by filthy water sloshing about. The whole thing was deeply unpleasant to look at.

Jace figured it was probably because summer was ending and autumn was drawing near.

Even though he'd told his parents he'd come back, he hoped that next time he returned here he wouldn't have to sneak around anymore, wouldn't have the Kirin Tor watching his every move.

But how exactly to accomplish that, he didn't know.

Even a decade and more from now, when warlocks — and even the demons they summoned — had entered the Alliance armies; even when King Varian Wrynn, a man whose parents had both died because of the Orc Wars and who had himself been victimized by dark magic, had accepted warlocks and their demons; even when warlocks and their demon servants had taken part in countless great battles against threats to the world thereafter... people's prejudice against this group still hadn't been fully erased.

Or perhaps it wasn't really prejudice at all.

After all, the warlocks of that era could be considered highly accomplished, but they also screwed up again and again.

Just when everyone felt the warlocks' achievements were enough to make them trust their abilities, they always managed to find some perfect opportunity to pull off a spectacular disaster, forcing everyone to recall the fear that shadow and demons had once sown.

In fact, never mind clearing the name of warlocks — just bringing himself back to "life" was going to be hard enough.

By the time he got back to Stormwind, the weather had already turned a bit cooler.

It was drizzling at midday. Jace hurried home in a rush and remembered that the rent he'd paid in advance before heading to the Dark Portal was about to expire.

It was already October. After the Dark Portal, Jace had stopped attending classes at the Wizard Sanctum and abandoned his regular routine. This stretch of time had been spent either jostling on the road or tossing on the sea — he'd almost lost all concept of time.

Before the lease expired, he had to find someone to explain things to the landlord, and he'd have to move too.

After all, he was nominally dead now. Continuing to rent this room would seem rather strange.

Stay in Stormwind, or move to Goldshire?

He'd thought about living at the Slaughtered Lamb. The basement had two empty rooms that were locked up. Tharle had the keys — letting him stay there would be doable.

But there was a problem. If there were Kirin Tor people watching him in Lordaeron, what about Stormwind?

If he went to the Slaughtered Lamb once or twice a week, he could say he was just going to that tavern for a drink. But if he went in and out several times a day, wouldn't that be far too suspicious? After a belated lunch at the Gilded Rose, Jace headed to the Mage Quarter. Right now, everyone he looked at seemed like a Kirin Tor investigator.

Fortunately, there was never anyone hanging around the entrance to the Slaughtered Lamb. If someone were tailing him, they'd stand out pretty easily.

Walking into the tavern, Tharle was washing cups. He glanced up, saw Jace, and said, "Jace, Galders has been wanting to find you. He told me to tell you to come see him when you're back and have the time."

"I don't know where he lives," Jace said.

"You don't even know where he lives?" Tharle said, pulling a scrap of old receipt from under the table and writing an address on the back. "He's sometimes here — should be by the canal on the north side of the Trade District."

"Thanks," Jace said. "There's something I'd like to ask your help with, Tharle."

"Go ahead, Jace." Tharle set the clean cups aside and picked up another to dry.

"I'm looking for a place to rent," Jace said. "I don't know if you know of anything cheap — preferably close to the Mage Quarter."

"Close to the Mage Quarter and cheap are two contradictory requirements, Jace." Tharle smiled.

Jace scratched his head. "Then keep an eye out for me. Farther away is fine too. For now... just find me something."

"I'll keep an eye out for you," Tharle said.

"Thanks." Jace nodded and went down into the basement. He opened the iron door and walked in — and saw an unfamiliar woman sitting by the bonfire.

He frowned warily. Who was this? Tharle hadn't warned him there was an outsider in the basement.

Most importantly, that hellhound hadn't reacted at all either, just lying in the passage.

The woman looked young — probably not much older than Jace. Her features could hardly be called pretty, but she had a rather distinctive, gloomy air.

Her skin was unhealthily pale. A straight fall of black hair draped over her shoulders. The pale purple robe with carved ornamental trim she wore clearly cost a fair bit.

One could say this woman was a better fit for the Dagger Society than anyone else in the group. From her hair to her expression to her looks, she was practically Galders's own sister.

Right now she was sitting in a chair reading a book, and she looked up when she noticed Jace.

"Who are you? Who brought you here?" Jace asked.

The woman stood. "I'm Galene Ailuo, a friend of Galders. He brought me."

"Ah, Galders. All right, Miss Ailuo." Jace noticed her glance at the shirt and pants he was wearing, as if sizing something up.

That one look suddenly reminded Jace — wasn't this the seamstress Galders had found? Galene Ailuo. The name was quite familiar.

"Just Galene is fine. You must be Jace Sesso," Galene Ailuo said. "Galders told me about you. Pleased to meet you. I've already joined the Dagger Society, so I'll keep everything about my comrades confidential — just like the rest of you have done all along."

Jace said, "I heard you're a master at crafting mage robes?"

"My father is. I'm still learning," Galene said. "I also studied enchanting at the magical institute of the Kingdom of Alterac. I heard you studied at the Wizard Sanctum too."

Of course, Jace could imagine how difficult it must have been to get a skilled old craftsman — one who made good money crafting robes for mages — to abandon his original trade and suddenly start collaborating with a bunch of warlocks to craft strange robes imbued with dark traits, all while keeping it a secret...

Finding a young person who had inherited such a trade and was willing to join the Dagger Society of her own accord was already no small feat.

Jace nodded. "Yes, I spent some time at the Wizard Sanctum. I'd like to know — is this robe you're wearing your own work? It looks beautiful."

He wasn't flattering her. The quality of this robe was genuinely excellent. Even setting the materials aside and looking purely at the craftsmanship, it was no inferior to the robes in Larson's place that went for two or three gold apiece.

Galene nodded and smiled. "I made it myself. Thank you."

"Then the Dagger Society welcomes you, Galene Ailuo."

As he spoke, Jace glanced once more at the robe on her body and silently prayed this woman wouldn't charge too much — hopefully she'd help out for free, like Ursula did. That way, when he went out wearing a robe to get in a fight, a slash across the sleeve wouldn't hurt worse than a cut to the flesh.
