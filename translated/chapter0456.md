## Chapter 456: The Most Professional Demon Hunter

"Yes, Master." Senlura answered lightly and walked into the inner room. Jace watched her back and said, "She's very obedient to you."

"Succubi can indeed be obedient," Galders said. "So I don't understand what Jagin was thinking. By all rights he should have been even harsher with his demons. Since he'd already subdued those two succubi, how could that have happened? Damn it."

"Apart from the incantations I mentioned earlier, there's another one we pieced together but haven't successfully tested," Galders said, taking the papers Senlura handed him. He flipped through them and passed one to Jace. "This one. It has both Demonic and Common phonetic notations written on it."

Jace studied the Demonic notations while speaking. "I also brought back an incantation this time that everyone can learn together. I've looked into it myself, and I feel it's hard to make much more progress on it."

"What?" Galders said. "You've already learned it, but you think there's still room for improvement?"

"Right," Jace said. "A shield woven out of shadow. I have a feeling that casting it might be connected to controlling your own emotions. Once you nail that casting feel... you can take the spell's effect up another level."

Galders nodded slowly, then said, "That's a guess I've never been able to confirm, Jace Sesso. You can really... manipulate your own emotions to influence a spell's effect?"

"On spells that are difficult to cast, the effect is pretty significant," Jace said.

Galders said, "Excellent. I've caught a hint of it occasionally, but I could never tell the difference clearly. Probably because the magic I use really is on the simpler side."

"We can talk more about it at the Slaughtered Lamb," Jace said, glancing at the incantation in his hand. "This spell... I can feel it's strongly offensive."

"You mean we should spend more money hiring someone to test it?" Galders asked.

"I'd say don't find a person. It could be dangerous. Let's find an animal first," Jace said.

Galders said, "Then I'll go talk to Tharle tomorrow and have him get a lamb."

Jace nodded. "There's one more thing. I want to know — are all of Jagin's incantation records here?"

"Of course," Galders said. "Want to take a look? I haven't had time to copy them yet. Wait for Senlura to copy a set for me and I'll give them to you. There are parts of Jagin's materials I still haven't figured out either."

"Senlura's willing to copy things for you?" Jace asked.

"Isn't Miss Molophil willing to?" Galders fired back.

Jace said immediately, "I, uh, haven't actually asked her... but won't your imp be willing to help?"

"Imp?" Galders laughed. "My imp doesn't have the strength to write more than a few words. Its efficiency is far worse than a succubus's. It does know Demonic, but its level is way below Senlura's. She's been a real help to me — and that's all thanks to you, Jace. Of course, we can't forget Jagin's sacrifice either."

Jace nodded. "Speaking of Jagin and the succubi... there's one more thing I wanted to ask you, Galders. It's about Musur."

Galders sighed and leaned back in his chair. "I knew you'd eventually come around to this guy. Fine — after all, he hasn't done anything to wrong us. Go ahead. But don't ask me where he is. I know nothing about it, and I have no interest in finding out."

"It's not about him personally," Jace said. "I heard Musur knew a female warlock before... I wanted to know something about her."

Galders gave him a meaningful look. "You mean Dasmora Darkeye."

"You know her name?" Jace asked.

Galders smiled. "Does this count as knowing? Just like Musur, it sounds exactly like a fake name someone made up for themselves to become a shadow manipulator. Dasmora Darkeye is quite well-known in the old warlock circles — I mean, among those who were practicing dark magic before the Dark Portal opened. Shadowforge City, Stratholme, Darrowshire, Stromgarde..."

"Is she human?" Jace asked.

"I really don't know," Galders said. "Dwarf, human, elf, even troll — any of them are possible. Might even be a demon. She has many legends, and she left behind quite a few works on demonology. I know Musur claimed to have met her, but I don't know if that's true or just that lunatic's delusion."

"If he never met that warlock, then where do you think Musur got all that demonic knowledge?" Jace asked.

Galders looked at Jace. "Hard to say. But you're not the first warlock who's wanted to find Dasmora, Jace. Jagin tried to figure it out too. Maybe even Musur himself wanted to figure it out."

At this, he smiled again.

"You just said Dasmora left behind a lot of books, right?" Jace asked.

"Yes."

"Are there any in her own handwriting?"

"Own handwriting?" Galders frowned and thought for a moment, muttering Dasmora's name under his breath. After a while he could only say, "Maybe Musur really does have some. But trying to think of it now... you could try asking some magic-scroll and antique collectors, especially the less legitimate ones who are willing to collect shadow-magic-related magical items. Like certain goblin tycoons."

Goblin tycoons?

The moment goblin tycoons came up, Jace immediately thought of the goblin alchemist Mishershield. The guy was more of a debt-honor than a tycoon, but there was no denying he had wide enough connections in the Trade Cartel. He knew the Risk Capital Company's Boss Kao, he knew the mayor of Gadgetzan — he might be able to dig up some relevant information.

What Jace was thinking was, if he could get hold of a scroll copied in Dasmora Darkeye's own hand — or at least something she'd touched — he might be able to use the "sympathy" the Reliquary had mentioned to him before, trace Dasmora's magical trail, and use that to search for clues she'd left behind.

Still, it would take a trip to Booty Bay.

Let the plan percolate a little more. For now, he intended to spend some time at Galders's place learning a few necessary incantations. Jace had a feeling the search for Dasmora Darkeye was going to run into more and more trouble.

Not to mention, next he had to consider helping Musur prepare for summoning that demon. It was hard to say which would be more difficult — succeeding, or failing without leaving any trace.

Also, to get in touch with the Reliquary, he'd need Vereesa's help. He could write a letter first and send it to ask whether there were any Reliquary members nearby. With a letter of introduction from a member of the Windrunner family, people would probably be more willing to talk.

"Oh right, I haven't asked — did you meet Galene Ailuo?" Galders asked.

"She's joined our Dagger Society. I'm sorry we invited her to join while you weren't present, but you're never around..."

"I've met her," Jace said. "She came to us while I wasn't there — that should be my apology, Galders. I can tell she's very capable, and I heard from Ursula that she's already started preparing her first robe."

"Right, she's already preparing, and so are we," Galders said. "You fought in the Battle of the Dark Portal, so you must know how many demon corpses were left behind on that scorched earth. Not just there — they're all over the Black Morass too. The Alliance doesn't have the strength to clean up all those demon remains, and their bodies don't decay quickly either. Now is a prime time to gather demon remains and craft casting materials and robe ingredients, Jace. If you're lucky, you might even find things left behind by those powerful Eredar demons. Of course, don't get your hopes up — I know the Wizard Sanctum and Dalaran are searching too."

"Anyway, Erland, Sulenna, and a few of their hunter friends have already set out. They should be back with some haul in a while. Oh, right — would you be interested in going along to earn some money afterward? They're heading east into the Black Morass from Lakeshire."

"Of course," Jace nodded. "Is it lucrative?"

Galders said, "You're strong, Jace, and you have plenty of experience fighting demons — I'd say... absolutely plenty. I'll have Erland negotiate a good price for you. Though, I know you're officially listed as having died at the Dark Portal front now, so you'll need a new name. Have you thought about that?"

"York," Janswer answered, deliberately imitating Grede's accent as he said it.

Galders couldn't help but laugh. "Good name."
