Chapter 458: A Familiar Yet Unfamiliar Magic

With Senlura's confirmation, Jace glanced at the incantation Galders had given him and tried chanting, "Yulaxinka, Bistaante..."

After speaking the incantation, although there wasn't a clear gathering of shadow power, he quickly felt a wave of depression well up inside him, like a weight of melancholy trying to break free.

At the same time, the whole room darkened for an instant. This wasn't an illusion — everyone present noticed the anomaly.

Senlura said, "Mr. Sesso, it's very clear... your sadness, your sorrow, a need for someone to comfort you..."

"What I need isn't comfort, Senlura — it's release. I just need to find the way to release it," Jace said, glancing at Galders. "So where did it go wrong... There are no casting gestures recorded on this paper. Did you try using gestures to guide the shadow energy?"

Galders said, "We tried several and none worked. Whether it was the Curse of Agony casting gesture or the Fel Breath one — you know those two gestures, with slight modifications, can guide a lot of shadow magic. But these two were completely off."

"This is clearly an offensive spell," Jace said. "Fel Breath, that kind of self-buff incantation, would guide the shadow power completely astray. I think..." At this, he recalled a rather distinctive casting gesture.

Actually, it shouldn't be called distinctive — it was an extremely common casting gesture. It's just that for this whole crowd of warlocks here, this kind of casting gesture was something none of them had ever tried.

And that was Fireball...

He took the Blood Mage staff wrapped in elven cloth off the wall, and while chanting the incantation, used the shadow gem of Gorefiend to gather shadow power on it with the other hand.

Depression... For Jace, that wasn't a hard emotion to access. He just had to think back to the cathedral in Lordaeron, watching his mother's back as she walked away... damn it.

That was already painful enough.

This emotion was like a half-rotted bone dropped into water, rapidly drawing out the hungry black shadow energy all around, which hurried over following the scent. Jace used the incantation to guide them to gather on the staff the way he envisioned.

— Yulaxinka, Bistaante.

That's right, this gesture meshed perfectly with the incantation's guidance...

Combined with shadow power, depressed emotion, and misdirected release...

If he wasn't mistaken, this was Shadow Bolt!

Still, he couldn't use the crude Fireball-guiding gesture to channel shadow entirely. By comparison, shadow was much "gentler" — he needed to be more patient. But with the help of the Gorefiend gem, plus his experience channeling shadow energy when guiding the Hand of Gul'dan, under the incantation's direction the shadow power obediently coiled around the gemstone, forming a chaotic black mass.

When the black mass gathered to a certain point, Jace even felt the shadow begin to take physical form, emitting ghostly echoes. Inside were the faces of unnamed monsters trying to break free, groaning and wailing in agony — but the sound was indistinct, reverberating as if from the Void itself.

Ursula had been watching Jace's expression closely, her face grave. Galene Ailuo's face showed a trace of bewilderment, as if unsettled by those sounds. Galders was studying Jace's casting intently, probably comparing it against his own.

By now, Jace condensed the energy into an increasingly tangible form. When he felt he could release it, he immediately turned to his target — the lamb.

The shadow orb was fully formed and ready to be hurled at any moment. But Jace hesitated.

After all, this wasn't a game — you didn't strictly need a target to cast a spell...

Although there was a strong urge in his heart to hurl all the resentment in his hands at the helpless, pitiful creature before him, to destroy its fur, its flesh and bones, to corrode it into a filthy mass of purple rot.

But at the same time he was resisting, restraining that impulse — and this restraint quickly gained the upper hand.

Jace decisively hurled the Shadow Bolt at the wall. With a piercing wail of ghosts, the Shadow Bolt slammed into the wall and burst into a spreading darkness. No thunderous explosion — just a creeping force of corrosion.

Jace untied the lamb, picked it up whole, and carried it to the side. "Stand back a little, everyone — to the other side of the room... this shadow..."

The others also nodded and backed away, until the shadow energy slowly dissipated.

Shadow Bolt.

Jace remembered the day he and Grede had dealt with Hogger, and the necromancer they'd encountered in the forest held by the Riverpaw tribe.

The spell the other side had cast was clearly also a Shadow Bolt — or at least a similar shadow orb.

Back then he'd thought Shadow Bolt must be an extremely easy spell to cast, since even a gnoll could do it...

But thinking about it now, that had been a dead gnoll. The pain, despair, and depression of its own death and forced resurrection had given those undead creatures a natural affinity for shadow.

And they, the living, had to seek out those feelings for themselves.

"This is... Shadow Bolt," Galders said. "A very powerful and effective shadow magic — and the one I've been searching for. Actually, Jagin found this incantation very early on too, Jace."

He sighed and continued, "It's just that we always thought there might be something wrong with the incantation, or some other deficiency or missing piece... We never imagined casting this spell would be this difficult."

Galene, looking at the lamb in Jace's arms, said, "You didn't need to save it, Jace. It was going to end up in the kitchen either way."

Jace said, "By that logic, I didn't need to kill it either. Shadow Bolt isn't like Curse of Agony — it doesn't need a specific target for you to see the effect. Even if it goes to the kitchen we get an extra meal out of it, and it saves Galders some money too. If I killed it with shadow, we'd have to go to the trouble of disposing of the corpse."

"More importantly, I've always felt that compared to using emotion to move shadow... what matters more is not letting the shadow take control of our own will in return. If I don't want to kill this lamb, there's no reason to change my mind just because the shadow power brings bloodlust. I can still tell which impulses are my own will and which are the energy's."

"Well said, Jace," Galders agreed.

Galene nodded too, but Jace couldn't help thinking back to that chaotic night facing the Pit Lord Azgoth.

When the roar of the soul and the howl of shadow grew that powerful, telling them apart had become next to impossible.

Jace closed his eyes for a moment, pulling himself free from the sticky memories, then said, "This isn't an extremely difficult spell — once you grasp the technique. I think the key... still lies in guiding the shadow, whether that's control over your own emotions or the casting gestures."

Galders said, "Then let me be the next to try."
