Chapter 460: The Peculiarity of the Incantation

By evening everyone was exhausted, except for Galene, who refused to admit defeat and kept practicing for a while after the rest had results. This time she could gather the shadow power, but she still had problems with the final stage of discharging the energy.

It counted as basically successful, but she couldn't convince herself of it.

After a simple dinner in the basement, the group chatted about what had happened in the basement during the few days Jace had been away, including the business of Sulenna summoning that male succubus.

After dinner, Ursula headed back to the alchemy lab to keep organizing her potion bottles, and Galene also got up to pack her things and prepare to go home. Galders glanced over. "Jace, come with me. I'll give you the things Jagin recorded."

Arriving at Galders's room, he opened a cabinet and pulled out a stack of brand-new papers, handing them to Jace. "These are the incantation materials Erland searched out from Jagin's house. Some of them we've already organized using your Demonic materials, like Curse of Recklessness, but most haven't been touched."

Jace took the papers, and Galders pulled out a few more sheets. "These on top are the incantations we organized over the past few days. Fel Breath, which can exchange gases using shadow power and prevent suffocation in most situations... and Corruption, which — after being organized with the Demonic materials — has become much more stable in taking effect. Since your Demonic dictionary was compiled by you, there's only one copy, right?"

"Right," Jace said. "I've been thinking — let's keep this dictionary at the Slaughtered Lamb for now, so everyone can flip through and reference it whenever they want. After some time I'll organize it properly and bind it, then have Galene cast a preservation enchantment on the book so it doesn't fall apart from overuse."

"Fine."

Jace said, "Anyway, I only made this dictionary to organize incantations in the first place. If everyone can help out, that's even better."

Galders said with a smile, "After going through some twists and turns, I feel like our group is finally about to get on the right track."

Jace nodded, then said, "Oh, do you have any extra blank paper? I want to write a letter."

"Of course." Galders pulled a sheet of paper out of the drawer under the desk and handed it to Jace. "I have another question, Jace."

"That incantation you told Jagin before — Curse of Weakness. You remember it?"

"Of course I do," Jace said.

"That incantation isn't short," Galders said. "Logically, based on the pronunciations in it, you should be able to find some clues by looking them up in your dictionary. There are some incantations Jagin found where we couldn't find a corresponding word for every term either — but at the very least we should be able to find one or two. But that Curse of Weakness..."

"Because that curse isn't Demonic," Jace said. "Senlura should have been able to tell."

"She's not entirely confident in her Demonic either," Galders said. "Are you certain it isn't Demonic?"

Jace said, "Remember? I got this incantation on the western coast. This should have been left behind by the naga... and the naga don't learn magic from demons. They have their own system."

At this, Galders thought for a moment with a grave expression. "I originally thought that finding a Demonic dictionary would let us translate all incantations smoothly. Looks like it's not that simple. We can't exactly go communicate with the naga, can we?"

"My suggestion is to set that Curse of Weakness aside, Galders," Jace said. "There's no negotiating with naga. Since that curse takes effect smoothly and there's no problem using it, let's just keep using it for now. If we get another chance in the future, we can find a way to correct it."

"So what language is the Curse of Weakness in?" Galders asked. "The naga's own language?"

The language the naga used was a variant of ancient elvish, somewhat close to the language used in modern Quel'Thalas. But this incantation wasn't in the naga language — it was in Sarghathan, the language of the Old Gods and their servants.

Seeing that Galders still looked reluctant, Jace said, "You should know about the naga and the deity they worship, right?"

"I've heard some things from the Kul Tirans," Galders said.

"Then you should also know the legends about the naga and the sea god's madness," Jace said.

"I've heard those too," Galders said. "But the booklet where I collected incantations actually has quite a few incantations with similarities to the Curse of Weakness. Does that mean those magics are also in the naga language?"

"Shadow magic incantations come from many sources, Galders. Even demons use magic that originates from those gods," Jace said. "But we'd better limit ourselves to just using them, rather than turning from a bunch of collectors and researchers of demon and orc magic into some kind of ancient god-worshipping cult. If a spell works, we can try using it. If it doesn't, then toss it. Don't dig any deeper."

Galders said, "I'm always very careful about these things, Jace. I just wanted to ask about what's strange about that incantation."

As he spoke, he began packing his bag. "I remember you also live by the canal, right? Why don't we head back together."

"No thanks," Jace said. "I'm too much of a target right now. Walking with you might invite unnecessary trouble. It's already dangerous enough."

Galders smiled. "Relax, Jace. I have friends in many places. If the Slaughtered Lamb attracts unwanted attention, I'll be the first to know, and I'll notify everyone quickly. And I can find out exactly what caused the attention. The reason I dared find a place like this in the Mage Quarter with Jagin and Tharle Mor and organize all these people together wasn't on a whim."

"I know. Thanks for the hard work, Galders. But I have business in the Mage Quarter tomorrow," Jace said.

"Then I'll head off first." Galders nodded. "See you around."

Arriving in the main hall, Jace watched the dark-haired man's back as he left, still unable to set his mind at ease.

Galders was certainly much calmer and more rational than Jagin, but he was in contact with warlocks from different places around the clock. It was hard to say whether he might run into worshippers of the Old Gods.

Clearly, the Old Gods' influence had already taken root among the Dark Iron dwarves. The Ragnaros that the Dark Iron Emperor Summoned had himself been a servant of those ancient gods in ages past.

Jace couldn't do anything about the Dark Iron dwarves, but if anything leaning toward the Old Gods and their servants appeared within this warlock group, he had to root it out.
