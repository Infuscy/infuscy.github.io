## Chapter 461: The Alchemist Who Never Made Money

Jace finished writing the letter — the one asking Vereesa about the whereabouts of the Reliquary members — and sat back in his chair, thinking about his attempt to cast Shadowbolt that afternoon.

A whole afternoon of practice had left the entire basement smelling of a faint, fishy reek of shadow. Jace was a little worried the stench might drift up into the common room of the Slaughtered Lamb.

He needed to get this spell down cold as soon as possible. In fact, it ought to become the spell he was most proficient with... Given his level, pure shadow energy had to be far more powerful than fire magic laced with shadow power.

Once he'd practiced Shadowbolt enough, he could engage enemies from a distance like the powerful frost mages or fire mages in the Wizard Sanctum, instead of relying on that searing spell with its unreliable range and inconsistent damage.

He opened Jagin's spellbook, flipped to the page Jagin had shown him before. That page also recorded the Eye spell, Curse of Recklessness, and of course the Life Drain that Jagin had already taught him.

The Life Drain incantation here hadn't been corrected yet — it was still the misprinted version Jagin had originally learned.

Looks like Galders hadn't had time to organize everything Jagin had written down using his demonic-language materials. He'd leave that task to Sazeno and Molophil.

Just then, Galene Ailuo came out of the tunnel with her pack. When she saw Jace, she said, "Galders said you speak Orcish, Jace."

"A little," Jace nodded.

Galene handed him a notebook stitched together from the thick hide of some unknown creature. "Galders told you about the Shadow Council robe-making method, right, Jace? I tried translating it myself using some Orcish translation materials others had, but it was really difficult, and it's hard to guarantee there are no mistakes. This isn't a novel where an inaccurate translation doesn't matter much, so I'll leave it to you."

She said this in a cold tone. When Jace nodded, Galene thanked him and left directly.

Looks like her unsuccessful Shadowbolt practice that afternoon had really put her in a bad mood.

Jace took the notebook and opened it. It indeed had illustrations of robe patterns, designs and placements of dark sigils, plus a series of Orcish notes listing required materials and how to obtain them. Quite a few of the materials he didn't recognize.

After stuffing these things into his warlock's bag, Jace returned to Ursula's alchemy lab, dropped into a nearby chair, and let out a deep breath.

What he'd told Marin that morning about wanting to go into the library wasn't entirely an excuse. His Orcish clearly wasn't good enough yet and needed more practice.

Grammar, pronunciation rules and such he was fairly familiar with — he just lacked practice partners and opportunities to exercise them. His biggest weakness right now was vocabulary.

Ursula was over by the cabinet packing potion bottles into a crate. When she saw Jace come in, she glanced over. "Planning on sleeping here tonight, Jace?"

"I need to find an elf mage tomorrow morning and ask her a favor, so I won't bother going home tonight. Two or three hours of travel each way." He tilted his head. "Is tonight inconvenient?"

"No inconvenience. Sleep here if you want," Ursula said, smiling at him.

Jace looked at the crate of potions in her hands. "Ursula, I lost the potion pouch you gave me back at the Dark Portal. Could you give me another one?"

"Lost it?" Ursula turned around. "Alright, I'll give you mine. I'll buy another one someday. This thing's pretty expensive..."

"How much? I'll buy it," Jace said.

"About fifty or sixty silver. It needs to be custom-made by a tailor." Ursula handed him a worn pouch. "Don't you dare look down on it."

"Can't Galene help make one?" Jace asked.

"Her family makes robes," Ursula said. "They have no idea how hard an alchemist's potion pouch is to make. They'd think it's just a low-skill rag bag. If you asked her to make one, she'd think you were insulting her. She'd be lucky not to get angry."

"Then forget it." Jace pulled out a gold coin and handed it to Ursula. "Here. Consider it compensation for the last pouch I lost, Ursula."

Ursula took the coin and asked, "You're not short on money now?"

"I'm not short on a gold or two," Jace said. "Made a little profit on the trip to Lordaeron. Seemed like you were pretty tight on cash."

Ursula smiled awkwardly. "Yeah, well. I'll take it, then."

Jace knew Ursula had been making money by brewing and selling potions, flipping medicinal ingredients, and occasionally Erland would help her move product. Other members of the Dagger Society would help her gather ingredients too.

By rights, having been here this long, Ursula's skill was quite high — even Vereesa thought so.

So where did all her money go? Bought a house? Or spent it all on materials?

"By the way, where do you live?" Jace asked.

"In Stormwind, the area south of the Trade District near the city gate," Ursula said. "You never asked Tharle where I live. Why the sudden question?"

Jace said, "I'm thinking about moving. Don't know if you know any landlords. I did ask Tharle to help me find a place, but I figured I'd ask around too. Is your house rented or bought?"

"Rented, of course," Ursula said. "How could I possibly afford to buy in the Trade District? I couldn't even manage a tiny partitioned room. I can't help you there, Jace. I hardly interact with anyone in Stormwind. Most of my time I'm either here or at home. I almost never go to outside taverns. After word came about what happened to you at the Dark Portal, I agonized for a long time before going to the Lumere mother and daughter for alchemy supplies. I'm just not good with people I don't know well."

Jace nodded casually. "So you're pretty much a homebody."

"What?" Ursula asked.

"Just means you like staying indoors," Jace explained immediately.

"Right, that's what it means. Familiar places are comforting," Ursula smiled.

"Well, I'm going to sleep now." Jace leaned back. "Practicing spells today wore me out."

"Thanks for today, Jace," Ursula said. "I didn't expect to learn a spell even Galders found difficult so smoothly... Shadowbolt. Earlier, Jagin told everyone that if we could find the Shadowbolt incantation, every warlock in this group would see their power reach a whole new level."

Jace lay back in the chair, looking up at the ceiling. "I think your talent for shadow magic might be a lot stronger than you think, Ursula. I mean that sincerely."
