## Chapter 465: The Reply

That noon, Jace had just finished discussing with Galders about gathering again to teach everyone the Shadow Shield incantation so they could practice.

He was about to head to the Slaughtered Lamb to ask Tharle how the housing search was going when he walked into the Blue Heron to grab something to eat and spotted that elven mage by the wall.

He still didn't have a deep impression of Aysalin's face, but that pale purple robe and the pale golden hair were distinctive enough. He was probably right — there weren't exactly a lot of elves left in the Mage Quarter, after all.

When Jace looked over, Aysalin noticed him too. Since they were a bit of a distance apart, Jace just nodded politely, but when she saw him, she beckoned him over.

"Master Aysalin, thank you for sending that letter for me... Honestly, I couldn't have found anyone else to help." He came over and stepped aside. "Did you need something, Master?"

"Don't mention it," Aysalin said. "I wanted to let you know — I delivered Lady Vereesa Windrunner's reply to your teacher yesterday."

"That fast? I thought you said it'd be a week. It's only been three or four days..."

"That was just an estimate."

"Then I'll go find my teacher right away..."

"Hold on a moment." The elf lowered her voice. "Mr. Sesso."

"What is it, Master?" Jace asked.

Aysalin leaned in a little closer. "When you executed Azgoth with that magic, the sword you held was the Song of the Azure Sky, wasn't it?"

"Yes," Jace said.

The elf narrowed her eyes. "Do you have that sword on you right now? Could I take a look?"

Jace glanced around. "We'd need to go outside..."

The two of them left the Blue Heron and found an inconspicuous corner of the Park. Jace pulled back his cloak and drew the sheathed sword from beneath.

He'd already replaced the scabbard, swapping it for one from an ordinary longsword of similar length. When he drew it free, the arcane light in Aysalin's eyes seemed to flare up all at once.

"Could I..." The elf reached out. Jace nodded and handed her the sword.

Aysalin took the sword and drew a deep breath. It was a long moment before she spoke. "Praise the Sun — this sword is a true work of art! Actually, back in Quel'Thalas, some archmages who knew of it had questioned the Windrunner family's decision to give it away. But because Lady Vereesa Windrunner slew Quel'Thalas's sworn enemy — Teron Gorefiend — together with you, her request was hard to refuse."

"But after this business at the Dark Portal, all those doubts have vanished. What could be more romantic than the song of the Song of the Azure Sky being played at the heart of a demon-ravaged wasteland? Not to mention using the death of a lord of the abyss as the finale..."

With that, she handed the sword back. "A lord of the abyss is a legendary demon even to Quel'Thalas — only the deepest records in the archives document the Lord of the Abyss who descended upon the golden city of Zin-Azshari ten thousand years ago."

Jace took the Song of the Azure Sky and carefully slid it back into its scabbard.

"Thank you regardless, Jace Sesso. May the foul blood of demons never shake your inner peace. Now and always."

The mage bowed slightly and turned to leave. Jace watched her go in a daze, slowly beginning to understand why so many elves recognized him.

The Quel'dorei grew up surrounded by ancient Highborne legends. The Highborne were destroyed by the demon invasion that shattered the world, so to them, the opening of an alien gate and the descent of demons was the absolute, unbearable return of the apocalypse.

That he had personally slain the demon general Azgoth probably carried far more significance for the elves than it did for humans.

Though going by what Aysalin said, he seemed to have become something of a celebrity in Quel'Thalas.

He didn't know if he could live up to a reputation like that.

After all, when he thought back to that fearsome demon Azgoth — his terrifying visage, his hideous roars, his unmatched strength... Even after his weapon had been knocked off-course by magic, he'd still managed to tear a gash in the enchanted walls of the mage tower at will, nearly splitting the tower clean in half.

Against that thing, Jace's frame was no better than an ant's. When he'd started channeling the Hand of Gul'dan, that arrogant demon hadn't even bothered to glance in his direction.

And yet the one who'd lost was him.

Thinking of that, he took a deep breath and steadied himself a little, then headed for Marin's study.

When he entered, Marin was pressing a hand to his hair, looking miserably at the papers on his desk. Hearing the door, he looked up, and his tightly knotted brows finally loosened a fraction.

Jace noticed Marin's expression. "Master... what's wrong?"

"I don't know what's happened to this batch of apprentices — after half a year of study, they've gotten dumber." Marin clicked his tongue. "Right, Mr. Sesso, there's a letter here from Quel'Thalas. It's from Lady Vereesa Windrunner, for you. A reply to the last one?"

Jace nodded and took the letter his teacher handed him. "That's exactly why I came here, Master."

He hefted the envelope. It felt a bit heavy — there might be something else inside.

After thanking his teacher, he went outside and sat on a bench in the Park to open it. A silver ring slipped out as he unfolded the letter.

The ring was shaped like an eagle's head, engraved with exquisite feather-like patterns, and bore a line of Thalassian runes Jace couldn't read.

"Jace.

The Reliquary has two outposts in the south — one at the Farstrider outpost in Loch Modan, and one at Booty Bay at the tip of Stranglethorn Vale. I imagine Booty Bay is closer for you. Stormwind has ships that go straight there, but it's a chaotic place — watch yourself.

If you want the Reliquary to help you for free, bring this ring. It's my family's sigil. They won't know whether it belongs to Sylvanas or to me. Make good use of it.

This is not a gift. Remember to return it to me. If you dare sell it, there will be consequences.

Finally, I wish you and your family an early settlement in Stormwind — on the condition, of course, that you don't sell this ring.

—Vereesa Windrunner."

Reading that last line, Jace felt his image in Vereesa's mind had solidified beyond doubt — the greedy human mercenary.

Still, since the Reliquary had an outpost in Booty Bay, they must be keeping an eye on the magical artifacts the goblins were selling.

Anyway, he was going to Booty Bay to ask the goblins about Dathmora Dark-Eye, plus he needed the materials for making the robes...

Jace hadn't been keen on going back to Booty Bay, but it looked like he didn't have a choice.
