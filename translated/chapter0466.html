Chapter 466: The Difficulty of Crafting

That evening, Jace wrote out the Shadow Shield incantation, the casting gestures, and the key points to keep in mind — including the emotional requirement he and Morophil had summarized: be as cold as possible, even contemptuous. He noted it all down on a sheet of paper.

The next day, after finishing lunch, he headed to the Slaughtered Lamb at the time he’d agreed on with Zardeth.

Jalane Ayrole and Zardeth hadn’t arrived yet, but Ursula was already there waiting.

Jace recalled his earlier conversation with Jalane about those robes — Jalane had been thinking of going to Booty Bay to see if the goblins had any elemental essences for sale.

Since Ursula was right here, he might as well just ask her directly.

“Jace.” Ursula saw him come into the tunnel and called out with a smile.

“Ursula,” Jace said. “That Shadow Council robe I discussed with Jalane earlier — do you know about that robe?”

“Of course I know,” Ursula said. “She hadn’t even finished the first one before she started thinking about the second. Jace, I’ve been preparing the dyes for that robe this whole time too.”

Jace said, “I found out that robe requires elemental essences of fire and undeath. Where can I get those? Any ideas?”

“Fire, I know about,” Ursula said. “Deep in the Burning Steppes, near Blackrock Mountain, there are lots of fire elementals. Jace, the Dark Iron dwarves probably know more about it. If there were a way to defeat those fire elementals and take the fire embers they leave behind, you should be able to find alchemical raw materials in them to extract Essence of Fire.”

“Of course, that’s all from books. I’ve been wanting to get some Essence of Fire myself — several of my recipes need fire elemental essence to formulate… But going deep into Blackrock Mountain is just too dangerous. That’s where the orcs and Dark Iron dwarves are fighting…”

“And now there are black dragons added to the mix too,” Jace muttered under his breath.

“Yes, black dragons,” Ursula said. “That’s why I’ve always felt too embarrassed to ask you to go.”

“What about Essence of Undeath?” Jace asked.

“Essence of Undeath…” Ursula raised her eyebrows. “I’ve only ever heard of it. I’ve never seen it. I do have the extraction method written down here — it’s not complicated, but I’m lacking the raw materials.”

“Raw materials?” Jace asked. “What’s needed? Undead brains? Or hearts?”

“I’m pretty sure it’s hearts,” Ursula said. “Though I’ve heard destroying a zombie’s head can stop it from moving, according to the books, an undead creature’s dark heart stores far more deathly power than anywhere else.”

“Duskwood has plenty of zombies,” Jace said. “Would those zombies’ hearts work?”

“If you want to make Essence of Undeath…” Ursula shook her head. “The deathly power in those zombies probably isn’t up to snuff. This kind of essence is extracted from undead creatures that possess their own will.”

The Scourge invasion hadn’t happened yet. Where was he supposed to find that many undead creatures with their own will?

Jace nodded to show he understood. Making this robe was turning out to be a lot harder than he’d imagined.

Actually, when he’d first met Jalane Ayrole, he’d just planned to get a couple of wearable robes from her, as long as he didn’t have to keep throwing silver down the drain at Larson’s.

But when Zardeth told him about the existence of those Shadow Council orcish robes, his curiosity had been hooked instantly.

Not to mention, this robe was originally the first one prepared for him. The more he thought about it, the less it felt like crafting a robe and more like recovering something he’d lost… He just hoped the power this robe contained really lived up to the difficulty of making it.

At that moment, Zardeth appeared from the tunnel, followed by Senlora materializing from her phased state. Jalane was right behind the two of them.

“Here’s the Shadow Shield incantation.” Jace handed the sheet of paper to Zardeth.

Zardeth glanced at the incantation, read it under his breath, then frowned.

“Just getting a feel for it, I can tell it’s quite difficult. Reading it like this, I can’t even feel this incantation gathering shadow power. Why don’t you demonstrate it for us first, Jace.”

Everyone drank a little Shadow Protection Potion to guard against leaked shadow energy harming their bodies, then headed to the practice room. Jace walked to the front of the other three and said, “This spell is quite dangerous. If it’s touched, it can rebound and lash out with shadow damage at everything nearby. When I tried it with Erlan earlier, he got hurt.”

“Ah… that time.” Ursula nodded. “I remember now. Erlan complained once about being used as a target for shadow magic and getting injured.”

“That guy and his nonsense…” Jace sighed. “I didn’t know this spell would hurt the attacker. I just wanted Erlan to test its protective power, that’s all.”

“Then let me demonstrate first.”

He spoke the incantation, found the right emotional frequency, and began channeling the Shadow Shield into expansion. The others saw a thin film of shadow hovering around Jace and their eyes all went wide.

Shadow was a corrosive energy — it corroded flesh, corroded the mind… Spells that used it to protect the caster were little known. It was clear Zardeth had never seen anything like it either.

After all, this was one of Teron Gorefiend’s signature tricks.

Jace still remembered the way he’d raised the Shadow Shield in Dalaran to block Vereesa’s Arcane Shot, looking completely immovable.

And then his spell failed.

Jace watched, helpless, as a spell he’d long since mastered — one he’d practiced so many times with Grede, one he’d even managed to cast successfully in the tense environment of Uldaman — just flickered and failed. He shouldn’t have let his mind wander.

Coldness.

He didn’t know why, at the moment when he should have emptied his mind and regarded everything with indifference, he’d gone and thought about Dalaran…

Jace took a deep breath and looked at the three people around him. “Sorry. My mind wandered a bit.”

This time he emptied his mind as much as possible, recalling the feeling from when he’d practiced the Shadow Shield over and over with Morophil’s help. Finally, he managed to sustain the spell for ten seconds or so.

Then he released the channel and let the Shadow Shield dissipate, breathing out hard. “That’s it. I’m still a long way from casting this shadow magic flawlessly.”

“It’s already astonishing,” Zardeth said. “That’s the first time I’ve seen such a powerful shadow protection spell. You have to understand, the shadow protection I knew about before was just specialized shadow protection wards that blocked shadow energy specifically.”

“Let me give it a try next.”
