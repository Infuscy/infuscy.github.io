## Chapter 47: Beyond Wildest Dreams

The staff shop was called Stormwind Staffs — or rather, Storm Staffs. Jace couldn't tell if the name was supposed to mean "the staff shop of Stormwind City" or "our staffs can whip up a storm," but either way, it was a hell of a name.

The shopkeeper, a man of about thirty, wore a white shirt and neatly combed mid-length hair, his beard clean and closely trimmed. He was turning a short, branch-like wand over in his hands, examining it closely.

Then he glanced over and said coldly, "I don't serve outsiders."

"I'm picking out a staff for my boss."

"Who's your boss?"

"Eden Marin. I'm his assistant." Jace had gotten very used to throwing Eden Marin's name around to clear a path.

"Master Marin?" The shopkeeper set down his wand. "Very well. I'm Allen Hafgan. Mage Marin is a regular here. He mentioned the other day that he'd taken on an assistant named Jace…"

"Sesso," Jace supplied. "Jace Sesso."

"Mr. Sesso." Allen Hafgan smiled. "What brings you in today? Mage Marin was just here yesterday — said he wanted to swap out the power crystal on his staff, and he'd already placed an order. Did he change his mind about the specifications?"

"Is that so?" Jace paused. "I thought my boss wanted to replace the whole staff entirely, so I came here today to ask about a suitable new one. Didn't expect he'd come in himself first — I hadn't heard about that. Well, if the boss has his own plans… I won't take up any more of your time."

"Feel free to come again." The shopkeeper had just picked his wand back up when Jace quickly said, "Oh, right — the other day I saw a convoy of wagons transporting orc prisoners through Redridge."

"Hmm?"

The shopkeeper set the wand down and raised his eyes. There was a hint of impatience in his gaze, but since they'd been having such a pleasant chat, it seemed a bit rude to show any outright negativity now.

Jace pressed on. "Among those orc prisoners was a warlock wearing a robe that seemed to be covered in orcish magical runes. But more importantly, the soldier escorting him was carrying a very strange staff."

The moment he said "staff," the shopkeeper's expression shifted. He carefully placed the short wand into a box and leaned in a little. "Could you describe the appearance of that staff in detail?"

"The shaft was black." Jace put on an expression of careful recollection. "But it seemed to have purple or green patterns on it too — I couldn't say for sure what color, exactly. Not purely purple, anyway. It was like it was shot through with some other color of light. The soldier was using the stick as a walking cane, and the sound it made touching the ground didn't sound like metal or wood… it was really, really strange. I also noticed there was a mounting at the top of the staff, apparently for holding a power gem."

"That's right, only the power gem is missing." Allen Hafgan explained, "There were many warlocks like this during the war. After the Horde's defeat, they lost their access to shadow gems, so all they had left was their staff housing. But even without a gem, their housings still held a certain amount of power — they could amplify a warlock's dark magic. Remarkable, isn't it?"

"Can't our staffs do that?" Jace asked.

The shopkeeper said, "Some can, but they have to be made from wood of Quel'Thalas. The trees of the elven kingdom have been steeped in the Sunwell for so long that they're no longer ordinary trees — they're all enchanted magic-wood, like star-wood. The staffs they make are the best. Even Dalaran… well, Dalaran has its own methods now, but I still believe they can't compare to Quel'Thalas's natural materials. The alternative is to skip wood entirely and use metal — mithril, for instance, or something even stronger, like thorium. Or even the bones of magical creatures — dragon bones, for example. But those are treasures you can only come across by chance; there's no way to mass-produce them, and the price is unimaginable."

"So what material are the orc warlocks' staffs made from?" Jace asked again, since that was what he actually wanted to know.

Allen Hafgan looked Jace over for a moment, then rubbed his fingers together. "My knowledge isn't free, lad. Twenty copper."

Jace dug through his pockets and scraped together twelve coppers, laying them out on the counter. "That's all I've got."

"Fair enough," the shopkeeper said. "I know a fair bit about Draenor's minerals. After all, before the Expedition crossed the Dark Portal, they needed to do some surveying of the natural landscape and environmental resources on the other side. As it happens, I did research on that a few years back when I was in Hillsbrad, and I interviewed some orcish prisoners too. Most orc warlock staffs are made from a type of wood that's been heavily influenced by shadow energy but hasn't rotted. Those trees grow in a place called Shadowmoon Valley."

The moment Hafgan mentioned Shadowmoon Valley, Jace knew he wasn't talking nonsense.

There really was a place on Draenor called Shadowmoon Valley. It was one of the regions most ravaged by fel corruption. If there were trees tough enough to be touched by dark power without dying, it'd make sense to use them as raw material for warlock staffs.

Hafgan gave Jace a sidelong glance, looking rather like he was enjoying the process of showing off his knowledge.

"The second most common material is Draenor's unique minerals. The main one is a metal called fel iron. But fel iron is tainted by fel energy and unstable in nature — you can use it to make armor, but not staffs. It needs further refinement. You fuse it with a rare mineral called khorium to stabilize the fel iron, producing a metal the orcs call felsteel. Only, felsteel is emerald-green. I've seen one felsteel staff."

"If it's a black staff and you can't tell whether it's metal or wood…" The shopkeeper stroked his chin, thinking for a moment, then asked, "Tell you what — could it be a demon's staff? Demons aren't what most people think — mindless, rampaging beasts. Many demons wield magical power and need staffs to amplify their spells. The orcs collaborate with demons, so it's possible some warlocks carry staffs provided by demons. If you had the physical object, I could make a more accurate judgment."

"Are there any demon staffs available for purchase?" Jace asked.

"Ha." Allen Hafgan gave a strained laugh. "That's not a funny joke. Only a goblin would consider selling something like that openly, and the price would be terrifying. There was an eredar warlock's mystic staff auctioned at Booty Bay once — it sold for two hundred and forty gold. I heard the buyer was an elf. Oh, do you know about the eredar?"

"Aliens," Jace said.

"That's right, aliens. Otherworldly beings, just like the orcs — but ten times more dangerous."

Leaving the staff shop and arriving at Marin's study, Jace swept the room and realized he had no real sense of what two hundred gold even meant.

Seven gold would already have been frightening, but once a number climbed that high, it was probably like hearing a billionaire's net worth — you just took it as a story.
