## Chapter 470: The Collector

Following Mithershield to the back mountain of Booty Bay, they walked through a path lined with palms and arrived at a beach.

From here Jace could see the other side of the bay where Stormwind's ships were docked, and the statue of Revilgaz out at sea, but the trading port and half of Booty Bay were completely hidden behind the sea cliffs.

Just off this beach, Jace could already make out several ships of different sizes anchored out on the water. One of them might have been a pirate ship.

But they probably wouldn't make trouble this close to Booty Bay — after all, there were guards loyal to Trade Prince Revilgaz keeping watch here too.

"Don't be nervous," Mithershield said, as if he'd read Jace's mind. "Even the Southsea Pirates come here to do business. They wouldn't dare make trouble on the Trade Cartel's turf."

As he walked, he waved to a few sailors in white shirts sitting on the beach around a bonfire, roasting meat, swords and long knives at their hips. Those men smiled back — they seemed quite familiar with him.

Mithershield brought Jace over to a group of goblins in red clothes and chatted with them in the goblin tongue, then pulled a seal out of his pocket.

The goblins examined the seal, nodded, and made a path for them. One of the goblins hopped onto the small boat they were guarding, signaling for Jace and Mithershield to get on.

Mithershield hopped on without hesitation. Jace glanced at the goblins around him and climbed aboard too, though his hand under his cloak stayed gripped on the hilt of Azurewrath.

The goblin worked the oars against the waves, rowing seaward with all his might. By the time they reached the red sailboat among the anchored ships, Jace was nearly shaken sick.

"Welcome, welcome, Mr. Mithershield! And this friend of unknown identity!"

A sharp, shrill shout came from the boat, but Jace couldn't make out who was yelling from below deck.

They climbed the lowered ladder to the top, and saw a gobl in a light yellow luxurious outer robe spreading his arms wide as he came forward, embracing Mithershield tightly.

"Mr. Porkodiaz, very good to see you again." Mithershield extricated himself from Porkodiaz's grasp and tugged at Jace's sleeve. "This is Jack Slovakia, a human mage who came here from Stormwind — or maybe Lordaeron."

"Mr. Slovakia, this is the ship's owner, Porkodiaz!"

"I can see he's a human, Mithershield." The goblin called Porkodiaz gave Mithershield an awkward smile.

Mithershield said, "You don't know him, Mr. Porkodiaz. If you'd known him earlier, you'd have a lot in common!"

"I'm from Stormwind," Jace said. "Pleased to meet you, Mr. Porkodiaz."

"A fellow who speaks with a Lordaeron accent and claims he's from Stormwind." Porkodiaz twitched his nose. "He makes a very good first impression on me. I think we can be friends!"

"I knew it would go smoothly, I just knew it," Mithershield laughed.

Porkodiaz clapped Mithershield on the shoulder. "You seem to be in a great mood, Mithershield. Paid off all your debts to Revilgaz? Last time he said he wanted to use your blood to lubricate the gears of his automatic barber machine at home."

Mithershield's face chilled. "Don't always bring that guy up. I came this time to help Mr. Slovakia here solve a small problem — a problem that nobody in Booty Bay besides you can solve."

"Ah, ah — since you put it that way." Porkodiaz shook his head and looked at Jace. "You're not a Kirin Tor mage, are you?"

"Of course not," Jace said, as he unslung the staff from his back and untied the elven silk wrapping it.

When Porkodiaz saw the Gorefiend gem underneath, his eyes lit up at once.

"This..."

"This is my collection, Mr. Porkodiaz," Jace said.

The ship's owner glanced at the alchemist beside him and nodded. "You do know some extraordinary friends, Bacchus."

"Hah!" Mithershield laughed. "Of course I do."

"Come with me, lad. Tell me your difficulty — let's see if there's anything I can help with." Porkodiaz patted Jace on the knee. "Wait here a moment, Mr. Mithershield. The sea wind on deck is too loud — I'd like to have a proper talk with him."

"Funny, it's been ages since I came aboard a ship to enjoy the sea view and the ocean breeze." Mithershield headed for the stairs leading up to the higher deck, humming a tune as he went.

The ship's owner led Jace down the deck, into the corridor of the cabin, and finally into a luxuriously furnished small room.

Though it was a room meant for goblin habitation, the furniture was all sized for a human, so Jace didn't feel uncomfortable here.

"Have a seat anywhere." Porkodiaz nimbly hopped onto a wooden chair covered in the fur of some unidentifiable creature. "Now you can tell me your difficulty."

"It's about a warlock — Dasmora Darkeye," Jace said.

"Dasmora Darkeye." Porkodiaz said, "I knew this name would find its way back here one day. You know this isn't a real name, right."

"I know," Jace said.

"Tell me what you're planning first, Mr. Slovakia." Porkodiaz looked up. "Are you looking to buy the Dasmora Darkeye collection from me? Or do you just want to know some of the leads I have about her?"

"I'd like to borrow your collection," Jace said.

The smile on Porkodiaz's face vanished. He polished his dark brown pipe. "If Mithershield hadn't brought you, I'd throw you off this boat immediately, Mr. Slovakia. You know some things lose all their value once they're borrowed a single time. You're practically making a joke of me."

"I don't need any magical items or consumable treasures, Mr. Porkodiaz," Jace said. "I just need something Dasmora Darkeye touched. Anything will do — it could be a piece of paper, it could be a staff, it could even be a small knife..."

"I know what you're trying to do," Porkodiaz laughed. "You think only mages know about the existence of magical traces? You think only you mages know how to use sympathy to track a spellcaster? There are countless mages among goblins, and our kind are spread across the entire world — and I don't just mean the Eastern Kingdoms, I don't even just mean Kalimdor... I've searched for Dasmora Darkeye's trail too. She's a remarkably mysterious demonologist, but to this day I don't even know whether she's a person or a ghost. So you're planning to find her all on your own?"

"That's exactly what I'm thinking," Jace said.

The goblin fiddled with the tobacco in a small box as he spoke. "I'm not an arrogant goblin, Mr. Slovakia. What I mean is, the human kingdoms certainly have secrets we don't possess, but let's just say — I hope you have some special method. After all, right now you're taking on the work of several years of my own effort all by himself."

He looked up. "Well, several years of effort isn't really much either. But I don't have that long to live in the first place — I'm not an elf or a naga. I can't devote all my energy to tracking down some inexplicable warlock. After all, the Dark Portal is right here. There are more worthwhile leads to follow... more precious things."

"I have my own ways," Jace said.

"Here's what I can do — I'll help you, but if you find any leads, remember to come back and tell me everything in full," Porkodiaz said. "I'm not paying much attention to this demonologist anymore, but at least... I'm still a little interested."

"Of course, Mr. Porkodiaz," Jace nodded.

Porkodiaz took a light draw from his pipe, narrowed his eyes, then said, "Oh right — since you're also a mage who pays attention to demons, do you know about that battle where the Alliance coalition fought demons at the Dark Portal a few months ago?"

"Of course," Jace said.

"They say a terrible demon lord was killed by an Alliance mage. Damn it — such a precious collection, and it got carried off by a bunch of humans." Porkodiaz scratched inside his ear. "If it were me, I'd have that demon's head mounted on the prow of my ship. Even if I got poisoned to death by fel and shadow energy, it'd be worth it."

"Do you know that demon lord?" he suddenly asked.

"I know him," Jace said. "Azgoth."

"And do you know what kind of demon that demon was?" the goblin asked again.

"An Annihilan. A Pit Lord," Jace said.

The goblin smiled. "I have here a ritual dagger that's said to have been used by Dasmora. This dagger has already lost its magic, but the mages I hired tracked the residual magical trail on the dagger to an ancient centaur sanctuary and couldn't follow it any further... Do you know the centaur?"

"Maraudon," Jace said.

"My god, looks like I've been underestimating you," the goblin laughed. "You might actually find something. Wait here — I'll go get it for you right now."
