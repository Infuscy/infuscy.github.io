## Chapter 498: The Abandoned Village

That afternoon, Jace met Grede outside the small inn that sat along the road to Stormwind's main gate. He noticed the area around the gate was already crawling with scaffolding and workers coming and going.

The bridge over the Valley of Heroes had basically been rebuilt. Jace had already spotted it on the way back from the Swamp of Sorrows — the pedestal right in front of the gate, the one belonging to Marshal Turalyon, was finished.

After staring at the workers atop the gate for a moment, Grede set his bags down beside him and asked, "What are you looking at?"

Jace turned around and found him wearing a black metal helmet of a distinctly aggressive design — its crest looked like a broken blade jutting off the top.

"What the hell is that?" he asked, staring at the dwarf's helmet. "Where'd you get that?"

"Didn't you ask me if there was a reward?" Grede said, slapping his helmet. "This is the reward. You have any idea how long I had to search to find one that actually fit my head?"

"Hold on," Jace said, blinking. "Is that a Felguard's helmet?"

"That's right." Grede grinned. "You have any idea how much fire and shadow resistance this ugly thing has? A Mo'arg breathing flame right in my face, and all I've got to do is tilt my head and I'm fine — assuming the gorget's up to snuff, of course."

"Still, it doesn't really fit you," Jace said, wobbling the helmet by its broken-blade crest. "It's bigger than your head. Feels like it'd fall right off."

Grede laughed. "Once I transform, it'll fit perfectly!"

"And what if your next form's even bigger?" Jace asked.

"Uh." Grede patted the helmet. "A smith can always modify it, make it roomier."

"Never mind that. The important thing — did you get the holy water, Grede?"

"Of course." Grede said, "When the priests at the Cathedral of Light found out I'd fought at the Dark Portal, they gave me extra. Enough that we could practically drink it. Though for dealing with ghosts, I'm guessing your magic's going to be essential too. How many mana potions did you bring?"

Jace patted the fresh potion pouch at his belt — Ursula's recent restock. "Plus three potions my alchemist gave me before the last trip. The whole thing went so smoothly we never ran into a real fight, so I never touched them."

Come to think of it, last time he'd gone down to the cellar to meet with Sulenna and Erland about the new robes, Ursula hadn't been there either.

He had the feeling she'd been spending less time in the cellar these past few days.

Still, as long as that felhound Basadim was still crouched down there guarding the door and behaving himself, she should probably be fine.

Ever since the Dark Portal campaign had kicked off, Nightvale had become more familiar to Jace than Goldshire. He'd passed through it several times as a way station, and after the Dark Portal fighting ended, the town had actually gotten livelier than before.

What's more, more and more people were calling it Nightvale now. Jace could clearly feel the name "Grange" slowly being forgotten.

They set out from Raven Hill again and followed the supply wagons heading to Nethergarde Keep for two days, deep into the valley at the heart of Deadwind Pass. When Jace and Grede told the caravan they were getting off here, everyone on the wagon was a little surprised.

After all, every time they passed through the valley, every caravan and patrol wanted to get through as quickly as possible — nobody wanted to linger a second longer than they had to. The last thing anyone expected was for someone to actually get off here.

After the caravan leader talked himself blue trying to talk them out of it, and finally confirmed that these two idiots weren't looking to throw their lives away but genuinely had business here, the wagons agreed to move on.

Jace led Grede toward the ruined village he and Obryk's team had spotted on their last trip here — the one tucked away in a distant mountain hollow. By now it was the middle of the night. Both of them were exhausted, so they planned to scout the place out and rest for the night.

From a distance, the village was just an abandoned settlement at the mouth of the pass — seven or eight houses, a few broken livestock pens that might have held cows or pigs, dull and unremarkable.

But as they reached the village entrance, with the grey mountain ridges in the distance, the grim blackened deadwood all around them, and a chill, unsettling stench of shadow hanging in the air, Jace couldn't help the prickling at the back of his neck as he stared at those pitch-black doorways.

He glanced sideways at Grede. The dwarf's gaze was flicking back and forth between the different buildings. He was clearly rattled too.

"Two soldiers who stood and fought the demon army head-on at the Dark Portal… I think we're making fools of ourselves here, Jace Sesso," the dwarf said.

Jace gripped Azuresong at his belt. "You can never be too careful."

"I think we'd be better off sleeping in the forest than in this village," Grede suggested.

"There are ogres in the forest," Jace said. "You haven't heard?"

"Well, at least I'd hear an ogre coming," Grede said.

"Remember ogre rogues?" Jace asked. "If they've got orc sages, you think ogre thieves are far behind?"

Grede moved up to the doorway of a small house and peered inside. "I still don't believe ogre rogues are a real thing."

"But ogres have mages — you know that much, right?" Jace asked.

"The two-headed kind," Grede said. "I know."

They moved toward the next building, a larger two-story house. Jace pushed open the half-closed door and glanced inside, then said, "You know footsteps can be silenced with arcane magic. There's a spell that makes a person so light they float down like a feather from any height and land without a sound. So even an ogre, with a mage's help, could sneak up in the middle of the night and slice your kidneys out."

Grede straightened his lower back. "Sounds pretty nice, actually."

"Huh?" Jace said.

"I mean," Grede said, "that slow-fall spell — if every Gryphon Rider had a mage cast one on them before battle… no, can't do it. Gryphon Riders can never abandon the brothers they fight beside."

Jace pulled Sazeno out of the bag and set him on the ground. "Go check out the upstairs, Sazeno. We'll search down here."

"You're such a coward, master!" Sazeno said. "You should've brought Morophil. That banshee would charge into the other side of the Dark Portal for one sip of your life force, let alone climb a flight of stairs!"

Despite his words, he darted up the stairs in a flash, leaving behind a trail of faintly fel-lit footprints.

Grede edged closer and shouldered Jace, asking in a low voice, "That 'one sip of your life force' — did I hear that right?"
