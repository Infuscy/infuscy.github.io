Chapter 500: The Gloomy Valley

The grey valley looked the same in every direction. Jace and Grede found a small room to sleep in, and when they woke up the next day, it was still a murky darkness.

The clouds overhead were black and heavy, poisoned through with shadow power. Jagged thickets of trees covered the hills and ridges, looking ready to spit out some monstrous insect or beast at any moment.

Following the marked positions on the map, they walked southwest for the better part of the day before Jace spotted a tall windmill on the bank of a dried-up riverbed.

When he first saw the windmill, he almost thought it was a tower — and that he was looking at the real Karazhan… until he made out the broken fan blades, which put his mind slightly at ease.

The two of them skirted along the edge of a completely fallow field toward the town. As they walked, Grede suddenly grabbed Jace's shirt and yanked him aside.

"Look over there." The dwarf pointed across the riverbank. Jace squinted and finally made out a tall, swaying grey figure inching slowly forward.

It was an ogre — a gaunt, two-headed ogre, dragging a long staff behind it. Behind it trailed a few low, shuffling shadows. Jace used Detect Demons and saw they were a pack of felhounds.

Looked like a fair number of ogres who'd fled the Dark Portal had settled here. Before long, a sturdier ogre emerged from behind the felhounds, hauling a crude warsword and wearing scraps of battered armor.

"Those two won't be easy to deal with," Grede said. "Let's wait for them to pass…"

"They're heading toward the village," Jace said. "And look how relaxed they are — barely keeping watch. Could this village already be occupied by ogres?"

"Could be," Grede said. "Good thing we spotted this big bastard ahead of time. Otherwise we'd have blundered straight into the village and gotten ourselves in trouble."

They waited quietly for a while, then suddenly a burst of dumb laughter erupted from the village in the distance.

Ogre laughter. They'd called it exactly right.

"I was hoping to find wandering ghosts in this village," Jace muttered. "Looks like even if there were any, the ogres would've chased them out."

"Let's go around. Find somewhere else," Grede said.

The two changed course, heading out toward the field, putting as much distance between themselves and the sounds of the ogres as they could. When they reached the edge of a small wooded area, the imp said, "Master… I sense a soul nearby."

"Do you have the goggles, Grede?" Jace asked.

The dwarf nodded, put on the goggles, and looked around, then pointed toward a stretch of abandoned farmland. "There… there, I see it."

The two of them hurried over. As they reached the edge of the field, Jace watched a phantom figure slowly solidify — a soul in tattered clothes. The moment it saw Jace and Grede, it let out a shriek and bellowed, "Get away! Don't come near me! This is mine… I can never leave this place… I can never leave this place!"

Grede set the machine down. The arcane core in the middle of it had already started to glow.

Did that mean this soul could yield necrotic essence?

"It's going after you, Grede!" Jace said, clutching a bottle of holy water. "Watch out!"

"Sazeno, get ready!"

Grede stepped aside to dodge the hunter soul's lunge, and the imp immediately started cranking the handle. The soul tried to hurl itself at Grede again, but seemed to get yanked backward, drifting toward the machine as if pulled by something.

This little machine didn't look like much, but its pull on the soul was surprisingly strong. With each turn of the crank, the ghost was drawn closer.

Jace quickly pulled an empty vial from his pack and fitted it to the outlet tube, watching as the soul was sucked in. It came out the other end as a wisp of pale purple mist that slowly settled into a faint dusting of purple powder at the bottom of the vial.

"That's it?" Grede sighed.

"Maybe it's because this soul is just too weak," Jace said. "Let's keep looking. This is a good start at least."

"The machine's magic core is getting weaker, damn it!" the imp shouted, pointing at the device. "Take a look, master! At this rate, we won't even fill a vial before the power runs out!"

Jace leaned in and examined the arcane core. It did seem a little dimmer than when they'd first pulled it out.

Then again, could it be that the soul had vanished, so it was no longer reacting?

Did this thing actually need recharging?

Jace pulled out the instruction manual and flipped through it. He couldn't find anything about recharging.

But looking at the dense block of Goblin-language instructions next to the comparatively short Common translation…

"Do you speak Goblin, Grede?" Jace asked.

"How the hell would I?! You'd have better luck asking the imp," Grede said.

"Then we're definitely out of luck," Jace said helplessly.

That's when he thought of the Flowingfrost Crystal.

It was arcane, and both it and this machine needed recharging… could the Flowingfrost Crystal restore this thing's power?

He took the Flowingfrost Crystal out of its case and brought it near the machine — and sure enough, the core brightened slightly.

"It works, it works!" Sazeno laughed. "This little bastard actually works!"

Jace handed the Flowingfrost Crystal to Grede. "Carry this, Grede. Keep it in the same bag as the machine so it recharges continuously. Don't want to run into a powerful soul and find out we're out of juice."

Grede nodded and packed everything away, then asked, "What if we didn't had this crystal and the machine ran out of power? What then?"

"How the fuck should I know," Jace said. "When we get back, I'm going to have a serious talk with that goblin."

The two of them packed up and set off again, heading toward the next village settlement. This time, though, they'd have to cut through a stretch of woodland between the valleys.

The farther south they went, the less light there was, and the darker it got.

It was three or four in the afternoon, but it might as well have been the middle of the night. The sliver of sunlight that filtered through the shadow-choked clouds was dimmer than the light of the two moons on a night in Elwynn Forest.

They walked for hours without finding a second soul wandering the wilds, but by now Jace had the feeling that someone was following them from behind.

Treasure hunters?
