## Chapter 51: A Valuable Spell

Zardeth's voice was deeply magnetic, and he spoke with a gentle, amiable manner — the sort of person who had clearly been raised in a noble household.

But whenever Jace looked at him, he couldn't shake the feeling that something sinister clung to the man.

Jace didn't think he had any special ability to see through someone's magic. He was just an ordinary guy. If even an ordinary fellow like him could tell something was off, then mages and priests would be all over it.

How had this man managed to stay in Stormwind without being discovered? And where did that ominous aura even come from?

Jace followed him to the practice room. Gakin was already waiting inside.

"Let him have a look, Zardeth."

Zardeth nodded. "You can show yourself now, Zigi."

Who was he talking to?

Just as Jace was wondering, a flash of arcane light flickered at Zardeth's feet, and a small figure suddenly materialized from it.

"As you command, master..."

An imp!

A real demon!

Jace stared at the imp crouching on the ground. It had a pair of forward-jutting horns, bulging eyes glowing with an eerie yellow light, and was about the size of a medium-sized dog. Its body was covered in what looked like gray flames that rippled and swayed with its movements.

Gakin said proudly, "Summoning used to be an extremely difficult branch of magic for Azeroth's dark arts practitioners. But all that changed when the Dark Portal opened. It drew us closer to the Nether — and closer to the creatures we summon."

"And made it much easier for me to serve my master!" the imp called Zigi added excitedly.

When Jace saw the demon, he realized he'd genuinely misjudged this warlock circle. It turned out some of them actually knew what they were doing.

"Quiet, Zigi." With that, Zardeth turned to Jace.

"Imps like this can be a great help. They can turn invisible and move about unseen. But maintaining invisibility requires intense concentration, and imps... aren't exactly known for their focus. Once they go invisible, all they can do is follow you — the moment their attention slips, they become visible again and expose themselves to everyone. So if you don't want to cause trouble for yourself and for us, you'd better not set it on any foolish errands. You know what I mean. There are people in Stormwind who know even more about demons than we do, and they won't tolerate demons running wild in the city."

Jace turned the words over in his mind. "You're going to teach me the incantation to summon an imp?"

"That's what I mean by a spell of tremendous value," Gakin said. "You're not scared, are you, Jace? Surena and Erlan refused to learn imp summoning — they don't want anything to do with demons. And Ursula... she seems to lack the talent for summoning demons."

Zardeth said, "According to the hermits in Ironforge, in this age when the Dark Portal has already opened, any warlock who can't even summon an imp doesn't deserve to call themselves a warlock at all. But those Ironforge lot are arrogant — we don't need to pay them any mind. Ursula is a useful companion. Even if she can't summon an imp, she's still a great asset to the group. So don't pressure yourself. Even if you can't master it right away, you can still contribute to this circle in other ways."

Summoning a demon — Jace couldn't say he had no reservations. But this was indeed an extremely valuable incantation. Gakin wasn't exaggerating.

"I came here to learn new things."

"Excellent." Gakin walked to the wall in front of the two of them, faced the open space ahead, and said, "Let me demonstrate—"

"Leineia, Taeni, Anwei, Suolemi, Oerjiasuobimusi."

As Gakin chanted, wisps of shadow light began to gather around him. The glow was faint — even in this dim chamber it was only barely perceptible — but the shadows seemed to grow clearer with each word, their edges sharpening, as though condensing into runic characters that imprinted themselves onto the floor.

Before long, a rift appeared among the runes — a black rift, blacker than the lightless shadows of the chamber. Then a small creature leaped out of it.

"Who... where have you brought me?"

This imp looked different from Zardeth's. Zardeth's imp was gray all over; this one was dark yellow.

"They might not be the same kind of demon," Jace said.

"Quite possible," Zardeth explained. "Imps come in many varieties. They don't even recognize each other, though we don't fully understand it yet."

"You can try now," Gakin said. "Need me to repeat it?"

"No need." Jace was confident in his short-term memory.

He mimicked Gakin's gestures and chanted: "Leineia, Taeni, Anwei, Suolemi, Oerjiasuobimusi."

"Perfect," Zardeth murmured. "You're a natural caster, Jace Sesso. No question about it."

As the incantation took hold, shadow-forged runes materialized, and an imp leaped out of the rift.

Jace felt a wave of dizziness, as if something had been torn out of him from the inside. A hollow, drained sensation hit him hard.

He steadied himself and got a clear look at the little demon he'd summoned. It was dark blue from head to toe, chattering away incessantly, though he hadn't the faintest idea what it was saying.

"I can't understand it. Is that normal?"

"This weak little thing doesn't know our language," Gakin said. "And you don't need to communicate with it either. Now I'm going to teach you how to put this demon down. Remember — this incantation can only terminate your own summoned demon. You can't use it to kill other demons."

Jace nodded. It was probably some kind of contract-bound dark magic that strangled the summoned creature from within. He could more or less guess.

Gakin chanted: "Bimusiennuoxi, Kuteyinni, Ailamaxijin."

The moment Gakin's incantation ended, the earthy-yellow-skinned imp he'd summoned let out an agonized scream, as if its throat had been seized. It shrieked something in broken Common — "Why? Why?" — then vanished in a burst of shadowy flame. The whole room brightened for an instant, then darkened again.

Zardeth frowned. He clearly didn't care for that display.

The imp Jace had summoned panicked as well. As Jace began chanting the same incantation, it screamed wildly — but it didn't try to flee, nor did it attempt to fight back against anyone in the room.

The incantation completed, the blue imp too dissolved into a ball of flame before everyone's eyes. A burnt, sulfuric stench filled the room.

"Don't be like Black Claw." Gakin shot a disdainful glance at Zardeth. "He wanted to befriend an imp. Absolutely ridiculous."

Zardeth smiled. "Don't try to control what other people think, Gakin. Mutual respect is the foundation of working together."
