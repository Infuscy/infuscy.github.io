Chapter 511: Starting Point, Ending Point

They arrived at Booty Bay a week later. The journey had been uneventful, and there’d been no trouble on the ship either.

Feeling the salty wind hit his face, Jace figured this had to be a good omen for the journey to Kalimdor that lay ahead.

Once the ship docked, Grede came up onto the pier and looked around. “Well, I’ll be. Not a lick of change since I was last here. This is probably the only place in the whole south that wasn’t touched by the Orc Wars!”

“As long as there’s money to be made, don’t matter who you make it from,” Jace said, coming up beside him. “Orcs, demons — if it weren’t for the Kirin Tor, goblins would be doing business with the undead too!”

“That’s a little scary, Jace,” Grede said.

Jace was hardly making it up. In the years to come, the Steamwheedle Cartel’s trade and cooperation with the Horde would grow more and more frequent — and the Horde included the undead that called themselves the Forsaken.

And their competitor, the Bilgewater Cartel, would go and join the Horde outright, becoming allies of the dead.

Booty Bay didn’t have much of a Winter Veil atmosphere. A port at the southern tip of the continent didn’t exactly have much of a winter feel to it, and there weren’t many Winter Veil-loving dwarves stationed here either. By the time they arrived, it was almost enough to make you forget what season it was.

Grede asked, “So where to next? Go find Vereesa, or find somewhere to have some fun first?”

Jace said, “Let me stop by the Reliquary’s office first. Then I’ll head to that goblin’s place and sell the Essence of Undeath — that’ll cover some travel expenses. Fun… what do you even want to do?”

“Fishing.” Grede pointed to the midsection of the harbor, where a small pier hung with all kinds of big fish and little fish, then pointed to the far end of Booty Bay. “Or we could check out the Auction House.”

“Or haven’t you been to the Salty Sailor Tavern? There’s more to do there, more interesting stuff…”

“Let’s save our money,” Jace said.

“Ah, we finally get here and this is it,” Grede said helplessly.

When they arrived at the Reliquary’s quarters, Jace knocked on the door. It was opened by the same examiner as last time, Sirith Lightchaser. This time, when he saw Jace come in, he said with some excitement, “Mr. Jace Sesso — it is you, I was right! A friend of Lady Vereesa from the Wizard’s Sanctum, a young human interested in demonology and the shadow — there really wasn’t much room for any other possibility.”

“It is me, Mr. Sirith,” Jace nodded.

Sirith led the two of them inside. “Silvermoon has already informed me — you’ve accepted the mission to travel to Kalimdor in search of an unknown Highborne shadow mage.”

“Right,” Jace said. “The Reliquary found me a Farstrider. Do you know when that person can get to Booty Bay?”

“The one from House Goldthorn — within three or four days,” Sirith Lightchaser said. “This little house doesn’t have enough guest rooms for the two of you, so I can book you and Mr. Grede Thunderfist four nights at the Salty Sailor Tavern…”

“You can book rooms at the Salty Sailor Tavern?” Grede asked in surprise.

That was exactly what Jace had been about to ask. Looked like Grede had also gotten a taste of the Salty Sailor Tavern’s attitude before.

“Of course I can,” Sirith said. “The Reliquary gets along well enough with Booty Bay’s ruler, Baron Revilgaz.”

As he spoke, he walked into the back room and came back with a cloth map, spreading it out in front of the two of them. “Now let me handle the matter of your passage tickets.”

“This is a map of southern Kalimdor, drawn by the Steamwheedle Cartel. A rough topographical chart of the Tanaris desert and the mountains and wastelands to the north of the desert. Goblins have explored this area far more thoroughly than we or any other race have, Mr. Sesso.”

Jace glanced at the map. Tanaris did match his impressions of the game quite well — including the ancient capital of the sand trolls, Zul’Farrak, the relative positions of Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port, and the canyon leading into the Shimmering Flats from the north…

Honestly, what intrigued him most wasn’t any of those places — it was the Caverns of Time.

The Caverns of Time was the home of the bronze dragons, deep in Tanaris. Led by the bronze Aspect Nozdormu, the great dragons monitored the convergence of countless timelines, keeping the flow of time from being corrupted and destroyed.

The Caverns of Time didn’t appear on this map, though. It seemed the goblins had explored the Tanaris desert inside and out and still hadn’t discovered the place.

Of course, Jace wasn’t surprised in the least. Setting aside whether the bronze dragons would want to be found by outsiders, perhaps in this world even the bronze dragons and the Caverns of Time existed in a way completely different from the game.

“If we sail from here to Kalimdor, can we only go to Gadgetzan?” Jace asked.

“Kalimdor is not the Eastern Kingdoms, Mr. Sesso,” Sirith explained. “In Kalimdor, you can only operate out of goblin-built outposts. Neither humans, dwarves, nor Quel’dorei have managed to establish any other settlements there — or at least, any settlements friendly to ordinary folk. Who knows how many smuggling dens or pirate nests are out there?”

“At present, the goblins only have one port in Kalimdor where a large ship from this side can safely dock — Steamwheedle Port in Tanaris. As far as I know, the Steamwheedle Cartel is indeed trying to build a second port, currently sited along the middle of Kalimdor’s eastern coast. But the goblins couldn’t defeat the quilboar and murlocs living there, and their funds have run dry… so I don’t have much faith in this investment.”

The second port Sirith mentioned was Ratchet.

In the game, Ratchet had grown into a place far livelier than Steamwheedle Port, because it sat between the future orc capital of Orgrimmar and the humans’ largest settlement in Kalimdor, Theramore, serving as a neutral trading hub.

Hearing what Sirith said, Jace understood why Ratchet, though supposedly neutral in the game, always leaned more toward the Horde. After the orcs crossed the Great Sea and arrived in Kalimdor, they’d defeated several quilboar and harpy tribes before settling in that area — which had laid the foundation for the goblins to build a new port there.

Still, that goblin collector Pokodiaz had said his mercenaries had already tracked the magical trace on the Eldre’Thalas dagger to Maraudon.

Maraudon was sacred ground of the centaur, located farther north than the area this map covered — deep in Kalimdor’s interior.

If they set out from Steamwheedle Port, there was no telling what they’d have to cross to get there.

These few dozen gold were definitely not going to come easy.

Sirith said, “Mr. Jace Sesso, Mr. Grede Thunderfist, plus the Farstrider — three in total. I’d better buy your return tickets here, because if you arrive on the other side without a return ticket, those bloody goblins will skin you alive. They know you have no way back except on their ships.”

“That comes to seven gold and twenty silver for the passage.”

“Seven gold? That… we don’t have to pay that, do we?” Grede asked.

“No,” Sirith said. “The three of you will have your travel expenses covered by the Farstrider and the Reliquary. But any other expenses along the way — we won’t be reimbursing those.”

“That’s enough,” Grede said with a huge sigh of relief.

When Sirith mentioned “three people,” he’d emphasized the number especially, then given Jace a meaningful glance at the end.

Jace remembered when they’d arrived at Booty Bay, the goblin had stressed that even a warlock’s summoned demons had to pay for tickets.

Don’t tell me that going to Kalimdor, he’d have to buy ship tickets for the succubus and the imp too?
