## Chapter 512: Everything in This World Has a Price

The Sailor's Home was a rough-and-tumble place, noisy all the time and a complete madhouse by evening. A demon like Morophil standing openly in the doorway drew barely a glance from anyone.

Grede was actually a little surprised by that. He swept his eyes across the ship-hold-like tavern, then said, "Looks like demons really aren't anything special around here. When you said outside the elf's place that you wanted your succubus to come out for a walk, I was a little worried."

"Quite the opposite," Jace said. "In Booty Bay, walking around with a demon at your side means a lot fewer problems. Everyone knows you're not someone to mess with."

The dwarf nodded, eyeing the succubus sidelong. "I believe that."

Just then a goblin came running over. "Put away your weapons, demon lady!"

When the succubus didn't react, he turned to Grede. "Hey, sir, tell your demon minion not to wave weapons around in here…"

The dwarf glanced at him. Jace looked at Morophil and nodded. The succubus hid the black blade behind her back. The goblin looked at the dwarf, then at Jace, looking a little surprised that the warlock who'd brought a demon servant to Booty Bay was a human.

"We've booked rooms here," Grede said to the goblin barman, clearing his throat with great gravity. "Two rooms, in fact."

"Ah, then give me your name, dwarf!" the goblin said. "I'll go ask the boss!"

"Grede Thunderfist." Grede shot Jace a look, then swallowed the rest of what he'd been about to say.

"Jurke," Jace said. "Or Jack Slovakia. Check under both."

The goblin nodded and squeezed into the crowd. Grede asked curiously, "Jack Slovakia, what kind of name is that…"

"Just something I made up when I first came to Booty Bay," Jace said offhandedly. "Don't dwell on it. No special meaning."

At that moment, a burst of cheers and a few hoarse, strange laughs sounded from the corner of the tavern. Jace glanced over curiously and saw a group of people competing in an axe-throwing contest — among the humans and dwarves, there were even a few trolls mixed in.

A bald troll was gripping a small axe, staring hard at a large wooden target, on which a goblin was straining every muscle trying to yank an axe out of the dead-center bullseye.

"You want to join?" Grede shoulder-bumped Jace.

"Honestly? Not much interest," Jace said, watching the crowd. "You want me to compete with those trolls on who throws an axe straighter?"

"Mr. Grede and Mr. Jurke, your rooms are ready!" A goblin in a light-purple suit ran up to the two of them. With that pointed nose, big ears, and the little yellow hat on his head, you wouldn't have been surprised if he'd run out of a circus.

Following the goblin up to the top floor of the Sailor's Home to the room the Reliquary had arranged, Jace found it was actually a "sea-view room" with a direct view over all of Booty Bay.

It still had the feel of a ship's hold, but the wooden and metal furnishings were exquisite and well-appointed — more like a room prepared for a traveling noble or merchant, not quite living up to the name "Sailor's Home."

Maybe it really was built for sailors originally. But as more and more people wanted to stay here, they kept bidding higher and higher for the rooms, and bit by bit, the place turned into somewhere meant only for the wealthy merchants passing through Booty Bay.

If the Reliquary hadn't been footing the bill, Jace would never have been willing to shell out for a place like this.

After unpacking and getting settled, the two of them came back out into the hallway. Grede raised his eyebrows and angled his eyes toward his room. Jace nodded. Both of them felt the rooms were a little too gorgeous.

"I'm going to find my goblin friend and sell the necrotic essence," Jace said. "You coming?"

"No," Grede said. "I'm going down to throw some axes."

"Does it cost money?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "I asked that goblin earlier — there's a small entry fee, but if you place, there's a little prize money! Don't worry, I'm going to earn money!"

"Fine," Jace sighed. "Whatever you do, just don't gamble, Grede. I don't want to blow all our travel money to Kalimor before we even set off."

"I only bet with friends!" Grede clapped Jace on the shoulder. "Go, and don't worry!"

Jace gave a reluctant nod.

Leaving the Sailor's Home, Jace climbed all the way to the top of Booty Bay to Bax Mithershield's alchemy lab, and found the fellow prying open a wooden crate with a crowbar, no idea what was inside.

"Mr. Mithershield!"

"Huh… if it isn't you, Mr. Slovakia. It's been almost two months. I thought you'd died — or worse, forgotten about my business!"

"Necrotic Essence Collector, Model 5," Jace said, setting the box on the ground. "Why didn't you tell me this thing needs recharging?"

"It says so right in the manual!" Mithershield said with an innocent face.

"The Goblin-language manual?"

"You're a mage and you don't speak Goblin?"

"Is Goblin some kind of important language?"

"Oh, goodness, that strikes right at my racial pride! And it was already fragile enough as it is!" The goblin grinned, walked over, and picked up the box. "Recharging — a trifle. I knew you mages would figure something out. You must've got the necrotic essence by now."

Jace took three vials out of his pocket. "One vial, at least six gold, Mr. Mithershield. Two full ones, and this half vial makes three gold. The trouble we ran into along the way was way worse than you could imagine."

"You actually got this much?!" Mithershield asked in surprise. "You didn't charge into Karazhan to catch ghosts, did you?"

"Fifteen gold for the lot," Jace said.

The goblin put his shocked expression away, glanced at the purple vials in Jace's hand, and nodded. "That price is acceptable — call it a reward for your bravery! I went to find Pokodias earlier. He said the moment he saw you, he knew you were someone who could help him. He even had me investigating just who you were, but seeing as you're practically my business partner now, I couldn't do something like that to a friend."

"Guess he wasn't paying you enough," Jace said.

"Ha, well, that's not wrong either. After all, everything in this world has a price, Mr. Slovakia." Mithershield laughed loudly, walked back into his alchemy room, and returned with a heavy little money pouch, handing it to Jace. "Count it — fifteen gold."

Jace hefted it, opened it, and glanced inside. "Looks good. Pleasure doing business, Mr. Bax Mithershield."
