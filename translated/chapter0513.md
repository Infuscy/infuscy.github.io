## Chapter 513: Elf and Demon

After two days in Booty Bay, sampling quite a few of the inn's specialty dishes, Grede had been thoroughly absorbed in the axe-throwing tournament. Jace hadn't paid much attention to his results, but it looked like he'd actually earned back a few silver — at least he hadn't lost money on the entry fees.

"He seems to have won against someone again, master," Morophil said from across the table, watching the crowd of axe-throwing enthusiasts gathered in the distance. "That dwarf's got some real skill."

"You interested in axe-throwing too?" Jace asked.

"I'm not interested in that kind of contest," Morophil said, taking a sip of wine from her glass. "But if master wanted me to give it a try, I'd be more than happy to — because it's master's command, and that's what interests me most… It's like drinking. I'm not interested in drinking, but as long as master wants me to keep you company, suddenly it's a delight."

"Don't tell people you're not interested in drinking when you're keeping them company," Jace said.

"Is that a command too?"

"It is."

"Very well, master, this wine is really—"

Mid-sentence, Morophil's eyes suddenly went wary, turning toward the inn's entrance. Jace's first thought was that some demon had shown up.

He turned to look behind him at the door and saw the familiar tall, cloaked figure of an elf. But before he could raise a hand to wave, the elf was already walking straight toward them.

To pick out Jace's corner in a crowd like this — yep, that was her all right.

Had she noticed the demon?

She must have. Morophil wasn't invisible — she was sitting right here staring straight at her. There was no way she hadn't noticed.

The elf arrived at their table, pushed back her hood, glanced at the demon beside her, then looked at Jace. "I'm here, Jace."

"So this is the special partner you told me to brace myself for," Vereesa said, sitting down next to Jace and looking at Morophil. "I'll be honest — I did a lot of mental preparation, but I'm still a little surprised."

"There's me too," Sazeno said, flipping up from under the table. "Lady Vereesa, is this our first meeting?"

"What a lying little demon. We've met before," Vereesa said, propping her chin on her hand and looking at the imp.

"Uh," Sazeno froze, glancing at Jace helplessly.

"You didn't think that after casting all that magic fighting that black dragon, and popping out right over my head, I didn't notice a thing, did you, imp?" Vereesa said, narrowing her eyes.

"Ha! I really thought you hadn't seen me!" Sazeno gave an awkward laugh.

"But I've never seen this sayaad before," Vereesa said. "Why didn't you introduce her to me, Jace? I think you summoned this demon a long time ago, didn't you?"

"It has been a long time," Jace said. "Back then I was worried that if you thought I was a demon worshiper, you wouldn't hire me."

"Your worry was justified," Vereesa said. "If I'd known back then you were running around with a sayaad, I never would have worked with you. Even with my limited magical knowledge, I know about the demonic invasion of Dalaran."

As she said this, there was a clear note of barely-suppressed displeasure in her voice. Jace could feel it.

"I can't go on this Kalimor trip without her, Vereesa," Jace said, lowering his voice. "You know we're going after a Highborne who might be connected to Eldre'Thalas. If Quel'Thalas knows anything about Eldre'Thalas, they should know something's gone wrong there."

"An unknown demonic disaster," Vereesa said.

"And Morophil is a professional demon hunter," Jace said. "Even I wouldn't dare say I'm better than her at tracking or dealing with demons."

Vereesa frowned, opened her mouth as if to say something, hesitated, then finally said, "Fine. You have your reasons."

"And the most important thing — she doesn't take a cut," Jace said. "A powerful helper who doesn't take a share and doesn't need food or water. What could be better than—"

"I don't take a cut either, master, I don't care about money!" the imp shouted.

"I'm not saying you count," Jace said to Sazeno. "Keep shouting over me and I'll stuff you back in the bag."

"Let him stay," Vereesa said.

Jace looked at her in surprise. "All right. Anyway, Vereesa, I want this trip to have as much insurance as possible. And you've been to the Dark Portal — you know what a sayaad can do. Her invisibility can help us a lot."

The elf's lips moved. After thinking for a moment, she said, "I know that. And I'm here to assist you in completing the mission. I don't really have the standing to interfere with what methods you use to fulfill the Reliquary's commission, Jace. The Reliquary never expected you to have absolutely nothing to do with demons. They know full well what warlocks are — just like the target we're tracking. They told me all this before I came."

"I'm curious — you're impersonating Aethwin Goldenthorn. Didn't anyone notice something was off?" Jace asked.

Vereesa said with a hint of pride, "This time I have the support of Silvermoon's Ranger-General, Jace. The Sunstriders can manage Silvermoon and Quel'Danas just fine — they really don't need to meddle in too many other people's business. It's tiresome."

"General Sylvanas is supporting you coming here too?" Jace said in surprise. "No wonder."

"Covert support," Vereesa smiled, then her gaze drifted back to the succubus. The corners of her mouth, which had been turned up, went cold again.

"Where's Grede Thunderfist, by the way?"

"He's over there competing in axe-throwing with the others," Jace said, pointing into the distance.

"Axe-throwing? Sounds interesting, Jace. I've been feeling a little cooped up here too," Vereesa said, raising her voice. "This journey here was exhausting, and I came straight here to find you before I'd even rested. I'm going to go find him and have some fun, loosen up my hands and my spirits."

She glanced over and muttered under her breath, "Damn it, why are there trolls too!"

That said, she tossed her cloak onto the table, stood up with a thump, and walked over.

Looking at Morophil again, the succubus was clearly barely holding back a laugh.

"Why aren't you saying anything," Jace said. "At least introduce yourself, show some friendliness."

"I think it's better not to say anything in this situation, master," the succubus said with a smile.

Jace took a slow, deep breath, watching the elf weave through the crowd and gradually disappear. He had a feeling he'd just managed to get on Vereesa's bad side.
