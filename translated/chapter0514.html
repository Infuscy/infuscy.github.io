Chapter 514: Passage Tickets

At night, plenty of people poured into the Sailor's Home — groups of sailors, crew from who-knew-where, even pirates, all gathering to drink and boast, tankards clinking, fists and hammers pounding the tables.

The noise rolled over them in waves. Jace stared at a bowl of Booty Bay specialty murloc-fin soup in front of him, at the strange bone fragments floating in it… and thought about everything they might encounter after reaching Kalimor.

"Right, right, right — that fellow's a troll, but he's got shit for talent! If he was really that good at axe-throwing, would he be hanging around Booty Bay?!"

"Any Amani axe-thrower from back home could beat him with one hand tied. What a disappointment!"

"Can't believe you've been fighting trolls all this time and actually picked up some of their skills, little elf!"

"Learning from the enemy's strengths. But I'm still better with throwing knives."

"That's right, that's right… uh."

Grede glanced over. The sudden quiet and the now-steady table jolted Jace out of his thoughts. He looked up at the two of them. Vereesa lowered her head and took a sip of lemon tea.

"What are you thinking about, Jace?" the dwarf asked.

"Tomorrow we set out for Kalimor. Aren't you giving our route some thought?" Jace asked.

Grede laughed. "No need, Jace. Didn't you hear what the goblins said? A trip to Kalimor, if you're lucky, is still a month at sea. We'll have plenty of time to discuss it on the way. And even if the worst comes to worst, we don't know anything about Kalimor anyway — any plans we make now might just be a waste of time. Since we're in Booty Bay, we might as well enjoy ourselves."

Though Jace couldn't say he knew nothing about Kalimor, given how far the game's content diverged from this place, he certainly couldn't claim to know it well either.

So all he could do was nod and ask, "How was your axe-throwing competition?"

"You mean to tell me you didn't hear a word of it just now?" Grede laughed and clapped the elf on the shoulder. "This little girl went and won the championship just like that. Compared to her, I'm basically a hobbyist!"

Grede had just finished saying that and was about to give Jace the details, but then realized he'd already told most of it to Vereesa just a moment ago. Going through it all again, he found he wasn't that enthusiastic anymore. The most awkward part was that Vereesa didn't seem to have much to say either.

He glanced at the succubus standing by the wall toying with her black dagger, then at the elven ranger who'd cleared her throat twice, and his smile just froze on his face.

"No surprise there, Vereesa," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded.

Jace cleared his throat. "It's getting late. We're boarding tomorrow. I'm going to turn in."

"You not going to drink your soup?" Grede asked, pointing at Jace's bowl.

"Who the fuck can drink that shit?" Jace said, then squeezed through the crowd toward the stairs. The succubus scooped up the imp, who'd been hopping around on the windowsill, and followed her master.

That night, Jace lay in bed tossing and turning, unable to sleep. Every so often he'd catch Morophil sitting at the little desk watching him from the corner of his eye, and he couldn't quite describe the look on his own face.

Jace didn't want to presume that Vereesa actually had feelings for him, but from the way things looked, Vereesa did seem a little upset about the succubus.

It was obvious she had no ill will toward Sazeno the demon — she'd known about his existence for a long time and hadn't thought any less of Jace as some kind of demon worshiper because of it.

"This is the one you care about," Morophil said suddenly.

"Huh?" Jace rolled over.

"You forgot? When you first summoned me, master — the person I saw in your eyes," Morophil said with a smile. "It's just that back then I still didn't know enough about Azeroth to accurately describe exactly who it was you cared about."

"Back then Vereesa was staying in Stormwind, and we were about to help her track down clues about the death knights. Of course I cared about her affairs," Jace said.

"Then master's caring…"

Morophil drew the words out. Jace said, "I'm trying to sleep. Don't start with this crap."

The next morning, the three of them brought their luggage down to the harbor. Sure enough, the goblin ship bound for Kalimor was already docked at the pier. Quite a few ogre mercenaries wearing the Trade Cartel's gear-badge were hauling cargo with all their might, and some passengers were already boarding.

As they got closer, Jace noticed a slightly shorter two-headed ogre in the distance arguing with the pier clerk.

"What kind of sense is this?!" the ogre said in oddly-pitched Common. "I never had this kind of trouble in Nagrand!"

"That's because in whatever-the-hell-land, you idiots never thought the problem through to that level!" the goblin said, pointing at the horned head. "Go on, ask it — are you one person or two?!"

"Two people," the horned head said coldly.

The one-eyed head was shocked. It slanted its single eye at the horned head. "What did we just agree on?"

"I didn't agree to anything," the horned head said.

"You smart-mouthed little—!" the one-eyed head said. "And now look — this goblin wants us to buy two tickets."

"Why?!" the horned head said angrily.

"You… you stupid…" the one-eyed head was so mad it couldn't get the words out, then forced itself to dial it back. "Shut your mouth."

He smiled, fixing his single eye on the goblin. "We are the same individual. Individual."

The ogre carefully sounded out this not-often-used Common word, then continued with some satisfaction, "So — we only take up one person's space!"

The goblin laughed twice. "If we're talking space, my boss would charge you at least ten tickets, you two clubs. Our boss is already being generous!"

"How dare he call us both clubs!" the horned head suddenly roared.

The one-eyed head's mouth fell open in surprise, then it bellowed, "I'm going to twist this parasite right off!"

"Who's scared of you, you damned fool!"

The horned head roared back. Then the two-headed ogre started spinning in place, flailing its arms wildly, both huge hands slapping itself across the face. After a few steps it slipped right off the pier walkway and plunged into the sea with a splash, its endless cursing turning into screams for help.

The pier instantly descended into chaos. The goblin who'd just been laughing at the two-headed ogre changed his face in an instant and shouted, "Ah! Hurry, you big idiots over there, get in the water and pull this fool up — otherwise we won't even get the price of one ticket!"

Jace watched the scene, finding it a little hard to imagine what it looked like when an ogre mage cast a spell — if the two heads disagreed on an incantation, what would happen…

"Your two demons need tickets too, right, Jace?" Vereesa asked. "You willing to buy tickets for them?"

"Demons? What demons?" Jace said blankly, looking around.

Vereesa turned to look back. The succubus who'd been right behind them had completely vanished without a trace. She felt Jace's warlock bag — completely flat.

Invisible. And a kind of invisibility that a ranger like her couldn't detect at all.

The elf let out a silent sigh.
