## Chapter 52: Sazanilaz

Deep in the night, Jace sat on the edge of his bed. He knew he wouldn't be able to sleep — there was no way in hell he'd get another full night's rest unless he followed through on this idea.

Which was:

Take that mysterious staff and perform an imp summoning.

Based on his last experience wielding that orcish staff to cast Fear, the consequences were very likely to be unpredictable. He might end up summoning a whole swarm of imps, or some terrifying unknown demon might come crashing through...

He stared at the incantations he'd written down on the desk — the demon-summoning spell, and the spell to terminate a summoned demon... then fixed his gaze on the termination incantation. That was the one that mattered most.

Jace recited it silently, over and over, who knows how many times, until he'd burned the demon-killing incantation into his brain so deep that even when he stopped saying it, his mind just kept replaying it on loop, and still he wouldn't let himself stop.

Bimusiennuoxi, Kuteyinni, Ailamaxijin.

If the spell went out of control, he'd recite this incantation and wipe out every last thing he'd summoned, no matter how many there were.

He wasn't about to do what Galders or Jagin did and just summon some run-of-the-mill imp servant and call it a day. Since those two had brought up those arrogant Ironforge dwarf warlocks — the ones who claimed that summoning an imp was nothing more than a warlock's basic requirement — then he absolutely refused to settle for summoning an ordinary imp.

Something that easy wouldn't do anything meaningful for his path forward, even if he pulled it off. If he was going to do this, he was going to go big.

Once the decision was made, all that followed was execution.

It could hurt. It could be a struggle. He might even regret it. But in the moment of decision, he had to decide — that was what they'd taught him.

Jace gripped the staff with his left hand. He felt a strange connection form between the staff and his mind — whether it was an illusion or something real, he couldn't tell.

He cleared an open space in the middle of the room, carefully rehearsed the hand movements Jagin had used, ran through them silently a few times to make sure there were no problems, and only then began the incantation.

Leineia, Taeni... no turning back now... Anwei, Suolemi, Oerjiasuobimusi.

Sure enough, the staff — still wrapped in coarse cloth — began shooting ominous purple light through the cracks, and the violet radiance pouring off Jace was far stronger than the shadow glow he'd produced while casting in the basement.

He dragged a bedsheet over the window and watched as the summoning array unfolded before him beyond his control, a dark rift splitting open down the middle.

Papers, books, cloth bags, all sorts of junk in the room were swept up by the surging magic and tossed about, rattling nonstop.

Jace clutched the two notes the warlocks had left him, silently reciting the termination incantation in his mind, determined that the moment anything strange crawled out of that rift, he'd start chanting and strangle it on the spot.

He wasn't about to summon a succubus, was he?

Back when he played the game, he'd always thought that if he were a warlock, he'd totally get a succubus and go at it every single day until the sky went dark and his bones turned to dust.

But now that he was actually in this world, he realized a succubus was anything but a good time.

He had to do everything he could to avoid having his mind twisted. A succubus was hands down one of the fastest ways to drag a warlock into corruption.

Not to mention they were far more powerful than imps in a fight. If he summoned a succubus, Jace seriously doubted he'd even get the chance to cast the termination incantation before getting charmed on the spot and turned into the succubus's slave.

Every gain demands a price. The most extreme pleasure always ends in the most extreme pain. Succubi weren't running a charity.

Before the rift had even fully opened, he was already braced to cast the termination spell.

The moment this rift kept expanding beyond the size needed for a single imp to pass through, he'd start casting immediately!

With the staff's power behind him, forget a succubus — even if a felguard came through, he should be able to execute it on the spot, right?!

Or... get executed himself?

Fortunately, the rift didn't keep expanding. It stayed the same size as the one he'd produced while casting in the basement beneath the Slaughtered Lamb.

A thin little claw reached through the rift, then the whole creature suddenly leaped out and landed on the wooden floor. The summoning array vanished at once, and the rift sealed itself shut.

The room fell quiet, leaving only the creature and Jace, who was leaning against the window.

An imp — that was right. Two long horns thrust forward, eyes blazing with emerald arcane fire, a little hairless monkey-bare thing even smaller than a cat.

But its body was a dark gold all over, its skin looking like metal rather than flesh.

Regardless, Jace was fully prepared to put this little monster down.

At that moment, the imp stared at Jace and babbled something in a language he couldn't understand at all.

"Do you speak Common?" Jace asked.

"Common?" the imp said. "So this is Azeroth! Ha — my master is a human, and I've come to Azeroth! How marvelous! You have no idea what a magnificent feat you've accomplished, my master... you just rescued me from the Ghost King Yarozbar! I was about to be put to death by him!"

What a load of nonsense.

Jace asked, "Where are you from?"

"Where am I from? The master asks, and I answer!" the imp said, hopping back and forth. "I serve the Ghost King Yarozbar. He's an imp lord on Nastoi, with his own palace and legion, and I was his personal guard. I just made a tiny little mistake — just a tiny one — and he wanted to throw me into the forge and melt me down into soul shards. What a cruel creature!"

Nastoi, Yarozbar — Jace had never heard of these bizarre names.

Grede had said that these imps might come from places that even demons themselves had never heard of. Given that the Twisting Nether was so vast the Burning Legion couldn't conquer all of it, an imp hailing from some obscure corner wasn't all that strange.

"But none of that matters anymore!" the imp said. "I've come to Azeroth — he can't catch me now. And I've signed a contract with such a powerful warlock, one who could bring me all the way here from distant Nastoi! How wonderful! From this day forward I begin a new life, a new life here on Azeroth. You have no idea how famous Azeroth is in the Twisting Nether — even the Burning Legion's leader Sargeras wants to invade this place! Ah... well, Lord Sargeras wants to attack here, that seems rather... inauspicious."

The imp scratched its pointed chin and said, "Still, he shouldn't be able to get here anytime soon. Ha, ha!"

"You're not part of the Burning Legion?" Jace asked.

The imp said, "The Ghost King Yarozbar would love to join the Burning Legion, but he hasn't had the chance. Not a single eredar would summon a ghost king to be his servant! Since he's not, then of course I'm not either. And I don't want to join the Burning Legion anyway. I'm an imp who loves freedom!"

"Ah, freedom, contract, master, servant — a bit contradictory, doesn't matter. I don't think you're all that strong, though. You might kick the dust in a few days, and then I'll be completely free!"

"Mm." Jace nodded, picked up the paper, and began to read: "Bimusiennuoxi..."

"Wait!" the imp shrieked. "Stop, stop stop!"

Jace crumpled the paper and mimicked its shrill tone: "You're hoping I die, so I'll just kill you first. Fair's fair!"

"Too fast! Way too fast, I can't take it!" the imp grumbled unhappily. "What I said — was any of it wrong? A weak warlock is definitely going to die fast. This world is too dangerous, there are too many powerful beings around. One careless moment and you're gone — I'm not wrong, am I? Like, what if a dragon flies over here and accidentally breathes fire? Could you take that? What if a great demon walks by and casually exhales and you don't even have bones left! They might not even know who you are!"

"But that doesn't mean I'm hoping you die, my new master. If you get stronger, then of course I get stronger with you. If you live forever, then I live forever too. Still, we have to be realistic about things, haha! But here's to eternal life for you anyway! Not that the odds are great!"

Jace felt like he'd swallowed something foul and couldn't get a word out, but the little imp wasn't actually wrong about any of it.

"Then can you help me get stronger? Help me live forever?"

"Living forever is hard to say — mortals can die pretty suddenly. But how does the master want to get stronger?" the imp countered.

"For instance, learning more incantations?" Jace said.

The imp hopped a couple of times and scratched its head. "Learning more incantations doesn't necessarily mean you'll get stronger, my dear master. Some astromancers only know one or two spells and can still run rampant across the Void. Some weak races learn seventy or eighty spells and die all the same!"

Jace had the feeling this imp was talking nonsense. An astromancer who only knew one or two spells? You'd need at least a shielding spell to keep from getting burned to death by free-floating shadow in the Void, and a teleportation spell to hop between planets — that was two right there.

Could it not know a single attack spell? If it ran into a stray dog it'd get bitten to death. What kind of astromancer was that?

"So do you know any spells?" Jase asked.

The imp let out a sharp cackle. "Of course. My kind are born with magic! Phase Shift, fire magic! But these magics can't be learned — what a pity! You're not of our kind, so you can't channel the magic in our bodies!"

This didn't make him any different from an ordinary imp, then. The imps in the game could even buff your max health with Blood Pact — and this one hadn't even mentioned that.

Still, the little thing didn't seem to have any malice in it, so Jace let his guard down a notch.

"You said a warlock who could summon you ought to be very powerful, but any warlock can summon an imp. So you can't be an ordinary imp. Tell me — what makes you different from those regular imps?"

"I'm certainly not ordinary! A lot of imps have very weak Phase Shift, my master. They can only fade from this world — they can't truly turn invisible, can't actually shift to another plane. But I'm different. I'm a powerful imp from Nastoi. I can achieve true invisibility — even you won't be able to see me. Like this."

A flash of arcane light, and the imp suddenly vanished before Jace's eyes.

Now Jace panicked. Where did it go?

Unlike Greders's imp, which still gave off a faint ominous aura, this one had vanished as if it had never existed at all!

Jace didn't want to invite trouble. He quickly pulled out the note and began to read: "Bimusi..."

"Aiya!" the imp suddenly leaped back into view in a burst of light, bouncing and yelling. "Stop chanting, stop chanting! I was only gone for a moment — I wasn't going to hurt you, great master! I think you're going to become the greatest warlock in the entire world someday, after all. You're even more ruthless and cunning than a nathrezim, ready to cast such terrifying magic at the drop of a hat! It frightens me!"

This imp seemed fairly obedient, more or less. Jace asked, "First things first — what's your name?"

"Which name do you want — my name, or my true name?"

Every demon had a true name that could only be spoken in demonic. Once someone learned a demon's true name, it was like having them by the throat — speak the true name and any demon, no matter how powerful, could be easily suppressed by the one who spoke it.

"Say both."

"You can call me Sazeno!" the imp said this, planted its feet, and the flames in its eyes seemed to swell.

"Next, I will speak my demonic true name, master — because you commanded it."

This imp, who called itself Sazeno, spoke these words in a voice that gradually shed its earlier shrillness and slyness, growing unnervingly low and dark.

"Sa—za—e—ni—la—z!"
