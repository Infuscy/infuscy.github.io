## Chapter 527: Nature Magic

Musa Woolbraid was badly injured. Even with the healing potion, she still limped when she walked.

She led the three of them to a hidden little cave, her eyes warily fixed on the two demons following Jace the whole time, and she never said a word to Jace.

Jace knew that if he hadn't helped save the tauren's life, she probably would have attacked him on the spot and fought him as another demon. So he shouldn't expect any friendly attitude from her right now.

She did look at Grede and Vereesa with considerably kinder eyes, though. Tauren might look like cattle, but their eyes and facial expressions were clearly a lot more expressive.

Grede was talking a lot less now. He sat down next to Jace and asked in a low voice, "You don't think so either, right?"

"Don't think what?" Jace said.

"She can't possibly like me," Grede whispered. "I'm a dwarf, she's a cow. That's way too far a stretch, isn't it?"

"How the hell would I know what tauren think," Jace said, clearing his throat and stealing a glance at Musa. "Why don't you go ask her yourself?"

"That'd have to go through the elf translating? I'm not having her say stuff like that," Grede grumbled. "Makes it sound like I actually care!"

Over there, Vereesa was eating some basilisk meat she'd gotten from the Shimmering Flats earlier, and the tauren was curiously sniffing it, wanting a taste.

Grede immediately glared. "Look, look — look at that, Jace Sesso. Damn it. The cow's going to eat meat."

"Tauren aren't cows. What's wrong with a little meat?" Jace said. "She's that big — she probably eats more meat in a year than you do. Don't forget, she said she's a hunter. You think a hunter harvests wheat?"

Grede nodded as if a light had gone on. "That makes sense."

"Besides," Jace said, "liking you is better than hating you, isn't it? I feel like this tauren wants to smash me to death any second — at least she's not thinking about doing that to you. And we're only in Kalimor for this one trip. When we go back, nobody'll know anybody. By then she'll have forgotten about you. And aren't you some kind of romance expert? Why are you acting like a lovesick teenager, all anxious and insecure."

"If a jungle troll or an ogre liked you, let's see how calm you'd be!" Grede pressed close and said through gritted teeth.

Jace cleared his throat, glanced at Vereesa, then at Grede. "All right, definitely couldn't stay calm. My bad."

"If it really bothers you that much, I could try to make her hate you right now, Grede," Morophil said from the side, a mocking smile still on her face.

The dwarf looked at the demon. "I knew it — this is your doing behind the scenes, you love-toy succubus!"

"Me?" Morophil rolled her eyes. "It's clearly your charm successfully crossing the gap between race and body type. Besides, without master's orders—"

"Jokes are jokes, but don't you dare use any magic on that tauren," Jace warned. "She already hates the three of us. We finally found a guide — let her lead us properly for now. Don't go causing more trouble."

"Of course, master," Morophil said, leaning against Jace. "I promised to behave before we left, after all."

Grede eyed the succubus cozying up to Jace, grimaced in discomfort, then looked over at Vereesa's side. Vereesa immediately turned her head away.

"Hey, Vereesa — haven't you talked about how we go on from here?" the dwarf asked.

"Oh, right," Vereesa snapped back to attention. "She was telling me just now — if we want to get to the northern plains, she knows a little path that climbs out of the Stone Pillar Valley while avoiding the areas the centaurs control. About three or four days' walk. But she also warned me that there might be some quilboar patrolling up there."

"Azgoth," Jace said. "That's the quilboars' home. If we want to get to the north, we have to go through there."

Hearing what Jace said, the tauren looked over and nodded, repeating, "Azgoth."

Well, at least they were talking now.

Just then, Musa stood up and said a few things to Vereesa. Jace asked, "What's she doing?"

"She's going to cast a spell," Vereesa said.

"Cast wh—"

Jace had only gotten halfway out when the tauren closed her eyes and carefully began chanting an incantation.

To Jace's ears, the incantation sounded exactly like the lowing of a cow. But soon, a cool, fine breeze carrying the fragrance of fresh earth and the scent of grass blew into the cave, stirring the dry blades of grass around the tauren into a spinning dance.

What was surprising was that the grass had been dry and brittle, but after spinning for a while, it slowly turned green and tender.

This wasn't Jace's imagination. Sazeno next to him had noticed it too, pointing at one of the big leaves and tugging at Morophil's arm in a low voice. "What kind of weird magic is this?!"

"Life magic," Morophil said evenly.

As the leaves gradually regained their vitality, the tauren's mane grew visibly glossy and smooth, and her skin filled out. When she finished casting and opened her eyes again, the whole person looked much more spirited than before.

She tentatively touched the wound under her ribs where the spear had pierced — and it had basically healed by now, with only some dried blood clinging to the mane.

So she'd also learned some nature healing magic?

"This is more effective than the priests at the Cathedral of Light," Grede said in a daze.

"It's the power of Life," Jace said, patting Grede's pack. "Like the healing potion you're carrying — purely for healing wounds, it's definitely going to be more immediately effective than your average priest."

The tauren looked at Grede and asked something in Thalassian. Vereesa said, "She's asking if you were injured in the fight."

Grede quickly waved his hands. "No, not at all. How could I be hurt by those crude beasts?!"

But before Vereesa could say anything, Musa raised her hand and cast again. Jace stepped aside and watched the wind of nature wrap around the dwarf.

"Really no need to heal me," Grede said, standing up and leaning against the wall. After the nature magic swept over him, he took a deep breath, looked around with wide eyes, and said, "That… that feels amazing. I feel like I'm not tired anymore!"

Seeing Grede's delighted expression, Musa nodded in satisfaction, then sat down on the ground all at once. When a two-meter-tall monster like that sat down, the whole little cave seemed to shake.

That's when Jace suddenly noticed a trickle of blood coming from Grede's nose.

"Hey, hey, Grede," he said, pointing to his own nose as a hint.

Grede wiped his nose, looked at the blood on his finger, and frowned. "What's this about?"

"You didn't have any injuries, and she gave you too much life force," Jace said. "You forget about when I drained life before?"

Grede nodded. "I thought only greedy and evil warlocks pulled stunts like this."

Jace muttered unhappily under his breath, "Greedy and evil…"
