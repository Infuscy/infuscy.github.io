## Chapter 528: Deep into the Stone Forest

Venturing deep into the Thousand Needle Flats, Jace felt nothing but oppressive weight.

The valley was a tangle of stone pillars stacked one upon another, blocking the view in every direction, stretching upward until they vanished into a hazy shroud of cloud.

Musa Woodmane led the way up ahead, pausing now and then to glance upward. Watching her, Grede asked curiously, "What's up there?"

The tauren turned her head at the dwarf's voice and looked back at him. Wenresa spoke with her for a moment, then explained, "Some of the local inhabitants have taken up residence atop these stone pillars. They may be watching us — harpies, quilboar, or centaur."

"People can live up there?" Grede craned his neck, surprised.

In fact, they could — and there might even be small settlements up there too.

Jace knew that in the game, several tauren encampments sat right on top of these stone pillars. Each massive column could hold a small settlement, and bridges sometimes connected them.

He didn't know the current state of things in the flats, but whatever force controlled this valley surely wouldn't give up the enormous expanse atop the pillars without a fight.

Musa walked ahead. When she judged a stretch of path safe, she'd chat with Wenresa. When she entered an area where she sensed centaur might be prowling, she'd fall silent.

The tauren was tall and powerfully built, but when she moved carefully, her steps were deathless quiet. Each time one of her dark hooves, covered in brown mane, touched the ground, it landed gently and deliberately. This was a seasoned tauren hunter, no question.

Wenresa said, "She says her Woodmane tribe was once protected by a tauren clan called the Grimtotem. Later, some of the Grimtotem's leaders wanted the Woodmane's aging chieftain to teach them shapeshifting and nature magic in exchange for that protection, so the Woodmane left them."

"Why not just teach them?" Grede asked.

"Well, I didn't quite catch all the details," Wenresa said. "Apparently the tribe's leaders considered magic a natural blessing that couldn't be traded away… or that those people hadn't received the blessing, or something. In any case, it's the kind of thing that doesn't translate cleanly into our language. Maybe you should learn tauren, Grede. You'd get a much clearer picture."

"Not a chance," Grede said immediately.

Wenresa smiled, walking backward for a moment as she looked at Jace. "I remember you were going to learn Thalassian. How's that going? Have you started yet?"

"I've been waiting for a real elf like you to teach me," Jace said.

Wenresa raised an eyebrow. "How about we start with calling us quel'dorei instead of just 'elves'?"

"I'll do my best to remember," Jace said.

"Quel'dorei," the tauren repeated the word, then said something to Wenresa.

Jace noticed Wenresa's expression darken slightly as she listened to Musa.

He could guess why. The tauren had probably brought up the War of the Ancients.

Among the ancient inhabitants of this land, quel'dorei was by no means a complimentary term.

In fact, quel'dorei wasn't a name exclusive to the elves of Quel'Thalas. Ten thousand years ago, only the highborne had called themselves that — quel'dorei meant "Children of the High."

Back then, the highborne had made a show of distinguishing themselves from the kaldorei commoners, even physically walling off their palaces from the lower quarters of their cities.

But deep down, they knew they were still the same species as the rangers in the jungles and the priestesses in the temples. So they'd treated quel'dorei as nothing more than a title.

After Dath'Remar Sunstrider and his band of highborne sailed across the ocean to another continent, they had truly made quel'dorei the name of their entire race.

But to the native peoples of Kalimdor, quel'dorei were the villains who had summoned demons into the world during the War of the Ancients — mages who had ravaged the plains with magic and burned the forests with the arcane.

Even ten thousand years later, those terrible memories were still etched into the hearts of these ancient races.

Which was exactly why that tauren in Gadgetzan had taken one look at Wenresa and known she was an elf from the other continent — and his attitude had soured on the spot.

The group walked through the stone forest for two days. Jace felt like everything he saw looked the same no matter where he went. He couldn't detect any change in the scenery, couldn't find a single landmark to get his bearings by.

But Musa seemed utterly confident, pressing straight ahead. By the second night, she led the three of them to a cliff face. Whether he looked east or west, the cliff stretched on without end.

Jace knew they'd finally reached the northern edge of the Thousand Needle valley.

Grede asked, "Why didn't we just follow this cliff from the start? If we climb this thing, we'd reach that Agamago place you mentioned, right?"

Wenresa relayed his words to Musa. The tauren explained at length, and Wenresa translated, "Once we're up there, Agamago isn't far. The reason we didn't come this way earlier is that the eastern side of this cliff has been overrun by centaur. The only way here is to feel our way through the stone forest."

"So we went around a whole warband?" the dwarf muttered. "I didn't catch a whiff of centaur the entire time."

"Those centaur are formidable hunters," Wenresa said. "If we'd been close enough to smell them, they would have noticed us too."

Jace asked, "If Musa Woodmane can lead us around the centaur warband we just passed, can she take us straight to Feralas? Keep circling around the tribes ahead?"

Wenresa took the map from Jace's hand and explained for a while. The tauren shook her head and said something. Wenresa translated, "The centaur have built wooden walls and watchtowers across the western approaches to keep the tauren from escaping into the Feralas jungle. Getting through there is next to impossible."

Musa added something else. Wenresa said, "She was trying to flee into Feralas a few days ago — that's how the centaur chased her all the way east to where we met her…"

"Are the centaur trying to wipe the tauren out? Every last one?" Grede asked.

"The war between tauren and centaur has been going on for thousands of years, Grede," Wenresa said. "It's a fight to the death. Whoever wins gets to claim vast hunting grounds across Kalimdor."

"I don't see what's so great about this place," Grede said. "When we got off the ship, I saw a few palm trees, a couple of big cacti in the desert, and I haven't spotted a single plant that could pass as healthy since. And now we're here — this is basically the Dustwallow of hell!"

Musa kept asking Wenresa what the dwarf had said, but Wenresa hemmed and hawed and eventually brushed her off with something vague.

After all, she had no desire to tell Musa that the land she and her ancestors had lived on for tens of thousands of years was being dismissed as worthless by an outsider.

Watching Grede's disgusted expression, Jace found himself looking forward to seeing the Mulgore plains.

This was the homeland the tauren had always dreamed of settling… and even rendered through the game's models, it was breathtakingly vast and beautiful. Though at this point in time, Mulgore was still firmly in the grip of a powerful centaur host.
