## Chapter 53: You Still Need a Professional to Teach You

Sazeno — a little cat-sized monster — had roared out its true name like a raging beast, and the room's light had been swallowed by shadow in an instant, the windows shuddering from the force of it!

Jace was spooked cold down the back of his neck and had to pick up the note with the termination incantation again.

After finishing, the imp panted in exhaustion. Seeing Jace pick up the note once more, it opened its mouth and stuck out its tongue, gasping and heaving for air, one hand feebly pointing at the note but unable to make a sound — the expression on its face looking like tears were about to flow.

But its eyes were nothing but flames, so no tears were coming out.

Jace knew what it meant and put the note away again. The imp then collapsed flat onto the floor with a thud.

"Shouting my true name is exhausting, master. You've memorized it now — don't ever make me shout it again!"

Jace asked, puzzled, "Do imps even get tired? Don't you have stamina? You're a demon — a magical being."

"We don't have stamina the way you mortals do," Sazeno replied, its voice shrill once more. "But speaking a true name drains the spirit and drains magical power. With that one shout, I can't use any magic at all anymore. I need to rest..."

"But your true name is too hard to remember." Jace rubbed his nose. "When you shouted it just now, the sound was so loud I couldn't make it out clearly."

"The master doesn't speak demonic? How strange." Sazeno's voice was thick with helplessness. "If you don't speak demonic, how did you manage to summon me here? And if you do speak demonic, remembering a demon's true name should be simple. The master is either a fool, or lying to me."

Heh. A fool.

Jace suddenly realized... right. Why not learn demonic from this imp? Have this imp take a look at that book written in orcish and some other unidentified language, and get it to translate for him along the way — wouldn't that work?

Why hadn't he realized the benefits of summoning a demon until now? He felt like his brain had completely stopped turning before — how had he not thought to summon a demon to teach him demonic?

Actually, it wasn't that his brain had stopped turning. It was that he'd been a normal person before!

Thinking it over, Jace felt like he'd become several times more unhinged than he'd been a month ago.

If you'd asked him a month ago to consider summoning a demon, he'd have thought he was crazy. Now, he'd turned around and thought the version of himself who hadn't considered summoning a demon was the crazy one!

"Enough of this fool business. Teach me demonic," Jace said immediately. "That's your master's order."

"Everything the master says is an order... the master's order I must carry out... ah, so tired, so many orders."

And with that, the imp actually fell asleep.

You can sleep at a time like this?

Jace patted the imp on the face. The moment he touched its skin, he felt a chill that made his whole body uncomfortable — but the chill also felt like it was searing his fingertips. The contradictory sensation made him instinctively feel like he'd touched something profoundly unnatural.

He checked his fingers but there was no visible damage. So this was what demon skin felt like?

The imp lifted its head in annoyance. "I want to rest... master, I want to forget the master's orders!"

"If you want to sleep, disappear first," Jace said coldly. "Phase Shift right now. Don't sit here showing off your appearance. Do you know what would happen if you exposed your true form in a human kingdom? From now on, whenever a third party is present, you will turn invisible. You are not to let anyone besides me lay eyes on your face!"

Sazeno said lazily, "I've remembered the master's request. But what would humans do to me anyway?"

"Do you know what the Holy Light is?" Jace asked.

"The Holy Light? The Holy Light?" Sazeno shrieked at the top of its lungs. "Don't tell me this world has Holy Light priests, Holy Light warriors?!"

"In Azeroth they're called priests and paladins," Jace explained. "There's a Cathedral of Light a few hundred meters north of here, and the priests and paladins inside number in the hundreds, more or less."

"No!" Sazeno leaped up. "It was you, master, who dragged me into this fiery pit! No! Azeroth was supposed to be a beautiful world brimming with arcane energy — why has the Holy Light taken over this place?!"

Jace snapped, "Shut your mouth, turn invisible, and then go to sleep!"

"I don't have the strength to turn invisible anymore... Having my demonic true name seized has severely weakened me. I can't do it, master!" Sazeno growled in exasperation.

Just then, there was a knock at the door.

Jace glanced out the window — it was probably two or three in the morning. And here he'd been shouting at the top of his lungs at this hour...

He kicked the imp under the bed, then opened the door.

"Mr. Sesso." His elderly neighbor lady leaned in at the doorway. "If something's troubling you and you can't sleep, you can come find this old lady to chat during the day. I don't have anyone to talk to either. But it's too late now."

"There's... nothing wrong, ma'am," Jace said. "I was rehearsing a play. I've got a performance tomorrow, and I was nervous I wouldn't pass. But I'm done now. I'm going straight to sleep!"

"Good, good!" The old lady tottered back toward the upstairs. Jace slammed the door shut and sat on the bed, his heart pounding.

He glanced under the bed — the imp had slept off completely.

Jace sat on the bed, replaying the conversation with the imp in his mind.

He'd realized something that didn't quite add up.

Sazeno clearly knew of Azeroth's existence, but seemed unfamiliar with Azeroth's current state — otherwise it wouldn't have been surprised that humans had already adopted the Holy Light faith.

Its understanding of Azeroth probably still dated back to the time of the Titans' creation, the ancient era when the elves ruled the world — at the very least, thousands of years back, when humans were still living in tribal times.

So why, then, could it speak Common?

Come to think of it, its Common did have something odd about it. It didn't sound like a Stormwind accent, nor did it sound like a Lordaeron accent.

When a demon died in Azeroth, it wasn't truly annihilated — its demonic soul would be banished back to the Twisting Nether. Once it had absorbed enough shadow and fel energy, it would reshape a body and be reborn.

If a demon died in the Twisting Nether, it died for real. That was why Sazeno had been so terrified of that ghost king melting it down as soul shards — because once that fate befell you, you were gone forever.

Could it be that a long, long ago, it had once been summoned to Azeroth, learned Common, and then died by accident and been banished back to the Twisting Nether?

He'd have to ask it properly once it woke up.

When had Common first appeared in Azeroth? The age of the ancient Arathi kingdom, or even earlier?

Jace realized he really didn't know much about this area of knowledge. He'd have to read more of the books at Marin's place.
