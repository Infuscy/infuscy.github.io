Chapter 530: Razorfen

Back in that cave, Jace had assumed the tauren was some kind of druid apprentice studying restoration magic — especially given how fragile she'd looked facing those three centaur. He hadn't expected much from her in a fight.

Turns out, she was a trained warrior after all.

Musa Woodmane fought with mounting ferocity. She grappled bare-handed with the quilboar champion at the front, and when it was clearly on its last legs, she kicked it off the cliff with one hoof. The next one came at her with an axe, but she seized it by the neck with her longer reach and wrenched the axe free.

Musa hurled the creature off the cliff with one hand and turned the stolen axe on another shield-bearing quilboar behind it, hammering away with a brutal series of strikes that made Jace's guts clench just listening.

The quilboar, who had been charging in bravely, started to get cold feet facing this two-meter-tall monster. A quilboar in the distance raised a short bow, lining up a shot at Musa at the front — but Wenresa's arrow took it through the skull first. The creature stiffened and toppled over dead.

The quilboar were already on the verge of breaking when Molofel appeared behind their ranks. She materialized from the shadows with her black blade already buried in a quilboar's neck!

The shrieks from behind threw the remaining quilboar into a panic. Caught between two fronts, they spun in every direction, unable to deal with enemies ahead and behind at once, and were quickly cut down one by one by Musa's axe.

The skirmish on the walkway had begun suddenly, and it ended just as fast. The quilboar were knocked off the cliff or beaten down, and soon the valley fell quiet again, save for the fading echo of a few miserable squeals.

The succubus ran a finger along the blade, smearing the quilboar's blood, rubbed it dry, and sniffed it lightly — wearing an expression of pure disgust.

Musa stared at the demon with an expression of absolute complexity. Wenresa said something to her in elven, then added, "More quilboar could arrive at any moment… we need to move…"

Musa walked past the demon and headed higher. As she passed by Molofel, Jace half-expected her to grab the demon and fling her off the cliff like she had the quilboar.

Quilboar sounds echoed everywhere through the valley. The group had long since stopped caring about the safety of the walkway — they pushed upward as fast as they could.

At the top, massive thorn vines were already spreading across the cliff face.

On the Eastern Kingdoms, even in the high peaks of the Hinterlands or the troll-infested Stranglethorn Vale, Jace had never seen thorn vines this enormous — thick as great trees.

The spikes on them were as thick as sharpened abatis stakes. Jace had the feeling these thorns had been grown to ward off the gnawing of giant creatures like kodos. Musa glanced back and forth at the thorn vines, waved a hand to signal them off the walkway, and pointed higher into the thorns — meaning they were going that way.

Jace craned his neck to look at the cliff top above, then at the tangled mass of colossal thorns crisscrossing one another…

From his memory of the game, this thorn-covered mountain was most likely the quilboar sacred site — Razorfen Downs.

And climbing through the thorns was supposed to be easier than the walkway?

But he only hesitated for a moment before falling in behind the others. Musa was the true local here, after all.

Sure enough, not long after they left the walkway, another group of a dozen or so quilboar came into view above, chasing each other down the walkway at a run, grunting and squabbling amongst themselves.

Musa immediately pulled the three of them into the shadows beneath the thorn cover and waited in silence until the quilboar had raced below and out of sight. Only then did they move on, climbing up through the thorns.

"I can't even remember the last time I missed having wings, master," Molofel muttered.

"So you want them to grow back now?" Sazeno asked.

Molofel shot the imp a look but said nothing. The dwarf chuckled and said to Jace, "Hearing her say that, I actually don't feel like she's a demon anymore."

Wenresa turned back and made a shushing gesture. "There are still quilboar around. Stay sharp, Grede!"

Grede scratched his beard. "Let them come. However many show up, we'll take them down. Those brainless monsters actually had the nerve to shoot at me from ambush!"

Under Musa's lead, the group gradually climbed higher. The cliff face blanketed in thorns began to level out bit by bit, and it seemed they had finally reached the summit.

After all that climbing, Jace's shoulders ached like hell and his hands were about to give out. He'd spent all those years running around Stormwind, but he'd never really trained in rock climbing. Without the black dragon blood helping him out, he'd have been holding the group back by now.

At this point, Jace was actually hoping a quilboar would pop out of nowhere so he could Life Drain it and replenish his stamina.

And it wasn't just him — Wenresa and Grede were clearly struggling too, watching Musa ahead of them as they panted for breath.

This hunter, who had been covered in wounds just a few days ago, was now no worse for wear — and seemed to have more strength and endurance than any of them.

Musa had clearly chosen a route up to the cliff's summit that was harder for the quilboar to detect. From here, looking out toward the depths of the stone forest, they could already see the uneven tops of the stone pillars amid the clouds.

One nearby pillar did have some ruined structures, or totem poles of some kind, and at the edge there was a destroyed suspension bridge hanging in midair. Jace couldn't tell whether anyone still lived there.

The group rested for a while in a dense thicket of thorns. The thorns here were so thick they blocked out the sun, making the place oppressively dark. The four of them huddled in a corner, surrounded by absolute silence — as quiet as a true night.

Musa said something to Wenresa. Wenresa turned back to the others, "We've reached Agamago!"

"I could've guessed," Grede said, wrinkling his nose. "The air reeks of quilboar. We haven't wandered straight into their home, have we?"

Wenresa said, "Musa says the upper half of the walkway is occupied by quilboar, so this is the only way. Trust her — at least the path she's led us on so far hasn't been a problem."

Jace said, "Good thing we ran into her. Otherwise the three of us would've had no idea how to find a way up. What a godforsaken place!"

Wenresa said something to Musa. The tauren nodded and relaxed, leaning back against a thick thorn vine.

After finishing with the tauren, the elf turned back to Jace's group, "We wait here until nightfall, then move on after the quilboar settle down. We can rest and have something to eat."

"I've got to say, this cow is something else," Grede murmured in admiration.

"Cow?" Jace said.

"A lady," Grede corrected himself immediately. "A tauren lady."
