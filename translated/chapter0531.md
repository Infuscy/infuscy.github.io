## Chapter 531: The Barrens

In the dead of night, the mountain ridge blanketed in thorns went pitch black. Musa shifted into the form of a lioness and led the group, circling around the quilboar patrol routes as they continued north.

Even in this big-cat shape, Musa still had a pair of faintly visible horn buds on her head, and her body was covered in dark brown mane. So while she had a lioness's face, the overall effect was… a little uncanny.

At every crossroads they passed, they could see the glow of quilboar camps deep in the ridge. The quilboar sentries standing watch through the night were tall and powerfully built — far sturdier than the quilboar patrolmen who had blocked them on the walkway.

They followed a narrow path covered in fine thorns out of the ridge and into a valley formed by gentle slopes. By now, waist-high wild grass grew everywhere, and under the moonlight, enormous, solitary trees of some unknown variety stretched their sparse canopies across the ridge.

Musa shifted back into her tauren form and seemed to relax considerably. By this point she'd started chatting with Wenresa again, laughing now and then. Wenresa looked happy too.

The elf was crouched down adjusting her boots when Jace couldn't help but lean over and ask, "What were you two talking about just now?"

"She said you don't seem like a demon," Wenresa said with a smile.

"Why would she say that? Of course I don't look like a demon," Jace said. "No hooves, no horns, no wings."

"I think what she meant is, you don't seem like a bad person," Wenresa said, standing up and glancing back at the succubing watching the rear of the group. "And she said it's strange to see a demon fighting alongside them against quilboar — it makes everything she was taught feel a little unreliable."

Jace said, "I hope she doesn't use my situation to generalize about all demons or demon summoners. Though… do young tauren know that much about demons these days?"

"They do," Wenresa said. "She said there are demons that have survived since the War of the Ancients lurking in Desolace, and they're working to summon more. And those ancient remnants in Dire Maul who still call themselves quel'dorei… they've summoned demons too. Some of those that escaped outside have been causing chaos ever since. Many tauren believe the centaur's corruption may have something to do with demons. In some tauren legends, the centaur of ancient times were allies against the demons."

"You think the demons they're talking about might be connected to the highborne we're looking for?" Jace asked.

Wenresa thought for a moment. "Should we tell her what we're after?"

"Absolutely," Jace said. "She's hardly likely to be in league with demons. She might even be willing to help us."

Wenresa nodded and caught up to Musa ahead.

Dawn crept in slowly. The group followed the gentle slope upward until they reached a cliff top. Musa stood at the heights, gazing into the distance, drew a deep breath that swelled her massive chest, and let it out slowly.

Jace walked up and looked out. Before him stretched an endless savanna, shimmering gold in the morning sunlight. The wind swept through the grass in rolling waves.

Compared to his impression of the Barrens, this place was missing the orcish watchtowers, the tauren villages and tents, or the roadside totems the tauren used as trail markers.

It was simply a world of wilderness — splintered deadwood, abrupt earthen ridges, barren hills on the horizon… it reminded Jace of the African savannas from documentaries.

"We finished off that meat from the salt flats a while ago," Grede said. "Does anyone have any left? I'm starving. Jace, do you still have any of those pastries from Gadgetzan?"

Jace said, "We'd better find food locally. The rations from Gadgetzan are for emergencies."

Wenresa chatted with Musa, then said, "Musa says our scent is hard to mask. The local animals will probably keep their distance. If we want to find food, we'll have to keep going a long way north."

Grede stood on his toes and peered around, even though the grass here was nearly as tall as he was.

"Aren't there any other monsters in Kalimdor? The kind that aren't afraid of us and actually want to come pick a fight?"

Musa said something to Grede. Wenresa translated, "If you'd like, she can take us looking for a kodo… Kodo — Jace, is that the giant beast we ran into in the desert?"

Jace nodded. "Should be. I know kodos are widely distributed across southern Kalimdor."

"That's the one?!" Grede said, astonished. "Where can we find them?"

Musa said a great deal more. Wenresa said, "Back when the tauren tribes were still strong, they considered slaying an adult kodo alone the mark of a full-fledged hunter. But that tradition's been abandoned for many years now. These days the tauren hunt kodos only to survive. Few go out into the deep grasslands on a dare just to prove something."

"Musa." Grede asked directly, "Kodo — have you killed one?"

As he asked, he made a stabbing motion. Musa seemed to understand what he meant, especially with the word "kodo" thrown in.

The tauren shook her head and said one phrase in Common: "Alone, no."

"Ah, she's speaking Common! That's a good start!" Grede laughed. "Still, I'd rather she learned Dwarvish first."

"You'd be better off teaching Jace Dwarvish," Wenresa muttered.

Grede grunted in agreement. "He learned orcish, he learned demonic, and he can't even string together a single Dwarvish swear! The man's got something wrong with his head."

"He even learned a few words of draconic," Wenresa said, looking at the dwarf. "But not a word of Thalassian."

"Who says?" Jace thought back. "Anu. Belo. Delana."

Wenresa blinked — then burst out laughing.

"All right, at least I understood that," Wenresa said, still laughing. "Who taught you that? Although it is a little strange for a human to ask the sun to guide their path, you could say — Anu, Al, Delana. Meaning, 'may the Holy Light guide us.' That would suit you better."

"Hah, he doesn't even believe in the Light!" Grede laughed.

Watching Wenresa's delighted expression, that dislocated feeling of time and space surfaced in Jace's mind once more.

Who would have thought that a voice line he'd learned in a game in his past life would actually be understood by the people of this world?

He drifted off in thought for a moment. By the time he came back, the dwarf, the elf, and the tauren had already set off, discussing as they went their plan to hunt a kodo deep in the grasslands.

"You seem a little down," Molofel said softly.

"I'm fine," Jace said. "Let's catch up. And if we actually run into a kodo, remember to steal its spirit, Molofel."

Molofel patted the pack on Jace's back. "Master, you're really going to feed soul shards to this little thing?!"

"What?!" Sazeno popped his head out, keeping his voice low. "How dare you disobey the master's orders?!"

Molofel gave him a look of pure disdain. "I'm the only one here who can siphon a soul. Show some respect."

The imp's expression changed in a heartbeat. "Ah… right, my lady. If I'm going to get stronger, I'm counting on you…"
