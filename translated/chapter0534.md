## Chapter 534: The Sweetwater Oasis

The vast Barrens were far larger than Jace had anticipated.

Following Musa across the savanna for over a week, all they'd seen was still a single expanse of desolation.

Aside from an overgrown dirt road that appeared and vanished from time to time, there was no sign of civilization anywhere.

Musa said the Barrens were home to vast numbers of centaur. But after nearly two weeks, they hadn't seen a single centaur — zebras, on the other hand, they'd seen plenty of.

By now Jace had lost track of how long they'd been walking. He'd gone a little numb to it. He raised his head and suddenly spotted a deep green at the edge of the endless wasteland.

An oasis? An oasis in the Barrens? Or just a miracle?

The thirst he'd been managing to endure became nearly the moment he laid eyes on that greenery. Jace touched the waterskin he'd been reluctant to open for a long time, then lowered his hand.

There was an oasis right ahead, after all.

The deeper they went into the Barrens, the worse Jace's water conjuring became — and he had the Flowing Crystal of Ice to fall back on for mana.

After all, casting the spell was really just summoning water elements and condensing them in front of him into a stream. And across the entire enormous expanse of the Barrens, there were hardly any water elements to be found. So the efficiency of Conjure Water kept dropping.

This wasn't Gadgetzan — that was a desert too, but at least it was right next to the sea.

So over the past few days, Jace had found it increasingly difficult to keep everyone supplied with water. The group had no choice but to ration it as much as possible.

Which was why the sight of an oasis in the distance filled everyone with excitement.

But even Jace knew what an oasis on this barren plain meant.

Most likely, the most competitive beasts for miles — or centaur and other factions — would all be gathered here, enjoying the water and the food resources.

Even in the game, the centaur tribes of the Barrens clustered mostly around the oases.

So Musa eyed the oasis with considerable wariness. She stopped behind a thick tree and watched for a long while. Only after confirming no enemies appeared did she lead the group toward it.

As she walked, she said something to Wenresa. After listening, Wenresa relayed it to the two behind her, "Long ago, most of this place was as lush as these oases. Then the Burning Legion invaded. A demigod named Lo'Gosh fought a great battle against the demon army here. Their ferocious battle destroyed everything, and in the ten thousand years since, the ecology here has never recovered its former appearance. The tauren see these oases as a sign that Lo'Gosh has returned to this land — a sign that the Barrens will flourish once more."

"This is the Sweetwater Oasis. It's one of the largest oases in this wasteland. We can rest here for a while, then turn west."

"Are there any centaur here?" Jace asked. "Ask her."

Wenresa relayed Jace's question to Musa. After getting the answer, she explained to Jace, "The centaur camped here before, but they've left in recent days. Musa says you can still see some abandoned centaur tents on the western edge of the forest."

Jace could more or less guess why the centaur had been unwilling to camp here.

The reason the Sweetwater Oasis was one of the largest in the entire Barrens was this: beneath the oasis lay a massive underground water cavern, home to countless mysterious and terrifying creatures.

Later, the night elves would try to send a druid named Naralex inside to investigate. Naralex and his fellow druid attempted to heal the wound beneath this oasis, to turn it into a clean water source that would let the Barrens turn green again. But the druid was soon dragged into the abyss of nightmare by the dark power lurking within the cavern — until he was rescued by the adventurers and mercenaries who entered after him, otherwise known as the players.

The centaur had probably sensed there was something unusual about this cave. Lingering here long-term wasn't a smart choice, so after discovering this oasis they'd abandoned it again in short order.

"I can sense the darkness here," Jace said, looking at Wenresa. "Tell Musa we shouldn't stay here too long."

Wenresa nodded and said something to Musa. After listening, Musa glanced at Jace and asked in Common, "You too?"

"What?" Jace didn't quite catch her meaning. Was she saying he was one of those dark things?

Musa's Common consisted of the few words she'd picked up over the last few days. Forming a complete thought was clearly still a struggle. She had to say something more to Wenresa, who translated, "She's saying she's like you — she can sense the evil beneath this oasis. Clearly, the tauren legends don't accurately describe why these oases flourish."

Jace nodded.

Tauren druids were probably more or less extinct by now. Half-baked druids like Musa, who'd grasped some nature magic and could feel the forces of nature, were probably few and far between. Without the night elves' help, of course they had a hard time detecting the darkness lurking beneath all this vegetation.

But at least on the surface, nothing seemed amiss. Musa led the group in, and by the time they reached the pool Jace knew so well from the game, they hadn't detected any obvious shadow.

After all, even when the night elf druids came here to investigate in the future, they didn't sense the evil until they went deep into the cave. Drinking some water and resting up here should be fine.

"What do you think, Sazeno?" Jace asked.

"I think this place suits the master. I certainly don't like it," the imp said in a low voice.

"The fact that you don't like it means it's safe," Jace said. "After all, anywhere you'd like is definitely some godforsaken hole no one could live in."

Catching sight of the pool, Grede couldn't resist tearing off his outer clothes and throwing himself in with a splash that sent water exploding in every direction — like someone had hurled a brownish-black boulder straight into the pool.

It had been about a month since they came to Kalimdor, plus the sea voyage before that. Jace couldn't remember the last time this bunch had taken a proper bath.

Of course, they all stank to high heaven, and after living together this long nobody could bring themselves to judge — not to mention that adding a tauren to the group had suddenly made their own smells seem downright manageable. But when you finally found a place where you could actually wash, of course no one could hold back.

Jace glanced at the elf beside him. Wenresa's eyes slid sideways toward the pool ahead. "You're not going in? Musa and I will keep watch out here. I'll come in after you're done."

"Then I won't be polite about it." Jace grinned, couldn't wait to throw off his cloak and robes, and plunged into the water right after Grede.

The last time he'd been able to soak like this was back in Stormwind — and the water here was a hell of a lot cleaner than Stormwind's canals.
