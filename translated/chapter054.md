## Chapter 54: A Stormwind Imp at Work

The next morning, Jace woke up and sat on the bed. The room was empty — nothing there at all.

Jace rubbed his eyes and looked at the piece of paper on the desk, crumpled and covered in creases from being handled, with the summoning-magic notes written on it. But the imp in the room had already vanished.

Outside the window was growing light. He felt like he hadn't slept more than a few hours at most.

Had the imp run away? Or had it never come at all — had last night been nothing but a dream?

He opened his mouth and, with some reluctance, tried saying: "Come out, Sazeno."

A sudden burst of light flared at his feet, and Sazeno leaped out of it, giving Jace a start.

"Master, I heed your command. Whenever a third party is present, I remain invisible at all times!"

"Third party?" Jace looked left and right, then checked under the bed, rummaged through the wardrobe, glanced behind the door, and after confirming that this five- or six-square-meter room had no realistic place left to hide a living person, asked, "What third party?"

"Outside," Sazeno said. "I hear too many people's voices. Are there a lot of priests among them? A lot of paladins?"

"Probably. You did well," Jace said. "That's exactly the kind of caution I want."

Even though he'd nearly thought his house was haunted, he was still pleased that the imp was being this obedient.

The imp hopped about in delight. "Does the master have any new orders?"

"House-sit." Jace grabbed his clothes. "Don't leave here all day. Stay invisible. Don't open the door for anyone."

"Understood!" The imp hopped onto the desk and vanished in a flash of light.

Just as Jace was about to grab his pants, his eyes landed on that staff propped in the corner.

"Come out first, Sazeno."

The imp appeared again. "Is there something else, master?"

"Do you recognize this staff?" Jace asked.

"Of course not." The imp grinned. "The eredar make far too many things. How could I possibly recognize every staff they've ever produced?!"

Jace felt his brain go blank for a second. "This is an eredar staff?"

"A staff manufactured by the eredar — but not necessarily one used by the eredar themselves, right, master?" the imp said. "Look at the material of this staff. This is a substance called shadowgold. In the entire Twisting Nether, it can only be found on Argus. There, a beautiful metal called celestial gold, after being transformed by fel energy and shadow, becomes what's left behind. And Argus... is precisely the homeland of the eredar!"

Transformed? More like corrupted, probably...

Sazeno touched the surface of the staff and shook its head. "Those eredar sorcerers love giving the artifacts they create to their slaves and servants as gifts, to display their might. The master wouldn't happen to be a servant of the eredar, would I then be the servant of a servant of the eredar? How impressive."

Jace was stunned for a moment, but Sazeno's words had actually reminded him of a possible origin for this staff.

Gul'dan had originally received guidance from Kil'jaeden, Sargeras's right hand and leader of the eredar, transforming him from an outcast abandoned by his people onto the path of shadow. Afterward, he'd founded the Shadow Council.

This staff was very likely one of the many demonic artifacts he'd obtained from the eredar demons among the Burning Legion after his cooperation with them began. To win the loyalty of Targon, he'd then passed this staff on to him.

"You know quite a lot, Sazeno." Jace nodded in satisfaction. "Don't slack off at home today either — help me with something."

"Gladly, master!" the imp said excitedly.

Jace said, "Here — take a look at this book."

He pulled that thick bilingual book out of the wardrobe and stuffed it into the imp's hands. "What languages are in here?"

Sazeno took it, flipped through a few pages, and said, "There's demonic, there's lordly... and there's a kind of runic language I don't understand."

"That's orcish," Jace said.

So this was exactly the kind of so-called bilingual dictionary — specially made for orc warlocks to study incantations.

"Here's what I want you to do. Take all the demonic in here, translate it into Common, write out the correspondences and pronunciations, and copy them onto these papers." Jace hauled out a stack of cheap rough paper he'd been using as toilet paper and a notebook.

It was only when he set the papers in front of him that he realized the ink and pens he'd prepared for copying out the Shadow Council's orders were nowhere near enough for a job this size.

He handed the ink and a quill to the imp. "Use these for now. I'll figure out a way to get you more ink."

The imp stared with its big flame-filled eyes at the book and stack of paper that were both bigger than it was, momentarily speechless.

"All right, get to work, my good servant." Jace rubbed his hands together. Just as he grabbed his clothes and was about to head out, he remembered something and turned back. "If anyone walks past the door, remember to pause what you're doing and turn invisible. But if anyone ever breaks in and touches that staff, I want you to blow them to smithereens with every spell you've got — no matter who it is!"

The imp shouted, "Yes! I obey the master's command!"

"Good!" Jace bolted out the door and charged straight for the canal bridge.

There were plenty of places in the city that sold paper and ink. The cheapest was the Academy of Magic and Scientific Research, affiliated with the university in the Mage Quarter.

Following the canal all the way forward, past the tower of the Stormwind Stockade, the academy building came into view. Jace took out ten silver — after getting a sword commissioned from the blacksmith and buying a set of clothes these past two days, this was basically half of what remained of his life savings.

Early in the morning, the Academy of Magic and Scientific Research was already packed with people. It was the second-largest library in Stormwind, but compared to the library in the Wizard Sanctum, this one was geared more toward ordinary folk and university students.

"I'd like to buy some ink," Jace said, running up to the librarian's counter. "And some paper too."

The librarian pulled out a bottle of ink, then hauled out a large bag of paper and began counting them out one by one for Jace.

"Ordinary-quality paper, one silver eighteen copper for a dozen twelve sheets. Ink, five silver a bottle."

He counted halfway and glanced at Jace. "How much do you want?"

Jace was still processing this absurd price. He'd literally crawled his way out of a wolves' den, risking his life, to earn a single bottle of ink.

But this money couldn't be skimped on.

If it were just a small amount of paper and ink, he could probably ask his boss Marin for some. But if the quantity got too large, it would inevitably draw suspicion.

He wasn't even an apprentice anymore. Even if he were taking an orcish language course, there'd be no reason to consume this much paper and ink.

"I'll... take one bottle of ink for now. And three dozen paper."

After buying the paper and ink, Jace sprinted all the way back home. By now the imp had already written quite a bit.

Jace looked at the paper and said, "Your handwriting is way too big, you know. Write smaller, write tighter. Water down the ink a little — as long as it's legible is fine! Save me some..."

"Understood, master." Sazeno nodded in a panic.

Jace hauled a bucket of river water up from downstairs, wiped the sweat from his face, and said, "My future depends on you. Do a good job and there's a reward."

"What reward?" the imp asked suddenly.

What reward — that actually stumped Jace for a second.

"You tell me. What do you want?"

"I want mortal souls..." the imp licked its lips. "Delicious soul shards, lovely screams, delectable agony and sweetness. Have you ever tasted them, master?"

"I'm not like you." Jace asked, "Do only mortals have the kind of soul you want?"

"Even beasts have souls — they're just not as interesting as those of thinking beings." The imp tilted its head.

Jace figured, in that case, the souls of the gnolls in Elwynn Forest would probably do in a pinch. After all, they more or less counted as thinking beings.

"Wait a little while. There might be a chance."

The imp let out a sharp cackle. "Aiya, the master is truly too good to me."
