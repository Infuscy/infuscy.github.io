## Chapter 541: Where Souls Go

In the morning, sunlight poured into the valley, illuminating the pool at its center. The line between shadow and light was sharp, making the glow all the clearer.

The air still carried a scorched smell, but a cool breeze was blowing, and Jace could already scent the pine gradually replacing the bloodshed of last night's battle.

The filth on the ground had been more or less cleaned up, and the harpy corpses outside had been carried away. Apart from the two burned houses, Sunblaze Hollow seemed to have returned to peace.

The tauren had gathered in the open ground at the center. The bodies of the tauren warrior who had fallen in the river last night and the young tauren had been wrapped in blankets embroidered with ancient tauren runes.

When they noticed Jace's group approaching, the tauren around them all lowered their heads in greeting, murmuring words no one could understand. Though Musa didn't translate, Jace knew it must be an expression of goodwill or gratitude.

Among these giants, several meters tall, the members of their group looked small and frail. But the tauren still respectfully cleared a path, guiding them to the center.

Just when Jace thought the ceremony would take place right there, several young and powerful tauren stepped forward, hoisted the fallen tauren warrior and the young tauren onto their shoulders, and walked toward the bridge above the pool.

The dozen or so residents of Sunblaze Hollow fell into line behind one another inwordless silence, forming a single file. The tauren elder nodded toward Wenresa and pointed at the young tauren walking ahead.

"I think he wants us to follow," Grede said.

Wenresa looked at Jace, then nodded and fell in step behind the tauren ahead. Jace joined Grede behind Wenresa.

Once the group set out, Musa and the other residents all followed behind.

The tauren's strides were long but their pace was slow. Caught among them, it was easy to lose your own rhythm. It wasn't just Jace — the dwarf and the elf both walked a little awkwardly. But the tauren behind them were considerate toward these outsiders who couldn't quite keep up, patiently trailing along.

As they walked, the tauren chanted words with a strong rhythm and cadence — like a spell, like a poem.

Even though Jace understood not a word of the tauren tongue, he could feel the beauty in the language, especially the pronunciation rolling out of those deep, heavy throats — immeasurably solemn and dignified.

No one wept. No one made any other sound. Jace couldn't feel grief or despair. It was like an ordinary morning assembly, an ordinary farewell.

But for some reason, he found himself moved by the calm.

Grede and Wenresa listened in a daze to the singing that seemed to have no melody. Jace noticed that Musa was chanting along with them too — only Musa seemed a little more emotional. Compared to the other tauren, her voice carried an extra trace of sorrow.

The tauren climbed the carved mountain stairway step by step. The three of them followed all the way up until they reached the clifftop — a high platform overlooking half of the Stonetalon Mountains.

The platform was strewn with sacks of grain, fruit, and ceremonial ornaments — totem poles, hangings woven from feathers, animal bones, and rope, and small flag-like things made of hide and branches that looked like kites.

Among the offerings and ritual objects, a wooden frame and dry firewood had already been arranged. The tauren placed the blanket-wrapped bodies onto the frame. Standing close enough now, Jace noticed the corpses had been carefully washed and groomed. The exposed faces and chests were free of even a speck of dirt or blood in their fur, as though they were merely asleep.

The firewood was lit. The elder stepped forward, cradling a tall staff in both hands, and began to pray in a low voice.

A smoky fragrance of earthroot drifted through the air. Jace had spent enough time around Ursula's place to recognize the scent easily. Perhaps for the tauren, burning earthroot as fuel to cremate the dead helped their souls return to the embrace of the earth.

The ritual was long and solemn. The tauren gazed at the smoke drifting into the sky. In the distance, deep valleys rolled away, covered in pine forest. Only the tall, crimson peaks jutted abruptly out of the dense green, like massive beast claws.

When everything was finished, the tauren collected the ashes and departed from the cliff on their own. Before heading down, they spontaneously came before Jace's group to bow and speak. Though Jace had no idea what they were saying, he fully felt the restrained warmth and gratitude radiating from these giants.

Before long, only two guardians of the sacred ground remained on the platform, along with Jace and the others.

Grede, watching the tauren guards sweeping up the scattered ashes, said, "This place reminds me of Kus Haeven."

"The sacred ground of the Wildhammer dwarves," Jace said.

"You remember." Grede looked at Jace in surprise.

"Of course," Jace said. "You think I forget everything when I'm drunk?"

Grede laughed. "I think our clan and the tauren would make powerful allies. We both live in the mountains, we share similar beliefs and convictions, and even the way we send off our dead is so alike. Well, they use fire — that part's a little different. But I can understand it."

"The tauren tame wind riders and kodos, while dwarves tame gryphons and goats," Jace added.

"Exactly." Grede nodded eagerly. "Next time I return to Aerie Peak, I'll tell them this story in detail — tell them that on the other side of this world, there lives a race of nine-foot-tall giants so similar to us. And of course, the story won't be complete without a human and an elf ranger — and a tauren shapeshifter."

With that, Grede clapped Jace on the back. "I'm heading back first, brother. Oh, and that bird leg's mine — I'm starving. Any objections?"

"No objections," Jace said.

Wenresa gave him a look. "No."

Grede frowned and waved a hand. "Fine, I'll save you some."

And he headed down the slope.

Wenresa watched the dwarf's back until he vanished completely among the pines, then said, "Musa says the tauren scatter the ashes into the river. They believe part of the body rises into the sky with the smoke, returning to the embrace of Father Sky. Part, once scattered into the river, journeys to Mother Earth. In this way, the tauren soul joins a great journey, guarding the land and their descendants forever. So they don't see death as a permanent farewell. If that's true, it sounds a lot more romantic than entering an endless realm of the dead."

"Do quel'dorei envy other peoples' legends too?" Jace asked.

Wenresa looked at Jace. "Why do you say that?"
