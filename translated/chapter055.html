Chapter 55: First Time as a Courier

The morning had already cost him a good chunk of time. Jace got the imp settled and immediately sprinted all the way into the Mage Quarter.

Ever since he'd found work in the Mage Quarter, he'd felt his stamina getting better and better. Sprinting several miles every morning, then cleaning rooms for a full hour without a break — it built endurance and patience in equal measure. If that wasn't a form of cultivation, he didn't know what was.

He'd barely finished cleaning and sat down to grab a book when Marin arrived.

He looked at Jace, who was clutching a book and still panting. "I've got a job. You're looking pretty wiped — shall we talk about it later?"

Jace set the book down. "Just got some exercise in, Master. Go ahead."

"Where's the tea?" Marin looked at the empty teapot. "Forget today?"

"Ah." Jace scratched his head. "Sorry. I got here late this morning."

"It's fine," Marin said. "Let me tell you about this job first. I'm looking to have someone deliver a letter and some materials to Stonewatch. Think you've got time to help out sometime?"

"Stonewatch in Redridge?" Jace asked.

"Right." Marin hauled out a big bag from under the desk. "Deliver these things to Stonewatch, along with this letter. It was originally meant for a mage, but he's never around, so I wrote the name of his student on it — save you the trouble."

He placed the sealed letter into the package and continued, "Best to deliver it within this week — three days, that is. If you set out right after your afternoon class, the timing's still fairly generous. You'll definitely want to spend the night in Lakeshire and Stonewatch, after all."

"No problem." Since Jace had already agreed to take on a bit more work from Marin each month for an extra four silver, delivering a letter this simple was hardly an issue.

Marin reminded him, "Still, don't push too hard. You'd best go when the Stormwind soldiers are out on patrol — don't travel alone. The gnoll problem still hasn't been fully dealt with over there, so there's still some risk."

"So can I rent a horse for free?" Jace asked, unable to wait.

"Ah, right." Marin pulled a note from the drawer, wrote something on it, and stamped it with his seal. "Take this to the stable master. The man's fat enough that you'll recognize him at a glance. He'll lend you a horse."

Jace clutched the note in his hand, feeling like he was about to burst from excitement.

In the midst of his excitement, he suddenly thought of the imp situation.

He wondered whether Marin could sense anything unusual from him — like the demonic taint one might pick up from contact with a demon, or the dark energy lingering around someone who'd summoned a demon, that sort of thing?

After brewing Marin some tea, he sat to the side and watched Marin's movements, wanting to see whether the tea would be affected in some way that Marin might notice. But Marin just drank his tea casually and went back to reading, as if nothing had happened at all.

Was this imp really that good?

Or was it precisely because it wasn't good at all that the energy it gave off was so weak nobody could sense it?

Either way, since Marin hadn't noticed a thing, ordinary people would be even less likely to sense anything unusual.

He'd actually been a bit worried that after summoning the imp, he wouldn't be able to set foot in the Wizard Sanctum for a while. Now it seemed the effects of summoning a demon weren't as significant as he'd imagined.

Come to think of it, in Dalaran there were plenty of warlocks operating even more brazenly than the ones here in Stormwind — and that was a whole city under the protection of Archmage Antonidas's magical barrier.

When he got home at noon, Sazeno was still translating away without pause.

Jace flipped through the pages. The progress wasn't fast — this book had upward of a hundred pages and only the beginning had been translated.

But he understood the misery of translation work. Some words simply had no equivalent in another language; all you could do was explain them as best you could. And in this world there was no translation app to do the whole job for you in one click.

"Human pens are impossible to use!" Sazeno complained.

"Be glad you've got a pen at all," Jace said. "Use it carefully. I can't afford to buy a new one if you break it."

He looked at his wallet, now more than half empty, and sat on the bed in helpless resignation.

By his reckoning, it'd be about another week before payday.

He'd get Grede to treat him to a meal today. Eat his fill, go to his orcish lesson, and then hit the road.

The two of them sat down in an Ironforge-style tavern in the Dwarf District. Grede was clearly doing better lately. Jace looked at the dark yellow outfit he'd just bought and said, "Going for the Bronzebeard look now?"

Grede grinned. "The Bronzebeard clan and Ironforge don't have a monopoly on understated colors, Jace."

"Speaking of clothes." He took the baguette the serving girl handed him, tore off a piece, stuffed a length of blood sausage inside, and tossed the whole thing into his mouth. Chewing, he said, "I had a set of clothes made out of those Lakeview Farm wolf pelts. Had a dwarven tailor do it — came out really nice. Made to your height. Come by my place sometime and try them on."

"It's still hot out, no rush," Jace said. "My tiny rundown place can barely fit a single extra set of clothes anymore!"

"Right," Grede said. "About going to Aerie Peak for Winter Veil — have you thought it any more? It's October now. I'd want to set out at least a month early, just in case anything comes up along the way. Look at it this way — you'd want to start preparing from mid-November or so."

Jace picked up a piece of blood sausage and took a bite. He turned over Grede's words in his mind, then thought of the imp he'd just summoned, that terrifying Fear spell, and that staff...

If he went on a long journey, he absolutely had to bring that staff with him. There was no way he'd leave something that valuable sitting in Stormwind for a whole month.

But if he brought so many dark-aura-tainted things to Aerie Peak, wasn't that just asking for trouble?

On the other hand, flat-out refusing Grede felt strange too.

After all, for Winter Veil he also wanted a chance to head back to the Kingdom of Lordaeron to visit his parents. The Hinterlands and the Kingdom of Lordaeron weren't far apart at all — only the Alterac Mountains lay between them. If he went back to Lordaeron and didn't stop by the Hinterlands, it would look like he was deliberately avoiding Grede.

Not to mention the gryphons. He wanted to see gryphons. Real gryphons. And if he agreed, he'd get to see them.

"Of course I'm going." He swallowed the blood sausage and took a sip of grape juice. "No problem."

Grede said, "Your 'of course' hesitated for a full minute."

"Was it that long?" Jace said. "I was just thinking about the road. Crossing Baradin's Bay, getting over the Hillsbrad Foothills — you know those orcs..."

Grede picked at his teeth with a knife and said, "Over in Hillsbrad, Terenas II just appointed a new lord to Durnholde Keep — a war hero named Aedelas Blackmoore. Heard of him? The man's got a real system. They say he's been hunting orcs all across the Hillsbrad Foothills, rounding up orcish fugitives, locking them in the keep, carving out an arena where he makes the orcs fight beasts for the entertainment of Lordaeron's nobles, who pay to watch. Then he uses the funds to keep hunting and rounding up more orcs. I figure the bigger his operation gets, the safer Hillsbrad becomes."

Jace knew this Blackmoore, of course. The Horde's warchief Thrall had grown up as a young orcish gladiator under his hand.

"You're pretty well informed."

"Of course," Grede said. "I have to think about the safety of the road home too. I need to have a handle on these things. Come with me — it'll be fine, Jace. I've got it all figured out."

"In that case, I'll leave it to you," Jace said. "I'll come meet you in Southshore after I'm back from the Lordamere area, and then we'll head to the Hinterlands together. How's that?"

"Fine. I'll make you come to Aerie Peak one way or another — come see my home." Grede took a sip of milk. "The route doesn't really matter at this point."

Jace eyed the milk clinging to his beard. "Got work today?"

"I do." Grede wiped his mouth. "I've got a job heading east the day after tomorrow — the day after that at the latest. A commission came through the Fighters' Guild."

"East? Eastvale, or are you going to Redridge?"

"Redridge."

"That works out perfectly."

Jace set Marin's rental note on the table. "Why not go today? I'll come with you. On horseback."

Grede glanced at the note. "You're going to Redridge too?"

Jace nodded. "I've got a delivery to make at Stonewatch. We can go together — it's right on the way."

Grede grinned. "That'll save on travel costs, then."

"Come to think of it, why didn't you call me yourself about going to Redridge?" Jace asked.

The smile that had been hanging on the dwarf's face seemed to freeze.

"I didn't even know what the job over there was yet. What if it was the same kind of thing you do — just delivering a letter? Bringing you along would've been a waste."

Jace wagged his finger. "Since when does the Fighters' Guild deliver letters for people? You clearly knew going there meant fighting someone or catching someone — definitely not some boring letter-running or hauling job. So why didn't you call me?"

Grede set down his cup and said helplessly, "...Fine, I'll be straight with you."

"After we got back from Lakeview Farm, I had nightmares for days on end, Jace. I'm certain it had everything to do with your magic. If I hadn't recovered a good bit these past couple of days, I think I would've lost it. If we run into trouble again and you pull out something even bigger, I really can't take it."

"I promised you I'd try not to use that magic anymore, didn't I?" Jace said. "And it's not like I can just use it whenever I want — that magic is hard to work with. I was just feeling my way through it before."

Grede said, "All right, how about this — let's agree up front: unless it's a life-or-death situation, you're not to use that magic."

"Fine. As long as you stop making every situation life-or-death." Jace said in exasperation.

The dwarf waved a hand. "All right, I get it. Then — let's meet at the old spot by the city gate in an hour."

Jace said, "An hour's too fast. I've still got a class to teach in a bit. Make it three hours."

"You've still got class? Then I'll see you in three hours."
