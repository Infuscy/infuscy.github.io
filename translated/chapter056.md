## Chapter 56: A Chance to Get Rich

The afternoon orcish class — with his mind stuck on the imp and the Redridge business the whole time — Jace was distracted through the entire lesson.

After class he hurried home and found Sazeno still hard at work. "I want to learn more about that phase shift of yours."

"Ask me anything, master." The imp kept scribbling away on his paper the whole time.

Watching him bustle about, Jace actually felt a twinge of guilt. He'd never imagined he'd feel sorry for a demon.

"I want to know — when you phase shift, are you just turning invisible, or are you actually changing your real position? What's the difference between phase shift and an invisibility spell?"

"There's a huge difference!" Sazeno leaped up. "What mages call invisibility is just them twisting light with the arcane, creating a space that the blind idiots around them can't notice, then sneaking through it… But you can twist light without twisting actual matter. Chuck a fireball into that blurry space — BOOM! — and you get to watch a roasted mage tumble out, hah! Phase shift, on the other hand, is how weaker demons fade out of the main plane to survive. Only demons born in the Twisting Nether can pull that off. Not even the eredar can manage it, because we're not mortals!"

"Invisibility I can sort of understand, but phase shift is impossible for me to picture. Say I put you in a box and you phase shift, then I carry that box three miles to the Stormwind gates. When you reappear, are you in the box or still back in my house?"

"In the box, obviously," the imp said. "What, you think I just vanish completely?!"

"What if the box gets crushed? Would you die?"

"Maybe, maybe not." Sazeno raised a finger. "Depends on the box. If it's a magic prison built by the eredar and you crush it with me inside, I'd be dead and banished back to the Twisting Nether for sure. But an ordinary box? If it can't hold magical things, there's no way it could!"

"Phase shift can't let me escape Azeroth, master. It's more like I hide in a pocket dimension parasitic on this world's main plane. I'm still subject to a few harmless little side effects!"

Jace opened the warlock's sack. "Get in. Phase shift."

"Yes, sir!" The imp hopped right in and vanished in a flash of light.

Jace noticed the sack just caved in on itself.

"You… come out again."

POP!

The collapsed sack suddenly puffed back open. The imp thrashed around inside for a moment. "Aren't you going to let me out, master? I've got work to do!"

"You can stay in there," Jace said. "Now disappear!"

"Master, stop making me pop in and out! Each shift costs mana. I'm almost dry!"

Jace said, "Stay disappeared this time. Don't come back out."

"Got it!"

A crisp shimmer of arcane energy, and the sack caved in again.

Jace opened it and looked inside. Aside from a few silver coins and the paper scrolls he'd stored in there, it was empty. The imp was like it had completely vanished.

He was starting to feel uneasy. Maybe he should find a way to keep track of the imp even when it was gone.

In the game, an imp in phase shift still showed up as a faint outline to the warlock and their party. But then again, that was probably just a gameplay concession.

Still, if there were people in this world who could actually see a phase-shifted demon, then the imp's invisibility wouldn't be all that reliable, would it?

Cross that bridge when they came to it. For now, the fact that it could hide itself so well was something he should make good use of.

He was about to head out when he spotted that wooden stick in the corner.

It wasn't just a stick anymore. It was a staff crafted by the eredar.

Considering its ties to the Shadow Council, to Teron Gorefiend and Gul'dan, Jace felt a chill run through him the moment he touched it — like some horrific demon had fixed its gaze on him.

After a moment's thought, he brought it along anyway.

He slung on the warlock's bag and the parcel Marin had given him, pocketed six silver and a few coppers, strapped on his sword, then stuffed all the loose book pages, fragmentary volumes, and everything related to orcish or dark magic under the bed and covered it with a cloth. Only then did he dust off his hands and head straight for the Mage Quarter stables.

The whole way there he watched the people around him, making sure that not the apprentice mages, the mage students, or even full-fledged mages had any clue there was a demon hidden in his bag.

The few people who did glance his way were only looking at the sword on his hip — probably finding it odd that someone who wasn't a battle mage was wandering around the Wizard Sanctum armed.

At the stables, Jace handed the note to the stablemaster, an impressively overweight middle-aged man. He glanced at the note from his chair, then at the badge on Jace's finger. "Jace Sesso, that right? You're headed to Redridge — Lakeshire or Stonewatch?"

"Stonewatch."

"Come on, then." The man hauled himself up with all his strength. Jace had a feeling this was the fattest human he'd laid eyes on since arriving in this world. Did he sneak horse feed when no one was looking?

The stablemaster led out a chestnut mare. "She's been running the Stormwind–Goldshire–Lakeshire–Stonewatch route for years. She was trained as a warhorse originally, but they retired her for being too skittish. They handed her over to us, but she's still braver than your average horse out there. Don't worry about gnolls — this lady won't panic, and she'll definitely outrun the lot of them. You take good care of her!"

Jace swung up onto the glossy-coated, tall mare and immediately felt the difference from the nags tied up outside the merchant shops back in Lordaeron. This mare paced firm and powerful beneath him, her stride so smooth that even sitting fully in the saddle, he didn't feel like he was being rattled to pieces.

He turned the horse and circled around the wall toward the main gate. He was riding so fast, having such a blast, that guards along the way kept shouting at him to slow down and not run anyone over.

Passing through the main gate, threading through the stream of wagons pulled by pack horses, Jace — sitting a full head above everyone — actually felt like a soldier. This must be what it felt like to drive a luxury car.

Still, when the real Stormwind patrol knights on their warhorses rode by, he was put in his place and looked like a messenger boy again.

At the tavern where they'd agreed to meet, Jace tied the horse to the hitching rail by the trough and reached back to stroke the mare's mane, thinking about when he might have a horse of his own.

That short ride had been so thrilling he was already getting hooked.

"Hah! So these are the Mage Quarter's horses — gorgeous!" Grede came laughing out of the tavern. "I thought some noble lord had pulled up. Turns out it's just you."

Then the dwarf noticed the stick on Jace's back. "You brought that thing?"

"It's too valuable to leave in a rented room," Jace explained. "I'm not planning to go into battle with it or anything. Besides, it makes a pretty decent club. You carried it for miles through Duskwood and it held up fine."

Grede asked curiously, "So, you figured out what it is?"

"Might be a staff the demons made for the orcs," Jace said, lowering his voice. "Not that unusual on the orc side, but it's unusual enough around here!"

Grede leaned in close. "How much can we get for it?"

"I heard there's a bunch of warlocks in Ironforge," Jace said. "If we get the chance to sell it to them, it should fetch a few gold."

Grede's eyes went wide. "Good thing I didn't sell it to those Bronzebeard fools in the Dwarf District. Or did they already know someone in Ironforge would want it and try to cheat me out of it?"

The two of them crouched by the door and sorted through their gear. Jace said, "Speaking of selling things, though, I heard about a place that specializes in rare curios like this."

Grede pulled a strip of dried meat from his bag, stuffed it in his mouth, cinched the bag shut, and asked, "Where?"

"Booty Bay."

"You mean that goblin town on the Stranglethorn coast."

"Right." Jace said, "They've got an exchange there, goblin-run. Word is all sorts of rarities sell for a pretty penny. Remember that letter we looted off that orc corpse?"

"That letter?" Grede asked. "You're sure it was a letter?"

Jace raised an eyebrow. "You know who Teron Gorefiend is?"

"Of course. Death knight… the Horde's vanguard!"

"That was a letter Teron Gorefiend wrote to one of his subordinates."

"Really?"

"Really," Jace said. "Guess how much that letter would be worth to a collector of important orcish relics? That's Teron Gorefiend!"

Grede licked his lips, stared at the ground, and thought for a moment. "Damn, chances to make a fortune really are everywhere. While the orcs haven't all been wiped out yet, shouldn't we be hunting for more opportunities like this? Line our pockets with a little 'war profit'? You want to bring your parents to Stormwind, right? Working day labor here, you'll never afford a house in the city."

He scratched the beard on his chin. "But Stranglethorn's way too long a trip. Ships from Stormwind Harbor to Booty Bay don't run often, and the fare isn't cheap either. If we don't get a good price — or can't sell it at all — that'd be a hell of a loss."

"True," Jace said. "So let's wait and see. Or I'll ask around Stormwind if there's anyone willing to buy something like this. We can always make a trip to Booty Bay later if the chance comes up."

Grede nodded. "Let's get moving. Any longer and we won't make it to the Eastvale waystation before dark."
