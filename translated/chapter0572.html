Chapter 572: A Fellow Hunter, or an Enemy?

After hearing Jace's words, Celebras fell into thought.

It was a good while before he spoke. "My father told me that Princess Theradras has lost all reason, and her crimes against the whole of Kalimdor are unforgivable. But I also understand that killing her is nearly impossible..."

With that, he turned and walked step by step toward a lush patch of grass, stretching his thick arms entwined with vines and branches, and began channeling a powerful nature spell.

Beneath Celebras's solid black deer hooves, yellow and red flowers sprouted from the grass. A warm, gentle breeze swirled about him as he began chanting incantations in an unknown tongue — or perhaps these weren't incantations at all, but a call. Between the words he called out Zaetar's name, as if seeking an answer.

Before long, Celebras suddenly opened his eyes and turned his head toward Jace. All the winds of nature magic gently dissipated, and the surroundings fell quiet.

"I can sense that the great Zaetar has given his answer! It must be because I have understood his intent — he truly does not wish to leave, because he wants to save this dead land and redeem the mistakes that were committed. I can feel it. He did not wish for me to rashly enter Theramore unprepared, and so he refused to answer before..."

"Thank you, human. Cenarius taught us that balance always exists between all things, and there are example after example demonstrating that demonic power does not always bring pure madness and evil."

"Example after example?" Jace suddenly grew curious. Where exactly had Celebras seen other examples of demonic power bringing benefits?

Celebras said, "After a host of Dark Portal invaders was defeated by humans and fled to this continent, aside from the demons, there were also orcs and ogres — as you call them. They hid in the southern jungles; some retreated deep into Kaldorei ruins, plotting new dark conspiracies in this land. But among them there are also the lucid ones who hunt the fallen villains. I know a wanderer who recently came here from another continent, seeking out the orc warlock cabals in the south that are attempting to summon more demons, disrupting their summoning rituals."

"That orc doesn't carry the demonic taint the other orcs do, Celebras," Caewindra corrected.

"But he is still a member of the invaders, and he carries the weapons the demons forged for the orcish armies," Celebras said.

"An orc who fights against demons?!" Grede said. "Now that you mention it, I'd actually like to meet him. As it happens, we're heading to the Feralas jungles to continue hunting demons too, sir. Do you know where he lives? Maybe we could swap notes!"

Celebras shook his head. "He has no fixed abode. My companions have only observed him from a distance. But he is far bulkier than a normal orc, and he is always accompanied by a bear. If you run into him, you'll know him at once."

If Jace had only been slightly suspicious when the forest guardian described this wanderer, the moment "enormous build" and "always with a bear" came up, there was no other possibility. This wanderer had to be Rexxar, the Beastmaster — a half-orc who carried ogre blood in his veins, which was why he possessed a size and strength far beyond that of a normal orc.

So he'd been wandering Kalimdor this early?

"We don't know anything about that orc, Grede," Wenresa said displeasedly. "Just because we both hunt demons doesn't mean we'll automatically be friends. Let's just stick to the original plan."

"All right, I'm just curious, Wenresa," the dwarf soothed her. "We won't go looking for the orc. I never had much love for orcs to begin with."

"So can we leave?" Jace asked, looking at Caewindra before him.

The dryad nodded. "I think I was over-worried about you. My sincere apologies, friends from another continent."

"Please accept this." Celebras reached up and snapped off a segment of one of his antlers. Grede's eyelids and the corners of his mouth both twitched watching the act.

Honestly, Jace felt a little sick too — the gesture alone looked painful, especially with that crisp crack.

Celebras's stern expression eased, and he even smiled slightly, as if he found the two of them being uncomfortable rather than amusing.

"It'll grow back quickly, no need to worry, you two." He placed the antler-tip, short-staff-like, into Musa's hands. "If you enter Feralas, you're likely to encounter Kaldorei sentinels. They may not try to discern your intentions the way we have, traveler. But if you present my token to them, it should clear up some misunderstandings."

"Much obliged," Wenresa said.

"I should be thanking you," Celebras said. "You killed the demon who sought to corrupt this place, and you brought Zaetar's will... Now I can return to my father and explain what his elder brother has been thinking, instead of continuing to wander this dangerous region pointlessly. Farewell, travelers."

Celebras's voice gradually faded. Without anyone noticing, Cenarius's offspring had already vanished, leaving behind only a flourish of blooming flowers to prove they had been there.

Wenresa looked at the antler in Musa's hand. "I have to say, I didn't expect even the forest guardians to be so sure we couldn't avoid the Kaldorei."

"The Kaldorei have managed to survive here for ten thousand years for a reason," Jace said. "If this antler can truly help us avoid conflict, it'll have helped us clear up a major hidden danger."

"Thing is, we were lucky we ran into Theradras, Jace," Grede said. "If we hadn't stirred her up and gone back the same way, we wouldn't have run into these forest sprites at all."

"Just as the demigod Cenarius said..." Jace imitated Celebras's tone and cadence. "Balance always exists between all things. Who knows whether a thing is good or bad, in the end?"

"There's some truth in that," the dwarf nodded.

"I'm a bit worried about that wanderer they mentioned too," Wenresa said. "The forest guardian just said that fellow goes around hunting warlocks and demons, Jace..."

She glanced around, then looked at the warlock's pack at Jace's waist. "You're a warlock too, and there are two demons in our party — and we've got nothing that would convince him otherwise."

"Don't forget Jace speaks Orcish, Wenresa," Grede said. "Relax. This guy talked an orc shaman into sparing him once. It'll be fine."

Wenresa helplessly dropped her shoulders. "I can't imagine an orc having a civilized conversation with us. But hopefully."
