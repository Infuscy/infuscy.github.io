## Chapter 573: The Culprits Who Stirred Up the Sacred Land

Jace leaned against a dirt mound, watching the Maraudine centaurs coming and going through the mouth of Spear Valley. They looked frantic, and shouting echoed continuously from within.

What felt even more off was the black smoke rising in column after column from inside the valley, drifting high into the sky — as if fires had broken out everywhere and only just been put out.

He glanced at Wenresa beside him. "What do you make of it?"

"The unrest deep within Maraudon is having a bigger impact than I expected," Wenresa said.

"You think," Grede said, "they'll pin the earthquake on us?"

Musa said, "On us."

Grede shot her a look. "I didn't teach you Common so you could throw it back in my face, Lady Musa."

The tauren didn't answer. She just smiled at the black smoke, watching the centaurs rushing back and forth with the look of someone enjoying the show.

"I have a feeling Vilethorn's head might be impossible to hand in after all," Jace said, scratching his head. "Any other way I can cash it in? I can't exactly take it home with me."

"What about the other centaur tribes?" Grede asked.

"The other tribes can't wait for the Maraudine to collapse and hand Spear Valley over to them," Jace said, sliding down the mound. "What would they want with a demon's head? The best we can hope for is that they don't blame us for helping the Maraudine."

Grede swore under his breath. "So a five-gold gem just went up in smoke?"

"Look on the bright side — who knows what kind of 'treasure' those centaurs might dig up," Jace said. "He could hand you a big chunk of light-blue glass and swear it's the Tears of Theradras, and what are you going to do about it? Their brains are all messed up. Getting angry won't help."

Grede drooped his head. "Guess that's the only way to comfort myself. What was it you said before — what that forest guardian said — everything has a good side and a bad side. Is this the bad side?"

"I'd suggest saving that line for when something good happens, Grede. I'm a little annoyed right now." Jace stood up and brushed the dirt off his clothes. "Hopefully the magical materials Vilethorn stashed away will fetch a decent price. Did we lose anything back in those waterways?"

"No," Grede said. "At least I didn't."

"Me neither," Wenresa said. She asked Musa, who tugged the strap of her leather pouch and shook her head.

"Then it's not all that bad," Jace said.

Heading south from Spear Valley, across a plain littered with enormous beast bones, Jace gazed at a serpentine skeleton stretching tens or even hundreds of yards along a distant hillside, and a skull several times larger than a kodo's. He thought for a long time and still couldn't figure out what these creatures had been.

Before the Kaldorei appeared before the Well of Eternity, before the troll empires ruled ancient Kalimdor, Azeroth had gone through a long and savage age.

Wild gods, lost Titan servants, and elementals that had survived the fall of the Black Empire walked the surface of the world, fighting and competing for growth, gradually forging the prototype of the modern world.

Who knew how many terrifying behemoths had appeared and perished back then?

Among these skeletons, centaur patrols still roamed. Jace and his group used the bones and hillslopes for cover, keeping their distance from the centaur camps and villages, avoiding the roads the centaurs held, and heading toward the southern valleys.

After roughly three days of walking, the shadows of the southern mountains came into view. Kalimor was full of nameless peaks like these — every one of them looking more steep and towering than the Redridge Mountains, impossible to cross.

Fortunately, starting here they could finally see some plants struggling to grow, which was a vast improvement over the total deadness of Desolace.

The mountain wind blew fiercely, making it feel as if there was some kind of hostility in the air. Not only that, but Jace could smell a faint stench on the wind — not the scent of fel or shadow or any dark power, but enough to make a person deeply uncomfortable.

"What's that smell?" Jace asked.

"You caught it too," Grede said. "Smells like a dead goat. Nasty."

Not long after, they spotted a cage woven from dry grass and branches hanging from a low tree by the roadside, stuffed full of... heads.

All kinds of heads — some fresh, some rotting. This was probably the source of that strange stench.

Jace fought his disgust and leaned in for a closer look. The heads inside were a bizarre assortment: several quilboar heads, mostly centaurs', and most strangely, a huge ogre head crammed in among them.

Circling the sack, Jace caught sight of an elven woman's skull in the corner.

It should have been a night elf, but since it had dried out somewhat, this gaunt, deformed head really did look a bit like a high elf's at a glance.

Wenresa stared at the head for a moment, then looked toward the far end of the valley. She spotted another such cage hanging from a low tree on a higher cliff face — though the height and slope of that spot made it basically impossible for them to climb up there.

Musa let out a word in the tauren tongue. Jace asked, "What?"

"Harpies," Wenresa said.

Musa repeated after Wenresa. "Harpies. Warn centaurs."

"Ah, I get it," Grede said, realization dawning. "She means the harpies here put these heads up to warn the centaurs to the north not to enter their territory."

"So we've wandered into harpy territory?" Wenresa said, looking up at the high cliff.

As she spoke, she unslung her bow. Arcane light flickered in her eyes. "I can't sense any elemental disturbance here yet. Judging by the harpies we ran into back with the tauren, their elementalists don't seem to know how to hide their power very well. I think even if there are harpies lying in ambush around here, they won't be that strong."

"Strong or not, they're a bunch of flying nuisances," Grede muttered, pulling down the bow that had been hanging at his side — Vilethorn's bow. "If I were riding a griffin, let them try strutting around over our heads."

Though the group stayed alert, watching the path ahead as they advanced and sticking to ground where they wouldn't get pinched from both sides, after walking all afternoon through this valley where no living thing seemed to breathe, they didn't actually spot a single harpy. Only the occasional shriek of some unidentifiable beast echoed from deep within the valley.

Jace figured the harpies must weigh the strength of their opponents too. A group of travelers who clearly looked like bad news, and who were most likely just passing through — not worth attacking rashly.

By evening, the sky gradually darkened. Jace and the others came to a low cliff at a mountain pass and gazed out toward the distance below. They could already see a deep green shrouded in mist and clouds.

That should be the Feralas jungles.
