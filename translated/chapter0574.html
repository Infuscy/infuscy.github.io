Chapter 574: The Summoning Motive

Thanks to Theradras's greed, Feralas and Desolace were separated only by a range of mountains, yet their climates were complete opposites.

Here it was wet and stifling. After pushing deeper, lifting your head revealed layer upon layer of enormous canopy — you could barely even see the sky.

Walking through it felt like being buried alive in a green tomb, hard to breathe.

The group followed what appeared to be an ancient road left behind by the Kaldorei, threading through the jungle, but even this road had been all but swallowed by the surrounding vegetation.

As they walked, Grede asked, "Have any of you thought about why the satyrs wanted to summon Dathmora's doomguard servant?"

He quickened his pace to catch up with Jace and went on, "If Wenresa is right, and that long, troublesome incantation was the satyrs trying to rescue a doomguard from the elven city, with that little red sphere as the casting material — then why? Those satyrs don't look like they're cooperating with other demons either, aside from those weird little things that might or might not be imps. I think a doomguard is way more powerful than them. Were the satyrs summoning that demon servant here to find themselves a chieftain?"

"Maybe the satyrs are looking for allies for their next move?" Wenresa said, turning her head. "We know that every demon of the Burning Legion staying on Azeroth has only one goal: to summon more Burning Legion demons here to assist them. Their ultimate aim is to summon the master of the Legion to this world. So seeking out more allies is a natural motive."

Jace said, "Maybe we should find time to take a closer look at the clues we found."

"Good idea," Grede said, pointing at Jace. "Ever since we ran out of Maraudon we've been running and running and running... aside from resting on that bit of coastline, we haven't really stopped."

"That couldn't be helped. We needed to leave Desolace as quickly as possible," Wenresa said. "Not to mention the centaur harassment before — we didn't have much way to get food either, and the rations we'd stored were basically gone. Look at this forest, dwarf. Life everywhere, things to eat everywhere. It's a lot more reassuring than that wasteland before."

Grede took a deep breath, hand on his waist. "It's only you elves who'd find a dense forest even more exaggerated than the Hinterlands reassuring. I'd say this is only marginally better than Stranglethorn Vale, and the trees here are packed so tight — tree after tree — the animals all hide inside where you can't see them. Even if you spot one, you can't catch it."

He kicked viciously at a thick bush beside him. "Look at this stuff tripping us up. Nearly knocked me over several times. We can't live on grass from here on out, can we!"

Wenresa stared at the dwarf and said softly, "You really have forgotten who I am."

"Who are you?" Grede plopped down on a tree root beside him. "A pointy-eared maniac who eats more than a griffin?"

The dwarf grinned after he said it. Wenresa rolled her eyes and lifted her chin to look up through the dense foliage overhead.

She closed her eyes and chanted an obscure incantation. The incantation was almost like singing — the voice wasn't loud at all, and even standing very close it sounded gentle and soothing, yet it carried far into the distance.

The dwarf looked around with the air of someone watching a show. Jace set down what he was holding and watched to see what would happen.

Before long, the sound of flapping wings came from every direction. Bird after bird of every color circled down to the group's side.

They wouldn't land. Wenresa waved a hand, signaling Jace and the others to move back. The few of them retreated behind the trees, and the birds eagerly descended upon the elf, some even landing on her shoulders.

Wenresa conversed with them in some unknown language, occasionally responding to their chirps with whistles. Before long, her gaze fell on a brightly plumed, thick-bodied junglefowl.

The ranger called out softly to the junglefowl. When it came before her, without any warning Wenresa shot out a hand with lightning speed, grabbed it, and wrung its neck. All the birds were suddenly startled and fluttered in every direction...

In the blink of an eye, it was quiet around Wenresa, leaving only a mess of feathers drifting down in every direction.

She turned around, holding up her prize, and tilted her head at Grede with a triumphant smile.

"I'm impressed, Lady Windrunner. I'll go find firewood right now," Grede said immediately, slapping his backside and dashing into the forest.

That water-cave trip through Maraudon had ruined a good deal of the dried herbs and leaves they'd been using as spices, but at least the bottled seasonings were still intact.

Getting the kind of experience you'd have at a Stormwind inn was pretty much out of the question, but as long as you sprinkled some salt on roasted meat, it was hard to make it taste bad. Being able to eat chicken in a place like this — you couldn't afford to ask for anything more.

"If we were in the Hinterlands, I could probably dig up some seasonings from the forest," Grede said, gazing at the crackling campfire. "You know, plenty of things that grow on trees or in the grass that add flavor over a fire. Of course, sometimes you have to fight the trolls for them."

"What trolls like can't be anything good," Wenresa said.

The dwarf wagged his finger. "Don't be so one-sided. Every now and then there are decent ones..."

The tauren sat nearby, carefully examining the antler Celebras had given her — stroking it, sniffing it from time to time. By now the antler had actually grown a small bit on its own, the broken surface turning into a small branch that the tauren could grip just right, really looking like a beautiful little short staff.

Jace sat to one side, reading through a demonic scroll, half-listening to Grede and Wenresa chat about trolls in the Hinterlands and Quel'Thalas. The scroll in front of him described the Eredun, the race doomguard belonged to. The finer details he couldn't quite make out.

After all, his demonic script was still a bit shaky for reading a scroll this complex. He could only get a rough idea of what it was about.

Then Sazeno beside him said, "Master, the contents of this scroll aren't in demonic script..."

"If it's not demonic, then what, the language of the Nathrezim?"

Jace took the scroll the imp handed him and looked again. No — these were elven characters.

"Wenresa."

"Hmm?" The elf, who was in the middle of arguing with Grede about troll appetites, looked over.

"Take a look at this," Jace said, handing her a scroll. "What's this about?"

Wenresa held up the scroll and read it over, muttering to herself. "Demon... Sathduthus... wait — this is a Kaldorei method for dealing with demons."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked. "Is this talking about the demon prison in Eldre'Thalas?"

"No," Wenresa said. "This seems to be teaching a person how to create a magic circle that can weaken and trap demons."

"I know!" Sazeno suddenly flipped open a scroll it had looked at earlier and pulled it out. "Then it all makes sense! This is a spell for enslaving demons, Master!"
