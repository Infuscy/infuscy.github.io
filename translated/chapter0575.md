## Chapter 575: The Ranger-Captain's Competitive Streak

Morophil, who was sitting nearby, set down the scroll in her hands. "You think I've never heard an eredar chant a spell for enslaving demons? I believe that one is for a warlock to communicate with a powerful demon lord. I think the warlock who created this spell intended to converse with the demon they were searching for across planes in some way, while avoiding provoking the other's wrath and getting themselves killed."

"Would a demon actually change its temper over a spell like that?" Grede asked.

"Don't forget the effects of Fear, Curse of Recklessness, and the rest of those mind-affecting magics, dwarf," Morophil said. "But your suspicion isn't without merit. Demons like the Nathrezim are very hard to influence. As for the rest... actually, there are plenty of powerful demons who can be pleased this way. The brainless Annihilan, for instance, eat this stuff right up. And some arrogant eredar can be taken in too."

"No, no, no, Succubus," the imp said excitedly. "If it weren't for that demon-weakening circle, I might agree with your reading. But look at these two lines..."

It spread the scroll flat on the ground, hopped up onto it, and pointed at the lines above, chanting several sentences of demonic that Jace couldn't understand at all. "These two lines, combined with that Kaldorei weakening circle, supported by a sufficiently powerful mana source — this isn't about lowering hostility at all. It's active control and enslavement on a far more dramatic scale, forcibly establishing a master-servant bond!"

"Before, I could roughly grasp the motive behind this spell, but it was clearly missing something, so I couldn't be sure. If there's a circle that can trap a demon inside and weaken its power, then this spell achieves exactly what it was designed to do! This is a specially modified and tailored spell! Of course, it's not your fault either — these satyrs' demonic script is a mess, lines scattered everywhere, plus a bunch of markings in languages I can't read. It's genuinely hard to fully make sense of."

After Morophil heard this, she didn't say anything to mock Sazeno. She stared at the scroll for a while, then ran her tongue over her lips. "You've got a point, imp."

"It's not that I've got a point — it's just how it is!" the imp said. "So things are basically clear now. Those transformed satyrs went through all that trouble because they wanted to summon the doomguard belonging to that warlock we're looking for, trap it, and put it to their own use."

Wenresa asked, "So what's so special about this doomguard that they'd go through all this?"

"You could find a jungle troll," Grede said, tossing a huge chicken bone to the side. "Then throw Vilethorn's head into their magic cauldron and ask the head directly what it was trying to do."

Wenresa shot him a look. "This isn't the time for troll jokes, dwarf."

Jace said, "The reason the satyrs wanted to summon it might be the very reason the inhabitants of Eldre'Thalas sealed it in their prison in the first place."

"Looks like we won't know for sure without actually going to Eldre'Thalas," Grede said, sucking his fingers. "I'm getting a strange feeling now, like an invisible hand is pushing me toward that ancient city."

"A hand made of gold coins, Grede," Wenresa reminded him. "Don't forget how much you'll get from the Reliquary if we can find Dathmora..."

Before she could finish, Wenresa's ears suddenly pricked up. She whipped her head toward the distance, exactly like a wildcat that had heard something.

"Move."

She said just that one word. Jace and Grede immediately smothered the fire and buried the chicken bones. The demons rolled up the scrolls on the ground — some turning invisible, some packing up. Musa softly spoke an incantation and the surrounding bushes and weeds instantly swallowed the campsite, then she transformed into a large cat and followed the others into the jungle.

They moved slowly deeper into the jungle. Jace felt the strength of Morophil's invisibility growing stronger and stronger beside him, until it was so strong he couldn't make it out clearly even with Sense Demons — he could only detect the faintest trace through the Soul Link. Clearly, she too sensed something extremely threatening closing in.

Wenresa didn't explain what she'd sensed. This probably wasn't the time for talking. After reaching a densely shaded area where the vegetation was especially thick, she tugged Jace's cloak, signaling him to stop.

Once everyone fell silent, the deep jungle was terrifyingly quiet.

A beetle crawled past Jace's foot. The scratch of its legs against his leather boot was clearly audible.

Then, from some indeterminate distance away, came the sound of conversation.

It sounded like several women speaking. Once Jace confirmed they were female voices, he furrowed his brow.

Kaldorei sentinels?

The people over there blew several whistles, and a few bestial, savage growls came from that direction. Wenresa seized the chance to raise her head and glance over. Jace tentatively stretched his neck to look as well, but she immediately pushed him back down.

The sound of undergrowth being trampled and parted slowly faded into the distance. Wenresa leaned back against the tree, let out a breath, and said softly, "I saw them."

"What were those things?" Grede asked urgently. "Why did I hear tiger sounds?"

"Not ordinary tigers," Wenresa said. "A pack of Fangblade panthers — the Kaldorei's ancient mounts. They've been loyal allies for ten thousand years."

"So we've run into the local elves?" Grede shifted a couple of steps closer and whispered, "What did they look like? Ugh, I should have taken a peek just now."

"Your big head would have immediately given away our position, dwarf." Wenresa narrowed her eyes. "I'll give you a simple description. Dark silver, or lavender skin, draped in blue-green hair, tall, powerfully built... exactly like the books say."

"They wore leather armor, carried war blades, and had longbows on their backs — about four or five of them. Frankly, I can't judge how strong they are. If the information from the Reliquary is correct, quite a few of these Kaldorei may have lived for several thousand years. Not a single one of them can be underestimated. Best not to start any trouble at all."

"Several thousand years?!" The dwarf raised his eyebrows and took a deep breath, then seemed to remember something and pointed at the leather pack on the big cat crouching nearby. "Don't we have that antler the forest guardian gave us? What's there to worry about?"

"That's a last resort for clearing up misunderstandings," Jace said. "Don't forget the issues between Quel'Thalas and them, Grede. If we can avoid meeting them, we should."

"Those are all secondary, Jace. What matters more is I genuinely don't believe the Kaldorei could catch me." Wenresa pulled her hood lower and gave a cold smile. "From the looks of it, they're nothing special."
