Chapter 578: The Half-Orc

"At least among the dwarves, this wouldn't count as a proper diplomatic occasion..." Grede muttered.

Wenresa shot him a look and walked on in silence.

The sentinel captain and two escorts rode tall Fangblade panthers at the head of the column, one of the panthers carrying their luggage. The sentinels flanked the several bound captives following behind, stretching into a long line.

Jace noticed their luggage had been placed neatly, with no sign of having been opened or rummaged through. He had no idea where Sazeno had gone — Jace couldn't see him at all with Sense Demons. The imp had probably fled together with Morophil.

If those two had been caught by the elves, they certainly wouldn't have been treated this kindly.

His staff and the Song of the Blue Sky were also hanging from the side of one of the mounts, though the sentinel captain kept glancing at that sword, looking quite interested, but she never actually reached out to draw it and take a look.

Rexxar never said a word. He just silently followed beside Jace, step after step. Every time his foot hit the ground, Jace felt as though there was a tremor. Judging by the look of him, this probably wasn't the first time he'd been arrested by the night elves.

The she-bear, hood over her head, followed at the very end surrounded by several sentinels, and was also behaving herself without any intention of resistance.

"You weren't trying to kill me," Jace said in Orcish.

"Neither were you," Rexxar said. "You're not like the other warlocks I've killed. I saw that a long time ago."

"A long time?" Jace asked. "How long?"

Rexxar didn't answer. After a while, he asked quietly, "How do you know my name?"

"There's a story circulating in your tribe about a half-breed," Jace replied. "I felt you matched that description — your build, your looks, your way of befriending beasts."

Rexxar said nothing. He walked on in silence for a while, then said, "Tell me your story. Why did you come to this side?"

Jace said, "I came here looking for a warlock too. So I said there was no need for us to fight — in a sense, we're in the same line of work. It's just you wouldn't listen to me."

"There are plenty of warlocks here," Rexxar said. "Ogres, orcs, demons — who are you looking for?"

"Have you heard of Dathmora Dark-Eye?" Jace asked.

"No," the half-orc shook his head.

Of course Jace wasn't surprised in the slightest. The orcs hadn't had any close contact with the races of the Alliance, let alone Rexxar, a lone wanderer who'd been roaming the wilds for God knows how many years. It was perfectly normal that he hadn't heard the legends that circulated within the Alliance.

"He's an elven warlock," Jace said. "He should have some connection to Eldre'Thalas. Do you know Eldre'Thalas?"

"Ancient elves?" Rexxar asked, glancing over.

"You know the place," Jace said. "Have you been inside?"

"It's been occupied by the Gordunni clan. They don't welcome me — even though I look like one, I'm not an ogre," Rexxar said.

"I'm surprised you're here looking for someone," he went on. "I took you for a group of gladiators who'd come a long way."

"What?" Jace asked.

"Gladiators. You haven't heard of them? Fighters who make money fighting others." Rexxar said. "The Gordunni clan has been holding arenas for a long time. They did it on Draenor, and they're doing the same thing here. A bunch of fools. Quite entertaining, actually."

"Have you competed in an arena?" Jace asked.

"Ten years ago, maybe I would have been interested," Rexxar said. "But right now, I'm tired of meaningless fighting."

"Then what are you doing hunting warlocks and demons out here?" Jace asked.

Rexxar still didn't answer. He just walked on in silence.

After a while, he spoke again. "You first — why are you looking for that warlock?"

"For the money," Jace said.

"That simple?" Rexxar couldn't help but laugh.

"Not simple at all," Jace said. "I need money to buy food and drink. I need money to find a proper place for my family to live."

Rexxar nodded. "I'm doing it for my family too."

"Your family?" Jask asked.

The half-orc glanced back at the giant bear. The bear grunted a few times, as if answering him.

"I don't want them hurt by more dark things," he went on. "But clearly my strength alone isn't nearly enough. All I can do is try my best."

"I feel my ties to my homeland growing weaker and weaker, to the point where I can't feel them at all anymore. So I can't afford to lose this place too. I imagine it's hard for you to understand that feeling, human."

"Quiet," the sentinel beside them warned in Common.

Rexxar shot her a look but fell silent.

Though he said nothing, Jace felt he probably understood this feeling even better than Rexxar did.

He looked at Grede and Wenresa walking side by side up ahead, then gazed at the path leading deeper into the jungle. This was everything he had now. This world.

Rexxar couldn't lose Azeroth anymore. Neither could he.

By evening, the sentinel column rested at a bend in the road. The bound few were arranged to sit in the shade of a tree. The giant bear Misha curled up beside them and fell fast asleep, as if she couldn't care less about the sentinels. Rexxar leaned against her, picking up stones off the ground and tossing them to kill time.

Grede watched Rexxar's game for a while, then leaned over to Jace and Wenresa and whispered, "That guy... he's the one who..."

"Yes," Jace said.

"How come he's not afraid of these elves?" the dwarf asked curiously.

"As far as I know, the Kaldorei here only drive outsiders who aren't allowed into their patrol zones back out. They don't hurt them," Wenresa said. "Of course, aside than demons. From their conversation, I could tell they've already sensed the presence of demons — they just weren't sure if it was because of you, or because of something we're carrying, or if there really are demons nearby."

She gave Jace a meaningful look. Jace said quietly, "I'm certain there are no demons nearby."

"Good," Wenresa nodded.

"Didn't you say you wouldn't be caught by them, Wenresa?" Grede asked with a grin.

Wenresa looked at Grede coldly. "I admit my skills were lacking. I have nothing to say."

"Don't blame yourself. Who knew we had a guy like him tailing us the whole time," the dwarf comforted her. "He threw all your arrangements into chaos."

"I'm not making excuses for myself, but I did sense something was off a while ago," Wenresa said. "Not long after we entered Feralas a few days back, I felt like some of the birds in the trees were acting strange. It wasn't until I actually fought him that I realized that fellow could get beasts to help him. From that angle, maybe a lot of those birds were his eyes and ears. He didn't even need to follow us the whole time — animals would keep reporting to him."

"Can't guard against that," Grede murmured. "Too many strange characters around here."

"That half-orc mentioned Eldre'Thalas has been invaded by ogres," Jace whispered. "Might be easier to sneak in than we think."

"Ogres?!" Grede exclaimed. Wenresa was equally surprised.

"Right now, I just hope their general recognizes Celebras's antler and lets us go," the ranger sighed. "What a mess."

Jace wasn't particularly worried about the outcome, because that general was Shandris Feathermoon. Ever since ten thousand years ago, she had followed the present leader of the night elves, High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind. There was no way she wouldn't recognize a forest guardian's token.
