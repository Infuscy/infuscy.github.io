Chapter 58: Something New for Jace

From the Lake Everstill bridge, the white walls and red-tiled roofs climbing from the lakeshore up the hillside were ringed by deep green forest, layer upon layer. The tall bell tower sat among them like an old man in a red cap surrounded by children in their finery, gazing down at the approaching travelers.

The reflection of Lakeshire shimmered in the water, interweaving with the town itself into a giant, gorgeous butterfly sprawled between mountain and lake.

Jace sat atop his horse staring at the oil-painting beauty of it all, almost forgetting the dangers lurking in these mountains.

On the bridge, camp tents clustered and swayed, soldiers' silver armor and spears jostling together. Even though the war was over, the Lake Everstill shore remained one of the most dangerous stretches in the whole Kingdom.

Looking out from the bridge into the distance, through the last of the morning mist, Jace could already make out the faint outline of the fortress watchtower on the far side of the lake. That was Stonewatch — the lookout that overlooked the entire Redridge Mountains and the Lake Everstill valley, and the most important military stronghold in the region.

During the war, orcs and humans had fought bitterly over this stronghold for many rounds, leaving Stonewatch battered and ruined by years of magic and explosives. It wouldn't be fully rebuilt even twenty years from now.

But Lakeshire itself seemed to have recovered well enough. Only the construction materials and scaffolding still left along the sides told the story of the disaster that had struck here a few years back.

The bridge hadn't been fully repaired yet, progress was slow, but it could already handle pedestrians and individual horses.

They got off the horse at the Lakeshire stables, and Jace couldn't tear his eyes away from the glittering surface of the lake. He wasn't the only one enchanted by the view — plenty of soldiers who'd just arrived from Eastvale Logging Camp were sitting in groups along the dock's edge, gazing out at the water and chatting.

Further along the shore, a few children splashed and played in the lake while their older sisters or mothers stood on the bank shouting anxiously. An old man carrying a rod and fishing tackle walked off into the distance, probably looking for a quieter spot away from the crowd.

"I need to head into town center and ask the boys at the Warriors' Guild what exactly we're here to do," Grede hitching up his trousers as he came over. "The air here's real nice, though. Staying too long in a place like Stormwind's no good for your health."

"Never mind health right now," Jace said, looking at him. "Don't tell me you came to Lakeshire without even knowing what the job was?"

"I'm a sergeant now, Jace," Grede said, patting his belly. "Even if I don't know what the mission is, I know it won't be too dangerous. It matches my rank. That's the beauty of rank, see? And the payout's not bad either — the guild told me there's at least ten silver in it for this one. At least!"

"Remember that time we nearly died for two silver?" Jace reminded him.

"That was an accident. An accident," Grede said with a grin. "Who'd have thought a simple hauling job would run into the biggest headache in post-war Stormwind Kingdom? That giant gnoll's a prime target for Westfall Keep now. You could say we were downright heroic."

Put like that, Grede wasn't exactly wrong. Assigning jobs based on guild members' performance and combat ability, making sure everyone had a role, got some training, and wasn't constantly risking their necks — even if the guild skimmed a bit off the top, it wasn't such a bad deal.

"I'll trust you one more time. Where's the inn around here?" Jace asked.

"Over there." Grede said. "On the other side of that little grove. See that three-story building, the one half-hidden behind the bell tower?"

Jace looked where he was pointing. "I'll wait for you over there."

The inn in Lakeshire didn't have some flashy name like the inns in Stormwind. It was just called the Lakeshire Inn.

But this plainly named inn was anything but small, and the variety of food and drink on offer was remarkably rich — plenty of things you wouldn't even find in Stormwind, like vulture meat and all sorts of lake fish that Jace had only ever heard about in the game.

Skipping past the wild boar liver, the stew, and the rest of the usual fare, Jace's eyes came to rest on one item on the menu board — and he couldn't look away.

"Roasted… roasted raptor meat?"

He stared at the bald innkeeper sitting behind the counter. The man looked up. "Raptor meat, that's right, young sir. Didn't you know? Stranglethorn raptor meat is more tender than chicken and crisper than fish, and with local Redridge spices you can even taste a hint of dwarven flavor. The price has gone up a bit because of the trade route situation, but it's still well worth a try."

Jace snapped out of it. Right — there were raptors in Stranglethorn Vale.

"I'll have one."

"One silver thirty copper, sir. Raptor meat's our specialty here."

"One and a half silver for a single serving of roasted meat?!" Jace glanced at the menu again.

That was raptor meat!

"I'll take a plate. And a pitcher of moon-plum juice." He gritted his teeth, slapped a silver and a handful of coppers on the table, and the innkeeper swept the money over and immediately hollered for the kitchen to get the fire going.

Over by the hearth on the other side of the common room, a few soldiers out of their armor were singing some song. Jace rested his chin in his hand and stared at the parcel sitting on his table, thinking about how there was a tiny demon hidden inside it — it all felt incredibly unreal.

So he put the bag under the table and clamped it with his foot.

Lakeshire was several times more chaotic than Goldshire. Adventurers from the north and south alike gathered and stayed here, so there weren't just humans — there were dwarves and gnomes too. Outside the inn, Jace was pretty sure he'd even spotted an elf.

A lot of these folks were armed travelers. Someone like Jace, carrying two sacks, an unremarkable long staff on his back and a sword at his hip, didn't stand out at all.

In the corner, a hooded figure in a mask — couldn't tell if they were an elf or human — sat silently sizing up everyone in the inn. Dressed like that, they probably thought they were the most low-profile person in the place, but everyone in the inn was clearly a little afraid of them. Mostly of that long blade on their hip.

Just as Jace was trying to make out what the human soldiers were singing, Grede suddenly dropped into the seat across from him.

"Things are about to get exciting, Jace!"

"I haven't agreed to go along with you yet."

"You will. I'm a hundred percent sure!"

"Why?"

Grede gave a mysterious smile. "The target's a hiding orc mage… Well? Well?!"

Jace immediately understood what Grede meant. "Orc mage" — in reality, an orc warlock. Ordinary townsmen didn't draw any strict distinction between warlocks and mages. Of course they'd lump together anyone who chanted incantations and chucked fireballs as a mage.

Since they'd looted so many good things off that last orc warlock's corpse, who knew what a living one might have on him?

He had to admit — Jace was tempted.

"You're sure he's alone? What if that orc's got a whole squad of bodyguards with him?"

Grede said, "If that were the case, of course we'd forget it. But the intelligence says he's a lone deserter, hiding in Redridge, too scared to keep heading south and rejoin with the main force back to the Dark Portal."

Just then the roasted meat arrived. Grede stared at the big, marbled, strange-looking slab for a long moment, sniffed it, and said, "Raptor meat?"

Jace was stunned. "You can smell that?!"

Grede gave a cold snort. "My da used to be called the Raptor Killer, you know. He killed over a hundred raptors at Grim Batol — more than the bugs you've ever stepped on."

"I've stepped in an ant nest," Jace said.

"You're so tough! Stepping in an ant nest like it's something to brag about!" Grede reached over, tore off a huge chunk of meat, and stuffed it in his mouth before Jace could stop him.

That slab of meat had cost over a silver! Jace hesitated for a moment, then had no choice but to dig into the glistening hunk of meat himself fast — any more worrying about gentlemanly manners and there'd be nothing left!

"Let's spend today having fun in Lakeshire, Jace. Tomorrow we head out and find that orc," Grede said between bites. "How's that sound?"

Jace said unhappily, "I only have three days."

"How long can delivering a letter take?" Grede said. "Play today, tomorrow we take down that orc deserter, day after we deliver the letter and head home. It's not like it's as far as Sentinel Hill from Stormwind. Quick."

"I've got a feeling," Jace said.

"What kind of feeling?" Grede studied Jace's face, then warily glanced around.

Jace said, "Every time you start reassuring me and telling me it'll probably be fine, we're about to have bad luck."

Grede tossed a bone onto the plate. "Then tell me — what's wrong with this plan?"

Jace asked, "A wanted, hiding orc — what are the odds we find him in a single day?"

"There are still some leads. Besides me, nobody here has any experience fighting mages, so I'm shouldering some responsibility and ridding the people of a menace." Grede rolled his eyes at Jace. "Tell you what — let's search tomorrow. If we can't find him, day after tomorrow you head straight to Stonewatch and then back to Stormwind. I'll stay here and keep looking. If you remember your old buddy, come back and keep me company while I search. Either way I'll split the bounty with you. How's that sound? The bounty's thirteen silver total — the Warriors' Guild plus the Lakeshire magistrate reaching into his own purse. Thirteen silver. What do you say?"

Thirteen silver — Jace's heart skipped.

He'd been spending a bit too freely these past couple of days, and payday was still a while off. If he didn't pick up some quick cash, he'd be chewing on dry bread for a while.

And not the kind of greasy, soft, sweet bread you could find at any bakery back in his old life. This was bread coarser and drier than a cornbread bun, harder to chew than a week-old flatbread, and you'd regularly bite into mysterious unknown black hardened chunks.

Eat that for three or four days and a healthy body would slide into a semi-dead, near-death state.

"What if that warlock's actually strong?"

"Reliable evidence says the guy's got no real skills. He's just hiding out and poses a potential threat, but hasn't actually done any damage. The Kingdom garrison at Lakeshire can't spare the men to root him out, so the magistrate's put a bounty on him and left it to us mercenaries."

Orc warlocks were mostly officers among the Horde — the most fanatical and vicious of the orcs. But since he'd deserted, there had to be something lacking in the ability department.

But an orc… was still an orc. Let's search and see.

"It's a deal, then."

Jace took a big gulp of moon-plum juice. Grede laughed and tore off another huge slab of meat. Jace saw that and refused to be outdone — he pulled off every last bit of meat still clinging to a bone.

One of the other biggest perks of coming to Azeroth was never having to worry about calories again. Never having to worry about gaining weight. All the desserts, pastries, meat, and drinks — eat and drink whatever you wanted, whenever the chance came.

For one thing, the food here was nowhere near as calorie-dense as the twenty-first century. Even when something was greasy or cloyingly sweet, to a soul that had been baptized by industrial-strength flavors it always felt like it was missing something — like the difference between diet cola and the real thing.

The other thing was, poverty here genuinely helped him keep the weight off.
