Chapter 580: Feathermoon Stronghold

After walking through the forest for three or four days, being taken prisoner by the sentinels wasn't all bad — at the very least, food and water no longer required them to go to the trouble of sorting it out themselves.

Although most of the food the night elves provided was fruit and tubers, with not much meat, for Jace and the others, who had been eating nothing but roasted meat and dried meat the whole way before, it did at least balance out their diet a little in the beginning.

But by the fourth morning, Jace couldn't help but feel his mouth and stomach were both parched and dry, and his body was getting weaker and weaker.

After the catastrophe of the Well of Eternity's explosion, the dragons planted the World Tree Nordrassil on Mount Hyjal. They blessed the tree and the night elves with their own power. As long as this tree lived, the night elves would possess eternal life.

In other words, the primary source of energy for these night elves wasn't food at all. But Jace was a human — he had no blessing of the World Tree. Eating this kind of thin, bland diet day in and day out, he simply couldn't hold out.

By noon, the sentinels led the group to a cliff head. Gazing into the distance, there was an empty stretch of mudflat, and beyond that a gray, hazy mass where nothing could be made out clearly. Still, this was the most different scenery they'd seen since entering Feralas.

Following a small path down the high cliff onto the mudflat, the small company was gradually enveloped by the vast gray darkness, walking straight into the fog.

Before long, when Jace turned his head again, he couldn't even see the forest behind him. Only the shadow of the enormous canopy high overhead was still visible in outline, or the occasional jutting cliff with a faint silhouette.

But after walking a while longer, even these last traces were gone.

At some point, a solitary star of light appeared ahead, guiding the column forward. Only when they drew near did they realize this arcane starlight sat atop a night elf wooden gate shaped like a torii, cradled by a crescent-shaped wooden ornament.

Behind this wooden gate was an old pier, where a small purple wooden sailboat was moored. Several guards stood quietly, gazing this way without moving a muscle.

This star of light was the entire dock's only illumination. Aside from this small dock, there was nothing else on the tranquil beach that had anything to do with civilization.

Staring at this place, a powerful sense of loneliness seized Jace by the chest. He truly couldn't imagine what it would be like to live in an environment like this for several thousand years.

Time and space both became meaningless on this vast sea. The thick fog swallowed everything here.

After boarding the ship, even Musa had gone quiet, let alone Grede. Wenresa kept sizing up those night elves and the impossibly simple single-masted sailboat, her face full of astonishment.

In Silvermoon City, even in Dalaran, everything related to the high elves went to extravagant lengths. To many high elves, the living conditions of their distant relatives here probably looked no different from a bunch of savages...

But even Jace could sense an ancient, heavy aura from the night elves' clothing, their weapons, and this boat. As their kin, Wenresa must have felt it far more strongly than Jace did.

They had been sailing the seas for decidedly less than a day. Even with the sea fog blocking his vision, he could roughly judge the time by the light and dark of the sky. But this one day felt possibly harder to endure than sailing from Stormwind to Menethil Harbor.

When the boat docked, Jace still felt somewhat dazed. After stepping off the ship, looking at the forest ahead — made impossibly dim by the fog blocking out the sunlight, or the moonlight — and the towering stone towers and wooden houses among the trees, he really had a hard time connecting the word "stronghold" with these scattered buildings.

But this was indeed Feathermoon Stronghold, exactly as it had been in the game before Deathwing brought about the Cataclysm.

Jace rarely saw places where reality and the game scenery were almost indistinguishable. Whether it was the royal city of Lordaeron or Stormwind City, both were orders of magnitude larger and more complex than in the game. But Feathermoon Stronghold... it was almost exactly like the game.

He could almost point out directly where the sentinel general Shandris Feathermoon lived — right below the most prominent two-story wooden house in the center in the distance. In the game, Shandris Feathermoon commanded the sentinel forces defending the entire forest from inside.

Sure enough, not long after this company arrived, the sentinel captain hurried into that wooden house.

Jace and the others waited here for a while. The whole of Feathermoon Stronghold was uncomfortably quiet. It wasn't as if it were empty — Kaldorei occasionally passed by, mostly sentinels, of course, but also residents of the stronghold or workers with other duties. But the vast majority of these people remained silent the whole time. Even when they spoke, they kept their voices low, making the already deathly still forest village even more lifeless.

"Have you noticed, Jace?" Grede did his best to keep his voice low, trying not to break the strange quiet.

"It seems all the soldiers here. Look at those wearing armor, carrying those strange weapons, patrolling with bows on their backs — they're all female elves."

After saying that, he glanced at the Wenresa beside him and asked, "Or am I just mixing up men and women again? I couldn't tell your genders apart very well the first time I saw high elves either."

Wenresa took a deep breath and said, "The Kaldorei sentinels are composed entirely of women."

"What about the men?" Grede said. "I haven't seen a single male since I got here."

Wenresa didn't answer this topic anymore. She seemed a little tense, staring — just like Jace — at the largest, most prominent building in the center, the seat of General Shandris Feathermoon.

"If Sylvanas knew I'd been captured and brought here, meeting the leader of the Kaldorei looking like this, she'd curse me to death. Damn it," she muttered under her tongue.

"Then just don't tell her," Jace said. "Anyway, no one besides us knows it happened. And the elves here aren't going to leave Kalimdor specifically to report this meeting to your sister."

"Then you two keep your mouths shut too," Wenresa said, looking at him and the dwarf.

"Of course we won't say anything!" Grede said.

"What about you, Jace? Hurry up and promise!"

"All right, all right, I promise, I promise..."

"Musa? Never mind..."

Just then, a female elf in a long gray-purple robe appeared in the doorway of the wooden house. Several of them immediately fell silent.

That female elf was quite tall. Her deep blue hair was tied high into a ponytail at the back of her head. The silver light in her eyes flickered as she looked this way.

A tall male night elf escorted her, his hair purple, his skin pitch-black as night. He was clad in sturdy, heavy dark green hard leather armor.

When Grede saw the male elf, his eyes widened and he muttered under his breath, "How is he that tall?"

That's right — night elf males were much taller and more powerfully built than high elves. Their stature might not even fall far short of Musa's.

He and the sentinel captain who had brought Jace and the others to Feathermoon Stronghold accompanied the deep-blue-haired female elf, slowly approaching Jace and the group.

The female elf sized up the people here. Her gaze lingered on Jace for a moment, and she frowned slightly, then looked at the Celebras's antler in her hand.

Jace felt as though she had detected the shadow power on him. He had never harbored any illusion that his dark magic could escape her eyes. Still, she didn't say anything more about it.

"I am Shandris Feathermoon, commander of the sentinels stationed here by order of High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind." The female elf raised her head and said in remarkably fluent Common. "This is indeed the antler of the guardian Celebras. I have known both him and his father for a very long time. I am very sorry we brought you here in this manner. Untie them now, Kalanvina."
