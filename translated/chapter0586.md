## Chapter 586: The Brink of Mana Depletion

Leaving the beach, the sentinels led them back into the jungle, until they had been taken beyond the night elves' patrol range.

Jace followed the Soul Link to sense Morophil's presence, advancing bit by bit along the feeling, hoping to find his two hidden demon servants sooner rather than later.

He could also feel through the Soul Link that the demons were constantly moving toward him as well.

When they'd left Maraudon, Jace had already thought about the summoning incantation the satyrs had written on those scrolls. Wasn't that a fixed method of summoning?

If it could be modified — for instance, stripping away the complicated phrases and extracting the sentences specifically used to calling a particular demon — could he swap out the name or some other details and use it to summon Morophil or Sazeno?

That way, he could have them hide away in some corner of the Twisting Nether in ordinary times, and summon them when needed — much more convenient. No need to always risk traveling with demons in tow, inviting all sorts of unnecessary trouble.

Night fell quickly. The group planned to leave the main road and find a relatively dry place to rest for the night.

Unfortunately, not long after they started looking, a light rain began to fall. They found an ancient stone house, half-collapsed in the middle of the forest, to use as shelter and cover.

This stone house looked like a guard post, or a lonely shrine in the forest. What exactly it was for, Jace couldn't say for sure.

Broken pillars were buried everywhere around it. The stone house itself was partly buried in earth. After stepping inside, the sloping floor was overgrown with all kinds of plants.

For all he knew, this might have been a high tower once, with only the uppermost portion still exposed above ground.

Though they'd been lucky to find a place to shelter from the rain so quickly, they'd still been caught in it for a short while. Everyone's cloaks were soaking wet, putting the whole group in a foul mood.

But even getting rained on couldn't interfere with Grede admiring his new set of armor. He stroked the two huge chitinous breastplates on his chest and asked, "Tell me — this bug shell doesn't have any weird fishy smell at all. Is it real or fake..."

"You've asked that a thousand times, Grede," Jace said. "I should have refused that general's offer right then and there. Wouldn't have to put up with you agonizing over it."

"Too late now!" Grede said with a laugh.

"Jace, you must have chatted with her a bit when you handed over that demon's head, right?" Wenresa asked.

"I asked a question," Jace said. "About traces of the silithids that had come out of Silithus. Back in Stormwind, my alchemist friend wanted me to help look for silithids on Kalimdor, to see if they could be of any use for alchemy potions."

"Are there any in this forest?" Grede asked.

Jace said, "Yes, roughly to the south. If we're going to Eldre'Thalas, we should need to keep heading east, following this highway to the deepest part of the forest, so it's not really on the way. We don't need to go out of our way to look for them. Let's go to Eldre'Thalas first. After all, I need to get some materials off the bugs to bring back. Carrying that much extra stuff into Eldre'Thalas would only slow down our investigation. And the silithid hive isn't going anywhere — it'll still be there waiting."

"What about your demon servants?" Wenresa asked curiously. "They won't run into other sentinels, will they?"

Jace frowned and felt the strength of the Soul Link. "No, she seems to already be..."

Before he could finish, Morophil suddenly threw herself in through the door and threw her arms tightly around his neck. Her already ice-cold skin, soaked by the rain, stuck to Jace's neck and felt even colder.

She looked at Wenresa with a hint of provocation. "You want me to be killed by the sentinels, Quel'dorei?"

"Of course not," Wenresa said.

The succubus narrowed her eyes and stared at the ranger for a while. "You're not actually lying."

"Of course she doesn't want you to die, Succubus!" the imp jumped from the succubus's neck onto Jace's neck. "You saved her life! Ah, but you're a demon, and mortals hate demons! She should want you to die. What a contradiction!"

"Why would I want you to die?" Wenresa said, pushing her golden short blade into the earth and lacing up her boots. "Never mind that you saved me once — even if we didn't know each other, you've never hurt me, and you've never threatened me."

"I won't just not threaten you — I'll protect your safety. If you're always thinking about wanting me to die, that would break my heart." The succubus said softly, wearing a half-smile.

Wenresa said nothing and awkwardly lowered her head.

Morophil glanced at her lightly, said nothing more, and turned her head. "Master, ogres are hiding in the ancient ruins not far from here. We need to be careful of their ambushes..."

Grede muttered, "I'm not surprised. If they can occupy Eldre'Thalas, they can occupy other ruins too."

"Have you found the location of Eldre'Thalas these past two days?" Jace asked.

"Two days isn't enough for this lazybones to walk there, Master!" Sazeno said with a grin. "I did seriously urge her to hurry up and scout the path for Master, but it didn't do much good. Though I'm not making excuses for her — Eldre'Thalas is still at least four or five days away, and that's going by the highway. Even from here I can feel the mana surges that have been there for God knows how long. Truly impressive. Those highborne really have something extraordinary about them!"

"I can feel it too," Wenresa said, closing her eyes. "I can even feel... a heartbeat..."

"That's right, that's right," the imp said. "The heartbeat of the powerful demon that's trapped there, powering the whole city!"

"I'm already close to being unable to feel anything at all," Morophil said mournfully.

"That just means the elves' demon seal is quite effective," Sazeno said with a laugh.

"No," the succubus said softly from behind. "My mana is already exhausted, Master. Back in that cave, you promised you'd replenish my mana once we got out. Remember?"

"It's not a good time right now," Jace said.

Morophil suddenly flashed around to stand in front of Jace, looking into his eyes. "Master's mana has already fully recovered. I can feel it. Why still no?"

She glanced at the people beside them and asked softly, "Is it because they're here? We can find a corner..."

The rain grew heavier and heavier, the sound of the rain nearly drowning out the two of them talking. The others sat in the corners of this ruined, crumbling temple, silently eating their food, or staring blankly into the forest — either they hadn't heard, or they were pretending not to have heard.

Jace narrowed his eyes. He felt as though Morophil was being unusually impatient about this.
