## Chapter 587: A Demon's Torment

He'd held off on letting her drain him before, true, but he'd never seen her this unable to hold back — especially not to the point of turning on her own companions.

"Careful, Morophil. I think you're about to lose control," Jace warned her.

"I only want your mana, master. You'll recover quickly, but I won't." The succubus's tone grew more frantic by the second. "Without mana I'll only get weaker. There are threats to you everywhere around here, and I won't stand for being too weak to help when you're in danger..."

"There's no threat right now," Jace said.

"Because of her?" The succubus pointed at the elf beside them.

"Enough." Jace took her arm and pulled her aside. "Don't start trouble the moment we meet someone, all right?"

"You think I'm the one picking a fight, master?"

Morophil completely ignored Jace's words. Her tone carried more hostility than before, and as she spoke she yanked free from Jace's grip and moved toward Vereesa.

What the hell was she doing?

"Molesphilissalitor!"

Jace's lips moved silently, speaking the name in demonic. Though the sound was barely a whisper, the impact of that dark incantation — a true name — was anything but small.

His eyes even darkened, stained by shadow. Mosa, behind him, bristled as if every hair on her mane was standing on end. Grede looked up, eyes wide, staring at the scene, the food in his mouth frozen mid-chew.

The succubus, absolutely crushed by the power of her true name, seemed to deflate all at once. She collapsed to the ground, sobbing uncontrollably.

Vereesa stared blankly at Jace, then at the succubus, her expression complicated — clearly a little surprised that Jace had blown up like that.

"I'm sorry, master. I can't disobey your command." The succubus was as terrified as a kitten that had just been thrashed by its owner. "I lost my head. You being held by those sentinels put me on edge. Draining my mana dry made it impossible to stay calm. I... it made me no different from those mindless, lesser demons."

Under the absolute weight of her true name, she cowered in fear, apologizing in a frantic, stumbling rush. Jace felt a pang of guilt.

He thought back to earlier, when Morophil had thrown herself at Rexxar like a madwoman to block an attack for him — not even flinching when the soul link dealt her a heavy blow.

"Don't do this again, Morophil. You know I don't like things spiraling out of control," he said, keeping his voice as gentle as he could. "And you know I don't want to do this."

The succubus coughed violently in the rainwater leaking into the ruined building. She nodded hard. Jace pulled her over to a tree root unearthed from a cracked stone wall. She crawled into the deep hollow beneath it, trembling.

It wasn't from the cold. It was from extreme weakness. A demon whose true name had been spoken fell into unimaginable vulnerability — a mortal who couldn't have budged their power under normal circumstances could kill them easily, in this state, once the name was spoken.

Even Sazeno fell silent at the sight. Not a trace of his usual schadenfreude on his face.

When Jace's gaze drifted toward him, the imp immediately ducked back into the warlock's bag and hid.

"Sorry, Vereesa," Jace said. "When you rest, I'll stay awake and keep her away from you. Just let me know when you're turning in."

"It's fine, Jace," Vereesa said softly. "I'm not so helpless that I wouldn't notice a threat closing in. You're underestimating me."

Jace nodded. After a while, once Morophil had calmed down, he went over to her. "I don't believe you have no other way to draw mana."

The succubus was quiet for a moment, then looked up. "You won't like it."

"Try me."

At that, Vereesa couldn't help but glance over. Grede slid his eyes sideways too.

The succubus pulled herself up and ran her tongue over one of her sharp demon fangs. "I don't want to do this. So is it an order, master?"

"Yes," Jace said.

Morophil nodded and began chanting a demonic incantation. The whole dim room flickered with an eerie violet glow of shadow magic.

Mosa couldn't stand it — she walked out of the building and found a collapsed pillar outside to shelter under the rain. Clearly she had no intention of standing around while this terrifying shadow magic was being cast right in front of her. Vereesa frowned too; she had no idea what this succubus was about to do.

A shifting blue-purple light flickered in Morophil's hands, and Jace felt a searing pain in his chest.

His legs buckled and he nearly hit the floor. He caught himself against the damp, slippery stone wall, coughing hard.

Pain — endless, searing pain and stabbing agony spread through his chest, from his throat to his skull. He felt himself growing weaker and weaker. Slowly, Morophil's familiar form shifted in his eyes, reverting to that hideous, terrifying she-demon. She even seemed to grow a little larger, her horns casting longer shadows under the glow of the shadow magic.

"Jace." Vereesa couldn't hold back. "She's going to kill you!"

Grede marched over with his axe, but Jace held up a hand to stop him. "Don't. It's fine. I know what I'm doing."

The dwarf frowned and halted. He twirled his axe, swung it once through the air, then glanced at Vereesa. Vereesa had her hand on the longblade at her waist. But in the end the two of them just shot anxious glances at each other and said nothing more.

Even though he'd said he knew what he was doing, Jace had no idea when this would end.

The pain spread without limit. His insides were churning like crazy, but at least his mind stayed clear and his perception of the pain remained sharp and distinct — which was proof the shadow magic wasn't enough to kill him.

The last time he'd taken a lethal blow of shadow damage, he'd lost most of his bodily sensations all at once.

He didn't know how long it went on... Jace was certain it hadn't been long, because every second dragged like an eternity of agony. Finally he couldn't hold it back anymore and vomited a mass of black blood. The shadow magic that had been eating away at him stopped dead.

Morophil swayed where she stood, as if she'd snapped her own spellcasting short. Her legs wouldn't stop shaking. Her arms, her fingers, even her lips trembled — but the fel fire in her eyes was burning fiercely again.

But when her eyes met Jace's, that fire dimmed a little.

"You all right, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

Jace wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth with his arm. "I'm fine. She won't kill me."

"You look half dead to me," Grede said.

"Well, the other half's still kicking." Jace looked at the demon. "Enough?"

Even as he asked, he dipped a finger in the blood he'd coughed up. It was pure black-purple.

"Enough, master."

Morophil's voice was much calmer now, back to the familiar tone he knew. When she heard Jace's voice, her body stopped trembling.

She clenched her fist, feeling her power. When she tightened it, Jace even sensed the demonic energy coursing through her was noticeably stronger than before.

Had he just not been paying attention to her strength for a while? At some point, her horns had even grown a faint fork.

- **Rexxar**: Canonical half-orc hero (雷克萨) — the earlier fight where Morophil shielded Jace.
