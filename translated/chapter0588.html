Chapter 588: The Ancient Wall

Morophil was clearly much stronger now — her skin smoother, her frame more powerful — but Jace leaned against the wall for a good long while, still feeling weak all over, as if he'd been hit by some strange fel poison. His vision was blurred, his legs barely strong enough to keep him upright against the wall.

It felt something like mana burnout, but not exactly.

After he'd recovered a bit, he asked, "What kind of spell was that, a mana drain? Can I learn the incantation?"

"It's Sayaad magic. Nearly impossible for you to learn, master," Morophil said. "Of course, I know the Eredar created similar dark sorcery, but I don't remember the words. Unfortunately I'm not as knowledged as that little wretch."

"Hard to admit that, you lazy bastard!" Sazeno piped up, finally daring to stick his head out.

"Tell me the incantation. I'll give it a shot," Jace said.

"Since my master insists..." Morophil repeated the incantation under her breath, looking none too pleased.

It really was the same incantation from before, but when she spoke it, Jace could barely make out which organs were producing the sound — for all he knew some of it was magic rather than voice, and it didn't quite match her lip movements either.

"That's not demonic," Jace said.

"It's the Sayaad tongue, master. It's very hard for a mortal body to speak," Morophil said. "That's precisely why I said you probably can't learn it."

"Ta-ki..." Jace tried to mimic it, but the moment he opened his mouth he knew it was completely wrong.

"Forget it. I'll stick to getting the hang of satyr incantations," Jace said, covering his mouth to cough. He glanced at his palm and let out a breath. "Since you're back, and you've got your strength again, Morophil, let's get back to organizing those satyr scrolls."

"Work... work..." the mutter. "It's the same everywhere — Stormwind, Kalimdor, work that never ends, ugh!"

He looked at Morophil and asked, "I'm curious — a spell this devastating, and you never use it against other enemies?"

"It's a mana-drain spell," Morophil explained. "Naturally it's more effective against mana-rich enemies. And most of the time I don't have the space or the time to chant it, master. Honestly, it's a spell I used long ago to torture helpless opponents who couldn't fight back..."

She leaned close to Jace's ear and lowered her voice. "And after bonding with you, everyone else's mana simply doesn't satisfy me anymore. Especially satyrs — or the energy of other demons. It's revolting, frankly... Let me give you an example. You want my fel heart surging with a pit lord's fel energy? Make me as berserk, brainless, and short-tempered as them — a bomb waiting to go off at any moment? I hate to admit it, but that's exactly what I was like when I served Overlord Brutallus. Nothing like I am now. Or say, Nathrezim dark mana..."

She spoke with a watchful little smile, as if waiting to see whether Jace would get angry hearing all this.

But Jace only asked, "So right now your body is filled entirely with my mana, Morophil. Does that mean you're becoming more like a mortal?"

Morophil raised an eyebrow, thinking it over. "Mortal? I don't know. But I'm certainly more like you now."

"After all, I'm yours, master."

To a dwarven explorer pushing deep into the Feralas jungle, or any ordinary traveler, Eldre'Thalas was the kind of place you could wander around for weeks without finding. Even following the ancient roads left behind by the Highborne, you'd inevitably hit a dead end, or find yourself led straight into an ogre-occupied ruined town — and a one-way ticket to your grave.

But for Jace's little group, the deeper they pushed into the forest, the more strongly they could feel the aura of ancient magic hanging over Eldre'Thalas and its surroundings — especially now that the city's magical seal had been shattered by the ogres. There was no hiding it anymore.

After walking toward Eldre'Thalas for four or five days, restored by the Flowing Ice Crystal's healing, Jace felt more or less back to normal. Though Mosa's glances at him had grown a good deal colder, as if Jace had really gone and done the deed with that demon right there in front of a dwarf and an elf.

Then again, for a tauren, trading mana through dark magic was probably not much different from the real thing anyway.

Early that morning, not long after breaking camp, they caught sight of a massive silver-gray wall rising between the distant colossal tree canopies.

The wall was on a scale unlike any ruin they'd seen before. Everyone stared at it in silence. Nobody said a word, but every one of them knew exactly what this was: the wall of Eldre'Thalas.

Jace tucked the Flowing Ice Crystal back into its box. There was no telling how many ogre mages and warlocks were in there, or where demons might be lurking, or escaped Kaldorei. A powerful arcane artifact like that would draw exactly the kind of attention they didn't need.

He had no desire to get blindsided by some random mage the way he had in Deadwind Pass — and the threat of a long-dead Dalaran frost mage brought back from the grave was nothing compared to a roaming Highborne mage hunting for prey.

"Which city do you think is bigger — this one or Stormwind, Jace?" Grede asked, curious.

"Hard to say," Jace said. "From here we can only see one wall..."

And that single wall already stretched beyond sight in both directions.

Before long, they heard several ogre shouts echoing from beyond the wall. Everyone tensed at once.

"We're gladiators, remember? Don't forget that," Grede said, looking over at the tauren, who was nervously scanning the top of the wall back and forth. "Gladiators. You know what that means, Mosa?"

"Money. Slaves. Killing," Mosa said.

"Thorough enough understanding," Grede said, stretching. "Look at our lineup — a tauren, a dwarf, an elf, a human, and two demons. A ragtag bunch like us is the perfect arena team."

"I'd like to point out that nobody here actually wants to go into that arena and fight for their lives except you, Grede," Vereesa said. "If it itches that bad, you can enter the solo matches... get thrown in with real beasts and torn apart like one."

"I'm not saying I want to go! It's just roleplaying!" Grede looked at Jace. "I think it's time we let Her Ladyship Morophil stop hiding. Walking around with a demon at our side is way more convincing — those ogres will buy it much easier."

Grede had a point. Plenty of ogre warlocks here summoned demons. Jace nodded and gave Morophil the signal that she didn't have to stay hidden anymore.

The succubus stepped out of the shadows and revealed herself. Grede gave her an extra glance. "I feel like she's a little taller than when we set out. Am I imagining that?"
