Chapter 589: Eldre'Thalas

Sections of Eldre'Thalas's wall were battered and crumbling; other sections still stood intact. The ruined parts had long since been overgrown with green, with saplings and low shrubs sprouting from the cracks. The intact areas weren't exactly pristine either — Jace could feel faint traces of arcane magic lingering on them. In other words, the wall's defensive energy hadn't failed all at once; it had decayed piece by piece, section by section.

Exquisite vine motifs were carved into the wall, and occasionally one could make out elven reliefs. One depicted a female elf with both legs broken off, gazing upward. Her figure was slender and long-limbed, dressed in what appeared to be a silken robe — a style completely at odds with the sentinels at Feathermoon Stronghold. This was likely a portrait of a Kaldorei empire noble: a Highborne.

In those days, sentinels and guards who wielded blades and bows were held in little regard, and nature magic was some rustic faith that had crawled out of who-knows-where.

There were no sentinels on the wall — no ogres, no elves. A black creature, hard to tell whether it was a cat or a weasel, scurried swiftly up the greenery clinging to the wall. No sign of caution or wariness — it reached the top and vanished. Clearly it knew the route by heart. The animals had long since made their homes in these places.

Following the wall east, after about twenty minutes they reached a corner — but here the wall turned north.

Grede was walking ahead, about to head north looking for a gate, when Jace said, "We shouldn't go north."

"Why not?" Vereesa turned back to ask.

"The gates of Eldre'Thalas face south," Jace said. "If we keep heading north we'll only get further and further away."

"Why didn't you say so earlier?" Grede turned back.

"I thought we'd run into the gates if we followed the wall east the whole way. We went the wrong way. This city is a lot bigger than I imagined," Jace explained.

Vereesa walked back. "Where did you learn that?"

"From those books about Eldre'Thalas we found among the satyrs," Jace said.

"Oh, right, there were some of those," the ranger nodded.

Jace had absolutely no way of connecting the complex arcane-defense diagrams in those books to the actual terrain here, and besides, those massive tomes of ancient knowledge hadn't been carried out when they fled. Jace was relying entirely on his knowledge of the game.

Morophil was watching him from the side. He knew the succubus could tell he was lying a hundred percent — but she would never expose her master in front of the others.

After walking back for a while, by the time the sun was more or less overhead, Jace saw a cluster of leaning, towering ruined spires ahead. The upper portions of these towers were similar in style to the ruined building where they'd rested a few days ago.

"By the Mountain King." Grede ran a little ways forward, craning his neck to look off to the right. The others looked too. Jace couldn't help but draw a sharp breath.

The main gate of Eldre'Thalas.

Even gnawed away by greenery until it was a picture of desolation, even covered in cracks and scars, the broad, straight ramp leading up ahead, the engraved grand pillars and steles flanking it covered in runes, the towering archway dim and deep, the endless corridor within, and the elegant stone statues one after another...

"I thought after seeing Ironforge, no second city would ever make me gasp like this again," Grede swallowed. "Still... Ironforge beats it. Just barely."

He pinched his thumb and forefinger together to show the difference. Vereesa said, "That's only because you've never been to Silvermoon, dwarf."

"Then let me into Quel'Thalas!" Grede said. "Keeping your treasures under lock and key where no one can see them is the same as not having them at all."

Jace frowned. Sure enough, as expected, Grede clapped a hand to his breastplate and said, "Just like my new treasure here — fits well, looks handsome. A bit heavy, sure, but it's exactly the kind of thing a fine warrior like me should wear..."

Vereesa and Jace both let out a helpless sigh at the same time.

"Fits well. Looks handsome," Mosa repeated the key words from the dwarf's speech.

Grede chuckled in satisfaction. "The closer a race walks to nature, the better it understands natural beauty. This is precisely why the Wildhammer clan and the tauren were destined to become allies..."

"Ugly," Morophil said.

Vereesa clearly almost burst out laughing. Grede said at once, "I have nothing to say to a demon..."

"Let's go, let's go..." Jace pointed at the ramp ahead.

"Are we just walking straight in through the main gate?" Vereesa glanced around. "Did you notice there don't seem to be many people here?"

Vereesa's remark reminded Jace. In the game, when he entered Dire Maul he'd always charged straight through the main gate, ignoring every ogre and hyena he pulled along the way.

But the ogre guards who were supposed to be patrolling this ramp and the open ground in the game were nowhere to be seen.

Grede walked a little ways forward and looked up the ramp. "Didn't that half-orc say earlier that the ogres were holding an arena here with lots of people coming to compete? Why is it so deserted?"

"Grede, get over here." Vereesa beckoned urgently. She pointed behind a terrace-like stone ledge, and everyone ducked behind it.

A moment later, a series of heavy footsteps and creaking, groaning wood came from the great arched gate at the top of the ramp.

The sounds grew heavier, the vibrations clearer. Jace saw the rainwater pooled on the stone ledge rippling with each footstep.

The footsteps stopped abruptly nearby, followed by a crackling, snapping sound like trees rubbing and breaking. Vereesa pressed against the wall and stole a glance outside, then immediately pulled her head back. Jace looked up and saw an old man's wooden face staring into the distance from high above, great wooden fangs extending right over their heads.

A war ancient!

These colossal walking trees were ancient allies of the Kaldorei. The Kaldorei guarded the forests of Kalimdor, and in turn these ancients obeyed the Kaldorei's command, garrisoning elven towns and key strongholds.

There was a boss in Dire Maul called the Warheart — a giant war ancient just like this — but that one guarded a magical gate deep inside Dire Maul's seal. This ancient walking around outside Dire Maul didn't seem to exist in the game.

In other words, the ogres might not have fully occupied the gates of Eldre'Thalas yet. This nearly unkillable ancient had simply been left here, unchecked.

Grede glanced at Mosa, probably wondering if she could communicate with the ancient. The tauren shook her head — clearly she had no way of talking to it either.

The ancient probably hadn't even realized the city it was guarding had long since been breached from elsewhere. Jace could feel that it had been badly corrupted by shadow energy. It had likely already lost its mind, patrolling back and out of nothing but ten-thousand-year-old habit.

After it turned and walked back through the archway, Vereesa pointed east. "I'm hearing something over there. Let's go that way."

Translator Notes

Flags

- Eldre'Thalas: Canonical name for 埃雷萨拉斯 — the ancient Highborne city (later known as Dire Maul / 厄运之槌). - Highborne: 上层精灵 — the Kaldorei nobles who ruled the empire; the wall relief depicts one. - War ancient / Warheart: 战争古树 / 扭曲古木 — the colossal walking tree creature. The game boss "Warheart" (扭曲古木) guards a sealed gate deep in Dire Maul; this one patrolling outside has no game equivalent, suggesting the ogres haven't fully taken the gates. - Ironforge / Silvermoon / Quel'Thalas: Canonical locations (铁炉堡 / 银月城 / 奎尔萨拉斯) — Grede's boastful comparison. - Wildhammer clan: Canonical dwarven clan (蛮锤氏族) — Grede's people, allied with the tauren. - "By the Mountain King": Grede's dwarven oath — kept as a bluff exclamation. - POV: Third person throughout.
