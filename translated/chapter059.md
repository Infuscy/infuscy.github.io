## Chapter 59: The Legend of Redridge

That afternoon Jace went fishing with Grede and a few local middle-aged men. They didn't catch much, but at least they got to properly enjoy the beauty of Lake Everstill and heard plenty of interesting myths and legends.

Like the Lake Everstill sea monster, the mermaids of the lakeshore, and so on.

The locals told these as legends, but Jace knew these things were probably real.

The so-called Lake Everstill sea monster — the locals described it as a gigantic strange fish. Every time it appeared, rain would pour down and storms would sweep across the lake. Even the orcs were terrified of it.

Jace knew it was actually a plesiosaur living at the bottom of the lake — at least in the game. He gazed out at the lake stretching almost past the point of sight, that deep, unknowable mystery making it hard to imagine what the lake monster really looked like.

As for the mermaids, they were very likely naga lurking in the waters here.

The people of Lakeshire also knew not to venture too far into the waters alone. Not just because these legends sent a chill down your spine — more importantly, Lake Everstill was also the largest murloc habitat in the whole Kingdom of Stormwind. The murlocs living here could build another kingdom.

Murlocs — those little monsters with frog-like legs and arms but carrying a huge, finned fish head — were a specialty of Azeroth's waters.

Some said they were the slaves of the naga. Some said they were twisted worshippers of evil gods. Some said they were the most primitive and earliest inhabitants of Azeroth, transitional intelligent creatures who'd first come from the sea onto land.

In any case, murlocs didn't communicate with any other race, nor did they live alongside outsiders. But they had their own social logic and could even wield magic. The best other races could do was stay as far away from them as possible.

In the real world of Azeroth, the Redridge Mountains weren't just this little patch of hills around Lakeshire. They were a vast mountain range stretching from the Stormwind coast in the north all the way down to Karazhan in the south — all of it part of the Redridge.

The people of Stormwind liked to call this place the Redridge Mountains because they lived on the Elwynn plain and had to pass through a stretch of the Redridge range to get here. The people on this side preferred to call it the Lake Everstill valley.

Listening to all the various rumors and little stories about the Redridge, then looking out at the rolling Redridge range in the distance, Jace more and more felt that finding a hiding orc in mountains like these — wasn't that just asking for trouble?

He watched the float on his fishing line and asked Grede, who was beside him, "You said this morning you had leads on the orc. What leads? Why not talk about them now?"

"That orc's a freshwater seafood enthusiast," Grede said. "The Lakeshire militia spotted him a long time ago. They say he's incredible at catching fish — in an hour or two…"

He lowered his voice, avoiding the nearby fishing companions. "…he catches more than all the amateur anglers here put together in an afternoon."

Grede said with a confident face, "He likes catching fish when it's overcast, especially when it's raining… The weather here in Redridge has been overcast for the past couple of days. The town scholars say it'll probably rain tomorrow. When it does, we'll head to that stretch of lakeshore and wait for him."

"The militia tracked him and he didn't notice?" Jace said, skeptical. "Are orcs that incautious?"

"He's a spell-chanting type, not a hunter," Grede said. "If he were one of those orc scouts, there's no way the local militia would've spotted him. That mage probably thinks nobody's found him and plans to settle down here."

"If he's not a hunter, how's he so good at catching fish?" Jace asked.

"With magic, obviously," Grede said. "Didn't I tell you these people think he's a mage? It's because they saw him catch fish with magic! He stands by the river chanting, and the fish in the lake get killed by the magic. All he has to do is wade in and pick them up — like gathering fruit planted in the ground."

Jace frowned. He had a vague, indescribable sense of familiarity about this description. What kind of magic could kill fish in the water? Frost magic dropping the temperature? Corruption spreading into the water?

Grede cut into his thoughts. "According to the militia, some of the fish don't even die — they're still flopping around when he pulls them out. Tell me, what's a mage who can't even kill a fish supposed to be afraid of?"

"I don't know about that," Jace said thoughtfully.

"I've fought mages before, Jace," Grede said. "As long as you're fast and close the distance before they've figured out what spell to chant, they're as dumb as a fish slapped onto the shore. Even if you run into a powerful mage — an elf, say — the spells that can hurt you quickly still need to be incanted. Just put an arrow through their mouth. You know why there are so many mages on the battlefield but very few who travel alone? That's why. Let a mage keep their distance and give them room, and they can unleash terrifying power. But once you get in close, they're fragile."

The dwarf tilted his head. "There are exceptions, of course. Like the Prince of Quel'Thalas — that old undying bastard. I'm sure he's been alive for at least eight hundred years. I'm confident I couldn't beat him with a sword either. Of course, we're not going to run into an orc Warchief or his son out here in the wild, right? Someone like Gul'dan, say. If that orc's magic was really that powerful, why doesn't he just attack Stonewatch himself? He's been stuck there all this time just catching fish?"

"I still think you and the militia are being too subjective," Jace said.

"Neither me nor the local militia are people who haven't actually fought orcs. But there's another possibility," Grede added. "Like you said before — these people are treating an orc warlock as a mage. That might make things a bit more complicated. From what I know, warlocks' self-preservation methods might be even worse than a mage's. But then again, I haven't had many chances to go one-on-one with a warlock, so who can say for sure?"

He stared at Jace and emphasized, "I mean a real warlock."

Jace gave him a flat look. "I brought three Shadow Protection Potions. The effect lasts an hour. If we actually run into that orc, we can each drink one."

"You actually thought we might have to fight an orc warlock and prepared in advance?" Grede said, surprised.

"Originally it was for the gnolls."

Jace remembered there were quite a number of gnoll spellcasters in Redridge — a gnoll tribe called Darkhide that had a whole pack of warlocks. He just hadn't expected to use the potions on an orc before even running into them.

"Same difference," Grede said. "Excellent. That puts my mind at ease. Mages are easy to deal with, warlocks we've got a counter for — tomorrow'll be a walk in the park. Let these Lakeshire greenhorns see what we're made of!"

Jace, for his part, had no idea what kind of "ability" their duo had that was worth showing off to an orc.
