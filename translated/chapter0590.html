Chapter 590: Into the Ancient City

The ancient's strides were enormous, and it left quickly. Everyone knew it was probably pacing back and forth on patrol, and could come back at any time, so the group hurried up the terrace, keeping to the side of the ramp.

After heading west a short way, Vereesa caught some kind of sound up ahead and signaled everyone to press against the wall.

Once they'd quieted down and listened carefully, it turned out to be snorting, huffing breaths.

Vereesa nocked an arrow and slowly rounded a stone pillar — then suddenly stepped out, drawing her bow to full tautness and aiming behind the pillar. A yelp came from the other side, and a grumpy, heavy voice said, "Don't point that arrow at me!"

The speaker was using Common. A dark-gray-skinned ogre nearly three meters tall — a full head taller than Mosa — stepped out. "The big tree is over there. Don't you go making any trouble!"

The ogre looked dull-witted, even a little cross-eyed. He didn't seem particularly afraid of Jace's group. He kept glancing toward the main gate, and before long said again, "The big tree's coming back! Ouch!"

Vereesa drew her bow to full tautness and aimed between his eyes. "What are you doing hiding here?"

"Krokk not hiding. Krokk waiting for you!"

It was clear the ogre was trying his best to keep his voice down, but it still rumbled like thunder.

"Waiting for us?" Jace asked. "You knew we were coming?"

"You not gladiators?" the ogre asked.

Jace and Grede exchanged a glance. They'd been planning to put together a careful disguise, but before they could even say anything, the other side had already believed it on their own.

"Of course we are," Grede said at once. "How do we get in to compete?"

"Big tree walking back and forth. No competition!" the ogre said. "King tell Krokk wait for gladiators, show you way. Tree sleep in ten-and-some days. Tree sleep, competition start!"

"Why don't you just deal with that tree?" Grede asked.

"Deal?" the ogre laughed. "Deal with tree!"

"Kill it. Defeat it. Take it down," Grede added hastily — the ogre didn't seem to understand "deal with."

"Kill tree, we no do. But bounty! Gold treasure, King Gordok!" the ogre said, getting a little excited and starting to spit out words in a string.

Grede looked like he had an idea and was about to speak, but Jace stopped him. "No need, Grede. You want to help these ogres take this city faster? Don't forget whose side they were on to begin with."

That reminded Grede. "The Horde."

"We're here to compete, not to sign on as mercenaries for a bounty," Jace said to the ogre. "Take us gladiators inside, Krokk."

"Krokk happy serve, Krokk happy help! Long live Gorduni! Long live King Gordok!" the ogre let out a silly laugh and turned to squeeze into a crack in the stone wall smashed open by who-knows-what.

Vereesa watched the ogre effortlessly wedge himself into the crack, his filthy, fat bulk scraping against the wall and sending down a shower of dust and debris. All she could do was frown and follow him in.

The ogre squeezed his massive body through the path ahead, leading the way. Jace's group followed behind. After passing through the outer wall, they entered a vast walkway sandwiched between two walls. Looking up, beneath the enormous canopy, the silver walls and the ancient stone beams linking them seemed to trap everything inside in a cage of emerald green.

Tattered purple banners — if you could even call them banners; they were really just strips of ragged cloth — hung from the stone beams, hanging still like corpses strung up upside down. The sight was enough to make your skin crawl.

The ogre knew the route like the back of his hand — every crack, every tunnel, no hesitation at any fork.

"What do you think the ogres are holding arena fights to earn money for?" Grede asked.

"Probably buying things from goblins," Jace tried. "Good food, good fun. Ogres like to enjoy themselves too. Think of Booty Bay."

"King Gordok like big house," the ogre said, turning his head. "Earn money buy big house!"

"You and this king would really get along, Jace," Vereesa said with a smile.

The ogre went on: "King Gordok bounty kill big tree! Kill big tree, take gate! King Gordok pay kill pointy-ears, kill pointy-ears!"

Vereesa's smile vanished in an instant. The ogre turned his head back. "Pointy-ears hide. No guts!"

"If this idiot wasn't leading us in right now..." the ranger muttered something in Thalassian. It definitely wasn't anything nice.

"Quilboar willing help King Gordok build big palace. Now only big tree left. Kill big tree!"

"What kind of palace could Quilboar build?" Grede said, half laughing, half exasperated.

Listening to this ogre chatter away, he seemed eager to spill every last detail of the arena — how most of the gladiators lived in the underground tunnels, bought food from goblins, or hunted animals on the city's outskirts to fill their bellies; how the ogres had already occupied the north but still couldn't get through some magically sealed gates... But he never once mentioned the word "satyr" or "demon."

Jace guessed that at this point in time, the satyrs might genuinely not have entered Dire Maul yet.

After squeezing through a tunnel so narrow the ogre had to crawl on his belly, they entered a broad plaza.

In the center of the plaza stood an oval-walled structure like the outer facade of an ancient Roman colosseum. The aged building had collapsed halfway, and enclosed within it was a giant pit — the arena itself. Here, Jace could already hear the voices of ogres talking behind the walls, up above.

They were speaking a strange kind of Orcish. Jace could catch fragments — or rather, he could follow the general topic of what they were saying, but not the specific content.

This plaza was very familiar to Jace. This was the spot outside the dungeon entrance in the game, where the three wings of Dire Maul branched off: West, East, and North.

In the game, Dire Maul West was home to many Highborne and elven spirits; Dire Maul East was occupied by satyr demons; and Dire Maul North was held by the ogres.

"Find place to stay, wait for competition start. No cause trouble, or King Gordok personally slap your face!" the ogre named Krokk finished his instructions and turned to leave.

Grede was amused by that line — until he turned and saw a towering ogre soldier passing by outside, and immediately stopped laughing.

This ogre was easily over three meters tall. Even next to Mosa he looked positively gaunt by comparison. He was draped in scraps of battered armor, some of them even bearing Orcish markings — clearly refitted from orc armor. The wooden-hafted giant blade he carried was longer than Jace's entire body.

Jace knew the Gorduni ogres were a clan that revered martial challenge. Their king was unquestionably the strongest fighter in the whole clan — at least considerably stronger than this guard.

Getting slapped by a man that size would be no joke.
