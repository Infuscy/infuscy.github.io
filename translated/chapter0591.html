Chapter 591: The Arena Square

The plaza was quite lively. It wasn't just ogres — there were plenty of other groups too. Quilboar, gnolls, goblins, humans from who-knows-where, dwarves who looked like they were from the Bronzebeard clan, and even a couple of tauren.

They clustered in twos and threes. Some stood around talking, some paced back and forth in the corridors, and some lurked in corners observing the whole plaza — just like Jace's group.

"Gnolls everywhere," Grede squinted. "Bronzebeards I can live with, but I honestly didn't expect to run into gnolls here of all places."

Mosa had no interest in the two tauren. She didn't want to go talk to them — didn't even want to look at them. Those two tauren were likewise acting as if the new arrivals on the plaza didn't exist.

Those two tauren had dark black manes and wore heavy fur armor, carrying massive axes and warblades. They looked nothing like the peaceable tauren of Sunspring Post.

Jace guessed they were probably Grimtotem tauren. Mosa's own Woolmane tribe had clashed with them before.

Then again, the Grimtotem tauren had tense relations with most other tauren tribes.

But even putting other tribes aside, it was perfectly normal for Mosa not to want to talk to them.

Jace noticed several humans over by the corridor wall also looking their way. They wore simple armor like bandits or thieves — the look of pirates or drifters who'd come here looking for a chance to make money. Just from their getup, Jace had zero interest in getting involved with that lot.

Not far off, an ogre warlock sat against the wall dozing. On the other side, an orc in dark red had a succubus sitting beside him.

The moment she spotted Jace's succubus, she stood up and stared hard in their direction. Morophil couldn't have cared less about her.

Jace swept the whole plaza with Sense Demons. Aside from that succubus, there was a small demon's shadow in the distance that he couldn't make out clearly. In short, this wasn't a place crawling with demons, but the air reeked of demonic presence.

Even he could smell it, and that was nothing compared to Morophil.

The group passed the colosseum-like outer wall and reached the edge of the oval pit in the middle of the plaza. The arena itself was built remarkably beautifully — the walls and the arched gateways for entering and exiting the grounds below were purely elven in style, with very little ogre remodeling.

Before the ogres arrived, this must have also been a place where the nobles of Eldre'Thalas came to amuse themselves.

The moment they got close to those stone walls, Jace caught an unpleasant stench from behind them. He reached the pit and looked down. Blackened blood and messy gouges covered the floor of the whole pit.

Two ogres were fighting inside, grappling and rolling around on the ground. Probably just a regular scuffle, not any kind of match. Aside from Jace's group, nobody in the whole plaza had the slightest interest in watching this clumsy brawl.

"This isn't quite the Eldre'Thalas I pictured," Vereesa said, her tone tinged with disappointment. "Before coming to Kalimdor, I'd heard Eldre'Thalas was one of the greatest cities of the ancient Kaldorei empire, standing with Suramar to the north and Zin-Azshari like the stars and moon in the night sky... I never expected the remnants of the Horde would have wrecked it into this — nothing but ragged, stinking leather tents and fences, a pack of savage bloodthirsty monsters treating it as their home."

"If it were just another completely collapsed ruin like the ones outside, that would be one thing." She raised her head and looked up at the giant walls and Kaldorei spires. "But... these are still standing just as they did ten thousand years ago. That contrast is heartbreaking..."

"My father would understand how you feel, elf," Grede said. "When he saw those black-hearted beasts wearing dwarven skin storm the great city of Grim Batol and deface that magnificent work of art with their filthy dark magic, he must have been just as grieved as you are."

"Don't forget what that guide ogre Krokk said," Jace reminded him. "There are still Highborne in here. They haven't been wiped out yet. We've been let in as gladiators — what we need to do now is figure out how to go deeper and find the prison holding the doomguard."

"You mean we should go ask an elf?" Grede asked.

Jace pointed at the tall towers behind the wall across the plaza. "A city this big, and you want us to search it corner by corner?"

"I'm not too keen on talking to another bunch of old-timers who've lived for thousands of years," Grede took a deep breath. "That sentinel general earlier was already stressful enough."

"Don't forget that general gave you that suit of armor, Grede," Jace said.

"That's true," Grede said. "But I still think we should ask the ogres first, see what they know. After all, they've been living here for years — they can't know absolutely nothing..."

Jace was watching Grede talk when he noticed a small ogre sneaking up behind them, creeping up behind the gesticulating dwarf.

The ogre looked at the human, elf, and tauren staring at him, put a finger to his lips in a "shush" gesture, then gently lifted the demon helmet off the top of Grede's head and placed it on his own.

It fit perfectly!

Grede turned back in shock. The ogre grinned and tried to run, but Grede grabbed his belt and yanked him back, then leaped up and threw a punch straight at the helmet.

With a loud clang, the ogre sat down hard on the ground, shaking his head dizzily. Grede snatched the helmet back and buckled it onto his own head again, then kicked the ogre square in the forehead, knocking him flat on his back and leaving him motionless.

"Is he dead?" Vereesa asked.

Grede walked over and took a look. The ogre sucked in a huge breath, puffed up his belly, then let it out, his lips flapping with a snoring sound — as if he'd fallen asleep.

"What the hell is going on?!" Grede turned back, glaring.

"The legendary ogre thief," Jace leaned against the railing, unable to hold back a laugh. "So — you still want to ask these ogres about the demon prison?"

Grede raised an eyebrow. "Then ask the ogres where the elves are hiding."

Vereesa said, "Did you forget? We're gladiators. A bunch of gladiators show up and instead of asking about the competition, they want to know about demons or where the other elves are hiding. Don't you think that'd raise suspicion, Grede?"

"What's wrong with being curious?" Grede said. "Especially you — an elf from another continent, curious about the homeland's situation. Isn't that perfectly normal?"

"This isn't my homeland..." Vereesa corrected.

Asking the ogres was a viable option. If the satyrs hadn't entered Eldre'Thalas yet, that meant the eastern district might still be under the control of the local elves. As for the state of the other districts, he couldn't judge entirely by the game's situation either. But Vereesa's concern was valid.

Jace looked at the ogres in the distance. Several guards stood to the east watching over the whole plaza. Farther off, on a high stairway, stood a two-headed ogre mage.

"Let's go ask those guards over there," Jace said. "Two heads are smarter — they might notice something, like Vereesa said."

Vereesa nodded, and Grede agreed too.

They rounded the arena and headed east. The ranger suddenly fixed her eyes on a huge wooden door to the east, tightly shut. "I can feel it... the magical trail we've been looking for is right behind this door, Jace."
