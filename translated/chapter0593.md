## Chapter 593: The Malicious Garden

That night, Jace's group approached the entrance to the East district side door the guards had mentioned. An ogre sat in the passageway, looking dead on his feet — but every time he started to doze off he jolted back awake, stubbornly refusing to fall asleep.

Clearly, the Gorduni ogres in Eldre'Thalas were stretched thin right now. If the elves inside didn't have troubles of their own worse than the ogres, leaving them no strength to counterattack, there's no way this handful of ogres could have seized such a huge swath of the city this fast, let alone hold an arena right under everyone's noses.

They waited there for a while. The ogre still wouldn't fall asleep. Mosa simply stood up and said, "I'll go."

Under the slightly baffled, slightly expectant gazes of the others, Mosa crept up to the ogre guard. The gesture was absurdly incongruous for a tauren, but relying on her hunter's experience she really didn't alert him.

She pulled a handful of some kind of powder from her pouch and tossed it in the ogre's face. The ogre had barely noticed the tauren looking his way before the powder hit him full in the face. He let out one sneeze and toppled over, sound asleep.

Mosa beckoned. The others immediately followed, darting into the passageway and hopping over the ogre's legs.

Through the long, dark corridor, they emerged onto a platform. Just past the edge ahead, dense tree canopies stretched up from below.

Jace still remembered this place. In the game there was an imp here — talk to it and it would immediately start running... Chase it down and kill it, and you'd get the Eldre'Thalas key.

Of course, there was no imp here. Just like Morophil had said, once they'd entered, the demonic presence was even thinner than it had been back in the plaza. Which meant the demonic energy Jace had sensed on the plaza earlier had been coming from the power source deep in the city: the voidhound Immol'thar.

Reaching the edge of the platform and looking down, the bottom was a deep, secluded garden. The canopies of towering trees of every variety reached all the way to the highest point. Between the bases of the great trees grew flowers of every species and plants that couldn't be identified at a glance.

The space was enormously wide — impossible to see the other side from here. Mist drifted between the tree trunks, obscuring the view. A finely built stone road led off into the distance, and arcane streetlamps still glowed, making it clear this was a cultivated garden rather than some primeval forest growing wild.

Grede asked, "Is this the plant life the guard said is protecting the elves?"

The dwarf had barely opened his mouth when the plants below rustled as if stirred by a breeze. Jace warily glanced over and held up a hand. At least here there was no sense of any air current at all — those plants were moving on their own.

Vereesa whispered, "The source of the magical trail is deep in the forest."

Grede followed the path from the high platform with his gaze, finally spotting something that looked like a way down on the far side.

"Can we get down over there?" he pointed and asked.

Jace surveyed the platform. He knew it wouldn't be that simple to get across from here. Soon he saw what he'd spotted from outside — a great tree lying fallen across the enormous wooden door beyond. Probably because of this tree, the door couldn't be opened from outside.

And that tree was alive. An old man's face on its trunk stared at the door's gap with pure hatred.

Farther off, in the direction Grede had pointed, there were two more of these wandering ancients. They just weren't moving at the moment — standing there, or leaning motionless against the wall.

"Look at those trees, Grede," Jace said. "We saw what they can do outside the gate. You sure you want to charge straight across and take that ramp down? If we startle one of them crossing over, the rest might all stand up and come after us. Think about why those ogres refuse to storm this place head-on."

"Then we go straight down?!" Grede said in surprise.

"Shh — keep it down, Grede," Vereesa warned.

Before the words were even out of her mouth, another rustle of leaves came from below, like the forest voicing its grievances over the peace being disturbed.

"Are there ancients like this below too?" the dwarf whispered.

"We'll need Mosa to help us find out," Jace said, looking at the trunk of a massive tree growing up right in front of them. "At least this one isn't a walker. We can use it to get down."

Vereesa explained to Mosa and pointed toward where she sensed the magical trail. Mosa peered into the depths of the forest and said, "Follow me."

The tauren shifted into the form of a great cat, leaped straight off the platform, and caught the trunk. She rumbled something at the others behind her, then nimbly began climbing down.

Even though the trunk was far too thick to wrap your arms around, Vereesa had no trouble finding a foothold on an uneven spot and grabbing a protruding knot with her hand. Relying on her agile frame, she quickly found handholds and soon kept pace with the druid.

"Damn it, I'm not ready yet..." Grede glanced down over the edge, backed up a few steps, took a running start, and hurled himself out, jamming the spike of his hammer into the trunk. He hung there swinging for a while, then barely managed to hug the tree and inch his way down.

Jace looked at where Vereesa had climbed past and tried to jump over and grab on the same way. His hands caught, but his boot didn't find purchase and he slid down a good ways. Just as he was about to plunge into the pitch-black depths below, something hooked him by the shoulder like a rope. He grabbed on tight.

He looked up — it was Morophil, who had snagged him at just the right moment with her tail.

"Careful, master."

Jace nodded, let out his breath, and stepped down onto a thick branch below. Vereesa was looking up this way, apparently already reaching out to grab him — when she saw Jace was out of danger she relaxed too.

"If I went back and told the Kirin Tor that Jace Sesso fell out of a tree and died, what do you think they'd say?" Grede called down from above.

"They'd probably have a glass of wine after dinner to celebrate," Jace said.

Grede laughed. Jace helped each other along with him, and by the time they reached the base of the tree together, Mosa had more or less scouted out the area around the great tree.

Looking north from here, a grim, towering wall could be seen, along with a huge sealed archway.

The group came within range of this gate. They could see the stone road they'd spotted earlier leading out from inside the gate and stretching deep into the forest.

"The magical trail isn't inside here," Vereesa said, pointing in the direction the road extended. "It's on the other side."

Mosa nodded, turned, and walked a few steps into the thicket, signaling the others to follow.

"We can't just follow the main road?" Grede asked, pointing at the arcane lamplight faintly visible under the canopy.

Mosa shook her head, tapped a claw on the road, then made a hissing intake of breath as a warning.

Grede understood immediately — it meant there was danger on the road too.

Even though Jace couldn't see far down the road, he knew exactly what Mosa meant by danger. Even in the game, this garden had treants charging back and forth that could lunge at you at any moment, and this was the real thing.

On the main road, if you found the pattern or got lucky you could make it through — but that was built on the experience of pulling aggro and dying over and over again when you first arrived. Here, they had no room for trial and error. Dead was dead.
