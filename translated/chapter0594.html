Chapter 594: The Last Warden

Mosa picked her way carefully through the trees, stopping now and then to observe the distance, sometimes pausing for several minutes or longer. Jace's group patiently followed the lioness. They all knew that in a garden like this, nobody was more reliable than a druid.

After wandering through the forest for who knows how long, Jace finally caught sight of the shadow of a colossal wall up ahead. Arcane lamplight burned before a tightly sealed stone gate, quite conspicuous among the tree shadows.

The group pushed through the bushes they'd been using as cover and arrived at the gate. Mosa shifted back into her tauren form. Vereesa kept her back to the others, bow raised, watching the forest in case any of the living plants had followed them.

Jace swept the forest and the area around the walls with Sense Demons, even checking high up on the great wall — because in the game several satyr assassins lurked here, and a moment's carelessness could get you ambushed. Even though the ogre had said there were no demons here, he couldn't let his guard down. After all, the ogres had never ventured deep in here either.

But Sense Demons didn't detect a single demon's shadow.

And in the game, this gate at the end of the garden was open. Here, though, it was tightly shut — and seemed to hold a trace of magical energy.

Was it sealed by magic?

"What do we do now?" Grede asked.

"Try knocking?" Jace suggested.

Vereesa glanced back, examining the gate. Grede walked straight up and called softly, "Anyone in here? We're not ogres!"

"The elves here probably don't speak Common," Jace said.

Vereesa looked at Jace. "You want me to go ask, then?"

The others all nodded.

A little reluctantly, Vereesa stepped forward and greeted the gate in ancient elvish, then said something else. But after several minutes the gate still hadn't budged.

Grede glanced at Jace and muttered, "Pity our mage is a half-trained amateur. Otherwise we could try dispelling the magic on this gate..."

Jace certainly had nothing to argue with there. Vereesa beside them said, "I can give it a try."

"You can manage?" the dwarf said in surprise.

"Of course." Vereesa slung her bow, raised her hands in a casting gesture. "Didn't Lady Valendris Swiftblade ever talk to you about Quel'dorei magic?"

"What-what-what..." Grede suddenly couldn't get his words out. "You... you... how did you know that?"

Vereesa said nothing, only smiled. Her eyes released a glow of arcane light, then slowly closed.

She didn't chant any incantation. The arcane energy in her palms flowed like water along her fingertips, and in an instant the seal on the gate vanished.

Grede nodded slowly in admiration. Jace was a little surprised too — but the most shocked was actually Vereesa herself. She opened her eyes and looked at the stone gate, which had automatically begun to slowly swing open. Her lips moved, and after a moment she said, "I genuinely didn't think I could actually dispel the seal on this ancient gate..."

"The gate is ancient, but the seal isn't necessarily," Sazeno poked his head out of the bag and whispered. "This is a spell that was cast not long ago, elf. Can you not feel it?"

"What difference does it make if I can't feel it? I'm not an archmage," Vereesa said.

Just as the group was about to go in, a shout suddenly came from inside.

"Tena! Ishnu! Dros'thelasri!"

It was clearly elvish — Jace could tell even without understanding a word.

The voice carried a trace of intense joy. After a flurry of footsteps, a figure appeared in the passageway behind the gate. But on seeing Jace's group outside, they suddenly came to a halt.

The newcomer was stunned for a moment. She was a Kaldorei woman, much thinner than the sentinels Jace had seen at Feathermoon Stronghold. Her skin was an unhealthy dark tone, and pale white hair fell to her waist. The arcane energy in her eyes was mixed with clearly distinguishable shadow power.

Her gaze jumped back and forth between the group, finally settling on Vereesa.

"Quel'dorei?"

Hearing that term, Vereesa's expression grew complicated. She knew it meant Highborne here, not a resident of Quel'Thalas.

She said something in elvish. The woman before her clearly couldn't quite believe it, and only replied after a long pause. The rest of them simply stood and watched the two elves talk.

The unfamiliar elf's expression grew more and more despondent. After the conversation wound down, Vereesa turned to Jace beside her and said, "The ogres' assault broke the magical defense array here. She and several other companions were defending this place, but once the defenses were broken, things inside spun out of control. Some of their companions went inside to repair the magical array and never made contact again. Later, demons tried to come out from within, so they had no choice but to magically seal the inner door and wait here for reinforcements. She must have mistaken us for the reinforcements who'd finally arrived."

"What demons?" Jace asked.

"The demons in the prison," Vereesa said. "The city's leader, Tortheldrin, wanted to use the power of those demons as a backup energy source, so they were sealed in the depths of this district. The ogres' assault threw off the balance of magical power... which also caused the energy in those prison seals to spiral out of control."

Jace said, "So the reason we couldn't sense any demonic presence is because the inner door was magically sealed."

"That should be right," Vereesa said.

Grede asked, "Are there other elves inside?"

Before Vereesa could answer, the elf urgently said something. Vereesa looked helpless and argued back a few sentences.

"What'd she say?" Grede asked again.

"She wants us to leave, because Eldre'Thalas doesn't allow outsiders to enter," Vereesa said.

Jace said, "Who's going to enforce that? If anyone still could, this elf wouldn't be trapped here. The ogres are already strolling through the streets of their city."

"That's what I said too," Vereesa said. "I told her that right now she had no one to count on except us."

"You planning to rescue her?" Grede asked.

"She might be able to help us find Dathmora," Jace said. "Did you not hear what she said? The demon prison is right inside there, Grede. What if the demon that warlock summoned is in there?"

"Fine," Grede said.

The Kaldorei woman glanced outside and said something, pointing inside the gate. Vereesa said, "She wants us to go in. The plants outside are dangerous."

"Aren't these plants their allies?" Jace asked.

"No," Vereesa said. "She says these plants are controlled by the Shen'dralar behind another gate. Even she would be killed if she recklessly went in and provoked the plants inside. But at least these plants are protecting this district, keeping the ogres outside from attacking easily — so she's been protected too, in a way."

"Another gate?" Grede asked. "You mean the one we saw when we climbed down?"

"I'm not sure," Vereesa said.

"Let's talk more inside," Jace said.

The group followed the Kaldorei into the passageway. After a long corridor, they entered a hall.

It was easy to tell the hall had been exceedingly beautiful and elegant once, but now it was full of collapsed stone pillars and statues, and large sections of the walls had cracked. Thick vines squeezed through the cracks and gripped the walls, as if trying to tear them open even wider.

In the middle of the hall was a shallow pool. The moment Jace saw the pool, it jogged his memory of the time he'd come here in the game to solo the water-elemental boss on his alt warlock...

Even the damaged steps along the edges and the stones that had smashed into the pool and lay exposed on the ceiling above were in remarkably similar positions.

That water elementals had been provoked into manifesting because the satyrs' arrival had tried to enslave all the elemental power in Eldre'Thalas. Now the pool was perfectly calm, without a ripple — which confirmed that the satyrs had never entered this city.

A male elf sat by the pool, apparently dead. His body showed no signs of life at all.

The Kaldorei woman walked to his side and glanced at him, her gaze seeming already numb.

On the other side, above the pool, was a raised platform. Jace remembered that in the game there was a satyr minor leader up there. The open doorway at the end of the hall to the left — you should be able to walk straight onto the platform from the corridor.

As for the passageway to the right... its door was sealed shut, and a fairly complex magical seal had been placed upon it.

The ranger had been fixated on that door ever since she'd come in. Jace asked, "Is it in there?"

"Yes," Vereesa said. "The magical trail is already very strong, Jace."

Translator Notes

Flags

- Dathmora: 达斯莫拉 — the warlock they're tracking (short for Dathmora Darkeye). - Tortheldrin: Canonical prince of Eldre'Thalas (托塞德林) — Vereesa explains he sealed the demons as a backup energy source. - Shen'dralar: Canonical Highborne faction (辛德拉) — the group controlling the plants behind another gate. - Valendris Swiftblade: 瓦琳德丝·迅刃 — a Quel'dorei mage who taught Vereesa; Grede is stunned Vereesa knows about her. - Water-elemental boss: Jace's memory of soloing the game boss by the pool — the calm pool confirms no satyrs have entered. - Ancient elvish: 古代精灵语 — Vereesa uses it to communicate with the Kaldorei woman. - Sense Demons: 侦测恶魔 — Jace's sweep finds nothing; the inner seal is blocking demonic energy. - Quel'dorei vs. Highborne: the Kaldorei calls Vereesa "Quel'dorei" (奎尔多雷), which here means Highborne (上层精灵) — a distinction Vereesa is sensitive to. - POV: Third person throughout.
