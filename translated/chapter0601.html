Chapter 601: Answering Your Call

"Master, I really helped! I used all my magic, I used the Blood Pact seal, I even tried using Immolate to draw off those arcane energy beings — but they weren't afraid of my fire magic at all! Master, this isn't my fault, that tauren..."

"I know, I know. Keep going — which way!"

"This way! This way!"

Sazeno pointed at a junction in the passage. The group immediately followed. Jace's mind was a complete blank. Grede hadn't said a word the whole way, just ran like a madman. Vereesa clenched her teeth and kept silent as well...

Leaving the passage, they entered a broad, empty hall. A toppled stone pillar still leaked arcane energy. The ground was covered in burning arcane flames and ash-like mana residue. Blast cracks and fissures from explosions scarred the walls and floor everywhere.

Surprisingly, this hall held several Kaldorei corpses.

Jace had neither the time nor the energy to wonder why these elves were here. He and the others hurried past the corpses and found the tauren lying on a nearby set of steps, breathing heavily.

"Mosa. Mosa Woodmane."

"Mosa! How are you!"

Grede held her arm and called out to her. Vereesa shouted her name too, wiping the blood from her nose and eyes.

Jace came over and took one look. Mosa's chest, belly, and neck had been blown open by arcane energy in several deep wounds. Though the burns from the arcane energy had already charred them black, bright red blood still seeped out without stop. But Jace could tell — the blood had already been tainted by Shadow.

Jace took out the Rimecrystal, only to find its energy nearly spent, unable to activate anymore. He didn't remember triggering its power...

Wait. That golden light.

When he'd been on the verge of being drained to death by the demon, the Rimecrystal had saved his life. Not only had it restored the life energy that had been drained from him, it had also interrupted the demon's casting.

"Treatment potion," he said, looking at the two beside him. Grede shook his head. Vereesa said nothing either.

"Get her out of this prison," Grede said. "There's Shadow power everywhere. If we stay here, she'll be corroded to death by it!"

Everyone agreed. The moment they'd broken through the seal and entered this space, they'd gotten a taste of the place's dark mana. The color of Mosa's blood was proof that the weakened her was enduring the corrosion of the dark energy floating in the air.

The group lifted Mosa together and retraced their steps. All the way, Mosa was swallowing something — blood, or something else... Jace didn't want to think about it.

He was the one who'd sent Mosa to destroy the magic tower. The decision had been his.

Jace drew a deep breath, forcing himself not to let his thoughts spiral, doing everything he could to keep Mosa from taking worse damage from the jostling.

Grede and Vereesa, though they'd fought themselves to exhaustion, though everyone was injured to varying degrees, still tried their best to keep steady, carrying the tauren to the stone door outside.

Once past the sealed gate they'd breached earlier, the air became much fresher. But Mosa's condition didn't improve.

The female mage who'd been maintaining the seal here earlier had already fled who knew where. The whole empty hall was devoid of people now, with only a male night elf's corpse lying there.

"Mosa." Grede held Mosa's body and murmured, his head bowed. "Don't..."

Vereesa anxiously rummaged through her pack, searching for anything related to healing or life — a potion, an item, anything at all...

...In the end, it was all in vain.

The tauren had stopped moving. Only her throat still twitched faintly. Even her chest had stopped rising and falling.

"Grede."

With the last of her strength, she murmured just that one name. Jace heard it and felt as if a heavy hammer had slammed into his chest. A roar of tinnitus crashed into his mind. He couldn't think of anything else. He just stared blankly at Mosa, lying on the ground.

"I shouldn't have drunk that healing potion," Grede muttered to himself. "Even without that potion I wouldn't have died. That demon couldn't have killed me. I didn't need that potion at all."

"Her injuries aren't something half a healing potion could have fixed, Grede," Vereesa said.

The imp beside them stammered, "Those arcane energy beings explode when they die. So scary. The tauren was amazing — after she got blown up, she still had the strength to push that tower over. Scared me half to death. How she managed it..."

Grede's lips trembled. He stood up and roared, "What the fuck! Why can't the plants out there, that natural energy, that goddamn life force come in here and save this druid?!"

"Cenarius, Zaetar, Celebras, Remulos?!"

He raised the Horn of Remulos, clenched tight in the tauren's hand, and bellowed, "Where are you?! Where the hell are you?!"

Vereesa leaned against the wall and looked out through the door at the swaying trees in the darkness of the night. Not a single leaf answered the dwarf's shouting. "Anyone. Please. Anyone who can save him."

Grede ran to the entrance and roared toward the gate outside, "Whoever you are! Come here! Save this woman, damn it! Life! Elf! Elemental! Arcane!"

"Elemental... elemental..."

He seemed to suddenly remember something. He took out his war hammer and, without a word, the hammer suddenly erupted in a bolt of lightning that lit the surrounding walls as bright as snow.

The dwarf looked up at the hall, then at the ground, his voice trembling. "Save her. I'm begging you. The elementals here..." Then he muttered a long string of Kalimdor tongue that Jace couldn't understand at all, and continued, "I don't speak the elemental tongue very well, but I know you understand what I'm saying. Save her... Damn it! What the hell is this?!"

Grede roared at the top of his lungs. Jace felt his nose sting. He leaned against a pillar and breathed hard. The dwarf walked over and knelt beside Mosa, laying himself across her body. "Now. Quick. Whoever you are..."

At that moment, the once-calm shallow pool suddenly rippled. Before it could draw everyone's attention, the middle of the pool began to boil eerily.

Even Grede raised his head and stared at the center. A burst of water suddenly shot up and coalesced into the upper half of a humanoid form. Surging currents of water rolled back and forth, maintaining the existence of this strange shape. The dwarf snapped back to his senses, stretching his arms out to shield the tauren's body, watching the monster before him take shape, slowly lowering its head to gaze at him.

A water elemental?!

Jace stared at the water elemental, stunned. Was this the water elemental boss that haunted this hall in the game? Why had it come out now?

He picked up the staff at his feet. Vereesa warily reached for her waist as well. But the water elemental didn't attack them.

It slowly walked toward Grede.

"Sto, nu, ka, klanu, a-i, glu, ma, do."

A thunderous voice rang through the hall. It sounded like the water elemental was speaking, but Jace's ears couldn't quite localize the sound to the massive elemental creature before him.

As it spoke, it extended a powerful arm covered in white spray from the ceaselessly churning water and placed it atop Mosa's head.

Fingers formed from the torrent pressed down little by little, stroking the tauren's face and neck. Clean pool water gushing out quickly soaked her entire body.

Grede, not caring that he was drenched through and through, stared blankly at its movements. Before long, a string of violent coughing from his arms made him flinch.

Mosa kept coughing up water. Grede asked, flustered and anxious, "Mosa, Mosa? Can you hear me?" Jace noticed that the wounds beneath the tattered fabric of her robes had turned white under the soaking — clearly healing at a remarkable speed!

The tauren's arm moved, propping up the cold ground. Vereesa also ran over in surprise, hugging her arm. The powerful female giant let out a sob and, with three thick fingers, stroked Grede's face.

She turned her head to look over, touched the sheath at her waist — Azuresong, its sheath already split by the arcane blasts — and said, "Jace Sesso. I killed it. The magic tower."

Jace nodded. "You did, Mosa Woodmane. You saved all of us."

Grede nodded fiercely even as he buried his face in the tauren's neck and broke into loud sobs. Vereesa's eyes had already gone red too. She turned her head to the side, as if unwilling to let anyone see her cry.

Jace had almost forgotten the last time Grede had cried like this — back then his eyes hadn't been healed yet, and he'd nearly forgotten he was a gryphon rider.

The water elemental stood quietly by the pool. Its body began to break apart, to scatter, and eventually melted back into the pond. Everything returned to calm, as if none of it had ever happened.
