Chapter 604: Converging Paths

When Jace first heard "necromancer," he almost thought the orders were referring to Ner'zhul.

When the Dark Portal was closed, Ner'zhul was the warchief of the Horde on Draenor. The reason the entire Kingdom of Stormwind had felt the tearing of fel and Shadow a few months ago... was that he'd activated three artifacts on the other side of the Dark Portal, attempting to teleport the Horde to another world, which had torn all of Draenor apart in the process.

Was he dead?

According to the other timeline's history, he'd used the power of the Staff of Sargeras to enter the Twisting Nether, only to be captured by Kil'jaeden.

To punish his betrayal, Kil'jaeden destroyed his body and imprisoned his soul in ice, casting it down into Northrend to become the Lich King.

By now it was already the ninth year since the Dark Portal, and the tenth year was fast approaching. Had Ner'zhul already become the Lich King? How much time had passed between Ner'zhul's flight into the Twisting Nether and Kil'jaeden's capture of that band of orcs?

Jace had no idea.

Since the Dark Portal had closed, he hadn't heard a single piece of news from Northrend. Northrend was a relatively closed-off place. Most of the humans living there were fishermen, or mountain village hunters who rarely communicated with the outside world. The only place that could be called a town, Wintervale, also had very little contact with here.

Jace still remembered the last time he'd seen a Northrend native — it was at Sentinel Hill, a mercenary he'd run into on the road to Duskwood with Grede looking for Grave Moss.

"Give me that scroll, Morophil," Jace said.

The succubus handed it over. Jace carefully felt the surface and sniffed the traces of demon blood left on it. "How long ago do you think the writing on this was done?"

"Exactly how long is hard to say, master," Morophil admitted. "Approximately?"

"Approximately... five hundred to a thousand years," the succubus answered.

"That's a pretty wide range — several hundred years' span," Vereesa said. "Could it be within the last year?" Jace asked.

"Impossible," Morophil said almost immediately. "If it were that recent, the blood on it wouldn't smell like this. This is Mo'arg demon blood. I know that smell well."

Vereesa nodded. "This pouch may have been preserved inside this demon's belly for more than a year at least."

"A necromancer from several hundred years ago," Jace murmured, looking at Vereesa. "Does that ring any bells? Could be someone from Quel'Thalas..."

"A necromancer hiding in Eversong Woods would attract quite a bit of attention, Jace," Vereesa said. "Even Shadow mages conducting Void experiments in private can't escape the notice of the Spellbreakers, let alone someone communing with souls and raising the dead. There might really be someone that terrifying hiding out, but I think the odds are slim — especially someone who's managed to stay hidden for a thousand years."

"What about the rest?" Jace shook the scroll in his hand, looking back and forth over its contents.

Morophil pointed at a line of text. "Here, in Nathrezim script, it mentions a trapped soul. The rest is mostly Eredun."

Jace frowned and read aloud: "Trapped soul. Several hundred years ago. Necromancer. Nathrezim script..."

"If this necromancer has been around for several hundred years, you'd be better off asking the Kirin Tor, Jace," Vereesa said, sitting to one side and rubbing her legs. "Five hundred years ago, Quel'Thalas's main focus was still on the Amani Empire. The Reliquary was even smaller than it is now. I very much doubt the Reliquary even existed back then."

"And what would the Kirin Tor know?" Morophil asked.

Vereesa looked at her and explained, "By then, the Order of Tirisfal had already been fighting your kind of succubi demons, along with the undead creatures you brought with you, on Azeroth for thousands of years. The last Guardian of Tirisfal, Archmage Aegwynn, inherited the Guardian's power eight hundred years ago. By then the Guardian system was already quite mature. The Kirin Tor, as the custodians of the Order of Tirisfal's legacy, would definitely have more records — kept in the great library of Dalaran."

"Vereesa's right," Jace thought it over.

"What about these two remaining scrolls?" he asked, pointing at the scrolls left.

The succubus shook her head. "These two are both written in Eredun, master. The demonic script portion is minimal, and it's mostly connective language. I genuinely can't make them out. If all else fails, you could take these scrolls to that imp and let him have a look."

"He's never mentioned he speaks doomguard language," Jace said, a bit distracted. "Forget it, let me look at this first."

"If you don't need me for anything, help me dig out this demon heart, succubus," Vereesa said, looking at the succubus beside her. "This demon's ribs are a lot sturdier than I expected. They even seem to have arcane residue in them..."

"This demon's mana has already been drained dry by me," Morophil said, pulling out the Nathrezim black blade and spinning it in her hand. "You're just running out of strength, that's all."

Vereesa glared at her unhappily, raised her long blade, and drove it into the demon's chest and belly, muttering, "Stop talking and help. Careful not to damage the heart."

"Worried a powerful Saya'dar Zhoakexier can't cleanly extract a heart?" the succubus said as she squatted down beside the ranger, leaning in close to the split-open demon's chest.

The two of them worked away while muttering to each other. The succubus laughed at one point. Vereesa complained a bit in irritation. But Jace had no mind to spare for their chatter. He just stood to one side, carefully thinking through every necromancer that existed in the game.

At this point he'd even considered Kel'Thuzad, but right now Kel'Thuzad was still far from the "necromancer" stage. At best he was just a Kirin Tor mage with an interest in necromantic magic.

Of course, Kel'Thuzad was also just a human in his fifties or sixties — nowhere near the eight-hundred-year span. Could it be a dreadlord?

Every powerful Nathrezim, by Azeroth's or the Burning Legion's standards, absolutely qualified as a necromancer. They could raise the dead, rip out souls, drain blood...

Actually, there was one powerful necromancer Jace had overlooked — Kil'jaeden. It's just that compared to Kil'jaeden's power, magic, and numerous other aspects, his necromantic knowledge was nothing particularly special.

Yet the creation of all the orc death knights, including Teron Gorefiend, and the emergence of the Lich King were all connected to him. But it couldn't be ignored that the Eredar were the first race to study necromantic magic.

Jace chanted an incantation and cast Arcane Intellect on himself, sitting to one side to think further, digging up every memory from this life and the last, trying to connect it all together.

He didn't know if Arcane Intellect was really that remarkable, but after sitting here thinking for a while, he suddenly made a connection to a clue he'd uncovered not long ago.

Jace snapped back to awareness as if he'd been electrocuted. He immediately flipped open his travel bag and rummaged through it nonstop. After going through several, he finally found the Karazhan treasure hunter's handbook he'd been keeping.

He'd never dared leave this thing in his rented room in Stormwind, so he carried it with him everywhere.

He flipped to the last page with a rustle and looked at the demonic name there. Jace read it softly: "Ulthalesh."

"Ulthalesh?" Morophil, over there, glanced over at the sound of the name.

"You know this name?" Jace asked.

"That's a very distinctive Nathrezim name, master," Morophil said. "I once had an Eredar warlock master who was obsessed with finding a demonic holy artifact called Ulthalesh. It's said this weapon harvested the souls of countless powerful demons — a good number of them its own former masters. It's said that long ago, the master of the Burning Legion gifted it to an Eredar necromancer to carry out an important mission, but no one knows what happened to that necromancer afterward."

Vereesa had just wrapped up the demon heart she'd cut out. Hearing the succubus's words, she asked, "Is it the same necromancer?"

"How could she just happen to be on this world," Morophil looked at her, but then seemed to think of something and turned to Jace. "Master, you've heard the legend of this artifact too — is that why you thought of this necromancer?"

"I've heard a bit, after all the demons and warlocks I know," Jace said. "Though it might also be me reading too much into it." Morophil narrowed her eyes as if she'd caught onto something, but Jace stared her down for a moment and she didn't dare say anything more.

Because Jace hadn't been reading too much into it. That Eredar warlock was the one the Dark Titan had sent to Azeroth, wielding Ulthalesh to carry out destruction.

He looked at the scroll again. It was still covered in all those bizarre, alien words he couldn't read at all. Could it be connected to Ulthalesh?

Could it be that Das'mora's master or superior wanted her to find this necromancer and take back Ulthalesh? Or to kill this necromancer and clean house for the Burning Legion? After all, Jace remembered that this Eredar necromancer had later betrayed the Burning Legion...

Then Das'mora had her hands full — trying to find a way to summon Archimonde on one hand, while taking commissions from the Twisting Nether to run all over Azeroth on the other, cleaning up the Burning Legion's messes.

No wonder she'd tried to communicate with warlocks on another continent, getting them to help her, and written so many books, trying to spread demonic knowledge across Azeroth.

Not only that, during this time she'd had to scheme against the Highborne of Eldre'Thalas, the Kaldorei search parties, even the Order of Tirisfal — and get chewed out on top of it all.

A real ten thousand years of torment.

Yet a demon's power was ultimately limited. If Jace and his group hadn't shown up, her final fate would probably have been getting summoned into Maraudon by the satyrs, controlled, and interrogated for the locations of every demon or Highborne artifact, including Ulthalesh.

Wait — could those satyrs also have wanted to summon Das'mora for the sake of Ulthalesh? That would probably require fully understanding everything those satyrs had written.

Thinking about so many things at once, Jace grew more and more afraid of turning into an idiot once Arcane Intellect wore off. Marin was right — the more you used Arcane Intellect, the more likely you were to develop a split personality.

Of course, no matter how powerful Arcane Intellect was, it only helped you remember things more firmly, made past memories clearer, and made thinking sharper. It couldn't dig up things you fundamentally didn't know. Without learning the obscure Eredun dialect, there was absolutely no way he could make out the rest of the scroll's contents.

By that measure, the satyrs' approach was much smarter — summon Das'mora over, control her, and interrogate her.

Jace steadied his mind, put the scroll away with the others and tucked them into the pouch, and swallowed. Then he glanced at the small black dagger in his hand.

If this were in the game, and the satyrs had eventually summoned Das'mora into Maraudon, wouldn't this dagger be that purple weapon from the game that dealt Shadow damage and drained mana — the Endless Darkness Blade?

Jace's eyebrows slowly rose. So that's how it was.
