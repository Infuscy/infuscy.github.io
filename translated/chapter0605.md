## Chapter 605: That's Settled

After wrapping up Das'mora's demon heart, the group left the magically sealed underground palace and returned to the hall where the pool was.

Mosa was lying on a mat of woven vines, sleeping peacefully. Grede put a finger to his lips as the three of them walked back in. Jace nodded and carefully sat down on a broken stone pillar, leaning against the wall behind him to rest.

Vereesa sniffed the demon blood on her hands and clothes and frowned. She glanced at the pool in the center and was just about to reach in and wash when Grede and Jace both immediately reached out to stop her.

"Don't..."

"What?"

"This water hasn't been contaminated yet," Grede said in a low voice. "We still need to live on this water for a while. If you wash your hands in there, that's it — all ruined."

Jace nodded. "We need to wait for Mosa to recover and then get us out of here. Don't forget the garden outside, Wen." Vereesa understood immediately and pulled her hand back. "Sorry."

Jace stared at the pool for a while. Actually, even setting aside the drinking water issue, if the demon blood contaminated this place, the water elemental that had just saved the tauren's life would probably jump out and pick a fight with them right about then.

"So what do we eat?" Vereesa asked.

Jace shook his head. "No idea what this mage survived on while hiding here."

"Can't these elves survive on mana alone?" Grede looked at Vereesa. "Why can't you?"

"There's no Sunwell here," Vereesa said. "The arcane mana in this place makes me uncomfortable. Isn't its source that Void demon Immol'tar? Just thinking about it makes my skin crawl."

"There should still be something on the level above," Jace said, looking at the passage entrance across the way. "I'll take a look up there later. Don't get your hopes up though."

"I'm coming too," Grede said.

"Then I'll stay behind and look after Mosa. You two go ahead." Vereesa said.

"We could eat that," Morophil said, pointing at the Kaldorei corpse nearby.

"We're not demons!" Grede glared at the succubus. "And you'd better not eat either. No matter how many people you've eaten before, you'd best change your habits now that you're part of this team."

Morophil said, "Just a suggestion."

"Disgusting," Vereesa muttered under her breath.

"You haven't seen what's truly disgusting about a succubus, Quel'dorei!" Sazeno chirped from the side. "Back in the pack," Jace glared at him. "You won't be getting a single Soul Shard before we're back in Stormwind."

The imp's face went cold. "No, no, master, that's not what I meant, I meant the one in the basement..."

"Shut it."

Sazeno covered his mouth and obediently crawled back into the pack. Jace glanced at the smiling succubus, then at Vereesa, who was lowering her head and saying nothing, then at Grede over there picking at his fingernails and staring at the ceiling, and scratched the stubble on his face in exasperation.

Just then, he noticed Mosa was awake and watching them. "Sorry — are we being too loud?"

Mosa coughed and shook her head. Grede only then noticed she was awake. "Mosa? How are you feeling?"

"Good," Mosa said. "A few days... and I can cast spells to heal myself."

"Brilliant," Grede said, helping Mosa's shoulder. "I want to get the hell out of this place too. It's truly no place for people."

"Don't push Mosa too hard, Grede," Vereesa said. "We're not in any rush. Even if there's nothing on the upper level, there's still a garden outside. I refuse to believe there's nothing edible in there — there ought to be bugs and small creatures living in it at least."

"Vereesa's right," Jace said. "And I can use these next few days to properly sort through all the materials we've found with these two demons. The past few days it's been all rushing around, dealing with one surprise after another — those sentries, that half-orc... the surprises just keep coming, with no time to rest at all."

"What about after?" Grede asked. "Once we're out, what do we do next? Head back to Gadgetzan the same way? Go around that huge stretch of road again?"

"Damn it," Jace rubbed his face hard. "I completely forgot about the centaurs in Thousand Needles."

He glanced at Mosa, who'd been silent the whole time. She probably didn't have a good solution either, or was just too weak to want to think about it.

"Maybe we could ask those Kaldorei for help," Jace said. "I'm thinking of heading south through the Feralas jungle looking for signs of a silithid hive. If we can find any, we could trade that intelligence to the Kaldorei in exchange for their help."

"How would they help us? Help us attack the centaur tribes?" Grede asked.

"Hippogryphs," Vereesa said. "Remember those blue bird-headed monsters the sentries at Feathermoon Stronghold were keeping? Beasts that looked a bit like gryphons — those are their mounts. If they can carry a Kaldorei, they can definitely carry us."

"But they can't carry Mosa," Grede said.

"Well... that is a bit of a problem," Vereesa said.

"You're from another world," Mosa spoke up suddenly.

"Uh," Jace looked at the tauren. "Yes? What about it, Mosa? Why bring that up all of a sudden."

"I can't leave Kalimor. I can't leave my people," Mosa said, thinking as she spoke. "I need to protect my people... until we find a new place to settle."

"Traveling with you has been a joy. Killing demons, making Kalimor cleaner — that makes me happy. Traveling with you, I've learned so much I never knew. Outsiders can be kind — even a demon helped me. I never knew that before. I was surprised."

Mosa wiped her eyes. "But I need to stay. I don't want to leave you. I don't want to leave you, Grede Thunderfist of Aerie Peak. But I have to stay."

"Of course, Mosa," Grede said, sitting beside her and patting her belly. "You're a responsible warrior. Right now, your people need brave fighters like you."

"I'm a hunter," Mosa corrected.

"This 'warrior' means you're a fighter, a champion, a brave soul — not that you've got to be someone carrying an axe..." Grede choked back half a sentence, then finally sighed. "Fine. You're a brave hunter."

"I am a brave hunter." Mosa said it again and drifted back to sleep. She was too weak. Normally every word she spoke was full of vigor, but stringing these sentences together seemed to take everything she had.

"Let's go take a look upstairs, Jace," Grede stood up. "Find her something to eat first. I'm not hungry right now anyway."

"How are your injuries?" Jace asked.

Grede laughed and slapped the carapace armor beside him. "With this armor on, what injury could I possibly take? And you don't think that without my Avatar I turn back into that useless wreck from before, do you?"

"All right, all right, let's not bring up that armor," Jace said quickly.

Vereesa looked at the dwarf, then at Jace. Jace met her eyes and they exchanged a glance. Vereesa looked toward the dark doorway. Jace nodded.

Grede picked up his axe and walked through the doorway. Jace followed. The two of them walked upward through the deep, narrow passage in a silence that sat uncomfortably.

"You weren't planning to bring Mosa back with us?" Jace asked.

"There are no tauren where we're from. You can't expect Mosa to live in Booty Bay forever — she's not suited to that kind of chaotic shithole," Grede said.

He glanced at Jace, then said, "Not everyone is as irresponsible as me, damn it. Just up and abandon my people, run off who-knows-where, not a shred of guilt, no word for years."

Jace said nothing. Grede asked again, "Tell me, Jace — am I a coward who fled the battlefield?!"

"What?!" Jace asked, startled. "What kind of nonsense is that? Didn't they refuse to let you be a gryphon rider?"

"I don't know," Grede stopped and turned around. "Maybe I could have done something else on Aerie Peak — like stand sentry, watch out for trolls or leftover orcs trying to raid... and instead I just ran off..."

"A one-eyed sentry?" Jace asked.

"Fine," Grede waved a hand. "That really wouldn't have been appropriate. But Mosa makes me feel like crap. She's like... how to put it... a true warden. Compared to her, I... you know what I mean."

"I get it. But Aerie Peak isn't facing anything like what the tauren are going through, Grede. I don't think there's much comparison," Jace said. "Besides, when you left the war was basically over already. How is that fleeing the battlefield?"

Grede nodded and kept walking.

"You're upset just because of this?" Jace asked. "What else?"

"What else would there be?"

"When Mosa got hurt, you cried like an idiot."

"Piss off," the dwarf said. "Haven't you learned a few nicer words after studying in the Mage Quarter this long?"

"I'm saying," Jace grabbed the dwarf. "Do you love Mosa Woodmane?"

"She's a tauren!" Grede yelped.

Jace said nothing. The two of them stared at each other for a while. Grede gritted his teeth, his eyes flickered, and he leaned against the wall with a sigh.

"There's never been anyone who treated me this well, who took care of me like this," he murmured. Jace frowned. "Am I not good to you?"

"You're not a woman," Grede said. "What good does you being nice to me do?"

"You..." Jace swore and kicked Grede. He hadn't expected the normally stocky, solid dwarf to collapse against the wall from one kick, going "ow, ow."

Jace was taken aback. "Hey — didn't you say you were fine?"

Grede stood up, supporting his waist and baring his teeth. "You damn demon worshipper..."

"All right, stop dodging the question," Jace said.

Grede took several deep breaths. "Love, all right? So what?"

"Tell her," Jace said. "Like she did — when we first met she straight-up told you she liked you. You want to take that regret back to Stormwind?"

"She could meet one of her own kind, have a better life, instead of always thinking about me," the dwarf said. "The problem is there isn't one of her kind around right now," Jace said.

Grede thought for a while, then suddenly seemed to remember something and stared at Jace. "You haven't even figured out your own problem, and you're lecturing me?"

"What problem?" Jace asked.

Grede looked at Jace, raised one eyebrow slowly, and said, "You tell me." Jace realized immediately what Grede was getting at.

"Fine," he nodded. "You're right, Grede. Here's the deal — if I can make it back to Stormwind alive, buy a house, and get my parents settled, then I'll face my problem. That's what I said before too."

Grede nodded. "All right. Since even you're saying it that way, then once Mosa's better, I'll tell her how I feel."

"That's settled then."

"That's settled."

The two of them went quiet in the passage for a while longer. Grede looked up toward the heights. "Let's go."

"Let's go," Jace answered.
