## Chapter 61: Wind, Rain, and Thunder!

Jace wiped the rain from his eyes to clear his vision. The thing was picking its way steadily down the slope, heading straight for them.

Just from the silhouette, that was no human. No dwarf, either. It towered over any human or dwarf by a wide margin… and here in Redridge, there was really only one possibility.

It was the orc!

"Run, Grede!"

"He—"

Jace shoved Grede — words still half-formed — hard toward the lakeshore and leaped after him. The two of them half-ran, half-slid down the steep slope.

They skidded into a patch of muddy flat, scrambled up, and bolted toward Lakeshire. The orc came down right behind them, but a hell of a lot faster.

He charged like some kind of raging athlete, brimming with power — nothing like the frail image of a gentle seer or prophet. This was a hardened warrior who'd survived a hundred battles.

The sand and water kicked up behind him like the spray from a speeding jeep's tires. At this rate he'd just run the two of them straight over.

"We're not outrunning him… damn it!" the dwarf roared, whipping his small round shield up and spinning around.

Jace didn't like it, but he knew Grede was right. Keep running and they'd just burn stamina until the orc caught them anyway.

Through the torrential downpour the brute's form grew clearer: a dark-green monstrosity gripping a long staff, wild brown hair whipping in the wind, eyes locked on the two of them, every muscle tensed like stone.

He bellowed and charged, swinging his staff. Jace could clearly see a whirlwind of sand and grit swirling around the orc's arms and muscles!

Grede raised his shield to block — but the orc, channeling the full force of his charge, hit with such absurd power that he sent dwarf and shield both crashing to the ground. Forget a jeep — this thing was a goddamn locomotive!

Having flattened the dwarf, the orc closed the distance to Jace in a single stride. Jace raised his sword to parry — and the orc smashed it clean in half with one blow from his metal bracer. There went five silver, down the drain.

The orc raised his staff. Jace hauled up the staff from his own back to block the blow — and the orc froze for a heartbeat when he saw it. In that heartbeat, Jace got a clear look at his face.

The creature's face was less a fusion of beast and man and more a skull draped in skin.

Between his heavy, barely-closed lips, a pair of tusks jutted upward with aggressive prominence. His eyes bulged wide, like an enraged tiger's.

In that moment of hesitation the orc snarled — and Grede, who'd somehow gotten back up, drove a sword straight into his calf. The orc roared and smashed his bracer right into Grede's face, sending him flying. The dwarf spun twice in the air before hitting the ground.

Jace's mind went blank. We're dead.

The orc yanked the blade out of his calf, tossed it aside, and turned back around. His expression had gone ten times more savage than before.

Jace knew he wasn't angry about Grede's stab. He was angry about the staff in Jace's hands.

He'd assumed every shaman in the Horde had long abandoned the way of the elements and turned to shadow. This lone orc had indeed drunk the demon blood — his skin had shifted from an orc's natural brown to green — so he should have walked the path of fel and shadow too.

Yet based on what Jace had pieced together earlier, this orc was clearly still holding to the shamanic path. Which meant he'd recovered, at least somewhat, from the bloodlust and rage the demon blood had brought on, and had started to question what the demons had done to orcish society.

Orcs who'd come back to their senses hated everything tied to demons with a passion.

Jace had still been guessing — but the sudden shift in the orc's expression confirmed it!

He'd recognized a relic of the old Horde. As a shaman — a member of what had once been the most revered class in the Horde — his hatred for the architects of the shamans' corruption, the upper echelons of the Shadow Council, would be bone-deep!

The orc roared and swung his staff down in a brutal arc. Jace rolled aside; the staff slammed into the sand, sending up an explosion of water and mud.

The orc pressed the attack furiously. Jace kept dodging, clinging to the dwarf's advice: turn your weakness into a strength, read the opponent's offense.

The orc was enormously powerful, but his body was huge too — his movements were wide and sweeping, which made them easier to predict.

A shaman wasn't a trained warrior. The racial strength was terrifying, but his close-combat experience couldn't match a real fighter's. All Jace had to do was stay patient, focus on dodging, and wait for Grede to jump back in…

…if Grede could even wake up, after being swatted that far.

Jace backed off step by step, dodging two more strikes. Just as the orc cocked his staff for a vicious thrust, an arrow suddenly sprouted from his shoulder.

"Over here, you bastard!" Grede bellowed from a distance. "Go ahead, ignore me — see if I don't turn you into a pincushion!"

Grede had somehow recovered his quiver, but even from here Jace could tell there couldn't be many arrows left after that mess.

The orc didn't seem interested in acknowledging the arrows at all. He snapped the shaft off and raised his staff to crush Jace — and another arrow hit his shoulder.

"What, you think one eye means I can't aim?" the dwarf laughed.

The orc turned and began chanting something. Suddenly the sky lit up with a crack of lightning and thunder.

A ball of electricity wreathed in mist gathered in his hand from the sky, crackling and bursting, its white flashes stabbing at Jace's vision. But the orc was clearly aiming at Grede.

"You've gotta be kidding me!"

The dwarf yelped and tried to scramble away. Jace shouted, hauled himself up, and swung his staff hard at the orc's lightning-wreathed wrist.

The blow scattered some of the energy from the orb. The orc snarled and thrust his palm forward with force — a chain of lightning flashed between his hand and Grede, lighting up the lake, the shore, and the forest in one blinding instant. The scattered blast caught Jace full-on and sent him flying.

Grede, sprinting in the distance, froze in the searing white light and collapsed.

Jace turned and ran, shouting Sazeno's name — but the imp didn't answer.

That's when he realized the bag with the imp had been left back on Grede's side of the shore.

Damn it…

The orc turned back to the fleeing Jace and began chanting again, a ball of flame gathering in his hand. Even in the pouring rain, he could still summon the element of fire!

Watching the fireball in his hand grow bigger and bigger, Jace thought, This has got to be the part where I die, right?

Fear! Cast Fear!

He turned and murmured the incantation under his breath, leveling his long staff at the orc. When the spell completed, it felt like half his spirit had been ripped out of him. His knees buckled and he nearly went down.

He looked back at the orc. The flames in his hand suddenly guttered, scattering into a shower of sparks across the ground. The orc's whole body shuddered, and he dropped to one knee, roaring something over and over.

It worked. The Fear spell worked.

But for how long?

He forced himself alert, ran to grab the sword the orc had thrown aside, turned, and drove it hard into the trembling orc's chest!

Thunk.

The blade caught between his ribs! The monster!

Should've gone for the belly…

But it was too late for second guesses. The pain jolted the orc awake. He seized the blade with one hand and swung a fist with the other. Jace had to let go of the sword and dodge back.

The fist nearly took his nose off — but the force of his own retreat caught his foot on the soft ground and sent him sprawling.

No time to nurse his strength. Jace cast Fear again. The orc's eyes went wide with rage and he dropped to one knee, roaring without pause.

He forced himself together and fixed his eyes on Grede's unconscious form off to the side. He didn't have much fight left in him. This time he sprinted over, hauled Grede up, and ran.

But he hadn't reached the dwarf before the orc started moving again.

Less than ten seconds…

This orc's willpower was every bit as terrifying as Grede's.

The orc watched the fleeing human. He didn't reach for fire this time — he tossed aside the sword and called down the lightning again with both hands!

This time, from farther away, Jace saw it clearly: a bolt of lightning from the sky struck into the orc's hands, coalescing into a savage ball of electricity. It was almost beautiful.

The orc seemed to bridge heaven and earth with his own power. And Jace had never even heard of this Redridge shaman — meaning he was probably just an average shaman by shaman standards. So this was the terrifying power of the elements?

Whoever you are…

Jace immediately began murmuring the Fear incantation. When the spell completed he collapsed on the spot, a wave of hypoglycemic dizziness hitting him so hard his vision went black for a moment.

But the orc wasn't faring any better. His eyes went wide and vacant, his body rigid. The lightning in his hands exploded outward, discharging into the sand and the lake, currents rolling across the shore in a chain of flickering white light.

This time, though, he only stood there panting and growling for a moment before snapping out of it again.

He hurled a string of orcish curses, hauled himself up, and limped toward Jace, gathering another ball of lightning in one hand with a roar.

The orc was clearly running on fumes too. Each burst of lightning was weaker than the last. This had become a battle of willpower.

Jace grabbed Grede and shook him, but the dwarf was out cold. Still, he had breath and a heartbeat, at least.

That lightning spell had hit him dead-on. The fact he was alive at all was a miracle.

He turned back, ready to pour everything he had left into one more Fear — when the orc's lightning discharged.

Jace watched a wall of white light swallow him whole. Everything vanished under a sound that hammered his eardrums, and an indescribable, searing pain shot from the top of his head to the soles of his feet, as if his organs had been scalded.

But when the shock passed, he staggered back two steps in agony — not dead. Not even unconscious.

His hands and feet were so numb they barely obeyed him. He crouched on the ground, unable to stand, but he was still conscious.

How the hell was he more durable than a dwarf?

Then he noticed the veins and skin of his arms traced with faint crimson lines — not capillaries, but something like writing! As the strange marks flowed, a heat that made his blood surge rolled through his whole body.

The orc, clearly taken aback by this absurd lightning resistance, hesitated for a moment — and then a shriek from the distance yanked both their attention toward it.

"Master, thank me for saving your life! This is the power of the Blood Pact!"
