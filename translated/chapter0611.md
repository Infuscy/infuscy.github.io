## Chapter 611: The Spread of the Silithid

The two of them chatted about the Dark Portal campaign, recalling the difficulties they'd faced back then and looking at the present. The gap between them was already so wide it didn't feel like it had happened to the same people.

The rain let up a bit. After a stretch of silence, Vereesa asked, "Suppose the Farstriders really do get me promoted... would you attend my promotion ceremony, Jace? That might be the most important ceremony of a Farstrider's life, after all. A Ranger-Lord is second only to the Ranger-General, and the Ranger-General isn't a position you can earn through strength or military merit."

"Of course I'll be there," Jace said without hesitation.

At that, Vereesa's eyes flickered. She lowered her head. "I almost forgot — you're currently 'killed in action.' Showing up in public at an occasion like that could cause major problems. Never mind, then. Your safety comes first. I definitely don't want to piss off the entire Kirin Tor just to have you share a meal or attend a dance with me. It's all just for show anyway — I shouldn't care too much about it."

Let alone Vereesa — Jace himself had nearly forgotten his awkward situation on the other continent.

Traveling across Kalimor these past two or three months, he'd almost completely forgotten what Bizo Eschard had said in the cathedral back in the royal city.

If the Reliquary really could come up with more than a hundred gold as a reward this time, Jace could actually buy a house outright when he got back — and in the Trade District, no less.

These two days since leaving Eldre'Thalas, he'd already been thinking about which location to buy in.

But he couldn't go back to Lordaeron in person to bring his parents to Stormwind. He could only write or ask someone to escort them on their own... Could he pick them up at Southshore? Southshore was also crawling with Kirin Tor people — that would probably be a problem too.

Old Vic was a pretty tough guard captain, and his mother was certainly no pushover, but thinking about the dangers along the way — especially the defenseless stretch of Baradin Bay — Jace still felt his heart lodged in his throat, no matter how hard he swallowed.

With Vereesa's help, fishing went a lot more smoothly, and they went back to eat a fairly hearty dinner.

After a terrifying night of thunderstorms, the next day the group continued traveling south.

After setting out again, the atmosphere among them grew considerably more awkward and silent. Morophil kept wearing a strange smile, as if she'd witnessed something extraordinary. Jace quietly confirmed with her several times, making sure she hadn't played any strange tricks between the tauren and the dwarf, before he left her be.

By afternoon, the tauren and the elf were walking far ahead. Grede seemed like he was finally about to burst if he didn't say something. He walked beside Jace several times with words on the tip of his tongue, and only after a long while did he finally say, "Hey, that..."

"I know," Jace said.

"Ah... hmm." Grede swallowed his words again. "How does it feel?" Jace asked.

Grede nodded deeply. "She loves me, Jace."

"I can tell," Jace said. "So what are you going to do next?"

"I won't abandon her." Grede seemed to have his floodgates opened by that one question. "Once I become a true Mountain King, I'll come back here at once. I'll help her and the tauren survive the centaur threat — no, I'll help them defeat the centaur. You think I'm bluffing? I don't think those deformed creatures can shake my strength. They don't have what it takes. By then, I'll make the Wildhammer clan and the tauren of this world become allies. The sages will support it too — there's no way a Wildhammer dwarf wouldn't like the tauren."

"I think so too," Jace said.

"Don't say it so casually," Grede said. "You think I'm talking nonsense? This isn't some impulsive decision I've made on a whim, Jace. I feel like I've found what I'm meant to do — something that's not only good for the Wildhammer, but a new trial for myself."

"I don't think you're making a rash decision, Grede," Jace said seriously. "Mosa's a good girl. She deserves—"

"Hey, look ahead," Vereesa called back.

Jace raised his head and looked forward, his gaze passing over Vereesa to what lay beyond. Among the trees, there was something tall rising and trembling, over and over.

It was a silithid limb, thick like a claw, standing there like a tall, thin tree.

Jace knew what this was. These strange limbs were everywhere in the silithid hives in the game. He had no idea where they grew from — maybe they belonged to some enormous brood mother buried underground, or maybe the entire hive was a single composite living organism...

For all he knew, right now countless silithids might already be crawling back and forth through the complex tunnels beneath their feet.

Going by what Shandris Feathermoon had said, Jace had felt they'd need to travel very far south before encountering the silithid. So he'd figured they hadn't walked all that far and hadn't gotten into the kind of alertness needed to handle surprises.

"Where are we?" Jace asked in a low voice.

"Not far," Vereesa said. "I remember you mentioning before that to see the hives, you'd need to walk all the way to the sloping hills in the south of the jungle..."

"But this clearly isn't any hill country. We're still deep in the jungle," Grede said.

"Exactly," Vereesa said. "The hives have spread even more recklessly than those Kaldorei sentries detected..."

Grede said, "Those old bastards who live for thousands of years — who knows the last time any of them came out to survey the direction the hives were spreading."

"Mark this location down, Vereesa," Jace said, watching the limb sway again. "We're not going any deeper. We'll look around the periphery and then go find the Kaldorei sentries to report in."

"The trees tell me... something foreign is approaching," Mosa warned.

The moment she finished, Grede and Jace looked around and still hadn't seen anything — when suddenly a giant bug the size of a calf burst out of the undergrowth. It had four thick, powerful legs, its armored abdomen arched high from behind, and a pincer-like maw slanting toward the group as it lunged!

Jace's reaction was almost instinctive. He turned and thrust his hand out, just as Das'mora had done to him that day... "Duk, Heduniluoyinmosi!"

Shadow power, following Jace's whispered incantation, seized the silithid and halted its rapid advance, wrenching its head to a grotesque angle as it crashed to the ground... But in the blink of an eye, an arrow wreathed in arcane energy pierced straight through the weak gap in its armor with a whiz. The giant bug let out a shriek, its burning body lurching into the bushes on the other side.

Before Jace could even confirm whether his spell had worked, the rustle of carapace came from the bushes on the other side almost simultaneously...

Mosa immediately chanted an incantation. Two giant bugs that had just pounced out hadn't even landed before they were wrapped in the surrounding leaves and vines, unable to move — and then a buzzing sound came from the sky.

"We've been detected. Damn it." Vereesa looked up at the tangled branches and enormous leaves crisscrossing the sky. "I don't have many arrows left, Jace. If they come from above, it might be hard to deal with..."

"Then we pull back. As long as we remember this location, that's enough," Jace said immediately.

Shandris Feathermoon had gotten at least one thing right — when it came to running into silithid, their group had absolutely no initiative to engage in combat.

"Don't forget this."

Mosa reached out and grabbed the head of a silithid warrior trapped by the vines she'd summoned, planted her hoof on its shoulder, and wrenched the whole head off, carrying it over to the group.

She handed over the giant bug's head, still snapping its mandibles, bigger than a watermelon. "Jace Sesso. Will this do?"

Jace nodded. "That's more than enough, Mosa. More than enough."
