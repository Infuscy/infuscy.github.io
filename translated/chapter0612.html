Chapter 612: Seeing Them Off

Pushing westward through layers of jungle and fleeing the territory of the silithid, the group was exhausted.

After a brief rest for the night, they continued west the next day. Their plan was to enter the night elves' territory and then deliberately get themselves captured by the sentinels, to report that the silithid hive lands were expanding rapidly northward — and, while they were at it, ask whether the night elves could help them get to the Tanaris desert.

Sure enough, this time Vereesa no longer led them on a route that dodged the patrols. After masking their trail, it was only a matter of hours after they spotted the night elves' warning arcane lamps before a band of sentinels had them surrounded.

"We mean you no harm, travelers, but you must turn back."

The leader of the night elf sentinels wore a long purple cloak and rode a pure-black nightsaber. She wore a silver helm etched with a tree-veiled pattern that covered the upper half of her face, and carried a three-bladed moonglaive as she stepped out from the treeline.

When she caught sight of Vereesa, the expression on her face softened considerably. She looked up and greeted her, "Ishnu-alah, sister from another continent. I still remember you. But Commander Shandris Feathermoon already let you and your friends leave. Why have you returned? Whatever your intentions, this is a provocation."

"We found the edge of a silithid hive less than fifty miles to the southeast of here, Kaldorei," Vereesa said, cutting straight to the point.

"Impossible. The southeast is still close to the ruins of the Kaldorei Empire," the sentinel said. "That area is still very, very far from Silithus. We know the hive has been spreading outward, but it can't have reached that deep."

"She's telling the truth. When I asked Commander Shandris Feathermoon earlier, she held the same view as you. But we hadn't gone far south before we were ambushed by a swarm. Your intelligence needs updating — it may be that your outpost in Silithus has already lost its grip on the hive's movements."

Vereesa handed over the map on which she'd marked several points along the edge of the hive. The sentinel on the black nightsaber conferred with the sentinels beside her, then said, "Thank you for the warning. I will inform the sentinel commander at once and coordinate with Cenarion Hold regarding the activity of the qiraji silithid. This is a grave threat — they are already close to our lands."

"Could you do us a favor?" Grede stepped forward. "We need to get to Gadgetzan. We heard the elves here raise some kind of flying mount. We just need you to get us past the centaur in Thousand Needles, in the direction of the Shimmering Flats."

The sentinel looked at the dwarf and said, "All the hippogryphs are busy flying back and forth to Mount Hyjal and Astranaar. They have to cover tens of thousands of miles, and be careful not to get attacked by the chimaera in Stonetalon Peak along the way. I'm afraid we can't spare any to carry you…"

She glanced at the tauren. Mosa said immediately, "I'm not going."

"Three people still won't work," the sentinel said.

"Is there any chance you could take us by sailboat to the coast of the Tanaris desert?" Jace asked.

The sentinel hesitated for a moment. Grede held up the giant insect's head and said, "Look here — we even killed a few of your bugs along the way and made our little contribution to your safety."

Upon seeing the bug's head, the other party finally said, "Follow me, travelers."

Following the sentinel company westward, after two or three days of walking they finally reached that fog-shrouded stretch of coast.

Along the way, none of them had said much. It was an unusually heavy silence. Jace didn't know what was weighing on the others, but for his part, he'd thought that leaving Kalimdor and returning to Stormwind would be a joyful thing. Yet for some reason, the closer they drew to the night elf dock, the more a wave of sadness welled up in his chest.

Perhaps it was because of Mosa Woodmane. After traveling for months with this brave tauren girl, Jace had truly come to regard her as a friend he could trust with his life.

Everything along the way — facing down the thunder lizards, the battle with the harpies in Stonetalon, the grand adventure in Maraudon, the demon war in Eldre'Thalas…

On the beach, he glanced at the tauren, who stood over two meters tall. Mosa was off in the distance, gazing at the pale sails faintly visible in the white fog, standing perfectly still as though lost in thought.

The night elf sentinel was talking with Vereesa about something. After a while she came over and told the group, "They can take us to a cape on the eastern side of Tanaris. On the map, it looks like we'll still need to walk two or three days north to reach Steamwheedle Port. They said getting too close to Steamwheedle Port risks running into goblin or pirate warships."

"That's acceptable," Jace said. "However rough the beach is, the environment is still far better than the deep desert. We can walk to Steamwheedle Port on our own…"

"Mm." Vereesa glanced at Mosa and said, "Mosa Woodmane, then we part ways here."

Mosa opened her broad arms and pulled Vereesa tightly into an embrace. Vereesa buried her head deep in Mosa's chest. Jace saw her shoulders tremble gently a couple of times. When she pulled away she kept her head down the whole time, unwilling to let anyone see her face, and turned toward the pier.

"Mosa, I'll come back," Grede said, patting Mosa on the waist. Mosa lifted him up bodily and hugged him. "Then I'll be waiting for you, Grede of Aerie Peak. I'll wait forever."

After Mosa set Grede down, Jace looked at her and said, "If you run into trouble, come find us on the other continent, Mosa. Give the elf's name — Vereesa Windrunner. Any long-eared, light-haired elf who looks roughly like her will be sure to lead you to her."

The tauren walked straight over and scooped Jace up. Jace hadn't been prepared for it at all. All along the journey he'd felt that Mosa had been disdainful of him — after all, tauren hated demons, and they didn't care much for warlocks who wielded shadow magic. Mosa had barely exchanged a few words with him, far fewer than with the other two.

After setting Jace down, Mosa said in a low voice, "Jace Sesso, I know you are a good warlock. Take care of Vereesa. Take care of yourself."

Jace acknowledged her. He'd been about to say something meaningless and polite, like "take care of yourself too," but that was when he caught sight of Celebras's antler hanging from Mosa's belt.

"Listen. Head north to Moonglade, to Mount Hyjal… Mosa, seek out Celebras and his kin, the keepers of the grove. They are the teachers of druids. Learn more magic from them, and help your people. They will know you are a descendant of nature worshippers, and that you aided Zaetar's spirit in Maraudon — they will welcome your coming."

"Also, trust your own instincts. Seek out allies among the other tauren tribes, Mosa. Don't let your people fight as scattered individuals."

Mosa paused, seeming to digest what Jace had said, then replied, "I've remembered all of it."

"I'm usually right, aren't I?" Jace said, then turned and followed Grede and Vereesa.

The tauren remained alone on the beach, nodding thoughtfully to herself.
