Chapter 615: Farewell at Booty Bay

The Sailor's Home Inn was as lively as ever that night. When Jace got back, he spotted Grede waving at him from a corner across the room. He tried to squeeze through the crowd to get over, but the moment he started pushing his way in, the people around him glanced over — with a distinct hint of disgust.

Had they sensed the fel energy on him?

Ever since leaving Eldre'Thalas, Jace had tried using the Flowing Ice Crystal several times to purge the fel blood from his system, but the results hadn't been great.

He didn't feel nauseous or uncomfortable much anymore, but every now and then he could still feel the fel energy lurking in his body, tugging at his emotions.

He had a bad feeling about this — that this fel energy might end up coexisting with him just like the black dragon blood. Was he getting less and less human by the day?

Just like the fel-corrupted blood elves: eyes turning green, skin darkening…

Or maybe it was just that he'd gone too long at sea without bathing or washing his clothes, and his body odor had gotten genuinely offensive. After all, there were real demons here — could his fel and shadow aura possibly be worse than the real thing?

"How'd it go?" Jace squeezed in and sat down across from Grede, looking at the elf opposite him. "You went and asked — how much is the Reliquary planning to pay?"

"Lady Gellei Lightchaser can't inspect a lead of this caliber for us, Jace," Vereesa said.

"Gellei Lightchaser?" Jace said. "That's a woman? I thought the examiner here in Booty Bay was a man."

"That's her clansman," Vereesa said. "I need to take everything back to Quel'Thalas and report to the Reliquary's headquarters in person. She can't even tell us how much the Reliquary will ultimately pay us. All she can do is contact a Dragonhawk Rider immediately to get me back to Quel'Thalas."

Hearing that, Jace looked up. "So that means you're not coming back to Stormwind with us?"

"There's no helping it." Vereesa shrugged, prodding at the black bread slices on her plate with a fork. "Grede, Jace — no matter how many times it happens, I never get used to saying goodbye to you two."

"Neither do I," Grede said, chin in his hand.

"Same here," Jace said.

"As do I," Molphis put on a sorrowful expression.

"Don't ruin the mood, demon," Vereesa said coldly.

Molphis smiled. "You'd better watch your little life from now on, Quel'dorei. Don't let some troll skin you alive — I won't be around to protect you."

"I don't need you to worry about me at all."

After saying that, Vereesa stared at the succubus for a moment longer, then said, "I know what you and these two demons plan to do with those scrolls, Jace…"

"What?" Jace asked. "Why bring that up all of a sudden?"

The ranger kept her eyes on Jace. "You've been memorizing those incantations the whole way — getting familiar with the grammar, the pronunciation, that Kaldorei demon-binding circle. I know what you're planning. You're trying to secretly summon a Burning Legion Ered'ruin and enslave it to help you decipher the contents of those scrolls, aren't you?"

"Fine. Yes," Jace admitted.

"What's on those scrolls that's worth taking that kind of risk? Does it have something to do with the necromancer?" Vereesa asked.

Jace looked at Vereesa and Grede. He had to watch out for the goblin collector, be wary of the Reliquary, even be careful of the Kirin Tor — was he supposed to keep his own friends who'd been through life and death with him in the dark too?

"Yes. I think there might be more clues about the necromancer on the scrolls," Jace said.

Grede's eyes went wider and wider as he listened. "You're planning to go by yourself?"

"I haven't even decided to go yet," Jace said honestly.

"But you don't want the Reliquary to know about this," Vereesa said.

"That's right," Jace said. "Because I suspect Ulthalesh is in that demon's hands."

"Who's Ulthalesh?" Grede asked. "Did I miss something?"

"Molphis mentioned it — it's a demonic artifact that can harvest souls," Vereesa said.

Molphis said, "Master, didn't you say that thing might not be on Azeroth?"

Jace glanced around to make sure no one was paying attention to them, then said quietly, "I don't know either, but I have a feeling. Think about it — I think the satyr summoned Dath'mora for this very reason, and those black-robed men might have been after it too. Grede, those guys are probably Medivh's treasure hunters. What could be worth Medivh's undead servants crossing Kalimdor to break into Eldre'Thalas? At this point, you still think my guess has no basis at all?"

"Anyway, I won't be able to rest until I get to the bottom of this. So I'm planning to summon a doomguard I can control, and then figure out whether Ulthalesh is or isn't on Azeroth."

"I'm not doubting you, Jace. It's just — why not notify the Reliquary?" Vereesa asked.

"Because that thing would be extremely dangerous no matter who got their hands on it," Jace explained. "If it really is on Azeroth, the safest outcome is for it to never be found by anyone."

"Including you?" Vereesa asked.

"Including me, of course," Jace said. "So many powerful demons and warlocks have been destroyed by that thing — what makes me think I'd be any better?" He wasn't lying to the two of them. The danger posed to Ulthalesh really was beyond what an ordinary person could imagine.

Honestly, even now, Jace was still hesitating over whether he should dig into any of this at all. If Vereesa hadn't suddenly asked, he probably never would have brought it up with them — he'd have just pretended none of it happened.

"Since that's the case, I'll keep this secret for you, Jace," Vereesa said, lowering her head. "But I also have a favor to ask — don't tell anyone else, either of you. If I find out a third person knows…"

"You'll go straight to the Reliquary with the whole Ulthalesh thing," Jace said.

"No," Vereesa said. "But I won't ever be able to trust you again."

Grede, who'd been drawn in by the Ulthalesh story, immediately shifted his attention to Vereesa. "That's threat enough. Go on," Jace said.

"If you really manage to summon that Burning Legion demon," Vereesa paused, swallowing hard, "remember to ask it for me — is Alleria Windrunner alive or dead. Is that too much to ask?"

"It's not too much," Jace nodded. "I'll ask for you. But don't get your hopes up."

"I know," Vereesa said.

The next morning, before the merchant ship bound for Stormwind, Molphis and Sazeno had already boarded early and found a place to hide.

Vereesa said, "After I get the bounty from the Reliquary, I'll have someone send the package by teleportation. Same address as last time, right?"

"Remember to change the name to Yurk," Jace said. "And find a mage you trust to teleport it — this time it's definitely not a small sum."

"I know," Vereesa nodded. "I'm not going to lose your money."

Grede smiled and said, "A Quel'Thalas mage wouldn't risk offending House Windrunner over money, I'd think."

"I'd think so too," Jace said.

"Next time there's something to do, remember to find me, Jace," Vereesa said. "You too, Grede."

"Of course," Grede said. "Where else are you going to find a fighter this good who doesn't ask for a cut?"

Vereesa smiled. "Then that's that."

"We're off, little elf," Grede spread his arms. Vereesa bent down and gave the dwarf a gentle hug, then looked at Jace.

She pulled her hands back slightly, rose up on her toes a little, and Jace walked over and hugged her. Vereesa buried her head in Jace's shoulder and wrapped her arms tight around his waist.

"We're going, Wen."

"Take care, Jace," the ranger murmured into his shoulder.

"You too."

Vereesa nodded and slowly let out a breath.

Grede leaned on the ship's railing waiting for Jace to come over. When he got there, Grede said, "I'm telling you, Vereesa Windrunner must really like you, Jace Sesso."

"Why do you always spring that on me out of nowhere?"

Jace asked it leaning back against the railing. He tilted his head back slightly and glanced down at an angle, but from this position he couldn't see where the ranger was standing below.

Grede said, "Have a good sniff of yourself. You smell like a dead salted fish. I don't get it — we've all gone about as long without a bath, so why do you have to smell so uniquely awful? If it were me, I'd rather get stomped to death by the Earth-Wardener than hug you."

"You smell like a pile of shit. You're just used to your own stink," Jace said.

Grede sniffed his own collar. "Really? Don't be so blunt about it… let me smell again…"
