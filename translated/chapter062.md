## Chapter 62: Effective Communication

Despite the torrential rain, the tiny imp — no bigger than a cat at this distance — stood wreathed in demon-fire and shrieked, "Behold the power of the pact… this is our magic, the terrible power that exists only between the bound! Master, as long as I live… you don't get to die first!"

The Blood Pact — so that was the Blood Pact? He could cast this spell, and he'd never mentioned it?

The orc stared toward the imp. At this distance Sazeno was barely the size of a bug, while the orc was a giant. One stomp and the imp would be paste.

The imp shrieked out a demonic incantation, sparks already crackling from his hands. The orc lunged to close the distance and swung a fist — but before he reached the imp, flames suddenly erupted across his whole body.

Immolate?

This little imp could cast Immolate?

"You can't judge this world by the game…" Jace told himself, darting forward to grab the Stromgarde sword the orc had tossed aside, still slick with orcish blood.

The orc didn't panic at the flames engulfing him. His expression cold, he shook both arms — and the fire blew out as if scattered by a gust of wind.

Jace seized the opening and drove the blade into the orc's side — but the orc caught the edge with lightning reflexes, gripping the blade with his fingers and forcing it deeper himself!

The sheer madness of it stunned Jace for a second. The orc turned and glared at him viciously, red blood seeping from his palm.

His skin was green, but his blood was red. Not demon blood.

The orc muttered something in contempt, then let out a cold laugh — and Jace somehow knew exactly what he meant.

Because buried in that sentence was one orcish word Jace understood: "little thing."

That's right… orcish. Jace did know a little orcish. How had he not thought of it in the middle of that brawl?

Think. What could he say to stop this orc?

The shaman held the blade fast so Jace couldn't pull back. This time Jace didn't want to let go — lose this weapon and he had no way to fight at all.

The orc smashed the flat of the blade with his bracer. The sword shuddered violently. It didn't snap, but it bent, and the impact nearly tore it from Jace's grip.

He roared and wrenched the blade free, spinning around. The orc in front of him was already doubling — no, tripling. Afterimages.

The string of Fear spells had left his mind scattered, but the orc wasn't pressing the attack either. Blood was flowing freely from his waist and calf, and the repeated elemental summonings had taken their toll.

"I'll kill you!" the imp screamed, flames igniting in both hands. The orc whipped around toward him. Jace tried to draw the orc's attention, but his foot slipped on the wet ground before he could even take a step.

A fireball sailed from the imp's hands and struck the orc's neck — which was thicker than Jace's waist. The orc barely flinched, brushed the flames from his hair, and kept staring at the imp.

Now Sazeno was getting nervous.

"Uh… keep attacking!"

He kept gathering fire magic — when a burst of wind erupted from the orc's hand. The gale caught the falling rain and made it visible, a shockwave of wind and water that slammed into Sazeno. The imp yelped and went down.

After unleashing the wind, the orc coughed several times and dropped to one knee, clutching his side and glaring viciously in their direction.

Jace glared back with every ounce of menace he could muster.

Then the orc pulled a crudely made stone knife from beside his foot, its carved markings still fresh and obvious.

Gritting his teeth, he forced himself up and limped toward them, coughing with every step. This time he was really fighting with everything he had left.

Jace backed off a few steps. He could probably run. The orc could barely move.

But what about Grede? He glanced sideways. The dwarf was still lying there, motionless. Damn it…

His brain churned like stirred paste for a long moment — and then an idea hit him.

"Frostwolf."

He shouted it in orcish. The clan names were the kind of thing that showed up in orcish dictionaries.

Hearing his own language out of nowhere, the orc frowned in confusion.

"Frostwolf," Jace repeated.

The orc slowly lowered his knife and muttered something.

Jace didn't understand a word, but he knew the idea was working.

He'd shouted "Frostwolf" because among the orc clans that had come through the Dark Portal, the Frostwolf clan was apparently the only one still following the shamanic path. Every other clan's shamans had joined Gul'dan's shadow.

Jace pointed to his own chest and said in halting orcish, "Know. Frostwolf."

The viciousness in the orc's expression faded somewhat. He lowered his head, thought for a moment, and said something else in orcish. Jace still had no idea what it meant.

"Know. Frostwolf. Alive," Jace tried, stringing together the limited vocabulary he had. The orc's expression finally changed.

He was silent for a moment, then asked in Common, "Where?"

"You speak Common?" Jace asked, surprised.

"Common," the orc said. "Know. Little."

A little was better than nothing.

"Alterac. Frostwolf," Jace said quickly.

"Alterac." The orc nodded. "Know Alterac. North."

He pointed north. "Right?"

"Right, Alterac is in the north," Jace said.

The orc turned to look at the northern mountains, and his expression turned vicious again. He shook his head. "Human. Liar. Not trust."

He'd guessed Jace might be trying to lead him away.

He needed real proof. Jace tried pronouncing it the English way: "Drek'Thar."

Drek'Thar was the leader who'd led the Frostwolf clan to survive in the snowy mountains of Alterac. If this orc was a Frostwolf, he should definitely know Drek'Thar.

Dropping that name might make him trust what Jace was saying.

But after hearing the name, the orc stiffly repeated the pronunciation, simply mimicking the English rendering with a blank look on his face. He clearly had no idea what it meant. Maybe Drek'Thar's name in orcish sounded nothing like the English or Chinese versions, so he couldn't make the connection.

But the orc no longer wanted to kill him. Jace knew he'd found the key. This was probably a Frostwolf orc who couldn't find his people — or if not a Frostwolf, then a wanderer searching for others who'd returned to the shamanic path.

One more name… think of another name, fast. The orc could lose patience any second.
