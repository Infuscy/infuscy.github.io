Chapter 623: The Stormwind Registry

The next day, Jace met up with Grede and headed to the registry at Stormwind's main gate. Jace remembered that in the game this place was called the Census Office — where you formed guilds.

Exactly when it had been renamed, Jace wasn't too sure. If he had to guess, it was probably after the Kingdom of Lordaeron was destroyed and massive numbers of people migrated here.

Early in the morning there was already a crowd packed at the entrance — though that was normal for here. Every time Jace came he saw plenty of people.

When Stormwind was being rebuilt, this registry had been built to handle a city of only tens of thousands. But Stormwind's population had clearly grown much larger since then, and plenty of day-to-day matters — especially miscellaneous affairs like housing transactions — still had to be handled by a few clerks in this small two-story building. It was extremely cramped.

"What happened to your neck?" Grede asked, pointing.

Jace touched his neck — and it hurt. He felt a long scratch.

He'd taken a careful shower last night, washed all his clothes, changed into fresh ones, made sure there wasn't too much of the succubus's scent left on him — and yet there was still a wound he hadn't noticed.

"I got careless while handling Vilethorn's demonic sigils with Molphis last night," Jace admitted.

"Mm-hmm. I believe you," Grede nodded with exaggerated earnestness.

Jace shot him a sideways glance, then gave up and rested his hands on his hips — only to press on a wound on his waist and wince.

Yesterday, to carve those complicated sigils, Jace had burned through one reserves of energy, then drained what was left after drinking a mana potion. If not for the Flowing Ice Crystal nourishing him through the night, he might not even have had the strength to walk here this morning.

After waiting in line for two or three hours they finally got inside. The room reeked of summer sweat. A clerk with a thick beard sat before them — wearing an extremely thin shirt that was still soaked through with sweat. He set aside a stack of letters, didn't even look up, and said, "How may I help you, gentlemen?"

"I'd like to buy a house," Jace said.

The clerk glanced at the small chair beside him, then at the dwarf, then at Jace. "You're looking to rent?"

"Buy a house," Jace repeated.

"You want to buy a house in Stormwind, sir? Are you coming from Ironforge? You know the paperwork for a dwarf buying a house — even in the Dwarf District — is quite troublesome…"

"Not me," Grede pointed at Jace. "Him."

The clerk glanced behind Jace, then said, "Judging by your accent, you're Lordaeronian, correct? Buying your own house in Stormwind isn't a simple goal. If you've only just arrived here, I'd suggest looking into renting first to get settled. I can help with that too."

Jace pulled out the Wizard Sanctum's insignia. "Yurk. I'm a mage of the Wizard Sanctum. Here's my sigil."

The clerk took the badge and examined it. "Well, my apologies, mage. You're terribly young. You know, there are plenty of young people these days who come to Stormwind from all over knowing nothing about the living situation here, carrying only a gold or two in their pockets — or even just ten or twenty silver. And you're even younger than them."

As he spoke, he pulled his chair aside and took out a thick, rough ledger from beside him. "I'd love for all of them to find a place of their own within the walls, but the reality is it's incredibly difficult."

Jace and Grede exchanged a glance and said nothing.

The clerk opened the ledger as he spoke. "Here we have some old houses and small dwellings for sale in Old Town and the Dwarf District, plus standalone houses around Stormwind and in Goldshire and Eastvale. These are all relatively cheap, and the paperwork's straightforward — I can help you contact the owners directly for a transaction."

"Then there are the new houses in the Trade District and some higher-quality homes in Old Town. Most of these belong to nobles, merchants, or craftsmen — warehouses, unused shops, or residences belonging to their relatives, for instance. Some are Church property. In any case, they can all be converted into dwellings or whatever other purpose you want. That costs money too. Just contacting them is a hassle, but fortunately some have hired agents — I can help you find those as well, though it'll definitely take more time and cost considerably more."

"What are the rough prices for each?" Grede asked.

The clerk said, "The first category runs from a few gold to about a gold or two — and of course that doesn't include renovation costs. For renovation prices you can ask around Old Town or Eastvale. The second category starts at thirty or forty gold for the cheap ones, and the expensive ones — who knows. If you're rich enough, the king might sell you the whole Stormwind Keep for all I know."

He finished with a smile. Grede asked curiously, "How much is Stormwind Keep?" The clerk's smile vanished and his face darkened instantly.

"Never mind I asked," Grede said.

After listening to the clerk's whole spiel, Jace was starting to regret not coming to test the waters sooner.

"I'm looking to buy a house in the Trade District, sir," he said, recalling the layout of his parents' house back in Lordaeron. "Preferably with two floors, by the canal, and close to the market."

"You mean the kind with a stable on the lower level, right?" the clerk asked. "Yes — one that can keep a horse would be ideal," Jace added.

Thinking that, he wondered if Arna still remembered him. If he got the chance, he'd have to find the king and ask to get that horse back. "Houses like that start at around sixty gold, young man," the clerk said.

"See, Jace?" Grede leaned in and whispered. "The prices I asked about were about the same."

Jace nodded and said, "If possible, could I get a chance to see these houses in person?"

Seeing that Jace was genuinely planning to buy a place, the clerk raised his eyebrows slightly. "Let me take down your name and contact details, and your current address. I can connect you with an agent — he'll take you to view the houses, then contact craftsmen to inspect the property's condition, and put together a renovation estimate based on your requirements… Though I should tell you upfront, all of this comes with fees. The craftsmen are professionals."

Jace gave a stiff nod and wrote the name "Yurk" and his current rental address on the little brown slip of paper the clerk put out.

"Roughly when would the appointment be?"

"The day after tomorrow, in the afternoon — how's that?"

"Fine," Jace said.

They finally made it out of the registry, and both of them let out a long sigh at the same time.

A cool breeze blew past. Jace rubbed his eyes and said, "Is buying a house in Aerie Peak this much of a pain too?"

"That cave was carved out by my dad a hundred years ago, together with the Thunderfists." Grede rapped his knuckles against a nearby hitching post. "Who'd dare charge money?"

"Impressive," Jace said.
