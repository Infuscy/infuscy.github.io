Chapter 24: A Warlock's Lie

With the appointment with the agent set, Jace didn't have enough free time in the next day or two to go anywhere too far. Heading back to Moonbrook to look for Mosul was out of the question.

So today he planned to find a fairly secluded spot in Elwynn Forest and try out the incantations he'd brought back from Kalimdor.

Dath'mora's purple demon bag was clearly much sturdier and more durable than the warlock's green bag he'd picked up from the orc in Duskwood two years ago. Jace couldn't tell what kind of hide it was stitched from, but the fact that it had survived this long inside an Ered'ruin's stomach with its surface still intact — not even the patterns damaged — meant it was definitely no ordinary item.

This time he'd stuffed the soul shard bag, the bag holding the kodo-hide incantation scrolls, and two mana potions Ursula had given him all into it, had Sazeno hide inside to watch over everything, and brought Molphis with him to Goldshire the afternoon of his registry visit.

After finishing the sigils on Molphis in the basement, her increased height hadn't been an illusion. Comparing heights in the room, Jace felt she was already about a meter seventy — meaning she was roughly the same height as Vereesa.

But Jace was already about a meter eighty now, so overall she was still a bit shorter. It was just that her slender, toned figure, the long legs above those reverse-jointed ankles and hooves, made her appear quite tall. Jace, who was already fairly lean with decent proportions for an ordinary human, still looked like some savage who'd jumped out of nowhere next to Molphis.

Add in the visual effect of the glowing sigil flames lighting up her whole body in the basement, and Jace had the illusion that her build was especially intimidating. Still, even at a meter seventy, she'd grown quite a bit compared to when she'd first started.

Jace still remembered that in the game, as a character's level increased, their pet's size would grow too — whether it was a hunter's beast or a warlock's minion.

Although here Molphis didn't automatically grow as his strength increased, Jace clearly remembered that the male succubus Jagin summoned was noticeably shorter than the one Sulenna summoned, Jakres. Even back then he'd felt that the size difference between sayaads more or less reflected the gap in their power.

Molphis was clearly stronger now than when she'd first left Stormwind for Kalimdor. The moment she casually turned invisible, Jace had a hard time clearly sensing her presence through Detect Demon — there was only a faint, ghostly shimmer.

Of course, that also gave Jace a bit of a crisis.

In the Eldre'Thalas dungeons, his Detect Demon ability had failed to detect a true demon for the first time — but that was because Dath'mora had used a rather impressive disguise. Now Molphis was challenging his ability too, and that made him a bit uncomfortable.

Leaving the Trade District and rounding the inner gate into the Valley of Heroes, Jace was startled by the towering armored knight statue before him.

He'd gotten used to walking this road, used to it being empty, always looking like it had just been through a war. He'd almost forgotten about the Valley of Heroes from the game.

Before him stood the statue of Turalyon, leader of the Alliance Expedition — it felt far taller than in the game.

Jace's head didn't even reach the soles of his feet. He had to walk a long way toward the bridge before he could take in the full figure.

Turalyon's statue looked quite young, with a head of gilded long hair. One hand held a sword to his chest; the other held a heavy tome at his waist. His head was slightly lowered, eyes tightly shut, as if deep in thought.

Apart from him, the other pedestals were still empty — but even this single statue was enough to anchor the entire Valley of Heroes's presence.

As a warlock, staring at the Holy Light sigils covering Marshal Turalyon's armor one by one, Jace felt uncomfortable for some reason. Even after walking far enough to reach the outer gate and looking back, he felt like Turalyon's statue was warning him of something.

The pedestal to the west of the statue was probably where Alleria Windrunner's statue would be placed — Turalyon's wife, Vereesa's eldest sister.

Thinking of it that way, if he ever found a way to meet Turalyon in the future, that would count as knowing someone. At that thought, Jace couldn't help but raise his eyebrows.

When he'd first transmigrated here, he'd never imagined he'd have the chance to know someone like Turalyon — and that included Sylvanas Windrunner, Ranger-General of Silvermoon.

Without realizing it, he'd already come this far.

Once outside the walls, Jace led the two demons a ways toward Goldshire, then left the main road and headed deep into the forest.

At that point Molphis sensed they were sufficiently safe and stepped out of invisibility. Jace glanced at her — the slightly taller frame, the sigils covering her upper body, the somewhat thinner cheeks. Though no single change was that dramatic, taken together it felt like she was a different demon.

"Try charming me, Molphis. Let me see how much your power's actually grown," Jace said, looking at her. "Charm me, then ask me any question you like and see if I answer honestly. Remember — no matter what I answer, drop the spell after."

"Understood, master." The succubus cocked a confident smirk, reached out, and snapped her fingers in front of Jace's eyes. Jace instantly felt a wave of heat surge from his heart to his brain — like a sudden rush of excitement, followed by a clear, unmistakable racing of his pulse…

He'd almost forgotten what being suddenly charmed felt like. Only after this chain of sensations did he realize — yes, this was the taste of charm magic. He should have sensed it immediately…

But by the time he snapped back to his senses, the succubus seemed to have already finished asking her question. What had she asked?

"Who matters most to you, master?" Jace glanced at her. "Naturally… you."

He smiled as he said it. Molphis's expression immediately went cold.

The magical influence was released all at once. Jace suddenly felt himself freed from an almost suffocating mental pressure, gasping for air in great lungfuls. He had to admit, Molphis's mental strength had grown considerably — and this was right after the sigils were carved. Once she got used to this power, it should improve even further.

He gave himself a moment to calm down, tried to drive Molphis's mental control from his mind, then asked, "What, not happy with that answer?"

Molphis said in a low voice, "An easily seen-through lie. You're provoking me."

Jace smiled. "I've always wanted to ask — is it really that easy to tell when I'm lying?"

"Sometimes it's easy. Sometimes… it's hard to say," Molphis said with a hint of smugness in her tone. "But you've learned to manipulate your emotions — to use them to cover your lies, master."

"Fine, we'll talk about that another time. Next I'm going to try the Death Coil incantation," Jace said, untying the straps of his pack. "Starting with your soul."

"Wait, why?" Molphis said. "Just to test it? I mean, what if you succeed on the first try…" Jace looked at her slightly panicked, flickering gaze and cut her off. "How was that? Any improvement?"

The succubus blinked, and the fel fire in her eyes steadied bit by bit.

"Much better, master," she said with a hint of reluctance.
