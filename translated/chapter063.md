## Chapter 63: The Wanderer

Thrall?

Thrall was the son of the Frostwolf clan's former chieftain, and he'd go on to become the Warchief of the entire Horde — but he wasn't even ten years old yet. Who'd know him?

And "Thrall" was a human-given name, a Common word. No orc would know it.

His real name was Go'el, but that was a name his parents had given him in private. How many Frostwolf orcs would have heard it this early?

That's it — Thrall's father, the former chieftain of the Frostwolf clan, Durotan!

Jace immediately ran through several variations of "Durotan," and this time the orc understood.

"Durotan… Durotan!"

Fortunately the simple name was close enough across orcish, English, and Chinese. Jace pressed the advantage: "Durotan's son. Alive!"

"Durotan's son." The orc froze, then said quietly after a moment, "Drek'Thar. I understand Drek'Thar."

So the orc just hadn't realized it was a name. In his orcish pronunciation of Drek'Thar, the stress fell heavily on "Thar," emphasized strongly, while the first part "Drek" was spoken lightly and quickly, almost like a simple prefix. Jace had stressed it exactly the opposite way, so of course the other hadn't caught on. For all Jace knew, his pronunciation of "Drek'Thar" meant something else entirely in orcish.

At least it probably wasn't a swear.

"Durotan's son. Where?" the orc asked.

"Durotan's son. Durnholde," Jace said.

He scratched a rough outline of the Eastern Kingdoms into the sand with his staff and pointed to the two spots, roughly in the same place: "Alterac Valley. Durnholde."

"Durn-hold…" The orc tossed his dagger to the ground and raised both hands, signaling that he wanted to come closer, no threat.

But Jace knew this guy could kill him with his bare hands even without a weapon. Tossing the dagger didn't mean much.

Still, at this point there wasn't much choice. It wasn't like he could chop the orc's hands off.

The orc dragged his wounded body closer, studied the crude map Jace had drawn, and slowly nodded.

They were close enough now that Jace could clearly smell the demonic sulfur stench on the orc — the rain hadn't washed it off in the slightest. That was the smell of an orc who'd drunk the demon blood.

Then the orc looked up and pointed at Jace's staff. "Targon."

Just as Jace had guessed — every orc knew this staff belonged to Teron Gorefiend.

He tossed the staff aside, then bent down and picked it back up, saying in orcish, "Forest."

Then he pointed toward the distant southeast, in the direction of Elwynn. The orc nodded as if he understood, then looked over at the imp, who'd just sat up and was coughing nonstop.

"Not trust. Warlock. Demon."

"Not trust" was in Common, while "warlock" and "demon" were in orcish. Jace understood all of it, for once.

Jace pointed to the bulging veins on the orc's arm. "Demon. Alive," he said in orcish.

Then he pointed to his own chest. "Warlock. Alive."

The orc's expression immediately became incredibly complex. After a long moment, he sighed. "Human. Alive," in Common.

With that, he turned and limped off into the distance, until his figure was swallowed by the rain and the shadows of the forest.

Some orc shamans had chosen to abandon orcish tradition and the shamanic path, drinking the demon blood on Gul'dan's orders to become servants of the demons — not because they yearned for fel power or the Burning Legion.

They were afraid that defying Gul'dan and his master Kil'jaeden would lead to their entire clan being wiped out. That had happened more than once on Draenor.

And Jace had learned warlock magic for the same reason — to survive in this world.

The orc had understood that. Jace let out a long breath, grateful to have scraped out of this alive.

At least he'd gotten rid of the brute. Whether he'd get captured by the lord of Durnholde and forced into the gladiator pits, find the Frostwolf clan, or freeze to death in the snows of Alterac — that was up to fate.

More likely, he'd get caught by a dwarf patrol in Khaz Modan halfway there and sent home.

Sazeno came bouncing up to Jace's side. "The monster actually left! Where did the monster want to go?"

"Who cares where he went. Anywhere's fine, as long as he doesn't come back for me."

Now that he'd relaxed, Jace suddenly felt the pain left by that terrifying lightning coursing through his body. Grede still hadn't woken up.

"Right." He looked at the imp. "You… do you know any healing magic?"

"Absolutely not!"

He said it like he was thrilled about it.

"Then how about you go grab my bag for me?" Jace pointed in the direction the orc had gone. "It's over there!"

"Got it!" The imp darted off in a bounce, leaving a trail of flames from his eyes flickering behind him.

He collapsed next to Grede, lifting his arm to check the dwarf's breathing, see if he was still alive.

But he could see the dwarf's mustache fluttering with each breath, and his arm was too sore from the lightning to bother moving.

He didn't know how much time passed. Jace lay on his back on the lake shore, half-asleep, before Sazeno finally dragged the bag back.

Jace lay face-up, raindrops pelting his face.

"Thanks, demon. You played dead for most of that, but that one hit earlier — I'd be dead without you."

"Ha, thanks! Ha, thanks! What does that mean?" The imp kept repeating "thanks" while bouncing around like he'd been injected with adrenaline.

Jace had no idea where he got the energy. He himself was completely spent.

But he couldn't fall asleep. Stay here and the two lives he'd just fought for might get casually collected by the murlocs around Lake Everstill or some other monster.

Jace roared at himself to get moving, swung his arms, and sat up. His body didn't hurt quite as much as before.

He hauled the dwarf up and slung him over his shoulder — nearly getting crushed flat in the process.

The guy weighed close to two hundred pounds. In his past life, Jace wouldn't have dreamed of being able to lift him.

But in Azeroth he hauled heavy loads for a living. He'd carried things this heavy before. Just find that feeling again. One step… one step…

He walked and rested and walked again — he didn't know for how long. His mind had gone numb.

He didn't know how far he'd gone or where he was. The rain had swallowed everything in sight. He just knew to follow the road.

The rain kept falling. Everything he could see, everything he wore, everything he carried — soaked through.

Then, ahead, a light flickered.

Lakeshire militia patrols!

"Is anyone there?" a militiaman shouted from a distance. "Is anyone hurt?"

"Yes! Someone's hurt!" Jace shouted with what strength he had left. "Come help!"

Several militiamen ran over and helped carry the dwarf. Jace suddenly remembered the imp behind him, his heart pounding at the thought of the militia spotting it — but when he turned around, the imp had already vanished.

He found that the clasp on his bag had been undone at some point, the cord swinging back and forth. So the imp still remembered the order to disappear whenever others were around. Good.

Jace sat down with a sigh of relief. Several militiamen helped him up. He watched them carry Grede away, his heart clenching. He didn't know if that had been a Lightning Bolt or a Chain Lightning. Either way, he had no idea how powerful it was. He knew nothing about shaman magic. He didn't know if Grede could pull through.
