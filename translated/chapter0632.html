Chapter 632: Until the End of the World

So this was the elven kingdom of Quel'Thalas?

A wave of pain made Jace wince. He'd never been here before, yet it felt strangely familiar — he could even point directly toward where Silvermoon lay…

Yes, what he remembered from his past life… had he gotten old enough to nearly overlook even this? In any case, he needed to get out of this stream that was slowly destroying his legs.

Jace tried to shuffle step by step toward the bank. The numbness in his feet made the less-than-ten-meter distance feel like a long expedition. He let out a long breath and slowly sat on the ground, straining to recall everything.

Which part was real?

This place, or… damn it, he couldn't remember anything that had happened before arriving here at all. Had he zoned out again? Some bizarre dream?

Which side was the dream?

If that side was the dream, then what about now?

Jace heard Vereesa's laughter in the distance, which reminded him of Kalimor. Before he knew it, he'd hugged his skeletal legs tight.

Lifespan angst, huh? You bastard.

Jace grabbed the fish trap beside him and tried to stand sharply — only to feel a terrible, spine-snapping pain in his lower back. He lost his balance and toppled forward into the stream…

At that moment, a powerful hand caught his right arm and steadied him. "Sesso."

That voice was familiar too.

Jace turned his head and saw a man.

He was tall and slender, with a handsome face… no, "handsome" wasn't quite right.

Jace felt he'd never seen such a beautiful man — except in dreams.

Wait, that's right. This was the handsome man who'd appeared in that Northrend snow dream.

Why was he here? Jace felt like it had been a long time since he'd seen him in that strange dream.

"She can't hide her grief. You must see it more clearly than I do." The man's voice was low and deeply persuasive, leaving Jace no choice but to agree.

Vereesa's smile was different from the one he knew.

But he seriously doubted whether this was the so-called grief she couldn't hide, or whether that wasn't really Vereesa Windrunner at all. Jace braced his waist and steadied himself for a long moment before asking, "Who are you?"

The man said, "We don't need to beat around the bush. You know exactly who I am." Deathwing — Jace had guessed correctly.

This man before him was Deathwing's disguise in the Alliance court: Lord Daval Prestor, a noble of Alterac.

Facing the Father of Black Dragons, Jace felt he should be terrified, should feel fear, should feel despair. His knees should have been knocking, his voice failing him…

But he just looked at the man before him, surprised even himself by how calm he was.

Perhaps it was because he disliked being toyed with. Or perhaps compared to the situation he was facing right now, even Deathwing himself wasn't that frightening.

"What do you want?" Jace asked.

"The question is what you intend to do, Sesso." The man gazed toward a distant garden. "Did you want things to come to this? Saving Azeroth, yet unable to save yourself and the ones you love."

Jace was silent for a moment. "No."

"Then listen to me." The man turned his head back. "Krasus has concealed much from you, but I will be frank with you."

"I didn't go well in Draenor. I was forced to withdraw to Azeroth. Now I must hide myself and seek other opportunities to protect and strengthen my flight, Sesso. But everyone thinks I've failed — even died — including those who once claimed to serve me and follow me."

"Yet the Dragonmaw orc leader Nek'rosh still holds certain important artifacts. One of them is mine — this artifact is the key to controlling Alexstrasza. Without it, the Dragonmaw orcs in Grim Batol cannot withstand her wrath."

The Demon Soul, Jace thought. Or the Dragon Soul.

Of course, this artifact that had once been hoped to destroy the Burning Legion's invasion during the War of the Ancient had almost never been used for its original purpose. By now, no one was willing to call this evil-corrupted abomination the "Dragon Soul" anymore.

At that, the man's voice suddenly dropped.

"My children tell me that ever since the Dark Portal closed, Nek'rosh has believed I was dead. This artifact rightfully belongs to him, and the vast number of dragon eggs he originally promised me have all been wasted. This is unforgably foolish — an unforgivable, shameful betrayal."

"I won't have you openly oppose the red dragons or the Alliance. That wouldn't be fair to you. You only need to go deep into Khaz Modan, kill this traitor, seize the artifact, and after taking control of the Dragonmaw clan, have the orcs send out the dragon eggs Alexstrasza has laid from Grim Batol, hiding them together with that holy relic deep in the mountains to the south."

"This will make you a hero of the Alliance — think about it. Single-handedly destroying the Dragonmaw orcs' stronghold, resolving the dragonrider threat, permanently lifting the shadow that has long hung over the Kirin Tor and the entire Alliance."

"Not only that — you only need to do this much, and I will grant you a lifespan beyond your current imagination, letting you stay with the ones you love until the end of the world."

Hearing those words, Jace felt his heart skip a beat — even the stream seemed to stop flowing. "What about the Dragonqueen? You want me to refuse Krasus?" he asked.

"No," the man said. "You should accept him. He has a very peculiar item — with it, you'll have a much easier time entering Grim Batol and controlling those pitiful orcs. That's convenient for both of us. As for what becomes of dear Alexstrasza's fate — frankly, I don't care."

"If he discovers I'm working with you, he'll kill me," Jace said.

"His attention is on me right now. That's not something you should worry about." The man looked Jace straight in the eyes and smiled. "It seems you're already planning to work with me?"

Jace realized he couldn't refuse this offer.

Vereesa's smile flickered through his mind, alongside that ugly old face that could be her grandfather — these images twisted into a claw of violent desire, dragging him into this step, into the great web Deathwing had woven for him…

He of course knew what his ending might be, but it seemed that apart from the posture with which he fell in, there was simply nothing else to choose.

Could he refuse Deathwing?

"Can I really live as long as an elf?" Jace asked.

"Elves?" The man smiled. "Self-proclaimed mortals calling themselves eternal is laughable, Sesso. You will become immortal."

"I understand," Jace said.

"You truly are an unusual person. Krasus has made many mistakes, but at least he found you." As he spoke, the man stepped forward and gripped Jace's frail arm.

Jace lowered his head and saw a black dragon claw carving a smoky, blood-seeping sigil into his skin.

"This magical mark allows me to hear your voice. If you encounter difficulty, I won't stand idly by — after all, the fate of my future brood rests in my hands. It's just that I can't attract too much attention right now, so I can't appear directly. I hope you understand my difficulty."

"How did you find me?" Jace asked.

The man said, "In any case, your body flows with my blood, Sesso."

The moment the words fell, a thick black shadow devoured everything. Jace felt as though the ground beneath him had suddenly vanished. His whole body shuddered and he bolted upright.

Sunlight was already streaming through the window. The grey-white Stormwind prison tower still stood beneath the blue sky. Was it already morning?

It had just been a dream.

He swallowed. The terror of the dream still lingered in his heart, and his forehead was already beaded with sweat. He didn't know whether he was afraid of Deathwing in the dream, or afraid that day would really come.

Wait — Vereesa hadn't even said anything yet, and he'd already dreamed of marrying her and growing old together. He couldn't help but laugh at his own self-importance.

He reached up to touch his damp forehead, but from the corner of his eye caught sight of a faint black sigil on his arm. This was real. What had happened in the dream was real.

Deathwing could hear his voice. But honestly, Jace wasn't surprised at all.

What had he been hoping for? That it was just an ordinary dream?

This sigil was definitely some form of surveillance. Deathwing wanted to ensure his loyalty.

Just sound?

Could it possibly read his thoughts, or even… his emotions?

The moment Krasus had found him, Deathwing had infiltrated his dreams that very night. How he'd learned all this, Jace had no idea.

A fierce fear gripped his throat.

No — Deathwing's magic definitely couldn't read what he was thinking. Otherwise, all that knowledge related to Warcraft from the past to the future would have been dug out long ago.

And when he'd spoken to him, his attitude certainly wouldn't have been what it was.

But that didn't change anything. Was he doomed to be monitored by Deathwing like this for the rest of his life? Still — perhaps it wouldn't last long.

He knew full well that Deathwing had no shortage of followers.

Even if he complied with Deathwing's demands and accomplished everything smoothly, the moment Deathwing proved he'd returned from his failure in Draenor, proved he was still powerful, the moment he took all the dragon eggs and that holy relic — countless dormant forces across Azeroth would cry "Father of Dragons."

And by then he'd just be an insignificant pawn, killable at any time, with no power to resist at all. Jace was gradually getting a clear picture of what he was facing. Immortality?

He absolutely could not allow himself to sink to that point. He knew what Deathwing's "end of the world" implied — this arrogant, big-mouthed madman.

Vereesa's face in his memory — beautiful as it was — was nothing more than an illusion. The real her would never allow him to become Deathwing's puppet for the sake of immortality. The thought of it brought a bitter taste surging into his heart.

Things had come to this. This was already a duel to the death.

On one side, him. On the other, the most powerful dragon that had ever existed in this world.
