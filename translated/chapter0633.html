Chapter 633: A Strange Orb

Putting on the Void Robe and stepping out the door, Jace touched the collar that had been blown open by a volley of Dath'mora's shadow bolts.

He gazed at the glittering canal, looked up at the sun, and felt the gloom of last night's nightmare had been dispersed a little.

Arriving at the Mage Quarter, he headed straight for the dwelling where the elven mages lived, but spotted Krasus at a glance in the Park.

This red dragon disguised as a High Elf was a full head taller than the average High Elf man — extremely conspicuous. Add in that blood-red robe, and it was as if he was afraid people wouldn't notice he was a tough customer.

Still, around the Wizard Sanctum he didn't attract any particular attention. After all, elven mages in outlandish outfits and bizarre getups couldn't exactly be called a common sight, but they weren't rare either.

At the moment the red-robed mage was sitting on a stone bench reading. Jace sat down beside him. "Master Krasus."

"Jace." Krasus turned a page. "Have you thought it through?" Looking at the book, Jace suddenly wondered — could Deathwing detect things from writing? Jace didn't know.

He looked at Krasus and realized that from now on, no matter what he did, he had to be as careful as possible. Deathwing couldn't read his mind — that was certain. Everything else, he couldn't be sure of.

For all he knew, the other party might even be able to see through his eyes.

If Deathwing discovered his ultimate goal was to betray him, he'd definitely find a way to end this immediately. He had no room for trial and error.

Could this sigil blow him up at any moment?

Jace realized he still didn't know enough about this sigil. That night had passed in a daze — he hadn't even had time to think it through properly.

"I've thought it through — if this is something that must be done by me."

"Thank you, Jace." The worry between Krasus's brows eased at once. He continued, "To be frank, this isn't your responsibility. But if you don't do it, I don't know where our future will lead."

He pulled a purple-red orb from his robes. "Take this."

"What is this?" Jace asked.

Was this the extraordinarily unusual item Deathwing had mentioned?

"This is an illusion orb crafted by a trusted friend of mine," Krasus explained. "Trigger its power and it will turn the user into an orc."

This definitely wasn't an Orb of Deception, was it?

The Orb of Deception was a game item that let you take on the appearance of the opposing faction — an Alliance human could become a Horde orc, a night elf could become a Forsaken, and so on.

Jace stared at the orb for a moment. Krasus said softly, "Don't use it here, Jace."

"Ah, I know."

Jace looked around. He hadn't actually planned on testing it — but he really had momentarily forgotten he was in Stormwind. If he suddenly turned into an orc here, that would really blow the lid off.

Krasus instructed, "Its mana is strong — it will transform the clothes and accessories on your body to match an orc's build. If undisturbed, it can last at least two days. When it's close to wearing off, you'll clearly feel its mana weakening — that's your cue to reactivate it."

"But because the time window to craft it was very narrow, it has a very obvious drawback: casting spells while in orc form will break the integrity of the magic and expose you. Wielding weapons and speaking are no problem, of course. And don't channel Azure Song, Jace — the arcane energy disturbance that sword puts out is stronger than an Arcane Missile. The illusion can't support that much mana."

Jace asked, "Can this fool the orc-controlled red dragons?"

Krasus smiled. "After this is done, I should introduce you to the person who made it. Rest assured — if anything, I'm more worried the orcs might see through something. After all, the one playing this orc is a human. Orcs will be more sensitive to certain details in their own kind."

The moment he said that, Jace felt he'd been overthinking it.

After all, with Alexstrasza and her other consorts imprisoned in Grim Batol, Krasus was the highest authority among the red dragons at the moment. He couldn't possibly have gotten this wrong.

"I understand," Jace said. "I'll be careful."

Krasus said, "Then I need to return to Dalaran as soon as possible, Jace. The Council needs me to track down the black dragons' trail — Deathwing himself in particular."

At the mention of the name Deathwing, Jace noticed the red dragon's eyes clearly flicker. The ease that had come from finally releasing the pressure vanished in an instant.

This dragon was also afraid of what he was doing, afraid of the situation he faced. But for the one he loved, he had no choice but to face everything.

Still, it made Jace feel a bit more balanced, at least.

"If I'm the only one who can do this, Master Krasus, I hope the red dragons will remember my decision today." He tried to keep his tone as polite as possible, but what came out the other end was quite cold.

Krasus paused, looking into Jace's eyes, then said, "They will remember, Jace Sesso. And so will I — no matter the final outcome."

As he said this, Jace felt he'd shed some of that arrogant, unpleasant attitude from yesterday. Would he take these words as a threat?

Jace hoped he would take it as a threat.

Watching Krasus disappear at the end of the Mage Quarter street, Jace touched his arm. He wondered what Deathwing would think, hearing all this.

Was he listening all the time, or only occasionally? Did he need to chant some spell, cast some magic, employ something like a scrying crystal to observe?

And thinking back further, in that dream, the Father of Black Dragons had seemed to believe quite quickly that he was willing to serve him. Why was he so certain Jace wouldn't deceive him?

Was it overconfidence? Believing that as a mortal, Jace would stop at nothing for immortality, and that given his status and power, a speck like Jace could never possibly defy him?

Or did he actually know Jace wasn't deceiving him? Jace thought of Molphis.

Could it be from the emotions revealed through speech?

Molphis had told Jace to control his emotions when lying — that way he could deceive her and keep her from detecting the lie.

Black dragons were unquestionably masters of shadow. It wouldn't be strange at all for them to see through the disturbances of dark power to tell whether the other party was lying. If he couldn't see through lies, Deathwing was trusting him far too much.

In the dream, had Jace lied?

He hadn't lied. He of course desperately hoped to avoid everything he'd seen in the dream. He of course wished he could live forever. He of course hoped every story would have a happy ending. And he of course knew he couldn't refuse Deathwing.

But what about after waking up?

Jace realized that not only could he not say the wrong thing — he couldn't even express the wrong emotions. Perhaps all of this was just his own paranoid imagination, but he couldn't ignore the possibility.

Again — he had no room for trial and error.

The slightest slip would make Deathwing realize he was secretly deceiving him, trying to pull something else — like freeing the Dragonqueen, or seizing the dragon eggs and destroying the black dragons' chance to reproduce, or… killing him.

Jace felt a chill run down his back. At this moment, even just having these thoughts cross his mind, he felt as if Deathwing already knew.
