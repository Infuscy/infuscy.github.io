## Chapter 634: This Time, He Goes Alone

The Pig and Whistle Inn in the evening. A group of young dwarves were piling into the tavern right now, shouting at the boss to bring more drink. They were yelling the names of several unfamiliar Ironforge brews — the kind Grede had never ordered — happily gathering around a big table in the empty corner of the hall.

That big table was placed there specifically for people to drink and watch people fight. Countless dangerous brawls — even armed ones — had broken out on that little patch of open ground.

Looking at that spot, Jace remembered the way Grede had looked dueling the young master of the Thorbain family. Deep down, he didn't really want to carry all this.

No Dragonqueen, no Krasus — just drinks he'd never tried, meat marinated in spices he'd never eaten before, from animals whose names he'd never heard.

"Smoked wolf meat, gentlemen." The barmaid smiled and set the plate on the table. Well, familiar animal meat worked too.

Grede glanced at the still-sizzling slab of meat, then looked at Jace. "What's with you? You just bought a house — you should be happy. This is a celebration, damn it, but from start to finish you've looked like your dog died. Come on, what's wrong? The new house got weevils?"

Jace looked back at him, thinking about what he'd considered before — that he needed to control his emotions, couldn't let Deathwing detect any sign of betrayal…

He mulled it over for a moment before saying, "Thinking about something. Zoned out a bit."

Grede wagged his finger. "Something's off. Tell me — what did that mage say to you yesterday?"

"He's sending me to the Wetlands to run an investigation. It's related to that old business about the connection between the orcs and the dragonflight — remember? That period when I kept going to Lakeshire." Jace answered.

"Ah, that business. That orc war banner, right? I remember," Grede asked. "Then do you need my help this time? How long's it gonna take?"

How long?

Jace looked up at Grede. "I don't know. How could you judge something like that in advance? For all I know the orcs might never show up — or maybe the first day I go, the orcs will take my head and…"

He drew a hand across his neck, then said, "But I'm guessing there probably won't be much fighting, Grede. You'd be bored coming along with me. And this is a Kirin Tor job — no pay. They're working me like a draft horse."

Grede laughed. "Then I'll use this time to head to Lordaeron and bring your parents over."

"Don't," Jace said immediately. "Not for now."

The dwarf was taken aback. "Why the change of heart?"

"Wait until I'm back. After I return from the Wetlands," Jace said after some thought. "I want to see them the moment they arrive — even if it's just on the sly. Whatever the Kirin Tor thinks. I don't want them to get here and immediately hear their son's gone off somewhere on some mission again."

In truth, he was worried — for some reason, at this point in time, linking his parents to Stormwind felt extremely dangerous.

The Scourge invasion was still a decade away, but Deathwing was already breathing down his neck.

"All right, I can understand that," the dwarf said, rapping his fingers on the table. "But then I've got nothing to do. When I first got back from Kalimdor I was planning to rest and have a good time for a while. But three weeks have passed and I'm getting bored again."

"Watch the house for me, Grede," Jace said. "I'll give you the key when the time comes."

"Watch the house?" Grede gave him a sidelong look. "What do you take me for…"

"You can live in it," Jace said, instinctively dodging the dwarf's gaze. "Also, I'm leaving the deed and the contract with you for safekeeping. And the money — about fifty or sixty gold, give or take. Part of it's for the follow-up renovation costs. I have a feeling that agent might've underquoted the price."

"You're giving me the money?" Grede's eyes went wide.

"Help me watch over the men renovating the house," Jace said. "Keep an eye on them. Don't let them screw anything up."

"Uh." Grede nodded hesitantly. "Well, all right. If that's the case, it's in good hands."

"Of course it is," Jace said.

What else.

As he spoke, Jace tallied it up in his head — what else was left to take care of? What did he still have left? Some bizarre demonic incantation scrolls? Those had already been handed over to the Reliquary by Vereesa.

Right. Vereesa.

One of Jace's eyebrows twitched involuntarily.

He regretted it now — he should have explained everything back in Kalimor or Booty Bay. Why did he have to wait until after buying the house?

Nothing but that pointless inferiority complex carried over from his past life, or some meaningless sense of ceremony — the idea that once he achieved the goal he could go back home and get married or what have you. Honestly…

Now there was no chance to take it back. He couldn't even see her one more time.

In any case, he felt he wasn't ready to face this. But when would he ever be ready?

He felt countless random questions flooding his mind all at once — and in the end, it was the worry that these questions would shake the steady emotions he'd been maintaining as carefully as possible that troubled him.

Ever since realizing Deathwing might be able to read his mindset and emotions, even living an ordinary life and having ordinary conversations in his normal state had become as draining as casting shadow magic.

Was he scared? He thought so.

Probably scared. But honestly, he didn't know what he was feeling inside right now. — But not every hardship comes with enough time to prepare.

"There's always a time when the unexpected arrives."

Jace's lips moved slightly, as if answering a line from his memory.

"— Maybe the real difference between me and those heroes is that even when the unexpected comes, they can bring two hundred percent of themselves to meet it."

"— The main difference between you and a hero is that your particular unexpected hasn't arrived yet, Amy."

Recalling this scene, Jace couldn't help but smile. He realized that the things he'd said to Amy Marin back at Nethergarde Keep — now that it was his turn, he understood it was far from that easy.

Of course, what had landed on his head this time wasn't some ordinary unexpected — it was Deathwing. It was only natural it wasn't easy.

In any case, his unexpected had arrived now. Besides meeting it with two hundred percent, there was no other choice.

"True enough." Grede slapped the table. "Who'd have thought trouble would come right after buying a house — and one you can't even refuse. When your parents get here, let's have a proper gathering. This really doesn't count as anything — that elven mage ruined the whole mood. Right, elf — we'll have to invite our little elf too."

"We'll see when the time comes," Jace said absently.

After he finished speaking, Grede stopped moving and fell silent for a moment, his gaze fixed across the table, watching Jace hastily grab a piece of bread, dip it in the grease beneath the smoked meat, and stuff it into his mouth.
