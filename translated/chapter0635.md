## Chapter 635: Routine Preparations

The next day, Jace arrived at the Slaughtered Lamb and spotted Tharle. "Is Ursula around?"

Tharle chuckled. "In a hurry, are we?"

"Drop it," Jace said, lowering his voice and glancing back at the patrons. "I've got important business. Is she back?" The last time he'd come by, Ursula hadn't been in the basement either. Jace figured she'd probably gone off to that fancy alchemy lab of hers.

Tharle's smile faded. "She is. Got back the day before yesterday."

Jace didn't waste any more words with him and headed straight for the hidden door.

The familiar smell of simmering potions had drifted up through the entire basement. Jace made his way down the passage to the alchemy lab, rapped on the wall, and called, "That especially powerful, ultra-mysterious potion of yours — had any luck with it, Ursula?"

"Just been waiting for you to show up." Ursula wiped her forehead with a towel, walked back to the cabinet, and pulled out a green, conical flask with a grin.

She came over to Jace and said, "Two weeks, like I promised — not a day over. You thought I was stalling you?"

Jace took the flask and watched the bubbles leaping inside, agitated by the potent magic. Almost to himself, he murmured, "Refined Wisdom Elixir."

"How do you even know about that?" Ursula's expression shifted. "You little bastard — you've ruined my whole dramatic reveal, my carefully paced introduction."

"I'm a mage, Ursula," Jace said. "But I'll admit, this flask still caught me off guard."

"It'll give you a massive boost of mana, let you go toe to toe with Andormas for a short while, Jace," Ursula said.

In the game, a Refined Wisdom Elixir provided an enormous increase to maximum mana — it was one of the rarest potions out there.

Jace understood that here, mana capacity was far more than just fuel for casting spells. It also represented a person's stamina, amplified magical effects, sharpened the mind…

Even so, being able to "go toe to toe with Andormas" was nowhere near enough — not in Jace's mind, anyway.

Of course, he wasn't about to rely on a single flask to trade blows with Deathwing either. As long as it could help him out inside Grim Batol, at the critical moment… that would be enough.

"I'll need some other potions too, Ursula. I'm heading out again."

Ursula let out a small sigh. "You really are a busy man. I thought after you got back from Kalimor those mages would've let you rest for a while."

"It's not the same bunch sending me out this time, but I can't refuse either one," Jace said.

Ursula shrugged. "Then there's no helping it. I was actually hoping you'd stick around and help me with a few things."

"So you were hoping I wouldn't leave just so you could send me out on your errands, is that it?" Jace asked.

The alchemist wiped the sweat beading on the back of her neck with the back of her hand. "I'm not going back out there. You have no idea what kind of risks I took getting to that alchemy lab to brew this potion for you. I'm on the Eastweald hit list, you have no idea."

You have no idea whose list I'm on, Ursula, Jace thought to himself.

"That's not the whole of it, though." Ursula smiled, then lowered her gaze to the bubbling cauldron beside her. "I just hope we can spend a little more time together. I don't have many friends, Jace. You're already one of the most important ones I've got."

"Enough of the useless stuff. Tell me what potions you want and let's see if I can help."

Jace pulled out a slip of paper he'd already written up — a list of potions he'd need, based on the troubles he might run into and the plans he'd already settled on.

"First and foremost this time, Fire Protection Potions."

"Fire?" Ursula asked. "Where are you going — the Burning Steppes?"

Jace raised his eyes and gave her a look. "Secret mission, Ursula. Don't ask. The more questions you ask, the more trouble you bring down on yourself."

"You little bastard," Ursula said, displeased. "I'm just trying to offer some advice. Fighting an enemy isn't much different from treating an illness. When treating a patient, the doctor looks at your symptoms to figure out what kind of trouble you've run into, then prescribes the right treatment — what medicine to take, what to eat, how to recuperate… And dealing with an enemy, you have to judge their abilities to figure out what kind of trouble you'll run into, then use the right potions to counter those troubles. It needs a whole plan too."

"So you think I don't understand the danger I'm walking into?" Jace asked.

Ursula gave a guilty twitch of her eyebrows. "Fine. I won't ask. You talk, I'll listen."

"And then make the potions I need," Jace said.

"Exactly." The alchemist folded her arms and leaned against the cabinet beside her. "Go on."

Fire Protection Potions — naturally, those were for dealing with the red dragons and their dragonkin servants. The dragons' mother had been enslaved by the orcs, so of course they had no choice but to follow orc orders and help fend off intruders. Compared to the orcs themselves, these terrifying draconic creatures were likely to be far more troublesome.

Unlike the black dragons, the red dragons' power was pure, blazing flame — not much else mixed in. That at least simplified things; he wouldn't need to stock up on too many Shadow Protection Potions.

Then again, Shadow Protection Potions would still come in handy against warlocks. This time it was just Jace and his two demons — no Grede or Vereesa to help draw the enemy's attention. All the damage would land on him. Plus there was the succubus to think about.

He could only hope that Vilethorn's shadow protection charm was strong enough to let Morophil absorb as much as possible for him. That way he'd have an easier time dealing with the Dragonmaw warlocks too.

Of course, the best outcome was to avoid fighting altogether. Rely on evasion, on charm, on deception… and not fight. After all, if he went deep into Grim Batol and picked a battle he couldn't wrap up quickly, what awaited him could be the entire city mobilizing to hunt him down. At that point, forget about finding the Dragonqueen — even getting out would be a problem.

"Invisibility Potions — I'll need two," Jace said. "And of course, the ever-essential Mana Potions. At least five, no, six. And none of that regular or lesser stuff this time. I need the best you've got, Ursula."

He thought it over — he probably wouldn't need too many Healing Potions this trip.

So many orcs, so many living blood packs packed into a fortress. If they dared surround him, he'd show them what it meant to feed off the battlefield.

"By the way, is that Restorative Potion still here with you?"

Ursula said, "I'll just give you a full one. But what are you planning to do that needs a potion this good?"

"Don't ask," Jace said.

Ursula frowned. "I'm worried about you, you damn bastard. Fine — do whatever you want. I'll tell you the materials you need to make all these. Several of these potions require ingredients that aren't easy to find. You'll need patience, Jace."

Jace pulled out a purse containing a dozen or so gold coins and set it on the table. "Get them ready as fast as you can. Don't worry about the price, Ursula. Keep whatever's left."

At the sound of gold clinking inside, Ursula's throat bobbed. She opened the purse and said, "Day after tomorrow."

"Good," Jace said. "I'll come find you the day after tomorrow. Thanks."

Leaving the Slaughtered Lamb, Jace ran into Jaylene, who'd just arrived.

"Jace?" She quickly glanced at the collar of his robe. "Does this need mending here?"

Jace felt the crack in his collar and thought it over. "Hold off for now. Thanks for remembering, Jaylene." An orc warlock who'd been active in Azeroth for years, still wearing such a neat and proper robe — would that even look right? Krasus was right. If he wanted the orcs to believe his disguise, he had to get every single detail right.
