Chapter 64: Back on the Road

Lakeshire had only one clinic, perched on a hillside at the town's highest point, run by an apothecary family named Gelos.

Unlike Stormwind, the herbalists here doubled as doctors and sold medicines and potions on the side. For a sizable town, there were almost no priests.

In a crossroads town like Lakeshire, where folks from every race north and south passed through, the Church of the Holy Light didn't have the pull it did in Stormwind. The one small church the Church had built had become a gathering place for people of all sorts of strange faiths. Aside from the Holy Light sigil hanging over the door, it had about lost its original purpose.

Late that night, Jace sat outside the infirmary waiting for news of Grede's injuries. He knew nothing about healing and didn't want to get in the way. It felt exactly like waiting outside an operating room.

"Mr. Jace Sesso, yes?" The clinic's owner, a gray-haired middle-aged man, came out. "The dwarf's injuries are severe, but his life is no longer in danger. It may just be a few days before he wakes up. Fortunately he's a dwarf — if a human had been struck by lightning, it would be hard to say."

Good news, sure — but "struck by lightning"?

Jace was done trying to explain that it was an orc who'd attacked Grede. Everyone in town, from the guards to the apothecary, thought he was hallucinating when he talked about an orc shaman.

According to them, if you encountered an orc, survival was impossible. And even if you did survive, there was no way an orc could summon lightning or anything like that.

He should have grabbed something from the orc as proof — might have even earned a bounty. Instead he had half the town misunderstanding him, and there was nothing he could do about it.

"Will he make a full recovery?"

"He should, but in my experience even a dwarf needs at least two weeks. Tomorrow I'll go across to the camp and ask a priest or paladin for help — might speed up the recovery, and save on costs too."

"How much will it cost total?" Jace asked.

"The Living Root and the rest of the recovery medicines needed for a lightning injury aren't cheap," the apothecary said. "Stabilizing his condition has already run up some cost. It's three silver now. The rest depends on how long he stays here."

Jace sighed and felt around in his bag. He'd brought six silver; he had four left.

Grede's pack had probably been lost in the fight. When Jace had dragged him back, the last thing on his mind was grabbing a pack.

It was probably still back by the lake, but there was no point looking for it. Odds were the murlocs had already stolen it.

He handed over the three silver, then showed the apothecary his assistant's badge. "I'm with the Wizard Sanctum, sir. I came to Redridge working for a mage — my employer is Eden Marin. Take good care of this dwarf. Whatever follow-up treatment he needs, don't throw him out just because the money runs low. I'll finish my business and head back to Stormwind, and I'll bring the rest of what you need within a week. How does that sound?"

"Of course I'm happy to trust someone from the Wizard Sanctum, and I'd never throw a patient out regardless," the apothecary said. "But do hurry. I have to purchase medicinal herbs myself, and without enough coin, the herb-hunters won't give me credit."

"Herb-hunters" — they were being that blunt about it here?

On a normal day, Jace probably would have loved to ask what prices they paid for herbs around here.

Redridge seemed like a fine place for herb-gathering, but with Grede lying in there and business still to attend to, he was in no mood to ask.

This whole trip, and he hadn't even gotten the Fighters Guild bounty — he'd lost money on top of it. A complete disaster.

At least the apothecary said he'd fully recover. If there were any lasting effects, he'd regret it for the rest of his life.

The next afternoon, Jace bought a few carrots at the inn to feed the horse. The mare was so hungry she nearly bit his fingers off with them.

He was almost out of money anyway. The horse shouldn't expect anything better.

Even after resting half the night and the whole morning, his whole body still ached and swelled, but by noon it was already much better than that morning.

Jace wasn't about to complain. If not for Sazeno's Blood Pact, he'd be in far worse shape than Grede.

After checking in on Grede, he decided his body could hold up and set out for Stonewatch. Time was tight.

Today was the last of the three days they'd agreed on, but at this point he wouldn't make it back to Stormwind until tomorrow noon at the earliest. He'd probably miss another morning of work.

But since this was official business, Marin would probably understand. Maybe dock his pay a little, or not at all.

After the rain, Redridge looked impossibly clear and bright, sunlight washing over the valley. Beyond the unbroken forest, Stonewatch Keep on the far cliffs across the lake was clearly visible — looked close enough to reach out and touch.

But at that distance, it still took an hour or two on horseback.

"Master!"

The imp's sudden appearance made him jump.

"How'd you pop out on your own?"

Sazeno gave an ugly grin. "Because there's no one else around, Master. You don't count this big animal as a sapient being, do you?"

The chestnut mare beneath him seemed nervous at the sudden appearance of a demon, stamping her hooves on the road. Jace spent a long while calming her down.

The imp fumbled with the horse's mane for a while before steadying himself. "Master, is the dwarf dead?"

"No," Jace said coldly. Hearing the imp say that, and the tone he'd used, fury surged into Jace's chest.

"What a shame!" the imp sneered.

Jace grabbed him by the neck and hauled him up. The imp coughed violently, staring at Jace in disbelief, and croaked, "Master… are you sending me back to the Twisting Nether?!"

Ignoring the itching and numbness from the demon's burning skin, Jace yanked him up to his face. "You want him dead? I get it now. I get it. If I ever find Grede dead under mysterious circumstances, you won't live either!"

"Master!" The imp clutched Jace's hand weakly. "Why do you protect that dwarf? You want to grow powerful. You want to live forever. And all that dwarf wants is to make you into a mediocre, worthless nobody! Rely on the swordplay he taught you, rely on the self-preservation he taught you — you won't last long! He's a stumbling block on your path to greatness! I thought you knew that!"

"You can hear everything we say?" Jace asked.

"Are you even listening to me, Master?" the imp said urgently. "He hates powerful demonic energy. He hates the unfathomable power of the Void. But his hatred of Shadow magic is his problem, my master. He can't control you!"

Jace stuffed the imp back in the bag. "He's not controlling me. He's just worried."

"Worried about what?" The imp poked his head out. "Worried you'll surpass him. Worried you'll become a great mage, outshine him in every way, leave him so ashamed he has nowhere to stand, make him suspect he's just a useless waste."

Jace snorted in contempt. "What kind of cheap, low-rent manipulation tactic is that, imp? I'd like to know what you gain from saying this stuff, or if demons are just born with this nasty streak."

"I'm just making a realistic guess, and it's one hundred percent for your own good." The imp slowly poked his body out of the bag, grabbed Jace's arm, and leaned in close. "I have no other motive, Master. I just want you to grow strong. Strong enough to at least protect yourself. If that dwarf didn't interfere with your growth, I wouldn't care about him at all!"

"Look at what so-called 'evil power' has given you. If you couldn't cast Fear, if you hadn't summoned me, what would have happened against that elemental servant? Think about it, Jace Sesso, my Master!"

The imp said urgently, "Azeroth is a world brimming with powerful magic. In all the dark Void, it shines like a gorgeous gem. Countless demons, countless shadows, countless dead and living all covet its beauty. In a world like that… if you don't find a way to make yourself stronger, you won't survive. Maybe you can scrape by for a few years. But decades? A century? A thousand years? Eventually…"

"Let me give you the most realistic example. This kingdom has been through a great war, a terrible war between invaders and the natives. I can feel a powerful magical surge to the south, constantly radiating its influence this way! It's a portal, a portal forged by demons — the very channel through which the invaders arrived! My foolish Master, think about it — how did the native races fight back against these invaders and reclaim what was rightfully theirs? With power… and maybe a little faith, but power is the foundation of everything."

"I don't really need a lecture from you, imp," Jace said coldly.

"What a pity, my esteemed Master." The imp smacked his lips mockingly. "Much as I dislike it, I must obey your command and stop telling you the truths I've witnessed. Even though I'll have to watch you slide little by little into the bottomless pit you think leads to light and progress, eventually arriving at a poisoned pool called regret and powerlessness, your soul drowning in wailing for years — still, I will…"

"Shut up."

The imp coughed twice and retreated back into the bag.

Jace cinched the bag shut and gave the cord a tug.

"Reciting poetry at me now, are we."

The horse trotted lightly down the main road. The sun was setting, and the walls of Stonewatch were already in sight.

Jace pulled the letter out of his pack and confirmed the recipient's name: Martin Harlow, a mage apprentice of the Wizard Sanctum.
