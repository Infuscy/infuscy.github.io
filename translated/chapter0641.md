## Chapter 641: Another Debt

"Ha! You actually remembered what I told you — most people would've forgotten, or never even listened in the first place!" The dwarf laughed, then went on. "My mother renamed me, but I never forgot why my father gave me my name in the first place. I always knew that sooner or later I'd return to Grim Batol — as a true Wildhammer warrior. You've got even more reason to be here than I do, Vereesa? I've been waiting for this day for decades. So the moment I realized that idiot was heading off to the Wetlands to get himself killed, I knew — I knew exactly what he was up to, and there was no way I could sit this one out."

"Orcs, Deathwing — what's the difference, huh?" He drained his beer in one gulp. "It doesn't matter who we're up against, damn it! If Mosha Woodmane found out I'd let you throw your life away while I hid behind doing nothing, she'd never marry me. A coward like that isn't the Grede Thunderfist she knows."

Vereesa listened to the dwarf's rambling and nodded. "I can't just stand by either, Jace. I genuinely don't understand what you were thinking."

Jace just listened and said nothing. But those two hit him harder than any death threat from a black dragon.

Just from the time spent talking here, he could already feel himself losing his grip on his emotions.

But he understood that the moment he let his guard down and some slip-up convinced Deathwing that he truly meant to free the Dragonqueen, that he truly planned to destroy the Demon Soul… it would all be over before it began.

That black brand was still on his arm.

"How did you two even get here?" he asked.

"Gorjet — didn't you see him? He's right outside," Grede said. "I went to borrow a griffin from Stormwind, and who should be there but Gorjet. Me and Gorjet… how do I put it — the moment he heard my feet he wasn't about to listen to any of those griffin keepers. And that griffin keeper was a Bronzebeard from Ironforge — he doesn't understand the bond between an old griffin rider and his griffin, that kind of family-brother connection. Truth be told, you don't understand it either, Jace."

Ah, Gorjet. Jace had nearly forgotten the griffin's name. This was the very same giant griffin that had carried Grede and him when they'd set out that night to engage the felbat and doomguard force.

Jace should have remembered right away. Maybe he'd deliberately avoided thinking about anything tied to Grede and Vereesa, since he'd only just managed to accept the fact that he was going to Grim Batol alone.

"How did you figure out I had another objective coming to the Wetlands?" he asked.

"Hah." Grede laughed. "Any fool could see that."

"He said he was leaving all his money to me," Vereesa cut in.

Jace thought back to what he'd said at the time — it really did sound like a dying man's farewell.

But back then his head had been full of Deathwing and that Quel'Thalas dream. He hadn't known how to relax and explain things properly.

"Right, but—"

"It's no use saying anything now, Jace," Grede cut him off. "I told the agent to put the renovations on hold for now and gave the money to your orcish-language teacher to hold on to — Caro Dushan, in the Mage Quarter. I figured he's a good man, cares about you, trustworthy. My first instinct was to give it straight to the King — I'm a hero of Nethergarde Keep, after all, he'd have to help me out. But I trust Warian Wrynn, I don't trust the people around the King. In the end I figured leaving it with someone in the Mage Quarter was the safer bet."

Vereesa said to the dwarf, "You should've brought the money to Quel'Danis."

Grede said impatiently, "I needed to travel light — no unnecessary baggage. Gorjet's big, sure, but he's got to fly thousands of miles back and forth, all within ten days! Dragging that much money around would've cost who knows how much extra time. You think if I hauled all that stuff he'd make it to Menethil Harbor before this guy sets out?"

Jace nodded. "And Master Dushan's trustworthy. That's fine, Wen."

"Fine, then," Vereesa agreed reluctantly.

"So," Jace said. "You just rode a griffin straight to Quel'Thalas like that?"

"Of course." Grede raised his empty mug and pointed north. "I went through the Hinterlands, got that woman to help me get in touch with the Windrunners. Truth be told, only a griffin as powerful as Gorjet could pull that off — swap in any other griffin, and never mind the Hinterlands roosts, even a griffin from Alterac couldn't have done it. And you couldn't necessarily find another griffin rider who could either. So like I said before — it wasn't you or anyone else who brought me here. It was decided from the start that I was the only one who could help you. No one else could."

"No one at all, huh?" Vereesa said flatly. "Your dear friend Mr. Gorjet ate through half a month's worth of feed rations for the dragonhawks at Quel'Danis in two days. That's enough for two dragonhawks. Did you stockpile that food yourself? Without those rations, you think he had the strength to fly back?"

Grede cleared his throat and muttered, "Would've been a waste to leave it."

"A waste?" the ranger sighed deeply. "I'll have to answer for all that when I get back. Damn it — I'm about to be promoted to Ranger-Lord, and if that gets delayed because of this, or falls through entirely, I'll personally pluck every grey feather off that chicken-headed monster outside."

"Ungrateful," Grede muttered under his breath. "I thought you were different from those Quel'Thalas elves." The elf looked like she was about to blow her top. Jace said quietly, "Thank you."

"A 'thank you' is it?" Grede said, pointing at Vereesa. "You'd better pay Quel'Danis back in coin when the time comes, and don't you dare delay her promotion to whatever it is."

"Ranger-Lord," Vereesa said, still a little huffy but clearly having cooled down quite a bit. "Ranger-Lord," Grede nodded.

"I will," Jace said.

"Anyway," Grede said. "Gorjet can't take us to find Grim Batol — honestly, on the flight back I could already tell he was pushing it. We ought to switch to a different griffin… though with three of us, a regular griffin might not cut it."

Jace said, "Even if Gorjet was fine, I don't think we should ride griffins there. Grim Batol's full of orc dragon-riders — in the sky we'd be no match for them, it'd be hard to stay hidden, and we'd just be asking for more trouble."

Vereesa said, "Jace is right. Even if he's much bigger than a regular griffin, he'd still have his hands full with whelfs, never mind the fact that the Dragonmaw orcs definitely have more than one whelp."

Grede thought it over for a moment, then waved a hand. "Fine, all that can be sorted out later. Let's drink first." Vereesa reminded him, "There's a critical mission right in front of us and you're still drinking?"

"How big do you think the Wetlands are?" Grede said. "Drink up today — once we're in those mountains around Grim Batol, you'll forget you ever drank in this life."

He smacked the table and raised his voice. "Barkeep! Three more dark ales!"

"I'm not drinking," Vereesa said. "You two go ahead."

"Make it four, then!"
