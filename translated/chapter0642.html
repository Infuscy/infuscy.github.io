Chapter 642: Drifting Off Course

Jace drank with Grede until late into the night. By midnight, the two of them had to call it quits just to retain enough sense to crawl into bed. They took a break and stepped outside the inn to cool off in the breeze.

Jace leaned against the inn wall, listening to the raucous noise coming through the windows. "Judging by the state of the Deepwater Inn, I don't think we're getting a room tonight. Should we find some abandoned shack to crash in, or…? Actually, how long have you two been here?"

"About two days," Grede said.

Jace asked again, "The Deepwater Inn's been this packed the whole time and you two still had rooms?"

"Not the inn." Vereesa raised her eyes and glanced toward the fortress inside the walls. "We're staying in there." Jace asked, "They let you stay?"

Grede tapped his chest. "I'm a veteran of the Nethergarde Keep campaign — I've got a bit of a reputation, Jace. And this Farstrider here is the sister of Silvermoon's Ranger-General. She's a Farstrider ranger-captain, no less. The war's over now, and the fortress has a few empty rooms. They couldn't exactly make us sleep on the street, could they?"

"So that's how it is. That solves a big problem," Jace said. "When I got here I was worried — if it kept raining tonight I had no idea where we'd sleep. I don't know a single soul here."

Vereesa studied Jace's expression, then asked, "Did they promise to restore your name once you're done with this?"

Jace answered, "That's what the mage said. Krasus."

"You met Krasus?" Vereesa had just pulled a handful of crushed nuts from her pouch and was about to eat when she clenched her fist around them and asked curiously, "I heard he's the most secretive one on the Kirin Tor's Council of Six. What does he look like?"

Jace said, "Tall, pale blond hair. Other than that, I don't have much of an impression — probably because I can't really tell you Quel'dorei apart."

"He's been to Quel'Thalas too," Vereesa said. "I heard from Sylvanas — apparently Krasus came to the Farstriders before, looking for a ranger to take part in a secret Kirin Tor operation. But Sylvanas turned him down. That wouldn't have anything to do with Grim Batol, would it?"

"Probably. But why did Sylvanas refuse?" Jace asked.

"She said too many Farstriders had been killed or wounded in the War of the Dark Portal," Vereesa said. "And no one volunteered either."

"The Council of Six isn't the group that rules Dalaran, are they?" Grede belched. "Powerful mages like that should have high standing in Quel'Thalas too. Why were you so cold toward him?"

"I'm not sure either." Vereesa tossed a nut into her mouth and chewed as she spoke. "He seems to have been a member of the Silvermoon Council even before my two older sisters were born. Since he's been staying in Dalaran all this time, I'm guessing he switched his allegiance there at some point. Mages like him get treated as traitors by a lot of people — both the Silvermoon Council and the Magisters Platform look down on them."

Grede and Vereesa launched into a discussion about the legends surrounding Krasus. Jace listened from the side and found his thoughts drifting, unbidden, to the War of the Dark Portal — Sylvanas and her Farstriders hunting Eredar warlocks, holding the line alongside the soldiers of the human kingdoms against the demons' advance.

Actually, back at Nethergarde Keep, he'd been wondering — in the other timeline's history, had there even been this brutal a battle? So many heroes involved, and in the end a pit lord had even shown up. Why wasn't there so much as a mention of it in the other timeline?

Jace turned over that suspicion he could never quite pin down: that he and Vereesa had killed Teron Gorefiend early, back on Azeroth, and that had thrown off the fighting on the other side of Draenor between the Expedition and Ner'zhul's Horde — which in turn had made the closing of the Dark Portal far more troublesome than it should have been.

Setting those murky differences aside for the moment — in the original timeline, this Grim Batol mission had gone like this: the mage Rhonin from Dalaran hadn't been noticed by Deathwing's minions until after he'd set out. He'd only reported back to Lordaeron that someone meant to rescue the Dragonqueen after the fact, and that was what put Deathwing on Rhonin's trail.

But in Jace's case, he could clearly feel that Deathwing had been watching Krasus's movements from the very moment Krasus started looking for someone to rescue the Dragonqueen — and the instant he'd confirmed Jace as the chosen man, Deathwing had entered his dreams to intervene.

The difference was far from subtle.

And this wasn't the first time he'd thought about it, either. Ever since setting out from Stormwind's harbor, Jace had been turning the possible factors over and over in his mind.

He'd found that aside from Archmage Kel'Thuzad, he could think of almost no one else who'd have both the opportunity and the ability to cause a difference like this.

It had to be Kel'Thuzad. After recommending Jace to the red dragon mage Krasus during the Kirin Tor Council's discussions, he must have found a way to pass Jace's name to Deathwing.

But in the other timeline, hadn't Kel'Thuzad cooperated with the black dragon? Hadn't he informed Deathwing that the red dragon mage Krasus was planning to rescue the Dragonqueen? He should have known about it.

Barring accidents, Jace could only guess that in that timeline, Kel'Thuzad hadn't yet earned Deathwing's full trust — or their relationship hadn't been as close as it was now. Maybe they'd only cooperated briefly during the chaos of the Dalaran Eye heist, before parting ways, or even betraying each other…

Whether in the game, the novels, or the movies, none of this was ever mentioned — or if it was, Jace had missed it. Details like this, he couldn't be a hundred percent sure he still remembered correctly.

If that hadn't happened over there, then how had Kel'Thuzad and the black dragon built such a close working relationship here? Wait — had the Eye of Dalaran really been stolen from Dalaran's vault by his own hands?

Back in Southshore, he and Grede had helped Lordaeron's soldiers hunt down the young black dragon Mafral. That had caused the Quel'Thalas Farstriders who'd been chasing it to lose their target, leaving them stationed in Dalaran — which made Teron Gorefiend and Deathwing's plan to invade Dalaran far more difficult. So they'd improvised: Teron Gorefiend and his death knights had taken the streets to draw the Farstriders' and other Dalaran guards' attention, while someone else snuck into Dalaran's vault to steal the sacred artifact, the Eye of Dalaran…

Jace had always assumed that was Deathwing himself. But now it seemed quite possible that it had actually been Kel'Thuzad who broke into the vault, stole the Eye of Dalaran, and handed it over to the Black Dragonflight.
