Chapter 643: The End of an Unknown Journey

He remembered how the orcish-language study group had been cut short. When the mage had barged in to report that a black dragon was attacking the city, Kel'Thuzad had hurriedly told everyone to stay put and left the hall for who knew where.

Jace had assumed he was going to help organize the defenses. But looking back now, it was very likely he'd gone to carry out the mission the black dragon had given him.

After that, Teron Gorefiend's physical body had died in Dalaran and his soul had fled to the Blasted Lands. To the black dragon, he was no longer of any use.

Kel'Thuzad had helped Deathwing obtain the Eye of Dalaran, which naturally made it easier to earn Deathwing's trust. From then on they'd kept cooperating — and even after Deathwing's return, he'd leaked more Kirin Tor intelligence to him. That, at least, was just barely plausible.

He still remembered that dwarf griffin that had been scorched by black dragonfire and crashed into Southshore…

Grede had picked up his war hammer, ready to fight that black dragon to the death.

When Jace had followed him deep into the burning forest, he'd never imagined it would end up affecting so many things. Of course, this was all just his own speculation — he had no way to verify any of it himself.

Maybe it was the alcohol, but he felt his thoughts spiraling deeper and out of control. Or maybe it was the black claw mark Deathwing had branded on him, seeping deeper into him with shadow influence, making him paranoid and pessimistic, prone to steering everything toward the darkest possible interpretation…

Maybe if he cast an Arcane Intellect on himself, he'd realize all of this was just coincidence — nothing but his own forced connections.

Even after a moment of self-reassurance in his head, Jace couldn't shake one worry — Krasus.

Krasus had mentioned before that finding Deathwing's hiding place was an operation the entire upper echelon of the Kirin Tor had taken part in. Which meant Kel'Thuzad would certainly have been involved.

In the other timeline, Krasus had also dueled the Father of Black Dragons — even gotten wounded — but in the end he'd survived, escaped, and after confirming Deathwing's identity, sought aid from the other Dragon Aspects. That was because even though Deathwing had many eyes and ears, he hadn't been aware of the Kirin Tor's secret operation…

But if here Kel'Thuzad was willing to hand the Kirin Tor's plans over to the black dragon, then wasn't Krasus walking straight to his death?

Arrogant as this red dragon had been — and Jace's first impression of him had been pretty terrible — after finding Deathwing he still needed to seek help from the other Dragon Aspects. If he got himself killed out of nowhere like that, what was Jace supposed to do?

By the normal progression, shouldn't it have all built to every Dragon Aspect gathering at Grim Batol to besiege Deathwing together, with Jace finding a way to shatter the Demon Soul at the critical moment to restore the Aspects' power and drive Deathwing back as one?

But now — no way to shatter the Demon Soul in sight, Krasus's fate uncertain, and whether the other Aspects would even show up an open question.

And he couldn't back out. All he could do was grit his head and keep pushing east toward Grim Batol. It was as though the only way to survive was to submit to Deathwing.

Was this all part of Deathwing's calculations too?

No wonder he was so confident he couldn't even be bothered to put on an act. Right from the start he'd laid his goal out in the open, not caring what Jace thought or considered — probably because he knew that the moment he'd entered Jace's dream and branded that black claw mark on him, Jace had already run out of roads to take.

A sea breeze swept past. Jace felt a chill across his forehead and the back of his neck, and his whole body shuddered.

Right now, he was already regretting not having spilled everything to Krasus right then and there, at the door of that house he'd just bought — not having told him straight out that Kel'Thuzad was a scumbag, that he was in cahoots with Deathwing and plotting to ruin Azeroth.

Getting thrown into the Violet Prison as a shadow-corrupted lunatic making wild accusations, then getting casually killed by Kel'Thuzad in some magical dungeon on a trumped-up charge — that probably would've been a better deal than the situation he was in now.

As things stood, with this black sigil controlling him, he was nothing but a puppet who could do nothing but overthink. What this Grim Batol trip would ultimately turn into, he had no idea at all.

That night, the three of them went into the fortress. Jace followed Vereesa and Grede down the long corridor. The garrison soldiers and officers they passed along the way would salute the two of them.

The room they were staying in was an empty soldiers' bunk with several triple-tiered wooden beds. Cramped as it was, it was already incomparably better than sleeping outside…

The moment Grede walked in, he clambered onto a random bed and passed out with a snore. Vereesa sat on the bed next to his and watched, from the shadows, a demon pull the door shut.

Her hand shot to the dagger at her ankle — then froze. She glanced at Jace. "Am I seeing things, or is that…"

Jace sat down on the bed across the way. "I forgot to mention — I brought her along too."

Vereesa pointed at the succubus. "No, I mean — is that Morophil?"

"Touched." The succubus sighed softly, leaned against the door, and twirled the black dagger in her hand. "I was moved to tears by the heartfelt scene of you three reuniting once more. And now you're telling me you don't even recognize me."

Vereesa shoved the dagger back into her boot. "I thought he'd summoned a stronger demon to help in Khaz Modan. The fel markings on you reminded me of a Legion soldier."

The demon raised one corner of her mouth. "And the Legion's minions are necessarily stronger than us?"

"Aren't they?" A hint of challenge crept into the ranger's tone. "The Legion picks the finest warriors from the Twisting Nether. You're just the strays they passed over."

The smile on Morophil's face stiffened bit by bit. She gripped the dagger in one hand and said, "We ought to find a time to test that, ranger — let you see that the Sha'tharet aren't just the cheap trash you ran into at the Dark Portal."

Jace had been lying in bed thinking about something else when he suddenly realized the conversation was heading somewhere it shouldn't. He turned his head and said, "Don't go picking fights over nothing, Morophil."

"I think we should," Vereesa said in a low voice.

"What the hell…" Jace stared at her in surprise.

"Aren't you two tired?" Grede's muffled, irritated voice came from the far side of the room. At that, everyone went quiet.

Vereesa blew out the lamp. The room plunged into darkness. The demon vanished entirely, melting into the shadows.

If Morophil hadn't suddenly raised her voice to throw down the gauntlet at the very end, Jace wouldn't even have noticed what they'd been talking about. Lying in bed, he couldn't settle his mind at all.

Because by now he'd grown afraid of the dark, of the night — or to be precise, afraid of falling asleep.

Even though Deathwing hadn't invaded his dreams again during the days at sea, this time he'd run into Grede and Vereesa. A whole evening of conversation, of emotional ups and downs — he had no idea what Deathwing would make of it, what he'd think.

Right now Jace felt like he was facing an exam — one where failing meant falling into the abyss. And even if he passed, there'd be another one the next day, and another the day after that…

At this point, he didn't even want to close his eyes anymore.
