## Chapter 644: An Orc — and a Proper One at That!

Flames… nothing but flames, blazing fire filling his entire field of vision.

Flowing lava, skin already seared black, nostrils scorched raw — every breath only filled his chest with more choking soot… Jace woke with a start, sitting bolt upright, drenched in sweat, his back soaked through.

Grede was still snoring away. Vereesa was asleep too. The succubus was leaning against the door, watching him. The imp was poking its head out of the pack, staring over.

"Just an ordinary nightmare, master," Morophil said.

Jace nodded. It was just an ordinary nightmare — no Deathwing, no self-important, twisted ranting from that damned lunatic.

Another day got through. He was almost grateful for it.

He stepped outside. The sky hadn't brightened yet. Jace took his soaked shirt and bedsheet out to hang them up to dry. Looking at the soldiers patrolling the fortress corridors and the grounds outside, he could confirm that this wasn't another dream woven by Deathwing either. He was truly awake.

Had Deathwing stopped caring about him?

Or had everything fallen into place, and so long as he headed for Grim Batol the black dragon simply didn't care anymore? The night's rest, the bright sunlight, the soldiers coming and going — all of it helped Jace pull himself free from last night's gloom, just a little.

Just keep going forward. Just stick to his original plan. Get into Grim Batol, find the Demon Soul, find a way to free the Dragonqueen, and leave the rest to the others.

At least now he had Grede and Vereesa by his side. He ought to have felt more at ease than facing all this alone back in Stormwind.

At noon, the group bought some supplies at the Deepwater Inn, then set out to leave Menethil Harbor and push into the Wetlands.

Along the way Grede kept telling Vereesa about the things that had happened the last time he'd come out here with Jace — the massive creature lying in ambush in the swamp, the crocodiles staring them down by the roadside, the howling of the gnolls hiding deep in the Wetlands, the runes carved by the orcs at Dun Algaz…

Hearing "orcs," Jace suddenly remembered the transformation pearl Krasus had given him.

They were already a good distance from Menethil Harbor, and there weren't many dwarf patrols out here. Before this he'd either been in Stormwind or on the ship, always with people watching. Jace had been worried that testing the pearl's magic without full control might cause an accident, so he'd never actually tried it.

Now that they were on the road to Grim Batol, he ought to test it out in advance — wouldn't do to get flustered at the crucial moment.

In the evening, after eating some dried black mushrooms they'd brought from the Deepwater Inn and a couple of fat toads Grede had caught by the creek, the three of them sat on a little slope not far from the road and chatted about things in Kalimor. Jace pulled the pearl from his robes and said, "Krasus gave me an artifact — for infiltrating Grim Batol."

"You've got something like that too?" Grede asked. "Is it for turning invisible? Like an Invisibility Potion?"

"Way more dramatic than invisibility. It's for transformation — it can turn me into an orc."

"Orc?" Vereesa and Grede exclaimed at almost the same time.

Jace turned the pearl back and forth in the firelight as he spoke. "I'm planning to use it now, so you two can memorize what I look like — don't accidentally kill me thinking I'm a Dragonmaw orc once a fight breaks out."

"That's going to be way too hard," Vereesa asked. "Don't all orcs look the same?"

"Some aren't exactly the same. Some are yellowish, some green, some black," Grede said.

"Then remember the clothes," Jace said. "According to Krasus, this pearl only changes the magical appearance — it's not like when Grede turns into stone, where he actually becomes a boulder. So it can transform what I'm wearing along with me."

Grede said, "But your clothes already look like orc clothes, don't they?"

That was true. Jace's robe had been made according to the Horde Shadow Council's formula. "Then remember this sword." Jace patted the sheath at his waist — Azuresong.

"We've got it," Vereesa nodded.

Jace held up the pearl. If Vereesa hadn't taught him how to activate the power within the Flowing Ice Crystal back at Nethergarde Keep, he'd have had no idea how to use an artifact like this at all.

When Krasus had handed him the pearl, he hadn't mentioned how to use it either — clearly, activating the power inside an artifact was something even an apprentice would understand.

Felt like getting punched in the gut. Again.

He followed the same method he'd used to activate the Flowing Ice Crystal, groping for the mana within the pearl and trying to resonate with it. The moment he felt himself touch the mana, he seized it — and instantly felt surrounded by a flame-like force.

Though the flame was scorching hot, it didn't actually burn. Jace felt his body rapidly growing taller and larger under its baking, his clothes and accessories scaling up by the same proportion.

When the flames faded, he was already a full head taller than Morophil beside him. The dwarf was craning his neck to stare up at him in shock.

Vereesa's hand instinctively went to the long knife at her waist. Jace looked at her. "Hey — what are you doing?"

The moment the words left his mouth, even Jace himself was startled — that earth-shaking chest resonance, that raw power in his voice was the real deal.

"N… nothing." Vereesa let go of the handle. "But that really is too convincing." Who the hell made this thing?

Krasus, good friend of a red dragon, orc transformation… Jace suddenly remembered Vaelastrasz, that red dragon who'd infiltrated the Blackrock orcs as a spy in Blackrock Tower, waiting for the players. Was this his handiwork?

Jace stuck out a fist — it felt like a chunk of dark green stone. He couldn't help but stretch. His bones and muscles had all bulked up several times over.

He waved his staff, threw a few punches at the air, making sure these violent movements wouldn't break the transformation. Morophil was staring fixedly at him, already unable to stop running her tongue over her lips.

Jace glared at her to stop being so obviously perverted, cleared his throat, and roared in orcish, "For the Horde! For Ner'zhul!"

Damn, that felt good!

"Lok'tar! Lok'tar ogar!"

Those orcish roars were so powerful they made the campfire's flames flicker. Grede frowned and asked, "Are you an orc disguised as Jace, or Jace disguised as an orc?!"

"You're asking the wrong question, dwarf." The orc let out a cold laugh and roared in Common with a monstrously thick accent: "The shame of the Dark Portal is behind us. The Horde will soon sweep across the world once more. What you ought to be asking is when you and your pitiful kind will meet your end!"

"This isn't funny anymore, Jace…" Vereesa said hesitantly.

"Sorry."

Jace dropped the strange accent. But he had to admit — just that one outburst had vented a good deal of the frustration he'd been carrying all the way here.

He drew Azuresong and swung it through the air. Sure enough, the arcane light it gave off made the orc's form rather unstable — wave it a few more times and plenty of places immediately became patchy and incomplete, the body rapidly withering.

Krasus was right. Azuresong's magic was too powerful — swinging it wasn't much different from casting a simple arcane spell.

So from here on he ought to use other weapons more often. The Blade of Eternal Darkness, for instance — even though it was a lot shorter, it was also more discreet.

Seeing him quickly return to his original appearance, Vereesa let out a breath. "All right, that really was something. I'll admit I was a little scared."

"That just goes to show my acting is pretty damn professional."

Jace said proudly, sliding Azuresong back into its sheath with one hand. Vereesa couldn't help but smile and nod.

"I don't doubt your acting skills," Grede said, chewing on a dried mushroom. "But when you're an orc and the demon can turn invisible, how are the two of us supposed to get in?"

Vereesa tossed the bones she'd finished eating to the side. "We coordinate from inside and outside, covering each other. That's a lot safer than him going in alone."

Jace nodded. Now even if his cover was blown inside Grim Batol, he wouldn't be left with no choice but to wait for death on the spot.
