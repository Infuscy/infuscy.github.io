## Chapter 645: Remnants of the Battlefield

Entering the great Wetlands once more, Jace wasn't as out of his element as last time.

At least he knew this stretch of road well — the "highway" running straight from Menethil Harbor to Dun Algaz. Of course, this time they wouldn't turn south toward Dun Algaz midway. Before the turn, they needed to keep heading east, into the depths of the swamp that no one had set foot in for over a hundred years — not since the Wildhammer dwarves had left Grim Batol.

Whether it was the Kingdom of Stromgarde to the north, Ironforge to the south, or the Wildhammer dwarves who'd once departed from here — they all kept tight-lipped about this place. Even the Explorers' League rarely mounted any expeditions into those mountains.

Maybe only invaders coming from another world would think that was a suitable place to settle down.

After five days of walking through the marsh, the group left the high road and stepped into the mud, pushing deep into the distant, fog-shrouded dark green.

A few years ago, Orgrim Doomhammer's Horde army and the Alliance forces led by Anduin Lothar had fought a great battle right here. Standing here now, Jace could still almost feel, deep in his bones, the clash of steel, the orcish war cries, the shouting of soldiers.

Even though all that met the eyes was a blanket of white, dense fog, and the vast, empty Wetlands were as quiet as death — not a sound to be heard except for the squelching footsteps right beside him.

Was that the voices of souls?

Jace glanced at Grede and Vereesa beside him. They didn't seem to have any particular reaction to the depths of the swamp — just kept their heads down and pushed forward.

The waterways in this area wound and crisscrossed. Walk a little ways and you'd hit a small river. That wasn't unusual on the high road either, but most of the waterways had stone bridges built by the dwarves, and even where a bridge was broken there'd be a temporary wooden one thrown up. But once you left the road, you had to wade straight through.

That was nothing to Jace and Vereesa, but to Grede it was often a huge problem. So when crossing rivers they usually had to keep a firm grip on him, or a strong current would drag him right off his feet and into the water.

Sometimes they'd hit a river they couldn't get across at all and have to make a detour. Travel speed dropped by half compared to following the high road.

By now it was already evening. Under their travel plan from before, this was about the time they ought to find somewhere and rest. But the whole great swamp was nothing but wet, squelchy ground, and the creeks beside them could flood at any moment. Looking in every direction, they couldn't spot a single place that qualified as dry.

The tall, fluffy hair-like water weeds were everywhere, covering most of the ground. No one knew whether what lay beneath those weeds was a pool of cold water you'd sink into or solid ground you could stand on.

Gradually, the dim sunlight shone in flat from the direction of the northwestern sea, piercing through layers of mist, draping a half-shadow of darkness over everything in sight — making the Wetlands look even more desolate and sinister.

That was when Grede suddenly stopped. "I'm getting dizzy."

"This is the old Wildhammer home we're talking about, Grede." Vereesa walked a few more steps, then stopped and looked back at him. "And yet you're the one handling it the worst this entire trip. Look at Jace — he's a human, no thick skin, no big feet made for walking."

Grede pointed back at Jace. "Take a look at that sour face of his — he's probably so exhausted from the walk he can't even string a sentence together anymore."

"More or less," Jace sighed.

Aside from Morophil, who never tired so long as she had mana, Vereesa was the most energetic-looking of them all. She looked back and forth across the water weeds and ponds, then gazed off in a direction ahead.

"Over there — there's a place to rest. You two follow me." Jace looked where she was gazing but couldn't see a thing.

"What'd she spot?" Grede asked.

"No idea," Jace said.

The two of them followed Vereesa for a while, and only gradually did a raised shadow begin to emerge from that direction.

It was a stone slab jutting out of the muck at a tilt. The raised end had broken off and slapped flat against the ground, forming a platform of sorts.

The stone slab looked as though it had fallen straight out of the sky. There wasn't a single other structure anywhere around it — just this clearly dwarven-carved slab lying here all on its own.

The moment Grede saw the slab, he scrambled up onto it with a joyful leap, flopped down on his back, kicked his boots off to one side, and muttered, "Finally a place to rest my feet — my toes are about to rot clean off in this filthy water."

Vereesa looked around. "See if there's anything around here that'll burn. I'll go find something to eat."

Grede tilted his head. "Why don't you use that… that beast-summoning trick of yours and let the food come to us?"

"Maybe I could." Vereesa gazed at the hazy, sinking sun. "But I have a feeling there are plenty of hostile creatures around here. I don't want to draw too much attention. If I actually blow my whistle, what follows the sound might not just be swamp animals — could be murlocs too…"

"Or swamp beasts," Grede added, then lay down on the slab. "Then I'll leave it to you, little elf. Best if you bring back a few toads while you're at it."

"You don't hear any toad croaking around here at all."

Even as Jace said it, he walked off a ways with his staff, poked at the water weeds beside the creek, and thought he'd try flushing out some toads or frogs. But when he pulled the weeds aside, he saw something round, white, and pale curled up in the water.

He poked at it with his staff, and felt the staff get tugged by something — he instinctively gripped it tight.

The pale thing spun in the water. It was a human face.

Not a skull — a face that God knew how long had been soaking in the water. Strangely, after all that time submerged it hadn't swollen or rotted badly. It had even shriveled a little, except for the eyes, which bulged out so severely they were nearly bursting through the eyelids.

There were corpses in the swamp — that was no surprise, with the war only four or five years past.

It was just that Jace couldn't tell whether this was the head of a strong human soldier or a dwarf's, seeing as the ears were long gone and hair was hard to make out too.

He swallowed and said, "Grede, didn't you say all the dead here had pretty much been eaten by the crocodiles?"

"What?" Grede called from over at the slab.

Before Jace could repeat the question, he caught a faint fishy stink. It wasn't the rot of a corpse — it was a faint smell of shadow corruption.

Vereesa, who'd been packing up her boots and getting ready to head out in search of prey, tensed up at once and looked toward Jace. Jace looked back at that head — and saw that it was staring straight at him.

Undead?!

Wait — slowly, from the river beside it, a rusty, slightly curved broken sword emerged. The kind carried by a Stromgarde or Lordaeron soldier. It rose straight up, held by something unseen.

Vereesa stared at the sword as it bit by bit surfaced, and the water around it bulged upward too — like a hand gripping the blade.

"What the hell…" the dwarf sat up and rubbed his eyes.
