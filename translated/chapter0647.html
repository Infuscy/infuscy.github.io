Chapter 647: A Convenient Identity

None of them knew how long they'd been running at full tilt. Not until the black sky had cleared considerably and they could even make out both moons, and the ground beneath their feet had turned firm and solid, did they finally stop to catch their breath.

Far behind them, at the edge of sight, the swamp was still giving off a hollow, wailing sound — no idea if that was coming from those slime creatures.

No wonder there weren't any waterfowl in that whole stretch of swamp, or any croaking toads or chirping insects.

The fog wasn't as thick here anymore. Looking east from this point, by the light of the moon they could see a dense forest stretching on until it vanished into the night.

The place they were standing seemed safe enough. After that desperate sprint, they'd finally walked what felt like an endless stretch of dark muck. The moment the three of them realized it, their nerves relaxed — and aside from the demon, none of them could stay on their feet.

"What the hell were those monsters?" Vereesa asked.

"Slimes, sludge beasts, oozes — lots of names." Grede straightened up, bracing his knees. "You've never heard of this kind of creature?"

Vereesa gave him a guilty look. "I've never seen a real one. Why are there so many of them here?"

"Who knows." Grede looked at Jace. "Why not ask someone out of the Wizard Sanctum?"

Jace shook his head too. "I don't know either. If I had to guess, I'd say too many orcs, humans, and dwarves died on this battlefield. The tormented souls and the necrotic energy on the corpses slowly poisoned these ponds, reanimating the animal and human remains inside — even the remains of swamp beasts — into what they are now."

"Or…" He looked at Grede. "Maybe it was that witch who destroyed Grim Batol. Her artifact did this to the place."

"Modgud." Grede nodded, coughed, and said, "I knew it. That sorceress's dark power didn't just corrupt Grim Batol — it made the whole damn swamp twisted beyond recognition. That woman will always be a criminal of Khaz Modan, and all the Dark Iron dwarves who followed her. One day they'll pay the price!"

"But tonight I can't make anyone pay anything." He deflated considerably, rubbed his eyes, and looked around. "Let's find somewhere to rest — somewhere properly safe. I'm hungry, exhausted, and freezing."

"Same here," Jace said.

"Let's eat some emergency rations tonight," Grede said. "I'd say this qualifies as an emergency."

The dwarf pointed toward the silhouettes of a few lone old trees in the distance. "We can rest over there. If it rains again in the middle of the night, at least there's some cover."

The others agreed. They followed Grede toward the trees, dragging their heavy steps. Jace dropped onto the muddy ground and leaned against a tree. He didn't want to move another inch tonight.

He tossed aside the hooked shortsword he'd carried out of the great swamp, pulled a chunk of dwarven cheese from the little food pouch at his waist, and bit off a piece. It was damn salty, but even that one small bite satisfied something deep inside him.

Grede picked up the broadsword. "Orc work. I know Blackhand's shield-guards love carrying this kind of blade."

"You mean the Blackrock orcs?" Vereesa asked.

"Right," Grede said. "The lot holed up in Blackrock Mountain as we speak."

"I heard the orcs in Blackrock Spire have been planning to rebuild the Horde here." Jace stared at the orc sword in Grede's hand. "Would they be in contact with the Dragonmaw orcs here too?"

"What are you getting at?" Vereesa asked.

"I'm thinking," Jace said. "What if I disguised myself as an envoy of the Blackrock clan to get into Grim Batol? I think that'd be safer than disguising myself as one of the Dragonmaw — and it'd give me a better excuse to get a direct audience with the Dragonmaw leader."

"I was planning to disguise myself as a member of the Shadow Council, but given the intelligence the Kirin Tor gave me, the Dragonmaw leader is a warlock too. He might be very familiar with the Shadow Council — might even be a former member. He doesn't know me, and neither do the rest of the Dragonmaw. That'd make my weak points stand out a lot more… But if I'm an outsider — an envoy from Blackrock Mountain — wouldn't that be a lot easier to sell?"

Vereesa asked, "But if the Dragonmaw and Blackrock are in contact, there might be other Blackrock orcs here too, Jace. They won't know you either…"

"Of course I'd have to watch out for that!" Sazeno said. "That's why you two need to help my master. If sweet talk doesn't work, then you step in and handle it the hard way — help him deal with those potential threats!"

"True," Vereesa added. "If his cover's at risk of being blown, we can cause a distraction at the right moment to give Jace a chance to slip away in the chaos."

"That would put you two in danger," Jace said.

"A lot better than you being surrounded by orcs every second of every day," Grede said. "And of course, if the orcs come after us too hot, you'll need to throw them off the trail a little too."

"I know. I'll do my best to cover my identity and keep that from happening," Jace said. "And I'll have Morophil helping me then too. If they're really going to expose me, that's when she shows up and puts her mind magic to work. A warlock with a succubus at his side — that's not exactly strange, is it?"

"All right, not strange at all," Vereesa said.

"Always at your service, master," Morophil said softly. "Whether it's killing… or lying."

Actually, Jace wasn't too worried about this part.

The black dragon must have already started cooperating with the Blackrock orcs — he'd known that for a while now.

Last time he and Vereesa had gone to the Blasted Lands, they'd run into the black dragon hunting down the Shattered Hand clan on the way, and dragonkin patrolling the ruins of Thaurissan — that was the best evidence. If the black dragon hadn't allied with the Blackrock clan, there'd have been no reason for him to specifically hunt orcs of one particular clan.

And then there was that dragon-mage who'd tried to recruit Twilight's Hammer cultists near Lakeshire. If they hadn't been coordinating with the Blackrock orcs, they couldn't have moved around the Burning Steppes so freely.

Jace's knowledge of Blackrock Tower's interior was probably deeper than that of your average Blackrock orc. He'd gone through Blackrock Depths and Blackrock Spire more times than he could count, memorized every quest detail and every twist of the terrain, and had smashed his way through the Lair of the Black Broodgod only God knew how many times.

Not to mention — right now he was coming to Grim Batol on Deathwing's orders. That put him way above some lackey or slave of Prince Nefarian. Even Rend Blackhand himself wouldn't have been more legit than him.

If he actually ran into some real Blackrock Legion envoy, who'd be the real deal and who'd be the fake was anyone's guess.
