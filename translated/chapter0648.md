## Chapter 648: Tol'rona

When Jace opened his eyes the next morning, Vereesa had already gathered some unfamiliar yellow fruits and laid them out. Grede was holding one and gnawing on it — judging by his face, the taste didn't seem to be anything to write home about.

He took a bite himself. Sour and astringent, but with a hint of sweetness — compared to nothing but dried meat and mushroom all the time, this was satisfying beyond measure.

"This is all I could manage." Vereesa looked at Jace, who was blinking against the sourness. "There are gnolls roaming around here. I was worried that lighting a fire to cook meat would draw their attention."

"This is more than good enough," Jace said. "I was thinking I wouldn't have to find my own food until we got into the Barthor Mountains. This whole swamp is nothing but danger — I just wanted to get out of here as fast as possible."

He looked off to the southeast. Beyond that great stretch of forest, the outlines of mountain silhouettes were already faintly visible through the clouds and mist.

After a simple breakfast of fruit, the group set out toward the forest.

Compared to the swamp deep in the Wetlands, this forest was more or less normal — no different from the woods near the Hinterlands close to the Hillsbrad Foothills.

Maybe because they were gradually approaching the mountains, the elevation slowly rose, and pine trees became more and more numerous.

When they'd first entered, they could still hear a few bird calls in the trees. But after a few hours of walking, the environment had gone quiet. Aside from the wind rustling through the canopy and their footsteps on the thick carpet of pine needles, there wasn't another sound to be heard.

Jace stepped up onto a large upturned pine root and was climbing toward the ridge above when Vereesa, who'd already reached the top, suddenly raised a hand for them to be quiet.

She looked up into the canopy. Then, from a distance, came the rhythmic sound of enormous wings beating, drawing closer.

Vereesa slid back down and led Jace and Grede into the space beneath the pine roots. Morophil slipped off to one side and vanished into the shadows under the trees. A shadow swept past in a flash, accompanied by a sharp, piercing screech, along with a string of not-quite-coherent cursing.

Obscured by the canopy and branches, Jace hadn't gotten a clear look at what that huge dark shape was — but there was no mistaking that the cursing was orcish.

"A red dragon?" Grede whispered.

Vereesa nodded. "The scent of a red dragon. That's definitely an orc dragon-rider. It means we're heading the right way."

"You can smell a red dragon?" Grede looked at the sky. "I can't smell a damn thing. It passed too fast."

"I've dealt with more than just black dragons, Grede. Don't forget what the red dragons did to my homeland." Vereesa said, and walked out from under the roots, continuing upward.

Grede screwed up his big nose and said, a little sheepishly, "Sorry, little elf."

"It's fine." Vereesa looked back. "If I've decided to help Jace find the Dragonqueen, then that means I know exactly who our enemy is."

"I've actually never really understood why the Dragonqueen got captured by a bunch of orcs in the first place," Grede said, hauling himself up by the roots as he spoke. "When I was a lad, my father told me that when a red dragon flew into a rage, nothing in this world could stand in its way — and that goes double for their queen."

"The Dragonmaw warlocks have an extraordinarily powerful artifact," Jace explained. "They're using it to control the Dragonqueen, and through her they force the entire Red Dragonflight into serving them. As long as we find that artifact, we can rescue the imprisoned Dragonqueen."

Grede shrugged. "So it's you mages' fault after all. Truth be told, even now I have a hard time accepting that the red dragons are actually the good guys."

"They're not the good guys yet," Jace said. "We have to be careful around all dragons. Only after we rescue the Dragonqueen can we be sure they won't be hostile toward us. Right now the red dragons are still under the Dragonmaw's control — to us, they're no different from black dragons."

As he said it, Jace felt his right arm start to itch. Could that brand be infected?

He observed Vereesa and Grede walking ahead of him, confirmed they weren't looking at him, and rolled up his sleeve for a look. There was no change.

Lately, he'd been sleeping more and more soundly at night.

If he remembered correctly, the Father of Black Dragons should be stirring up trouble in the Lordaeron court right about now.

Using mind magic and illusions, he'd successfully influenced King Terenas of Lordaeron, convincing the King that the Alterac noble he was impersonating — Lord Daval Prestor — was trustworthy and loyal to Lordaeron and the Menethil royal house. That was how he'd gotten the King to push for handing the vacant Alterate throne to him.

Maybe he was busy with politics now, or tied up in court trivialities, with no interest in bothering with this bumbling mortal struggling his way through the swamp. After all, what news could there be from flailing around in a swamp for days on end?

As they pushed deeper into the forest, the environment changed so noticeably that Jace almost forgot they were still within the borders of the great Wetlands.
