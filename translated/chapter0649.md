## Chapter 649: The Cage

In complete, absolute darkness — not a single thing to be seen — Jace's eyes were wide open. Where were Vereesa and Grede? They should have been sleeping right beside him.

He couldn't remember how he'd gotten here. The memories in between felt like a broken thread.

Shouldn't he be in Tol'rona? They'd finally found an abandoned dwarf town — a place to rest that was dry and sheltered. Tol'rona might be deserted, but it shouldn't be this dark. Moonlight, starlight — there ought to be at least some light, enough to make out something.

Jace tried to shuffle a step to the side, meaning to feel for the wall of the ruined house where they were resting — but what he touched was a perfectly flat, vertical wall.

This wasn't a dwarven stone house. This was somewhere else. He could more or less guess what was going on.

Ever since leaving Stormwind, Jace hadn't entered a dream again. Even though the journey with Grede and Vereesa had been wind and rain, noise and chaos all the way, it hadn't made him forget that he was still being watched by Deathwing's death brand.

He'd thought maybe the Father of Black Dragons had come to trust him — or decided to let him off the hook to some extent, maybe because he hadn't shown any sign of betrayal, or suspicion.

So why now?

Did Deathwing have something to say? Or had some doubt surfaced in the depths of his own mind that Deathwing wanted him to resolve? Jace didn't know.

Or maybe this was just an ordinary nightmare?

Jace stretched out his arms and felt around in every direction, trying to figure out where he was. But he quickly realized the space was extremely narrow — like a dead end. More precisely, like the end of a corridor. Or like a small room locking him inside.

He had a bad feeling, but he didn't dare put it into words.

Not until his hands closed around bar after bar of black iron did he realize that feeling was correct. This was a prison.

Even if it was a prison in a dream, not a real one, it still made his nerves wind tighter and tighter.

Had Deathwing discovered his true purpose, seen through his real intentions, and in a rage thrown him into this dream-prison?

Or was this some kind of symbol. A warning.

A flame flickered to life in a black brazier on the other side of the bars, illuminating a narrow space. A man was kneeling there, his arms chained up by iron locks, fixed firmly to the thick black walls on either side.

Jace gripped the black iron bars and stared at the man. He saw that the man's head was covered in dried blood, his short, messy hair dirty like a clump of weeds that had been roughly torn through.

But even so, by the light of the suddenly lit brazier, you could still tell his hair was a pale gold verging on white.

Was he the prisoner here?

Then what was he… Were these just two adjacent cells?

Jace felt his mind turn to mush. In the dream he couldn't think properly at all. The man coughed twice and said something in a low voice — in a language Jace couldn't understand at all.

Who was this?

Was this even Deathwing's dream?

Jace frowned. He felt like his brain had become a tool for anyone to toy with. This couldn't be his own nightmare — he was certain of that. The scene was clear, the logic coherent, the timeline consistent…

"Who are you?" he asked. "Who am I."

The man spoke Common this time, and the voice sounded very familiar to Jace.

It was just that, in a setting like this, in a dream, Jace couldn't quite recall where he'd heard it…

Almost in the next instant, Jace realized with a start that it wasn't that he couldn't remember — it was that on a subconscious level he was desperately avoiding letting himself hear this voice in a situation like this.

"Krasus?"

"I thought you'd forgotten me." The man on the other side shook his head. "I'm not surprised to hear your voice here, Jace Sesso."

Every word he spoke struck Jace's consciousness like a boulder, thud after thud. This prison held both Krasus and himself — what did that mean?

Jace looked around, trying to find anything else, any clue… but in the end he brought his gaze back to the red dragon. Had the man ended up like this because of him?

Because he hadn't told Krasus everything about Kel'Thuzad? Because he hadn't confessed his connection with Deathwing to him?

Or had he done something else wrong even earlier — and how far back did it go? Did Krasus think he'd betrayed him?

The flood of self-reflection, recollection, and regret surging through his mind was crushed to nothing in a matter of seconds by the will that rose up from within him.

It wouldn't change anything. No matter what, it wouldn't change a thing. And this was nothing but a dream. Jace told himself that, his hands instinctively gripping the metal bars tight.

That was when the man lifted his head. That handsome face was covered in blood. His chest and shoulders had been pierced through by black-purple spikes. One eye was nothing but a bloody, hollow socket. Both legs had been broken at a grotesque angle, lying limp and powerless on the ground. One of his hands was missing several fingers…

"Don't make that face. I don't blame you." He forced his lips apart and spoke slowly, his mouth full of blood and pus. "I can't expect a mortal to refuse Deathwing's terms. It's just a pity — I've failed on every front. Maybe they're right. The situation is beyond saving. Nothing I do will make a difference… but I don't regret it either, because I've done everything I could. Everything here can bear witness to my final loyalty to Queen Alexstrasza. Including you."

"If anything, the one who should apologize is me. It was my stubbornness that led you down this dead end."

Jace fell silent. He stared at Krasus, who'd been tortured beyond recognition, and waited for that figure to appear. Before long, a shadow approached from behind. A hand came to rest on the metal bars, stopping at Jace's left side.

Jace felt like Deathwing had never been this tall in any of his previous dreams — like a bronze statue standing at his side, immovable to the core.

Deathwing looked at the prisoner behind the bars and said, "Look who it is. Now you can carry out your mission without restraint, Sesso. Get into Grim Batol early and take charge of the Dragonmaw orcs. And while you're at it, put his queen out of her misery."

Hearing that final order, Krasus lifted his head, clenched his teeth, and fresh dragonfire-blood began to seep from the corner of his mouth.

"I remember you saying you didn't care about the Dragonqueen's fate," Jace said.

"I was still planning to offer her my apologies — being forced to lay eggs by orcs is, after all, a little too absurd." Deathwing smiled, then his voice turned cold all of a sudden. "But it seems that wasn't enough. These red flies running amok are far more vicious than I imagined. Not only does the one before me mean to assassinate me — Lord Daval Prestor, sole legitimate heir to the Kingdom of Alterac — he also means to lead a bunch of circus performers in purple costumes to wipe out my entire family. Truly uninhibited. Reckless beyond measure."

"I have to say, your earlier concern about him was justified. This venomous insect cares about nothing but his queen — you least of all. Even with nothing left to his name, his kind on the brink of extinction, in such dire need of a mortal's help, he still feels he can't lower himself to speak as an equal to your kind. He thinks a human like you should charge into fire and water for a single careless word from him. His chest may seem to burn hot, but the truth inside is chilling, Sesso. Can't you feel it?"

"Once you've rescued the Dragonqueen and you've outlived your usefulness, he'll cast you aside without a second thought. At best he'll toss you a few laughable titles — Beneficiary of the Dragons, Savior of Khaz Modan. Then you'll be worshipped by a bunch of mortals who have absolutely nothing to do with you, except they'll mispronounce your name from time to time, because no one truly cares about your sacrifice."

He looked through the bars to the other side. "You know I'm right, Korialstrasz."

Krasus didn't respond. He lowered his head once more, staring at the ground. Viscous blood dripped one drop at a time from the tip of his nose.

"I'm doing this for your own good." The Father of Black Dragons turned his head back. "You've come this far. If you don't kill Alexstrasza, do you think she'll spare you in the future?"

Under the twin weight of a tortured Krasus and Deathwing, Jace's surface calm was barely holding together — inside, he was already trembling on the verge of losing control.

He felt he was on the edge of losing the ability to keep up the act. Forget controlling his emotions, he couldn't even keep up the appearance…

All he could do was fix his gaze on Krasus, hanging his head, arms fixed in chains — stare at his scalp, torn and bleeding — and say, "I will complete my mission."

At those words, the red dragon's shoulders shook twice, as if laughing — laughing at a mortal's cowardice, laughing at his own stupidity. "Don't be so vague," Deathwing said.

He looked at Jace. His yellow, demonic eyes had become true reptilian eyes — a thin, vertical black pupil, an iris rolling like molten lava. Jace stared into his eyes and felt as though his very consciousness was about to be crushed, burned, and dissolved into a filthy puddle of muck in the dream.

But in the end, he held on to the last shred of his reason, barely surviving under Deathwing's power — enough to force himself to say the next sentence.

"I will kill the Dragonqueen. I will find the Demon Soul and hand it over to you."

Deathwing blinked and finally looked away.

Jace felt as though he'd just been unchained from a rack. The effort it had taken to squeeze out that single sentence felt like months of cruel torture.

"And the dragon eggs — you always forget those." Deathwing's tone carried a hint of casual complaint. He went on, "Without Alexstrasza, those eggs become all the more important. Especially the ones that'll hatch females. Let's hope at least one of them grows up to be as strong-willed as our Prince Korialstrasz here — only then can she contribute to the continued growth of the Black Brood."

The black dragon snorted out a laugh — an extremely stiff, even bizarre sound.

Jace suppressed every emotion he had as best he could — fear, tension, suspicion… maintaining a fragile calm. "I understand, master."

"Then carry on. I have other matters to attend to." Deathwing said coldly.

The tall figure of the black dragon rapidly blurred and distanced. Jace felt himself seized by a scorching black claw and hurled out of the prison in one motion…

He jolted upright, gasping for breath in great heaves. Even the cold drizzle against the stone wall outside couldn't calm him. Would Krasus die?

Or was this yet another illusion woven by Deathwing — meant simply to put Jace at ease and make him stop worrying about the red dragon threat.

Either to make him give up completely, stop caring what happened to the red dragon.

Or to test him through a scene like this — to see whether he was truly loyal to the black dragon, or still working for Krasus.

Had he passed the test? Did Deathwing believe him?

And yet his gut told him it was real. That was truly Krasus himself — that powerful, blazing heat, even under Deathwing's dark power, even bound by those black chains, still swelling like a rising flame. It was nothing like everything else he'd seen in his dreams before — like that Vereesa Windrunner who, looking back now, had been no more convincing than a stage actress.

It was just that Jace didn't want to believe all of this had already happened. If Krasus hadn't escaped Deathwing's trap and been captured by him, then the fear he'd harbored earlier had become reality.

Krasus couldn't break free, which meant he couldn't inform the other Dragon Aspects that Deathwing had returned. — No other Dragon Aspects would be coming to Grim Batol.

The night in Tol'rona had suddenly grown darker than the dream.

He covered his eyes and took a deep breath. That was when a searing pain shot through his arm.

He rolled up his sleeve and looked at the sigil on his arm. It was glowing like fire in the rain-soaked night. Startled, he quickly covered the mark again and pulled his sleeve back down.

After a while, he sensed Morophil hiding herself in the heights, keeping watch for any uninvited guests who might approach. Grede was snoring away beside him.

But in the dark corner ahead, two faint blue glimmers slowly appeared. "You do have something you're hiding from us," Vereesa said softly.

"Sorry," Jace said.

"You think I can't tell you've been heavy-hearted this entire trip?" Vereesa said with a quiet sigh. "Grede knows you better than I do — he sees it as clear as day. I don't know what you're going through. All I know is, no matter what, don't shut us out again. Since we chose to follow you here, we've made our preparations for anything that comes."

"To be honest, dying doesn't frighten me at all. I'm only afraid you'll stop being you." Jace fell silent for a long while, his staff in his arms, as the patter of rain outside once more drowned out the sounds within. "So… can I trust you, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

Jace's lips trembled. He raised his head and met the elf's eyes. "Yes."

"Then nothing else matters." The ranger smiled, leaned against the wall, and closed her eyes. "It's still a long time until dawn, Jace. We're about to head into the mountains. Rest a little longer."
