Chapter 65: Stonewatch

Stonewatch Keep was the most important military stronghold in Redridge. Its walls were built thick, the keep itself massively sturdy. Stonewatch Falls poured down through the bridge beside it, the roar of the water audible from far off — quite a sight.

For centuries it had been the front line against the dwarves to the north and the gnolls of Redridge. Against dwarves with their fearsome heavy siege weapons, and gnolls who fought like madmen with no regard for their lives, thin walls and a small keep simply wouldn't cut it.

Stonewatch's very existence guaranteed that no enemy could attack Lakeshire without taking it first, let alone push on to Eastvale or Goldshire. It had become the security heart of every human holding in Redridge, and each successive king had piled on more fortifications until its defenses had become something outrageous.

When the Dark Portal had opened years ago, even the vanguard led personally by the Horde's then-Warchief Blackhand had nearly broken itself against these walls. Only when the main army arrived — outnumbering the defenders by ten or twenty to one, backed by the terrible magic of the Shadow Council's elite warlocks — did they blast through the stone and take the keep.

Now Stonewatch, with several breaches in its walls, had lost its former glory. Damage and gaps were everywhere, and the difficulty of rebuilding was enormous, so progress had been painfully slow to this day.

Jace dismounted at a camp outside the keep. Maybe it was the fresh Redridge air, or maybe it was the view down into the valley that lifted his spirits, but the ride had left him feeling much more relaxed, body and mind. He hoped Grede would bounce back just as quickly.

A guard, seeing he was from the Wizard Sanctum, led him straight across the bridge and into the keep to meet the commanding officer.

Stonewatch's commander was crew-cut. He looked Jace up and down and didn't say anything for a long while.

Jace stood in the middle of the keep's courtyard feeling deeply out of place. He wondered if veterans of the Orc Wars were more sensitive to dark magic or demons than mages themselves, and if he'd somehow given himself away.

"What are you doing here?" the commander asked.

"Jace Sesso," Jace answered. "From the Wizard Sanctum in Stormwind."

"You don't need to keep stressing that you're from the Wizard Sanctum. I know what that place is." The commander rested a hand on his belt. "We have a few mages in this keep. Mages aren't anything special."

"A mage asked me to deliver something here," Jace said. "A very important package."

The commander nodded with a sneer. "A mage's things are always important."

"My employer wants me to put this package directly into Martin Harlow's hands," Jace said.

"Martin Harlow." The commander glanced at the man beside him. "That young fellow? The one who went bald in his twenties?"

The man beside him nodded. The commander said, "So you really are from over there. I'm just a little surprised someone like you could cross the whole valley alone and make it to Stonewatch. Didn't run into any trouble on the way? Gnolls, murlocs, anything like that?"

"He's a mage, Colonel," a soldier beside him reminded. "Mages always have their ways."

"Didn't ask you." The commander — the one they called Colonel — glanced at Jace. "Is that horse yours?"

"No, sir," Jace answered honestly. "The horse belongs to the Mage Quarter. Once my errand's done, I need to bring her back, so I'd like to be quick about it."

"Call me Colonel Todermann." Colonel Todermann waved to a young soldier beside him. "Take him to see the mages. Looks like the valley's gotten a lot more peaceful these days."

"And feed that horse. Look at the pretty girl, starved half to death. Tsk."

The colonel kept his eyes on the package in Jace's arms until the guard led him into the keep's interior. Soldiers, guards, and clerks bustled back and forth — the place was busy.

But the people here wore different uniforms from Stormwind's army or the Lakeshire militia. Jace asked his guide curiously, "Are you with Stormwind?"

"We're sworn to King Wrynn, of course," the soldier leading him answered. "But after the Orc Wars, we became an independent force under Colonel Todermann, garrisoning Stonewatch to keep the fighting between the Blackrock orcs and the Dark Iron dwarves to the north from spilling over. We also handle security in the surrounding area — mostly gnoll problems these days."

"The Wizard Sanctum mages did us a great service in last year's campaign to retake Stonewatch, sir." The soldier climbed the stairs, looking back with a smile. "Colonel Todermann didn't mean anything personal by the way he talked. It's just that things are tense right now. Gnolls are pulling every trick in the book — poisoning, setting bombs, stealing — always trying to cause trouble."

"I understand," Jace said. "The Crossroads have been tense too. The kingdom's deployed a lot of troops there."

"Really? Are the gnolls still that rampant there?" The soldier sighed. "I have a sister doing business in Granville who travels back and forth to Lakeshire regularly. I haven't been back in years. I hope she's doing well."

On the second floor, the soldier knocked on a wooden door. "The Wizard Sanctum's mages are usually here, reading and doing research. Anyone in the keep who's hurt or sick has to turn to them. They work so hard."

A dark-haired middle-aged man opened the door.

"Master Morgas," the soldier said. "He's here from the Wizard Sanctum in Stormwind, says he's delivering a package. Call me if there's a problem — I'll be downstairs."

"Thank you," the man said.

Morgas?

Jace remembered this man. Morgas had been a mage in Stormwind, but he'd been expelled from the Wizard Sanctum for studying forbidden dark magic. He'd held a grudge ever since, and eventually betrayed the kingdom — helping the Blackrock orcs seize Stonewatch.

Clearly, he hadn't been expelled from the kingdom or the Wizard Sanctum yet, and no other mage had caught on to his involvement with dark magic.

Morgas wasn't old yet — probably not even forty. He had a thin, long face and a deeply gloomy air about him.

He wore his black hair long, and was dressed in a fitted shirt and trousers. He looked at people with his chin slightly raised, a touch haughty.

Morgas looked Jace over. Jace suddenly remembered the orc's warlock staff still strapped to his back.

He hadn't expected to run into a wizard this powerful here. Would he be found out immediately?

But Morgas didn't give his back a second glance. He just looked at what Jace was carrying — maybe not Marin's package, but the warlock's bag at Jace's waist.

"You were sent by Master Marin? Come in."
