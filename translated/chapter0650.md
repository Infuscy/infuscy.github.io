## Chapter 650: The Narrowing Path

Heading south from Tol'ronar, Jace had left the forest behind and entered an utterly unfamiliar mountain hollow by the time the trees ran out.

Grede had been right. Just gazing out at these layered peaks — the so-called Bator range — Jace could feel the shadow power still clinging to the mountains. There was no question: the place ahead where the darkness hung thickest had to be Grim Batol. Probably only orcs who'd drunk demon blood could have lived in a place like this for so many years.

Ever since dreaming of Krasus's prison, Jace had been turning over what to do next.

Deathwing could torment his will in dreams, scramble his thoughts, but at least so far he'd never directly reached into Jace's head in the real world and broken something.

So as long as there were no dragon riders circling overhead, and no sign of gnolls or raptors prowling about, he'd steal a moment to think while he walked.

Setting aside the kind of self-defeating notions — like defecting to the enemy — that only surfaced when the headaches got bad, the best option he could come up with was this: get hold of the Demon Soul, and before Deathwing could react, find a way to destroy it.

That would not only free the Dragonqueen from her bonds — it would release all the Aspects' power on the spot, returning their strength to the Aspects themselves.

If the other Aspects sensed that power returning, the ones who weren't at Grim Batol would realize immediately that the Demon Soul had been shattered, and they'd know at once that this had everything to do with Deathwing.

Once the Demon Soul was broken, the first to reclaim her power would undoubtedly be the Dragonqueen, Alexstrasza.

She was right there at Grim Batol, closest to the scene. If she could rally the other Aspects to come help, they'd stand a real chance.

After that, it'd be a race to see who flew fastest. Jace guessed the Spell-Weaver, Malygos, would arrive first — after all, as the most powerful arcane manipulator in the world, he probably didn't even need to physically fly.

If the Bronze Dragon Aspect, Nozdormu, was willing, he'd likely show up fast too — maybe even faster than the Blue Dragon King, though Jace didn't know if he could actually travel through time.

As for the Dreamer, Ysera… Jace had no idea what kind of power she'd bring to the fight, but however long it took her to arrive, as long as three Aspects with their restored power stood together, even if they couldn't kill Deathwing outright, they should at least be able to drive him back.

Chances are, Deathwing might not even dare show himself at that point.

Jace remembered Deathwing mentioning that things hadn't gone well for him on Draenor. He must have been referring to that incident where the archmage Khadgar had defeated him with magic.

Having held the Demon Soul for so long, its terrible power had slowly torn Deathwing's body apart over the ages. So he'd hired a crew of goblin craftsmen to patch him up with metal plates bolted onto his outer hide, just to keep him from falling completely apart. In that clash with Khadgar, the mage had used arcane incantations to rip open that seemingly indestructible armor, causing the Black Dragonfather's power to suddenly collapse — and it had nearly killed him.

Even grievously wounded as he was, Deathwing remained the most powerful of all the Aspects. But if three or four restored Aspects gathered at Grim Batol, he'd never show his face lightly.

Jace would be living with a black dragon's vendetta from here on out, but at least he'd survive this crisis. Anything else could wait — assuming he was still alive to worry about it.

If he was really going with this plan, two major problems stood in front of him. The first: would the Blue Dragon and the Bronze Dragon even come?

Even with their Aspect power restored, given the state those two were in, Jace couldn't be sure they'd show up here immediately.

He just had the feeling that once the Dragonqueen broke free, her influence would be enough to bring the mentally fragile Malygos and the elusive Nozdormu to Grim Batol to back him up.

The second problem — and the big, old one — was how on earth to smash the Demon Soul. Would the brand on his arm, the one Deathwing had placed there, actually work?

Press the brand against the Demon Soul?

Or use this branded hand to crush the Demon Soul in his grip… It sounded strange either way.

Jace clenched his fist. A simple motion like that probably couldn't destroy the most indestructible artifact in the world.

But this brand… it had to mean something. Maybe use this hand's power to enchant a weapon, or… maybe the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza would know.

He stared out at the endless mountains, the forests, the rivers threading through the valleys.

The thought that his next moves would affect the most powerful dragons in the world made the whole thing feel unreal. There weren't any other choices. He'd just have to gamble.

The specific details were fuzzy, but Jace had the impression that in the novels, the mage Rhonin hadn't known the black dragon scale on the ring Deathwing gave him could destroy the Demon Soul either. He'd tried it in desperation after exhausting every other option, and to his surprise, the golden artifact had actually split open.

The funny thing was, Jace didn't know whether this brand Deathwing had placed on him could destroy the Demon Soul either. He could only try. And this situation counted as desperate enough, didn't it?

This world couldn't keep throwing nothing but obstacles in his path without a single positive coincidence, could he think. He remembered the gnome in Stormwind who'd fixed his pocket watch for him.

— Trust your own choice, Mr. Sesso. Was he a bronze dragon?

He'd better be, Jace thought.

Honestly, he'd had his suspicions ever since that room — both the gnome's name and his bafflingly powerful magic, far beyond what an apprentice mage should have been capable of.

At this point, maybe it was time to believe in a little mysticism.

The moment he tried to destroy the Demon Soul, success or not, Deathwing would immediately catch on to his intentions. If he didn't get it done in one shot… well, at least Deathwing would take some time to come flying over wreathed in world-ending fury, which would give Jace a few more moments to struggle before he died.

As long as he didn't sleep during those moments, he'd have more chances to try.

Right now, Deathwing was probably in the royal court of Lordaeron, discussing the affairs of the Kingdom of Alterac with King Terenas II and the Alliance's other envoys and nobles.

How long would it take to fly from the capital or the Alterac Mountains to Grim Batol? How fast could Deathwing fly?

Jace stopped himself from chasing these endless, boundless thoughts. Things he knew absolutely nothing about were a waste of energy no matter how much he turned them over.

He walked through the mountains for nearly two full days, until not even a wisp of mist from the swamps was visible anymore. Then, one morning, climbing a ridge and looking toward where the dark aura was thickest, Jace finally saw, on the face of an opposite mountain, a stretch of dark wall and towers that blended almost seamlessly into the cliff and rock.

Grim Batol.
