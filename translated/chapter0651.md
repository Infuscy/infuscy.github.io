## Chapter 651: Five

"Over there," Jace pointed.

"Grim Batol." Grede squinted. "I knew it. This place isn't as hard to find as everyone thinks. It's just that the evil in these mountains has scared everyone off."

"From here on out we need to be twice as careful," Vereesa said, scanning the trees and undergrowth of the nearby gorge and hollow. "The scent of red dragons and orcs is getting stronger."

"Then let's divide up the potions here, in case we run into trouble out of nowhere." Jace pulled out his potion bag. "There aren't many, because I only prepared them for myself. But I can sneak in using an orc disguise, so at least the Invisibility Potions shouldn't be as necessary for me. One each for you two."

Vereesa and Grede each took an Invisibility Potion. Jace held up the bottle of Greater Shadow Protection Potion and thought for a moment. "Who wants this? It's the most powerful Shadow protection potion I could find. I have a feeling it should be able to take a single blast of a black dragon's Shadowflame."

"There are no black dragons here," Grede said.

"You should still take it, Grede," Vereesa said. "Who knows how many warlocks are hiding in that fortress. I won't need it — I'll put an arrow through their mouths before they finish chanting their spells."

"Fine, but try to put an arrow through their mouths before they start chanting at me too, Vereesa," Grede said, glancing over. "What about you, Jace?"

Jace shook the round bottle in his hand. "I've still got a Lesser one. Since you took the Greater, remember to charge to the front once the fighting starts."

"You don't have to tell me that. I've been doing it this whole time." The dwarf tucked the Greater Shadow Protection Potion into his pocket.

"Mana Potions." Jace pulled out two and handed them to Vereesa. "If something goes wrong, don't hoard your magic, Wen. There are a lot of orcs in there — remember the spell that fires a whole bunch of magic arrows at once."

"I know." Vereesa slipped the bottles into the pocket beneath her cloak.

Jace said, "As the only spellcaster in our group, I don't think there's any controversy if I keep most of the Mana Potions and this Flask of Distilled Wisdom for myself, right?"

"Give me a Mana Potion too," Grede held out his hand. "If something really goes wrong, there'll be no telling how many orcs we'll be fighting. Summoning lightning takes a toll on me too."

"All right." Jace handed a Mana Potion to the dwarf, then eyed the full Restorative Potion sitting below it.

"This one, I'm planning to use when I deal with the head warlock. Hopefully it'll help me dispel his curse magic."

"No problem," Grede said. "Warlock versus warlock."

"Fire Protection Potions," Jace said. "Two each for you two. If we have to deal with the red dragons — whether it's dragonkin or some other monster — remember to drink up."

"Not keeping any for yourself?" Vereesa asked.

Jace tugged at his robe. "Do you have any idea how much fire essence went into making this robe? This is its time to shine. And I'm not exactly afraid of fire — I've still got my mana shield and Mage Armor to help out. You two just focus on keeping yourselves alive."

"Fine, since you're so sure, Jace," Vereesa said.

Jace handed the demon bag containing the imp to Grede. "Let Sazeno stick with you two."

"Why?" the dwarf asked.

"I can see the shadows of demons," Jace said. "My own demon even clearer. As long as you've got the imp with you, I'll know where you are. Once any orcs start heading your way, I'll find a way to stop them and buy you more room to hide."

"You never mentioned you could see demons," Vereesa said.

"Warlock's little tricks. I know a few more — I'll explain them one of these days." Jace tapped his head. "Anyway, rest easy. And Sazeno can help you contact me. If you run into a crisis you can't handle, or — you find where the Dragonqueen is being held before I do, have him shout my name."

"How?" Grede asked.

Sazeno poked his head out of the bag. "With magic, of course! It's something your puny mortal brains couldn't possibly understand!"

Jace explained, "It's a kind of demon incantation. Shouting my name so I can hear it in my head — not actually making a loud noise."

Grede asked, "Then if he can only shout your name, how do we tell different messages apart?"

"You can distinguish them by the intensity of the spell," Morophil said.

The imp said, "A strong one means we found the queen dragon we're looking for! A normal-strength one means we need help! The master knows what my normal-strength shouting sounds like."

"Then that's settled. And Morophil stays with me." Jace turned to the succubus leaning against a tree nearby, watching them. "Having her at my side will make them believe I'm a powerful enough warlock. Plus, she can intimidate the other warlocks' succubi and keep them from trying to mess with my mind."

"Keep him safe, Morophil," Vereesa said.

"Rest assured, I won't let him die before I do," Morophil replied. "I hope you mean that," Vereesa said, staring at her.

"Next." Jace stood up, straightened his pack, and pulled out Krasus's transformation orb. "Morophil and I will make contact with them from the front and draw their attention. You two try to find another way into Grim Batol."

"Leave it to me," Grede said. "A city this massive has got to have windows or small doors, cracks you can squeeze through — especially one that's been abandoned this long. My father told me Grim Batol has several rings inside, split into multiple levels, a bit like Ironforge. When he left, a lot of the walls had already been blown to pieces by the Dark Iron dwarves. I've seen paintings of Grim Batol's interior, and I've heard the elders tell stories about the place. With me as your guide, don't worry too much."

Listening to Grede, Jace recalled how the Grim Batol dungeon map in the game was just ring after ring wrapped around the central Iron Anvil.

He'd originally been planning to rely on his vague memory of the game's map, combined with the library's name, to brief Grede and Vereesa on what the inside might look like. But since Grede had said all that, and given the gap between the game and reality he'd already realized, he figured there was no point in lecturing them. A game scene couldn't possibly beat a Wildhammer dwarf's own depiction.

"Sounds solid, Grede," he said. "Then I'm counting on you," Vereesa said.

"Of course." Grede puffed out his chest. "Still… three of us attacking Grim Batol. If anyone else had told me that, I never would have believed them. This is one of the most terrifying fortresses in the world."

Morophil said, "I believe it's five, dwarf."

"That's right," Vereesa said. "Five."

"Indeed," the dwarf said.

"It's five!" the imp laughed.

"Five of us," Jace nodded. He straightened his robe, tucked the Flowing Ice Crystal into his chest, strapped the sheath of Skysong tight, stowed the Blade of Endless Darkness, cinched every pouch on his waist, placed the fearsome-looking purple demon bag on the outside, propped up Gorefiend's staff, pulled the orb from his pack, took a deep breath, and gazed up at that towering wall.

Holy Light, the Sun, Elune, the Earth Mother, or the bronze dragons — if any of you can actually protect me, now's the time.
