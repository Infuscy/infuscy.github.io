Chapter 653: The Dragonmaw Warlord

The deeper Jace followed the orc officer into Grim Batol, the more grateful he was that he hadn't tried to brief Grede on the interior based on the game's dungeon map.

The dungeon map in the game had only a single level, but in reality Grim Batol had many levels and many rings, and the connections between them weren't simple at all. If the officer hadn't been leading the way the whole time, Jace would have had no idea how long it would have taken him to find his own way around. He could only hope Grede could figure it out.

It was still daytime. Surrounded by a sea of orcs, Jace didn't dare pull out his pocket watch, but it should have been noon at the earliest, maybe just past into afternoon. He still couldn't sense any sign of the imp entering. Grede and Vereesa probably wanted to wait for nightfall to slip in.

That was a sensible choice. Jace had a feeling he wouldn't make any progress this afternoon either.

The ultimate goal of this trip into the city was, of course, to find a way into the chamber where the Dragonqueen was imprisoned. Once he was inside, Nekros would surely accompany him. And if he was going to enter Alexstrasza's prison, he'd definitely bring the Demon Soul with him.

Right now this artifact was the only thing propping up Nekros's grip on the Dragonmaw orcs at Grim Batol. Even under normal circumstances he probably kept it on him at all times, just in case — never mind when going to see the Dragonqueen.

In any case, even though Alexstrasza was currently bound in chains enchanted by the Demon Soul, she was still one of the most powerful beings in the world. Nekros was a talented warlock with considerable strength, but without the Demon Soul's protection, he probably wouldn't dare enter that room and face a red dragon's fury head-on.

Jace could reasonably guess that the chamber holding the Dragonqueen should be in the deepest part of Grim Batol. The most likely spot was the lowest level — and sure enough, when they crossed another bridge over an abyss, he glanced down and counted the layers below. There were two more districts deeper down from where he stood.

Jace had a rough plan forming in his head.

Enter the Dragonqueen's prison. Nekros would come in with the Demon Soul. Then defeat him, seize the Demon Soul, and find a way to destroy it before Deathwing and all of Grim Batol could react.

If the destruction process didn't go smoothly, the three of them could hold the prison gate and, with the red dragon's help, fight off the orc army trying to get in while they kept trying to shatter the Demon Soul.

After all, once this thing was in his hands, the Dragonqueen and every red dragon in Grim Batol would have no reason to keep serving the Dragonmaw orcs.

Of course, Nekros wouldn't lightly take him into that prison. He'd need to earn the warlord's trust and give him a reason he couldn't refuse.

If worst came to worst, he'd have to test whether Morophil could forcibly charm Nekros into taking him in.

He couldn't drag things out too long in Grim Batol. The longer he dragged on, the higher the chances he, Vereesa, and Grede would be discovered and exposed.

And Jace wasn't planning on sleeping.

He didn't want to sleep in Grim Batol. Because if he didn't sleep, he wouldn't dream, and he didn't want to talk to Deathwing again. He didn't want to fall into another nightmare woven by that dragon.

Jace figured he could stay sane through at most three nights without sleep, so he had four days at most to resolve everything.

But would not sleeping really keep Deathwing from influencing him?

He thought of the black dragon brand suddenly flaring with power at the gate of Grim Batol, and the low murmur that had seemed to drift through.

This wasn't the time for wild speculation.

Following the orc officer through a narrow passage, he emerged from a gateway into sudden, sweeping openness.

It was a vast circular hall, at the center of which stood a furnace that could only be called magnificent. One after another, black anvils were built along the furnace's rim. This must be the heart of Grim Batol.

Much as Grede loathed the Dark Iron dwarves and everything about them, Jace still felt this city resembled the center of Shadowforge City in the game more than Ironforge — the area in the middle of Blackrock Depths guarded by a fire elemental lord, both in architectural style and in the overall feel it gave off.

But whether it was more like Shadowforge City or Ironforge, Grim Batol's center was enough to leave a person in awe. Jace did his best not to gawk around like some country bumpkin who'd never seen the world, and instead maintained a calm expression as he followed the officer. After all, a warlock from Blackrock Spire, by the standards of his cover identity, shouldn't be impressed by a sight like this.

Finally, after climbing several flights of stairs, the officer brought Jace to a stone door. The broad doorframe was carved with dwarven runes Jace couldn't read. Judging by what he'd seen at Aerie Peak, this had probably been the residence of some elder when the Wildhammer dwarves still lived in Grim Batol.

The orc officer stepped aside and struck his chest. "Go in, warlock. The Warlord is waiting inside."

Jace didn't look at him, just gave a casual nod and ducked through the low doorway.

Beyond the dwarf-low door was an upward-sloping ramp, dark enough that he could barely see his hand in front of his face.

He climbed to the top and emerged into a fairly spacious hall. Several orcs stood at the far end, deep in discussion. When Jace appeared, they all turned to stare at the doorway at once.

High-hung braziers burned, illuminating their faces. He could tell they ranged in age, all wearing different styles of armor and carrying short swords or other close weapons. When they saw Jace arrive, they moved to the sides and sat, making way for a gray-haired old orc in the center.

This old orc was bare from the waist up, his upper body solid as a bull's, sporting a fat, protruding belly. Hanging from his waist were pouch after pouch, along with a short hammer-mace. He looked like a savage gladiator. Next to him, Jace's own transformation really did come across as a scrawny scholar.

So where was Nekros? Where was the warlock?

The question had barely crossed his mind when Jace noticed the old orc in the center was missing a leg. One of his legs was a prosthetic.

"Greetings, envoy from Blackrock Spire." The old orc's voice was hoarse and low, and his tone carried a distinct edge of hostility. "We have never met. I am Nekros. Tell me your name."

So he was actually Nekros? A creature who looked more savage than a barbarian and more powerful than a strongman… was supposed to be the rumored Shadow prodigy, the finest warlock of the Dragonmaw clan?

Jace suddenly recalled his surname — Skullcrusher. Seeing the man in person, he understood this was likely a title bestowed on him by other orcs. Combined to the multi-spiked hammer-mace hanging at his waist, Jace realized this was probably a Skullcrusher in the literal sense. And warlock… was probably just his side gig.

No wonder the Dragonmaw orcs had so naturally accepted him as their Warlord rather than some kind of magic advisor.

"I am Yuruk." Jace touched his chest with one hand and lifted his chin slightly. "I pay my respects, Dragonmaw Warlord. Warchief Rend is most grateful for your efforts in defending Grim Batol."

"Is he now?" the old orc said. "I seriously doubt that."
