Chapter 655: Shaking Your Resolve

Leaving that gloomy room, Jace felt a twinge of disappointment.

When Nekros had agreed to let Jace tour the fortress, he'd thought the Warlord was going to show him the Dragonqueen personally. He hadn't expected the man to send his own son instead. And even if his son took him to see the Dragonqueen, it would be pointless — Nekros wouldn't be there, the Demon Soul wouldn't be there, and the whole effort would be wasted.

He didn't give a damn about how many orcs Grim Batol had or whether they could hold off the Alliance, so the tour that followed was long and dull — an endless parade of dwarven statues, houses and walls, chasms in every direction, and ruins…

When they reached the lowest level, Nakrosh led him into a hall no smaller than the Iron Anvil.

The moment he stepped inside, a wave of fervent energy hit him in the face. The dazzling red glow before his eyes was so intense that for a moment he couldn't make out what was happening at all.

"This is the Hatching Hall."

Nakrosh pointed below — to an entire vast chamber filled with red dragon eggs.

These eggs contained an extremely fierce life force, fierce enough to make Morophil standing beside him uncomfortable.

Every egg was plump and beautiful, its rough shell covered in dark red spikes and crimson mana crystals. If this Warlord were given a few years, he might really be able to raise another powerful red dragon legion.

This must be where the dragon eggs were that Deathwing had been so eager for him to get his hands on.

But more than the eggs, Jace was far more interested in the heavy door on the far side of the hall. "Where does that door lead?"

"That door will not open without my Father's permission," the young Nakrosh said. "And it has nothing to do with our tour. You can see how magnificent these eggs are, warlock. They are the future of Grim Batol and the entire Horde."

"Truly. Breathtaking," Jace agreed, already all but certain the Dragonqueen was behind that door.

So Nekros wasn't even willing to let him catch a glimpse of the Dragonqueen? Or maybe he didn't dare let his own son see the Dragonqueen — worried that without the Demon Soul as leverage, Nakrosh might suddenly drop dead in that room.

In the evening, the Dragonmaw clan arranged a fairly tidy room for Jace to rest in. Sitting in that little house, Jace was furious.

He felt along the walls and corners, making sure the Dragonmaw orcs weren't monitoring him with some strange magical item or spell, before sitting down.

"Don't be so tense, Master. If they tried to eavesdrop on you, I'd be the first to notice," Morophil said lazily from where she leaned. "That warlock wouldn't even bother trying such a useless stunt if he had the faintest understanding of succubi."

"So can you just charm Nekros for me?" Jace asked.

"Unfortunately, his will is very strong." Morophil sighed softly. "His resistance to Shadow is not much weaker than yours. What's more, he doesn't seem all that devoted to dark magic, yet his mana is no less than any zealous warlock's. He's an extremely gifted Shadow manipulator too."

Jace wasn't surprised in the least. If the man hadn't been that gifted, Zuluhed wouldn't have let him wield the Demon Soul.

Morophil went on, "And as a warlock, the moment he realized I was trying to charm him, he'd turn on you with open hostility. Then you'd have the whole city bearing down on you. That's too much risk."

"So that's a no," Jace said.

"…Not entirely, either." Morophil smiled. "If I'd been freshly summoned by you, I probably couldn't have done a thing to him. But I'm different now, thanks to Master's feeding of power, and those powerful runes…"

Hearing that, Jace rubbed his temples and glanced at the runes he'd carved into the succubus's chest. "Get to the point…"

Morophil said, "If his mana is drained to the point of exhaustion, or his spirit is badly wounded, I can enslave him directly and suppress his will."

"So in other words, I beat him to a pulp first," Jace said. "And if I'm fighting him openly in the middle of Grim Batol, what do I need you to charm him for?"

"Since we can't fight, then there's another option," Morophil said. "If his will wavers, if he's in a moment of hesitation, I can try to seize the chance and subtly influence his mind with Shadow. This can't fully control the other party's mind — it can only nudge their thoughts a little — but it's almost impossible to detect."

"Have you ever used this 'influence' on me?" Jace asked.

"That's also a form of charm, Master," Morophil said at once. "I obey Master's orders. I would never cast this magic on you."

"Fine. Either way, it's at least a workable path," Jace said. "Let me see if I've got this right — I need to find a way to make him hesitate, make him seriously consider taking me to see the Dragonqueen, and then you can try to nudge his will from 'won't take me' toward 'will take me'?"

"More or less," Morophil said.

Jace frowned. So how was he supposed to make him hesitate?

He didn't want to ask the Warlord directly to see the Dragonqueent — that would be too deliberate, too obviously suspicious. He had to handle it more naturally.

He needed to make Nekros feel that only by letting this Blackrock envoy witness the mighty Dragonqueen with his own eyes — rather than these ragged mongrels running around Grim Batol — and letting him represent Blackrock Spire in witnessing Grim Batol's true strength, would Warchief Rend reconsider the black dragon alliance.

Also, this Warlord was too confident in his fortress. He needed to give him a sense of crisis. He needed to make him feel these dragon eggs wouldn't be battle-ready the moment the Alliance arrived at the gates — only then could his will be shaken into considering unleashing the Dragonqueen and her consorts into the fight.

So how?

Jace's frown deepened. Morophil had already leaned against his shoulder, her hand drifting slowly downward. "Master, don't fret so. You've got a whole night to get through. Why don't you let me try the orcish—"

"Cut it out," Jace brushed her hand away. "In the middle of an orc stronghold?"

Morophil suddenly lifted a strand of silver hair and asked coldly, "Master won't do it with me, yet he's happy to sneak around with that elf?"

Jace blinked, staring at the hair. "What the hell are you… where'd you get that?"

"Under Master's robe. There's another one on your trousers." Morophil smiled and picked up a second strand. "You're so worried about us having fun exposing you, yet you don't worry at all about elf hair stuck to your clothes?"

"Damn it…"

The three of them had slept in cramped spaces huddled together for warmth more times than he could count. The great swamp nearly froze them to death before it could freeze Deathwing. Jace hadn't washed his clothes this whole journey — it must have gotten stuck on there who-knows-when.

Jace snatched the two strands of hair back, but an idea struck him in the same moment.

"Don't cause trouble," he said. "Slip out of Grim Batol right now and find Vereesa and Grede. Tell them not to come into the city tonight… Find a way to take out a small patrol — a Dragonmaw orc patrol — tonight. Make sure you leave no trace… nothing that ties back to the Wildhammer dwarves or the elves. No arrows, nothing. The more professional the better. I want Nekros to know the Alliance is at his doorstep."

"Ah, I see…" Morophil smiled.

"That's right." Jace pinched the hair. "I — a member of the Shadow Council, a student of Gul'dan, wielder of Gorefiend's staff, a Blackrock Spire warlock skilled at tracking magic — will find every trace of the elves that all the Dragonmaw orcs couldn't. That'll be enough to prove that Quel'Thalas's most elite Farstriders are ready to dismantle Grim Batol's defenses, and that the Alliance's assault on Grim Batol is about to begin in full force… Of course, there's no need to make Nekros actually believe it. I just need this bastard to be a little less confident. Concentrate and watch his state, Morophil. Look for your opening and seize it, just like you said. Don't forget our ultimate goal: making him decide to take me to see the Dragonqueen personally."

"If we take care of the orcs, have Sazeno shout my name — one strong, one weak. Remember, strong first, then weak… twice. I hope his mana's enough. If it's not, have him drink a potion! Tell them sooner rather than later, and come back as early as you can. Don't let them notice the demon at my side has suddenly vanished."

"And tell them where the Dragonqueen is. If they need to get in, they might have to use the Invisibility Potions."

"I understand, Master."

With that, Morophil melted into a shadow. When Jace tried to track her silhouette with his demon detection, he'd already lost her — she'd darted out the window and reached the distant street.
