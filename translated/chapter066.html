Chapter 66: The Wall at Night

Jace followed the middle-aged man inside. The room held two young men who looked like apprentices or apprentice mages, plus an older woman. Morganth was the eldest of the group — he must have been the teacher here.

"I'm Master Malin's assistant. Master Malin asked me to deliver a package to an apprentice named Martin Harlow." Jace held out the package. "There's something inside, along with a letter."

"I'm Martin Harlow." One of the young apprentices stepped forward and took it.

"Eden actually sent an assistant all the way here with a delivery?" the woman said skeptically. "And such a young one, at that."

Jace said, "Master Malin hired me as an assistant specifically to help with the Redridge business, ma'am."

"I see." Morganth asked, "Do you know what's inside?"

Jace shook his head. "I haven't opened it."

"Well, regardless, stay the night here." Morganth pointed to the right. "Go out the door, turn right, and the room at the end is empty. There's no key, but this is an army fort — no need to worry about safety. You can sleep easy."

The apprentice set the package down on the table and began discussing something in a low voice with the other young man.

"Teacher, look." The one named Martin unfurled the orcish battle standard inside and held it up for Morganth to see.

Jace said, "Then I'll take my leave, Master."

He'd barely turned around when Morganth called after him. "Do you know what this banner represents?"

Jace glanced back. "That's the emblem of the Dragonmaw clan."

Morganth opened the letter, read through it, and said, "It seems Master Malin had good reason to have you help him track down the red dragons, Mr. Sesso. Malin is right — the Dragonmaw orcs didn't follow the rest of the Horde through the Dark Portal and flee Azeroth. They've maintained a presence here, and they're still enslaving the Red Dragonflight. This banner has put paid to my earlier hypothesis."

"That said, the letter claims this was found by the mages at Menethil Harbor in Khaz Modan, not Blackrock Mountain — which doesn't quite match his earlier theory either. He wants me back in Stormwind. Andromath is organizing an expedition to push deep into the Wetlands for reconnaissance… to find the Dragonmaw orcs' true stronghold."

"Could it be Grim Batol?" the female mage asked.

"Do you really believe orcs could find that cursed dwarven city?" Morganth said. "And no living soul has come out of Grim Batol in the two hundred years since the Wildhammer dwarves vanished."

"I've heard there are dragons living there," Martin said.

Morganth said, "Some claim to have seen red dragons there, or black ones. True or not, it's an extremely dangerous place. Perhaps Andromath shares your suspicions, but I don't think launching a deep expedition into the Wetlands right now is a sensible decision."

He sighed and sank into his chair, looking at his students. "Our High Sorcerer Andromath wants to achieve deeds surpassing even the Kirin Tor's. He wants to uncover the connection between these green-skinned invaders and the red dragons before Archmages Antonidas and Krasus do. And Eden Malin is a man who'll stop at nothing for status and fame — he may even crave it more than Andromath. What a perfect pair those two make. But since the Wizard's Sanctum wants to mount an expedition… damn it all, I have no choice but to comply. After all, I'm just as trapped in these people's games as anyone else."

Morganth gave a wry laugh and pressed a hand to his forehead. "I'll write a reply letter tonight. Tomorrow I'll entrust it to you, Mr. Sesso, to deliver back to Eden. Oh — and what I just said… don't tell him."

Jace agreed, turned, and had just opened the door when a guard came up the stairs two at a time, brushing past him into the room.

"Master Morganth, we've found traces of dark magic in the woods to the east. The colonel thinks that gnoll shadow mage has shown himself again. The last few hunts all failed — this time the colonel means to lead the troops himself to wipe it out. He wants you to come along and help. He wants to put an end to this menace once and for all and be done with Stonewatch's trouble."

Hearing that, Morganth stood. "I'll go at once. Tell the colonel I'm ready."

Jace walked to the door of the room Morganth had indicated, but by then Morganth had already hurried downstairs with the guard.

Given what Jace knew about what Morganth would eventually do, he couldn't shake the feeling that the man wasn't heading out to help the soldiers hunt that gnoll warlock at all — but rather to scavenge the mage's belongings. Spells, artifacts, and the like, for his own use.

Or maybe he was actually helping the gnoll warlock escape the hunt. For all Jace knew, Morganth was already in league with the Redridge gnolls.

Then again, he supposed he was projecting a bit.

Jace set his bag down and walked straight out the door at the end of the corridor, out of the fort proper.

He climbed up onto the wall and looked out toward the eastern woods, wanting to see for himself what these supposed traces of dark magic actually were.

It was already quite dark. The sun had sunk behind the Redridge Mountains, leaving only a sliver of red afterglow struggling to keep the sky from going fully black.

Even so — even with Jace's sensitivity to dark magic — he could still feel a faint, sinister aura drifting from those woods.

Back in the courtyard below, the sound of hoofbeats was already rising. Soldiers shouted, and the colonel's voice rang out above the rest.

In the game, the most powerful gnoll warlock in Redridge was a fellow named Fangore — which in the local Redridge tongue meant "Bloody Claw," a name that said everything you needed to know about the gnoll's cruelty.

And even more terrifying than his name was his dark magic. Jace didn't know whether Fangore had been born yet, or whether he'd already amassed that much power — but the dark energy prowling those woods meant that at least some warlocks of his caliber had already emerged among the gnoll tribes.

Then he heard a door open and close. It was that apprentice Martin, who'd also come up onto the wall to look east.

Jace took the opportunity to ask, "Can you feel the shadow energy in there, Mr. Harlow?"

"What dark aura?" Martin glanced over. "It's too far from here — you can't see a thing. By the way, you're just an assistant, aren't you, Mr. Sesso?"

"But I think there really might be something dark in there," Jace pressed on.

"The soldiers here are all veterans of the Orc Wars. Their sensitivity to shadow is far sharper than mine," Martin said. "But that doesn't mean your perception is necessarily accurate — sensing magic takes talent, too."

Jace kept quiet. What bothered him wasn't so much Martin's dismissal as his own abnormal sensitivity to shadow.

The apprentice couldn't feel a thing, yet Jace could see something.

Perhaps each use of shadow power, each stirring of the darkness within him, had deepened his connection to the Shadow — enough that he could detect anomalies in those woods more keenly than this apprentice could.

Or maybe it was just his imagination. Jace ran to the watchtower on the other side of the wall, climbed the ladder, and reached the sentry's usual lookout post, scrutinizing the woods more carefully.

Wasn't this a perfect opportunity to train his magical perception?

He searched the woods carefully for the direction the Stonewatch cavalry had taken. He could faintly hear hoofbeats and the knights' war cries, but with the canopy overhead, he couldn't pin down their exact heading.

"I hope they make it back safe." Martin, down on the wall below, sounded like he was talking to Jace — or maybe to himself. With that, he headed back toward the fort.

Jace had barely glanced his way when Martin, before he even reached the door, was suddenly yanked off the wall into the darkness below with a sharp whoosh through the air — not even a scream.
