## Chapter 67: Surprise Attack

What the hell?

Jace wanted to look over the wall's edge, but that would be far too dangerous.

If an apprentice had been dragged away this silently, how could someone like him, a mere assistant, hope to escape the same fate?

Who had attacked him? Orcs?

Unlikely. The Blackrock orcs were currently locked in a massive brawl with the Dark Iron dwarves up in northern Redridge. If they'd come through the Burning Steppes and into the Redridge gorge in force, the Lakeshire militia patrols would have spotted them long before.

By all rights, aside from the odd wandering orc shaman, the whole of Redridge shouldn't have had any host bold enough to assault a fort.

Which left only one possibility — gnolls. After all, it couldn't be murlocs.

The local gnolls belonged to the Darkhide tribe, the darkest and most savage tribe in the entire Kingdom of Stormwind. If it was them… should he hole up inside the fort, or hold this watchtower?

Counting this one, the fort had four watchtowers of varying sizes. Right now, the other towers still had sentries holding up torches — only the central tower at the very top of the fort was unguarded at the moment.

He'd barely had a few seconds to decide on a strategy when a strange, cackling laugh suddenly came from behind him. Jace turned. A clump of brownish-black fur crested the wall, and a dog-like head popped up, its swollen, jaundiced-yellow eyes locking right onto him.

When the gnoll spotted Jace, it shouted, "Ha… fresh meat!"

No choice — he had to hold this tower!

Jace grabbed his long staff and charged, bringing it down hard on the gnoll's head. The gnoll yelped, lost its grip, and tumbled off the wall.

Just as he was letting out a breath of relief, an iron grappling hook came flying up with a clang, nearly catching him around the neck. Jace stumbled back a few steps. The hook's prong scraped across his ribs, drawing a thin line of blood, then bit deep into the wall and the rope attached to it began shuddering violently.

These gnolls were actually climbing the wall with grappling hooks?!

The hooks were enormous, bristling with barbs. The rope wasn't hemp — it was an iron chain!

This had to be Horde siege equipment, abandoned here after the war and picked up — or looted — by the gnolls, who'd turned it against the human walls!

Jace pulled a rusty longsword off the nearby weapon rack, trying to pry the hook loose. But he hadn't expected the grapple to bite in so deep — especially now that the gnolls below had started climbing, their weight pulling the hook even more firmly into the stone.

He'd barely managed to work it loose when an iron blade came swinging up from below without warning and smashed against his sword. His hands went numb from the shock. He tried to keep prying, but the gnoll wielding the blade scrambled up the chain in a flash and slashed at him horizontally!

Jace ducked his head back just in time and retreated several paces. The gnoll in front of him wasn't as tall as Jace, but its fur was far darker than the gnolls in Elwynn Forest, its body covered in spots, its arms and legs like skin stretched over bones — it looked even more unhinged and cunning.

It clearly didn't take Jace seriously, charging straight at him with a gleeful bark. Jace sidestepped the gnoll's first overhead chop and drove his sword deep into the thick mane of fur around its neck.

The gnoll's excited howl turned into a choked sputter. It whined, shuddered, and collapsed to the ground, thrashing a few times before it couldn't get back up.

Jace realized this was a gnoll raid — but the fort's main force had already ridden out through the gates after that so-called gnoll shadow mage. A diversion?

He grabbed a torch and waved it frantically toward the watchtower on the other side, shouting at the top of his lungs, "Gnoll raid! We're under attack by gnolls!"

He'd been shouting for quite a while before he noticed that the shadows atop the other tower, lit by torchlight, were already the hunched, twisted silhouettes of gnolls… The sentries there had already been taken out!

Stonewatch was descending into chaos. The clang of metal, screams, howls, the gnolls' shrieks — it was already too late to warn everyone inside. Pointless.

He had to warn the cavalry in the woods and get them to fall back and defend the fort.

Stonewatch was in such an uproar that even if some corners were still quiet, shouting into the woods from here wouldn't accomplish anything. Wasn't there some horn or bell or something that could carry a warning?

Then he remembered — when he'd first entered the fort, there had been a bugler messenger. But where were they now?

This little tower at the top of the fort had no horn, no bonfire — nothing that could make a conspicuous signal. There was only a single torch. What if he hurled the torch into the sky?

Just as he was scrambling for ideas, another gnoll leaped up onto the wall, waving its axe and cackling like a lunatic.

No… staying here much longer would only get him killed by waves of gnolls.

Jace didn't even have the "two fists can't beat four pals" problem — the ones coming up now were just gnoll grunts. If a real warrior came up, he'd be in deep trouble all on his own.

He had to get back to his room, find his warlock's bag, and let the imp out.

Wait… the imp?!

Right — the imp could throw fireballs. He could have it shoot them into the sky as signal flares!

The gnoll swung its axe in a wild arc. It didn't have much strength — the axe looked like a massive orcish greataxe, absurdly heavy and unwieldy.

Jace dodged it easily. The gnoll smashed the axe into the stone wall, and before it could steady itself, Jace ran it through from behind with his sword. The gnoll let out a hoarse, rattling groan and toppled off the wall, landing on the wall below with a heavy thud.

That spot — where the apprentice Martin had been standing — was now crowded with several gnolls.

They were frantically hammering at the iron-studded wooden door. That irritating clanging noise from before had been them. But precisely because of all that racket, these gnolls hadn't even noticed Jace fighting their companions in the tower right above their heads.

That fallen gnoll had gone down hard enough, and still none of them had noticed.

"Sathrovarr!"

Jace called out in a flurried voice, not really expecting the imp to hear. Because if he raised his voice any louder, he'd draw every single one of those gnolls below — and then he'd be in real trouble.

Better to pick off the ones climbing up one by one. That way he'd last a little longer.

He was still working through that thought when the sound of claws scraping stone came from behind him.

This gnoll leaped up wielding two short axes. The moment it steadied itself, it let out a howl and swung them back and forth like some kind of circus act.

Jace's head was already full of the techniques Grede had drilled into him — keep your distance, blade pointed forward.
