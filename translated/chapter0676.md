## Chapter 676: The Chain of Will

Evening. The Pig and Whistle Inn.

The difference between the Gilded Rose and the Pig and Whistle… was almost like the difference between what you saw as a tourist in Stormwind and what it actually felt like to live there.

What you saw at the Pig and Whistle was a little more real and unvarnished than the Gilded Rose. At least that's how Jace felt.

If he was going to drink with Grede, he'd rather spend the better part of an hour crossing half of Stormwind to come here than go to the closer Gilded Rose — especially after an adventure like the one they'd just had.

Grede was telling the barmaid what food to bring up. Jace stared at the chain around his neck and, once the barmaid left, asked, "Have you ever tried what that necklace of yours can actually do?"

"Haven't tried it." Grede pulled the gem from inside his shirt — it gave off a faint, dark golden glow. "Guide my direction… Honestly, I wanted the Dragonqueen to explain it more clearly, but would I have looked like an idiot if I'd asked that?"

Jace looked at the gem. "I don't think so. You saved her — how could she think you're an idiot? Maybe it makes your transformation bigger? Makes your skin harder, something like that?"

"I haven't tried transforming since we got back," Grede said. "What about you — the essence the Dragonqueen gave you… what exactly is it?"

"Last night I found a place and tested some fire magic." Jace grabbed a piece of smoked jerky from the plate the barmaid had brought and stuffed it into his mouth, chewing as he spoke. "Looks like it doesn't just make it easier for me to summon fire elementals — it even makes me more fireproof."

"Fireproof?" Grede asked. "You mean not afraid of heat, or not afraid of burning…"

"My clothes could catch fire and I wouldn't get hurt." Jace showed Grede his wrists and neck. "These places were all burned. Look — not a trace left."

"So they are," Grede said. "That outfit you're wearing today — you actually look like somebody with a bit of money now. You planning to wear that to see your parents?"

"Yes," Jace said.

Grede said, "Then you'd better be careful. Don't accidentally set your clothes on fire along the way."

"Can you say something nice for once?" Jace glared at him. "These clothes cost two or three gold, Grede! This time I really bled for them."

Grede sucked in a breath. "Damn, you actually spent that much… Did you get those clothes just to go to Quel'Thalas?"

Jace said, "Trying to out-dress those elven nobles — two or three gold wouldn't make any difference, and even twenty or thirty gold probably wouldn't either. I just thought a demon-cloth robe would make me look too evil. Those elves would recognize it for sure. But my usual everyday clothes would've been too shabby. So at least I dressed up properly, to show a formal attitude."

Grede took his beer mug from the barmaid. "Makes sense. Then leaving the fire stuff aside, anything else? What other benefits does the essence's blessing have?"

"Still not sure. It might have improved my constitution, but I don't know how to test it yet," Jace said. "Why don't I punch you and we'll find out. Simple and direct."

"Get fucked," Jace said flatly.

Grede chuckled, then lowered his voice. "I'll ask, then — what's it like being held by the Dragonqueen?"

"What?" Jace blinked.

"That was the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza, for fuck's sake!" Grede leaned across the table. "The one holding you was a terrifying monster out of myth and legend. A real red dragon. The queen of the red dragons! She was probably born when the world was created, the kind of being that makes her name on the very first page of the history books."

"So her… uh." The dwarf patted his chest and waggled his eyebrows. "Big?"

"You son of a…" Jace stared at him and cleared his throat. "Big."

Grede slapped his forehead. "I knew it, you little brat. Couldn't wait to take advantage of her."

"Take advantage? She was the one holding me… not me holding her."

Jace suddenly lowered his voice, because the barmaid came by again with a plate of apple pie.

The two clinked glasses and took a sip. After seeing the barmaid off, Grede said, "All right, no more jokes, Jace. What about your chain?"

At the mention of the chain, Jace's expression turned serious all at once.

Ever since he'd gotten this so-called "Chain of Will," he'd been reluctant to bring it up — reluctant even to think about it.

Hearing Grede ask, Jace took the iron chain that had been hanging around his neck and set it on the table.

It landed with a clatter — it really did look like nothing more than an ordinary dark iron chain. Though compared to an ordinary chain, it bore no trace of rust at all, and its smooth surface held a faint hint of purple-red that you'd have trouble making out unless you were right up close. Was this the color of Elementium?

Elementium was unlike any other metal in Azeroth. It was said to come from the Elemental Plane, a supremely powerful substance created by the elementals themselves. Forging a weapon or suit of armor from this metal required extraordinarily special craftsmanship.

If the Elemental Lords had relied on it to fight the Old Gods in the ancient wars, then the material of this chain — even among Elementium — was probably the highest grade there was.

So it was easy to see that the elementals had zero interest in making the things they created look attractive.

"The Dragonqueen said this was created by the Elemental Lords together." Grede swallowed. He seemed to want to reach out and touch it, but pulled his hand back at last. "The Elemental Lords — you mean the mother of the Stone Princess? What's her name…"

"Therazane," Jace said.

"Right." Grede set down his beer. "And Ragnaros, the Fire Lord the Dark Iron dwarves worship?"

Jace nodded. "And the other two — the Water Lord and the Wind Lord. Legend says they ruled this world a long, long time ago."

"Sounds terrifying, especially after seeing that Stone Princess." Grede stared at the iron chain. "Since such terrifying monsters made this thing, have you tried what it can actually do?"

"I summoned a demon from the Void, my friend," Jace said. "I plan to test it on that demon first and see whether this chain can help me suppress his will. That demon isn't very strong, but for the initial stage I don't want to make things too difficult either."

After all, every being that had possessed this chain before — whether the Elemental Lords or Deathwing himself — had failed to resist the temptation of the Old Gods. In the end they had all become the Old Gods' slaves.

That was enough to show this chain didn't grant absolutely incorruptible will and spirit the moment you got your hands on it. It had to have its flaws — a defect that even the Elemental Lords and the black dragon hadn't been able to escape.

The Dragonqueen believed he didn't have that flaw, which was why she was willing to entrust it to him.

But Alexstrasza hadn't explicitly said what exactly made him different from them.

Of course, he was, after all, a mortal, and all the previous owners of the Chain of Will had been practically gods by comparison. There were far too many differences.

But Jace couldn't immediately think of a quality they lacked and he possessed that would make him more suited to wield this artifact than those ancient, powerful beings.

"Fighting a demon, huh. All right. So when are you planning to set out for Lordaeron?" Grede broke into his thoughts.

Jace took a sip of beer. "As soon as possible."

Grede smiled slightly. "I know you can't wait to see our little elf again, but you know Winter Veil's coming up, Jace. Ship tickets are hard to come by, so you'll have to be patient."
