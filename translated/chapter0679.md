## Chapter 679: A Proposal

This should have been Jace's second time entering Stormwind Keep.

Ever since he'd learned the King wanted to see him, he'd been uneasy these past few days.

He'd always been nervous about occasions like this. He didn't like receiving honors, especially didn't like being praised in front of a huge crowd. He'd been extremely uncomfortable the first time he met Varian Wrynn at Stonewatch, and he'd made a fool of himself on top of it.

"We should go in, Jace. Those soldiers are all heading inside," Grede said quietly. It was a good thing the dwarf was with him this time, he thought.

Following the palace promenade upward, every time they passed a Stormwind royal guardsman in heavy armor and a cloak, the man would salute the two of them. Jace nodded back to each of them a little sheepishly.

"What do you think the King will give us?" Grede asked in a low voice. "A gift? No idea," Jace said.

"Didn't those old men at the Wizard Sanctum tell you?" Grede asked.

"They said I might be admitted into the Royal Mages," Jace said. "And other things — the King has a gift prepared for you too, but I think you should ask for something yourself."

"That's what I was thinking too," Grede nodded. "And I've already got something in mind."

"What?" Jace asked.

"Wait and see," Grede said. "You'll want me to ask for it too, I guarantee you."

The guards deep in the palace called out his and Grede's names. Walking all the way to the throne room at the very top, Jace saw Varian Wrynn sitting on his throne, watching their approach.

Seeing this fellow sitting there so properly, Jace still felt it was a little out of place. But Varian wasn't wearing a crown or any circlet representing his status as ruler — just his signature military-looking blue outfit, like a rebel leader who'd charged into the palace, beaten the real king half to death, and stolen the throne for a joyride.

The murmuring among the nobles in the hall was growing louder by the minute. The King raised his voice. "Silence. Jace Sesso and Grede of House Thunderfist have arrived."

Varian Wrynn said something quietly to the tall knight beside him — Lord Bolvar Fordragon. The Lord nodded, then immediately rose from his seat and strode down the steps toward Jace.

He threw his arms around Jace and hugged him hard. "They wouldn't let me come find you, Jace. After Nethergarde Keep, I should have come to you — that was my fault." In an instant, Varian Wrynn transformed from that fellow on the throne into a young man only a little older than Jace himself.

He gripped Jace's shoulders, then looked at Grede. "Death-wing, right, Jace? You entered the Dragonmaw's fortress and stood face-to-face with that terrible black dragon who's plagued the world for God knows how long, and ultimately prevailed. You rescued the Dragonqueen and made it so the orcs could enslave her children no more. I hope that queen honors her promise and stops standing against the Alliance — that way we'll never have to live under that red shadow again."

"Mr. Grede Thunderfist, this comes from the bottom of my heart. The Alliance owes you thanks — not just Stormwind and Ironforge, but the entire Alliance."

A noble beside them said, "Indeed it does, Your Majesty — especially Kul Tiras. They had countless warships burned by the red dragons during the war. They have blood feud with the Dragonmaw orcs."

When he'd actually reached the Wetlands, Jace had felt that maybe even getting his name restored wasn't that important. He just wanted to survive, to not drag the world in a worse direction, to have at least some positive influence… and, in the end, to not let the black dragons have their way.

Hearing the King's words, he truly comprehended the impact of what he, Grede, and Vereesa had done.

"I think the time has come." Varian looked at the mages in the throne hall. "I sincerely invite you into the Court Mages, Jace. With you joining us, my magic advisers will be far more than a match for the evil forces within the kingdom. This is a decision already discussed and agreed upon by the previous royal council and the Court Mages — not a whim of mine. And it was the agreement between us, wasn't it?"

"It is, Your Majesty." Jace lowered his head slightly. "I am willing to accept this appointment."

"Excellent." Varian let out a breath. A round of applause rippled through the throne hall.

A royal attendant carrying a jewelry box stepped forward. Varian opened the box and took out a ring, handing it to Jace — an exquisitely crafted gold ring inlaid with a sapphire and engraved with a lion motif, bearing the name Jace Sesso.

"This is the Signet Ring of the Stormwind Court Mages," Varian said. "Countless great mages have worn this ring. Now it is yours, and yours alone."

"Thank you, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"This isn't all, Jace." The King lowered his voice. "I have one more thing to discuss with you later."

Jace nodded.

"Then, Mr. Grede of House Thunderfist." He smiled. "I know what you want, and this time I intend to grant your wish."

"Really, Your Majesty? You really know what I was going to ask for?" Grede asked in surprise.

"Gorjet is yours," Varian said. "This precious, breathtakingly huge silver-gray Alterac gryphon now belongs to you alone. I know the Wildhammer dwarves revere gryphons as their own family and kin, so I won't say he'll become your private property. But from now on, no one but you… will have the authority to deploy or command him at will."

Grede immediately asked, "Then can I ride Gorjet and take Jace to Dalaran tomorrow?"

"You can do it today, as long as you can afford Gorjet's feed bill," the King said.

"Wonderful!" Grede threw his arms around Varian's waist. "That's it — there's nothing else I want!"

"This is actually what you've earned, Mr. Grede," Bolvar Fordragon said.

Grede nodded. "I'm still grateful to Stormwind for entrusting him to my care."

Having finished with the dwarf, Grede yanked hard at Jace's belt, gritted his teeth, and whispered, "You hear that, you little shit? We can ride a gryphon north. Think about how those noblemen strutting around on their fancy horses will have to crane their necks to look up at us… and it's Gorjet!"

Hearing Grede say that, Jace was so excited he could barely keep from laughing.

He thought he'd definitely have Grede make a stop at Southshore — let Gorjet, with those hatchling-dragon-huge wings, land in the open ground outside the town and blow everyone's hats off, just like that Kirin Tor mage on a gryphon they'd seen last time… no, Gorjet was even bigger than that Alterac gryphon of his.

After the ceremony honoring the heroes concluded, the palace held a lavish banquet in the reception hall. Jace had never seen most of the food laid out on the longest table in the center. Aside from the fruit placed alongside the main courses, he didn't even know how to tackle most of it.

Grede, for his part, threw himself right in — grabbing and eating whatever he pleased, telling the children around him stories of their adventures and making them laugh one moment and cry the next.

Honestly, he felt like he wasn't cut out for occasions like this. Standing in a crowd of people made him a little ill at ease. In the end, all he could do was turn the Court Mage ring over in his fingers and sit to the side, listening to Grede blow his own horn.

"Jace." The King Varian appeared from behind him and said softly, "There's something I'd like to discuss with you. About a mage tower."

"A mage tower?"

Jace's heart was already pounding the moment Varian said it. What was he up to?

"You've been to Stonewatch. You must have seen that mysterious tower in the forest. It has a name — Ilgalar," Varian explained slowly. "Ever since the Horde seized that tower during the Orc Wars, the orc warlocks have used it as a base for studying magic."

"That tower has belonged to the Wizard Sanctum ever since the war ended, but the Shadowhide gnolls have always had their eyes on it. Not long ago they even seized it while we were caught off guard… and you know Stonewatch has always been short-staffed. It's hard to look after a building isolated out in the forest."

Jace thought back to his last visit to Stonewatch. The Wizard Sanctum's mage Morgas and his several apprentices had all been living inside the fortress walls alongside the soldiers, rather than in the mage tower outside — so it was because that tower had been in constant danger.

Varian continued, "Next spring we plan to assault that mage tower again and root out the Shadowhide gnolls' attempt to retake Ilgalar Tower for good. But that tower is currently occupied by Shadowhide warlocks who wield dark magic."

"So I'm hoping you'll take part in this operation, Jace. You know dark magic well, and you know how to deal with gnolls. With your involvement, we'll avoid a great deal of unnecessary casualties. Once the tower is recaptured, it can rightfully become yours. Its previous master died in the Karazhan Cataclysm, and the Wizard Sanctum has been looking for a new master for it this whole time. Archmage Andormas and the Archmages of the Wizard Sanctum have already agreed. All that's missing is your own answer."
