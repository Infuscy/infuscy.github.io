Chapter 68: Top of the Watchtower

He steadied his sword, took two steps forward — but the gnoll suddenly charged with both axes flailing and a howl, not caring about its own life. Jace thrust straight between the two axes, using his weapon's longer reach to drive the blade right into the gnoll's open mouth. The gnoll's arms were short and its weapons even shorter — it swung twice in the air, then convulsed and dropped to its knees.

Jace had just wrenched his sword free when a gnoll's claws appeared on the wall right beside him. He immediately swung to chop at the hand — but the other gnoll caught the blade with an iron rod, deflecting it hard enough that Jace nearly lost his grip.

This one lunged and drove the rod straight at Jace's midsection. Jace nimbly stepped back just in time, but behind him he heard another cackling, chattering shriek.

Damn it. More of them.

Worse, he could already feel his stamina flagging.

"Fekhad."

Jace pointed at the gnoll in front of him and cast Fear — he was quite proficient with this spell by now.

The gnoll, which had been working itself up to charge, went pale in an instant. It mustered its breath, let out a huge roar — and then leaped straight off the wall, leaving only the crashing sound of it plowing through the canopy.

That Fear spell hadn't cost him much energy. The gnoll behind him, watching its companion get scared into throwing itself off the wall, froze in bewilderment. While it was still stunned, Jace lunged forward and drove his sword precisely into its chest, using the momentum of his step to shove the whole creature off the wall — and nearly went over with it.

Fear. Should he use Mass Fear now?

Jace's hand reached back and closed around the long staff on his back — but he hesitated before casting.

Based on last time, Mass Fear didn't just scare off enemies — it scared off allies, too. Did the humans here have resistance to Fear as strong as Grede's? Unlikely.

And these gnolls… they were lunatics, some of them massive savage warriors. Their resistance to Fear might be no worse than a human's.

And after unleashing that high-cost Mass Fear, he'd burn through a huge amount of mental energy — he wouldn't be able to keep fighting like this.

The result of carelessly casting Mass Fear would be: beat up his own teammates, help his enemies, and end up exhausted and dogpiled by gnolls.

Thinking that, he let go of the staff.

"Sathrovarr… can you hear me? If you can hear me, get out here…"

He whispered it, not really expecting anything — but a burst of arcane light suddenly flashed before his eyes. Sathrovarr grabbed Jace's collar and hissed, "What the hell are these things, master?!"

"How did you—" Jace started, startled.

"Imps can turn invisible! Imps can shift between planes!" the imp cackled. "Those idiots couldn't see a thing! I climbed over from the window, master!"

Jace grabbed the imp with both hands and shook him. "Cut the crap and fire fireballs into the sky, now!"

"I don't know why, but if the master says so, I obey! But let go of me quick!"

Jace tossed him to the ground. The imp stood up, shook his head, and started muttering something completely unintelligible, the flames in his hands growing brighter and brighter.

By now, the soldiers in the middle of the courtyard were fighting back desperately — but without armor or weapons, they could only grab whatever odds and ends were at hand to fight the gnolls.

Both the wall and the two watchtowers had already been overrun by gnolls. The watchtower on the far side that hadn't fallen yet was seeing fierce fighting too — it looked like those two sentries were about to be overwhelmed.

If the gnolls took the wall completely, they could use the soldiers' own crossbows to fire down on the defenders barely holding out in the courtyard. Stonewatch would fall soon!

A burst of fireball sounded behind him. Jace turned — the imp's fireball had already flown high into the sky and detonated, blooming into a cloud of gray smoke.

Would that do any good? Would the colonel be able to see it?

"Again, keep firing!"

"I hear you, master!"

Suddenly, a watchtower in the distance — one the gnolls had already taken — erupted in chaotic howling. Jace looked over and saw that all those gnolls' yellow eyes were fixed right on this spot.

They'd noticed… That was no surprise. If the cavalry in the woods could spot it, there was no reason to expect these gnolls to turn a blind eye!

"Stop, stop." Jace grabbed the imp, cutting off his casting. "Save your strength. When the gnolls come up, you kill them — use every trick you've got."

Sathrovarr bounced around. "Understood, master! Should I give you as much magical support as I can?"

"Yes! Yes!" Jace said. "Blood Pact… if you've got it, put it on me. If I die, you're not living either."

"Master, you're wrong about that — if you die, I'm free!" the imp corrected him.

Goddamn it… Jace wanted to kick this little bastard to death. But just then a gnoll scrambled up behind him, and that fury was snuffed out in an instant.

He swung back with his sword, but the gnoll ducked its head aside. Jace kicked it square in the face and sent it tumbling off the wall.

Jace muttered to himself. Just like this. Just like this. As long as he kept repeating it… all night long.

Another gnoll had just poked its head up on the other side when Sathrovarr chanted a short spell — and the gnoll suddenly burst into flames, shrieking as it fell off the wall. The imp cackled, and that demonic laugh was plenty unnerving in the night.

"Burn! Burn! Caw caw!"

Immolate… Every time a gnoll was set ablaze, screaming and thrashing in agony, Sathrovarr would leap right onto its head, shouting and dancing wildly in the flames.

"Devour your souls! Devour the filthy souls of you lesser creatures!" The tone was hysterical.

Watching the gnolls on the wall swarm toward this spot like mad, Jace felt his throat getting drier and drier. He'd never imagined facing an entire gnoll horde's siege like this.

He glanced sideways toward the woods. The dark aura was gone, and he couldn't make out any sign of the cavalry. How much longer would this night last?

He was staring when his vision was suddenly filled by a gnoll wearing a bizarre breastplate.

This one had bounded straight up from the base of the wall, brandishing a huge spike-studded club, drooling and howling as it leaped at him. Jace started and threw himself to the side. The club smashed into the wall, snapping off several spikes.

The gnoll howled and swung horizontally. Jace dove aside and barely dodged — but he felt the wind of the club grazing his ear, close enough to hurt.

Jace backed up a few paces, pulled himself up against the wall. The gnoll steadied itself and turned to face him. He pointed at the imp. "Sathrovarr, burn this thing!"

"Like I needed you to tell me!"
