Chapter 680: In the Name of a Test Flight

Ilgalar Tower.

Jace still remembered that old Redridge quest from the game: Morgas had betrayed the Wizard Sanctum and thrown himself into dark magic, allying with orcs and gnolls to brew poisons and black magic to plague the Kingdom of Stormwind. Players had to fight their way into this tower, climb to the top, and kill him and his demon servants.

Although this tower was nowhere near the scale of the Wizard Sanctum's great mage tower, it was still the only mage tower on the shores of Lake Everstill — and the fact that it sat right near the military stronghold of Stonewatch spoke to its importance.

But after everything it had been through during the Orc Wars, plus the endless tug-of-war between Stonewatch's soldiers and the gnolls, the tower probably wasn't in great shape these days.

A few years from now, the Blackrock orcs of Blackrock Spire would invade Redridge once again and seize Stonewatch, which was how the traitor Morgas came to occupy it. From then on it became a genuine tower of shadow, riddled with all manner of dark-magic traps and inhabited by dark creatures. Demons.

Even if this tower really would be handed over to Jace in the future, he had no idea whether he could actually manage a mage tower — surrounded by gnolls on all sides, with the threat of orcs to the north in the Burning Steppes...

"Hey, Jace." Grede waved a hand in front of Jace's face. "What are you thinking about?"

Jace snapped back to reality. He didn't know when the two of them had ended up back at the Pig and Whistle Inn.

"Did I agree to Varian Wrynn?" Jace asked.

"Did you drink yourself stupid?" Grede said, propping his chin on his hand. "Ah, right — you barely ate a thing at the banquet, just drank. I shouldn't have ordered such strong stuff. I only brought you here to get some food in you."

"I'm talking about that mage tower!" Jace stressed.

"Of course you agreed. Of course. How could you possibly say no to a king?" Grede smacked his lips. "You didn't get so drunk you blacked out, did you? Forgetting something this important."

"Maybe." Jace looked at him. "Why do you seem like you're in a bad mood?"

"Nothing wrong, it's just that..." The dwarf dipped a finger in his ale and traced something on the table. "I'm going to have to start covering Gorjet's expenses, Jace. Do you know what that means?"

"It means you've got your own gryphon," Jace said.

Grede scratched his face hard. "Varian Wrynn said Gorjet could stay in the new gryphon roost they built on the Stormwind city walls — but that's as far as it goes. If we want to go anywhere, I have to provide his feed myself. You have no idea how much a gryphon that size can eat... I did some quick maths. If the two of us ride him to Dalaran, the cost of the food he'd burn through would be more than the two of our ship tickets combined! And that's not even letting him fly full tilt."

Jace asked cautiously, "So you're thinking of..."

Grede paused. "Can you cover it?"

Jace hemmed and hawed for a moment, then said, "Right — why not have him catch some cattle or sheep in the hills along the way? We'll be passing right over Khaz Modan. Let him grab a mountain goat!"

"Wouldn't causing trouble on Bronzebeard territory be a bit... inappropriate?" Grede said, scratching his beard. "Then let's have him catch a bear in Hillsbrad. Or a mountain lion," Jace said immediately.

"What if he gets hurt?" Grede pressed. "And another thing — having him stay in the Dalaran gryphon roost, that's probably going to cost a fair bit too. You don't think the Kirin Tor is going to cover his upkeep, do you?"

"Why does it have to be this complicated..." Jace ran a hand through his hair, then scratched his neck. "I'll pay."

"Really?" Grede asked.

"What about it?" Jace said. "You're doing this to take me there. I can cover that, no problem."

Grede grinned. "You're just desperate to see your little elf, aren't you? Thinking about Miss Vereesa Windrunner sitting in Dalaran waiting for you — bet you can't even sleep at night, huh?"

He chuckled. Jace couldn't help but twitch the corner of his mouth upward, then quickly took a swig of ale to wash the grin back down. After they'd eaten their fill, Grede slapped the table and said, "Jace, come with me to the city wall."

"What?" Jace pulled on his cloak. "You want to go see Gorjet?"

"Not just see him." Grede said. "I'm going to ride him out for a spin. And as for where... we'll go to that Ilgalar Tower!"

"That whole area's crawling with gnolls, Grede! And it's almost dark," Jace warned.

Grede said, "That's exactly why we dare go take a look now. Some of those gnolls carry crossbows — quite dangerous, right? So how are they going to aim in the dark? Those bastards are either half-blind at night or fast asleep. We sneak up from the sky, take a look at the tower, and come right back. So — are you coming or not?"

"I'm in," Jace said.

"Then let's go."

The two of them left the Pig and Whistle and ran all the way to the Trade District, then climbed the stairs hung with Stormwind lion banners up onto the city wall.

"Keep your voices down, the gryphons are sleeping!" The gryphon keeper shuffled over, rubbing his eyes. "Ah, isn't this..."

Grede came straight out with it. "Where's Gorjet?"

"Got an audience with the king this morning, and now you can't wait to ride him out the same evening?" The gryphon keeper laughed and punched Grede in the arm. "Come on, I'll take you to him. And Mr. Sesso, of course!"

The dwarf keeper led them through a low corridor, asking as they walked, "Did the two of you really... rescue a princess from Grim Batol?"

"It was the Dragonqueen, Baudi!"

"Why rescue a red dragon?!" the keeper asked, stunned.

"It turned out the orcs had captured the mother of all the red dragons and were forcing the red dragons to serve as their mounts," Grede explained. "So if we rescued their mother, all the red dragons wouldn't have to cooperate with the orcs anymore!"

"I thought those red dragons had gone mad, willingly making themselves slaves to those otherworldly monsters." The keeper nodded. "Well, damn, that must've been one hell of a trip... Say, what's Grim Batol actually like, Grede? Is it really as impressive as in the paintings? Like Ironforge?"

"Honestly, I think it's a bit smaller than Ironforge," Grede said. "But I didn't get the chance to look around. You should ask the human beside you — he turned into an orc and lived inside for several days..."

"Turned into an orc?!" the keeper exclaimed.

"Just a very convincing illusion spell. Not a real transformation," Jace explained. "Grim Batol might not be as big as Ironforge, but it's no small place either. Just the parts I walked through were three stories high, and there were more levels above. The depths were bottomless."

"I envy you, Mr. Jace Sesso. I really hope I get to see it someday." The gryphon keeper shook his head. "As for me, I'm stuck here in Stormwind serving a prison sentence. No chance to go out. Not many humans are any good at riding gryphons. I thought training human gryphon handlers would be an easy posting, but living here is more miserable than being back at Aerie Peak. Grede, I'd rather go teach the elves in the Hinterlands how to drink."

"I think, brother Bardi," Grede said, "teaching humans to ride gryphons is a good deal simpler."
