Chapter 681: Ilgalar Tower

When they reached the gryphons' resting place, the gray beast lying at the very back seemed to sense something and craned its long neck toward them, as if trying to make out who had come.

The moment it saw Grede's big face, it let out a string of puttering sounds exactly like a tractor engine turning over.

Strange as it sounded, that was just what the gryphons here did when they were in high spirits, so after hearing it enough times, you got used to it.

"Well, looks like the two of you have some kind of telepathy," keeper Bardi said. "Look at him — he can't wait to go out for a stroll either. All he does when I'm watching him is sleep!"

"I always said he should be mine," Grede said, hands on his hips. "Right, Gorjet?" Hearing his name, the gryphon let out a hoarse cry in response.

"Get on, Jace." Grede climbed up into the already-secured saddle and hauled Jace up after him. Bardi stepped aside. "Hey, what's this thing they're calling Deathwing?" he asked.

"Something powerful," Grede said, patting the gray gryphon's thick neck. "Let's go, Gorjet!"

The gryphon leaped out of the roost, its talons scraping great chunks of brick from the wall as it flung itself clear. It spread its wings and beat down a gust that sent all the other gryphons into a shrieking panic, then plunged straight into the dark sky...

In the Blasted Lands, everything had been barren hills, plains, and monotonous half-dead forests... and that day, riding on Gorjet's back, Jace had been wound tight in combat-readiness the whole time, with no mood to take in the scenery around him.

This time, even though it was nearly dark, it was enough for him to gaze calmly at the distant horizon — the boundless Elwynn Forest stretching into the shadows, the broad River Neltharion, and of course the rapidly shrinking Stormwind gates behind them.

Jace gripped Gorjet's saddle tight, listening to Grede's whoops of excitement, and felt that the whole terrifying ordeal at Grim Batol had been worth it.

The speed of this enormous gryphon was truly astonishing. A journey that would normally take at least a day — he'd barely had time to zone out and it was already coming into view. The Eastvale Logging Camp, the lumber mill, the log yard, and the great stumps whipped past beneath them, and the sun hadn't even fully set yet. The sky was still deep blue.

By the time the two moons hung clearly overhead, Gorjet was already bearing down on Lakeshire...

Stonewatch was a vague shadow in the moonlight. The gryphon swept over the Lake Everstill bridge, skimmed the surface of the lake, then suddenly climbed. Jace could even make out the startled faces of the night-watch sentries swiveling to follow the gryphon's path.

"We're almost at Stonewatch, Jace," Grede said. "If you really do become the master of Ilgalar Tower someday, I can ride the gryphon and carry you here too. What do you think? Honestly, you could even ride a gryphon here yourself — I'll teach you... Gorjet likes you. He'd definitely be willing to help you learn this important skill early."

Whether Gorjet understood or simply heard his name, he flapped his wings and let out a low cry. The master of Ilgalar Tower — it actually sounded like something.

Though God knew how much effort and money it would take to maintain the tower in the future, just the thought of truly living in his own mage tower, where he could slowly research and experiment with dark magic... Jace felt a thrill of excitement already.

"I'll learn to ride a gryphon well," he said.

"You'll pick it up fast, Jace. I know you've got the talent," Grede said. "I could tell back when we flew from the Hinterlands to Southshore! Now climb higher, Gorjet — don't give those gnolls so much as a single feather to snatch..."

At Grede's command, the gryphon beat its wings a few more times and soared upward, circling around Stonewatch's massive walls and out over the forest canopy. That was when Jace finally caught a glimpse of the mage tower.

Compared to the Wizard Sanctum's great mage tower — one of Stormwind's landmarks — this one looked downright squat, even built as it was on a sudden rocky outcrop in the forest. Buried amid the tall treetops, only a hint of its red eaves was visible. At night, it was even less conspicuous.

Up close, Jace could see that several of its windows were damaged, but a faint dark light still glowed from within, which meant someone was living inside — most likely a gnoll warlock.

At this distance Gorjet stopped beating its wings, easing its movements and gliding slowly over the treetops, circling slowly above the dilapidated mage tower.

Then several gnoll barks rang out from the forest. The gryphon quickly climbed higher, away from the sound, and the canopy swallowed Ilgalar Tower once more.

Clearly, the Darkhide gnolls had fully occupied this place. Fighting your way into the forest to seize this magic tower would be no easy task, to say nothing of the gnoll spellcasters inside. No wonder the king hadn't immediately ordered Stonewatch to take it back, and instead wanted to seek the Wizard Sanctum's help.

"So, Jace — shall we head back to Stormwind?"

"Sure, Grede. Thanks for bringing me out here."

"It's nothing..."

Just as Jace wanted to turn for one last look at the tower's silhouette, the gryphon beneath them suddenly beat its wings hard several times and dove toward Stonewatch, catching even Grede off guard — the dwarf was jolted sideways...

"What's going on!" Grede muttered under his breath.

The gryphon made a low puttering sound. Grede seemed to understand what he meant and scanned the night sky around them. "Over there... Jace, quick, look." The dwarf pointed north.

Jace followed his gaze and caught sight of a vague, indistinct shape slowly disappearing into the moonlight over the Redridge Mountains in the distance.

The shape looked small from here, but Jace knew — judging by the size of the rooftops on those far-off Redridge peaks — that it was already considerably larger than Gorjet.

It was very likely a young dragon. A young black dragon. Black dragon scales, even black dragon sightings appearing in Redridge wasn't exactly a recent rumor anymore.

Even though Grim Batol had been destroyed, Blackrock Spire remained firmly allied with the Black Dragonflight — of that there was no question. Many years from now, the Burning Steppes would become the hunting grounds of the Black Dragonflight's brood.

What Jace had told Nekros about the warchief reconsidering their relationship with the black dragons had been nothing but bluffing. He knew the Blackrock clan was already on the verge of being controlled by the black dragons, and there was no force that could stop that tide.
