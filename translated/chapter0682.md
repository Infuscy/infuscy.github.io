## Chapter 682: Shadowfury

Although the business at Stormwind Keep had set him back by a few days, Jace still wanted to deal with the demon in the Slaughtered Lamb's basement before leaving Stormwind for Dalaran.

After all, with that demon sitting there next to Ursula and the others, Jace couldn't rest easy. There was no telling when the circle might fail, when the sigils on the practice room door might weaken, and that void walker might suddenly burst out and attack everyone in the basement. Relying solely on the felhound Mr. Basadim and Ursula — who wasn't much of a fighter — was far too dangerous.

The day he'd arranged to meet with Erland and Galders arrived, and Jace came early and sat down in the basement.

At this hour, apart from Ursula sleeping in the alchemy lab and Morophil sitting to the side idly playing with her tail, there was no one else in the basement.

Jace couldn't help but think of that dark shadow he'd seen when he went to Ilgalar Tower.

If Blackrock Spire ever decided to attack Stonewatch with the black dragons' help, then Ilgalar Tower would be right on the front line. He'd have to help Stonewatch's soldiers hold that place to the last... otherwise it would be either abandon the tower or lose his life.

Come to think of it, he felt a bit trapped. But who could have predicted, just a few years from now, that the black dragons would help the orc tribes — who had been lying low in Blackrock Spire all this time — launch yet another war?

The king and his privy council, and probably the Wizard Sanctum too, likely just wanted him to help suppress the Darkhide gnolls' expansion in Redridge. They couldn't possibly expect him to hold off a full orcish assault, so he had no grounds to blame them for anything.

Just then Ursula walked out of the alchemy lab into the main hall. The moment she saw the succubus beside Jace, she let out a startled cry.

"Jace... this is..."

"Morophil," Jace said. "Don't you recognize her anymore?"

He glanced at the succubus, and only then realized that Morophil looked very different now from the last time the Dagger Society members had seen her.

Morophil tossed her tail behind the chair and asked, "Am I prettier, or more seductive? Dear Ms. Ursula?"

"More terrifying," Ursula said.

"That's not a bad evaluation either," Morophil said with a smile.

"Last time I brought her here to inscribe the sigils, I remember you were here too, Ursula," Jace said.

"I didn't watch you inscribe... ah!" Ursula said, as if suddenly remembering something. "That table, Jace — the one you burned to cinders? Remember to compensate Erland for it. That table was worth three silver."

"Got it," Jace said.

A moment later, Erland and Sulenna walked in through the door. Erland had his succubus with him too.

It seemed they could all now bring their succubi past the Mage Quarter's mages' surveillance and lead the invisible demons down here.

But Jace also knew that if they didn't enter from the west entrance, the north entrance from the Park district could bypass the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower entirely.

Besides, only language teachers from the university passed by here frequently, along with the occasional mage apprentice living in the Park or near the port. Very few instructors or full mages ever lingered in this area, so bringing a succubi in this way was much safer.

Still, he had to admit that their skill in controlling demons had grown by leaps and bounds during the time he'd been away.

But the moment these two walked in, the succubus beside Erland was so frightened she stopped dead on the stairs and didn't dare move — because she'd seen Morophil.

And it wasn't just her. When Galders came in afterward, Senlora — who was already very familiar with Morophil — had been looking perfectly relaxed, but the sight of her made her freeze too.

Morophil's presence made every demon servant in the basement tense with fear. Whether it was Senlora, Erland's new succubus, or Mr. Basadim — all the demons shed their wild, arrogant demeanor and meekly followed at their masters' sides, while Morophil sat there with her legs crossed like the queen of a demon dungeon, looking down on all of them with contempt... until Jace opened his mouth.

"Come, Morophil. Let's go take a look at that void demon in the practice room."

"Ah... yes, master."

Morophil responded and promptly stood up to stick close to Jace's side. Paired with the youngest person in this basement — and the one who looked the most honest and harmless — the combination was jarringly incongruous.

"Welcome back, Jace," Galders said as he reached the practice room door. "I feel much more at ease with you here. A void walker is an unfamiliar sort of demon servant even to us — to the Dark Iron dwarves, and actually, based on my sources, even to the orcs."

"We're not even sure it is a demon," Erland said. "I don't think there's anything about it we can use."

"Where did you get the incantation to summon a void walker?" Jace asked.

"This time someone told me, Jace. He couldn't let me say it too explicitly," Galders said.

"Honesty, Galders," Jace raised an eyebrow. "Isn't that what you demand from all of us?"

"It's difficult to achieve one hundred percent," Galders smiled. "But I can assure you that everything I've kept hidden is to protect the sources of our incantations and knowledge from being compromised, Jace. This summoning was successful enough to prove the source is reliable. The problem is, now that we've summoned it, we can't control it."

Jace said, "A void walker is essentially a mass of shadow energy. My demon servants told me that controlling it should be just like using an incantation to manipulate shadow energy."

"So it requires a corresponding incantation too?" Galders asked.

Jace said, "You can try attacking it with your shadow magic first, disrupt its shadow energy, and see if it will submit to it."

"We wouldn't dare attack it with shadow magic without you here. But since you put it that way..."

Galders spoke as he wiped away the sigils on the practice room door, breaking the seal. Erland yanked the practice room door open, and a mass of shadow poured straight out of the door, drowning the light in the corridor outside.

The warlocks all stepped back, but the thing inside didn't charge out immediately — this was simply the shadow energy radiating from its very presence.

Jace tried to glance inside, and saw two drifting flames of shadow in the pitch-black room, staring out from within like eyes.

"Send... me... back..." the mass of shadow said in Demonic. "Obviously not," Jace said.

At his words, the ghostly flames suddenly flickered and fixed on his face.

"I can obey you, mortal who walks in shadow and flame... but you must promise to eventually let me return to the Void. I do not like this world. Do not like... do not like..."

Hearing its slurred, halting Demonic, Jace was taken aback. Galders, however, immediately stepped forward and said, "The one you should obey is me, demon!"

He immediately began chanting an incantation Jace had never heard before, while raising his staff and pointing it into the room. A burst of shadow energy suddenly exploded inside!

The demon let out an ethereal shriek, barely able to hold its form. It struggled incessantly, but could only remain in the center of the circle.

Outside the room, everyone was hit by the shadow energy blasted out from within and scrambled to avoid it. Jace was the closest and took the most intense shock. Caught off guard, the sheer force of the shadow energy stunned him... but fortunately it didn't cause any serious corruption.

It was as if this power had been invented specifically for the purpose of shattering a person's mind. In that instant, fear, doubt, and a tangle of complex emotions all rushed into his head, making it nearly impossible to make a decision or take action.

Jace could easily suppress those emotions, but looking at the others, it was clear from their faces that they were all in rough shape.

"You shouldn't have unleashed that incantation in such a confined space, Galders," Ursula coughed, speaking in something close to a warning tone. "That was the agreement we made with you beforehand."

"It proved effective," Galders said. "We should have done this cleanly from the start." The void walker inside growled incessantly... but eventually quieted down.

"Shadow bows to your will, my summoner and master..." the void demon said slowly.

"We should have done it from the start? What if this shadow magic makes that void creature even stronger?" Erland stepped in front of Galders and asked. "Can you live with the consequences? Think about what happened last time we summoned a succubus, Galders!"

Galders's lips moved uneasily. He controlled himself and slowly let out a breath. "Fine. It was my fault. Jace confirmed my thinking, so I got impatient and wanted to bring it under control."

Jace glanced at him, then said, "The problem now is that it seems I'm the only one here who doesn't know this new incantation."

"I'll tell you right away," Galders said. "The orcs call this magic Shadowfury. We all think it's a very fitting name."
