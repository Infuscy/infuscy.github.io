Chapter 684: A Winter's Journey

Time crept into December, and the cold grew fiercer by the day. Jace stood on the city wall in the mountain-lion-pelts cloak that had survived a little over a year, waiting for Grede. Today they would set out for Dalaran. Ever since he'd left his room, he'd kept reaching around to his back, his waist, his heart fluttering with anxiety every time. Because now he had no staff, and no Song of the Blue Sky either. All he carried on his person was the purple demon bag, and the Blade of Eternal Darkness hung from his waist in a random dagger sheath. In the past, every time he went out the door he'd check to make sure he'd brought these crucial life-saving tools with him, so now that they were suddenly all gone, he couldn't settle his nerves. Aside from the imp, the demon bag held Galders's incantations that Sazeno had organized, plus the death knight report he'd only half-finished. Both of these needed to be completed on the Lordaeron continent... although he had a feeling he'd very likely push both of these most important tasks clean out of his mind along the way. But he'd try his best to remember, and do what he could. The thought of going home and seeing his parents warmed his heart. But the thought that he might run into Kel'Thuzad in Dalaran chilled it right back down again. Still, he didn't need to say anything to Kel'Thuzad. That fellow valued his position as a member of the Kirin Tor Council of Six just enough that he wouldn't openly make trouble for him right now — and probably wouldn't dare make a move in the shadows either, for fear of adding to the risk of exposing himself. Find Krasus as quickly as possible and explain everything, and the situation would improve a great deal. And of course, most important of all — see Vereesa as soon as possible. "Jace, how long have you been waiting here?" Grede came up the stone steps from below, looking over as he asked. "Not long," Jace said. "Just a bit cold. Can't stand it for more than a moment." Grede patted Jace on the chest. "Then let's get moving. Everything ready?" "Ready," Jace nodded. As they walked down the corridor, Grede said, "I want to make a trip back to Aerie Peak, Jace — while you and Vereesa go to the royal city. From Dalaran to the Lordaeron capital you just take a ship, no need to book passage in advance. I don't think there's any reason we have to go barging over on a gryphon, right?" "No problem, Grede," Jace said. "When we see Vereesa, we'll pick a time and place to regroup." When they reached the city wall, Gorjet wasn't waiting there. Jace was just wondering about it when Grede walked to the edge of the wall and let out several piercingly loud whistles. From the other side of the wall immediately came a frenzy of enormous wingbeats and a string of dwarven curses. That gryphon — the one that was way too goddamn big — suddenly popped its head up from behind the wall, sending a flurry of great gray feathers flying as it swooped toward Grede. Grede shouted an apology in Dwarvish at the top of his lungs, then leaped straight onto the gryphon's back. "What was he doing just now?" "Playing around with him," Grede said. "But what entertainment could compare with tearing across the sky together? Right?" "Right," Jace echoed. Once Jace was seated firmly, Gorjet plunged off the wall at Grede's command, shooting almost straight up into the sky. The jolt hit Jace so hard it brought back the sheer terror of riding that wild gryphon in the Hinterlands. The gryphon spun several times before stabilizing. When he looked down again, Stormwind lay spread out flat beneath them. It was the first time Jace had ever beheld Stormwind from this angle — a great... gray-whiteskirt-edged flatbread, covered all over in sesame-speckles. As they swept past the Cathedral of Light, the spire felt close enough to scrape his pants off. The patrolling gryphon riders shouted over, clearly unhappy with Grede's sudden maneuver. But before Grede could even say anything, Gorjet let out an unprovoked, shrieking cry that sent the other gryphons wobbling and fleeing into the distance, accompanied by the gryphon riders' curses. Traveling by gryphon didn't just cut the journey time several times over. As they crossed the snowfields of Dun Morogh, Jace finally beheld Ironforge — a sight he'd never seen with his own eyes. Only when he saw that gate, built into the mountainside and forever hanging open, did he truly understand why, centuries ago, the three great clans — Wildhammer, Dark Iron, and Bronzebeard — had launched a civil war over the right to live in this city. Nestled among the mountains, the gate of Ironforge was already a magnificent fortress in its own right. The ancient bronze ornamentations on it glittered in the sunlight, and those patterns — along with the bronze anvil emblem at the center, visible clearly even from the sky — were probably thousands of years old. Flanking the gate stood several tall towers set into the mountainside, stone walls, year-round blankets of white snow, and towering dark green pines rising among them. By comparison, Grim Batol fell far short. Jace lowered his head and stared at that gate for a long time. Grede, seemingly sensing his silence, said, "Even today, Jace, there are still those on Aerie Peak who want to march back to Ironforge sooner rather than later. If the Dark Iron dwarves hadn't committed one unforgivable atrocity after another, I dare say there'd be far more Wildhammer dwarves wanting to pick up arms against the Bronzebeard than there are now." Not far off, several gryphon riders had noticed their presence and were slowly moving closer to escort them. Gorjet barked in displeasure, trying to scare off those golden gryphons, but the riders kept a safe distance and refused to leave. "We shouldn't have gotten this close to Ironforge," Grede muttered. "These annoying... Gorjet, don't pay them any mind. Let's move further away — over to that snow mountain." Grede pointed at a high plateau on a distant snow mountain. The gryphon beneath them immediately accelerated and flew toward it. Soon the golden gryphons behind them shrank to dots one by one, until they couldn't be seen at all. They landed near a pine forest at the foot of the snowfields. The two hopped off the gryphon. Grede rubbed his hands together and said, "Let's rest here a bit. Let our gray brother go out and have some fun, stretch his legs — after all, we've been flying a long while." He shouted a few words in Dwarvish toward the forested slope. The gryphon excitedly lifted off the ground and bolted toward it, leaving behind a great shower of snow shaken down from the nearby trees... Jace brushed the snow off himself, pulled his hood tight, and smiled. "Didn't you say you weren't going to let him run wild on Bronzebeard territory?" "What's this counted as running wild?" Grede said. "Besides, he's from Alterac. He likes places like this, Jace." Jace asked, "If the mountain goats here die, will the Bronzebeard dwarves be upset?" "What mountain goats?" Grede looked around, then said, "I don't see any mountain goats. We'll just eat our own bread. Don't tell me you're craving Bronzebeard mountain goat? I've got to say, Jace, you're a greedy bastard." The ship would have taken about twenty days' sailing. Gorjet covered it in less than six — and that counting the time spent resting, eating, and everything else. When they passed Southshore, Jace and Grede had plotted the whole way, planning to have Gorjet swoop down and give everyone a little bit of a... but when they caught sight of Southshore's watchtowers, both of them unanimously decided: forget it. No point in causing more trouble for this town that had finally found some peace and quiet. This night they didn't rest, and flew straight over the Alterac Mountains. When morning came and they beheld those brilliant purple spires wreathed in clouds below — like something out of a fairy-tale castle — Gorjet was still full of energy, beating his wings hard as he bore down on them. Soon, two purple-robed mages wielding glowing staffs rode up on two small gryphons, shouting at them: "Land at Krasus Square! Go any further and you'll crash into the Violet Citadel!" Krasus Square? Jace knew that in the game, Dalaran had a special place for players to land their flying mounts called Krasus Landing — but that was something that only appeared after Dalaran was destroyed and rebuilt into a floating city. Could there be a platform with a similar function here too? He didn't remember a place like this from his last visit. Guided by the two mages, they turned and flew toward the east side of Dalaran. Sure enough, behind a section of wall not far from the Violet Citadel, they spotted a white square enclosed by low purple walls. So this was Krasus Square? It looked much larger than in the game... Quite a few gryphons were taking off and landing here at the moment, and there were also a number of high elven dragonhawks circling at lower altitude. Grede finally landed beside a gryphon roost. When the other gryphons saw Gorjet approaching, they all grew restless. "This one's too big." The purple-robed Dalaran gryphon keeper hurried over, shouting up at Grede on the gryphon's back. "I need to find a special place for him to rest, dwarf sir. You'll have to come with him — I'm not confident I could command this beast without his master here." Grede turned his head and said something, but the cries of the gryphons, the beating of wings, and the wind all blurred together into a cacophony. There was no way to make out what the person right next to you was saying. "What?!" Jace shouted. "I'll go with him first! Wait here for me!" the dwarf repeated. "Give me your name, dwarf," a nearby keeper called out. "This gryphon saddle is Stormwind's, isn't it? You're from Stormwind!" "Right," Grede shouted back. "I'm Grede Thunderfist, from Aerie Peak!" "Of Grede of Clan Thunderfist... very well." The keeper muttered as he wrote it down. The two of them dismounted the gryphon. He glanced at Jace's hand and saw that ring. "Are you a court mage of King Varian Wrynn? I received no notice of a royal mage's visit..." Jace said, "I'm a member of the Stormwind court mage's office, but I'm only going back to Lordaeron to visit family." The keeper asked again. "It says here Jace Sesso. Are you Jace Sesso?" "Of course," Jace said. "This is my ring." "Archmage Antonidas said to inform him when you arrived." The keeper handed what he was holding to a young mage apprentice beside him, then said, "Wait here a moment, Archmage Sesso. I'll have someone go to the Violet Citadel at once."
