Chapter 69: Shadow Fury

The imp rapidly muttered a spell, and the gnoll immediately burst into flames.

But instead of falling back, the gnoll only grew more frenzied. It shrieked, hefted its club, and smashed at him again. Jace raised his sword and deflected the club to the side, stepping out of the way — but the lightest of touches sent his whole right arm nearly numb with shock. His sword wobbled and clattered to the ground.

The gnoll warrior, seeing Jace disarmed, laughed and raised its club to bring down on Jace's head. Jace quickly cast Fear — the gnoll's grin vanished on the spot. It froze, its shoulders trembling twice. Jace drew the Jia Jin dagger from behind his hip and drove it up under the gnoll's jaw, punching straight through its mouth and out the side of its lip.

Jace wrenched the dagger free and plunged it into the gnoll's eye socket!

The two stabs came lightning-fast. The gnoll only just snapped out of its terror, let out a howling scream, and toppled off the wall — but Jace didn't have time to pull the dagger back out of its eye socket.

"My… hell!"

That dagger was worth quite a bit, too.

He watched the gnoll crash into the woods below. On the nearby wall, another pair of dull, vacant eyes poked up from below and fixed on him.

"Ahh!"

The imp shrieked. Jace turned to see a burning gnoll already grabbing Sathrovarr and lifting him up. Jace snatched up his longsword, lunged forward, and hacked the gnoll's arm off, kicking it square in the face and sending it tumbling from the wall.

The imp shook free of the gnoll's grip and roared with excitement, "The master still cares about me! The master doesn't want me to die!"

"Watch yourself!" Jace barked — but the gnoll that had been staring at him just now was already on him.

Watching the gnolls swarm up onto the wall in wave after wave, Jace and Sathrovarr alone couldn't possibly hold them back any longer. Suddenly, Sathrovarr chanted a completely different spell.

An explosion of flame erupted in Jace's right hand — he found his sword wreathed in roaring fire!

"The master's been buffed — get in there!" Sathrovarr cackled.

Buffed?

Once the sword caught fire, something swelled in Jace's heart — a strange, surging courage.

The flames hissed and howled, bright enough to hurt his eyes — but as he stared into them, he felt like he could see souls writhing within.

He gripped the sword with both hands and charged at the two gnolls in front of him, swinging hard. The one in the back was so startled by the blazing inferno that it stumbled and fell right off the wall.

When a gnoll tried to circle behind him, Jace spun and swept the blade at them. The gnolls who'd just climbed up immediately shrank back several paces until their backs hit the wall.

They seemed terrified of that roaring fire, unwilling to come anywhere near it. Even the ones still approaching hesitated now.

Jace knew why. This wasn't ordinary fire elementals at work — this was fire manipulated and stoked by an unclean force. Tainted fire.

And that courage surging through him just now probably wasn't the kind of bravery that flares up in the face of an enemy — it was the chaos and slaughter born of demonic power flooding through him.

Every time he swung the sword, he could hear the flames screaming through the air like wailing ghosts, while the little demon behind him piled Immolate onto the already-terrified gnolls, deepening the eerie atmosphere atop this watchtower.

Somehow, though, he wanted to embrace this madness.

Because these gnolls wanted to kill him. Wanted to strangle him with ropes, hack off his limbs, drive an iron rod straight through his mouth from front to back, roast him and eat him alive.

They wanted to take Stonewatch. Wanted to kill every human here.

And Jace would never allow that.

He was going to kill these monsters, burn their pelts to cinders, chop their limbs to pieces — these half-dog, half-human things who thought they were so clever didn't deserve to live at all.

A surge of hatred roared up from deep inside him. He stared at these dull-eyed, twisted-limbed, spasming humanoid abominations — like a batch of mistakes hybridized together for some warped reason. He raised his longsword, the flames on it surging like a scorching gale wrapping around his body, drowning out even the imp's excited shrieks with the wailing of the fel fire.

The storm of flames shifted from its original bright hue gradually darker, redder, giving off an eerie purple glow.

"Die! All of you!"

Jace moved faster than he thought himself capable. One sword thrust punched straight through a gnoll's skull — its head burst apart in a crackling sizzle, brains and fluid splattering. Jace wrenched the blade out sideways and cleaved straight into the next gnoll's shoulder, the sword hacking all the way into its chest, the stench of scorched flesh filling his nostrils.

"You wanted to kill me, right? So who's dead now?! Where's your head? Where are your arms and legs?"

Jace kept shouting it over and over, cutting down the gnolls who'd climbed up into ragged, charred heaps in moments. He swung again and again and again, until he looked around and couldn't see anything still standing. The ground beneath his feet was carpeted with gore and severed limbs. The gnolls' terrified wails rang without end.

Everything seemed to fall quiet again.

Then Jace heard the chaotic beat of hoofbeats drawing closer, and a horn blast drowning out all the gnolls' howls.

The monsters were retreating, fleeing like mad. Some were so scared they leaped straight off the wall into the woods. The few gnolls in front of Jace scattered too — they'd already been on the verge of breaking. They must have been terrified of the cavalry, terrified of Stonewatch's main force returning. That's why they ran, Jace told himself.

The severed remains, the torn-off limbs, the gnoll corpses missing half their bodies — all of it burned with black-tinged, red fel fire.

He charged forward and stamped, stamped, stamped until he'd crushed out every last flame. But by then only one word remained in his mind — Shadowflame.

Was this Shadowflame?

Theoretically, impossible.

Shadowflame was the breath of black dragons — searing magma spewed forth by black dragons corrupted by shadow energy.

And he was nothing but an ordinary human, while Sathrovarr was just a weak little demon. How could they possibly conjure such a catastrophic fel conflagration?

The simplest guess: the imp had ignited his sword, and the shadow energy within Jace had tainted the imp's fel fire, ultimately producing something resembling a black dragon's breath — shadow fire.

But before he could work through the mechanics, his whole body gave out at once, his head swelling fit to burst.

After all, his body simply couldn't sustain that kind of frenzied combat. It had been nothing more than a short-term burnout.

"Master?" Sathrovarr asked tentatively from behind him. Jace pressed a hand to his forehead and turned to look at the imp — he'd never seen the little creature this cautious before. Jace leaned against the battlement, his skin traced here and there with faint or livid blood vessels that hadn't been there before, his brain buzzing in time with his heartbeat.

Suddenly a searing pain flared in his right hand — enough to make him drop his sword. At some point the blade had burned black and bent out of shape, its crossguard warped by the heat, scorching his index finger and the web of his hand.

Jace watched the skin on his hand slowly discolor under the shadow's corrosion. He fumbled at his clothes, looking for something to wrap it with — then realized that bandaging a shadow burn wouldn't do any good anyway.

"Master, take this — drink!" The imp held up a bottle of Shadow Protection Potion. "It's the last one, master!"

Jace snatched the bottle and downed it in one gulp, leaning against the wall. The acrid burn in his throat snapped him awake more times than he could count.

The searing pain of the shadow corrosion on his hand eased considerably, and the fel fire burning in his chest slowly guttered out.

Though the ongoing damage had been neutralized, the burn scar already carved into his right hand wouldn't heal.

He stared blankly ahead until the scorching night cooled and the sounds of human voices in Stonewatch gradually drowned out the gnolls' howls.

"Did you fire the signal?"

Jace looked up sharply. He didn't know when several soldiers had appeared in front of him — and the imp was gone.
