Chapter 693: Meeting the Parents

If this were Stormwind, the sky would be completely dark by seven or eight o'clock, even in winter.

But in Dalaran, the sky was still dimly bright. A thin, gloomy mist hung over the lake; in the distance were verdant pine groves.

Actually, this was the day-night rhythm he was familiar with — after all, he'd grown up in the royal city and Brill, even further north. But after just a few years in Stormwind, he was already on the verge of losing his feel for all of this.

Or perhaps the unease these supposedly familiar things brought him actually stemmed entirely from the anxiety in Jace's own heart.

He didn't know whether his parents could accept an elf coming into the house. After all, old Vik quite detested even those Stromgarde folk, and the Arathi were practically indistinguishable from Lordaeronians in both ear length and hair color... To him, an elf was basically an alien.

But no matter how you looked at it, this was still a happy sort of trouble.

He was finally going home again. This was a right he'd wagered his life to earn. He shouldn't be worrying like this.

The next evening, the passenger ship arrived at the foot of the royal city. The walls flying Lordaeron's white banners, high up, had remained exactly as they were fifteen years ago. Following the carriages and travelers through the gate, there were no mages or guards from Dalaran or Lordaeron itself stopping him — only the occasional curious citizen or soldier staring at this young human leading an elf woman along the edge of the street. In Lordaeron, this wasn't nearly as common a sight as it was in Dalaran.

God only knew when it had last snowed. The snow still hadn't melted, soaking the ground cold and wet, with icy mud puddles everywhere. Jace walked in front, Vereesa followed behind, her head raised the whole time as she marveled at the dark-brown massive walls, the tall towers, and the golden and green domes of the palace in the distance.

As she walked, her dress — something that would fetch a fortune in Stormwind — picked up plenty of snow and mud, but she seemed completely unaware of it. She didn't even try to lift the hem a little, just letting it drag along the ground as it pleased...

Jace was now thoroughly convinced that this elf had never really traveled like this before — at least, she'd never left Quel'Thalas in an outfit like this and made it all the way to his front door. Jace took off his hood, knocked on the door, and asked softly, "Dad, Mom — I'm back."

Vereesa watched Jace take a deep breath and fix his eyes on the door, her long ears pressed flat against the back of her head. Jace had the feeling that when she'd faced down countless Dragonmaw orcs charging into the Dragonqueen's prison, she probably hadn't been this nervous.

The murmur of conversation inside the house stopped abruptly. A moment later the door opened, carrying the savory aroma of meat soup. Hennie stood frozen, staring outside.

"Jace... they let you come back?"

Jace nodded. Hennie came out and threw her arms around him. "I thought I didn't know how much longer I'd have to wait to see you again..."

"...Jace, and I, your father, well..."

Only now did his mother notice Vereesa standing to the side. "This is... this is a lady elf?"

"That's right, I'm an elf," Vereesa nodded, then gave Jace a sidelong glance. "I come from Quel'Thalas, Mrs. Sesso."

Jace cleared his throat awkwardly. "Her name is Vereesa Windrunner."

At that, Vik also poked his head out from inside. "Vereesa? So this is the Lady Vereesa Windrunner?"

"There should only be one Vereesa among the Windrunners of Quel'Thalas, Mr. Sesso," Vereesa said with a smile. "You two..." Hennie looked back and forth between them, as if at a loss for words all of a sudden.

"We're together now, Mom," Jace said.

"Then come in quickly, Jace," Hennie immediately stepped aside. "Please come in, Lady Vereesa."

"Just Vereesa will do, madam, sir." Vereesa lifted her hem and stepped over the threshold, only then realizing how dirty her dress had gotten — she'd tracked mud from outside straight into the house. "I'm sorry..."

"It's nothing," Hennie said at once.

Confronted with this scene of a family being so polite to each other it bordered on awkward, Jace beside them felt uncomfortable all over.

His father looked at him and said, "Jace, you've grown quite a bit. I feel like you're already taller than me. I never expected you'd still grow this much at twenty."

Jace rubbed his head, then reached up and felt the doorframe. Now that he looked at it, the height difference was quite obvious. All he could say was — thank the Dragonqueen and those two unlucky black dragons.

"Probably because I eat better in Stormwind?" Jace said with a smile.

"After leaving home a few years ago, you've started speaking for the southerners," Vik said with his hands on his hips and a sigh. "But I have to say, those folks from Elwynn did bring some really distinctive food recipes and such..."

Hennie said as she closed the door, "That's only natural. The Kingdom of Stormwind has close ties with the dwarves of Khaz Modan, and dwarves know how to enjoy life, Vik. By the way, there's still some cheese and bread at home. Have you two eaten?"

His mother was already heading for the kitchen as she spoke. Jace glanced at Vereesa. Vereesa said, "We ate, madam, right in the city."

"Ah." Hennie nodded, looked back and forth, and in the end just clasped her hands together. "Jace didn't even send word ahead of time... so we were caught completely unprepared. I'm truly sorry."

Jace hung up his cloak. "We did get back a bit late, Mom."

"Open that barrel of wine, Hennie," his father said, glancing toward the stairs. "Remember that one?"

His mother nodded and immediately headed for the stairwell. Vereesa glanced at her back, ill at ease, and said, "I can help."

"You sit right there — you're our guest, Vereesa," Vik said, clearing the table.

Jace was in the middle of helping him tidy up the plates and dishes, thinking he'd find a way to get his father to be a bit nicer to Vereesa — he absolutely had not expected him to be this friendly toward an elf.

At that moment, Vik looked at Vereesa, who was sitting there behaving herself, and said, "I hear every member of the Windrunner family is a formidable warrior."

"Not everyone is like that," Vereesa said. "It's more that the family has a tradition of it, making it easier to be influenced by the elders and walk this path — or to put it precisely, to become a Farstrider."

Vik said, "I'm quite familiar with the Farstrider corps. A long time ago, when I was patrolling up in the mountains north of Hillsbrad, I saw those elven rangers in green. Oh — Captain Alleria Windrunner, she's your sister?"

Vereesa said, "She's my eldest sister. She raised me from childhood, sir."

"She's a true hero to us Lordaeronians, Vereesa," Vik said. "She hunted orcs through the forests and hills of Tirisfal and Hillsbrad, holding back the orcish army's advance. If not for her and your elven warriors, God knows how many towns and villages would have been slaughtered. Whatever conflicts existed between us before, the Orc Wars brought us all onto the same side."

"Indeed they did, Mr. Sesso," Vereesa said. "We are now close allies. May nothing ever sever this bond."

As she spoke, her tone was starting to sound once more like a Farstrider leader arriving on some diplomatic mission.

"I remember..." His mother came to the table carrying the wine, looking at Vereesa. "The words on that memorial stone for Jace in Brill — it was you who wrote those, wasn't it, Vereesa?"

"I wrote them," Vereesa said.

"So when Jace fought at Nethergarde Keep, you must have been there too," Hennie asked as she sat down.

"Jace was injured — I was right by his side," Vereesa lowered her head. "It's hard to imagine he really did it all, really pulled through. He saved all of Nethergarde Keep, and that's no exaggeration."

"As a soldier, when something like that happened at Nethergarde Keep, I hid here," his father said. "Many of the soldiers who fought at the Battle of Hillsbrad were older than me, but I was only assigned to guard the wall. That's always been a regret of mine — not to mention, my own son has started fighting on the front lines."

At that, Hennie's expression clearly soured.

The atmosphere in the room grew heavy. Jace spoke up. "Those things are all in the past now, Dad. The most important thing right now is — what I promised before, I've done it. I bought a house in Stormwind, about the same size as this one, and it's inside the walls. The location isn't remote either — it's close to the Cathedral of Light. I came this time to tell you that you can move south whenever you're ready."

"You earned that much?" Hennie asked in surprise.

Jace said, "You can ask Vereesa. I did some work for Quel'Thalas a while back and earned a large sum, enough to buy a house."

"That's the truth," Vereesa explained. "Jace helped the Reliquary find some very important things, which will be of great use in resisting the Horde and the demons they summon. So the Reliquary paid him a handsome reward."

"You've only been in Stormwind about five years," Vik said. "Truly remarkable, Jace. Your mother and I have lived in the royal city all these years and don't seem to have earned enough to buy another house. Still, we still have a lot of affairs to settle here. We can't just pack up and move to Stormwind right away this winter — don't forget I still have a post in the kingdom."

"No need to rush," Jace said. "If you can come later, just send me a letter. I'll come help you."

"We'll be sure to let you know, Jace," Hennie said.

Vik said, "King Terenas has been ill lately. Quite a few affairs in the city have been affected. No word on when he'll recover."

"What illness?" Vereesa asked.

"No idea. News of him falling ill came out of nowhere," Vik said. "King Terenas is already very advanced in years — no knowing whether he'll pull through. I heard the last orc stronghold in Khaz Modan, Grim Batol, has already fallen. No knowing whether the orcs sent their warlocks into the city to curse the king, to avenge themselves against the Alliance."

"Impossible," Jace said.

"You heard about Grim Batol too?" Vik asked.

"Vereesa's the one who was involved," Vereesa said. "Or to be precise — Jace led it, Mr. Sesso."

"What?" His father looked at the elf in surprise. "What does Jace have to do with it? Wasn't he required by Dalaran's mages to... to pretend he was dead? How could he have taken part in such a dangerous operation?"

"Ve..." Jace scratched his head. "You don't have to be that blunt."

"That's exactly right," Vereesa said, not paying Jace the slightest attention, and continued explaining in detail. "So in order to restore his name, to be able to see you officially once again, he agreed to the Kirin Tor's demand to go to Grim Batol and reconnoiter the orc defenses. Later, he found the imprisoned Dragonqueen inside Grim Batol... and discovered it was only because the orcs had captured the mother of all the red dragons that they were able to enslave the red dragons to fight for the Horde. Rescue the Dragonqueen, and Grim Batol quickly lost its defenses."

"Is that true, Jace? You really... I can hardly imagine it," Vik said in astonishment. "So that's why the red dragons angrily routed the orc army? I heard Grim Batol was practically turned into a sea of fire."

Vereesa said, "Black dragons tried to interfere. Those dragons fought inside the castle, so that fortress ended up in the state it's in now."

"So Jace... rescued the Dragonqueen?" Hennie asked, almost word by word. "I wasn't alone," Jace said. "She was there too."

"And a dwarf named Grede Thunderfist," Vereesa said. "Also... er, that's it, the three of us. But if it weren't for Jace, none of us would have made it out alive."

"Grede Thunderfist," his father said immediately. "I've heard his name. Quite a few soldiers here know he's a hero of Nethergarde Keep, a Mountain King..."

"A good thing we didn't bring him," Vereesa said with a smile. "Otherwise, hearing Mr. Sesso call him a Mountain King, he'd be so excited he'd probably tear this house down."

"I doubt that," Jace said, propping his chin on his hand. "At worst he'd smash this table."

The family burst into laughter all at once. Jace and Vereesa chatted a bit more about how they'd met that dwarf... Just then, Vik asked curiously, "Jace, how did you get inside that orc fortress? Three people couldn't have just forced their way in — not even with a Mountain King."

"That's right. I used a little magic trinket the Kirin Tor gave me to turn into an orc and sneak inside," Jace explained. "I studied Orcish for a long time in Stormwind, Dad — that's exactly why the Kirin Tor decided to send me. Hey — would you like to take a look?"

Vereesa suddenly frowned. "What, wasn't that orb..."

"Turn into an orc?!" Hennie exclaimed.

"I'd like to see it," Vik said.

"My dad wants to see it," Jace said, looking at Vereesa.

"Er." Vereesa hesitated, and before she could get a word out, Jace immediately chanted, "Vaelastraszanoth!" Nothing happened.

"Forgot." Jace pulled the dark-brown cloak hanging on the wall over, draped it over himself, stepped back, and called out again, "Vaelastraszanoth!"
