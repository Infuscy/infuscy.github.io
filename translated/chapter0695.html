Chapter 695: The Throne of Lordaeron

A deep rumbling rolled through the hall as the palace doors swung open inch by inch. Jace watched the view unfold before him, one soldier at a time, as silver-armored men in deep blue cloaks turned their gazes his way.

Compared to the cramped, castle-like passageways of Stormwind Keep, the Lordaeron palace truly felt like the heart of a vast empire. Blue and white hangings cascaded from the massive stone walls on either side, and the Lordaeron cipher — that shield-and-sword monogram — was everywhere: on the soldiers' shields, on their cloaks, on the draperies.

Vereesa, who had been eyeing everything in the Old Craftsmen's Quarter with open curiosity earlier, didn't seem particularly impressed now that they had entered the palace. Compared to the ramparts and the royal finery, she appeared far more interested in one thing: exactly who sat upon the Lordaeron throne.

"Jace Sesso, Court Arcane Advisor of the Kingdom of Stormwind, and Lady Vereesa Windrunner, Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas, have arrived at the palace..."

The long, rolling announcement, delivered in an ancient Tirisfalian cadence, echoed through the corridors and off the walls. Jace leaned toward the high elf beside him and murmured, "The promotion ceremony hasn't even happened yet... and they already know you're a Ranger-Lord?"

Vereesa whispered back, "I'm already a Ranger-Lord. The Farstriders, the Sunstrider royal house, the Silvermoon Council — they've all acknowledged it. In truth it was more or less decided before Grim Batol, it's just — you know how those old bastards who've lived for centuries handle things... Anyway, the ceremony can happen whenever. That depends on me, or rather — on you."

"All right, I get it," Jace muttered as they walked.

The tall knight in silver armor and white cloak led the way up front, one hand resting on his longsword, metal boots clanging against the flagstones. Jace heard the announcement echo down the corridor again and again, and the title "Court Arcane Advisor" made him feel more and more like a fraud.

By his current abilities, he could probably just barely pass the Wizard Sanctum's apprentice mage exam. A proper licensed mage? Not even close — he was a hundred thousand miles away.

Sure, as an advisor on dark magic within Stormwind's walls, he probably qualified.

But the truth was, his actual strength had long since fallen far short of his reputation. After this trip back to the Kingdom of Stormwind, for one thing he needed to help his parents finish decorating their new house properly; for another, he needed to seriously study dark magic and shore up his knowledge.

Whatever you do, don't ask the king to test my magic, Your Majesty, he prayed inwardly.

Crossing the wooden bridge over the inner-city canal, the knights' boots made the planks shudder underfoot. Jace glanced at the red walls and golden towers of the palace on either side, at the guards staring back at him, at noble men and women peeking quietly from the windows — the whole scene felt strangely familiar, as if he had seen it somewhere before.

Of course, the nervousness of being about to meet King Terenas II of Lordaeron left him little bandwidth to dredge up memories from this life or the last. The doors to the royal chamber were right ahead.

As the final palace gate swung open and they stepped inside, a ripple of whispered conversations fell silent.

It was a solemn, somewhat somber great hall. Beneath their feet, a circular pattern like a spell array radiated out from the Lordaeron coat of arms at the center — the moment you stepped inside, it felt as though some legalistic incantation had trapped you there.

On the high terraces to either side stood gorgeously dressed noblemen, or armored knights and generals, and their downward gaze only doubled the pressure.

At the far end, seated upon the throne atop a flight of steps, was a figure who looked as though he had been cast from the same mold as the throne itself.

This was the first time Jace had seen King Terenas II so clearly. He wore a spiked, gem-studded golden crown and a breastplate of pale gold. A chain strung with Holy Light sigils and the lion of royalty held his heavy fur-trimmed cloak upon his broad shoulders. In stark contrast to this magnificent, weighty ensemble, the king's face was as withered and decayed as an old wooden effigy.

Terenas II tilted his head slightly, regarding the two figures who had just entered. Then, from one of the high terraces above, a nobleman called out, "Bow before the King!"

Jace and Vereesa both bowed in unison and greeted His Majesty. The king managed a faint nod. Jace raised his eyes again, adjusted to the dim light of the hall, and saw a young man standing not far from the base of the throne's steps.

He had golden hair and wore an opulent blue cloak; beneath it, his vest, belt, and trousers glittered with gold ornaments and gems. This young man had less of Wrynn's martial bearing, but more handsomeness.

That was Arthas Menethil, Jace knew perfectly well.

At that moment, Prince Arthas of Lordaeron was staring at him with those blue eyes. He was tall and powerfully built, as handsome as a golden-maned warhorse, as though he were the true protagonist of this place.

Jace knew he was fifteen. The last time he had seen him in the great cathedral, the boy had still seemed childish, and Jace had held on to that impression all this time, forgetting that two Winter Veils had slipped by without him noticing.

Of course, in the eyes of the Alliance's rulers and nobles, Arthas Menethil was already the Alliance's bright future. He was the living embodiment of the ever-rising Kingdom of Lordaeron — a resplendent golden king, prosperous and glorious.

Then Terenas II murmured, "Lady Vereesa Windrunner, welcome to Lordaeron. Jace Sesso... perhaps I should not treat you as a Lordaeron guest. You grew up in Brill and the capital — you are a child of Tirisfal."

"I have heard of the terrible disaster at Grim Batol, Your Majesty. The flames of the black dragons and red dragons destroyed that place, and your bravery spared us from having to send our young heroes to shed their blood in dragonfire... upon those ancient dwarven walls. I am grateful. The whole Alliance is grateful."

Getting those words out seemed to have drained half the king's remaining strength. He had to lean on the throne and catch his breath.

"As Farstriders, serving the Alliance is my duty, Your Majesty," Vereesa said with a slight bow of her head.

"The Wizard Sanctum has been investigating Grim Batol's connection to the dragons for a long time, Your Majesty," Jace said. "Finishing that work was something of a mission for me."

"The Wizard Sanctum?" said a court mage in white robes beside them. "Had you chosen to stay in Lordaeron and study at Lordaeron's academy of magic, rather than leave for the orc-ravaged Kingdom of Stormwind, perhaps you wouldn't have had to take so many detours. Your magical talent would have found a more proper outlet."

Hearing that, Jace was taken aback. Vereesa frowned. A nobleman on one of the upper terraces — probably a member of the royal council — said, "Your Majesty knows what happened at Nethergarde Keep, Mr. Sesso. Though many, myself included, feared the consequences of that incident, His Majesty does not believe it makes you a danger to the kingdom. On the contrary, he is willing to give you a chance to redeem yourself. And the crisis at Grim Batol only proves that you are indeed a noble and good warrior — one who simply lost his way. I wish to offer my apology for the rash judgments I once made against you."

"Redeem himself?" Vereesa couldn't help but fire back.

"Do you want your friend to keep company with dark magic, demons, and even orcish incantations, Lady Vereesa Windrunner?" another nobleman called down from above. "Remember Quel'Thalas, five years ago."

"Silence." The king's feeble command rang out weakly amid the clamor, but the throne room fell instantly quiet.
