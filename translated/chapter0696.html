Chapter 696: The Boon

The throne room fell quiet for a moment. Terenas II raised his head and fixed the noblemen on the high terraces with a flash of anger. "The way you speak, one would think it was you who entered Grim Batol. This matter was settled long ago. There is no need to keep dragging it up — it is a waste of time."

He caught his breath, glanced at the golden-haired youth at his side, and smiled faintly. "Arthas."

Arthas nodded and descended the steps. The king said, "Forgive me for being unable to leave this seat and present Lordaeron's gift with my own hands. At my age, one is beset by all manner of troubles — those self-important fools up there and this illness. Hard to say which is more vexing."

"Lady Vereesa Windrunner, I have been hearing stories about you and your sisters since I was very young." Arthas took a jewel case from an attendant as he spoke and opened it, revealing a silver ring set with a deep blue gem.

Vereesa nodded expressionlessly, accepted the ring, but did not put it on right away. She closed her fingers around it.

The king coughed twice and gave a look to the attendant beside him, who announced, "This is the Lordaeron Knight's Seal. Wearing this ring entitles you to many privileges within the kingdom. No one in the capital will bar your way anywhere. It represents His Majesty's absolute trust in our heroes."

Arthas came before Jace and said, in the tone of someone reciting a lesson, "Mr. Jace Sesso, I hope you will have time to tell me more about Grim Batol someday. I'm sure what actually happened was far more thrilling and extraordinary than what those people told me..."

"I would welcome that opportunity, Your Highness," Jace said softly, accepting the two rings Arthas held out. He slipped one onto his own finger.

The king said, "And of course, Master Grede Thunderfist. It is a pity he could not come to the capital today, but I will not forget him. Lordaeron has long looked forward to forging a stronger alliance with the Wildhammer dwarves."

"I will place this ring in his hands, Your Majesty," Jace said.

The king nodded slowly. "Furthermore, I have written to Archbishop Alonsus Faol, asking him to accept you into the Church for study. In time, you may become a member of the Order of the Silver Hand."

The prince glanced over with envy. Jace blinked and said, "I'm afraid my faith in the Holy Light is not yet..."

"That does not matter," the king said. "You are a young and good-hearted warrior, Jace Sesso. Many who are now paladins were not as resolute in their devotion to justice as you are when they first began."

"His Majesty speaks truly," said a dark-haired paladin in silver armor beside them.

Jace understood. To Terenas II and the nobles here, a hero who had saved the Alliance through dark means was something they could no longer abide.

They saw sending him into the Church as a kind of "cure," a "cleansing" — a boon, a way to help him become the sort of hero a true subject of the kingdom could accept.

Vereesa understood too. Jace could feel her fury barely held in check. She was an elf, not a human, and had no obligation to swallow her anger for these nobles.

Just as she was about to open her mouth and let loose, Jace said quickly, "My parents will be overjoyed to hear this, Your Majesty. They are both devout believers in the Holy Light."

At those words, Vereesa almost flinched. The look on her face softened at once.

The king nodded with satisfaction, and the surrounding nobles immediately warmed. The whole throne room relaxed in an instant. Jace even felt the sunlight streaming in from outside had grown a little brighter — was the Holy Light planning to take him in?

At the banquet that followed, the Lordaeron Knight's Seal on Jace's finger felt like a chain wrapped around him, far heavier than the Chain of Will around his neck, and it sat wrong no matter what.

Vereesa kept picking at the most expensive dishes on the banquet table, eating nonstop. No matter who tried to strike up a conversation with her, she ignored them completely. She had entirely abandoned the poised image of a Quel'Thalas noblewoman she had maintained since stepping through the palace gates — as if she had nowhere else to vent her rage and intended to eat the royal house into bankruptcy in a single evening.

But the Menethil royal house was not the Stormwind one. Eating like this probably wouldn't cause them much damage.

Between the courses, Jace thought he heard someone mention "a prodigal son's return" and "turning from darkness toward the light." Before long, he noticed a young woman in a white gown appear beside the king — Calia Menethil, Arthas's elder sister and princess of Lordaeron.

She had not changed much since the last time Jace had seen her in the cathedral. She was far easier to recognize than Arthas.

Jace remembered the joke Andormas had made back in the Wizard Sanctum — "The King of Lordaeron ought to marry his daughter off to you." He had been referring to this princess.

She was speaking with the king about something, glancing over from time to time, her complicated expression jarringly out of place at a banquet whose mood was otherwise cheerful.

Of course, Terenas II would never in a million years marry his daughter to some clueless kid who'd grown up on the muddy streets of the Old Craftsmen's Quarter.

Even if that kid had blown a Pit Lord to pieces with a single shot, or thought up a nasty trick to blast the "Destroyer" Deathwing out of the sky — still no.

And Jace even doubted that anyone here had any idea what Deathwing actually was, even if every one of them had seen the great dragon in the flesh — in the form of Lord Daval Prestor.

Calia Menethil's future husband would be the King of Alterac, whoever that turned out to be. He was Lordaeron's leverage against both Gilneas and Stromgarde at once.

Unless Jace was Medivh reborn, capable of wiping Stromgarde or the city of Gilneas clean off the map with a single spell, the King of Lordaeron was unlikely to be that generous.

Then again, Jace had zero interest in that saintly young lady anyway. He only liked silver-haired, pointy-eared elves.

"Wen."

"Hmm?"

Vereesa turned her head. Her lips were still smeared with cream. She picked up some unidentifiable pastry and said, "Want a try? This one's pretty good."

Jace took her hand and kissed her on the mouth, tasting the sweetness between her tongue and teeth, until Vereesa couldn't help but close her eyes, tossed the pastry neatly back onto the tray behind her, and wrapped her arms tightly around his waist.

A wave of applause and gasps rose from the nobles around them. Someone even shouted, "Long live the friendship between Quel'Thalas and Lordaeron!" Jace thought, These guys really know how to find the right words.
