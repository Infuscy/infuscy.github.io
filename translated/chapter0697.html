Chapter 697: Return to Its Rightful Owner

After the evening banquet, neither of them wanted to spend the night in the palace, so they took their leave of the king and left. The two of them strolled along the canal on the way home. The capital's streets were still quite lively, and Jace noticed that no small number of people here were already celebrating the arrival of Winter Veil. War had a way of bringing together the different races and cultures of this land — groups that had once been riddled with conflict or had nothing to do with one another — and their customs had spread rapidly over just a few years. "This isn't fair to you, Jace," Vereesa said. "I wasn't expecting them to accept me quickly," Jace said. "Tirisfal has suffered the calamities of dark magic and demons for a thousand years… It's hard to blame the king and those nobles for their caution." Vereesa nodded. "It's a good thing you've already decided not to stay here." "Are you getting sick of this place too?" Jace asked. "Sick of that palace, but not of this place, Jace. Especially your home." "Then let's stay a few more days here before heading back to Dalaran," Jace said. "And find a chance to go hunting in the Tirisfal forest with my dad — let him see what a real bowman looks like. I can't stand it anymore, that man's been showing off his archery for over ten years. You've got to teach him a lesson for me." "That's not really appropriate…" Vereesa said, but she was already struggling to suppress a smirk. Jace picked up a stone and gazed at the river. "What's not appropriate? My mom's probably itching for him to suffer a setback too, or he'll really take it upon himself to head south and hunt demons on his own…" As he spoke, he flicked the stone out over the water. It skipped a dozen times and sank. Ah… such a simple, satisfying pastime. Vereesa casually picked up a stone and tossed it. It skittered and clattered an unknowable number of times, vanishing into the black of night. God only knew how many more times it had skipped in the shadows. Jace glanced at her, picked up another stone meaning to compete again, then thought better of it and casually tossed it into the water. That didn't satisfy him at all. "Master Jace Sesso? Lady Vereesa?" An unfamiliar voice sounded from behind. Jace turned. A fairly young man had appeared there at some point without either of them noticing. He wore the leather jerkin and trousers common among travelers, with a deep blue cloak and hood covering his hair. The man's face was quite narrow — he didn't even look quite human — yet he was broad-shouldered and powerfully built, which didn't seem elven either. A half-elf? He had to admit, the man's looks would be considered remarkably handsome even among half-elves. Wait… Jace felt his outfit was extremely familiar. It was just that he'd never expected to run into him here. "Yes?" "My name is Kalec," the man said. "I have come on behalf of my master to return to you something that rightfully belongs to you." Kalec? Wasn't that Kalecgos, the blue dragon? Jace stole a careful look at the hair hidden beneath the hood and saw it was an unnaturally deep blue. Kalecgos might not have been the most powerful among the dragons, but he was certainly a brave and trustworthy mage. Which was precisely why, after Malygos completely lost his mind and tried to destroy the world and was finally slain by the allied forces, Kalecgos inherited the Aspect's power and became the new Guardian of Magic. As he spoke, Kalec unfastened from his waist a longsword wrapped in an elegant blue scabbard and slowly drew it free. When Jace saw the sapphire set into the pommel and crossguard, he recognized it at once — it was that very… "Song of the Blue?" Vereesa exclaimed. "It's been repaired?" "My master restored it," Kalec said. "He was most wroth at Song of the Blue's destruction. He gave us a proper dressing-down over it, and reproached Quel'Thalas for leaving this sword in the hands of a mortal who tried to… stab that thing with it." Jace recalled it — using a dragon-forged weapon to stab the dragons' bane had probably been a bit too stupid. But at the time, he truly hadn't had any other choice. "Your master is… the Spell-Weaver?" Vereesa asked. "Indeed." Kalec's voice even carried a hint of grievance. He said quietly, "But worry not overmuch, Lady Vereesa, Master Jace Sesso. We know my master does not truly blame anyone. He has… always been this way. I believe he only regrets that he was not at Grim Batol himself." "After all, he personally reforged this sword, and he all but forced me to hurry up and be rid of it. I took that to mean he wanted me to return it to you. And so I have come." So saying, Kalec drew Song of the Blue fully free, and Jace realized the blade was no longer anything like it had been before. It was still silver, but it no longer seemed to be pure metal — the edges were even slightly transparent. Jace could hardly tell whether this was a trick of the material's refraction, the work of magic, or some combination of both. Kalec seemed to read Jace's doubts. He lightly touched the blade with his finger and said, "This is a scale of my master's." "The Spell-Weaver's scale?" Jace asked in surprise. "Correct. Song of the Blue is no ordinary enchanted sword, and the manner of its destruction was no ordinary matter. There was no other good way to reforge it. My master used his own scales to replace the original blade and bound the entire sword together with magic to achieve what is, on the whole, a passable result." "Passable?" Vereesa said. "I think it's far more powerful than before." "I think so too," Jace said. "I am glad the two of you are satisfied with the result," Kalec said. "Though my master did not say so directly, I am certain he is deeply grateful for all you, Lady Vereesa, and Master Grede did at Grim Batol. My brothers and sisters and I feel the same. Now that the Demon Soul no longer exists, there is likely nothing left in this world that could damage this sword so easily." "Thank you, Master Kalec — and your master," Jace said with a nod. "I am merely doing my master's bidding as best I can," Kalec said. "Now I shall return north to report back to him." "Wait," Jace said. "The north — you've come from Northrend?" "Yes," Kalec nodded. "Is there anything unusual in Northrend of late? I've heard black dragons have been spotted there, and I wonder what effect the business with Deathwing at Grim Batol might have had on them." Kalec thought for a moment. "For the time being, all is well in Northrend, Master Sesso." "Perhaps I'm worrying over nothing," Jace said. "Very well." Kalec looked at the two of them. "I won't intrude on your evening any longer." He turned and left. Vereesa watched him go, murmuring, "His brothers and sisters… so he's a dragon too?" "Quite likely," Jace said. Though he was answering Vereesa's question, Jace's thoughts were on Northrend. Had the blue dragons not noticed Ner'zhul's spirit-ice falling into Northrend? Perhaps they simply hadn't detected it — otherwise the Lich King could never have developed in secret in Northrend for so many years until the situation became unmanageable. Perhaps penetrating deep into the frozen mountains of northern Northrend was an extraordinarily difficult undertaking. The blue dragons were weak in numbers at present, and the Spell-Weaver's mind was not entirely sound. Rashly investigating the Lich King's existence might bring even more terrible consequences. After all, the Scourge's great aerial power — the frost wyrms — drew their primary source from the corpses of blue dragons. Vereesa held Song of the Blue upright before her, listening to the faint, musical hum it gave off. "I thought this sword might never be as beautiful as it once was. I never expected it to be even more beautiful than before." "I'll take good care of it," Jace said. "That's what you said last time," Vereesa said. "It was a special circumstance…" Jace said helplessly. Vereesa smiled. "Then be more careful next time."
