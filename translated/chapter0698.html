Chapter 698: Return to Dalaran

Like Vereesa, Jace had never expected Song of the Blue to be repaired by Malygos himself, using his own scales. The scales of the Dragon Aspects were beyond precious. Aside from those still on their bodies, every fallen scale was exceedingly rare — let alone one personally selected by an Aspect to be forged into a weapon. That was rare even among rarities. Jace should have known this. Which was why, after the destruction of Grim Batol, he had actually imagined scouring the ground for fallen scales of Deathwing's… …That had truly been a pipe dream. Fortunately, Vereesa had been able to enlist the help of the gryphon riders in Dalaran, giving Jace a few extra days in Lordaeron to spend more time with his parents. He could tell that even though his father kept complaining about how tiresome his mother's family was, and his mother had long wanted to escape this place, Lordaeron was still the land that had borne and raised them. After all the joys and sorrows they had experienced here, the mere mention of leaving stirred something indescribable. Under the circumstances, Jace couldn't very well rush them. In truth, when he had first arrived in Stormwind, he hadn't imagined he'd be able to buy such a lovely, fitting house in as little as three to five years. He had originally estimated he might have to grind away right up until the eve of the Scourge invasion — until his family felt the plague pressing at the door — before they would agree to move. And that was assuming everything went smoothly. After all, earning money by the sweat of his brow… his parents might well end up renting a place alongside him in the end. Of course, once the Scourge was ravaging Lordaeron, renting wouldn't even count as a hard time by comparison. Now that time was far less pressing, there was no need to force them to leave so soon. Better to let them prepare themselves mentally and pack their belongings properly before setting out. After staying in Tirisfal for roughly five or six days, Jace and Vereesa sailed back to Dalaran. When they arrived at Dalaran's harbor on the afternoon of the sixteenth of December, the city's Winter Veil celebrations were already underway. Still, Jace and Vereesa were eager to get to Amy's and see how her sympathetic magic studies were coming along, so they passed on the entertainment stalls set up by the goblins and gnomes lining the streets. Actually, before coming to Dalaran, Jace had considered bringing Vereesa along to join in the fun — but that enthusiasm had been thoroughly dashed by that stone-skipping contest on the capital's canal. He simply could not give this Ranger-Lord any more opportunities to show off. In the evening, the two of them arrived at Amy's doorstep and knocked. Amy opened the door remarkably fast. She pulled the two of them inside and shut the door, silently poured the tea, sat down, took a deep breath, and looked at them. "I know what you did." "You actually… defeated Deathwing. When I first heard the news, I could hardly believe it was true. Deathwing? The Father of Black Dragons? That demon who had threatened the entire world since ancient times?" Amy frowned. "Jace, do you understand what Deathwing was? If you did, you couldn't have said a single word about it when you saw me earlier. Vereesa Windrunner — you too. You can't possibly not know who Deathwing was." "How could we not know?" Vereesa said. "The moment Deathwing showed himself, he destroyed half of Grim Batol with a single spell and burned several valleys into a hellscape of flame… Against the assault of dozens of whelms and adult dragons, he was virtually unscathed. At that moment, no legend could compare to what we saw with our own eyes. I'm certain Jace felt it even more deeply than I did." Listening to Vereesa's description, Jace felt as though he had been transported back to that place. His feet, which had lost their boots, seemed to burn once more from the cinders and ash. The voice of the Father of Black Dragons sounded in his mind once again. It was the voice of death and doom — and the voice of betrayal. "I will give you one last chance, Sesso…" "Let's not talk about this." "Uh." Amy was taken aback. Vereesa glanced over as well. The words had left Jace's mouth almost involuntarily, but the moment they did, he felt his tone may have been a touch too cold. He wasn't facing Deathwing — he was facing Amy Marin, who was no different from family. Damn it, what was he doing. He glanced at the scar on his arm, which had long since lost all its power, and let out a quiet sigh. "I'm sorry," the young mage said softly. "I thought you'd be proud of yourself for defeating so powerful an enemy." "Of course I'm proud," Jace said. "I survived a catastrophe like that. And more importantly, Vereesa and the others survived too. It's not that I don't want to talk about it with you, and it's not as though what I did was some trivial thing, but…" Jace was unwilling to admit that he was running from everything he had experienced before. Even though the shadow turmoil Deathwing had inflicted on him had long since been purged, and the scar on his arm no longer pained him, many things didn't simply fade away as though they had never happened, wounds or no wounds. Amy said, "Perhaps what I've been through can't compare to what you did, but I think I have some idea. After Nethergarde Keep, when I returned to Stormwind, I couldn't sleep night after night. Those demons… Ah, enough of that. I'm sorry, Jace. You came to ask about the sympathetic magic, didn't you? I believe I've more or less got the hang of it." "It's only been a few days," Vereesa said in surprise. "How long did it take you to learn trace-magic from the Reliquary?" Jace asked. "Quite a while," Vereesa said, then added hastily, "Of course, I didn't learn it specifically just to go to Kalimdor with you." Amy smiled with a hint of pride. "All right, I admit it — these past few days I haven't been focusing much on my regular studies. I've been devoting myself to researching how to perform magical tracking. It set me back a little, but I feel it was well worth it." Jace asked, "So how do you know you've mastered the incantation?" "Don't ask, Jace," Amy said. "I've made my determination, and that's that." "Understood," Jace nodded. Amy cleared her throat. "So — who exactly are you planning to track?" Jace took a sip of his tea. "Specifically, I'm trying to track a Quel'dorei. A magister." "A magister?" Amy looked between the two of them. "That… is no simple task." "Which is exactly why we need you, Amy," Vereesa said. "But you won't need to take any risks tracking down magical items connected to that magister to follow his trail. We just need you into Dalaran's Great Library to help Jace find — Amy, can you access the section of the library that holds the records of the Order of Tirisfal?" "I can," Amy said. "I have clearance for that section of the library."
