Chapter 70: The Company Will Take Care of You

One moon had already hidden itself behind the Redridge peaks, and the second moon was about to set. In another two or three hours, dawn would break.

He remembered last night, when he'd lain on the wall and looked down — the sun hadn't even set yet.

How long had he been sitting here? Had the whole night really passed that quickly?

Jace could no longer tell whether the battle itself had lasted longer, or the time he'd spent sitting here in a daze afterward.

The one who'd asked was Colonel Tordman. The look in his eyes had completely lost the earlier contempt he'd held for some clueless kid.

"I… yes, I fired it," Jace nodded. "Not long after you left, the gnolls came. I was trapped up here."

"What did you use to send the signal?" a soldier behind the colonel asked, kicking at a severed gnoll limb on the ground.

Jace paused. Right — what had he used to send the signal?

Fireballs. No — a signal flare. There was no signal flare here. Damn it, in a moment like that, who had the presence of mind to think about how to explain it afterward? He scratched his head and looked around.

The soldier asked, "What are you looking for?"

"I'm looking for… looking for…" Jace said suddenly, "A scroll. A scroll — I brought a Fireball spell scroll from the Wizard Sanctum. I was going to use it for self-defense on the road, because I haven't learned any magic yet. I used the Fireball scroll to signal you. I thought there'd be some left, but…"

The explanation actually sounded pretty convincing. Not a single soldier looked suspicious. The colonel pressed on: "You killed all these gnolls by yourself?"

Before Jace could answer, the colonel picked up the blackened, club-like wreck of a sword, turned it over, and asked, "What happened here?"

Looking at that sword, Jace felt a chill so deep no amount of layering could keep it out. He hadn't just burned through his strength — he'd been severely drained by that inexplicable enchantment.

He'd been about to open his mouth and answer, but the sudden cold made him shiver so hard he could barely get the words out.

It took him a while to pull himself together enough to speak. "I don't know. They were all dead. I had to… if I wanted to live, I had to kill every gnoll that climbed up."

"I don't know about that sword." Jace swallowed. He decided to pin it on the gnolls.

"The… the sword just caught fire out of nowhere. It had to be dark magic."

"He's right — it looks like some evil gnoll magic," a soldier, examining the sword, said. "I can smell the stench of shadow, just like those orcs, Colonel Tordman. We never found that shadow mage — maybe he just used some magic to lure us away, then joined in on the raid himself. These savage gnolls — when they couldn't win with blade and axe, they tried to burn his weapon. If we'd come back any later, the consequences would've been unthinkable."

"Look at his hand, damn it… the poor bastard."

"Mr. Sesso? Mr. Sesso?"

Someone patted Jace. He looked up. Colonel Tordman was looking down at him.

"You know, you saved Stonewatch alongside the brave men of this company," Colonel Tordman said. "The soldiers down below told me that if you hadn't drawn most of the gnolls' attention up here with the fire, they might all have been killed. The other watchtowers were all taken out by the gnolls first — only this one… didn't fall, because of your heroic resistance. It's far worse up here than I imagined. I'm having a hard time… I can't even begin to imagine what you went through tonight."

"We have plenty of wounded, plenty of men down — but Stonewatch still stands," Colonel Tordman said. "This has to be reported to King Wrynn."

"Get some rest, Jace Sesso. Dawn's almost here, and I imagine you've spent every last drop of strength you had. Your eyes are shot through with blood — you look just like an orc."

"His hand needs treatment — that burn's no light matter."

"Fetch those mages, the priests, quickly."

"A lot of folks are hurt… the priests might be a while yet. We lost a mage, too — they found his body in the woods."

"Martin Harlor, Master Morgas' student. Looks like the gnolls took his head."

"Even a mage got killed?"

"Hard to believe — this teenage kid single-handedly killed seven or eight gnolls."

"He's a Lordaeroner, I think."

"The Kingdom of Lordaeron…"

"General Turalyon was a Lordaeroner too."

Listening to the soldiers' chaotic chatter, Jace felt a growing unease in his chest.

It wasn't because of the gnoll attack — honestly, after all the life-or-death scrapes he'd been through with Grede, he was a lot calmer about these things than he used to be.

What he was uneasy about was that dark power that had suddenly erupted from within him — the fear that his shadow secret would be discovered by the soldiers of Stonewatch, or even more people.

Truth be told, he'd rather this didn't get reported to Stormwind at all. If this blew up too big and someone caught on to his secret, the one getting driven out of Stormwind wouldn't be Morgas — it'd be him.

Right… and then there was Morgas. As a scholar who dabbled in shadow magic in secret, would he notice something off about Jace? If this reached the king, as a matter tied to a gnoll shadow mage, it would certainly draw an investigation from the Wizard Sanctum.

And the one handling it would almost certainly be Morgas.

It was all such a mess. This trip was supposed to be nothing more than delivering a package — how had things suddenly turned into this? He'd barely managed to fight off all those gnolls, which should have been something to celebrate, and now he had to worry all over again.

But then again, without that darkness, would he have had any chance of surviving?

"Will you tell the king my name, Colonel Tordman?" Jace asked.

"Of course we will. Even though you're not one of ours, we won't steal your credit." Colonel Tordman smiled. "I was thinking — could you leave my name out of it?"

Everyone turned their heads. Colonel Tordman asked, puzzled, "Why? You'll be commended by the king. Even if you don't get a personal audience with His Majesty, if luck's on your side they might toss you a couple of gold coins or so. I don't quite understand — this is what you've earned."

Right about now, the prospect of a few gold coins didn't hold much appeal for Jace at all.

"Honestly, the scroll — I took it without permission. I'd heard the road was dangerous, that's why." Jace said. "If the king finds out about the signal flare and tells my boss, and he learns I can't do any magic and I'm just a delivery assistant, I'll definitely be punished."

"I see." Colonel Tordman scratched his bristly hair. "That is a bit of a problem."

"We could dress it up a little," a soldier beside Tordman said. "Say he picked up a dwarven signal gun that a sentry dropped in the fighting, and kept firing it to get our attention — something like that. The end result's the same either way."

"That's a good point — John, you write the report then." Tordman finished, then clapped Jace on the shoulder. "Nothing to be afraid of now, lad. The company will take care of you."
