## Chapter 706: The Importance of Language

Jace had not expected the Sun King to appear in person. Numbers of elves called out his name and the title "High King," and high up in the Farstrider Enclave, many elves raised their cups to him enthusiastically. A good number of magisters even came before him to bow and address him at length.

Anasterian did not ignore the fervor of these elves, but responded to each of them as best he could — including those expressing their admiration from the high terraces above. He seemed less like a king and more like a warm-hearted elder of a great family.

Accustomed to how the nobles of Stormwind and Lordaeron comported themselves around their kings, Jace even found the scene a little hard to get used to.

But considering that the Sun King had ruled Quel'Thalas for over twenty-eight hundred years, and was quite possibly the oldest being in the realm, over the course of that vast span his name had become completely bound up with the titles "Sun King" and "King of Quel'Thalas."

More than two thousand years ago, King Anasterian had personally ordered elven mages dispatched to Strom, to teach humanity magic so that together they could defeat the invasion of the Amani Empire. To the humans, he was already a figure of myth and legend.

The Sun King was virtually the only elf Jace had ever met from whom one could actually sense the mark of age upon his appearance. His face was no longer perfectly smooth — it bore some resemblance to a human of thirty or forty — and his pure-white hair hung long over his shoulders. Yet his stride was still powerful, and nothing in his bearing or movements betrayed the slightest hint of frailty.

Like every other elf, in the intervals between meeting and conversing with those around him, he glanced over this way. When his gaze fell upon Jace, he nodded slightly. What surprised Jace was that the king's eyes were golden, radiating the light of the Sunwell.

Grede could not help saying, "The elven king is looking at us, Jace."

"Elven king," Kael'fal said in a low voice. "A term that could get you seized and given a few lashes as punishment, Grede." Grede corrected himself: "King of the Quel'dorei."

"The prince you're looking for is over there — do you see him?" Kael'fal said, pointing to a tall elf at the Sun King's side.

Jace looked where he pointed. Beside the Sun King, surrounded by the crowd, that elf did not appear particularly dazzling — but his head of brilliant golden hair was still easy enough to make out.

He looked as though he had been stamped from the exact same mold as the Sun King, his face as handsome as a temple statue. But compared to the Sun King, he looked younger and more spirited, only… he was a far cry from the Kael'thas Sunstrider in Jace's memory.

Kael'thas was not wearing his signature blood-mage robes, and the three emerald arcane orbs no longer floated above his head… Instead he wore only a simple silver gown, arms folded, smiling and chatting with the magisters around him.

He appeared to be enjoying this gathering — quite relaxed, glancing from time to time toward the heights of the Farstrider Enclave as though anticipating something. Then his gaze swept this way and happened to notice Jace's stare. He nodded back politely as well.

"That human — when Nathanos was promoted to Ranger-Lord, neither the Sun King nor the prince came," Kael'fal said. "A foreigner appearing in Quel'Thalas is one thing, but for him to be granted such a lofty honor…"

He shot Jace a sidelong look, raised his long eyebrows, and said, "But by tradition they ought to have put in an appearance. Which is why this time, for Vereesa, they will make a far more solemn occasion of it — to make amends to the Windrunner family and the Farstriders."

"So that's how it is," Jace said, glancing in Nathanos's direction and indeed noticing that his expression was somewhat stiff. A horn sounded again from the heights of the Farstrider Enclave, and this time the whole area fell silent.

The nobles, mages, Farstriders, and priests tacitly cleared the plaza before the enclave, forming a circle around it. Even the Sun King and the prince stood off to one side, gazing toward the enclave's great doors. Vereesa returned to Jace's side as well.

Then Sylvanas walked out of the enclave, by now wearing the battle-dress of the Ranger-General.

She looked down upon the Farstrider guards standing in a row at the front, holding their tall lances, and declared something aloud in elven — but Jace understood not a word of it. Still, at the very end he caught at least one term: Sylvanas spoke Vereesa's name.

At that moment, every elf in the vicinity turned their eyes toward them. Vereesa gave Jace a smiling look, then walked toward the center of the plaza. Jace could tell she'd been asked not to walk too quickly — her pace was restrained.

Vereesa came to stand before Sylvanas, meeting her sister's eyes earnestly. Sylvanas did not look at her especially, nor did she show any pleased or gratified expression. She merely addressed Vereesa earnestly in elven.

Yet Jace, relying on his sensitivity to emotion, could perceive that the Ranger-General's heart was suppressing a surge of excitement and exhilaration that was nearly beyond containment — even from this distance, his warlock's keen senses detected it clearly.

She truly loved her sister deeply.

At last, Sylvanas draped a gold-and-green magical cloak over Vereesa's shoulders and drew a deep breath. The entire gathering was utterly silent now.

The Ranger-General murmured as though chanting an incantation:

Anar'alah, anar'alah, belore'selash, Quel'Thalas, belore'selash, doral shala, belore'selash, belore'doril.

Every character, every word — Jace heard each one with perfect clarity, yet he had no idea what they meant.

Vereesa knelt slightly before Sylvanas and said softly, "Quel'Thalas, sash, belore."

A wave of congratulations erupted from above, below, and all around the Farstrider Enclave. Now Jace deeply regretted never having properly studied Thalassian.

"By the light of Anar'alah," Kael'fal murmured at his side. "I bestow upon you the title of Ranger-Lord — belore'selash. May you guard Quel'Thalas, guard the justice of its people. Bear up under your burden, child of the sun."

"The Cloak of Anar'alah," he continued. "The dream of every Farstrider. I can recite this passage fluently by now, but to this day I see no hope of promotion for myself, Jace."

"You never seize your opportunities yourself," Grede said. "If you'd been willing to step forward and help us of your own accord when we went to Kalimdor, instead of letting Vereesa Windrunner sneak out and risk her neck, you'd be the one standing up there now."

"Hmph." Kael'fal shot him a look. "Tag along with you foreigners courting death? No thanks."

"Look at that," Grede said. "That's exactly why she's the one standing up there, while you can only stand here spouting nonsense." Kael'fal's face darkened. He folded his arms and stared toward the center of the plaza at the newly promoted Ranger-Lord, and said nothing more.
