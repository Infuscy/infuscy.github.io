## Chapter 707: What It Meant to the Elven Kingdom

Jace watched as Vereesa received the Ranger-Lord investiture. The Sun King now left the crowd as well, walking toward her and Sylvanas.

It suddenly occurred to him that the Wildhammer gryphon riders had of course also fought in the battle against the red dragons, and he could not resist asking the dwarf beside him, "When you went back to Aerie Peak, did they give you any reward, Grede?"

"They wanted to make me Lord Thunderfist," Grede said in a low voice. "But I turned it down. I don't want to be any lord."

Anasterian was saying something to the elves around him. After hearing Grede out, Jace asked, "Why? Isn't the Mountain King also a 'lord'?"

"But Lord Thunderfist is a real lord," Grede said. "Accepting that would mean I'd have to stay at Aerie Peak, Jace. The Mountain King is just an honor now — an empty title. Those are two completely different things. I'm used to fighting, not sitting around managing people. But they still gave me the honorary Thunderfist clan cloak — like this Anar'alah."

"Where is it?" Jace asked. "Let me see."

"In my pack. I left it in the Windrunner village," Grede said. "When we go back, I plan to bring it to my father, up in the Thunderstrike Mountains. His lifelong dream was to earn this cloak, but he lost his life before he could become the greatest hunter of the clan. I want him to know his son accomplished it. Would you like to come up the mountain with me? Call it sightseeing."

"I'd love to," Jace said. "That matters more to me than meeting any king, Grede."

"What?" Kael'fal suddenly looked at Jace. "The Grim Batol business — that was you?"

"Why do you ask all of a sudden?" Jace said. "Didn't you know?"

"I know now. The Sun King is calling your name, Jace Sesso." Kael'fal grabbed his cloak and tugged him forward. "You truly don't know a word of Thalassian? You should at least be able to make out your own name!"

"What…"

Jace looked toward the center of the plaza and only then did he notice that everyone was now staring straight at him. Vereesa was glaring at him, her fingers hooking urgently beneath her cloak.

He walked step by step toward the Sun King, Sylvanas, and Vereesa. At that moment the Sun King also called Grede's name. It was plain Grede was equally bewildered, but after a moment's hesitation he could only trot along after Jace.

The Sun King looked around and declared something loudly in elven. Waves of astonishment rippled through the surrounding elves. Only now did Jace learn that these nobles and mages had had no idea what had actually happened at Grim Batol.

When he'd entered Quel'Thalas, that guard had called him "Jace Sesso the Dragonslayer"… He'd assumed his deeds were already common knowledge throughout Quel'Thalas. As it turned out, only a very few people knew.

The guards had known, likely only because Vereesa or Sylvanas had informed them in advance.

No wonder the elves here had left him and Grede cooling their heels — but those high-ranking high elves who came would deliberately pay their respects to him. They had already received the word, but it hadn't yet been made public.

"Draw your sword, Jace Sesso," the Sun King said in Common, his voice low.

Jace nodded and drew Song of the Blue. At once a buzz of discussion rose from the elven mages present.

The Sun King took the silver sword set with its sky-blue gem, ran a finger across its shimmering sapphire-bright surface, and Song of the Blue at once gave forth a brilliant, musical chime.

He raised the sword high and said something in elven, relating something to the surrounding elves. All the elves immediately fell silent. Many turned to gaze toward the far north, where golden light streaked up from the sea into the sky.

At last he returned the sword with both hands to Jace. Jace, utterly clueless as to what was going on, took the sword back and could only incline his body slightly. He felt he ought to express something, at the very least.

"You, Grede Thunderfist, and Vereesa Windrunner are the saviors of this kingdom, Jace Sesso," Anasterian said quietly. "From this day forward, you may enter and leave Quel'Thalas and Silvermoon freely, without hindrance. The people of Quel'Thalas will treat you and your friends as benefactors."

"…Thank you, Your Majesty."

Though Jace said it, he still hesitated. He hadn't realized everything he'd done meant so much to Quel'Thalas. The Sun King said something more in elven, and the elves all voiced their agreement. Many cheered as well. Even Sylvanas smiled — a rare sight.

Vereesa looked at Jace joyfully, leaning against his side. He, for his part, could only look at Kael'fal in the crowd, bewilderedly watching everyone applaud him, thinking only: someone translate for me — what on earth is he actually saying?

Next, the Sun King declared the banquet open. Jace tried his best not to look at the food the elven servants were carrying in, decorated in all manner of elaborate and gorgeous forms.

"Wen… saviors of Quel'Thalas? Is this because I saved the Dragonqueen?"

"Not just that, Jace," Vereesa explained. "Didn't you hear the Sun King? The Sun… ah, right, you don't understand Thalassian."

She picked up a sliced roll stuffed with smoked meat and purple leaves from the long table beside her and handed it to Jace, taking one for herself. "It's not only because of the Dragonqueen, Jace. Nor only because of Teron Gorefiend. King Anasterian values most the safety of the Sunwell. He said that Deathwing's death would set the Spell-Weaver Malygos greatly at ease."

"Ah, I understand."

Jace now saw why the Sun King had made a point of displaying Song of the Blue — because it represented the Spell-Weaver's recognition.

The blue dragons had long monitored the functioning of the Sunwell. The Spell-Weaver had reforged Song of the Blue with his own scales, expressing his gratitude to the three of them. One could say it also meant the blue dragons would have no particular quarrel with the Sunwell.

Although the blue dragons had always approved of Quel'Thalas's use of magic, and recognized the Sunwell's role in stabilizing the entire arcane web, the King of Magic was temperamental, and no one knew what notion might suddenly enter his head.

Being able to see a scale of the Lord of Magic here was itself a comfort.

Jace looked toward the Sun King, who was conversing with Prince Kael'thas about something, and thought that probably only this ruler truly understood what Deathwing's death meant to the other Dragon Aspects.

"What are you standing there gawking for, Jace?" Grede clapped him on the shoulder. "Grab the good stuff and eat!"
