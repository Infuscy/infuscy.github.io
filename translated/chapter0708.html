Chapter 708: Silvermoon

The banquet carried on until the afternoon before gradually dispersing. A great many people had not yet left. Grede was drinking with a crowd of elves he didn't know, dead to the world — though in Quel'Thalas the wine emphasized aroma and sweetness and didn't much pursue potency, so Grede managed to hold up all the same. Vereesa had just finished chatting with a group of Farstriders and came over. Jace was sitting alone, eating sweets from a plate. "Jace, why don't you go talk to someone?" "I'm on the shy side," Jace said, observing the people around them. "Shy?" Vereesa glanced at his lips and said softly, "I would never have guessed." Jace gave an embarrassed laugh. "Did Dar'Khan Drathir attend your ceremony?" "He should still be in Dalaran," Vereesa said. "When attending a gathering like this, he'd normally be with Lord Lor'themar Theron. Lord Lor'themar's been with the priests all along, and I haven't seen a trace of Dar'Khan from start to finish. He holds a very high position among the magisterium — if he were here, he couldn't possibly hide so deeply. There'd be plenty of people seeking him out, or using his name to ingratiate themselves with Lord Lor'themar." Jace picked up a wine cup. "Did any senior members of the Reliquary attend?" "Yes," Vereesa said. "But if you want to investigate Dar'Khan, I think it'd be better to go to the Reliquary's headquarters in Silvermoon and find someone there. I have people in the Reliquary I trust. The people here I don't — and Sylvanas isn't particularly close to them either. I'm afraid some of them may be on good terms with Dar'Khan…" "I understand," Jace said. "And it'll be a good chance to have a look around Silvermoon, now that the Sun King has granted Grede and me entry." Vereesa asked, "Then I'll go tell Sylvanas, you get Grede, and we head over now?" "All right," Jace nodded. Jace found Grede and pulled him away from that crowd of elven guards. The fellow was in the middle of an animated account of how he'd single-handedly taken on six orcs outside the gates of Grim Batol. The three of them regrouped at the Farstrider Enclave junction and followed the column of guards returning to Silvermoon, heading north along the road. They passed one exquisite Quel'dorei manor after another, pools and plazas, and after going through a stretch of dense woodland once more, they came upon an even smoother, broader avenue. Looking north along this avenue, a broad gate flanked by creamy-white towers and decorations of flame-like gold and red lay across the end of their field of vision. The white walls on either side of the gate stretched on into the woods beyond sight, which reminded Jace of Eldre'Thalas — though the two cities were starkly different in style. If Eldre'Thalas was a mysterious, cold, massive tomb steeped in shadow and decay magic, then the Silvermoon before them was the way she had looked in life — a grand cathedral set ablaze by flame and surging arcane energy. This was not the Shepherd's Gate of the game. This was the true gate of Silvermoon. The broad walkway before the gate had, in the game, already become a Dead Scar running through all of Quel'Thalas, and the gate itself had been destroyed by the Scourge. After Arthas impaled his own father in Lordaeron with Frostmourne and invaded Quel'Thalas, he had advanced along this very avenue into Silvermoon, crushed the greater part of the city, and left behind that terrible blighted land. The surviving Silvermoon was merely a corner of the original city. The guards before the gate could not help staring at Jace and Grede, but after noticing Vereesa they one and all saluted and called out the elven title for "Ranger-Lord." One of the guards addressed Jace and Grede in Common: "Honor to the heroes of Grim Batol." The other guards said something in elven: "Nol Alakh." Jace had heard this word several times during the Sun King's address. Passing through the gates into Silvermoon, Grede quietly asked Vereesa, "What did that mean? That elven word the guards said to us?" "Savior," Vereesa explained. "But this isn't a common Thalassian word, Grede. It's reserved specifically for figures of great deeds. If you explained it using the Common word 'savior,' it might be a touch grandiose — but it seems there's no better equivalent." "I'm getting a warmer welcome in Quel'Thalas than I ever got at Aerie Peak," Grede said, none too pleased. "Over there they only said we were the men who flew through dragonfire — made it sound as though Deathwing was no different from those whelms." "What, you feel unpleasant being respected by a race you dislike?" Vereesa asked. "Of course not," Grede said. "Actually, this is fine. If they'd truly witnessed Deathwing's power, they definitely wouldn't let me leave Aerie Peak!" "It was the same for Jace in Lordaeron," Vereesa said. "Does it make you uncomfortable, Jace?" "It is a bit of a pity," Jace said, gazing up at the high walls and buildings on either side. "But as long as you two know, I don't care anymore how anyone else sees it." "That's true enough," Vereesa said with a smile. Grede punched Jace's arm. "He's always like this — picks up a few bizarre orcish phrases and shows off to me every day, as if he's terrified I won't notice. And then he goes and slays the most fearsome evil dragon in the world as though nothing happened…" Jace said, "That's only natural. The orcish I learned myself. The evil dragon had you two meddling — how's anyone supposed to show off with that?" "Hmph, I knew it," Grede said, leaning toward Vereesa. "This bastard is going to ditch us again and take a job on the sly sooner or later…" Vereesa glanced over and nodded in full agreement. The three of them chatted as they walked along the broad avenue running through the center of Silvermoon. This avenue was practically a plaza in its own right. Golden statues towered on either side — the figure of a ranger drawing a bow, the figure of a mage raising a staff to the sky. Jace didn't recognize a single one of them, but Vereesa's gaze kept being drawn to the tall statue of a female ranger. That ranger was simply dressed, yet she held a magnificent phoenix-like longbow and gazed up at the heavens. Was that Alleria? "Who's that?" Jace could not help asking. "My mother," Vereesa said. "Liressa Windrunner," Grede said. "I met her once. A great Ranger-General." "You met her?" Vereesa asked in surprise. "Of course," Grede said. "She came to the Hinterlands, and at the time I was fighting trolls nonstop… little elf." The two of them suddenly had a brand-new topic, and quite naturally fell to chatting about the war against the trolls in the Hinterlands. Jace even found himself feeling a little envious of Grede just then.
