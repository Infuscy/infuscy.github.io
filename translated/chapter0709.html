Chapter 709: A Clear Lead

Although the Sun King had announced the events at Grim Batol to all the elves present at the Farstrider Enclave, it was plain that the residents living in Silvermoon had not received the news in the first instance. Walking the streets, Jace could feel that these high elves understood full well that the human and dwarf granted entry into Silvermoon were no ordinary figures, and so they did their best not to cast any strange glances this way and maintain a normal demeanor — but from time to time they still looked over curiously. Gazing up at the high towers lining the avenue and their gorgeous ornamentation, Jace suddenly realized that these high elves did not yet call themselves "Sin'dorei" — children of blood — but the city still bore a great many red and gold tones, not much different from the Silvermoon of later days. Of course, there were fewer statues of Kael'thas Sunstrider, and no golden phoenix banners on a blood-red field. But before coming to Silvermoon, Jace had assumed a high elf city would be decorated largely in blues and silver… …After all, in the game, the Silver Covenant — the high elf organization that had left Quel'Thalas for Dalaran — was exactly like that. Still, the city was not lacking in blue and silver banners and decorations either. Plenty of people on the street wore blue or white robes — including Prince Kael'thas himself, who, after the Scourge invasion, had led the survivors in taking the name "Sin'dorei" and become the leader of the blood elves, seemed to appear in silver-white robes as well. Jace surmised that perhaps there had not been such a stark distinction between the high elves before the Scourge invasion and the blood elves who came after. After all, they worshipped the sun, and it was only natural that gold and red — representing sunlight and the Sunwell — were the dominant colors. It was likely that the high elves who later left Quel'Thalas had deliberately dressed in blue and white to set themselves apart from their blood elf kin, leaning closer to the Alliance's style. Vereesa led the two of them in through a gate on the west side onto a narrower avenue. Looking south, there was a broad fountain plaza. She looked south and said, "That's Eaglewing Plaza. The Reliquary's headquarters is just ahead. Do you see that white pickaxe emblem, Jace? Remember it in case you ever come here on your own. I'll go in first — you two wait here a moment." "All right." Jace nodded, looking at the white banner hanging beside a towering building. In his memory, the Reliquary's headquarters had been on the east side of the city… but that didn't matter in the least either. What he cared about more was Eaglewing Plaza. In the game, this was the first settlement that new blood elf characters reached after leaving the Sunstrider Isle starting area upon reaching level five. Jace had always assumed it was just a small town — he hadn't expected it to have been part of Silvermoon all along. But in the game, that whole stretch of walls had been destroyed. The ruined districts had been occupied by all manner of magical creatures, rogue constructs, and mana wretches whose minds had been devoured by fel energy — a place more dangerous than the wilds of Elwynn Forest, offering none of the sense of safety one expected inside a capital. Naturally it gave the impression of a roadside hamlet. "Hey, Jace," Grede asked. "That Nathanos Marris — the Lordaeron man — what's his relationship with her sister?" "Master and student," Jace said. "Lordaeron once wanted to establish a ranger corps of its own, so Nathanos was sent to Quel'Thalas for training. His teacher was Sylvanas." Grede cleared his throat, watched an arcane golem stagger past, and then said, "A lot of elves at the banquet said there was something more going on between them. Look — Vereesa's with you now, and Captain Alleria Windrunner married Turalyon. Then the second of the three sisters… Sylvanas promoted a human to Ranger-Lord despite the pressure from the royal house and all those nobles. There must be a reason, right." "You really do love sticking your nose into other people's business," Jace muttered. Grede laughed. "What else do you talk about at a drinking table? Either I go on about how I took out eight orcs at Grim Batol with nothing but a hammer…" "Wasn't it six? Never mind — let's not get into that," Jace said. "Let's talk about something real. When we leave Quel'Thalas, could you take me to Andorhal?" "Of course. But does Andorhal have a gryphon roost?" Grede asked. Jace said, "It should — that town's no small place. If not, you can fly to the nearest roost first and settle Goret there, then I'll meet you. Say, wait for me in Stratholme." "What are you going to do?" Grede asked. "Deal with a demon," Jace said. "A friend of mine is being threatened by a warlock in Andorhal. She wants me to help track down and kill his demonic servant." "Then I'm coming too," Grede said. "There's no money in it," Jace said. "Uh… what kind of demon? Is it a tough one?" Grede asked. "Just a succubus," Jace said. Grede frowned. "If there's no gryphon roost, you can handle it yourself… get that little imp of yours to help." The sky gradually darkened. Of course, in Quel'Thalas there were no pitch-black nights like in Elwynn with only moonlight — even after night fell, the stars were visible in the sky, and one could still feel light shining down from the north, much like the distant glow of a city seen in a past life. The two of them were leaning against a street-side arcane lamppost, already dozing off, when Vereesa finally emerged from the Reliquary's headquarters. "Hey," she said, approaching. "Jace, I have results." Jace immediately rubbed his eyes. "Well?" Vereesa said, "Dar'Khan did consult the scroll we brought back from Eldre'Thalas. I think he may already have made his own copy." "Don't let him know we looked into this…" Jace said. "It seems the traces Amy helped us find in the Dalaran library really may have been left by Dar'Khan. Otherwise, what other Quel'dorei would have any reason to look up such an obscure demon language?" "What were you two up to in Dalaran? I feel like I missed an awful lot again," Grede asked. "I'll explain later," Jace said. Vereesa asked, "Do you think Dar'Khan will try to summon an Ered'ruin demon?" "If he can't find the knowledge he wants in Dalaran, he'll probably try to summon one," Jace said. "But summoning an Ered'ruin is no simple ritual. The materials, incantations, and so on are all quite troublesome. Even I don't have the complete knowledge for it yet. I don't think he can move faster than me, but I need to hurry too." Vereesa said, "I can at least keep an eye on any demon-summoning activity in Quel'Thalas. We also have people we can contact on the Dalaran side. If I detect any move of his, I'll get word to you." "Then I'm counting on you," Jace said.
