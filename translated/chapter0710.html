Chapter 710: Parting at Quel'Lin

After a night's rest in Silvermoon, the three of them returned to Windrunner Village the next day, and Jace and Grede set about packing up to leave Quel'Thalas.

A day later, Vereesa joined the two at the Quel'Lin cottage.

She was a Ranger-Lord now, with responsibilities in Quel'Thalas that were naturally heavier than before — which meant she couldn't just up and leave whenever she felt like it anymore.

So here they were at another parting.

Over on his end, Grede was locked in a contest of wills with Gorjeth; Jace and Vereesa waited off to the side.

"Even though I know you're already King Varian's court mage, I still wish you'd stay in Quel'Thalas," the ranger said.

"Winter Veil's almost over. I need to get back to Stormwind soon — the king still needs me to help him deal with the gnolls," Jace said. "But I can come and go from Quel'Thalas freely now. If I get the chance, I'll come see you, Ven."

Vereesa smiled, watching the grey griffin's enormous wings beating in the distance. "Maybe I'll come see you. After all, I don't have to pay a dragonhawk's feed bill like Grede does."

"You're welcome anytime," Jace said.

The elf hugged him tightly. "I'll miss you, Jace. Remember to write."

"You write too," Jace said, stroking her silver hair.

"I know," the ranger nodded.

He'd thought that after spending so much time together this time around, the parting would come a little easier. But Jace found it far more painful than their last goodbye at Thelsama.

He could tell from Vereesa's expression that she felt it too.

After all, the last time they'd parted, they'd known they'd be seeing each other again soon in Dalaran. This time, there was no telling when they'd be together again.

Vereesa pressed against Jace's chest, her voice so low even Jace could barely make it out: "When you get back… are you planning to try summoning that demon soon?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Remember to ask for me," Vereesa looked up. "Don't forget."

"I know," Jace said.

"Do you think…" the ranger said softly, "is Alleria still alive? Do those Dragonmaw orcs have any news of Draenor? Anything at all?"

Jace was quiet for a moment. "I've heard the Dragonmaw clan believes their chieftain Zul'shid is still alive — probably some connection through the orcs' old soul magic or something like that… and Zul'shid has already led a contingent of Dragonmaw orcs back through the Dark Portal. If they have a way of knowing their chieftain still lives, then Draenor might not be completely destroyed. If those orcs can survive, there's no reason the Expedition couldn't hold on."

His reasoning was completely made up on the spot. The Dragonmaw orcs on Azeroth had been out of contact with Zul'shid for a long time.

But Jace knew Zul'shid was still alive. He was still maintaining the Dragonmaw's organization in the shattered Draenor, and in the not-too-distant future he'd even cooperate with the black dragons, harnessing the void energy that had poured in from the world's destruction to influence and mutate black dragon eggs — breeding new mounts.

The dragons hatched from those black dragon eggs were shadowy and ethereal as wraiths. The survivors of Draenor called them the Netherwing dragonflight; the Expedition preferred to call them void dragons.

"Sylvanas will like this news," Vereesa said. "Maybe you should tell her yourself."

"She doesn't call me 'little moon' anymore — because that was Alleria's name for me. I thought she could face it calmly once Alleria went missing, but I know she hasn't. And neither have I."

Jace was quiet for a moment. "Wait for my news, Ven."

"Jace! We can leave whenever!" Grede shouted from across the way.

Vereesa let go. "Take care of yourself, Jace."

"You too." Jace nodded and turned to leave.

Climbing onto Gorjeth's back, Jace said, "That was quite the rush."

"Any longer and you two'd be holding each other till nightfall. We wouldn't make it to Andorhal before dark," Grede said.

The griffin beat its wings and lifted off the ground. Jace looked back and saw that gold-green silhouette beside the cottage, looking up and waving.

He waved back with everything he had, wondering if she could see him. Hoping she could.

Gorjeth flew fast. In the blink of an eye, Quel'Lin was swallowed up layer after layer of pine forest. Without realizing it, Jace had grown used to griffin travel — none of that panic he'd felt the first time in the Hinterlands.

By evening, the griffin reached a granary farm near Andorhal. As they swept past, sentries below roared at them to halt.

Grede wheeled Gorjeth around and descended toward the source of the shouting. A handful of Lordaeron soldiers caught up to them, arms raised to shield against the gale from the griffin's wings, bellowing: "Good afternoon, sir! Griffin rider! Are you headed for Andorhal?"

"Yes!" Jace shouted back. "What's the problem?"

By now Gorjeth had steadied to a stop. The guards shot a startled glance at the absurdly massive beast, and the one in front said: "No problem, sir — it's just that Andorhal doesn't got a roost for a griffin that size. If you're only passing through, you'd best keep going."

Grede glanced back at Jace. Jace said, "You need to find one."

The dwarf nodded and called out: "Any griffin roosts nearby, lads?"

A guard pointed north. "Hearthglen's got one, but it's a fair ways off. The closest is the forest down by Lake Lordamere, south of here — there's a Stromgarde encampment there, and a Wildhammer roost too. Much closer than Hearthglen. Easy enough to spot if you're riding a griffin."

"Head for the southern camp, Grede," Jace said. "I'll find you once I'm done. I'll give it a couple days here — if I can't find it by then, I'll move on."

Grede leaned close and whispered: "You sure you can find a succubus in that town in two or three days?"

"Never doubt my demon-tracking skills, Grede," Jace muttered.

Grede nodded. "If I don't see you in two or three days, I'll come find you in Andorhal."

"Then it's settled." Jace hopped off the griffin. "Get going — it'll be dark soon."

Grede said something in Dwarvish that meant "no problem," then shouted Gorjeth's name and sent him launching skyward. The griffin snapped its wings once, whipping up a storm of dust and dead weeds that nearly flattened everyone on the ground.

The men coughed their way out of the settling cloud of dust. The griffin was already gone. Unburdened Wildhammer riders always flew like that.

"Where'd you even get that thing?" a guard muttered, craning his neck at the sky. "Biggest damn griffin I ever did see."
