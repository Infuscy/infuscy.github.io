## Chapter 711: Andorhal

Andorhal sat on a peninsula ringed by a great river. Even back in the days of the Arathi Empire, the grain trade flowing through from Tirisfal and the Eastern Plaguelands had made it a remarkably prosperous little town.

By now it had grown into a proper city. Goldshire barely qualified as a village next to it, and even Moonbrook paled in comparison.

The Church of the Holy Light here was a famous landmark across the Eastern Plaguelands, and together with Andorhal's town hall it formed the two tallest buildings in the city, facing each other from afar.

You could see the handsome clock tower in the center of town and the tall steeple of the church in the distance the moment you crossed the bridge. But the first thing Jace did upon arriving was stash his rings and change his clothes.

The little noble getup from Stormwind — all that blue — made him stand out far too much. He found a small inn on the edge of town and booked a room, changed into the old clothes he'd brought, put away the black dragon cloak, and pulled out the mountain lion hide cloak from his pack to wear instead.

When he stepped back outside, the sky had gone fully dark and a cold rain had started falling.

The cold of it didn't even feel like rain — more like crushed ice, or fine hail. Quel'Thalas was a long way off now; the power of the Sunwell was completely undetectable.

Pulling up his hood, he walked along several streets over soft, cold, muddy ground. Most of the people on the road were hurrying home. All around him were grey stone walls, dripping stone and wood houses, ancient and crumbling homes, and wide streets paved with stone bricks God knows how many years ago, now cracked and broken in every direction.

Jace walked street after street, stopped by the river, and tried using Detect Demon to search for any sign of the infernal kind. Nothing.

Did the warlock's minion simply not feel like going out in the rain?

As he walked he spotted a general store and figured he ought to buy a candle. Inns around here certainly weren't like the ones in Dalaran that stocked you up with all sorts of handy items.

Jace hadn't come to Andorhal to do anything above board, and he didn't want to rely on Light or any other arcane means to see in the dark. Given his talent with the arcane, casting even a small spell tended to leave traces that were all too easy to track. After his experience tracking down Dar'Khan together with Amy in Dalaran, he'd learned that when he was traveling without revealing his identity, he needed to be careful.

"I'd like to buy a candle, boss," Jace said as he stepped through the door.

"From the royal city? Here — two for free." The shopkeeper looked Jace over. "Hey, is it true the king's fallen ill?" So he'd caught the Tirisfal accent from the west.

Jace realized he'd come to Andorhal on Ursula's behalf to hunt a demon. Since Ursula wanted the local warlocks to know it was someone from Stormwind who'd done the deed, he ought to pass himself off as a Stormwind man.

Which meant he'd need to start minding his accent.

"Yes," Jace said. "With the help of all those paladins and bishops, he should be right as rain in a while."

"So it's nothing too serious?" the shopkeeper asked, setting the candles out.

"Nothing serious," Jace said. "I heard the king's still holding banquets in the palace."

"Light be praised, finally some good news," the shopkeeper said. "Have you heard about Khaz Modan? That place called Ger—"

"Grim Batol," Jace supplied.

"That's the one, that fortress the orcs took over. I heard the place got wrecked — should've been a good thing, but the orcs inside think King Terenas II sent mages to do it, so they summoned a demon to curse the palace. Sounds like it's just a rumor after all. I hope our kingdom makes it through this mess in one piece. Orcs, dragons, and now demons too — don't know when it'll ever end."

"I haven't heard anything about a demon in the palace," Jace gave him a look. "But I have heard there's a demon roaming around Andorhal."

"A demon? No way." The shopkeeper shook his head sharply. "Most likely another rumor."

Jace stared at the shopkeeper for a moment, and the man slowly began to look uneasy.

"Well, all I heard was that there's a Dark Iron dwarf living out west. You know how Dark Iron dwarves always like messing around with unholy stuff. Give it enough time and the story turns into 'there's a demon in Andorhal.' Andorhal's a peaceful place — there's never been any evil creatures here. Even the orcs never dared cause trouble in these woods. So I do hope all these outsiders go back where they belong… no offense, sir, I mean the dwarves and such, those strange folk, the elves and whatnot."

"I feel the same way, including those red-clothed Arathi people in the forests to the south," Jace said, picking up his candles. "But… for the Alliance. Anyway, thanks for the gift, boss."

The shopkeeper smiled. "For the Alliance."

The western half of Andorhal was more prosperous than the east. Jace had assumed the Dark Iron warlock would be hiding in some poor, out-of-the-way corner, so he'd focused most of his searching on the east side — never expecting the man would actually be operating on the west.

It wasn't the middle of the night yet, so he figured he'd try searching the west side too. After all, Grede was waiting for him to the south. He'd said he could wait two or three days, but Jace had no intention of making the dwarf sit there twiddling his thumbs while he accomplished nothing.

Still, even with this new lead, Jace hadn't turned up anything.

Andorhal wasn't Goldshire. You couldn't just walk down one street, sweep it with Detect Demon, and call it a day. His demon detection couldn't blanket a whole district at once, so it was perfectly normal to miss things.

Grede was going to laugh his ass off at him for this.

As the hours crept by, Jace started feeling drowsy. That's when he noticed a sizable inn and tavern perched higher up the street — much larger than that little inn he'd booked himself on the east side.

The sight tugged at a memory from the game. Andorhal's inn… in the ruins, that was where the bronze dragon Chromie lived.

Of course, Chromie couldn't possibly be living in there right now. And while the odds of a demon holing up in a tavern weren't high, Jace went in anyway.

He stepped into the main hall, pulled back his hood, and swept his gaze across the room. It was packed. Quite a few rain-soaked folks seeking shelter were standing against the walls and by the windows, watching his way.

The tavern was old, but the interior was spacious, filled with the thick smell of wet earth after rain and sour ale. The uneven floor tiles were covered in wet mud and puddles. The tavernkeeper and the customers hollered at each other in hard-to-follow Eastern Plaguelands and Stromgarde accents, punctuated now and then by the unmistakable dopey laughter of the thoroughly drunk.

Jace had never set foot in Andorhal before, but somehow it felt like he'd come home.
