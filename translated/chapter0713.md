## Chapter 713: Thunder Peak

Four days of flying southeast from the shores of Lake Lordamere brought Gorjeth to the skies above a range of towering mountains.

This was the eastern half of the Barthilas range, near the ocean. Three or four hundred miles to the west lay Grim Batol, but from here it was hard to see that scorched darkness.

Jace, though, could feel that darkness. He stared west for a long time without speaking, until Grede couldn't help asking: "What's wrong? Still thinking about Deathwing?"

Jace said, "No. I'm just wondering whether to fly over and check on the Dragonqueen — see if she's found that staff. But probably not. She said she'd tell me if she had."

"Besides, are you really sure you want to set foot in that place again?" Grede said with some concern. "Think about how you were before…"

Jace recalled how, every time he'd thought about Deathwing in the past, his nerves had tensed up, or a chill of fear had crept through him bit by bit.

But now, even sensing that he was approaching Grim Batol, he didn't experience nearly as much mental distress. If anything lingered, it was just a faint residue of dread.

Now when he pictured Deathwing's appearance, by that river, what came to mind more than anything was Vereesa holding his hand, glancing now and then at the other elves who might appear in the garden in the distance, while unable to stop herself from kissing his neck… and he couldn't help but smile.

"I think I've gotten a lot better these past weeks," Jace said.

"I can tell." Grede's gaze followed the mountaintops passing beneath the griffin's feet as he turned his head back. "Truth is, I've always been worried about you — not looking down on you or anything. Going through that kind of horror and not feeling pain would be the abnormal part. I'm a veteran, Jace. There are way too many dwarven warriors back on Aerie Peak who faced red dragons or death knights and never got over it, let alone the fact that you ran into the black dragon's boss… I'm glad to see you slowly pulling through."

"What about you?" Jace asked.

"Me?" Grede glanced back at him. "What do I have to get over? Brawling with orcs, then smashing a few monsters made of fire and stone, watching a black dragon fall out of the sky and crash to its death. Honestly… Jace, that's nothing. That's one of the reasons I didn't want to take on the responsibility of Lord Thunderfist. This has little to do with me, and I know that."

"That's a boring thing to say," Jace said, gripping the saddle as Gorjeth suddenly gained altitude and his body swayed. "We divide the work and play to our strengths — you and Ven do what you're good at, I do mine. That's what makes a team a team. Think about it — without you two, how would I have rescued the Dragonqueen all by myself? Impossible."

"Hah, when you put it that way," the dwarf laughed.

They flew through layers of thick cloud, and a colossal peak suddenly towered into view. Jace's eyes widened in complete surprise. Gorjeth shifted direction and began climbing even higher.

"Wrap your cloak tight," Grede warned. "It's going to get a bit cold."

"Thunder Peak?" Jace asked.

"That's right," Grede replied. "Gorjeth really is something, Jace. Carrying the two of us this fast and still having this much strength even next to the holy mountain — last time I brought my father's body here, riding Gweetha, she was absolutely worn out."

At the foot of this singular pillar-like peak rising from the Barthilas range sat a small town. Jace knew it must be Kirthaven, where the Wildhammer wise folk studied the ways of shamanism.

Grede had been here before as well. He'd mentioned last time that Gweetha was the griffin he'd landed here on.

This time, though, Grede deliberately ignored the town below, urging Gorjeth straight up toward the summit of Thunder Peak.

The very top of this peculiar mountain wasn't entirely flat, but there were several reasonably level patches of varying heights. The griffin landed on the lowest flat area at the peak. Jace hopped down and took in the view — from here, all you could see below was clouds. The ground was invisible.

On the nearby flat ground were many cairns. Jace could tell these weren't random piles of stone — someone had placed each rock deliberately. Every cairn held something special on top: feathers, rope, war hammers, ornaments strung from bones, and the like. Some had many items, some only one or two.

Grede patted Gorjeth, signaling him to go roam and play nearby. But Gorjeth didn't excitedly tear off like he usually did. He hesitated for a moment, stood up, and — just like Jace — gazed at the cairns before him, as though lost in thought.

"Is this a Wildhammer dwarf cemetery?" Jace asked.

"It's a cemetery for Wildhammer dwarves and griffins," Grede corrected.

The dwarf walked among the cairns, glancing toward the old stone steps leading higher up in the distance, and said: "Let's go up, Jace."

Following Grede upward, they reached another flat area with even more cairns. They seemed to be placed without much pattern, and yet not entirely without one.

Grede was right — the Wildhammer and the tauren had similarities in their beliefs. This really did remind Jace of the tauren funeral he'd seen atop the mountain at Sun Rock Retreat.

Grede rounded a few cairns and walked straight to one near the cliff's edge. This cairn was much smaller than the others, and it had no ornaments on it — just a worn leather cap held down by a rock.

This must have been his father's grave.

Grede called out "Father" in Dwarvish, then pulled the cloak from his pack.

It was a very heavy, dark-brown hide cloak, embroidered with lightning-bolt sigils that reminded Jace of the cloaks worn by the Bronzebeard noble warriors he'd seen at Nethergarde Keep.

If Grede put that on, Jace thought, he'd immediately look like a true lord.

Grede brought the cloak to the cairn and draped it over the pile of stones, then pressed it down with several large rocks, making sure the fierce winds sweeping over the peak wouldn't blow it away.

The dwarf lay down on the cairn and the cloak, murmuring something in Dwarvish for a long, long time — long enough that it became impossible to distinguish his voice from the wind.

Jace didn't know whether to regret not having learned Dwarvish or to be grateful he couldn't understand. If he spoke Dwarvish, there might be a lot of things Grede wouldn't be able to bring himself to say.

He listened in silence until Grede slowly got to his feet and came to Jace's side. "The bravest dwarven warriors, after death, go to the hall in the sky to meet their ancestors, their comrades, and their loved ones. The souls of griffins carry them there."

"My father wasn't a warrior. That hall in the sky probably won't have him. And he has no griffin to accompany him — maybe he simply couldn't fly up there in the first place. His soul might be lingering here all alone, and if I don't come visit him, he might get lonely."

"Thank you, Jace Sesso. Thank you for coming here with me. You're a truly brave warrior, and my father would be happy to have someone like you come visit him."
