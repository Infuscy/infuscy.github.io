## Chapter 714: Marin's Suspicions

By the time they returned to Stormwind it was already January. After landing, he and Grede had a drink at a small tavern near the city walls. He dropped his things at his rented room and headed straight for the Mage Quarter to find his teacher, Marin.

There were still two weeks before the Wizard Sanctum's classes resumed. Marin should have plenty of free time, and no apprentices or student mages coming to bother him — which meant Jace could take the opportunity to…

Thinking of that, Jace suddenly realized he no longer had to hide. He touched the ring on his finger — the name "Jace Sesso" engraved on it was perfectly clear.

He made his way to the Mage Quarter with the report on death knights he'd finished at his parents' home. It recorded everything from the first death knight that had hunted him in Dalaran, all the way through his direct confrontation with Teron Gorefiend; from the death knight he'd found in the forest where Hogger was hiding, to the werewolf legends of Duskwood, and finally to the decisive battle against Teron Gorefiend's spirit at Thaurissan's ruins. He'd written out the entire process and his own reflections on death knights as thoroughly as he could.

After all, his teacher had been asking for it for ages, and he'd dragged his feet long enough. It was time to show some sincerity.

When he arrived at the Mage Quarter, it seemed to have just rained — everything was still wet. Jace approached the door of Marin's study, glanced back at the Wizard Sanctum tower looming dimly under the overcast sky, and knocked.

"It's unlocked. Come right in." A familiar voice from inside. Jace pushed the door open, and Marin looked up.

"Mr. Sesso. You're back from Lordaeron."

"Yes, Master," Jace nodded, placing the document containing his report on the desk. Marin asked, "Is this the record on death knights? You've finished it?"

"Yes," Jace said. "I promised to hand it to you when I got back from Lordaeron."

"Excellent."

Marin carefully pulled the stack of papers from the bag and flipped through them briefly — but very quickly seemed to notice something off in the documents. His expression changed, and he became absorbed in the reading.

Jace sat to the side, watching Marin pull another file from a drawer and quickly flip back and forth between pages. "Is something wrong, Master?"

Marin said, "Actually… your account of what happened during the black dragon assault on Dalaran differs somewhat from the final version Dalaran gave us, Mr. Sesso. Of course, that was two years ago now — it's perfectly normal for memories to have some discrepancies."

"Where's the difference?" Jace asked.

Marin looked at Jace, then said: "You mention that when you fought Teron Gorefiend and his subordinates inside Dalaran, Dalaran's protective barrier was still in place."

"Yes," Jace said. "At the time I could clearly see the arcane glow when the black dragons' magic struck Dalaran's barrier, high up in the sky."

"Do you know what sustained Dalaran's barrier, Mr. Sesso?" Marin asked.

"The Eye of the Arcane," Jace said. "Someone from the Reliquary told me."

Marin nodded. "And that is precisely what the death knights and the black dragons collaborated to steal. Dalaran told us the Wizard Sanctum that Teron Gorefiend stole the Eye of the Arcane, and that once this treasure was removed from its rightful place, Dalaran's barrier would inevitably be severely compromised. How then could it have withstood the black dragons' assault?"

Jace said, "After Teron Gorefiend abandoned his body and fled, the barrier was gone."

"Correct," Marin said. "But the barrier was still there when you encountered Teron Gorefiend. That means someone else stole the Eye of the Arcane."

"I thought it was Deathwing…" Jace said.

"Deathwing?!" Marin's tone suddenly lost all composure. "Are you saying Deathwing went to Dalaran in person during that battle? How is that possible?"

Indeed, at the time in Dalaran, everyone on the Alliance side — except for Kel'Thuzad, the near-open traitor, and Jace, who'd been spoiled in advance — even the other members of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six hadn't known Deathwing had appeared.

Jace thought to himself for a moment, then asked: "So, Master, you think there's a traitor inside Dalaran."

"I don't think anything," Marin said almost immediately.

Jace swallowed. The two of them sat in silence for a moment. Marin spoke quietly: "A traitor with access to the Eye of the Arcane… I think that's practically an accusation against Antonidas himself. I'll discuss this matter with Master Andormas, see what he thinks, and then inform you. How does that sound, Mr. Sesso? On the condition that you can vouch for the truthfulness of what you've written."

Back in Dalaran, Jace had suspected there might be another insider within Dalaran based on this very point. But at the time, his only clue pointing to Kel'Thuzad was the bias he'd carried over from having read the spoilers in his past life — namely, Kel'Thuzad's interest in necromantic magic, and Deathwing being a master of necromancy… It made a kind of sense, but if you wanted to call it a stretch, it was definitely a stretch.

But after everything that had followed, including the Battle of Grim Batol, Jace could practically confirm it.

The only people who'd known about sending Jace on the mission to Grim Batol were the members of the Council of Six. Krasus could be ruled out. Kael'thas had no reason to help Deathwing. Antonidas — even less so. To go a step further, there was absolutely no way he would have handed over the Eye of the Arcane.

As for the other two archmages, Drenden and Modera — Jace hadn't known much about Drenden before transmigrating, since he barely appeared in the game and had little presence. Within the Council of Six, his and Modera's standing was indeed lower than the other four's, so getting access to the Eye of the Arcane might not have been so convenient for them.

However, Krasus had mentioned that other members of the Kirin Tor council had helped him uncover the connection between Deathwing and Daval Prestor. And Deathwing had contemptuously spoken of Korialstrasz bringing "a few juggling artists in purple robes" trying to wreck his mansion — most likely referring to those two.

So Kel'Thuzad's suspicion was by far the greatest. Coupled with what Amy had described — that he'd barely shown himself in public since Grim Batol…

Even if there was a one-in-ten-thousand chance it wasn't Kel'Thuzad, rooting out a traitor within the Kirin Tor was a good thing either way.

He wondered if Vaelastrasz had found Krasus in Blackrock Mountain. After all, he was now the key to identifying Kel'Thuzad.

It had been over half a month since he'd said he was heading to Blackrock Mountain. If the black dragons and Blackrock orcs really were holding Krasus prisoner, they'd be keeping it strictly secret and he wouldn't be easy to find. Better to give him a little more time.

If he helped King Varian take the Ilgal Tower and Vaelastrasz still didn't come find him, he'd really have to go see the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza in person.

"I can vouch for it, Master Marin," he said.

Marin nodded. "This might just stir up a little storm in the Kirin Tor." Jace could even detect a hint of satisfaction in his tone — whether it was his imagination or not.
