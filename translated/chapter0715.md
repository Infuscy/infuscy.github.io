## Chapter 715: Moving Forward

"Has the king come to the Wizard Sanctum, Master?" Jace asked. "I mean about the Ilgal Tower. I promised to help the soldiers at Stonewatch retake that tower. The king told me the operation would be this spring, but he didn't give me a specific date."

"No." Marin tidied up his desk. "It just snowed at Stonewatch recently. I suspect we'll have to wait another month or so. For specifics, you'd best ask the king directly. After all, you're the court mage, not me. Actually, strictly speaking, we don't belong to the same department anymore."

"So that's you kicking me out of the Wizard Sanctum, Master?" Jace asked with a smile.

Marin tilted his head slightly, giving him the look he reserved for misbehaving students, but ultimately smiled. "As long as you acknowledge it, you'll always be a member of the Wizard Sanctum, Mr. Sesso."

Jace said, "Since I'm a member of the Wizard Sanctum, Master, and still your student, when are you planning to teach me to control fire elements?"

"Anytime." Marin reached behind him to a shelf and, without looking, pulled down a well-worn old book that had been thumbed through by God knows how many people. "But I'd still recommend you read this first. *The First Burning*, by Dilthryan, Flame Master of the Order of Tirisfal. It's an introductory text on fire magic. You've already mastered Scorch and learned Fireball, but I think you'd do best starting from a basic understanding of fire elementals' properties. Also, the second chapter has a spell that teaches the most fundamental fire protection ward. That's also a prerequisite for deeper study of fire spells — an evocation mage first has to survive the learning process before there's any chance of improving. Though I understand you're quite good at keeping yourself alive."

Jace nodded and took the book. "I understand, Master. I'll be going, then."

"Hold on a moment, Mr. Sesso." Marin paused, then said: "The death knight report is very detailed — I'll read it carefully later on, and then hand it over to the wizard tower. But next, there's still the matter of Deathwing, isn't there… I know that must have been a truly harrowing experience, so I want to respect your wishes on this."

Jace knew he couldn't avoid this experience forever. Besides, everyone was supporting him.

"I can. I'll write up the Grim Batol events in full as soon as possible and hand them over to the Wizard Sanctum and the king."

"Excellent," Marin said. "Remember to let me have a look first."

"Of course, Master," Jace said.

Leaving Marin's place, the next stop was the Slaughtered Lamb.

The area around the Wizard Sanctum was as usual, and Marin hadn't said anything out of the ordinary. The Slaughtered Lamb probably hadn't had some uncontrollable felguard burst out and go on a rampage through the Park or anything.

Besides, he could sense that Morfiel was still inside, so the situation in the cellar should still be under control.

He arrived at the Slaughtered Lamb. Tharle was chatting with a customer. Jace glanced at him, the man nodded, and Jace headed straight for the cellar.

He entered the main hall. Morfiel was sitting in a chair, staring right at Jace. "What, you're not going to stand up and welcome your master back?" Jace asked.

"The stench of love. I smelled it the moment you entered Stormwind," Morfiel said bitterly.

Jace sniffed his arm. "Don't talk nonsense. It's obviously the soul link letting you sense I'm here. Did Gajin try to summon a felguard?"

"He tried." Morfiel tossed a black dagger in her hand. "But he didn't succeed. Could be a problem with the incantation, or the casting materials. Sazeno isn't here, so I can't figure it out."

Sazeno darted out of the pack and climbed onto Jace's back. "See, master? I told you, didn't I?"

The succubus's face went cold. She gripped her dagger and aimed at the imp. "That you've survived this long in the Twisting Nether surprises me more than Azeroth surviving the Burning Legion's assault."

"That just means you've seen too little!" the imp said with a laugh.

"Are the others here? Gajin, Ursula," Jace asked.

"Ursula's here," Morfiel said. "Gajin went to Ironforge. He said he wanted to double-check the felguard summoning incantation, but I think he's not just going to confirm the incantation, master."

"Explain clearly," Jace said.

The red dragon flames in the succubus's eyes dimmed by half. She answered in a low voice: "His command over Shadow is improving quickly. I think you noticed that before, too. But his affinity for Shadow is very different from yours, master."

Jace knew what Morfiel was hinting at, and he'd already guessed most of it. Had Gajin gotten to know some people he shouldn't have?

Ironforge… Dark Iron dwarves… These elements easily connected to those sealed presences deep beneath Azeroth.

The Old Gods.

It looked like when Gajin came back, Jace would need to have a proper talk with him. He remembered telling Gajin that no matter what, he mustn't have anything to do with matters connected to those presences. He didn't know whether the man had been deceived or whether he'd willingly forged a connection. In truth, there often wasn't much difference between the two.

"I'll talk to him when he gets back," Jace said. "What about you? How are you getting along with your new power?"

"So well I'm nearly forgetting what I am," Morfiel said. "But it's not a bad thing."

She pinched a flame between her fingertips and continued: "The fiery power you gave me, master — it's truly addictive. I wonder when I'll get to…"

"We'll see," Jace said, looking at the succubus smiling at him. "I'm going to find Ursula."

Out in the corridor, Ursula was dozing in a chair. Seeing her sleeping soundly, Jace figured he'd come back later. He might as well read the book Marin had just given him in the main hall while he waited for her to wake up. But just as he was about to turn and leave, apparently having heard the sound of his boots, Ursula's eyes snapped open with a gasp, giving Jace a fright too.

"What's wrong?" Jace asked. "Nightmare?"

"Jace!" Ursula let out a breath. "Why do you have to sneak around without making a sound? You nearly scared me to death. I thought another satyr demon was coming in to slit my throat."

"You still can't relax after all this time?" Jace asked.

"You kill a demon like killing a chicken, so of course you don't understand how terrified people like us are," Ursula said with a complaint. "If Bosadem hadn't been watching over me, I wouldn't have been able to sleep at all!"

Jace recalled the scene of fighting that satyr demon on the stairs — that was anything but killing a chicken. He glanced back at Morfiel behind him, thinking that practicing Shadowstep should be on the agenda soon.

"The Andorhal matter," Jace said. "That succubus has been taken care of, Ursula. That was the only demon I found in Andorhal. And it was a male succubus, but it disguised itself as a woman to deceive the locals there."

"A male succubus." Ursula let out a breath. "Well, that explains it. They told me the reports mentioned both men and women, and I started thinking there were a whole bunch of succubi in Andorhal. Did you pour the purification potion on like I asked?"

"I did," Jace said.

"Good," Ursula said. "I hope he understands I'm not a 'slaughtered lamb' — I can bite back too. Thank you, Jace. How can I ever repay you…"

"Don't forget, this is me thanking you, Ursula." Jace waved a hand. "Besides, there's no need for thanks between us."

"Actually, I'm a bit short on money right now. Next time there's a job, remember me — we can talk about payment then."

Ursula smiled. "Knowing you is my luck, Jace. Then next time there's work, I'll come to you first. Erland and Sulenna are just too inefficient."

"Then I'll be off," Jace said.

Ursula said, "Oh right, Jace — hold on a moment. There's actually something I'm not sure I should ask… You said you went to the Wetlands before, right?"

"Yes."

"You and that Grim Batol business…"

Jace said, "Yes, it was for that. Sorry for keeping it from you all this time."

Ursula was stunned for a good while before saying: "So it really was you… Gajin said the black dragon master, Deathwing, died at Grim Batol."

"You don't get to wriggle out of this either," Jace said. "Guess how much your potion contributed."

"I don't dare guess," Ursula said in a low voice. "Damn it, I'm starting to feel like I can't breathe. What have I done…"

"You saved my life," Jace said. "So killing a succubus for you is nothing. See you next time, Ursula. I'm going to see the king."

Ursula still looked a little dazed. After quite a while she nodded. "All right… see you next time, Jace."
