## Chapter 72: A Suitable Wish

Yesterday, when the colonel had mentioned telling the king about all this, it hadn't felt real. Though he'd been thinking there was a chance the king might reward him somehow, he hadn't actually expected to meet the king in person.

Then again, Varian Wrynn really did want to be a good king.

In the game's original level-60 version, Varian had already been split into two personalities by Onyxia — the black dragon princess who infiltrated Stormwind with dark magic. One retained Varian's original fighting spirit and courage but was cast out into the wilderness; the other was kind but obeyed unconditionally the noblewoman Lady Katrana Prestor, who was really the black dragon in disguise, becoming a puppet through which the dragons controlled Stormwind's politics.

In the end, the black dragon didn't even want this puppet anymore.

So, all the way up until the early level-80 version, when Varian reclaimed his throne and his whole personality and personally slew the black dragon princess, he was practically a nonentity. People just assumed he was an incompetent puppet king until he recovered.

But after actually living in Stormwind for a while, Jace realized he'd been overlooking the Varian Wrynn from before Onyxia's arrival.

At this point he was still a good king — or at least he wanted to be. Not only was he constantly working to solve the war's lingering problems — refugees, bandits, food shortages — but when a border stronghold came under attack, he'd actually braved the danger to come here in person to steady the troops and boost morale. That alone proved he wanted to get things done. But once that she-dragon truly descended, Stormwind and its king were probably heading for the pathetic, irrelevant phase that game players would come to know so well.

That evening, while Jace was out in the yard practicing swordsmanship with the soldiers, a man who looked about thirty and had the bearing of a nobleman was led to him by a guard.

"Mr. Jace Sesso?"

"That's me. And you are?"

"Baron Grey Ellerian, secretary to King Varian. We don't have much time — I need to return to Lakeshire as soon as possible — so let's skip the tedious formalities and get straight to it. Do you know you're meeting the king tomorrow?"

"I do, my lord," Jace replied.

He'd heard that Varian's wife, Queen Tiffin Wrynn, came from the Ellerian family — a noble house that held a small fief in Westfall. He wondered how this man was related to the queen.

"Since the king will be riding here from Lakeshire at first light tomorrow, I need to brief you on some points of etiquette for meeting the king in advance… because the king will have some conversation with you. You understand what I mean, right?"

"I understand."

Hearing the baron say this, Jace actually felt a bit more at ease.

After all, he had absolutely no knowledge of the etiquette for meeting a king. He didn't even know the etiquette for meeting a nobleman. His attitude toward everyone was basically to use whatever manners came to mind. When dealing with ordinary people — shopkeepers, even mages — everyone just faked it and got by. But if it were a real noble, like the king, he might end up causing trouble.

The baron led Jace into the fortress, to a small room in a corner. He looked Jace up and down and said, "Were you wearing this outfit when you fought the gnolls?"

"Yes."

"Good. Wear this same outfit to meet the king. Old and tattered is fine."

After the baron said this, he continued to examine Jace up and down. Jace was thinking — he'd bought this new outfit not long ago, so how was it already tattered and old?

Fighting the gnolls hadn't wrecked the clothes, had it?

Well, when he'd fought that orc shaman a few days back, the man's lightning magic had definitely burned a hole in the back. But in Azeroth, did a hole in your clothes even count as a problem?

A hole in your FACE didn't even count as a problem!

"How's your hand healing?" the baron asked again.

"Quite a bit better. Thank you for your concern, my lord."

"I'm not concerned about your hand. I mean your bandages."

"Huh? Do I need to take them off?"

"No. Don't take them off. Leave them on. Meet the king just like that — excellent." The baron gripped Jace's wrist and said quietly, "Quite a serious burn. Very effective. Good." Looking into his eyes, Jace really wanted to punch him right about now.

"Right." The baron suddenly turned his head and said, "Don't shake the king's hand. Don't wave at the king. Before the king walks up to you, don't say anything, don't make any spontaneous movements — maintain solemn silence. When the king reaches you, bow — a slight bow, don't ram your head into the king's chest — and say 'Your Majesty.' The king will take the initiative and ask you what your name is, or perhaps, 'Are you the civilian hero who saved Stonewatch?' That's when you say your name, or say that yes, you're that person."

"More or less got it."

"Watch your attitude. 'More or less' isn't good enough — memorize it firmly." The baron said, "Given that you're the hero who saved Stonewatch, I'm going to lay my heart bare and tell you some things I shouldn't be telling an outsider, Mr. Jace Sesso. The king is a young man about your age — you could even say he's still a boy. He hasn't been back in Stormwind for long, and he's determined to build the great legacy of King Lethan into something as powerful as Lordaeron. And an important prerequisite for all that is that people believe he can do it. So he doesn't want anything to go wrong. You should understand how sensitive a boy this age can be. He just came back from Lordaeron — he's met King Terenas II, the one beloved and blessed by the seven kingdoms."

So this was what the young Varian Wrynn looked like in other people's eyes?

Jace nodded obediently.

"Now let's get to the real business." The baron's expression grew serious. He sat down on a wooden chair nearby and looked up at Jace from there.

Wait, we hadn't gotten to the real business yet? What else was there — did Varian Wrynn think Jace had heroically defeated a huge gnoll army and wanted to challenge him to a duel on the spot?

"Stop letting your imagination run wild and focus on what I'm saying, Mr. Jace Sesso." The baron said sternly, "King Varian will ask you, at the end, what reward you'd like, or what wish you'd like fulfilled, as a hero of the Kingdom of Stormwind… and he'll do his best to grant it."

So that was it!

Man, Jace's mind was already flooding with ideas.

"Is it possible — a house?" he blurted out.

"Light have mercy, look what you've brought me." The baron raised his eyebrows. "Duke Bolvar sent me here to talk to you first — truly the sharp instincts of a veteran politician. Otherwise there'd have been a real disaster."

"If you just blurt that out like that, can the king actually refuse you? He's a young man not yet twenty, passionate about everything but knowing nothing about anything. He'd agree on the spot, and then I'd be the one scrambling to find you a place to live in Stormwind, where every inch of land costs a fortune. I'd be the one arguing and haggling with the nobles until every single property-owning lord in the city was furious at me, and I still might not find you your ideal place. And if I did manage to carve out a small house from some minor noble, do you think you could live there peacefully? Could you even survive in Stormwind? Those people are damned vampires — they'd never give up a single scrap of profit, even with the kingdom in crisis!"

Jace had the feeling this guy wasn't actually complaining because of his own situation.

The baron sighed. "Make a more realistic request. Or just name something else — I'll discuss the possibilities with you. I think you should keep it within what ten gold can cover."

"Then ten—"

"Ten gold won't work," he said flatly. "Vulgar gold isn't the kind of wish a hero of the kingdom should make. More to the point, the royal family can't afford to just hand you ten gold either, sir. They probably can't even pay the Stonemasons' wages right now."

At this, the baron suddenly felt he'd said too much. He wiped the corner of his mouth with his thumb. "They should be able to pay them. Anyway, don't trouble the royal family."

Jace thought it over again and said, "Then I want to enter the Wizard Sanctum."

"What do you mean? Aren't you already some assistant to a Marin or whatever?"

"An assistant isn't an apprentice. I want to become a mage apprentice and learn magic," Jace explained.

"I don't understand." The baron frowned. "Isn't an assistant an apprentice? Are you telling me the young men the Wizard Sanctum hires to run errands for the mages don't even have the right to learn magic? What kind of sense does that make?!"

Jace explained, "Assistants are just hired hands. To become an apprentice, you either have to get recruited when you're barely ten years old, or you have to have undiscovered talent. I'm already way too old, and I don't have any particular gift. But my longing for magic…"

"I think this shouldn't be a problem. It's just one apprentice spot — that big Mage Quarter can't fit one more apprentice?" Baron Grey Ellerian laughed as he stood up from his stool. "I'll report this to Duke Bolvar Fordragon. It's a good request — a hero who thwarted the gnolls' despicable invasion attempt, striving for self-improvement and seeking knowledge, wanting to become a mage to continue serving the kingdom… Ah, this could practically be written into a story."

"But I think, for those scholars at the Wizard Sanctum, if you don't produce results or pass their assessments, even if you entered by the king's grace, they won't promote you to a full mage just because of connections. You'll still have to rely on your own effort to keep going."

"I understand, my lord," Jace said.

"Good. Then that's about it."

The baron taught him a few more necessary lines, then dusted off his clothes and walked out of the room, turning back to add: "No need to see me off. I'm in a hurry to get back to Lakeshire and report."
