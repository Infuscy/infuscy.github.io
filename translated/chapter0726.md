## Chapter 726: Something Goes Wrong

Jace recalled Morfiel with his mind and shouted behind him: "Get your succubi back to the altar! No point watching out here anymore — it's meaningless now. Come interfere with this demon's mind, all of you! We can stop him from fighting back against our circle!"

"Right, exactly." Mosur nodded. Hearing Jace's voice, he shouted toward the other side as well, reminding the warlocks who couldn't hear clearly to have their succubi return across the altar and come to their sides to help channel the enthrallment magic. Under the suppression of a whole crowd of warlocks and demons, the doomlord finally lost the swagger he'd had a moment ago and lowered his head again, the fel fire in his eyes dimming considerably.

Seeing that, Mosur let out a breath.

"Hold the… binding circle! Marsha, it's on you — hold on!"

His voice was already hoarse from shouting, but Marsha Stane was from Dalaran after all. Her mana was no weaker than Gladys's, the woman she'd replaced. The circle regained its original strength, and the doomlord before their eyes looked more and more exhausted.

Had the danger passed?

"Master, the circle, the circle! The circle itself is breaking!"

Sazeno shrieked from Jace's waist. Jace looked down and suddenly noticed the demon's fel blood flowing outward along the altar's engravings, some of it overflowing and damaging the integrity of the Blood of Azgoth circle!

Jace roared toward Mosur: "Look at your feet, Mosur! We have to perform a banishment, now!"

Mosur, who had just started to relax, looked down at the doomlord's fel blood and went white in an instant.

He gasped and said: "Yes, yes, this isn't right. Get to your position, Jace! Get to your position, Eli! The three of us are sending him back to the Twisting Nether! Now!"

Eli, the eldest summoner, was also rattled. He hurried back to his original position and immediately began chanting the incantation. Mosur joined in right after. Jace also began chanting at once, channeling the power of the circle and the Storm Altar to empower Eli's incantation alongside Mosur.

But barely halfway through the incantation, just as void energy began seeping from the doomlord's body, he suddenly roared several times and struggled to lift his leg. The binding circle's power, interfered with by the fel blood, could no longer fully restrain his movements.

Just then things took a very ugly turn. The goblin Gorsich bellowed: "Master Mosur, sorry! This really isn't the kind of scene for me…"

A burst of flame erupted beneath his feet and the whole man shot into the sky, flying toward the outside of the altar — the bastard was fleeing on rocket boots…

But the goblin hadn't flown a full circle out of the cliff before he smashed headfirst into a stone statue with an "ow" and dropped out of sight. No more sounds from him.

Mosur paid him no mind whatsoever. Jace could only force himself to ignore the chaos outside — after all, once the banishment incantation was complete, everything would be fine.

The doomlord's lifted leg came down in a violent stomp, and the entire altar shook and rumbled. Mosur and Eli, who were closest, couldn't hold their footing and fell to the ground. Jace stood firm, but the sudden interruption of his casting felt like a string snapping in his head, and a severe ringing pounded into his brain.

"Forget the banishment incantation — kill him!" Mosur shouted, backing up several steps. "Kill this demon! Slaughter him before the binding circle fails completely! Get away from the center, Jace! Eli!"

The warlocks remaining on the edges all began hurling damaging incantations at the demon lord in the middle. The demon minions around them also obeyed their masters' orders and rushed to the middle to hack and stab at the demon lord's legs and lower back.

The demon lord still couldn't move his lower body, but his upper half could barely move now. He swung a hand and shoved away a Mo'arg minion, then tried to turn his head to swat off the succubi trying to climb onto his back. But the more exaggerated his movements, the harder the binding circle yanked at his limbs.

Eli picked up his staff and chanted a Curse of Agony at the demon. Mosur was channeling a Shadow Bolt. Jace's Shadow Bolt had already struck home, hitting the demon square in the shoulder — but the moment it touched the demon's skin it seemed to sink right in, having little effect. The others' spells were even more powerless.

"This demon's resistance to Shadow is too strong!" Eli said. Jace shouted to the warlocks on his side: "Switch to something else!"

The enormous Storm Altar and the absurd circle everyone had drawn provided enough energy to bind this demon, but they also made the site ridiculously large. Communication among the dozen or so people had become extremely difficult amid the erupting lava and the demon's frenzied roars.

Shadow Bolts weren't working, so Marsha Stane immediately began chanting the Frostbolt incantation. The gnome warlock Sparckel simply picked up the Dark Iron dwarf's firearm and took aim to shoot.

Eli, who had expended enormous energy leading both the summoning and the interrupted banishment, couldn't cast any more spells after just a few incantations.

Jace also felt his energy reaching its limit. He opened his pack and reached for a mana potion inside — but his gaze immediately shifted to the Refined Wisdom flask.

Eight gold coins and all… but if not now, when?

He resolutely drank the entire flask in one go. The abundant arcane power of the Refined Wisdom flask not only boosted his willpower and his ability to manipulate magic, but directly replenished his energy.

The familiar sensation immediately reminded him of Grim Batol.

Before him was merely an Ered'ruin. Compared to that… it was nothing. Granted, it was a bit bigger.

After drinking the flask, Jace felt confidence surging back. He drew the Song of the Blue Sky and began chanting the Fireball incantation. To his surprise, the crystal-blue sword immediately burst into flames, and fire sprang from his eyes as well. Jace even felt that the fire elements hadn't been drawn by arcane energy — they'd been summoned by him, purely and simply.

The abnormally searing magic caught the demon's attention. Though he still couldn't move, he couldn't help roaring at Jace: "You think I, Kazzrez, overseer of the Legion, would fear your flames, warlock? I was born from fel fire!"

Jace ignored his voice. The lava around the altar churned as he chanted the Fireball, causing the fireball gathering between his sword tip and palm to rapidly swell into a rolling star of flame. With a great shout, he hurled it straight at the demon lord's head!

The fireball exploded against the doomlord's head, colliding with fel fire and blasting out a piercing roar. As the flames swept across, a crack actually split the demon's skull!

Kazzrez, who had nearly gotten back to his feet, dropped to one knee again and spat mouthful of dark-green fel blood flickering with bright embers.

Jace began chanting another Fireball, but the binding circle was even weaker than before. The demon spread his wings, shrouding the center of the altar, and reached out toward Jace, who was chanting in the distance. His palm swelled with a green-tinged, sinister glow.

The binding circle could no longer stop the demon from casting!

Struck by the unknown dark incantation, Jace felt a powerful dark magic press down on the top of his head, making his spellcasting motions and the gestures gathering the fireball unbearably sluggish and slow. The fireball he'd already gathered scattered and fell to the ground under this sudden suppression.

Was this Cripple, the spell doomguards were best at?

Jace relied on his formidable dark magic resistance to power through and regain clarity — only to see the doomlord cast a Cripple on the orc Kargel as well! Kargel, who was struggling to maintain the circle, was forced to his knees and vomited a mouthful of black blood.

The binding circle couldn't hold any longer. After the orc went down, the remaining four all collapsed from exhaustion — some fainting, some just sitting on the ground.

The doomlord broke free of his restraints, stood up, and roared: "Now it's my turn!"
