Chapter 728: Forced to Betray

The Storm Altar reeked of fel fire and scorched flesh. The collision of void energy made the towering black cliffs rumble and groan.

The churning of the lava had calmed slightly compared to before, but now that the altar had fallen quiet, the surging magma and the gurgling of popping lava bubbles were actually clearer.

Even kneeling here with his head lowered, Kazzrez still stood over four or five meters tall — taller than any ogre Jace had ever seen.

The demon's mouth no longer dripped burning fel blood. The warlocks who still had their senses stood or leaned against stone pillars, clutched their staves, or let their demon minions support them, gathered around in silence. No one spoke.

But this was no time for standing blankly. While Jace could still control this demon, he had to make sure he wouldn't cause any more trouble.

"It's not over yet." Jace stared at the demon and said to Mosur beside him: "We need to continue the binding circle to keep this demon trapped here."

"You've already enslaved it, Jace. He has no choice but to obey your orders," Mosur said.

Jace said, "I just drank a Refined Wisdom flask. The flask's effects probably only last a few hours — after a battle this intense, maybe even less…"

The most critical thing was that Jace was worried that if the demon's power wasn't restrained by the binding circle, the Storm Altar might instead become a source for him to draw upon dark power.

The doomlord was in such a weakened state that Jace was already struggling to control him. If he were allowed to recover his strength using Azeroth's power, he might quickly turn around and suppress Jace's will. There was no telling what kind of level this fellow commanded in the Twisting Nether.

He glanced at the exhausted warlocks around him, then at the several bodies on the ground — one charred black, another with its throat torn open by felhounds, and a third that was a half-satyr, half-dwarf creature.

The effects of the Polymorph seemed to have become permanent on him, reminding Jace of the Polymorph disasters he'd seen at the Wizard Sanctum.

"I can keep going." The summoner Eli tossed aside an empty potion bottle. "I can take part in continuing to bind this demon."

The orc Karol also pulled himself to his feet and said: "I can keep going too."

Marsha Stane said: "I think I can still manage as well, Jace Sesso."

Luther, who by now looked no different from a human hunter, also nodded and said: "Same here."

"Then let's clean up the fel blood and repair the circle first." Mosur held his staff and propped himself up. "Those planning to take part in restarting the binding magic, go rest on the side for now. The other warlocks who can still stand, come help. Move the wounded to those orc camps."

Marsha came to Jace and the demon's side. She first looked up at the demon lord with some uncertainty, then looked at Jace and said: "To be part of such a great summoning… to witness such a great summoner, Jace Sesso — it makes every price I've paid before worth it."

"Even the Shadow Council warlocks would be terrified by this sight. Some of them practically worship demons… willing to be slaves to these monsters." The orc Karol finished with a spit.

Kazzrez gave a snorting laugh and said: "Ignorant inferior creatures."

Albert mustered his courage and shouted at the demon: "Ignorant? The man before you is the powerful warlock who killed the pit lord Azgoth with Shadow magic!"

The demon's eyes, blazing with fel fire, flickered. He looked curiously at the gnome Albert and said in a low voice: "So Azgoth died at his hand. Then I'm not surprised. I hear Lord Kil'jaeden has long been curious about what sort of person managed that."

He looked back at Jace and said: "It seems I can conveniently kill you and return to the Twisting Nether to report to him — at least that would make this trip to Azeroth seem less absurd."

"You dare return to Kil'jaeden's side, Kazzrez?" Morfiel looked up and asked in Demonic, as if provoking him. Kazzrez lowered his gaze and stared at this strange demon with brilliant flames blazing in her eyes.

Morfiel continued: "A mortal now knows your true name. Do you think Kil'jaeden would still let you serve as a high-ranking commander of the Burning Legion? If your superior were the Destroyer Mannoroth, I don't think you'd be in this predicament — after all, he wouldn't particularly care what sort of rabble idiots his subordinates are…"

"Shut your mouth, Shivarra," Kazzrez said angrily. "My loyalty to Lord Kil'jaeden…"

"I believe in your loyalty, and I hope your Lord Kil'jaeden trusts you as much as I do." Morfiel put on an exasperated look. "After all, our master isn't the type to keep secrets like me — he might just write your true name into some strange demonic tome and sell it for a couple of mortal gold coins. He'd really do something like that."

"Once your true name makes the rounds of every warlock on Azeroth who can scrape together a few gold coins, you'll become a complete joke. Guess what Kil'jaeden will do with you — the esteemed Lord Kazzrez — when he learns of this and you return to the Twisting Nether?"

After Morfiel finished, Kazzrez fell silent and said no more.

Jace sensed the demon's silence and looked up in that direction.

He gazed at the demon lord's expression. Kazzrez wasn't hiding his emotions at all — his desire to kill had boiled over so intensely that Jace could clearly perceive it even from this distance. After all, if he killed the warlock before him right now, there'd be no need to worry about his true name being leaked.

But if he could really have killed Jace, he would have done it already. It was too late now. The reason Kazzrez was silent was because he knew Morfiel was right.

Kil'jaeden was a suspicious and ruthless ruler. Once Kazzrez had been compromised by a mortal, even if he returned to the Burning Legion he'd become a landmine that could go off at any time. Kil'jaeden couldn't tolerate an uncontrollable demon commanding the Legion's soldiers.

Morfiel had to be said to have hit the nail on the head. The doomlord before him had actually been caught in a dilemma ever since Jace enslaved him.

But Jace knew this didn't mean Kazzrez would willingly stay on Azeroth either. Plenty of demon lords existed outside the Burning Legion — for instance, Morfiel's former master Brutallus, who had been a powerful demon king in his own right before Kil'jaeden conquered him.

Demons who were determined to break away from the Burning Legion and set up on their own were by no means rare. For example, the dreadlord Kurusath, after obtaining the Draenei holy relic — the Ata'mal Crystal — believed he now had the power to stand against the Burning Legion, and was prepared to flee into the Twisting Nether with his minions and betray the Legion.

In the end, this summoning ritual cost four lives. Most of the others were injured to varying degrees.

The Bronzebeard dwarf Hober Lightforge had fallen into the lava — there wasn't even a body to find now. Gorsich, despite deserting and slamming into a stone pillar, had only fallen onto the rubble beneath the altar with a broken leg. He'd passed out but hadn't died. "Short Staff" Gladys was still unconscious from severe mana exhaustion, though she'd been lucky enough to dodge the Shadow Bolts. But Kol Tobias, who'd also been maintaining the binding circle, had been killed by the felhounds. Regis'var had unfortunately died from the Shadow Bolts. The gnome Sparckel had also been seriously injured by the Shadow Bolt bombardment. As for the Dark Iron dwarf Fezuthias… there was nothing to be said.

After cleaning the altar and redrawing the circle, Albert Fizzwidget and Luther Peaceman were tasked with gathering everyone's healing potions and Shadow Protection Potions and tending to the surviving wounded at the altar. The remaining warlocks would be responsible for completing the binding circle.

Gorsich had been securely tied up and thrown next to the lava to roast. He was now screaming at the top of his lungs begging Mosur for mercy, though the commotion had quieted down a lot compared to before.

Under Jace's enslavement, the doomlord put up no resistance whatsoever, so completing the binding circle this time was without difficulty.

After the ceremony ended, Kazzrez's massive form became slightly translucent, flickering with faint pulses of fel light — it felt very much like the "Banish" effect applied to demons in the game.

But Jace knew this wasn't a true Banish. What passed for Banish here was actually closer to a "banishment incantation" against a demon he hadn't summoned — flinging the other party straight back to the Twisting Nether. That was the real "Banish."

Once the binding was complete, the warlocks came to the orc camps outside the altar to rest — after all, being roasted in the middle of lava was torment that ordinary people could hardly endure.

By now the effects of the Refined Wisdom flask had probably worn off, but Jace didn't feel any strain from enslaving this demon lord. Perhaps it was the suppression of the binding circle, or perhaps it was the chain around his neck. Jace couldn't say for certain which had the greater effect.

He came to where Mosur was sitting and drinking water, looked down at the demon lord trapped below, and asked: "How long can this circle hold?"

"As long as this Storm Altar isn't destroyed, the circle drawing on the altar's power should be able to sustain itself indefinitely," Mosur answered, setting down his waterskin. "So the real danger lies in whether the Blackrock orcs will try to retake this tower. But Kargel Shadowfist will inform us of the Blackrock orcs' movements — as long as he still wants to seek knowledge from this demon."

"But this demon now answers to you… Jace. So it still comes down to you."

Though Mosur put it that way, Jace didn't have a clear idea in his mind yet of how to control this demon.

Enslaving Kazzrez had been an impulsive decision. He might have thought about how to handle a doomguard minion, but he'd never considered how to deal with a doomlord of this caliber.

After all, he was simply far too big — much larger than a doomguard. He wasn't something you could hide in a house, a cellar, or a warehouse.

Jace glanced at this demon and even felt a headache coming on.

He couldn't bring this demon back to the Kingdom of Stormwind — at least not before he was certain he could control him. Bringing him back to Stormwind City was absolutely out of the question. If King Varian saw this monster squeezing through the city gates, he'd probably run Jace through with his sword on the spot.

Forget bringing him anywhere — Jace didn't even know how to move this monster out of here. Just walking made the Storm Altar rumble and quake. That alone would draw God knows how much attention along the way.

"I can only leave him here," Jace said.

Mosur immediately said: "I'll stay here and guard this demon for you, Jace, until you decide to change your mind. Many of us here can stay and help keep watch on him. Besides… we can learn things from this demon that they'd never be able to find out anywhere else."

Jace nodded. For now, that was the only option.
