Chapter 729: More or Less an Agreement

By nightfall, the warlocks were resting in the orc tents on the hillside above. Jace came to the middle of the altar and looked at this demon whose form had turned translucent.

Kazzrez's two small eyes, bright as fel-fire lamps, fixed on Jace. "What, little thing? Are you planning to let me go? Enslaving a being like me is the stupidest decision you've ever made in your life."

"Trust me, this doesn't even crack the top three stupidest decisions of my life." Jace walked up to him and said: "I have questions for me, Kazzrez."

"Go on. Let me see what important questions you have."

Kazzrez lowered his massive head, and his great black horns came down on either side of Jace. At this distance, a single swing of his head could send Jace flying God knows where.

"Whatever the question, you must answer truthfully. That's an order," Jace said expressionlessly. Kazzrez gritted his teeth for a long time before saying: "I obey my master's commands."

Jace stared at him, planning to start with a lie-detection test. He asked: "Where is Ner'zhul?"

"Ner'zhul?" Kazzrez said: "That orc warchief is dead, Jace Sesso. His broken soul was imprisoned in an icy dungeon by Lord Kil'jaeden as punishment for betrayal and cast into…"

"Continue," Jace ordered.

Kazzrez drew his enormous wings tight against his body, making those massive brown wings look like an exaggerated heavy cloak. He was resisting Jace's command, forcefully suppressing his will, not wanting to continue. But driven by the power of Jace's enslavement and trapped by the binding magic, he could barely hold out.

In the end, the demon's teeth ground back and forth and shifted.

"Ner'zhul's soul still has value. Kil'jaeden needs him to help himself, help himself… continue… attacking… ur… Ah…"

"Azeroth," Jace finished for him, then said: "So Ner'zhul's soul is on Azeroth. If you return to the Twisting Nether with this information you've given me, will your soul be thrown into a dungeon by Kil'jaeden as punishment for betrayal too?"

"Anar'khael… Kale," Kazzrez cursed under his breath.

"Well, I don't understand it anyway. Second question." Jace continued: "What's on the other side of the Dark Portal? A lot of people say Draenor has been destroyed."

The demon lord paused for a moment, then said: "It's far from truly destroyed." On the subject of Draenor's condition, he spoke without much psychological resistance at all.

"Ner'zhul had no idea how to fully wield the power of the Sargeras staff, so part of that world survived — vast stretches of land left floating in the dark void. But Magtheridon, under Lord Kil'jaeden's orders, has already descended upon that world through the portal opened by the Sargeras staff. The Legion will soon occupy that place once more. Kil'jaeden will not abandon that world. Everything remaining on it — whether orc or Azeroth soldier — will soon be crushed by the Legion's power. A fitting reference, Jace Sesso — Azeroth's fate won't be much better."

Jace thought that compared to the other timeline, because of his intervention, Teron Gorefiend had died far earlier — after all, originally it was in the Shadowmoon Valley that this death knight's body had fallen to Turalyon's hand.

As one of the Horde's key leaders, Teron Gorefiend's death would bring what sort of changes to the situation on Draenor — Jace wasn't sure in his heart either.

But from what Kazzrez had revealed, the situation on Draenor seemed to have undergone no major changes. But one crucial piece of news was that the pit lord Magtheridon had already entered the shattered Draenor — that is, Outland.

Magtheridon was second only to the Pit Lord King Mannoroth among the pit lords. Though both were Annihilan, his strength far exceeded that of a warleader like Azgoth.

He was a high-ranking commander of the Burning Legion and Kil'jaeden's right hand.

Relying on the portal opened by the Sargeras staff, he had led a vast number of demon soldiers to rapidly occupy Outland and crowned himself the Lord of Outland. A decade and a half later, his power had grown so great that even Kil'jaeden suspected him of plotting to betray the Burning Legion.

"Do you know the specific situation of Azeroth's army on Draenor?" Jace asked.

"Send me back to the Twisting Nether and I'll find a way to ask Magtheridon for you." Kazzrez finished with a cold laugh. It seemed this fellow didn't know much more than he did.

For the next topic, Jace planned to discuss that terrifying demonic artifact… the soul scythe, Ursula. Jace glanced back at the lights in the orc camp in the distance. This topic had to be handled as carefully as possible.

He couldn't let the warlocks here catch even a whiff of it. The matter with that Dark Iron dwarf had made it clear that these people would stop at nothing to obtain greater power. Even the admiration they expressed toward him now was merely worship of strength — or an expectation that Jace could bring them greater power and more knowledge.

Even having gone through the life-and-death ordeal of the summoning ritual together was no comparison to the bond he shared with his own friends. He couldn't place his trust in these warlocks.

"Tell me — what is Dath'mora's mission on Azeroth?" Jase asked.

"You haven't enslaved her?" Kazzrez asked.

"You must know things she doesn't," Jace said.

"I… won't… tell…"

Kazzrez gritted his teeth and resisted Jace's command. Forcing commands on such a powerful demon consecutively was now exhausting Jace as well.

"Tell me her mission on Azeroth, or I'll make you die in agony," Jace added.

"Die?" Kazzrez gave a laugh, then said through suppressed rage: "You think I truly fear returning to the Twisting Nether? Returning to Kil'jaeden's side? This world will be doomed sooner or later. No matter how many people you tell my true name to… in the end it'll all be reduced to ash, you self-righteous fool. You really think a few words from a Shivarra could frighten a lord of the Ered'ruin?"

Jace could tell that Kazzrez was lying. He was indeed afraid of Kil'jaeden, afraid of returning to the Twisting Nether. But Jace also had no doubt that he was willing to risk going back and trying, just to escape the control of a weak mortal like him.

So a more direct threat was needed to make him yield further.

"You may have heard of an incantation." Jace cleared his throat and said: "It should be a Nathrezim incantation. It goes something like this: Koorelios, Feraos, Paku…"

"Stop!"

Kazzrez clenched his fists and let out an uncontrollable roar. Clearly, he too had recognized the Death Coil incantation.

His armor clattered with the trembling of his rage. The demon thrust his head forward until it nearly pressed against Jace's face — but restrained by the binding circle, this was the furthest he could go.

"You cannot possibly use my soul to release a Kor'kathium! Your power is nowhere near sufficient… no one can do it!"

"I don't mind trying," Jace said. "If you plan to break free of my control, I don't mind drinking a few more Refined Wisdom flasks when I'm about to lose my grip on you, setting up a circle here for support, and seeing just how much destruction your demonic soul can cause!"

He pointed to the northeast and said: "See Blackrock Mountain over there? It's currently occupied by a whole army of orcs. Ah, it's hard to see from here — it's all blocked by the cliffs — but you can at least see the firelight leaking from the cracks in Blackrock Mountain. When the time comes, I can perform a ritual here and send your demonic soul over there. If you really are as powerful as you say, you might be able to blow a few layers of skin off the walls of Blackrock Tower. By then I'll at least have made a small contribution to the Kingdom of Stormwind for all the trouble of summoning you."

"So — do you dare make that bet, Kazzrez? Bet that I'm incapable of turning your soul into a giant tracking bomb."

The demon wanted to say something more in his fury, but stopped himself several times. After a long while, he finally forced out a sentence: "Dath'mora is looking for an opportunity to guide the Legion to Azeroth."

"Worthless information. Even the rats in the sewers know what your Burning Legion is planning," Jace commanded. "Tell me something the rats don't know."

Kazzrez continued reluctantly: "Lord Kil'jaeden ordered me to dispatch her to recover the Great One's treasure lost on Azeroth. But I know she has her own agenda. She's hidden a lot from me…"

At this the lord suddenly extended his pointed tongue and licked his razor-sharp fangs, and said: "I know it now, Jace Sesso. You want to find that treasure too — that's why you want me to help you. That's your real purpose. Stop beating around the bush. What exactly did Dath'mora tell you?"

"Why are you suddenly so worked up?" Jace asked. "What, is that treasure important? Important enough to make Kil'jaeden forgive your betrayal?"

The flames in Kazzrez's eyes dimmed considerably. He fell silent and gave no answer. Jace kept staring at him.

By now it was the middle of the night. After this stretch of communicating with the demon, constantly driving him to obey his commands against his will, Jace felt his energy about to give out.

But in the end, he inexplicably had the feeling that he and this demon had reached some sort of strange consensus about seeking Ursula.

"Last question for today — can you make yourself any smaller?"

"No."
