## Chapter 73: Long Live the King

At some point, the walls of Stonewatch had been planted with blue banners bearing a gold lion — the royal crest of House Wrynn, and the emblem of the entire Kingdom of Stormwind.

You could see them everywhere in Stormwind City — white bricks, blue banners, and gold crests all blended into one. But here in Redridge, this unnatural deep blue formed a striking contrast against the red mountains in the distance, the dark green forest up close, and the yellow earth.

Jace kept his eyes on those banners. He stood in the middle of the drill ground, at the very end of the same soldier formation from the last defense of the keep. Then a horn sounded from the walls — urgent and rhythmic, drowning out the roar of the waterfall.

"The king approaches!"

"The king approaches!"

"All hail the king!"

"Long live the Alliance!"

The cries rang out from different heralds. A company of spirited cavalry came galloping through the gates of Stonewatch — even the chestnut horse Jace had ridden before snorted in alarm.

These cavalrymen wore heavy armor, and even their horses were wrapped in thick metal plate and caparisons. They lumbered along like underpowered machines, but every stride kicked up a cloud of dust.

At the head of the column was a tall young man with his black hair tied back, built solid as a calf. His broad chest and powerful thighs — straining against his tight breeches — made even the sturdy bay horse beneath him look small by comparison.

He wore no headdress, no crown, nothing that would mark his identity — just a bordered blue outer cloak that showed he was at least no commoner.

But when he dismounted and his gaze swept across everyone in Stonewatch, he carried himself as though he had built these walls with his own two hands.

This was Varian Wrynn, without a doubt.

He was definitely older than that "Golden Prince," Arthas Menethil — and by quite a bit.

Jace felt his memory might be off. He remembered reading in an official novel, or maybe it was in the game, that Varian had once said the Prince of Lordaeron, Arthas, was his peer.

You could only say Varian wasn't wrong if being within five years counted as "the same age."

Judging by appearance alone, this guy — forget "not yet twenty" — Jace would believe him if he said he was almost thirty.

Varian had to be close to a meter ninety tall, and was built so solid it was hard to imagine how he trained on a normal basis. Even walking among a crowd of soldiers and attendants, he looked like an underdeveloped orc.

He'd grown a bit of a beard, apparently to make himself look more mature, but his age clearly limited how full the stubble could get. It was only in this detail that Jace could barely make out that he was actually a big kid not yet twenty.

For some reason, from the moment Varian Wrynn came into view, Jace couldn't stop thinking about one thing.

What would happen if this guy ran into that orc shaman who'd summoned lightning by the lake?

The young king walked to the center of the crowd and fell silent for a moment, until the voices calling his name gradually quieted.

"You have endured hardships beyond my imagination, warriors. Both I and the people of Stormwind are still basking in the joy of victory over the orcs — and have nearly forgotten our ancient enemy, those despicable, shameless, cruel gnolls who still ravage our land and homes. But you have proved that even after the orcs' brutal devastation, even after Stormwind itself was burned to the ground… by the Holy Light, the Kingdom of Stormwind and her people still stand here, and will never be brought to their knees!"

"It is because of heroes like you…"

He looked over in this direction, his eyes flickering. Jace noticed that on his resolute, chiseled, statue-like face, there even appeared a trace of hesitation.

Varian fell silent again. Every soldier present stared at him, as if waiting for him to gather his emotions.

Jace knew it — the man had forgotten his lines.

"It is because of you. It is precisely because of you that all of this has been made possible."

Varian turned and strode toward them with big steps. Jace noticed Baron Grey Ellerian in the distance letting out a deep sigh of relief.

It was only then that Jace finally noticed the people who had accompanied the king here.

Varian Wrynn was so dazzling that he overshadowed everyone in his retinue. Jace saw Baron Grey Ellerian constantly saying something to a slightly older, powerfully built man beside him.

That man had brown hair and a thick beard that made him look older than his real age. He wore extremely sumptuous attire, draped in a magnificent cloak, wearing a silver circlet — probably the highest-ranking person here besides the king.

That was Bolvar Fordragon.

More than a decade from now, after Varian disappeared under Onyxia's manipulation, his young son Anduin Wrynn would succeed to the throne, and Bolvar would become Regent of Stormwind. Right now he was also the highest-ranking lord and general in all of Stormwind, serving as the king's counselor and helping him become a qualified ruler after the war.

The slender noblewoman standing beside Lord Bolvar, wearing a long silver gown, wearing a blue pendant that was clearly visible even from this distance, with pale golden hair cascading down — that must be Varian's wife, Queen Tiffin Wrynn.

Jace searched the crowd eagerly for a woman in a red dress with black hair, but found nothing. As he'd expected, the black dragon princess Onyxia hadn't arrived yet.

"Ahem."

Jace snapped his eyes back. Varian was already standing right in front of him — and standing rather too close.

It was only then that Jace noticed he seemed to be standing a bit farther forward than the other soldiers.

Had the other soldiers already finished their conversations? Was it his turn already?

"You are… Jace Sesso?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

He suddenly remembered all that etiquette the baron had drilled into him and snapped off a bow.

Thunk.

His head hit something slab-like — had he just headbutted… damn it.

When he looked up again, several nobles in the distance had covered their eyes.

A bizarre mix of sweat and cologne hit him in the face. The king's sweat had already seeped through his black hair and run down to his forehead. Right now it was hard to say which of the two of them was more nervous.

But compared to his stiff expression from before, Varian seemed to have loosened up quite a bit — after all, he'd just discovered he wasn't the biggest fool in the whole yard.

He said in a low voice, "You are the hero of Stonewatch. I learned of your deeds from Colonel Todman's letter — single-handedly defending a watchtower, saving the soldiers of Stonewatch, keeping them from being attacked from all sides of the wall with stones and crossbow bolts. Without you, our casualties might have multiplied several times over, and this fortress might have fallen into gnoll hands."

Although the king's voice was still a bit tense and stiff, it was already magnetic and compelling. After he finished speaking, Jace clearly heard him let out a breath and then say, "If you have any wish, speak it now. I will do my best to grant it."

"Serving the kingdom loyally is my greatest wish, Your Majesty," Jace said, reciting what the baron had taught him.

Varian said, "Those who serve the kingdom loyally must also receive tangible rewards, Mr. Jace Sesso. You are not a member of the military, so I cannot grant you honors at my discretion. You may state your request directly."

"I…" Jace pretended to hesitate for a moment, then said, "I hope to become a formal apprentice at the Wizard Sanctum, Your Majesty."

"What an ambitious young man," one of the nobles nearby murmured.

"Your dedication to learning shames me," Varian Wrynn said. "It shall be as you wish. I will go to the Wizard Sanctum in person and petition the mages and elders there for your qualification to become a formal apprentice and study magic. If they are willing, I hope you will also cherish this opportunity, strive hard, and eventually become a royal mage the likes of Nielas Aran — a pillar of magic for the kingdom, making even more outstanding contributions to the kingdom and all the people of the Alliance!"

"I will be waiting for you at Stormwind Keep, Mr. Sesso."

Somehow, Jace hadn't been thinking that far ahead, but now that Varian had said it, he suddenly felt a surge of emotion. A royal mage… it was a pie in the sky, but man, it smelled good.
