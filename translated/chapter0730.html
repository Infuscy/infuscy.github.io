Chapter 730: A Clue, or an Command?

That evening, back at the orc camp, Jace took out the scroll that Das'mora had hidden in her demon pack — the one about the Burning Legion's necromancers — and gave it another look. His original plan had been simple enough: summon a doomguard, then pull out the scroll and force him to translate it. If that doomguard couldn't manage it, find a way to kill him, or banish him and summon another one and ask again. Now Mosur had summoned a doom lord instead, and he'd even managed to enslave the thing. On the surface, that seemed like things had gone even more smoothly — after all, a lord who knew infinitely more than a doomguard was a massive upgrade. The chances of figuring out the Eredeth language had just gotten a lot better. But in reality, the situation had gotten more complicated. Because Jace first needed to figure out whether Kartzel actually knew what was on that scroll. In other words: was this a command from the demon lord ordering the search for a treasure, or was it Das'mora's report to Kartzel containing clues about the artifact? If it was a command — if the Burning Legion and Kartzel himself already knew what it said — then he could just interrogate the demon normally. But it also meant the scroll didn't contain much of real value. After all, the Legion knew, and Das'mora knew, and yet none of them had managed to find the scythe based on this information. But if Kartzel didn't know — meaning this scroll was a report Das'mora had been trying to deliver to Kartzel, loaded with critical clues about the scythe that she'd never been able to get through — Then she'd needed ritual materials, a ritual dagger, a communication circle to reach her demon superior... and now, given the similarities between Mosur's summoning incantation and that circle, it was almost certain that the Burning Legion officer Das'mora had been trying to contact was Kartzel himself. So if Jace just showed the scroll to Kartzel, wouldn't that accomplish exactly what Das'mora had been trying and failing to do all along? Jace had to consider the possibility that this doom lord might one day break free of the binding circle — or even throw off his enslavement entirely. If the contents of this scroll were extremely critical, and Kartzel eventually tried to make up for his "failures" by seeking Kil'jaeden's trust and handed over everything Jace had shown him directly to Kil'jaeden himself, and Kil'jaeden then sent far more capable demons to this world to seize Ulthalesh... then Jace would have no advantage left whatsoever. Jace had a nagging feeling the scroll's contents were very likely critical. He recalled the black knights he'd encountered in Eldre'Thalas — chances were, Medivh's undead servants had found Das'mora's scroll and quickly tracked down the Ulthalesh scythe, then brought it back to Karazhan. If Das'mora hadn't been locked up in prison, she might have found it herself. Of course, until he figured out what all those dense Eredeth words on the scroll actually meant, nothing was certain. "Sazeno, come out." "Does my master need my help?" The imp crawled out of the pack. "I thought that demon lord could already answer all your questions!" "Not yet." Jace spread out the scroll in his hands, glanced at the demonic writing on it — clues like "trapped soul," "necromancer," "Nathrezim," and so on — then asked, "I want to know: in your view, is this scroll a set of clues Das'mora wanted to report to Kartzel, or is it Kartzel's description to Das'mora of the thing she needed to find?" Sazeno held the scroll and studied it for a moment. "How am I supposed to figure that out... Most of what's written here is in Eredeth, my master. I can't tell the tone either." Jace flipped open the demon pack, rummaged inside, and pulled out a few sheets of paper he'd been planning to use for his report on Deathwing. "I want you to copy every Eredeth character and word from this scroll onto here." "What are you up to, my master?" the imp asked. "Ask out of order," Jace said. "These alone won't be enough. I need to find other sources and mix in more Eredeth writing so he can't see the connections. Oh — throw in the Eredeth from that scroll full of profanity too. And of course the writing from Das'mora's other scrolls and circles. Get it all in there." Jace figured he should write a letter to Amy, asking her to copy down some of the Eredeth writing from the Order of Tirisfal's records — the parts they hadn't been able to decipher — and send them to him... Maybe he should pay a visit to the doomguard that Mosur and the others had captured first. Even if the creature couldn't make out every Eredeth character on the scroll, he could at least contribute some Eredeth vocabulary he did know to pad things out and add confusion. Once all the words were completely scrambled, he'd have Kartzel translate them without the demon lord knowing what he was actually reading. The next morning, Jace left the orc camp. At the edge of the cliff ahead, Morophil was staring down at Kartzel, who knelt motionless below. By now the thick clouds over Blackrock Mountain had thinned considerably compared to the night before. In the sliver of sunlight that finally managed to leak through, the doom lord's tawny fur and the horns on his head were much clearer than they'd been the previous night. Surrounded by fel-green light, he looked less like a demon commander dwelling in the Twisting Nether and more like a giant minotaur. Sensing Jace's gaze from behind, Morophil turned her head and glanced at him. After confirming nothing was amiss, Jace headed toward the direction of Mosur's battered tent. After the battle of the previous day, it was obvious that every surviving warlock held Jace in the highest regard. They greeted him proactively, even addressing him as "sir" or "master." He had to admit these warlocks were rather pure in their way — the moment they realized how powerful your magic was, they'd worship you on the spot. Mere reputation from some unknown source, on the other hand, hardly earned their respect. Mosur was sitting in front of his tent flipping through parchment, apparently reading up on Eredeth demons. The moment he heard Jace's footsteps, he looked up. "Jace, I'm curious — is the demon-enslaving incantation you use different from ours? The scene was too chaotic yesterday; I couldn't hear clearly." "The same," Jace said. "Which is how I could tell what that Dark Iron dwarf was trying to pull." "Did you learn that incantation from Das'mora?" Mosur asked. "More or less," Jace said. "Do you have a problem with me killing Das'mora?" Mosur smiled slightly. "What did the people at the Slaughtered Lamb say — did they think I'd fallen in love with that warlock woman, Das'mora Dark-Eye?" "I'll be honest with you — back then I admired Das'mora quite a lot. About six or seven years before the Dark Portal opened, like a lot of people who'd strayed from the righteous path, after seeing how powerful the shadow magic of the trolls in Zul'Gurub was... carrying some hatred, or some hard-to-articulate purpose, I walked that road too. Some dwarves in Ironforge told me about the existence of demons, and so I began researching demon summoning. It was during that period that I came into contact with many of Das'mora's writings, and even met some warlocks who claimed to have met Das'mora in person. From them I obtained a great deal of knowledge about demons and the Twisting Nether." "The scroll for summoning the demon lord Kartzel was obtained around that time. Back then, many warlocks wanted to summon this demon to Azeroth to serve as our teacher and instruct us in demonic magic... but now I'm the only one of those warlocks left. The reason I went to the Slaughtered Lamb back then was to gather another group of people to summon Kartzel, but they were more interested in learning shadow magic and had no interest in summoning a powerful demon. They couldn't help me." "When you summoned Kartzel, it didn't look like you were summoning a teacher," Jace said. Mosur explained, "In the process of dealing with demons, you discover they're not actually that much stronger than us. Though I only realized that after encountering the orcs following the Dark Portal and seeing more and more demon minions. From that point on, I decided to stop worshipping these creatures of the Twisting Nether and instead seek to learn more about the Twisting Nether itself from them — and, of course, to make them my helpers rather than my masters and objects of worship." He paused for a moment, then said, "And in the end, the Lady Das'mora I'd always wanted to meet turned out to be a demon herself." "A demon disguised as an elf," Jace said. Mosur nodded. "That's why she knew so demonology. Truly disappointing." "Now the summoning ritual is complete," Jace said. "Time to honor your promise, Mosur." "Share what we know," Mosur said. "Come back to Moonbrook with me, Jace. I'll show you all the incantations and demon-related information I've gathered. Or is there something you're particularly interested in? I recall you mentioned wanting to summon a demon." "Now I'm more interested in your summoning circles," Jace said. "As for summoning demons — didn't you lot capture a doomguard? Now that the ritual to summon Kartzel is complete, you should be able to take me to see him, right?" "Of course," Mosur said. "The records of the summoning circles are in Moonbrook. Do you want to see that doomguard now?" According to the timeline Bolvar Fordragon had given him, Jace had to reach Stonewatch before February — which gave him less than two weeks now. He needed to think carefully about the timing. If it was too far, he'd have to wait until after Stonewatch's offensive against the gnolls before making any plans. "Where is he?" Jace asked. Mosur said, "In a small village on the Nafeltari River, south of Lakeshire. 'Short-Staff' Gladis keeps some of his materials there. He has a basement, and it's far from Lakeshire. Magino Dumas doesn't pay much attention to that place. Getting there from the Black Swamp doesn't require passing through any Kingdom guard checkpoints, so we took the doomguard there." "Doesn't sound too far," Jace said. "How long to get there from here?" Mosur said, "If we set out now and head south over the Redridge Mountains, we'd be there in three or four days." After having gone back to the Kingdom of Lordaeron and gotten used to flying around on dragonhawks and gryphons, Jace felt his patience for walking had worn thin. Forget walking — even riding Alna back and forth to Moonbrook, he'd found himself getting impatient. If it took three or four days on foot to reach the village Mosur described, then he wouldn't have time to return to Stormwind. He'd have to head straight to Lakeshire after seeing the doomguard, then on to Stonewatch. He patted his pocket to confirm he was wearing the court mage's sigil, and said, "If you take me, who'll watch the Storm Altar?" "Ely can handle it. He's more skilled with the circles than I am, Jace," Mosur said.
