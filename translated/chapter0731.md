## Chapter 731: The Warlocks' Hidden Hideout

Jace left Morophil in charge of helping Ely watch over the great demon, then set out with Mosur. Leaving only Ely and a group of mentally exhausted warlocks to guard the Storm Altar didn't sit well with him at all.

The place Mosur had mentioned was close to the in-game "Three-Point Crossing" — the three-way fork where the roads to Lakeshire, Goldshire, and Duskwood met.

After reaching the bridge heading toward Duskwood and turning east, following the banks of the Nafeltari River forward, winding into the valleys of the Redridge Mountains, they'd walked barely five miles before a mill and the faint outlines of rooftops among the trees came into view in the distance.

Jace didn't remember this village from the game. The brown-red mountain ranges on either side layered with tiers of green trees, the river flowing underfoot, the deep blue winter sky at noon...

If not for the nagging worry about whether something might go wrong with that demon lord at the Storm Altar, and the matters waiting at Stonewatch and Ilgara Tower afterward, he honestly would have loved to spend a few days properly enjoying this place.

As Mosur led him to a remote little house, Jace swept the interior with Detect Demon and faintly sensed two demons inside. One wasn't very powerful — felt like a succubus. Because they were indoors, the impression wasn't very clear. The other — was that the doomguard? Its aura felt weak as an imp's.

Mosur knocked on the door. It was opened by an extremely fat, slovenly woman.

"Mosur..." she said with a smile. "The ritual you and my master performed..."

She was halfway through her sentence when she suddenly noticed Jace beside him, her eyes going wide. "And who is this? Your scent is truly captivating."

This was a succubus in human form.

"This is Jace Sesso," Mosur said to the woman. "Meet our new friend, Madame Segna. As for that ritual... your master didn't exactly cover himself in glory."

"That's not surprising. My master is a rare coward, but what can I do — he's my master." Segna asked curiously, "So is this Master Jace Sesso here to see me, or is he here for the Eredeth?"

Jace looked toward the staircase leading to the basement. "The Eredeth."

"Well, your tastes are truly extraordinary," the succubus said with a smile.

Jace glanced at her. The succubus realized this young human wasn't much interested in joking around, so she wiped the smile off her face and tactfully stepped back. "My apologies, Mr. Sesso. Please, come in. That demon is waiting for you nice and obediently."

Following Mosur into the basement, amid a dense, chaotic mess of orcish circles glowing with fel and shadow light, a doomguard with horns scraping the ceiling was chained firmly to the walls on both sides. One of the creature's wings had been chopped off by something, and under the circle's restrictions, it seemed incapable of healing itself and regrowing the wing.

The entire basement reeked of a foul stench of demon blood. Having not smelled it outside the door, Jace guessed they must have used some kind of magic to seal in the demonic stench down here.

"Zatharin," Mosur said, looking at the demon before him. "Pay your respects to the warlock before you, then answer his questions honestly."

"Yes, master." This doomguard named Zatharin nodded obediently to Mosur. The degree of his submission was completely at odds with his ferocious appearance.

So Mosur had already found a way to enslave this doomguard?

That wasn't strange, given that among their number was someone bold enough to directly enslave a doom lord.

Perhaps it was precisely because the process of enslaving Zatharin had been unexpectedly easy that it had given that Dark Iron dwarf the courage to try enslaving Kartzel.

"Talk with him. I'll wait outside, Jace," Mosur said.

Jace knew Mosur was doing this to earn his trust, but since this was a demon Mosur had enslaved, anything Jace said could easily be extracted from the demon by Mosur afterward. Whether he stayed outside or not was purely a formality.

After Mosur left the basement, Jace took out Sazeno's transcription sheets, found the portions with fewer words related to Ulthalesh, and held them up to the doomguard. "Do you recognize these words? This is the language of your Eredeth kin."

The doomguard lowered his head and stared at the small characters on the paper. There was something indescribable about having this demon — a vanguard who had once frenziedly slaughtered his comrades and allies on the battlefield, arrogantly declaring he would annihilate the entire world — this close, now obediently following his orders. It felt like some kind of inexplicable revenge, or the satisfaction of having the upper hand. In any case, it was a gratifying feeling.

Perhaps this was exactly the feeling that warlocks who researched demon summoning and enslavement were seeking, despite the enormous risks they ran.

"The Eredar do not permit us to use our own language within the Legion, master's friend," the doomguard murmured in Demonic. "Death after death and rebirth after rebirth have long since caused me to forget my original tongue. Even the name Eredeth is distant to me now. Today we are the guards of the Burning Legion, serving the supreme Great Master, the harbingers of doom to the worlds he watches over — not some fel inhabitants of another world."

The doomguard's tone sounded like his brain wasn't working very well. By comparison, Kartzel, who spoke in a halting manner, didn't seem so dull after all.

The Eredeth race as a whole was like this. Whether these doomguards or the powerful doom lords, while they were admittedly more adaptable than the Annihilan pit lords, on the whole they weren't exactly quick-witted.

"Not a single word you recognize?" Jace asked.

The doomguard hesitated for a moment, then lowered his head and pointed at several words, explaining haltingly in Demonic: "Void... mage..." Jace listened to the doomguard painstakingly dig out two words he recognized. That was at least a little better than Sazeno. Still, he wasn't particularly disappointed.

Because this confirmed his guess: ordinary doomguards basically no longer knew Eredeth. If that was the case, then people like Darkann, or other mages in the Reliquary who had learned of this scroll, would have a much harder time figuring out its specific contents if they wanted to know.

"Tell me as many words and runes in ancient Eredeth as you can. I don't care if you know what they mean or not," Jace said, summoning Sazeno. "Let this imp help you record them."

Comparing against the characters he'd copied from Das'mora's scroll, the imp recorded each word the doomguard produced one by one. Most of these words were colloquialisms, or even profanity. They didn't even have basic pronouns like "you, I, he" covered. It seemed the doomguards had truly abandoned this language.

So why had Das'mora chosen to communicate with Kartzel in a language they themselves had mostly forgotten? Was it for "encryption"?

Listening to the doomguard strain to recall his "native vocabulary," Jace thought about how Das'mora had been imprisoned by the Highborne while satyrs outside had been trying to forcibly summon her out for questioning. Both groups had no shortage of mages skilled in Demonic.

Had it been to prevent them from learning the whereabouts of the Ulthalesh scythe that Das'mora had chosen to record her clues in Eredeth? Whatever the reason, it was impossible to know now. All that remained of Das'mora was a single shard of her soul.
