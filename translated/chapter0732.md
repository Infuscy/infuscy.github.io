## Chapter 732: Return to Stonewatch

After bidding farewell to Mosur and leaving the village, Jace set out for Lakeshire at once.

He explained his identity and purpose to Lakeshire's magistrate, Magistrate Solomon, who immediately lent this court mage a horse and a mounted Lakeshire guard, wanting to provide protection for the journey to Stonewatch.

Though he knew that as a court mage in the king's service, such treatment was perfectly normal, he still wasn't quite used to all the enthusiasm.

And so he politely declined the magistrate's kind offer and rode off eastward on a pack horse that normally shuttled supplies between Stonewatch and Lakeshire.

That evening, in the forest by the shores of Lake Everstill, he tied up his horse to one side and sat down to rest under a tree. Spring either hadn't arrived yet, or it had come too quietly — the forest at night was utterly silent.

Right now, thinking back on everything he'd experienced after leaving Grim Batol and traveling to Lordaeron and Quel'Thalas, Jace had to admit he didn't feel particularly resentful about the Alliance's somewhat cold treatment of him.

Because he was more accustomed to being a nobody — or rather, living life a bit more low-key.

If Deathwing hadn't come looking for him, he probably wouldn't have accepted Krasus's request to go to Grim Batol. If he hadn't read the archmage Rhonin's script in advance and known that Deathwing himself was the key to shattering the Demon Soul, he didn't think he'd have had any chance of defeating Deathwing and rescuing the Dragonqueen.

Not to mention that even with all that, without Wynethra and Grede's help, he never would have made it to the final step.

Thinking back now to the fervor he'd felt when heading to Menethil Harbor — that conviction that he alone would save the world — it had really just been a rush of hot blood ignited by his young body. If Grede hadn't noticed something was off with him and taken it upon himself to bring Wynethra along to intercept him, he might very well have ended up as one of the piles of rotting corpses in the Wetlands marshes by now.

So how could he face the Alliance's commendations and adulation with a clear conscience?

So the fact that the Alliance these days didn't really understand how powerful Deathwing was, and was unwilling to give him his due rewards because he practiced dark magic — that actually made him feel a little more at ease.

The next morning, Jace arrived at Stonewatch.

From a distance, Stonewatch still looked the same as before. The repair work was progressing extremely slowly. The massive breaches from before now just had some scaffolding added — it looked like there'd been some patching, but also maybe not.

A sentry hailed him from afar. "State your name and your business here, mage. Stonewatch is currently on high alert and does not accept travelers seeking lodging."

"Jace Sesso. I'm here on the king's behalf," Jace called out.

"Master Jace Sesso?"

"Inform the commander that Master Sesso is here!"

"Please show me your sigil, master."

Just then a horn sounded from atop the castle walls. Jace swallowed awkwardly, glanced at the top of the ramparts, then took out his sigil as requested and showed it to the soldier before him.

Before long, Stonewatch's commander, Captain Tordman, came running up to the gate.

"Apologies, Master Sesso!" The crew-cut commander strode up to him. Jace felt he'd lost a good deal of weight since their last meeting.

Tordman directed the guards to take the horse to the stable while sizing Jace up and down. "You've changed a great deal, Master Sesso."

"Last time I was here I was only seventeen, Captain Tordman," Jace said.

"Yes, three years have passed in the blink of an eye," the captain said. "Many things have changed, but unfortunately, the gnolls around Stonewatch are still rampant. Even demons pouring out of the Dark Portal and ravaging the gnoll forests haven't managed to reduce their numbers one bit. I'm starting to suspect they're in league with the demons."

"I'm sorry, master — you didn't send word ahead or write to let us know you were coming, so there's no proper welcome ceremony prepared." He turned to look at two hastily assembled rows of tall guards standing at attention on the drill ground and said, "But you know we deeply welcome a master of your exceptional skill who understands the gnolls' foul magic coming to Stonewatch, to help us retake Ilgara Tower. Because of the loss of that tower, the lads at Stonewatch haven't had a peaceful winter."

"It's my honor to be of help," Jace said.

Tordman smiled and said, "If you'd like, you can join us for lunch first. This afternoon I'll brief you on our plans in the war room."

"No problem," Jace said.

After eating a simple lunch in the great hall on the castle's lowest level and resting briefly, Jace followed the captain up to the war room on the highest level.

Several squad leaders had already arrived. Jace even recognized a few somewhat familiar faces among them — people he'd gotten to know during the previous siege of Stonewatch. Two of them had been standing right beside him when the king bestowed his commendations. Even Jace himself was surprised he still recognized these people.

"Master Sesso."

"Good afternoon, Master Sesso. We heard you were coming long ago."

Jace had been expecting these soldiers to reminisce with him, to talk about the fierce battle they'd fought side by side that night stopping the gnolls from scaling the walls. Instead, they all saluted him with rigid military formality and addressed him by his honorific. The distance made it feel as if they didn't know him at all.

In the center of the round table sat a map, already laid out, depicting Stonewatch, the surrounding forests, and Ilgara Tower.

Captain Tordman pointed to the marker for the mage tower in the center. "Ilgara Tower was established after the Gnoll War by King Bathralian and the Wizard Sanctum, with the purpose of defending against further gnoll incursions. In the first several decades, this mage tower faithfully fulfilled its duty. Under its protection, not a single gnoll dared set foot in this forest, Master Sesso."

"When the orcs attacked this tower, they expended enormous manpower and effort, but the Horde's army was, after all, not gnolls. They were disciplined and possessed far more powerful warlocks who knew how to use dark magic to suppress this tower's power. But later, after we retook this mage tower, we found it was nowhere near as powerful as before."

"After the war, the tower was maintained by the Wizard Sanctum, but when the gnolls launched their surprise attack, it failed to help the mages inside foresee the enemy's approach, nor did it use its magical light to notify Stonewatch of the approaching danger. The entire watchtower seemed as if its magic had been stolen by some dark magic of the orcs..."

"The mage tower was built on a ley line nexus, Captain Tordman," Jace explained. "Do you know about the Karazhan Cataclysm? That accidental explosion severely affected the ley lines in this area, so it's perfectly normal for the mage tower to have lost its effectiveness. It may not necessarily be due to war damage."
