Chapter 733: The Mage Tower's Power Source

"Regarding Karazhan's effect on the ley lines, Master Morgas offered that explanation before," Tordman said. "But the problem is, this tower is still providing magical support to the gnolls living inside. Our soldiers attempted a direct assault once and found that those shadow mages inside seemed able to cast spells continuously and strike at us while we were fighting the gnoll guards. Fighting it out that way would've been nothing but a waste of lives with zero chance of success. That's why we decided to request aid from Stormwind."

"When you say magical power — do you mean arcane force or shadow energy?" Jace asked.

Tordman turned to look at a soldier in chainmail beside him. "You saw it most clearly last time, Captain Tabor. Tell Master Sesso about it — I barely noticed anything that time."

"It was definitely shadow, Master Sesso," the captain said. "During the last assault, I saw a dark, violet-black light pouring from the top of that tower. It wasn't at all like the pale purple-white glow from before the orcs captured it. I've seen that kind of arcane light at the Wizard Sanctum."

There was a shadow energy source inside this mage tower?

Jace asked, "When you retook this tower from the orcs, didn't you find any magical artifacts the Horde had left behind?"

"No," another captain said. "When the orcs withdrew, they took everything imbued with magic, along with all their records. Master Morgas from the Wizard Sanctum was furious about that."

Tordman thought for a moment, then asked, "Is it possible the gnolls found something the orcs left and brought it in?"

Jace seriously doubted that guess. Recalling the last time he'd come here with Grede, he hadn't sensed any shadow energy source inside. Ilgara Tower had been just a run-down, ordinary watchtower. Aside from the fact that people were living inside it, there'd been nothing special about it at all.

Could it be that they only activated this "energy source" when they came under attack?

"Did that strange glow appear on the tower before you launched your assault?" Jace asked.

"No," Tordman said. "That's why we thought the tower was no longer functional and launched the attack. It was only after we started fighting the guards at the watchtower that the tower suddenly lit up. It caught us completely off guard."

"Looks like there's no figuring this out without taking a look myself," Jace asked. "Does Stonewatch have a roost?"

"It does," Tordman said. "Not only do we have a roost, we've actually raised a gryphon here — and it was trained in Ironforge, far more obedient than those trained in Stormwind, master."

"Can you have the gryphon carry me up to take a look first?" Jace asked.

"No problem," Tordman said. "Tonight, then. I'm worried a gryphon would be too conspicuous during the day and alert the gnolls to our intentions to attack that tower."

That evening, Jace followed a guard captain up onto the ramparts. Stonewatch's gryphon was a brownish-yellow Hinterlands runt, but that already exceeded his expectations.

He figured that if he needed to travel back and forth between Ilgara Tower and Stormwind in the future, he could make use of this roost and ride Goljet back and forth. Stonewatch's gryphon keeper was a human, but since he'd been assigned to guard gryphons at such a critical location, his riding skills probably weren't an issue.

Still, the moment he mounted the gryphon, Jace felt the gap between himself and Grede.

This Stonewatch gryphon keeper often felt like he'd shout commands several times without getting a response, needing to yank hard on the reins or even point with his hand to make the gryphon understand. When Grede rode a gryphon, it felt like he only needed to let out a roar venting his fury and the gryphon would immediately understand that he wanted to turn back and counterattack. Just a call toward the distance and the gryphon would understand he wanted to retreat and immediately try to break off from combat.

Still, just as Tordman had said, this gryphon was remarkably docile and obedient. As long as it understood what the rider wanted, it would carry out the instruction immediately.

Perhaps it was precisely because human riders had coordination issues with gryphons that Ironforge had chosen such a gentle, obedient gryphon to send here to help.

Skimming over the canopy of the forest, Jace caught sight of the dark silhouette among the trees in the distance — the eaves of Ilgara Tower.

The keeper flew very carefully, staying as high as possible to avoid being spotted by gnoll sentries, but that also made it considerably harder for Jace to observe the magical conditions at the top of the tower.

Still, after flying several circles, Jace still couldn't observe any obvious presence of shadow energy. It wasn't that there was absolutely none — he did sense a breath of shadow buried within the tower, but with this little trace, you wouldn't notice it at all unless you were deliberately trying to perceive it. Probably just residue from the warlocks inside attempting to cast spells.

Could this little amount of energy really support the entire tower in withstanding the Stonewatch garrison's assault?

"Can you make anything out, master? Should I descend a bit lower?" the gryphon keeper asked as the gryphon once again banked around the eastern side of Ilgara Tower.

This circuit had brought them slightly closer, making Jace feel that the shadow power within this tower was purely strange. He seemed to have sensed similar shadow somewhere before.

It was inside the Slaughtered Lamb... it was a voidwalker's energy.

Jace immediately cast Detect Demon and glanced inside. The result made it feel as though the entire tower was suffused with an extremely faint, shadowy film. What was going on — was this mage tower a demon?

No, no. Jace noticed the core of this shadow film at the very bottom of the tower.

The entire tower wasn't a demon. Rather, some magic had bound a demon to this tower, causing the entire structure to take on a faint taint of demonic energy.

Considering this faint shadow, along with the purity of the shadow it emitted, and given that these gnoll warlocks probably couldn't summon any powerful demons anyway, Jace guessed it was most likely a voidwalker.

These gnolls had actually summoned a voidwalker to power the entire mage tower? They really had some ideas.

No wonder the mage tower only lit up when someone attacked — a weak voidwalker couldn't sustain the entire tower's function for long. It definitely couldn't power the tower ceaselessly like a complete ley line nexus, turning it into a lighthouse that stood guard here watching for all hostility at every moment.

But this also gave Jace an idea. If he could learn how the gnolls had bound the demon to the entire tower, wouldn't that mean he could also summon a demon in the future and make it the tower's energy source?

Then he wouldn't have to worry about the tower's weakened magic and it being seized again by gnolls — or even orcs or black dragons.

Wait, could this tower even fit Kartzel inside?
