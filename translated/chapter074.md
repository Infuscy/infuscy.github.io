## Chapter 74: Reunion at Lakeshire

Jace barely heard a word of the rest of the king's speech.

He was going to actually enter the Wizard Sanctum — not as some peripheral assistant, but as an apprentice. It could be said that he had truly joined the ranks of mages, even though he hadn't yet sensed the ley lines, even though he couldn't cast a single arcane spell — but at least in name…

In truth, the mages' reluctance to take on older students who lacked talent was mainly about not wanting to waste resources. It wasn't that it was completely impossible to learn magic past your teens.

Arcane magic had so many useful applications — conjuring water and food, Invisibility, even Teleportation… Of course, he wasn't counting on learning Teleportation anytime soon.

For offensive magic, he could make up for it with Shadow and by honing his martial skills. And as long as he specialized in just one or two utility spells in arcane magic — he didn't need to learn too many — it would be a qualitative leap in his combat power.

Moreover, at the Wizard Sanctum, and in the future if he had the chance to enter Dalaran, Jace believed he could study the principles behind this world's energy even more deeply. Perhaps he could minimize the negative effects of Shadow energy on his body and mind as much as possible. That was one of his main goals.

After the ceremony, on his way back to his room, he heard plenty of people praising him. Everyone was immersed in joyful excitement — they'd won the battle, survived, and been reviewed by the king.

But because that night's battle had been so dark, Jace actually felt a little embarrassed.

Could courage stirred up by Shadow power really count as true courage?

He returned to his window and flipped over his shoe soles — there were still scorch marks, traces of being seared by the Shadow fire from the corpses.

Those gnoll corpses… would the people disposing of them notice the Shadow fire?

What would Morgas think when he saw those gnolls killed by those ominous flames?

"I killed those gnolls with a sword that had been set on fire by a gnoll warlock…" he muttered to himself in a low voice, as if practicing his excuse in advance.

"Jace Sesso."

Hearing the voice behind him, Jace whipped around.

But it was Colonel Todman. Right — Morgas had been in a hurry to get back to Stormwind. He shouldn't be here.

"You did well today, lad. You're the first person I've seen who'd dare headbutt the king with his own forehead! Haha!" Todman said with a loud laugh.

Jace's mouth twitched.

"No need to be so nervous." The colonel clapped Jace on the shoulder. "It's normal for a young man to be nervous in a moment like that. I'd say the king was even more nervous than you. When are you heading back? You can stay here as long as you like — the king might be staying another day or two to observe our training."

"I have business at the Wizard Sanctum," Jace said, tightening his pack. "And I have a friend in Lakeshire. He was injured a few days ago — I need to go see him."

The colonel pointed downstairs and said, "As it happens, a patrol is about to head out. Want to go to Lakeshire with them?"

"That'd be great," Jace said. "I'll go get my horse. Give me a moment."

Jace couldn't wait to leave this place, to get as far from those gnoll corpses as possible.

That afternoon, Jace returned to Lakeshire with the Stonewatch patrol. The moment he arrived, he made a beeline for the herbalist's clinic — but halfway there, he spotted a familiar figure at the dock.

"What… you… Grede?"

Hearing Jace's voice, Grede turned around and asked, "Where the hell have you been?"

Seeing him sitting there completely intact, Jace could barely contain his excitement — the last time he'd seen him, the dwarf had looked half-dead.

"The herbalist said you might be unconscious for a while longer."

"Who's he to look down on me?" Grede laughed heartily. "I got zapped, that's all — it's not like it was my first time. I'm a Gryphon Rider, lad — my own hammer is a lightning hammer blessed by the elders. What'd he take me for?"

"That's really great," Jace said. "Now you can pay me back my medical fees. Three silver. I've got no money left for food."

"Your sword-training fees aren't paid yet either, my friend," Grede said calmly. "And three silver wouldn't cover it — I chipped in some of my own. Did you think I was some pauper like you who can't hold onto money? We may have left Ironforge, but not a single one of the dwarves' finest qualities got left behind."

"Your bag's gone — where'd you even get money?"

"Who keeps all their money in their bag? You want a thief to lift it and send you straight back to poverty?"

Jace glanced down at Grede's boots. "Don't tell me you keep it in your shoes — don't they hurt?"

"Who said anything about shoes? That's a secret." He gave Jace a sidelong look and said, "Come to think of it, I heard from the herbalist that you were heading back to Stormwind. I thought you'd already gone."

"Got held up at Stonewatch for a couple days."

Jace sat down beside him. Grede noticed the bandages on his hand and asked, "What, didn't behave yourself and got your palm smacked by a Stonewatch mage?"

"How do you even come up with this stuff?" Jace shook his head. "The gnolls raided the fortress last night — walked right into it. Tell me that's not bad luck."

"Did you use that sword technique I taught you?" Grede said. "I'm guessing you didn't at all. Without me there, you definitely went wild with dark magic and turned the whole place into a disaster!"

"And how would I still be alive then? The soldiers there would've chopped my head off for an orc."

Grede stared into his eyes and licked his teeth. "Fair point."

Jace clutched the warlock's pouch to his chest and said, "Anyway, I survived. The commander at Stonewatch thinks I defended the fortress, and he went and told the king about it!"

"What?!" the dwarf roared. "The other day the king passed through Lakeshire on his way to Stonewatch — so it IS connected to the gnolls? What kind of attack was it that it got that young king rattled enough to come himself?"

"What'd you think?" Jace said with a smug laugh. "He promised to get me into the Wizard Sanctum when he gets back and make me a real mage."

Grede shouted, "A real mage? You're already walking the wrong path!"

"Keep your voice down!" Jace hissed.

"Fine, fine." Grede let out a long sigh. "Brilliant. Why don't I ever run into strokes of luck like that? I'd settle for the human king tossing me a few gold scraps!"

"Oh, you want to bring that up?" Jace said. "Who swore up and down we definitely wouldn't run into any high-difficulty jobs? Who told me if we went looking for that orc mage we'd split a dozen silver? Who took one lightning bolt and went down like a sack of potatoes and wouldn't get back up, and I had to drag you all the way back to Lakeshire from even farther east than the logging camp? So why do you think you didn't get lucky like me?"

Grede knew he was in the wrong and gave an awkward laugh. "Truly sorry about that. That orc was just… a bit too powerful, honestly."

Then, as if remembering something, his expression changed. "Right — how'd you actually beat him?"
