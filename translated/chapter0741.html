Chapter 741: Kallrez’s Confidence

Carrying out the ritual to forge the pact actually consumed a great deal of mana, much like the rituals for summoning a succubus or other minor demons.

Not to mention that Jace’s target this time was a powerful doom lord. After all, just summoning him from the Twisting Nether had required three people channeling the power of the Altar of Storms together to cast the spell.

So after the pact was forged, it wasn’t that Jace didn’t want to immediately start interrogating Kallrez — he was genuinely mana-depleted to the point of drowsiness.

There wasn’t anything urgent right now anyway, and he didn’t want to waste the mana potions Ursula had given him from his pack, so he went back to the orc camp on the high ground across from the altar bridge and rested for a while.

That evening, Luther Pickman and Spackle returned from outside. Spackle’s Mo’arg minion was even carrying a large green scorpion on its back.

This scorpion was even more massive than the ones Jace had seen with Vereesa on the Steppes. An arrow was still lodged in the gaps of its carapace. Luther Pickman walked ahead with his head held high, carrying that bow of his in hand, as if afraid others wouldn’t know he’d been the one to bag it.

Though it looked terrifying, and when dragged over it oozed a green pus that looked like demon blood, after the orc Kagel processed and roasted it, it at least looked presentable — like a giant grilled shrimp.

When the orc sprinkled on spices, Jace smelled the sweet roasting spices commonly used by people of Kingdom of Stormwind, with a hint of heat. Hunters in Elwynn Forest liked using this to roast wolf meat. This orc really knew his stuff.

Jace held a thick leg, cracked open the shell, and took a bite of the white meat. It had a texture like crab meat, but mixed in with the roasted meat aroma was a rather strong, bitter, fishy taste unique to insects. Not as good as it looked.

But being able to eat meat like this in an environment like this was already pretty good.

But as he ate, he realized the atmosphere of this “roast dinner” was a bit strange.

The people sitting around hadn’t expressed any thanks to the orc for roasting dinner. The others talked in low voices with each other, or said amusing things and laughed from time to time, but it was as if there was no communication whatsoever between them and this orc.

Jace could actually understand their mentality. Though he was a transmigrator, he’d also lived a full twenty years here, experiencing the opening of the Dark Portal, the orcish invasion…

An orc who had grown accustomed to life on Azeroth seemed like a signal — that the orcs were truly going to stay and live here forever. This strong fellow sitting here skillfully preparing food, like an ordinary traveler, probably made people more uncomfortable than when he fought Alliance soldiers on the battlefield.

Still, in the previous battle, Jace had been able to tell there was something special about this orc. He’d also become a member of this hastily assembled warlock group, so everyone could at least maintain a surface-level peace. But completely breaking through the barriers of war remained very difficult.

That evening, Jace sat to one side, using Morophil’s ever-burning fel eye for light, planning on paper what he wanted to ask Kallrez tomorrow.

He was a bit keen to learn that Cripple spell — the one that could slow an enemy’s movements, producing a slowing effect on casting, attacking, and movement alike.

Jace even felt that the Cripple spell could interfere with thought itself, making the speed at which the brain turned slow down. That was his genuine feeling. All in all, it was a considerably powerful control spell.

If he could learn the Cripple spell and combine it with Curse of Weakness, then for warriors who weren’t particularly afraid of Fear — such as berserk orc warriors, or undead — it could be called a devastating blow.

Against distant spellcasters — the kind you couldn’t reach with Fear or Asphyxiate, who kept bombarding from afar with ranged spells, and whom a felhound couldn’t reach in time — the Cripple spell could also serve as a temporary solution.

He wondered what other incantations this doom lord still knew.

Actually, regarding just how powerful Kallrez truly was, Jace still felt a bit uncertain, since there were no level or hit point numbers here for reference.

Especially when the other side mentioned his desire to compete with Kazzak for the so-called position of “King of the Ered’ruin.”

A doom lord fully summoned to Azeroth was terrifyingly powerful. Take Kazzak himself, who had come to Azeroth following Archimonde’s power, or Archimonde’s lieutenant Kaz’rogal — both were beings capable of single-handedly holding back the armies of the Alliance and the Horde.

“Have you heard of this demon Kallrez?” Jace asked Morophil beside him.

“No,” Morophil said. “But his name alone is enough to prove his standing. The Ered’ruin use ‘Kal’ or ‘Kaz’ as honorifics for those who rule.”

“How does he compare to Kazzak?” Jace asked.

Morophil replied, “Kazzak’s reputation resounds throughout the entire Twisting Nether. Even the ever-arrogant Brutallus cannot treat his power lightly. Kallrez… although he was nearly killed by the collapsing teleportation gate when he was summoned over, and it’s evident that his soul and body have been severely damaged, I imagine that even if he had been transported over intact, he still couldn’t compare to Kazzak.”

“Then what gives him the nerve to compete with Kazzak for King of the Ered’ruin?” Jace asked.

“One characteristic of a fool is always trying to prove he’s not that foolish,” Morophil said, leaning closer. “Think about it, master. If he hadn’t always been eager to show off that he’d seen through your tricks, he wouldn’t have been so easily bound by a pact with you, would he?”

“Master Jace Sesso, I have something I’d like to discuss with you privately.”

The moment Morophil finished speaking, a shout came from outside the tent. Judging by the voice, it was that Demisette Cloyce from the altar that afternoon.

Jace recalled Mor’zul mentioning she was a warlock from Lakeshire.

“Come in,” he said, tidying up what he’d been scribbling. He noticed Morophil glance at him warily. The female warlock lowered her head and ducked into the tent. Fortunately, the tent Mor’zul had arranged for him was a relatively large orc tent. If it had been one of the ones Luther stayed in, sitting three people would’ve been extremely cramped.

Demisette Cloyce was wearing a brown linen outer robe with the collar hanging open quite low.

Her long straight black hair reminded Jace of Jalane Ayrole, but she was much prettier than Jalane and didn’t have that look of slight contempt she wore around everyone. She even had something of an Eastern look about her.

Actually, from the moment he’d seen her beside Kallrez during the day, Jace had felt he had some impression of this name.

In the game, she seemed to be one of the people in the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb, but for some reason she still hadn’t gotten to know Zardeth or the others in the basement until now.

Could she have been led to the basement by Gakin?

Because Gakin had died early, she’d never come into contact with that group from the Slaughtered Lamb?

Jace didn’t think too much about it, since because of him, many parts of the entire world were already completely different now. After ducking in, she glanced at the stone slab beside her. Jace nodded toward it, gesturing for her to sit.

“I know a veteran who fought at Nethergarde Keep, Master Sesso,” Demisette said as she sat.

Jace frowned. “Just call me Jace, or Sesso, or go ahead and use my full name. I don’t much care for people calling me ‘master,’ Lady Cloyce.”
