Chapter 743: Pronouncing Like a Demon

Demonstrate flipped open the tent flap and strode out quickly. Watching her retreating figure outside, Jace realized that as the magic he mastered continued to grow, this could also become a way to make money — to subsidize the costs of renovating the mage tower.

Of course, he couldn't just teach any magic, and he'd need to be selective about who he taught. In any case, this was something that required careful consideration.

He could even go back and charge those Dark Iron dwarves for lessons, then make some small modifications of his own, just like them... mount a proper counterattack.

But that would mean he'd need to take the initiative to go out and hunt for incantations, the same way Galders did.

From his conversations with Galders, Jace could sense that most of his incantations had been learned from other warlocks, especially warlocks from Ironforge. This meant most of the incantations he'd gathered already had some degree of circulation on Azeroth.

If Jace wanted to seize the initiative across all of Azeroth, he'd have to find a way to obtain powerful magic known only to orcs and no one else on Azeroth — things like Hand of Gul'dan or the Dark Shield.

The next morning, Jace came to the Storm Altar. The one keeping watch here today was Karol Shadowfist, who was currently sitting on the toe of a statue.

From the moment Jace appeared within his field of vision, Kartzel had been staring at him.

Coming before this towering demon lord, Kartzel said in a low voice, "What strange idea do you have today, you bastard? I'll crush you to death sooner or later, so you stop appearing before my eyes every day to disturb my mood."

"Fine," Jace said offhandedly. "When I get tired of living someday I'll amend the terms of our contract and change my condition to suicide. That way, even if Kazzak really does die, the contract between us won't end."

Kartzel said contemptuously, "My desire to kill you isn't nearly enough to establish a contract that binds me. Do you think your worth is that significant to an Eredeth lord?"

Jace said, "That's a good thing. At least it shows you don't want to kill me that badly."

Morophil, who had been tilting her head examining the shadow energy within the Endless Dark Blade, couldn't help but let out a laugh at Jace's words.

Kartzel's sharp teeth ground together, but in the end he said nothing.

"I've come to learn from you today, Lord Kartzel," Jace said in a grave tone. "I think this should be an important first conversation between master and servant. Why don't you start by telling me what magic you know."

"The magic I command is beyond counting," the doom lord said proudly. "But nine-tenths of it you've never even heard of, and most of the rest you as a mortal couldn't possibly learn. What would be the use of telling you? In fact, many 'magic' are nothing more than ordinary abilities to us Eredeth, but you mortals make a great fuss over them..."

"Like flying." The doom lord tried to flap his enormous brown fleshy wings, like a giant umbrella about to unfurl — but this motion was firmly locked down by the binding circle, causing the demon lord's body to shudder and emit an unhappy growl.

Seeing this, the smile on Morophil's face changed somewhat. Jace could tell she wasn't indifferent to the matter of having her wing torn off.

Jace said, "That I genuinely can't do. How about we start with the incantation you cast on me."

Kartzel muttered the name in the Eredeth language, then said, "This incantation, if spoken in Demonic, would be Cripple. Are you certain you want to learn this incantation? I don't believe your little meat-ring mouth can perfectly chant our ancient incantations."

"Humans don't rely on this 'meat ring' to vocalize, demon," Jace said. "But there's no way to know without trying, since I'm already learning the Eredeth language now, aren't I?"

"It wouldn't hurt to teach you," Kartzel muttered, trying to raise his arm. The circle flared again and yanked him hard. This motion seemed a bit too large and caused quite a considerable reaction. The area around the Storm Altar became somewhat unsettled again, and the warlocks resting in the camp in the distance all looked over this way.

Jace raised his arm high to signal that everything was fine. Kartzel growled low, "Let me go. In this state I can't do anything at all, let alone teach you how to cast spells!"

"You just need to tell me the incantation," Jace said. "I've already witnessed the casting gestures for this spell with my own eyes."

Kartzel's sharp teeth ground together for a while, then he recited resentfully: "Yin... ka... ke... wei... la... li... ka... jia... de!"

When this doom lord switched to speaking in the Eredeth language, the sensation of vocalization indeed became completely different. Recording it in full was simply impossible. Right now, Jace could only barely approximate the pronunciation in Common or other languages in his mind.

No wonder players in the game couldn't learn this skill — only doomguards could cast it. So this was where the difficulty lay.

Jace cleared his throat. "Yin..."

The moment he produced this sound, he immediately felt it was far too thin by comparison. Did you truly need a demon's throat to pronounce the incantation?

The orc beside him had been listening the whole time. Jace looked at him and asked, "Can you pronounce this incantation, relying on your race's voice?"

"Make me pronounce this kind of demonic incantation... impossible," the orc said. "This is the voice of fel magic, Master Sesso. If we wanted to imitate it, we'd probably need to rely on Curse of Tongues."

"Do you know Curse of Tongues?" Jace asked.

Kartzel immediately burst out laughing upon hearing this.

"That is a kind of curse," the orc said. "A person afflicted with Curse of Tongues will speak gibberish. Though what comes out is all demonic language, they themselves can't control it. Relying on this to imitate demonic vocalization would be one thing, but casting spells and chanting incantations is simply impossible."

"Cast it on me and let's give it a try," Jace said in orcish. "Karol Shadowfist."

"Make me cast Curse of Tongues on you?" the orc said with great surprise, also speaking in his native tongue. "Yes," Jace said.

The orc nodded, drew the short staff at his waist, and recited a brief incantation at Jace. Jace immediately felt as though his entire head and neck had been sealed by an intense burning sensation.

He wanted to ask whether the incantation had taken effect, but what came out was nothing but a string of incoherent demonic gibberish, which sent Kartzel into a fit of laughter.

Though it was a string of demonic ravings he himself couldn't understand, Jace had to admit that the orc was right — his voice had indeed become much closer to an Eredeth's, thanks to this incantation. At least imitation had become a lot easier.

Jace frowned and calmed himself for a while. He could feel where the power of this curse lay.

He wondered if he could resist the suppression of this incantation? After all, the caster of this incantation was merely an ordinary orc warlock.

He glanced at the orc Karol, opened his lips, and vocalized with difficulty.

At first it was an uncontrollable string of demonic language, but afterward, he felt as though he was finding the feel of it. "Yin-ka... yinka..."

Hearing the opening of the Cripple incantation, Kartzel immediately stopped laughing.
