Chapter 745: Spells and Evil Power

After a night's sleep, his throat still ached faintly, but Jace headed straight for the Storm Altar.

This time Kallez said nothing at all — just stared at him. Jace noticed the other warlocks had gathered too, including the orc Karol, Martha Stein from Dalaran, Albert Fizban from Dun Morogh, and "Shortstaff" Gladys, who'd been injured before and still didn't look fully recovered. They'd all come to the altar platform and were standing guard not far off.

Jace figured they'd seen him coming to the Storm Altar every day to interrogate a demon and wanted to listen in themselves.

So why hadn't these people asked their own questions before? Probably either Kallez couldn't be bothered to talk to them, or they simply didn't have the guts to ask.

Standing before the Doom Lord, Jace said, "I've got a new question today. About soul shards."

"Go on," Kallez said.

"How do you convert soul shards into stones that can restore power?" Jace asked. "Do you know the spell for that?"

"You mean those fel-powered stones you can swallow to replenish your strength, Lord Kallez!" the imp couldn't help shrieking as it poked its head out of the bag. "You must know how to make those! You absolutely must!"

The Doom Lord acted as though he hadn't heard the imp at all. He just let out a low "hm" and said, "The Eredar know the incantation for converting these soul shards, Jace Sesso. But I do not."

"I've seen Gul'dan convert these stones," the orc said. "The orcs and demons in the Shadow Council call them Healthstones. They say swallowing those fel-fire-burning stones can heal wounds… I'm deeply skeptical."

"So you do know how!" the imp said immediately. "Orc, you'll help us! Wonderful!"

"I can't," the orc said. "Sorry to disappoint you, yellowish little imp."

"Aren't you a member of the Shadow Council, Karol?" Gladys asked, leaning on his staff.

"I am a member of the Shadow Council," the orc said. "But the Shadow Council isn't your Kirin Tor's Council of Six. There are many people in it, and they have high and low ranks. Gul'dan is the head of the council. People like Taron and Vorpil surround him — those core members naturally get to learn Gul'dan's secrets. I'm just the outermost member of the Shadow Council. A humble servant."

"I feel like the dark magic you know is no less than any of those Shadow Council orc warlocks, Karol," Martha asked. "Are you really just an outer member?"

"After leaving the Shadow Council, I used some… unsavory methods to get a few incantations from my old master, Stein," the orc said with a grin.

"What methods?" Gladys asked.

"Don't ask, 'Shortstaff,'" Albert said. "Do you really want to know what an orc's 'unsavory' methods are?" Gladys cleared his throat and said nothing more.

Jace looked at the Doom Lord with suspicion and said, "Since you're a Legion commander, and even the orcs who serve demons in the Shadow Council know how to convert Healthstones, you don't?"

Kallez said with contempt, "Because I can convert them directly with my own power. I don't need to use the Eredar's language to communicate with fel fire and shadow, to get the energies of the Void to do my bidding. I am the source of power itself, you disrespectful little creature!"

"Don't talk a big game. Show me," Jace said, pulling out a soul shard.

"Release me from this binding circle!" Kallez growled low. "I can't do anything like this!" Jace glanced around at the others. The warlocks looked over too, and everyone exchanged glances.

Sazeno said anxiously, "Let him out, master. You already know his true name. If he doesn't behave, you can shout his true name and he'll be even weaker and more helpless than he is now! Remember what happened to that female Sayad back in Kalimor!"

Morophil gave the imp a cold look and said nothing.

The other warlocks' imps were all urging their masters to release Kallez too. It seemed these little guys really wanted to get their hands on those stones.

"Wait for Mosul to come back," Jace said, pulling a piece of paper inscribed with Eredun characters from his bag. "For now, translate the words on this for me, Kallez."

"I will not do work as lowly as a servant's!" Kallez roared, and the fel breath he blasted out nearly blew the paper from Jace's hand.

Jace shook the paper. "I will absolutely not release you from the binding circle until you finish translating what's on this paper. What do you think your situation is right now, Kallez?!"

Sazeno burst out laughing. Jace looked at it and said, "You stay here. Write down whatever Kallez translates. You don't think that hand of his can hold a pen, do you, Sazeno."

The imp's laughter seamlessly turned into wailing. "I don't want to stay next to this monster… master!"

"That's an order," Jace said, looking at Kallez. "Translate it properly. That's also an order."

Over the next two days, besides gathering these Eredun vocabulary entries, Jace also attempted to counter the Language Curse's suppression with the help of the orc Karol. The other warlocks tried too. Except for "Shortstaff" Gladys, the rest could barely see any hope of resisting it — and of course, Gladys could only manage to force out a little of what he wanted to say.

But that was already quite impressive. After all, Jace didn't know if he was resisting the Language Curse with the help of the Chain of Will. Gladys was purely relying on his own will to fight against the spell.

Still, he didn't dare take off the chain around his neck and try again, because the warlocks here were watching his every move. If he took the chain off and tried again, and it didn't go as smoothly as before, these warlocks would definitely start paying attention to that seemingly ordinary-looking chain.

Jace hadn't forgotten Alexstrasza's words: don't let people pay too much attention to this iron chain. Having it also meant protecting it well.

In the process of practicing resistance to the Language Curse, Jace naturally learned the spell itself. It wasn't a particularly difficult incantation. According to the orc, the spell was probably invented by the Eredar as a prank, and only later did they realize that this kind of spell, which forced people to speak gibberish, could also hinder enemies from casting incantations — so they gradually improved it into its current form.

This couldn't help but make Jace wonder: could he, like the Eredar, invent spells too?

He'd thought about it before, but the Eredar had been demons for tens of thousands of years. This highly civilized race, renowned for wisdom and knowledge even before their fall, had probably spent tens of thousands of years studying the manipulation of fel and shadow down to the last detail.

Him trying to reinvent the wheel in Azeroth — any weird spell he came up with would most likely be something the Eredar had already tried countless times.

However, when he shared this thought with Morophil in the evening, her reaction surprised him quite a bit. "Hasn't master already 'invented' a spell?"

"What?" Jace said, astonished. "I don't know I've invented any spell."

"Remember Shadowstep?" Morophil reminded him. "Of course, that might not be quite what you imagine invention to be." Jace said, "Could you be a little clearer?"

"It was you yourself using the demon tongue to call upon the aid of shadow power, master. You could teach this method to others," the succubus explained, leaning to one side. "Of course, casters who don't have your kind of shadow affinity probably would hardly be able to replicate your method. But there are countless spells that only one person knows and that no one else can learn at all."

Jace said, "But there are also many different spells invented by various demons or mortal warlocks that end up achieving more or less the same effect."

"That's right," Morophil said. "Take the simplest Shadow Bolt, for example. The incantations Nathrezim and Eredun use when casting it are completely different."

"I more or less understand," Jace nodded.
