Chapter 75: Jace's Legend

"How I beat him." Jace lowered his voice and whispered in Grede's ear. "As for how I beat him — naturally, I ruthlessly whipped that arrogant shaman with the dark magic he despised most. Fear first, then Shadowflame, Corruption all at once. In the end he died miserably in pain and terror, confessing every wrong he'd done in his life with his dying breath."

"That's a bit much, even for an orc," Grede said with a frown. "Still… I suppose dark magic does work. Otherwise why would so many people in this world willingly sink into depravity chasing after it?"

Jace shot him a subtle glance. Grede noticed his look and immediately said, "You're lying to me right here, aren't you? An orc confessing his wrongs to you — does he even speak Common? You're making up nonsense that doesn't even make sense!"

"Hah, you're wrong about that," Jace said. "He really did speak Common."

"What?" Grede said. "What'd you say to him? Orcs can't communicate with humans — aren't they all madmen who eat human flesh and don't spit out the bones?"

"Eating human flesh doesn't stop them from speaking the human tongue," Jace said. "Remember that class I mentioned to you? Before we set out."

"I remember."

"An Orcish language class — that's what I took."

After Jace finished, he recited a few of the most basic runes in Orcish. The dwarf listened and only shook his head. "That's genuinely unbearable to listen to!"

"Never mind whether it's bearable — it worked," Jace said. "That orc was actually a shaman who'd gotten separated from his tribe. I told him where his tribe was, and he just left."

"And he just believed you?" the dwarf asked skeptically.

Jace said, "I'd heard plenty about orc tribes back in Lordaeron. Of course I had to give him some credible evidence — otherwise he'd have cracked my skull open."

The dwarf nodded. "Though given how bright orcs are, being swindled by a sly fox like you is only normal."

"I thought I was the most courageous human you'd ever met?" Jace said. "How'd I turn into a sly fox?"

Grede said, "A swindler can be brave too — these things aren't mutually exclusive. Right, tell me about what happened at Stonewatch. I want to see how this Alliance hero faced down a gnoll army that fought to the death without cheating with dark magic."

Jace gave him a rough account of what had happened at Stonewatch, with special emphasis on how that apprentice had looked down on him for a while and then suddenly gotten a rope around his neck and dragged off the wall by gnolls.

As for the fighting afterward, he left out the imp and the Shadow power, vividly crafting an image of a lone hero holding the top of a stone tower against an army. When he finished, Grede stared with his one eye and said, "I'd be an idiot to believe you."

Jace laughed. "Well, the Stonewatch soldiers can vouch for me. They even escorted me back to Lakeshire. You have no idea how many people I saved."

Grede gave Jace a sidelong look for a while, then could only slowly nod. "I'll believe you for now."

Jace stretched his arms out and lay on his back, looking up at the tree branches stretching over from the side, the blue sky and white clouds. "Ah, it's good to be alive. Don't drag me out on any more jobs after this, Grede. I don't want to die. I'm starting to feel like my life's getting more and more valuable."

Grede said with a contemptuous snort, "When I land a job worth a whole gold, don't come running after me with your tail wagging."

"A whole gold?" Jace gave a dry laugh. "What next, they'll have you solo Rend Blackhand in Blackrock Spire?"

"Hold on!" Grede grabbed Jace by the forearm. "You said you didn't use dark magic, huh? Then why do these exposed wounds look like burns from Shadowflame?"

Jace asked in confusion, "How do you know these are Shadowflame burns?"

"Lad," Grede said. "The wounded soldiers brought back, burned by flames the orcs animated with Shadow — they all had burns like this. You want to fool me? You're not getting away with it."

"I'm fooling you?" Jace said. "Besides, I can't exactly use it on myself — you think I burned my own hand?"

"Then how do you explain this? The gnolls." Jace said, "The gnolls have Shadow warlocks too. They joined in the night raid."

"Fine." Grede tossed Jace's hand aside. "You pass. This time."

Jace hissed in pain, touching the bandages on his hand. "Still hasn't healed yet," he muttered.

Grede clicked his tongue. "Shame. I was planning to keep training you with the sword tonight — but since you haven't recovered, forget it."

Jace said, "I could still train. But… neither of us has a sword anymore."

Grede's eyes went wide. "You didn't bring back that Stromgarde sword?"

"I could barely carry you — how was I supposed to carry anything else?" Jace shot back.

"What a waste." Grede lay down next to Jace. "Knew it — shouldn't have come to Redridge."

That evening, Jace noticed that the number of soldiers in Lakeshire had grown a lot. They must have followed the king here from Stormwind.

Thinking about it, he'd actually met Varian Wrynn that morning — and spoken to him face to face. It really did feel unreal.

It had nothing to do with him being a king or a noble. It was just because that was Varian Wrynn, in the flesh.

A living, breathing World of Warcraft hero. Even though he wasn't even twenty yet, and compared to the Varian of later years he differed quite a bit in both bearing and appearance… but back when he'd played the game, Jace hadn't had any particular feelings about the character Varian Wrynn either.

Because in the game, he'd actually played the Horde side quite a bit. As the leader of the Alliance — the opposing faction — Varian had just shown up here and there in cutscenes and quest storylines, made a cameo, done a bit part. At best he was more than a familiar face. Not to mention that in the early days, every time he showed up he'd spend half the time cursing out the Horde.

But now that he'd actually spoken to him in the game — er, in person — he really did have this indescribable sense of familiarity, as if he'd known him all along. Anyway, compared to the other people he'd met here, it was this weird feeling of a fictional character and a real person overlapping and blurring together.

That night, Jace and Grede were drinking at the tavern. A lot of people in the tavern were talking about the gnoll attack on Stonewatch. Many said the Second Gnoll War was coming soon.

Quite a few also felt that the Orc Wars had only just shown signs of winding down, and now the kingdom was immediately going to have to deal with another enemy — a bit too unlucky.

Jace also sensed another reason the king had come all the way to Stonewatch to reward the soldiers. If this had been done in Stormwind, the gnoll attack would have been blown up into a huge uproar. Then it wouldn't be Lakeshire panicking — the whole kingdom would be thrown into turmoil.

Late that night, he sorted through his pack. The three Shadow Protection Potions he'd brought were down to one. One had been thrown by Grede during the fight with the orc shaman, one had been drunk at Stonewatch, and this last one had barely made it back from the lake shore — he'd thought he'd lost it.

Jace had now joined the Slaughtered Lamb crew, so he had a new avenue for learning Shadow magic spells. On top of that, the biggest threat in the entire Stormwind region was actually a bunch of physical brutes — whether gnolls, murlocs, or wild beasts.

Even among those, the ones who could cast spells were few and far between. Most of them relied on biting and slashing.

Right now, as Jace saw it, what he needed most wasn't Shadow Protection Potions — it was Healing Potions and Mana Potions. And lots of them.

While he'd been searching for the Shadow Protection Potion, he'd come across recipes for all kinds of Healing Potions and Mana Potions — some stronger, some weaker, with wildly varying ingredients. He hadn't deliberately memorized them. If he wanted to make them, he'd have to go back and look them up again.

But… in the game, health and mana were just numbers. How would they be represented here?

He more or less understood the effects of a Mana Potion by now. These past few days, Jace had experienced the feeling of mana exhaustion — listless, weak all over, unable to concentrate, let alone connect to the ley lines, channel Shadow, or cast spells.

So a Mana Potion probably filled that feeling back up, reinvigorated you, cleared your head again. In game terms, that was mana.

Right now Jace's "mana pool" was way too small. Two or three Fears and he was spent. How was he supposed to guarantee his safety and his partner's?

If he went into every fight from now on carrying a dozen Mana Potions, that orc shaman would definitely be crowd-controlled to death — Fear, stab, Fear, stab. Forget about him, even Orgrim Doomhammer couldn't escape. Of course, it wouldn't be that simple. Either the Mana Potions would be really weak, or cost a fortune, or stop working after a few bottles, or drinking too many would give you a racing heart and kill you like overdosing on coffee. That's what he'd guess.

Otherwise, wouldn't it mean that as long as you learned one crowd-control spell, brought a cleaver and a whole burlap sack of Mana Potions, you could run rampant across the seven kingdoms?

And what about Healing Potions?

Jace poked the bandage on his hand with a finger. A sharp, burning pain flared up.

If I drink a Healing Potion, will this pain go away? However he thought about it, that seemed a bit far-fetched.

But he remembered that elf he'd met in Duskwood. He'd had a vial of pink liquid — Grede had said it was a Revival Potion.

In the game, a Revival Potion was a high-level Healing Potion. That elf had claimed that if you drank a Revival Potion, even a wound from a trap could be repaired in a short time. Was that really true?

Still, that elf wouldn't be gambling with his own life. And judging by Grede's reaction, he hadn't raised any objections either.

"Grede, Grede, are you asleep?" Jace called out in a low voice.

The snoring from behind was deafening.

Guess he wouldn't know the real answer unless he drank one himself.
