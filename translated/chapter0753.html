Chapter 753: News of the Burning Legion

Vereesa had come to Stormwind?

Hearing Grede say this, Jace lost all patience to finish his remaining ale.

He hurried all the way to the Mage Quarter, came to the residence of the elven mages, and explained the situation to the guard at the gate. The guard went inside, and not long after, Vereesa pushed the door open and rushed out.

The sky was overcast. She was only wearing a single grass-green garment, not the special cloak Sylvanas had bestowed on her the day she was named Ranger-Lord. She looked no different from when they'd first met in Dalaran.

"How many days have you been waiting?" Jace asked.

"I got here the day before yesterday," Vereesa said. "I received your letter… and rushed over immediately, Jace. Fortunately there were dragonhawk riders heading to Nethergarde Keep, and they gave me a ride here. You summoned that demon, didn't you? Where is he? Inside Stormwind?"

"He… can't appear in Stormwind, Wen," Jace said, jerking a thumb over his shoulder. "Shall we talk at the Blue Heron?"

"All right," Vereesa said.

With that, she hurried to the front.

The Wizard Sanctum's new term had already started, and the Blue Heron had gained another batch of young new faces.

The refugees who'd left the kingdom due to the war were gradually returning. The darkness of Duskwood was driving more and more villagers living in Nightvale, Raven Hill, or other small settlements to Goldshire and Stormwind, so the pool of talented potential mages seeking to enter the Wizard Sanctum was growing.

Vereesa's appearance caused a small stir at the Blue Heron. Clearly, some of the new apprentices were seeing an elf for the first time.

But the side-glances at this other race, or the quiet whispering, the current Ranger-Lord no longer cared about at all. She wasn't wearing a cloak, wasn't wearing any accessory that could conceal her face. She just sat candidly by the window, brushing up her silver hair as she looked across the table at her lover.

Jace ordered two mugs of goat milk and a plate of salted crackers from the barmaid, smiled, and said, "Are you here to see me, or are you itching to know what that demon told me?"

"Can't I care about both at the same time?" Vereesa said. "I really want to see you, and that demon's news gave me a legitimate reason to come see you. Of course, the news itself is important enough…"

She took a deep breath, then lowered her head. "Go on. I've prepared myself well enough."

"The Sons of Lothar are still alive. the Expedition is still resisting in Draenor," Jace said. "But the situation…" Vereesa gripped the table, bit her lip, and avoided Jace's eyes.

"And her?"

"The Burning Legion's objective is—"

"And her?!" She looked up.

"…to break the Expedition, Vereesa," Jace forced himself to finish. "She's the leader of the Expedition. The Sons of Lothar are still resisting the Burning Legion, and there's no sign of them collapsing. Isn't that enough to say it all? At the very least, I can confirm that the catastrophe in Draenor didn't kill Alleria Windrunner and Marshal Turalyon. But the Expedition's situation isn't good right now, Wen. Demon warlords are invading that world. So as for what the situation is at this very moment, I definitely can't make any definitive claims."

Vereesa nodded, trying her best to stay calm. "She's the finest Farstrider, and the most formidable ranger in all of Quel'Thalas. Even Sylvanas can't compare to her. Demons absolutely cannot kill her, it's that simple. As long as Draenor hasn't been smashed to dust, she has a way to survive."

Jace picked up a cracker and took a bite. "That's exactly what I meant."

"Damn it." Vereesa held her forehead. "I was almost coming to terms with, accepting her… What do I do now, Jace? I want to know what I can do."

"Hint your sister first?" Jace tried.

"She…" Vereesa blinked, staring at a crack in the table. "I don't know how to tell her. That demons from the Burning Legion revealed this? That you summoned a Burning Legion demon?"

"Not just any demon. A Doom Lord," Jace confessed. "Ordinary demons wouldn't know this. I and a group of warlocks tried summoning an Eredun lord. Even he didn't know the details, never mind that Doomguard they summoned before. That fellow couldn't even get his own language straight."

Vereesa was slightly stunned, then furrowed her brows. "Then there's even less we can say. What else?"

"What else?" Jace said. "Getting to Draenor can only be done through the Dark Portal, so for the time being—"

"There's no other way besides praying," Vereesa finished. Jace nodded.

The elf caught her breath gently, steadied her emotions, and smiled. "No matter how you look at it, this is still the best outcome I could have imagined, Jace. I shouldn't feel sad."

As she said this, her expression suddenly turned serious.

"About that Doom Lord — since you're still alive, and Stormwind looks perfectly normal, I'll assume you have a way to control him."

"For now, everything's normal," Jace said.

"If it becomes 'not normal'—" Vereesa leaned in and warned, "you must not hide it from me."

"Of course," Jace said. "But I've already completed the enslavement of that demon. I reckon as long as I don't suddenly die, there won't be a problem."

"Feels like I'm falling into darkness right along with you," Vereesa said, shaking her head. "If Alleria knew what the two of us have been doing, I wonder what her reaction would be."

"Sounds like you regret knowing me?" Jace asked in a joking tone.

"Knowing someone who's mixed up with demons, maybe," Vereesa said, folding her arms on the table. Then she stared into his eyes and said softly, "Knowing a dark mage who fought Teron Gorefiend to the death? Not one bit."

"But all of that together is the complete you, Jace Sesso."

The elf leaned across the table, and right in the middle of the bustling inn, the two of them kissed for a while, completely oblivious to everything around them. When they parted, Vereesa smiled, took a deep breath, and let her emotions settle gradually.

After the two of them ate some crackers, she asked after a while, "About that thing you're looking for — any further news?" Jace glanced at the young mages chatting away at the next table. "Too many people here. Not a good place to discuss this."

"Then come back to my quarters first?" Vereesa asked.

"I don't have the Wizard Sanctum's ring right now. They might not let me into such a sensitive area," Jace reminded her.

Vereesa said, "It's fine. Don't forget what the Sun King said, Jace — you and Grede will always be welcome among the Quel'dorei."

Jace drained the milk from his mug in one gulp. "Then we'll talk when we get there."
