## Chapter 757: Obsidian Fragments

"Miss Morophil," Galders said, somewhat displeased. "You could have just walked right in. There's no need to hide your presence among us. This makes me very uneasy."

"I'll be more careful next time," Morophil said.

Jace glanced at Senrola inside and said, "I'm here because I want the method for summoning a Voidwalker. At the same time, I obtained a rather useful spell from someone else during this period… a summoning circle. I don't suppose you've heard of it?"

"The incantation for summoning a Voidwalker can certainly be given to you. Actually, you should have taken it last time, but you seemed completely uninterested in summoning that demon," Galders said, sitting across from him. "What exactly does this summoning circle refer to? A circle that can strengthen summoned creatures?"

"No," Jace said. "It's a circle that can summon people."

"You mean a summoning circle that requires one person as the primary caster and two people to channel void magic?" Galders said in surprise. "Are you sure it's a real summoning circle? I know a group of demon scholars seem to have learned this magic from the orcish warlock council. How did you get hold of it?"

Jace glanced at the books on the storage rack, along with the various casting materials. "Confidential."

"Of course," Galders said, his tone relaxing somewhat. "If everyone in the Dagger Society could learn to use this summoning circle, then we could return to the Slaughtered Lamb from anywhere in the world at any time… as long as there were three people in the basement, that would do. That would greatly speed up our efficiency in gathering spells and searching for artifacts."

"That's what I thought too," Jace said, looking at him. "As the most gifted person among us in shadow casting, Galders, you'd definitely be the fastest to learn. Given the importance of this magic, I think the sooner you learn it, the better."

"You're too modest, Jace," Galders said. "I imagine you probably already learned it before coming to tell me, right?"

Jace said, "At least this time it's not. I brought it over before I even had a chance to look at it properly. Actually, I'm curious, Galders. Your ability to use shadow magic lately has been advancing by leaps and bounds, and yet I seem to have hit a bottleneck… Everyone and I are very curious about how you've managed it. Did the dwarves in Ironforge tell you some special way of using shadow magic? Like tricks for manipulating emotions."

He paused, watching Galders' reaction. The other party seemed a bit pleased that Jace had noticed his tremendous progress.

Jace continued, "Remember the Dagger Society's mission you told me? Sharing. So don't try to get past me on this. I know there are quite a few warlocks in other places who are picking up demonic knowledge very quickly. It's been ten years since the Dark Portal. If we keep advancing at this slow pace… we might not be able to keep up with the progress of warlocks elsewhere in the world. Don't forget, the warlocks of Quel'Thalas and Shadowforge City were studying dark magic who knows how long before the Dark Portal, never mind Dalaran."

"I didn't expect you to be this eager to pursue progress," Galders said.

"Then, let me answer your question this time." He took an earring from a small black box on the shelf, hung with a strange gem fragment that was purple with a tinge of dark yellow.

"This is a type of obsidian from Silithus, Jace. It possesses extraordinary shadow power," he said with a hint of pride. "Just by touching it, you can feel the powerful force contained within. These obsidian fragments are extremely hard to come by. I only managed to get it through layer after layer of obstacles from a warlock I'm closely connected to, who bought it from an expert at the Ironforge Explorers' League. You know there's a group of orc and ogre warlocks who sailed across the ocean and fled to Kalimdor? It's said they heard the legends of Silithus and went searching for this obsidian to strengthen their dark incantations. It's said the warlocks of Shadowforge City already know this method."

With that, Galders handed this obsidian fragment to Jace. Jace took it in his hand and felt a ringing in his ears.

He felt the pads of his fingers holding this fragment grow hot, and the Chain of Will around his neck grew hotter and hotter too.

Of course, this wasn't a physical sensation. After a strong sense of wariness surged up, he became extremely sensitive to every change. Because the "obsidian" of Silithus had nothing to do with volcanoes and lava.

It was a dark stone used by the servants of the Old Gods to communicate. The Ahn'Qiraj temples that sealed the Old Gods were full of these sinister things. The silithid also lived alongside these stones day and night.

After the Twilight's Hammer cultists entered Silithus, they used those stones to summon fallen elements that obeyed the Old Gods, and even more terrifying things…

Was the fragment in his hand a part of these fallen stones?

Jace didn't know. It could be real, it could be fake. Perhaps some reckless adventurer really had brought this thing from Kalimdor to the Eastern Kingdoms. Or perhaps the servants of the Old Gods had already begun spreading their influence here through the hands of these people.

"I remember we talked about this before, Galders. What we seek is the power of demons, not the worship of some ancient fiend."

"What do you mean?" Galders asked.

"If this really is Silithus obsidian, that means you're not far from those crazed cultists," Jace asked, gripping this fragment. "Do you want to dedicate your mind and soul to those abominations?!"

Galders frowned. "How do you know?"

Jace said, "These stones are connected with the insects in Silithus. I just got back from Kalimdor. Even the night elves who've lived for ten thousand years don't dare have anything to do with these things, because they know that once you're drawn in, very quickly the whole person becomes a plaything for those ancient evils. Galders, do you want to enslave the shadows, or be enslaved by them?"

"I absolutely will not be enslaved by shadows," Galders said coldly. "But I can't just because you say so, I—"

"Tell me, how do you use the power of this fragment?" Jace cut him off.

Galders hesitated for a moment. "There's an incantation. By reciting it, you can strengthen…"

"Your connection with this thing," Jace finished.

"Yes," Galders admitted somewhat reluctantly. Jace sighed. "Tell me that incantation."

Galders took a piece of paper from his robe and recited, "Kish, gagh, uk, gal, iz, aghso…"

"Do you know what language this is?" Jace asked.

Galders said hesitantly, "Probably some kind of ancient language. Ancient Kalimdor tongue, or a variant of ancient elven, something like that. They didn't explain it to me very clearly."

"This is Shath'yar, 'Blackclaw' Galders!" Sazeno poked its head out from the demon bag beside Morophil. "That sentence sounds very much like an incantation those void servants use to call upon power!"

"You know a lot, imp," Galders said.

"Of course!" Sazeno said. "If you don't believe me, you can go ask your Voidwalker servant. That monster dragged here from the void plane by you guys definitely knows what this means!"

Galders swallowed. "I'll go to the basement now."

"I'll come with you," Jace said.

The two of them rushed to the Mage Quarter. By now it had reached evening. Along the way, Galders looked grave the whole time, clutching that box in his hand the whole way. Jace followed behind him, calculating how to deal with this problem.

If this really was an obsidian fragment connected to Silithus, then Galders was probably not far from becoming a Twilight cultist.

If it continued, he would only turn the entire Dagger Society into an Old God worship organization. Jace could either eliminate these people, or stay far, far away from them…

Eliminating Galders, or even the entire Dagger Society, was something he couldn't bring himself to do.

Right now, Galders just looked like he knew nothing about this thing. But after sensing its terrifying nature, he'd immediately become suspicious, rather than doubting that Jace might be jealous of his progress in shadow manipulation.

Jace felt he wasn't beyond saving.

Jace himself had been able to resist the corruption of the shadows up to now, and Galders was also someone quite gifted in shadow magic. Before coming into contact with this fragment, he'd had enough calmness and self-control too.

He should at least give Galders one chance. One chance to voluntarily step away from the Twilight — as long as Galders could realize sooner what he was doing.

To take the most conservative view, in the game, ten years later, the warlocks in the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb had nothing to do with the Twilight's Hammer either.

Back in the basement, Galdes brought Jace to the Voidwalker.

The shadow this thing hid in was nearly pitch black in the room. He could only barely see the two flickering ghost flames, serving as a reference for the Voidwalker's location.

"Kulmos," Galders called its name.

"Master, I await your orders…" The Voidwalker echoed with a reverberating voice.

"I'm going to recite a passage," Galders said to that patch of darkness. "You tell me… what language that is. If you can understand it, translate it into the demon tongue for me."

"Understood, master," the Voidwalker responded. Galders took out the slip of paper and recited the passage again.

After hearing it, the Voidwalker's shadow spread all over, emitting a slightly piercing hollow resonance. Finally it said, "This is the language of the void plane… Shath'yar…"

Galders frowned, and the hand gripping the slip of paper trembled slightly.

The Voidwalker continued, "This passage means: witness my will, master of the world… answer."

"I'm sorry, Jace," Galders said, looking at this small box. "I almost… Now we need to destroy this thing."
