Chapter 76: The Mage Academy’s Goal

By the time they made it back to Stormwind, it was already the middle of the next night. Jace and Grede had followed the patrol back to Eastvale, then pushed on without rest the whole way — they didn’t even stop in Goldshire, because if they’d paused any longer, they’d have been late for work the next day.

That evening Jace had the imp keep copying books, and come morning he’d overslept again.

He didn’t even bother with a bath, just rushed straight into the Mage Quarter. The moment he threw open the door to Malin’s study, he found Master Malin and his daughter Amy Malin already inside.

“Uh — sorry.” Jace caught his breath. “Master Malin, I’m late.”

“I’ve been covering for the past two days,” Amy said, turning around with a half-joking look. “Come to think of it, shouldn’t you be paying me wages?”

“I’m really sorry, Amy. Really sorry, Master Malin — I genuinely ran into trouble,” Jace said.

Amy laughed. “You’re some assistant… you haven’t even collected your first paycheck and you’ve already skipped work I don’t know how many times.”

“I’m afraid Mr. Jace Sesso’s days as an assistant may be numbered.” Malin set the letter down on the desk, and only then did Jace notice the envelope — which had already been opened — was absurdly ornate, complete with a golden lion’s head pressed into the wax seal.

“Numbered? You don’t have to fire him, Father,” Amy said. “After all, a trip to Stonewatch is genuinely dangerous right now. I was only joking just now.”

Malin held up the letter. “A message from Master Andromath, sent by His Majesty from Stonewatch.”

Amy took the letter from Malin, glanced at it, and said in disbelief, “Stonewatch was attacked by gnolls? How come we haven’t heard a word of this down here… Jace Sesso is the hero of the Stonewatch crisis? Is that how your hand got injured? The gnolls…”

“Mr. Jace Sesso encountered a gnoll warlock. His hand was burned by fire magic,” Malin said. “Master Andromath told me the messenger himself was beside himself with excitement, vividly recounting how our Mr. Sesso fought off the gnoll siege from the watchtower — after all, the messenger himself had nearly lost his life when Stonewatch was attacked.”

“Incredible,” Amy murmured.

Jace looked at her and said in a joking tone, “I figure even if I’ve got no magical talent, I probably have a bit of a gift for a good old-fashioned brawl.”

“Don’t take it the wrong way,” Amy said quickly. “Actually, having too strong an affinity for the Arcane isn’t always a good thing — if it’s not handled well, it can lead to disaster. That’s why the Wizard’s Sanctum seeks out gifted individuals as early as possible, while still taking in young students.”

“Whether he has talent doesn’t matter anymore,” Malin said. “Keep reading. His Majesty wants the Wizard’s Sanctum to accept Mr. Jace Sesso as a formal apprentice. For the Sanctum, that’s no trouble at all. Master Andromath said that if I’m unwilling to take him on as an apprentice, he’ll take him on personally.”

Amy paused for a moment. “A direct command from the King?”

Malin said, “Strictly speaking, it’s a personal request — but in practice, both the Wizard’s Sanctum and the Royal Mages serve the Crown, so the King could easily have phrased it as a command. He’s only just returned to his throne and wants to leave a good impression on everyone. That was probably Lord Bolvar Fordragon’s advice. As a matter of fact, I’d been hoping Mr. Jace Sesso would get a chance to study magic — it’s just that the academy has certain regulations, and if he became an apprentice, it would be of greater assistance to my research.”

“Whether you study under Master Andromath or myself — that decision ultimately rests with you.” He looked at Jace and continued, “Master Andromath specializes in Arcane evocation, with a focus on frost magic. His path is more offensive in nature. I, on the other hand, am more skilled in transmutation and protective wards, with some research into fire magic as well. Mr. Jace Sesso, if you truly intend to walk this road, this choice is very important. Don’t consider the relationship between us — think it through carefully.”

Jace had already made up his mind well in advance. Shadow power could solve his problems on the offensive front — when it came to the Arcane, what he needed wasn’t fire elementals manipulated by arcane force, or Frostbolts, or the straightforward Arcane Missiles. All of those could be replaced by potent Shadow energy.

What was truly irreplaceable about Arcane magic was its sheer versatility, its extraordinary flexibility — so his best choice was to have Malin as his teacher.

“I’ve made my decision, Master Malin,” Jace said. “I intend to continue studying under you — not only because we already know each other, but more importantly because I believe transmutation and protective magic will be of greater use to me in repaying His Majesty’s favor and protecting the kingdom’s people in the future.”

Amy nodded in agreement. Eden Malin stood up and smoothed out his robes. “That settles it. This afternoon I’ll go speak to Master Andromath about this, and you can officially become a member of the Wizard’s Sanctum. From then on, you’ll be my apprentice. However, I do hope you’ll continue to assist me in matters concerning the relationship between the red dragons and the orcs.”

Jace certainly didn’t want to give up that task — after all, it still paid ten silver a month.

After Amy left, Malin suddenly asked, “I heard from Amy that you have difficulty connecting with the ley lines, is that right? She once tested your control over Arcane energy with a small rune, and you couldn’t get the rune to respond at all.”

That incident had nearly become Jace’s personal nightmare for a while — confidently showing off in front of an apprentice mage, only to be publicly humiliated on the spot.

But that had been nearly a month ago.

Jace said, “I refuse to believe that a ten-year-old child could master it through diligent practice and that an eighteen-year-old like me would be any worse.”

“There are still some differences,” Malin said. “But there is absolutely no unbridgeable chasm between the two.”

“Of course eighteen is old enough to begin studying magic. The reason many magical institutions are unwilling to accept students of this age is that, after the Order of Tirisfal was founded through the cooperation of elves and humans, humanity went through a relatively peaceful period, and the Order also wished to trim away large numbers of low-level members.”

“So during that time, for most academies, magical achievement shone brighter than the sheer number of magic students — mediocrity brought no benefit to magical institutions whatsoever. From that point on, most magical institutions shifted their goal: to compete with the elves, to let humanity surpass Quel’Thalas in magical achievement, and to fight for influence within the Order of Tirisfal. Do you know what humanity’s greatest disadvantage is compared to the elves in magical research?”

“Lifespan,” Jace said.

Malin continued, “Yes. Lifespan.”
