## Chapter 760: The Archmage's Solution

"Ah… it's about fire magic." Jace pulled out the book *The First Burning* and said, "I've hit a wall with Fire Ward, Master."

"A wall?" Marin said, surprised. "This spell is far simpler than Mage Armor, Mr. Sesso. And I recall you have quite a talent for fire magic — how could you possibly fail to learn Fire Ward?"

Jace gave an awkward laugh. "Which is why I've come to ask the teacher."

Marin pushed the chair aside, sat on the desk, and beckoned to the center of the room. "Cast for me. Let me see exactly what stage you've reached."

Jace walked to the middle of the room and glanced up at the high window, recalling how he'd felt the first time he learned Mage Armor. Of course, back then he'd been a complete novice, just wanting to learn a decent spell to protect himself so he wouldn't get chopped to death by kobolds or murlocs out on the road. And he'd known he had no arcane talent — just being able to recite an incantation and stir up the slightest ripple in the arcane energy around him had thrilled him.

Now the Dragonqueen had granted him a powerful affinity for fire, and if he still couldn't cast Fire Ward properly, he had no business putting himself in that old mindset at all.

By his original estimate, he should have been able to handle every fire magic with ease.

As it turned out, only his Fireball had gotten significantly stronger than before — learning new spells was proving an uphill battle.

"You're not using the Azure Song to help you cast?" Marin asked.

"I… would still rather manage without a focus first, Master," Jace said. He recited the incantation from the book: "Polionde, Brokralifelige, Elder!"

The incantation wasn't especially long, and it wasn't one of those strange tongues like Nathrezim or Ered'ruin — Jace had been memorizing it for days now, and he spoke it quite fluently.

But the arcane responded to Jace's call, yet couldn't smoothly drive the arcane to hunt down the fire elementals drifting through the air the way the Mage Armor incantation did…

It wasn't that the fire elementals were scattering in every direction. It was that Jace felt as though he'd summoned both the arcane and the fire elementals to his side at once — the fire elementals danced wildly, and the arcane power was nowhere near enough to bind them together into a protective ward.

When the incantation ended, Jace let out a breath, the arcane dispersed into the air, and the fire elementals turned into a cloud of gray smoke around him and vanished. Marin waved away the singed smell in front of him and said, "The arcane and fire elementals have never gotten along, Mr. Sesso. That's why apprentices who walk the path of the fire mage face more danger — Fire Ward has to be learned ahead of Frost Ward."

"I can feel it. The elementals favor you for some inexplicable reason. Most apprentices learning this spell run into trouble summoning arcane power but being unable to catch the fire elementals in the air — but your problem is the exact opposite. You summon arcane power just fine, but the fire elementals arrive faster than the arcane…"

Marin narrowed his eyes. "So… have you considered… skipping the arcane altogether and calling on the fire elementals directly for help?"

Jace blinked. "Is there an incantation for that?"

"An incantation — I don't know," Marin said. "But I know the sages of Aerie Peak are skilled at communing with the sky, and the Tide Sages of Kul Tiras can whisper to the sea… though these envoys of nature aren't skilled in wielding the arcane, they can still get the elementals to help them accomplish things ordinary people could never do. I know the orcs have this ability too — Master Dushan told me that before the orcish tribes touched demonic power, the shamans were the most revered among them."

Was Marin trying to get him to become a shaman?

It sounded absurd, but he did have a point.

The essence of the red dragon that Alexstrasza had bestowed on Jace brought an affinity for fire, not the arcane — and the fire-shaping curriculum taught at the Wizard Sanctum, or even the Kirin Tor for that matter, fundamentally taught how to use the arcane to manipulate — or, to put it less kindly, enslave — fire.

So to truly master these arts, it still came down to one's affinity for the arcane, which was plainly Jace's weak spot.

Marin's words had suddenly jogged Jace's memory — this was exactly why the Dragonqueen had warned him to be careful when casting spells. Because Jace couldn't properly control fire with arcane power; the fire came to him, not to the arcane.

"I understand, Master," Jace said.

After leaving the Mage Quarter, that evening Jace met up with Grede for drinks at the Gilded Rose.

Jace got there early, ordered the blood sausage and cornbread that Grede liked, and got himself a portion of dried wolf meat. Grede came through the door, looked around, and sat down next to Jace. "Where's the little elf?"

"She's already gone back," Jace said.

"That early?" Grede said. "Sneaking out again, was she?"

"Most likely," Jace said. "Which is why I didn't try to keep her."

"Was really hoping we could all get together for one more meal," Grede said. "Oh, right — I've got it sorted. A few Bronzebeards doing renovations in the Dwarf District. I showed them your place. The one in charge said the overall condition's not bad, just some problems with the roof. They're willing to do the overhaul for five gold. What do you think?"

"Not expensive," Jace said. "And way cheaper than that middleman's quote. Keep an eye on things for me once it starts."

"No problem," Grede said.

Just then the barmaid brought over the dark ales that had just been ordered. Jace pushed one over to the dwarf's side. Grede eyed his hand and said. "What, you need a favor from me too?"

"I want to learn the Kalimdor tongue," Jace said.

"What? Why the sudden urge to learn the elementals' language?" The dwarf bit off a chunk of blood sausage, chewed twice, and said, "Trying to become one of those human sages? I didn't think your lot cared much about that sort of thing."

"Not quite…" Jace said. "Just more interested in elementals lately. You remember the blessing the Dragonqueen gave me — I was thinking about whether I could learn better ways to communicate with fire elementals."

Grede took a sip of his ale. "If it's fire you're playing with, you want to find the Dark Iron dwarves, Jace."

He laughed a few times, then watched Jace's serious expression and grew sober after a moment.

"Hey, I'm joking — you're not actually taking this seriously, are you? You really want to go to Shadowforge City to learn how to talk to fire elementals?" Jace asked. "Are there Dark Iron 'sages' like that in Ironforge or Stormwind?"

Grede thought for a moment. "I know there's a group of Dark Iron dwarves who broke away from Shadowforge City. They call themselves the Thorium Brotherhood. Those folks definitely know more than I do."
