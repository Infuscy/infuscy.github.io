## Chapter 767: Half-Blood

Hearing Ursula ask this, the playful look on Jace's face vanished in an instant. He sat up straight and said seriously, "Not yet. Back then we were just good friends."

"You'd better be," Ursula said, pressing a hand to her forehead. "Damn it, I don't want to get on the wrong side of the Windrunner family over one night of impulse! When I heard about this, I nearly had a heart attack. I've heard some elves like to hire killers to deal with third parties. I don't know what their nobles would do — probably something even more extreme. I don't want to shake off demon assassins only to end up targeted by elves."

"Really?" Jace said. "But I'm not lying to you — every word is the truth. We've been together less than half a year. That time… it was all before I left for Kalimdor."

"Then I'll believe you," Ursula said, fixing him with a stare. "You don't look it, but you're far from innocent. You put on this act of being honest all day long."

Jace raised an eyebrow. "Sorry, I admit it… but are you sure the people in Eastweald aren't still after you?"

Ursula said, "My friends in the royal city told me they've stopped cooperating. That merchant in Stratholme seems to have been frightened off too. But there's a rumor going around that I hired a paladin to go to Andorhal and kill that succubus. What's going on there? Did you spend the money to hire a member of the Silver Hand in Lordaeron?"

"The Silver Hand probably wouldn't take money to kill demons, would they?" Jace asked.

"You never know," Ursula said. "Even saints have to eat. The Light is merciful — it would understand."

"I didn't use demons, and I didn't use any dark magic either," Jace said. "They probably figured anyone who'd execute a demon in broad daylight like that was most likely a paladin."

"Thank you — really. I'm grateful you helped me resolve this," Ursula said.

"It's nothing compared to the help you've given me," Jace said. "By the way, how's the investigation going — the conflict between your father and that man from Stratholme?"

Ursula sighed. "I've made some progress, but I don't know how to tell you, Jace. Sorry."

"It's your private matter," Jace said with a nod.

The two of them fell silent for a while. The alchemist put away her tools, closed the box, and rested her hands on the lid. "If you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner get married someday, you'll have children, right?"

"Why bring that up all of a sudden?" Jace looked up. "I haven't seriously thought about that…"

Ursula said, "Then your children would be a bunch of half-elves who aren't accepted in Quel'Thalas or here, Jace. Have you thought about that? When they go on to have wives or husbands, their descendants will be half-elves forever — neither human nor elf."

Jace said, "There are also half-elves who've achieved great things. Take Archmage Alood of Dalaran, for example. Have you heard his story?"

"Of course I've heard it, but what does one person prove?"

There was barely-concealed anger in Ursula's tone. Jace had almost never seen her this upset. Ursula had always been even-tempered — so much so that she couldn't even tap into anger to cast shadow magic and had to rely on rage potions.

Jace was of course used to that kind of alchemist. So even though her tone now was far from "angry" — just a little barbed — it still made the back of his neck prickle.

"Or maybe the fact that over thousands of years, only a handful of noteworthy half-elves have ever appeared is precisely the point. Maybe people like us are just destined to be this way."

"What about your future children, then?" Jace asked.

"I won't have descendants," she said coldly. "I've seen what half a lifetime looks like for a half-elf, so I won't try to bring more half-elves into this world to suffer the way you are."

Jace was stunned, at a loss for words for a moment.

After Ursula finished, she seemed to realize something. She rested a hand on the box and said softly, "I'm sorry, Jace. I'm truly sorry. I must be going mad. We're good friends — you're my best friend. You've found someone else, and no matter what, I shouldn't have said such stupid, pointless things to you."

Jace sat up, no longer lounging lazily against the back of the chair. He rested his hands on his knees and paused for a moment before speaking. "You really don't need to apologize, Ursula. I may not be a half-elf, but growing up, I've seen how some half-elves live."

"Actually, I came here to talk to you about the felhound. You're the warlock here who's best at handling felhounds, after all — you spend every day with Besadim."

"I'm definitely not as good as you, Jace," Ursula said.

"Don't be too modest," Jace said. "I just spoke with Galders, and now I'm telling you the same thing. I've worked things out with someone from SI:7. I'm planning to have warlocks who are skilled with felhounds help them root out the black dragons' disguises. Even though I told Galders it would be the Dagger Society cooperating with them, I think you're the most suitable. And it just so happens you've shaken off the Stratholme problem. Even if that merchant still wants to make trouble for you down the road, with SI:7's help you'll have a much easier time dealing with them, won't you?"

"Can I really do this?" the alchemist asked hesitantly.

"Unless you don't like going out and prefer being alone," Jace said with a smile.

Ursula thought for a moment. "If you really trust me, I'd be willing to give it a try, Jace. As long as those people don't mind that I'm a half-elf."

"As long as you agree, I think it's more or less settled," Jace said. "Then I won't disturb your work any longer, Ursula."

He stood up to leave, and Ursula said, "Don't take what I said earlier to heart, Jace. I sincerely wish you and Vereesa Windrunner a good future. And I believe your children… will be remarkable people who change how folks like us are seen."

"Thank you," Jace said with a smile.

Ursula smiled too, watching Jace leave the alchemy lab.

Walking through the passage, Jace glanced at Morofell, who had been silent the whole time. She wore that same smiling expression as always — she seemed to have noticed something, and also seemed not to have noticed anything.

Next, it was just a matter of waiting for SI:7 to let him know the prisoners were in place, and seeing whether the felhound could identify the disguised dragonkin based on their magical signatures.

But Jace understood very well that even if they could identify disguised dragonkin, that didn't mean the felhound would be able to recognize true black dragons.

After all, even someone as powerful as Krasus couldn't see through the black dragons' disguise magic. Counting on a felhound was probably wishful thinking. To guard against the real Onyxia — or her black dragon subordinates — showing up in Stormwind, they'd need a more reliable method.

Thinking of this, Jace once again thought of Marshal Reginald Windsore's slate — the one recovered by the Dark Iron dwarves — and the incantation that could force Onyxia to reveal her true form… Where on earth had that come from?
