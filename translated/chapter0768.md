## Chapter 768: Reliable Magical Discernment

The Pig and Whistle was packed. Jace leaned against the wall, watching a group of humans who looked like sailors gathered up front and shouting at each other, playing some kind of drinking game.

But by now the game had descended into total chaos. One guy had had his hand raised from start to finish yelling, "Another round, Miss Langston!" — yet he lay on the table the whole time, struggling to keep his eyes open, trying to prove he was still sober, only to fix his gaze on a companion's backside the entire time.

"If it's all right with you, work on your house starts tomorrow," Grede reminded him.

"I appreciate it," Jace said.

Grede tore off a piece of chicken and said, "I took a job from the Fighters' Guild for thirty silver — going to Duskwood to deal with a…"

Halfway through his sentence, the dwarf seemed to choke on something. He paused, squeezed his eyes shut, and forced the bread and dry chicken in his mouth down in one gulp. He caught his breath and continued, "A ghost — at least that's what the requester said. Now that I have to keep an eye on your renovation for you, could you go in my place? We'll split the pay eighty-twenty — me two, you eight. How about it?"

"Not interested," Jace said.

"You've changed," Grede said, putting down the bone. "Five silver would have you thrilled out of your mind, and now it's thirty silver. And isn't a ghost going to be easier to deal with than a young black dragon? For a scoundrel like you, that's practically free money! And you've got Lady Morofell to help — you might even walk away with a soul shard!"

"Take a look at everything I've got going on," Jace said with a deep breath. "For one thing, the king wants me to help investigate the black dragons' infiltration of Stormwind — and I shouldn't be telling you this, Grede, so don't repeat it to anyone. For another, a friend of mine arranged for me to meet with the leader of the Stonemasons' guild to visit Irgal's Tower and finalize the renovation plan and price. Third, I really do want to find the Thorium Brotherhood soon and ask them about fire elementals. Fourth, the trip to Kalimdor is more or less set — I need to write a letter to Vereesa and tell her, and have her find a suitable excuse through the Reliquary to come along with us. Fifth, I still have several incantations here that I haven't learned yet — I need to finish learning them, and I also need to go exchange spells with the other warlocks. There are a few very important magics… magics related to teleportation."

Jace's mouth rattled all this off like an incantation — fast, and completely without emotion. Grede was already looking a little dazed.

"I also summoned an archdemon with a group of warlocks, and that archdemon is locked up inside Blackrock Mountain right now. And now you want me to go to Duskwood with you for a thirty-silver bounty… Grede, this is thirty silver. Do you remember how much King Wrynn put up as a bounty on Hogger? Sixty silver. You remember how long it took us to root out Hogger — three or four days, more or less. And that was in Elwynn Forest. This time we'd be going to Duskwood — a week just for the round trip, and with tracking down your ghost, probably half a month. I don't have half a month to earn thirty silver, Grede. I could sell a single soul shard I brought back from Maraudon for a few silver — that's enough food and drink for days."

Grede patted the table lightly and said, "That was back when the king was short on money. If that gnoll boss were alive today, the bounty would be a lot higher. Haven't you heard? The Dark Ironhide gnoll warlord in Redridge has a bounty of three gold on him, Jace. From what I know, that fellow's a lot shorter than Hogger, and plenty of mercenaries are already itching to get on the road. Wait — what did you say? You summoned an archdemon?!"

"Don't worry too much," Jace said. "It's safe and under control."

"I don't trust you warlocks," Grede said, propping his chin on his hand. "One of these days that fellow's going to drop right into Stormwind and bash your head in… But you're sure we're getting ready to go to Kalimdor?"

Jace said, "We'll have to wait for word from Vereesa's side. For now, help me finish renovating the house first, Grede."

The dwarf picked off a chunk of cheese with his fingers, poked it into his bread, stuffed it in his mouth, and chewed twice. "At least that's some good news. I can't wait to see Mosha Woodmane again in Kalimdor."

"I was hoping things would be a little quieter these days so I could properly learn swordsmanship from you again," Jace said. "But the mages from Quel'Thalas aren't giving me the time. There's a mage named Dar'Khan Drathir who's only one step behind me. I'm worried that fellow will find that thing first. Oh, and there's the Dalaran matter too — the Kirin Tor might have me come identify Kel'Thuzad's crimes after this… According to the Wizard Sanctum, the investigation against him is about to begin, or has already begun."

"Isn't that a good thing?" Grede said. "Swordsmanship can be learned anytime. We can even practice more in Kalimdor!" Hearing Grede say this, Jace thought it over and realized he was actually looking forward to returning to Kalimdor himself.

Thinking back to that last trip to Kalimdor, even though the whole journey had been perilous, not knowing where the next meal was coming from, nearly losing his life several times, and getting captured by night elves along the way — the adventures and experiences along the road had made him feel like that was the life he truly yearned for.

After returning to Stormwind, it felt as though the whole world had come crashing down on him all at once.

When he left the Pig and Whistle, it was already dark. Grede headed northwest back toward the Dwarf District. Jace was just about to head home when he saw the SI:7 agent — Nedden Hert — sitting on the steps of a house across the street.

The moment he spotted Jace, he stood up.

Looks like it was time to test Dartholme the Fourth's abilities.

Following Nedden Hert to the SI:7 headquarters, Jace brought the felhound out of the magic holding cell in the basement and headed to the open ground among the trees behind the villa.

By this time, several guards with spears and Mathias Shaw were already there, escorting three prisoners.

One of them, a woman, immediately lost control and screamed the moment she saw Jace and his minion. "Keep that demon away from me, damn it! You think the black dragons are evil, and yet you're conspiring with demons?!"

Mathias glanced at her and said coldly, "The demon can help me carry out the king's orders. That's enough."

"Are all three of these people Cult of the Dragon warlocks?" Jace asked.

"No," Mathias said, pointing at the prisoners. "This one's a blacksmith. This one's a Lakeshire guard. That woman is a hedge wizard from Lakeshire. They jointly led a kidnapping operation by the Cult of the Dragon in Lakeshire. We arrested them and kept them in Goldshire for interrogation, but they've barely said anything of value."

"I have reliable intelligence indicating that the mastermind behind this kidnapping operation was a dragonkin. After a process of elimination, it should be one of these three. Because we suspected a dragonkin was among them, we never sent them to the Stormwind prison to be treated as ordinary criminals. But just feeding those three in Goldshire is no small expense. Today I'm hoping your Dartholme the Fourth can help us solve this problem."

"Based on the clues you smelled out earlier, Dartholme," Jace said, looking at the three people before him, "root out the dragonkin among them."

The felhound received its orders and charged straight at the youngest-looking guard among them. Two thick tentacles waved back and forth in midair. The guard was startled at first by the demon lunging at him, then suddenly swelled up in a cloud of arcane smoke, transforming into a scrawny dragonkin. He whipped the felhound aside hard with his long tail!

Jace immediately recited a Curse of Weakness. The dragonkin's hind legs buckled and he fell to the ground with a cry. He howled and tried to get back up, only to be stabbed hard in the shoulder and back by the guards' lances, and collapsed in pain.

The dragonkin cursed feebly at everyone before him, but was already being bound tight by more and more ropes. Mathias also rushed over to help subdue the creature, forcing him to change back into the form of the guard.

But this sudden transformation left him completely unable to conceal the foul shadowy stench of the dragonkin, or those serpent-like pupils. The disguises these dragonkin had been wearing before must have been carefully masked — and not by their own power alone.

After taking the guard-turned-dragonkin to the basement, Mathias walked out, breathing hard. He glanced at the felhound and smiled. "Efficient and accurate, Master Sesso. It just makes the months of torture we put them through before seem a bit laughable. I hope that fellow's screams don't ruin the good dreams of Stormwind's residents tonight, but I know I'll sleep well."
