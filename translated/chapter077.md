## Chapter 77: Light (Part One)

Marin said, "Elves are prone to arrogance and self-satisfaction, looking down on the world. Those who aren't quite so proud tend, over the long centuries, to gradually develop feelings of inferiority and sorrow, losing the drive to do anything at all. Their excessive lifespans make them unimaginably cautious, which drags down their ambition — and these elders who live for thousands of years wield terrible authority. On top of that, an insular Quel'Thalas narrows their horizons... But if they didn't have these shortcomings, humanity would have no chance of surpassing them in the magical arts."

"Even so, a lifespan of seventy or eighty years is far too short. Elves can live for over a thousand years. Sun King Anasterian once established a covenant with the empire's founder, Emperor Thoradin, to impart magic to humanity — and that was already two thousand eight hundred years ago. As things stand, there is only one way for a human to reach such a lifespan: to become a Tirisfal Guardian. But a single person cannot compete with the numerous members of the Quel'Thalas school. What's more, now that the Order of Tirisfal has been dissolved, the Guardian is already the greatest threat to the Kirin Tor and the entire magical world."

At this, Marin shook his head and continued, "Of course, pursuing such things now is meaningless. Azeroth is facing the greatest threat in hundreds of years — clinging to the old traditions of a bygone peaceful era is no longer appropriate. But everyone has grown accustomed to this model, haven't they?"

"Enough of that — let's get straight to the point. For you to learn magic, you must first master communication with the ley lines. For gifted children, this process may already be complete while they're still in the womb. You, on the other hand, will have to practice relentlessly."

"What do I need to do?" Jace asked.

"Contact with the ley lines is a sensation that's very difficult to describe," Marin said. "You'll need to expend a great deal of energy feeling out their currents, much like trying to detect the heartbeat of an invisible person's chest in the void. It surges and calms, races and slows, and countless other energies existing in the spiritual realm confuse you, making you mistake them for the true heartbeat. But if you're truly led astray, you'll lose contact with the ley lines and fall back into reality — and in the worst case, it could invite unforeseen disaster."

As Jace listened to Marin, he had a feeling of déjà vu.

Wasn't this exactly what he experienced when casting Fear?

It's just that every time he spoke an incantation of Shadow, what he was reaching for wasn't some heartbeat in the void, but a genuine... heartbeat from within himself. This heartbeat wasn't the beating of a real heart, but the dark power flowing through his body. Jace had to find it and marshal it for his own use.

Marin said, "I can teach you a very basic and simple spell: Light. It's a spell that gathers free-floating Arcane energy to produce light. This incantation can help you sense the subtle connection between that free-floating arcana and the ley lines as a whole, thereby guiding you to gradually touch the ley lines themselves."

"I more or less understand," Jace said.

"Ah... I can hardly wait to set you on the path of magic, Mr. Sesso." Marin stood up. "Why don't we start your first introductory lesson right now — the paperwork can wait." He walked around the desk to the middle of the study, gauged the distance between himself and the bookshelf, and raised his right hand.

"Liyouisa!"

There was a boom like wind billowing up, followed by a string of light chimes like wind bells. Jace was reminded of the sound Sazeno the imp made when he vanished and reappeared — that little demon must have used Arcane channels to travel between planes as well.

A point of light rose in Marin's palm. Although in the morning sunlight it wasn't particularly dazzling, if cast in the darkness of Elwynn at night, it would undoubtedly become the most eye-catching focus for miles.

The old mage pinched the point of light out between two fingers, then looked at Jace. "You can try now."

"Is it really that simple — Liyouisa?"

The words had barely left his mouth when a burst of flame suddenly erupted from Jace's hand. Completely unprepared, he startled and flung his arm — and the flame actually set several books at his side on fire!

Marin's eyes went wide. He immediately pulled his wand from his belt and pointed it at the bookshelf — the entire shelf was instantly coated in a layer of frost, and the flames turned into a wisp of white smoke that slowly dissipated.

He ran over to the bookshelf, breathed on the frost to melt it away, and kept running his hands over the books, trying to see what damage the fire had caused.

When he made out the words on the spines, Marin raised his eyebrows and muttered to himself, "So it was these two books. Burned is burned, I suppose."

Jace stammered, "Was... was that some kind of fire magic?"

"No, that was Arcane ignition," Marin said, looking up and adjusting his spectacles. "Didn't Amy say you had no magical talent? Why..."

He said, as if suddenly understanding something, "You may lack a natural sensitivity to the ley lines, but your incantation... you spoke your incantation... with remarkable accuracy. Dushan mentioned this last time as well — he said that when you imitate others' Orcish pronunciation, you do it uncannily well. He also told me that if you could meet a real orc, it would be even better — it would improve your skills by a great deal."

Jace thought to himself that he had indeed seen a real orc before, but whether that was a good thing was another matter entirely.

Marin continued, "The ley lines and the Arcane — indeed, all forms of power — possess their own will. Of course, they have their own likes and dislikes, their own dispositions, and they choose their own users. The quickest way to develop an affinity with them is to produce sounds that they find pleasing — not only making them understand your commands, but making them enjoy your commands, even grow fond of you personally. You could say this is a crucial component of magical affinity."

"Of course, more complex magic can't be mastered by knowing the incantation alone — there are other casting conditions, such as gestures, environment, the manner and depth of contact with the ley lines, your mental energy, and so on. Arcane energy requires not only summoning and command, but also further guidance to produce more changes according to your will. You can try again."

Jace tried raising his hand, this time farther from the bookshelf, and spoke "Liyouisa" once more.

He felt as though he'd sensed something, and as though he hadn't. Remembering the simple test he'd done with Amy before, Jace felt a little discouraged. Still, that burst of flame just now at least proved he'd triggered some kind of reaction — it must have been the result of making some sort of contact with the ley lines, right?

Thinking this, he fixed his eyes intently on his fingertip, recalling the driving force he'd felt when he'd successfully cast Fear in the past.

Liyouisa!

A pale white flash flickered from his fingertip, like a lighter that was running out of fuel.

"Did it work?" Jace asked. "Is... is that considered working?"

"I'd say so, more or less — at least there was some effect," Marin said. "However, this is already far stronger than I expected. If all goes well, you'll clearly feel fatigued. Rest for a day before attempting more. Mental overexertion is one of the most important lessons for every mage — you must avoid overexertion causing permanent damage to your body and mind."

Jace gave a self-deprecating laugh, but in truth he didn't feel fatigued at all.
