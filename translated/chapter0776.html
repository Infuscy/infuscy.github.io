Chapter 776: Renowned Across the Three Hammers

The guard grabbed Jace's hand and said something to several nearby dwarves, and those dwarves got excited too. One of them, a dwarf with a golden beard, came up to Jace and said, "You're Jace Sesso — you're really Stormwind's Jace Sesso?!"

Jace nodded in a daze, because he had no idea what these people were saying at all. The golden-bearded dwarf immediately reached up and slapped him hard across the ribs. "I've finally laid eyes on the real deal today! What brings you to Ironforge, Jace Sesso? Here to chat with King Magni?"

"No, sir," Jace said. "I'm here looking for someone."

The golden-bearded dwarf pointed to a doorway at the far end of a street corner and said in Common, "The guards here don't speak Common very well. Follow me, Dragonslayer — I'll take you to an inn."

With that, the dwarf straightened his belt, hoisted his pants up hard, and turned to walk toward the doorway, waving for Jace to follow.

Only then did Jace realize that whether it was slaying Teron Gorefiend or handling that business in Grim Batol, the news had reached Ironforge first, and Ironforge had informed the King of Stormwind.

And since the orcs inside Grim Batol were the greatest threat to Khaz Modan, the collapse of the Dragonmaw clan naturally benefited Khaz Modan's capital, Ironforge, the most. So it wasn't surprising that the name Jace Sesso was even better known here.

It was just that he'd never been to Ironforge before, and hadn't seriously considered whether to travel or handle business here. Now that he'd actually come, he could genuinely feel the impact.

Though the dwarves' enthusiasm made him a little uncomfortable, that was his own personality's problem, not the dwarves'. Regardless, it still warmed his heart.

Following the golden-bearded dwarf into the passage, they turned onto a flight of stairs at a fork. It was incredibly dark and damp, with only braziers at the corners for light.

Narrow staircases crowded with dwarves going up and down. Every time they passed each other, they would smile and greet one another. Jace initially thought they all knew each other, but he slowly realized that whether they knew each other or not, these Bronzebeard dwarves would try to greet each other in all sorts of ways — and the greetings were all different, some long, some short, none of them simple things like "hello" or "good morning."

After walking for who knows how long, during which Jace nearly lost track of the golden-bearded dwarf in the crowds several times, they finally emerged from the stairway passage at a corner. The scorched smell was gone here, replaced by the smell of bread and ale fermenting that filled the streets. Unlike the fifth level, the ceiling here wasn't high, and the houses on either side of the street weren't so towering. Dense little doorways and windows dotted the rock face, glowing with lamplight. The atmosphere of daily life was much richer.

And there were many other races appearing here — humans, elves, occasionally visible. Jace himself stood out far less.

"This way, Jace Sesso!" the golden-bearded dwarf said, leading him through the crowd. "I know an innkeeper — follow me and you'll be fine, guaranteed."

Watching his back, Jace was reminded of those touting drivers who drummed up business for guesthouses in his past life, so he instinctively grew wary.

But as he observed the shop signs on both sides of the street, sure enough there were plenty of "bed" and "goblet" symbols appearing — meaning this level of Ironforge was probably a residential or dining district. Since this golden-bearded dwarf had brought him here, he figured as long as the inn's price wasn't outrageously unreasonable — ripping him off like a fool — he'd just accept it.

But as they walked along, Jace noticed a shop door marked with an arrow and a stag. He asked the golden-bearded dwarf ahead, "Is that a hunting supplies store, sir?"

"No," the golden-bearded dwarf said. "That's a Hunters' Guild, sir."

"Nesingwary?" Jace asked.

"Hemet Nesingwary — you even know the name of this legendary hunter?" The golden-bearded dwarf was quite surprised, but he still shook his head. "Of course, their family is quite famous, Jace Sesso. It's just that I'm not a hunter, so I can't quite remember the names of these organizations. All I can tell you for certain is that's not Nesingwary's hunting party — it's another Hunters' Guild."

Hearing the golden-bearded dwarf's explanation, Jace looked back at the stag on the sign with an arrow through its chest.

That last trip to Kalimdor, there was still one important thing he hadn't done… Strictly speaking, there was still a significant sum of money he hadn't earned — the trail of the crocodile Rotgrip deep in Maraudon.

Jace had originally planned that after returning from Kalimdor, he'd prepare to head to Ironforge together with Grede to find the legendary hunter Hemet Nesingwary and chat with him about the trail of this crocodile… He hadn't expected to be delayed by the Grim Batol affair after returning.

Now that he was already in Ironforge, he didn't even need to find the Nesingwary family — after all, this legendary hunter had plenty of competitors, and plenty of dwarf hunters wanted to surpass him. In this regard, other hunters should have even more motivation to pay a high price.

Why not come to this Hunters' Guild first thing tomorrow morning and see how much the dwarf hunters were willing to pay for the title of "Rotgrip Slayer."

Following the golden-bearded dwarf through several passages, they arrived at a middling-sized inn. The golden-bearded dwarf pointed to the smiling crescent moon on the sign and said, "This is the place."

Ducking through the low doorway, a few drunken dwarves were muttering amongst themselves in the cramped front hall. The dwarf behind the counter saw Jace and unabashedly sized up his clothes, accessories, the rings on his fingers, and the size of his coin pouch, then immediately shouted excitedly in Common, "Welcome! Our most steadfast and courageous human ally! Only two a night…"

Before he could finish, the golden-bearded dwarf immediately rushed up and slapped the table, saying a whole string of something furiously. The innkeeper frowned at him, clearly unhappy at being interrupted mid-greeting, but the more the golden-bearded dwarf said, the more his expression shifted from displeasure to surprise, then he looked over toward Jace.

Hearing the golden-bearded dwarf's words, the drinking dwarves also turned to look toward the counter, then curiously looked over at Jace's side.

"A real Dragonslayer gracing my inn?!" The innkeeper vaulted over the counter and ran up to Jace, grabbing Jace's cloak and looking up at him in Common. "And not just any dragon — a black dragon, the ancestor of all black dragons! Good God! Why didn't some king or lord give me a heads-up and let me know this news?!"

"Your fame has drifted from Grim Batol to Ironforge, and then on to Aerie Peak — Franclorn Forgeweather probably heard about it from that great fire pit he dug up! How could I possibly charge you money, mage? Forget what I just said — stay as long as you like…"
