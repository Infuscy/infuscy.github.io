## Chapter 777: A Lead on the Legendary Crocodile

Opening his eyes, Jace sat up from the bed, which carried a stifling, damp smell. It was eerily quiet all around.

Ironforge was a city built inside the mountains of Khaz Modan. Lighting relied entirely on roadside braziers, and the deep lava trenches along the edges of the streets… so the light and dark were extremely uneven. Looking out the narrow window, which was like an arrow slit in a battlement, the city streets outside — patches of shadow on the yellowish walls, patches of blinding light — but everywhere was deserted, with hardly anyone around. Of the few figures walking the streets, there was even an elf.

Jace shifted to glance the other way and found it was the same. Compared to the lively scene of last night, it was as if the entire dwarf population of the city had suddenly moved out.

He glanced at his pocket watch — it was already past eight in the morning.

Walking downstairs, a dwarf was clinging to the wall and climbing back up, squinting and groggy. Hard to tell if he was sick, hadn't woken up properly, or was still drunk.

He muttered something in Dwarvish when he saw Jace. Jace thought he was greeting him, so he replied in Dwarvish — "Good morning." The dwarf stared at him wide-eyed in surprise for a while, but didn't say anything else.

Jace was starting to suspect the fellow had been cursing him, an outsider — he didn't know that many Dwarvish swear words after all. If Grede had been around, it probably would have been much better.

He wondered whether he'd run into more of the same trouble when he went to the Hunters' Guild later.

Stepping out onto the street, as he passed the residences he could even hear the thunderous snoring of dwarves from the stone windows along the street.

By now, shops were opening one by one and guards were appearing on the streets, but the clanging hammer strikes, the boisterous laughter and noise from last night were nowhere to be seen.

Looks like being a heavy sleeper wasn't Grede's own problem.

Returning to the Hunters' Guild doorway he'd passed last night, Jace confirmed the sign of the wild stag pierced by an arrow. He walked up the steps and knocked on the door. It was a long while before someone finally opened it.

The dwarf inside raised his head, a mess of hair like a bird's nest. He moved his lips, his thick brown braided beard swaying along with them. He squinted to make out Jace, who was looking down at him, then glanced at Jace's dark outer cloak and hood and asked, "Who are you? Who are you here to see?"

"I'm a traveler, dwarf sir," Jace glanced inside and said, "and I'm looking for a hunting party with the ambition to become famous across two continents."

Hearing Jace's words, the dwarf thought for a moment and asked, "You're sure you said 'with the ambition to,' and not 'already is'?"

"I'm sure," Jace said. He knew the other party was hinting at Nesingwary, but he still said, "I heard some hunting parties in Ironforge have been gathering information on legendary beasts."

"Every hunting party in Ironforge wants to become a group famous across Lordaeron and Khaz Modan, human," the dwarf suddenly looked much more alert than before. He pulled the door all the way open and stepped aside. "Come in quickly, mage sir."

"Not Lordaeron and Khaz Modan," Jace said, stepping inside and pulling off his hood. "But here and Kalimdor." The dwarf was taken aback. Jace pointed to the hook with a wide-brimmed hat hanging on the wall and asked, "May I hang my cloak over here?"

"Of course," the dwarf said quickly with a nod, then stared straight at Jace's cloak and asked, "What material is this cloak made of, mage? I've never seen anything like it… it feels a little like the twin-sail dragons of Searing Gorge."

"Black dragon hide," Jace said.

"Black dragon," the dwarf said. "You mean the black ones, the twin-sail dragons?"

"No," Jace said, turning his head. "A real dragon."

"Impressive, impressive."

The dwarf exclaimed this, but Jace knew he didn't believe him right away — he'd pegged Jace as a blowhard who liked to bluff. You could tell even without sensing the other party's emotions.

"I'm Hajik," the dwarf said, shaking Jace's hand briefly. "Just call me Hajik."

"Jace Sesso," Jace said.

"That sounds like a familiar name," the dwarf said with a nod. "Probably because human names all sound familiar to me. Let's get straight to the point — Mr. Sesso, did you hear news of some legendary beast during your travels? Like Nesingwary's hunting party, we'll pay the informant a fair reward for any leads! You don't need to worry about that at all."

After Hajik finished speaking, he smiled. Jace said, "Theramoke."

Theramoke — the true name of Maraudon, and also the place where the crocodile god of hunter legend, Rotgrip, was said to dwell. Just mentioning this name would make those who understood naturally understand, and those who didn't, Jace saw no need to waste his breath on.

"Theramoke?" Hajik repeated in a low voice, shaking his head. "The name Theramoke is indeed connected to Kalimdor, human, but the number of people who claim to have been to Kalimdor far exceeds the number who've actually been there. You'll need to prove you've really been to that place."

Actually, Jace hadn't brought back anything from Kalimdor that could prove he'd been there. Things that truly could — like those scrolls and packages related to Darthremar, or even the Blade of Eternal Darkness — this hunter probably wouldn't have a clue what they were.

But Jace couldn't be bothered to prove anything either. Grede was right — when you had privileges, you used them. So he took the Court Mage's sigil off his finger and placed it on the table.

"What's this?" Hajik picked up the ring and examined it for a while. "A gold ring… my, this is quite a beautiful eye-stone. The lion emblem on it — this is something from the Kingdom of Stormwind? Sorry, mage, I don't really recognize this ring, but this thing is indeed very valuable. Are you looking to use it as collateral for your information?"

"What collateral," Jace said, snatching the ring back. "This is the Court Mage's sigil of the Kingdom of Stormwind, Hajik. I'm Mageregent Varian Wrynn's arcane advisor. I think this identity alone is enough to make you take my words seriously, don't you?"

"Wait," Hajik said, stunned. "I know where I've heard your name before, Jace Sesso!" He lowered his head and thought for a moment, then said, "Wait — I need to get someone up."

The dwarf stood up and ran upstairs, pounding on a door, shouting "Salban, Salban!" — then followed by a string of incomprehensible Dwarvish. The person he'd woken up went berserk and roared for a while, but the moment he heard Hajik mention "Theramoke," he immediately opened the door and came running out.

This dwarf named Salban was clearly a lot older. He followed Hajik to the staircase and saw Jace sitting below. As he came downstairs he asked, "You're the Jace Sesso who destroyed the Dragonmaw orc army?"

Destroyed the Dragonmaw orc army?!

Well, it kind of was, but it also didn't seem quite accurate. But Jace couldn't be bothered to parse the difference.

"That's me," he said. "But this time this matter has nothing to do with the Grim Batol incident. I traveled in Kalimdor for a period and discovered the legendary Theramoke by accident. With my name and status, I should be able to vouch for the truth of what I say, no?"

"Of course, of course, Mr. Sesso," Hajik said, pointing to the dwarf beside him. "This is Salban, the most senior hunter in our guild and one of our leaders. He once went to Kalimdor to investigate the news of Theramoke and Rotgrip, but came back with nothing but rumors. You can tell him about it."

"To many residents of Kalimdor, Theramoke is a legendary place, esteemed Mr. Sesso," Salban said, coming down and sitting on the table. "Even the goblins who explore everywhere searching for treasure have never found it. It's not that I doubt you, but Theramoke is a name that's been forgotten." Jace said, "Neither the centaur nor the tauren tribes use that name anymore. Only the elemental beings who have lived there for hundreds of years, or even longer, still use that ancient name to address it."

"Centaurs and taurens?" Salban nodded. "Is this something you heard from others, or did you really communicate with those natives?"

"Then what do you mean by the residents you mentioned?" Jace asked.

"The goblins, of course," Salban said.

"The goblins are also outsiders to Kalimdor," Jace explained. "You might even find some records related to 'Theramoke' in a high elf library, after all they're the descendants of exiles who crossed the sea from Kalimdor seven thousand years ago."

"Then where is Theramoke right now?" Salban asked eagerly.

"How much are you willing to pay to buy this information?" Jace asked.

The two dwarves exchanged a glance. Hajik said, "Often the informant is also our guide, Mr. Sesso. Of course, we definitely can't afford to hire a Court Mage of the Kingdom of Stormwind to take us to Kalimdor to hunt a legendary monster, so…"

Salban said something to Hajik in Dwarvish, and Hajik responded in Dwarvish. After confirming that Jace couldn't understand Dwarvish, they didn't hesitate any longer and went straight into a continuous back-and-forth argument.

"I hope it's a fair price," Jace said, looking at them. "After all, Ironforge has more than one Hunters' Guild, and Nesingwary's price certainly won't be low."

Hajik immediately said, "We're willing to pay four gold."

Hearing this price, Jace's first reaction was that he'd priced the Hand of Kul'gan a little too low.

If, as agreed, the Demon Empowerment incantation was worth five gold, then Demiseth Kraus only needed to scrape together ten gold to hand over to him… That would be about the same as the rewards for these two leads?

But considering this lead on the giant crocodile — over the years, no hunter had ever obtained it — and the fact that he'd learned this information partly from memories of his past life, and partly from the entire dangerous journey through the depths of Maraudon with Vereesa, Grede, Mosha Woodmane, and two demon companions, nearly unable to make it back alive.

Those Stranglethorn Vale beast leads certainly couldn't compare to this.

Seeing him lost in thought, Hajik said something more to Salban, then gritted his teeth and said, "If we really manage to hunt that legendary crocodile in the end… we'll add another ten gold on top, at least ten gold. How about that, Mr. Sesso?"

Another ten gold?

Jace felt that was about enough. This way they'd gotten a little bit closer to two gold — after all, many a little makes a mickle. Just thinking this made him feel like he'd gotten carried away — fourteen gold was now a "little" too!

"Fine," he said, forcing himself to appear calm.

Salban went down to the basement and rummaged around for a while, finally producing a small coin purse. He counted out four gold pieces and placed them on the table. Jace picked up a gold coin and glanced at the severely worn image of Mountain King Modan on it. "Theramoke is Maraudon."

"How can that be?" Salban said in surprise. "I know Maraudon — plenty of goblins have heard of that place. That's a huge tomb! You're saying that crocodile is hiding inside the centaur burial grounds? How does he live, by eating centaur corpses?"

Had to admit this dwarf knew quite a bit. Jace had originally thought he'd need to explain what Maraudon was — looks like this Hunters' Guild actually had some substance.

"Deep inside the centaur burial grounds is another world," Jace said. "In the ancient legends of the taurens, they once summoned an ancient deity at that place called Maraudon, and the centaurs are the offspring of that deity. Later they angered this deity and were driven out of the cavern. From then on, they treated the entrance to that cavern as sacred ground."

Salban said, "So to hunt that crocodile, we'd have to break through the heavy defenses of powerful centaur tribes?"

"I won't let you waste your money," Jace said, extending his hand. "Paper and pen."

Hajik immediately spread out a beautiful piece of parchment and handed Jace a griffin quill. Jace dipped it in ink and thought for a moment, then roughly sketched the western part of Desolace on the paper, including the relative positions of Spear Valley and Maraudon, then roughly drew the coastline, and even sketched the forest beside it.

Finally, he drew a cross at about the position of the cavern from which their party had finally exited Maraudon, pointed at it, and said, "Right by the coast, a cavern buried by the woods by the sea. The waterway inside leads all the way to the deepest part of Maraudon, saving you the trouble of dealing with the centaurs, or facing the enraged ancient elementals inside the cavern head-on."

"What?" Salban asked. "There really are ancient elementals inside?"

"There really are, and they're quite powerful. I advise you to keep things as low-key as possible and move through the waterway — don't leave the waterway and enter the deep parts of the cavern," Jace warned them. "If you notice the surrounding vegetation is overly dense, that's when you stop. Once you provoke something you shouldn't provoke, your chances of coming back alive are slim."
