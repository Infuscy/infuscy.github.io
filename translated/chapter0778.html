Chapter 778: All a Misunderstanding

"Maybe we should cooperate with the Explorers' League," Salban said, then discussed something more with Hajik.

Jace stuffed the gold into his bag and said, "That's not my problem. You discuss how to proceed amongst yourselves. I've told you everything I know. Good luck."

"Of course," Hajik said. "Thank you truly, Mr. Sesso, for providing such important information. Theramoke… it had almost been forgotten by the hunters of Ironforge. Being able to hear news of that crocodile again is exciting for all of us. Is there anything else we can help you with?"

Jace was just about to say no, when he suddenly thought of the main purpose of this trip to Ironforge. "I heard there are members of the Thorium Brotherhood in Ironforge. I'd like to meet them."

"If you ask me, there's no need to deal with those ill-tempered Dark Iron. The Great Anvil has plenty of highly skilled Bronzebeard blacksmiths, Mr. Sesso," Hajik said with an awkward smile. "If you want to have a set of armor or weapons made for yourself or a friend, you can completely have your wishes fulfilled at the Great Anvil — even magical items would be no problem."

"I'm here to investigate something," Jace said.

"Related to dark magic?" Salban put down the scroll and looked up to ask.

"I'd rather not say," Jace said.

"So that's how it is. Fine — they live on the other side," Hajik said, glancing at Salban. "I'm not mistaken, am I?"

"That's what I remember too," Salban said. "That side's not easy to get to, Mr. Sesso."

The two dwarves conversed in Dwarvish for a while. Salban said, "We have someone here who knows where the Thorium Brotherhood is. I'll get him up and have him take you there."

Jace stood up and picked up his cloak. "Much appreciated."

"Oh, right," Hajik said. "So that cloak… it really is black dragon hide?"

"Of course," Jace said.

Hajik stared at Jace's hood and swallowed.

Earlier, Grede had mentioned that the Thorium Brotherhood was under surveillance in Ironforge. Jace had thought he'd need to get in touch with Ironforge's authorities to meet them — he hadn't expected it to be this convenient.

Jace really didn't want to stir up the king here again, because who knew what kind of ceremony he'd have to attend then, or how long he'd have to stand before those dwarf kings and nobles. This half-year, from Stormwind Keep, to the royal palace of Menethil, to the Farstrider Enclave, going through these repetitive commendations, banquets, and all kinds of pleasantries over and over again, he was already utterly exhausted.

Going to Grim Batol had been to resolve a problem — who knew the problems would keep coming one after another.

Leaving the Hunters' Guild and that whole stretch of inns, Jace walked toward the opposite side of this ring-shaped district, led by the hunter. He gradually entered an area full of smoke and heat.

This kind of environment, if placed in Stormwind or the royal city of Lordaeron, would naturally be recognized as a slum. But the word "slum" seemed a little too much for this area, because there were still many houses here that looked quite well-built — even more refined than the walls over by the inn district he'd first entered. It left Jace a little confused about exactly who lived here.

Judging from the appearance and dress of the residents here, they were generally older, powerfully built, and had stern expressions — very likely home to blacksmiths, laborers, or retired soldiers.

Jace figured that as an outsider, he probably wouldn't be able to figure out the specific structure of Ironforge's residential districts in a day or two.

The hunter brought Jace to a secluded corner and pointed to an iron gate on a flight of steps ahead and said, "This is it, Mr. Sesso — the Thorium Brotherhood. Go talk to them yourself!"

"Thank you for your trouble, sir."

Jace nodded, said his goodbyes to the hunter, and looked toward the old gate that looked like it had quite a bit of history.

Several lines of Dwarvish runes he couldn't read at all were carved into the pillars beside it, looking rather solemn. Jace suddenly thought he should have asked the hunter to help translate — but the hunter had already left.

Would these Dark Iron dwarves write on their gate that Dark Iron dwarves lived here?

Regardless, it was blazing hot here. Jace could clearly feel waves of heat rushing out from between the buildings along with the low rumbling coming from within the stone walls, baking him.

Walking up the steps and arriving at the gate, the scorching sensation became even more intense.

He knocked a few times on the iron gate, which felt like a prison door, then peeked through the small hole in the gate. There didn't seem to be anyone inside. He knocked hard a few more times — and to his surprise, the gate actually opened a crack on its own.

Wasn't it locked?

Jace pushed hard and shoved the gate open. Inside it was stiflingly hot, feeling like an oven — even his body, blessed by the red dragon, found it a little hard to take.

"Is anyone here?" he asked in Common, but no one answered.

Jace poked his head inside and looked around. A small brazier hung from the ceiling providing light, but there was no way this one little brazier could bake this stone room this hot, was there?

There wasn't much inside — it looked like an empty warehouse. But there was an open doorway inside. Jace hesitated for a moment, walked over, and looked in through the doorway. It was a corridor leading downward, lit by braziers one after another. There seemed to be light inside, and some noisy commotion — hard to tell if there was anyone in there.

"Hello!" Jace wiped the sweat from his forehead and shouted in Dwarvish down the corridor, but it was quickly swallowed by the enormous rumbling coming from within the walls.

Maybe it was simply because the noise here was too loud, so the people inside couldn't hear him speaking. Logically, if no one was around, the door shouldn't have been left wide open like this.

Jace walked down the steps, watching the flickering light at the bottom draw closer step by step, the air growing hotter and hotter. The atmosphere was getting a little eerie. Just as he was about to reach the very bottom, the light suddenly flickered.

"Hello?" Jace stopped and asked again in Dwarvish.

The moment the words left his mouth, the firelight suddenly grew brighter and the air rapidly heated up. Jace watched with wide eyes as a blazing mass of fierce fire shot up from below!

What the hell?!

His whole body jolted and he immediately turned and ran. The ball of fire climbed up the steps, uttering one heavy, unintelligible murmur after another as it chased after him. Jace glanced back and saw the flames waving two arm-like pillars of fire, lunging straight at him — and on those fire pillars, there were even two gleaming elemental bracers!

Those were bracers used to bind elemental beings to this plane — this was actually a fire elementals summoned here!

Seeing the other party closing in, Jace felt like his clothes were about to catch fire. He immediately turned and recited an incantation at the burning monster charging at him. The shadow in his hands gathered more and more densely, even rapidly overwhelming the blinding bright flames that nearly seared his eyes!

He roared and hurled a Shadow Bolt straight into its face. The shadow power drilled into the heart of the flames and immediately devoured most of its brightness. The fire elemental struggled on the spot and gradually went out, the cracked bracers clattering to the ground. The corridor echoed with the ethereal, low voice of the elemental.

"Welcome… to the Thorium Brotherhood's quarters… human. Are you here to have something forged… or to learn… stranger… welcome… mm… I'm free…"

Jace was dumbfounded — this was actually Common.

The air cooled down quite a bit. The sweat was ice-cold. The reek of shadow was everywhere — not because of some sinister Dark Iron dwarf, but because of his own dark magic.

"Everything's completely fucked up!" a roar suddenly came from behind.

Jace turned his head. A Dark Iron dwarf was at the top, his blood-red eyes wide open, aiming the black muzzle of a gun straight at him.

"I just went out to buy a bread, and my Fedrei got sent back to his hometown by a single one of your moves! I only just managed to summon him — what the hell are you trying to do, warlock? I don't know you — do we have some kind of grudge?!"
