## Chapter 779: What a Warlock Can Offer

"Misunderstanding!" Jace raised his hands. "Don't shoot — I mean no harm, I'm here to—"

"Misunderstanding?!" the Dark Iron dwarf roared, cutting him off, waving the gun in his hand. "You killed your pet and then tell me it's just a misunderstanding — so can I blow your head off and say it was just a joke?!"

Jace watched the dwarf, who, as he yelled, had his finger twitch on the trigger. Jace's palm tightened, and a red line suddenly yanked the long gun in the dwarf's hands!

A gunshot cracked. The bullet bounced back and forth through the narrow corridor, setting off a string of harsh noise. Jace frowned and gripped the gun — the barrel had already been wrenched out of shape by a powerful Bloodgrasp.

This was even harder to explain now.

"I'll kill you!"

The Dark Iron dwarf pulled a dagger from his pocket and leaped down from above. Jace grabbed his wrist with one hand and pressed his head down with the other. The Dark Iron dwarf shrieked and kicked and flailed, his strength absurdly immense, but Jace could at least still restrain him — no matter how he kicked or swung, he couldn't land a blow.

"It really is a misunderstanding, sir. I'm not here to kill anyone. I just came to visit. I never expected a fire elemental to come charging out — I was just trying to protect myself on reflex!"

"And I'm protecting myself too! Protecting my home!"

After the dwarf finished shouting, he muttered a few incantations. The dagger in the hand Jace was holding suddenly became wreathed in tongues of flame!

The flames wrapped around the dagger, spinning faster and faster, and in a moment they'd burned onto Jace's sleeve. But Jace didn't let go at all, letting the flames scorch his wrist, burning through his sleeve — his skin only gradually turning red, with no severe burns at all.

The Dark Iron dwarf was clearly startled by Jace's formidable fire resistance. He gritted his teeth and wrestled with Jace, cursing all the while. "Who the hell are you?! What the hell are you?!"

"Jace Sesso," Jace said. "I'm from Stormwind…"

Hearing his name, the Dark Iron dwarf froze for a moment. "Jace Sesso — am I thinking of the right person? You killed Deathwing? I don't believe it… with those two bits of yours, you could fight the most terrifying father of all black dragons in this world? Either the red dragons have gone mad, or I have!"

Jace gripped his wrist and said, "Because strictly speaking I didn't kill him, I just… damn it, it's really hard to explain. Put the dagger away first, sir!"

"Let go… let go!" The dwarf's voice dropped from a roar to a low groan. "You don't look all that solid, you little whelp, so why are you this strong? Let go — my wrist is going to shatter!"

"Then I'm letting go — you're not allowed to attack again." Jace said.

"I can't even beat you!" the dwarf shouted.

Jace tried letting go and took two steps back. The dwarf glanced at the firearm Jace had thrown on the ground, then looked at the damaged elemental bracers that had rolled down below. "Fedrei speaks Common. Couldn't you understand what he said?"

Jace replied, "The noise around here is too intense, sir. This is my first time in Ironforge — I'm not used to the environment here yet, so I didn't hear what he said clearly. I'm sorry."

The dwarf straightened his clothes and beard and fixed a cold stare on him. After a long while he asked, "You really are Jace Sesso?"

"The genuine article," Jace said, extending his hand to show the rings on his fingers. "My name's even on these."

The dwarf looked at those rings. His skin was almost pure black, and his brownish-red big beard moved as he spoke, like leaping flames. When he stared intently at the rings on Jace's hand, Jace noticed his eyes — terrifying, like a pool of blood.

Was this the appearance seared out by the flames of the Firelord Ragnaros himself? Or some dark curse of the Old Gods…

"Court Mage's sigil of Stormwind, Lordaeron knight's insignia," the dwarf said. "That Court Mage sigil looks right, I just don't know how to tell real from fake. But that Lordaeron ring looks real to me, all right."

"My name's Ruige. As you can see, I'm a Dark Iron. I suppose this really might be a misunderstanding. What are you here for, Sesso? You looking for the Thorium Brotherhood?"

Jace let out a breath and confessed, "I heard in Stormwind about the Dark Iron dwarves — especially your Thorium Brotherhood's skill in manipulating fire. So I came here to learn fire magic."

Hearing Jace praise the Dark Iron clan's connection to fire, the dwarf raised his head slightly, looking a little pleased.

"Fire magic? I don't know any 'magic.' If you want to learn magic, go to Dalaran."

"Not magic, exactly," Jace said. "But the techniques for communicating with fire elementals." The dwarf frowned. "You want to learn our forging techniques?"

Jace shook his head. Ruige glared and said, "Then you want to become a Flamebender?!"

"Flamebender?"

"Flamebenders are in Shadowforge City — a group that's… hard to explain to you. In short, they're those who don't intend to put fire to use in the smithing trade. But you're not a Dark Iron dwarf. Being a Flamebender isn't something you can just become if you want to. First of all, communicating with elementals is a talent! We have to see whether you have that kind of talent!" The dwarf grumbled, picked up his firearm wrenched out of shape by magic, looked it over, and continued, "And first, let's resolve the matter at hand."

"Whether you're Sesso, or Turalyon, or some other famous figure, you have to compensate me for killing my minion. I went through all that trouble to summon that fire elemental servant here — the rune tablets, the energy spent… it's all worth a fortune, not to mention I only just taught him to say a few words of Common. I memorized that whole long speech, and you destroyed it all! Whatever you're planning to find the Thorium Brotherhood for, it won't happen without compensating me for my losses!"

"I'm truly sorry," Jace said. "I admit I have some prejudices about your Dark Iron kin, so I was too tense when I came in. I saw flames and thought someone was ambushing me — and the fire elemental you summoned did indeed possess very powerful energy…"

"I'm a Dark Iron dwarf, but I'm not a member of the Dark Iron clan. Watch your words, human," Ruige said, pointing at him. "No point making excuses. I don't want to waste any more time with you. Thirty gold — and that settles everything."

"Thirty?!" Jace nearly had his eyeballs scared out of his head. "I can't come up with that much money."

"That much?" the dwarf said angrily. "You're a Court Mage of Stormwind — don't think I don't know how rich your kind are. Look at the black dragon hide cloak you're wearing. That single piece alone is worth I don't know how many gold — a hundred, several hundred? What kind of person walks the streets wearing something that expensive?!"

"But you know this cloak can't be bought with money alone," Jace said. "I really don't have the cash. Is there any other viable way to compensate? Like you just said — summoning a fire elemental takes a tremendous amount of energy. As it happens, I might have a bit more energy than most. I could try summoning a fire elemental to compensate you for your losses, sir."

Ruige snorted. "Have you been spending too much time with Bronzebeards, thinking all dwarves should be as dumb as those lime-covered things? You want to use this chance to get me to teach you how to summon a fire elemental? Not a bad calculation, human."

He looked the young "invader" up and down and said, "But you've reminded me of something. You wiped out Fedrei with a shadow spell, didn't you?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Then it's no mistake to call you a warlock," Ruige said with a smile. "Since you're a warlock, then you must have some unusual abilities. If you can provide something special that I need, I can consider that as compensation."

Could it be that this Ruige wanted to learn some dark incantation?

Even Dark Iron dwarves who'd broken away from the Dark Iron clan wouldn't immediately become a bunch of kind, upright people. What's more, they had a higher tolerance for dark magic to begin with. If he wanted to learn some shadow magic, Jace wouldn't be surprised in the slightest.

And if he could learn the so-called "flamebending" techniques by teaching some incantations in exchange, without spending any money, that wouldn't be bad either.

"What do you want?" Jace asked.

"As it happens, I want to know some things related to demons," Ruige said, slinging the bent gun over his shoulder and picking up the now-colorless elemental bracers one by one. "Come down with me, human. Don't stand around here like a fool."
