## Chapter 78: Light (Part Two)

Jace kept thinking about that pathetic little spark he'd conjured — he wanted to try again, because he didn't feel for a second that casting at this level had reached his limit.

Marin rarely got anything wrong, but this time the fatigue he'd mentioned was completely absent.

That's not to say there was nothing at all — he did feel less refreshed than when he'd first woken up that morning — but how much of that had to do with two measly incantations?

Even granting the worst-case interpretation, it was nowhere near a level that demanded immediate rest.

"Master Marin, can you train your mind by casting spells nonstop? For example, if I speak two incantations now and feel a little tired from communicating with the ley lines twice, and I keep pushing myself, can I improve that capacity after a period of time?"

Marin nodded. "Of course you can, but remember what I just said — don't overestimate what your body can handle. Take it step by step. Moreover, the quickest way to increase the number of spells you can cast isn't by training your mental endurance, but by honing your casting technique and concentration."

"Casting technique refers to standard incantation pronunciation, gestures, and so on — in other words, the casting-related conditions I mentioned earlier. Every spell has techniques, developed through practice, that reduce its cost. Some Dalaran mages call these mana conservation techniques. Many scholars regard mental energy as a consumable and replenishable resource — mana, in other words — much like the ley line energy that runs through this world. Viewed that way, seeking techniques to conserve mental expenditure can certainly be seen as a form of energy efficiency."

"Concentration, on the other hand, focuses more on your inner self. If casting technique is the skill you need to master, then concentration is the degree to which you focus yourself on the act of casting during the process. The more focused you are, the less mental energy you consume. This is also why mages rarely throw themselves into the front lines of battle — they provide support from outside the fray. They need to cast as focused as possible, that way they can sustainably support their allies."

"Mastering these two things is far more effective than recklessly training your mental willpower. And as you train these two abilities, you'll gradually find your mental ceiling rising as well."

Jace understood now. The reason he'd felt completely drained after casting Fear just a few times before was probably because he'd been flailing about in panic, watching the enemy's lines of attack, trying to squeeze spells into whatever openings he could find — all while paying zero attention to casting technique or proper incantation. Casting done that way was guaranteed to be both ineffective and wildly inefficient.

But then again, was Shadow magic really the same as Arcane magic?

There should be some common ground, at least.

"Let's take it one goal at a time," Marin said. "Let's start with Light. Your first task is to maintain Light for over a minute. For an apprentice, this is a very fundamental exercise, and for an ordinary person, this requirement won't cause serious overexertion. If you achieve it, come to me for a simple test. Once you pass, we'll move on to the next step. I'll give you two weeks to try."

He pulled from behind him a book with yellowed, curling pages — *Felis: Fundamental Arcane Casting Techniques* — and said, "Page twenty-two, the essentials of casting Light. Read the first part for now. Don't worry about the advanced variations in the back — just focus on nailing down the basics."

"Yes, Master." Jace took the book and flipped through it. Light really was an extremely simple spell — its principle was straightforward: just gather arcane energy and emit light. When he was a child in the capital of Lordaeron, he'd seen young apprentices and even children fooling around with it.

After his Orcish lesson that afternoon, Jace planned to head to The Slaughtered Lamb and ask Ursula Delyn in the basement about the methods for brewing healing potions and mana potions.

After entering the tavern's basement, he spoke a "Liyouisa" and cradled a tiny point of light in his palm, illuminating the cell door and the dark passage before him.

The point of light lasted several seconds and felt quite stable, so he reached for the cell door. But just as his hand was about to grasp the handle, the light suddenly flickered out of control and dissipated into the air, leaving behind only a faint Arcane afterglow tinged with pale purple.

He sighed, stepped through the cell door, and walked into the darkness.

That afternoon, the basement of The Slaughtered Lamb was empty. Gagin, Sulenna, and Erland were all out. Only Galdus was sitting in the main hall, reading.

His servant, an imp named Jiaoji, was holding a book. All it took was a look from Galdus and Jiaoji would immediately understand his meaning and hastily turn the page, while his master lounged lazily in his chair, completely unconcerned with how long the imp had been holding the book up.

"If only Sazeno could be this well-behaved," Jace thought to himself.

"Jace Sesso, you're here." Galdus looked up. "How are you finding that summoning magic? Has any demon suited to your temperament decided to stick around?"

"Running around Stormwind with an imp in tow is still pretty dangerous," Jace said.

Galdus said, "If you have too much business in Stormwind, your concern is understandable. But if you rarely wander about like I do, keeping a demon around isn't much trouble — especially since imps have their own methods of invisibility."

"Although an imp will unconditionally obey its master's commands, it still has its own temper, personality, even likes and dislikes. I don't want you to treat summoned demons as disposable tools the way Gagin does. For one thing, summoning magic itself is costly, and each time it requires a safe environment to cast. For another, every summoning brings a demon you've never met before, with no familiarity or rapport built up — I find that unsettling. I can't trust it."

"Thanks, Galdus. I'll think about what you've said," Jace replied.

"Don't mention it. By the way, is there something you came for?" Galdus said. "I don't recall Gagin calling a meeting."

"Is Ms. Delyn here?" Jace asked.

Galdus said, "Ursula is in the room at the very end of the middle doorway — you'll smell it. Our alchemy lab."

Following Galdus's directions, Jace walked to the end of the corridor and indeed caught a whiff of a potent medicinal stench.

There seemed to be Grave Moss in that smell. In the alchemy lab, a woman wearing only a loose tank top and rolled-up wide-legged trousers was squatting there stirring a crucible, completely oblivious to Jace's footsteps behind her.

The room was already unbearably hot. Although the weather had turned cool, the basement's ventilation wasn't much to speak of — there was only a single vent of unknown effectiveness leading who-knows-where. It probably helped a little, but only to the extent of keeping the room from literally steaming someone to death.

"Ms. Ursula Delyn?" Jace greeted her softly.

Ursula turned around, her forehead and face already beaded with sweat, her once-voluminous hair damp and clinging to her neck.

She caught her breath, nodded, and said, "Jace Sesso — I still remember your name. What can I do for you?"
