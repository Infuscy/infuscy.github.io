## Chapter 780: Replacing the Minion

Something to do with demons?

Could this Dark Iron dwarf be trying to summon a demon minion to replace the fire elemental?

No, that couldn't be right. Ironforge would never allow a demon to openly stand guard here for the Thorium Brotherhood — working as a gatehouse sentry or a front-desk receptionist, or whatever.

But Jace didn't ask. He just followed Ruige down the staircase.

Below was a deep underground corridor. The rooms on either side held a good number of metal objects — some displayed on shelves, others hanging from the walls. Some looked like weapons, some like tools, and quite a few were probably unfinished pieces.

Ruige tossed two damaged element wrist-guards casually onto one of the tool piles. Jace asked, "Are you the only one here?"

"Most of the time," Ruige said. "This place is too far from the Great Forge's anvils. When Thorium Brotherhood members come to Ironforge, they generally don't want to stay down here. I'm here to watch over the Brotherhood's property — this building — and see if I can help drum up some business for them."

"This is the Thorium Brotherhood's property?" Jace asked. "Did you buy it here in Ironforge?"

"No," Ruige said. "A lot of dwarves have forgotten their history, same as you. The Thorium Brotherhood existed long before the War of the Three Hammers. It's just that back then, our leader chose to stand with the Dark Iron. In the end, the Dark Iron clan was defeated and driven out of Ironforge, but the Bronzebeard sovereign didn't liquidate the Brotherhood along with them. He let us keep our property. This house was one of those holdings."

The dwarf led Jace across the way to a dim room, brushed the dust off a stone ledge with his hand, and said, "Sit."

He sat himself down on a low stool opposite the ledge. After a long silence, he said, "You can summon demons, can't you, Jace Sesso? I've heard some rumors about you. You killed a demon lord named Azgoth at Nethergarde Keep with some terrifying dark magic, and you've got thirteen demon maids attending you at all times."

"I know succubi can turn invisible." He glanced meaningfully at the space beside Jace. "Are they here right now? Can you let me have a look?"

"I don't have thirteen demon maids," Jace said. "And don't you think this room would be awfully cramped for fifteen of us?"

The Dark Iron dwarf nodded. "I figured that rumor was a load of nonsense. If you had thirteen demon maids, how would you ever keep up with all of them? But that's a real shame — I was hoping you might let me have one…"

So that was how it was.

Jace leaned against the warm stone wall. "So it's a succubus you're after."

"What's wrong with that?" Ruige said. "Summoning a succubus… what man wouldn't want one? If you can't pay back the thirty gold, just give me a succubus and we'll call it even."

"That fire elemental of yours isn't all that important to you. It's nowhere near worth thirty gold," Jace said.

"It's extremely important. But if I can have a succubus, then it doesn't matter anymore, Sesso." Ruige asked, a little anxiously, "So even though you don't have all those demon maids, you really can summon a sayaad demon, right?"

Jace had the feeling this fellow had already half-forgotten about the fire elemental. The dynamic had shifted — it no longer felt like Ruige was doing him a favor. It felt like Ruige was the one asking.

"Of course," he said evenly.

Ruige clenched his teeth in excitement and let out a dwarven phrase, then said, "Summon a succubus. Make her obey my every command — every command. If you can do that, I won't hold you responsible for killing Fedre. I can even teach you some basics on how to communicate with flame, as a starting point…"

"You can teach me?" Jace asked, surprised.

"Isn't that perfectly fair?" Ruige said. "You introduce me to demons, I introduce you to fire. We each get what we need. I was an apprentice fire-tender once, back in the day. I parted ways with Shadowforge City over a difference in philosophy, but the fact that I was able to join the Thorium Brotherhood means I've got at least some real skill — far more than a mere apprentice's level."

"Introducing you to demons isn't a problem," Jace said. "But I can't help you summon a succubus, Ruige. The incantation for summoning a succubus is part of the pact itself. A succubus I summon into this world means she's accepting *my* invitation. So even if I forced her to follow your orders, she'd still be *my* minion, not yours."

Of course, what Jace was actually worried about was Morofil's reaction.

The last time he'd so much as gotten close to another succubus out in the world, Morofil had been furious when he got back. Now he was openly summoning another succubus and forming a pact with her… God only knew what the consequences would be. Even if Morofil obeyed his orders unconditionally, Jace wouldn't be able to sleep at night.

"In other words, you'd be summoning a succubus of your own."

"Really? You'd teach me the incantation?" Ruige said. "I've barely even met a warlock who could summon a succubus, let alone one willing to teach me the incantation!"

"Just remember your promise," Jace said.

"No problem." Ruige stood up excitedly. "Besides the incantation, what else do I need to prepare? Spell components, a ritual circle, an offering?"

The fellow really did know his stuff. Teaching him to summon a succubus shouldn't be too hard.

Jace said, "For components, you'll need a soul shard. I'll handle the ritual circle. For the offering, you'll need a token connected to love."

"A token of love?" the Dark Iron dwarf asked. "What's that? Can you be more specific?"

"For instance…" Jace said, "a wedding ring, a love token — anything connected to love will do. What kind of demon you attract depends on how intense the emotion tied to that object is."

"So the range is pretty broad." Ruige nodded in understanding. "Then that's not a problem. As for the soul shard… what is that? Sounds hard to come by."

"Here." Jace pulled a small soul shard from his pack. "Since I killed your fire elemental minion, I'll use mine — as part of the compensation."

After a full day of preparation — gathering materials, memorizing the incantation, and so on — by the next evening, Ruige had hung a sign written in Dwarvish on the outside door and locked it tight.

Jace asked, "What do those dwarven runes by the door say?"

"'The Friendly Thorium Brotherhood — Wondrous Flame Elements Shall Guide You to Know Our Craft,'" Ruige translated into Common. Then, as if something had just clicked, he turned back and said, "You don't understand Dwarvish! So that's why you killed Fedre…"

"Again, my apologies," Jace said.

"Well, no matter. Dragon-slayer. It would be my honor to teach you the ways of flame. Compared to that, a not-very-bright elemental creature barely counts for anything."

The Dark Iron dwarf smiled. His attitude was completely different from before.

Jace figured that to him, titles like "dragon-slayer" or the feat of storming Grim Batol probably didn't matter all that much. What mattered most was getting someone to help him summon a succubus.

Jace came to an empty room and, with practiced ease, drew a shadow-enhancing ritual circle using the wolf's blood Ruige had bought from a hunter. After his earlier attempts in the Dagger Society, he'd learned that this kind of shadow-enhancing circle was a big help for a novice summoning a demon for the first time. It had been enormously useful even for a human like Erland Dodmoor, who didn't have much natural shadow talent — let alone for a Dark Iron dwarf who already knew a thing or two about summoning.

Ruige watched Jace finish drawing the circle, his eyes wide with admiration, his tongue clicking in awe. Jace glanced at him and held out his hand. "The summoning token, Mr. Ruige."

The dwarf pulled a black iron ring from his pocket and handed it to Jace. "This thing. Hope it works."

Jace nodded. He had no interest in asking about the story behind the ring. As long as the dwarf was certain it was connected to love, that was enough.

He placed the token on one side of the circle, tossed the soul shard over it, and Ruige took the slip of paper with the incantation Jace had written out and silently read it through once. "I've more or less memorized it. Can we give it a try? Will you keep me safe? Can you handle a succubus on your own, Mr. Sesso?"

Jace said nothing. He just drew Skysong partway from its sheath and slid it back in. In that instant, a brilliant arcane radiance flooded the entire room with a silver-blue glow. That suppressed, powerful magical energy — anyone with even the slightest connection to magic would have felt its force.

Ruige swallowed. "Then I'll begin."

Jace nodded. The Dark Iron dwarf gazed at the symbols on the circle, then slowly closed his eyes and began chanting the incantation in Eredun. "Inhajet, tulioksenoni…"

His pronunciation was remarkably standard, with only a minor, forgivable dwarven accent. Jace even suspected he might have studied Eredun before. This might just be a racial talent of the Dark Iron clan — after all, both their Dwarvish and their Common had that indescribable, off-kilter flavor, as if they were viciously muttering Eredun under their breath.

Halfway through the incantation, Jace noticed the iron ring tremble. He held his breath and raised his guard. That meant the incantation was starting to take effect.

Dark Iron dwarves really did have far more talent for dark magic than humans. No wonder those Dark Iron warlocks looked down on the humans of Stormwind so much — no wonder Galders got so pissed off.

"…Tayatansa yidini, binoduxisi."

The incantation completed, and the whole room went dark. That was the sign of a sayaad's arrival.

"What's happening?" Ruige looked around. "Did it fail? Why hasn't a demon appeared?"

The soul shard had already expended its energy. There was no doubt that Ruige had made contact with some entity in the Twisting Nether, established a connection, and that the other side had answered his call.

Jace used Detect Demons to scan the space ahead and immediately sensed a shadow skittering along the wall, closing in fast — and Ruige had no idea what was going on!

"Duk, hedu niloinmos!"

He shouted the incantation at the top of his lungs and thrust his hand toward the shadow. Chaotic void energy in the room instantly obeyed his command, coalescing into chains of shadow that slammed the shadow into the corner!

The Suffocation spell clamped tight around the demon's neck. The sayaad materialized in the shadows, thrashing and struggling, clawing to break free of Jace's magic.

This was a remarkably powerful succubus — muscles taut all over, her garments a savage, crude arrangement of iron chains and leather straps. She carried a hooked battle-lance in her hand, looking more like a bloodthirsty female thug than the kind of creature that lured mortals with beauty and lust.

She looked up at Ruige, then at Jace. "So which one of you wants to be my master, hmm? My summoner doesn't look much like a warlock! But you, human — I want you more… Ha! Let me go, and I'll give you a night you'll never forget!"

The succubus howled wildly. Jace kept the demon suppressed and said, "This might not be quite what you were expecting, Mr. Ruige. Want me to kill her and try again? I'm thinking it might be the token."

"No," Ruige said.

Jace looked over at the Dark Iron dwarf and found him staring, transfixed.

"This is exactly what I wanted… I'm after this type. I… ahem… what do I do next? How do I become her master?!" Ruige asked eagerly.
