Chapter 783: The Difference Between Elements and the Arcane

The next morning, Jace went out into Ironforge and spent a few silver on a proper scabbard. After all, walking down the street carrying a sword reforged from the Blue Dragonlord's scales — radiating arcane light and humming a faint melody wherever it cut the air — was way too conspicuous.

The previous robe hadn't been completely destroyed, but it was covered in burn holes, so he went up to the upper level to buy a new outer garment.

Finding clothes in Ironforge that fit a human properly was damned near impossible. He could only go ask at the human-run trade houses, and in the end he just bought a secondhand garment someone else had worn before.

On the bright side, it was cheap. He didn't need to keep up any appearances here in Ironforge.

When he got back to the Thorium Brotherhood's basement, the narrow corridor echoed endlessly with the dwarf's grunting and the sayaad's cries. Jace couldn't help but frown.

He couldn't tell whether the two of it had been going at it all night, or whether they'd woken up in the morning and picked up where they'd left off — but listening to the dwarf's ragged coughing now, he was starting to feel a twinge of guilt.

When the dwarf pushed the door open at noon, a rich sayaad musk wafted out of the room. Jace couldn't help waving his hand and turning away. Ruige stumbled out, propping himself against the wall. Since his skin was pitch-black, you couldn't tell if he had dark circles, but his blood-red eyes were clearly not as bright as they'd been the night before.

"Sesso, is your succubus mean to you?" Ruige fastened his belt, hitched up his robe, and tottered to the door. He took off his hat and said, "Because otherwise, I can't imagine you going on a long trip without your succubus. Or is your servant just not good at turning invisible?"

"She's very good to me," Jace said. "It's just that I need mana to protect myself, and she can never restrain herself from draining too much. It affects my condition the next day, so you'd better watch out too."

"So you're just not man enough," Ruige said.

"Remember this, Ruige," Jace warned him. "Succubi are ultimately after their master's mana. Demans are creatures that pursue mana — from pit lords, to felhounds, to imps… so you'd better rein it in a little. Don't hurt yourself."

"Miss Gaiada's been so good to her. What's the harm in letting her have a little more mana?" Ruige snorted. "Otherwise, what am I saving my mana for? Right now the Brotherhood just has me standing guard here. I'm not involved in any real work, and I don't need to get into conflicts with anyone."

"I'm just advising," Jace said. "Remember to order that succubus not to use her seduction magic on you — her master. Whether you love her or she loves you, you don't want to slowly turn into her flesh puppet, do you?"

Ruige hesitated, licking his lips. "You're right. I'll remember. Alright, I'm heading out. I'm going to borrow that book for you. Probably won't be back until evening. Wait here. And remember — most of what's in here isn't my personal property. You'd better not go conducting any magic experiments on your own and turn my room into a disaster. I won't be able to explain myself to the Brotherhood if you do. And if you still want to stay on good terms with the Thorium Brotherhood, it won't do you any favors either!"

"I understand," Jace nodded.

The iron door clanged shut from up the corridor, leaving only a faint echo. Everything went quiet, leaving only the muffled rumble of Ironforge's depths.

Jace sat there alone, propping his cheek with his hand, thinking about what he'd just said to Ruige. There was something of an outsider's clarity and an insider's blindness to it.

He knew in his heart that the reason Morofil had answered his summons was simply because she was drawn to his mana. Like he'd said — demons all pursued mana, pursued growing stronger. A weaker demon sought out a master to follow, seizing the chance to grow stronger along the way, which offered far better odds than drifting aimlessly through the Twisting Nether.

And for a demon, dying in the Twisting Nether meant true annihilation.

But often enough, the things Morofil said and did would move Jace despite himself. He had to constantly remind himself that she was a demon just to stay somewhat clear-headed.

Thinking of it, that smug smile of hers seemed to appear before his eyes again.

—— Am I about to win your heart, Master? Your willpower isn't as strong as you think.

Jace knew his willpower had never been as firm as others believed. Beneath the surface of decisiveness and courage, there was far too much hesitation, confusion — even cowardice and retreat.

And Morofil understood that far better than Wendersa, or even Grede. This demon…

While he waited for Ruige to come back, Jace couldn't afford to sit idle either.

He just couldn't practice fel fire incantations here in Ironforge, and Ruige had specifically told him not to practice magic in this room. That didn't leave him with much to do.

So Jace simply took out some paper and started writing the report on Deathwing that Marin had asked for.

When Marin first told him, hoping he could summarize the Grim Batol affair, Jace had agreed readily enough. But for a long time after, every time he thought about starting to write, he'd felt reluctant to face that period.

But after returning from Lordaeron — or rather, after returning from Quel'Thalas — he'd more or less accepted the scars that period had left on his mind.

He traced the scar on his arm, now faded and indistinct, and thought of that river outside Vereesa's village…

As long as she was still by his side, as long as everyone he cherished was still here, as long as these people understood what his efforts were for — then there was nothing to fear.

That evening, Ruige came back from outside, carrying a thick tome.

He set the book down on the table. Jace glanced at the cover and saw a long string of Dwarvish characters.

"Khalimag — the elemental tongue. Or, well, the Kalimdor language." The Dark Iron dwarf slapped the book's stiff cover. "Over the next stretch of time, you can use this book to practice communicating with elementals."

Jace flipped through the book a few pages and then realized that apart from the elemental language sections, everything inside was in Dwarvish. "I don't know Dwarvish. What do I do?"

"Dwarvish isn't hard to learn, especially for someone from a mage academy," Ruige said. "The Wizard Sanctum might not be as impressive as Dalaran, but I know its official mage credentials still carry real weight."

"I'm not an official mage," Jace said.

"Aren't you Varian Wrynn's magical advisor?" Ruige asked.

"That's a court mage. It's a completely different organization from the Wizard Sanctum — no promotion track between them." Jace said. That was how it was supposed to work, but Jace still cleared his throat, a little sheepishly.

"Isn't there another edition of this book? Like a Common translation?"

"I don't know. Why don't you check the Grand Library in Dalaran?" Ruige said. "But as far as I know, nobody's translated it into Common. Because people who plan to learn the elemental tongue… most of them already know Dwarvish."

"So I need a Dwarvish-Common dictionary too." Jace rubbed his temples. "Can I take this book back to Stormwind?"

"Sure." Ruige said. "Just leave collateral. This book is the Thorium Brotherhood's property."

"How much?" Jace asked.

"Thirty gold," Ruige said.

"Thirty gold again?" Jace frowned. "Why do you keep asking for thirty gold? Did you owe someone thirty gold and plan to have me pay it off?"

Ruige dodged Jace's eyes and coughed twice. "Save the nonsense. If you don't pay, you don't take it. You can read it here. I'll find you a Dwarvish dictionary."

Thinking he didn't have much else to do for the month, Jace figured he might as well stay at the Thorium Brotherhood and further his study of communicating with elementals.

He just needed to write a letter to Wendersa, letting her know he wouldn't be in Stormwind. "Then I'll read it here."

Ruige stared at Jace. "You're really going to stay and read here?"

"Can't I stay?" Jace asked.

"It's not that you can't." The dwarf hesitated a bit. "Then suit yourself. I suppose we're friends now."

Jace didn't take the bait on that "friends" business. He said, "Does this book contain any actual incantations? Like the one you taught me last night."

"Strictly speaking, that doesn't count as an incantation. If you insist on understanding it as one, I can't stop you," the dwarf said. "What do you mean?" Jace asked.

Ruige sat down across from Jace, pulled a cloth wrap from his pocket, and set it on the table. When he unfolded it, there was a whole baked loaf with a crispy crust.

He tore off a piece, stuffed it in his mouth, and said while chewing, "Of course, your arcane incantations are also a process of communicating with arcane energy. It's just that as I understand it, for the arcane energy drifting through the air — or in the 'ley lines' and 'arcane veins' — as long as you have the energy to convey your meaning to the arcane through an incantation, it will answer your request, right?"

"More or less," Jace said. "It's just that some people are more favored by the arcane, and some don't have that talent — or that arcane affinity."

Ruige nodded and continued, "But elementals are completely different. Elementals might answer your request, or they might not. It doesn't depend so much on the summoner's side. Some people are more favored by elementals — like you, last night. I don't know why the fire elementals answered your call so enthusiastically… but even people favored by elementals can be abandoned by them. For instance, if you use elementals to do things they dislike, or harm them."

"I know that," Jace said. "I've heard stories of people begging elementals for help, only for the elementals to stop answering them."

"Right. Dark Iron dwarves seem like they'll never be abandoned by flame."

Ruige's eyes went wide, as if Jace had said something idiotic.

"The Dark Iron clan in Shadowforge City will of course never be abandoned by flame — because they don't ask flame to do anything. They don't cooperate with flame. They willingly become flame's slaves! They are loyal hounds of the Firelord Ragnaros! So you understand now — fire elementals aren't as pure a thing as the arcane. I don't want to speak too ill of fire, but that's the facts."

"Are you going to keep trying tonight?" he asked Jace, staring at him. "Wielding fire elementals."

"Of course," Jace said.

Ruige said, "I'm quite interested in your flame affinity, too. Maybe someday you could join the Thorium Brotherhood and become a pretty decent blacksmith."

"Blacksmith?" Jace was taken aback. That was truly a path he'd never imagined.
