Chapter 784: The Wrath of Flame

In Ironforge, there was no difference between day and night at all, but you could tell the time from how lively things were outside. The livelier it got, the later the hour. When the whole city went quiet, that meant morning was near. When the noise picked up again, it was basically noon.

Stripping off his shirt, Jace followed Ruige to the room where the Dark Iron golem was. He could still hear the occasional burst of dwarven cackling drifting through the narrow window.

Ruige looked at the scars on Jace's body and said, "You're not quite what I pictured a human mage to be. You're more like a warrior, or an adventurer type."

"I'm not really a mage," Jace said.

The dwarf shrugged. "Yesterday was just a bit of trial — getting flame to help enchant your weapon isn't hard, after all. Fire elementals are always looking for something to burn. Today we'll do something harder: calling on fire elementals to protect you."

Marin had mentioned something similar. Using the arcane to manipulate fire elementals to destroy enemies was easy — like the Scorch spell, or Fireball. But getting flame to form a protective barrier wasn't so simple.

"So what should I say?" Jace asked.

Ruige said, "You can try saying: Endur, vendal, logal, menita. It means, 'Flame, please appear and protect me.'"

"Doesn't sound that hard," Jace said.

Ruige gave a soft laugh. "Making a request is always easy. You could walk up to the Bronzebeard king's throne and ask him to reward you with an Azerothian diamond. See if he agrees."

Jace nodded, looked toward the glow of the fire through the crack in the window, closed his eyes, and said, "Endur, vendal, logal, menita." This time, he carefully experienced the process of communicating with the fire elementals — wherever they were hiding — trying to sense the presences in the air that were answering him. It really did feel like he was having a conversation with someone.

He felt the area around him grow warmer bit by bit. When he opened his eyes, rings of fire had coiled around him.

It reminded him of the fire barrier Marin had cast, of course. It wasn't nearly as brilliant. It was even a little… thick.

"The fire elementals granted your wish right away!" Ruige said, astonished. "I don't understand. Did you secretly make some kind of offering to the Firelord? Do you know other Dark Iron dwarves?"

"You want to know how I did it?" Jace asked.

"Of course!" Ruige said. "Even among Dark Iron dwarves, not many are this favored by flame. At least, there aren't many in the Thorium Brotherhood."

The fire slowly died down. Jace held out his hand. "Thirty gold, and I'll tell you."

Ruige put on a cold face. Jace added, "Or letting me take that elemental language dictionary would work too."

The dwarf said, "I'm just curious. If you don't tell me, it's not as if I lose anything. It's probably something I couldn't do anyway. Don't try to take advantage of me, you scheming warlock."

"Then I won't tell you," Jace said.

"Can you sense the fire elementals answering you?" Ruige asked.

"Vaguely, a little. After I finished saying that incantation… it was like someone was answering my call," Jace said.

Ruige warned, "I'll tell you again — that's not an incantation. Strictly speaking, it's a kind of prayer, a wish. If you treat it as an incantation… it will seriously hold back your communication with fire elementals. And it's my fault — I shouldn't have used an incantation as an analogy earlier. That was a teaching mistake on my part."

"Here's what I can do. I can tell you the prayers that I or other fire-tenders use to call on flame to protect ourselves. But at this point, it's clear there's no benefit in it for me. You already found the trick of talking to flame last night. I'd rather you seek their help on your own. You still don't know the Kalimdor language. You can say what you want to say, and I'll translate it into the elemental tongue. Then you try repeating it."

Jace pointed at the golem. "Translate this for me: 'Flame, destroy this Dark Iron golem.'"

Ruige smiled. "I don't think you'll get a response that way, Sesso. That's a command — there's no respect in it at all. Elementals like respect. That's why our prayers are full of honorifics… I can help you modify it a bit."

With that, the Dark Iron dwarf raised his hands high and called out briefly in the elemental tongue. Then he stretched out his palms toward the golem. A ball of fire immediately exploded on the golem's body, black smoke pouring out, sparks crackling and flying before burning for a while and fading away.

"Flame Shock," the dwarf explained. "Very similar to what you just wanted me to translate. If you take out the honorifics…"

He raised his hands again and shouted a shorter sentence, releasing Flame Shock at the golem once more. But this time, aside from a wave of hot air sweeping past, the golem took no damage at all.

Ruige caught his breath. "The feeling of being rejected by flame is really unpleasant. You can experience it for yourself."

Jace nodded. Using the prayer Ruige had modified — the one with honorifics — he called on the fire elementals. When he raised his hand and pointed at the golem, he felt the eager, surging heat of the flame. A fierce fire gathered from the air and exploded on the golem along the line of his pointing. The whole golem shuddered.

Although the power wasn't as strong as what Ruige had called up, Ruige still nodded with satisfaction. "Not bad, not bad! That's it. You really are good fire-tender material, Sesso. And Ironforge really is a great place to learn to be a fire-tender! Now, try it without the honorifics. You need to get used to the feeling when the elementals don't answer your call sometimes. When that happens, remember to rely on yourself."

"Flame, help me destroy that golem."

Jace said it once in Common, then repeated it in the language Ruige had used the second time, pointing at the golem. As he communicated with flame again and again, accompanied by the sweltering heat that filled the deepest parts of Ironforge, he even began to feel the emotions of the fire!

He clearly sensed the fire's disgust, its unwillingness to help him. But they weren't silent. They weren't "not responding."

The fire elementals were expressing a different kind of answer through the rumbling sounds deep in Ironforge — they hated his command. They hated his attitude.

And yet Jace still felt a sudden blaze surround him, carrying the fire's angry curses and rebukes as it slammed into the golem before him. This explosion was even more violent than the last one!

BOOM!

The breastplate of the golem clanged to the ground, snapping Jace — who had been savoring the fire elementals' emotions — back to his senses. It even startled the dwarf.

This Flame Shock was far more powerful than any Jace had managed before. But Jace could also clearly feel the heat of the fire rapidly leaving him.

"The elementals are angry, Sesso." The dwarf leaned against the window and looked outside. "Your rude behavior offended the elementals!" Jace said innocently, "I just wanted to try… and I was following your instructions."

"I didn't think the elementals would respond to you at all!" Ruige sighed. "But it's no big deal. You can try to beg the elementals for forgiveness. I'm sure it's only temporary. After all, elementals only disappoint you if they have expectations of you. A complete fool doesn't even have the capital to offend elementals!"

"So what should I do?" Jace asked.

Ruige answered, "Wait a while. Calm the fire's anger. Or perform a simple ritual to appease them. There's no time for all that today, Sesso. Let's call it here for today. I'm hungry and want to eat something. Don't be so impatient. I'll be honest with you — your progress is already frightening. Maybe it's because you learned some fire magic before, or maybe it's because the fire elementals are a little afraid of you and don't dare disobey your orders… anyway, communicating with elementals is just that chaotic. Their temperaments are unpredictable. Let me clean up this room. Get out."

"There's one more thing," Jace said. "Have you heard of an incantation that can force a black dragon to reveal its true form?"

"Huh?" Ruige had just picked up the broom and now leaned it against the wall. "An incantation to reveal black dragons? I don't know. But I know there's an ancient incantation that can suppress black dragon magic and break black dragon illusions. That incantation should also be able to break a black dragon's transformation. Is that what you mean?"

Jace couldn't be sure either. He knew next to nothing about that incantation tablet he'd only had the barest contact with in the game.

The dwarf went on, "Shadowforge City is currently at war with the Blackrock orcs who've seized Blackrock Spire. Somehow, those orcs found black dragons to help them… so Shadowforge City has been searching everywhere for ways to suppress black dragons. They clearly have some kind of lead. The news has reached us too, but we're just a bunch of blacksmiths and merchants. We don't know any more than the people in Shadowforge City. What, is a black dragon looking to settle a score with you?"

"Not yet. But I have to consider the possibility," Jace said.
