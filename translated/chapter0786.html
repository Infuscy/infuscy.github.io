Chapter 786: The Dark Iron Among the Bronzebeards

Flame affinity and being familiar with fire elementals were two different things.

Jace only came to understand this after arriving in Ironforge, getting to know this Thorium Brotherhood fire-tender, and slowly learning the elemental tongue and how to converse with elementals.

The Dragonqueen's blessing had granted Jace a remarkably powerful flame affinity. But that didn't mean he could quickly achieve mutual understanding with fire elementals and communicate without barriers.

The fire elementals were willing to answer his calls and follow his instructions. But how they understood those instructions and executed them with precision required a deeper familiarity — and that was what Jace needed to learn from the Dark Iron dwarves.

It's just that, going by what Ruige had said, fire probably wouldn't answer his calls for a while.

If that was the case, he might as well just use the arcane to manipulate flame. After all, whether it was the Scorch spell or Fireball, even if the power was a bit inconsistent each time he cast them, at least as long as he still had mana, he wouldn't fail to cast them.

By comparison, the power of elementals was far too unreliable. No wonder Dalaran had no interest in the shamanistic path. Still, a simple attempt or two wasn't enough to prove anything.

After all, the most powerful shamans could shake the seas, move mountains, summon the Firelord Ragnaros, or elemental lords as terrifying as Thunderaan, Prince of the Wind.

Giving up just because he'd cast a Flame Shock and summoned something like a fire barrier would be way too impatient.

Besides, for Jace, the arcane path was genuinely hard to walk.

The next noon, Jace and Ruige went to a tavern outside. It was a tavern on the lowest level of Ironforge. Like the general character of this level, it was a bit dirty, cramped, and shabby — nothing at all like the cozy little taverns up in the inn district.

And the dwarf customers sitting here mostly had stern expressions. Some could even been described as unfriendly.

During the time he'd been staying with this Dark Iron dwarf, Jace had almost forgotten about the man's skin color and hair color. But the moment they walked into the tavern, the Bronzebeards over there almost all glanced over in unison.

Ruige didn't pay it much mind. He sat down in a corner and scratched his neck, scraping a raspy sound across his rough skin. That little sound was almost jarring in the suddenly quiet tavern.

He looked up and glanced at the bar. No one was there yet. The barmaid had gone off somewhere.

Jace sat down across from him and said in a low voice, "I want to try calling on elemental power again."

"You've already offended the elementals. They definitely won't pay you any attention this time," Ruige said. "Still, I agree you should try again. After all, you haven't truly tasted rejection yet, have you? Even if you offended the elementals… at least they gave you a response."

As he spoke, the Dark Iron dwarf caught sight of a dwarven woman appearing behind the bar and immediately raised a hand to flag her down. Then he looked over and asked, "What do you want to drink, Sesso?"

"Ale," Jace said.

"Good choice," Ruige said flatly. "Ironforge's ale isn't quite like it is elsewhere. I'll have the same."

"And I'll take two black breads and a bowl of meat soup. Any meat is fine," he added to the barmaid. "I've heard Ironforge has a famous dish called haggis."

"Haggis," Ruige said. "It's just sheep offal stewed with oats, with whatever spices are on hand. Nothing special. What's special about sheep guts? Dun Morogh isn't short on goats."

"You think we can't understand Common?" a dwarf nearby asked sharply.

"Sorry." Ruige raised a hand. "Just a bit of grumbling."

"The only reason you're allowed to set foot in Dun Morogh is because Magni Bronzebeard showed you special lenience!" a Bronzebeard dwarf reminded him.

All at once, Jace hadn't even figured out what had happened. He'd originally thought the Bronzebeard dwarves were angry because Ruige had implied the dwarves of Ironforge were goats or something. Then he realized — it was because a Dark Iron dwarf had mentioned Dun Morogh.

Then another dwarf said in Dwarvish, "Behave yourself, black chestnut!"

Ruige smiled and lowered his head slightly to that dwarf. After the atmosphere around them had calmed a little, he whispered, "Sorry, Sesso. I should've kept my mouth shut."

Jace said nothing. As an outsider, it was best not to say anything right now that might make things worse.

Two other dwarves off to the side were discussing something in low voices. Jace heard them mention "Thorium Brotherhood," but he wasn't sure if he'd heard right — after all, he'd only been learning Dwarvish for a week.

The awkward atmosphere seemed hard to endure, but time passed quickly enough. The barmaid soon brought the ale and food.

Ruige dipped his bread in the meat soup and ate. Since a single comment about goats had caused so much unpleasantness, Jace wasn't interested in sampling Ironforge's "haggis" either. He just ordered a portion of wild boar jerky and black bread and had a simple meal.

Ironforge's food was a bit on the sweet side and saltier than Stormwind's. After eating, his mouth always felt uncomfortable.

But what was even more uncomfortable was the silence of the Dark Iron dwarf across from him.

In the tavern, which had grown lively again, Ruige still said nothing. He just quickly polished off everything in front of him, gulped down his ale, and left Jace — used to chatting while eating — feeling completely out of place.

After the meal, the two of them didn't linger. They left the tavern quickly and walked back to their lodgings at a brisk pace.

The moment they got downstairs, Ruige started calling Gaiada's name in an unhappy voice, stripped off his clothes, tossed them onto the stone ledge, and carried the succubus into the room. Soon, grunts and moans came from inside.

Jace stopped paying attention to him. He planned to try calling on elemental power again. Following the Dark Iron dwarf's advice, he'd try tasting the flavor of rejection.

He went to the room at the end of the corridor where the golem was. Using the prayer Ruige had taught him, he called on the fire elementals to destroy. But when he shouted it out, he clearly felt a searing force answer his voice and gather toward him — just not as powerfully, and not as decisively.

Jace thrust both hands out and guided the fierce flame to slam into the golem's surface with a boom, making the golem shudder. Its joints creaked and groaned all over.

Although it was weaker than the Flame Shock he'd cast before, Jace could still feel that it was even more powerful than his Scorch spell without any shadow mixed in.

A rapid series of footsteps came from behind — thud thud thud thud. Ruige ran all the way over and asked, "What happened? Did you hear that noise just now? Where did it explode? Is it the Great Forge? We're right below the Great Forge — if something goes wrong, we'll be the first to go!"
