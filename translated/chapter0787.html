Chapter 787: A Simple Agreement

"No, I tried another Flame Shock," Jace said.

"What…" Ruige looked at the embers still lingering on the Dark Iron golem. "The elementals didn't reject you? You offended the elementals, but you can still get a response from them?!"

Jace said, "I tried summoning fire power the same way I did yesterday. The elemental power was definitely weak this time. I could feel it."

Ruige seemed to breathe a sigh of relief, then said, "Since the power was weaker, that means the elementals really do have a negative attitude toward you. But they're still willing to help you… I can only say I underestimated the fire's tolerance toward you."

Jace asked, "You mentioned earlier some kind of ritual to beg the elementals for forgiveness. How does that work?"

Ruige explained, "Whatever method you use, it's all about letting the fire feel your remorse. Let them understand that you weren't trying to enslave them or force them to do things — that it was an unintentional offense. Then see if they're willing to accept it and answer your calls with their full strength again. The simplest way is to try having an exchange with them that involves no asking and no taking, Sesso. Listen carefully to their voices. Feel their emotions."

Jace could only say he half-understood these words. Over the time that followed, he continued studying Dwarvish and the elemental tongue diligently. When he got tired of studying, he'd sit in the room with the Dark Iron golem and rest, close his eyes, try to meditate, and work at sensing the elementals' presence.

But more than a week passed, and listening to all the chaotic noise of Ironforge, the echoes deep beneath the mountains of Dun Morogh, he still couldn't hear the "voice of fire" the way Ruige described.

He thought it was because his Flame Shock had offended the elementals earlier, and they weren't willing to talk to him anymore.

That day, he heard Ruige passing by outside, so he called out, "Ruige, I can't sense the presence of fire elementals. It's been a week since I 'offended' them. Isn't that a bit abnormal?"

Ruige said from outside, "Some fire-tenders, or other sages who try to maintain contact with elementals — after they accidentally offend the elementals, the elementals never answer their calls again. The fact that fire still favors you now is a good thing, although I don't know why. Hot-tempered fire is often unwilling to accommodate an ordinary mortal so indulgently."

After saying that, he came to the door and peered in at Jace. "Of course, you're definitely not an ordinary mortal either."

"How do I make contact with flame again?" Jace asked.

"Have you heard even a whisper of the elementals' voice, sitting here these past few days?" Ruige asked.

"No," Jace said. "I can't pick out the elementals' voices from all this chaotic noise…"

Ruige explained, "The elementals' voices aren't sounds that just anyone can hear with their ears, Sesso. You need to feel them with your spirit… with your consciousness. You're a mage, a warlock. I'd think you'd know better than an ordinary person who's barely touched magic how to perceive things on a spiritual level. Have you ever spoken to a soul? Have you ever conversed with a demon in the Twisting Nether? Of course, that's just a metaphor. If that doesn't work, you can try calling on the elementals and having them speak to you directly. That makes it easier to hear. Just remember not to have any ulterior motive. Don't turn it into asking them for help again!"

"I'll give it a try," Jace said.

Ruige nodded and left the room. Jace continued sitting with his eyes closed, trying to use the little ancient Kalimdor language he'd learned to say, "Please hear my voice, fire…"

The moment the words left his mouth, he felt as if he'd heard the churning of lava. Jace opened his eyes slightly and saw the narrow window flicker with a faint glow.

Was that the fire elementals speaking?

Jace felt as if these sounds had some kind of pattern. He seemed to be able to make it out, but he couldn't understand it — after all, he was still basically familiar with the pronunciation of the elemental tongue and was nowhere near the stage of memorizing large amounts of vocabulary.

So he could only vaguely write down these sounds, one by one, in the elemental pronunciation he was familiar with, then take the elemental language tome, open it up, and look them up and compare, bit by bit.

After half a day of fiddling around, Jace finally translated the vaguely heard sounds. Fortunately, the elementals had repeated the sounds many times and spoken very slowly, giving him the room to translate.

Although he'd been at it for a long time, the elementals had really only been repeating a single simple phrase — "Dark power, hard to trust."

But this was enough for Jace to understand the fire elementals' meaning. They were probably trying to say that Jace was a warlock who wielded shadow, so they couldn't trust him?

Now that he understood the fire elementals' meaning, Jace was running out of patience to learn the elemental tongue completely before communicating with them again — after all, the elementals were already willing to talk to him.

So he just took the Dwarvish dictionary and the elemental language dictionary and pieced together what he wanted to express by cross-referencing them. Although it cost him another half day's effort, he finally managed to put together a complete sentence.

"I can master shadow, not be mastered by it. I can earn your trust." He tried shouting this sentence out, then waited for the elementals' response.

A long time passed — long enough that Jace started to suspect he'd said something wrong. But slowly, the sound of the lava still began to boil.

Although it was faint, Jace could already tell, based on his earlier experience hearing the elementals' voices, that this wasn't some meaningless fire noise. It was a voice carrying real information.

He took out paper and pen again and began recording. After writing it all down, he used the same old trick to translate it. But there was one word he couldn't find the meaning of anywhere.

After repeating it several times, he realized — it was the name of the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza! Putting the Dragonqueen's name in, the final result came out roughly like this.

"One blessed by Alexstrasza's blood may be granted a chance." Was the fire elementals willing to give him a chance?

Jace repeated the phrase. Not long after, the elementals began reciting another sentence in a low voice. After another painstaking round of translation, Jace could roughly understand the meaning of this sentence.

"Those enslaved by darkness will be forsaken."

Although it was hard to translate, the elementals' meaning wasn't hard to grasp.

The fire elementals were willing to draw near because of the red dragon's blood, and equally hesitant because of the dark power.

Now that they had Jace's response and understood his attitude, they were willing to give him a chance. If he was controlled by shadow in return, they would leave.

That sounded fair.

Although countless elementals had been enslaved by the Old Gods, there were still vast numbers of elementals in the world willing to help mortals. These elementals definitely didn't want to be controlled by the Old Gods and shadow again. Having this concern was perfectly normal.

"I agree," Jace said in the elemental tongue.

Amid the churning of the lava, Jace could almost hear the fire's voice align with his own. He didn't even need to go through the trouble of translating anymore.

Had some kind of agreement been reached? Between himself and the elementals…

Jace stood up and looked at the Dark Iron golem once more. Using the elemental tongue he'd become familiar with, he said, "Help me destroy this golem."

He didn't use any honorifics. He just issued the command. A ball of fire instantly exploded on the golem's chest. The flames that burst out quickly engulfed it. The firelight and high heat radiated through the whole room, making Jace feel the searing heat was almost hard to endure.

The flames howled, roared — even more wild and fierce than the first time Jace had called on them with honorifics. Watching this scene, Jace could hardly say his heart wasn't racing.

It wasn't just the excitement of such powerful fire. More importantly, he could feel that all the churning flames in Ironforge had opened their hearts to him. At the same time, he could even carry on a dialogue with these flames. This experience was truly impossible to explain to someone who'd never spoken with elementals.

No wonder so many shamans were willing to stay in places where it was easy to communicate with elementals — to learn, to live, even to never leave for the rest of their lives.

At that point, he decided to try another trick.

That Blast Wave Ruige had released when dealing with the succubus Gaiada.

Of course, Blast Wave was a fire mage spell in the game. But in the game, shamans could also use totems to release a similar spell — Fire Nova.

Jace wasn't quite sure how Ruige had released that Fire Nova. But Ruige had also said not to treat calling on elemental power as chanting an incantation. You had to let the fire carry out your intention and realize your idea.

He flipped through the elemental language tome and found the vocabulary he wanted to express.

After piecing it together, he put the book outside the door, stayed in the room, closed the door, closed his eyes, and sought the presence of the fire elementals. "Flame… sweep away everything around me."

Jace had only tried saying it like that when he felt a large mass of unstable fire elementals surround him!

He didn't need to control it. He didn't need to release it. This mass of fire elementals violently exploded outward on its own, carrying a wave of searing wind and a whistling sound as it slammed into the walls!

Absolutely exhilarating!

The thrill of unleashing fire to wanton destruction was too easy to get drunk on. Jace even started to imagine — if a pack of gnolls tried to close in on him, he'd just need to ask the elementals for help like this… and he could instantly repel and set all of them ablaze, without going to the trouble of using Fear to drive them off and picking them off one by one.

But communicating with elementals wasn't just about listening to sounds. It was something that consumed an enormous amount of energy. After the series of conversations with the elementals just now, plus the two attempts to summon the elementals to help him, he already felt a severe drain on his energy.

You couldn't become fat in one bite. Jace still needed to continue learning Dwarvish while mastering the elemental tongue to communicate with elementals better. The communication efficiency of wasting half an hour to an hour just to say two sentences, like he'd just done, was really too poor — and too draining on the spirit.

This was when he truly understood why Marin had told him to find a shaman. Summoning fire to help him, with the arcane power he wasn't particularly close to standing in the middle, really was a huge waste of efficiency.

It was already very late. Time to get some rest. When he returned to the room where he slept, Ruige was standing by the next door, watching him.

"Since this afternoon, I've been feeling an unusual restlessness in the elementals here, and that restlessness just peaked a moment ago… I told you to try communicating with the elementals and beg their forgiveness, but I think you've been forcibly channeling elemental power again."

"I think I've reached an agreement with the fire elementals," Jace said.

"Reached… an agreement?!" Ruige looked at him with an expression of complete incomprehension. "It seems your communication with the elementals has gone very smoothly."

"More or less," Jace said. "I've already imposed on you long enough here, Mr. Ruige. Tomorrow I'll head back to Stormwind. Over the next stretch of time, I'll study the elemental tongue seriously. Once I've got this book figured out, I'll come back and return it to you."

Ruige said, "Alright. Just be careful not to damage the book."

"I'll take good care of it," Jace said.

The next day, Jace came to the entrance of the Deeprun Tram in the fifth-level Artisan's District of Ironforge. Ruige stood here. Bronzebeard dwarves passing by occasionally stole glances over.

Jace did a final check of his luggage, his accessories, and so on, and checked all the books he was taking back. Ruige watched him pack and said, "You're a good fellow, Mr. Sesso. I have to say, you don't seem like a warlock at all. Maybe it's just that I haven't met enough warlocks."

"You're not the only one who's said that." Jace picked up everything and looked toward the tram entrance. "Let me stress this again — remember to keep Gaiada invisible at all times. Set enough rules for her, or she might disobey and cause you trouble. Remember, you're her master — not the other way around!"

"I know, I know… I've summoned creatures from another plane too. I'm not some clueless rookie!" Ruige said impatiently.

Jace said, "Getting yourself thrown in a jail cell for causing trouble is one thing. Dragging me into it would ruin everything."

"Fine, fine." Ruige said impatiently.

"Still, these past few days have been good. Whatever you plan to do next, I wish you the best of luck, Mr. Sesso."

"You too," Jace said. "And you're not like the Dark Iron dwarves I've heard about, either. Prejudice is always hard to break."

"Because prejudice isn't always a bad thing," Ruige said.

Jace looked at him, nodded, and turned to walk into the tram entrance.
