Chapter 788: Sworn to Protect

By the time he got back to Stormwind, it was morning two days later. The journey had been agonizingly bumpy, but being able to cross such a vast distance so quickly, relatively safely, and — most importantly — for free, Jace couldn't ask for anything more.

The gnomish engineers said the Deeprun Tram still needed at least two years before it could actually begin carrying ordinary passengers. So by then, the ride quality and safety should be much higher than it was now. Still something to look forward to.

Returning from underground to the surface, out onto the streets of the Dwarf District, Jace looked at the extinguished forges by the roadside and felt as if he could already see — or hear — something different in all these fire-related things.

In the less than a month he'd spent communicating and interacting with fire elementals, he'd picked up some tricks and started being able to perceive things he'd always overlooked before — the voices of the elementals that had always existed in this world.

He probably couldn't yet recognize the voices of earth, air, or water elementals, but for fire… he was already extremely sensitive.

Whether it was the sages of Aerie Peak, the orcish shamans, or the Kul Tirans, the first reason they learned to converse with elementals was definitely not to summon elementals for battle. It was to gain knowledge from the elementals — knowledge closely tied to their people's lives, like weather and sea conditions — and use that knowledge and information to help their people's production and daily life.

Jace had gone looking for the Thorium Brotherhood for knowledge of flame, of course, to fight and make himself stronger. But at the same time, he felt that fire could definitely help him in other ways too.

It's just that his Kalimdor language skills were still only at a beginner's level. He needed more study.

Right now, he could hear the residual heat in those furnaces, and the hard-to-detect low humming of flames from somewhere. Although he couldn't understand it at all and couldn't grasp the meaning, based on his experience communicating with elementals in Ironforge, he could judge that these easily overlooked noises might also be the elementals pouring their hearts out to him.

Once he fully mastered the elemental tongue, that should count as fully unlocking all the value contained in the Dragonqueen's essence.

Back at his rented house, he pushed open the door. Morofil was leaning on the table, glaring at him coldly.

A "bloop" came from under the bed — the sound of the imp scurrying to hide.

Jace looked at Morofil's expression, then at himself, and asked, "What's wrong?"

"You know perfectly well, Master."

When Morofil said the word "Master," she put particular emphasis on it.

"The smell on you is so strong I feel like another sayaad has licked you from head to toe. I'm about to be sick. Where did you hide her? Or did you use some special method the Dark Iron dwarf taught you to summon a sayaad far more powerful than me — one whose invisibility I can't detect at all — and now she's hiding right beside you, openly mocking my pathetic state? I emphasized this, Master — your mana belongs to me alone, and you promised me that. I don't make many demands on you. The most important one is just this one, isn't it? I don't want to share your power with any demon in the Twisting Nether — no, with anything in the entire plane, the entire dark void."

Jace had almost never seen Morofil say anything with this much seriousness. She'd shed nearly all of her joking tone.

She didn't look like a succubus at all right now. She was more like a demon instructor delivering an important rule at some cult gathering. Especially paired with her tall, slender frame, the demon tattoos exposed between her undergarment, and those beautiful, forked long horns on her head — she looked utterly solemn.

In the past, when Jace came back from the basement, Morofil would often catch the scent of succubus on him and tease him — saying he must have secretly been with Senrola, or maybe let Gakres have his way with him. Jace would just tell her impatiently to shut up, or go cool off somewhere by herself.

But this time, seeing her apparently serious attitude, Jace didn't feel right brushing it off casually either.

"I didn't summon any succubus, Morofil," he said, tossing his things to the side and throwing his sword onto the bed. "I can swear by anything you like. I went to Ironforge and met a Dark Iron dwarf who could teach me how to unlock the value of the red dragon's blood. You can convert that blood into your own power, but you're a demon, and I'm not. I need to study further and tap into these powers, Morofil. And that Dark Iron dwarf wanted something of sufficient value from me in exchange. So I taught him how to summon a succubus."

He took a breath after saying all that in one go. Seeing Morofil's expression was still a bit cold, he went on: "The entire summoning process — whether the ritual or the completion of the pact — he did it all himself. I just drew a shadow-enhancing ritual circle for him, gave him a soul shard, told him the incantation and the summoning method, and some important points about getting along with a demon minion. That was it."

"Tell me about that succubus," Morofil said.

Jace said, "A very strong succubus. Her name is Gaiada. I don't know if that's the name the dwarf gave her, or if she calls herself that. Ironforge is also a city that doesn't allow demons to appear, so the dwarf had to keep her in his own home. And I was at his house, reading and learning how to communicate with elementals. Most of the time I spent there was definitely in the basement of that house."

As he spoke, he pulled the Dwarvish dictionary and the elemental language tome out of his pack and tossed them to the other side of the bed.

"That being so, I couldn't help spending too much time with that succubus. And that dwarf had no idea how to remind his succubus to keep her scent under control — or maybe that powerful succubus simply didn't know the first thing about that. I was stuck in there getting smoked by her for weeks. I'll be honest with you, Morofil — I was about to lose it too."

"You didn't sleep with her? Cooped up in a room with a sayaad, a female sayaad, for more than twenty days, and you didn't sleep with her?" Morofil narrowed her eyes.

"Absolutely not," Jace said.

She moved closer, pressed her hand to Jace's chest, and slowly lifted it with her index and middle fingers, step by step, up to his neck, his chin, his lips.

This demon hand, skinny enough to be all skin and bone, moved like an extreme ballet dancer. Her whole body's skin was flushed red, threaded with sickly blue veins. Ever since Morofil had gradually grown into this form, Jace had never carefully observed what her hands had become.

"Let me see if you're lying to me."

Morofil whispered in his ear, slowly pressing his lower lip with her index finger and carefully probing into his mouth.

Jace didn't resist. He let her slide her finger into his mouth past his teeth and press it to his tongue. If this was a way to make him convince her, then so be it.

With her finger in his mouth, catching her scent, Morofil had already gotten very good at concealing her presence. By now it rarely leaked out for Jace to sense. But Jace didn't find it unpleasant at all.

Although he could tell it was a sickly kind of heavy fragrance that could easily make people feel cloyed.

Soon, Morofil slowly pulled her finger out, stared into Jace's eyes, suddenly slipped her finger into her own mouth, closed her eyes and sucked on it for a while, then stuck out her tongue and licked the back of her hand. Finally, she showed the first smile Jace had seen since he'd walked into the room.

"Master isn't lying to me. That's not easy. You know what I'd do if you'd entered that sayaad's body?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "Kill me?"

"You know perfectly well I can't kill you." Morofil lowered her head, her voice heavy. "I'd just be heartbroken. In pain. I'd feel like my stay in this world was meaningless, Master. I'd feel like you weren't mine anymore. I'd feel like I wasn't that important to you at all."

This succubus really had found his weakness, Jace thought.

Her saying this really was more lethal than threatening to kill him, or finishing him off the way Gakres would. But Jace also knew this demon was deliberately saying these things to cut him.

Jace stroked Morofil's black hair. "I would never touch another succubus, as long as…"

"As long as what?" Morofil asked eagerly.

"As long as you stay loyal to me forever. As long as you never do anything to harm me."

"That's already my sworn creed."

"Sworn? Heh."

"Returning to the Twisting Nether is a huge price too, Master… don't say it so lightly."
