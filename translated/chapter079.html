Chapter 79: Murloc Magic

"I wanted to ask about some potion recipes," Jace said. "Is there a cheap and easy-to-make recipe for mana potions?"

"That's not an easy potion to brew," Ursula said, wiping the sweat from her forehead with her arm as she stood up. "Are you planning to use them yourself, or sell them, or give them to someone else?"

"For personal use," Jace admitted.

Ursula nodded and stood there thinking for a moment. Jace realized he was struggling to keep from stealing glances at the curves beneath her thin tank top.

She wasn't exactly beautiful, and she was at least ten years older than Jace. If this were before his transmigration, Jace probably wouldn't have had any other thoughts at all.

But now he was a seventeen-year-old youth. No matter who he used to be, this body had everything it was supposed to have, and even with an older soul, it was hard to forcibly suppress the impulses of youth.

Sometimes he even felt that his thoughts and desires were being influenced in return by this body.

Not to mention that Ursula Delyn, surrounded by steam, with her damp skin looking more like a teenage girl's, plus her slim but slightly muscular figure, standing there with her hand on her hip so completely unguarded...

After all, this wasn't a cyber world where you could find all sorts of strange resources at the click of a button. Still, Jace tried his best not to dwell on such things — he was here for help, and if she noticed his gaze and took him for some creep, that would be disastrous.

Ursula pondered for a moment, then said, "Mageroyal, briarthorn, and Silverleaf — those are the essential materials. These three ingredients, in different proportions, can produce different kinds of mental energy restorative agents — what you call mana potions. If you can help me gather the materials, I can brew them for you. After all, you're one of us, so it's free. Or were you planning to make them yourself?"

Jace said, "I'll gather the materials and commission you to make them — I don't know alchemy."

"Ha, no need to be modest," Ursula said. "When we first met, I smelled the scent of converted Shadow power on you. You were worried someone here might mean you harm, so you drank a Shadow Protection Potion in advance, right? Aside from me, no one in this city is brewing Shadow Protection Potions right now, so I'm guessing you made it yourself."

Jace gave an awkward laugh. "But I really haven't brewed any potions to speak of. That draught... was just practice, and I was far from skilled, so I'll still have to trouble you."

"I can teach you some alchemy techniques," Ursula said. "Right now there's only one person working here — me — and it's honestly more than I can handle. These warlocks need quite a lot of potions, whether for experiments, for mitigating the negative effects of Shadow incantations, or for detoxification..."

"That would be great — I'd love to learn more alchemy."

Watching this alchemist who could rattle off mana potion recipes from memory, Jace suddenly thought of a question.

"By the way, Ms. Ursula Delyn — is there a potion that can cure bat venom?" Jace asked.

"Bat venom?" Ursula scooped up a bit of potion with her spoon, tasted it lightly, turned the heat down a little, sat in the chair beside her, and looked over. "What bat? The poison mist bats of Southshire?"

Jace said, "The riding bats of the Amani forest trolls, from the Hinterlands. Do you know anything about that venom?"

"I know it," Ursula said. "That bat's venom is similar to a species of cobra in Stranglethorn Vale. After being poisoned, it causes paralysis — devastating to a person's sensory and motor functions."

Jace nodded. "A friend of mine was poisoned by that kind of venom, but the toxicity has already passed. The lingering aftereffects have left him blind in one eye, and he often can't control a tremor in his hands. Is there any medicine that can treat these aftereffects?"

"In that case, it's a completely different problem, Jace," Ursula said. "There aren't many options, and each one is hard to obtain, because what you need is to restore the body's normal functioning — ordinary anti-venom medicines simply don't address the root cause."

"Let me tell you the best place to go," she continued. "Ask the elves. Quel'Thalas has been fighting the Amani trolls for thousands of years — I imagine their experience battling troll riding bats is far richer than ours. What's more, elven alchemy is inherited from a legendary ancient elven civilization, and it differs greatly from the human tradition."

"Elves," Jace muttered. Was this supposed to be the easiest of the few options?

Right now, never mind traveling to Quel'Thalas — ever since the Expedition set out, spotting a single genuine elf in Stormwind had become extraordinarily difficult.

The occasional elf-looking individuals you ran across were all half-elves cast out by Quel'Thalas, forced to scrape by in human society. Aside from their appearance, they were no different from ordinary humans whatsoever.

But Ursula's words did give him a bit of hope — maybe Grede's disability really could be healed by the elves.

"Looks like that needs more long-term planning. Let's get back to the mana potions."

Ursula nodded. "I have two fairly standard recipes here. One is the Minor Mana Potion commonly used by beginning mages; the other is the Lesser Mana Potion commonly used by more advanced, skilled mages. Which one were you after? As warlocks, we don't cast as frequently as those conjurers at the Wizard Sanctum, so I'd say the minor potion is a fine choice — because the stranglekelp needed for the lesser potion grows in the sea, and for ordinary people it's genuinely hard to harvest, and the price isn't cheap either."

Jace said, "Let's start with the minor one then."

Ursula took out a sheet of parchment and wrote as she read aloud: "Mageroyal, half a pound. Silverleaf, three pounds. One briarthorn. This will produce about four potions. Just bring enough materials, along with the corresponding number of vials, to me, and I can brew them for you."

"Where does Mageroyal grow in abundance? You planning to harvest it yourself?"

"Of course. I certainly don't have the money to just buy it."

Ursula scratched her neck. "Northwest of Sentinel Hill there's an old abandoned mine called the Jangolode Mine. From that mine, heading toward the coast — that is, going west — you'll come up onto a large rise. It catches the sea breeze, and quite a bit of Mageroyal grows there. But the problem is, if I know that spot is good for Mageroyal, everyone else knows too... Watch out for gnolls. The gnolls have been rampant lately. There are rumors of gnoll tribes around the Jangolode Mine too — they harvest herbs and brew potions as well."

"Thanks for the warning."

Jace was about to leave when Ursula called out to him. "Oh, right, Jace — if you're heading to Westfall... I have some news. About the murlocs in Westfall."

Jace asked, "What news?"

Ursula explained, "The patrols at Westfall Keep found murlocs on the northern coast who have mastered dark magic — they can manipulate Shadow... attacking passersby, including soldiers. I suspect those murlocs most likely learned their magic from orc warlocks. Perhaps they have books recording incantations, and have grasped the techniques of casting — or at the very least, they've heard incantations from orcs."

"I originally wanted to go see what these murlocs can actually do — it might enrich our grimoire of incantations. But as you know, I still have plenty of potions to brew, and I haven't had the chance. Could you go scout out the situation? If it turns out to be some new spell, maybe you can find a few willing companions to go together and see how they learned it."

"I could. But — a grimoire of incantations?" Jace asked. "We have something like that?"

"Of course," Ursula said. "Our circle is a circle that values sharing — anyone can consult the grimoire. But most of what's in it is ineffective incantations — a lot of guesswork, hearsay, or things recorded by ear in a muddled fashion. It's kept by Galdus. Don't expect to find anything good in it on your own. Gagin sometimes calls us all together just to test whether the incantations in it actually work, including when you first..."

After hearing Marin's explanation of casting techniques, Jace realized just how dangerous these inaccurate incantations and casting methods were — and the appeal of these muddled, secondhand collections had faded considerably.

Ursula said, "Whatever you find, you must come show it to me first when you get back. Westfall Keep doesn't want the local inhabitants knowing that murlocs have learned this kind of magic — after all, the news that murlocs have learned orc magic is pretty alarming. I only managed to learn of it through heavy secrecy."

Jace asked, "So confident that the murlocs must have some kind of spellbook or the like? Can murlocs even read Orcish?"

Ursula said, "You're underestimating murloc intelligence, Jace. Even without a grimoire of incantations taken from orcs, they must have their own method of record-keeping. Oh, and don't go alone — you need at least one person watching your back. Individual murlocs may be weak, but they know their own weaknesses and never act alone."

"I understand." Jace thought to himself. There was absolutely no way he could drag Grede into this — no tangible reward, the goal being to find warlock magic, the enemies being murloc warlocks — it was like it had been designed to get under his skin in every possible way.

But besides Grede, did he have any other friends who could help? Find another warlock?

In this basement, there wasn't a single person Jace was willing to partner with — Gagin, Galdus... He would never entrust his safety to any of these people.

Then again — wasn't Sazeno a partner?

If he really brought someone other than a warlock along, that would mean Sazeno could never reveal himself.

But Sazeno's loyalty and abilities far surpassed any helper he could find in a few Stormwind taverns.

And no squabbling over loot, no arguments, no friction...
