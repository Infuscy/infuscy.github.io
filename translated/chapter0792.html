Chapter 792: Getting the Servants Deeply Involved

The shadowy void aura drifting through the Slaughtered Lamb's basement — the corridors, passages, and adjoining rooms — was even heavier than before, lingering and slow to dissipate.

Although Erlan had been pulled back from the brink by the powerful healing potion, he was still extremely weak, speaking in a faint, breathless voice, lying in his room resting.

But even injured like that, he was especially thrilled at having been suddenly teleported here from Goldshire. He didn't blame anyone in the slightest.

Jalane was looking after him, while Ursula had run back to her alchemy lab to hurriedly brew more potions related to healing, in case Erlan showed any other worrying symptoms.

Right now Jace was sitting in the hall, watching Surena Caledon by the passageway staring at Erlan's door with her head bowed, saying nothing. Zardeth was carrying a bowl of scalding-hot meat stew Jarel had cooked, heading over toward Erlan's side. As he came up to Jace he said, "Erlan got hurt, but I'd say this was a success for a first attempt at the summoning ritual."

Jace nodded and said, "After all, we really did get a person here. But why did you choose Surena as an assistant?"

"That's only temporary. Eventually everyone needs to be proficient in the assistant position," Zardeth explained. "Right now the problem is that Jalane's in charge of leading, and I've temporarily locked down one assistant position. Erlan needs to travel out frequently to contact Booty Bay or Moonbrook. Currently Ursula is the one among us most skilled at getting along with felhounds, and she's willing to deal with SI:7 — she may be leaving the Slaughtered Lamb often for a while. That leaves Surena Caledon as the only one with nothing to do. And although Surena doesn't like using shadow magic, her mana isn't weaker than Ursula's — it's stronger than Erlan's. With you not around, she's the most suitable choice."

"So it still comes down to unfamiliarity with the incantations," Jace said.

"Possibly," Zardeth said. "Do you think it could be that the emotions are off? Could the summoning ritual be affected by emotions? Your notes didn't have anything about that."

Jace explained, "Because the person who wrote down those incantations and casting procedures might not even have known about the connection between shadow magic and emotions. So for now we can only follow what they wrote. If we want to figure out exactly which emotions are involved, we'd need a succubus's help — let them detect the emotional shifts. Of course, that would also require many attempts. I think we can practice on demons more — not just felhounds. Zardeth, bring succubi into the summoning ritual. Whether it's their mana or their build, they're much closer to mortals. They're easier to summon than mortals, but compared to felhounds they're a higher-difficulty attempt."

Zardeth looked at Jace and said, "You're right. The felhound summoning went too smoothly — it made us rush forward recklessly."

"Can't a succubus serve as an assistant?" Jace asked.

"Senlora and Erlan's Sathris can't," Zardeth said. "You'd think we hadn't considered it, but their mana isn't even enough to support summoning a felhound."

"I could try, Master, if you'd give me the chance," Morophil said.

"Your mana is definitely sufficient, Lady Morophil," Zardeth said. "But even if you succeeded, it wouldn't help much. You're often at Jace's side, and Jace doesn't return to the Slaughtered Lamb often."

"Then let Gakres try," Morophil said. "He's a pleasure demon — combat-wise he's a bit outmatched, but his mana is strong."

Zardeth said, "All right. Surena doesn't bring Gakres here often. After she decided to participate in the summoning, we never tried having him assist. That's indeed a feasible idea. Next time, then."

With that, Zardeth carried the hot stew toward the room where Erlan was resting. Jace said, "What spell did Gakres use to replenish Surena's mana?"

"Do I have to tell you?" Morophil asked reluctantly.

"Yes," Jace said.

Morophil glanced at Gakres, who was comforting his master, and said softly, "Dark Pact — an ability many powerful succubi command. A pleasure demon like Gakres… he's quite familiar with those mana-exchange incantations. Remember when he mentioned before that he once served a Matron? Offering up his own mana to the Matron is basic training for them. Now Surena is his new 'Matron.'"

"Can you do it?" Jace asked.

"I'm the one who hunts demons, not the one who pleasures them," Morophil said with a smile, leaning close to Jace's ear. "Pleasuring my Master is just a hobby."

"So can you do it or not?" Jace asked.

"No."

Morophil shook her head, and her towering demon horns swayed with the motion — it made her long neck, covered halfway in demonic runes, look like it might snap.

"Tell me the truth," Jace said. "That's an order."

"Even if you screamed my true name I still couldn't," Morophil said. "And even if I could, the mana I lost would ultimately have to be replenished from you, wouldn't it? Master, I reckon Gakres is going to give Surena Caledon a night she'll never forget tonight. Look at that incubus with his starving eyes, tsk."

Jace sighed and said, "Won't transfer mana to the master, can't shapeshift…"

"And can't fly," Morophil finished for him. "Are you starting to find me disappointing, Master?"

She'd suddenly brought up her own wings. Jace glanced at her and actually felt a little reluctant to say anything disparaging. Morophil smiled triumphantly — this succubus clearly wanted to play the pity card to manipulate his feelings again.

But the helpless thing was, even when he recognized the problem, there was nothing he could do about it.

The next noon, the Gilded Rose.

Jace came to the inn and saw Grede sitting at the table counting silver coins for fun. He looked up when someone came in, and the moment he saw Jace the two ends of his mustache perked right up.

Even though the spread on the table was simple — sliced radish, dried meat, and bread, plus two bowls of meat stew — there was both meat and vegetable, and it looked quite appetizing. Jace hadn't been particularly hungry, but seeing it made him lick his lips.

"You didn't order anything to drink, Grede?" Jace asked.

"I was planning for us to go practice swords together this afternoon," Grede said. "So no ale — I can get some milk or water."

"Good idea." Jace sat down across from him. "Just the thing — get some exercise, clear our heads…"

"How was the trip?" the dwarf asked.

"What?" Jace asked. "You mean Ironforge, or the Dark Iron dwarves?"

"I've been to Ironforge a few times," Grede said. "I'm not interested in the Dark Iron dwarves. I mean the tram!"

Jace licked his lips and said, "Thrilling."

"Lend me your ring. I want to ride it too." Grede reached out his hand.

"Isn't the Lordaeron Knight's Seal King Terenas gave you good enough?"

"That thing only works within the Kingdom of Lordaeron," Grede said, rubbing the ring on his finger. "Fine, don't lend it. I already made a deal with a gnome friend — when it officially opens for business… I'll definitely be one of the first passengers."

"Did you meet anyone from the Thorium Brotherhood in Ironforge?" Grede asked. "Were they willing to teach you how to commune with flame?"

"They were," Jace replied.

"That's strange," Grede craned his neck. "They actually agreed — what did you give them, gold? I don't believe it myself."

Jace propped his cheek on his hand and tapped his fingers guiltily on the table a few times. "I taught them how to summon succubi."

"Those people can't summon succubi?" Grede said. "That I wouldn't have guessed. I thought most of the Dark Iron dwarves were already spending every day with demons. A lot of Wildhammer dwarves told me when I was little that the Dark Iron dwarves have demon blood. Of course I'd never believe it, but then again — dragons fly in the sky."

Jace started and asked, "What dragons fly in the sky?"

"A dragon doesn't leave its nest for no reason," Grede said, pointing at Jace. "Isn't that a saying of you Lordaeronians? It means rumors don't spread without grounds…"

"Never heard of it," Jace said. "Probably a dwarven saying of your own."

"Uh." Grede cleared his throat. "All right. Let's talk about the elements."

"That dwarf gave me a lexicon of the elemental language to study — the Kalimdor tongue," Jace said. "You learned it on Aerie Peak too, remember?"

"A lexicon of the elemental language?" Grede frowned. "On Aerie Peak, the Kalimdor tongue texts are treasured by the sages like heirlooms — they won't even let you make a copy. Those Dark Iron dwarves were just willing to hand it over to you?"

"Why?" Jace asked. "Don't the sages want more people to communicate with the elements?"

"They believe spreading this language could have dangerous consequences," Grede said. "Think about it — if anyone could talk to the elements, and they angered the elements, or made the elements do evil… especially if they made the elements of the Hinterlands stop trusting us, we'd be one force short in fighting the trolls."

"So that's why," Jace nodded. "Still, the elemental language lexicon he gave me has Dwarvish explanations. I also got myself a Dwarvish dictionary. On the way to Kalimdor you'll have to teach me carefully."

"No problem," Grede said in Dwarvish. "So when do we leave?"

Jace was stunned for a moment. He felt like he'd understood what Grede said, but suddenly he wasn't quite sure.

Hesitating for a bit, Grede sighed and switched back to Common. "Looks like I really do have too much to teach you. I asked when we're leaving."

"Ah… I understood," Jace said. "I just didn't expect you to speak Dwarvish all of a sudden!"

"Well, that's a good start!" Grede said again in Dwarvish.

"Stop speaking Dwarvish," Jace said. "We can start preparing now. We're just waiting for Vereesa."
