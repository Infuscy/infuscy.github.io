Chapter 794: Arrangements Before Departure

The last thing to do before heading to Kalimdor was to put Kanrethad and the mage tower affairs in order.

After all, a round trip to Kalimdor was two to three months. Add in the journey to Darkwhisper Gulch, and who knew how much longer it might take — it might turn into another half-year, just like last time with Dire Maul.

Jace had actually considered having the Dagger Circle warlocks pull them back from Kalimdor one by one, which could save more than a month's time — and the travel fare, of course.

But after that night's attempt, Jace thought it better to let them practice more first.

After all, summoning Erland Dolderman had been that taxing. Summoning a caster like himself — no telling what could go wrong. And compared to a month-long voyage and one highly unreliable spatial journey across the Great Sea, the trip didn't seem so dangerous or drawn-out after all.

Jace wrote a command scroll in Eredun for the Stonewatch captains, and instructed the two Dazarim to listen only to the captain, to prevent anyone else from stealing the scroll and using the hellhounds to cause trouble.

It was already spring now, and the forest near Stonewatch Falls was much greener and more alive than when they'd seized Ilgalar Tower from the gnolls.

The camps and traces of life left behind by the Rothide gnoll tribe had been swept away almost entirely by the soldiers. Fallen leaves and soil covered and blended the traces that couldn't be cleaned. The green ivy on the mage tower's walls and on the high slope where the tower stood merged quite harmoniously with the nearby trees and forest.

The gnolls no longer dared come near here, and the whole region had grown much more vibrant.

Of course, compared to a normal, peaceful forest, there were still some discordant notes around Ilgalar Tower — and Jace could feel it.

Aside from the odd bug and mosquito, there was almost no sign of any animal activity at all.

No birds sang in the forest, no beasts called — not wolves, not deer, not bears, all creatures quite common within the Kingdom of Stormwind.

Because of the demonic aura.

The animals could all sense the evil scent emanating from the two hellhounds inside Ilgalar Tower. Of course, this was also the main reason the gnolls had never dared press forward.

The peace that Jace and his demonic servants brought came at a certain price. If Kanrethad ever came here, the situation would be far worse.

When the demon's power fully recharged the mage tower, the surrounding forest and land might even wither rapidly from the fel and shadow, gradually turning this forest into the same dead, blighted landscape as the area around the Dark Portal.

This was something Jace had to take into account.

Returning to the Altar of Storms, Jace spotted from a distance a hellhound hiding up high, watching the direction he was approaching. He couldn't tell whose summoned hellhound it was, but at least it meant everything here was normal.

It seemed there were a few fewer warlocks living around the altar. Jace didn't know who had left, but at least Mosh'ure and his goblin student Gothzik were still here. "Shortstaff" Gladys and Luther were sitting at the camp's edge chatting about something, and the orc Karol was angrily berating his succubus servant. But when Jace appeared, everyone fell quiet and greeted him.

"Welcome back, Jace." Mosh'ure crawled out of his tent, smiling. "Are you here this time to take the dreadlord away?"

"Why would you think that?" Jace asked.

"I know you'll monopolize him sooner or later," Mosh'ure said in a low voice. "And the other warlocks know it too. I just want to remind you — before we've excavated enough knowledge… you'd better not forcibly take him elsewhere. That would provoke the dissatisfaction of all the warlocks."

Even though it sounded like some kind of threat, Jace knew Mosh'ure was right.

Even though he had enslaved Kanrethad, the dreadlord had still been summoned by everyone together, and in that process several lives had been spent.

These warlocks all seemed to have their own agendas. Jace wasn't keen on making close ties with people he couldn't trust, but he didn't want to antagonize everyone at once either.

Many of them were probably like him — back in their hometowns or places of residence they knew other warlocks, and likely held considerable standing among them. If they went back and spoke ill of him, the days ahead would get rather difficult.

"I'm just here to ask him a few questions," Jace said. "I'll be leaving for a while — possibly a long while — so there are also some things I need to go over."

"Leaving? Where are you going?" Mosh'ure asked.

Jace glanced at him and said nothing. Mosh'ure sensed Jace's wariness and said, "At least tell me roughly how long."

"Two months at the shortest. At the longest… hard to say," Jace replied.

Mosh'ure nodded. Jace could feel him thinking something over — perhaps trying to guess the general scope of the journey from the length of absence. This fellow was genuinely quite interested in where exactly he intended to go.

Not just him — every warlock here probably wanted to know how he'd managed to obtain so many special things, whether it was incantations, power, treasures, or even Molophil.

"Then we have at least two more months to communicate with this dreadlord. Not long, but better than nothing," Mosh'ure said.

"Don't talk like that," Jace said. "Even when I come back, I won't be able to take him away. Forget about me monopolizing him — other than the Altar of Storms, I don't have any place to house such a huge monster."

"I'm glad you realize that," Mosh'ure said. "In the future, once we've extracted enough information from him and the Altar of Storms becomes unsafe, we could even help you move him to a safer, more private location. We can help you find that place."

"I appreciate it," Jace said.

Mosh'ure had no idea that Jace might stuff Kanrethad into Ilgalar Tower — after all, it was a plan that even Jace himself found absurd.

But other than that tower, there was no more suitable place in the world.

Far from the city, a closed-off place under his own management, capable of gathering magical energy.

"Kanrethad."

Jace came before this enormous demon and deliberately spoke his name with the Eredun pronunciation. After more than a month without seeing him, he'd almost grown unaccustomed to the demon's sheer size.

The dreadlord looked down at him and opened his mouth slightly, speaking in Eredun: "Pitiful little master. I thought you feared my power and didn't dare come before me. If you truly feared what I am, you should release me and let me return to the Twisting Nether. Considering you haven't used my true name to grievously insult me, I could petition Kil'jaeden on your behalf to grant you a swift annihilation."

"You still keep your Kil'jaeden on a pedestal?" Jace said. "He's probably already forgotten you and struck your name from the Burning Legion's rolls."

Kanrethad said nothing.

"I guess that joke wasn't funny at all. My apologies."

Jace smiled slightly and looked back. The Altar of Storms was empty at the moment, so he opened his bag.

Sazeno tremblingly reached out and handed Jace the Burning Legion communication circle scroll that Dasmora had used to contact Kanrethad. Jace took it, said "thanks," and unrolled it to show Kanrethad.

"Do you recognize this?"

Kanrethad leaned in and examined the scroll carefully. "My name is on this. This is a circle for contacting me. It has the scent of the Eredun… hmm, I'd guess this is something you found on Dasmora."

"Can I use this circle to contact you?" Jace asked.

Kanrethad said, "It needs a few modifications, because you're not a demon."

"You tell Sazeno and have him help modify it," Jace called out the imp's name. The imp reluctantly climbed out of the bag, glanced up at the dreadlord's massive head, and weakly jumped to the ground.

"What, have you truly grown so cowardly that you can't even bear to look at me, Master? You need to use this circle to get knowledge from me?" Kanrethad said with a cold laugh.

Jace said, "Do you know the distance between Kalimdor and this continent? Can this circle function across that distance?"

"That barely qualifies as distance," Kanrethad said.

"Good." Jace looked back toward the Altar of Storms once more. "Not a single word of our conversation may be revealed to these warlocks. That's an order."

"Mm." Kanrethad made a satisfied sound, then said, "You don't want those warlocks knowing you're going to Kalimdor. I know — you're looking for something… a treasure related to Dasmora? You're worried they'll get in your way, even fight you for it… you greedy, selfish little thing. If you ask me, you'd be better off cooperating with them to find it. After all, that's something even Kil'jaeden fears — at least that way you won't die alone… heh… mm."

"Shh," Jace said. "Say one more word and I'll turn you into a Soul Bomb and hurl you into Blackrock Mountain."

Kanrethad's strange, rasping laughter quieted. "I'm of two minds. On one hand, I look forward to your death, so I can break free of your control and gain my freedom. But on the other hand, I want to see that thing with my own eyes… that thing that can make Kil'jaeden afraid."

Jace said, "Stay here obediently, Kanrethad. Don't tell those warlocks too much that's important — especially knowledge about me and this thing. If I really come back, and if I find it, I'll let you have a look. Also, help me make five Healing Stones."
