Chapter 799: Another Way to See Ghosts

After resting through the night, early the next morning Jace followed the wooden planks all the way up to the highest point of Booty Bay. By the time he arrived at the front of the alchemist Mixxelder's laboratory, daylight had fully broken.

From a nearby house Jace could hear Mixxelder chattering and making a racket, but another person's voice was inaudible — it sounded as if that goblin were communicating with some sort of telephone-like tool.

It wasn't early, and Jace had no intention of waiting for him to finish, so he called out directly, "Is Mr. Mixxelder in?" The chattering gradually died down. Mixxelder muttered a few last complaints, then opened the door and glanced out.

When he saw Jace he was startled for a moment, then immediately stretched his grin up to his ears.

"Ah, isn't this the young Mr. Slovakia? What a rare sight. What brings you to my place out of the blue?"

"Surprised I'm still alive?" Jace asked.

"Being a mage is that dangerous?" The goblin chuckled a few times and came down the steps. "What brings you here? I suppose you heard I have another guest. But since it's you, every other matter just got bumped down in priority."

"How are the potions — have they started selling?" Jace asked.

The goblin rubbed his hands. "Of course, of course. After my improvements… no, the potion you and I improved together has been successfully named the Noggenfogger-Mixxelder Potion and launched for sale in Gadgetzan. What a pain in the ass that was…"

Hearing the name, Jace asked, puzzled, "Noggenfogger-Mixxelder?"

"It's no surprise. Noggenfogger's got a lot of clout. But considering he wanted to hog the naming rights for this new drug all to himself at the start, this is already pretty good." Mixxelder gave a strained laugh. "So he still has to hand over a cut, and that means your cut shrinks right along with it."

"Where's my money, then?" Jace asked.

"Uh." Mixxelder coughed, swallowed hard, glanced back at the laboratory behind him where he kept his "test subjects" imprisoned, and said, "It's being shipped here from Kalimdor as we speak. You know how it is — gold is heavy stuff… takes ages to transport, and the journey from Kalimdor here is a dangerous one! That ship might meet naga or talking storms halfway across. You ever seen a talking storm? After seeing a thing like that, you wouldn't even be surprised if real gods existed."

"Did you spend it yourself?" Jace asked.

"Oof." The goblin sighed. "All right. I used your share to pay off my debt to that vampire Revilgaz. I won't bother making excuses — you're no ogre, you can't be fooled that easily. But if I hadn't paid him back, I wouldn't even have kept this laboratory. You wouldn't want to see an alchemist kicked out of his own alchemy lab, would you?"

"How much?" Jace asked.

Mixxelder said immediately, "Not as much as you'd think — it's all that bastard Noggenfogger muscling in! In the end it came to roughly thirty gold coins — about thirty-one gold and some odd silvers. I can pay it back. Give me a year. No, six months. I'll definitely pay off all my debt, then deliver your share plus all future royalties to Stormwind. How's that sound?"

Thirty-odd gold?

That was quite a bit more than Jace had imagined.

Of course, he had no idea who all these goblins were selling the potion to. After all, this wasn't the game — this potion's sales couldn't be that high. It definitely couldn't be sold everywhere like a Mana Potion.

Besides, he had no idea exactly how Mixxelder had calculated the royalties for him, but from the very beginning he hadn't expected to get a single copper out of this alchemist.

Since the man was now promising thirty gold, that was still a lot better than pretending the debt didn't exist. So Jace nodded and said, "If I don't hear word from you in Stormwind after six months, I'll come ask you myself, Mixxelder."

The goblin nodded repeatedly — then his gaze suddenly fastened onto the palm tree leaning against the cliff's edge, and his face changed in an instant. "The Burning Legion… why is there a demon of the Burning Legion in Booty Bay!"

Jace turned and saw Molophil leaning against the tree trunk, watching them. "She's not a member of the Burning Legion. She just looks the part."

"That's your… servant?" the goblin asked.

"Yes."

Mixxelder let out a breath. "All right. I thought warlocks' summoned demons didn't have tattoos like that. The Dark Portal business has Booty Bay on edge. You must know there's a world of difference between Burning Legion demons and your demonic servants."

"Yes, they're generally none too bright," Molophil called from afar.

"Truly." Mixxelder nodded repeatedly and said loudly, "You're right, my lady."

"One more thing, Mr. Mixxelder," Jace said.

"Another thing?" The goblin scratched his head. "Do I still owe you something?"

"About that mission to extract Necrotic Essence in Deadwind Pass last time…" Jace said.

"I remember I paid you in full," the goblin cut him off almost immediately.

"I know," Jace said. "Among the tools you gave me for that mission, there was a pair of Spectral Goggles. Do you still have those?"

"Spectral Goggles — of course." The tension in Mixxelder's face eased considerably. He put his hands on his hips. "Is your house haunted? I can lend you a pair. If you break one, the compensation is six gold, Mr. Slovakia. How's that?"

"Wasn't it four gold twenty-two silver?" Jace asked.

"Uh." The goblin was stunned for a moment. "Someone actually read the fine print, huh? Well, that thing's outdated. After the Orc Wars ended, prices have been rising everywhere. Material costs aren't the same, so the compensation has to go up too."

"That's not important. The thing is, I need more than one pair," Jace said.

The goblin shook his head. "I've only got two pairs total here. The other pair I need for myself — I can't lend it to you. But it sounds like if you need help, the trouble isn't as simple as a haunted house."

"It's not that simple," Jace said. "The ghost I'm hunting this time is a bit harder to deal with."

"Hunting?" The goblin scratched his head. "You should have used the Spectral Goggles when you extracted Necrotic Essence last time. Once you put them on, you can't see anything except ghosts. If you're going after ghosts in some unfamiliar or dangerous place, there might be dangers other than ghosts lying in ambush. Last time there was a mercenary who used Spectral Goggles to track a spirit in Duskwood — he ended up getting his neck bitten through by a spider that had been hiding in that run-down house. That spider was later caught by the guards and measured — over ten feet long, a whole lot bigger than a you human. You understand what I mean? For someone wearing Spectral Goggles, a ten-foot spider monster could lie in ambush right under their nose. In the end it turned out the spider had only been imitating a ghost's wail to lure over idiots like that mercenary…"

Mixxelder chattered on and on, and Jace realized he'd overlooked this problem.

Darkwhisper Gulch was located in a stretch of land between the snow mountains of Winterspring and Ashenvale. Even without ghosts, it was a place bristling with dangers everywhere. If they got ambushed by other lurking monsters while searching for ghosts, that would be a real headache.

Not to mention that since both Dar'Khan and Dasmora were searching for Ursales, they might run into other demons or treasure hunters there too.

"An Undead Detection Potion could solve this problem," a raspy voice came from the doorway behind the goblin. Jace looked over and saw an exceptionally tall female troll standing there.

She wore a deep blue robe, carried a basket stuffed with all manner of herbs and unidentifiable fungi, and stared straight ahead with lifeless eyes.

In the sunlight, the troll's red hair — even tied high up — formed an extremely stark contrast against her green skin. Standing there, she looked as comical as a dead wooden idol that a child had randomly painted with bright colors.

Still, even though Jace was a bit face-blind when it came to trolls, he recognized this troll by her skin color, hair color, and the distinct shape of her teeth.

"Sylia?"
