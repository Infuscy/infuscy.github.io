Chapter 80: What's on Grede's Mind

That evening, Grede proposed a meal at The Pig and Whistle to celebrate the two of them making it back alive — and the fact that Jace had "somehow" become a hero of the kingdom.

The Pig and Whistle was especially packed tonight. Someone had even set up a long table in the middle of the hall for a banquet.

Right then, a gnome — not a real gnome, but a human with dwarfism — was dancing and singing on the table, while two or three dozen people below followed his rhythm, singing and laughing, drinking and eating heartily.

The surrounding guests, though seemingly squeezed to the edges, were all celebrating something of their own. The whole tavern was constantly bombarded with all kinds of chaotic racket.

The two of them ordered Redridge goulash, baked golden apple, and pan-fried stonescale trout — a spread full of Redridge flavor. To drink, they went for a light ale, a specialty of the Dwarf District. Grede was making a small compromise for Jace's sake, drinking something with a lighter taste.

When ordering, Jace practically had to shout at the top of his lungs, because if he didn't, the serving girl couldn't hear a word he said. After they'd settled on everything, he even felt a little oxygen-deprived, slowly slumping down onto the table like a robot that had powered off.

Grede, who knew what he was thinking, hadn't said a word the whole time, just watching the person with dwarfism dance happily on the table.

That person was dressed in gnome clothing, wearing the small hat gnomes liked to wear, dancing gnome dances. Except for the ears and eyes not being as prominently protruding as a gnome's, he was practically a gnome. Seeing Grede's silence, Jace's thoughts slowly drifted to Westfall.

He was planning what to bring for the trip, the timing, what troubles he might run into, how to carry the imp around, when the imp could come out and when he absolutely couldn't, and so on.

He was lost in thought until the baked golden apple was brought out by the serving girl, and Grede's expression suddenly changed.

"What's going on?" he snapped at the serving girl.

The serving girl froze for a moment. When she realized there was nothing on the table, she suddenly caught on: "I'm so sorry. There are too many guests right now — I sincerely apologize for the mistake."

"Quick, send this apple to where it's supposed to go!" Grede bellowed.

"Yes, ma'am." The serving girl flinched, picked up the baked apple, and fled.

Jace said quietly, "It's just the two of us eating. There's no need to make it so formal, Grede."

"Formal? What's that got to do with anything?" Grede said unhappily. "If you eat something sweet before the main course, how are you supposed to have any appetite left for meat and sausages? It's only after you've had a proper meal that a little something sweet serves its purpose — lifting your spirits, making you happy, cutting through the grease, and opening your appetite back up, ready for the next course!"

Jace listened to this nonsensical spiel and just stared at Grede. He knew Grede definitely wasn't angry just because the serving girl had brought the dessert too early. "So what's really going on?"

Grede glanced over, dazed for a moment, before realizing what Jace was asking. The displeased look on his face slowly faded.

"Fine... I got knocked flat by a single swing from that orc."

At first Jace didn't follow his train of thought at all, then he realized what this guy had been stewing over this whole time, and understood what had set him off so unreasonably.

"You mean that shaman."

The dwarf said in a whimpering, wailing tone: "The sages on Aerie Peak are all refined and gentle — I've never seen them fight, never even seen them hit anyone. They're like a bunch of kind teachers and elders, patient with every kind of trouble. I figured orcish wise men ought to be about the same."

Jace said, "Maybe that orc was a refined teacher and elder back in the Horde?"

Grede let out a sudden laugh, just as the serving girl set a plate of pan-fried fish in front of him.

"Then being an orc must be miserable," he said, dipping his finger in the fish broth and putting it in his mouth. "I still don't understand that force... that ferocity. I can't make sense of it. I've never fought an orc directly, but I've seen dwarven warriors who've fought orcs. Dwarves aren't afraid of orc warriors at all. Orc warriors are very strong, sure, but not to the point of... Because I was the best of those warriors! It was precisely because I was outstanding that I was chosen as a Gryphon Rider! Dwarves shouldn't be that much weaker than orcs — there's no way I shouldn't have been able to last even one exchange."

Jace speared a piece of fish with his fork and said, "Orc shamans are like that. When he charged at us, I already saw him channeling wind elements to bolster his attack. Didn't you feel it?"

"Wind elements?" Grede thought for a moment. "When he struck my shield, I did feel an extremely powerful force of wind. Can wind elements help a shaman strengthen their muscles? Come to think of it, my left arm is still aching faintly. Really wounds the pride."

Jace said, "Wind elements definitely amplify their impact — otherwise why would that orc have summoned wind elements when attacking?"

"That does make me feel a bit better, Jace. Damn magic again." Grede dipped his finger in the fish juices again and popped it in his mouth.

"You — stop that," Jace said, pointing at his finger. "One dip is enough — don't get your saliva all over the fish! There's a fork right there."

The dwarf chuckled, wiped his finger on his clothes, and picked up his fork.

Jace speared another piece of fish and put it in his mouth. "Think about those human warriors who fought the orc armies in Black Marsh, in Stranglethorn Vale, in Elwynn, in Redridge, in Westfall. I'm certain that orc shaman was by no means a standout or particularly powerful figure in the Horde — and even so, he still managed to fight the two of us into such a miserable state. So you can imagine what price the Alliance must have paid to finally drive them back." Grede said, "Of course I know. Not to mention the Amani trolls, not to mention their..."

At that, the dwarf hesitated for a moment. "Let's not talk about this anymore. There's nothing more to say — it's all in the past anyway. The orcs are getting fewer. In the future, I won't recklessly drag you into the fire without first figuring out what the mission actually involves."

"So you do know it's a fire," Jace said. "That orc shaman was as massive as an elephant. I stabbed him twice with my sword and both times it got caught on bone — it was simply... that thing was not a normal creature!"

"That was my bad," Grede said. "By the way, have you ever seen an ogre?"

"No," Jace said.

The serving girl finally brought out the goulash. The plate, covered in a thick brown-red sauce, was piled with a heap of glistening offal and meat still on the bone, steaming hot. Chunks of fat wobbled as the plate hit the table — utterly irresistible.

"Dig in," Grede said, directly grabbing a length of intestine with his hand and stuffing it into his mouth. He said leisurely, "A unique flavor, Jace. I know you can't stomach animal intestines — you Stormwind humans are all like that. You simply don't understand what good food is."

Jace gave a soft snort at his bumpkin enthusiasm. "Let's talk about ogres instead."

"The Wildhammer legions encountered ogre forces of the Horde in Arathi." The dwarf licked the oil off his fingers and said, "Those ogres were each a full ten feet tall — one and a half times a human, you understand? I say that and you might have trouble picturing it, but when you actually stand in front of one, you know it's truly staggering. Those things weren't just tall — they were burly, fat. A dwarf's sword would stab into their belly and come back out without a drop of blood — all yellow."

"Shit?"

"Fat!" Grede said, poking at the chunks of fat on the plate with his finger. "This kind of fat, you understand. They don't care at all about stab wounds — an attack that would skewer a troll does nothing to them. Swing at their neck and the sword gets sucked in by the flesh, impossible to pull out. You won't see blood unless you use a long weapon. You can't even imagine that creatures like that can also use magic."

"Not to mention dragons," Jace said.

"Not to mention dragons!" Grede nodded vigorously.
