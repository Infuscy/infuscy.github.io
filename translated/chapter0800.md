## Chapter 800: Just Don't Think About It

"So the dragon-slaying Jace Sesso still remembers my name." The troll witch doctor stepped down the stairs, swaying slightly as she came up to the two of them.

"Did you tell her my real identity?" Jace asked the goblin.

Before Mixxelder could speak, Sylia said with a laugh, "News of Vereesa Windrunner's arrival in Booty Bay has reached every forest troll's ear here. And the human accompanying her is unquestionably Jace Sesso himself. You didn't think I'd be ignorant of the outside world just because I live alone in that cramped cave, did you?"

"All right. Good to see you again, Sylia," Jace said flatly.

The troll witch doctor nodded slightly. The goblin looked between the two of them and said, "Then the problem's solved. Sylia can help you. She has the potion formulas the Amani Empire's witch doctors used to communicate with spirits. Am I right?"

"I have plenty of this potion," the troll said.

"Tell me about its effects. I know nothing about it," Jace said.

The troll said, "It's a pale green potion. After drinking it, it takes about half an hour to take effect. If there are ghosts nearby, during that half hour you'll gradually begin to see them more and more, until you can perceive them clearly… and it hardly affects your normal vision at all."

"How long does it last?" Jace asked.

"Depends on how much you drink. A small sip lasts about half a day — and I mean a human's small sip," Sylia answered.

Jace nodded. "Then I might need quite a lot. Is this potion expensive?"

"Twelve silver a bottle," Sylia said.

"How much liquid is in a bottle? In other words, how big is a bottle?" Jace asked.

"A very small bottle," Sylia said. "If you need a lot, I can also put it in a leather pouch for you. A mature potion like this isn't as delicate as those potions the Dalaran alchemists cook up. If you wanted, you could even drink it out of a bowl."

"Enough for three to four people for about a week," Jace said.

"You're planning to attack the Shadowlands?!" the goblin asked in surprise.

"It's not that dramatic, Bax Mixxelder," the troll witch doctor said. "I'll charge you two gold. How's that, Jace Sesso?"

"Fine," Jace said.

"Then come with me." The troll flexed her thick fingers and walked down the stairs.

The witch doctor's cave was almost exactly as it had been last time he'd come here — tree roots tangled everywhere.

Jace stopped at the cave entrance. Sylia looked back at him. "You're not coming in? Or would you rather stay outside with this strangely scentless succubus?"

"I'll sit this one out," Jace said.

The troll nodded, gave a cold laugh, and disappeared into the doorway.

Molophil crept close and whispered, "Can't you feel it, Master? She wants you to go in… Just go ahead, I won't tell the Quel'dorei about this."

Jace pulled his hood tighter, counted out two gold coins from his pocket, and said, "Stop talking."

A moment later the witch doctor came out of the cave carrying a dark brown waterskin. "Here." Jace handed her the gold coins. "What's this made of?"

"Not human skin," Sylia said. "If that's what you were wondering."

"It's not elf skin, is it?" Jace asked again.

"Of course not," Sylia said. "So you don't need to worry about offending that Farstrider. But I don't want to say too much. I can only tell you this hide is tough and won't tear easily — rest assured. But this requires an extra four silver. I think that's fair. If it were elf skin, it definitely wouldn't be this price."

Jace nodded, added four more silver coins, and Sylia took the silver and reminded him, "Remember — only a small sip each time. Drinking too much will cause hallucinations, and prolonged ghostly vision will disturb you weak humans' minds. You haven't had the training we have."

"I'll remember. Thank you very much."

Jace pulled out the stopper and sniffed the potion inside. He caught a whiff of blood, plus a faint, not particularly pleasant floral scent — like a rose tainted by shadow.

"Let me try it first."

"But there are no ghosts here for you to test the effect," Sylia said.

"Let me see if I can get used to the medicine first."

Jace took a small sip. The texture was no different from ordinary water, except for a bitter taste.

He swallowed it and waited a while. Feeling no discomfort, he nodded, stoppered the opening, and tucked the pouch into his bag.

Sylia looked at him and said, "You've changed a lot, Jace Sesso — in every respect. Appearance, build, and… spirit. Last time we met you were like a wanderer who didn't care about anything. Now I can feel you've grown more powerful, but also more constrained. You've started caring about too many things, and those things are ropes — they'll bind you more and more, and in the end they'll destroy you."

Her voice was extremely raspy. If you didn't look at her cat-like face, you'd really think it was some elderly witch mocking an ignorant brat in a taunting tone.

"Are you some kind of prophet in the Hinterlands?" Jace looked up and asked.

"No," Sylia said.

"Then that's just the sort of generic nonsense that sounds profound no matter who you say it to," Jace said.

"I mean well." The troll bared her fangs in a smile.

Jace said nothing more. He glanced at Molophil, signaling her to follow, and turned to leave the witch doctor's lair.

Before he'd even reached the Sailor's Home inn, Jace could already hear the dwarf's thunderous laughter from inside. Grede must be having a great time.

The sky had already darkened, and the inn's doorway was packed with people — most of them dressed in ragged clothes.

Every customer who wanted to go in had to squeeze through these people, clutching their bags tightly, eyeing these ill-intentioned men cautiously, terrified they'd reach out.

But when Jace approached, Molophil's presence behind him immediately cleared a path wide enough to pass through comfortably. Every time he came to Booty Bay, Jace felt there were occasional perks to being a universally despised warlock — after all, the "people" here didn't distinguish between good and bad, high or low.

Deep inside the inn, a group of sturdy, muscular men were shouting and hollering around a massive wooden target. A bare-chested goblin was hefting a short axe and aiming at the target while the goblins around him cheered him on.

Grede stood to the side with his folded arms, looking coldly at the goblin's movements, a look of contempt on his face. He was so absorbed in the game he hadn't even noticed Jace appear at the door.

"Over there, Master," Molophil murmured in his ear, pointing toward a corner. Jace looked where she pointed — Vereesa was raising her hand at him.

The inn was packed shoulder to shoulder. Even though everyone was a bit afraid of a demon like Molophil, they had no way to clear a path for Jace, so he could only slowly wriggle through with the succubus and come to the table, sitting down next to the elf.

"He's been at it for a long time," Vereesa said with a shake of her head. "Should we eat something first, or wait for him?"

"Let's wait for him," Jace said.

Vereesa twitched her nose, pulled Jace's cloak over, and sniffed again. "A foul smell."

"Foul?" Jace sniffed in some embarrassment. "I really haven't washed in a long time."

"I'm not talking about how you smell." Vereesa leaned in and said softly, "Troll. What have you been doing?" Jace explained, "I went to a troll witch doctor and bought a potion that can detect ghosts."

Vereesa frowned. "A witch doctor? I thought you were going to the goblin for Spectral Goggles? What was that goblin alchemist's name again, Buck…"

"Bax Mixxelder," Jace said. "But the Spectral Goggles have a lot of problems. Relatively speaking, the potion is more suitable. As it happens, that goblin knows a troll witch doctor who can solve this problem."

"Using troll stuff feels really strange to me," Vereesa said. "Never mind their creepy witchcraft and hexes… just their recipes. These trolls like to use all kinds of bodily fluids — not just blood, but sweat, spit, urine and the like — to make potions. Doesn't that disgust you?"

"It does. I just choose not to think about it," Jace said.

"You." Vereesa rubbed her brow in exasperation.

Jace took out the troll's potion pouch and set it on the table. "Want a taste? See if there's anything weird in it?" Vereesa glanced at the pouch, her throat bobbed, but she still shook her head.

Sitting here, watching the customers and inn staff coming and going before his eyes, Jace was actually thinking about how to do a bit of simple publicity for Ursula.

Was he just supposed to stand up and shout, "Everyone, listen to my announcement"?

When he'd agreed to it he hadn't thought much about it. Only now did he realize he had no talent for advertising whatsoever.

Just then Grede came beaming through a forest of thick legs and uneven waists, and slammed a black short axe onto the table. "Look at this."

"No idea," Jace said.

The dwarf said immediately, "This is a Dark Iron captain's family heirloom — it's called Ribsplitter! How about that? I won it off some Shadowforge City idiot!"

"Incredible," Jace said flatly. Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "You two are really no fun."

Grede plopped down, put the axe away, and his gaze landed on the potion pouch. "What the hell is this?"

"Troll potion," Vereesa explained. "Jace says it's a potion that lets you see ghosts. Though I don't know if it actually works that well."

"Half an hour after drinking it to take effect, lasts about half a day," Jace said. "I took a sip to try it when I got the potion. Anyway, it's not harmful. By rights I should be able to see ghosts now, but there aren't any ghosts for me to look at, so I don't know if it actually works."

"We can try it on the beach tomorrow," Grede said. "Vereesa says our ship won't leave until the day after tomorrow. There might be sailors who died in shipwrecks, or poor souls killed by naga, whose spirits can't move on — we can test the potion's effect."

"The odds of running into something like that are way too slim," Vereesa said.

"There are some troll ruins around the Gurubashi Arena. I remember there's a troll graveyard there — we could wander around and see if any troll ghosts are lingering," Jace said.

Vereesa said, "That sounds more promising."

Grede gave her an irritated look. "Oh sure, everything Jace Sesso says is right, huh!"

Even as he spoke he picked up the pouch, turned it over a couple of times, and then his expression grew more and more grave. "The person who sold you this — did they give you this pouch?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Did they tell you what it's made of?" Grede asked.

"Uh, no."

As he said it, Jace felt something was off.

Grede slammed the table hard. "This is dwarf skin, goddamn it!"
