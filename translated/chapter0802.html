Chapter 802: Protection Too Much to Bear

The Reliquary had booked two rooms for Jace and the others. Grede had the small one to himself, while the large one was left for Jace and Vereesa.

It was a remarkably spacious and refined room. In a place like Booty Bay, a room like this was truly hard to come by.

Carved wooden chairs, a dark brown carpet, a wide cushioned bed, a bookshelf and wardrobe — it even reminded Jace of the quarters the instructors lived in back at the Wizard Sanctum.

He even felt this wasn't the sort of lodging you ought to find in the middle of an adventure.

Watching Jace examine every corner of the room, Vereesa smiled with a hint of satisfaction — this had certainly been her deliberate arrangement.

She hung up her cloak, slipped off her outer garments one by one, and sat down beside him wearing only close-fitting Underwear inscribed with golden tree-runes. Jace drew a sharp breath at the faint trace of her abs visible at her waist — then a heavy snore tore right through the mood.

That bastard Grede's snoring could be heard clearly even over the noise from downstairs, even through the wall.

Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "Remember the last time we went to Kalimdor? Grede's snoring scared every animal in the whole forest into silence."

Jace set the Kalimdor language lexicon on the nightstand and looked at her. "Never mind the animals in the forest — even that kodo barely made a sound the whole way. Must've been terrified."

The two of them laughed together. Vereesa climbed onto the bed and leaned against Jace's side, looking at the elemental language lexicon. "If only you put a quarter of the effort you're putting into learning the elemental tongue into learning Thalassian. What, you think learning Thalassian is useless? Don't forget, a considerable number of arcane incantations are written in our language."

"I'm trying, Ve," Jace said with a smile.

"Then let me test you." Vereesa thought for a moment. "If one day you met an honorable Quel'dorei and accidentally cast shadow magic, causing a misunderstanding — and now he's furious — how would you explain yourself?"

She twirled her finger. "Right now arcane light is already dancing in his hands!"

Jace cleared his throat and, in fluent and perfectly accented Thalassian, cried softly: "Have mercy on me, I beg you…"

"You're going to say that?" Vereesa frowned, then suddenly realized where this guy had learned that line and slapped him hard across the forehead.

"Don't you copy that!"

"It's for rapid improvement," Jace muttered, holding his forehead.

Vereesa cursed with a laugh, climbed up, and sat squarely on his chest, pressing down hard.

Don't let her slim figure fool you — in combat she moved as lightly as a stoat, but this press actually felt pretty heavy. Jace coughed a couple of times and looked up. Eye to eye, one high and one low, they gazed at each other for a moment, and the smile on her face slowly faded.

"Right, where's Molophil?" she said. "Don't tell me she's hiding somewhere in this room. Though I can't sense her."

"Why do you ask?" Jace glanced toward the door. "She's on the stairs outside, keeping watch for the three of us. Booty Bay isn't a safe place."

Vereesa lowered her head and fell silent for a moment. "I don't know, Jace. Even though I constantly remind myself she's just a demon, not a person, that she's merely your summoned creature, I still can't completely let my guard down. After all, she's no hellhound or felbat. And… every time I use the fact that she's a demon as a reason to tell myself to stop worrying, I think back to the scenes of her saving me again and again. At Maraudon, at Dire Maul, at Grim Batol. Grim Batol… do you remember?"

"Of course I remember," Jace said.

"She caught me, and we tumbled down that high slope together." Vereesa said in a low voice. "She was badly wounded, bled a lot — I mean, demon blood. But at the time, frenzied earth elementals and fire elementals were everywhere. I wanted to pull her up and leave together, but she shook me off, saying she didn't need my pity. But when those elementals charged over, she rushed to protect me without hesitation."

Jace was about to say something, but Vereesa stopped him. "I know it's your orders, I know… you've said many times that you ordered her to protect me, but…"

Jace understood her feelings. Let alone a ranger like her — even warlocks who knew demonic natures inside and out couldn't tell whether a succubus was a companion, a pet, a friend, a lover, or simply a tool.

Or perhaps those warlocks themselves didn't want to figure it out.

Vereesa said in what was almost a commanding tone, "Don't let her protect me anymore. Release her from that order, Jace. If this goes on any longer, I'll only feel more and more torn."

"Then be prepared yourself," Jace said. "If I don't give her the order, don't count on her lifting a finger to help you. I once watched a warlock's succubus look on indifferently as the warlock's dying friend lay there — even though that demon could have saved the friend with a single spell. She's a demon, after all. The only reason she doesn't want me dead is because of the contract, because of my mana — not because she has any feelings for me. At least, not feelings as we'd understand them."

"I know," Vereesa said. "But regardless, I don't want to owe that demon any more debts."

"All right." Jace nodded.

The elf was quiet for a long while before lowering her body and slowly settling onto Jace's chest.

Vereesa Windrunner was a formidable warrior, so embracing her was never as soft as hugging other girls. At moments like this, he could clearly feel the inhuman strength contained within her slender body, beneath smooth skin — unyielding, tough.

But at this moment, that strength placed its full trust in him, holding him tight, bringing about a completely different kind of gentleness and warmth.

She whispered in his ear, "Her fel eye doesn't just hold the power of the arcane and the shadow. That fire wasn't kindled by those forces — it was life. The fervor of life… that's the Dragonqueen's power. I can feel it. I imagine she's siphoned mana from you."

"Yes," Jace nodded.

"How unfair." Vereesa practically breathed the words into his ear. "So compensate me. Now."

Jace felt her lower herself gradually, sliding down, the warmth and wetness beneath his belly growing, until slowly they melted into one another.

Vereesa lowered her head. "It's really hard to imagine — the man who defeated Deathwing is now inside me, Jace." She smiled, then gradually frowned and closed her eyes as Jace slowly pressed deeper.

Jace looked at her trembling silver brows. "Sounds like you only fell in love with me because I defeated Deathwing."

"Of course not." Vereesa let out a breath, her face slightly flushed. "Mainly because simply imagining this idiot in front of me entering my body isn't difficult at all."
