Chapter 803: Setting Sail, the Second Journey to Kalimdor

Though the ghost that had popped up the past couple of nights had made rest a little less than pleasant, for Jace, Grede, and Vereesa, ghosts were hardly a threat anymore.

Of course, a hostile specter wouldn't dare mess with them either — especially not with a demon in their company.

On the day of departure, Jace got up at dawn and looked out the window toward the harbor. A ship that looked to be forty or fifty meters long was already docked at the pier, and several ogres with gnomes perched on their shoulders were diligently loading cargo. The gnomes' shrieking insults at the ogres could be heard even from this distance.

The group packed their luggage, made sure nothing had been forgotten, and left the room. From afar they could see the gnomes leading ogre security guards in checking passengers' identities on the pier.

Jace turned his head and said to the succubus beside him, "Go on ahead, Morofir. Slip onto the boat. Don't let them spot you."

"That's going to be difficult, Master," the succubus said, looking miserable.

"Why?" Jace asked.

She leaned in and whispered, "These past two nights, feeling that joy coming from deep within... I was so happy for you that I haven't been able to steady my emotions and hold my invisibility. You know how much shadow magic demands control over one's feelings..."

"I clearly didn't make any sound, Succubus," Vereesa complained.

Grede coughed and said, "I didn't hear a thing!" Then he strode off toward the pier with big steps.

"A lot of things can be clearly perceived without any sound at all. Don't you think so, Master?" Morofir said. Vereesa's face darkened and she quickened her pace to follow Grede.

Jace said coldly, "Behave yourself. Go invisible and get up there. Don't cause trouble, all right?"

"Fine, whatever Master says." Morofir smiled, watching Vereesa's back.

"One more thing," Jace said. "From now on, you don't need to protect Vereesa Windrunner around the clock."

"Oh, well then..." Morofir looked at Jace in surprise, paused for a moment, then said, "What a relief. After all, protecting just you alone has nearly exhausted me..."

"What?" Jace said. "Are you saying I'm a handful?"

"No, of course not. Powerful and wise Jace Sesso..."

Morofir smiled, then quickly melted into the shadows and slipped toward the pier. Jace sighed softly and jogged to catch up. By the time they reached the pier, Grede had already charged to the very front.

He'd been ranting these past two days about never coming back to this godforsaken Booty Bay, so he couldn't wait to jump on board.

Vereesa followed him up, and Jace followed close behind, showing his ticket. Not far off, a gnome foreman shouted something, and the ogre in front of Jace planted a foot right before him, hands on his hips, and said in a nearly unintelligible Common accent: "You — no pass!"

"Why?" Jace looked toward the gnome.

"Where's that succubus?" the gnome said, jumping down from a crate. "Just now — just now I saw you with that succubus, warlock. Don't think I don't know a demon like her can turn invisible."

"Big Mouth!" he shouted toward the distance. "Get to work!"

"Please, call me Mr. Gadobros."

A shrill, eerie echo came from behind the crates.

Jace had never heard that kind of tone before — not as void-like as a Voidwalker, but sharper and hollower than a demon's voice.

A squid-like thing suddenly rose up from behind the crates. It wasn't large — the whole body probably wasn't much bigger than the ogre's head — with an exaggerated mouth full of fangs that opened and closed as it complained incessantly to the gnome.

It had no wings, no propellers or anything of the sort. It just floated there in midair, bobbing as it drifted closer. Jace could tell it was levitating with void magic, but the shadow energy wasn't being summoned by it — it was radiating from within its body, surrounding it and lifting it into the air. Yet it wasn't a pure shadow creature like a Voidwalker.

Compared to this little monster, Jace's shadow affinity... probably didn't even count as shadow affinity.

Midway, a large eyeball suddenly opened on top of the big mouth, along with six or seven smaller eyes around it. They looked at the ogre first, then turned toward Jace's group.

Grede, who had almost reached the deck, heard the noise behind him and glanced back curiously.

When he saw all those eyes open on the creature, his whole body shuddered uncomfortably. It wasn't just him — Vereesa, standing in front of Jace, also froze in surprise, even instinctively reaching for the bow on her back.

Grede shot Jace a wary look, as if seeking his opinion. Jace slowly waved a hand, signaling him not to do anything rash.

"What is this... is it dangerous?" Vereesa asked warily.

"I have no interest in fighting. As long as you don't break Booty Bay's rules, no one will get hurt," the strange demon said in fluent Common, drifting closer and closer as it spoke.

It was an eye demon! A living, breathing eye demon!

Eye demons were a deeply mysterious type of demon. Many demonologists believed they came from beyond the infinite dark, rather than being born in the Twisting Nether.

Though no one knew where they came from, their abilities were well known. While their strength wasn't all that impressive, their eyes could see through nearly every kind of invisible creature, pierce hidden planes, and unravel concealed illusions.

But eye demons were extremely difficult to summon. How could these gnomes... possibly have conjured an eye demon?! No, these gnomes certainly hadn't summoned it.

Jace thought of the two-headed ogre he'd seen earlier in Booty Bay. These gnomes must have struck some kind of deal with an ogre warlock capable of summoning an eye demon, which was why this one was working security here.

"Help me find that warlock's succubus, Mr. Gadobros!" the gnome said, pointing behind Jace. "I'm sure she's hiding right here! With me watching, nobody's getting on without a ticket!"

The eye demon's teeth clacked together, and it slowly flew toward Jace. It looked back and forth, then said in Demonic, "The succubus on the boat was summoned by you, human. She slipped up there early, but she can't escape my eyes."

"Are you planning to expose me to that gnome?" Jace asked in Demonic.

"My master ordered me to obey that gnome's commands. He ordered me to inspect this place, and I am carrying out his orders now. However, my master did not require me to report everything I find to him." Gadobros floated past behind him, inspecting as he went on in a low voice of Demonic: "Still, I don't want any trouble. I don't want to provoke you, and I even less want to provoke that succubus. But at least make sure she isn't discovered on the boat — that includes that imp, or my master will be very unhappy. Give me your word, and I can pretend I saw nothing."

"Is that succubus really so hard to find?" the gnome shouted from not far off.

"You have my word," Jace said in Demonic.

The eye demon suddenly turned its head toward the gnome and screamed with its bloody maw wide open: "Nothing here, my green friend!"

"Impossible?!" the gnome shouted. "I clearly saw her!"

"I am an honest man, sir," Jace said, raising his hands.

Grede snorted a laugh and spat into the sea. Vereesa also turned her head and gave Jace a white look of pure contempt. Jace reached out and flicked her ear — that pointed, long ear wobbled back and forth from the flick.

Vereesa gasped, covered her ear, and glared back at him, gritting her teeth as she said in Thalassian, "How dare you..."

"Dare what, dare what. Hurry up and go!" Jace stared at her.

Vereesa glanced down at the gnome supervisor who was eyeing the group, then turned and stomped off onto the deck in a huff.

The Moving Pandaria was a fairly large cargo ship, its holds big enough to accommodate several hundred passengers. The tickets the Reliquary had purchased all came with cabins — though they were so narrow that even straightening your back and spreading your arms was a struggle, at least they were private quarters.

The first time Jace heard the name, he had been a little surprised that these people even knew about a place called Pandaria. Later he asked around and learned that legends of the mysterious continent of Pandaria had long been circulating among fishermen and sailors.

After stowing their things, they came up on deck to take in the view for a while. By then the captain and sailors were shouting as they raised the anchor, and the ship's movement startled two flocks of white seabirds that had been resting high on the masts.

Watching the people waving on the shore, the gradually receding pier, the upside-down giant hammerheads, and the giant barracudas that weren't much smaller, Jace felt that this departure from Booty Bay to Kalimdor was far less relaxing than last time.

Although he was a bit stronger now than before, with a deeper understanding of this world, the difficulties waiting for him...

Last time, the mission had only been to investigate the traces of an unknown Highborne warlock. With Vereesa and Grede's help, it had been several times easier — especially since the Reliquary hadn't even demanded they find the warlock, just that they uncover further leads.

Of course, ending up coming face to face with the princess of Maraudon, and nearly being strangled to death in the depths of Eldre'Thalas by a demon wielding boundless magical power — that was something he had never anticipated.

But what about this time?

Jace turned around and leaned against the railing, pulling out his copied version of Dathomere's report scroll from his bag. He looked at the Eredun letters on it, along with the annotations he'd made.

Uth'ales, the Headwind Reaper.

This time, the thing he was facing was far from simple.

Even if there were no surprises at all, even if that scythe-like staff were just sitting there waiting for him to pick it up, the danger it posed by itself was far greater than Dathomere, that doomguard leader.

Ever since he'd made the decision to go to Kalimdor, Jace had been constantly thinking about whether he truly wanted to seize that artifact. After all, this wasn't a game — binding his soul to the weapon stored within that staff wasn't as simple as right-clicking to equip it.

Uth'ales, who had forged the scythe with his very soul, was himself one of the most powerful dreadlords to have ever existed in the Twisting Nether.

And its previous owner, Sathel, whose soul had also been reaped by this scythe, was a madwoman ambitious enough to try to harvest Sargeras's soul.

Although she had unsurprisingly failed, the fact that Sargeras had been willing to hand Uth'ales to her, and that she had wielded the weapon for so many long years... was proof enough of her strength.

It was just that the whispering souls in Darkwhisper Gorge were very likely the result of Uth'ales reemerging in the world for some reason. Guardian Scavel's seal on the weapon had clearly been crumbling bit by bit with the passage of time. No matter how powerful a guardian was, it was hard to account for what would happen eight hundred years from now.

Either find a way to help Scavel repair the seal, or... Jace gripped the Chain of Will around his neck.

Not far off, Vereesa and Grede were leaning against the railing, pointing at the shadow of a cluster of islands at the northern edge of their vision and saying something, their voices carrying the occasional argument — but they looked far more at ease.

"Don't stand there, human." A Jace sailor appeared behind him at some point and said loudly, "Careful you don't get caught by a rope and fall into the sea. No one's fishing you out."

Jace moved aside and said, "Excuse me, sir, how do you get from Gadgetzan to Everlook?"

The gnome put his hands on his hips and said impatiently, "By airship, of course. There are two airships departing from Gadgetzan every week. It takes about a week of flying to reach Everlook and come back."

"How much is the fare?" Jace asked.

The gnome scratched the corner of his mouth and said, "Uh, I wouldn't know. I have no interest in going to Everlook, so I never asked the price. It definitely can't be more expensive than a ticket across the Great Sea."

"Have any airships ever crashed halfway there?" Jace asked.

The gnome immediately shouted angrily, "What kind of nonsense are you spouting? Airships built by the Trade Cartel have the finest safety record in the world! No other company's airships can even compare. Besides, rather than worrying about an airship having trouble halfway through, you'd be better off considering whether you can survive Winterspring! Get out of the way, you big lunkhead. Don't think that just because you paid and came on board we're going to treat you like a boss! Jumping to conclusions without knowing anything — this world is full of people like you wasting precious time, which is exactly why it's so barbaric and stupid!"

"I was just asking a question..."

Jace muttered as he gave way another step, watching the gnome stride off to tidy up the tools piled up nearby, feeling as if his single question had triggered some kind of stress response.

Come to think of it, the Trade Cartel was probably the only ones in this world manufacturing airships, wasn't it? This gnome had said a whole lot of nothing.

Seemed no use asking gnomes. Most of the gnomes in Booty Bay were members of the Trade Cartel — they'd never say a bad word about their own.

Maybe once he reached Gadgetzan, asking the citizens who lived in that city but had little to do with the Cartel would get him more accurate information.
