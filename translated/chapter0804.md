## Chapter 804: The Eredar's Game

The more time Jace spent on ships, the more he felt like he could read the personality of the sea beneath his ass. The mood swings of Baradin Bay, the restlessness of Booty Bay, the indifference of the Hillsbrad coast.

And the Great Sea was like a giant watching you at all times. Every breath it took could easily destroy you, but it had no intention of doing so, leaving everyone sailing on its surface in an anxiety they could never escape — either slowly going mad, or... becoming numb.

So the longer he spent at sea, the more respect Jace had for the gnome sailors working on the ship. Even when their tone was occasionally awful and they pointed fingers at him, Jace didn't much mind.

One or two months of sailing was this agonizing — he couldn't even imagine what kind of hardships the sailors who spent years at sea had to overcome.

Beyond the hardship and misery of sailing itself, there were naga, the legendary ghost pirates, and the murmurs rising from the sea that the sailors occasionally mentioned but Jace had never truly felt for himself.

That night, the hull began to grow unsteady.

The sky was black as thick ink. Scratches of rain scraped against his face. Rumbling stirred in the clouds, but there was no lightning to be seen. It seemed the *Moving Pandaria* ultimately hadn't been able to completely avoid the storm bearing down on them.

Jace went back from the deck to his cabin and shut the door, which muffled the sound of the rain and wind by more than half.

Two weeks had already passed since leaving Booty Bay. Life on the ship had inevitably grown more and more dull. He'd been studying Dwarvish this whole time, and right now even the copy of *Runes* sitting on the edge of his bed made his head spin.

"Doubt I'll sleep well tonight either."

He said this while looking at the succubus in the deepest part of the room, scratching his head impatiently.

"Then let's play something interesting to pass the time, shall we, Master?" Morofir tilted her head slightly.

"Makes sense." Jace pulled out his coin pouch. "Let's play a game of chess, then."

Sazeno excitedly jumped onto the table and asked, "Is it the kind of chess with kings and knights? I want to try too."

"But we have neither a board nor pieces," Morofir said.

Azeroth did have a game somewhat resembling chess. The most famous board was undoubtedly the chess hall in Karazhan, but under the current conditions there was no way they could play that kind of chess.

Jace pulled a handful of gold coins from the pouch, then a handful of silver, found a blank piece of paper, and drew a grid on the back.

"You take gold, I'll take silver, Morofir. Line up five pieces in a row on the grid and you win. Simple enough? You go first."

Morofir picked up a gold coin and looked back and forth over the paper for a while. "Anywhere is fine?"

"Anywhere's fine," Jace said.

She carefully picked a spot slightly off-center and placed the coin down. But Jace had played countless games of Gomoku back in middle school during class. Even after twenty years without touching it, even if his opponent was a real demon, there was no way she could beat him right off the bat.

Once he immersed himself in the game and carefully thought through the patterns from his past life, Jace suddenly felt as if he'd returned to his middle school classroom. He didn't even notice the ship's gentle rocking anymore, and the misery of seasickness quickly faded.

Sure enough, after just a few rounds, the succubus had been effortlessly led into a trap. The imp burst out laughing, but Morofir immediately saw the problem.

"Master is far too familiar with this game. Would winning against me like this even be fun?" she complained.

Jace rolled a silver coin between his fingers and said, "Play a few more times and maybe you can beat me. Or... do you know any other games?"

"You mean a demon's game? An Eredar's game?" Morofir asked.

"What do they play?" Jace asked, curious now too.

Morofir said, "Most of the Eredar masters I've encountered have no interest in carnal pleasure or games. They yearn only for profound spells and mighty power, and destruction is the simplest way to prove everything they pursue."

"But it's not as if there's nothing at all. There was one Eredar warlock, actually." She reached out and casually pinched a gold coin between her fingers, flipping it back and forth a few times. "Not quite like my other Eredar masters. He was very fond of some kind of otherworldly chess and forced me to learn it so he could play against me."

"Tell me about it," Jace said.

"But that board and those pieces were summoned by him with the Arcane, Master." Morofir's tone even carried a hint of mockery.

Jace's expression cooled. "Then forget it. Keep playing my game."

"Is that an order?" Morofir asked.

"Don't do it if you don't want to. What fun is there in forcing you to play?" Jace tossed the silver coin aside.

"I'll play, Master!" The imp grabbed a gold coin. "Let's play, the two of us. Forget about this monster. See, I'm not getting more useless, am I?"

"Fine." Jace set the paper back up again.

He glanced at the succubus. She had drawn back and slowly sat down on the bed, crossing one leg over the other, staring indifferently toward the door.

A flash of lightning slipped through the crack in the door, lighting up the whole room for an instant and illuminating her cold, pale-red face and the horns on top of her head as clear as day.

The life flame burning in her eyes had mostly gone out, leaving only a dim socket with the faintest glimmer. Jace rarely saw Morofir like this.

"What is it?" he asked curiously. "Don't tell me you actually miss that Eredar? Did you ever love him?"

After a dull roll of thunder, Morofir smiled and said, "Master, you don't believe I've fallen in love with you, yet you think I fell in love with an Eredar? Heh... I can smell the jealousy..."

"I have no fondness for those Eredar masters. Most of them are members of the Burning Legion, and even those who aren't have close ties to the Legion. They're basically no different from the Legion's running dogs." She lowered her voice. "I just suddenly felt that I've been alive for too long. So much of the past has turned into fragments in my head. When I think about it now, there's a strange feeling. I can't quite describe what it is."

"You're the first demon I've met who dwells on the past this much," the imp said, clutching a gold coin and placing it on the paper with effort. "Is it some kind of aftereffect from Master's fire essence burning you, huh?"

Morofir gave him a disdainful sideways glance but didn't immediately fire back with a rebuttal.

"You sure you're all right, Morofir?" Jace asked.

"What could be wrong?" The succubus waved a hand. "A sentimental creature like me — what harm is there in being a little melancholy now and then? Unless Master gives me a bit more mana? Maybe I'll perk up right away."

"Spare me." Jace immediately lowered his head and reached for a silver coin.
