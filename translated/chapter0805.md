## Chapter 805: Fire Elementals at Sea

Jace didn't know how long it had been when he woke from his groggy half-sleep. The *Moving Pandaria* still hadn't steadied. The corridor outside was soaking wet all over. The sky was overcast and gloomy, impossible to tell day from night. He could only vaguely feel that the sunlight seemed to be stifled behind him, and a cold wind was blowing across the sea.

Grede was lying not far off, gazing out through the window into the distance. Jace came up beside him and said a rather long "good afternoon" in Dwarvish.

The dwarf frowned and turned his head. "Nobody greets people with that, you idiot."

"Why?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "It's a farewell blessing. Think about how stupid it would be to say 'see you tomorrow' to Vereesa first thing in the morning. And hardly any dwarves use it in daily life these days. You might hear it in a play, maybe. Even back in the era of the High King, only the scholars talked like that. You've been studying Dwarvish for this long and this is the level you've reached? I'm starting to doubt how you even learned Demonic and Orcish..."

"I learned Orcish from contemporary orc records. As for Demonic, I've got two demons at home who talk to me all the time," Jace said. "But the only Dwarvish dictionary I have on hand right now is *Droken Goldbeard's Khaz Modan Runes*."

"No wonder. I've heard of that book. An old fossil," Grede said.

"It's more than enough for learning Kalimdor tongues," Jace said.

"Oh, right." Grede said, "I'd like to take a look at that Kalimdor-language book of yours too, Jace. Since you want me to help you learn Dwarvish, how about lending me that book to have a look?"

He turned to gaze out at the sea and went on: "I'd like to try talking to the elements the way those sages do — really talking to them, not just using them to call down lightning. Storms, the sea..."

Just then a gnome sailor hurried past, looked at the two of them, and said, "I strongly advise you to go back to your cabins. The storm could pick up again at any moment, gentlemen."

"We will. Thank you." Jace nodded. After the gnome sailor ran to the end of the corridor, he went on, "Haven't you heard the sailors mention the whispers in the sea?"

Grede coughed. "Talking to the sky alone is fine, isn't it? I don't reckon I've got much to do with the sea anyway."

"No problem," Jace said.

Grede tried saying in Dwarvish, "You learned to speak with elementals from the Dark Iron dwarves in Ironforge." Jace thought about what he meant for a moment, then answered in Dwarvish: "Mainly fire elementals."

Grede nodded and continued in Dwarvish, "Can you hear the voice of fire elementals out at sea?"

Now that he mentioned it, Jace realized it had been a long time since he'd spoken with fire. Ever since leaving Ironforge and returning to Stormwind, he'd been going back and forth between places like the Slaughtered Lamb, or making preparations for the trip to Kalimdor, then adjusting to life at sea.

His elemental language had improved a lot compared to when he was in Ironforge. By rights, fire should be even easier for him to reach now.

With that thought, he closed his eyes and felt the rhythm of fire in the air. Compared to Ironforge, the voice of fire elementals here was so thin it barely counted — more like a faint, almost imperceptible trace. But when he called to the fire in the elemental tongue to gather at his fingertip, a bit of warmth still coalesced, forming a glow of flame.

The fire vanished in a blink, quickly extinguishing in the mist, but Grede still widened his eyes in surprise. Then he said a long string of things in Dwarvish related to fire elementals and summoning, even mentioning Zin-Sarow at one point.

Jace said, "Just speak Common."

Grede sighed helplessly and said, "I'm just impressed, Jace. That power you wield when calling elementals — it reminds me of the troll priests of Zin'Sarow."

"Try finding a better comparison."

"Because the flame you summoned, although small, is every bit as aggressive as those trolls'! It's hard for me to connect you with those sages who borrow fire to roast meat!" The dwarf laughed, then asked, "But I'm curious, Jace — since you can call fire elementals in the middle of the sea, why didn't you try to get fire to help you that day in Elwynn Forest when you were fighting me?"

"I've still got plenty of tricks I didn't use, Grede," Jace said in a boastful tone. "If my imp Sazeno had been by my side, I'd have definitely shown you some new moves. Ever heard of a doomguard's Cripple?"

"You think I didn't experience that bizarre fel magic at the Dark Portal?" Grede was completely unfazed. "Next time, bring your imp along and we'll have another round. I'd like to see how much stronger your Cripple is than those demons'."

"No problem." Jace nodded, then said, "But speaking of fire, I'm still not familiar enough with calling fire into battle. Especially in a situation like that, when you were pressing me so hard, I didn't have much room to work. I need more practice before I can integrate it into real combat."

"You could try with our little elf," Grede said. "When it comes to dealing with troll fire priests, I think she's far more skilled than I am. Don't you think?"

Jace nodded — Grede had a point.

At that moment, he tried to carefully listen to the elemental voices in the air. The voice of fire was completely absent, and both the sea and the sky seemed to be completely silent toward him, unwilling to give any response at all.

"Can you hear the elements talking to you here, Grede? Didn't you say you once studied with the sages of Aerie Peak on how to communicate with elementals?"

"I could, occasionally, the last few days, but I don't know what they were trying to tell me," Grede said. "That's why I wanted to borrow your elemental tongue from you. But the sages said it's fine even if you can't understand the elements' words. As long as you revere the elements, they can feel your good intentions. Sometimes knowing too much is actually no good."

"I'm going to go get something to eat, Jace. You head back early too. Didn't that gnome say the storm hasn't fully passed? Don't fall into the sea. And if you do fall in, at least yell loud enough, or I won't know where you ended up." The dwarf patted Jace on the lower back and said in Dwarvish, "Also, hope you sleep well tonight. You look like you haven't had enough rest."

Jace smiled and said in Dwarvish, "You too."

He realized that Grede was probably more favored by the sky and the storms, which was why they were sometimes willing to talk to him of their own accord. In that respect, Jace couldn't hold a candle to him.

He wondered how the fire elementals of Kalimdor would treat him.
