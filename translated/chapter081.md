## Chapter 81: Grede's Future and Past

After a sip of ale, Grede grinned and shook his head, letting out a long breath. "I can move around normally now, sure, but my legs and hands still don't quite obey me. I'm planning to take it easy for a while — let myself fully recover before I go back to taking jobs."

"I'm with you," Jace said. "We earned a bit last time, so you can afford to live it up for a few days."

"What've you got planned, then? Oh — how'd that thing with the Wizard Sanctum go? The king personally put in a word for you?" Grede asked, cup in hand.

Jace took a swig of his own and looked at Grede's face, all lit up with anticipation. He sighed. "What, you think the king would do that? They just sent a letter asking the Sanctum to take me on as an apprentice. I'm not official yet, but Marin's already taught me one trick to practice."

"Yeah? What trick? Show us." Grede set his mug down and waved him over.

Jace held out his left hand, focused on his fingertip, and murmured, "Liusa!"

A ball of arcane energy gathered above his fingertip, and a burst of bright light flared outward. A few nearby patrons glanced over with curiosity. Jace immediately snuffed it out.

"Impressive!" Grede clapped twice. "You learned Light that fast?"

"It's just reciting an incantation," Jace muttered.

Grede asked, "A little trick like that's gotta be nothing for you by now, right? I mean — you know." He winked.

Jace offered a noncommittal smile, but inwardly he felt the dwarf had become considerably more tolerant of shadow magic.

The dwarf picked a piece of fish off his plate and tossed it into his mouth. "Truth is, that trip to Redridge… it got me thinking. If you don't know any magic at all, it's damn near impossible to fight those things. And now even the monsters are casting spells. Even going pure strength, having the elements on your side makes you that much stronger — what kind of sense does that make? But that's what happened. An old veteran like me got taken down like a stray dog, and the orc who could use magic won."

Jace stayed quiet, watching where the dwarf was going with this.

Grede narrowed his eyes. "Tell me — d'you think I could learn some magic too?"

Jace burst out laughing. "You're only, what, sixty or seventy — and you're just now thinking about learning magic?"

"Didn't you say something about 'live till you're old, learn till you're old'?" the dwarf shot back. "But honestly, I really doubt I've got any talent for it. Though that's beside the point. Actually, Jace, these past trips out with you made me realize something important about myself: I'd been treating my gryphon's strength as my own."

The smile faded from Jace's face. And just like that, he seemed to understand why this dwarf — one-eyed, hands too unsteady to hold a weapon right — still looked down on everything and charged headlong without a second's hesitation at whatever enemy crossed his path.

And at the same time, he understood more deeply just why Grede harbored such bitter resentment over no longer being a Gryphon Rider.

"I'd been a Gryphon Rider for too long," Grede said, taking a drink. "I was riding gryphons and fighting Amani trolls years before the Dark Portal even opened. By the time the Orc Wars started, I'd nearly forgotten how to fight on the ground. The gryphon had become my legs, my wings, my… everything."

"What really drove it home for me was when I got on that mare you'd scrounged up from the Mage Quarter. It felt familiar — the animal under me, it was like being back in the Hinterlands. Except I couldn't control her, the same way I can't control my own body now. I… I can't explain it."

"When that orc knocked you down, he threw me a long way. When I got back up, I almost shouted a command for my gryphon to carry me back in — because on my own two feet, there was no way I could've saved you before he killed you. It wasn't until I realized I wasn't on a gryphon that I remembered I still had a bow… In other words, I've been off a gryphon for years now, and I still can't shake the habit. Anyway, I don't want to keep living like this. Arcane, fire… I'm only halfway through my life. I can't just fade into mediocrity. Say — d'you think I could convert to the Church?"

"Hard to picture, but you could try," Jace said. "Though weren't you always worshipping Danath Trollbane? He's no priest, no paladin, doesn't know any spellwork — just his own sword, and he could beat enemies you wouldn't think possible. At Lakeview Farm you said you wanted to improve alongside me. What happened, you lose your dream all of a sudden?"

"My injuries, Jace," Grede said helplessly. "No matter how much I train, I can't stop the shaking. Let alone this eye. When that orc charged, I couldn't even judge how far away he was — I had to rely on experience and guess. I genuinely don't know… how far I'll ultimately be able to go."

He lowered his head and didn't raise it for a long while. All you could see was his beard moving.

"Have you thought about going to Quel'Thalas to ask?" Jace said.

"Ask what?" Grede looked up.

Jace said, "About how to restore your eyesight… and the hand problem."

Grede let out a hollow laugh, but his expression sank almost immediately.

He stuffed a piece of fatty meat into his mouth, nearly gagged on the grease, and took a long pull of ale before he could speak. "No chance. I'll admit those obsessive scholars might actually have a way, but why would they ever lift a finger for a dwarf like me? I'd never ask a single elf for help — I'd die first. You might as well let me die blind."

Jace coughed in exasperation. "You've bedded that many elf women, and not one of them would be willing to help you out?"

Grede squinted at Jace for a moment. "Well. Not that many, actually."

Jace took a drink and needled him. "I figured since you'd worked out a whole theory for the bedroom and everything else, your sample size oughta be big enough to be convincing."

"She just wanted a good time — you know what I mean? We each got what we wanted!" Grede said. "After the fun was over, back to our own lives the next day. I didn't wanna go kill anyone for her, and she sure as hell didn't wanna cure me. Simple as that."

"She?" Jace asked. Singular?

Grede coughed, sucked in a sharp breath. "They. They. My Common's not great."

"You dated an elf?!" Jace asked, incredulous.

"Fuck off!" Grede slammed the table and shouted. "Dating? That bitch's pointy ears — I oughta cut 'em off… cut 'em off and string 'em around my neck! Me, dating? Fuck you!"

Jace couldn't hold back a snort. Grede glared at him and chugged another mouthful of ale, then realized his mug was empty and started hollering for the barkeep.

"No more light stuff! No more light! I want dark ale! The dark stuff!"

Then he turned to Jace and jabbed a finger at his mug. "You drink up too!"

Watching the dwarf this worked up only made it harder for Jace to keep it together, and he had no choice but to take a hasty swig.

Watching Grede like this, he figured the poor bastard had probably been played by someone at some point.

The elves of Silvermoon were famous for their decadence — they didn't go easy on their own kin, let alone outsiders. Maybe to that elf woman in Grede's memory, he'd been nothing more than a miniature, self-propelled fleshlight.

But then why was Grede this angry? Wasn't it supposed to be a fair trade — you have fun, I have fun?

Or had he actually caught feelings?

The dark ale arrived. Grede downed half of it in one go, then slumped in his chair and stared blankly past Jace. A long while later, he let out a belch.

Jace said, "Remember what you said? That elves help people too — they just wait until they have no choice."

Grede's eyes slowly rolled over. "So how d'you make 'em have no choice?"

"Well… at the very least we'd have to actually meet an elf first," Jace said, spearing another chunk of boar belly with his fork and popping it into his mouth.

"You sure about that?" Grede muttered. "You really think the elves'll have a cure for my poison aftereffects?"

Jace said, "I asked an alchemist. She told me the elves and the trolls have been fighting for so many years that they must've treated plenty of injuries like yours, so they probably have a lot of experience with it. By the way — your eye… how'd you actually lose it? Were you beaten blind, or did it just… go?"

Grede took off his eye patch. "I just can't see anything."

Jace looked at the blind eye. It had a cloudy look, like a mild cataract, but not quite that pale. On its own it didn't look that serious, but next to the normal one it was unsettling — no wonder Grede kept it covered.

"Definitely worth asking about," Grede muttered. "But at the end of the day, it's still just a guess."

"Don't be so pessimistic — you're questioning the depth of a ten-thousand-year-old civilization," Jace said. "I'm thinking we shouldn't limit our horizons to Stormwind, or even the kingdom. Dalaran's full of elves — at least more than Stormwind. I want to find my boss… no, I guess he's my teacher now — ask him if there's any chance of a work trip to Dalaran by year's end. That way I can sail to Lordaeron without paying out of my own pocket."

The dwarf's eyes lit up. "Seriously? Your job comes with perks this good? Think you could get my ticket waived too?"

"Uh…"

"Forget it. Saving on one person's fare's still something."

Jace said, "Anyway — once we're in Dalaran, I'll find a chance to ask around for you. Just asking… Those elves can't possibly be hostile enough to ignore me outright, can they?"

"I'm not getting my hopes up," Grede said, picking at his teeth with a fork. "Come to think of it, the meat here just isn't as good as Redridge. You eat at the inn in Lakeshire once, and coming back here everything tastes like cardboard."

Jace wasn't in any mood to critique the food. His mind had already flown to Dalaran.

That evening he got home, and the little imp was still copying books without rest — it had already piled up quite a stack.

Jace picked one up, chose a word at random, and slowly read it aloud using the Common phonetic notation written beside it: "Anak."

"No, no, no," Sazeno said. "That's still Common, master. To pronounce it in Demonic, you have to use the Eredar's distinctive inflection. Follow me… A—nak!"

When Sazeno voiced the "A," it was like a string of explosions going off in its throat — almost like a harsh bark. And by "nak," it sounded like it was crunching through bone.

All in all, even this simple word left Jace struggling.

"Too complicated."

"The fact that you can even tell it's complicated means you've got excellent Demonic talent, master!" the imp said. "You have to pronounce it precisely… you must. Remember the pronunciation rules — that's the prerequisite for unleashing a spell's full power with the Demonic tongue!"

"Anak," Jace repeated, copying the imp's pronunciation. The imp clapped. "Yes, yes, that's the feeling! The Eredar can use magic to enhance their pronunciation, which in turn amplifies the strength of their spells, making their incantations more powerful than ordinary people's. But I can't do that part — I'm just a little demon, rawr."

Jace nodded and read, "Anak… means misery."

So what would happen if he used this pronunciation to say "Fekht"?

He'd been about to test it on the imp right then and there, but watching its busy back, he thought about how he might scare it so badly it jumped out the window and set a few houses on fire, and that'd be more trouble than it was worth — so he let the idea go.

"I need a set of pronunciation rules," Jace said. "Can you write out a pronunciation rule table?"

"A Demonic pronunciation rule table?" the imp turned to stare at him. "You're joking, right, master?"

The imp's sarcasm didn't bother Jace. English had fused together words and pronunciations from many older languages, with countless irregular sounds. And Demonic was a fusion of the primitive languages of countless bizarre demons from the Twisting Nether — the vocabulary of Eredar, Demonic's backbone language, probably didn't even make up a large share.

Every demon species had its own way of pronouncing things. If Demonic actually had pronunciation rules, you'd probably need another whole book to cover them.

Jace flopped onto the bed with a thud. His whole body felt sore and tingly. A cold autumn breeze drifted in through the window and swept across his chest and belly, drawing out the day's fatigue bit by bit, little by little.

It would be perfect if not for the constant demonic stench the imp was giving off.

"Go sleep in your bag."

"Can I rest? Wonderful!" the imp excitedly dove into the bag.

Now that was perfect.

Jace closed his eyes in comfort.

His stomach was a bit full.
