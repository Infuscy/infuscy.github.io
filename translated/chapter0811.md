## Chapter 811: Out of the Storm

The airship pushed forward through the rain and wind, its tail fins swaying left and right from time to time, trying hard to avoid the massive boulders that kept emerging from the pitch black.

Grede stood at the bow, gripping Neltharion's necklace, feeling the presence of the earth elementals. Jace stood not far from him, one hand gripping the hanging rope and the other reaching outside the gondola to feel the rain, doing his best to blast the stone pillars with Fireballs the moment they appeared.

A flash of lightning from overhead instantly illuminated the entire sky, and in the blink of an eye the pillars of stone beneath the airship all revealed themselves. As the airship climbed higher, the pillars threatening it became less densely packed.

A rumbling thunder followed right behind. Vereesa shouted toward Grede's side, "You can call down lightning, Grede..."

"What do you mean by that?" the dwarf looked at her and said. "You want me to talk to the sky and have it call off the storm?! You overestimate me. Not all elementals are willing to obey your commands!"

"The elementals willing to cooperate with mortals are always a small minority, Ve," Jace told her.

Grede glanced down at his hammer and shouted toward Vereesa, "He's right. I'll tell you something, little elf — in this situation, the storm might not even respond to my voice. They've completely lost it! This isn't the first time. Good thing we have this necklace."

Jace nodded.

Vereesa anxiously watched a stretch of jagged, rugged stone swept by the searchlight, her throat moving uncomfortably as she swallowed.

Jace knew that as a ranger, she was genuinely unable to help at all right now. All she could do was watch Jace and Grede wield elemental or fire magic against those boulders.

The situation made her extremely uneasy. One hand clutched the rope beside her, at a loss, while the other gripped the dagger at her waist tightly. But whether it was the blade or the bow, neither was of any use at the moment.

"I told Eldridge we never should have torn the cannon off our airship. The war may be over, but there's still no shortage of trouble! If it weren't for these passengers helping out on this trip..."

"If that cannon had still been on the bow, we might not have dodged that last boulder. Even with that dwarf chanting, it would've been close."

"That cannon could have blown that boulder straight to pieces!"

"You serious? That cannon couldn't even sink that warship. Don't you remember..."

"Stop the chattering back there, Alzamanc, Dorus! Go check in the back to see if the ropes have snapped. Something feels off about our attitude."

"I'm watching the back, boss. Everything's fine back here!"

"Left side's fine too!"

"Then report in at all times, damn it!"

This was followed by a string of Goblin that sounded like complaints, and also like warnings of danger. Then the navigator up front, Gulrok, bellowed another reminder: "Speak Common, damn it! Our crew isn't just gnomes anymore!"

"Yes, sir!"

"Yes, sir!"

Although the gnomes were running around the deck in a panic, tripping and dropping things from time to time, the airship was still climbing steadily. The situation seemed to have stabilized.

After that grudgingly endured rainy night, at some point the sky gradually began to brighten. On the horizon ahead, a vast grassland bathed in pale purple morning light was already visible.

By now all the gnome crew members had calmed down quite a bit. Jace also felt a wave of drowsiness wash over him. He couldn't help but ask the navigator, "Mr. Gulrok, I'd like to know..."

The gnome turned around excitedly and said, "We all thought you were just a demon summoner, sir. We had no idea you commanded such powerful fire magic. Really, thank you so much for you and your friends' help."

"Don't mention it. We were saving our own lives too," Jace said. "I wanted to know — do you run into this kind of trouble every time you set out, or did our voyage just have particularly bad luck?"

"I can hear your accent, lad." Gulrok looked toward a hill the airship was about to pass over and said, "Airship travel definitely isn't as safe as a cruise on Lake Lordamere, but even on Lake Lordamere there are murlocs about. You take my meaning?"

"I suppose we won't turn around and go back through the stone pillars again, will we, gnome?!" Grede called over.

"Thank you for your powerful magic, dwarf sir!" the captain wearing a hat up front said. "We've unquestionably made it through the most dangerous phase. Even if we run into a stronger rainy night again, it won't be any problem. What awaits us next is a vast expanse of flat grassland and forest..."

"Then I'm going to catch up on sleep." Grede strode toward the cabin hatch. "Don't any of you think about waking me up again!"

"Men, let us together thank this great dwarf sage and fire mage for helping us escape danger!" the captain roared, waving his telescope.

"Thank you! The *Sharkbite* thanks you!"

The gnomes shouted in remarkable unison, with almost no discordant voices. Grede was quite enjoying the feeling, waving his hand as he entered the hatch and descended the stairs, like a triumphant general. But Jace felt something was off.

"They're a little too practiced at that, Jace," Vereesa murmured.

"You think so too, right?" Jace said helplessly. "Which means..."

"They probably thank their passengers quite often," Vereesa finished his thought, then said, "Still, Noggenfogger said the airship's only had one serious accident in recent years. If they can keep operating like this and nothing ever goes wrong, there's not much you can say about them."

"Don't forget — the threshold for a serious accident is three deaths, Ve," Jace looked at her and said. "If every incident kills fewer than three people?"

Vereesa's eyes widened. "There are maybe twenty people on this airship right now. If we'd crashed into that boulder last night, do you think only three people would have died? These gnomes might have their own way out. The five of us might have found a way to survive, especially those two demons. But I don't believe those passengers down below would have made it. They don't look like they can defend themselves, and they don't know any magic either."

"I'm just guessing," Jace said. He tucked a strand of silver hair that the wind had blown across her forehead to the side and smiled. "Let's hope so."

Vereesa gently took Jace's hand and pressed it against her face, looking at him a moment longer. There wasn't the slightest trace of satisfaction on her face of having convinced someone — she probably couldn't even convince herself.

"By the way, how did Grede do it?" she asked. "You mean that boulder last night," Jace said.

Vereesa said, "I always thought he could only call down lightning, like those gryphon riders. I had no idea the earth would answer his call too."

"Neltharion was once the Guardian of the Earth," Jace said. "I'm not surprised at all that the treasure he gifted the Dragonqueen can command earth and stone. Grede already knows a good deal of the Kalimdor tongue. I bet when he went back to Aerie Peak that time, he must have found the sages there to teach him how to awaken the power contained in that necklace. That guy's been secretly studying and training without telling the two of us. I'd bet he was pretty damn satisfied showing off in front of us last night..."

Vereesa smiled and nodded. She released Jace's hand and picked up the emerald-green necklace on her chest. "When the Dragonqueen gave me this necklace, I never tried to awaken the power within it. I was too busy showing it off to my sister and other kinfolk. I heard Grede say you went to the Dark Iron dwarves to learn how to speak with fire elementals, right?"

"A relatively kind group of Dark Iron dwarves," Jace tried to clarify.

"I know," Vereesa said. "Grede's trying to communicate with the elements, and you're gradually adapting to the Dragonqueen's blessing. Even that Succubus among us has gotten a lot stronger. It seems like I'm the only one standing still."

"You're already strong enough as it is, Ve," Jace said with a smile. "You're the sister of Alleria and Sylvanas Windrunner."

The elf frowned and said, "Actually, I'd prefer it if you didn't bring those two up when you're complimenting me. It seems that even after everything at Grim Batol, and becoming a ranger-lord, I still can't make that wish come true."

Jace gave an embarrassed laugh. "Sorry."

In truth, Vereesa's reputation within the Alliance was already no less than that of her two sisters.

It was just that to Jace she was too close, stripped of too much of the halo that fame brought. And his memories of those famous figures from his past life were too hard to wear away, so he couldn't help but bring them up.

"Ve, I think Grede and I... we've experienced far too many moments of powerlessness before. Before you helped Grede find that botanist to cure his disability, his strength and self-confidence were nowhere near what they are now. And I, before I found my own direction, was a guy who struggled even to deal with wild wolves. But you — the first time we fought side by side in Dalaran, Teron Gorefiend grappled with you in close combat... if not for the shadow magic at his disposal, in pure martial skill he almost couldn't match you. And that was Teron Gorefiend! I heard from Kafal Mingwing that it was the first time you'd gone out on a mission with the Farstriders, wasn't it?"

Hearing Jace mention that death knight, Vereesa's brow relaxed slightly. She nodded and said, "More or less."

"Grede and I are single-minded about getting stronger. You just don't have as much drive to pursue power," Jace said.

"After Grim Batol, I realized there are far too many things in this world that I'm powerless against." Vereesa's face was lit on one side by the gradually rising sun. She squinted slightly to avoid the somewhat harsh light and went on: "Ranger-lord... it's just a respectable title in Quel'Thalas. Once you leave there, it's nothing. I should be setting my sights further."

"Whatever you decide to do, I'll be right by your side," Jace said.

"I know."

Vereesa smiled and embraced the warlock in front of her. Jace held the rope with one hand and wrapped the other around her, pulling her into his arms.

Her tousled brown hair and her silver hair tangled together. He looked toward the gradually brightening horizon ahead and genuinely wished time could linger a little longer.
