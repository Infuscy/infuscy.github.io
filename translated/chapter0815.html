Chapter 815: The Black Dragon's Target

Following the burning debris and the woods all the way to the airship's crash site, they were met everywhere with the gnomes' clamorous shouting.

"Don't let him die! Are there any more Healing Potions? Damn it, if only we had a priest on the airship."

"The Sharkbite is done for! Done for completely!"

"Pull yourselves together!"

"Everyone who can still move, get over here!" the captain up front roared.

"What's the use... my Sharkbite is gone..."

A gnome engineer sat against a nearby tree, sighing miserably. Jace and Vereesa came up to him. He raised his head and said, "You know what, human? This ship was originally one we were planning to sell to your Alliance, because she was designed to withstand the fire of those dragons. You understand? But you refused to have any contact with us, so in the end she still took part in the battle against the Kul Tiras fleet..."

Looked like this airship had died a worthy death, in its own way.

"Where's the dwarf?" Jace asked.

"I didn't see him." The gnome lowered his head. "My Sharkbite is done for. What business is it of mine to keep track of that?"

"Didn't the birds you called find Grede?" Jace asked the elf behind him.

Vereesa shook her head and said, "I thought Grede was with you." Jace lowered his head and suddenly heard a strange roar from behind.

"What do you mean you didn't see him?! He's that big a man!"

"Grede?"

Jace and Vereesa followed the sound, pushing through a stretch of fallen branches to the other side, and saw the dwarf staring right at them.

"My gods, if you two had taken any longer to show up, I'd have gone into this marsh looking for you." Grede stumbled over the uneven, wet mud as he ran over. "When I fell just now, I had no choice — I even prayed to the Holy Light. Hoped it would bless you two and keep you safe. Don't know if it answered me this time."

"Maybe it did. We're really all right. Maybe your prayers worked." Vereesa smiled. Just then Jace noticed the limp figure sitting on the ground by a tree.

It was Morofir. She was looking at Jace. The flames all over her body had mostly dissipated. Jace had never seen her looking so weak — and she was a demon, after all.

Seeing Jace's gaze, Grede glanced over and said, "If it weren't for Lady Morofir, I couldn't have held on down there for so long. Those fire elementals were really a handful. They came in waves."

Jace came up to Morofir and asked, "Are you hurt?"

"I'll recover soon, Master." Morofir clutched her lower abdomen. "I'm sorry to interrupt your happy reunion with your friends. I'd rather I wasn't injured, so the scene would be a bit more relaxed."

Jace noticed she had a rather horrifying gash in her abdomen. Purple-red blood had spilled all over the ground.

"Jace, don't blame me for not taking good care of your demon," Grede sighed. "I didn't know what to do either. Is treating a demon's wound the same as treating a normal person's? I'm not familiar with this, Jace, so I didn't dare make a decision on my own. I could only wait for you."

"That's got nothing to do with you, dwarf. I just need to rest here a while." Morofir caught her breath. "About half an hour and it'll be healed, Master. If you'd arrived a little later, you wouldn't have seen this pathetic sight of mine. Now, do you think I've made a fool of you?"

"Don't talk like that right now, Morofir," Jace said.

Vereesa said hesitantly, "Could a Healing Potion be of some use? I don't know about demons..."

"Don't joke around, Quel'dorei." Morofir cut her off.

Jace pulled out a Healthstone glowing with fel-green fire and held it in front of Morofir. "This should work, right?"

"Ah, this... hmm..."

Sazeno peered out from the bag, mumbling reluctantly as if he was especially reluctant to let Jace give this stone to the succubus.

"It's not a mortal wound. I won't lose my demonic soul over a scratch like this, my good Master," Morofir said.

Jace shook the stone in his hand. "Take it. I just want you to pull yourself together faster. Looks like we're going to have to cross this marsh on foot. There's no telling what we'll have to deal with next. And I've got three more of these. Don't sweat it, Morofir. You're important to us, and I don't want to see you like this either."

"Fine." Morofir took the stone that cast an unholy green glow in the firelight and glanced sideways at Vereesa beside her. Vereesa uncomfortably drew a deep breath, frowned, and turned to look toward the gnomes in the distance. Then she said, "Those gnomes have been running this route for so many times... how come they got attacked by a black dragon this time? I can't figure it out. Where did these black dragons come from?"

Grede asked in a low voice, "Because of us?"

"I hate to admit it, but it's very likely." Jace thought of that black dragon roaring on the airship: "That black dragon called himself 'Devourer' Theramore. He said he wanted to kill me to avenge Deathwing."

"But how would he know we were on that airship..." Vereesa said hesitantly.

Grede coughed guiltily and picked up the necklace around his neck. The golden light on it was still faintly visible. "Could it be because of this? I wielded the power of Deathwing, and these black dragons sensed it."

"I can't sense anything special about this necklace's magical power, dwarf. Although it is indeed very powerful," Morofir said. She had just swallowed the stone, and even her voice had become much clearer.

The speed of a demon's recovery was truly visible to the naked eye. Jace saw her originally torn abdomen rapidly growing back together under a blaze of fire. Though the color of the fire was quite complex too — now the sickly fel-green of fel energy, now a normal flame.

These two flames didn't seem to be cooperating to repair the wound. It looked more like they were competing with each other, vying to seize the initiative. Jace could sensitively feel them suppressing each other, just like on the airship's envelope when he'd called for the flames to scatter — the scene of the still-sane fire elementals fighting against the fire elementals enslaved by the black dragon's shadow.

It was just that during this process, Morofir didn't show the slightest sign of discomfort. But she noticed Jace's gaze studying her carefully and smiled slightly toward him, as if responding to her master's concern — yet her calm expression was as if the fiercely healing wound wasn't on her body at all.

"Just because you can't sense it doesn't mean those dragons can't. Don't forget — this is their father's thing!" Sazeno said, pointing at the necklace unhappily. "Think about it! It's like how you and Master can see demons, but this dwarf can't detect them at all — same principle! That stone you swallowed should have made you a bit smarter!"

"Sorry for wasting the rightful power of that stone, imp," Morofir said, giving him a white glare.

Grede looked back at the gnomes who were searching for the dead and wounded and moving surviving cargo and sighed. "I'm really sorry. I never expected to invite this kind of trouble."

"There's no need to apologize to us, Grede," Jace said. "If you hadn't used that necklace, the whole airship would have crashed at the stone pillars. If you want to regret something, regret that we went to Grim Batol. What needed doing is done, and what needed offending is offended. If they still want to come, let them come. I'm not unprepared."

"I think so too," Vereesa said.

"Now that everyone's all right, let's go check on the airship," Jace said. Grede also nodded.

With the help of the four of them, the rescue work progressed quickly. But by the time they'd pulled out all the wounded and salvaged supplies from the fire, it was already midnight.

There was one human passenger who didn't know if he'd died at the hands of the black dragon or during the airship crash. By the time they dragged him out, he was already burned beyond recognition.

Another gnome couldn't be found no matter what. Didn't know if he'd fallen into the forest halfway.

Most of the remaining people had been injured to varying degrees. Three people were seriously hurt. They'd now drunk Healing Potions and were resting on a spot cleared for the wounded, out of reach of the fire and smoke.

The captain paced anxiously nearby. Gulrok said to him, "We should go back to where the airship passed through and search again, Barmand. We can't let Almanzic and Wilf just die like that."

"Wilf couldn't possibly have survived. He was thrown down by the black dragon. Didn't you see, my dear Gulrok? You're our navigator. Your eyesight and powers of observation should be better than anyone's. Wilf is dead. Don't waste any more time!" The captain waved his arms and shouted at Gulrok: "The most important thing right now is to make sure not a single person here dies. The death toll absolutely must not reach three. Heal their injuries. As for Almanzic... he's just missing. We can wait for reinforcements and look for him then. That way only two people die. Damn it, that fragile human is really grating... how the hell did he survive the Great Sea! I thought travelers brave enough to ride an airship would at least have some skills of their own!"

"The black dragons could find this place any moment, captain," Vereesa said. "Now, before they get here, we should move somewhere else as soon as possible. The burning wreckage of the airship is far too conspicuous a target."

"No." The captain turned and pointed at the elf. "I'm grateful you defeated those two black dragons, elf. But you're good at fighting — you don't understand the Trade Cartel's rules. I must stay here to protect our property and not leave an inch. I'll wait for the Trade Cartel's reinforcements! Noggenfogger might not care whether we live or die, but he definitely won't abandon the Cartel's property!"

"Isn't there a gnome outpost in Dustwallow Marsh?" Jace asked.

"No, sir." The captain Barmand wiped the sweat from his forehead. "I don't know what kind of person had the bad idea to set up an outpost here. Ah, of course, you're not familiar with this area. It's normal you wouldn't know the problems here. Dustwallow Marsh is no place for any intelligent person to live."

Although the gnome said that, in the game there was indeed a small gnome town in Dustwallow Marsh called Mudsprocket. It seemed that at this time the town hadn't been established yet.

Jace guessed Mudsprocket must have been established by the Trade Cartel after Theramore was built, in order to maintain trade relations with the Alliance. So at this time, besides crocodiles, spiders, and raptors, the entirety of Dustwallow Marsh was black dragons. Even the ogres probably hadn't made their way here yet. There really was no need to take the risk of settling here.

"Besides, we don't have the means to leave right now either." Barmand looked up at Vereesa. "Look at these wounded. Look at our luggage. We have no carts and no kodos. How are we supposed to move them quickly? Whether black dragons will come I don't know, but wandering around aimlessly in a marsh like this with nothing to rely on — those who are injured will definitely die. Sickness, infection, all sorts of trouble. The supplies on the airship can at least help them live a little longer until the rescue team arrives!"

"He's right, Jace." Grede leaned in and whispered. "Maybe we should leave. If those black dragons are coming for us... we should get far away from these gnomes. That would actually protect them. What do you think? After all, they've been flying back and forth on this route for so many years without ever encountering a black dragon. That shows the black dragons have no interest in them."

Vereesa nodded. Jace looked at the gnomes who were sorting through the surviving supplies and thought that this really was the only option. "At least we should confirm Almanzic's situation first," Gulrok said from the side.

"Stop obsessing over your Almanzic, damn it." The captain covered one eye. "Anyone would think he was your brother, or your male lover! Secure the safety and stability of the people here first, then talk about going out to search. Otherwise, if the search team you send out all die out there, can you take that responsibility?!"

"You're not Trade Cartel people." He turned to look at Jace and the others. "So if you plan to leave, I won't stop you. But I still hope you'll stay and help us. Although on the airship earlier... I've never seen a black dragon, but I've seen a red dragon. I know how terrifying those creatures are. What I mean is, I'm grateful you stepped up to fight that fire-breathing monster, and I'm grateful to this dwarf sir. I've never seen a man with so much strength who could grab hold of those ropes. Actually, I thought we were all going to die! So now, I don't dare ask too much of you brave outsiders."

"Sorry, we still have a ways to go." Jace glanced at Grede, then looked at the gnome. "Then we can only wish you luck."

The captain Barmand sighed softly and said, "All right, all right. At least let me know your names. I'm Daffield Barmand, the third captain of the Sharkbite. And it looks like, the last."

"Jace."

"Grede."

"Vereesa."

"Vereesa?" Barmand's eyes widened. "I only know one elf named Vereesa!"

"I am Alleria Windrunner's sister," Vereesa nodded.

"Of course. You're that Alliance ranger-captain Alleria Windrunner's sister. I know that," Barmand said. "But more importantly, I heard you killed Teron Gorefiend. Is that true? That Horde chieftain? He'd been in contact with the Trade Cartel before, wanting to buy a gnome airship. But then it seemed he found a new partner, so he simply stopped negotiating with us... probably a Venture Company airship. Those are ours too! But I'm getting a bit off track. But if you could kill a dark monster as terrifying as Teron Gorefiend, then dealing with those flying fire-breathing beasts isn't surprising at all!"

Vereesa looked at Jace and reluctantly nodded.

"It's an honor to meet a hero like you," Barmand said. "And you lot — traveling with a lady like her certainly can't be bad either!"
