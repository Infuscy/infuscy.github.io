## Chapter 816: The Opponent We Expected

Compared to Dustwallow Marsh, the Wetlands seemed almost too "clear." By comparison, even Black Marsh seemed peaceful and tranquil.

Perhaps it was because Black Marsh had been threatened by demons that most of the local beasts and indigenous inhabitants had been forced to hide away, making it as quiet as death. This Dustwallow Marsh, on the other hand, maintained a wild, primeval state. Passing by every stream or bog, Jace could even directly see the hungry eyes in the water.

Those driftwood- or silt-like reptiles never stopped watching you, waiting for you to show fatigue, to expose your weakness, and then they'd strike when the time was right to take your life.

Although Jace and his group weren't much worried about the threat of these beasts anymore, who knew whether the gazes in this forest might be hiding something else among them.

"Where to next, Jace?" Grede said, stepping over a stone slab half-buried in the earth. "Do we head back to Gadgetzan, or just keep pushing north?"

Vereesa said, "If we go back to Gadgetzan, we'd have to trek across quilboar and centaur territory on foot. The stone pillars... think about it, Grede. We're already at the northern edge of the marsh. According to what the navigator said earlier, we just need to keep heading north until we reach the coast and see the Rage River. After that, the road gets a lot easier."

"I agree," Jace said. "Let's keep heading north. It's been three or four days since we left Gadgetzan, which means the airship's already flown about half the distance. Going back now... god knows how long that would take. And most importantly, do we really want to fly that route again? What if we get targeted by black dragons again?"

"All right, all right, you two." Grede spread his hands. "I get it. Don't make me look like a fool. I was just asking about our plan."

"How are you doing, though, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

"No problems." Jace patted his chest. "After all, Alexstrasza is the Life-Binder, Ve. The essence she gave me has definitely made me sturdier and healthier, and I recover faster."

Actually, saying it like that, his back still hurt a bit from the fall. But he was also certain that if it had been him three years ago, a fall like that would probably have scattered his arms and legs.

"And you?"

"I'm fine of course. Just a small wound. A Healing Potion is more than enough to handle it," Vereesa said. "Actually, when you pushed me away earlier, besides worrying about you, I was a bit angry too. I thought you were just playing the hero. Now that I've calmed down and think about it, if I'd stayed there and taken that breath attack with you, I'd probably be burned beyond recognition by now. No way a single Healing Potion could've fixed that."

"I'm talking about you two." Grede stopped and asked, "Doesn't anyone give a damn about me?"

"It's not that we don't care about you. It's just genuinely hard to imagine you getting hurt in a situation like that, Grede." Vereesa turned to look at him.

"I think so too," Jace said.

"What a backhanded compliment." Grede shook his head. "But it is a convincing argument. After all, I am too strong. I can't avoid having opened up a gap in strength between me and you two fragile little brats."

"Who was it that begged for mercy after I beat them in Elwynn Forest?" Jace asked.

Grede swallowed and coughed. Vereesa frowned again and asked, "Who are you calling a brat?"

"Hah!" The dwarf immediately pointed at her. "I know you elves live a long time, but I can tell from several angles that this little one here is definitely not over forty. No, not over thirty... at my age, calling you a brat is no loss to you!"

"Don't jump to conclusions too early, Grede," Vereesa said, looking ahead.

"Oh, really? I'd hate to doubt myself," Grede said immediately. "I'm almost seventy years old. To be precise, sixty-seven this year. Your turn."

"Since you're so good at guessing." Grede stared at her. "Why don't you guess my age down to the last digit and show me how capable you really are."

Grede smacked his lips and said, "Heh, even if I guess right, what can you do if you just don't admit it?" Jace disappointedly shifted his gaze back. He'd actually been looking forward to Vereesa admitting her age with her own mouth.

Although the group was in a sorry state, a few jokes like this had relieved a lot of the pressure from the airship crash and the black dragon attack just now.

After this, they just needed to keep heading north, cross those hills, and leave Dustwallow Marsh. The road ahead would get much easier.

After all, according to the game's map, heading north from here to the Barrens, they'd soon reach Northkeep, the human fortress that would later be built there, and further north, the gnome city of Ratchet.

Although these two outposts didn't exist yet at this time, the fact that cities could be built here was enough to show that this area was still quite habitable — at least a lot better than the marsh.

They hadn't walked long before Vereesa suddenly fixed her eyes on a patch of bushes beside a large tree not far off. "Something wrong?" Jace asked.

"I'll go take a look."

The ranger said this as she walked over quickly. She examined the area around the bushes for a while, then pulled out a dark fragment from inside. "A black dragon scale?" Jace asked.

"No," Vereesa said. "It's a dragonkin scale. Remember the dragonkin that surrounded us in the Thaurissan ruins, Jace? This scale is exactly the same as the ones on their bodies. Quite different from a black dragon's."

Jace frowned. Actually, compared to black dragons that swept in with powerful shadow force and raging winds, he was genuinely more worried about the dragonkin lurking deep in the marsh.

Those powerful dragonkin were Onyxia's lair guards. They were spread throughout every cave and hollow in southern Dustwallow Marsh, ready at all moments to hunt every uninvited guest who tried to approach the dragon eggs. They were probably the true source of Dustwallow Marsh's terrifying legends across all of Kalimdor.

Black dragons were big targets and easier to spot when they appeared. And in the deep, dense marsh forest, black dragons flying in the air might not necessarily be able to find these few people.

But there were enough dragonkin to pursue and intercept them from every direction. And the dragonkin mages commanded all sorts of strange dark magic. If they'd already crept nearby, they must be planning to settle the score for the "Devourer" Theramore, whose life or death was still unknown. "Sazeno, can you sense the presence of dragonkin?"

The imp said helplessly, "Their shadow aura is too faint, Master. And this whole marsh is full of shadow..."

"This scale means the dragonkin may have already gotten ahead of us," Vereesa said in a low voice.

Jace looked toward the dark forest to the north and had to admit she had a point.

"I'll scout ahead." Vereesa looked at the demon. "Morofir, you can turn invisible and cover our rear. See if there are any dragonkin following us."

Morofir looked toward Jace. Jace nodded, and the succubus's figure gradually faded into the shadows.
