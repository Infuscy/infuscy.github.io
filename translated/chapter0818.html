Chapter 818: Bremmugo

The Dustwallow Marsh, which reeked of blood and filth from night into the following morning, seemed much calmer than before. The beasts that had been lurking in the shadows to watch them the night the airship crashed were nowhere to be seen around here anymore.

Jace knew this meant something far more terrifying was prowling about.

The dragonkin were far more numerous than the group had imagined. No matter where they went, they could see dragonkin patrols moving between the shadows of the trees.

Although these dragonkin didn't know how to hide themselves and were easy for either Vereesa or Morophil to detect, they occasionally released some kind of shadow magic—probably to strengthen their fellow dragonkin, or to use some shadow technique Jace had never heard of to help track the group's movements. The traces of these spells could be sensed by Jace from a great distance.

But their search formation was extremely tight. Even if you dodged one group, another was waiting further out.

Several times, when they tried to head north, they were blocked by the dragonkin coming back and forth and forced to turn back. In addition to patrols led by powerful dragonkin captains, there were also clearly more keen-eyed dragonkin scouts guarding key positions, so even though they could detect the group's trail, it was hard to slip past them.

And Jace had the distinct feeling that the dragonkin net was slowly squeezing the space they had to maneuver in.

The dragonkin were slowly closing in from the west and north. Jace and the others had been trying to break through to the north and had unconsciously arrived at a coastline. Climbing a slope, he gazed out at the gray sea. The sky looked like it could unleash a torrential downpour at any moment. Black clouds hung thick and heavy, as if the entire Dustwallow Marsh were shrouded in some kind of gloom, even though it wasn't yet dusk.

They had trudged through the mud, walking through what seemed like endless chaos. His boots and cloak had grown quite heavy. Arriving all at once at such a vast, open place, he even felt his mood relax a little.

Not just him—Grede and Vereesa both seemed to breathe a little easier too.

This was the eastern shore of Kalimdor. Jace looked across the bay at a barren peninsula, and his thoughts drifted back to when he'd played the game in his past life. After all, although the details here differed a lot from the game, the overall atmosphere was strikingly close.

That was where Jaina Proudmoore would later build Theramore. Now there was nothing there but a forest.

Thinking that Jaina was only thirteen or fourteen right now, Jace felt an indescribable sense of the vast passage of time sweep over him, colder even than the chill wind here.

"We've come back to Tidefury Cove—last time by ship, this time on land," Grede said, pointing at the sea. "Tell me, what if we jumped into the sea and swam to that shore up north? We could slip past the dragonkin that way."

"Look at the shore over there, Grede," Vereesa said, pointing at the coast where Theramore would stand. "Those shadows—can you see them? Those are dragonkin patrols. Never mind the dangers in the water. Even if we made it to shore, they'd still be waiting to block us."

Jace glanced warily toward Vereesa, but he couldn't see a thing. Grede stared hard at the distance for a while and then shook his head. Both of them quickly gave up trying to match a ranger's eyesight.

"Dragonkin everywhere. I say we just find a weak point in their line and smash through. What do you two think?" Grede said.

"That would mean exposing our position," Morophil said.

"Then let them race us on foot," Grede said. "I don't believe those heavy bastards can outrun us!"

Jace nodded and said, "It doesn't look like we have much choice. At this rate, we'll be trapped on this coast, and that'll be even more trouble. We might as well try to break through."

Once they reached an agreement, they walked north along the coast, gradually pushing back into the marsh.

After dodging two groups led by powerful dragonkin captains, they zeroed in on a post guarded by a dragonkin mage and two dragonkin scouts.

These scouts were extremely keen. Vereesa couldn't get too close. Even from a distance, the moment she caught sight of their silhouettes, the scout seemed to sense something and looked in their direction, whispering to the dragonkin mage.

Morophil had already used her stealth to creep close to the three dragonkin. Once she was ready, she sent Jace a signal through the Soul Link. Jace nodded to the two beside him, and Vereesa moved forward a distance and drew her bow.

Just that movement was enough for the dragonkin to sense something wrong. The dragonkin mage immediately raised her black staff, trying to send some kind of signal, but let out a hoarse grunt—Morophil had already landed on her head, a blade piercing through her skull!

Almost at the same time, the arrow from Vereesa's bow shot forth, piercing the chest of another scout who was trying to draw his bow at an almost inconceivable distance. But the other scout, sensing something amiss, quickly backed away a few steps and darted into the marsh like a lizard. In the process, he had swiftly melted into the shadows. Did this kind of dragonkin know Shadowstep too?

Jace and Grede charged over at full speed, hoping to help, but there wasn't much the two of them could do at a moment like this—the distance was simply too far. Morophil was faster. Just as the dragonkin sank into the shadows, she seized the creature's neck at even greater speed and dragged it back out of the darkness, a dagger plunging through the back of its head.

But just as Vereesa slightly lowered her bow, the scout who had already fallen suddenly lifted her upper body and shouted toward the heights: "Bremm..."

Although she didn't finish before another arrow pierced through her mouth, the cry had already echoed through the marsh. When the group reached her side, the mage's body slowly curled up, her lips still mumbling: "...Bremmugo."

Grede kicked her head sideways. "Let's move, before they catch their wits."

The group immediately headed north. As they ran, Vereesa said, "I'm running low on arrows, so I hesitated on the second shot... that's my fault."

"What does Bremmugo mean?" Grede asked.

Jace shook his head. He didn't know either, but it was definitely some kind of distress signal.

"It's coming... it's coming... it's coming..."

Sazeno popped up from who knows where, grabbing Jace by the collar. "There's a dragon, master!"

"What—" Vereesa started, when Jace's peripheral vision suddenly flared with light. Dragonfire!

"Get down!" Jace shouted. The group scattered in all directions. A mass of blood-red fire poured down from the sky, instantly engulfing a large swath of jungle, accompanied by a piercing shriek that tore across the sky.

"Feel the punishment of Bremmugo, and pay the price for all the crimes you have committed!"

Vereesa flashed out from the side, immediately drawing her bow toward the source of the voice. But the whelp quickly flew far away behind the cover of the dense tree canopy, and they soon lost track of its shadow.

She cursed under her breath in Thalassian, lowered her bow, and said, "It's hard to get a clear shot in here!"

In the distance came the whelp's peals of laughter, as if it had heard her voice.

"This time you won't escape. You won't escape again, laughable mortals!"

Jace knew this creature was planning to attack while buying time, waiting for all the dragonkin to surround them.

He gripped the Song of the Blue Sky at his waist, but the brilliant light it released when it exerted its power would undoubtedly make it even easier for them to expose their position.

But Vereesa suddenly looked at Grede and said, "When it comes around, transform and throw me up into the air."

"How do you come up with these dumb ideas? What if it bites you out of midair?" Grede asked.

"Then we'll see if that monster still has the strength to open its mouth after getting shot through," Vereesa said in a low voice.

"Since you're that confident." Grede transformed into a terrifying gray stone golem with a grinding crunch of gravel. By now he could hardly be called gray at all—he was practically a silver monster radiating a metallic gleam, and the Neltharion pendant around his neck shone with light at the same time.

Perhaps drawn by the pendant's light, the sound of massive wings churning the wind came from the sky once more. Vereesa locked onto the direction of the voice and pointed at Grede. "Now!"

Grede nodded, grabbed Vereesa by the back of her leg, and hurled her high into the air. Jace watched the elf turn a somersault in the sky, draw an arrow, and loose a bolt glowing with arcane light. The radiance pierced through the layers of tree canopy and directly struck a shadow that was suddenly swooping in, accompanied by a savage roar. The whelp, wreathed in chaotic flames and black smoke, crashed into the forest, uprooting and snapping tree after tree.

"Hah! Damn, little elf, you're something else!"

Grede laughed and charged over at full speed. The whelp lifted its head and spat a mouthful of blood, only to see this stone-sprite monster barreling toward it. Startled, it frantically flapped its wings to take off again. Jace rushed up and quickly cast a choking curse, seizing its serpentine neck.

The whelp was choked by the throat and couldn't muster the strength to fly. Jace immediately followed up with a Curse of Weakness, and this time it collapsed on the ground completely, struggling for a long time before it broke free of the shadow's grip. By the time it tried to take off again, Grede had already reached its side!

"Get away, dwarf!"

"Feel the strength of a mortal, ugly thing!"

Grede cut off Bremmugo's muttering with a shout, leaped up, and swung his axe in a full arc, cleaving its head clean off its serpentine neck, leaving a neat, bright red stump.

The whelp's roar came to an abrupt halt. Its head convulsed and tumbled to the side. Its body went wild on the ground, smashing through several small trees before rolling over into the mud nearby. Fire and dragon blood splattered everywhere.

Vereesa dropped down from a nearby tree, looked at the scene, and turned her head. Her silhouette trembled slightly in the blood-red light behind her.

She caught her breath and said, "The black dragons are definitely all heading this way, Jace. We need to pick up the pace."
