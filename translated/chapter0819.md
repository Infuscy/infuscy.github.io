## Chapter 19: A Gift from the Dream

Even without any magical perception or detection, Jace could feel the treetops deep in the marsh stirring, and the occasional furious dragonkin roars echoing from a distance.

Bremmugo had been a small but young and volatile whelp, and his death had clearly enraged the black dragons even more—to say nothing of the confirmed death of the "Devourer" Tremeth. Judging by what the dragonkin had been saying, it seemed to be the leader of the black dragons in this region ever since Onyxia's departure.

So what was hunting them and closing in was practically a dragonkin army. Once surrounded, the outcome would be grim. There truly wasn't a moment to rest now.

What was worse, Vereesa's quiver had fewer than ten arrows left, and most of them were black arrows she'd scavenged from dragonkin hunters when she had the chance.

If more than two more whelps took to the air, they'd be completely stuck in a passive, beat-up position. At that altitude, Jace's attack spells could barely reach, and Grede would have a hard time throwing his hammer both fast and accurate.

For a moment Jace even found himself wondering what Vereesa would do once they actually reached Darkwhisper Gorge, given her lack of arrows—whether they should head to Ashenvale to borrow some from those night elves...

Of course, in the middle of all this tension, these thoughts only got the briefest consideration.

The four of them sprinted north the whole way, the ground gradually rising. Even though none of them were ordinary folk and climbing slopes didn't tire them quickly, their pace still slowed a little.

Grede had clearly underestimated the dragonkin's running speed. The duck-like waddling gait those dragonkin used on foot patrols had given them a serious misperception. Before long, Jace saw a dragonkin in gleaming red armor charging toward them like a crazed, sprinting crocodile.

Jace thought of the dragonkin mage in that black mountain hollow—even running along a cliff face, that one had been astonishingly fast. And these were Onyxia's own nest guardians.

Of course, the dragonkin mage had only worn a few scraps of dark red rags, but this nest guardian was decked out in heavy full-body armor and still ran this fast—it was practically a charging heavy tank.

Grede clearly realized the problem too, cursing "damn it, damn it" as he ran. Vereesa adjusted her stride, preparing to draw an arrow and fire back, but seeing how little gaps there were in that dragonkin's armor, she hesitated. The creature's protection had to be far sturdier than a whelp's scales.

Jace held out a hand to stop her. "I'll handle it."

Vereesa glanced at him, nodded, and kept running. As the nest guardian closed in further and raised his long axe to strike, Jace turned around and cast a Curse of Weakness. The guardian let out a low growl, his legs gave way, and he stumbled forward, nearly face-planting into a dirt pit.

The stumble kicked up a huge cloud of dust, and by the time he recovered his posture and tried to pursue again, he'd slowed considerably. Against a charging opponent like this, Curse of Weakness was perfect—at that speed, the slightest wobble in the legs threw them badly off balance.

But just as Jace confirmed this dragonkin guard couldn't keep up, a faster shadow from the nearby treeline caught up to them. He should have figured—if a heavy-armored brute like this could run that fast, the scouts and dragonkin hunters had to be even faster!

"Master! Right side!" Morophil suddenly shouted. Jace looked toward the eastern treeline and saw a dragonkin running on all fours while its upper body drew a bow with both arms, taking aim.

Before he could even finish incanting Dark Shield, the dragonkin loosed his arrow. Jace ducked his head. The arrow struck his shoulder but failed to pierce the black dragon-hide cloak, grazing off it and flying past his ear to bounce behind him.

Jace immediately cast a Dark Shield for protection. Before the dragonkin hunter could nock a second arrow, a dark figure wreathed in flickering flames had already blurred past Jace at a speed the naked eye couldn't follow.

Before the dragonkin got his next arrow set, Morophil burst out of a cloud of black smoke wreathed in blazing fire and thrust her flaming dagger forward. The dragonkin raised his arm to block, but the succubus's other hand drove her black shortblade into his chest. Coughing, he crashed to the ground.

Far ahead, Grede roared, drawing the attention of both Jace and Morophil. A heavily armored dragonkin guardian carrying a small round shield and a large cleaver had charged in from the flank, and Grede was meeting him head-on with his great axe.

He hadn't activated his transformation yet. His battle axe slammed hard into the dragonkin's shield, and the dragonkin deflected it back with the shield, then slashed in return—a blow Grede nimbly dodged. But that single exchange had already made the back of Jace's neck run cold.

The dragonkin was simply enormous. Setting aside the massive reptilian body behind him, even his upper body stood as tall as a tauren—a full head taller than Jace, to say nothing of the dwarf.

But being short had its advantages. Grede dodged the slash, pivoted a full three-sixty, and swung his axe into the dragonkin's front leg. The axe bit hard into the leg armor. It didn't split the armor open, but it gouged a dent in it—clearly hurting the dragonkin badly enough that his whole body staggered and he couldn't land a second cut.

At that moment, a shadow chain suddenly seized the dragonkin's neck and chest, pinning him in place. All that came out were a series of clicking coughs. Seizing the opportunity, Grede leaped up, stepped onto the shield, and hacked his axe into the dragonkin's neck. When he wrenched the axe free, dragon blood sprayed into the air.

A dragonkin further back drew a bow and shot at him. Grede hefted the dead dragonkin's shield to block, and that dragonkin was immediately pierced through the ribs by one of Vereesa's arrows, crashing to the ground in agony.

Jace looked over toward the elf. Aside from the one arrow in her hand, there were only four black arrows left in her quiver. The arrows she'd brought herself had probably been exhausted long ago.

More and more dragonkin were pouring out of the sparse treeline in pursuit. Now there were even the shrieks of whelps in the distance—calling more companions, perhaps, or issuing a threat.

As they ran, the imp had been trembling with fear, clutching Jace's chest and scanning the sky in every direction, but he could never tell exactly where a whelp might be closing in.

It might have been the consecutive deaths of three whelps that kept the others from charging in as vanguard again, preferring to let the dragonkin wear them down first.

As he ran, Jace said to Vereesa, "Stop using arrows on the dragonkin, Vere. Other than you, we don't have a good way to force those whelps down to the ground. I'm pretty sure they know it too. Once your arrows run out, those whelps will swarm us in no time..."

"All right." Vereesa rubbed the dragon blood off her fingers. "I realized that too. I was just worried the hunters would hurt you."

"Right now the most important thing is how to shake these dragonkin." Grede trotted alongside Jace carrying that golden round shield. Though it had been a small shield in the dragonkin's hands, in Grede's grasp it had become a normal-sized round shield.

Jace looked at the ring pattern on the shield and felt it was strangely familiar, though he couldn't remember where it dropped. But this was no time to be thinking about his past life.

The dwarf added a curse under his breath, then said, "They run too fast. I really thought that was a brilliant idea."

"We didn't have many choices at the time, Grede." Jace glanced at Vereesa, then noticed the green pendant swaying by her ear as she ran. "The Dragonqueen said that pendant could help you hide in the forest, didn't she? Have you tried figuring out how it works?"

Vereesa said, "I... haven't. But what good would it do for just me to hide?"

Jace said, "Even if just you could hide, that's still better than all of us being exposed and surrounded by dragonkin. Besides, this was originally a gift from the Green Dragon Queen to the Dragonqueen, meant to conceal her form. Think about how huge that red dragon was in Grim Batol! What if we huddle together and see if her power can hide all of us?"

"All right."

The elf moved closer and took Jace's hand. The dwarf ran over and grabbed Jace's cloak.

Vereesa held up the pendant, the arcane light in her eyes glowing faintly. Jace had been worried she might not know how to activate the item, then realized he was worrying about a Quel'dorei not knowing how to use a magical artifact—what was he thinking.

"The dragonkin won't be drawn by the glow in your eyes, will they..." Grede had barely muttered this when the emerald suddenly radiated a soft silver light, gentle as moonlight.

Jace felt a haze quickly settle over everything around them. The trees, the hillside—none of it felt quite real anymore.

"What? What's going on?" the imp poked his head out and looked around. "Where is this? Is this my plane? How did I get here?"

Not only the imp—Morophil, who had been invisible not far away, was suddenly revealed as the haze washed over her.

She paused, looked around, and said, "This thing can drag everyone nearby into another plane, master. We don't need to be touching each other."

Grede let go of Jace's cloak and walked off to the side, looking around. "That's right. It's exactly like being in a dream."

"Maybe not exactly." Vereesa said, "the Green Dragon Queen is also the Queen of Dreams. This might actually be a dream..." She was right, but was this the Emerald Dream?

Could Ysera's gift actually pull people into the Emerald Dream?

No—Jace remembered Alexstrasza had said that the Green Dragon Queen Ysera had given her this pendant so she could hide herself and observe the life of the world. That meant after concealing someone, they could still observe everything around them, rather than entering another plane entirely.

Jace looked behind them. The shadows stirring in the treeline were very blurry, but still visible. Those must be the dragonkin trailing after them.

"It's still different from a dream," he said, pointing at the shadows. "We can see the dragonkin chasing us, but they... don't seem to notice us."

"But we'd better keep our distance from them, master," Morophil said. "What happens if a dragonkin walks into the area this thing covers too?"

"Then they might see us all of a sudden, just like this demon did." Grede took off his helmet. "So we still have to run."
