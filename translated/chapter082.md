## Chapter 82: The Shadow Over Grim Batol

Early that morning, Jace arrived at Marin's study.

Ever since he'd been approved as an apprentice, he felt like the flowers and grass of the Mage Quarter, the bricks and tiles, all looked just a little prettier than before. Even the idle chatter of the kids loafing around the district didn't grate on his ears quite so much.

He pushed open the door, ready to whistle a little tune.

His lips had barely pursed when he saw Amy Marin sweeping the floor.

"Miss… Amy Marin?"

Amy turned around. Seeing Jace, she said in surprise, "I thought you wouldn't come, so I came over to help my father clean up. You know he'd never do it himself."

Jace raised his eyebrows. He hadn't realized that going from assistant to apprentice meant he didn't have to do the chores here anymore.

So the Mage Quarter really did draw a hard line between assistants and apprentices.

"But your father's still paying me," Jace said. "And that pay surely includes coming in to clean in the morning, right? Don't forget, I still need help with the red dragon problem. Since I've got nothing else going on, I'll help him clean."

"That's wonderful, thank you, Jace. I thought from now on I'd be his free cleaning lady again." Amy leaned the mop against the table. "I've got an Enchanting class later — now I can take my time getting ready."

Not long after Amy left, her father walked in. Seeing Jace still working, he looked equally surprised.

"I thought you wouldn't come. I even told Amy last night to come check."

"I should at least finish out the month." Jace stood up, put on an exaggerated show of weariness, bent his waist, and deliberately cranked his neck until the vertebrae popped.

Marin chuckled and sat on the desk. "Strictly speaking, you're not an apprentice yet, so finishing your assistant's duties is only proper. But this noon I'm going to Master Andormas to finalize your apprenticeship paperwork. Starting tomorrow morning, you won't have to do these odd jobs anymore."

Jace nodded. The old mage went on: "That said, if you're still willing to help out, you're welcome to — and I won't short you on this month's pay. Nor the pay after that. I can well afford to hire a dedicated assistant, Mr. Sesso. Even if you weren't an apprentice, my expectations of you go far beyond an assistant's duties."

"By the way — you know Master Morgas is back, don't you? He swapped postings with another mage to fill the vacancy at Stonewatch. Master Andormas wants him back in Stormwind to discuss a special plan — an expedition to the northern Wetlands of Khaz Modan."

"Grim Batol?" Jace asked.

"You think the orcs are hiding in Grim Batol?" Marin asked offhandedly.

Jace said, "Isn't it said that the Wildhammer dwarves' old capital, Grim Batol, lies in the highlands of northeastern Khaz Modan? I'm just guessing…"

Marin thought for a moment. "It's hard to say whether the horrors within Grim Batol or the Alliance's pursuit frightens the orcs more. You must have heard the old stories about Grim Batol?"

"I've heard some. Ancient artifacts and a dwarf witch," Jace said.

Jace actually knew these matters better than Grede and Marin put together. The dwarf witch — Moira, wife of the Witch King Thaurissan — had wielded an artifact called Xal'atath, an ancient weapon of evil.

Legend held that the dagger was the remnant limb of a dead elder god, and that over untold tens of thousands of years its power had passed through who knows how many ancient tribes, bygone empires, and secretive orders.

Every time a new power took possession of it, a catastrophic massacre swiftly followed — and the most recent had been the Grim Batol disaster, two hundred years ago.

Marin said, "Pinpointing Grim Batol's exact location is still only a secondary objective. If we find traces of the Dragonmaw orcs or small hideouts deep in the Wetlands, and we're confident the Dragonmaw clan has an even larger base, then we'll shift our gaze to the highlands farther east — or, as you said, set our sights on the legendary Grim Batol."

"The Dragonmaw orcs have the means to control red dragons, so there must be warlocks among them — and they're cooperating with the black dragons too…" Jace muttered to himself, then looked at Marin. "If they're numerous — if they're occupying Grim Batol the way the Blackrock orcs sit in Blackrock Spire — wouldn't we need an entire grand army to be safe?"

"You're right," Marin said. "So if we're forced to go there, that would be the worst-case scenario. At that point we'd have to notify the Kirin Tor, because a mission like that could never be accomplished by the Wizard Sanctum alone — it would require the combined efforts of multiple factions. Not to mention, if the Dragonmaw orcs have the ability to occupy Grim Batol, then they must have found a way to deal with Grim Batol's corruption. They might possess powers we can't foresee — perhaps related to the aid they receive from the black dragons, or their ability to control red dragons."

Even if his reasoning wasn't perfectly accurate, Jace still admired the old mage's sharpness.

At that, Marin's brow shifted. "Still, the northern Wetlands of Khaz Modan is a vast region beyond your or my imagination — even the local dwarves would claim no great knowledge of it. So the investigation could drag on for years, and circumstances may change by then. We don't need to fret over it this early."

So when was it that Rhonin, Windrunner, and Falstad Wildhammer launched the Grim Batol rescue mission?

Jace couldn't remember anymore. But if it hadn't happened yet, it couldn't be more than a few years off.

Perhaps it was precisely because the Wizard Sanctum's Wetlands search had turned up nothing that the red dragon mage Krasus had narrowed the orcs' most likely hiding place to the legendary Grim Batol, and thus set the rescue mission in motion.

Right now, not a single nation in the Alliance could field an army capable of assaulting a fortress-city like Grim Batol. A direct attack on the Dragonmaw clan was out of the question — which was probably why Krasus wanted to push the Kirin Tor to assemble a strike team to take out the Dragonmaw orcs by surprise.

No wonder Rhonin had felt this was a thoroughly suicidal mission. In his past life, Jace hadn't felt much of the weight of that story — he'd focused all his attention on the sparks between Rhonin and Windrunner.

But after experiencing the battle with the orc shaman in Redridge, hearing Grede recount the dark history of Grim Batol, and seeing how these mages shied away from even the subject of finding Grim Batol, speaking of it in hushed tones — he could finally understand why, after Rhonin completed his mission to save the Dragonqueen, he'd gone from a somewhat disgraced ordinary mage to one of the highest-ranking archmages in the Kirin Tor in a single leap.

It truly was a feat that could shake the entire continent.
