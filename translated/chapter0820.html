Chapter 820: The Hermit

With the protection of the Dream, the dragonkin were completely unable to track their presence, and fleeing became much easier.

They climbed a hill, and as they walked along a bare earthen slope, a whelp suddenly swept across the sky on patrol.

But right around then, Jace felt the dreamlike haze around them gradually weaken, and things became much clearer. That whelp even lowered its eyes and glanced their way, making Vereesa tense and reach back for her bow.

But the whelp quickly pulled away, circling back toward the southern skies above the forest. This allowed the group to cross the slope without incident and make their way into a forest in a valley.

By now the dreamlike atmosphere around them had strengthened again, and Jace breathed a sigh of relief.

Still, even deep into the night, there were still signs of dragonkin nearby. Though they couldn't see Jace's group, they always seemed to linger close by.

"How do these bastards keep finding our position?"

Grede spoke as he warily watched the dragonkin shadows. Jace caught sight of the necklace around his neck—the Earth necklace—which still held a faint trace of golden light.

"When you fought that whelp Bremmugo earlier, you activated your transformation. The power of that necklace hasn't faded yet," he reminded him.

Grede lowered his head. "Ah, damn it, you're right."

Jace observed the shadows and said, "Our target was too big when we were on the airship. The black dragons sensed the necklace's power and could easily pinpoint something flying in the sky. Now that we're hidden here with the Dream's protection, I don't think they'll find us that easily. They can just sense our presence, that's all."

Vereesa said, "So we should keep hiding like this for a while, until the power of Grede's necklace fades. Hopefully this pendant's power will last long enough to let us completely shake off the dragonkin."

"Let's hope so," Jace said.

With that, the group found a low hollow to hide in and played cat-and-mouse with the dragonkin that came looking. Before long, the dragonkin seemed to have more and more trouble keeping up.

By now the sky was about to brighten again. Light was already faintly creeping over the other side of the mountain, and the sky had shifted from pitch black to deep blue.

After two consecutive nights of fighting, fleeing, and no sleep at all, Grede was so drowsy he could barely keep his eyes open. He'd probably fall asleep the moment he dropped.

Vereesa was, of course, completely fine. Jace felt a bit tired too, but at least he was more alert than Grede.

With the environment a little safer, Jace couldn't help but recall how, when they'd crossed the slope earlier, the power of the green dragon had seemed to weaken quite a bit. What was going on? Was its power unstable?

This pendant seemed able to drag everything within a large area into this half-awake, half-dreaming hazy world. So was the stability of its power related to the number of people covered, or the type of beings covered?

Could it have something to do with the two demons? They weren't life-forms of Azeroth, but fel and shadow constructs from the Twisting Nether—would they interfere with the Dream's power?

But ever since they'd hidden in this mountain hollow deep in the forest, the pendant's power had never thinned again. It had been stable for practically the whole second half of the night, so it probably had nothing to do with them.

"Vere, have you noticed that the emerald pendant's power seems a little unstable?"

"On the slope, I felt it too." The elf nodded. "It was as if it didn't have enough power to keep going. I almost thought that whelp was going to spot us."

"On the slope..." Jace muttered her words, when Vereesa suddenly seemed to remember something. "I know. Earlier, when the Dragonqueen handed it to me, she told me it could help her 'vanish into the forest.' Could it be that once we leave the forest, it loses its source of power? After all, she put it that specifically."

"Ah... yes." Jace smacked his head. "I actually forgot about that detail she mentioned. I only remembered it had something to do with hiding."

"I don't think the Dragonqueen wanted you using this thing to steal things in Silvermoon, did she?" Grede laughed.

"I'd use this to steal things?!" Vereesa glared at the dwarf unhappily, then sighed. "But I always thought this emerald pendant could only hide the wearer. If I'd tested its power back in Quel'Thalas, we wouldn't have had to flee this miserably."

Grede patted the round shield he was cradling. "Good thing you didn't test it. If we'd fled early, I wouldn't have gotten this. Look at this—it's a dragonkin guardian's shield. It might've been forged personally by that black dragon princess, Onyx-whatever! We ran into so many dragonkin, and only that one was carrying a shield like this. This might be a priceless treasure!"

"Onyxia," Vereesa said.

Now that the dwarf mentioned it, Jace suddenly remembered too. This seemed to be the Dragonkin Shield dropped by General Drakkisath, the final boss of Upper Blackrock Spire—a towering figure wearing no armor at all, wielding a dark greatsword and carrying a massive Blackrock Spire banner on his back.

But in the game, only Drakkisath himself dropped this shield, not—as was the case here—one you could snatch from every dragonkin carrying this type of shield.

Still, judging by the armor that dragonkin had worn and his formidable combat ability, this shield, while not unique, wasn't something just any dragonkin was qualified to carry. It might not fetch a sky-high price, but it was indeed a fine piece of loot.

Noticing Jace staring at the shield, Grede's expression darkened. "What the hell are you thinking? Don't even think about selling it for money. It's mine."

"It's yours, it's yours." Jace waved a hand impatiently.

"That's more like it." The dwarf leaned back against a tree, tucked his head into his breastplate—already stained red with dragon blood—and muttered, "I want to sleep for a bit. If we keep walking like this, I think I'm going to pass out."

"I'll wake you if there's danger," Jace said.

"Hold on." Vereesa stared into the distance. "What's that over there?"

Grede forced his eyes open again and looked where the elf was pointing. Jace looked too.

Amid the hazy, dim trees, in the deepest part of the valley where the light of dawn couldn't reach at all, there seemed to be a vague, indistinct human shadow.

That shadow wasn't a dragonkin. It seemed to be outside the half-dreaming plane, yet also within the range of the Dream's power. The strangest thing was that the shadow seemed to be staring straight at the group, as if it could see them.

"Can you two see that thing?" Vereesa asked.

Jace hummed in acknowledgment and carefully stood up. "I'll go take a closer look."

"Let's go together," Vereesa said.

Grede said nothing, just warily got to his feet as well. The group walked a ways toward it, trying to get closer.

Though the shadow made no movement, it always seemed to maintain a fixed distance—they simply couldn't get any closer.

"Damn it," the dwarf muttered.

"What if we try withdrawing the pendant's power, Vere," Jace said. "The dragonkin haven't shown up in a long time, and Grede's necklace isn't glowing anymore."

Vereesa nodded, pinched the pendant, and closed her eyes. The silver light slowly faded, and the things around them became clearer and clearer. Jace felt slightly dizzy, but after a moment the shadow became clear too.

It was a woman draped in a brown-gray cloak and hood. By her build, she was either an elf or a human.

From this distance, Jace couldn't sense even a trace of magical power on her, like some ordinary noble or townswoman. But she was definitely not as simple as she appeared on the surface. Jace was absolutely certain of that.

Then an extremely terrifying thought bubbled up from the bottom of his heart, considering this location, the timing of this woman's appearance...

"Hello, miss," he tried calling out.

Grede glanced back and whispered, "Aren't you calling a bit loud? Aren't you afraid the dragonkin will come after us?"

"The dragonkin have all gone back." The woman had heard his whisper clearly. She continued, "The question now is: what is a Lordaeronian, a Wildhammer dwarf, and a Quel'dorei doing in Kalimdor?"

Her voice was slightly weak, but already clear enough to hear.

And her Common had almost no accent at all. There was perhaps a touch of Lordaeron flavor, but it also carried some Stormwind characteristics. Her manner of speaking reminded him of King Terenas's cadence, but it wouldn't seem out of place coming from one of Stormwind's nobles either—especially Bolvar Fordragon.

"We're just passing through, miss, and we accidentally ran into trouble in the marsh," Vereesa said. "We don't mean any harm, but you'd best identify yourself."

"What are you?" Grede called out as well. "Why don't we need to worry about the dragonkin anymore?" Jace glanced at the two of them in embarrassment. These two definitely had no idea who this woman in front of them might be.

But he couldn't just tell them directly either. Compared to someone who had mysteriously appeared and knew her true identity, this kind of wary questioning was probably completely insignificant to her—entirely understandable.

"I mean no harm either, ranger. No need to be so tense." The woman lowered her hood. Her head of silver hair, combined with her speaking voice, easily made one think she was advanced in years, but her face wasn't as old as her voice suggested—probably only thirty or forty, more or less.

It was hard to describe her looks as beautiful or ugly. She was just remarkably gentle and approachable, making people unconsciously let their guard down.

Seeing this, Jace was almost certain of his guess.

She was Aegwynn—the woman who had shattered the avatar of Sargeras in Northrend, the eight-hundred-year-old Guardian of Tirisfal, Medivh's birth mother.
