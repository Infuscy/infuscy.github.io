## Chapter 83: An Unexpected Test

"The Wetlands matter won't be decided immediately anyway — it'll depend on the Sanctum leadership, Andormas and the rest." Marin licked a finger and flipped open the book before him. "Now then — let's talk about your Light spell, Mr. Sesso."

"Ah… Light," Jace replied. "I practiced a few times yesterday, but like you said, my energy is limited. Once I felt tired I didn't dare keep going, so I was thinking of getting some mana potions to intensify my training."

"Practicing with mana potions?" Marin turned his head. "Got money to burn?"

Hearing Marin say that, Jace felt a flicker of uncertainty.

What made mana potions expensive? The materials? Mageroyal grew all over Westfall — it shouldn't be particularly pricey.

As for Silverleaf… wasn't that everywhere? Last time he'd gone out with Grede to Duskwood he'd just picked some along the way, and later when he'd hunted wolves by the riverbank he'd spotted some too. If he'd brought a big herb bag, he probably would have brought some back.

Briarthorn… that seemed like the kind of thing you could buy for two or three silver a stem.

Was it the vials for brewing?

"Are mana potions expensive?" Jace asked tentatively.

Marin said, "The materials aren't cheap, but they're not the main driver of the price. The trouble is the brewing method. Most potions that rapidly restore mana require an extremely skilled alchemist to craft. Are you sure you can handle it on your own? In any case, I won't help you make them — not for any price. At my age my eyes aren't what they were. If I go smelting potions at this point, I'll go blind before I'm too old to walk."

Jace said, "That's how it is, then. But I've got to try it myself — and it'll be good alchemy practice on the side."

"That actually makes sense," Marin said. "Using a potion whose materials are cheap but whose brewing method is difficult to practice your craft — that's a solid way to sharpen your skills."

If that was the case, why had Ursula Drury been willing to brew for him for free?

Recalling the earlier conversation between Ginc and Galders, they'd mentioned Ursula had her value at the Slaughtered Lamb — perhaps this was exactly that value.

By leveraging her exceptional alchemy skills in exchange for unconditional spell-sharing from others, she could practice her brewing with ingredients others provided, practice shadow magic on the side, and have a place where she belonged.

Since crafting mana potions was this technically demanding, taking on the tasks Ursula had entrusted to him didn't feel quite so much like being taken advantage of.

While Jace was lost in thought, Marin asked: "So you're planning to gather herbs yourself, then? Most new apprentices start with the most basic mana restoration draught — that usually calls for mageroyal. There's plenty in Westfall; in my youth I used to go there with the academy's alchemists all the time. You've looked like you wanted to say something this whole time — is that it? You want to go take a look over there?"

"I do have that plan," Jace said. "I was just about to ask for time off, actually."

Marin said, "Well, there's no need to ask for leave — after noon today you won't be an assistant anymore. As long as you complete the tasks I assign you going forward, you won't have to work here every morning. Just follow the class schedule, and the rest of your time is your own."

"Are the magic classes scheduled like the Orcish lessons?" Jace asked.

"No," Marin said. "Each class generally meets only once a week, because students need ample time to practice and exchange ideas. Magic courses aren't scheduled as tightly as the humanities or language classes. But if you plan to specialize in Alchemy, Enchanting, and so on — that's a whole different matter; those are both intensive programs. You'll know in due time."

"I more or less understand," Jace said. "By the way, Master — there's one more thing. At year's end, during Winter Veil, I'm planning to head back to Lordaeron to see my parents. Is there any chance…"

"They'll send you on a work trip to Dalaran, right?" Marin looked up, wearing the expression of someone who'd seen this coming.

Jace smiled. Marin said, "Honestly, there really isn't any business requiring a trip to Dalaran at year's end right now. I don't know if the Wizard Sanctum will suddenly have some task come up down the line. But what I do know is that your Orcish teacher, Master Dushan, has an opportunity for an Orcish language study exchange in Dalaran at year's end. If you can secure that spot, he'll cover your travel expenses to Dalaran."

"Huh?" Jace hadn't expected that. "How do I secure that spot? Master Dushan never mentioned it."

"Simple," Marin said. "Get first place in the Orcish exam on Tuesday, November 12th, in the afternoon. I'd say that'd more than do it. The reason he doesn't bring it up is that he doesn't want the trouble. A lot of the mages here would love a free trip to Dalaran — once word gets out, they'd all be pestering him behind his back, and Dushan's the type who hates hassle. My telling you this is already going to make him unhappy."

"In that case," Jace frowned, "you want me to take first place among a bunch of people who've already been studying for a while? And there's only a month left — am I supposed to capture an orc to learn the language?"

Marin said, "That's your affair — I'm no Orcish expert. Otherwise, you could come here every morning and I'll cast Arcane Brilliance on you to give you a slight edge. But if I do that every day, you might develop a split personality within two weeks — gradually coming to loathe the stupid, slow version of yourself once the spell wears off."

"Is Arcane Brilliant that terrifying?" Jace stammered. "Never mind, then. But Master — could you at least leak some of the exam content?"

"This isn't a math test. Even if I did know, telling you wouldn't help." Marin said, "A language exam comes down to the same things: translating passages, giving a report, writing, and answering questions. Based on the two exams Master Dushan's run before, I'd expect this one to be no different — you'll be asked to write an article in Orcish about an issue related to the Kingdom of Stormwind or within the Alliance, deliver an on-the-spot report, and then he'll ask you some related questions. Of course, the focus is on comprehension and command of the language — don't worry about how profound or logical your article is."

For that kind of exam, leaking the content really would be pointless — in the end he'd have to produce the work himself.

Just thinking about it gave a headache. How was he supposed to stand out from that crowd and earn the right to represent the Wizard Sanctum at the Dalaran Orcish exchange?

With only a month left, he'd just have to carve out every scrap of time he could to study and practice.
