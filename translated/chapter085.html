Chapter 85: A Veteran's Longing

In the afternoon, Jace packed his herb-gathering cloth bag and tools, along with the warlock bag for the imp and a Shadow Protection Potion, took his staff, and set out.

The original purpose of this trip to Westfall was to gather herbs, and along the way see if he could run into any murlocs or the like — help Ursula look into the intelligence she wanted, about the shadow magic murlocs used and so on.

But after talking with his teacher, especially learning about the important Orcish exam coming up, mana potions had become a lot less critical.

After all, he'd be throwing himself into Orcish study for the next month. If it weren't for wanting enough energy left over to keep practicing spells, he wouldn't have come out at all.

Used to traveling with the dwarf, sitting alone in a caravan wagon in quiet procession along the roads of Elwynn Forest felt oddly unfamiliar.

He rested at Westfall Keep in the evening. Jace planned to pick up a discarded weapon from someone here. An old soldier with a rather strange tone came up and asked him right away what he wanted a weapon for. Jace stressed several times over that he just wanted something for self-defense against the occasional wild animal, and only then did the man agree to sell him one.

Following the soldier to a more secluded tent, he saw a mountain of scrap metal piled in the corner.

"Go ahead," the soldier said, pointing. "Ten copper apiece. If you find one that still works, count yourself lucky."

Jace picked through them for a long while before finding a knife that was more or less still usable.

The soldier, shirtless, bent down to tidy up the mess of old weapons Jace had scattered. Jace asked, "Where did all these weapons come from?"

"Scavenged, worn out — a lot of them were used by gnolls or murlocs." The soldier turned his head and grinned. "You want an orc weapon? We've got those too. More expensive, but most of them can still chop wood. The bigger ones you probably can't handle, but the smaller knives, short swords — you could dual-wield those, I'd say you've got the strength for it."

The man's expression looked like he was deliberately mocking something. Jace wisely shook his head.

He remembered how thick that club the orc shaman in Redridge had been holding looked up close, yet from a distance it had seemed as pitiful as a twig in his hand. That alone was enough to imagine the scale of orc weapons.

"Do you run into a lot of murlocs?" Jace asked again.

The soldier said, "These past couple of years, wrecked ships and soaked supplies have been washing up on the western shore over here, drawing all the murlocs from the west coast over, so it's gotten more troublesome. It used to be you wouldn't see a murloc unless you went to the western shore."

"What shipwrecks?"

"Warships, of course. Kul Tiras, Stormwind — we've got all kinds. Even orc ones."

"Orc?"

"Right. Because one time on patrol we saw murlocs roasting orc meat for a meal."

"How'd you know it was orc meat?"

The soldier patted his own head. "I can tell an orc's head at a glance — I know 'em too well."

"So you've seen orcs with your own eyes," Jace said. "You're an Orc Wars veteran, huh?"

"What d'you think?" The soldier laughed. "I'm not just an Orc Wars veteran — I was among the first to enter the swamp on King Laine's orders. Not many of those first few batches ever made it back. I was one of 'em. Back then my belly was full of nothing but rage, all I wanted was revenge. So later I crossed Thandol Span and fought at Hillsbrade with the Silver Hand."

"Then you must've held a high rank."

No sooner had Jace asked than he realized he'd probably said the wrong thing. This man was here selling scrap weapons — he didn't look anything like a high-ranking officer.

"No. Because at Hillsbrade I deserted," the soldier admitted frankly.

"But where could you run? Orcs, trolls, wolves bigger than cows — they were everywhere. So I went back. Because I'd earned merit before, and the new Stormwind's reconstruction was short on hands, so I wasn't executed. I came back to help with labor, and later got recalled to the army for logistics. I've been a soldier half my life. I don't know how to do anything else."

Jace looked at the man and could hardly imagine what a warrior who'd survived being hunted by orcs in the Black Swamp must have gone through at Hillsbrade.

That battle had had death knights and red dragons too. If you hadn't truly been part of that war, no one could say for sure they'd have kept their composure and held out to the very end.

And this man spoke of things that should have haunted him for life as if they were someone else's story.

"What do you want with murlocs?" the soldier suddenly asked.

"Oh — I'm heading to the north coast, so I was asking where the murlocs are so I can avoid them as much as possible," Jace answered, then asked, "If I wanted to go up that way, where's the best place to start? Is there anywhere to stay?"

"Cross the bridge, keep heading west." The soldier said, "Follow the road about ten-odd miles and you'll see an abandoned village — no one there. Past the village there's a farm, not far off. It belongs to the Furlbrows — they grow pumpkins. A few miles north of that there's a tiny fishing village, only four households. That's where you'll see the coast. You can find a place to stay at any of these spots."

Jace thanked him and was about to leave when the man asked again: "Right — do you plan on going to that fishing village?"

"I'd like to take a look."

"My wife was born there." The soldier said, "Come to think of it, I haven't seen her in a long time. If you go, pass along a word for me — tell her I'm doing fine."

"I can do that," Jace said. "What's her name?"

The soldier said nothing. He just looked at Jace for a while, then turned and left through the tent's other exit.

Jace wasn't surprised by the man's incomprehensible reaction, because from the very start he'd felt this fellow wasn't quite right in the head.

Of course, it wasn't that he didn't want to help the poor bastard — but without the soldier's name or his wife's name, how was he supposed to deliver the message?

Walking out of the tent, he felt more and more that something about the man's words was off.

Fishing village, murlocs, wife, a veteran who wasn't right in the head.

He wasn't about to get a Shadow over Innsmouth pulled on him, was he?

There wasn't any creepy fishing village in the game, and Westfall didn't have any Cthulhu-style events. There were some ruined, dilapidated houses up on the north coast, though.

Since the soldier said the fishing village had only four households, finding the family whose daughter had married a soldier shouldn't be hard.

Jace thought about the talk of revenge and rage the veteran had mentioned. Considering the wife might have perished in the war, it was probably because of that that the soldier's mind had gone, and something had broken on the battlefield that got him labeled a deserter.

If that was the case, telling the wife's family how the veteran was doing would at least give them some closure.

He spent the night at the inn at Westfall Keep and set out again early the next morning.

He'd heard of Furlbrow Farm — it existed in the game, after all — but the abandoned village and fishing village the soldier had mentioned, he had absolutely no memory of.

Westfall was big. It might be a lot larger than the Elwynn region. Last time he'd come here with Grede, just getting to Sentinel Hill had taken a day and a night, let alone Moonbrook farther south.

And judging from the map, the carriage road from Westfall Keep to Sentinel Hill only covered a short stretch through the large eastern portion of Westfall.

There were probably plenty of little unknown settlements — places that might've been quite well-known before the Orc Wars, before the Dark Portal opened, but had faded into obscurity after the orcs swept through.

By noon Jace was getting hungry and was debating whether to eat something right then when he finally spotted a farm.

It had already been harvested. From the dried, withered leaves lying around the farm, it looked like a pumpkin field. Could this be the Furlbrow Farm the soldier had mentioned earlier?

Come to think of it, Jace hadn't seen any town or village either.

"Hey, you there — here for pumpkins? October? The Harvest Festival's already over."

Someone was shouting from the house in the distance. Jace looked over and saw a tall man in a brown coat.

"I'm not here to buy pumpkins, sir!" he called back as he walked over, making out that the shouting man was a young fellow.

"Mr. Furlbrow?"

"Yeah — Rendor Furlbrow! The owner of this farm!"
