Chapter 857: Blade of Ill Omen

As Jace's gaze swept over those Black Riders, they looked over at the same time. Even though he couldn't make out their faces beneath their dark hoods, Jace could clearly feel every single one of their gazes locked onto him. Jace glanced back at his companions in the snow. The laughter and joyful shouts all felt unreal now. Should he call them over? Did these Black Riders know him? As the Black Riders appeared on the street, the merchants and travelers around them all gave way rather unnaturally. At the same time, a creeping sense of fear slowly seeped into his heart. The Chain of Will grew faintly warm. Someone was casting psychic magic on him. Through his keen perception of shadow — and, of course, his still-intact, rational will — Jace had already traced the magic to its source: the leading knight in the dark-purple long cloak. Even from this distance, Jace could see the layers upon layers of dark metal armor hidden beneath his cloak and outer garments, like the plated body of a scorpion, layer covering layer. He looked somewhat heavy when he walked. Still, in a bustling, rough-and-tumble place like Everlook, where there existed a band of mercenaries perfectly happy to have a snowball fight, there naturally also existed groups who preferred not to show themselves under the sun. Merely dark-dressed, strangely armored figures wouldn't make this leading dark rider stand out on appearance alone. What truly made Jace uneasy was the sword at his waist, wrapped in a slightly yellowish-grey burial shroud. From the parts of the blade and hilt exposed from the burial cloth, Jace could see fang-like or bone-like spikes faintly jutting outward. A skull-like eye stared outward. This leading Black Rider didn't seem to mind exposing some of this thing's details — perhaps because he was proud of it, and the act of concealing it was itself a kind of performance. Jace couldn't help but take a second look. And then he realized the source of this fear wasn't that Black Rider — it was the sword he carried. This dark demon blade was radiating fear at every moment, forcing everyone nearby to not want to look directly at these Black Riders' faces, forcing them to wish they'd never seen this group at all — and even if they had seen them, to forget it immediately. Black Riders. A terrifying demon blade. A fact Jace had always been reluctant to accept surfaced in his mind. Was this "Apocalypse"? The Apocalypse was a true Nathrezim blade — unlike the Nathrezim black blade in Morophil's hands, which was merely a tool the dreadlords' minions used to wreak violence everywhere. The Apocalypse… was a dark treasure forged jointly by countless dreadlords and demons from the depths of the Twisting Nether, a fallen weapon capable of spreading fear, plague, and death. During Dalaran's period of magical chaos, countless demons had entered that city to wreak destruction, and it was during that time that this weapon was brought to Azeroth by demons. In the game, death knight players had to seize this artifact from the hands of a Black Rider leader and become its master. Jace had forgotten how the Black Riders had come to find this weapon, but that didn't matter anymore. This Black Rider leader before him had already gotten his hands on it. When Jace first resolved to search for Ursulus, he hadn't considered at all that he'd cross paths with other artifacts. He never imagined that the first artifact he'd lay eyes on would be this Apocalypse. And the worse part was, it was on someone else's person. Once Jace confirmed that the dark power pervading Everlook was coming from that sword, he could almost feel the bone eye half-wrapped in the burial cloth on that weapon staring right at him. He lowered his head and averted his gaze, letting the power of that fear slowly dissipate. The Chain of Will grew hotter and hotter. Before he realized it, snow had piled up all over his hood and shoulders. The five or six dark riders mounted their tall warhorses draped in brown, black, or dark-red capes, one after another, passing in front of Jace with the clank of metal horseshoes, heading toward Everlook's gate. The horses' eyes all glowed blood-red. Jace suspected they might be dead zombie horses too. He looked back at the others. Musa had played herself out and was sitting off to the side resting. Grede was still roughhousing with that ogre. Sola was not far off, watching Everlook's gate. Their eyes met. She walked over. "You saw that group too." "I did," Jace said. Just then Windrunner came over as well. She looked toward the shadows disappearing into the snow. "They're here." Sola asked, "They? I can feel a heavy dark power radiating from them — it's almost impossible for me to… look directly at them. Do you know something? Are these people also connected to the corruption of the Emerald Dream?" "Probably not to the Emerald Dream. It's connected to us. We need to get back to the inn and talk right away." Jace looked toward the succubus who'd come closer. "Get Grede and Musa back here, Morophil." The succubus nodded. Sola watched her run into the snow and drew a deep breath. "What's going on?" Grede walked in clapping the snow off his shoulders. In Windrunner's and Jace's room, everyone had already gathered. Sazeno had been lying on the windowsill watching outside — he turned back now. "We're in trouble, dwarf! You wouldn't believe… there are monsters blocking our path." "That monster itself is nothing much," Morophil said lightly, flicking the black blade in her hand up and catching it. "The key is the thing it's carrying." "Don't leave me hanging," Grede said impatiently. "Tell me — what exactly are we dealing with?" Windrunner frowned at Morophil. "What do you mean? Are you saying these Black Riders aren't quite like the ones we saw in Eldre'Thalas?" "Ah." Grede nodded. "So it's those living dead. So they finally tracked a lead and caught up to us. Damn it, I didn't expect them to come this fast." "Maybe we spent too much time delayed on the road," Jace said, pinching the bridge of his nose. Sola stared blankly at their exchange. Jace looked at her and said, "Since you're planning to travel with us, Sola, I have to tell you the purpose of this trip too." Jace then laid out for Sola the whole journey — how they'd discovered traces of demons and Ursulus in Eldre'Thalas, all the way to how they'd tracked it to Winterspring — and placed Dasmora's report on the table. When he was done, he pointed out the window. "The leading dark rider is carrying an extraordinary weapon — the Apocalypse. Have you heard that name?" Windrunner nodded. "An evil sword the Order of Tirisfal recovered from the demons. I know of it. It once destroyed a human village. the Holy Relics Society has records of it. At first I even thought Ursulus and the Apocalypse were two different names for the same object." "What's so special about that sword?" Grede glanced at the Song of Azure Skies at Jace's waist. "How does it compare to this?" "When all's said and done, the Song of Azure Skies is still just a sword of mana forged by the blue dragons — even now that its blade is made of Malygos's scale…" Windrunner sighed softly. "The Apocalypse is a weapon whose mere appearance is enough to cause chaos and destruction, dwarf!" the imp said. "Ah, master, I'm not belittling your sword — your sword is certainly formidable — but the Apocalypse… that is the Apocalypse!" "Everlook still looks all right to me," Grede glanced out. "Things were a bit chaotic just now, but it definitely has nothing to do with that so-called 'Apocalypse.'" Grede's question wasn't without basis. In legend, the Apocalypse was wielded by dreadlords to invade innocent worlds and sow plague and death. But judging from what just happened, even though the weapon's appearance had brought him considerable mental pressure, it hadn't immediately brought disaster and terror the way the legends described. "As far as I know," Morophil waggled her index finger, "that might actually be worse."
