Chapter 859: Canyon Entrance

The mountains echoed with hollow, ethereal roars, indifferent to distance. Accompanied by a gale that scraped across cliff faces and icicles, snowflakes danced in disarray. Unnoticed, the storm grew stronger and stronger. This storm was clearly unnatural. Jace could tell it was definitely connected to the Frosthammer giants' howling. Especially when Solamriel tried to gain altitude, pulling away from the chunks of ice and boulders the giants hurled, the giants' roars quickly grew angrier, and the storm intensified accordingly. But no matter what, Solamriel was flying with increasing difficulty. After struggling in the storm for a while, she was finally forced to land in a snow valley to take shelter from the onslaught. Although they had spent the whole night darting through danger under the threat of the Frosthammer giants, they couldn't rest — after all, they were racing against a group of undead who didn't even know what tiredness was. Even those horses might be reanimated zombies. Jace looked at the map. Based on the position of Hyjal Summit, the approximate distance from Everlook to the north, and the distribution of the surrounding snowy mountains, he estimated they had arrived roughly to the north of Darkwhisper Gorge. Considering Frosthammer giants were everywhere in the mountains around them, it might be better to keep a low profile and use the cover of the snow valley before them to slip into the gorge. After all, who knew what was actually inside Darkwhisper Gorge — SI:7's warning couldn't have been without cause. By a distant cliff face, a massive ancient Kaldorei ruin — a stone pillar — stood tall, like a stone guardian protecting the valley. After ten thousand years of wind and snow, its surface was quite rough; the human figures and patterns were battered, worn, and hard to make out. Still, Jace could recall a few things about these stone pillars. In the game, Darkwhisper Gorge was full of terrifying demons — even max-level players at the time had trouble handling them alone. He had once kited a doomguard elite around a stone pillar at the entrance of Darkwhisper Gorge to wear it down. Was it this very pillar? Of course, in-game models couldn't be equated one-to-one with everything here, but the Kaldorei ruins more or less signaled that they were approaching Darkwhisper Gorge. The dense pine forest faintly visible beyond the pillar also confirmed the situation. Before Darkwhisper Gorge was burned by demons, it had been a vast forest — just as SI:7 had described. "We didn't provoke those giants," Grede muttered unhappily as he checked whether anything from the luggage had been lost or left behind. "What are they doing? Just because we passed by... they want to wipe us out? It's ridiculous. If these fellows were carnivores I'd let it slide and not argue with them, but these guys grew up eating stones, right? I've heard since I was a kid that those mountain giants all grow up eating stones. The giants here, even if they're different, probably just grow up eating ice." The green dragon reverted to her mortal form and straightened her cloak and collar. "The Frosthammer giants have been guarding these snowy mountains since before the Well of Eternity exploded, Grede. Ysondre told me not to set foot in their territory and disturb their slumber, but I've rarely heard of Frosthammer giants attacking outsiders with such fury..." "Maybe it's because they're just a bunch of maddened monsters to begin with," Grede said, shouldering his pack. The group packed up their luggage and walked deeper into the snow valley. Jace said, "It might be because of me and Morophil — demons, dark power. Mount Hyjal hasn't been spared from demon attacks. The War of the Satyr, which Sola mentioned earlier. Besides, during the War of the Ancients, the war between the demons and the Kaldorei also spread to this land." "I've done my best to conceal my power, master," Morophil said innocently. "These ancient giants may have a perception beyond ordinary understanding," Jace said. "If that's really the case, then it does make sense," Sola said. "But it might also be because of me — after all, I couldn't resist the corruption of the Nightmare and turned into that state. These giants may be able to sense what I've been through." Grede waved his hand. "Then you should put your mind at ease even more, Sola. Be more cheerful, more happy — stop dwelling on what happened in that nightmare. Maybe you'll recover faster that way." "If being positive really could resist corruption, then perhaps nothing would be so troublesome," Sola said. "Actually, Grede isn't wrong," Jace said. "Being optimistic and happy can definitely help resist the Nightmare's corruption." Sola asked, "Really?" Jace nodded. "It might sound like a joke to you green dragons, but shadow is affected by emotions far more than you'd think, Sola. So think about things that make you happy. If you don't believe me, you can ask Morophil." Morophil said, "Shadow is certainly affected by emotions, no question — but I wouldn't presume to confirm for the master that optimistic emotions can resist the Nightmare's erosion. After all, I don't understand optimism, and I understand the Nightmare even less." "If that's the case, I just realized a problem, succubus," Grede asked as he trudged through the snow. "If optimism can fight shadow, then you're smiling all the time, acting like everything is a spectacle to you, and even when you're injured you never forget to tease our little Jace — so you're probably the most optimistic one in the whole group. As a dark creature who came here from the Twisting Nether, why haven't you annihilated yourself?" "I suppose it's because darkness can't be explained so simply, Grede," Sola said. "Simple — because her optimism and happiness are all fake, all an act. That's what a succubus is, dear everyone!" Sazeno poked his head out of the pack, wiping away a snowflake that had suddenly smacked his face. "She's a master at this. Don't be fooled by her!" "I still think Mr. Sazeno's explanation is more reasonable," Windrunner said with an emphatic nod. The imp excitedly raised his hand. "Ha, exactly! The esteemed Lady Windrunner always stands on my side!" "Yes, just like my love — all fake, which is why it gets so overlooked." Morophil lowered her head, putting on an injured expression, then smiled. "Including my contempt for this booger of yours is fake too — but you still get provoked so easily. It's truly baffling." "No, that's real!" the imp fumed. "The booger is real?" "The booger is fake!" "So is it real or fake?!" "Aaaaagh! Damn sayaad!" A burst of flame shot from the top of the imp's head. He shrieked, pointing at the succubus. "This is exactly it — you mix the real and the fake together, which makes it even easier to fool people!" "Then how can you be sure my other feelings are fake?" Morophil asked. "Me? I don't care!" the imp roared. "All right, all right." Jace patted the imp on the head. "Stop howling. Go back inside — what if the fel fire on you attracts the ice giants around here?" "The master told me to go back. That doesn't mean you won, you damn she-demon!" the imp said grumpily, crawling back into the pack. "Mmm, that doesn't mean you won..." Morophil mimicked the imp's tone in an affected voice, then chuckled. Sola couldn't help but smile too. Windrunner raised an eyebrow with a sigh and looked ahead. Grede, seeing Windrunner's expression, looked like he wanted to laugh but didn't dare, and could only force out a couple of coughs as he kept walking. The storm still raged, showing no sign of abating. Everyone trudged against the howling gale and barely made it to a snow slope. Jace looked back toward Everlook. The heavy snow obscured everything in his field of vision. But there was no doubt — the Black Riders were somewhere on the road heading this way. Actually, the possibility that the Frosthammer giants attacked them because of the dark power on Jace actually made Jace feel a little more at ease. Since the giants would attack them, they would certainly attack the Black Riders too — and the Black Riders would have to cross those canyons and cliffs on land. They would have a much harder time dodging the giants' attacks than the green dragon. He hoped these giants could slow them down a bit. The green dragon had taken more than a day and night to fly here — even pushing through wind and snow, the riders would need at least a week to arrive. Before reaching Frosthammer Canyon, Grede had gotten some sleep on Solamriel's back, so he looked fairly fresh right now. Windrunner didn't even feel sleepy in such a short time, and around Mount Hyjal her mana addiction didn't seem to flare up much either — she was now walking at the front of the group, full of energy. As for the group's newest member, a green dragon — she could even fly while asleep, even without entering the Dream. So the most awkward one was Jace. He was already a bit sleepy now, but couldn't sleep. He was starting to regret not asking the green dragon to put him to sleep right when they first took off. So now he watched Sola walking along and wondered if he could ask her to transform back into a green dragon and carry him on her back — but felt that might be asking too much. But then again, this would save time for the group and preserve their combat strength. Just as he was wrestling with himself, a sudden rumbling sound came from ahead. Having just gone through a whole string of escapes through canyons and cliffs, everyone here immediately recognized what the sound was. "The Frosthammer giants are ahead of us," Windrunner said. "Damn it." Grede leveled his warhammer. "So we end up having to fight after all?" The words had barely left his mouth when a gust of cold wind passed and the environment quieted somewhat — and within the wind, they could actually hear intermittent, indistinct beastly growls. Jace wasn't the only one who noticed. Windrunner's expression changed. "Those are furbolg voices." "Frosthammer furbolgs?" Jace asked. "Could the furbolgs be fighting the Frosthammer giants?" "Let's go take a look," Grede said.
