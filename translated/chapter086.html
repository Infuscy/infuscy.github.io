Chapter 86: Faolble's Farm

Jace remembered this Faolble. In the game, he and his wife had already left the farm, taking their old horse — the one named Brench — to go looking for help near Westfall Keep to recover his pocket watch.

Right now, though, he looked pretty young.

Rendal Faolble gave Jace a once-over and said, "Not here for pumpkins? Then what are you doing here?"

"I was wondering if there's a fishing village to the north?" Jace asked.

"There is," Faolble said. "But when the Orc Wars broke out, most of the fishermen fled to Stormwind. What happened after that, I've no idea — I haven't been up that way in a long time. Even if some of them came back, there can't be many left. You can see how dead things are around here — it only gets worse the further north you go."

Jace looked off to the north. No sign of the sea from here, nor any fishing village. A high slope blocked the whole view; all he could see was a patch of gnarled trees dotting the far edge of a yellow-green field.

"Sir, I'd like to buy some food — bread, soup, whatever you've got. I'm heading to the coast this afternoon."

Faolble said, "Sure thing. Come inside with me. I'll have my wife whip something up for you. Just toss us a few coppers so we're not out of pocket."

"Much appreciated." Jace followed the farmer into the farmstead.

The farm was big, and the house was big too, though it had seen better days. After the war, it looked like orcs had quartered here — damage and scars everywhere.

Now there were only two Faolbles living here, him and his wife. They probably didn't have the energy to renovate a whole house this size, so they were just making do.

Faolble wiped down the table and said, "These days people only stop by around Harvest Festival. The rest of the year, not a peep out of anyone. My wife's always going on about whether we should move to Stormwind. I swear she doesn't take this place seriously at all."

"Faolble!" a woman's impatient voice called from the other room. "What if the orcs come back? Last time someone said the orcs were coming and you didn't believe it, and we ended up hiding in a cave! You put me through hell! Everyone else believed it, but not you — you've got to be the boldest one here, and you've got to be the one who gets the worst of it!"

"The worst of it? Well, are you dead?" Faolble roared. "Where are all those alarmists now? Probably feeding the fish in Baradin Bay! At least you're still standing here, running your mouth about the husband who's saved your life who knows how many times!"

The woman fell silent for a moment. Jace spoke up. "Actually, it's unlikely the orcs will come back."

"Why?" Faolble asked.

"You heard about the expedition?" Jace said. "A few Alliance generals led a force of veterans to the Dark Portal. They're planning to cross into the orcs' world and settle this crisis once and for all."

"Beyond the Dark Portal?" Faolble slapped the table. "Now that's some good news! Frona, did you hear that?"

"I don't know about that," the woman said from the other room. "All I know is, even if the orcs are wiped out, nobody's going to care about us out here."

Faolble hesitated, then said with a resigned sigh, "The expedition will have pulled away the most elite soldiers, won't it? That means even fewer garrison troops they can station out here. The Light preserve us — here's hoping things get better."

"Let's hope so." Jace asked, "What was that cave Mrs. Faolble mentioned earlier…?"

Rendal Faolble let out a long breath. "When word reached us that orcs had entered Elwynn Forest, everyone around here fled. The refugees said the orcs were green-skinned monsters seven or eight feet tall with fangs, and that they ate people on sight."

"But that's what they used to say about the Kul Tirans, too. Oh, the Kul Tiran pirates have blue skin, gills and fins all over them — that sort of thing. And then what happened? When Proudmoore of Kul Tiras came to Stormwind, everyone saw he didn't look any different from us, really — just a bit taller, that's all."

As he spoke, the teasing look on his face slowly gave way to something helpless.

"Who knew they were telling the truth this time? Things that stood a full two heads taller than me, with fangs and green skin — monsters you couldn't even dream up in a nightmare. By the way, you're a Lordaeronian?"

"Yes. I came from the north," Jace said.

"Then you're a lucky man," Faolble said. "When the orcs held this place, my wife and I hid in a few abandoned mines around here. When one mine was discovered, we'd run to another with whoever was left. Went on like that for about two years, until Lothar's soldiers pulled us out. But a lot of people died on the way. Ah, it's all old history. I didn't catch your name."

Jace looked at the woman who'd just walked in. "I'm Jace."

The woman had light brown hair and looked barely out of her twenties. This was Faolble's wife, Frona — meaning she'd probably still been in her teens when she was running and hiding with Faolble.

"It's good to see people, Jace — especially young folks like you. These days, that's no small thing," Faolble said. "The south's all right, but up north there are so many gnoll rumors that people are too scared to come this way. A few farms out west have been abandoned, too. And now that there are fewer people around, the wolves have come back."

"Lots of wolves around?" Jace asked.

"They don't dare come onto the farm. If they do, we'll see whether their skulls are harder than my solid shot."

"Full of it," the woman muttered, setting a plate of stew and a large bowl of meat soup on the table with an obvious bang.

Faolble looked at the soup and said, "Pay her no mind. Nothing too fancy left here — that soup's made with meat. If you don't like it, I won't charge you a copper."

Jace sniffed it, then took a sip from his own bowl.

For someone who'd eaten Redridge stew, the flavor was on the bland side — but he was hungry enough that it didn't taste bad at all.

"The soup's quite good. Oh, right — sir, do you have any Mageroyal growing around here?" Jace asked. "I heard this is where Mageroyal grows. It's a kind of herb."

Faolble said, "I don't know much about herbs. Can you describe what it looks like?"

"Purple." Jace pulled out the picture and description he'd copied from the library and said, "Big bright pink flowers, long stems…"

Faolble took the picture from his hand and studied it carefully. "Four-petaled flowers, bright pink four-petaled flowers, with long stamens in the middle. I remember those."

"Wife!"

"What now?"

Frona called down from upstairs.

"Bring that potted plant down here — the one with four petals."

A moment later, Frona Faolble came downstairs carrying a potted plant. It looked perfectly ordinary — nothing but thick stems sticking up and green leaves, none of the four-petaled flowers Faolble had described matching the picture.

"That's the one," Faolble said, pointing. "Around April or May, this thing bloomed flowers that looked exactly like the ones in your picture."

"Really?"

Jace hadn't expected to get this kind of crucial lead right in a farmer's home. When he'd been copying down the ingredients for a Mana Potion, he'd forgotten to flip through a botanical encyclopedia or the like — he'd almost forgotten that flowers even had blooming seasons.

No wonder he hadn't spotted a single trace of Mageroyal along the way. He might have already walked right past one.

"Where did this plant come from?" Faolble asked his wife.

Frona said, "Last time I took the Sading family's kids to play in those abandoned farms out west, I saw it."

"Then that must be it," Jace said. "The information I got said there's plenty of Mageroyal growing on the hillsides near the mines to the west. But… aren't those mines overrun by gnolls?"

"Most of it's just rumors," Faolble said with a shake of his head. "Plenty of people say our farm's been taken over by gnolls, too. Say that ruined village to the east is held by gnolls, the mines are held by gnolls. The folks spreading those rumors ought to lose their heads — they've scared off everyone who used to come buy my pumpkins. Now I have to haul them to Sentinel Hill or Westfall myself. Tough young pumpkins turn into old ones, and old ones turn into rotten ones!"

"Westfall Keep's been harassed by gnolls too," Jace said.

Faolble said, "I suppose some bastard's been spreading more rumors, then?"

Jace said, "I was attacked there myself. Nearly lost my life. Stormwind's putting out a bounty on the head of that gnoll pack right now."

At that, Faolble froze for a moment.

"Well, regardless — the Light guide you to find what you're looking for, lad." He raised his bowl of soup.

"Thank you. And the Light bless the farm." Jace raised his bowl too, and the two of them drained their soup like they were toasting.

Faolble wiped the grease from his lips and said, "I don't know what you're doing at the coast, but if you're planning to go, I'd suggest going at night."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"You know a good number of murlocs have been coming in from the west, right?" Faolble said. "Those murlocs don't see so well at night. Big as their eyes are… their night vision is actually a lot worse than in daylight. If you go during the day, they might spot you from a long way off. Trust me, Jace — once those murlocs lock onto you, they'll chase you for at least half an hour."

That little speech made Jace's scalp crawl.

In the game, being chased for half a minute had been enough to make him want to puke. What would being hunted for half an hour be like — were they all marathon runners?

"If the fishing village is empty, can I stay there during the day?" Jace asked.

"Of course," Faolble said. "Even if there are still people there, they probably wouldn't turn you away. The few families living there should be old-timers, good-natured enough."

Jace asked again, "Won't murlocs move into the empty houses?" Faolble shook his head. "Murlocs generally don't enter human dwellings, even abandoned ones. They go out of their way to keep their distance from every other race. Still — be careful no matter what."
