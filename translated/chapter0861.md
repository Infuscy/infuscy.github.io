## Chapter 861: The Only Path

In the game, past the cliffs where the frost giants lived, after crossing a suspension bridge, you'd be close to the Dark Ravine — already in demon territory.

Aside from demons, the ravine was completely inhospitable to any other creature. And the demons' arrival had left the whole place littered with charred dead trees, blackened trunks toppled everywhere. Demons strode through smoke and open flame, turning the place into a burning hell.

At this point in time, though, the Dark Ravine was still a snow-blanketed canyon. The furbolgs led the way up front while Jace and the others followed behind. Jace kept glancing back and forth, trying to connect this place with the terrain from the game.

But aside from the familiar-looking cliffs and the ruins of ancient night elf structures, he could hardly find any common ground. Relying on his in-game experience to navigate was basically impossible.

He pulled out every map he had related to the southern part of Winterspring, one by one — the maps gifted by the night elves, the Kalimadormap he'd bought in Gadgetzan, the ones he'd copied from SI:7. Not a single one depicted the Dark Ravine's terrain in any detail. They all just sketched in some forests, hills, and so on. Only the night elf map had marked the rough layout of a settlement here, but it didn't match what he was seeing at all.

None of them were the slightest bit helpful for finding a way through.

SI:7's map was the worst of the lot — they'd drawn this place as one big forest. But looking ahead, there was clearly just a narrow valley, and it got narrower as they went. At one point they even had to cross a tight stone bridge sheathed in ice and snow.

Gazing down into the bottomless snow-filled gullies on either side, feeling the ice beneath their feet that could send anyone slipping with one wrong step, nobody in Jace's group looked too happy about it.

Except for Sora, that is. She was probably the one here who cared least about falling.

Crossing that bridge, Jace even wondered whether the black knights would have a hard time riding their horses across. Glancing back at the bridge, he almost wanted to blow it up with a spell right then and there, cutting off any notion the black knights might have of getting in.

But destroying this bridge would probably also cut off the Frostmace furbolgs' only connection to the outside world. No medicine, no food supply — the demons wouldn't even need to show up; they'd doom themselves first.

Not to mention the bridge itself seemed sturdy enough. From the Kaldorei map, it was clear that the Dark Ravine had been a night elf city just like Kaynethril before the Well of Eternity exploded — otherwise there wouldn't be so many stone pillars and structures. So this bridge, along with everything else, was probably part of the original city.

It was just that centuries of accumulated frost and snow had wrapped the once-exquisite bridge in layer after layer of ice, making it look like a massive ice road now.

He remembered that in the game, there was a path through the Dark Ravine that led into Mount Hyjal.

Of course, given how vaguely the night elf map depicted the Dark Ravine, with its sparse markings, Jace found it genuinely hard to imagine that if there was a route to their sacred mountain, they would have drawn it so ambiguously.

So he figured it was quite possible that right now, between the Dark Ravine and Mount Hyjal, there might still be impassable cliffs or precipices. After the Battle of Mount Hyjal, it was the fleeing demons who had blasted open that downward path. To keep the Alliance and Horde armies from pursuing them, they'd sealed the caverns with fel magic, which was why in the game's early days that underground passage had been blocked by a green magical seal that nobody could get through.

Or maybe it was simply that the game developers had been too lazy, scrapped the completed Mount Hyjal terrain, and just blocked it off so players couldn't pass. Maybe Jace was overthinking the whole thing.

But rather than stand here guessing, the furbolgs probably knew more. So he turned to Sora beside him. "Could you ask the furbolgs for me, Sora — is this bridge the only path into the area ahead?"

"I'll ask," Sora said.

She exchanged a few words with the furbolgs up front, then turned back to Jace. "From the north, this bridge is the only way in."

"Sounds like there are other ways in from other directions?" Grede asked.

Sora explained, "The furbolgs say the south is a mountain no one has ever gone into, Grede. At least, they rarely go there, and those who do rarely come back. So they don't know whether there's a way out or not."

"Just think about it, Grede," Vereesa said as she walked. "The furbolgs have lived here for God knows how many centuries. If there was a way out to the south, they would have found it long ago."

Following the furbolgs, they picked their way through scattered ancient broken pillars and walls, climbed a slope, and arrived at a dense forest of snow pines.

Looking at these towering trees, Jace figured this must be the Dark Ravine forest that SI:7 had described. Except he hadn't heard any "dark whispers" yet.

According to SI:7's description, just getting close here you'd hear the gloomy murmuring of ghosts, which was why they warned all agents not to approach the area.

But right now, aside from the occasional snap of a branch, he couldn't hear a thing. Had he not yet reached the spot SI:7's agents had described?

And there was no "green maze" either.

As he walked, Jace kept looking over the Eredun scroll of Das Mora's that he'd copied down.

It mentioned several clues that hadn't been verified yet — including the sound of a calling soul, traces of elves, magical enemies, a deep green giant wall…

Traces of elves — no problem there. At first Jace had thought this scroll was referring to the night elves in Ashenvale, but actually coming here, the clue was even more precise than he'd guessed. Ancient Kaldorei city relics were everywhere, so that checked out.

As for magical enemies… did that mean these furbolgs?

There were shamans among the furbolgs. If you stretched it, you could call them magical enemies, but Jace didn't think Das Mora would describe the furbolgs that way.

The elven ruins might hold enemies like the specters in the Kaynethril ruins. If ghosts really did exist, that would be much closer to the description of "magical enemies." Either way, they needed to stay alert.

The sound of a calling soul — SI:7 had mentioned that before. They hadn't ventured deep yet, so they couldn't confirm whether it actually existed or not.

The most mysterious was the deep green giant wall. If it was just a "giant wall"…

Jace gazed at the broken ancient Kaldorei walls. Their buildings had probably been close to white marble, maybe even silver. Around Mount Hyjal or in the Ashenvale ruins, the elven ruins were covered in vines and moss, so they took on a slightly greenish tinge.

But no way you'd call them deep green, much less a… giant wall.

What the hell was Das Mora even talking about? In this snow-filled ravine, the most baffling thing was this so-called deep green giant wall.

Jace looked at the scroll again. This time he really hoped he'd misread it, misremembered it, or mistranslated it. Otherwise the concept of this deep green giant wall would stick in his mind like a thorn.

Thinking that, he gazed off into the depths of the snow pines. Hopefully he'd find the answer in there.

"Be careful," Vereesa said. "A lot of furbolgs are hiding in there."

"Where?" Grede asked, looking around.

"Have you really been a hunter before, Grede?" Vereesa asked skeptically.

"Of course," Grede said. "My father was a seasoned hunter who hunted raptors in Grim Batol. He was a pretty famous hunter in Thunderfist Hall… a lot of people called him the Brown Bear Slayer. And I followed him hunting in the forests of the Hinterlands since I was a kid…"

Before he could finish, a snowdrift not far away suddenly shook, cutting Grede off mid-sentence. He whipped around at incredible speed and drew a dagger in one motion, pointing it straight at the snowdrift.

The snowdrift turned around — and turned out to be a furbolg holding a short spear.

Grede awkwardly waggled his dagger. On the surface it looked like he was showing off his reflexes, but it sure as hell looked more like he was covering up the embarrassment of not having noticed the danger…

Vereesa looked at Grede's expression and laughed. "From now on, anyone who mocks me for not being able to see clearly — you can skip that, Master Hunter of the Hinterlands."

The dwarf sheathed his dagger. "I never said I was leading the party in scouting or evading anything. You took on that responsibility, so do it properly."

"Then let the professionals handle professional work," Vereesa said softly.

"Are you from the Hinterlands, Grede?" Sora asked curiously.

"Aye — Aerie Peak, Thunderfist clan!" Grede said.

"Oh, I remember now. You said you wanted to twist my head off and take it to the Hinterlands as a decoration," Sora said, nodding.

Grede quickly put on an apologetic smile. "I was just worried you weren't a good person back then, Sora. Don't take those words to heart. I was really anxious about finding Mosa, so I'm apologizing to you here."

"It's fine, I was about to forget," Sora said softly.

Vereesa edged closer to Jace and whispered, "Who knows whether she really forgot or is just pretending. Are green dragons all this good-natured?"

"I don't know. I don't know any other green dragons," Jace said quietly.

"But how do you know about a place like the Hinterlands?" Grede asked, still curious.

Sora said, "The Emerald Dream isn't the same place as Azeroth, but it's not completely separate either. You could say… a lot of things in the real world have corresponding prototypes in the Dream. Take the Hinterlands, for example. I've been there in the Dream. I know what it like. Does that count as me having been to the Hinterlands?"

"Of course it counts," Grede said, immediately excited. He moved closer to Sora and asked, "If you walked to the Hinterlands in a dream and then left the Dream, would you appear directly in the forests of the Hinterlands?"

"I wouldn't dare try that there, but I suspect it's quite possible," Sora nodded.

"What a shame. Otherwise we might have met and become friends sooner," Grede said. "If I get the chance, I'd really love to walk into the Emerald Dream and take a look."

Actually, in the game, the Hinterlands itself had a "Dream Portal," guarded by the nightmare dragon Lethon. Jace just wasn't sure whether that Dream Portal was open at this point, since he'd never personally gone deep into the Hinterlands mountains to check.

Hearing this, Sora didn't respond. She just lowered her head slightly. Jace knew Ysera didn't want her to return to the Dream, so bringing it up was probably putting her in a bit of a sour mood.
