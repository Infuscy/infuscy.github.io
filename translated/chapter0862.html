Chapter 862: The Frostmaul Tribe's Plight

They passed through the snow pines and reached a sheltered slope. The lead furbolg pointed at several boulders high on the slope and said something. Sola explained that this was the furbolgs' den.

Jace glanced back at the area. Compared to the massive number of furbolgs around Timbermaw Hold, the furbolgs here were few and far between. Not to mention that beneath Timbermaw Hold there were several large furbolg camps, each one livelier than the area around this cave.

There were still furbolgs hauling corpses out of the cave. Jace examined the bodies — most of them looked similar to the furbolgs who'd been infected earlier. Their open eyes were extremely clouded, their expressions agonized, and they'd all been pierced through the heart and head with spears.

It was easy to imagine what this furbolg tribe had been through to be reduced to this state.

At the cave entrance, a grey-skinned Frostmaul furbolg was leaning on a wooden staff, waiting. From his posture alone, it was clear he was very old.

Jace and the others exchanged glances. Vereesa spoke up directly in Elvish. "Greetings, elder."

The old furbolg nodded. "You're a rather unusual-looking Kaldorei, but I can still tell you're a Kaldorei. It's been a long time since a Kaldorei came here. But I'm not the elder — the elder died of the illness a few years ago. We haven't found a successor yet. I was his student, but my body isn't up to the task."

The furbolg's Elvish was halting, but at least it was understandable. He continued, "The sentries told me you helped us hold off the giants, and that you brought medicine all the way from the other side of Mount Hyjal. I know how difficult that must be, so I'm grateful."

"Why did the giants attack you?" Jace asked.

"They were afraid we'd spread the curse to the outside… Actually, those giants are kind-hearted too," the old furbolg said helplessly.

As he spoke, he took the bead necklace from his neck, removed one bead, and handed it to Vereesa. "Take this. Give it to Timbermaw Hold. Tell them the Frostmaul are grateful for the Timbermaw chieftain's protection — we won't forget. Please, come in. We'll treat you to the best food we have to offer."

The furbolg pointed into the cave with his long arm. Grede, who understood not a word of Elvish, perked up immediately and couldn't help asking, "Does that mean it's time to eat?"

"Sounds like it," Vereesa said, taking the bead.

"Brilliant," Grede said, rubbing his hands together.

The old furbolg led them into the cave. It was nowhere near as massive as Timbermaw Hold — the tunnels were narrow, there weren't many branching paths, and not many furbolgs lived inside.

Many furbolgs came up close to Sola and sniffed her carefully, then bowed their heads respectfully. Some of the guards even prostrated themselves on the ground.

They'd probably heard that a green dragon had arrived at the Frostmaul settlement, so they hurried over to get a look. Just like the furbolgs at Timbermaw Hold, they worshipped these emerald guardians of the forest.

The old furbolg scolded the young furbolgs crowding around in furbolg tongue, probably telling them not to be rude to the guests. Then, as he walked, he continued, "I hope you'll eat your fill and enjoy our food. But after you eat, you need to leave quickly. We don't know if this disease can infect outsiders, but all the animals living in the Frostwhisper Gorge are no longer pure. Some carry the plague — they haven't died, but they've become even more savage. The demons in the valley have cursed everything."

Demons in the valley?

Jace's alertness spiked.

"Why don't you leave here?" Sola asked.

"This sacred valley protected our ancestors, honored green dragon," the old furbolg explained. "Thousands of years ago, during the war between the satyrs and the Kaldorei, the furbolgs were targeted for retaliation by demons for helping the Kaldorei. If not for this sacred valley, the Frostmaul furbolgs would have been hunted to extinction by the satyrs long ago. Now that this valley faces its trial by those demons, the furbolgs will repay its protection and defend it to the last. This time, no Frostmaul will choose to flee."

Ulthalesh was a soul-imprisoning demonic artifact forged by Sargeras himself. Its original power came from the soul of a powerful dreadlord deep within the Twisting Nether. So if its curse was a demonic curse… that actually made a certain kind of sense.

But Jace knew the furbolgs couldn't possibly know the origins of Ulthalesh, and they wouldn't conclude the curse in the valley was demonic for that reason.

From the old furbolg's tone, it was clear they believed the satyrs had returned, looking to finish off the Frostmaul furbolgs they hadn't managed to kill during the War of the Satyr.

"Have you seen any satyrs in the valley?" Jace asked.

"No," the old furbolg said. "Satyrs are skilled at hiding their corrupted forms. That's why the Kaldorei can never wipe them out. But we're not afraid of them…"

Jace carefully swept the area with Sense Demons. Aside from Morophil, who was hiding outside watching them, and the faint shadow of Sazeno flickering in his pack, there were no other traces of demons.

At least in this valley and the surrounding area, the satyrs hadn't opened a dream rift like the one in Kel'Theril to interfere with Jace's demon detection.

"As long as they try to leave that dark temple to commit evil, they'll reveal themselves. Then we can use our strength to kill them. The satyrs aren't as terrible as the Kaldorei make them out to be, outsider." The old furbolg's tone grew heated. He seemed to think Jace was doubting the Frostmaul furbolgs' judgment about the enemy in the valley.

But Jace was very interested in the dark temple he'd mentioned. "What is this dark temple? A satyr nest?"

The old furbolg explained slowly, "Long ago, evil satyrs built that cursed temple with magic in the valley… At first we thought it was a shrine built by wandering ancient Kaldorei. Then, several decades ago, it was discovered that the temple had already been opened. Evil had long since seeped into the forest and the valley. Every tribesman who went there to explore was cursed into an undead. At first they didn't realize they'd been cursed — it was hidden and hard to detect. By the time they returned to our camp, they would spread that strange disease, Ulbergarvalk — turning people into toxic, soulless walking corpses."

He led them in front of a chamber. From far off, Jace had already smelled the aroma of simmering meat broth and fruit. But listening to the furbolgs describe the disaster they were facing, the appetite that had been whetted had faded away.

Grede couldn't understand what they were saying, but by now even he could sense the severity of the problem from the expressions on Vereesa, Jace, and Sola's faces. So he'd lost his earlier enthusiasm too, and despite his stomach growling, he had no interest in charging in to eat.

The old furbolg stood at the entrance and continued, "This disease once spread far, all the way to Timbermaw Hold. But the Timbermaw furbolgs were able to find a cure for it in Ashenvale — that medicine. So they were cured. We're grateful to them. Without that medicine, we would have been wiped out long ago. So we won't leave here and bring disaster to them again."

After the Battle of Mount Hyjal, the demons who came here had destroyed everything related to this valley. If Jace hadn't come here searching for Ulthalesh, the story of the Frostmaul furbolgs might never have been known to outsiders.

Once they left here, he would definitely record what happened here and spread the word. At the very least, he had to tell Malin.

"Where is this dark temple?" Jace asked.

"You want to go deeper into the valley?" the old furbolg said. "That's far too dangerous."

"Actually, we came here specifically to investigate the demons," Vereesa said.

The old furbolg was stunned for a moment. His frozen bear face didn't show much expression, but Jace could feel his emotions were completely unguarded — pure shock and bewilderment.

"Don't throw your lives away, Kaldorei. Ancient evil is not something to be trifled with…" the old furbolg said in a low voice.

"We've come a long way to get here, and we're very close to our goal," Vereesa said. "We wouldn't be able to live with ourselves if we left without taking a look."

The old furbolg fell silent for a while. Understanding Vereesa's Elvish seemed to take some effort. Finally, he shook his head. "Actually, I'm not sure of its exact location either. The valley is extremely complex, and the temple itself is even more hidden. The demons definitely don't want anyone to find its precise location."

Hearing the old furbolg say this, Jace grew more and more suspicious that this so-called dark temple was the place where the Guardian Scavell had hidden Ulthalesh eight hundred years ago.

The Frostmaul furbolgs had hidden here to escape the satyrs' pursuit, so when they realized evil had appeared in the valley again, it was natural for them to assume the satyrs had tracked them here.

If that was the case, Guardian Scavell had thought nothing lived here, and that it was remote enough — and that it was at the foot of Mount Hyjal, a place demons wouldn't dare touch. So hiding Ulthalesh here made sense. But this choice had inadvertently brought misery to the furbolgs who had lived here in seclusion for God knew how long.

Then how had this temple been broken into?

One of the furbolg guards who had followed them whispered something to the old furbolg. The old furbolg panted a few breaths, then looked at them and said, "We have an experienced hunter… He knows how to find that temple. If you trust him, he can lead you there."

Hearing this, Jace's eyes lit up. If that was the case, their group could further build their speed advantage over those Dark Riders.

The old furbolg continued, "Protect our hunter. He's one of our best, outsiders. This is our thanks to you for bringing us the medicine, but we don't want him to die for nothing."

"We'll do everything we can to protect him," Vereesa nodded.
