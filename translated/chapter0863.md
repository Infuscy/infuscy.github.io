## Chapter 863: Shadows in the Forest

After eating their fill at the furbolgs', Jace and the others set out on their journey under the guidance of the furbolg hunter, heading deeper into the Dark Ravine.

The food the furbolgs had stored was actually quite plentiful — plenty of dried meat, fruit frozen solid like ice cream, and a fragrant stew of vegetables and meat. There wasn't much in the way of spices, and the flavors were very plain — either salty as hell or sweet as hell.

But then, Jace's group was either blessed folk with strong constitutions, a dwarf who'd merged with an earthen, or a genuine dragon. Vereesa, who was used to trying all kinds of food, was actually the most ordinary one among them.

So for them, the furbolgs' food was nutritious and energy-rich. After resting in the cave for a bit, they set out the next day before dawn under the furbolg hunter's lead.

Before leaving, Jace warned the furbolgs about a group of ill-intentioned black knights heading their way. He described the demonic sword he carried, Apocalypse, in as much detail as he could, and convinced them not to go looking for these outside intruders — otherwise a lot of innocent furbolgs might die.

Maybe because Jace himself was quite wary of that sword, his description was rather terrifying, and it left the furbolgs a little unsettled. However, upon learning that the black knights had also come here searching for that curse, the furbolgs relaxed slightly.

What they feared most was that these outsiders were trying to destroy the valley. Since the evil outsiders and the evil in the valley were going to deal with each other, they were happy to watch the show.

Before leaving, Jace glanced back at the Frostmace furbolgs' dwelling. It looked fairly well hidden.

And judging by the direction the guide furbolg hunter had indicated for the dark temple, the path from the outside ancient Kaldorei bridge to the temple was quite far from the furbolgs' dwelling. So unless the black knights were specifically looking for trouble with the furbolgs, the two sides probably wouldn't cross paths. Only then did he set off with peace of mind.

The furbolg hunter's name was Kenluk. As a relatively young Frostmace furbolg, his white fur was extremely thick, and his whole build was broad and robust. He stood roughly two-point-three to two-point-four meters tall, by rough estimate.

More importantly, he knew some Thalassian, which was no small feat in this remote Frostmace Valley.

After all, even their leader — that old furbolg — had Thalassian skills about on par with Jace's, and probably not even as good as Jace's current level.

As they walked, Kenluk carried a short wooden spear, the tip of which appeared to be an elven arrowhead or a small elven spearhead. His arm never seemed to tire.

Despite being an experienced hunter, he still swayed when he walked, as if his movements weren't very coordinated.

But no one in the group dared underestimate his strength. His thick neck and shoulders were hung with many feathers, bone fragments, and other ornaments, and he even wore a grey bead necklace. This represented the young hunter's very high status within the Frostmace tribe. He likely possessed a fighting strength that wasn't apparent on the surface.

Mainly because, on the surface, he really did look quite dim.

"What… are you going to the temple for?"

Kenluk walked along, turning his head to look back as he asked.

"The calamity that threatens you threatens us too, Kenluk," Vereesa replied. "So we intend to eliminate the threat at its source."

"I don't believe you can do it," Kenluk said.

Vereesa said unhappily, "We did you a huge favor, and this is how little you think of us?"

"I just don't believe it. No insult intended," Kenluk said, one word at a time.

Grede seemed to detect a hint of tension in the exchange. He sighed and said, "I really should learn more Thalassian."

"You definitely wouldn't want to hear what this guy's saying, Grede," Vereesa told him. "He thinks we can't handle that curse."

"What?!" Grede bristled his beard.

"Most furbolgs are straightforward," Sora explained. "I don't think he's deliberately looking down on us, Vereesa. He's a hunter. He's probably been through a lot of pain because of that dark temple — illness, even the death of friends and family. For their tribe, it's been decades, even centuries, without breaking free of that temple's curse. It's only natural that they don't believe it could suddenly vanish because of a group of outsiders."

"Quite convincing," Vereesa shrugged.

"But I hope you can do it. The other tribespeople hope so too," the furbolg muttered in Thalassian, then continued his clumsy walk forward.

Sora smiled, glanced at Vereesa, and Vereesa nodded. It seemed the furbolg really did say exactly what he thought.

Jace hadn't paid much attention to their conversation. He'd been staring at the snow pine forest the whole time, trying to find something connected to the clues he'd uncovered earlier.

But whether it was the "deep green high wall" or the "murmuring" in the forest — there was nothing. Gradually, he'd started to suspect that SI:7's intelligence might be flawed.

But deep down, he really didn't want to reach this point only to realize the intelligence had gone wrong, because he'd relied almost entirely on SI:7's investigation of Winterspring to get here. If this intelligence was flawed from the source, then all the difficulties and trouble along the way would have been a waste of time, wouldn't they?

Back in Stormwind, he probably should have had a thorough chat with Mathias Shaw about this. But Winterspring was a fairly important investigation project for SI:7. If he brought it up with Mathias, the man might try to investigate why Jace was heading to Winterspring, and then the matter of Ursalias would be hard to keep under wraps.

But the fact that the black knights had already tracked them here was proof enough. They couldn't have come here for anything else, could they?

By evening, the forest had grown dark and the air was getting colder. Even wrapped tightly in his black dragonhide cloak, Jace felt the chill seeping through his clothes. Vereesa also noticed the abnormal temperature drop. She glanced back at them, and Jace knew what she meant — she suspected there were ghosts nearby.

Jace pulled out the witch doctor's ghost detection potion from his pack. He glanced at a broken wall buried in the bushes not far away, then stopped Grede beside him. "Let's drink this potion before we go any further."

The dwarf stopped and was just about to speak when Kenluk, at the front, suddenly made a gurgling sound and raised his unoccupied arm, signaling everyone to be quiet.

Grede's mouth hung half open. He exhaled a white breath, then slowly closed his mouth.

Jace shoved the troll's potion back into his pack and reached for the hilt of Azuresong at his waist. But he looked back and forth through the forest and couldn't see a thing.

What was going on?

Vereesa held her bow and scanned around. It was clear she sensed something too, but couldn't pin it down either.

Jace wanted to ask, but everyone was silent at this moment, and he worried his voice might attract something that shouldn't be attracted.

Just as the tension was building to the point of near-stillness, Kenluk suddenly said in Thalassian, "Careful… careful." His tone was hesitant, as if he wasn't sure what was approaching either.

"He seems to have spotted an enemy," Jace translated for Grede beside him in Common. The dwarf immediately widened his eyes. "Where? Where? Where?"

"We can't see it," the furbolg muttered, turning his head. "We need to leave this place slowly… We can't provoke it."

"Can't see it?" Jace frowned. "A ghost? Should we drink the potion?"

Vereesa nodded and was about to walk over when Sora stopped her, speaking in a voice that was barely audible. "It's not a ghost, but it may not have noticed us yet."

"Then do you know what it is?" Jace asked in surprise.

Sora nodded, said a word in Thalassian, then raised a finger to her lips and looked up at the sky.

Her expression didn't look like she'd encountered an enemy. It was more like she'd come across some timid little animal. Except Jace had no idea what Sora had said, and Vereesa didn't seem to get it either.

But seeing Sora look up, they followed her gaze upward. Could it be something that flies?

Yet even though Sora didn't seem to care much, a powerful sense of fear radiated from the furbolg. Jace could feel it clearly.

Despite the furbolg remaining calm on the surface, gripping his long spear and ready for battle, he was enormous — there was no reason he should be this terrified.

What the hell was it?

Right then, a warm wind drifted over against the ice and snow. Jace felt it distinctly. He glanced in the direction the wind was coming from but sensed nothing at all.

What's more, this warm wind hadn't immediately been frozen into a cold wind. That unnaturalness made Jace extremely uneasy.

And he caught a whiff of a hard-to-describe discordant smell in the wind — like the off-putting stench of rotting food, though it wasn't easy to just call it "stink." It inexplicably brought to mind rotting corpses — especially for someone like Jace, who had seen no small number of them.

He wasn't the only one who smelled it. The furbolg clearly smelled it too. He couldn't keep his composure any longer, his teeth chattering loudly.

Then Jace sensed something unusual sweep past the heights nearby, but there was no actual image. Was this the invisible creature the furbolg had mentioned?

He immediately tried to scan the area with Sense Demons, but found nothing at all.

At that moment, a more violent warm wind suddenly swept through the forest, carrying a cloud of dark yellow mist that rushed toward them. Sora's eyes locked onto it immediately. "No… careful, this one's different!"
