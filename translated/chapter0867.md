## Chapter 867: The Black Knights

They'd spent so long inside the Pendant's Dream that it was becoming hard to tell where the dream ended and reality began.

In the deep, blurred realm, the gloomy cellar hall and the dim forest overlapped, as if they stood in the garden of a fallen forest god — walls crawling with malicious plants, a canopy too thick to see through, sunlight that seemed to filter through and yet didn't. Though the space was vast, it felt almost suffocating.

Time ticked by. Jace didn't want to look up at the sky anymore. Without the Will-Chain, he felt the oppression here growing heavier by the minute.

Deep, fearful emotions quickly gripped his heart. He started to worry that Sora might encounter some unexpected danger in this dream fragment that they hadn't seen in the Pendant's Dream.

It was no accident that this dream was filled with evil necromantic things. Judging by the old furbolg's description, this underground vault had been out of control for decades, perhaps even a century. Which meant the corruption of the primordial forest in the Dream by Ursalias would be no less than that.

He knew this was the moment he most needed to push out the negative emotions. Sora was a green dragon — a powerful green dragon who had once broken free from nightmare corruption and now wore the Will-Chain. Even though she was just a whelp… the severity of the disasters she'd endured probably exceeded that of many older adult dragons.

The Dream was the green dragons' domain. Even if the rest of them went in, they probably wouldn't be able to help much.

Grede, on the other hand, kept looking up at the sky, as if hoping Sora would suddenly appear and land on top of that staff. But the forest in the Dream remained quiet. Nothing was happening at all.

After the moonlight, Sora had vanished like a drop falling into a river. A long time had passed since then, and now even the ripples were no longer visible. Jace glanced at the bone-like longstaff planted in the pedestal, then lowered his head again.

If this wasn't Ursalias, then what was it?

Jace still had an impression of what Ursaliais looked like in the game — a curved handle wrapped with red bindings, a broad sickle blade on top that lived up to its name, "Reaper Against the Wind."

Then what about this one in front of him?

He couldn't recall anything similar in his memory. If he had to name one… it was probably quite similar to that Shadowflame Staff that dropped from Nefarian. But that staff was straight, and its head was a black dragon's skull rather than a demon's horned head.

Another one that was somewhat similar was the Headmaster's Staff. No one knew where the Headmaster's Staff came from, and it was indeed closely tied to necromantic power. But the Headmaster's Staff had many curved rib-like protrusions on its bone handle. The other problem was the same — it was straight too.

"She's been in there too long, Jace," Grede said with some unease. "Could something have gone wrong?"

"Don't forget what she said." Vereesa stood with her arms folded, leaning against the wall. Her earring still radiated silver light, sustaining the entire hazy dream. She continued, "She said it herself — entering the Dream from the real world can't land you precisely at the corresponding location in the Dream. What we need right now is patience."

"All right, it's just that this place is too oppressive. Staying here too long makes people uncomfortable."

Grede moved aside. Beside him, Kenluk had been pacing in place the whole time. After hearing Vereesa's words, he was already somewhat annoyed. He pointed at the furbolg and said in Thalassian, "Enough. You're annoying."

The furbolg, called out by the dwarf, sniffed twice, hefted his spear, and shuffled aside to sit down. Jace looked at Grede. "So it turns out you do know a few words of Thalassian after all."

"Spend a few months with an elf and you pick up the simple stuff," Grede said, tilting his head. "Those didn't sound all that simple to me," Vereesa said, unable to help laughing.

Grede and Jace laughed too. But the lighthearted atmosphere didn't even last until their smiles faded. Vereesa suddenly raised her eyes and locked onto the space above.

Jace noticed the change in her expression. "What is it?" Molofel appeared from the doorway. "They're here."

"Who's here?" Grede asked.

"Who else?" Jace said, standing up. "Damn it. Why so fast?"

He shoved Azuresong back into its scabbard. The whole room immediately dimmed, leaving only the faint sunlight filtering down from the sky within the Pendant's Dream, giving the place just enough visibility.

"Should we take back the pendant's power?" Vereesa asked.

"No," Jace said. "Let them come in and see the shadow of that staff."

"What if they figure out this is the power of a green dragon's earring?" Grede asked. "Who told them?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "They can get information straight out of Karazhan…"

"You think Medivh knows exactly what the Dragonqueen has hanging on her?" Jace said softly, gazing at the dream's tree trunks, bushes, and shadows. "We'll rely on the power of that earring to hide ourselves and let them walk in on their own. Also, drink whatever combat potions you have now. The black knights should all be undead, and they use shadow magic — Shadow Protection Potions will definitely come in handy. And Consecrated Oil."

"I know," Vereesa said.

They each drank the witch doctor's ghost detection potion — who knew what strange magic these black knights had at their disposal. Grede opened his pack and took out the Consecrated Oil, smearing it onto his hammer. Jace tossed a Shadow Protection Potion to the furbolg and said in Thalassian, "Hide."

Kenluk grunted an "Mmh," swallowed the whole bottle in one gulp, crushed it in his mouth, and swayed over to a corner with his short spear.

Jace glanced at the broken glass shards on the ground. "Once I've got the leader restrained, attack immediately. Use everything you've got. Kill as many of those black knights as possible."

Everyone nodded. Grede braced his shield and hid behind a fallen arcane golem. Jace pulled his hood up and sat down behind a collapsed section of wall in a corner on the other side.

Vereesa walked to the deepest part of the hall and slowly crouched down. She blended most perfectly into her own hazy dream. Even before she pulled her hood down to hide the arcane fire in her eyes, Jace could already barely make out her form.

Although Molofel had difficulty turning invisible in this Dream, when she reined in her power and the flames on her body died down, her dark red form hiding in the lightless corner of the room was also hard to detect with the naked eye.

What's more, Ysera's pendant had twisted the magical energies throughout the room. The black knights might detect the magic outside the vault, but once they were inside, sensing the presence of the few of them would be extremely difficult.

Jace remembered there were five or six of these black knights in total. Aside from the leader who carried Apocalypse, the rest shouldn't be much to worry about.

He rummaged through his potion bag. Only after drinking the Shadow Protection Potion did he notice the Distilled Wisdom elixir inside.

He could have sworn he'd already drunk it on the airship when fighting the black dragons. But thinking back, when would he have had the chance to drink a potion in that situation? He hadn't even had time to drink a healing potion.

He smeared Consecrated Oil on Azuresong, shoved the cap back on, and shook the demon pack at his waist. "Curse of Tongues, Sazeno. Quick."

"Yes, master…"

The imp popped out in a panic, chanting the Curse of Tongues spell. After he finished, he trembled and said, "It's coming… it's coming, master. Apocalypse…"

"Shut up!" Jace hissed, stuffing him back in. His throat began to burn intensely, and a surge of furious flame welled up in his heart. He wanted nothing more than to fight the dark-clad figures about to enter this hall to the death.

He knew this was the fel corruption making him lightheaded.

The Will-Chain was gone. He had to rely on himself to resist the enslavement of these magical energies.

But at this moment, the Will-Chain was far more important to Sora than to him. She needed to pass through the Dream and face everything that had once corrupted her — and take that staff.

If that staff really was Ursalias…

His throat had just started to feel better when the sound of iron boots stomping the ground rumbled up. The noise grew close to the doorway, then suddenly went still.

Had they noticed something was off about the Dream? "I see it."

"Yes, that satyr deceived us, Eredin."

A voice suddenly came from behind the door. Jace could even detect a Lordaeron accent in those few sentences, which made him extremely uncomfortable. "Why did he deceive us?" a deep voice asked.

"Tristan Dreadhoof wanted to make himself useful so he could gain entry to Karazhan. That's obvious…"

Tristan Dreadhoof?

This was the satyr who had died in the Kaynethril dream — the deputy of Prince Savariss.

In the game, he hid in a library deep within Karazhan. No one knew what he was planning in there.

So Tristan Dreadhoof had traded the clue to Ursalias in exchange for the black knights' granting him entry to Karazhan? That was quite a surprise to Jace.

Another unfamiliar voice said, "We don't need the satyrs' help. Ursalias is right there, Eredin."

"But Ursalias doesn't look the same as the Eredar's records described," another black knight said.

"We just need to take it back and hand it over to our master. The rest is for our master to judge," the deep voice said. "To our master," the black knights said in unison.

No sooner had the words faded than the clang of metal came even more vividly — almost as if passing right behind him. Jace felt a sudden, crushing fear grip his neck.

Without the Will-Chain on him, Apocalypse was even more powerful than he'd imagined.

He even worried a little that the others might be crushed by this force of fear, unable to lift their heads.

But considering one was a Mountain King, one was a Ranger-Lord, and one was a pure demon, right now it was probably they who should be worried about him.

And then there was a furbolg — Kenluk. Although he'd seemed a bit panicked when dealing with the faerie dragon, the courage he'd shown outside Skavel's vault was worthy of his reputation as a veteran Frostmace hunter.

One by one, black shadows were cast upon the wall by the dim sunlight in the Dream. Jace watched them step closer and closer to the hazy longstaff. The lead black knight arrived in front of the pedestal. The atmosphere grew heavy.

He reached out, attempting to touch the staff. Jace could even hear the sound of his heavy robe and the metal plates of his gauntlets rubbing together. Then came a faint…

"Hm?"

The black knight's fingers passed right through the staff.
