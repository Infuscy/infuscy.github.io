Chapter 876: The Timbermaw's Gratitude

The road from Timbermaw Hold to Winterspring had been full of hardships when they'd walked it.

Cold, hunger, getting lost in the forest — not to mention that suddenly appearing, powerful frostwind chimera.

But now that they had Sora — no, at this point it should be Somial — skimming the tops of the giant pines, passing the edge of the massive roots that Nordrassiil stretched down the mountain, and soaring between cliffs and ravines, it was as easy as a sightseeing trip.

Her long body moved gracefully through the sky like a serpent, gliding on outstretched wings. Sunlight spilled across her scales, shimmering with an emerald luster.

Jace lay on his thick cloak. Against his neck, he could feel that the scales below him weren't cold at all — they were even slightly warm.

Grede was excitedly telling Somial about his glorious history of battling trolls in the Hinterlands. Jace listened quietly on the side, then secretly compared the version Grede was telling at this moment with the version he'd told him in the past, to see what was different.

There were actually far too many differences. It got more and more exaggerated as he went, until he could only look for parts that were roughly similar.

Mosa, in her great cat form, sat beside them. She, who should have been the one most enjoying Grede's boasting, didn't have any energy to spare for saving her lover's face at all.

She was clinging to the scales on Somial's back, her whole body tense. She was definitely terrified of flying. So she hadn't made a sound the whole way. But she didn't dare extend her claws to grip more firmly, afraid of hurting the green dragon's skin beneath the scales.

"I didn't expect a tauren who'd lived in the Thousand Needles for so long to be afraid of heights," Jace teased. Mosa growled in the great cat's voice. "Mountain… sky… different…"

Jace said, "I heard there are druids who can turn into crows or hawks and fly high into the sky, Mosa. If you're this afraid of heights, how are you going to learn that shapeshifting ability?"

"I… only focus on… cat shapeshifting…" Mosa spoke these few words with difficulty through the great cat's mouth, then shook her head, as if she couldn't care less about the ability to fly in shifted form.

Jace smiled and raised his voice. "Sora, why don't you just carry us across the Great Sea? That way we won't have to fly to Gadgetzan to wait for a ship…"

"I don't know if I can do that, Jace," Somial said earnestly. "Even in the Dream, I've never flown that far."

"I'm just joking," Jace smiled. "Sora, don't take it seriously. I just feel too comfortable on your back. The thought of having to take a ship back in a little while is already making me feel seasick…"

Somial let out a low growl — half laugh, half complaint.

Jace rarely heard such a friendly tone in a whelp's throat, since almost all the ones he'd encountered before were black dragons and crazed red whelps — either hysterical shrieks or death throes.

"That tone of yours doesn't sound like you're joking at all, Jace," Grede said. "Even a dragon can't fly across the Great Sea, can it?"

Vereesa said, "Some in Dalaran say that black dragons carried orcs across the Great Sea, sneaking them from the Dark Portal up north to Dalaran, and even brought them to Kalimdor."

Jace knew this group of black dragons who'd carried orcs to evade Alliance pursuit. They were led by Sabellian, Deathwing's son — that is, the brother of Nefarian and Onyxia.

After Deathwing had entered the Dark Portal, he'd also followed through to Outland. He should still be there now.

In the future, if they really could use Kaldrez to open a portal to Outland after locking him in the mage tower, they would very likely face the threat of the Black Dragonflight led by Sabellian once they went in.

Still, he probably didn't know the news of Deathwing's death yet. After all, there were currently no channels of communication between Azeroth and Draenor.

"Black dragons are simply everywhere," Grede looked over. "Those black dragons we ran into in the swamp — they said that was their lair. If these dragons wanted to go support their kin on another continent to do evil, they definitely wouldn't take a goblin ship. So they must have flown across the entire Great Sea too! Of course, Sora, I'm not actually asking you to fly across the Great Sea. I just mean those black dragons… they'll do anything to go against us."

"I can try," Somial said. "Flying north from here, crossing a not-too-wide strait, you can reach another frozen continent. There are mortals living there too. Flying east along that continent's coast would be much safer. There are places to rest when you're tired. When you reach the eastern coast of the continent, crossing one more narrow sea will bring you to your homeland, Jace — Lordaeron."

"Northrend," Vereesa said. "I've never been there."

"Neither have I," Grede murmured. "I only heard that Muradin Bronzebeard mastered the power of the Earthen on that continent."

"Would it be too tiring, Sora?" Jace asked.

"No," Somial said. "I get to see new and exciting things every day here, and carrying you all on this trip doesn't bore me at all. Yesterday Sazeno told me a lot about the Twisting Nether. What makes me ashamed is that I had never considered what kind of fate demons face when they die, the depths of the Twisting Nether, Argus…"

"Brilliant, anyway I don't want to walk through the desert again!" Grede shouted. Vereesa also nodded happily.

Only Jace gazed at the mountain range ahead with a complicated expression. After all, they would have to pass through Northrend… It was still early before the Scourge invasion. Even if Ner'zhul had already fallen to Icecrown, he probably hadn't had time to spread the plague across the entire continent yet. The southern coast should still be safe.

They flew over range after range of snow-covered peaks. The snow blanketing the forests and ridges grew thinner and thinner. Somial descended among a grove of pine trees. Shadows began to appear on the ground, pacing closer. As they got closer to the ground, they could see clearly — they were Timbermaw furbolgs.

The furbolgs paid their respects to the green dragon in their own language. Jace looked toward the distant hillside to the east and could still faintly see the entrance leading into Timbermaw Hold's cave.

Before long, the green dragon, sitting steadily in the forest clearing, was surrounded by all kinds of fruit and grain. There were even small ornaments and little gifts. The furbolgs carefully set down their offerings, said a simple prayer, and then slipped away. The sight of these fierce-looking, robust half-beast, half-human creatures being so careful not to offend the green dragon was both endearing and amusing.

Faced with the furbolgs' generous gifts, the green dragon looked this way and that, at a complete loss.

The furbolg warrior leader — Kruton, who had led the Timbermaw furbolgs in ambushing the sentries and Jace's party — squeezed his way through the crowd of furbolgs with his tall frame. Seeing Jace and the others, he said excitedly in Thalassian, "The elder said you would return, and that you would bring news from Winterspring. The elements have spread the good news throughout the north. Now you've really returned — and you've brought the blessing of the Emerald Dragonflight!"

Somial hesitated for a moment and said in Common in a low voice to her companions beside her, "I don't know how to… give a blessing."

Grede patted the green dragon's front leg. "How hard can a blessing be? Just say something nice."

"Uhh…" Somial awkwardly swiveled her head, looking up at the heights, then into the deep forest in the distance. For a moment, she simply didn't know what to say.

Right then, a familiar furbolg figure squeezed over. It was the furbolg herbalist. Although it was hard to distinguish one furbolg's appearance from another, this one was short and small, with a coat of light-colored fur, and wore all kinds of herbs and flowers on him — so he was quite easy to recognize.

Jace had just been wondering how they were going to find that pharmacy in the Timbermaw camp. Now things were conveniently taken care of.

"Did you deliver it? The medicine for that disease — Ulvargarvarg!" the furbolg herbalist squeezed in and asked.

"Of course." Jace took the Frostmace tribe's bead from his bosom and handed it to the herbalist.

"That's right, that's right, you really delivered it!" the herbalist said excitedly. "Come with me, come with me!"

"You stay here and bless them," Jace said, looking at the green dragon. "We have something to do over there."

"Don't…" Sorial lowered her head. "Don't leave me here."

Jace said helplessly, "We don't know how to give a blessing either, Sora…"

Then the group left the green dragon there anyway. They followed the herbalist through the crowd of furbolgs who were excitedly watching the green dragon, crossed a small river, and arrived at the herbalist's hut on the opposite bank.

The herbalist opened the door, waving his two long arms as he spoke. "To thank you, the elder has allowed me to teach you a formula, honored outsiders. This is a great potion passed down by the Timbermaw from ancient times. I myself have never successfully completed it a second time, because the materials are extremely precious and hard to come by."

"To complete this potion, you need blood grass grown from the fel blood left behind by a demon slain by the great Ursoc during the War of the Ancients. Then use a kind of black lotus to neutralize its evil and filth, leaving behind pure power. The methods for finding the materials and the process of making it are all here."

The herbalist took out what appeared to be a scroll made of deerskin and handed it to Jace. Jace took it and looked — it was covered densely with furbolg writing that looked like little drawings.

But Sora knew furbolg language, so translating this shouldn't be too difficult. "What does it do?" Jace asked.

"After drinking it, you can possess the great strength and resilience of Ursoc! Fight invincible, just like the great Ursoc!" the herbalist said excitedly.

"Sounds like something Grede would like," Vereesa whispered in Common. "I wouldn't want to become as heavy as a bear."

"What bear?" Grede looked blankly at Jace and Vereesa. "What heavy?"

The dwarf and the elf discussed the potion's effects with the furbolg. Jace looked at the completely illegible writing on the scroll and thought — blood grass grown from the blood left behind by a demon Ursoc killed during the War of the Ancients. Wasn't that requirement a bit too narrow?

And Ursoc's battle with the demons was ten thousand years ago. How would they even know by now… "Do you have this blood grass here?" Jace asked.

The furbolg shook his head. "Very precious."

Jace scratched the curly stubble at the corner of his mouth. He thought he'd translate it and bring it back to show Ursula — maybe she could come up with some other solution.
