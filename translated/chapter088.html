Chapter 88: The One from the Abyss

"Hey! Can you hear me?"

No response.

"Who are you?"

Silence again.

Now Jace felt something was off. Did everyone connected to this fishing village just not like answering questions?

That song — he could more or less follow the meaning. Could this woman be the soldier's wife?

His instincts told him to step back. If this were Earth, he'd have charged straight in to pull her out without a second thought.

A woman squatting at the edge of the tide, staring motionless at the sea — chances were she was trying to kill herself.

But this was Azeroth… and things weren't so simple.

What if she was a ghost? Azeroth… did actually have ghosts.

If she turned around right now with a face straight out of The Grudge, Jace would be getting hit with a Fear spell for real.

Just then, the singing trailed off.

Jace was already getting a bad feeling. He took two steps back, then two more. Better to mind his own business.

It had gotten eerie enough that even if he left her here and she really did get dragged off by murlocs or something afterward, Jace figured he'd done his part — she was the one who'd ignored him, after all.

Right now the survival instinct was overriding any impulse to play the hero.

That was when Jace saw the woman's long golden hair lift slightly, as if caught by a sea breeze.

But in that moment, he hadn't felt the slightest hint of wind.

And the hair, once lifted, never came back down.

Every goosebump on Jace's body stood on end.

That lifted golden hair wasn't golden hair at all! It was fins! Golden fins!

The woman slowly turned her head… A dead-fish-stiff face, skin slick and glistening wet in the moonlight, swollen eyes glowing with arcane light. A bright red tongue shot out from between thin lips and twitched.

Naga!

That singing had never been longing for a husband — it was the enchanted lure used to drag sailors to their deaths!

A jolt ran through Jace's whole body and he turned and ran, half-sprinting, half-leaping toward the houses up the slope. Suddenly he heard the naga mutter something behind him. The quiet of the night made it sound as though she were whispering right into his ear.

Something like "Anunaji…"

Jace felt his lower body go weak. His legs pumped several times but couldn't hold him up, and he collapsed face-first into the sand.

He tried to push himself up, but his arms were too weak even to raise his upper body.

He looked back and saw the naga slowly emerging from the tide, her lower half revealed, her scales shimmering with an eerie phosphorescence in the moonlight.

Her bare upper body was no different from a human's. The graceful lines of her waist and the curve of her chest were the kind you couldn't find the match of among most humans.

But at a life-or-death moment like this, Jace was in no state to appreciate any of it. He knew exactly what kind of debilitating magic he'd been hit with — he could clearly feel the foreign magic invading him, running wild through his muscles and blood vessels, wrecking his senses.

He opened his bag, reached in, and grabbed the Shadow Protection Potion. He wrenched it open and gulped a mouthful, shouting, "Sazeno! Sazeno!"

The moment the potion went down, a burning, achetic fire shot through his chest and into his esophagus, but Jace barely had time to swallow once before he felt strength returning to his arms and legs.

The imp materialized in a flash of arcane light. Facing the pursuing naga sea-witch, he showed no fear at all, still wearing that same strange expression — smug and cocky as ever.

"Had to go and get in over your head again without me, didn't you, Master, hah?" he shouted, even as he swept both hands forward and hurled a ball of flame. The naga, suddenly confronted by this demon, hesitated for a moment, braking hard — her serpentine lower half whipping around and flinging sand across the tidal flat.

Flames suddenly erupted across the naga's whole body. She let out a hiss and cast a hazy incantation; a burst of water erupted from beneath her, drenching her from head to toe — but it failed to put the fire out completely.

"Demon…" the naga snarled in Common. "Get out of Azeroth."

The naga made no further attempt to dispel the imp's Immolate. Instead, she began casting another spell right where she stood. Words tumbled from her lips as her hands released a wave of void energy, and the imp, who'd still been brimming with confidence, panicked.

"Master, she's trying to banish me! Quick — Phase Shift me!"

"Can't you just get out of here yourself!" Jace shouted.

"I can't abandon Master and save my own skin!"

"Phase Shift!"

At Jace's shout, the imp vanished once more in a flash of arcane light.

"Hss!" The naga broke off her casting, and her eyes snapped straight to Jace — except by now Jace had only just managed to unslung the staff from his back.

Seeing that the naga was already turning her attention on him, he turned and bolted, sprinting toward the houses again. Jace figured the naga's body was a massive serpentine form — she definitely couldn't move fast on land. As long as he got out of her casting range and kept running, he'd make it!

The naga began chanting again, and this time Jace heard every word clearly — "Anunaji, Durzala, Kana!"

It didn't quite sound like demonic, but Jace was in no state to work through a question that complicated right now.

He felt his legs give a little. Damn — had that debilitating spell taken hold again?

But after a long moment, his legs just felt somewhat weak. He could still run. He could still sprint with everything he had. The Shadow Protection Potion was working.

As long as he didn't feel the telltale signs that the potion was about to wear off, it meant the naga's shadow magic hadn't broken through the potion's protection — so he didn't need to panic too much!

And yet, only a few seconds later, he heard an extremely shrill ringing — the kind of noise arcane energy could make, but Jace had never heard an arcane sound that urgent and piercing.

A blinding flash erupted behind him, bright as someone shining a searchlight straight at him. Jace glanced back out of curiosity and saw a bolt of arcane energy tear through the air, stabbing straight toward him!

Shit!

Jace threw himself to the side and rolled across the ground. The arcane missile slammed right into the spot he'd been standing, setting off a violent flash and explosion!

Dirt and sand sprayed up and rained across his back. Jace scrambled to his feet, about to run — and his vision was struck by another missile. The violent blast nearly knocked him flat. If he went down at a critical moment like this, he was done for! This was a mage… a powerful mage, quite possibly stronger by an order of magnitude than those posers he'd seen in the Mage Quarter. Jace could feel his own heartbeat pounding so hard he could sense it in his eardrums. He pumped his arms and ran for all he was worth, never mind what was behind him.

He didn't stop until he'd sprinted between a few of the houses. When he finally looked back, the naga was gone.

Where'd she gone?

Jace's gaze darted between the houses. He didn't dare keep running further from the coast now — what if she was waiting for him out there?

What if this naga knew some kind of teleportation spell… if that was the case, was there even a goddamn way out?!

Damn it… nobody said there'd be a naga here?!

Why was there a naga here, of all things?

Or had these murlocs been driven here by the naga? Was their real objective to harass Stormwind?

What the hell was going on.

Jace did his best to throw all this wild speculation out of his head. Right now, killing this naga was priority number one. Everything else was secondary. Kill this naga…

He kept running the incantation for Fear through his mind, ready to hit her with everything the moment she showed herself. He recalled the pointers Master Marin had given him on conserving energy while casting — stay calm, stay focused… Night. The surrounding houses loomed like dark hunched giants, squatting together and watching the pathetic human in the middle of the clearing, and he was the lone gladiator, facing an enemy he couldn't see, not daring to let his guard down for an instant.

Suddenly, a flash of arcane light flared up not far away. Jace felt the shadows and light in that direction start to blur. The imp would never show himself on his own without an order — so whoever was appearing had to be…
