## Chapter 880: The Highborne Artifact

"This does indeed look like a misunderstanding, everyone." Azuregos's tone settled considerably. His voice sounded like a middle-aged man, not a beast attempting to speak like a human.

Recalling all the dragon voices he'd heard back in Grim Batol, they all seemed to share this trait. But whether it was a black hatchling or Sulenna, they all carried a reptilian rasp when they spoke — nothing as clear as this.

The blizzard calmed rapidly, and the sky returned to clarity. But the entire ruins were already blanketed in snow, ice walls standing everywhere, crystal-cluster-like ice spikes jutting from the earth, reflecting the morning sunlight that had just begun to rise on the horizon.

It all made you feel as if you'd been transported back to Winterspring — except the cliffs in the distance and the tall red pines still held to their original forms. The contrast gave Jace the illusion of standing inside a deliberately constructed winter set.

"It's been a very long time since I've seen a friendly face on this coast," the blue dragon said in remarkably polished Common. "This place is full of disobedient, foolish dragonkin who won't listen, and petty naga who think of nothing but killing me every day. And having to deal with them, I'm tormented every day by the absurd responsibility I've been saddled with. All of it forced on me by Malygos — he can't live a comfortable life himself, and he doesn't want his subordinates living comfortably either."

Though his voice was steady and his bearing solemn, he was long-winded.

After listening, Jace exchanged a glance with Grede, who was limping over. Not far off, Vereesa was looking their way with a suspicious expression too.

Yes, everyone felt that even though the blue dragon had regained his senses, his mental state was still a little off. Hearing him speak of his responsibilities, Jace recalled something.

Was it because of the fragment of the Scepter of the Shifting Sands?

The key that sealed the insectoid Scarab Wall in Silithus — that scepter had been broken into several pieces, and the blue portion was the one guarded by Azuregos.

But centuries later, he couldn't bear the prolonged mental and physical torment of holding a shard of that powerful scepter. So he'd given the scepter to a murloc, and then the murloc had been eaten by a giant shark.

Which meant that in the game, to obtain the scepter fragment, you had to go kill that shark…

Perhaps the scepter was still in Azuregos's possession at this point, which was why he sounded so anguished whenever he mentioned that responsibility?

Given that the Scepter of the Shifting Sands was an artifact of immense sensitivity and importance to all of Kalimor — it sealed the Scarab Wall, keeping the insectoid legions of Ahn'Qiraj from suddenly pouring out — Jace had no way of getting to the bottom of this with the guardian of the fragment right in front of him.

Azuregos lowered his heavy head. Flakes of snow drifted down from his cheeks one by one as he continued, "Of course, that doesn't mean I don't respect Lord Malygos. He trusts you, and I trust you."

"Glad you feel that way, Azuregos," Jace nodded, while slinging Ursoloth back across his back.

Jace noticed the blue dragon's gaze flick almost imperceptibly toward the staff, but he said nothing. Perhaps he'd sensed something ominous about it but didn't know what it was.

After all, a "dark mage" carrying a staff that radiated a dark aura was perfectly reasonable.

Azuregos slowly raised his head again, looking up at the Sulenna hovering in the heights. He raised his tone a notch and said with a slightly lighter air, "So, what motive brought you all the way here? There's even a little one who escaped the Emerald Dream in your company — how rare. Queen Ysera probably thinks you're not obedient enough. Be that as it may, I'm glad you're not here scavenging for ancient Kaldorei treasures like the goblins or the naga — otherwise I'd have had to go to the trouble of driving you out… I have to admit, dealing with you little things was really quite a chore."

Hearing the blue dragon say that, the group exchanged glances. This was genuinely awkward.

"And I even had to deal with a friend who uses dark magic, without being allowed to kill him — what a headache… a headache, always a headache." The blue dragon kept rambling, as if talking to himself.

Jace cleared his throat, about to speak, when Vereesa shook her head firmly.

He raised a palm slightly to signal that he'd give it a try, and Vereesa could only sigh — though her bow hand had already tightened again.

"Honored Azuregos…"

Grede's eyebrows jumped hearing Jace's voice. Jace glanced at him and went on, "We've come here based on an ancient Kaldorei legend, searching for a gemstone…"

Azuregos suddenly whipped his head around to stare hard at Jace. His whiskers at the corners of his mouth swayed violently. He was about to speak when Jace said quickly, "But in the name of Azure Song, we only wish to behold its true appearance — we don't want to take it. For the sake of study and research, we only want to see what kind of object it is. We would never use it to do anything wrong related to magic."

"Hmm?" The blue dragon seemed to relax considerably. He lifted his bulk and sat firmly before the group, folded his wings, and coiled his enormous tail, studded with crystals and ice spikes, between his legs — looking exactly like a house cat more than ten meters tall.

"Then, in the name of Azure Song, tell me about this gemstone, Jace Sesso. I'm curious — what sort of gemstone would a little fellow like you, who's so fond of fallen magic, take an interest in?"

"Actually, it's not me who's interested," Jace said.

"It is me," Vereesa said. "What we're looking for is something called the Quel'dorei Voldanaral."

"Ah, ah, ah…" Azuregos tilted his head back in thought. "I know that…"

The words had barely left his mouth when Jace felt as if the space around them had been warped by a surge of frenzied arcane energy. Before he could even process what was happening, he felt his consciousness suddenly wrenched by a powerful force into an empty channel suffused with pale violet light.

His mind hadn't even started turning… the shadows cast by the sunlight, the damp air — all of it was replaced by snow and frost.

Mosa, Grede, Vereesa, and himself hadn't shifted position relative to one another at all. Even Sulenna's position in the sky was exactly the same. But the buildings around them — everything else — was completely different.

In the blink of an eye, they were standing in the midst of a colossal ruined city. Palaces, towers, and pillars overgrown with green algae. Many of the streets were submerged in shallow seawater.

The blue dragon's teleportation magic… this was a lot cleaner and more decisive than the portal in the Wizard Sanctum that led to Stonewatch. Grede was still touching his face and head repeatedly — clearly he hadn't caught up yet.

The blue dragon before them had vanished, replaced by a High Elf standing roughly two meters tall.

This High Elf had gray-blue hair that fell to his shoulders, and the arcane radiance in his eyes flickered like a pair of Arcane Missiles. He wore a blue robe studded with crystal ornaments and held a golden longstaff, its head sculpted into an abstract dragon shape with a burning arcane flame held in its jaws.

The most startling thing was that the top of his head still retained two enormous, grayish-brown ram's horns. It was safe to say this fellow had definitely not taken mortal form to blend in among ordinary folk.

He was pacing back and forth right now, looking for something. Jace knew what he was looking for.

Mofir and Sazeno had been teleported over too, but they'd stayed invisible the whole time — now doing their best to keep their distance. Azuregos must have sensed that he'd teleported more than five people, which was why he kept glancing around curiously.

At that moment, a wild deer bolted out from somewhere, limped through the seawater in a panic, and fled deep into the streets. The blue dragon watched its shadow until it vanished, then slowly let out a breath.

"Take mortal form too, little green one," he said, pointing up at the green dragon. "We're going to be crawling through some tight passages ahead. You definitely won't fit staying in that shape. If your elders never taught you this ability, don't be embarrassed to tell me — I can turn you into a little green turtle."

Sulenna heard this and immediately folded her wings and landed on the ground, transforming into the form of a golden-haired High Elf amidst dreamlike moonlight. Azuregos blinked and nodded. "Impressive. I haven't met many hatchlings who've mastered shapeshifting. Let me think — who were they again?"

"I'm getting off track. Now, I'm going to take you to see that thing. It's a Highborne magic mirror. It's already shattered, but it's said that in those fragments you can still see the reflection of your own soul. Think about it, everyone — a soul. Sometimes I can't help but admire the creativity those Highborne displayed in their pursuit of elegance… but you may look. You may not touch."
