## Chapter 882: Reflections

"Hmm?" Jace looked up.

"Come try it out. This mirror fragment is really interesting," Grede waved him over.

"You want to see what my soul looks like?" Jace forced a smile, drew a breath, and walked over. Vereesa seemed to sense his unease — her smile was strained too, and Jace noticed it.

She'd probably been about to tell Jace that if he didn't want to look, he didn't have to — after all, not everyone was willing to peer into their own true soul. But in the end, she couldn't bring herself to say it. Jace knew she was curious about her own soul too.

Leaning in close to the stone, Grede and Mosa made room like spectators at a show. Vereesa, watching him, also stepped aside, her expression looking vaguely guilty.

Jace glanced at her, set Ursoloth to the side — he didn't want to know what that staff looked like in the stone, and more importantly, he didn't want the blue dragon here to know what it was.

Then he squinted and looked inside.

A thousand possibilities flashed through his mind. Black hair and black eyes, or an orc hunter, an undead warlock, maybe a blood elf death knight.

Or a voidwalker? Or… a black dragon.

But in the end, all he saw was himself — really, just like looking in a mirror. Though not exactly the same.

Through that pale face, in the darkened eye sockets from the poor lighting, he could see a flickering flame — a purple-red fire, there and gone.

But it was that tiny flame that made the whole reflection unfamiliar. The longer he stared at that fire, the greater the difference seemed.

"Something off?" Vereesa leaned in beside him to look.

Watching her reflected appearance, Jace understood what Grede had meant by "ghost."

Vereesa looked more or less the same too, but her eyes were like those transparent, floating arcane will-o'-the-wisps — far hollower than her real self.

A thin mist-like arcane aura radiated from her entire body, blurring the edges of her silhouette.

"I can't see any difference between this and you in person, Jace." Vereesa smiled and slowly leaned against Jace's shoulder. Even the ghostly fire in her eyes softened in the gem's reflection.

By comparison, his own face looked stiff as a corpse.

Then a dwarf — indistinguishable from an ordinary black-bearded Wildhammer — barged into the frame.

Grede turned his head back and forth. "Disappointing. I thought I'd see a lord of the abyss in there."

Mosa also squeezed into the frame, coming to stand between Vereesa and Grede. She was a pure tauren too — every horn, every strand of fur identical to her real self.

She looked at Jace's reflection and grinned. "Evil fire mage. Darkness has corrupted your soul."

"That really isn't the sort of thing you say with a smile, Mosa," Jace said.

Grede and Vereesa both burst out laughing. Then suddenly, a lizard head covered in green scales poked in from behind Jace, startling everyone. They whipped around.

Sulenna was staring wide-eyed at the reflection in the soul mirror. The group dissolved into laughter.

Mosa wrapped her arms around everyone in a hug. Jace, squeezed in the middle, laughed along happily. The evil soul in the stone might have been grinning like an ultimate villain, but at least it was a grin.

Sulenna was laughing too, and that goofy lizard head in the mirror kept coughing and flicking its tongue, looking as innocent as some reptile prey just caught by this motley band of hunters.

Watching how ridiculous she looked, the group laughed even harder, doubled over.

But watching that lizard head — watching Jace, hugged in the middle of a bunch of happy people joking around with each other — his smile slowly faded.

So, could this mirror see through a dragon's disguise?

But the moment that thought surfaced from the bottom of his heart, the gray-blue-haired High Elf that barged into the frame next dealt him a crushing blow.

Azuregos walked up behind the group and looked in. In the mirror, his mortal form didn't even have the horns on his head — he looked even more "mortal" than the mortal guise he'd revealed to them.

"If I were you, I wouldn't be laughing at all, Miss Sulenna. Look at your ridiculous appearance in this mirror — like a stunted dragonkin. If I discovered my loyal servant had turned into your sort of spectacle after the Ascension ceremony, I'd tear his head off with magic on the spot and throw the corpse into the sea for the sharks."

The smile on Sulenna's face froze. Slowly, quietly, she shifted her reflection's gaze away from the mirror.

"You didn't have to put it that harshly," Vereesa said, displeased.

"Spur one to progress," Azuregos said.

"The principle isn't wrong," Grede said, scratching his beard.

"So this mirror can't fully reconstruct a soul, then," Jace said.

"This artifact was crafted by Highborne nobles, Jace Sesso. Though its creator was immensely powerful and deeply trusted and favored by Azshara," Azuregos said. "Its original purpose wasn't even to reveal souls at all — it was only meant to present a three-dimensional likeness, so those noble ladies who loved preening could examine the full picture of themselves from an outsider's perspective. It only ended up manifesting the soul of whoever looked into it because, for various reasons, it pursued realism to the point of touching the essence. And this is just one fragment of the original mirror, so it can only manage what an ordinary mirror does — showing a reflection on its surface."

"And I am Azuregos, guardian of the ley lines, warden of the arcane," the blue dragon continued. "If this Kaldorei magical creation could expose my 'soul' and see through my disguise, wouldn't that be utterly laughable? Malygos would kick me out of the Nexus as a useless fool!"

His eyes shifted, and he added, "Though there's not much difference at this point anyway."

Jace had been thinking — if this thing could reflect a dragon's soul, then if they brought it back to Stormwind, wouldn't they not have to worry about Onyxia or some other black dragon suddenly showing up and seizing control of Stormwind Keep?

But then Azuregos's appearance had just extinguished the hope he'd kindled. This thing really wasn't as powerful as he'd hoped. Its magic had its limits after all.

Still, he did notice something off about Azuregos's reflection in the mirror at the end.

Compared to an ordinary High Elf, his eyes weren't like arcane flames — they were like two translucent arcane sapphires, crystalline and beautiful.

Though there wasn't a single trace of dragon about him, just comparing this appearance to Vereesa's true High Elf soul-form, you could still barely tell they weren't the same race.

"Your mortal form is still distinguishable from the Quel'dorei, Azuregos. Does that mean it's still possible to use this thing to identify dragons among mortals?" Jace asked.

"This is, after all, a refined magical artifact, Jace Sesso," Azuregos explained further. "Besides, that's just me being too lazy to fine-tune my magic bit by bit. That subtle difference is impossible for an ordinary person to distinguish. But handling it all properly would multiply the mana and effort required several times over. My mortal form is simply a matter of personal aesthetics. I have no intention of using it to deceive anyone."

And he wasn't wrong about that — otherwise he wouldn't have walked around with two great dragon horns out in the open.

Still, looking at it this way, though the mirror couldn't fully reveal a dragon's soul, it could still expose a small difference. And that would be enough to flush out the disguises of adult black dragons hiding among people.

As long as they could use this mirror to identify the telltale details of a black dragon's disguise, and convince King Varian or Lord Bolvar Fordragon that there was a black dragon posing as a noble among them, that would be enough to prevent a great deal of trouble.

"I have to say, I'd very much like to take it back with me, Honored Azuregos," Jace said. The others heard this and looked over in shock, then turned to the blue dragon.

Azuregos couldn't help but let out a laugh, then said with a cold face, "Are you deliberately trying to provoke me, mortal?" The dragonkin guards and dragonkin watchers around them all tensed, gripping their weapons.

The blue dragon drew a deep breath, then said, "Don't think that just because you saved the Dragonqueen I won't dare teach you a lesson. I'm certain she's not in the mood to fly all the way to Azshara just to beat me up personally. I've got nothing to fear!"

Jace shook his head. "It's not for the money or anything like that. It's that, as of now, I have reliable intelligence that the Black Dragonflight is infiltrating the mortal nations — from Lordaeron in the north to Stormwind in the south. Deathwing's fall hasn't curbed their audacity…"

Hearing Jace's reasoning, Azuregos's lips moved, but he said nothing. He looked at the others beside him, then back at Jace. "Kalecgos did tell me about Deathwing disguising himself as a human noble."

He fell silent for a long time, then said, "Actually, there's something I hadn't planned to mention, but it's been lodged in my heart. Since you intend to take this thing away, I might as well be frank with you all."

"When Deathwing descended on Grim Batol, I heard the call of the red dragon queen, Alexstrasza. She was calling my name — Azuregos — hoping I would go to Grim Batol to help you. But I didn't answer her. At the time, I told myself I had my own responsibilities, the mission Malygos had given me, and the duties other dragonflights had entrusted to me. And the coast of Eldallas had too many evil naga and foolish poachers. If these artifacts were to fall into mortal hands, it would cause far too much serious trouble. But afterward, I realized this was nothing more than an excuse I'd fashioned for my own cowardice. Because I didn't dare face Neltharion."

"Besides, this isn't anything that important. Malygos didn't send me here to guard these trinkets in the first place. Take it, Jace Sesso."

As he finished, Azuregos seemed to remember something and looked back to add, "Just this one thing. Don't tell the other blue dragons."

"Really?" Vereesa asked in surprise.

Jace snatched the fragment off the pedestal and stuffed it into his bag. "Thank you very much, guardian of the ley lines — Honored Lord Azuregos."

"Your objective is achieved. Leave," Azuregos said with a wave. "Next time you come see me, I hope it'll be for something simpler — like having a chat, or playing some entertaining game — and not coming to rob me again."

Leaving the palace and returning to the bleak coast, the dragonkin who'd followed them each drifted back into the ruins. Vereesa watched their backs and let out a long breath. "I honestly didn't think we'd end up getting the thing, Jace."

"Still, I do need to keep it, Vere," Jace said.

"I know," Vereesa said. "But now that we have it, I can record more of its characteristics — including magical traits, even sympathetic resonances. That should definitely earn us a bigger reward, no?"

"True enough." Jace nodded, gripping the mirror stone.

Mofir came up beside them. Jace held up the stone, looked at her, and asked with a smile, "Want to see what your demon soul looks like, Sayad?"

"No." Mofir's faintly smiling expression suddenly turned cold. "I don't want to see it." Her tone was resolute — she didn't even crack a joke on the way out, which caught even Vereesa a little off guard. Jace, of course, wasn't surprised.

He remembered she'd mentioned before that her former superior Brutallus had torn the wings from her soul, so they'd never grown back. It was perfectly natural that she wouldn't want to see her soul in its broken state.
