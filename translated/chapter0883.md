## Chapter 883: Parting on the Cliff

On the cliffs of Azshara, the cold wind barely ever let up.

Jace gripped his cloak and watched the two figures — one tall, one short — not far away. Ever since leaving the Kaldorei palace occupied by the blue dragon, Grede and Mosa hadn't said much to each other.

Because they both knew this Kalimodor trip had reached its end, and the two of them had to part ways.

"My decision to stay isn't some impulse, Mosa," Grede said suddenly. "I'd already been thinking it over for a long time before that. If it weren't for you, who'd have been willing to travel this far and suffer through this with these two restless idiots? Couldn't I have just taken a few cheap jobs in Stormwind?"

"Even if you stay, we won't see each other for a long time, Grede," Mosa said, looking down at him. "I made a mistake. I shouldn't have gone against the will of Cenarius's children. I left Moonglade without permission, hunting for that staff — the wrong staff."

She looked over at Jace and went on. "Jace is right. Werewolves could do even more severe damage to our home. I let this savage power and hatred cloud my mind, and nearly made an irreparable mistake."

Hearing her say this, Sulenna lowered her head in embarrassment. "I became an accomplice of the satyrs, and I dragged you down too."

"Don't say that, Sulenna. You didn't want this either," Mosa said, fumbling on. "The Bloodhoof tribe has many warriors far stronger than me. The chieftain wants me to pursue the druidic path, and he's right — it's more useful than me joining the fighting. I was eager to resolve everything quickly, but in reality I overestimated how much I was in a position to decide. I only drifted further and further from my original goal. Staying in Moonglade isn't just about helping my people understand the forces of nature better — it's also about admitting my fault to my teachers. Like the other druids, dedicating what little of my soul's strength I can offer to protecting and sustaining the Dream. And through all of that, there's no way you can be at my side, Grede."

When Mosa said "chieftain," did she mean Caine Bloodhoof, the tauren chieftain? Jace looked at her, thinking.

If that were the case, it would mean Caine had already realized how precious Mosa was — one of the very few remaining members of the tauren tribe who could even be called a druid. He wondered what impact this would have on the tauren's future struggle against the centaurs.

Grede sighed. "If I listened to you, God knows how long it'd be before we saw each other again."

"Maybe you'll dream of me, Grede — in the Emerald Dream," Mosa said.

"Get out of here." Grede glanced at Sulenna.

"There's a good chance," Sulena said.

"Sounds like a joke," Grede snorted. "That makes me feel a whole lot better."

Grede went off to pack his things. Mosa looked over at Jace. "Take care of Grede for me, Jace."

"It's always been him taking care of me, Mosa, so don't you worry too much," Jace said with a smile.

"He's not as strong as you think," Mosa said softly. "I imagine you know that too."

Jace nodded. The two of them hugged lightly. Jace let out a slow breath and came over to sort through his own pack, staff, and the rest.

Mosa said something to Vereesa over there. Jace could sense the mood between the two of them sink a little — Mosa seemed worried, and Vereesa… also looked very serious.

The two of them hadn't talked long before Mosa sighed, as if she hadn't been able to persuade Vereesa of something. But in the end she still put on a smile and said goodbye to Vereesa.

By now, Mosa had also gone to chat with Sulenna, who was crouching at the cliff's edge. Vereesa came over to Jace and said, "Mosa is worried about that thing of yours, Jace. She wanted me to keep a closer eye on that staff, while also looking after your feelings — so you wouldn't feel like everyone was watching you. But I don't want to hide it from you, and I know these things can't be hidden from you anyway."

Actually, Jace had indeed suspected Mosa was worried about Ursoloth just now. Vereesa knew Jace well. Though he wasn't that sensitive to arcane magic, he read the emotions that stirred shadow power with remarkable clarity.

"It's not just her. I'm worried too," Jace said. "And every time I draw on this weapon's power, I can hear things — voices. I can't make them out clearly yet, but I'm certain those words aren't meaningless murmuring. They mean something."

"Do you want to find out what those words mean?" Vereesa asked.

Jace took a deep breath. "I don't know if I should."

"I just know you won't be able to restrain your curiosity," Vereesa said.

"If I ever have any new plans, I'll tell Grede first, and write to you too," Jace said. Vereesa looked over at the tauren and the green dragon. "Hearing that puts my mind somewhat at ease."

"But remember, Jace," she said, looking back. "If the day comes when you betray the things you cherish now — or go mad and forget the person you are today — I'll kill you myself. Don't expect me to hesitate because of… everything we've been through together."

By the end, her voice trembled slightly.

"I know, Vere," Jace nodded. "I'll do everything in my power to keep that day from coming. But if it really does come to that, I'd want you to do it too."

Vereesa wiped her eyes. "Let's not talk about this anymore."

"You two — are you still planning to leave?" Grede waved from beside the green dragon.

"Ah, we should get going." Vereesa said it and walked toward the cliff's edge.

Climbing onto the green dragon's back, Sulenna leaped off the cliff and soared out over the sea.

The tauren waving at the cliff's edge grew smaller and fainter, gradually vanishing into the sea mist — and then the whole coast with her.

The green dragon flew far faster than any ship, as if unwilling to give anyone time to brew their feelings. Grede and Vereesa were silent too, staring at the endless ocean around them. Jace felt as if a piece had been torn from his heart.

Kalimdor — the Land of Eternal Starlight. Who knew when they'd set foot here again.

The green dragon flew northeast across the sea for two days. Though she spent most of the time gliding on the wind, by the second day even flapping her wings seemed to take a lot out of her.

After all, Sulenna wasn't the black dragon prince, and she wasn't Deathwing. She didn't have the strength to cross the Great Sea. Jace wasn't even sure she could make it to Northrend.

On the evening of the second day, it began to rain. The warmth Sulenna had been maintaining around them with Dream power was pierced bit by bit by the relentless icy rain, growing weaker by the minute. The group sat on her back, shivering despite their outer robes and cloaks, and had no choice but to dig out the winter clothes they'd worn through Winterspring and drape them over themselves. In the process, they accidentally lost a small bag of dry bread, which tumbled into the sea.

Grede watched the bag fly toward the sea and vanish without a trace, and couldn't stop muttering, "If we had a mage like Azuregos, he could've just casually pulled it back with an arcane spell."

"If you really cared about those few pieces of bread, I could pull them back with my magic too," Jace said, clutching his cloak.

"You'd just tear everything to shreds," Grede said, sitting back down.

"Hey, we might be there," Vereesa said.

Jace and Grede both looked in the direction the ranger was pointing. In the distance, a gray, hazy shadow — land, or just a thick bank of clouds? "Is that Northrend? Or just some island north of Kalimdor?" Grede asked.

"I don't know…"

Vereesa had barely spoken when shadows appeared in sheet after sheet along the horizon — reefs, beaches…

"Land! It's really land!" Grede shouted. "You're incredible, Sulenna. You flew us all the way to Northrend."

"Hmm?" came a vague sound from the direction of Sulenna's head at the front, like a raspy yawn.

"You didn't see?" Grede asked.

Sulenna murmured, "I was just sleeping. I didn't hear what you were saying."

"Sleeping?!"

Grede and Vereesa exclaimed at the same time. Grede said, "You were sleeping while carrying us through the sky?! What if you'd plunged straight into the sea?"

"We're still flying, aren't we? If we fell into the ocean, I'd wake up, everyone," Sulenna said.

"Of course you'd wake up," Grede said. "But all our luggage and clothes would be done for! Can't you feel how cold it is here?"

"Uh. Sorry," Sulenna said quietly. "I forgot about the luggage, but I…"

Jace was stunned. He'd slow the green dragon's wingbeats were from exhaustion — turns out this female dragon had been sleeping? This couldn't even be called fatigued driving anymore. This was literally sleep-flying…

"Ah, Northrend!" the green dragon said suddenly, with a tone of surprise. "I've always wanted to come see this place. You see it? Northrend's right in front of us!"

Jace rubbed his face in agony. Grede and Vereesa looked at each other. Mofir, who'd been holding back her laughter for a good long while, finally let out a snort she couldn't contain. Even the demon bag at Jace's waist trembled.

"We need to find somewhere to get out of the rain, Sulenna," Vereesa said, draping an unused cloak over the luggage. "Any more of this and everything's going to be soaked through."

"All right." Sulenna flapped her wings a few times and accelerated toward the land.
