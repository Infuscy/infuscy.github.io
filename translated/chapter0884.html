Chapter 884: Shadow of the North

The shadow grew clearer. A faint haze hung over the reefs, the beaches, and everything ashore shrouded in gray. The dark gray earth lay exposed in some places and blanketed with snow in others, like the patches on a cow.

Sulenna landed at the base of a seaside cliff. The group found a hollow beneath the cliff to take shelter from the rain. Looking far off toward the coast to the east, they could make out the shadow of a tall, slightly curving black stone pillar — hard to see clearly through the mist.

After stowing the luggage, Vereesa straightened up and looked into the distance. "I'm going out to find something to eat. Watch the luggage."

"I'll go too," Grede raised his hand. "I've been sitting on a dragon's back so long my legs barely work. I need to stretch my legs and see what the fabled Northrend looks like for myself. And I'm a pretty excellent hunter too, aren't I, little elf?"

"True enough," Vereesa said offhandedly. "Stay with Mofir and watch the luggage, Jace. And don't disturb Sulenna's rest."

Sulenna had already closed her eyes and fallen asleep. Jace leaned back against a black rock behind him. "Got it."

The elf and the dwarf set off toward the coast with their weapons. Jace listened to them talking and laughing as they went, rested his head on his arm, and stared blankly at the dark sea.

Mofir stood on a rock not far off. Right now she was doing her best to dampen the flames all over her body, so she wouldn't stand out like a lighthouse on the dark coast.

At this point, Jace couldn't help but pull out the mirror stone again, wanting a closer look at what his own "soul" actually looked like.

He took out the stone, stared at his own reflection, and turned his head this way and that. It was honestly no different from what he'd seen back at Azuregos's — the look of a poor wretch slightly corrupted by shadow and flame, with an ominous flicker of fire in his eyes.

Then a little imp made of fel fire darted out from behind him in the mirror.

"See that, master?" Sazeno said, pointing at the mirror. "You see my soul? Pure and beautiful, brilliant and glorious! I'm a genuine demon — a true fel creature!"

"That Sayad definitely isn't a demon, I knew it…"

Even as the imp said this, it ran over, grabbed the mirror from Jace's hand, and aimed it in Mofir's direction toward the coast. Then its expression froze.

On the reef, Mosa crouched — her reflection in the stone was a horned skeleton wreathed in shadowy fel flames.

Her remaining pair of wings had been reduced to two shattered stubs of bone, one long and one short, jutting from her back. They were even still streaked with what looked like gory, dripping flesh.

Jace had of course been mentally prepared for the state of her wings — though it was even more gruesome than he'd expected.

What really surprised him was that Mofir's demon soul was far larger than her current form, but riddled with mutilations and hollows. Only the massive spiked tail on her back was still intact.

It reared high, making the whole shattered soul look like a mangled dragon with its wings sheared off — hideous.

Seeming to sense something from this direction, Mofir glanced over. Reflected in the mirror, she was a flaming skeleton suddenly fixing its gaze this way. Jace turned the mirror and looked at himself again.

Sazeno had clearly been frightened. He swallowed and said, "All right, all right. Looks like, more or less, she's still… a demon."

"Why does her soul look like this?" Jace asked. "Is this normal?"

"It means she last died in a place occupied by demons — somewhere close to the Twisting Nether," Sazeno said softly. "That death tore her soul apart. So she was resurrected, but lost a lot — power, magic, and of course her wings. Judging by this, maybe what she said about having hunted Nathrezim before is true! But I don't know Brutallus, so I don't know what grudge he held against the Nathrezim, or what he had this Sayad do. But at any rate, I definitely don't like the Nathrezim, or their black servants! If the Dreadlord King forced us into the Burning Legion, I'd become the slave of those big-winged monsters, to be ravaged and trampled at their whim… but I've heard their stronghold — Nathreza — holds boundless dark knowledge. If I could go there and have a look… forget it. I don't want to go to that place."

"Don't talk so loud," Jace said. "Go back in the bag. I want to sleep too."

"Yes, master."

The imp slipped reluctantly into the bag. Jace lay back against the stone, felt his head grow heavy for a moment, and then felt someone shaking him.

"Jace. Wake up."

He lifted his head. The sky had brightened faintly.

Vereesa was in front of him. "We found a group of local Tuskarr over there…"

Grede scratched his beard. "A bunch of fat walrus-heads walking around on two legs. But they seemed pretty friendly — not as savage as they look."

Vereesa nodded. "Besides their own language, they can't speak Common or Thalassian. But they do speak a little draconic — I don't know why. Only my draconic is limited to a few simple words. Didn't you study draconic for a while at the Wizard Sanctum? They've got plenty of food there, but they don't seem to trust us."

Jace pointed behind him. "Why not send Sulenna? She's the professional."

"She…" Vereesa glanced over. That dragon was still sleeping.

Grede rubbed his head. "Let her sleep. God knows how much farther she'll have to fly. We don't need to discuss philosophy with the locals."

Jace nodded. "Then watch our luggage. Look after Sulenna with Sazeno, Mofir."

"Don't worry, my master!" Sazeno said.

"Understood, master." The succubus acknowledged too.

Tuskarr was the elven word for "walrus people" — though of course, it was also transliterated from the Tuskarr's own name for themselves in their own language.

The Tuskarr walrus-people lived along the southern coast of Northrend, mostly distributed across the Borean Tundra to the west and part of the Dragonblight. They were peace-loving, and willing to trade and communicate with other friendly races.

The reason they spoke draconic was probably because they'd been dealing with their blue dragon neighbors to the north for thousands of years. Though their relationship was anything but friendly.

What puzzled Jace was that whether it was dwarves, elves, or humans, none were particularly rare races in Northrend. The walrus-people should have encountered these folk all the time. And according to the records in Stormwind's library, there had been no conflicts between the walrus-people and the southern races either. So why were they unwilling to trust Vereesa and Grede?

Could it be that they'd been tainted by too much evil aura from his presence, and the walrus-people's wise men or shamans had taken offense? Thinking this, Jace touched the silent Ursoloth on his back and worried, vaguely, that they might be heading for a confrontation.

After walking east for about an hour, they could see the arched huts built from giant bones and hides up on the cliffs. On the faroff beach, a simple dock could be made out, with small boats moored beside it.

A few round, squatting figures sat on the ground caught sight of the three people approaching. One by one, they sat up.

These plump walrus-people, their faces more than half hidden by beards, each held a crude stone ax — clearly on high guard.

"Hello. We've come from the south," Jace said in draconic from a distance.

Hearing the dragon tongue, the walrus-people looked at each other. One of them said in draconic, "You truly speak the dragon language."

"Yes, but not fluently." Jace took out the small draconic vocabulary booklet he'd brought, flipped through it for a moment, and said, "We mean no harm. We only want some food."

"Food. Not much." The walrus-people waved their hands while speaking in draconic. "Food's getting scarcer, human. We have none to give you."

Grede watched him wave his hands and said to Vereesa, somewhat disappointed, "No better than the two of us."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"They say a shadow has come to the coast…"

As one walrus-person said this, another blocked him, grumbling unhappily in their own language — a long string of incomprehensible noise.

By now, a boat was pulling toward the coast through the wind and waves. The walrus-people aboard disembarked, and the walrus-people on shore gathered around them. But after a lot of bustling, it didn't look like they'd managed to bring anything much off the boat.

"Leave this place. Go look to the east, southerners," a walrus-person waved a thick hand. "The east has no fighting. The east…"

"Fighting?" Jace asked. "What fighting?"

"Calamity from below," the walrus-person said, lowering his voice. "The battle of the underground creatures has reached the surface. They die, then rise again… then die again. This hasn't happened in hundreds of years. The animals have left here. Many Tuskarr have left here too. Perhaps one day we'll have to leave as well — either to the west or to the east."

Hearing this, Jace could basically confirm what was happening on this land.

The "shadow" they mentioned was very likely the Lich King Ner'zhul descending upon Icecrown — the walrus-people of this coast had already sensed the dark power he brought.

And the "underground creatures" the walrus-person referred to were probably the spider kingdom that had slumbered beneath Northrend's surface for tens of thousands of years.

After the Lich King arrived in Northrend, he began selecting his victims. His first target was these underground insectoid races. He spread the undead plague, raising the dead azhir as undead nerubians, setting the dead against the living who resisted him — until the dead replaced everything.

This was the War of the Spiders.
