Chapter 885: History Lesson

Leaving that beach and the walrus-people village, Sulenna carried Jace's group eastward along the coast.

The green dragon, having rested a full night, was brimming with energy, and the Dream power she radiated held up better against Northrend's cold wind too. Still, Grede couldn't help leaning over now and then to steal a glance, checking whether she'd fallen asleep.

Jace figured if she could guarantee smooth flying while asleep, it wasn't a big deal either — after all, this wasn't Dun Morog, Alterac, Redridge, or places like that, where you could crash into a mountain or cliff if you weren't careful.

Black, slightly curving, thick stone pillars could be seen on the coast from time to time.

When they'd first arrived on Northrend's southern shore, Jace had already spotted one dimly through the mist from a distance. Now, sitting on Sulenna's back as they swept past the vicinity of one of those pillars, he finally saw clearly what it was.

A symbol-like walrus-person head — like those mysterious statues on Easter Island — gazing out over the sea from afar.

On the tundra in the distance behind these stone-pillar heads, there were often huts built from animal bones. Those places might once have been inhabited by walrus-people, but now the walrus-people had all left, leaving behind nothing but abandoned ruins.

Judging from the state of the buildings and some traces of daily life, they hadn't abandoned the place long ago — probably somewhere between a few months and a year.

Perhaps it was because of the recent upheavals in Northrend that the walrus-people in that village had mentioned.

"These walrus-people heads make me uncomfortable. I feel like they're always staring at me," Grede said. One hand gripped a horn on the green dragon's back; the other crumbled the crushed biscuit crumbs he'd bought in Everlook into his mouth, getting it all over his beard.

"What do you think those underground monsters the walrus-people mentioned are?" the dwarf looked up at Jace, then at Vereesa. "Trolls? I mean, ice trolls. You know there are a lot of ice trolls living underground in the mountains of Dun Morog."

"I'm certain there are ice trolls living in Northrend," Vereesa said. "But I can't say for sure what those walrus-people were talking about."

"I think they're nerubians," Jace said offhandedly. "Don't you know about the enormous spider kingdom that exists beneath Northrend? A lot of Northrend's residents have mentioned that sort of thing."

In fact, he'd also come across related records in the libraries of Dalaran and Stormwind — though the one in Stormwind looked a lot like it had been copied directly from the account of that explorer in Dalaran's collection, recounting similar experiences and observations. Still, either way, it gave him a bit more confidence when bringing up the nerubians.

"Is that true?" Vereesa said in surprise. "A lot of Quel'dorei think that's nothing more than… troll legend, of course. I've never seen one either — just heard people talk about it."

"What troll legend?" Grede asked, curious.

Vereesa explained, "The Amani trolls believe their ancestors established an ancient empire — the Zul Empire — more than ten thousand years ago, and fought for dominance against an empire ruled by insectoids for over a millennium. In the end, the insectoids were defeated, splitting into several populations that fled to the edges of Kalimdor. Now the Kaldorei have discovered the Qiraji, which seems to match the trolls' records. And some dwarf explorer-traveler mages have also found nerubians in Northrend."

"They're not wrong," Sulenna spoke up suddenly. "Isendre told me a similar story. Long ago, the trolls and the insectoids fought for dominion over ancient Kalimdor. Though the trolls won, not long after, the Zul Empire also split apart. The most powerful branch became the Kaldorei empire, and they quickly drew upon the power of the Well of Eternity…"

"Nonsense!" Vereesa practically jumped to her feet. "I can't believe even wise dragons would believe such a ridiculous, baseless hypothesis! That's all nonsense fabricated by those insane, self-important Dalaran mages to smear their magic teachers — meaning the entire bloodline of Quel'Thalas — and cook up some bizarre lie!"

Jace sighed and covered his eyes. Vereesa stared at him. "You believe it too, don't you? You believe the Kaldorei originated from some 'dark troll tribe' by the Well of Eternity, right? You hopeless idiot! Your brain's been rotted clean by those libraries, damn it!"

"If the Well of Eternity could turn a tribe of dark trolls into Kaldorei, why are there no more 'dark trolls' now? Hmm? Where did all the dark trolls who never got near the Well of Eternity go? Don't tell me forest trolls have been able to carry on through the Amani empire, jungle trolls could establish the millennia-old Gurubashi, ice trolls in Northrend still send word now and then, and even the Zandalari trolls, who've been lost to sight for God knows how long, still occasionally show up on Amani territory — so where are the dark trolls who never found the Well of Eternity?! Don't tell me every last one of them turned into Kaldorei!"

The arcane flames in Vereesa's eyes burned furiously. After she finished, she stood there panting, glaring at Grede and Jace in front of her. Grede was completely frozen. He glanced at Jace beside him and stammered, "I… I've never even heard of dark trolls, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. I mean it."

"Because they live in Kalimdor, right at the foot of Mount Hyjal. They call themselves the Darkteeth tribe," Sulenna tried to explain.

"Sulenna…" Jace sighed.

Vereesa stared blankly ahead. "Fabricated!"

Sulenna said, "It's possible, because this was also what Isendre told me. I've never been to Darkshore, and I've never discussed these things with the Kaldorei…"

"So this is what you big green lizards come up with, staying in your dreams too long and making things up!" Vereesa roared. Her body swayed, and for a moment it looked as if she might fall off the dragon's back.

Jace moved to steady her, but Vereesa slapped his hand away violently and sat down off to the side.

That casual backhand from the ranger hurt like hell. Jace sucked in a breath, which slowly made her turn her head back. "Sorry," Vereesa said softly. "I just…"

"I more or less understand," Jace said, shaking his hand helplessly. "If I heard humans evolved from the gorillas of Stranglethorn, I'd be pretty upset too."

Grede burst out laughing. Vereesa still kept her face stern and said nothing.

"That's even funnier than elves turning out to be trolls, hahahaha…" Grede laughed, then opened his eyes to find the ranger already glaring at him.

"Ah, hmm…" The dwarf stared at Vereesa for a moment, then said, "Nice weather. I'm sleepy. I'm going to sleep." With that, he flopped face-first onto the dragon's back and started snoring.

"Sulenna," Vereesa said, displeased.

"Present," the green dragon answered immediately.

"Wherever you heard that, before…" Vereesa said, her tone serious. "It's wrong. The Kaldorei were born beside the Well of Eternity, and have absolutely nothing to do with trolls! That's the truth!"

"I'll remember that, Vereesa," Sulenna replied obediently.
