## Chapter 89: The Trapped Room

If he hesitated now, it would be too late!

Jace pointed at the spot where the arcane flash had been and immediately began to incant — "Feark—"

He'd barely gotten the word out before he felt something in his mind snap taut, as if a thread had broken. His mouth felt wrenched shut by something, and every muscle in his body seized up along with the shadow energy he'd just gathered, plunging him into a violent, wrenching pain!

This familiar sensation… it was a Counterspell!

The moment Jace realized what had hit him, he snapped back to full awareness.

But he'd already been driven backward by that blast of arcane energy meant specifically to interrupt his casting, and he tumbled down the wooden steps in front of a doorway.

The naga's shadow had just solidified when it quickly closed in. Jace scrambled to his feet and bolted hard toward the side of the house.

Damn it — it had to be the staff in his hand that had given away his identity as a caster, prompting the naga to ready a Counterspell against him.

Jace braced against the wall and sprinted to the side of the house. Behind him, the rapid hiss of naga scales dragging through dirt closed in fast. This sea-witch was a lot quicker than he'd figured, and she could turn invisible… or was it Blink?

Most likely Blink — invisibility wouldn't speed up her movement, and it couldn't have gotten her from the beach to the middle of the houses in an instant. He didn't have the ability yet to tell the difference between the two spells, but the bottom line was that this naga had some kind of rapid-movement magic. Just running like this was pointless.

Jace rounded the back of the house and spotted a window wide open up above.

Before the naga could come around and spot him, he leapt with everything he had, grabbed the wall, and scrambled up the uneven wooden boards, barely catching the second-floor window ledge.

He had to admit — his physical body was a hell of a lot stronger in this life than it had been in his last.

He hauled himself through the window and crouched down, listening to the rustling sound pass beneath him. He didn't dare poke his head out, terrified the naga would realize he'd hidden himself inside.

But that naga knew he couldn't turn invisible or use any other movement magic. If she circled the house once, she'd figure out he'd gone inside.

If he waited for the naga to leave and jumped out down there, the moment he made a sound she'd come right back and he was finished.

Which meant his only chance was to wait in this house.

Jace drew the battered blade he'd bought at the Westfall camp from behind his back, kicked off his shoes, and pressed himself against the wall, listening to every sound, waiting for the naga to enter the building.

The surroundings gradually grew quiet — quiet enough that he could hear his own breathing.

Slowly, he steadied his breathing too, making as little noise as possible. Before long, the only thing Jace could hear was a faint ringing in his ears — probably from that arcane blast earlier.

Jace ran through the tactics he could use once the naga came in — all of them built around an ambush from behind the wall.

Grede had said that no matter what kind of mage you were, as long as she still needed to chant, she'd fear getting up close. And that wasn't even counting the fact that this naga was long and tall — she'd never be able to maneuver properly in a cramped wooden house like this.

If Jace could land a successful ambush, whatever he managed to hack off her would give him an overwhelming advantage. As long as he could get away and hide again, all he had to do was wait for the naga to get fed up and leave, or decide the cost wasn't worth paying and withdraw on her own.

Thinking it over, Jace was starting to worry the naga wouldn't even come into this house.

If she didn't, Jace planned to wait here until dawn. Once the sun came up… the naga would have to get the hell out, right?

Wooden houses weren't like stone ones — any movement could set off an unpredictable creak in the timber. Jace didn't know how long he'd been waiting when he suddenly heard an unusual scraping sound from downstairs.

The serpent-woman was inside.

Maybe she'd taken a moment to steel herself before deciding to enter. Maybe she'd only just figured out Jace might be in this house. Maybe she'd already searched the others quickly and this was the last one left. However it was, she was in.

Jace, who'd been on the verge of dozing off, snapped back to full alert. He gripped the blade tight, thinking that if he'd known he'd be dealing with an enemy this nasty, he'd have spent the coin on a better weapon. But there was no changing it now.

The rustling got closer and closer. He could tell she was coming upstairs.

Then an extremely sharp scraping sound cut into his ears — so harsh it was almost painful to hear.

The sound was close. Already on the second floor. Most likely the accidental, eerie noise of scales dragging across a wall.

After the sound came, the rustling of scales stopped too — perhaps the naga had realized she was making too much noise and was letting the enemy track her movements.

Jace could only bet that this naga had never fought an enemy in a room this cramped before.

A few minutes passed, and the rustling resumed. This time Jace nearly held his breath — in truth, he'd been deliberately keeping his breathing quiet for a while now, and he was starting to feel the strain. If the naga didn't show herself soon, he really wouldn't be able to take it.

And then the rustling had reached the room next door!

Right out in the hallway…

Jace fixed his eyes on the door, blade already raised.

The moment he saw anything appear, he'd slash straight through!

When he caught a shadow flickering in the doorway, he stepped out in a flash and swung — but there was nothing there! No — down on the ground!

What he'd been hearing was the sound of the whole serpentine body dragging across the floor. By the time the sound reached the doorway, the slightly raised front half of the serpent-woman's body had already passed it. Now only half a tail remained, right at Jace's feet!

Sensing some kind of movement behind her, the naga whipped her head around!

Up close, the face of this female naga sent goosebumps exploding across Jace's entire body. Her eyes were as big as two leaves, set horrifyingly far apart. Where a nose should have been there were only two holes. But her lips were as vivid as if they'd been painted with divine lipstick.

Her narrow chin and slender face, her smooth skin — it all should have been the outline of a beauty, but with a few features that were decidedly not human set right in the middle, plunging the whole thing straight into the uncanny valley.

But Jace wasn't the least bit stunned by that horrifying face. He swung without hesitation, chopping clean through half of the naga's tail. The naga, completely unprepared for how ruthlessly fast this guy had moved, shrieked in pain.

Jace bolted like a shot to the other end of the hallway. The naga wailed and gave chase, her sharp claws raking wildly, clawing at the walls with a chaotic scraping and screeching — but she didn't lay a finger on Jace.

With a huge chunk of her tail severed, she couldn't keep her balance. For all her ferocity, she slammed into the walls several times in a row and never managed to close the gap.

"Warm-blooded vermin!"

Jace could only hear the naga's shriek echoing through the whole house. The sound made his head swim — the sea-witch's howl had to have a fear-inducing enchantment behind it.

He covered his ears and forced himself through it. When his head cleared, all he could hear was the sound of wooden walls being smashed and wild screaming from all over the second floor. The naga had completely lost it.

One more time. Just one more time.

Jace crouched in a corner where he could see down the hallway. The moment that serpent-woman came around, he'd strike again — this time straight for the kill. Chop the head off! Slash the neck!

The last ambush had gone so perfectly that he felt he could pull the same trick again. But before long, the frenzied commotion outside died down.

The whole second floor reeked of blood and dead fish. Had that serpent-woman been mortally wounded and died?

Even if she was dead, Jace wasn't going out there. He was going to sit right here and wait for that serpent-woman to bleed out… he absolutely would not be the one to make the first move.

But the moment he'd made up his mind, he suddenly heard a low murmuring from somewhere in another room. She was chanting again.
