Chapter 892: Adjusting to the New Environment

The next morning, after bidding farewell to Krasus, Jace and Grede left Dalaran aboard Solamiel.

Before takeoff, Krasus took care to remind them that Stormwind wasn't Dalaran — don't come barreling into the city on the back of a dragon, or they'd frighten the innocent citizens and stir up a whole mess of unnecessary trouble and rumors. After all, most of the Stormwind residents who'd had experience with dragons still remembered the red dragons that had burned and pillaged during the Orc Wars. The few who'd seen other dragons were veterans of the Battle of Alterac who'd fought black dragons. Of course, Jace had never planned to let Solamiel charge straight over the city walls to begin with — especially now that the warlocks had already caused trouble in Stormwind. He needed to keep a low profile.

After crossing the Alterac Mountains and passing through Hillsbrad, Jace had Solamiel land in the forest off to the side. He planned to head into town to buy "paint" for drawing a magic circle — various animal bloods — hoping to run into a farmer who was slaughtering livestock and willing to sell him some.

The towns of Hillsbrad and Southshore were the last two human strongholds left after the Kingdom of Lordaeron was destroyed by its own prince, and they survived all the way until the Dark Lady Sylvanas raised the Forsaken in the undercity beneath Lordaeron.

Perhaps it was because the Scourge had never come to conquer territory. Their ultimate goal in destroying Lordaeron had been to take Dalaran, seize the Book of Medivh from the mages' hands, and use it to summon the Defiler Archimonde.

Once Archimonde was fully summoned into this world, the human lands south of Dalaran became more or less irrelevant.

And Archimonde's sole desire was to reach Mount Hyjal and devour the power of the World Tree Nordrassil. He had zero interest in the other human settlements on the Lordaeron continent.

However, although the Scourge and the Burning Legion it then served no longer had the strength to continue ravaging the southern reaches of the Lordaeron continent after the end of the Third War, the Forsaken — who had broken away from the Scourge under Sylvanas's command — had never stopped attacking these remaining human strongholds.

In the game, Forsaken players received orders from the Dark Lady and her "advisor," the dreadlord Varimathras, to continuously harass Hillsbrad and Southshore. So despite having survived the Third War, both towns lived under constant fear and danger. Sylvanas stopped at nothing to seize these two strongholds — assassination, spreading plague, raising the dead to create chaos…

Gazing out at the Hillsbrad farms from a distance — the pumpkin patches, the grape trellises, the flocks of sheep crossing the ridges, the bright sun, everything lush and green and golden — the whole place teemed with life.

Jace figured that at least Hillsbrad still looked peaceful and prosperous, for now.

Walking along the dirt path through the fields, quite a few locals resting by the roadside glanced over. Jace had assumed they were staring at the staff on his back. Logically speaking, neither Azuregos nor Krasus had noticed anything strange about that staff — how would these farmers find anything off about it?

Ever since he'd broken free from that out-of-control dream fragment and taken it into his own hands, as long as he didn't use it, Ursolus had hidden itself inside the Black Knight's cloak and looked no different from an ordinary chunky wooden stick.

Only later did he realize he'd been flattering himself. These people were looking at Sola.

Apparently, to the residents of the Hillsbrad farms, elves were still a relatively rare sort of "foreigner."

What's more, when dragons took mortal form, they always made themselves as beautiful as possible. Azuregos's high elf form, though aged, looked even more handsome than the Sun King himself — even a lunatic in questionable mental shape knew to make himself as good-looking as possible. Back when Vereesa was around as a contrast it hadn't been as noticeable, but even in Dalaran Jace had been able to tell that Sola's mortal form was one of the more eye-catching among the high elves.

Right now Sola seemed to feel a bit uncomfortable too. On Kalimdor, most people had been focused on making money, hunting, and fighting, and everyone wore cloaks and hoods to conceal their identities — not many people paid attention to Sola's appearance. But here in Lordaeron, wearing a hood actually drew attention, so once they all came off, a single golden-haired elf attracted far too many stares.

"Those humans are all looking at me," she muttered under her breath. "Did I do something wrong? Is my tail sticking out?"

"Everything's fine," Jace said.

Grede chuckled. "Relax, Sola. I understand completely. When I first left Aerie Peak, I felt like there were humans everywhere and it was unbearable — I felt like everyone was staring at me. Only later did I realize they stare at everything. They just love gawking at outsiders. No malice in it. If you can't handle this, wait till we get to Stormwind — you're really going to have a rough time… You know Lordaeronians see elves on a regular basis, but plenty of people in the Kingdom of Stormwind go their whole lives without seeing a single elf!"

Sola frowned. She was clearly not at all comfortable with the attention heading her way.

"I'm curious — can you turn into a human?" Jace asked.

"Right!" Grede smacked his head. "Why don't you just turn into a human?"

"Because I like the Quel'dorei a bit more," Sola said honestly. "But to avoid drawing too much attention… I can try changing my appearance."

Jace's eyes widened. "You can actually do that?"

Sola nodded, looking around. She fixed her gaze on the back of an empty mill. "I'm going to try changing over there. Keep watch for me, you two — don't let anyone see the process of me shifting forms."

The two of them nodded and went to stand in front of the mill. Two men dressed as militia, carrying wooden spears, walked past. They could hear the two talking about orcs.

Yes, orcs. Jace had almost forgotten — for the townsfolk of Hillsbrad right now, although there was no threat from the Scourge or the Forsaken, there were roving orcs lurking in the forests, plus the orc internment camps to the north…

One was an immediate threat; the other was a ticking time bomb that could go off at any moment. Either way, there was no real peace to be had.

"Hey, you two — human and dwarf — what are you doing in Hillsbrad?"

A woman had appeared in front of them at some point, eyeing them both warily, her voice cold.

"Uh, ma'am." Grede immediately dusted off his backside and stood up, shooting a glance at Jace. Jace studied her — long golden hair, a yellow-green robe. Though her tone was aggressive, her eyes darted a little…

"Your accent's off, Sola," Jace raised an eyebrow. "But just looking at your appearance, you're convincing enough." The woman couldn't help but smile. "I'll get there with practice."

"You nearly scared me to death." Grede wiped the sweat from his forehead. "I thought I was going to be dragged off as this warlock's shifty accomplice!"

"Fuck off," Jace said, exasperated.
