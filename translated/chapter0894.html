Chapter 894: Finally Back in Stormwind

Logically, setting out from the southern coast of Hillsbrad, if your heading was accurate enough, you could fly straight over the sea all the way to Stormwind. In his memory, Stormwind's position was basically due south of Southshore and Hillsbrad. Merchant ships hugged the coast for safety while also dodging the violent weather over Baradin Bay, so their actual route was somewhat roundabout. However, Sola had almost never left the Emerald Dream. Even when she had, it was always on the Kalimdor side. She knew next to nothing about this region. So Jace and Grede recommended she fly southeast first, crossing the narrower stretch of Baradin Bay, make for Menethil Harbor, and then fly south along the coast — following the sea route between Southshore and Stormwind. Otherwise, if she lost her bearings and drifted off course, she might end up in Booty Bay or fly straight into the Maelstrom, and that would be a disaster. They took off from the coast. Jace and Grede calculated that at a young dragon's flying speed, they should reach Menethil Harbor within a day. But they'd taken off in the morning, and by noon Jace and the others could already see a stretch of land in the distance. They'd reached Menethil Harbor already? No, that wasn't right. Menethil Harbor was on a peninsula. If you were flying in from the northwest, you should see an endless coastline stretching from northeast to southwest, spread out flat before your eyes. But what lay ahead was an island. Well, possibly a peninsula jutting toward them — but Jace didn't remember any peninsula like this in these parts. At that moment, Grede shielded his eyes from the sun and muttered, "Is that Tol Barad?" "Tol Barad?" Jace blinked. Tol Barad was an ancient fortress island. A thousand years ago, humans had built strongholds and walls on that island — back during the days of the Arathi Empire. But Jace remembered the game's map. Tol Barad's position seemed to be off the Gilneas peninsula. If you were flying from Southshore toward Menethil, you should be heading in the exact opposite direction. Why had they run straight into it? "Let me see the map, Grede," Jace held out his hand. The dwarf pulled a crumpled, worn leather map from his pack. Jace's eyes darted across the jumbled mess of islands scattered across Baradin Bay. "Which one is…" Grede pointed to an inconspicuous little island in the far eastern corner of the sprawling archipelago. "This one." This was actually Tol Barad — Jace had never even noticed it before. Seeing that Jace seemed a bit lost, the dwarf immediately said, "You don't even know Tol Barad? A great battle was fought here during the Orc Wars. The story of Stromgarde's Redguards fighting the Horde's landing forces to the death is known clear as day even among the dwarves in the eastern mountains! If not for the battle of Tol Barad seriously slowing the Horde's northward advance, Lordaeron itself might have fallen by now!" "Still, Sola, you should fly a bit more to the west. You've drifted off course a little," he reminded her. "If you keep going like this, you'll end up in Dun Modr, not Menethil Harbor." "Got it, Grede. Will there be human soldiers on that island who might want to attack me?" Solamiel asked. "The Arathi abandoned this place long ago," Grede said. "There shouldn't be any orcs left either — most of the orcs here fled to Grim Batol. I think by now it's just a deserted, uninhabited island. Even if there are people up there, there won't be any proper army — at least not one that could take on a young dragon like you." "All right." Solamiel adjusted her course slightly, and the island shifted from head-on to slowly drifting past on the left side of their vision. As they swept past the island at close range, Jace could make out the black walls with collapsed gaps, the vast fields of damaged and abandoned siege engines, and the faint outlines of tattered red banners — all of them Horde war flags, too hastily abandoned to be cleared away. Orgrim Doomhammer's Horde must have poured enormous effort into taking this place. Just from the brutal state of the beach, Jace could roughly picture the scene in his mind. Grede was right. As a fortress, this island could provide mutual support with the dwarven city of Dun Modr on the mainland. If this island wasn't taken, the Horde could never push north to Lordaeron — neither across Thandol Span nor through Baradin Bay. If the island had been where it was in the game — further west, past Gilneas — it wouldn't have served this strategic purpose at all. As Solamiel flew past the island and Jace looked back at its far side, he saw a blurry green banner standing atop the walls. There were even figures moving back and forth along the battlements. Grede clearly saw it too. He nodded. "Kul Tiras." "It's not far from Kul Tiras," Jace said, unsurprised. "If the Arathi abandoned this island, the Kul Tirasians would definitely come take a look." In fact, in the game, this island was already occupied by the Kul Tirasians. They'd built a massive prison on it called Baradin Prison, used to hold insane prisoners — the kind of enormous demons that the Violet Hold couldn't contain. Thinking along those lines, Jace suddenly found himself thinking of Kallrez. If Baradin Prison had already been built by now, and the Wizard Sanctum had discovered Kallrez but couldn't kill him, they'd probably have no choice but to contain him, contact the Kirin Tor, and have him shipped off to Baradin Prison. After all, the Violet Hold was probably better suited to normal-sized magical criminals like Kel'Thuzad. After leaving Tol Barad, it wasn't long before the sea cliffs of Dun Morogh came into view — the distant snowy mountains, the pine forests, the stone keeps the dwarves built up high. This meant Solamiel was flying in the right direction. About three days later, the terrain grew flatter and flatter. The green dragon swooped down into a familiar bay — this must be King's Bay. On the horizon, a blurry expanse of gray-white walls, houses, and towers was already faintly visible. Solamiel kept staring straight ahead without turning her head, as if she'd been stunned into a trance. The closer they flew, the nearer they got. Any closer and they'd start drawing the attention of the sailing ships — even the harbor guards. "Let's not get too close to that area, Sola. Head for the woods on the cliffs to the southwest. Otherwise the gryphon riders might spot us," Jace warned. The green dragon seemed to snap out of her daze and acknowledged him. "Finally back in Stormwind!" Grede stretched. "My god, I actually feel like I haven't had enough. Traveling on Sola's back is incredibly comfortable — no bumps, no danger to worry about, and you can fall asleep the instant you want. It's even more comfortable than sleeping in my own bed…" "It's not often you meet a dwarf who enjoys being magically put to sleep," Jace said casually. "If it by a good friend's magic, it's fine. Still a hell of a lot better than some weirdo who likes being put to sleep by demons," Grede shot back sidelong. Jace guiltily scratched his head. Morophil smiled beside him. Still, hearing the dwarf say that, Jace was suddenly reminded of the green dragon's lodging problem. "Have you thought about where you're going to stay, Sola?" "I don't know," Solamiel said. "Can't I just stay with you two?" "We two don't live together, and our places are too small," Jace said. "How about staying at the house I bought for now?" "I remember you two telling me — that's the house you prepared for your parents, Jace," Solamiel said. "Actually, I could just live in a cave." "Let's not go that far…" Jace said. "That's not the only problem. The issue is we don't even know what condition the renovations are in yet," Grede said. "We haven't gone to check." Jace thought for a moment and said to the dwarf, "Could you help her find a place in the Dwarf District for now? It'd probably be hard to find anywhere else." "You want me to rent a place in the Dwarf District for an elf-looking outsider?!" Grede scratched his head, then remembered something. "Oh, right — Sola can turn into a human now. That makes things a lot easier! Hey, Sola, why don't you just turn into a dwarf? Pick up an Ironforge accent while you're at it — maybe you can fool those dumb Bronzebeards into giving you a discount as a fellow countryman." Grede burst out laughing. The green dragon quietly said, "I don't want to be a dwarf." The dwarf's laughter caught in his throat. He asked unhappily, "Why not?" The green dragon just clammed up and said nothing. Jace fought hard not to laugh. Grede snorted loudly. "I knew it — anyone who likes turning into those pointy-eared freaks definitely has something wrong with their taste!
