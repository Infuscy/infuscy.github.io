Chapter 895: The Long-Awaited Valley of Heroes

After landing in the woods outside the city gates, Jace glanced toward the distant treeline. From this distance and angle, he could just catch a glimpse of the parapet tops and tower peaks. He thought back to the ancient Kaldorei spires and stone pillars he'd seen not long ago on Kalimdor — the ruined walls — and it didn't feel like much time had passed at all. That was entirely thanks to Sola. Traveling with a dragon was a completely different experience. No wonder Grede had been in such a good mood the whole way. Even when Sola did something dumb and offended him, he hardly held a grudge. Right now, Sola was carefully straightening her clothes, making sure her human appearance didn't have any easily identifiable flaws. Jace looked at the silver crescent moon at her forehead — very heavy Night Elf style. "You'd better hide that ornament, Sola. Most people here worship the Holy Light. A pendant of the Moon Goddess Elune would draw a lot of attention in Stormwind. Of course, if that thing is important to you, forget I said anything." Sola touched the ornament at her forehead, took it off, put it around her neck, and tucked it inside her collar. "All right. I just thought it looked nice." Grede nodded. "I was wondering what seemed off about you earlier. It was definitely those ornaments. You look much more normal now." "How about it?" The dwarf looked the two of them over. "Shall we eat first? Bring Sola along." Jace looked at the succubus beside him. "All right, Morophil. We're back in Stormwind. Take Sazeno and head home. Lay low for now." "I wonder when I'll be able to openly accompany my dear master for a meal in Stormwind?" Morophil tilted her head, a touch of disappointment in her voice. "I really miss the time in Everlook, sitting in an inn chatting with all my good companions…" As she said this, the fel fire across her whole body dimmed. The melancholy she was putting on was so convincing that Jace even felt a little bad about shutting her down with a single sentence. War would it be when warlocks and their demons could appear together on the street? In the game, it seemed that even in the original version — twenty years from now — warlocks still couldn't openly show their faces or acknowledge their existence in Stormwind. Considering that Westfall had another demon problem on its hands, the warlocks' reputation in the Kingdom of Stormwind had probably taken another step backward. Morophil needed to be even more careful than when she'd left Stormwind last time. "All I can say is I'll do my best to work toward that day," Jace said. Morophil seemed a little surprised that Jace had answered so seriously. She raised an eyebrow. "Then I look forward to that day, master." Her tone was much more formal and heavy than before. She took the demon bag from Jace's hand, stepped back, and finally vanished completely into the dappled shadows of the trees. Jace remembered the things Vereesa had said to him when they'd parted at the border of Quel'Thalas — how Morophil had been willing to help her voluntarily, without being ordered to. When facing the Black Knights, Morophil had stepped in front of Jace of her own accord, trying to stop the ghosts resurrected by Apocalypse from reaching him — her actual goal being to let Jace help Grede deal with the other Black Knight acolytes… Not only had Vereesa been surprised by it. When Jace had heard Morophil say those things, he'd been a little surprised too. It was just that the battle had been urgent, and he hadn't thought much about it at the time. In any case, Morophil seemed to be changing in some way. It was hard to say when it had started. Was it because of Alexstrasza's blessing? Jace thought of the time on the shore of Borean Tundra, when Sazeno had forced him to glance at Morophil's soul through the mirror stone — the image of life flame and fel energy tearing at each other inside her. Still, Sazeno had said she hadn't changed in any fundamental way. She was still a true demon, a Sayaad. Maybe he should find a chance to have a proper talk with Sazeno about Morophil when she wasn't around. "What's wrong, Jace? What are you thinking about?" Grede waved a hand in front of his face. "Sola said she's hungry. Didn't you hear?" "Ah, let's go." Jace glanced at the green dragon beside him. Entering through Stormwind's main gate, a powerful sense of familiarity hit him head-on. Jace was stunned for a moment before he understood why. The Valley of Heroes' statues were all finished. Across the broad bridge, the five expeditionary force leaders stood on either side — some gazing up at the sky, others staring straight toward the main gate. Lifelike, majestic, and magnificent. Although Jace had never personally laid eyes on any of them except Alleria Windrunner, it felt as if he'd known each of them for over a decade. Even as a Horde player, he'd been awed the first time he'd entered Stormwind's gates in the game. Now that he was truly in Azeroth, although the Valley of Heroes in its current state probably didn't hit him as visually as Eldre'Thalas or the city of Dalaran, the feeling of the game finally coming to life was one of a kind. In other words — it had finally been built to look like it did in the game. The strange taste of intersecting timelines left him dazed for a long time. Beside him, Grede was telling Sola the story of the expeditionary force's Wildhammer dwarf leader, Kurdran Wildhammer. Jace, meanwhile, gazed at the statue of Alleria Windrunner and, further off, the likeness of Marshal Turalyon, thinking about the mage tower and Kallrez. Now that Ursolus was in his hands, at least there was no need to worry about it being seized by someone else — like its previous master Sathiel — and used to recklessly harvest innocent souls for power. Once this demon trouble died down, he could go find the Stonemasons' VanCleef to rebuild the mage tower, then stuff Kallrez inside and quietly research methods of communicating with Draenor. Walking through the streets of the Trade District, Sola gazed at the buildings, shop signs, and passersby on both sides, as if she could never get enough of it. Just then, a patrol of mounted soldiers rode past. The green dragon, who'd never been in a proper human city like this, stared at the beasts with curiosity for quite a while. "I bet these cavalrymen don't have the ability to communicate with these horses, Jace," she said. Jace was about to argue that cavalrymen had to be able to communicate with their horses, but he quickly realized that by "communicate" Sola meant actual communication — talking, conversing — not the simple commands and cues that humans used when working with animals. At that moment, Grede cut in. "Why does there need to be communicating? We can't understand a word gryphon riders say, and we still become brothers who can entrust our lives to each other." "I just feel like if you can't understand what the other party means, you'd worry about misunderstandings." As she spoke, Sola softly said something in an unknown language to a pack horse that had just passed by. The horse actually turned its head to look back at her and gave a soft whinny, even nodding slightly — seemingly in deliberate response. The rider was clearly startled by this unusual scene. He looked around, trying to figure out who had been disturbing his horse. Jace saw this and immediately warned Sola, "Don't show off your abilities in the city, Sola. Just don't cause trouble." "Sorry, I'll remember," Sola said. "I'm still learning how to be a human. I remember that for the Quel'dorei, communicating with animals is perfectly ordinary, right? Vereesa is very good at it." Grede snorted. "She's good at tricking animals over so she can slaughter them for meat. I don't know what you'd call that kind of communication." "At least you ate it too, didn't you?" Jace asked. Grede coughed hard. "So, anyway — you see that fountain over there? Past it is the Gilded Rose! Remember the Gilded Rose I told you about, little sister?" Jace laughed at the dwarf's flustered look. "Later, when we have time, I'll take you to visit the stables in the Mage Quarter, Sola. Let you see how humans communicate with animals. Even if we can't understand each other, it doesn't get in the way." Sola said, "Jace, I feel that although you're a Lordaeronian, Stormwind is more like your homeland." "What makes you say that?" Jace asked. "I don't know," Sola said. "I can tell you feel a deeper attachment here." "How could that be?" Jace gave an awkward laugh. "I grew up in Lordaeron and Brill, Sola. Even if I might end up living here in the future, I've only been here five or six years. I haven't even lost my accent yet." Sola thought for a moment. "Then there must be something truly special about this place, to make you feel such a deep bond." Jace thought to himself that it was definitely because, not long from now, the Kingdom of Lordaeron would very likely be overthrown by the unstoppable plague laid down by the "death king" they'd seen in Northrend. And because when he played his Alliance character, he'd spent most of his time jumping around next to this very fountain — of course he had a deep bond with it. Naturally, he couldn't say that out loud. So he could only take a deep breath, look around the whole square, and put on a show of not knowing how to answer.
