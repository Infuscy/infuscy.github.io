Chapter 896: The New Resident

Although Sola had followed Jace's advice and taken off or tucked away as many of her head ornaments as possible, she still drew some looks the moment she walked into the Gilded Rose.

After all, even in human form, her aura was simply too distinctive. She was, after all, a young dragon who'd barely ever left the Emerald Dream.

In a place like Everlook, the mercenaries who traveled far and wide had seen all manner of strange things — centaur, tauren, goblins, gnolls, even quilboar. A high elf would be nothing unusual even if she crawled down the street on all fours.

But this was Stormwind. Plenty of people here probably hadn't set foot outside Elwynn in their entire lives. Encountering such an exotic-looking human woman naturally aroused curiosity.

Especially with two cloaked, dust-covered humans and a dwarf trailing beside her — clearly not the sort of people to mess with — which made the whole picture even stranger.

The three of them found an empty table in an unoccupied corner and sat down. Grede beckoned to the barmaid and said to Sola, "Let me get some of the good stuff Jace and I always eat. Give it a try."

Sola gave an "mm" and stole a glance at the bar not far away. Jace shot her a look and asked quietly, "I'm a little curious, Sola — where did you see this human appearance of yours?"

"Dalaran," Sola said softly. "There was a very beautiful blonde female mage on the street who left a deep impression on me, so I remembered her looks."

"So you can't just imagine someone and turn into them, right?" Jace observed the people farther off who'd already stopped paying attention to them, then said. "For example, if you wanted to turn into a tauren, you could only turn into Mosa's appearance."

Sola admitted frankly, "That's right. I could probably make small changes, but it would look very unnatural. My magic still needs work. A great mage like Azuregos could do a thousand times better than me."

"He talks about you like that and you still respect him?" Grede snorted. "Didn't Master Krasus say he's just a babbling old weirdo whose brain doesn't work right?"

"But Master Krasus respected him too," Sola said. "Azuregos has always been one of Lord Malygos's most capable guardians. If not for him, the blue dragons' situation would be far more difficult than it is now."

"I don't think he could protect anyone," Grede shook his head, drumming his fingers on the table. Then he looked up. "After we eat, how about taking Sola around Stormwind, Jace? Help her get familiar with the place."

"You take her around," Jace said. "I might need to head to Stormwind Keep to see the king this afternoon. The demon situation can't wait any longer."

"I almost forgot," Grede said. "If there's any trouble you can't handle yourself, remember to come find me in the Dwarf District, Jace."

"Got it," Jace said.

"I hope I can help too, Jace. If it's about dealing with demons, I definitely won't stand on the sidelines," Sola said softly. "The original purpose of guarding the Emerald Dream was to let the Dream's power maintain Azeroth's tranquility. Cleansing the darkness and evil here is a different method, but the goal is the same."

"I'd rather the problems here didn't need your help, Sola," Jace sighed softly. "Hopefully nothing goes wrong," Sola said.

Just then, the barmaid brought out the dishes Jace and Grede always ordered — dwarven cheese, roasted pork chops, roasted fruit, and three mugs of pale ale. Jace picked up his mug and took a sip first. Sola asked, "How far is the Temple of Atal'Hakkar from here?"

"About two weeks on foot," Jace said, propping his chin on his hand. "If you fly, maybe two or three days."

"I wonder who that corrupted green dragon is. I hope it's not Lord Ysondre or his children. If so, I really don't know what they've gotten into," Sola said with worry.

"Unlikely," Jace said. "That corrupted green dragon we saw before was noticeably smaller than Azuregos. I'm guessing Ysondre, as the Queen's most powerful consort, should be quite large. Still, Sola, no matter what's happened there, don't take matters into your own hands — whether it's going in or dealing with something. If the problem inside is something Ysondre can't handle, then you, me, or Grede definitely can't either. If there's real trouble, wait for me to go find the Dragonqueen. Don't let your emotions get the better of you."

"I'm not good at fighting. I won't overestimate my own abilities, Jace," Sola said.

"Once that troll temple business is settled, Sola — do you plan to stay in Stormwind after that?" Jace asked.

Sola took a small sip of ale. "If Queen Ysera won't let me return to the Emerald Dream, then I don't know what I'd do."

"Since your mother and siblings aren't counting on you for anything, you might as well stay in Stormwind and make some money with us," Grede said, sucking meat off a bone. "Dragons live for thousands of years. What's a few years of fun?"

"If you two don't find me a nuisance, I'd love to," Sola said.

"Who'd find a good doctor a nuisance?" Grede laughed. "And one who's willing to heal you without having to thank the Holy Light first!"

Jace thought to himself that this dwarf had absolutely zero chance of ever becoming a paladin.

"Speaking of age." The dwarf drained the ale in front of him, then lowered his voice. "I've always been curious, Sola — as a young dragon, or a half-grown young green dragon, how old are you this year?"

Sola thought about it. Grede added, "In exchange — I'm sixty-seven this year. The Jace Sesso in front of you is twenty."

"Twenty?" Sola blinked. "I knew he was young, but I had no idea he was that young…"

"What, do I look old?" Jace asked.

"No — it's that relative to your age, your power is too formidable, Jace," Sola said.

"Aren't all humans like that?" Grede said, propping his chin on his hand. "It happens all the time — suddenly you hear about some incredibly powerful human nobody's ever heard of popping up in Dalaran or down south, but before you've even learned his name, he's already dead."

"That's true," Sola said. "Except for Aegwynn."

"Except for Aegwynn," Grede laughed.

"As for me — I don't know my exact age," Sola said, shaking her head gently. "I only know I was born not long after the War of the Shifting Sands."

"The War of the Shifting Sands?!" Jace nearly shouted, then caught himself — his voice had been a bit too loud. He glanced toward a few customers near the door to make sure no one was paying attention, then lowered his voice again. "Then aren't you a thousand years old?"

Sola said, "Green dragon ages are hard to calculate that way. We spend all our time in the Emerald Dream, Jace, and time in the Dream is very vague… it doesn't pass the same way as it does here. This is exactly why those druids can sleep for hundreds of the real world's years — in the Dream, they don't feel as though they've stayed that long."

"Then you're still a thousand years old," Grede said. "A thousand-year-old young dragon."

"Since you put it that way…" Sola sighed in resignation.

"I can't believe I've been calling a thousand-year-old ancient creature 'little sister,'" Grede muttered to himself.

Jace was completely speechless. Sola's whole explanation about the Dream hadn't been very convincing to him either.

After eating, Sola went with Grede to find a place in the Dwarf District. Jace headed straight for the canal.

He didn't plan to see the king directly. Instead, he wanted to stop by the Slaughtered Lamb first. Before meeting the king to discuss demon-related matters, he needed to confirm these things had nothing to do with the Dagger Society. And the Dagger Society — especially Erland — should know something about the situation. Better to touch base with them first.

While he was at it, he'd hand over the formula he'd gotten from the furbolgs to Ursula, and have her study it to see whether this medicine was truly a Titan Potion.

Although a demon plague had broken out in Moonbrook, at least the Mage Quarter still looked normal — no sign of tension. Students and teachers sat on the lawns or stone benches, chatting and laughing, discussing new knowledge from class or their own insights on magic.

But when he arrived at the door of the Slaughtered Lamb, he found it shut tight.

It wasn't the middle of the night. Tharle Mor never closed at this hour — and the door had been locked from the outside.

Jace looked up and down both sides of the street. No one was following him, no demons tailing him. He used Sense Demons to glance inside the tavern and couldn't detect the presence of a single demon. Basadim was gone too.

Where had all the people from the Slaughtered Lamb gone? Was it related to this demon plague?

Then he noticed a notice posted over the old recruitment-target advertisement next to the door. It read "Closed" in both Common and Dwarvish. The handwriting looked like Ursula's. Jace felt a little relieved.

They probably hadn't come to harm. Most likely they'd moved to lay low until things blew over.

Still, Jace became much more cautious. He didn't linger, to avoid making passersby suspicious that he'd come here for anything other than a drink. Then he returned to his lodgings and said to Morophil, "Come with me."

"Does master plan to go out and do something exciting?" Morophil asked with a smile.

Jace was in no mood for her flirtation. He said flatly, "The Dagger Society is in trouble. I'm worried someone's watching me too. Turn invisible and watch my back. See if anyone's paying attention to where I'm going."

The succubus said, a little disappointed, "No problem, master. Hide-and-seek works too."

With Morophil watching his back, Jace felt a lot more at ease. He changed into a lighter set of clothes. He'd planned to leave Ursolus at home for now, but in the end he just wrapped it in a different cloak and brought it on his back again. He simply couldn't let the thing leave his sight for even a second — at the very least it couldn't be out of both his and Morophil's watch at the same time.

After heading out, he first made a detour to the Trade District to order a new leather sword sheath, then circled around to the Mage Quarter canal on his way to Galders's place. After confirming through his Soul Link with Morophil that no one was tailing him, he knocked on the door.

Unsurprisingly, no one answered.

Gazing out at the canal toward the distant Stormwind Stockades, it occurred to him that he should go ask SI:7.

He'd been the one to broker the cooperation between the Dagger Society warlocks and SI:7 — using felhounds to hunt the black dragonkin hiding throughout the kingdom. Now that all the people in the Slaughtered Lamb had vanished, they ought to know something.
