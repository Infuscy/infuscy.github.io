Chapter 899: The Demon "Master"

"Master Andromath."

"Jace?"

Malin wasn't in his study, so Jace had come to the Wizard's Sanctum. Andromath's first reaction upon seeing Jace was clearly one of surprise — but the surprise quickly faded, replaced by a complicated expression.

Jace had the feeling this trip wasn't going to go particularly smoothly.

"I heard you went to Kalimdor. Master Malin has been hoping to see you again," Andromath said, then offered a polite smile.

"I was just looking for Master Malin," Jace said.

"He's in class." Andromath glanced at his pocket watch. "It'll be a while. If I recall correctly, he should be teaching Counterspell to a group of apprentices today. You know how it is…"

"Yes. That's a rather complex and difficult course," Jace said with a slight sigh. "But this matter can be discussed with Master Andromath too."

Andromath's smile faded. "You're here about Moonbrook, aren't you?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"This matter is currently being handled primarily by Master Maginor Dumas," the black-haired mage said. "A group of cultists were hiding deep in the Moonbrook mines conducting demon summonings. Some extremely powerful demons were summoned. But the situation is now under control. No need for concern."

His tone grew so distant so abruptly that Jace found it hard to adapt. "Is there anything I can help with, Master Andromath?"

"I don't think so. The Wizard's Sanctum can handle this," Andromath said casually.

Although Jace's expression didn't change, those words made him feel as though he'd already been excluded from the Wizard's Sanctum. Of course, strictly speaking, he truly wasn't one of the Wizard's Sanctum's people anymore.

"I have a student coming to see me next. I must go, Jace. Still, thank you for coming to the Wizard's Sanctum to offer your help." Andromath finished in a muffled tone and turned toward the hall door.

"It's what I should do, Master… By the way — what floor is Master Malin on?" Jace pressed.

"Second floor." Andromath tossed the words over his shoulder casually and pushed the door open. If he weren't the leader of the Wizard's Sanctum and were just an ordinary mage, Jace would have thought he was fleeing the place.

Several other people in the hall were stealing glances toward them. When their eyes met Jace's, they nodded respectfully.

Arriving on the second floor of the mage tower, Jace leaned against the corridor railing and casually picked up a copy of A Study on the Invention and Legacy of Ice Barrier to read, treating it like a storybook.

Sometime later, a dark figure blocked the light in front of him.

"Mr. Sesso?"

Jace cleared his throat and looked up. It was Malin — the bald old mage was standing right in front of him, craning his neck to stare up at him. Without realizing it, Jace had grown that much taller than him.

"Master." Jace smiled sheepishly and put the book back on the desk beside him.

Malin glanced at it and said, "Summer's already over. Isn't it a bit late to be learning this spell on short notice?"

"Such a powerful spell as Ice Barrier seems like overkill for cooling off," Jace said with a smile.

"For combat, wouldn't Conjure Water do just fine?" Malin said.

Damn it. Amy Malin must have told him every detail of his fight with that female demon in the Blasted Lands mines. A few of the apprentice mages and young students standing nearby chatting all burst out laughing at once, making Jace flush a little.

Malin looked at him. "You must have some urgent business to be waiting for me here. Come into the corridor. Not in the hallway — people are coming and going."

Jace followed Malin to an unoccupied window in the corridor. From here you could see straight out into Elwynn Forest. Leaning out, you could even catch a glimpse of the Nazferiti River's golden shimmer.

"Speak," Malin said.

"It's about Moonbrook, Master," he said bluntly. "Master Andromath told me the situation is already under control, but whether from the king or from someone else, I get the sense this matter is far from over. Even the king told me he felt you were hiding something."

"I think…" Jace paused. "The king asking me to come to the Wizard's Sanctum to understand the situation felt as though he were using me to go against you. It's really not a comfortable feeling."

Malin said nothing. Jace continued. "And I feel Master Andromath is keeping things from me too. Does the Wizard's Sanctum not trust me anymore? So, do you know something about what's going on? If you tell me not to get involved in this matter, I won't come bother the Wizard's Sanctum again."

"Not come bother the Wizard's Sanctum again," Malin repeated the words. "But you'll keep investigating this matter in private until you've turned the whole thing inside out, isn't that right, Mr. Sesso?"

Jace raised an eyebrow. "That's right. Demons, warlocks, dark magic, summonings — I can't stand aside, Master."

Malin said, "But it's not that the Wizard's Sanctum doesn't trust you. It trusts you enough."

"After our investigations and interrogations, countless clues point to you. Many warlocks claim their summoning methods and magic were all learned from you. Including some of the summoning methods, diagrams, and Demonic incantations we found — they're clearly in your handwriting, Mr. Sesso. If we didn't trust you, what would be waiting for you when you returned to Stormwind would be the cold iron bars of a prison, not this lovely window with a view of the forest."

Jace was stunned. Could that "guide" actually be himself?

Those things in his handwriting were most likely spread by the group of warlocks who'd taken part in summoning Kallrez together. He'd made quite a few modifications to that summoning ritual at the time and shared some notes and precautions.

Had he unwittingly become the founding father of a school?

No — if the guide really was him, there'd be no reason for them to abandon Kallrez, a doom lord who could teach them so much about the Twisting Nether, and instead run off to Moonbrook.

"Think about what would happen if these clues were reported to the Kirin Tor, to the crown, and to the privy council, Mr. Sesso," Malin continued. "The king may trust you, but not all the nobles do. Telling you this is a serious mistake, because the Wizard's Sanctum already has plans to investigate you. It's just that no one is willing to investigate a dark wizard who killed Deathwing, so there's no suitable candidate yet."

"By the way — those warlocks say a very powerful succubus is protecting you at all times. Is that true?" Malin asked with a frown.

"No," Jace shook his head.

"I thought so," Malin relaxed his brow. "Their descriptions of that demon were certainly exaggerated."

"The demon is real," Jace said. "Just not protecting me at all times. For example, she's not by my side right now."

Malin coughed uncomfortably and glanced at the corridor shadows that were growing darker as the sun set. "It seems it was wise not to send someone to follow you. Otherwise we might well have lost a brave mage capable of handling field work."

"Master — have all the warlocks who took part in the summonings been caught?" Jace asked.

"Compared to demons, they themselves aren't difficult to deal with. They've either been captured or killed," Malin said. "But we both know there are definitely more than what's on the surface, right? If you insist on going to Moonbrook to investigate this matter, be careful. If they're not worshipping you, then they're targeting you."

"I know, Master," Jace said.
