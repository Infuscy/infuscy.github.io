## Chapter 90: Fire and Icy Blood

What spell was it — invisibility?

Well, even if it's invisibility, you've still got to jump out and hit me. Jace kept his eyes locked on the room in front of him, ready to strike the moment an apparition appeared and then draw his blade. He didn't remember the naga carrying any weapon. She was going to fight him bare-handed?

So what if she was a serpent-woman? Now that her tail was chopped off, she wasn't going to strangle him with it, was she?

Oh, right — she still had claws…

But the apparition he was waiting for never appeared. Instead, a sudden explosive crash made his whole body flinch.

He looked toward the door. The pitch-black second-floor hallway was instantly lit up by firelight.

The naga started laughing — laughing like a maniac. Jace understood. She was going to burn this place down!

She had water magic to shield herself and escape, but Jace had zero means of protecting himself. At that point the naga would only need to block the door…

But the flames didn't catch right away. The house was far too damp — probably not as easy to ignite as an ordinary building.

Sure enough, the naga quickly began a second casting. He had no idea what fire spell she was using, but the whole house was already thick with black smoke. At this rate it was going to catch for certain, and in the end even if he didn't burn to death, he'd be smoked out…

Jace pinpointed the sea-witch's location by the sound of her chanting and charged straight out the door, stepping right through the blood pooled across the floor.

The moment he entered the room, he saw the naga, her back to him, suddenly turn her head. Her expression was terrifyingly calm.

She'd already picked up a rusty harpoon from god knew where, aimed right at Jace as he walked through the door. The black smoke rising from the flames all around her made her look like a lava demon straight out of hell!

— FEARKEKT!

Jace's incantation was flawlessly precise and fast. The naga's throwing motion was already underway, so she had no chance to react with a Counterspell. Her calm expression shattered in an instant.

She let out a choked wail and toppled backward. Even with four arms, she couldn't hold on to that harpoon anymore!

The naga cried out, slamming into the wall behind her, trying to escape — but there was no escape. Blood gushing from her severed tail doused a few of the flames that had already caught. Even thicker, fouler smoke filled the entire house.

"Hss… hss!"

The sea-witch's arms kept clawing at the wall behind her. Jace gritted his teeth, forced himself to stay calm, rushed forward, and kicked her in the back, knocking the already off-balance, tail-less sea-witch to the ground. He stepped on her head and brought the blade down on her neck — but it didn't chop all the way through. The naga broke free of Jace with some freakish strength, the half-severed neck still attached to the head by a strip of skin, and madly bolted for the stairs, sobbing and shrieking as she fled — though all that came out were strange, clicking "kacha-kacha" sounds.

Jace wanted to give chase, but his legs felt like they'd been filled with lead. A wave of intense nausea surged up.

This was terror. Pure terror.

From the very start he'd been forcing himself to ignore these horrifying scenes, to suppress every negative emotion, all so he could survive this close-quarters fight with the sea-witch!

But when that naga — half her neck severed, her head dangling by a flap of skin — staggered through the flames with her mangled body, sobbing and wailing as she madly fled downstairs, Jace's will was on the verge of collapse.

Was this the effect of a Fear spell? Or real fear? Did the naga's wail have a fear-inducing effect?

Or was there simply no difference between the two?

But Jace knew one thing for certain — the Shadow Protection Potion's effect had definitely worn off by now.

No. He couldn't let this naga get away. At the very least he had to get out of here as soon as possible, seeing as the fire had already caught on the second floor.

Jace shut his eyes, then charged down the stairs, following the trail of blood lit by the firelight all the way to the ground floor. Out in the open space some fifty or sixty meters away, the naga was thrashing wildly on the ground. No — the naga's body was thrashing.

Her head had been shaken loose by the violent motion and now lay tilted against a sand-covered window frame, its ferocious expression frozen in place.

Jace's fingers went slack and the blade dropped to the ground.

He walked over to a stone slab nearby and sat down, rubbing his face again and again, trying to calm down as quickly as possible.

The naga's mutilated body lay not far off, still twitching. The taut curves of muscle, the full chest, the slick scales and the mucus-slicked skin all shimmered faintly in the moonlight, streaked with traces of blood.

Jace stared at that body, thinking of only one thing — he couldn't have risked his life for nothing. He had to take something from this. He couldn't just walk away because the naga's corpse scared him.

Fighting back the nausea, he waited until the body stopped moving, then walked up to the naga and kicked the body with his foot — only for the naga's hand to latch straight onto his ankle!

Jace jumped clean into the air and kicked frantically several times before finally breaking free, even kicking the naga's arm out of joint.

This naga seemed to be nothing more than a low-ranking sea-witch. No jewelry, no staff — nothing but a bare body, except for a string of beads on her head.

"A bead string…" Jace muttered, wanting to move closer to the head — but the head was staring straight at him.

Was she watching him, or had she just happened to be looking that way when she died?

Jace took a few steps to the side and noticed the eyes followed him over.

He cursed under his breath, rubbed his face, and looked again — still staring right at him.

No illusion whatsoever.

"Why won't you just die?! Can't you stay dead?!"

He thought for a long while, then suddenly remembered — he hadn't come here alone, had he?

"Sazeno. Get out here."

The imp finally poked his head out of the bag and looked at the scene outside.

"That's awful scary, Master. What is that thing…"

"So you do get scared, huh?"

"If Master's not scared, I'm not scared!" The imp hopped out of the bag. "What does Master need me to do?"

"Here." Jace picked up the blade and tossed it to him. "Cut off the fins and bring me that bead string."

"Yes, sir!" The imp mustered all his strength to drag the blade along and started toward the naga's head. Halfway there, the naga's eyes suddenly locked onto him.

"Why's she still alive?!" Sazeno grumbled, stopping dead in his tracks.

"How are you more of a coward than me?!"

Jace took two huge breaths, spat on his hands, puffed out his chest, and strode over to snatch the blade back. He shut his eyes and brought it down on the naga's forehead. Bunk!

The blade bit in but didn't split the skull open — but the arcane glow in the naga's eyes finally dimmed.

Man and demon both breathed a lot easier.

Looking down from above, the bead string seemed like it could be lifted right off — no need to cut anything away.

Jace stared into the naga's eyes and slowly worked the bead string free. This time the naga made no movement at all. She was truly dead.

It was a shame Grede hadn't witnessed this with his own eyes — now that would've been something.

No, Jace thought. He had to bring something back, at least — something that could prove he'd killed a naga. That pearl bead string alone definitely wasn't enough.

As for the head… he really didn't want to touch it again, let alone drag the whole thing back.

Might as well take some scales.

Right — naga scales. Could they be used in alchemy?

"Sazenor," Jace said. "Time to earn your keep."
