Chapter 901: Brimming with Mana, and Concealable

They arrived at the Altar of Storms in the evening. The Burning Steppes was shrouded in dark clouds to begin with. At night, arriving at this deep, remote valley — a place no creature wanted to set foot in except for spiders bigger than wild dogs and feral wargs — it was nearly pitch black. Only the magma glowing like blood, seeping from the fissures created by Blackrock Mountain's explosion, illuminated the surrounding area. If anything, it made the place even more ghastly and terrifying. But no matter how grim or fearsome it was, it couldn't hold a candle to the female demon walking beside Jace on cloven hooves. And speaking of that demon, even the most terrifying existence deep in this eerie valley was his servant. Of course, the most dangerous thing of all was the object on his back that looked the most inconspicuous. At that moment, a pitch-black, towering, and bulky figure appeared in the distance. He was dressed in a black robe and holding a long staff whose head couldn't be made out clearly, watching this way. Jace knew that was the orc Carohl. "My succubus servant told me the news of your return, Jace Sesso. Welcome." "Thank you. I heard most of the warlocks here have left, Carohl." Jace climbed the steps up to the platform where the orc stood and spoke in Orcish. "Why didn't you leave with them?" The orc smiled upon hearing Jace speak Orcish and replied in the same language: "Like Martha Stein in Dalaran, we walk this path in pursuit of greater knowledge — not simply to grow more powerful or gain status. Otherwise, what difference would there be between me and someone like Gul'dan?" As he walked alongside Jace, he continued. "Lord Kallrez isn't willing to teach many spells — I suspect that's the reason the other warlocks left. But he's at least willing to share many things about the Twisting Nether that I'd never even imagined. About the Burning Legion, about demons… a lot of it is very interesting, Jace Sesso. It even makes me reexamine myself and my entire race, and think about what demons have brought us. It's a pity I have no kindred spirits to share it with. The entire tribe has grown more and more insane following Rend Blackhand." Hearing him say this, Jace found himself intrigued by this man. "Did you know Orgrim Doomhammer escaped Lordaeron?" he asked in Orcish. "I know." Carohl tucked his sharp fangs back behind his lips and shifted his mouth. "But Orgrim Doomhammer once ordered the destruction of the Shadow Council. I don't think he'd accept people like us again." "But you left the Shadow Council too, didn't you?" Jace said. "If someone who understands demonic magic is willing to help his own race eliminate the irrationality that demonic magic brings — like an anti-Gul'dan — I think there'd be orcs who've come to their senses who'd be willing to accept you." "I don't know, but… you seem to understand orcs quite well," Carohl narrowed his eyes. "Why are you willing to help me — even help us?" "I'm not putting on any act of being a good person." Jace looked at the massive demon crouching below the altar. "If one day Orgrim rises again — and rises without being goaded by demons or other powers — he might be able to help the Alliance deal with Blackrock Spire and the remaining Dragonmaw orcs in Khaz Modan. After all, your tribe needs a 'legitimate line' too, doesn't it?" Carohl laughed. "You're a candid fellow, Jace Sesso. Very different from other warlocks." "Not really," Jace replied. Then he asked, "How much do you know about the Moonbrook affair?" "Moonbrook is far from here," Carohl said. "I know I look like the type who could be involved in that kind of calamity, but I really haven't heard much about that summoning incident. What little we know are just bits and pieces that Mosur and Demetra Kraus mentioned when they came back here. You have to understand — human kings don't trust orcs, and neither do human warlocks." "So Mosur and Demetra Kraus were involved in this?" Jace asked. "Where are them now?" The orc shook his head. Jace said, "I'm planning to take this demon away, Carohl. Because of this incident, the Kingdom of Stormwind is becoming more and more sensitive about demons. Although this place isn't within the kingdom's borders, it's not that far away either. And I'm worried the Blackrock Spire orcs might get ideas — like using this opportunity to retake this altar. In any case, Kallrez isn't safe here anymore." "A pity, but I have to admit this is a very wise decision," the orc said. "Then where do you plan to take him?" "I'm still considering it," Jace said. "Or maybe I'll see if he has any opinions of his own." "I won't keep you, then," the orc said. Arriving between the Altar of Storms, Jace looked up at the slowly raising head of the demon. It seemed as though Kallrez hadn't made any sort of movement in a long time. As he stretched, dirt came pouring off him in streams — all the black volcanic ash that drifted across the Burning Steppes. Morophil couldn't help but laugh at his miserable state. The demon said in a heavy voice, "This is not the most tragic and humiliating torment I've endured, little Sayaad. No need to look at me with such pity." "I don't pity you because you're trapped here, Your Excellency Kallrez," Morophil said, her tone dripping with sarcasm. "Right, master?" she looked at Jace. Jace nodded, took the staff off his back, and glanced toward the heights of the Altar of Storms. The orc Carohl had already returned to his quarters. He slowly unwrapped the cloth binding the staff. The head of the staff was even a little stuck together from bloodstains, making Jace feel as though he were unwrapping a wound's bandage. A strange, barely-odor-like stench wafted out. The blood looked like real blood. It was hard to imagine the blood seeping out from within the staff — as if it were a living creature that had been injured, or as if its evil magic had grown dark enough to condense into something like blood. Perhaps because Jace's expression was a little pained, the great demon couldn't help but let out a snickering laugh. "What are you afraid of, Jace Sesso? Afraid of your own staff?" But before he could laugh a third time, his narrow demon eye suddenly locked onto the exposed demon skeleton. The eerie eye sockets, the curved horns, the connected vertebrae — revealed bit by bit. Kallrez looked as though he'd had his throat seized. His lips and the muscles of his face froze in place, and he couldn't make a single sound. "Do you recognize this, I say, Lord Kallrez?" Morophil looked up and asked. "Help me check — is this thing real?" Jace said. "The bones of the Master of Manton," Kallrez murmured in Demonic, almost as though making an announcement. "This is the skull of Ursolus. You truly found the Harvester of Evil…" "It's thanks to you," Jace said. "If you hadn't provided the clues, I might never have found this thing, Kallrez." "No, it's not…" Kallrez immediately stammered in panic, but then he fell silent again, staring at the staff with his gaping maw full of fangs, as if his power had been cut off. Jace noticed blood-like tears welling up and swirling in the demon's skull eye sockets. At that moment, the massive demon said, "It is yours. Ursolus has acknowledged you as its new master." "How do you know?" Jace asked. "Obviously," Kallrez leaned forward. "I can help you further understand its power. If you can fully unleash the power of this staff, then you won't need to fear… Kil'jaeden, or even the Burning Legion! And so will I, my master. I'm willing to assist you, until… until…" "Until Ursolus devours my soul," Jace said. "You understand it better than I expected," Kallrez said. "But I know the doom lord Karuphun — Ursolus's fourth master, former king of the Eredun, former lord of Kazzak. He wielded this weapon and served the Dark Master for over four thousand years, until a treacherous Nathrezim assassinated him and left him gravely wounded, and only then was he devoured by Ursolus. You're just a mortal. Did you ever expect yourself to live to four thousand, my master?" "We'll talk about that later," Jace said, rewrapping the staff. "Right now there are more important things." "As you command, master," Kallrez said. "What do you wish to destroy, whom do you wish to kill…" Jace raised an eyebrow. He was quite surprised by Kallrez's transformation. It seemed that the status of this thing called Ursolus within the Burning Legion was indeed beyond doubt. "Just moving him to a hiding place." The demon said with some dissatisfaction, "Now that you hold Ursolus and have the aid of me, doom lord Kallrez, there's no need for you to hide at all…" Morophil smiled and said in a faintly mocking tone, "This is precisely why the Burning Legion has failed again and again in their attempts to invade Azeroth. You don't have the slightest understanding of this place, Eredun." Kallrez snorted coldly. "Clearly, the Burning Legion has nothing to do with me anymore, Sayaad." Morophil glared. "Your change of attitude is truly astonishingly fast. Although I never understood why you had any sense of belonging to that bunch of raving lunatics of the Burning Legion in the first place…" "This is a good start, Kallrez," Jace said. The great demon looked at Jace. "Even though your timidity displeases me somewhat, I will follow your wishes. But the Altar of Storms is a place brimming with powerful mana. Simply abandoning it would be too great a waste. If you're going to hide me somewhere, let it at least be a place rich in mana, so I can recover the power I lost crossing the portal…" Jace said, "Rich in mana and able to hide are two contradictory conditions. Every place in this world that's rich in mana is crawling with people by now. There's no room left for us." "I do have an idea, master — if you're willing to hear it!" Sazeno poked his head out and said. "What is it?" "Deadwind Pass!" Sazeno said. "Remember that place full of ogres, master? It's saturated with dark power leaking from Karazhan, but besides the dim-witted ogres, nobody dares go there! And nobody would think of going there to look for a hidden great demon! I'm thinking — the great Lord Kallrez surely wouldn't be afraid of a few ogres!"
