Chapter 902: Finalizing the Route

Deadwind Pass?

Jace looked at the imp poking its head out of the bag. Honestly, it hadn't even crossed his mind to think in that direction.

Karazhan had always been a place he wanted to avoid. That mysterious tower, though masterless now, remained a terrifying presence — so profound that one could barely stand to look at it directly.

Even the most powerful mages in Dalaran, Antonidas included, went pale at the mention of it.

But precisely for that reason, it was indeed a good place for someone bold enough to lie low. Otherwise, so many ogres wouldn't have been able to hole up there for so long without anyone being able to deal with them.

The lie he'd fabricated at the orcish-language exchange meeting hosted by Kel'Thuzad — about finding orc corpses in Deadwind Pass — still hadn't raised anyone's suspicions.

Because absolutely no mage was willing to go near the place. They'd feared Medivh in the past; now they feared Medivh's shadow.

In the game, Deadwind Pass was a rather small zone, but here it was a genuinely vast valley. There were even a few abandoned villages and settlements inside, along with several tracts of dead forest.

He and Grede had learned that lesson last time — walking for two or three days inside, and you'd be lucky to even catch a glimpse of Karazhan's tower, and even then only by illusion.

Such a large, dark valley forest really was a good place to stash something wicked. Whether it had mana was secondary; the key point was that no one dared go there.

The only thing worth noting was the organization within the Kirin Tor responsible for investigating Karazhan's tower — the Violet Eye.

But the Violet Eye wasn't some highly efficient, massive organization. Even twenty years from now, they'd just be camped outside Karazhan on watch. Aside from someone like Dar'Khan, who was foolhardy enough to actually rush in searching for Ursolus and exploring the Eredun language, there were few mages who truly dared to explore the interior.

So their activities weren't frequent at this point either. As long as they stayed away from Karazhan's tower and hid somewhere in the valley, it should be workable. Holing up in one of those ogre-infested areas ought to be feasible.

All in all, Jace had never imagined he'd actually need to hide a four-to-five-meter-tall demon lord near Karazhan's tower. Honestly, this was probably a lot more audacious than anything Medivh had done back in the day.

"Where exactly is this place you're talking about?" Karlez murmured.

"Look south. Further south… further south still… I don't know if you can sense that endless darkness, Lord Karlez!" Sazeno shrieked, craning his neck. "That's the darkness radiating from Karazhan! Oh yes, I should tell you — you seem to have some connection to Karazhan's previous, deceased owner, Medivh."

"Many believe Sargeras's soul resided within Medivh's body, Karlez," Jace said.

"So it's that tower."

Karlez drew a heavy breath. "I'm beginning to look forward to feeling the dark power there, master."

This massive demon lord agreed without much deliberation. It seemed Sazeno's talk about "the dark power left behind by Sargeras" was genuinely irresistible to him.

"But how am I supposed to get you there?" Jace asked. "We're at least several hundred kilometers from Deadwind Pass. We'd have to cross the Redridge Mountains, then cross several more dark valleys. Worst of all, Stormwind's national territory lies right in the middle. I can't find that many warlocks to summon you over, and more importantly, not that many warlocks would be willing to follow me into Deadwind Pass."

Karlez spread his wings, only to be locked down hard by the binding circle. His whole body shuddered in pain.

"I can fly there myself, master."

"No way. You're a demon that size — flying straight over Lakeshire and across Lake Everstill? Are you out of your mind, or am I?" Jace said.

Karlez laughed twice. "I feel like I can almost hear Ursolus mocking your cowardice, timid master."

"Your reputation among the humans of Stormwind is already as dark as it gets anyway," Sazeno said, raising his hands excitedly. "Then let the mighty Lord Karlez demonstrate your power before them once more, and proclaim to the entire world—"

Jace smacked him on the head, cutting off his incantation. "Proclaim that I've got a death wish."

"Ah… er…" The imp covered its mouth.

"You could fly from here to the easternmost edge of this scorched earth," Morophil said. "From there, head south, cross the Redridge Mountains, and enter the gnoll forest east of Stonewatch, darling. Don't you remember? The forest we passed through when we went looking for demon remnants. We entered the Black Marsh from there. That's outside the Kingdom of Stormwind's borders, and there aren't many people there anyway."

"Since the people in Lakeshire say the demons at the Dark Portal have mostly been wiped out, and the elven and dwarven armies have already withdrawn, and there haven't been any mercenary bands hunting demons, overall it's a quiet area. So skirting around the entire Lake Everstill from that side — would that work?"

Morophil was talking about the place he and Vereesa had crossed on their way to the Badlands to find the death knights, about three to four days' walk from Lakeshire.

Aside from the occasional dwarf prospector passing through, there really weren't many humans willing to explore over there.

This was a viable travel route — no risk of being spotted by other humans. Morophil's reminder had more or less strengthened Jace's resolve.

After all, the Altar of Storms was known to too many warlocks; staying here definitely wasn't an option. And the mage tower wasn't finished being renovated yet, and he couldn't find anyone from the Dagger Society to help him summon and transport Karlez elsewhere. Deadwind Pass seemed like the most suitable temporary hiding spot for now.

"Fine then, Karlez. I'll break this binding circle and let you out," Jace said.

"Long overdue," Karlez muttered in Eredun.

Back at the warlock camp, Jace found the orc Carohl sitting there eating, and said, "I'm planning to release Karlez, Carohl. So I'm giving you a heads-up. Is there anything else you need to say to that demon?"

"No." The orc shook his head. "Martha Stein went to Stormwind to purchase spell components. When she gets back and sees that Eredun lord is gone, what am I supposed to tell her?"

"Tell her Jace Sesso released the great demon and sent him back to the Twisting Nether," Jace said.

"No one would believe that," the orc said with a laugh.

"I don't care," Jace said.

The orc grunted. "About that thing you mentioned to me earlier, Jace Sesso. About the Warchief. I've been thinking — I should head north to meet him, and express my willingness to help the Horde's survivors break free of the demonic curse. If he intends to kill me, then perhaps that's the price I must pay for all the harm I've done to my own kind by following the Shadow Council."

"I don't know if he'd intend to kill you," Jace said. "But I do know that the Orgrim Doomhammer of today isn't the 'Warchief' of the past anymore. The defeat at Blackrock and the Lordaeron captivity changed him a lot. Did you know that since his escape from prison two or three years ago, he's never once tried to rob or harm a human?"

The orc looked up at Jace, stopped chewing, and after a long moment, swallowed.

"And if you can't find Doomhammer," Jace continued, "try looking in the Alterac Valley. Another orc clan lives there. I think they still have shamans among them."

Carohl coughed out a laugh. "Are you sure your orcish isn't flawed? 'Shaman' doesn't mean 'mage' in orcish."

"I'm sure it's 'shaman,' and I'm sure I know what that is," Jace said.

Carohl shook his head. "Impossible. The elements have already forsaken us."

"The Frostwolf clan," Jace said.

The orc lowered his head in thought, then seemed to mutter to himself, "So those 'traitors' fled to the deep mountains of Alterac. They really do love hiding in snowy mountains. I agree with you — if there really are still shamans in the Horde… they're most likely with them."

He looked up. "Thank you for telling me these important things. I don't know if I'll actually make it there safely, but… thank you all the same. I know you must have demands of your own too. Rest assured — I won't tell anyone about you and the demon. Not warlocks, not other humans, not orcs."

"Then I wish you a smooth journey."

"I'll remember your name, Jace Sesso."

"Don't tell the people of Lordaeron you know me."

"I understand what you mean." The orc grinned.
