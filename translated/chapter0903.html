Chapter 903: The High King

In the middle of the Altar of Storms, Jace and Morophil worked together to break the integrity of the binding circle. The magma surrounding the entire altar began to churn and surge along with the destabilizing demon magic, and a suffocating stench of sulfur brewed throughout the valley.

Karlez tried to stand, but the residual power of the circle forced him down to one knee again. This time, though, he held his ground, pushing little by little against the binding force, standing straighter and straighter until he finally tore through the circle's power entirely. A thunderous rumble echoed all around the altar.

The glow of the binding circle faded completely, and the great demon stood upright once more. Jace craned his neck to look up at the demon as he straightened to his full height — it even felt like facing Doom Lord Kazzak in the game.

"How do you feel?" Jace asked.

Karlez coughed twice. "I thought I'd be sitting in this damn place for hundreds, even thousands of years. Didn't expect it to be only half a year. I've got to say, you short-lived races really are impatient."

He extended his hand. "Onto my pauldron, tiny master. I can carry you in flight."

Jace leaned forward, but the demon suddenly grabbed him by the waist and lifted him whole up past his head. For a moment Jace was convinced the bastard was about to bite his head off — he nearly started chanting Shadowstep on the spot.

Karlez, however, obediently set him down on his broad shoulder guard. Jace grabbed the fel-iron chains that fastened the pauldron to the demon's misshapen body, made sure he was steady, and by then Morophil had already climbed up his chest plate.

"Your name is Morophil," Karlez said. "I sense your power is strong, demon huntress."

"I've no interest in you ugly brutes, Lord Karlez. Save the flattery," Morophil crouched beside Jace. "Though my master almost never praises me, and doesn't know how to say a kind word, I'm not yet desperate enough for that…"

"Who said I never praise you?" Jace asked.

"Almost," Morophil shot him an unhappy look.

Karlez said irritably, "I've no interest in succubi either, self-important little wretch. You don't actually think your charm magic would work on a being like me, do you?"

"Charm magic might not work. Enslavement magic, on the other hand, is quite effective," Morophil said softly.

Karlez had that flung back at him and ground his teeth hard. "I serve Master Jace Sesso of my own free will now…"

"Hmph." The succubus snorted in reply.

The doom lord murmured, "I'm just curious — before you met Master Jace Sesso, whom did you serve? It's rare to hear of a demon huntress serving a mortal."

"Does it matter?" Morophil's voice turned cold. "Whomever I served before has nothing to do with you." The demon chuckled darkly a couple of times, his chest vibrating enough that Jace could feel an itch under his feet just standing on his shoulder.

Still, Jace glanced at Morophil. He didn't know why the succubus was unwilling to tell this former senior officer of the Burning Legion that she'd served Brutallus — maybe because Brutallus had now joined the Burning Legion, and she'd lose face?

But since she didn't want to say, Jace had no intention of forcibly digging up her "dark history" either.

The demon spread his wings and shot into the air. Jace barely had time to react before he nearly got flung right off. The demon's strength was too immense, and he couldn't care less about what was clinging to him — just flapping his wings and soaring higher, his whole body swaying with each massive downstroke, and within a few beats he had Jace reeling.

What made it worse was that this massive brute radiated increasingly swelling fel energy the faster he flew. For a creature this heavy, there was no way he could fly this fast without magic.

Jace felt that if this kept up, sensitive mages would be able to detect them from miles away. It was just like at the Dark Portal, when he'd watched the doomguard legions flying toward him from a distance and felt that energy.

So he reminded him at once, "Could you rein in your magic a little, Karlez? I don't want to attract attention."

"As you command, master."

Karlez responded in a low voice in Eredun, and the fel energy aiding his flight quickly subsided. Soon Jace could hardly feel any more of the magic radiating from the demon, the kind that burned and unsettled a person's mind.

On the flip side, the amplitude and strength of his wingbeats weakened too, and the speed dropped considerably. Jace looked down at the dark forest below and felt as though the demon hadn't covered much ground in half a day.

But for the sake of flying stealthy, this much of a price was worth paying.

Following the plan, Karlez traveled high above the Burning Steppes, avoiding the patrols of black whelps around Blackrock Tower all the way to the gnoll forest east of Stonewatch, then turned south. He flew for several days before finally catching sight of the dark valley of Deadwind Pass.

The great demon lowered his altitude slightly, flying over the boundless expanse of withered black forest. Jace looked back and forth across the valley — partly to find a suitable hiding spot, and partly to watch whether Karazhan was visible to the south. They couldn't get too close.

After circling the valley for a while, Jace noticed a ravine to the east, overgrown with tangled trees — it seemed like a good spot for Karlez to hole up.

But as Jace directed him to fly closer on his signal, he could make out several crude structures built randomly from stone and wood, draped with what looked like battle standards stitched together from the hides of some unknown beast. Judging by the size of those structures, they were probably ogre dwellings.

Not long after, the huge figures moving through the forest below confirmed Jace's guess — this was a place already occupied by ogres.

And the moment Jace spotted the ogres, they clearly noticed the shadow flapping enormous wings in the sky. They let out bursts of furious roars, some of which even mixed in Common.

"Get lost, black dragon! This is no place for you…"

"Watch me run you through with my spear! I'll rip your wings off for a tent!"

Karlez rumbled with laughter. Jace was about to tell him to find another spot when it suddenly occurred to him — why not use these ogres as cover?

Whether it was the Violet Eye or any other mages who'd been to Deadwind Pass, they probably all knew about the ogres hiding in here. And as long as the ogres didn't leave this dark-cursed valley to harass the residents of Duskwood or the garrison at Stonard, presumably nobody would be interested in picking a fight with them either — an ogre colony wasn't something one or two exploring mages could handle alone.

So these ogres would make perfect camouflage for evading outside searchers…

Ogre clan rules were, for the most part, crude and simple: as long as you defeated the king, you became the new king. Barring the special, smarter small tribes led by two-headed mystics, the rest — from the Gordunni ogres of Dire Maul to the Bladespire ogres of the Blade's Edge Mountains — all followed this same logic.

And as long as you defeated their king, whether you were an ogre, a gronn, a human, or some other strange creature, the ogres would accept you as king. A demon was no exception, of course…

"Would you be interested in becoming the king of ogres, Karlez?" Jace asked.

"Not interested. A pack of stupid lesser creatures," the demon said.

"Now you are interested," Jace said. "Land."

Morophil chuckled softly. The demon shot her a look, grumbled in displeasure, and slowed his wingbeats, gradually descending.

He touched down in a forest clearing, and the ogres naturally gathered around. One ogre roared and charged straight at Karlez — though he only came up to the demon's waist, he still raised his weapon and attacked without hesitation. Got to give him credit for bravery.

Karlez had just reached out to cast a shadow spell when Jace said quickly, "Don't kill him. These ogres are all useful."

The demon let out a pained "ugh" and casually swatted the ogre to the ground with his wing. The fellow rolled over, cursed a couple of times, and then passed out — or fell asleep, hard to tell.

The other ogres seemed somewhat confused about what this fellow was. They were all dim-witted brutes, and when they saw that the so-called "black dragon" was actually a towering, bipedal demon, they were even more bewildered, looking at each other.

"He looks like a demon," one ogre said in Orcish. "A doomguard — you ever seen a doomguard?"

"I've never seen one this big."

"He's the offspring of a doomguard and a black dragon, must be."

"Get out of the way and let us through, idiots."

A two-headed ogre wielding an iron club stepped forward. One of its broken-horned heads looked up, craning its neck to stare at the doom lord before them. "He's a demon, all right… and a human and a demon with him. Have you come here to die? High King Golkrash has defeated a gronn before, and you don't even know what a gronn is… To you, he'd just be a slightly bigger gronn."

The other one-eyed head said, "That's right. High King Golkrash fears big things the least."

Gronn were a kind of moving-mountain-like giants of Draenor, believed to be the ancestors of both ogres and orcs. Many ogres worshipped gronn.

When this ogre mentioned gronn, Jace was a little surprised that this so-called Golkrash was quite something. But when the ogre mage said a doomguard was just a slightly bigger gronn, Jace realized the gronn he'd defeated probably wasn't anything impressive — most likely just a juvenile.

"I don't want to be their king, Sesso, my master," Karlez muttered in Eredun.

"What do you think of this place?" Jace asked.

"Abundant shadow power — enough for me to draw on and recover my strength here… But there's no need for me to waste time with these idiots," the demon said.

Jace said, "But these idiots can cover for you. Trust me — challenge their king, High King Golkrash. No one would ever believe this pack of ogres is ruled by an Eredun lord. You can recover your strength here in peace."

The broken-horned head said impatiently, "Get moving, or we'll start a fight."

"We're not afraid of you! We don't even need our king to step in…" the one-eyed head said. "Kill these intruders together!"

"That's right — charge!" the broken-horned head roared. "Our patience is at an end."

The ogres in the forest were just about to come roaring forward when Jace shouted, "Hold on — he's challenging your king!"

"Challenging the king?" The two-headed ogre took two steps forward. The broken-horned head said, "You think you can defeat Demon-Slayer Golkrash?!"

"What, Demon-Slayer?" Karlez suddenly became interested.

"Doomguard don't even rank among Golkrash's conquests!" The two-headed ogre raised both arms, and both heads roared in unison: "A demon has challenged the Demon-Slayer!"

"Someone's challenged the king!" the other ogres began to stir as well.

A short ogre came tumbling into the forest, and the other ogres began shouting unintelligible slogans in their incomprehensible language, spontaneously clearing an open space.

Not long after, several laughs echoed from deep in the forest, and a massively built ogre walked out step by step. He wore a patchwork of armor, his body covered in battle scars, a full two heads taller than the brutes around him.

Karlez murmured softly when he saw the fel battle-axe in his hand: "A battle-axe forged by the Mo'arg. He might actually have killed a demon before."

"I am Golkrash," the ogre king said, hefting his axe. "It's been two years since someone last challenged me to become ruler of this gray valley! That fellow wasn't as tall as you, and he probably didn't die as miserably either."

"Should we get down?" Morophil asked.

Jace glanced at her, then at the great demon beneath him, then looked down, just about to find somewhere to step and jump off, when the doom lord suddenly asked, "Has the fight started?"

"Whenever you are. End this farce quickly, demon," Golkrash said, raising his axe.

The demon took a step forward, reached out, and grabbed the ogre king's head in one hand. The other clearly didn't react in time — he roared and tried to yank his head free, but it was all wasted effort.

"Golbeg me, Golkrash. The little thing on my shoulder doesn't want me to kill…" the demon murmured. "Excuses — excuses for how you can't beat me. You're going to die miserably!"

The ogre, head in the demon's grip, let out muffled roars and swung his axe wildly, chopping down on the demon's wrist armor with a clang — but without causing any real damage.

Jace was just wondering whether he should let the demon finish him off when Karlez spoke an incantation, released his hand, and the ogre immediately rolled his eyes back, collapsed to the ground foaming at the mouth, and began convulsing without control.

At the very least, he had genuinely followed the order not to kill the ogres.

"He's not dead, but he can't be king anymore," Karlez looked over the surrounding ogres. "I am your new king now."

The two-headed ogre was stunned for a moment, then raised both arms, and both heads roared in unison: "All hail… the new king! Our new king is called… called…

"You are not worthy to know my name, lesser idiots," Karlez said slowly.

"Yes… High King!" the broken-horned head nodded vigorously, and the other ogres immediately sheathed their weapons and roared: "High King!" Though these ogres were sincerely shouting Karlez's new title, the more Jace heard it, the more it sounded like they were being sarcastic.

"Right, keep a low profile here. Just in case — and I mean just in case — you actually get caught by some mage one day, don't say my name. Tall King, just say you fled here from the Dark Portal." He looked over the ogres as he said it.

"I know, timid master," Karlez snorted.
