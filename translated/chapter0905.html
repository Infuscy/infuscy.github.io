Chapter 905: The Alchemists

Heading north to the main road that cut through Deadwind Pass, fewer and fewer carriages and merchants had been passing through since the Dark Portal campaign wound down.

He walked west on foot for several days, and none of the carriages he encountered were willing to take on a stranger. Jace remembered it hadn't been this hard to hitch a ride before — maybe it was because neither his clothes nor his looks had as much approachability as they used to.

Of course, he couldn't exactly whip out his court mage's ring to prove the point. Since the Wizard Sanctum didn't want him meddling in the whole demon-summoning affair, he could only keep his identity hidden.

After walking west for two days and two nights, he finally caught sight of the watchtower east of Duskwood. Through the pitch-black forest, he could already faintly make out the lights of Duskwood.

For some reason, Duskwood seemed even darker than the last time Jace had been here. The dark power of Karazhan continued to leak out, and mist shrouded this entire stretch of land. The whole forest was silent, as quiet as death.

Considering that if not for the mountains blocking it here, this place probably wouldn't be much better off than Deadwind Pass, it was no wonder Duskwood was going from bad to day — and driving Lakeshire's prosperity right along with it.

Still, Duskwood itself looked quite lively, except that the soldiers who used to patrol the streets everywhere were gone, and the expressions on passersby were rather complicated.

By now Jace could clearly sense the emotions permeating the air here — unease, worry… probably concerned about the Moonbrook business spreading to Duskwood.

After all, compared to the west, Duskwood was much more the kind of place where a demon disturbance could kick up.

Duskwood was certainly not on Moonbrook's scale, but it was no small village either. Standing by the long-abandoned fountain in the square in front of the town center, Jace looked at the houses of varying heights lining the streets running north-south and east-west and thought — was he really going to have to knock on every single door?

He tried using Detect Demons to look around, but couldn't sense any trace of demons at all — not Master Basadim the felhound, nor the succubi summoned by Galders and Sulenna.

If the Dagger Society really was in Duskwood, they were most likely staying at Kayleyn Airo's place. She was a tailor, and an enchanter tailor at that. Jace wondered how well known she was in Duskwood.

Jace walked into the tavern. There weren't many people in at the moment; a few customers sat in the corners, not saying much.

He went to the front and booked a room, then said, "Sir, I'm looking for a tailor to mend some clothes. I don't come to Duskwood often — I'm not sure who to go to."

"The Shipside family," the waitress beside him pointed north. "Just cross the street, young sir. Look for the shop with two little girls in the family. They're all blond — easy to recognize."

"Can they enchant the clothes they make?" Jace asked.

"Mage?" the boss behind the counter asked.

"Apprentice," Jace said. "Not a proper mage yet."

"Sorry, mage," the waitress shook her head. "Back when everyone still called this place Greenville, there weren't any enchanting masters here. With the name change to something even less lucky, even fewer skilled folks are willing to come. You could try asking those half-elves living on the outskirts of town — they might've inherited some magical knack from their ancestors."

The bartender and the boss chatted a bit about the half-elves. Ursula was a half-elves too, but she was quite distinct from those mixed-race folks with obvious elven features. Jace didn't think she'd mix in with the people here, so he didn't pay much attention.

So he ordered a light ale, sat to the side, and pondered where he should start looking tomorrow. Just then, a somewhat weak, older male voice came from beside him: "Mr. Jace Sesso?"

Jace turned his head and saw a man of about fifty standing there. Clean-shaven face, wearing a respectable light-linen shirt and grayish-blue trousers — he looked like a clerk who worked at the town hall.

He seemed somewhat familiar, but Jace couldn't remember where he'd seen him at all.

Seeing the hesitation on this side, the man said immediately, "Thomas Waller. Do you still remember me?"

Jace frowned. That name sounded familiar too — he should know this person. "I'm sorry, sir. I should know you, but I truly can't recall."

As he spoke, he used Soul Link to signal Morophil to be careful. A stranger who knew his name in the current situation couldn't help but put him on guard.

The middle-aged man smiled. "The last time we met, you were with an elf… probably two or three years ago."

"Ah."

Only then did Jace remember. So this was the Duskwood necromancer, Abercrombie!

Of course, after Jace and Vereesa had killed his wife, combined with the Wizard Sanctum's intervention, he probably had a hard time becoming the corpse-controller from the game.

Still, Jace was quite surprised by his transformation. These past few years, he seemed to have walked out of the shadow of his wife's death and returned to a normal life. The whole man looked a lot younger — no longer that trembling old man. That probably had something to do with staying away from necromancy too.

"Sorry, Mr. Waller," Jace said. "I'm dealing with a lot of messy business right now, and it must have muddled my brain so I couldn't remember you."

"If you hadn't mentioned you were a mage, I wouldn't have dared to be sure I'd recognized you either," Waller said. "You're a lot taller than when we met, and a lot more solid too. But I still remember the sound of your voice, the way you walk… You saved my life, Mr. Jace Sesso, and you were willing to give me a chance to start over. I've wanted to thank you in person, but I never knew how to find you."

"As long as you've started a new life, that's the greatest thanks to me, Waller," Jace smiled, then asked, "What are you doing these days?"

"I picked up my old trade again — treating people's illnesses here in Duskwood," Waller said.

"That's wonderful," Jace said. "Back in Raven Hill I heard you used to treat people. A lot of folks there remembered you and didn't want anything bad to happen to you."

"I feel I owe the folks of Raven Hill an apology too," Waller sighed. "So I haven't given up the research I was doing before, but I've switched from focusing on necromantic resurrection to figuring out how to cure the undead plague and block the spread of zombie epidemics… Raven Hill is hardly livable anymore because of that big graveyard. I don't want Duskwood to one day be surrounded by ghouls and raised dead either."

"Are the Wizard Sanctum people still watching you?" Jace asked.

"No, they just come by occasionally to check on my situation," Waller said. "I'm actually quite grateful for their help."

Jace nodded. He thought: the Abercrombie of the future had gone from a corpse-hoarding necromancer to a doctor curing epidemics. Then all his effort this trip… had yielded a pretty good result, after all. The whole of Duskwood could breathe a little easier too.

At the very least, there wouldn't be a flesh-abomination built by this Abercrombie charging into town to slaughter everyone in the future.

But listening to the cause Waller was pursuing now, Jace also thought of the medicinal broth recipe he'd gotten from the furbolgs in Kalimdor to cure the Frostwhisper Gorge undead plague.

Many Wizard Sanctum mages believed that the undead resurrection in Duskwood was influenced by Karazhan's shadow and soul power, which seemed similar to how the evil power leaking from Ursolus into the dreamscape seeped into reality and poisoned the creatures of Frostwhisper Gorge.

So he took out the recipe that Sola had translated and said, "When I traveled to Kalimdor, I got a recipe from the local natives to deal with the undead plague, Mr. Waller. They seem to face a similar predicament."

"Really?" Waller took it and looked it over. "I need paper and a pen… Could you come home with me, Jace Sesso? Let me copy this down for now. It's definitely worth trying."

"Sure," Jace said.

Thomas Waller enjoyed a certain reputation as an herbalist across Duskwood and even the whole of Stormwind Kingdom. Jace remembered that Marin had mentioned he'd spent some time in Dalaran too.

If an anti-undead-plague medicinal broth like this could be improved and promoted by him, Jace wondered what kind of impact it might have on Duskwood, or even on Lordaeron in the future.

Even if this medicinal broth worked on furbolgs but was useless or even harmful to humans, the risks could be ruled out in advance through his experiments.

Arriving at Waller's house, he took out paper and a pen and began copying. Jace did his best not to look around the room — after all, he was still a little curious about where the man had buried his wife.

Still, he noticed quite many bags of packaged herbs stacked in the room.

Out of curiosity about the types and scents of herbs, he glanced at them a bit more. Thomas Waller noticed his gaze and said, somewhat embarrassed, "Because of what happened with my wife, I burned through all the savings I'd accumulated, including some inheritance my father left me. So I also do a bit of herb business to earn some money."

"Herbs?" Jace seemed to be reminded of something all at once. He thought for a moment, then asked, "Have you heard of the Arcane Star Alchemy Shop in Stormwind? The owner is named… Arkhana."

"I know of it," Waller nodded.

Ursula had told him to go to Booty Bay to advertise this strange alias for her. Jace had pondered for quite a while in Booty Bay and still hadn't figured out how to promote it to her — who'd have thought it would actually come in handy here.

If he hadn't learned Thalassian and been deeply impressed by the meaning of "Arkhana" — "arcane" — he probably wouldn't have thought of this name right now.

Waller added, "Not only have I heard of it — this alchemist needed some herbs related to trolls before, and she bought them from me."

"Before?" Jace said. "Are you still in contact?"

Thomas Waller hesitated for a moment. "What's your relationship?"

Jace said, "I'm something of her assistant. Quite a few of my alchemical knowledge comes from her, Mr. Waller. I know her alchemy shop in Stormwind ran into some trouble, so I came out here to find her."

Waller said in sudden understanding, "So the knowledge about shadow protection potions you knew during our first meeting was all learned from Lady Arkhana? I'm not close to her, but I could tell she's an alchemist of considerable learning. I don't know where she lives either — she only comes to find me when she wants to buy or sell something."

"Does she come often?" Jace asked.

"Honestly, it's hard to say whether it's often or not — there's no pattern to it. But she told me that if there's bloodvine for sale…"

At this, Waller bit back the rest of his sentence and said instead, "You're one of the Wizard Sanctum people, aren't you? I'm guessing Lady Arkhana probably wouldn't want the Wizard Sanctum to know how to get in touch with her. After all, just from your own introduction of your relationship, I can't fully trust you either."

Jace nodded. He hadn't expected Ursula to be quite so careful, but it made sense — after all, she not only needed to help the Dagger Society avoid the Wizard Sanctum's investigation, but also watch out for possible pursuit from those northerners.

"Strictly speaking, I'm no longer with the Wizard Sanctum, but I understand your difficulty. Then do me a favor, Mr. Waller — next time you see her, tell her Jace Sesso is waiting for her at the tavern in Duskwood."

"I'll definitely pass the message along, Mr. Sesso," Waller said.

After seeing to this, Jace brought the furbolg medicinal broth recipe back to the tavern and settled in to wait for news here.

He'd originally thought he might have to wait several days, so he'd even made a plan to continue studying the elemental tongue and Dwarvish in the tavern.

But after just one day, that morning when he came downstairs, he saw a familiar female figure sitting at the table by the door, anxiously rubbing the corner of the table with her hand.

"Hey, that's Lady Arkhana, right?" Jace called toward her.

Ursula Dring looked up sharply, stood up immediately, and the worried look on her face instantly turned to surprise. Seeing she was safe and sound, Jace finally set his mind at ease.

Ursula also looked like she wanted to say something, but then glanced at the counter — probably realizing this wasn't the place to start shouting names — and restrained the urge to yell.

After that, she walked over briskly with a straight face, and threw her arms around Jace in a hug. "I thought you weren't coming to find us," she said, letting go with a smile.

"I thought you'd all dumped me," Jace said, breathing out in relief. "Sorry for being away so suddenly and missing so much — I should have been here this whole time. But Kalimdor… I didn't go for nothing. I did as you said and brought you something good. You don't think I forgot what you told me to do, do you?"

"You just come back alive, you little bastard," Ursula said.
