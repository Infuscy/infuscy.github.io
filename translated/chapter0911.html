Chapter 911: The Sentinel of Sentinel Hill

Jace stepped out of Sola's room and tried not to dwell on what she'd said. The more he thought about Alexstrasza's reasons for giving him the Chain of Will, the less it felt like a reward and more like a burden. But everything powerful came with a negative side — that was unavoidable, and he understood that well enough. He headed to the staff shop. The Mirror Stone Staff he'd commissioned from Allen Hafgan was finished, and Jace picked it up. While he was at it, he also bought a roll of mageweave cloth to wrap the staff in. A single roll of cloth cost twelve silver and fifty copper — more expensive than the staff itself. But the Black Knight cloak he'd been making do with was falling apart, and the disharmonious aura clinging to it was the kind of thing a sensitive person would notice. A fresh wrapping was the smarter move. He pulled out the old cloak, tied on his mask, laced up his boots, hung the Song of the Blue Sky in its new sheath at his belt, and shouldered Ursolus, which — swaddled in mageweave — looked like a blueberry-flavored lollipop. Hand resting on the Soul Mirror Stone Staff, he straightened up and turned this way and that. He caught his reflection in the window. He was still dressed in the same thrown-together mess, but he looked nothing like that blue-and-green-clad adventurer idiot from before. Now he looked like a traveler with a story to tell — one who also happened to know a bit of magic. "More and more charming, dear," Morophil said, sidling up beside him and staring at the window. "Charming, or charming to demons?" Jace asked. "Sometimes there's no need to draw such a fine line between the two," the succubus said. "At least no human is born able to turn invisible," Jace said. "Now disappear. You're coming with me." The flames in Morophil's eyes lit up, and she said with a face full of glee, "I thought after our last brief time together, I'd have no idea how long I'd have to wait for another chance like this. I didn't expect I'd get to enjoy happy travels with my master again so soon." "This is a real mission. You'll be helping me track down demons," Jace said as he packed. "Then I'll need plenty of mana to be of any use," Morophil said, leaning in close. "Yeah… sure…" Jace humored her. Originally, Jace had planned to ride Alna to Moonbrook. But when he was about to head to the Mage Quarter, it hit him — Alna had something of a reputation around the Mage Quarter and the Wizard Sanctum by now. Since the Wizard Sanctum didn't want him involved in the Moonbrook investigation, he couldn't very well ride the mare the king himself had given him there. Plenty of mages would recognize that pretty horse who'd been lingering in the stables for ages. Not many people could ride gryphons around here anyway, so Jace was stuck with his old standby: hitchhiking. Fortunately, there were soldiers stationed there now, plus a good number of mages living in the area. So even though the demon troubles had driven plenty of people out of Moonbrook, the flow of supplies had actually picked up considerably, and Jace had no trouble finding a carter willing to give him a free ride. As they passed Sentinel Hill, he noticed the place had changed enormously since his last visit. There were far more barracks, and the whole area was busier by orders of magnitude. The watchtower, along with the surrounding logging camps, the little monastery, and the rest — all of it was suddenly packed with people, giving everything a chaotic feel. Despite the sheer number of soldiers, their expressions were distinctly unfriendly. And they were checking everyone who passed through with excruciating thoroughness, clogging up every road near Sentinel Hill. Judging by their tense attitudes, the situation was clearly not as rosy as the Wizard Sanctum had it sound. That was when he noticed the passengers in the cart ahead of him having a bundle of tools on their back torn apart and inspected by two soldiers. So what about the thing on his own back — Ursolus? Jace hopped down and explained to the carter that he should go ahead and get in line for inspection. If things went south, no point dragging someone else into it. He glanced out at the open fields beyond the road. The watchtower up at Sentinel Hill had a line of sight that basically ruled out sneaking past. And if he tried to slip away now, he'd be caught immediately by the mounted soldiers keeping an eye on things. Looks like his only option was to brazen it out. "Morophil, do you have a trick for this?" he murmured. The succubus was invisible, so she couldn't answer aloud. But through the Soul Link she sent him a feeling of calm, and Jace felt slightly reassured. He wondered what she had in mind. Enchantment? When he reached the checkpoint, a sentinel stepped up to him and asked, "Wizard Sanctum mage?" Jace showed him his apprentice mage token. The sentinel nodded, searched his bag — didn't linger too long on the incomprehensible writing inside — and when he drew the Song of the Blue Sky, he just gave an amazed whistle and said, "Mages sure do have all the good stuff." At that moment Jace couldn't help but be thankful that most humans knew absolutely nothing about dragons. Otherwise, someone would've recognized it at a glance and he'd be done for. The sentinel pointed at the staff behind Jace and said, "Unwrap that for me, mage." They had to check this too? Morophil, where's your magic? Where's your enchantment? Why haven't you done anything yet? But Morophil gave no response. Jace had no choice but to unwrap the cloth like an honest man. A demon's skull lay bare under the bright, unforgiving sunlight of Westfall, baked until it looked like a set of not-particularly-clean sheep bones. The one small mercy was that the bones chose not to shed any ill-timed bloody tears. The sentinel flinched with a sharp intake of breath and stared at the skull, stunned. Jace knew there was no way he could pass that off as sheep bones. "Demon bones, sir. I… I bought it from Moonbrook, to use as research material on demons and orcish warlocks. But it's a fake. I got scammed. I'm here to return it." "A fake?" The sentinel glanced at Jace, looking slightly less tense. He swallowed and said, "You know Moonbrook doesn't allow any transactions involving demon materials right now, right? These are special times, mage." Jace explained immediately, "I bought it before all this happened. The fellow told me he'd found it at the Dark Portal — a doomguard's bones. I was already wondering how a doomguard could possibly be that small, but I didn't think too much of it at the time. Sure enough, after I used it, I realized it was fake. I've got a feeling it'll be hard to track down that swindler now, but I spent a lot of money on it, sir. A lot of money. I can't just give up… If it won't work, I'll head back." He was running out of steam, but short of bullshitting his way through, he didn't know what else to do. The soldier looked at the skull uncertainly, then said, "I can't make the call on this, mage." He turned to an older man behind him and asked, "What should I do, Captain?" "Don't waste time with that mage. What's a dead demon worth looking at? Let him through," the man said impatiently. "Understood. Right you are, Captain." The sentinel nodded and waved him on. That was it? He was through? Jace shot a glance at the captain — frowning, wearing a look of pure distaste — and caught something off in his demeanor with a sharp intuition. So Morophil hadn't targeted the sentinel in front of him at all. She'd gone for the captain. And she hadn't used enchantment magic to seize control — she'd simply nudged his mind with psychic force, the same way she'd done to Nekros back in Grim Batol. This succubus really did have faith in his ability to talk nonsense.
