## Chapter 912: Lingering Dark Blood

Moonbrook, the richest and rowdiest town in the Kingdom of Stormwind, had clearly changed beyond recognition.

Heading south from Sentinel Hill, Jace arrived at Moonbrook one evening. The laborers who used to look for work on the dust-choked streets, the idlers with nothing better to do, the miners wandering to and fro — all of them had basically vanished. In their place were patrol riders kicking up even more dust, town guards in simple armor standing on street corners chatting with each other, and mages everywhere he looked.

He tried scanning the whole town with Detect Demons and couldn't spot the faintest trace of anything demon-related — not even a hint. Remembering the warlocks he'd seen last time in Moonbrook, strolling down the main street with invisible imps riding on their shoulders, he could see things were genuinely different now.

The entrance to the distant mine had been sealed off, manned by mages, soldiers, and makeshift barricades. Several of the mages there looked familiar to Jace — they were Magino Dumas's students and partners, all experts at tracking demons and dark magic. Of course, several of them also knew Jace quite well.

Judging by the look of things, getting into the crime scene for a look-around was pretty much out of the question.

On the surface, the Wizard Sanctum's claim that they had the situation under control was no empty boast — at least the situation in Moonbrook was more or less under their thumb.

Jace planned to find an inn and book a room, but when he got to the inn's front door, he found two mages in conversation with an officer.

The officer wore half-armor, held a helmet under his arm, and had Stormwind's blue cloak draped over him — by the look of things, a fairly high-ranking officer. And one of the two mages wasn't ordinary either; Jace could tell from the glint of the ring on his hand that he was a full-fledged mage.

Jace knew he couldn't risk slipping past these two with Morophil in tow. Over in the Mage Quarter, most mages didn't pay constant attention to whether there was an evil aura around — it wasn't like they went around on high alert for demons invading their school and dormitories all the time.

Besides, when people from the Slaughtered Lamb brought invisible demons into the Mage Quarter, they went by side streets and hugged the walls, doing everything possible to avoid getting close to any mages.

But here in Moonbrook, everyone was strung tight, and to get through the inn door the succubus would have to brush right past those two mages. That risk was too great.

Jace glanced at the alley behind the inn. He still remembered the layout of the rooms and the directions from his last stay here, remembered sitting up at night staring out the window at the tree shadows in the moonlight. So he used the Soul Link to signal Morophil to circle around the back and wait for him.

After that, he strode right up to the inn, nodding and greeting the officer and the mages with a pleasant "Good afternoon." By the time he walked into the lobby, he was even more glad he hadn't let Morophil walk straight in.

If not for the dust blowing through the streets outside and the chaotic, worn-down furnishings inside, Jace might have thought he'd walked into the Blue Heron.

No small number of apprentice mages and even mage apprentices were holed up here. Pale blue and purple robes filled the eye — they made up at least a third of the inn's guests. Quite a few of the rest were soldiers who'd taken off their armor and outer cloaks.

When Jace walked in, a few people glanced his way, but that was about it. His current look didn't stand out much from any other passerby on the street outside.

He booked a room, bought two chunks of sliced bread, and headed upstairs. He glanced out the window and spotted Morophil's shadow hiding under the eaves, and beckoned.

The succubus nimbly walked up the wall, leaped to the second floor, and climbed in through the window. She landed and materialized, lifting her eyelids slightly, brushing the dust off her backside, and sitting on the bed. She looked at Jace and said, "You're welcome for getting you through that checkpoint, master. Do you have any idea how hard it is to use psychic magic on a mortal while invisible? Even just making him a little less patient, a little more irritable by a hair's breadth… that drained me dry."

"Drained you dry," Jace said, sorting through the things in his bag while staring at her. "Was it Medivh you were controlling?"

"If it had been Medivh, I'm afraid he would've sucked you as dry as that old tree out there, poor thing," Morophil said with a grin. "So how is my master going to compensate me?"

Jace glanced at the half-dead, withered tree outside, its canopy with barely a handful of leaves left, and said, "This isn't the place, Morophil. There are mages everywhere below, and good ones at that. I'm a little worried Magino Dumas might be right around here — his ability to track demons is among the sharpest in the whole Wizard Sanctum. We need to keep a low profile here."

The succubus said at once, "Then I'll try not to shout out, but the master's lower half is really impossible to resist. If I do shout out, you can't blame me."

Jace gave her an exasperated sideways look and said, "Come with me to see Musul first."

He set down his luggage and bag, packed the essentials, and headed downstairs. He met Morophil, who'd climbed out the window, on the street, then left Moonbrook and headed up the northern hillside.

He'd walked this road so many times he knew it like the back of his hand, but he wasn't holding out much hope of finding Musul.

Like the other warlocks, Musul had left the Storm Altar. Even though he'd promised Jace to help keep watch over Karlez and had even talked about finding Karlez a more suitable place to stay, he'd now vanished without a trace — neither contacting the orc Carol nor reaching out to his former colleagues in the Dagger Society. With something as big as the Moonbrook incident happening, he hadn't come looking for Jace either.

Jace had even suspected he might be the ringleader behind the demon disaster in Moonbrook — or at least one of them — and that he might be locked up in a Stormwind prison right now. But something about it didn't quite add up.

A man like Musul had dared to deceive a doomguard lord like Karlez to his face. Would he really let himself be bossed around by a black dragon?

Then again, who could say? If a black dragon could really help that boxing warlock summon a demon guard, what kind of being could they summon for Musul? Under that kind of temptation, could a Twisting Nether scholar like Musul — a man obsessed with summoning powerful demons — really resist?

By the time he reached the hilltop, it was already very late.

Jace caught sight of the shadow of the house from a distance. No lights anywhere.

He waited a while, confirming no one was watching the house, then moved a little closer. From within a patch of trees, he used Detect Demons to observe the whole house and yard. No demon aura, no demon shadows — just like inside the town of Moonbrook.

So Musul really was gone.

Jace was a little tempted to go inside and see if Musul had left behind any traces or clues, but the Wizard Sanctum might have already searched the place. There could be magical traps inside too.

He decided to check out the circle where Musul had gathered those warlocks to try summoning Karlez. After all, aside from that trouble spot in the mine, it was the most professional demon-convening site in all of Moonbrook.

If only Vereesa were here — she had a knack for dealing with the little tricks mages left behind. What was he supposed to do, summon a felhound?

Even though this was outside Moonbrook, it wasn't nearly far enough, and Jace wasn't about to summon a demon right out in the open like some kind of showoff. Still, he had at least one backup option.

Imps were quite sensitive to the presence of mages, so he opened his demon pouch and murmured, "Sazeno, come out."

"What is it, master? Going to make me…"

Sazeno poked his head out, and seeming to pick up on the quiet atmosphere around them, he quietly lowered his voice. Jace said, "Help me keep an eye out for mages…"

"Got it, got it," the imp said, nodding.

Walking through the woods that Musul had led him through before, Jace thought he might get lost, but he eventually found the clearing.

A year had passed, and the traces of the summoning circle left behind for Karlez had been largely erased by time. They might not have come back to use it at all after finding the Storm Altar.

But after staying here for a little while, Jace caught a familiar scent. The smell of black dragon blood.

He'd been choked by the stench from a bottle of the stuff not long ago, and the impression was still fresh.

Now that he was smelling it again, even though it was faint enough to have practically merged with the surrounding scent of trees and rot, he keenly picked up on the difference.

He sniffed carefully, pacing back and forth near the circle, judging the strength of the scent, then followed this thread of a clue forward. Step by step he moved away from the circle and came to a dead tree off to the side — this seemed to be where the smell was strongest.

Of course, that was only compared to the tiny bit he'd smelled near the circle. Anyone else — even Jace himself, back when he hadn't suspected black dragons in Moonbrook a few weeks ago — would have had a hard time guessing it was black dragon blood.

Jace crouched down searching for the true source of the scent, but after looking for a while he found nothing — no bloodstains, nothing else related to black dragons.

Before long, he looked up and realized it seemed to be this dead tree giving off the smell.

"Master, look at this." Morophil pinched a tiny piece of glass — probably no bigger than a grain of rice — and handed it to Jace. Jace took it and sniffed. Sure enough, this was very likely a bottle that had held black dragon blood.

So a bottle had been smashed here, and this tree had been doused with splashed dragon blood, turning it like this?

Black dragon blood was precious stuff even if you couldn't drink it. Why smash the bottle? Had Musul done it?

"Master…" Sazeno tugged on Jace's cloak. "There really is one, there really is a caster approaching. I can feel it." Jace stood up and hid behind the tree, glancing warily into the woods around them. But an evening breeze swept past without so many as a human figure in sight.

"Getting closer and closer, master," Sazeno closed his eyes and tucked away his will-o'-wisp, then said, "What the…"

At that moment, a flash of arcane light flickered from not far away. A blindingly bright figure leaped out of it into the night.

"Don't try to escape… warlock!"
