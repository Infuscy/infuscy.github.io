Chapter 913: Even Like This, I Can Still Fight!

Invisibility? Before Jace could make out the other's face, he felt a Counterspell clamp down on his consciousness. But he just gritted his teeth and pushed through the arcane pressure. He wasn't in the middle of casting, so the Counterspell didn't achieve much. And after taking that Counterspell from Aessrethous, enduring these mages' magic afterward — without being conceited, the spells of the Wizard Sanctum's mages really didn't amount to much for him anymore. Maybe this was how Marin had felt when he'd practiced Counterspell on Jace back then. Still, the opposing mage reacted fast. Seeing the Counterspell didn't have the desired effect, a Slow spell immediately followed, wrapping around Jace's legs and hands. Jace suddenly felt as if he'd been bound by something soft and rubbery, being pulled back and forth, making it hard to move freely. He immediately channeled shadow to vault between the trees, putting distance between them. He hadn't expected that the sudden displacement would also break the arcane bindings. The opposing mage was clearly caught off guard by that one — the spell he'd been halfway through casting had to be aborted as well, since the trees blocked his line of sight and he couldn't see his target. That mage wanted to pursue, but Jace turned back to meet him, raised his Mirror Stone Staff, and spoke a Fear incantation. He only heard the mage on the other side suck in a sharp breath and cry out, and then he used Shadowstep to flash more than ten meters away and break free. But before Jace could even wonder if he'd shaken his pursuer, another Slow spell latched on. From the woods to the left, another mage shouted an incantation, and arcane energy swirled and gathered as he chanted. Before it had fully gathered, though — bang — the mage let out a grunt and crashed to the side. The hard-gathered arcane energy scattered in every direction, flying into the woods and illuminating a vast stretch of tree shadows. Morophil, having delivered that kick, glanced deep into the woods. The fiery glow of the tattoos all over her body flared up, and she turned and vanished back into the forest. "Demon!" Someone shouted in the woods. There seemed to be far more than just two mages chasing in this direction. "She went west!" Morophil had drawn away quite a few of the mages' attention, but Jace's situation wasn't much better. He didn't want to kill any of these Wizard Sanctum mages — not to mention they were surrounding him to capture him, not trying to kill him either. Morophil had understood her master's intentions too, which was why she'd only kicked that mage down. But under these circumstances, he couldn't let himself be caught either. To shake these mages, he'd really have to spill some blood. Thinking this, Jace pulled out an Invisibility Potion he hadn't drunk back in Kalimdor. He backed up a few steps into the woods and vanished in a flash of light. A blurry haze of ghostly shadows appeared before his eyes, and the figures chasing him disappeared along with it. Relying on memory, Jace sprinted down the hillside. He'd already run down the slope before the potion wore off, but just as the world around him was becoming clear again, a flash of Blink light suddenly produced another mage. "Invisibility potion, huh? Self-righteous evildoer!" Before Jace could make out the man's features, he could tell he was a very young full-fledged mage. The other immediately began chanting — a Polymorph incantation! Jace ran while rapidly chanting a Suffocation spell, trying to choke off the mage's Polymorph. But the other chanted too fast, and his mana was considerable. Even though Jace reacted quickly enough and successfully released the Suffocation, choking the man's neck and suppressing him, he himself turned into a sheep, dragged forward by the momentum of his human-form sprint, tumbling several meters and coming up covered in dust and rotten leaves. Damn it. No — he couldn't just let himself be caught like this. He looked at the mage, who was struggling hard with some unknown spell to break the Suffocation, and decisively flipped himself up from the ground. Did you think I was some ordinary warlock…? The power of the Red Dragon's blessing, kid! Jace made a bleating noise, backed up a few steps, lowered his head, and charged at the mage ahead of him with everything he had. The mage had barely shaken off the Suffocation spell and was startled to see a sheep charging at him like a madman. Clearly he'd never seen anything like this. For a moment he didn't even know whether he should use a control spell to stop it or Blink away. Probably because, as a full-fledged mage, Blinking away from a sheep charge was just too humiliating. He hesitated for exactly one second. Jace rammed headfirst into his stomach, sending him flying two meters, tumbling and rolling several times. The mage, hurting, groaned and tried to get up again. Jace didn't give him the chance to catch his breath. He steadied his hooves slightly and lowered his head again, charging at the man's head. One solid, crunching headbutt and the mage was knocked out cold on the ground, not moving. According to what Marin had said, Polymorph could be broken very easily by any external force. The last time Jace had been polymorphed, Marin had just slapped him and he'd returned to his original form. But this time he'd rammed the other party hard twice, and the mage had even been knocked out — so why hadn't he changed back yet? The hillside above seemed to have returned to calm, but Jace couldn't stay here. He grabbed his bag in his mouth and bolted across the fields. He had to admit, running in sheep form was pretty effortless — and fast. But it wasn't long before Sazeno poked his head out of the bag again and said, "Master… wait, master, how… is that you?" The sheep glared at him. The imp scratched his head for a moment, then pointed into the distance and said, "All right, all right, even masters have their unlucky days. But there are more mages coming now — quick, hide!" Jace glanced out beyond the tall shrubs and weeds at the field's edge. There did seem to be some movement. Having turned into a sheep, he might have been having illusions, but he felt like even his ability to sense predators had gotten stronger. He just didn't know if these mages were chasing him or had simply come here early to cut him off. Looking around, Jace decisively ducked into the old mill and hid behind a big pile of tools and millstones, muffling his voice and carefully listening to the sounds outside while waiting for the Polymorph to wear off. But several minutes passed and the magic still showed no signs of wearing off on its own. He didn't know if that mage had been too vicious or his mana had been too strong. Just as Jace was agonizing over whether he should try ramming the wall, footsteps came from outside. The imp immediately dove back into the bag. Was the other party going to search the mill? Damn it. He readied himself for another charge, planning to flip the man the moment he walked through the door and then run. But he didn't expect the shadow of a succubus to inch up close beside him. "There, there, little sheep. Let Sister Shivarra take good care of you…" Morophil said softly, her voice carrying a laugh she couldn't suppress at all. Jace didn't dare speak now — and even if he did, it would only come out as bleating. Otherwise he would have cursed up a storm. At that moment, the mage arrived at the door. The instant he appeared, the succubus hit him with an Enchantment spell. He glanced inside, his gaze sweeping past this way, then smacked his lips and turned to leave. In the quiet night, a clear sigh came from outside. "He got away." "As long as he doesn't enter Moonbrook, that's all we can manage." "Right. Let's go." The footsteps grew farther and farther away. Morophil glanced outside, then turned back and said, "What now, sheep master? I don't know Dispel Magic." She said this, but her face was the picture of schadenfreude. And Jace couldn't speak — he could only bleat. He kicked the wall with a hoof, then nodded. Morophil immediately caught on and said, "You want me to kick you?" Jace nodded, turned around, and presented his backside to the succubus, bleating once. The succubus couldn't hold back a laugh. Jace wished he could crawl into a crack in the wall. This was a goddamn utter humiliation — but it was still better than knocking himself unconscious ramming the wall. If the Wizard Sanctum people found him like this and spread the story with a little embellishment, the fearsome reputation he'd earned from the Dark Portal and the Battle of Grim Batol would be wasted overnight. "Here I come then, master. You'd better brace yourself." Morophil deliberately stressed the word "master" with heavy sarcasm, since right now Jace looked nothing like a master — if anything, the roles were reversed. Jace lowered his head. He waited, but nothing happened from behind. Just as he was about to turn his head, a hard hoof kicked him square in the backside! The demon's strength was absurd. Without any warning, she blasted him whole into the wall, where he cracked against it again. Jace only felt his head spin and the world whirl — it made no difference from ramming the wall himself. After a bad bout of ringing ears and nausea passed, Morophil's wicked, delighted laughter immediately reached his ears. He touched his head, shook it hard, and after sobering up a little, realized the magic really was gone. "Go ahead, laugh," Jace raised his head, wincing as he leaned against the wall, half his backside aching, and said in a low voice, "You just laugh all you want." Sazeno next to him was also grinning. Jace glared at him, and the imp dutifully dove back into the bag, muttering, "I didn't see anything, I didn't remember a thing…" "Since you're so unhappy, my dear master, why don't you kick me back?" Morophil said this as she turned around, swaying her backside and wagging her tail. Jace wearily rubbed his eyes and said, "Don't pull this stuff here. I won't fall for it." "Kick me… master, please, with all your strength." The succubus bit her lip. "Cut it out, there might still be mages outside who haven't gone far!" "I'm not kicking and that's final." Jace brushed the dirt off himself, braced his lower back, and stood up. "We need to hurry up and leave. Back to Moonbrook. Musul might have some connection to the black dragons — we need to find a way to track down where he went." Morophil sighed, a little disappointed. "But there are hardly any warlocks left in Moonbrook, master. If not a warlock, who else would know where that fellow Musul is?" "I don't know." Jace recalled what those two mages had said earlier. "There aren't any inside Moonbrook, but outside… we'll look as best we can."
