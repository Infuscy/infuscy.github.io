## Chapter 915: The Properties of Ursolus

"The lighthouse" presumably referred to the lighthouse on a small island off the southwestern coast of Moonbrook — the only lighthouse in Westfall, and one of the landmarks Alliance players remembered most vividly.

The ghost captain who kept permanent vigil by the lighthouse, trying to keep it lit at all times, and the murloc "Old Blindeye" had become fond memories for many players.

Of course, not just in the game — here too, the lighthouse's significance to the people of Moonbrook and the whole of the west was extraordinary. Because once you passed the lighthouse, it meant entering dangerous territory, whether you continued south or sailed west. The lighthouse itself was a dangerous place too. Murlocs frequently infested that stretch of coast, and back during the Kingdom of Stormwind's war with the Gurubashi ages ago, there were even legends of jungle trolls roaming there.

As a result, a phrase had become popular in Moonbrook for scaring children: "If you don't work properly, I'll send you to the lighthouse."

Still, Jace felt the ghost captain who kept permanent vigil by the lighthouse probably didn't exist yet. He remembered that in the game, the captain's ghost had only stayed behind because the lighthouse was already unmanned, intending to remain and tend to the lighthouse. But now, he'd heard the lighthouse keeper was still living there. Perhaps at this point in time, the murloc "chieftain" Old Blindeye hadn't yet made his move on the lighthouse — at least, hadn't taken any concrete action.

After all, the whole of Westfall hadn't yet become the "wilderness" it became in the game, torn apart by the conflict between the Defias Brotherhood and Stormwind. Moonbrook's defensive forces were still in place and could reinforce the lighthouse's defenses.

So that raised another question: if the lighthouse was still manned, how were the warlocks using that place to summon demons? Jace had never been to the lighthouse. Time to see it for himself.

He spent the night resting in a small abandoned wooden hut at the edge of the woods. He stared at Ursolus, placed to the side and now re-wrapped in a bolt of silk, recalling what had happened at Jonas's house when Ursolus devoured the demons' souls.

At that moment, he had clearly been able to hear the murmuring of the souls within Ursolus. It didn't sound like they were communicating with him, or that they intended to elicit any response from him — it was simply an expression of release, of enjoyment and satisfaction.

Jace didn't know what would happen if he let this scythe-staff keep devouring more souls, what it would bring him. All he remembered was the miserable state of the female succubus who'd tried to ambush him from behind, only to be shot out of the shadows by one of Morophil's flying knives, and then took the full brunt of his Corrosion spell.

Empowered by this staff, even a simple Corrosion spell of his had become powerful enough to startle even himself.

Jace remembered that in the game, Ursolus had the artifact property of devouring souls and strengthening shadow magic after killing demons. Could this staff be the same?

Based on twenty years of living in this world and comparing it with his gaming experience in his past life — spells like Life Tap and Fear, for instance — he could only say that even if things weren't exactly the same, there would be some similar manifestations.

"What is my master worrying about?" Morophil asked from not far away.

"Why couldn't those demons… sense my aura?" Jace said. "Before this, plenty of demons could detect my shadow power, couldn't they? Karlez could. The void walker Galders summoned could. Including you."

Morophil was silent for a moment, then said, "Those succubi only wanted to siphon arcane energy, master. The Burning Legion's demons only care about the power of twisted arcane magic… after all, they are hardly short of powerful warlocks. Eredar sorcerers, Nathrezim dreadlords — I imagine that compared to those fearsome fellows, the shadow aura my master gives off isn't particularly…"

"But they should at least sense danger approaching, instead of thinking a trouble-maker who walked right up to them was a 'worthless good-for-nothing with no mana at all,'" Jace said.

Morophil burst out laughing. "My master really is petty. There's no need to take the Shivarras' words to heart. When a few Shivarras get together and boast, I imagine they wouldn't even give Archimonde a second look."

She continued, "Even though Karlez had been heavily wounded by the portal, he was still a powerful high-ranking Ered'ruin. His sensitivity to demons was not something ordinary lesser demons could match. And void walkers — don't forget, they are shadow itself. As for me… I am a huntress, master. My job is hunting demons, so naturally I can sense demonic power that other Shivarras can't. As for them, they were nothing but absolute slaves serving Annihilan or Ered'ruin masters. These wretched servants probably couldn't even find arcane energy on their own, except perhaps to summon stronger demon masters — not for themselves."

"At the very least, I haven't sensed any difference in my master's aura."

"Maybe it's because you were too focused on getting that human woman to let our master into the house, and overlooked something instead, Shivarra," Sazeno said, raising his hand from the edge of the bag. "Perhaps my master unconsciously used emotion to influence his shadow power, reining in his mana…"

"I'm not good at concealing my mana," Jace admitted. "At least, I'm not confident I could hide anything in front of demons."

"I still think my guess makes more sense," Morophil said. "The Burning Legion's demons are always like a bunch of brainless idiots who don't care about anything."

"That's because once you join the Burning Legion, you no longer have to hide anymore! Even if your body dies, your soul can quickly be remade into a new body under the Legion's mighty power!" the imp said emphatically.

"And what about these three Shivarra?" Morophil lowered her head and stared at him. "They were members of the Burning Legion, genuine and certified. But where did their souls go? The Legion's power couldn't help them with that."

"It's because they ran into my master, ha!" the imp laughed.

The two demons bickered back and forth, one line each. Jace watched them from the side, while in his mind he replayed the scene from back then and made his guesses. Could it also be a factor of that staff?

He shifted his gaze back to Ursolus once more.

Ever since he'd taken Ursolus out of Skavir's vault, all the way here it had concealed its power as one of the most powerful artifacts in the Twisting Nether simply by being wrapped in a Black Knight's cloak. Whether it was high elf mages, dragons, or even demons… none of them could easily detect its presence.

That wasn't hard to understand. Ever since Ursolus had been brought to Azeroth by the Eredar witch Sathiel, she had been hiding from Sargeras's gaze while harvesting souls, planning to secretly use this staff to forge herself into a being capable of standing against the entire Burning Legion.

Of course, she ultimately failed — but she didn't die by Sargeras's hand either. She was stopped by the Guardian of Tirisfal, Skavir.

Judging by the fact that the pursuers Sargeras had sent over the past centuries had never been able to find Sathiel, it wasn't surprising that this staff was skilled at concealing itself.

Perhaps he really should find some time to explore the power and secrets held within this staff.

If in the future he was going to travel to shattered Draenor to find Alleria and Turalyon — or at least to find the Sons of Lothar — he would certainly have to face countless demons of the Burning Legion. A demonic implement that excelled at harvesting demon souls would finally have its time to shine.
