Chapter 921: The Island (Part 2)

The man looked to be around thirty, with no beard. His demeanor was confident and open — nothing at all like some outsider secretly mixing in with a crowd of humans. The warlocks around him, including the several summoners who'd brought Jace to the island, were all humans, yet they seemed to have no idea what exactly their host here really was. What was worth noting was that this island wasn't home only to spellcasters. There were also quite a few of those people wearing brown or gray cloaked coats — the sort Jace had seen before in the basement of the mill. They stood nearby holding weapons — axes or blades, for instance — watching the people in the middle of the summoning circle like guards, especially Jace, the new arrival. These people were expressionless. They didn't size up the newcomer the way the other warlocks did, nor did they wear the slightly false look of welcome on their faces like this dragonkin disguised as a human before him. They were like a bunch of wooden posts stuck in the ground — as if they had no feelings except to fight troublemakers at any moment. Relying on his keen sensitivity to shadow power, Jace could tell there was something off about these fellows' auras. Although it was different from the shapeshifter before him, were they dragonkin too? "Call me Celsus, Mr. Yurk," the man before him said. "I am the Pathfinder's deputy and assistant. I'm responsible for leading the accomplished spellcasters who've just arrived here to familiarize them with our new gathering place — the island beneath your feet, the Lighthouse Isle." "Mr. Celsus." Jace gave a slight nod, turning to observe the towering lighthouse behind him as he did. He scanned the inside with Detect Demons and could sense demon shadows within, obscured and made hard to discern by all sorts of circles and runes. There had to be quite a few demons and warlocks living in there. Of course, the upper half of the lighthouse was completely empty, with nothing at all. It might have been a limitation of Jace's Detect Demons range, but more likely the Pathfinder lived at the very top. Like a watchman, he looked down over the entire situation on the island — and even the distant fishing village and coast — not allowing anyone to reach the same height as him. "I can tell you can sense the power this lighthouse holds, Mr. Yurk," Celsus continued. "It wasn't like this before. This change has only come about since our Pathfinder arrived. From now on, this lighthouse will no longer merely light the way for the people of the Kingdom of Stormwind at sea — it will light the correct path for the whole kingdom's people… and for the entire Alliance." Jace responded with silence. Celsus stepped aside and said, "Come with me. I'll show you around and get things started for your future life, study, and exchanges here." Following Celsus away from the summoning grounds, they walked down between the white rocks and along a stone path — one that might have been natural or might have been carved later — curving down below, arriving at the area between the base of the lighthouse and its houses and buildings. Two guards in cloaked coats holding weapons stood here. When they saw Celsus they both nodded, then looked toward Jace with an indifferent gaze. Jace glanced at the little mirror stone on his staff. Although the auras of these fellows were somewhat off, at least judging from the mirror stone they were all ordinary people — their souls had nothing abnormal about them whatsoever. They were no different from the owner of the staff shop; in fact, their souls were even cleaner than his own. Where had these fellows been recruited from? Then Jace remembered what he'd heard from people chatting in the Lakeshire inn. The Cult of the Dragon in Lakeshire had disappeared. Even though after the Moonbrook demon disaster came to light, SI:7 could no longer cooperate with warlocks and felhounds to hunt dragonkin, the Cult of the Dragon in Moonbrook still hadn't regrouped. On the contrary, they'd grown fewer and fewer, until hardly anyone had heard of or seen traces of them. Could it be that those Cult of the Dragon members had all hidden themselves here? Many Cult of the Dragon members had probably started out with a genuine worship of dragons and gone in search of black dragon traces. Some might have been after black dragon magic, or were simply adventurers curious about mysterious knowledge, chasing after wealth. Take that pair of ranger father and son, for instance — they'd wanted to make money by getting more black dragon scales. But without exception, after these people came into contact with the dragonkin who manipulated the Cult of the Dragon, they would be mesmerized by their psychic magic and become utterly devoted to the Black Wing, just like these armed thugs before him. These cultists would either eventually be freed because the dragonkin manipulating them got taken out by SI:7, or die on the road of serving the black dragons. Only the luckiest ones would ever see a real black dragon and receive true ascension, becoming dragonkin. Of course, Jace thought to himself, for a warlock like him it was hard to say whether becoming dragonkin counted as good luck — because it was nothing more than trading psychic control through magic for shackles of the blood. Celsus pushed open a small door. Inside was a deep, damp corridor. Laughter came from within from time to time, along with the occasional sound of panting, plus a constant stream of conversation and the murmuring of incantations. Brought along by waves of the fishy stench of shadow hitting his face, even Jace — a man long accustomed to the dark spellcasters hiding in basements — felt this was a little too sinister. As they walked, Celsus introduced the place: "This is where the followers live. If you're willing, you'll become one of them in the future. Once you've earned the Pathfinder's trust and met with him, you'll understand everything we're doing here." Jace said, "I thought you'd go see that Pathfinder after you finished giving me the tour." Celsus turned his head and said, "I know you're a very gifted warlock and demon summoner, but you should know that everyone who can make it to the lighthouse is no ordinary person. Many are just as eager as you to receive the Pathfinder's audience and blessing, but not everyone has that kind of luck." Jace nodded. He wasn't particularly surprised. As they passed several rooms, he saw warlocks inside exchanging notes and experimenting with demon summoning incantations. There were dwarves among them, even high elves and goblins. In just these few rooms alone he'd already counted seven or eight people. There were other rooms on the lighthouse, possibly with even more. Passing by outside the doors, he saw the succubi inside — some narrowing their eyes with seductive charm, while others were extremely vigilant. A few of the people leading other demon summoners in experimenting with incantations caught Jace's attention. He noticed they were just like the guards outside — their faces were cold and expressionless, their complexions pale, and when they passed by his side he could even smell that distinct scent of black dragon blood. Had they already drunk black dragon blood and strengthened their powers? There were too many warlocks, cultists, and demons here. Jace knew he couldn't take them all on at once. Warlocks commanded an assorted arsenal of curse magic. Even a powerful Karlez couldn't face a dozen warlocks attacking him simultaneously. Not to mention that among the warlocks of the Storm Altar there were some half-decent ones who specialized only in summoning demons and weren't much good at curses. The warlocks here had all been hand-picked from the outside and delivered here as quality tools for the black dragons. And even if he could barely hold his own against these people, he had no idea what exactly this Pathfinder was. If he caused an uproar at the lighthouse ahead of time, the Pathfinder would definitely find a way to escape. The warlocks outside could teleport people in — they could certainly teleport people out too. If he startled the snake and let him leave the island, then all effort would be wasted, and these dragonkin would grow even more cautious and covert in their operations. So Jace had to seek an opportunity to be alone with the Pathfinder. He needed to see if there was a way to catch the leader first. If he could make these warlocks understand that the one guiding them wasn't some grand dark magic master but a dragonkin trying to enslave them with dragon blood, plenty of warlocks would surely take Musur's side and stand against them, helping him deal with these dragonkin. Moreover, those cultists and warlocks with something off about them were most likely being manipulated by that Pathfinder. As long as he took care of him, these people would naturally come back to their senses — just like those two rangers in the Redridge Mountains. By then they might even become allies in getting off the island, and could help deal with the remaining die-hards along the way.
