Chapter 922: "A Private Audience"

"Then how do I get that kind of luck — that is, earn the Pathfinder's trust?" Jace asked. "Demonstrate your talent. Show what makes you different from the others during magical exchanges with them, Yurk," Celsus explained. "Although our Pathfinder rarely shows himself, he keeps an eye on everything that happens at the lighthouse. Summoning demons — you can let those demons prove your ability." "For combat-savvy demons like felguards or felhounds, we have an arena beneath the lighthouse large enough for them to unleash their strength," Celsus said as he walked. "The demon that claims victory earns its master the Pathfinder's attention." Of course, succubi could also fight, but the Pathfinder would rather they proved their worth in other ways — for example, by enchanting the Wizard Sanctum's mages, protecting our very existence, or even siphoning mana from them and bringing it back to the lighthouse… That's how we operate, Yurk. That's why, after Moonbrook fell under the control of Stormwind's little king, we can still carry on our dark magic research here." "Aren't you worried that the demons you release will actually draw even more attention, Celsus?" Jace asked. Celsus said, "Those demons did draw more attention — but they also drew in more warlocks like you, coming to join us." "I'm sure you've run into plenty of misunderstandings in the process of learning dark magic and demon summoning. Countless people treated you as an irredeemable villain, as a potential criminal who'd already sunk into the abyss. Even if you learned dark magic only to survive, or to earn some money to feed yourself, or even used dark magic to help others — you'd get no understanding whatsoever. You came here not only to receive the Pathfinder's guidance and blessing, but also to find a sense of belonging, to join more people in changing the world's perception of people like us." "I don't understand," Jace said. "Maybe it's just that I was in Moonbrook not long ago and only just arrived at the lighthouse — I don't have a thorough enough understanding of the way you do things. I don't see how summoning demons and releasing them across the whole of the Kingdom of Stormwind, scaring those people so badly they don't dare close their eyes when they sleep at night, does anything to improve our public image." "That's what you came here to figure out, Yurk." Celsus said this, then looked up at a tall figure walking toward them. It was a felguard. Its eyes glowed with fel-green light, and the fel-tattooed armor etched into its body swayed and clattered with each step. When it emerged from the dark shadows, it looked for all the world like a ghost wreathed in fel fire. Not until its muscular build, its hardened skin, and its mouthfuls of fangs were fully exposed did people get a clear look at what it really was. The warlock who'd summoned this felguard was unquestionably an extremely powerful demonologist. Jace knew that even the felguard summoned by the Bleeding Hollow orc leader he'd encountered in the Black Swamp couldn't compare. Perhaps only that fearsome demon of Winterspring could match it. The moment it appeared, Jace started considering how he'd deal with this fellow if it really came to a fight. Morophil should be able to handle it, but the best approach would be to join forces with Morophil and take out the Pathfinder before this fellow could lay a hand on them. He didn't know if this felguard had been summoned personally by the Pathfinder. If so, taking him down would probably be quite a troublesome matter. Seeing Jace's expression, Celsus revealed a trace of a smug smile and said, "Korven tells me you can summon a succubus with no offering at all — just a soul shard. Trust me, you are quite the most gifted demon summoner we've ever seen. But even a spellcaster as capable as you still feels his heart tremble and hesitates at the sight of Kagamantus, and begins to feel genuine respect for the lighthouse and everything it represents from the bottom of his heart. What does that prove?" "What?" Jace asked. "Power… This is what power displayed right before your eyes can bring. Relying on saving others, on showing kindness and compassion — that will never make those ordinary people respect us and the cause we're working for, Yurk. Let me give you a more vivid example. You've perhaps heard the name Jace Sesso?" Jace nodded, watching the felguard's back. Celsus continued, "If you've heard of him, then you should know everything he's done. Even a man like him can't earn the Alliance's trust. To this day, even the Kirin Tor and his homeland of Lordaeron treat him as an outsider, a freak. Rumors of his death keep circulating throughout the Alliance — more than enough to show how desperately ordinary people want him gone. If even he faces that kind of situation, then what can you accomplish?" Jace put on an impatient expression and said, "Frankly, Celsus, I don't care what those people think of Jace Sesso. And you've misunderstood me too — I'm not that interested in changing the public's perception of us. I'm just worried those demons will bring the Wizard Sanctum down on us here. I don't fancy another stay in a Stormwind prison. Since you think it's fine, then it's fine. If summoning some powerful demons will get me a meeting with the Pathfinder, then I'll try summoning some as you say. I don't care what you do with those demons afterward." "Very well." Celsus nodded. "Now I'm curious — did the warlock who summoned that fellow earn an audience with the Pathfinder?" Jace asked, pointing at the felguard's back behind them. "That was a demon conjured jointly by multiple summoners," Celsus explained. "As the lighthouse's protector." Jace nodded, thinking, So that's about the level you're at. Nothing more to say then. After showing Jace around several buildings and basements beneath the lighthouse, Celsus brought him to his own quarters. It was a rather cramped underground dwelling, no sunlight — more like a cell. But at least it was no smaller than the room he rented in Stormwind. Jace hadn't expected to get his own room on this small island. It was probably because warlocks needed their secrets more. But that also meant the scale of this island was likely quite large. Especially since Celsus had mentioned an underground demon arena — that meant there was another section even deeper below. So he'd need to consider his actions here even more carefully. After Celsus left, Jace sat in the wooden chair inside, watching the doorway. Before long, Morophil appeared at the door. She'd been tailing the two of them the entire way. "Hello there, Yurk. Fancy meeting you again." The succubus smiled and sat herself down on Jace's lap, making the wooden chair beneath her backside creak immediately. "Hello, Morophil." Jace rubbed the succubus's horns with his fingers and said, "So? Did you figure out where that Pathfinder lives? Is he up in the lighthouse? How many people are there in total?" Morophil raised her eyes and thought for a moment. "Roughly fewer than twenty warlocks, plus more than thirty guards. As for demons… there are over twenty as well. Quite a lot of people live on this island, master. I don't know where the Pathfinder lives. The very top of the lighthouse — I can't get up there. After all, I'm only invisible, I can't walk through walls… The door up there is shut tight at all times." "The Pathfinder has only shown himself once at a gathering these past two days, since many people here are quite sensitive to the presence of demons. On several occasions I nearly got exposed, so I didn't dare get close for a clear look. If I got caught, master — would you risk your life to save me? The way you risked your life to save Vereesa Windrunner in Dalaran…" "Who told you I saved Vereesa in Dalaran?" Jace asked. "Vereesa herself told me." Morophil gave him a sidelong look. "She must have been trying to make me jealous, saying that on purpose…" Jace cut her off. "Damn right I'd save you. There are this many demons, warlocks, and guards here — do you think I could take them all on by myself?" Morophil lowered her gaze and said, "You could give that little blob of salsa a Blood Pact incantation to help pad the numbers." "He hasn't even dared stick his head out so far. Relying on him — ha." Jace snorted. "Who says I don't dare? Who says I don't dare!" The imp glanced outside, then said, "But there really are too many warlocks and demons here, ohh…" Morophil smiled and said, "You see, Sazeno? My master loves me, no doubt about it. He's just looking for excuses." "All right, enough nonsense." Jace shut down whatever the imp wanted to say with one sentence, then said, "I need to find a way to meet that Pathfinder, see what he's made of, and figure out how to deal with him. Under what circumstances did he show himself before?" "The night before last, at a ceremony attempting to summon a doomguard," Morophil said. "Although that summoning failed and one person got hurt, I imagine he must be quite disappointed… But do you remember that demon battle competition the fellow Celsus mentioned to you? I heard someone say he'd appear there to watch the demons fight. Many warlocks use that opportunity to show off their abilities, angling for a private audience with him." "What, master — are you planning to enter that demon competition? I can fight on your behalf, as long as you remember to replenish my mana afterwards." Morophil licked a finger with a smile and leaned against Jace's shoulder. "I'm not entering any of their competitions," Jace said. "I don't have time to slowly curry favor with some Pathfinder, Morophil. The longer we stay here, the more likely we are to be exposed. These people know more about me than I thought." "To be honest, I'm worried that some of them might have even seen me before. So before they start to suspect anything, I need to expose the truth of the black dragon's doings here as soon as possible." "How are you going to get a private audience with the Pathfinder?" Morophil asked. "Of course, if you intend to go head-to-head with that fellow right at the arena, although the risk is high and there are a lot of troublesome characters to deal with, dying to protect you would still be worth it…" "If you want to call me stupid, just say it straight," Jace said coldly. Morophil laughed happily, wrapped her arms tightly around Jace's neck, and asked softly, "So what's your plan?" Jace said, "Sola told me these dragonkin's method of enhancing these warlocks' dark power is probably achieved through incomplete ascension rites. And I saw several of them just now — they smelled of black dragon blood… and that particular kind of black dragon blood used in ascension rites. I'm guessing this is exactly the situation Sola was talking about." "That ascension rite is definitely conducted in private, because I'm guessing many people have no idea this place is controlled by black dragons. They really think it's some demonology scholar mentor showing them the way. Once the ascension rite is exposed, plenty of warlocks definitely won't stay here. But once someone undergoes the ascension rite, their mind is enslaved by the black dragon — and by then it'll be too late to resist." "So I just need to wait for someone who's earned favor to undergo the ascension rite… and won't that be the same as finding an opportunity to be alone with that Pathfinder? Of course, there's also a warlock involved, but that's still far better than going head-to-head against everyone at once, Morophil." "That does sound reasonable, master. So what should I do?" Morophil asked. Jace said, "Find the place where he conducts the ascension rites first. If it's some enclosed space, you'd better find a way to sneak in. When I need it, help me open the door, and then we'll… get the job done." "As you command, master." Morophil gave Jace a light kiss on the forehead, then asked, "So tonight, will my master help me prepare for the mission?" "A reason I can't refuse," Jace said.
