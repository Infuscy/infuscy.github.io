## Chapter 923: Making an Appearance

When the dragonkin shapeshifter Celsus had mentioned the underground arena, Jace had been thinking that even if these warlocks could summon plenty of felhounds, they couldn't possibly have dug a tunnel beneath the lighthouse and built a demon arena inside it within a month or two. Unless a few grand mages from the Kirin Tor came in person and used the kind of magic that had helped build Nethergarde Keep, there was barely a chance.

After expressing to Celsus his desire to watch the demon battles, Celsus led him away from the lighthouse, down the rocks, and into a natural cave on the shore.

It had been simply renovated into a cavern that could hold a few dozen people. The moment Jace walked in, he could smell the thick, fishy stench within. The surging, boiling fel energy of the demons was piercingly pungent and distinct to someone like Jace who knew demons well.

Every night, warlocks came here to fight other warlocks using summoned demons — mostly felhound versus felhound. It wasn't until his third day on the island that he finally heard someone planned to test a felguard's strength tonight, so he came early to wait.

Of course, he didn't care about the felguard battle at all. He'd seen plenty at the Dark Portal. He only cared about whether that Pathfinder would show up.

In the two or three days he'd spent here, he'd heard virtually every warlock mention the Pathfinder, but he'd never once seen him appear — even in public.

At the same time, the process of searching for the ascension rite site hadn't gone very smoothly.

The island where the lighthouse stood wasn't large, and the various-purpose areas didn't have very clear markings. Many rooms and halls served multiple purposes.

If that Pathfinder didn't pick a warlock for an ascension rite, there was no way to know exactly where it was. In the end, Jace decided it would be more reliable to have Morophil follow the chosen person directly.

As dusk fell and the sky darkened, the warlocks and demons in the cave grew more numerous, and even the sound of the waves outside was drowned out by conversation and the laughter of succubi.

Jace noticed a sailboat in the distance on the sea outside, sailing north from the direction of Booty Bay. He wondered whether, when they looked over here, they could see the faint fel fire glowing from the cave within the lighthouse's radiance.

"Tonight is Zuber versus Klato Jisasdes's felguard. I say, whose do you think will win? I can't remember their demons' names…"

"Zuber is certain he'll earn the Pathfinder's favor. He says his felguard once killed a Dark Iron dwarf mage."

"Shh, Jisasdes is here."

Jace watched a short human walk in through the cave entrance, followed by a tall, thin felguard. This felguard carried a broad-bladed chopper and surveyed everything and everyone before him with boundless arrogance.

Until a crowd appeared from another cave entrance, making the human warlock and his servant lower their heads and make way.

In their middle was an enormously tall human. Even wearing a cloak and a hood, you could tell he had a powerfully built frame, every step he took solid and firm.

In other words, he didn't look at all like a warlock studying dark magic. More like a born big lout of an Arathi barbarian.

Just as Jace was doubting whether this fellow could really be the so-called Pathfinder, quite a few people around them had already started calling out in waves.

"Our Pathfinder."

Those with blank expressions — the warlocks who'd undergone ascension and the suspected Cult of the Dragon members — were the first to lower their heads and bow. Many warlocks followed suit, bending at the neck and nodding.

Jace called out along with them while carefully studying the man. That was when he noticed the Pathfinder was looking at him too. He was the only newcomer on the lighthouse these past two or three days — nothing strange about the man looking at him.

Celsus followed respectfully at the Pathfinder's side, introducing him to the summoners whose demons would be fighting tonight as they walked forward.

The Pathfinder talked with him as they walked. As they passed by Jace's side, Jace casually glanced at his Mirror Stone Staff. Within the blurry human reflections, he saw that deformed head, plus those yellowish reptilian eyes.

So the Pathfinder was indeed a shapeshifter — no question there. But strangely, Jace felt he didn't seem like a warlock with much dark power — at least not enough to match the caliber of being so worshipfully revered by this many demon summoners.

Jace even felt he couldn't compare to Celsus beside him. Celsus looked like a caster skilled in hurling dark curses. Jace had originally assumed that since Celsus spoke of the Pathfinder with such reverence, the Pathfinder must be a formidable shadow manipulator.

But then again, most high-ranking dragonkin were not casters — Jace knew that much. Whether it was Drakkisath, general of the Blackrock Spire dragonkin army, or the gatekeeper of Blackwing Lair, the untamed Razorgore — both were melee fighters.

Could this dragonkin be one of these two?

Razorgore was the guardian of the black dragon Nefarian's lair, and Drakkisath was the commander of the Blackrock Spire dragonkin. They each had their own duties — would either of them be sent here?

Before long, a little imp came to the middle of the warlocks and cultists, shouting, "Tonight's duel is starting!" But in the slightly sunken arena in the middle of the cave, there were only two warlocks and one felguard.

Where had the other felguard gone?

Zuber was a tall Alteraci — a full head taller than Jisasdes. He stood confidently opposite Jisasdes as if he intended to fight the duel himself.

But just as Jace was doubting what on earth was going on, Zuber actually pulled out a soul shard and began chanting an incantation on the spot. The incantation he chanted wasn't the felguard-summoning incantation Jace had seen at the Slaughtered Lamb, but a string of very particular phrases he'd never heard before.

Before long, a void rift flickered into view, and a tall demon stepped out of it, grumbling in demonic, "Idiot, I remember we agreed — don't waste my power on this stupid competition!"

"This is also to make you stronger and more powerful, mighty Lagachris," Zuber said, raising his head slightly. "This cooperation will take my dark power to the next level. And when it does, as my servant, you'll bask in the reflected glory too, right?"

"You haven't won yet, Zuber, you arrogant little show-off," Jisasdes said unhurily. "Soon my servant Asoubri will smash that disgusting gray face of yours crooked!"

Jisasdes's mouth spared no mercy, but Jace didn't care at all about what they were arguing over.

Because he'd realized that Zuber had summoned a demon he knew — and this wasn't some summoning ceremony that required three warcasters working together, but a magic that independently summoned his own servant with a single soul shard!

Damn it. If he weren't in a hurry to destroy this place, he really would want to learn this incantation from Zuber.

And Jace wasn't the only one — the warlocks around him also started discussing this incantation when they saw it. But most of them didn't want to learn it. They felt Zuber had been hiding this incantation all along just to show off in front of the Pathfinder, to earn the qualification to receive the Pathfinder's blessing.

Soon, the surprise at this incantation turned into contempt for Zuber's behavior.

Jace glanced over toward the Pathfinder and noticed that by now several people had already crowded around their group.

One of them was pointing at the felguard below and saying something, drawing a laugh from the two disguised dragonkin. The Pathfinder seemed to quite enjoy this unexpected turn of events — he clapped his hands and laughed twice, his laugh both simple and casual, not even bothering to conceal the hoarse, gravelly voice of a dragonkin.

Of course, no one here would suspect he was a dragonkin. Even if his voice was a bit odd, it wouldn't make people think it fell outside the range of a human. Only someone like Jace, who'd heard dragons speak plenty of times, would find it familiar. But this also made Jace feel that the mystique this fellow had maintained along the way was somewhat wasted — he didn't feel like a leader lurking in the shadows.

"Looks like tonight's outcome is already decided," a female warlock nearby said in Thalassian to the high elf beside her. But the elf remained silent.

At the little imp's shout signaling the start of battle, Zuber's felguard servant Lagachris still confidently charged first. The two sides' blades clanged against each other — one slash from you, one hack from me — chopping and smashing furiously at each other's armor and weapons.

The fights of these Mo'arg creatures had always been plain and unadorned, with nothing superfluous. What the two sides competed in was basically strength, not a trace of magic, not even much technique.

After several minutes of furious chopping, Zuber's felguard was visibly moving slower and slower, the demon blood flowing down soaking his armor. Jisasdes's felguard Asoubri seized the opportunity and chopped a blade into his opponent's waist. This one cut nearly severed the other felguard in half.

The fellow staggered and dropped to one knee. The arrogant attitude he'd had when he was first summoned was still etched on his face, but the raging fel flames in his eyes were about all used up.

The watching warlocks couldn't help but snicker. That Pathfinder laughed even more happily, clapping repeatedly.

The high elf had been right not to jump to conclusions. Jace could also see that although Zuber had made a flashy entrance for his felguard, his servant wasn't more powerful than the other's, so he wasn't surprised by this outcome.

At this point, Zuber howled and demanded the fight stop, trying to prevent the other from piercing his felguard's felheart. But Jisasdes, amid the warlocks' whooping and hollering, still gave the order, commanding his servant to send his opponent back to the Twisting Nether with one stroke.

The fel spirit howled as it broke free of the flesh and departed for another plane. Almost everyone laughed and cheered at the sound. Even those Cult of the Dragon members and warlocks who'd undergone ascension, whose expressions had always been cold and indifferent, smiled with satisfaction — except for Zuber, who stared blankly at his felguard's corpse.

After several days of watching, rather than appreciation, learning, and exchanging demon-summoning incantations, this kind of activity was more like a form of release — a way to vent the oppression of living on this lonely, isolated island.

Not to mention that there might be Wizard Sanctum people watching from the shore at all times, waiting to throw everyone here into prison. But after this moment of violent pleasure, the Pathfinder still did what he was supposed to do.

He called out "Klato Jisasdes's" name, signaling the short human warlock to follow him out.

"I'm better than him, more outstanding than him. He just got lucky and summoned a vicious Mo'arg, that's all!" Zuber shouted in resentment. "Honored Pathfinder, I have greater value for all of us — whether at the lighthouse or in Moonbrook. You should understand that. I've followed you for months now, and to this day you won't permit me to receive your blessing?"

Celsus beside the Pathfinder gave him a cold glance. The warlocks around them mocked and ridiculed him even more freely.

Jace could feel that Zuber's command of dark magic was more accomplished than Jisasdes's. But clearly, here at the lighthouse, the dragonkin valued demon-summoning ability more — because they wanted to summon more and more powerful demons of all kinds to harass the garrisons of the whole of Westfall and Moonbrook.

These warlocks were only ten or twenty strong. Relying on them alone, even if they had some dark magic, they couldn't make much of a ruckus. If one got caught or killed by the Wizard Sanctum's mages, that was one head they could never replace. But demons could be "produced" from the Twisting Nether a few at a time, continuously.

They could cause greater destruction, travel farther, and were even more covert. Most importantly, they could kill more innocent people and draw more attention — not to mention how much terror those felhounds could bring to the Wizard Sanctum's mages.

At least all of this was going to end very soon.
