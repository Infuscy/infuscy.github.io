## Chapter 927: Definitely Keeping a Tight Lip

"What's the difference between black dragons and red dragons?" the boss asked curiously. "You don't hear much about black dragons around Moonbrook."

"Put simply, red dragons were enslaved by the orcs. They originally lived in the forests like mountain lions or wild bears. Because they were tough fighters, the orcs captured them and dragged them out to fight wars alongside them." Jace tried to explain. "But black dragons… they want to use the orcs who are still here to fight against us. See what I mean?"

"I thought all dragons were more or less the same damn thing." The boss took a swig of the beer the boy had brought over. "I had a cousin who was a sailor during the Orc Wars. He said his face got burned up by a red dragon. You think that's true or bullshit? The kids all think he's making it up."

"The orcs did ride red dragons in their attacks on Kul Tiras warships — it's not impossible," Jace said quietly. "If he was a sailor on a warship and got hit by fire, it really could've been a red dragon."

The boss said softly, "So red dragons don't have much brains, but black dragons are pure evil, huh?"

Jace coughed guiltily and didn't correct him. Alexstrasza probably didn't care much about her kind's reputation among mortals — she likely wouldn't mind an assessment like that.

"If what you're saying is true," the boss said, fixing Jace with a pointed stare and tapping the table, "then either we get tormented by black dragons, or we have to mess around with those demons… we've got to pick one of the two?"

"Unless there's a better way to deal with black dragons." Jace shoved a slice of cut pie into his mouth, chewing as he spoke. "But for now, the demons have caused plenty of chaos too. For the time being, there's definitely no working with those warlocks."

The boss said, "Does the king know about all this? I mean, the business about using demons to find black dragons? I can't believe that little king could've cooked any of this up."

"You didn't know the king hired a warlock as an advisor?" Jace asked.

"I… I knew a bit about that," the tavern boss said. "Isn't he the hero from Nethergarde Keep? The mage who killed a demon lord with a meteor. A lot of people say he can be compared to Medivh, who destroyed the Gurubashi army with lightning."

Jace sucked on his fingers, steadied his mug with his wrist, and took a sip of milk before saying, "All I know is that advisor knows a lot of dark magic — he even knows how to summon demons. You know what I mean. Don't think Wrynn's young and doesn't know what's going on."

"So Moonbrook should be more or less safe for now?" the boss asked.

Jace looked at him and thought it over. He hadn't heard any news of trouble from the Stonemasons, and if the Defias Brotherhood wasn't making moves, then Moonbrook should be fine for the time being.

After he got back to Stormwind, he should ask Erland Dudemore about this.

Or later, when the mage tower was being rebuilt, he could chat with VanCleef and sound him out on the payment situation for Stormwind's reconstruction work — as the kingdom of Stormwind's court mage, showing interest in that area wouldn't seem out of place.

But Jace couldn't shake the feeling that their little group of warlocks had ended up replacing the Defias Brotherhood's role.

If he hadn't destroyed Reshael's nest at the lighthouse, if they'd just let those warlocks at the lighthouse keep summoning demons and releasing them to wreak havoc in Moonbrook and the surrounding areas, it wouldn't have been a few years before the whole western region really did turn into a wasteland.

It might even be worse than in the game — imagine a nightmare zone crawling with demons and cultists, with black dragons thrown into the mix, and yet only two days' travel from Stormwind.

But if the warlocks were the Defias Brotherhood, then where was Onyxia?

"For now, it should be more or less fine," he answered.

"I was actually planning to head over to Lakeshire first and have a look," the tavern boss muttered. "Now I think I can wait and see for a bit."

"Lakeshire?" Jace said. "What do you think the black dragons are trying to accomplish by drawing the kingdom's soldiers all the way over here, sir?"

"You mean… Blackrock Mountain?" the boss asked.

Jace lowered his voice. "The king should realize that soon enough — or he already has. My advice is, don't settle in Lakeshire. At least not for the next few years."

"Understood." The tavern boss nodded. "Then, can I tell other people about this?"

"People you trust," Jace whispered. "I haven't received authorization from the senior mage, Master Andormas… strictly speaking, I shouldn't be saying any of this. But thanks for the grilled meat, the pie, and the goat milk, sir."

"I'll keep my mouth shut, mage," the tavern boss said earnestly.

"I know." Jace drained the mug of milk in one go, fumbled with the apprentice mage ring he'd just quietly slipped on, and said, "Oh right, boss — I took part in the lighthouse operation. When I was fighting the demons, I got injured. The healing potion might've taken a while to kick in, and the wound accidentally got blood on your bedding. That… shouldn't be a problem, right?"

The tavern boss grinned and whispered, "As long as it's not demon blood, don't worry about it, mage."

"Great, thanks. Then help me wrap up the rest of the pie and the meat." Jace nodded.

Carrying the wrapped food out of the tavern, he planned to buy a couple of new outfits and see if he could get his hands on a new cloak.

After chatting with the boss, the stuff he'd been bottling up inside had loosened up quite a bit, and he'd relaxed considerably. But the moment he stepped out the door, a familiar female voice called out to him.

"My good sir."

Jace caught his breath and looked over. Demisette Claus stood cloaked in gray in the corner, gazing into the distance. She wasn't looking at him — she was looking at several Wizard Sanctum mages by the plaza at the far end of the street.

"You know there are mages watching me here?" Jace asked.

"I know," Demisette said. "But they're over at the mine right now. And even you probably don't think a killer who just slaughtered every warlock in that lighthouse would come straight out and meet a female warlock right afterward, do you?"

Jace said, "I didn't slaughter every warlock in that tower. Not even most of them."

Demisette's lips trembled. It was obvious she'd been pushed to the brink of tension, but she still drew a long breath and said slowly, "That doesn't matter to me."

"You've got some nerve, Lady Claus," Jace said.

"I'm honored to be praised by Jace Sesso," Demisette said with a stiff smile. Jace glanced up and down the street. "Let's talk inside."

Demisette nodded and followed him back into the inn. There were quite a few more people inside than there had been that morning when Jace first came downstairs, and empty seats were scarce. The two of them pressed toward a corner by the window, far away from most of the crowd.

"What do you want?" Jace asked.

"You know perfectly well." Demisette fixed her eyes on Jace. "I don't care what you did at the lighthouse. After all, I knew early on that there was something wrong with that so-called blessing of dark power… But I did you a favor, Sesso. I took an enormous risk doing it — whether from the other warlocks or from the Wizard Sanctum. You can't be hoping those Wizard Sanctum mages will drag me off to the Stockades, so you won't have to teach me that spell anymore, can you?"

"I never thought that," Jace said, keeping his voice low. "I never thought things would end up that far. I underestimated how deeply those black dragons had gotten into people's minds. And I already taught you the incantation. I also told you the casting gestures, told you the casting materials, and even told you how important emotion is to that spell. After that, you just need to practice. What, am I supposed to guarantee that everyone who's ever been in contact with me and wants to learn an incantation from me will learn everything I know a hundred percent and be exactly like me?"

"At least guarantee that I learn it," Demisette said.

"Fine, fine, fine." Jace said, helpless. "How about this — come find me at Ilgal's Tower in two weeks. You know where it is, right?"

The female warlock nodded. "I know. Exactly two weeks from now?"

"Fourteen days from now," Jace added.

"Then it's settled. I hope you'll remember this promise."

With that, the female warlock stood up without hesitation and left the inn immediately. Judging by her movements, she looked like she wanted to bolt out of there in one go.
