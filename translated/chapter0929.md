## Chapter 929: The Wizard Sanctum's Token of Appreciation

The next morning, Jace hurried to the Mage Quarter. It was raining, and he threw on his dragonhide cloak — absurdly effective at keeping water out. But more importantly, he was protecting the sash at his waist: Angellista's Grasp.

Just as the king had said, once this red-gem-inlaid silver-trimmed sash was on, whatever else he wore got elevated along with it.

But by the same token, once you put this thing on, there was no being low-key anymore. If he went out later and needed to keep his identity under wraps, he'd probably have to hide it under his clothes.

He was still going to wear it, though. The thing's magic-stabilizing power was simply too strong. Especially here, magic stability didn't just improve hit rating — it also saved on casting costs.

In the great hall at the top of the mage tower, quite a few people had gathered. Many mages were busy here. When Jace appeared, a mage he'd never seen before in his life caught sight of this dark-gray figure and immediately drew a sharp breath.

"Archmage Jace Sesso."

Hearing his name, the nearby mages all looked over in his direction.

Noticing the commotion here, Andormas, who'd been chatting with several mages in the distance, also raised his head. "Jace…"

"Master Andormas."

When Jace addressed him that way, the younger mages spontaneously cleared a path — whether out of gratitude, respect, or fear.

"Archmage Magino Dumas has already reported the lighthouse matter to me, Jace. I'm sorry that in the end it still fell to you to finish this. I'd originally thought we wouldn't need to drag you into it." Andormas lowered his head with an apologetic expression. "But when it comes to dealing with black dragons and dark magic, it's clear our experience is still far from sufficient."

"As a court mage and the king's advisor, this matter is also within my duties, Master Andormas," Jace said. "But I didn't come today specifically for this either."

"Yes, I know. I also received the king's letter this morning," Andormas said. "We'll release Mr. Erland Dudemore as soon as possible, and we'll also offer compensation for the dozens of days we held him without sufficient evidence."

"Can I see him now?" Jace asked.

"Of course. I was just about to send someone to the Stockades — you can go pick him up there," Andormas said. "There's one more thing, Jace. Archmage Dumas sent two items over from Moonbrook. I don't think the Wizard Sanctum should keep them here in the Mage Quarter — they should be given to you."

"What items?" Jace asked.

Andormas was about to speak, then looked around at the younger mages and said in the end, "Let me show you."

The archmage's expression was quite mysterious and complicated. Jace's curiosity was piqued, so he followed Andormas out of the hall and downstairs, arriving at a hidden chamber in the back.

Jace knew this was the Wizard Sanctum's vault. After unlocking several doors, they came to a smaller, darker chamber. There wasn't much here — on a small table sat an iron box and a small wooden box.

Andormas glanced at Jace, then used another key to open the iron box. Immediately, a thick, filthy stench of dragon blood wafted out, making Jace feel as though he'd been transported back to that stone hall beneath the lighthouse.

Inside the iron box lay an enormous reptilian head, wrapped in arcane-enchanted felweave — Reshael's malformed head that barely qualified as a dragon, let alone a human. It looked like a stunted, horn-and-scalet-covered mutant sheep's head.

With its eyes no longer spewing flames, they were even more like two rotting, putrid grapes. Whether you looked at it or smelled it, it was enough to turn your stomach. The enchantment on the cloth wrapping the head clearly wasn't doing much.

"We retrieved this head from the collapsed ruins, Jace," Andormas said. "It survived the blaze largely intact, with not too much damage. I think this should be enough to shift the attention of those nobles and generals away from the so-called demon disaster and toward the black dragons — toward the threat they should really be watching for."

"That's exactly right, Master," Jace said.

"I imagine this would make quite a keepsake for you as well," Andormas said. "The Wizard Sanctum has no intention of keeping it here. You can take it. After showing it to the king and those nobles, bring it to Ilgal's Tower and give those black dragons something to think about if they ever try to come around."

Jace hadn't considered that, but now that the archmage mentioned it, he was genuinely tempted. "But it reeks too much."

"If you'd like, later on we can help you turn it into a specimen ornament that won't give off an evil stench — free of charge, mind you, as a small token of thanks and compensation." Andormas smiled slightly.

"Really?" Jace nodded. "Then I won't be polite about it."

Before Andormas locked the iron box back up, Jace stole another glance at the head.

In the game, the bronze dragons had once used Reshael to test players' teams, claiming that only by bringing back Reshael's head could they prove their strength and earn the bronze dragons' trust — entrusting them with a higher mission: obtaining the Scepter of the Shifting Sands, opening the gates of Ahn'Qiraj, and putting a permanent end to the silithid scourge in Silithus.

Now Reshael's head was in his possession. The bronze dragons wouldn't, in the future, send mercenaries to kill their way into his tower and snatch this thing to prove their strength, would they?

Jace found his own thought a little amusing. The bronze dragons couldn't possibly be that rigid. Probably not.

"There's more." Andormas's words interrupted Jace's strange train of thought. He picked up the other box. "And this — I think you'll be quite interested too, Jace."

"What is it this time."

Jace furrowed his brow and mentally prepared himself for a second shock, thinking this wouldn't be another piece of Reshael, would it? A heart, hopefully. Best if it was a heart.

But when Andormas opened the lid, although the scent that wafted out did carry a hint of blood, it was entirely unbearable — on the contrary, it made one feel as though they'd been saved, largely offsetting the physical and mental anguish brought by Reshael's head beside it.

Jace noticed a softly glowing orb inside — like an overly perfect, round drop of blood.

"This was a treasure Reshael kept on his person. After our initial research, we've found that he seemed to have relied on this to maintain his formidable stamina and endurance," the archmage explained. "Of course, in the face of your magic, this gem didn't seem all that effective."

If Andormas knew what Jace had used to chop Reshael to death, he probably wouldn't think this gem was "not all that effective."

Not to mention that during the battle, Jace had been quite astonished by this dragon lord's terrifying vitality. Time and again, battered by Ussaras's magic, time and again struck by the sickle blade, he'd still been able to get back up time and again and continue fighting with dreadful combat power — until Jace finally cut him clean in half. Even after lying on the ground, he'd retained a bit of consciousness and taken a good long while before quietly dying.

Could it have been because of this gem?

Judging by its appearance, Jace had pretty much guessed what it was. This was probably Reshael's "Life Gem" from the game — a trinket that temporarily raised maximum health and restored a portion of it. It was a prized piece of gear, especially beloved by warriors, particularly protection warriors.

Although Jace had never played a warrior, he'd seen plenty of warriors fight over this item. Combined with the glaringly obvious red bead on its icon, his impression of it had been quite deep.

Andormas closed the box and handed it to Jace. "Whether it's me, Archmage Magino Dumas, or any of the other masters, we all agree that this item should be in your hands to handle."

Jace took the box. "Thank you, Master."
