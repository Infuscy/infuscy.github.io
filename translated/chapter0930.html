Chapter 930: The Stonemason Who Shouldn't Have Been in Prison

At noon, outside the Stormwind Stockades, Jace was watching a group of kids who'd just jumped into the canal, thinking that maybe he should jump in with them and take a proper bath — wash away all the dust, all the unpleasantness he'd picked up from the west.

"Jace."

Hearing someone call him, Jace turned around and saw a thin, wild-bearded man standing behind him, drawing a deep breath. "I really don't know how to thank you."

"Erland?" Jace asked.

Erland Dudemore shrugged and spread his hands. "Is the difference that big? Don't look at me like you've seen a ghost. Life in prison definitely isn't as comfortable as Goldshire — no chance to put on any weight."

Jace smiled and said, "A shave and a trim would do you a lot of good. I heard the Wizard Sanctum is going to give you some compensation. What did they give you?"

"Five gold, plus a promise to cure me of any illness I picked up in prison." Erland patted his shoulder. "Look at me — what disease do you think I could've gotten? Five gold in compensation. If I hadn't been locked up, the money I'd have made outside in this time could probably buy the whole damn prison."

"Who'd you meet in prison — dwarves?" Jace asked.

Erland laughed. "Indeed. I was locked up with two Dark Iron dwarves. Aside from skin color, I couldn't lose to them in any department — whether dark bullshit or bullshit-blowing. How about we go grab a drink? The Wizard Sanctum's compensation money ought to be spent on something proper. Besides, I want to see if spending all that time with dwarves has improved my drinking."

"I can tell you right now — it won't do a thing," Jace said.

"Hah." Erland glanced at Jace. "You don't look like someone who spends a lot of time around dwarves. So where to — the Gilded Rose, or the Pig and Whistle?"

"The Gilded Rose. It's closer," Jace said.

They ordered some roast meat and stew — things clearly lacking in prison — plus Ironforge pale ale from the bar. The moment Erland sat down, he asked, "So what exactly happened, Jace? Those mages said you resolved the Moonbrook situation. How'd you resolve it — you didn't just 'resolve' it, did you?"

Saying the second "resolve," Erland drew a hand across his throat and widened his eyes.

Jace nodded. "In the end, a whole bunch of crazed cultists tried to kill me. I had no choice. But the warlocks who actually came at me were few. Aside from those who'd drunk the black dragonkin's blood and lost their minds, most of them are still alive. The Wizard Sanctum should have rounded them all up."

He thought of Demisette Claus, who hadn't been on the island at the time, and added, "But as for warlocks in other places, I'm not sure how they're doing."

Erland said, "That Guide had a list of his followers. If those mages can find that list in that tower, at least it'll make the warlocks outside keep their heads down. Even if they can't find it, those warlocks won't take that gamble."

"Between you and me, the warlocks outside don't know you, Jace. I knew it even from inside prison — if you ever came back from Kalimond and found out someone was making trouble in Moonbrook, those people definitely wouldn't come out of it well. Thinking about how a few years back I still wanted to beat the shit out of you to make you behave and stop listening to that lunatic Gajin… now I'm glad I never actually did. If a warlock like you held a grudge, I'd never sleep well for the rest of my life. Anyway, thanks again for pulling me out of prison."

"Don't mention it. You didn't do anything wrong — at least nothing they thought you did, right?" Jace said. Erland smiled and raised his glass. Jace picked up his own mug and they clinked.

"Is our crew still at the old place?" Erland asked.

"No," Jace said. "They're in the south now. I'm not sure if you know the exact spot…"

"Ah, I'm not familiar with that area either," Erland said. "After we finish drinking, point me the way when you head out. I'll go find them."

"Before I head back to Ilgal's, I need to stop by there too — my clothes are still at Kaylen's," Jace said. "We can head over together when the time comes."

"No problem," Erland said.

After saying that, he took a few drinks, then glanced at Jace, who'd been quiet this whole time, and asked, "What's on your mind?"

"I've dealt with cultists under mind control before," Jace said. "Those people enchanted by the dragonkin's magic — once you kill the dragonkin, the enchantment dissipates quickly and the controlled recover their senses on their own. When I found those dragonkin at the lighthouse, I'd originally thought it would be the same. As long as I killed the leading dragonkin, the people up in the lighthouse who were obviously under the black dragon's magic would break free of the control."

"But you ended up having to kill them all," Erland said, looking over. "So you think the dragonkin at the lighthouse weren't the ones controlling those people, right?"

"I think the dragonkin's power alone couldn't have controlled that many people," Jace said.

Erland asked, "You think there's a real black dragon still lurking in the Kingdom of Stormwind?"

"I don't know," Jace shook his head.

At that moment, the barmaid brought over a plate of sliced boiled chicken, and the two of them tacitly paused their discussion.

After the barmaid left, Jace added, "Either there's a real black dragon, or… there are more dragonkin lurking here than I thought. Either way, I feel like this matter is still a long way from truly being resolved. The king seems ready to restart SI:7's cooperation with us warlocks, Erland. If you have any relevant leads, remember to come let me know at Ilgal's Tower. Also, how are things with the Stonemasons lately?"

"How would I know?" Erland waved a hand. "I've been in prison for two months. I got out and came straight here to drink with you."

"Didn't you meet any other Stonemasons in prison?" Jace asked casually.

Erland looked up and glanced at him. "Should all of our guild members be in prison? The Stonemasons are a legitimate organization, not the Blackwater Pirates or some thieves' guild."

"I didn't mean that. I'm just asking," Jace said. "Then help me get in touch with Mr. Edwin VanCleef. I want to talk to him about the mage tower again."

"You've scraped together enough money to renovate the mage tower?" Erland asked.

"Still a bit short," Jace said. "But I don't think it's far off. Once I patch it together from here and there, it should…"

Jace hesitated, thinking that VanCleef's estimate for renovating Ilgal's Tower had been somewhere between two hundred and two hundred and fifty gold.

Because of all the fuss in Moonbrook, Jace hadn't had time to go ask Master Marin whether a package had arrived from Quel'Thalas. Wynssa might have already sent the commission payment for tracking down the Highborne soul mirror in Kalimond.

With that commission, plus what Sola had paid him in Winterspring for finding Mosa, plus Jace's current savings of about seventy gold, he wondered if it would exceed a hundred and fifty gold.

He actually didn't hold much hope that the Reliquary would pay a few dozen gold for some leads on the soul mirror's whereabouts. And even if it reached a hundred and fifty, how was he supposed to fill the remaining gold gap?

He didn't know if the Ironforge dwarf hunters had found that legendary crocodile in the underground channels of Maraudon. If they had, that would be another ten gold — provided they were still willing to honor their promise.

Where else?

Jace was actually considering going somewhere like Booty Bay to find that goblin dark-magic collector, Pucodias, and sell Reshael's head. After all, that head was hideously ugly, and how could a dragon beast's head compare to a real black dragon's head in terms of showing off?

He'd start the bidding at two hundred gold. After all, this wasn't just any dragon beast — this was a named dragon lord. This might even let him buy a small house in the Park district.

"By the way, do you think a goblin would be willing to pay for a dragon beast's head, Erland?" Jace asked.

"Dragon beast?" Erland repeated the clumsy Thalassian loanword, then asked, "What's that?"

"Similar to dragonkin — both are servants of the black dragons," Jace explained. "But they're a kind of human-bodied, dragon-headed monster. Have you heard of this sort of creature?"

"Ah… I know. I've heard orcs mention them." Erland shifted his posture, leaning on the table. "But I've got to tell you the truth — goblins aren't too keen on collecting black dragon stuff, Jace. Especially dragon claws, or dragon teeth, dragon scales, even treasures the black dragons collected aren't much better. Let alone a head. I don't know why, but that's how they are. Afraid the black dragons will come looking for revenge?"

Erland's words reminded Jace all at once — goblins did seem genuinely afraid of black dragons. Plenty of black dragons enslaved large numbers of goblins as their servants. He wondered if there was a connection there.

"You'd be better off asking in Dalaran. Those people have bigger balls. As long as it's got a bit of magic on it, they'll take it — never mind a black dragon's head, they'd take Medivh's head," Erland suggested again.

"Or sell it to the elves," Jace added.

"Or sell it to the elves," Erland nodded.
