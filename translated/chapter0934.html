Chapter 934: The Uses of a Mage Tower

Ilgal's Tower. He'd finally come here after returning from Kalimond.

The moment he stepped inside, the familiar damp smell of old wood and ancient stone hit him. The first time he'd smelled it, he'd felt a sense of age and history to an old building. Now, smelling it again, all Jace could think about was how much it would cost to renovate this place.

Two Dazharum were crouving on the stairs high above, watching this way. Jace waved and said in Eredun, "Thank you both for guarding this place while I was away, gentlemen. Now head up to the top and wait — we've got friendly guests coming."

The felhounds made a low rumbling sound in response, then turned and scrambled upward until both their figures and the noise vanished. Jace looked back at the tower's narrow entrance and said, "You can come in now, Mr. Edwin VanCleef."

VanCleef walked in and looked up, surveying the tower. Jace went on, "Remember what we discussed last time? About how to renovate this tower."

"Of course. It's not every day a mage hires us to renovate a mage tower," VanCleef said with a smile. "Clear out the entire lower level of the mage tower and build stairs along the side wall leading to the upper levels."

"And without disrupting the flow of magical energy through the entire tower," Jace added.

"That's a basic requirement. Of course we'll be strictly mindful of that." As he spoke, VanCleef carefully looked up and surveyed the interior of the whole tower.

After a moment he said, "May I ask — Archmage Jace Sesso, what do you plan to place in the lower level of this tower? For example, a massive magic runestone — like the kind the elves of Quel'Thalas set up, as a support for a mana shield? Or perhaps just a statue of special significance?"

"Does that matter?" Jace asked.

"Of course," VanCleef said. "If it's just an ordinary stone statue, the requirements for the stair materials and so on don't need to be so strict. But if it's something with magical energy, we have to ensure that everything we install can withstand the long-term effects of magic — and the corresponding price goes up too. Of course, the situation throughout the kingdom hasn't been great lately. Even mages are more inclined to save their money, Master Sesso. I can understand if you want to save a bit of budget. But considering that Ilgal's Tower is one of the very few mage towers in the entire kingdom, I wouldn't want the Stonemasons' work here to have even the slightest problem — ruining the reputation we built at Nethergarde Keep and Stormwind."

"This is the Stonemasons' reputation, and it's my reputation too. I certainly won't try to save money in this regard," Jace said. "Then please be as straightforward with the Stonemasons and with me as you can."

Jace gazed up at the top of the tower, mulled it over for a moment, then said, "I need materials that can withstand dark magic as much as possible — including shadow and fel."

"Dark magic?" VanCleef looked over at Jace with a complicated expression.

Jace lowered his gaze and looked at him, explaining further: "Some of the rumors in Stormwind are true — I am indeed a user of dark magic, Mr. VanCleef. But don't worry about it. If I didn't master dark magic, how could I help the king deal with some dark-magic troubles? You wouldn't want someone who's never touched a chisel joining the Stonemasons, would you?"

"And I don't want to hide anything from you either. I plan to keep demons in this tower."

"Demons?"

"Yes, demons. Both the Wizard Sanctum and King Wrynn have always wanted to know the true reason the orcs invaded our world. And now Dalaran and Lordaeron have reliable evidence showing that it was the demons who enslaved, deceived — or, one might say, urged — the orcs to come here. Of course, this is only speculation drawn from some of the orcs' confessions and some surface phenomena that have appeared in the Horde. If you want to know the real reason, you have to find a demon who knows the inside story and ask them personally, don't you?"

"By the Holy Light… so this would become a prison for demons?" VanCleef asked.

"A prison for demons, exactly. Just like the Violet Hold in Dalaran, or the prison the Kul Tirans plan to build on Tol Barad. Of course, mine would be on a much smaller scale, and more purpose-driven." Jace went on. "So I'm hoping the lower level of this mage tower can accommodate a sufficiently large demon-binding circle. I'll also need runestones or other magic devices to supply energy to the circle — enough space for me to bind a demon here for interrogation."

"Do the king and the Wizard Sanctum know about this?" VanCleef asked.

Jace said, "I'm in the process of applying. May His Majesty understand and approve my plan."

VanCleef thought for a moment and said, "I've heard some rumors that SI:7 is cooperating with demons — using enslaved demons' sensitivity to dark magic to search for black dragons hiding within our kingdom. King Wrynn does seem quite open-minded about this sort of thing. But if he accepts your plan, Master Sesso, the soldiers at Stonewatch probably won't be sleeping well."

"That depends on how good a job you and your brothers do, Mr. VanCleef," Jace said.

Hearing this, the hint of a joking smile that had been on VanCleef's face faded considerably. "I feel the weight of that responsibility."

"After all, this is Ilgal's Tower," Jace said. "If it weren't you and your guild taking on this project, I wouldn't have told you these internal matters. So remember to keep it confidential."

VanCleef was a remarkably calm man. Jace could tell he rarely showed strong emotions. But hearing Jace say this, he still glanced over with a trace of tension, as if somewhat regretting that Jace had been so forthcoming about the tower's future purpose.

Jace was telling VanCleef about this now in a deliberately ambiguous way — not just because he'd asked, but also with the future in mind.

If, in the future, some patrolling soldier or some mage apprentice or adventurer who'd come here on a whim happened to catch a glimpse of the doomlord inside, and word of it spread throughout the kingdom, he'd have a witness with enough influence to help explain the situation.

VanCleef was writing something in the notebook he'd brought, then said, "As far as I know, when Ilgal's Tower was built, the possibility of countering dark magic was already taken into account — that's why it was able to withstand the orc warlocks' assault without suffering fatal damage. So the key lies in the choice of stair materials. Either we use oak from Dawn Forest, arcane-infused wood from around the Sunwell in Quel'Thalas, or pine from the northern reaches of the Hinterlands… but the overall price would multiply several times over."

"Multiply?" Jace asked.

VanCleef looked toward the door, his lips moving slightly, then said, "It might end up in the range of four to five hundred gold. But if it's really done, the quality of Ilgal's Tower could even compare to the mage tower in front of the Dark Portal at Nethergarde Keep. According to Archmage Khadgar's instructions before he left for the Dark Portal, that tower has been built and maintained to the highest specifications from the start."

Jace wasn't surprised in the slightest, given that that tower could support the high elves' shield magic stones, withstand all that bombardment of infernal hellfire from the Eredar sorcerers — the energy fluctuations it brought weren't enough to make the tower's structure give way — and in the end, it even took a direct charge from a pit lord and still stood firm.

But did he really need to spend that much money using arcane wood from Quel'Thalas or pine from the Hinterlands?

"There's actually a slightly cheaper option that can achieve a similar effect." VanCleef stroked the old, cracked stair railing beside him. "Use oak or pine of good enough quality — like Khaz Modan red pine. But every few months to a year or so, have an enchanter come reinforce the magic resistance of these stairs. Especially dark-magic resistance, like you just said, Master Sesso. If the enchanter's skill is high enough, the protective performance might reach over ninety percent of what the best materials could achieve. I imagine it should be enough to handle anything short of the most extreme situations — say, a catastrophe on the scale of the Dark Portal…"

"Raising performance from five points to nine costs far less than raising it from nine to ten. I imagine that's easy to understand. This is also the most cost-effective choice."

Jace wasn't renovating Ilgal's Tower to meet the Burning Legion's army head-on. But in the future, he wanted to use the tower's own magic-guiding power, together with the ley line nexus beneath the tower, to power Kalrez and open a portal to Draenor. That meant he'd have to consider how much tearing force opening a void channel would bring.

Hire an enchanter to come to Ilgal's Tower?

The most skilled enchanters Jace knew right now were Kaylen Aylo and Wynssa. One was a tailor who specialized in enchanting mage robes, the other was a Farstrider ranger. Neither of them looked anything like the kind of enchanter VanCleef was talking about.

After thinking for a while, he suddenly realized — Dawn Forest was a mysterious stretch of woodland in the middle of Duskwood. Neither hunters, explorers, nor herbalists dared venture deep into it. It was said that anyone who entered would experience hallucinations.

What it had in common with the northern forests of the Hinterlands was that green dragons were sighted in both.
