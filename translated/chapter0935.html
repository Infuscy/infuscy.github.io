Chapter 935: A Blessing of the Dream

"Quel'Thalas wood has decent magic resistance — I can understand that. But why would trees from Dawn Forest and the Hinterlands have similar power?" Jace still wanted to confirm one more time. VanCleef replied, "I'm an engineer, not a geographer. But I know this much: it's said that the pine of the Hinterlands receives the blessing of the Forest Goddess, giving it extraordinary resilience and the energy to resist dark magic. Actually, I don't believe in the existence of any Forest Goddess — but the pine trees there are astonishingly tough. My guess is that because those forests have long suffered under the dark magic of the trolls, they've evolved some kind of special resistance. As for Dawn Forest… that's always been a mysterious place. Legend has it that the lumber for Karazhan was cut from there. But recently, because of the dark energy spreading from Karazhan and the increasingly dangerous state of Duskwood, the logging work there has been suspended for a long time." Although what VanCleef said wasn't very certain, Jace was fairly confident it was the influence of the green dragons. Among humans, legends of the Forest Goddess mostly came from green dragons. And didn't he know a real green dragon? If he could get Sola to bless the wooden stairs in this tower, wouldn't it have a similar effect? Of course, Jace didn't have much choice either way. He really had no good way to scrape together three or four hundred gold all at once in the short term — even if he could really sell Reshael's head for a high price of two hundred gold, there'd still be a huge gap. Not to mention that, from the looks of it now, the goblin sales channel didn't work either. "If we use Khaz Modan pine, roughly how long would the whole project take?" Jace asked. VanCleef said, "I estimate about six to seven months. Pay half the cost upfront so our designers and the gentlemen applying to the crown and the Wizard Sanctum for a work permit can get started as soon as possible — then work could begin in as little as a week, Master Sesso." "Although I'm not very familiar with the specific procedures, Mr. VanCleef," Jace said, "I am the king's court mage, and I'm also someone from the Mage Quarter. Not to mention that Master Andormas, Archmage Magino Dumas, and Master Marin are all my good friends and mentors — which makes me more than half a Wizard Sanctum council member already. At the same time, this tower isn't managed by me — it's my private property, gifted to me jointly by the king and the Wizard Sanctum. The contract is right here with me. So based on that, I'd imagine the permitting process should be far easier than for other mage tower renovation projects, wouldn't it?" "This mage tower was gifted to you outright, Master Sesso?" VanCleef said, a little surprised. "My apologies — Erland didn't make that clear to me. If possible, could I take a look at the relevant records?" "Of course." Jace went up to the very top and brought back the box containing the deed, the king's letter, and the records, then handed it to the engineer in front of him. VanCleef flipped through it for a while before saying, "My apologies, but based on our past experience renovating mage towers… we've never encountered this sort of situation. Ilgal's Tower is indeed your property, Master Sesso — the seals of both the Wizard Sanctum and the king are authentic and reliable." "In that case, only a third of the initial cost is needed to start work," he said, returning the box to Jace. "I'll need to go back first and have people come take more detailed measurements, then finalize the construction plan and the final price. A rough estimate — it shouldn't exceed two hundred and thirty gold." Jace nodded. This was within his psychological expectations. "How's the Stormwind reconstruction project going, Mr. VanCleef?" "Mostly finished. Just some final details remain in the Valley of Heroes and the Cathedral," VanCleef answered. "I was asking about the payment side," Jace said. "King Wrynn cares about that a great deal." "I'm glad to hear King Wrynn still values this matter." Edwin VanCleef sighed softly. "Although the payments have been coming in, they keep finding all sorts of reasons to delay. The recent excuse was the dark magic incident in Moonbrook — mobilizing soldiers, pacifying the townsfolk of Moonbrook, workers and farms all needed money. I heard the crisis in Moonbrook has basically come to an end recently, and the other day Lord Elgadin promised to resume payments immediately. That's good news. I hope everything can return to normal quickly. Which is why we value the Ilgal's Tower renovation project very much, Master Sesso — this payment can relieve a considerable amount of hardship. So please rest assured that we will definitely take this project seriously and ensure this mage tower achieves your intended goals." From what VanCleef said, it was clear that the Stonemasons hadn't yet fallen out with the nobles of Stormwind. Although the kingdom's not-doing-great financial situation, they were still trying their best to honor their commitments. In the game, if it weren't for Onyxia infiltrating Stormwind, the situation wouldn't have slid so rapidly into a point where both sides were at each other's throats. Could it be that here, the Black Dragon Princess really wasn't planning to show up and make trouble? Then again, Reshael's appearance was enough to show that Nefarian was far more anxious than Onyxia to divert Stormwind's attention to the west. His attempt to break into Redridge, seize Stonewatch, and create a buffer zone for Blackrock Spire — that ambition was already well known throughout the Kingdom of Stormwind. Which meant that, under Jace's influence, the black dragons' plan had been exposed at least ten years earlier than in the game. In that sort of situation, it was understandable that Onyxia would choose not to directly interfere in Stormwind's affairs. As for Nefarian, Jace didn't believe the orc forces of Blackrock Spire had the ability to take this place while he was stationed at Ilgal's Tower in mutual support with Stonewatch — unless he came in person. And taking one more step back — even if the Black Dragon Prince really did come himself without regard for anything else, Jace wouldn't be able to worry about that much either. He'd have no choice but to call on Lord Kalrez to show up in person and have a go at him, to see how they really measured up. If that day really came, hopefully Kalrez would have already gathered enough essence from Karazhan in Deadwind Pass by then. After VanCleef left, Jace immediately wrote a letter and gave it to a Stonewatch griffin rider, asking him to fly back to Stormwind and deliver it to Sola Shadow in the Mage Quarter. Three days later, in the early morning, Jace was in the mage tower studying Dwarvish and Old Kalimondian. He'd been reading for a while and was getting tired, and was about to bend his fingers to calculate the day Demisette Claus would visit, when he suddenly heard the crunch of footsteps on leaves outside. He went to the window and looked out. A golden-haired woman in green was looking up this way. "Jace Sesso?" "Sola — beautiful, kind Lady Somariel — welcome, welcome to Ilgal's Tower!" Jace waved his hand vigorously, then ignoring Morophil's disdainful look beside him, ran all the way downstairs and out the door. He came before Sola and gave a slight bow. "You got my letter?" Sola looked around at the forests here, then fixed her gaze back on the mage tower in front of her. "I came because I saw your letter, Jace Sesso. There's no need to be so polite and courteous. You said in your letter that you hoped I could help with something. I don't know what I can do, but I'll do my best." "Come in and talk," Jace said. The green dragon nodded and followed Jace inside. Jace first brewed a pot of tea — Goldthorn that Master Marin had given him over the years, which he'd always been reluctant to drink. "Remember I mentioned to you that I wanted to renovate this tower, Sola?" Jace looked at the stairs below, which were not only old and shabby but badly damaged. "Next I want to rebuild these stairs here — build them out of pine. When the time comes, could you…" "Bless the wood?" Sola asked. "That's right, exactly that." Jace smiled. "Because at that time, this tower might be filled with shadow energy. I figured a green dragon's blessing could protect this tower to some extent from erosion by these dark forces." "The forests near the Dreamgate have similar power, Jace. And I'd guess the blessing power of those dreamwardens is far stronger than mine," Sola said. "You could hire those people to harvest the wood nearby…" "Uh…" "Ah… yes, hiring those people to take the risk would no doubt cost a pretty sum." Sola sighed. "I'm starting to slowly understand what Grede said to me back then. Living in Stormwind really does cost money everywhere. So if I bless this tower for you, how much will you pay me as compensation?" Jace sighed and scratched his head. "You've fallen, Sola. This is undoubtedly our responsibility…" Sola burst out laughing, and after a good long while she said, "I'm joking, Jace Sesso. You saved my life, and after I was banished from the Emerald Dream, you were willing to take me in and find me a place to live. How could I have the face to ask for gold as compensation? It's just that my power is certainly not as strong as a dreamwarden's. After this tower's renovation is complete, I'll try to summon one of Queen Ysera's guardians here to help you." Hearing Sola say this, Jace was stunned for a moment, then asked: "Would they be willing to help?" "They just hope I don't go back to the Emerald Dream — they haven't excluded me from the green dragonflight entirely. They can't completely ignore my call," Sola explained. "And they're not helping me — they're helping you, Jace Sesso. You're the hero who rescued Queen Alexstrasza, not a fragile young dragon with weak willpower who can't withstand the corruption of the Nightmare. They have no reason to refuse." "Not being able to withstand the Nightmare's corruption doesn't mean much." Jace picked up a salted biscuit, chewed on it, and said, "You're a dragon — you should know better than me that the most powerful of the Dragon Aspects was also the first to be corrupted. And more importantly, you've already thrown off the corruption. In a way, this even means you've gone further than Neltharion." Hearing Neltharion's name, Sola trembled faintly all over. Jace went on: "Don't say it was with someone's help that you threw it off. There were far more people in this world who wanted to help Neltharion than us, and far stronger people too — and he still never managed to struggle free until the very end." After hearing these words, Sola sat there in silence. She was looking straight at the bright window across from her. Jace caught her expression from the corner of his eye, and it felt as though Sola had almost completely shed her human quality in that moment. Even though her appearance and features were all human, her demeanor looked exactly like a dragon gazing out at the scenery.
