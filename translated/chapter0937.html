Chapter 937: Bedtime Story

It didn't look like there was a problem, but it tasted strange — nothing like the potato-braised pork or red-braised pork he'd once known so well. The caramelized sugar seemed a little overdone, and the meat was somewhat bitter. The fragrance of garlic and Goldthorn was also a bit overpowering. He didn't know if it was that Azerothian garlic was particularly potent, but at least when eating grilled meat in Theramore, the rich garlic smell hadn't been this annoying. At this point, the wild boar's hard-to-remove gaminess had actually pulled the overall flavor back a bit, giving Jace some sense of familiarity. And probably because he didn't have soy sauce on hand as a salty supplement, he'd also undersalted it, so the flavor was a bit bland. He'd had to add some more salt to the dish and stir it as a remedy. Back and forth like that, this dish tasted like a cross between the Gilded Rose's signature stew and braised pork with potatoes — and the stunted kind at that. After swallowing the meat in his mouth, Jace murmured, "I failed." "How so?" Morophil skewered a piece accurately with her short sword, popped it into her mouth, chewed twice, and said, "I think it's not bad. The texture is good, the flavor is quite… novel." "Novel." Jace gave a sardonic laugh. "Even my demon minion can't bring herself to give a flattering word?" Morophil looked at him askance. "If I said a few nice words, would my master believe me?" "No," Jate said. The succubus smiled and said, "Then there you have it. I'm being completely honest with my master about all my thoughts right now. After all, if I were to lie, with your perception of emotion and shadow, you'd definitely see through it. And then you'd know I'd lied just to flatter you — and you'd definitely be even more heartbroken than you are now… And I don't want my master to be heartbroken." Jace said, "To summarize briefly — this dish is genuinely terrible, right?" Morophil couldn't help but burst out laughing. Jace sighed in dejection. The remaining chunks of meat and potato, gradually growing cold, became even harder to choke down. He picked up a piece of meat, looked at the succubus, and said, "It's not often we get a chance to be together without anyone else around, Morophil — there's not even anyone for several miles outside the tower. How about you tell me about the time you served under Brutallus?" "I told you a long time ago, master. I guarantee every word I said was the truth," Morophil said. "But you also hid a lot of things," Jace said. "Why did Brutallus tear off your wings? Just because he didn't want to see another demon possessing wings he didn't have?" Morophil furrowed her brow. "Is this an order, master?" "I'd prefer you tell me voluntarily. Otherwise I'll keep wondering," Jace said. Morophil stared at Jace for a while, then finally said, "Fine, telling you doesn't matter. I was once Brutallus's lieutenant. In that world he ruled — the world many demons called the Burning Scar — I was called the Reaper, the Daughter of Darkness, by many lesser demons." Hearing these titles, Sazeno couldn't help but burst out laughing. Morophil glanced at it and said, "Keep laughing, you little snot." "In that dragon-blooded lord Reshael's room, your performance didn't match the demons' honorifics at all, 'Daughter of Darkness,'" Sazeno snickered. "You think that stings?" Morophil averted her eyes, her gaze somewhat distant. "You think a Sayaad cares about a little imp's taunts? For hundreds, thousands of years, I'd nearly accepted my own weakness and powerlessness." "Maybe it was you, master, who let me see a glimmer of hope that I could return to my peak — and made my tormenting pride rear its head again. Otherwise… I wouldn't have lost my composure like this." "So you still care, you care immensely," the imp said. Jace shoved a piece of potato into his mouth, then pointed at Sazeno and said, "Say one more word of nonsense and I'll throw you down to the bottom of the tower." "Imps can't die from falling, master, haha!" the imp shouted. "Do it," Jace said. Morophil reached out her tail, hooked the imp, and flung it straight out the window. Accompanied by a faint, miserable cry in the dark of night, everything fell quiet. "Climb back up yourself!" Jace shouted. "No, no! I don't want to climb the stairs…" Several more miserable imp cries came from outside, too faint to make out clearly. Jace picked up another piece of meat and stuffed it into his mouth. "Continue." Morophil licked her sharp-looking teeth, seeming a little hesitant, but finally said: "Then came the Burning Legion. One day, the Deceiver Kil'jaeden appeared at the Burning Scar, before Brutallus's Overlord's Throne. The Deceiver's magic was powerful — even a fel projection was enough to make many high-ranking demons tremble in fear and dare not look directly at him." "I admit it — I was afraid. I was terrified Kil'jaeden would destroy everything. But Brutallus intended to make a stand against the Deceiver and his Legion, right there at the Burning Scar… And that world sat on the edge of the Twisting Nether. Lesser demons killed here would have their souls utterly annihilated; powerful demons would have the greater part of their demonic souls burned away, and even with bodily reformation they'd become trash anyone could push around. Kil'jaeden knew this. He knew that even if the foolish Brutallus dared to defy him, the other demons wouldn't willingly follow his orders against the Burning Legion's army." "But he didn't expect that Brutallus controlled a soul stone that could manipulate the souls of all his subordinates — including mine. No one could disobey his orders." Outside Ilgal's Tower, it was deathly quiet. In this increasingly cold season, you could barely even hear the chirping of insects. And here, far from Stormwind, far from every town — no lights, no human voices — outside the window there was only pure darkness and silence. At the pause in Morophil's story, it even seemed as though time itself had frozen. "I didn't want to die, so I tried to persuade him to submit to the Burning Legion." "What?" Jace asked in surprise. "There's nothing hard to understand about that," Morophil said. "Demons don't want to die either — especially not die in a place like that, master. I'll stress it again — that wasn't Azeroth. And at that time I was… anyway, Brutallus thought I was a cowardly bastard, unworthy of being his lieutenant. I asked him to release my soul and let me leave the Burning Scar, and he refused, so… I challenged him to a duel." "He didn't use the soul stone to force me to obey his orders — he accepted the duel. So… I lost. I lost my wings. But he didn't come out of it unscathed either — he lost his hands, lost them forever. Because at that time I was the Reaper Molserphildos — even the Overlord Brutallus couldn't walk away from me without a scratch." Saying these words, Morophil was practically gnashing her teeth, filled with hatred, while Jace was practically stunned. In the game, Brutallus not only had no demonic wings — he also had no fists. The ends of his arms, like the chieftain of the orcish Shattered Hand clan, had been replaced by two enormous fel-iron blade-fists by his hands. "After my defeat, I was imprisoned," Morophil went on. "Then Kil'jaeden and his Legion crushed the soldiers of the Burning Scar with ease. And I became Kil'jaeden's slave. That's right, master — I didn't manage to escape the Burning Legion. Instead, I became a test subject, a tool, cannon fodder for those Eredar sorcerers. They tormented my soul, made me charge into battle for the Burning Legion like a wild beast, until… Kil'jaeden, afraid that Brutallus would use the countless demonic souls stored within that soul stone to rebel against the Legion's rule, destroyed it. Only then did I finally get a chance to escape — but I lost my body and became a useless demonic soul drifting through the Twisting Nether." "Yes, master — in the past, I didn't understand the Burning Legion, and I paid a terrible price for it." "It was you who cut off Brutallus's hands?" Jace asked. "So you don't care about the torment I suffered in the Burning Legion — you only care about that bastard's outcome." Morophil said, looking disappointed: "Really…" "Because I know that one day you'll have a chance to take revenge, Morophil." Jace lifted Ussaras, wrapped tightly at his side. "After all, we're both thorns in the Burning Legion's side now, aren't we?"
