Chapter 938: Arriving on Schedule

"Is that enough?" "I want more, master… Didn't you want to see what the one who could sever a pit lord's arms looks like? Then you should redouble your efforts. That's only three times, my dear master — although it's already been wonderful." Morophil lay naked on the bed, the chains of her bound armor dangling from her fingers as she turned her head back and looked down beneath her. After this night, the flames in her eyes were burning fiercely, and the fel markings all over her body were glowing bright as well. The succubus swayed her tail, the spikes on it brushing against Jace's legs and waist from time to time. That sensation of being scraped by something sharp didn't just not hurt — that hint of danger actually stirred the nerves all the more. But no matter how much the nerves stirred, he had no strength left. He hadn't eaten well this evening, and then been drained three rounds by Morophil… And now it was already about four in the morning by his pocket watch — dawn hadn't broken yet. He was hungry, exhausted, and his head was spinning. "I wasn't trying to ripen you in one night," Jace said, lying over to the side. Morophil rolled over, extended her long, purple-red demonic tongue, and licked the sweat off his chest from bottom to top. She smiled and pressed close to his cheek. "You can try as much as you like…" "Not trying. I'm done." Jace admitted it flatly, then rolled over to the side. Just as he was starting to feel drowsy, as if his eyes were being pulled down by something, the succubus beside him suddenly sat up. She threw the chains over her body, and an evil, purple-red flame burned all over her. Her demonic eyes locked straight onto the window. "What is it?" Jace was wide awake in an instant. He climbed up, pulled on his trousers, and picked up Azuresong. "Someone's here," Morophil said. "Who?" Jace asked. "Wizard Sanctum mages, or Stonewatch?" He looked out the window. By now the sky had turned a deep blue, and the cooing of unknown birds came from the distance. After a while, the tension in Morophil's expression seemed to ease a little. "A warlock. Uh… I think it might be Demisette Claus." Ah… Demisette Claus. Jace sighed deeply and held his forehead. As he relaxed, his head started hurting again. That's right — it had been about two weeks now. Ever since Grim Batol, Jace felt like it had been a long time since he'd been this relaxed. These few days resting at Ilgal's Tower, he could do whatever he wanted every single day, and time flew by — naturally losing track of it. At the same time, he'd nearly forgotten his earlier promise. Jace glanced out the window. Through the hazy dawn light, he caught sight of an indistinct moving shadow among the trees, mingled with the sound of the wind and rustling leaves — footsteps that weren't very easy to pick out. "Master, do you want me to go down and meet her for you?" Morophil asked. "Don't you go," Jace said. "Oh my." At the stairway entrance, a tiny flame crept up bit by bit. Sazeno leaned against the stair railing and said, "My God, I think I've suffered more hardship than the fleeing Eredar. Ilgal's Tower must exist in some mysterious plane beyond mortal detection, so that I…" "Get down there now," Jace said. "What?" Sazeno coughed with effort and said, "Is that an order from my master?!" Jace lay on his side, propped his head on his hand, and nodded. "Go tell Demisette Claus to wait a bit. Say the master of Ilgal's Tower hasn't woken up yet." "Oh, come on!" The imp turned back toward the stairs. Jace wiggled a finger to remind him: "Imps can't die from falling. You can just jump straight down — it'll be faster." "Ah! You should go to Argus and play with those Eredar — you'd have so much in common!" With that, Sazeno yelled and leaped toward the outside. Accompanied by a long-drawn "Ahhh…" came a faint, barely audible "thud." When he opened his eyes again at noon, bright sunlight was already shining through the dust floating everywhere in the top-floor hall of the tower. Morophil was tidying up the tools and materials left over from last night's disastrous attempt at braised pork with potatoes. Jace thought to himself that he should find another chance to try it again. As for ingredients — scallions, it seemed, simply didn't exist in this world at all. Of course, it was also possible his knowledge just wasn't broad enough. As for soy sauce, Jace was still quite optimistic. Even if he had absolutely no idea how to make soy sauce from scratch, and couldn't find a single soy bean in the Trade District, he knew the pandaren of Pandaria did have soy sauce. He couldn't go to Pandaria himself, but he had indeed heard of pandaren wandering the outside world. In a place as rough-and-tumble as Booty Bay, there might really be someone who could help him find a pandaren. If he was lucky, the pandaren — who took food very seriously even while wandering outside — would probably still remember how to make soy sauce. Before yesterday, to be honest, Jace hadn't given much thought to trying the things he'd known in his past life. But after experiencing a chance failure, a stubborn notion had actually sprouted in his heart — he had to eat a proper meal, at least make it look the part. Even if he couldn't replicate it perfectly, it at least had to satisfy him. As he was thinking all this, Morophil had already noticed he was awake. She glanced over this way. If not for that long tail swishing back and forth behind her, plus the distinctly goat-like hooves when she walked, just the way she was squatting there tidying things up really did look like a hard-working maid. It was genuinely hard to imagine that this demon had once been a warrior who could face down a terrifying demon like Brutallus head-on. Actually, Jace had been able to sense some things a long time ago. Before accepting the fel markings, before receiving the life-force supplements — especially the blood of the red dragon — her power wasn't significantly greater than other succubi, but in technique and other non-raw-strength aspects, she far exceeded them. Especially when dealing with that felguard in the Black Morass, the feeling she gave Jace was as though she'd already dealt with thousands upon thousands of felguards with that kind of ease. Combined with the slightly contemptuous yet wary tone she used when mentioning the Nathrezim… among the various races of the Burning Legion, probably only a race like the pit lords — born with physiques that could crush dreadlords — wouldn't fear those scheming bat demons. Even among pit lords, Brutallus was one of the most powerful in terms of pure combat strength. He might not compare to Mannoroth, the King of Pit Lords, in magic, but even Kil'jaeden appreciated his pure violence and madness. Losing in an open duel to a madman like that could honestly not be considered a blemish at all — one might even say that surviving it could be counted as a kind of glory. But this Sayaad didn't treat it as something to brag about. Instead, she'd always been too ashamed to bring it up. Perhaps it was because she felt ashamed of her own past desire to defect to the Burning Legion. For a "wild demon" with enough pride, that wasn't hard to understand. Jace glanced at the mirrorstone staff placed at the bedside, looking at the shadow of the demon within, blurred by the distance. He couldn't deny that he was curious about what Morophil — no, the female demon whose true name was Molserphildos — had looked like when she dueled Brutallus.
