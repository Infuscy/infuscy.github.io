## Chapter 939: A Dangerous Question

After getting dressed and coming downstairs, the moment Jace appeared, Demisette Kraus immediately stood up and looked toward the stairs. "Good morning, Jace Sesso. I was starting to think you weren't coming down because you didn't want to see me."

"Well, here I am," Jace said.

"I know you don't want to see me," Demisette said. "Just let me learn that meteor spell quickly and I'll never bother you again."

"Think what you like," Jace said with a wave, wincing at the creaking of the nearly broken stair boards under his feet.

Catching Demisette's worried gaze fixed on his feet, he said, "Don't worry about it. Even the walls of the Lordaeron royal city, after being attacked back and forth by orcs and gnolls for who knows how many times, wouldn't be in perfect condition either. And I've already set about renovating this tower."

"Ilgal's Tower is yours?" Demisette asked.

"It is now," Jace said with a smile. "So we can practice magic here freely without being disturbed by anyone else."

"I envy you," Demisette said.

"Envy what? This tower?" Jace asked.

"No." The female warlock looked up at the two felhounds peering down from above. "Being able to openly wield shadow magic without hiding it, being able to summon demons — from the king to that 'department that doesn't exist' to the soldiers at Stonewatch, nobody cares about it. Of course, I also know what you paid to earn these privileges. I only envy your current situation, not your experience. I know if I'd been the one going through those things, dying ten times wouldn't have been enough."

"Pretty words won't speed up your incantation learning," Jace said.

Demisette smiled and pulled a paper bag from her pack. "This isn't just flattery. Also, a small gift."

"What is it?" Jace took it and sniffed. A faint greasy aroma drifted out.

He opened it to find a portion of rib racks done in unmistakable Lakeshore style! The gift from a warlock really was anything but subtle — straightforward and crude.

Still, this gift certainly couldn't be called expensive, but it hit the painful spot a little too perfectly. Jace hadn't eaten a proper meal since noon yesterday, and after last night's battle he was running on empty. That pinch of Silverleaf crushed in among the spices for extra flavor suddenly became the key seasoning that made his mouth water.

Jace couldn't help himself — he grabbed a piece right out of the bag and sucked the meat off the bone. Savory with just a hint of honey sweetness, with no detectable sugary taste, the slick grease making him involuntarily lick his fingers.

The dizzy, hypoglycemic feeling was swept away in an instant, and even his breathing felt stronger. "Delicious. Thank you."

"I've been practicing that black meteor incantation again these past few days, Master Sesso," Demisette said. "I still haven't gotten it completely right, but I think I'm close this time. I'd like you to take a look at the results of my attempt."

"Good." Jace put the rack of ribs aside and handed them to Morofell beside him, then pointed toward the clearing that had once been a gnoll camp. "Let's go over there."

This clearing had no trees to speak of — what few there were had been chopped down by gnolls — so it wouldn't easily start a forest fire. And this place was far from the Stonewatch River that flowed into Lake Everstill, so a single Shadow Meteor wouldn't pollute the water source the Stonewatch soldiers relied on or corrupt the food supply of Lakeshore's people.

Demisette Kraus held a slender iron rod with a shadow-stone head and gazed at the lower ground ahead. It was noon now, the sun right overhead. The whole forest was dappled with shifting shadows trembling in the wind, bright and clear.

But when this female warlock settled down and began chanting, Jace clearly felt everything around her grow overcast. She really had practiced — no question about it.

When she brandished her rod and guided the shadow energy to gather, step by step matching Jace's casting motions, Jace distinctly felt shadow power coiling above her head. Though the naked eye still couldn't make out the dark energy drifting in the air, the feeling was unmistakable.

But the whole process was dragged out by her intensely focused yet agonizingly slow incantation. And at the end, when Demisette finished chanting — by rights that meteor should have taken form and streaked toward the target — the shadow power ahead still wasn't visible to the naked eye. Demisette was only relying on the willpower she'd trained through her long dealings with shadow to forcibly restrain that last wisp of thin shadow from dissipating.

In the end, she still sighed and sat down on the ground, and that overcast aura scattered completely. The soul shards on the ground hadn't even been stirred — they still gave off a faint, ghostly glow. "Damn it. This time I did even worse than last time. Maybe I was too nervous."

"You should know that casting motions can't simply be copied one-to-one," Jace said. "The key is that you use the hand gestures in tandem with the rod's movements to guide the shadow mana into shape."

"But the mana consumption is just too severe." Demisette lowered her head, a little dejected. "I've tried many times — not following your casting motions exactly, but guiding it on my own… but my mana can't hold out for long."

Jace said, "But I can feel that you still have a lot of mana you haven't called up, Demisette."

The female warlock looked at Jace for a moment with some puzzlement. She mulled it over before saying, "I can't just drain all my mana in one go, Sesso. That would be way too dangerous."

Jace said, "The first time I tried this incantation, I nearly knocked myself unconscious too. But in the end, maybe I grasped the trick, maybe I was just lucky — I barely managed to pull it off once. After that, casting became much more familiar, and the mana consumption became more tolerable too, so I wouldn't hit my limit every single time."

"If I could really pull it off successfully once… maybe the emotion still isn't right." Demisette thought for a moment. "I can't find the corresponding emotion, so the casting efficiency is extremely low. I'm not you, Sesso. I don't know what you're actually thinking when you cast…"

"…Could you demonstrate one more time? A complete one."

"I'm low on mana right now, Demisette," Jace said.

Demisette glanced at Morofell watching from not far away, then immediately pulled two vials of Superior Mana Potion from the pouch at her belt.

Superior Mana Potion wasn't some ordinary potion you could buy for a few silver. Demisette wasn't some rich girl playing at magic either — this time she'd really dug deep.

Jace nodded helplessly, drank one vial, and tucked the other into his belt pouch. He walked to the edge of the cliff.

He raised his head and began chanting softly, one hand gripping two soul shards, the other raised high, guiding the shadow that answered his call to gather.

A cold, dark wind swept through the forest, and everything around them dimmed at once. Demisette Kraus kept glancing upward, then forcing herself not to look at the shadow gathering above, instead focusing her attention on Jace, wanting to feel the emotion he was channeling through the casting.

The soul shards darkened one by one, turning into pale flakes. The wailing of the shadow serpents in the sky grew ever sharper as they roared and coalesced into a single meteor. The instant Jace flicked his fingers, it howled and streaked into the distance. The moment it struck the ground, the swelling purple flames, accompanied by the piercing shriek of shadow, devoured the entire lowland in one gulp. Demisette couldn't help but grunt —

Blown back several steps by the blast wave, she shielded her face with her arm in a sorry state.

Jace brushed aside the scraps of hair the wind had blown across his face and quickly dispersed the burning shadow flames with an incantation in the elemental tongue. Even so, a web of glowing fissures was still left on the ground, like a wound torn open by some unfathomably enormous dark beast.

Demisette drank a vial of mana potion and said, "I think I felt something. I want to try again."

"Go ahead." Jace dusted the dirt off his clothes and stepped aside.

The female warlock straightened up again and began chanting the incantation. This time Jace could feel that she was truly feeling out the shadow energy, the way he'd told her to, rather than mechanically copying it.

At the same time, her emotions had shifted too — calmer, yet more dangerous. Was that hatred?

Jace frowned. He had the impression that Demisette really had read some powerful emotion off him, and had found one of the two key emotions of Hand of Gul'dan…

Demisette didn't have the spare attention to notice Jace's expression. She guided the magic seriously, and this time shadow really did appear swirling in the sky.

The shadows drew together one by one, coalescing into terrifying serpentine shapes. The soul shards before Demisette's eyes darkened in turn.

Jace couldn't help but step forward — after all, this was the first time he'd seen a second person channel Hand of Gul'dan. A strange feeling spread through his heart, as if he both wanted to see it and didn't want to see it.

As a teacher, he naturally had the desire to teach her, but at the same time he felt a vague sense of worry.

Yet just as he was feeling slightly conflicted, the black serpents in the sky disintegrated at once. Cold winds were blowing now, and the air felt damp — it looked like rain was coming.

Demisette sat down on the ground, unable to help but laugh happily twice. She looked toward Jace and said, "Am I close to succeeding, Jace Sesso?"

"You've got talent," Jace nodded.

"I know I'm borrowing your strength," Demisette said. "If you hadn't gathered the shadow energy here first, I wouldn't have gotten this far either this time. But… it really is remarkable. And through learning this magic I've come to understand the gap between us, Sesso — especially that you can even cast this magic successfully without a rod. I simply can't imagine how you manage it. I daresay even orc warlocks who've drunk demon blood couldn't do this."

"When I first tried it, I used more than one rod, and even called upon a magic sword, before I barely succeeded once," Jace said. "Once you're familiar with this incantation, you'll definitely be able to do it too — though the power might be somewhat reduced."

"I hope so," Demisette said. "Oh, right — could I take a look at your rod? I'd like to know what kind of treasure the legendary dark mage Jace Sesso uses."

"My previous rod was burned up by the black dragon at Grim Batol," Jace replied. "I haven't found a proper replacement yet. What I'm using now is just an ordinary rod I got at Hafgan's rod shop in Stormwind. You know Hafgan, right?"

"Of course I know Mr. Hafgan," Demisette said, still carrying the satisfied look of someone who'd just cast a spell successfully.

"I mean the one you had on your back before — it looked like it had a fairly thick head, and the handle was a bit curved."

"Ah, that wasn't a rod." Jace smiled. "That was the upper torso bones of a Sayaad demon, not badly damaged, quite valuable. I'd been planning to sell it in Moonbrook, who knew that even the underground merchants who dealt in demon leather goods had all fled."

"So that's what it was." Demisette couldn't hide the disappointment on her face.

Jace glanced at the mage tower not far away. "Take a rest. We'll try again tonight, Demisette. Drinking mana potions nonstop and burning through mana will take a toll on your body too. You don't want to catch a case of mana addiction, do you?"

"All right." Demisette Kraus nodded.

Jace looked at her, careful not to reveal his wariness.

When Demisette had asked about the rod, he'd almost thought this warlock had come to Ilgal's Tower specifically to gather intelligence, and that learning magic was just a cover. It was only after watching carefully for a while that he'd relaxed a little.

Demisette really didn't suspect anything about his rod — she was just curious. She still trusted him quite a bit, so she hadn't bothered to hide her emotions here. On top of that, her severe mana exhaustion left her with no defenses up at all — that much was easy to sense.

It's just that this female warlock didn't know she'd brushed up against the edge of something extremely dangerous. If she ever learned Ussaras's true identity, she wouldn't be leaving Ilgal's Tower alive.
