Chapter 940: The Overdue Guest

Demisette Kraus was a remarkably talented warlock, but Jace still couldn't trust her the way he trusted the people in the Dagger Society.

The chaos that had been stirring in the Stormwind kingdom lately had only made him more aware of just how hard warlocks as a group were to control — after all, even among the Dagger Society crowd, there had been the incident where Galders nearly got seduced away by a Twilight's Hammer agent.

So although he'd already told Demisette a great many details about casting Hand of Gul'dan, he still couldn't teach her with everything he had and pour out everything he knew.

Then again, Demisette was clearly more connected than the warlocks he'd dealt with in other places. Understanding a source of incantations and dark knowledge different from Galders' wasn't bad — especially one close to demonology.

If it weren't for Demisette, he might never have learned the Demonic Enhancement incantation.

The next morning, Jace had just woken up and stuck his head out to glance below the window when he saw Demisette already in the clearing in front of the tower. A fishy smell of shadow hung in the air, and several empty mana potions lay on the ground — she'd probably tried again who knows how many times.

And she clearly had no intention of stopping. Right now she was fiddling with another vial of pale purple potion — probably either a Shadow Protection Potion or a Shadow Power Potion to strengthen shadow magic.

Jace threw on a cloak and came down from the tower. He looked at Morofell, who was watching her from the side. "Morning."

"Morning, master."

Hearing the voice from behind, Demisette turned her head. "I'm sorry, Jace Sesso. I practiced magic at the foot of your tower without your permission. I thought of a few details that might be problematic last night, so the moment I opened my eyes I couldn't wait to try them out. It didn't go too smoothly, but at least now I can complete the first stage proficiently…"

"This tower hasn't started renovation yet." Jace pointed above his head. "I don't want you trying this powerful dark magic here frequently, Demisette. If you accidentally hit the tower…"

"I'll be careful," Demisette said.

"I mean, the Stonemasons will be starting work in a few days. I've already arranged it with their man in charge, Demisette." Jace cleared his throat.

"I understand." Demisette nodded. "I've learned enough this time. I'll leave this afternoon and go back to practice what I've learned, teacher. I know you're treating this incantation as a bargaining chip rather than actually wanting me to learn it, but I'd still like to call you teacher."

"I'm not ready to take on the title of teacher yet," Jace said. "There's one more thing I want to ask you."

"What?"

"Do you know someone named Zubel?"

"Of course — Zubel, I know this person."

"Is he still alive?"

"Alive," Demisette said. "In the Stormwind Stockades. Those warlocks were all locked up in the Stockades, separated from ordinary prisoners."

Jace looked at her. "How do you know that so clearly?"

Demisette smiled with a hint of smugness. "Because they had the good fortune of not being at the lighthouse at the time, and someone intended to use my skills to break the people inside out of the Stockades."

"Your skills?" Jace asked.

"You forgot about my succubus," Demisette said. "She can turn into anyone's appearance — including the guards, of course. But the Stormwind Stockades are different. The Wizard Sanctum must have carefully warded the place, waiting for someone like me to take the bait. And I'm guessing SI:7 has already been cooperating with the warlocks inside Stormwind again… your hand is in that too, isn't it? I didn't want my succubus going in there only to have to deal with the felhounds summoned by Jace Sesso. Anyway, I didn't agree. What did he do to you — does it have something to do with the lighthouse?"

"He knows how to summon his own minions, and he can do it casting alone," Jace said.

"After destroying the entire lighthouse, you're still planning to learn incantations from him?" Demisette shook her head. "You sent them all to prison — how could you possibly expect them to be willing to teach you anything? In the current situation, even I don't dare casually make contact with warlocks I'm not familiar with."

"Do you remember Clowen — the one who helped you get into the lighthouse? He was thrown into the Stockades too, and outside a lot of people think he's the suspect who helped you destroy the lighthouse. Quite a few warlocks hold a grudge against him. I could only try to pretend I'd been brought here under your shadow magic mind control, but that didn't fool many people either. Once they find out I'm still on the outside, I'll be in trouble too."

Jace said, "Sounds like even knowing that the 'guide' was a servant of the black dragon, and that becoming his subordinate meant being mind-controlled, there were still no shortage of warlocks willing to follow him."

Demisette said, "You think I'm someone who'd do anything for power, huh? Let me tell you — there are people far more reckless than me. Don't talk to me about being mind-controlled just to learn an incantation. Even dying would be nothing — plenty of warlocks believe that after death they'll go to the Twisting Nether!"

"That doesn't surprise me," Jace said. "Then do you know where Zubel might have learned his incantation for summoning specific demons?"

"Before coming to the lighthouse, he'd been hanging around with dwarves all along," Demisette said. "The Dark Iron dwarves have been dealing with demon minions for far longer than the Dark Portal was even opened. I'm guessing he probably learned it from them too."

Most of the incantations leaked by the Dark Iron dwarves had problems, and Galders had already eaten quite a bit of bitterness over that. If Zubel really had the ability to get his hands on genuinely important demon-related incantations from the Dark Iron dwarves, then however he'd managed it, it counted for something.

Seeing Jace deep in thought, the female warlock added, "There are too many warlocks in the Stormwind kingdom — even in the whole Alliance. All sorts of people. You ought to be careful too."

"Thanks for the reminder," Jace said.

After Demisette left, Jace felt as if a weight had been lifted — finally he could relax a little on his own. Though thinking that she might come back again gave him a bit of a headache.

The start of construction VanCleef had promised was drawing nearer, and the getting colder by the day — winter's footsteps didn't seem far off.

That morning a gray, gloomy rain began to fall. Jace opened his eyes, got out of bed, and felt sticky and uncomfortable all over. Hearing the rain, he looked out the window and worried the tower might be leaking somewhere.

He and Morofell carefully inspected it from top to bottom and confirmed that the mage tower at least had no problems on the outside before he set his mind at ease. Running up and down had tired him out somewhat. Jace sat on the steps at the entrance to the mage tower, staring blankly at the rain curtain outside.

He hadn't sat there long when he actually heard the not-too-clear sound of voices coming from the forest in the direction of Stonewatch.

Even though it was raining now, with the sound of rain and wind drowning everything out, the sound of people talking in the forest was far too conspicuous — Jace picked it up from far away almost immediately.

He went into the tower and told the demons to hide, then got his clothes and dressed properly. Before he'd even come downstairs, shouting came from outside. "Is Archmage Jace Sesso in the tower?"

An unknown young man was shouting.

Jace lifted his Mirror Stone Staff and came to the entrance, looking out. Four people in rain cloaks stood at the gate. Two of the men looked like Stonewatch soldiers — wearing the heavy outer garments commonly worn by the garrison there, with Stormwind short swords at their waists.

The two women clearly weren't from Stonewatch. Both were in their twenties, and the slightly younger one seemed to be the other's maid.

And the mistress, whether in bearing or in stature, was clearly out of the ordinary — it was just that she was wearing a hood that cast her features in shadow, making her hard to make out.

At that point one of the soldiers said, "This is Lady Katrana Prestor, come from Alterac. We've been assigned by the captain to escort her and her maid to Ilgal's Tower."

Katrana Prestor?!
