Chapter 942: Amidst Emotion

Ever since learning of the disaster at Moonbrook and suspecting black dragons might be behind it, Jace had been watching carefully every time he entered Stormwind — and especially the Keep — on the lookout for dragonkin disguised in soldiers' clothing, and above all whether Katrana Prestor had shown up.

After all, in another timeline, after Deathwing escaped Grim Batol, Onyxia had slipped into Stormwind's court disguised as this human noblewoman. No one had questioned her motives. No one had doubted her identity.

Because not only did she possess masterful magical disguises, she had also used the mental domination at which black dragons excelled to manipulate nearly every political faction in Stormwind — the king, the council, the nobles, even some high-ranking members of the Stonemasons — making them all believe every word Katrana Prestor said, believe she had everyone's best interests at heart.

Jace had once even suspected Onyxia might be disguised as someone else. If he'd had anything resembling a normal chance to meet her, he would've held up the mirror stone to Lady Pasonia Shaw just to check.

Either way, he'd never expected Katrana Prestor to show up unannounced at Illgynoth Tower — riding right up into his face, no less.

Though nothing on the surface gave him cause for alarm, he couldn't just let his guard down either…

"Tsk." The one beside him, Molophil, made a sound of warning, while glancing at the river ahead. "Oh…"

Jace snatched up his handmade fishing rod and pulled the hook from the water. The bait was gone. "Damn."

"I thought we were here to fish, Master. Right now it's looking more like we're here to feed the fish." Molophil rested her chin on her hand as she said it.

She was draped in Jace's black dragonhide cloak at the moment, its deep hood covering her horns and face, her complexion hidden, tail tucked away — from this angle she looked like an ordinary humanoid woman sitting on the riverbank of indeterminate race.

The fact that he could summon demons was common knowledge at Stonewatch, and most people had likely guessed that the master of Illgynoth Tower, having slain a succubus, would find nothing strange about summoning one himself.

Still, he wanted to show the neighbors at Stonewatch a basic modicum of respect.

Jace fished a wriggling nightworm out of the iron can beside him, threaded it onto the hook, and nudged the wooden bobber. "Fatten the fish up a bit, and the ones you catch later will be bigger too. Don't you know the meaning of sustainable development?"

Molophil put on an enlightened expression. "Ah, just like the Burning Legion treats Azeroth. Sargeras must've pulled back voluntarily ten thousand years ago so he could harvest even greater magical power from Azeroth in the future — even at the cost of getting half-cracked by the portal opened by the Well of Eternity."

"Who knows. After all, Sargeras is tall, so he sees further." Jace murmured it as he set the rod against the wooden stand he'd rigged up and lay down beside it.

He glanced over at Molophil's rod. "How's your end doing?"

"I'm guessing the Stonewatch River's fish don't care for demons either," Molophil said with a grievance.

"So you're no better off. Looks like spicy sunfish soup is off the table for tonight." Jace sighed as he finished. "I went to the trouble of getting the recipe straight from the Stonewatch cook. If Vereesa were here, a couple of spells and all these fish would come running on their own."

"I could do it too," Molophil said immediately.

"You could?" Jace asked.

The succubus extended her pale-red arm and, with a finger tipped with a sharp claw, lightly touched the surface of the river. Jace clearly felt a pulse of Shadow sink downward.

Before long, a dark-blue wide-mouth catfish churned up the silt and bolted to the surface, but its movements were profoundly unnatural — every twitch looked like it was trying to wrench its own bones apart.

Molophil watched this little creature poke its head above the water, eager to get closer to the demon, its lips slowly curling upward — the very picture of a blood-red predatory cat that had spotted a pitiful prey.

Soon the catfish's skin was riddled with Shadow-corrosion ulcers, and its movements began to convulse. Jace could even smell the scent of Shadow now, mixed with the stench of river mud and raw fish — even more unpleasant.

"Don't leave it in the river," Jace reminded her with a frown.

Molophil's tail slipped out from under the cloak and into the water, hooking the catfish and pulling it out. She grabbed it and bit the head clean off. "What a vicious spell," Jace said.

"A simple Domination," Molophil said. "It's just that this little fish had no magical resistance, and even this level of Shadow energy is enough to kill it. Of course, it's also possible that I… used a bit more power than necessary, to make it carry out my orders faster."

Jace looked at the half-tail still twitching in her hand, twitched an eyebrow, and asked, "When you cast Domination, can you hide all trace of Shadow completely? The moment I make a move, I can't hide my Shadow power no matter what — even being careful doesn't help…

"Not even when I'm not casting — just making big movements, like swinging a weapon or dodging someone's attack — I'm liable to leak the scent of Shadow."

"That's normal," Molophil said. "Hiding it one hundred percent is impossible for me, but concealing it as much as possible isn't a problem. The trade-off is that the spell's power takes a hit."

At that, she looked at Jace and asked, "You're still thinking about that noblewoman who came at noon, Master. Who do you really think she is?"

Jace murmured, staring at the fishing bobber swaying with the ripples, "I think she's the daughter of Daval Prestor."

Molophil looked at him silently for a moment before saying, "You mean the 'Prestor' we met at Grim Batol."

Jace nodded.

"Why do you think that?" she asked.

"Do you trust me, Molophil?" Jace countered.

"Of course," Molophil said. "I trust you completely, Master. But that doesn't mean you can't be wrong."

"True enough." Jace licked his slightly dry lips and said, "You couldn't see through her disguise, and I couldn't either — even the mirror stone's reflection showed nothing amiss. And she didn't betray even the slightest trace connected to Shadow.

"Recalling today's meeting at noon, Katrana Prestor's expression and movements — the more he thought about it, the more he felt something was off somewhere.

He tried to set aside the bias born of everything he knew about the future from his past life, tried to strip away his disgust for Onyxia and all things black dragon, tried his hardest to examine this meeting objectively — and from start to finish, this noblewoman who looked no older than twenty had done nothing wrong.

But where was the problem?

"If there really is something off," the succubus narrowed her eyes, "is she a priest?"

"I don't think so," Jace said after a moment. "I've never heard of any priests in House Prestor. Maybe there are — I hadn't paid much attention to that sort of thing before. What do you mean by that?"

"In the words of your humans," Molophil said, "don't you find this Lady Katrana Prestor a little too clean?"

"Go on." Jace sat up and leaned back.

"Emotions — especially extreme emotions — give birth to Shadow, my Master. Humans, orcs, elves, us succubi, and all the other demons — it's the same for all. Dragons too — think of Somnilith. Her fear of the unknown made her vulnerable to nightmare corruption. No exceptions.

"And our Lady Katrana Prestor first endured the pain of losing her father and then, as she said, suffered a year of discrimination and distrust from the humans of the North. In a single year she went from a rising star of high standing in the Lordaeron court to a reviled outcast whom everyone shunned. For a sheltered noblewoman who'd weathered few hardships, that kind of fall…"

The succubus paused a beat, then continued, "Perhaps she truly possesses an exceptionally firm mind and will. Perhaps she could easily transmute those negatives into strength — to find her father, to fight her way back to her former standing — but that too would give birth to another kind of extreme. Hatred toward the nobles who abandoned House Prestor, for instance… Think of Azuregos, Master. His self-righteousness made him all the more vulnerable to corruption by the very things he guarded.

"So — did you sense the Shadow her emotions carried, Master?"

Jace recalled Katrana's words and said slowly, "Weakness, exhaustion… but that's what I heard in her voice, what I felt from her tone and expression. Her maid, though — the Shadow from the maid's emotions was far more obvious. The barely concealed fear and timidity practically hit me in the face all at once. Yes… that's exactly the problem."

"Yes, yes." Molophil was almost excited now. She said at once, "I wasn't downstairs, so I didn't feel what the maid gave off. But if it's really as you say, Master, that only proves the maid is a genuine mortal, and has no need to hide it. And Katrana Prestor — whether she's a black dragon in disguise or not — must be using some special method to mask all of her Shadow."

Jace thought it over. "At noon today I was so focused on whether I could see through her disguise that I didn't pay enough attention to the Shadow her emotions carried. But I need to verify it further. She said she's staying in the Park in Stormwind. Once I've finished handing off the tower work to VanCleef, I'll go back and see her again — observe her more closely."

"So you plan to accept her offer, Master?" Molophil asked.

Jace let out a long sigh. "A letter of recommendation from King Wrynn, plus the chance to rescue a noble of Alteras, a guest of honor of King Terenas II — for a court mage like me, a 'Lordaeron knight,' it's hard to refuse. And when a noblewoman famous for her beauty across the seven kingdoms comes calling in person, carrying four or five hundred gold and the promise of more on top of it — refusing would be the strange thing to do. At the very least I need to hear what leads she has and what her plans are before I decide."

"So how do you plan to explain all this to Vereesa Windrunner?" Molophil asked with a smile.

Jace shot her a look. "By telling her the truth, naturally — except for the part where we suspect black dragons. After all, I don't know whether this letter will be intercepted by disguised dragonkin along the way. If she really is a black dragon princess, she'll definitely try to control our communications. I don't trust the security of messages between the Wizard Sanctum and Quel'Thalas."

"Hmm." Molophil slid her eyes toward the river. "What?"

Jace blinked, then looked at the water and saw the bobber being dragged under. He grabbed the rod. "Damn…"

The dangling hook came up empty again — the nightworm gone.

"At this rate, Master, your reputation at Lake Everstill is going to surpass even Lordaeron's," Molophil said, grinning.

Jace glared at her. "Quit the chatter. Go catch me a hare in the forest, or we'll go hungry again tonight." The succubus stood up wearing a look of pure disgust, then faded into the grass.

Two days later at noon, Jace was chopping wood at the base of the tower. By now he'd chopped himself into a bad mood and was sitting in a chair nearby taking a rest when he spotted VanCleef and a younger companion walking out of the forest.

"Good afternoon, Master Sesso." VanCleef came over, then looked at the firewood. "Do archmages light their own fires and cook their own meals too? I always assumed you conjured food with magic."

Jace stared at him for a moment. Though VanCleef's question seemed like casual small talk without any sarcasm, Jace still wanted very much to ask if the man was deliberately looking for trouble.

"I'm not from that school, Mr. VanCleef. I specialized in the School of Abjuration at the Wizard Sanctum — I have absolutely nothing to do with conjuring food or summoning water. Besides, chopping wood builds the body and keeps you healthy. After all, incanting spells takes a fair amount of physical stamina too."

With that, he looked at the two of them. They seemed completely unprepared for the level of detail in Jace's explanation.

"I suppose we should talk business," Jace said.

VanCleef nodded. "Then, regarding the renovation of Illgynoth Tower per your request, Master Sesso — the final cost comes to two hundred and forty gold. So per our earlier agreement, you only need to pay eighty gold up front and we can begin."

He glanced at the young man beside him, who pulled a stack of papers from a bag and handed them to Jace. "Here are the specifics. Take a look, and if everything's in order, you can sign."

Jace reviewed the line items, along with a rough design sketch clipped inside — all more or less matching what he'd described. The material prices were also in line with what VanCleef had discussed with him earlier, so he flipped to the last page, ready to sign.

"How long will it take?" he asked, looking up.

"It'll be done by next summer, fall at the latest," VanCleef said.
