Chapter 943: The Alteraci Princess

Once the first crew of Stonemasons arrived at Illgynoth Tower, Jace ordered two Dazarhms to patrol the surrounding forest, keeping the gnolls from using the tower's renovation as an opportunity to cause trouble. He also notified Stonewatch.

Next he needed to inform the king. After all, even though Illgynoth Tower was now his private property, it was still part of the Redridge defensive network. When a link on that side began repairs, it was only natural to let the king know.

It took about three days to get all of that arranged, then he set out from Stonwind and rode a griffin back to Stormwind, planning to report to the king first.

Of course, he also wanted to see exactly how Varian felt about Katrana Prestor.

The Stonewatch griffin rider was clearly a novice — he couldn't even keep the direction straight. They'd set out from Stonewatch in the morning and didn't reach Stormwind until the afternoon, starving Jace half to death.

The moment he arrived in Stormwind he headed straight for the Gilded Rose — partly because the food at the mage tower had been pretty miserable lately and he wanted to restore himself a bit, fast.

He ordered a fair spread at the Gilded Rose, including the pork-liver pie he always treated himself to. The pork-liver pie here fell a little short of Moonbrook's, but at least it was rich with grease, and a few bites in he felt thoroughly satisfied.

He was just about to order a beer to cut the richness when the conversation of a group that had just walked in through the door caught his attention.

"…the Alteraci princess."

"Really beautiful, and…"

"First time I heard about her father, I thought she…"

"Right, I thought this so-called princess might have a monster's head."

""If King Varian marries that princess, it'll definitely have a major impact on the Alliance's future…"

"Who cares about major impact? If Varian's not going to marry her, I will, heh heh."

"What the hell are you all blabbering about? What about Queen Tiffin?"

"You think a young fellow like Varian wouldn't lose his head seeing a princess like that?"

"Your pale ale, server."

The server set the mug down. Jace thanked him, took a small sip, and leaned back in his chair to keep listening. So news of the Alteraci "princess" arriving in Stormwind had already spread inside the walls.

Even though Katrana had already visited Stonewatch, most of the soldiers there had no idea, even days later, what this beautiful visitor was there for. Yet here in Stormwind, even a random tavern full of customers knew the whole story clear as day.

What worried Jace even more was this: the trouble Deathwing had brought down on her by impersonating her father had been swept clean the moment she showed her face in Stormwind. The once-rumored dreadful witch had transformed, in the blink of an eye, into a beautiful foreign princess wronged and driven from her homeland.

It wasn't strictly a case of "judging by looks" either, since the atrocities committed by the black dragons had nothing to do with the real House Prestor. But if Katrana really was a black dragon in disguise, this outcome suited her interests perfectly.

While Jace was still worrying about a disguised black dragon descending on Stormwind and using dark magic to influence the court and everyone in it, Katrana Prestor had, with nothing more than a brief appearance, maxed out the entire city's favor.

The next morning, Jace dressed himself properly, strapped on the mirror-stone staff, fastened Angelysta's sash, and headed for Stormwind Keep.

Now he eyed every one of the palace guards as though they might be dragonkin. He swept his staff across them one by one — and not a single one showed the slightest trace of dark magic or black dragon.

But that was only to be expected. If Onyxia had gone to such lengths to craft her disguise, how could she possibly stuff a bunch of crudely disguised dragonkin into Varian Wrynn's tightly guarded Keep afterward?

Jace even suspected the black dragons might already know he had a way of seeing through disguises. Could they already know it was because of this staff?

After all, this mirror stone had been fashioned by the Highborne for the sole purpose of admiring themselves. Expecting it to deal with ancient dragons that might have existed since the days of the Kaldorei Empire was really asking too much of it.

Lost in these thoughts, he walked up the ramp to the throne room without noticing the oncoming figure. "Master Sesso."

Mathias Shaw — the future head of SI:7 — saw Jace and smiled, nodding as he stopped. "Mr. Shaw." Jace nodded back.

"Thank you for everything you did at Moonbrook. You really helped SI:7 ease a crisis of trust," Mathias Shaw said.

"No need to thank me. After all, I'm the one who brought that crisis down on you," Jace said.

Mathias nodded and fell silent for a moment. Jace said, "I still have matters to report to His Majesty. I hope we'll have a chance to talk again sometime."

"Master Sesso." Mathias turned back. "Have you met Katrana Prestor of Alterac?"

"I have," Jace said.

Mathias said nothing. Jace couldn't sense any curiosity or goodwill toward this strange princess from his face — only a faint trace of worry.

Jace could feel it. In that same instant, Mathias was observing him too.

After a moment, Mathias nodded and said, "There's still a great deal I'd like to ask you about the 'Lighthouse,' Master Sesso. I look forward to meeting again."

Jace didn't think Mathias had any real interest in the Lighthouse. It had been over half a month already; as the kingdom's spymaster, he probably knew more about it than Jace did. Clearly, what he really wanted to discuss was what this Alteraci princess had come to Stormwind to do.

There were quite a few people in the hall at the moment. King Varian was sitting on the throne, arguing with someone dressed rather plainly below the dais.

From the exchange, this man was a farmer from near Moonbrook. Because of the demon incident in the Moonbrook mines a few months back, he'd sold his property for a song and planned to move to Elwynn. After the Moonbrook matter was resolved, he was no longer willing to honor the sale, and so a dispute had arisen with the buyer.

Although this fellow seemed thoroughly unreasonable, the buyer appeared to have snapped up a small farm for a mere two gold — a price that looked more like robbery.

None of these people seemed to have had any schooling. Their Common was thick with accent and halting in expression. Jace felt a headache just listening, let alone the king, who had to be worn down by these matters nonstop.

During the Orc Wars, the family that had ruled Moonbrook had been slaughtered to the last by the orcs, and to this day no suitable heir could be found, so most of the affairs there had fallen on Stormwind.

Varian noticed Jace in the crowd and gave him a look, signaling him to wait in the garden. Jace was only too happy to leave the throne room.

Out in the garden, the air quieted at once. Only the faint sound of arguing drifted in from the corridor outside.

After waiting a while, Varian walked over briskly and sat down on the stone steps beside him, breathing deeply. It was quite a while before he said, "Bolvar Fordragon is better suited to this sort of thing."

Jace cleared his throat. Anything he said right now seemed inappropriate.

"What is it, Jace?" Varian gestured at the stone steps beside him. "Sit."

"I've come to report on the renovation work at Illgynoth Tower, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"I know about that. Stonewatch already told me." Varian nodded. "Illgynoth Tower is yours. As long as you don't tear it down and it still serves its original function, do as you please, Jace. Oh — you've already met Lady Katrana Prestor, haven't you?"

"I have," Jace said. "I saw Your Majesty's letter too."

Varian said, "House Prestor is a cousin line of the Alteraci royal house. And you know those Alteraci — they may live in the snowy mountains, but every last one of them is filthy rich. She must have offered you a hard-to-refuse deal?"

"Several hundred gold." Jace exhaled. "I have to say, Northerners spend big."

"You say that as if you're already a Southerner." Varian laughed, stood up, and picked up a practice sword from a nearby rack, tossing it to Jace. "I heard your swordsmanship was learned from the dwarves at Aerie Peak. You know, Prince Arthas Menethil of Lordaeron also learned swordsmanship from dwarves. I'm sure King Terenas II's choice for his only son can't be wrong…

"So — do you plan to accept her offer?"

The king asked this as he suddenly lunged a step forward and swung. Jace instinctively parried the tip of his blade and took a step back, clearing his throat and staring at the sword. "I… of course. Just like you said, I can't refuse."

Damn it. So he was just going to duel Varian Wrynn out of nowhere? This was Varian Wrynn, for fuck's sake!

A guy who'd been thrown into an orc-and-ogre arena with zero experience and still had the strength to win the championship!

Varian held his sword steady — neither his hands nor his feet made any wasted movement, solid as a black panther that had locked onto its prey. Jace couldn't help but swallow.

"It's not just that she wants to find her father. Part of me genuinely hopes Daval Prestor is still alive."

Varian spoke as he launched another attack. Jace barely held off the quick strike, and after their blades crossed the two men swapped positions.

"Why?" Jace asked, lifting his eyes from his blade tip to Varian's, then quickly looking back down.

"In the Alterac conflict, it seems only two kingdoms have no voice, Jace. One is Dalaran, which never involves itself in any territorial dispute. The other is Stormwind." Varian looked at Jace, his blade held between them.

He went on. "Gilneas has Prince Isiden. Kul Tiras's warships still sit off the coast of Hillsblade. Stromgarde and Lordaeron's armies each occupy a slice of the Alteraci snow mountains. I know some of the Strombrae folk have already taken to calling Trollbane 'lord.'

"If any one of these kings and generals achieves his goal, it will shatter the balance maintained for several hundred years — and further isolate Stormwind in the South, Jace." He lowered his sword, his expression grave. "If Stormwind helps House Prestor rescue the true Lord Daval from the jaws of the black dragon…

"Without Lordaeron's backing, can Daval Prestor still contest the succession against the two Perenolde princes?" Jace asked.

"In any case, the Perenolde family name has been stained again and again by King Aiden," Varian said. "Whatever kind of man Aiden Perenolde is, at least both King Thoras and King Terenas have publicly questioned his competence. And Isiden… he's too young.

"If Daval truly survived — a man who was replaced by a dark sorcerer as fearsome as Deathwing, who endured untold blood and fire and lived to rise again — just think about it… Even if he died, the story of his daughter traveling across the seven kingdoms to fight tirelessly against the black dragons for her father's sake would be told everywhere. That's a far nobler honor than betraying the Alliance in the face of the orcish threat, stealing Medivh's relics to aid the black dragons and the Horde."

With that, Varian leveled his blade again. "Come, Jace."

Jace nodded, gripped his sword with both hands, stepped in, and delivered a clean, decisive overhead chop. Varian blocked it with effort, his eyes widening in surprise.

"There are those in the kingdom who claim black dragon blood runs in your veins, Jace. I don't believe it, but I have to admit — you're damn strong." He managed a strained smile.

The two men stepped apart. Varian rolled his wrist and smiled. "You should come more often. We'll improve each other, Jace."

Though the king had just been tormented by a tangle of tedious statecraft, he looked quite relaxed — even happy.

After hearing all that in the tavern last night, Jace was reluctant to think it, but couldn't help wondering whether this had something to do with Katrana.

So he tested the waters. "Your Majesty seems to be in good spirits today. Something good happen?"

"Hah, you finally asked." Varian set his sword down. "Tiffin's pregnant!"

"Really?" Jace asked in surprise.

Tiffin was Varian's pet name for Queen Tiffin. Their child would be the one to inherit the throne — the future High King of the Alliance, Anduin Wrynn.

Jace was genuinely happy for Varian — not only the happiness of a friend about to become a father, nor just the strange, displaced feeling of witnessing the imminent birth of a famous figure he already knew.

It was also because Jace could feel, from the uncontainable joy and love overflowing from this tall, strong young man with black hair, that he loved Queen Tiffin and his unborn child so completely — without the slightest reservation or doubt — that there was no space left for Katrana Prestor.

This was probably also why, later on, even after Onyxia had manipulated the kingdom's high officials and controlled the Defias Brotherhood, she'd still been forced to use magic to tear Varian into an obedient, docile persona before she could smoothly manipulate him into ruling the entire kingdom.

And now that Jace was here, she would never dare to cast such dark magic so openly and expose herself.
