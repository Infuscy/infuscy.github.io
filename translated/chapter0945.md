## Chapter 945: Going Too Far

Marin had once said that true dragons grew furious when mortals uttered the dragon tongue. Jace had indeed tried it once in front of a black drake, and it had truly enraged the creature.

Of course, Onyxia was no ordinary savage drake but a genuine mighty dragon, second only in power to the mighty Dragon Aspects. She certainly wouldn't lose control of her temper the way those drakes did.

Jace certainly wasn't counting on a single probing sentence to make her lose her temper and fly into a rage. He only hoped to coax out some trace of an off emotion, but there hadn't been any particularly obvious result either.

So the next step was to test whether she was a mortal at all.

"I didn't expect the black dragon's hiding place to be orc territory," Katrana said, thinking it over. "Doesn't that make things more complicated?"

Jace asked, "The orcs colluded with the black dragons in the first place. Did you think the orcs would be more dangerous than the black dragons?"

Katrana said, "I'd imagined it would be a massive lair, with Deathwing's brood guarding it. A huge dragon might overlook a human of smaller stature, so that archmage was able to successfully rescue Master Krasus. But if it's a fortress guarded by orcs, it must be full of armored orc soldiers patrolling everywhere."

Jace thought for a moment. "That's true. If your father really is imprisoned in Blackrock Spire, getting him out would probably require a joint army from Ironforge and Stormwind to lay siege to the place."

Katrana said at once, "If an army really marched to the foot of that tower, he'd certainly become a hostage of the black dragons — or even be killed…

"I thought I made myself quite clear on Illgynoth Tower, Prestor," Jace said, the temperature in his voice dropping by half. "Do you think there's any chance Daval Prestor is still alive after being imprisoned in a lair of black dragons and orcs all these years? I was simply being polite and didn't want to state it so plainly."

Katrana's eyes were already wide with shock. She looked as though she couldn't believe Jace would say such a thing.

Jace went on. "The gold you offered is indeed no small sum. It's just not enough to send me to my death in Blackrock Spire. But more importantly, I have no desire to become a pawn in power games like yours."

"You think all I want is to save my father so I can inherit his lands and title?" Katrana demanded. The politeness, the smile, the courtesy — all swept away in an instant.

Jace met her silence with his own. Katrana lowered her lips as if to speak, then stopped herself. After a moment she said, "Even when they slandered him as a warlock who meant to enslave King Terenas, as an evil sorcerer using a black dragon's death to exonerate himself — I never once thought of cutting myself off from the name Prestor."

Her voice went cold. "I once held out hope for you, Jace Sesso. And I'm not disappointed in you now either — because you're simply on the same level as everyone else."

With that, Katrana inclined her body in a quick bow and turned to leave. "Lady Prestor," Jace called after her retreating back.

"Go on, say whatever insult you still have," Katrana said, stopping.

"I… all right. I'm sorry for what I said." Jace said, "A lot of people told me your coming to Stormwind was nothing but political theater, and that seriously interfered with my judgment."

"You can't begin to imagine how absurd the things those people can make up!" Katrana said through gritted teeth.

Jace said, "It's just that infiltrating Blackrock Spire is by no means an easy or simple task, Lady Prestor. It may take some time to scout and draw up a plan."

Hearing this, Katrana turned back in surprise. "You're willing to believe me?"

"I've decided to trust what I've seen with my own eyes and heard with my own ears, rather than other people's rumors," Jace said.

Katrana smiled. "You have no idea what this means to me and to House Prestor."

Jace said, "In that case, you'd better not mention my mission to anyone else. There are still likely black dragons infiltrating Stormwind, even though many in the kingdom keep watch on movements inside the walls. Even so, there's no guarantee we've kept out every last one of those monsters skilled at disguise."

"I've heard about the dragonkin too," Katrana said. "I'll keep what's between us strictly to myself, Master Sesso. This concerns my father's life — and of course my own life too. I would never take it lightly."

"It's best if you recognize the danger in this," Jace said. "Then I'll make preparations first. If there's any progress, I'll come find you in person, Lady Prestor. Oh — and remember to have the money ready. The renovation cost for Illgynoth Tower is…"

"Four hundred gold," the other party cut him off.

Jace nodded. Katrana smiled, bowed, and headed toward the courtyard gate. Watching that young back, Jace rolled his neck, none too pleased.

He turned and walked toward the gate leading into the Park, thinking that the gold was probably not going to be earned.

When this so-called Prestor had grown angry, she hadn't revealed even a scrap of Shadow that could be detected.

To a warlock like Jace, even as she'd screamed herself hoarse and burned with fury, it had been as hollow as a muted film. She wasn't the real Katrana Prestor — at least not the original one.

Even if she wasn't Onyxia herself, she was certainly a marionette manipulated by something else. Judging from how she'd steered him toward Blackrock Spire, black dragon meddling was a certainty.

Seen this way, Onyxia probably knew Jace could detect the dark aura of the black dragons, so she'd used some method to mask the power Katrana gave off. It might be to cover the black dragon's own stench, or it might be the magic of a black dragon's mental domination.

In any case, this was more than enough to conclude that the woman before him was no ordinary mortal. So how to expose her?

Jace thought of the spell that had exposed the black dragon in the game — the one the captured Marshal Windsor had found.

The guard at Ironforge's gate, the Dark Iron dwarf Ruige, had more or less helped Jace confirm it. He'd mentioned that the Dark Iron dwarves of Shadowforge City had long been searching for a way to counter the black dragons atop Blackrock Spire — perhaps connected to that mysterious spell.

When that quest line reached the Stormwind stage in the game, Marshal Windsor — a marshal who barely knew any magic — had been able to forcibly force the mighty Onyxia into revealing her true form just by speaking that spell…

After leaving the Park, Jace took the letter he'd already written and headed for the Mage Quarter. He came to Marin's study, knocked, and pushed the door open when he heard a voice within.

"Ah, Master Sesso." Marin rarely smiled when he saw Jace, but he did now. He set down what he was holding and looked up. "It's all thanks to you for introducing me to Lady Sola Shade — she cleared up many of my lingering questions about the green dragonflight. I can sense she's holding things back from me, but that's understandable. The green dragons are among the most mysterious beings in this world; they've always been reluctant to deal with the outside world. Forget the Wizard Sanctum — even Dalaran knows precious little about the green dragons. This is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.

"I've already put together a report over this period, filling in a very important gap in my research on the relationships between different dragonflights. Once this report is submitted up the chain, it'll definitely cause quite a stir. Those old fellows on the Kirin Tor will be writing me letters. I'm really quite curious how you came to know her — and she speaks very highly of you too, of course none of that surprises me in the slightest."

"Glad I could be of help, teacher." Jace smiled. "I thought she'd tell you how we met."

"She only mentioned Winterspring," Marin said. "But that place is itself as mysterious as the green dragons. Telling the people here about it is about the same as not saying anything at all."

"Right." The archmage suddenly bent down and pulled out an exquisitely decorated little box from under his desk. "This came from Quel'Thalas. From Ranger-Leader Vereesa Windrunner."

As he said this, Marin smiled meaningfully. By now, most of Stormwind's high-ranking officials already knew about Jace and Vereesa's relationship.

"It's been sitting here for over a week. I kept not being able to find you, and I had no idea where you'd gone."

"It's payment from helping the Relics Association on Kalimdor earlier," Jace said, taking out the letter. "As it happens, Master, I came here this time to ask you to deliver a letter to Vereesa for me. I'm sorry for the trouble."

"No need to be so formal." Marin took the letter. "Oh — have you heard about that Alteraci princess? I heard she's been settled by the king in the northern Park. Quite the generous treatment, really. I've even heard rumors in the city that this Prestor woman has her eye on the Moonbrook lands — that place has no suitable heir right now, and it's been sitting in the king's hands without proper standing…

"I'm afraid her target isn't Moonbrook, Master," Jace said with a smile.

"I can tell you don't like that woman," Marin said, folding his arms. "But that's only natural, given she's a Prestor…

"I have no particular feelings about her or this family," Jace said. "It's just that she came to me before, hoping I'd help find Lord Daval. That's no easy task. The reason I'm reaching out to Vereesa Windrunner is that I'm hoping to find some help from her side."

Marin blinked. "So she came to Stormwind specifically looking for you? You've already met?"

"A few times," Jace said. "It's not exactly that she came looking for me specifically — King Varian introduced her to my ear, after all, on account of the black dragons and their dark magic. And I imagine she wasn't well received in the North and wanted to seek some support down here."

Marin said, "That makes sense. I wonder if there's anything I can help with, Master Sesso?"

Jace thought it over. "If I leave Stormwind to investigate Lord Daval's affairs, I'm worried the dragonkin might seize the chance to cause trouble in the city, Master. After all, this matter has always been handled by me and SI:7's Mathias Shaw. Katrana Prestor's safety especially — she's going around asking people to find her father in the black dragons' lair. That's bound to draw no small amount of black dragon hostility. I hope the Wizard Sanctum can keep a closer eye on Lady Prestor's personal safety, keep her under the mages' watch as much as possible, and not give those disguised dragonkin a chance to approach her."

"Your concern is well founded," Marin said. "The king takes that Prestor's safety very seriously. I imagine Master Andormas would be quite willing to use this opportunity to strengthen ties with the crown. As it happens, he and Magino Dumas are currently using those warlock prisoners to explore methods for identifying disguised dragonkin — this would be the perfect time to put it into practice."

Jace nodded. "That way, both Stormwind and the princess will be that much safer."
