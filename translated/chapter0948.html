Chapter 948: A Beginner's Guide to Disguise

"The ones locked in the dungeon are all heavy criminals, Sola." Jace looked at the dungeon entrance leading underground ahead and said quietly, "I'm guessing some of them must be quite famous where they come from, and there must be quite a few people who've been plotting to break them out. I'm thinking — if you took on their likeness, would you be recognized?"

"I'll make some adjustments, Jace," Sola said. "For instance, combining the features of two different people, or using magic to make slight adjustments to the placement of facial features. Trust me — even small changes make an enormous difference in the overall appearance, and it becomes very hard to recognize. It has to do with how you mortals perceive and identify others."

"Come with me, two mages." A jailer ahead beckoned with a set of keys. "Stay close. This prison is very old and has undergone many modifications, so it's easy to get lost down there."

Jace nodded. Sola pressed her lips together and asked, "Did you tell them I'm a mage too?"

"What else? You found some passerby from Dalaran, copied her likeness, live in the Mage Quarter, assist an archmage with his research, and you can even shapeshift — you're more of a mage than I am." Jace glanced ahead and whispered, "Be confident. Most of the people here haven't seen real magic. They won't ask you to put on a show."

Sola straightened her collar and nodded.

Last time Jace had come to this prison he'd been thrown in directly, and he'd been escorted out by guards on his way out too — naturally he'd had no mood to observe the place in detail.

This time it was his second time in, and he had the chance to browse freely, so he looked around with curiosity the whole way.

The dark cells had little lighting, which made the jailer's lantern ahead especially dazzling.

Even so, Jace could tell that the structure here bore some resemblance to the one in the game. One long main corridor ran straight to a place too dark to make out in the depths, with branch corridors running perpendicular to it on both sides.

But once you entered the branch corridors, things became very different.

Just as the jailer had said, the passages and cells here had undergone no small number of renovations. In some of the old cells, the cross-sections of the iron bars hadn't even been removed — they stood there like bamboo shoots along the side of the passage, simply bent over in a crude fix.

So once you entered the branch corridors, the prison's passages became extremely complicated. The jailer, however, walked them with practiced ease, without a hint of hesitation.

Most of the cells they passed were empty, standing open, and there weren't many guards. Jace asked curiously, "Where are the warlocks captured at the Lighthouse held?"

"They're not down here. They're in the dungeon across the canal, sir," the jailer answered. "That one is guarded by the Wizard Sanctum and guards sent by the king — it's not under our jurisdiction. Thank God for that. I haven't got what it takes to guard a bunch of hardcases who wield dark magic and can summon demons. You think these iron bars could stop them from casting on me, Master? Never mind the bars — even with a whole canal between us, it worries me."

Jace nodded, and was listening to the jailer recount the scene of how the warlocks had been brought in and assigned cells when a violent shout suddenly rang out from a nearby cell.

"Hey! Hold on!"

Immediately came a clatter of metal chains dragging across the floor. A black figure slammed against the bars with a bang, craning to look out. The jailer swung his lamp over. "Get back inside, behave yourself. Do you know who this is?!"

"I know who he is…"

Locked behind the bars was a Dark Iron dwarf — filthy, matted hair, grimy braids in his beard and hair — glaring at Jace with his natural blood-red eyes and speaking in Dwarvish: "I recognize you."

"I don't recognize you," Jace said in Common.

"He's a monster who summons demons, sir," the Dark Iron dwarf said, pointing at Jace and staring at the jailer in Common. "Arrest him now. He can summon succubi, and God knows how many times he's fornicated with them. This fellow is a cunning, cowardly demon-worshipper, but incomparably evil — compared to him I could be a bishop! I swear to the Firelord… no, I swear to your Holy Light, not a word of a lie!"

"You've got it backwards, you black dwarf," the jailer said, shaking his lantern. "This master is the king's counselor, specially tasked with dealing with your sort… You know how many cultists and the demons they summoned he wiped out at the Lighthouse? You picked the wrong target to slander…

"What? The king's counselor?"

The dwarf blinked first, then let out a cold laugh. "Him? He'd piss himself if you pointed a gun at him. And he's supposed to be a counselor? And wiped out demons?"

Hearing the dwarf say that, Jace immediately remembered who this fellow was.

He was that Dark Iron dwarf explorer Jace had encountered when he went to the Burning Steppes to help Ursula search for Dark Iron powder. Recalling the circumstances back then and looking at this man now, it suddenly felt familiar.

"It's you." Jace asked with a smile, "I pissed myself? Did you find your pants yet?"

"Ptah!" The Dark Iron dwarf spat and cursed in Dwarvish: "You filthy little soft-meat, you bug with a belly full of bad ideas!" He switched back to Common and roared, "I saw him with my own eyes robbing innocent travelers with that succubus! And your king hired this sort as a counselor?"

As he said this, the dwarf looked at Sola again. "Don't be fooled by him, beautiful lady, this bastard…"

Though the Dark Iron dwarf was cursing wildly and furiously, as a mortal who couldn't control his emotions he released everything to his heart's content. The panic emanating from the flustered jailer beside him tangled with it, and all of it was clearly picked up by Jace.

This only made him more certain of the strangeness of Katrana Prestor.

"Ah, damn it." The jailer called toward a distance. "Someone come help me shut this guy up — he's asking for another beating again."

"I'd be happy to oblige."

Sola said this and pointed a finger into the cell. The Dark Iron dwarf, whose furious features had been twisted together, suddenly went slack. His eyes crossed and he slumped down behind the iron bars, snoring, drool running from the corner of his mouth into his beard.

"Really a simple and effective sleep spell, mage," the jailer said admiringly. "Our people ought to learn it — it'd save a lot of effort. The ones locked up here are all male dwarves, Captain told me you came to see the female dwarves and orcs, right?"

"Yes," Sola said.

The jailer said, "Then keep following me. The female dwarves are just ahead. Don't pay these fellows' ravings too much mind — they'd love for all of Stormwind to sit in jail with them."

Come to think of it, Jace had never seen a female Dark Iron dwarf since coming to this world.

Arriving at this corridor, he saw a female dwarf sitting motionless in the depths of the dark cell, staring outward — only her blood-red eyes visible, sweeping over the outside — and immediately thought of the legend of the wicked witch Modgud that circulated throughout the Stormwind Kingdom and Dun Morogh.

Although for the Wildhammer and Bronzebeard dwarves, the females seemed more approachable than the males, with the Dark Iron dwarves it was the opposite — the females were eerily unsettling.

"How about it, Sola?" Jace whispered. "Can you see clearly?"

"No problem, Jace." Sola nodded.

"Might as well see a little more clearly," the female dwarf inside said coldly. "That way I can remember it before I die. I'd be honored."

Sola looked back at her without a trace of evasion in her eyes. The jailer said, a little unnaturally, "If you're done, we can keep going, you two. The orcs locked up here were only captured not long ago. They'll probably be sent to the internment camp in Lordaeron before long, per the agreement."

Following the jailer further down, they reached a deeper dungeon. Here there were only two simple corridors, the light dimmer, and a rotting stench hung in the air.

Jace could tell — it was the faint scent of demon blood. At Grim Batol, where the stench of orcs filled the entire fortress, it had left an extremely deep impression on him.

Strangely, facing the orcs they were about to see, this jailer wasn't as nervous as he'd been meeting the Dark Iron dwarves. When they reached the spot he pointed inside and said, "Here. Our patrols rarely catch female orcs — you're lucky."

Jace looked inside curiously. He saw a shadow sitting inside, about the size of a tall human male. She had dark-blue long hair, lowered her head to stare at the ground, and breathed feebly, her shoulders heaving.

"She doesn't look in good shape, Jace," Sola said. "Sick?" Jace asked.

"I don't know. At least not any illness I'm familiar with," Sola replied.

Jace immediately remembered — this was the so-called "orcish lethargy." He'd learned about it when he studied the orcish language, from the research report Antonidas had published to the entire Alliance.

In fact, it was a kind of "withdrawal reaction" that came over the entire orcish Horde after the power of the demon blood faded. Every orc who'd drunk demon blood suffered the same affliction.

"Truly a race well-suited to warfare," Sola said softly. "I think I've seen enough, Jace. We can leave."

"Is that all?" the jailer asked.

"Yes. Thank you for your help, sir," Jace said.

That evening, back at the house by the canal, Sola prepared to test her shapeshifting. Jace had also specially called Grede over — after all, when it came to identifying Dark Iron dwarves, he counted as an expert.

The two of them waited in the living room for a while, and a female Dark Iron dwarf walked out from the side room behind them. Jace noticed she had black hair, rather than the red or brown hair common among Dark Iron dwarves.

The dragonflight seemed quite particular about hair color — for a green dragon, managing a head of dark black hair was already pretty good; producing red was genuinely difficult.

Still, holding up the stone on his staff and shining it over, aside from the red of the eyes being a bit faint, there was almost nothing wrong. "What do you think, Grede?" he looked to the side.

"You want me to say this is a Wildhammer woman — she definitely wouldn't pass," Grede said, squinting. "Standing there without a single one of the fine qualities our women possess, like enthusiasm, cheerfulness, and all that… But if you say she's a Dark Iron dwarf, then there's probably no problem. Those fellows look at people with that exact expression, like they want to dig your heart out with their eyelashes."

Jace couldn't help but laugh. "The mirror stone shows no problems either, Sola. Next up — the orc round!"

The female dwarf smiled, nodded, and turned back into the side room. Before long, an exceptionally tall female orc walked out. Whether it was the narrow orcish snout, the tusks pressing against the lips, or the bristles on the face — everything was imitated flawlessly, down to the slightly labored breathing of that female orc.

"This one's yours to judge," Grede said with a laugh to Sola. "Our Master Sesso is a professional at playing an orc, Sola — you'll see for yourself soon enough."

"I hope I won't have to keep this appearance too long," Sola said, somewhat displeased.

"You only need to get through the orcs' encirclement looking like this, Sola. Don't worry." Jace said, holding back a laugh.
