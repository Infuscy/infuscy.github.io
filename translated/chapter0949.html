Chapter 949: The Noble of Lakeshire

Autumn had deepened in Lakeshire — red rooftops and dark-green pine forests, surrounded by withered oak clusters. Fallen leaves blanketed Lake Everstill, and as the cold wind passed, it stirred up vast, complicated ripples without pattern.

At the fork in the road by the bridge leading into town, Sola stood on the shore, staring unblinkingly at those fallen leaves.

She hadn't brought Jace here in her original form, after all — this was the time when they needed to stay most low-profile. From Goldshire to Lakeshire there were sentries everywhere, along with passing merchants and travelers. Even cutting through the forest, there was no guarantee someone near a farm wouldn't get a look.

Once word spread that a green dragon was heading for Redridge, and it reached the ears of the black dragon's hidden agents within the kingdom, the whole affair would become far more dangerous.

Still, compared to carrying a person on her back in dragon form, Sola seemed to prefer traveling in a mortal shape.

She'd been looking around with curiosity the whole journey — at the watchtowers by the roadside and the crops in the farms alike. At the same time she kept complaining that when she'd come to Stonewatch before, Lakeshire had happened to be shrouded in night, and she'd never had the chance to properly enjoy the scenery here.

"Wait here a moment, Sola. I'm going into Lakeshire to find someone," Jace said, taking out the note Mathias had given him. "He'll tell us about the distribution of orcs and dwarves in the north, below Blackrock Mountain. Even if we're going in disguise, we should avoid running into large groups of enemies as much as possible — that way there'll be far fewer complications."

"All right, Jace. I'll listen to you," Sola said. "But I've had a doubt all along."

"Go ahead," Jace said.

"How do you know about the spell that breaks dragon disguises?" Sola turned her head to look at Jace.

Jace explained, "There used to be an organization in Shadowforge City called the Thorium Brotherhood. I don't know if Grede mentioned it to you. This organization's relations with Shadowforge City have grown rather strained now, and many members have fled. I learned of it from a Thorium Brotherhood member living in Ironforge."

Another cold wind blew over just then, chilling Jace right through. It suddenly made him feel as though there was still something unfinished between him and that Ruige — and it had something to do with the cold weather too, what was it… So many things had happened since leaving Ironforge that the time had simply been too full.

But no matter what it was, nothing was more urgent than finding the spell to break Onyxia's disguise as quickly as possible.

Sola said, "I know that fellow. The one who taught you how to communicate with the elements…"

Ah, right — that ancient Kalimdor dictionary. He should return it before Winter Veil, when the Thorium Brotherhood people came to his place for their year-end review.

It was less than two months until Winter Veil. Going to Shadowforge City shouldn't take that long, right? It should still be in time. Seeing Jace suddenly freeze like that, Sola asked curiously, "Did you think of something?"

"Nothing." Jace rubbed his nose guiltily. "Go on."

"I mean…" Sola pondered for a moment. "Do you trust that Dark Iron dwarf? I just feel that the Dark Iron clan, after suffering the curse of the Firelord Ragnaros, became very different from ordinary dwarves — even very different from their former selves. And even if he is trustworthy, it doesn't mean the rumors in Shadowforge City are fact."

As she said this, she lowered her head again. "Perhaps it's just arrogance on my part making me doubt — deep down I feel there shouldn't exist a spell in this world that can so easily expose our disguises, especially magic cast by a mighty dragon like Onyxia. By your description, it sounds as if any ordinary person, even an ordinary person without any magical training at all, could make it work just by shouting that spell."

Honestly, even Jace didn't know whether he should fully believe everything that had happened in the game. Things that were easy in the game might be impossibly difficult here. How exactly this spell worked, no one knew.

Perhaps it had some special connection to Reginald Windsor, who had uncovered it. But right now it was still ten years before the events of the game. Windsor had no contact with the Dark Iron dwarves yet, had never been captured, let alone knew anything about this spell.

But since Ruige had helped corroborate that Shadowforge City was indeed searching for a way to counter the black dragons — and that there had been some progress — and the memories of his past life lined up with the reality here, Jace couldn't ignore this lead.

"I'm not the only one who's mentioned this to me, Sola. So no what happens, I have to go take a look," Jace explained. "I don't have a Dark Iron dwarf I can trust, and that Thorium Brotherhood fellow fled Shadowforge City — he definitely wouldn't be willing to go back in there and take risks for me again. Right now, besides me, there probably isn't anyone more suited to infiltrating Shadowforge City and asking about the spell."

"You're not worried the Dark Iron dwarves inside will casually expose your disguise?" Jace asked with a smile.

Sola frowned. "All right, this legendary spell really does make me uneasy. Exposing my true face among a crowd of corrupted fire worshippers — it's hard to imagine what the outcome would be."

Jace said, "If we really find this spell in Shadowforge City, I won't let anyone speak it in front of you. What's more, we have an ally — Molophil is right there. It would only take a thought from her to make the other party hesitate over whether to keep speaking."

Sola stole a glance to her side. "Mm," she said, though she was looking the wrong way.

"Then I'll head in first. Wait for me at the tavern."

With that, Jace followed the address Mathias had given him and found a room with a small courtyard behind a side path below the apothecary.

This courtyard certainly couldn't compare to the one Katrana lived in, back in Stormwind's Park, but it was beautifully decorated and looked like the residence of someone of considerable standing.

An SI:7 collaborator — or "liaison," rather — living in such a high-profile place?

But then again, it was hard to say. Some spies and agents liked appearing high-profile at all kinds of social occasions, even holding high office — that way they attracted less suspicion.

House Morgan. Jace hadn't heard this surname in Stormwind, but in the game he did know a possibly related location — Morgan's Sentinel in the Burning Steppes.

Morgan's Sentinel was the foremost outpost where the Stormwind Kingdom faced off against Blackrock Spire. It looked like an abandoned village that had later been used by SI:7 and the Stormwind army as a fairly concealed reconnaissance base.

At that moment, a middle-aged man practicing archery in the courtyard caught sight of a shadow on this side through the iron fence. He looked over and called out, "Are you lost, sir?"

"I don't think so," Jace said. "I'm looking for someone."

The man came closer, sizing Jace up through the fence — especially glancing at his fingers — then said, "Adrian Morgan. A mage. And you're looking for?"

"I heard there's a Morgan living here," Jace said. "I was sent by the king's people."

"Mathias Shaw," Adrian Morgan said with a smile, pulling open the iron gate.

"Jace Sesso." Jace extended his hand.

Hearing the name, Adrian froze for a moment before slowly reaching out his hand. "You're Jace Sesso?"

"It's me," Jace said.

"Forgive me, Master. I didn't know the king's counselor had come." The other man's expression brightened at once. "Please, come in."

Jace nodded and entered the courtyard, glancing at the garden around him. Adrian took Mathias's note and looked at it. "You're planning to go to Blackrock Mountain?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Related to the black dragon affair?" Adrian said. "I thought after the Moonbrook matter was settled, the king would forget about the difficulties on this side."

"It's related to that Alteraci princess, and also to the black dragons, I suppose," Jace said with a nod. "It's about her missing father."

Hearing it was Prestor business, a trace of barely perceptible disappointment crossed the middle-aged man's face — though of course that didn't escape Jace's notice.

Still, Adrian pointed at the house ahead and said, "Come with me, Master. Here we have a map of the orc fortifications on the outskirts of Blackrock Mountain, drawn up together by SI:7 and Stonewatch scouts. This should be what you're looking for."

"Yes," Jace said. "Thank you very much."
