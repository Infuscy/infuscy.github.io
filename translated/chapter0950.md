## Chapter 950: Racial Advantage

Adrian led Jace through the courtyard and into the detached house, where Jace immediately spotted the portraits hanging in the corridor.

These portraits didn't look like the sort that belonged in such a small house — they looked more like something hung in a castle. So in the narrow corridor of this house, they were particularly eye-catching.

Reaching the end of the corridor, they entered a somewhat dim room. Adrian drew the curtains, took a map from a box on the bookshelf, and spread it on the table. "This was drawn two months ago on the king's orders. In the past two months the orcs and Dark Iron dwarves haven't made any moves, so I imagine there shouldn't be much deviation in this map, Master."

Jace took the map and looked it over. Orc fortresses and strongholds dotted nearly the entire perimeter of Blackrock Mountain, extending all the way west near the Thaurissan ruins. If they wanted to approach Blackrock Mountain, the best route would probably be the same as when he'd gone with Vereesa last time — approaching along the ridge.

But two years ago there had been drakes patrolling even up there. Now, with the Dark Iron dwarves buckling under the orcs' offensive and even losing the will to counterattack, weren't the black dragons and their dragonkin legions far more rampant than before?

Even disguised as orcs, two figures standing alone on the ridge, walking straight toward Blackrock Mountain, would look rather odd. They'd probably draw the suspicion of the patrolling drakes.

Seeing Jace's grave expression, Adrian said, "Blackrock Mountain is indeed heavily garrisoned by the various orc legions, with little room for infiltration. And in recent years their numbers have kept growing — many orcs who failed to make it back through the Dark Portal have joined them, even exiles from the Dragonmaw clan. Still, I imagine a great mage like you must find a way in. Just be as careful as possible."

"Thanks for the reminder." Jace smiled, then pointed to a road leading from the Thaurissan ruins to Blackrock Mountain. There were indeed no large orc fortresses near this route.

"How about this route?"

"That's the Thaurison Highway, built by the Dark Iron dwarves. There are many orc scout cavalry from different legions patrolling it now, Master," Adrian said. "And there's no cover or concealment around it — a flat, barren wasteland. Once you're spotted, there's nowhere to hide. If it were me, I wouldn't try approaching Blackrock Mountain from this direction."

Jace thought it over. For a human agent, this was indeed a dangerous place. But for two orcs, it was actually an advantage.

If they were spotted near an orc stronghold or fortress, the soldiers inside would certainly know something about their own garrison's comrades, and a quick check of their identities would easily expose them.

But on the road in the middle, the patrol cavalry from the various legions, the scouts and messengers inside Blackrock Spire, and even orcs arriving from outside — a mixed crowd… Though there were many people, it was also easier to blend in. The patrolling drakes overhead wouldn't pay special attention to every single orc passing along the road, would they?

"Can I take this map with me, Mr. Morgan?" Jace asked.

"Be my master, Master." Adrian rolled up the map and handed it to Jace.

Jace nodded. "Thank you very much. I won't take up any more of your time."

"Oh — Master." Adrian said, "It was you who exposed the black dragons' attempt to infiltrate our kingdom, and even their plot to invade Stonewatch and Lakeshire. Do you know how much thwarting their scheme means to my family?"

"Your family?" Jace asked curiously.

"Yes." Adrian glanced at a portrait on the bookshelf of a nobleman with a full brown beard. "Two hundred years ago, my ancestors ruled the vast grasslands and forests north of the Redridge Mountains. At that time, Morgan lands were the wealthiest and most peaceful in all the Kingdom of Stormwind. Until the civil war in Ironforge spread south of Dun Morogh, and a dark volcanic eruption created Blackrock Mountain — and destroyed everything my ancestors had possessed."

Hearing this story, Jace was somewhat surprised. He hadn't expected that place in the game called Morgan's Sentinel to have a story like this.

"But my forebears did not give up. Even if our homeland was burned to ashes — it was still our homeland. With what property remained they raised an army to retake what belonged to us. It's just that generations of Wrynn kings have disapproved of us picking a quarrel with the Dark Iron — even when it was their magic that devastated the lands of Stormwind's own subjects."

As he said all this, from his face to his voice he was full of pride in his family's glorious past.

He stroked a longsword with an exquisitely crafted scabbard on the weapon rack beside him — one that looked as though it hadn't been touched in a long time — and continued. "My father thought that the orcs who invaded through the Dark Portal and seized Blackrock Spire would prompt the Alliance to storm that place in one stroke and utterly destroy all the evil forces entrenched within, driving the dwarves back north of Blackrock Mountain. He never expected that offensive would end in failure with the death of Marshal Anduin Lothar. The Morgan family's enemies went from the Dark Iron dwarves to the orcs — and now, fearsome black dragons on top of that. My father couldn't accept the Alliance's setback at Blackrock Spire and died of grief."

"I'm sorry," Jace said.

"Thank you." Adrian turned back. "You see, Master Jace Sesso — if the black dragons' plot hadn't been thwarted, Stonewatch might have fallen back into orc hands by now, and even Lakeshire would have suffered a second — no, a third occupation. By then my sister and I would probably both be dead, and the Morgan family's hope of revival would be utterly extinguished.

"I have what may be an excessive request, Master. When you return to the palace and see King Varian, I hope you can make it clear to him that we Morgans have always been loyal to the Kingdom of Stormwind and to the Lion of Wrynn, and that we have never forgotten reclaiming Stormwind's lost lands. I know he values the tranquility of Lake Everstill and the whole Redridge region more than his father or his grandfather did. As long as he's willing to extend his aid, he certainly won't regret the decision."

"I'll relay your words to the king, Mr. Morgan," Jace said. "But to be frank, the entire kingdom's military and finances are stretched thin right now. I'm afraid that even if King Varian wants to help you, he may lack the strength to see it through."

Adrian lowered his head and sighed softly. "I understand. All the Morgan family wants is for the king not to forget us."

Coming out of Morgan's residence, Jace looked back at the house with some helplessness.

Reclaiming the plains of the Burning Steppes that had once belonged to Stormwind would require not only defeating the black dragon prince Nefarian and razing the entirety of Blackrock Spire, but also overthrowing the regime of the "Emperor" of Shadowforge City, Dagran Thaurissan.

Even if a godly army comparable to a player raid team in the game accomplished both of these feats, by the time the Morgan family reclaimed their lost lands, what they'd have in hand would be a scorched earth teeming with fearsome beasts and wandering ogres.

It was probably for this very reason that generations of Wrynn kings had been unwilling to aid their efforts.

But for Adrian's family, two hundred years of striving had probably become the faith of the entire clan. As an outsider, he really had no standing to judge that kind of persistence from a cost-benefit perspective.

Coming out, he went to the tavern, wove through the crowd, and sat down across from Sola in a corner. "How did it go, Jace?"

"I got the map." Jace glanced at a group of Bronzebeard dwarves laughing loudly nearby and lowered his voice. "I've settled on a route — a road frequently traveled by Blackrock scouts. There are patrol cavalry from Blackrock Mountain's various legions there. A big crowd with a lot of chatter — exactly the sort of place for our kind to mix in."

Sola thought it over for a moment. "But we don't have horses."

"Horses?" Jace blinked. "Orcs don't ride horses. They ride wargs. You know those beasts? You might not — they brought them from Draenor… Hmm, right, wargs…"

If you wanted to pass as an orc scout, didn't you have to ride a warg? "What do we do?" Sola asked.

"Come with me to Stonewatch first," Jace said. "They have a lot of supplies the orcs left behind. There should be warg saddles — I remember there were."

"And the wolves?" Sola asked.

"If there are wild wargs roaming even in Elwynn Forest, Sola, there must be even more of them on the plains up north," Jace said. Of course there were — after all, in the game there were stray wargs everywhere in the Burning Steppes. Jace wasn't too worried about this. "I don't know if they'll understand my orders," Sola said. "But I'll try to persuade them to listen."

"If they won't listen to you, they'll have to listen to Molophil," Jace said.

Sola smiled sheepishly. "Leaving a demon mentally enslave a beast with dark magic — and as a warden of the forest, I feel no guilt at all?"

Jace wagged a finger. "Strictly speaking, those aren't Azeroth's beasts either. They're what the orcs brought over from the other side of the Dark Portal. Not under your jurisdiction."

Sola nodded and asked, "What if orcs try to talk to me? I don't speak Orcish."

Jace said, "No problem. I'll handle communicating with them, trust me — I have considerable experience talking with orcs. You just play the role of a taciturn warrior on the side. You'll probably only need to say a couple of simple pleasantries to handle their courtesies."

"For instance?" Sola asked.

"For instance." Jace cleared his throat. "Lok'tar."

"Orcs use this slogan as a greeting in wartime. I imagine the Blackrock orcs are in exactly that state right now. Learn this one phrase and you can handle most situations."

"Lok'tar." Sola repeated after him.

Jace was startled when he heard it. Aside from not copying his voice, Sola had gotten nearly every syllable and pronunciation exactly right.

This didn't sound at all like someone learning Orcish for the first time. Though the word was short, Jace had spent a considerable amount of time training himself just to imitate that distinctive orcish cadence — and Sola had nailed it in one go.

"No need to be surprised, Jace. Don't forget what I am. I'm not using my own throat to pronounce things." Sola smiled as she picked up a pastry.
