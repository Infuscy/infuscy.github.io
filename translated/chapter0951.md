## Chapter 951: Stonewatch's Stores

They arrived at Stonewatch right around evening. But upon learning that Jace had come to Stonewatch, Colonel Tordman still came out to greet them at once. "Master Jace Sesso is back!"

Ever since Illgynoth Tower had fallen into Jace's hands, Tordman had visibly put on quite a few circles. It was probably because the area around Stonewatch had settled into peace, but the supply lines from Lakeshire hadn't been reduced at all on account of Stonewatch's importance to the whole of Lake Everstill. Not only him — the soldiers of Stonewatch all looked more spirited too.

Of course, Jace remembered the dish the back kitchen loved — mashed potatoes topped with meat stuffing and baked with cheese — and reckoned it was no surprise the soldiers raised on that philosophy would soon start expanding.

"Colonel." Jace nodded slightly.

"Lady Sola." Tordman nodded respectfully. "Last time you visited us, it seemed as though the flowers we'd planted at Stonewatch bloomed even more abundantly. Quite a few soldiers have been looking forward to your next visit."

"This time I'm afraid I'll have to leave a little quickly, Colonel Tordman." Sola smiled.

"What a pity. But being an advisor to the archmages, having a lot of business is only natural. Just like your friend here, Master Sesso — the master of Illgynoth Tower who's almost never seen." Colonel Tordman laughed a few times.

He looked at Jace and said, "So what brings the two of you back to Stonewatch this time? I remember you went back to report to the king before, Master Sesso — that was barely a week ago. Did you forget something?"

"It's still that matter — the one related to that Alteraci princess."

As he said this, Jace followed Tordman with Sola inside Stonewatch's walls, speaking as he walked. "That princess commissioned me to go to Blackrock Mountain and investigate the whereabouts of her missing father. I thought an investigation of the whole of Blackrock Mountain would also help defend against the orcs there, and the black dragons who rule them, from invading. It would benefit both Stonewatch and the entire kingdom, so I didn't refuse."

"So that's how it is." Colonel Tordman stroked the curly beard on his cheek. "Is there anything Stonewatch can help with?"

"I'd like to borrow two orc warg saddles. I remember Stonewatch had a lot of supplies the orcs didn't manage to take when they fled. Did you throw all that stuff away?" Jace asked.

"I'd love to throw it away," Tordman sighed. "Just guarding all that stuff costs us no small amount of money and effort. We have plenty of orc warg saddles here. Do you want black-fur cowhide, or bearskin, or big Bloodtusk boar-hide?"

"Black-fur cow?" Jace asked. "What's that?"

"You're asking me? Nobody here knows either." Tordman shrugged. "The orcs we captured said those especially thick, heavy saddles were all made from the hide of a particular kind of cow from a place called Nagrand. They didn't bring this cow over here, so none of us have ever seen what it actually looks like. Come with me and you'll see."

The moment Tordman mentioned Nagrand, Jace almost immediately knew what kind of cow this was.

It was the clefthoof — a colossal beast of Draenor. Jace had seen it in orcish books. Because its build was truly enormous and its hide extremely thick, many orcish clans even used its hide to make armor.

Following Tordman to the armory basement of Stonewatch, the colonel brought them to a room at the end of the corridor that clearly wasn't visited often. Tordman took a heavy black-fur saddle off a wooden rack and said, "Take a look — something brought over from the other side of the Dark Portal. I can't see any difference. It's just like those orcs — once you chop them dead and they lie still, they all look about the same."

Jace looked at the saddle, which was bound with several ropes of straps. The straps were hung with all kinds of bone segments, and these bone segments were even inscribed with Orcish characters, most related to battle, victory, and blessings from ancestors.

This was definitely the saddle of an orc officer of considerable standing — perhaps even a commander. Another, slightly lighter-colored clefthoothide saddle, though not quite up to this one, also had special adornments.

It wasn't hard to guess that probably only orcs of high status could possess such a saddle.

"Do you want this one?" Tordman said. "If you two want them, we can give them to you for free — at least better than throwing them away."

"No." Jace shook his head. "I want the Bloodtusk boar-hide ones."

"Huh?" Colonel Tordman blinked. "Those are not only ugly, they've got a strange smell. Those orcs definitely didn't process them properly."

"I want those ones," Jace said.

"No problem at all, as long as you like them, Master Sesso." Tordman rolled up his sleeves, moved the rack here to the side, and squeezed inside to haul out two light-brown saddles.

These two saddles looked much cruder. Quite a few bristles were scattered carelessly along the edges, and just as Tordman had said, they carried a strange smell that was hard to tell came from the boar or the orc — one that could still be clearly detected even after being stored in the warehouse for so long.

Jace and Tordman both frowned slightly, but Sola took one of them without showing any change in expression. Tordman glanced at Jace with slightly widened eyes, as if to say "an advisor to a mage really is something else."

Coming outside the door, Colonel Tordman stared at the saddles in the two of them's hands and said, "I still can't help asking — what are you two going to do with these warg saddles?"

Jace looked at Sola. Before he could speak, Tordman immediately said, "You're not planning to disguise yourselves as female orcs and sneak into Blackrock Mountain, are you? Hah, I'll say — Jace Sesso's height, with your face painted, might just about pass. But Lady Sola, forget it. You absolutely could not be taken for a female orc. Your height may certainly not be considered short among our women, but put you among orcs and you'd be the kind they'd throw into the wilderness as a deformed runt — no way you'd be trained into a warrior…"

Watching the two of them staring at him with serious expressions, the joking look on Tordman's face gradually cooled. Then he said, "No way. You really are going to disguise yourselves as orcs?"

"Do you have any other good suggestions, Colonel?" Jace asked. "Besides the height."

"We still have some orc scout clothing. You might find it useful?" Tordman asked. "We use magic," Jace reminded him.

"I'd like to take a look," Sola said.

"Ah, Lady Sola needs some — come this way." Tordman said.

After entering the room where orc clothing and armor were stored, Sola carefully examined the garments piece by piece. Tordman leaned over to Jace and said, "Master Sesso, if Illgynoth Tower is going to be renovated, I'm worried the gnolls will seize the chance to make a comeback. I've already sent a few capable lads over to patrol and protect the Stonemasons' fellows, but I still want to know when the renovation can be completed. These past few days since you left, I've been on edge the whole time."

"Don't worry too much, Colonel," Jace said. "I've already set two hellhounds to patrol the forest and protect that tower. Never mind hiding in the dark — even if they're besieged, one of them handling seven or eight gnolls is no problem at all. And compared to ordinary grunts, they're especially good at dealing with those dark mages who can cast spells — and the gnolls who've had contact with orcs know this full well. When those dark mages plot a raid, they have to consider whether they'll die faster than their own underlings."

"Those hellhounds from before?" Tordman nodded. "That puts my mind at ease now."

"The only thing to worry about is the soldiers you've sent into the forest mistaking the hellhounds for stray demons and launching an attack," Jace reminded him.

"I'll give them a thorough briefing." Tordman nodded. "But those demons won't hurt my soldiers, will they?" Jace smiled. "They're smarter than you think. After all, they're not real dogs — they're demons."

"Strange enough — I actually feel at ease because there are demons patrolling outside the walls," Tordman sighed.

"You're not the first to say that," Jace said.

Tordman cleared his throat and tried asking, "So that night… I mean the night a few years ago when you were staying at Stonewatch and ran into the gnolls — the black magic that burned that sword, that wasn't the gnolls' dark mages, was it, Master Sesso?"

Jace raised his eyebrows slightly and said nothing. Just then, Sola arrived before them at exactly the right moment. "I think I've seen enough, Colonel."

"Then that'll be all, Colonel." Jace straightened up, holding his cloak against his chest as he looked at Tordman. "We won't take up any more of your time. Hope to see you again soon."

"Of course." The colonel inclined his body. "Stonewatch will always welcome you, Master — and Lady Sola."

On the way out of Stonewatch, Jace asked Sola with some curiosity, "What did you even do when you came to Stonewatch that day?"

Sola said, "Nothing much. They were curious about me helping the archmage with research and wanted to know what I usually do, so I told them a lot of stories about Kalimdor."

"No wonder," Jace said.
