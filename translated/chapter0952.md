## Chapter 952: Mok'Noloshin—Ka!

Following the mountain path between Stonewatch and Lakeshire northwards, after walking for roughly the day they entered a canyon.

In the game this was called the Stoneford Pass, because by that time this canyon was occupied by Blackrock orcs. After seizing Stonewatch they had taken control of the road and forest from here to Stonewatch, opened a corridor linking to Blackrock Spire, and posed a considerable threat to Lakeshire below the mountains. At the same time, the gnolls who had once been driven into the mountains by Stormwind's army had also seized the momentum of the orc advance to pour out of the hills, joining the orcs to form a pincer and besieging Lakeshire from different directions, nearly isolating the town.

In the end, when Stormwind and Lakeshire needed to communicate, they had to entrust daring mercenaries and adventurers to deliver messages. Quite a few travelers and messengers were intercepted and killed along the way by gnolls, orcs, even the black drakes raised by the orcs.

In the final phase of the Orc Wars, when the combined armies of Lordaeron and Khaz Modan arrived at Blackrock Mountain, the orcs preparing to make their last stand in Stormwind had built massive fortifications in this canyon — including ballistae, spike-tipped crenellated walls, catapults, and so on.

Some of these had been destroyed by the army led by Turalyon during the war, but many had yet to be cleared away by the orcs and humans who dared to come here after the war, standing there in a posture of readiness for battle at any moment.

The entire canyon reeked of the char drifting over from the Burning Steppes. Combined with these discarded relics of war, plus the occasionally glimpsed mutilated remains of unknown races — corpses that had been gnawed beyond recognition by various unnamed beasts, with no complete ones to be seen —

Though Jace had already entered the Burning Steppes more than once before, when he witnessed these testimonies of brutal war with his own eyes, his heart still stirred with a different kind of shock.

Raising his head, the ashen sky veiled by thick dust between the gray-brown mountain ridges made Jace nearly forget that just that morning he had been strolling through the birdsong-and-blossom forests on the shores of Lake Everstill.

Sola had been silent ever since entering this valley, her gaze never leaving these things for a moment. "Master." Molophil caught up and said, "We've got company."

Jace and Sola looked back. In the deep ravine of the shadows ahead, almost nothing could be made out.

"That way… Master, that way, look." Sazeno poked his head out of the bag and pointed at a height above.

Jace looked over and glimpsed an inconspicuous dark figure on the cliff face, seemingly watching this direction.

On the road behind them, among the damaged, toppled charred timber and bent metal frames, several beasts were also quietly stepping over obstacles toward this direction. When they reached a distance just close enough to see clearly, they crouched or stood, stopping in their tracks to stare.

They were several times larger than the wolves Jace had seen in Elwynn Forest. Though called wolves, they walked almost exactly like stocky black-furred ponies.

"Those are the orcs' wargs?" Sola asked.

Jace said, "Yes. I knew they'd be wandering near the battlefield. We don't need to go looking for them — they came to us. Let's pick up the pace and move ahead, make them think we're easy prey and come a little closer. You can try communicating with them, see if they're willing to listen to you, Sola."

"All right." the green dragon said.

With that, the three of them walked toward a black giant gate, only a faint shadow visible in the dust, in the distance ahead. But the wargs seemed still quite cautious, always keeping roughly the same distance without rashly closing in, as though they'd sensed these two travelers had some purpose.

The sky gradually darkened. The giant gate the orcs had built of blackrock and metal spikes drew ever closer ahead, yet the wargs still followed behind with great patience, as if vowed to wait until these two travelers were exhausted beyond measure before launching their attack.

Glowing with an evil red light in the darkness, those pairs of eyes suddenly gave Jace an idea.

As a skilled warlock who manipulated emotions, someone who needed to channel emotions to cast spells naturally also knew how to release the Shadow energy carried by emotions without casting.

He looked at those giant wolves and tried to stir up the fear within himself, making use of those terrible endings he'd once imagined, slowly closing his eyes.

Suddenly, a low wolf howl came from the distance. From the charred bushes and toppled war wagons in the ravine, seven or eight tall wargs emerged from God knew where, all charging straight in this direction following a lead warg.

"What did you do…" Sola looked at Jace as he opened his eyes again, startled.

"These wargs have followed the orcs for far too long — they've even learned to read people's expressions with Shadow energy. This skill is stronger than most warlocks in the Alliance," Jace said, staring at the wargs rapidly closing in. "But there are really too many of them. We only need… two."

When the wargs closed to within a few dozen meters, Jace suddenly intoned the language of the elements, calling upon the aid of flame. On this scorched earth of the Burning Steppes, flame came faster than anywhere else, immediately swirling into a violent firestorm at Jace's direction, surrounding Jace, Sola, and those seven or eight wargs in the middle.

"Burn!"

The implet caught sight of the flames and laughed at once — a laugh so piercing it made the fire seem all the more abrupt, piercing, and sinister…

The Shadowflame came so fast the wargs couldn't even escape in time. Seeing themselves surrounded by flames, they hesitated and halted their charge. Then, looking at Jace before them, the aura of fear had already vanished entirely.

Jace didn't know if they'd realized they'd been tricked. It was just that without any fear at all, all that remained was rage burning ever brighter. Amid the crackling of the flames scorching everything around them, these fearsome beasts from another world grew enraged, their bristles rapidly puffing up — looking as though they were ready to fight to the death.

Truly war beasts tempered by countless trials of blood and fire on Draenor — even facing Shadowflame, even facing a demon covered in fel runes right before them, they showed not the slightest intention of retreating.

Sola was chanting something in the dragon tongue to the side, but after a few lines she sighed. "They won't listen to a single word I say."

"I'm not surprised," Jace said. Then he spoke in the language of the elements: "Press in."

The wargs charged at the three of them together. The cyclone of flame also closed inward at the same time. Huge black and gray wargs surged forward one after another through waves of surging fire and scorching heat. The first vicious wolf to reach Jace was of an enormous build — practically a brown bear — dragging mist and flame across its body as though it had just pounced out of hell.

Yet before it could touch the warlock, it was struck squarely in the face by a hoof from Molophil and flipped to one side.

The succubus steadied herself and said, "They're very hard for me to dominate mentally right now, Master. Their will needs to be broken first."

A warg beside them charged to within a few meters, only to be tangled up thoroughly by charred thorns bursting from the ground and thrown to the ground. Sola looked at this maddened beast and asked, "What do we do now?"

Jace clenched his fists, and the flames in his hands were snuffed out by the squeeze, leaving only wisps of thick smoke seeping between his fingers. When he spread his fingers again, a dark, purple-glowing Shadow power had replaced the ash and smoke.

"Fek'th."

Jace pointed at the warg tangled in thorns and thrashing wildly, casting Fear. The warg's eyes shifted at once — from a furious roar to a whimpering of terror — then it strained to shrink back, trying to break free.

When Molophil looked at it again, that warg immediately settled down, let out a long breath, and lay prostrate on the ground.

By now most of the other wargs had been burned to death. Two more were trapped in the flames looking for a chance to attack. Jace pointed at them through the leaping flames and cast Fear again. One of them was hit by Fear and scared stiff on the spot. The other lost control, turned, and fled back out — only to be quickly swallowed by the flames, leaving behind nothing but helpless howls of agony.

Jace dismissed the fire elements. The cyclone of flame immediately scattered to all sides as though hit by a strong wind, leaving only drifting smoke that merged with the ash haze found everywhere in the Burning Steppes.

Before their eyes remained a single solitary black warg, trembling under the effect of Fear as it stared at the demon before him. In the next second he too was smoothly Dominated and lay prostrate, well-behaved.

"A little more troublesome than expected," Jace said.

"Mm." Sola nodded. "Even though they won't listen to a single word I say, I could feel their loyalty to the orcs, as though under some kind of curse — even if they've been abandoned here to go hungry."

Sazeno gripped the edge of the bag and looked down at the warg that had just freed itself from the thorns. "Clearly, these wargs have been infected by the power of demons, Lady Somnilith."

Molophil said, "After orcs drink demon blood, their passion for war… and the feelings for their own mounts, will little by little seep into the consciousness of these beasts, causing them to gradually 'demonize' as well. The blood of a pit lord has exactly this kind of power. Even the blood of the Eredar couldn't bring such pure fervor — otherwise Kil'jaeden wouldn't use this method again and again to transform countless races in the dark void into servants of the Legion."

"So that's why," Sola nodded.

"Of course, no matter how deep the infection, it can't stand in the way of the Master's magic." Molophil said with a smile.

Have to say, Molophil's technique of setting up a compliment with a lead-in still had its own style. Jace covered his mouth and coughed. "Let's take these two wargs and leave here as quickly as possible, to avoid running into more trouble. I'm worried the firestorm I summoned might attract the attention of nearby drakes."

The two of them fastened saddles onto the wargs. Jace looked toward the gate ahead and said, "Right — ahead is the Burning Steppes. Soon we'll be entering orc territory. Since we have wargs now, let's change into orc form and keep going."

"All right," Sola said.

"Vaelastraszanoth." Jace intoned the polymorph spell Vaelastrasz had told him. The black dragonhide cloak he wore immediately burst into a ball of flame that wrapped around him.

When the flames dispersed, Jace had become a fairly sturdy Blackrock orc. "This cloak Lord Vaelastrasz gave you?" Sola asked, somewhat surprised.

"That's right." Jace sighed with emotion. "This fellow even managed to use his name as an incantation…" His voice had now become rough and coarse too, very much like the howling of those wargs. Sola smiled. "Fits my impression of the red dragons. My turn next."

With that, she too intoned a spell in a low voice. In a hazy dreamlike atmosphere that enveloped everything, Sola's human form in the shadows swelled slightly. As the dream-veil faded, what stood in its place was a muscular, powerfully built female orc.

Jace noticed that the clothes she wore weren't the simple leather garments of the female orc they'd seen in the prison, but in the style of the scout clothing from Stonewatch — this made it all the more convincing.

"How's this?" the female orc asked, turning in a circle.

Jace raised a stony brow-ridge and said, "Watch your movements. I don't think a female orc warrior would turn in a circle for people to see how she looks."

"But I don't know how female orcs normally move," Sola said.

"Then do it the way you imagine a black dragon disguised as a human would move," Jace said. Sola thought it over, then slapped her chest. "Behold my new form, mortal."

Jace let out a grunt in his thick voice. "Not bad, not bad. You've got the feel of it. When you see those orcs, just stare at them with the gaze of a black dragon observing its prey."

"Got it," Sola nodded.

"Then let's move out." Jace slapped his heavy hand, raised his fist, and shouted: "Mok'noloshin—ka! (Engage the enemy!)" Hearing this Orcish phrase, even the wargs that had been lying well-behaved immediately grew excited, growling low.

Molophil gave Jace a look of resignation and whispered to the imp who was shaking his head from side to side from the warlock's violent movements: "He's getting into character again…"
