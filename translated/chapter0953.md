## Chapter 953: The Road to Blackrock

The places Jace had been in the Burning Steppes before — whether crossing through the Ashvale Canyon to the east to reach the Thaurissan ruins, or skirting the western mountains to sneak toward the Altar of Storms — were all out-of-the-way spots that bypassed the endless blackness of the plain in between.

He had never walked straight into the heart of the plains through this canyon from the Redridge Mountains — the very place where the Blackrock orcs and the Alliance armies clashed.

Two solitary orc riders sat steady on wargs' backs, picking their way across this scorched earth. Jace kept his eyes fixed ahead. Sola watched the surroundings vigilantly — the closer they drew, the more uneasy she seemed.

"I've never been to a place like this, Jace," Sola said, looking at a withered old tree stump beside them. "The earth seems dead. There's scarcely any scent of life in the air, and the few surviving creatures seem to have had their minds utterly corrupted by the darkness here, pouring out the ravings of madness beyond comprehension. Even in the vault of Secarus, at least the tree roots that bored through the walls and the shrubs struggling to survive the winter in the outer cracks still radiated an unyielding, tenacious vitality."

Though Sola wore the form of a female orc, when she spoke her tusks wobbled with her lips, occasionally even nudging them. But her tone, her manner, the expression on her face — none of these combined easily with what a real orc should be.

Still, there were no other orcs around at the moment, so Jace couldn't be bothered to remind her.

"That said, the reason the guardian Secarus built that vault was to seal Urasseous inside, Sola." He shook the staff wrapped in fel-cloth that he was carrying. "But the dwarves summoned the Firelord precisely to unleash his power and destroy their enemies — those cousins to the north."

"This is like a scar on Azeroth," Sola said, staring ahead. "No — not a scar. This is a wound torn open and grievously infected. I can see no hope of it healing."

"It's not so bad in its current state." Molophil, sitting in Jace's arms, glanced at the Blackrock Mountain ahead — through the dust haze, the lava rivers streaked across the mountainsurface like cracks.

She looked at Sola again and said, "A place always has to be occupied by some power or another. If that power can be the power of life, why can't it be the elementals of fire, or Shadow?"

Sola didn't answer her question directly. Instead she said, "We can be friends, Molophil, but there's no need to agree on every topic. You were born of the Twisting Nether, while I grew from here. It's only natural our views differ."

Molophil, surprisingly, didn't retort or mock either. She just nodded at the green dragon in her female orc guise.

Probably a demon long accustomed to arguing with everyone in the group would also find it hard to adjust to a green dragon with such a good temper.

"What does Blackrock Mountain look like in the Emerald Dream?" Jace asked.

Sola lowered her gaze, thought it over as she looked at the warg's back beneath her, and said, "There's no volcano like Blackrock Mountain in the Emerald Dream. My impression is that this was a place alternating between great forests and grasslands, with a great river running through, the bare red peaks of the Redridge Mountains setting off the green below… It reminded me of the Hillsbrighde outside Dalaran, but with more sunshine."

"Just hearing you describe it, it must be beautiful," Jace said. "Perhaps before Blackrock Mountain erupted, its appearance was very much like the Redridge Mountains in the Emerald Dream. You said the Emerald Dream is Azeroth as it's been untouched by civilization. My impression is that before Blackrock Mountain, dwarves and humans had scarcely set foot here. That area to the east was once ruled by a human family. Two hundred years have passed, and the people of that family still haven't given up reclaiming it from the orcs, ogres, or dwarves. Just imagine what this place once looked like, and you can more or less understand them."

"I can't sense people's emotions with Shadow power the way you warlocks do," Sola looked over. "But even though you're in orc form right now, I can feel the complicated feelings you hold for this land — very hard to describe, like a kind of… attachment from somewhere unknown."

Jace looked at her, his heart giving a start. Was she referring to… things from his past life?

If an ordinary person had said this, Jace wouldn't have been so shaken.

But even though Sola had almost never left the Emerald Dream and didn't understand humans, she was, after all, a dragon — and a green dragon, a guardian connected to dreams and illusions.

"I read about Blackrock Mountain's past in a book a long time ago. Perhaps I felt a little yearning," Jace said, lowering his heavy orc head. "But it's too late now. The whole place is ruined — just like you said, beyond healing. It's probably just that I came here, set foot on this land, and your saying that stirred up some memories."

Sola nodded thoughtfully. For a moment Jace didn't even know what he was talking about anymore — Blackrock Mountain, or something else.

Then, amid the rumbling of the distant mountains, a somewhat faint but slightly abrupt sound of a horn suddenly mixed in.

The sound reminded Jace of the orcish horns in Grim Batol, but it was slightly different — probably drawing close to the Blackrock orcs' fortress.

"Get into the state I told you about earlier, Sola. We may be about to see orcs," Jace reminded her. "All right." Sola responded, gripping the saddle.

Moving forward, the dwarven ruins on the ground and in the surrounding field of vision grew more numerous.

If Jace remembered correctly, the Thaurissan ruins should be somewhere to the northeast from here. The haunted city's silhouette couldn't be seen from here, but he could roughly judge that this area should lie between the Thaurisson ruins and Blackrock Mountain. In the past it was probably a town on the road from the capital Thaurissan to the altar of Blackrock Mountain. Following the eruption of Blackrock Mountain, it had been destroyed along with it.

By now the wargs' paws began stepping on flagstones from time to time, making a clicking sound. Jace took out the map Adrian Morgan had given him and felt they had arrived on the main road Adrian had advised them against taking.

Beside the road, a fire pit made of extinguished rubble, wood, weapons, and bones was smoking, giving off a choking smell of burning.

The smell came from the things speared on the tips of those weapons. From a distance they couldn't be made out clearly, but up close they were finally visible — charred, shriveled heads, human for the most part, and mostly dwarven.

Behind the fire pit, an orcish hide flag flapped wildly in the wind — the Blackrock orcs' fang-and-black-mountain totem, bound to a long-handled blade stained black with blood, displaying its might to every traveler approaching by the roadside.

The further they walked toward Blackrock Mountain, the more common these roadside fire pits became — like some kind of streetlamp, or markers of the Blackrock tribe's sovereignty.

When the sky seemed to be growing darker, they encountered the first group of orcs since arriving in the Burning Steppes.

It was a five-orc patrol, riding light-brown or black wargs, slowly approaching from not far away. The Blackrock orc at the front, wearing spiky reddish-brown metal pauldrons, was clearly an officer of considerably high rank.

Jace had briefly learned about orcish military ranks at Grim Batol. When the Dragonmaw and Blackrock orcs unified their ranks under Warchief Blackhand, the system had undergone standardization. It differed slightly in the details from the Horde ranks he'd known in his past life's game, but the general hierarchy was about the same, so he remembered it fairly smoothly.

"That looks like a high-ranking officer. I can't tell his exact rank, but he should be a blood guard. You should salute him and address him as 'sir.'"

Jace turned his head and quietly explained a few common Orcish phrases to the Sola beside him. Sola nodded, and the cavalry drew up before them.

"Sir." Sola thumped her chest in Orcish.

The officer glanced at her and said nothing, then looked over this way.

Seeing Jace and the succubus sitting sideways in his arms, he tilted his head. The expression visible through the gaps in his face-guard was clearly complicated.

"Hello, big fellow." Molophil waved and narrowed her eyes.

An orc lieutenant behind them couldn't help but slap his helmet. Clearly, the orcs here knew what kind of demon a succubus was, and they all knew the terrifying influence of their mental magic.

"Hello, soldier." Jace greeted him too.

"Warlock and demon." The orc muttered two phrases, then waved to the soldiers behind him, signaling everyone to hurry along.

The orc cavalry behind them didn't spare Sola a single glance. They hurried to follow their officer's footsteps while deliberately avoiding Jace's gaze.

After they had walked far enough that the dust covered the wargs' shadows, Sola said, "These orcs are so rude to an orc like you, yet they also seem afraid of you."

Molophil said, "That means the Master's skin is very convincing."

"Indeed." Jace snorted a laugh. "Although the Blackrock orcs have taken back the warlocks who fled from the Shadow Council, when all's said and done, the Horde's collapse had everything to do with Gul'dan and his disciples. So to this day, these orcs still see warlocks as potential traitors and dangerous elements. But at the same time they fear our dark magic. So this counts as an advantage too — the more they don't want to deal with us, the less likely we are to be exposed, isn't that right?

"'We'?" Sola asked with a smile.

"They." Jace corrected himself. "After all, right now I need to feel immersed, Sola — you'd best be too. Try your hardest to sink into your orc role. We're going to see more and more orcs from here on."

"Zog—zog." Sola mimicked Jace's cadence in response.

"Good, scout. Maintain." Jace said in Orcish, straightened up, and patted the thick neck fur of the warg, signaling it to move on.
