Chapter 954: Run, Hirt!

Just as Adrian had said, the closer they drew to Blackrock Mountain, the more orc patrols crowded the highway.

Most of them rode wargs of varying sizes and coat colors along the road. The occasional ones on foot were mostly soldiers from nearby legions.

And these soldiers all wore highly distinctive armor and carried beast-hide banners that proclaimed their legion's identity — impossible to pass off as one's own. The most common sight was a banner depicting a shield split by an axe; that must have been the emblem of the "Shattered Shield Legion" the orcs kept mentioning.

Much like the previous band of orcs' reaction to Jace, whether they were scouts or patrols from anywhere, no matter how warm or cold their greetings were when they ran into each other — even if they stopped to chat — once they caught sight of Jace they either fell silent or, if they were too far away to avoid it, called out a reluctant "warlock."

Sola basked in the reflected glow; not a single orc dared say much to this female bodyguard trailing behind a warlock. Even though by her gear she was no more than a lowly scout, no orc dared cast her the kind of dismissive glance they gave other scouts.

So the two of them were happy to enjoy the peace and quiet, riding their wargs at an easy pace up the road. Watching the high slope leading to Blackrock Mountain draw closer, this trip seemed to be going rather smoothly.

That night they rested beside the road in a cluster of abandoned dwarf tents. Jace had wanted to get proper rest for the next day's trek, but with orc bands passing by every so often he couldn't relax his guard. In the end, all he could do was watch the passing orcs as they trudged by.

Just when the monotony of soldier after soldier was starting to make his eyes droop, a distant rumble of wheels grinding over stone slabs crept in — "grumble, grumble."

He looked toward the firelit road. A prison wagon drawn by four wargs was rolling into view. When it drew close enough, Jace could make out the prisoners inside — a collection of feeble, haggard Dark Iron dwarves.

Jace weighed whether he should rescue one of them to serve as a guide into Shadowforge City.

But on second thought, letting a dwarf know that he and Sola were in orc disguise didn't seem like a great idea. Especially letting a stranger know that Sola was a spellcaster who could shapeshift at will — that risk was even greater than scouting his own way into Shadowforge City.

Still, as he watched and mulled it over, he suddenly realized there was a human in that cage. And he was alive.

The man was slumped against the back of the prison bars, staring helplessly down the road behind them. He was already two or three days' ride from the Stormwind Kingdom's border — and getting farther. Once he was dragged into the orc-held Blackrock Spire, god only knew what horrible fate awaited him.

But the more Jace looked at him, the more the fellow seemed familiar.

Especially that set of brown, filthy clothes, the head of black hair, and a face so ordinary he could've been a lost hunter. It hit Jace all at once.

Wasn't this Neden Hirt — the SI:7 agent who'd brought him into the intelligence agency? How the hell had the orcs gotten their hands on him?

Though Jace couldn't be absolutely certain he wasn't misremembering, he still stood up and barked a command in Orcish — a shout used to drive wargs: "Stop!"

At Jace's roar the wargs hesitated and slowed. The wagon let out a series of creaks and ground to a halt.

The orc driver was clearly annoyed. He hopped down and was just about to blow his top when he caught sight of who had shouted and swallowed his anger.

Several orc laborers sleeping in a tent nearby lifted their heads with irritated glares at Jace's yell, but when they caught sight of his warlock robes and staff, they exchanged glances and washed down their curses and complaints with a gulp of saliva.

"I'll go have a look, Sola." Jace clenched his fists and walked with an unsteady gait over to the prison wagon, studying the prisoners inside. The Dark Iron dwarves cowered at the back of the cage as Jace approached, hurling Dwarvish abuse at each other and grabbing at each other's hair and beards trying to hide.

"What do you think you're doing, warlock?" the orc driver asked cautiously. The soldiers on the wagon were all peering over this way.

"Whose prisoners are these?" Jace asked.

The orc puffed up his chest with pride. "These men are going to be thrown into the Blackrock Spire arena to face the Bloodaxe Legion's champion, Huck Bashgud, to prove his strength and pay tribute to the Warchief and his esteemed advisor, Lord Victor Nefarius."

"Including that scrawny human? He's going to prove a legion champion's might?" Jace asked.

"Ah, that human." The orc glanced at him. "He was skulking around the Bloodaxe Legion's fortress, thinking his disguise was flawless. But he never stood a chance against a whelp's eyes. He's definitely an Alliance scout. He's got no right to duel our champion. When the time comes, he'll be thrown into the warg pit and served up to our loyal battle-mates."

Now Jace was sure. An intelligence agent operating in the Burning Steppes, someone he recognized — this had to be Neden Hirt.

He'd been working under Mathias Shaw at SI:7 handling the black dragon problem. Looks like the dragonkin matter had wound down for the moment, so he'd come out here to run reconnaissance.

"What a waste," Jace said, stroking his beard. "We could have tried to squeeze some information out of him about the human king — if he really is a spy."

"The Bloodaxe Legion's sergeant already interrogated him inside the fortress, but couldn't get a thing out of him. Push any further and he wouldn't have a life left to lose. Anyway, wargs don't like corpses — they prefer their meals alive."

"You don't need to explain warg preferences to me." Jace shot him a glare.

The orc moved his lips but didn't dare speak. Inside the cage, Neden Hirt coughed and said in Common: "Just kill me."

"He can't understand a word you're saying — your defiance is pretty pointless." Jace smiled and switched to Common. "And you're not going to die that quickly, human. You have a more important use."

"I'll hand him over to Lord Nefarius," Jace said in Orcish, turning to the orc soldier. "The Warchief's advisor doesn't need a prisoner to open his mouth — he can learn everything he wants straight from the man's soul."

"True enough." The orc glanced at the cage. "We can hand him over to Lord Nefarius. But there's no need for you to be the one to deliver him, warlock. What, you planning to curry favor with the Warchief and his advisor using our catch?"

"I'm afraid Lord Nefarius doesn't have the leisure to deal with a living, breathing mortal. His time has always been far too precious." Jace said, "If you knew a lick of soul magic, you'd know it's far easier to see into a man's true nature through the fragments of his soul. And my servant…"

"That would be me." Molophil had crept up behind the orc at some point and spoke softly. "I specialize in extracting the souls of pitiful mortals for my master."

"Think about it — you drag this filthy human all the way up to Blackrock Spire, right in front of Lord Nefarius. Now think about what he'll do with you." Jace said.

The orc stared blankly at Jace, then at the human. Molophil smiled and sidled up to Jace, leaning against his broad shoulder and swishing her long tail back and forth.

"You've got a point, warlock. Now that you mention it, I'm not so keen on handling this human myself." With that, the orc fished a key out of his coat. An orc soldier up front said, "The Warg Master won't be happy. The Bloodaxe Legion already promised him live prisoners for feed, sir."

"Send over that traitor we caught last week. Besides, there are plenty of dwarves here — much sturdier meat."

The orc officer waved his hand, opened the cage door, dragged the human out, and flung him to the ground. The Dark Iron dwarves inside couldn't help but snicker, chattering in Dwarvish about the miserable fate awaiting this human now that he'd fallen into a warlock's hands.

"I will offer up his soul to Lord Nefarius in the name of the Bloodaxe Legion, soldier," Jace said.

"You'd better, warlock." The orc pointed at Jace. "I hope at the next gladiatorial gathering, I hear the Warchief and his advisor praising the Bloodaxe Legion's contribution."

"I hope that day comes soon." Jace nodded.

With that, the orc walked to the front, mounted his warg, and led the prison wagon away down the road. Jace muttered under his breath: "Or never, for all I care."

Molophil gave a soft laugh in Demonic: "These orc soldiers have weaker wills than wargs."

"Did you hear what he said? 'Traitor.'" Jace hauled the captured scout up and spoke in Demonic. "There are still plenty of orcs in Blackrock Spire who don't accept black dragon rule. A little wavering in their loyalties is only natural. But wargs have simple minds — they're loyal to orcs, period. That's a lot harder to shake."

"Come with me, human," he whispered in Common into the man's ear.

"I won't tell you anything. Kill me!" Hirt roared.

Jace grabbed the chain binding the man's ankles and dragged him. Hirt screamed curses behind him but was just as powerless as he was hauled all the way to an abandoned dwarf ruin off the path.

"I love your fighting spirit." Jace tossed him forward. "Now run. Let me feel the tenacity in your soul — that'll make the soul shard I extract all the more potent."

"You want to toy with me like a dog?" The agent sat down right where he was with a cold smile. "If you're going to do it, then do it!"

Jace was caught flat-footed. He could only look at Molophil and say: "Let's skip the theatrics. Give me a hand."

Molophil sighed and reached out her eyes. The flames in her eyes began to burn bright.

"Run from here, Mr. Hirt. Make it back to Lakeshire. Let's see what you've got… and try not to get caught by whelps this time."

Hirt shot to his feet as if he'd never been tortured in his life, standing bolt upright. "Yes, my lord. Yes, my gracious queen. I will give everything I have! There is absolutely no way I'll be caught again! Last time was just... a ridiculous mistake! I'll prove it to you!"

With that, he checked the movement outside the ruins, then slipped away quietly. Before he left, he even saluted Molophil with an SI:7 hand sign.

"Let's go." Jace waved them on.

Molophil said, "You know, I've never actually heard that extracting soul shards makes it easier to see into someone's true nature… after all, those aren't soul-stone prisons. Just imagine — if every piece of casting material in your bag had a will of its own. How terrifying would that be?"

She said carefully, "I don't mean to question you, dear master. Which demon did you hear that from? Some Nathrezim?"

"I made it up on the spot," Jace said.

"Ah..." Molophil couldn't help but laugh and fell into step behind Jace.
