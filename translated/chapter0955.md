## Chapter 955: The Dark Iron's Creation

After who-knew-how-long trudging through the Burning Steppes, Jace would rest when tired and move on when awake. Day and night had long since blurred together here, and the closer they got to Blackrock Mountain, the harder it became to tell them apart.

The first time he'd come to the Burning Steppes, back at the ruins of Thaurissan, Jace could still tell day from night by the faint light filtering through the clouds and the general feel of the plains. But deep in the heart of the Burning Steppes, the only way to keep track of time was a pocket watch. Even then, after walking for ages without checking it, he couldn't tell whether the numbers meant morning or afternoon. Soon enough, he lost all sense of time entirely.

Blackrock Mountain loomed at the edge of his vision, its peak forever belching thick black smoke — the source of the sky-obscuring haze. The molten lava, flickering with an evil red glow, had become the only light source in the entire Burning Steppes, a bloody sun.

After three or four interrupted stretches of sleep, Jace finally noticed the road ahead starting to rise, angling upward toward the heights.

The foot of Blackrock Mountain. Finally.

Without realizing it, the number of orcs on the road had multiplied beyond counting. Here Jace even encountered orc warlocks, many of them accompanied by imp servants — some trailing behind, others perched on the orcs' broad shoulders.

Occasionally he spotted succubi or even demon guards as attendants. The warlock who commanded a demon guard was noticeably more arrogant; with a demon guard taller than a wolf rider by his side, he looked down on everything.

But when he saw Jace, a flicker of caution crossed his eyes.

This wasn't special treatment for Jace. Most of the orc warlocks he'd met on this journey didn't talk to each other, except those who'd been traveling together from the start.

From the letters and records left behind by some orcs, Jace could piece it together: when Orgrim Doomhammer had purged the Shadow Council, some warlocks had joined the hunt against their own Shadow Council colleagues, hoping to survive the purge by participating in it.

Some of those warlocks later accepted necromantic magic and became death knights, trading their battle prowess for Orgrim's and the Horde's trust as the war grew more desperate. The rest were exiled or thrown into prison, counting themselves lucky to have kept their lives.

After the Alliance's final victory, those who hadn't managed to flee through the Dark Portal stayed behind. Many of them had come to Blackrock Spire seeking to join the Blackhand brothers' Horde.

Which meant the warlocks in Blackrock Spire right now weren't all on the same side. They'd betrayed and slaughtered each other before. And when meeting a strange warlock, nobody knew what that person had done to the Horde — or to them.

Compared to the newcomers from the Bleeding Hollow or Dragonmaw clans, the Shadow Council defectors were a much more complicated mix.

Aside from those occasional warlocks, the most noticeable change was the shrinking proportion of soldiers. Deep green-skinned and black-skinned peons now made up a significant portion of the population.

Jace had already seen some orc peons back in Grim Batol, but the peons there hadn't seemed much different from soldiers. Here, though, the peons were visibly thinner and more timid. Even when soldiers openly bullied and shoved them, they only dared to bare their teeth and growl a bit, never putting up any real resistance.

Thinking back, the Dragonmaw orcs of Grim Batol had probably lost a lot of peons, or maybe never had the chance to bring many when they seized the place. They'd had to make soldiers do the labor. That's why their facilities were so crude — aside from the rickety, barely habitable dwarf buildings, the orcs could only huddle under hastily thrown-up animal-hide tents.

But it was different here. Jace could see tall orc watchtowers in the distance, clearly not built by dwarves. Even though Jace knew nothing about construction, he could tell they were solid, stable, and hard to destroy.

The orc fortresses and military camps he'd passed along the way were all on a considerable scale.

Considering the orcs had only occupied Blackrock Mountain for ten years, and that Warchief Blackhand's two sons had retreated here after their defeat by the Alliance barely four or five years ago — and had been fighting the Dark Iron dwarves for control of the Burning Steppes and Blackrock Mountain the whole time, building while battling — it was honestly astonishing what they'd built. The countless orc peons had made an enormous contribution.

Whether the Shattered Hand orcs acknowledged the "true" Horde rebuilt at Blackrock Spire or not, and whether the Bleeding Hollow orcs were willing to leave the Black Morass and pledge allegiance to the "new Warchief" here, one thing was certain: the Blackhand brothers were dead serious about making this work.

But no matter how impressive the orc peons' fortifications were after years of labor, they couldn't compare to the miracle Thaurissan's Dark Iron dwarves had accomplished here.

Unlike the mountain paths leading to Grim Batol or the gates of Ironforge — roads laid along the natural slope of the mountain — the road to Blackrock Mountain's gates rose like a magnificent black iron bridge, supported by enormous cut black stone blocks that filled the gaps between it and the mountain face, forcibly creating a wide, flat, sloping thoroughfare.

The Dark Iron dwarves who built this place must have been incredibly arrogant, brimming with a mad, world-conquering ambition.

Jace and Sora released their wolves at the roadside, letting them find the orcs or just flee into the plains, then set out onto the mountain road with Morofel.

Even where the bridge crossed the lava river, there was nothing on either edge — no curb, railing, or handhold.

Jace peered down from the bridge's edge. Below, in the mountain gully, a river of lava rolled down from Blackrock Mountain's peak, magma bubbles swelling and bursting, tongues of fire leaping up with billowing black smoke.

The orcs around him all stayed close to the middle, unwilling to approach the edge, terrified that a single shove would send them sliding off into the churning lava below. And the lava below howled and stirred like a pack of untamable beasts, waiting to devour any victim unlucky enough to fall.

In the distance, the charred black corpse of a dragon lay silently by the lava pool. It had probably been shot down by the Dark Iron dwarves, but now it looked like a sacrifice to the volcano, a warning to all who dared to underestimate this place.

Jace glanced at Sora beside him. She'd noticed the dead black dragon too, her expression solemn.

In this blistering volcanic heat, just existing here felt like being slowly roasted. The orc soldiers on the bridge still wore thick leather armor, some even full plate. The Smolderthorn Legion soldiers especially carried enormous shields, heavy axes, and warhammers, marching in formation with roaring steps.

Though the orcs and Dark Iron dwarves were mortal enemies — not even from the same world or the same species — at this moment, their temperaments merged perfectly together.

This Blackrock Mountain seemed tailor-made for the orcs who'd come here. These orcs didn't even look like outsiders — they looked like war-monsters born from the volcano's flames.

Just crossing the stone bridge to Blackrock Mountain's gates was a painful enough ordeal. Jace felt like his boots could barely withstand the mountain's heat anymore. Even though they were big boots twisted by red dragon magic to fit an orc's feet, the heat underfoot constantly made Jace wonder if the soles had burned through.

Sora behind him remained completely calm. She wasn't even sweating — compared to the orcs who'd started climbing alongside them, she looked like she'd barely moved.

Jace couldn't imagine how many times Grede would have cursed his mother if he'd been here climbing this road.

At the top of the bridge, Blackrock Mountain's gates gradually came into view. Though this exaggerated black gate was only two hundred years old, Ragnaros's relentless fury had baked and scorched it, making it look like it had weathered more ages than the gates of Ironforge, which had stood quietly in the snowy mountains for a thousand years.

Each door panel was carved with the face of a Dark Iron dwarf sage, their expressions stern and imposing, gazing at every visitor like guardians of the mountain.

But clearly, the sages' blessings hadn't stopped the orc invasion. Even under their eternal gaze, the orcs came and went without obstruction, showing not a shred of respect or fear.

Jace, Sora, and Morofel followed the orcs through the gap in the gates. The crowded orcs cursed wildly, while the peons simply lowered their heads and endured in silence.

The stench of sweat, dried feces and urine, the rusty smell of metal — mixed with the volcanic smoke and sulfur that permeated the whole place — made it feel like a sweltering, roasting hell.

An orc blood guard wearing heavy, spike-covered armor brushed past Jace. The metal plate on his armor scraped across Jace's hand, and Jace immediately felt how hot the armor was. He couldn't help but stare at the orc for a moment.

Inside the Blackrock gates, the wide corridor lined with sternly carved black stone pillars was now so crowded with lounging orcs that there was hardly room to step. Jace looked down the corridor to where it turned a corner and guessed there were hundreds of orcs just in this one area — not counting the deeper interior of Blackrock Mountain, or the upper and lower levels of Blackrock Spire.

Looking at it this way, with orcs wandering in from all over to the Burning Steppes, the Blackhand brothers had probably assembled a force of ten thousand here. And that wasn't even counting the peons.

What's more, every orc warrior here had survived the extreme conditions of Blackrock Mountain and the Burning Steppes. And with the Dark Portal closed, Blackrock Spire was the Horde's last bastion on Azeroth — there was no fallback. Their will to resist was incalculable.

Add to that the Amani trolls and Spirestone ogres that Jace hadn't seen yet but knew lived in Blackrock Spire — plus the drakonid legion and even the black dragons that had dropped in from who-knows-where — heaven only knew what it would cost to take this place.

No wonder the hot-tempered Dark Iron dwarves who'd lived here for two hundred years had been driven back underground, unable to show their faces. Even the Alliance still hadn't agreed on a viable plan to attack Blackrock Spire.
