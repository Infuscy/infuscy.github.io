## Chapter 956: The Scorching Passage

"Move it!"

"You looking for a fight..."

"Let me through."

"Get your foot out of the way, scum!"

The corridor was sweltering enough already, but with the orcs' constant cursing and shouting, and the fire basins that lit the place while adding more heat to the already awful air, their flickering flames and unstable shadows danced back and forth, making the dim corridor even more of a demon's den.

Jace and Morofel took point, forcing a path with brute strength and the orc peons' fear of demons. Sora followed close behind, afraid of being shoved away and lost by the orc soldiers.

After what felt like an eternity of shuffling to the end of the corridor, the air grew redder and brighter. The crowd pressed forward, and waves of scorching hot wind slapped against Jace's face, almost pushing him back again.

Breaking free from the crush of orcs, Jace looked around. They'd arrived at an internal bridge-road inside Blackrock Mountain.

This was a massive hall built around a central lava pool and stone pillar. The bridge-roads were built into the stone walls surrounding the hall, connecting Blackrock Spire to the path leading to Shadowforge City. It looked very similar to the game.

The stone pillar rising from the lava pool was secured by enormous chains connected to the surrounding walls. That pillar was where Franclorn Forgewight's tomb sat — the architect of Blackrock Spire and Shadowforge City.

The Dark Iron dwarves had destroyed the stone bridge from Blackrock Spire into Shadowforge City to stop the orcs from invading. In the game, the only way into Shadowforge City was to climb the chains to Franclorn's tomb, then take another chain to the door leading into the city.

But when Jace reached an open spot on the bridge and tried to survey the area, he realized there was a pretty serious problem.

The structure here was indeed similar to the game. The massive chains securely fastened the pillar at the center of the lava pool, but those chains, while enormous, weren't really something you could walk on comfortably.

In the game, players could walk on them because the climbing process was simplified into walking on a premade model for gameplay's sake.

Of course, given Jace's and Morofel's abilities, climbing down those massive chains wouldn't be hard. Jace had even considered this possible difference beforehand.

But was he really going to climb down those chains in plain view of everyone on the surrounding bridges, heading straight for Shadowforge City?

In the game, the Blackrock orcs seemed to have reached some agreement with the Dark Iron dwarves, or maybe the long war had left both sides in a stalemate. Either way, aside from the guards at the entrances of Blackrock Spire and Shadowforge City, the chains and stone bridges in between were empty.

Only a black dragon patrolled in circles, and that dragon was basically decorative — it wouldn't actually attack players who entered the area.

But here, the black dragon was no decoration.

Jace watched as a large black whelp clung to the rock wall like a bat, its long snake-like head hanging down, observing the orcs coming and going on the stone bridge with one yellow, grotesque eye.

If he just openly climbed onto the chains, tried to make it to Franclorn's Tomb in the middle, then climbed down the chains shrouded in lava and fiery dust toward Shadowforge City, he probably wouldn't make it halfway before the black dragon roasted him with Shadowflame and the orcs shot him full of arrows.

So he'd have to try another approach?

In the game, there was another way into Shadowforge City: just jump into the lava and swim to the slope near the entrance.

In the game, falling into lava meant taking damage ticks, not instant death. If you climbed onto the slope fast enough before burning to death, you'd pass through fine.

And here?

Jace looked down at the magma below and swallowed.

He doubted that when the Dragonqueen gave him the Life-Binder's blessing, she'd envisioned him swimming in the Firelord Ragnaros's flames.

Jace followed the orcs toward Blackrock Spire, observing the chains, the stone locks securing them... thinking about every possible way to get down to the lava level.

He realized the chains here were harder to climb than in the game, and there were more watchers. But the terrain and other structures were more complex than in the game — which meant more room to maneuver too. It wasn't a dead end.

Sora walked beside him in silence. Speaking Common among the orcs would draw attention — you never knew which of them might have learned it.

Watching Jace observe everything around him, she seemed to understand he was figuring something out. So she just followed quietly, not making trouble.

Just then, Jace spotted a headless statue under one of the stone bridges. It was another stone lock carved into the shape of a massive dwarf, seated firmly amid the lava. Between its waist and the mountain wall, down to the lava surface, there was clearly a narrow, rocky ledge just wide enough for a person to traverse.

Right now, the entire lava pool below was obscured by smoke and firelight, impossible to see clearly. But based on his observations of Blackrock Mountain's interior layout and the position of that central pillar tomb, Jace could roughly estimate the entrance to Shadowforge City — the entrance to the Blackrock Depths dungeon in the game — using his game experience. It shouldn't be far from the rocks beneath that dwarf's waist.

The problem now was how to get down from that stone bridge to the statue without being noticed.

"Follow me."

Jace turned to Sora, changing direction toward the corner edge of that stone bridge.

Sora and Morofel followed him to the edge. Not far off, some orcs were crouched, sitting, resting, or dozing. Jace whispered to Morofel in Demonic: "Go over there and attract their attention. Don't let them look this way. Sora and I are going down."

"Once we're down, find an opportunity to turn invisible and follow through the soul link. Don't draw their attention down below."

"Yes, master," Morofel said.

Jace watched Morofel step out of the corner, one step at a time. She didn't need any special moves — as a Sayyid, her mere presence was enough to draw attention.

Making sure nobody was watching the shadows by the wall, Jace said to Sora, "Follow me down, Sora. We're heading for the broken head of that dwarf statue over there. You good with that?"

"No problem," Sora replied.

Jace grunted in acknowledgment, strapped his staff to his back, and once he was sure no one was looking, slid into the gap between the rock wall and the bridge.

He scraped and clawed his way down the uneven rock face until he grabbed the dwarf's arm and climbed up. Sora slid down after him, but nearly slipped straight past the dwarf's shoulder. Jace was quick — he grabbed her leather armor and hauled her back up. They both sat down by the statue's neck and caught their breath.

Next, they just had to go around the dwarf statue's neck, jump from the other arm to the waist, then walk over to the cliff face. From here, Jace could even vaguely make out the enormous chain crossing before him through the haze. If he remembered correctly, that should be the chain that led to the passage to the Dark Iron city.

Shielded by the stone bridge above and the dwarf statue, the orcs above hadn't noticed what was happening in this nook and cranny. Victory was right in front of them. Didn't seem so hard.

"Over here," Jace said, turning to Sora. She hummed in acknowledgment and glanced at the lava below, apparently still shaken from nearly slipping earlier.

After all, if she were forced to revert to dragon form and fly here, the situation would be far more chaotic than just falling into the lava.

Jace leaped from the dwarf's belt to the rock wall. It would be easy to slip into the magma from here, but the rough, uneven surface at this angle was enough for someone with Jace's strength to keep his footing. Sora managed to stabilize herself and follow. Looked like she was fine.

He looked ahead. From the shadows in the smoke, it seemed like more of the same rock wall ahead. Steady walking to the root of that chain shouldn't be a problem. The magma's heat, the smoke's sting... none of it mattered much now.

But just as Jace was feeling confident, a hiss sounded from above.

He looked up. A dark shadow streaked overhead, circling behind the dwarf statue behind him. Damn it — had that black dragon finally noticed something was off down here?

Jace had no free hand to unstrap his staff. He threw back his cloak and reached for the Azuresong Mageblade at his hip, ready to take down the black dragon with everything he had, then sprint for the passage before the orc army could react.

But Sora stopped him. "Let me handle it, Jace."

She looked up, fixing her gaze on the shadow in the smoke. Her eyes glowed with silver moonlight. When the black dragon's shadow swept past again — close enough for Jace to see its yellow snake-eyes clearly — it seemed to look right through them and flew on by.

Jace eased his grip on his sword, looking at the green dragon with some surprise. Sora cautioned, a little nervously, "The illusion magic I cast on a dragon like that won't last more than a few minutes. Sorry, Jace. So we need to hurry..."

"That's enough. Let's go," Jace said, nodding.
