Chapter 957: The Enemy of My Enemy Is My Friend

Picking their way across the uneven slope that could send them sliding into the magma at any moment, Jace and Sora pushed through layer after layer of skin-burning smoke. After another few dozen meters, the enormous iron chain ahead became clearer through the fire-haze with each passing moment, and the fire-glowing runes on the stone pillar anchoring the chain gradually came into focus.

Looking at the slope leading down beside the stone pillar, Jace estimated the distance. Should be jumpable.

He glanced back at Sora, then up at the heights. Franclorn's Tomb at the center of the lava lake was now above them, and on the fire-lit great bridge overhead, he could still see blurry, shifting orc figures.

The black dragon was still circling above the lava lake. They didn't waste any more time. Jace backed up a few steps, took a running jump, landed on the slope, and slid a bit before catching himself. He turned back to look up at Sora, who was about to jump down. She landed steadily this time — no repeat of her earlier mistake.

As soon as Sora came down, Morofel appeared above them. For a demon like her, a jump from this height was nothing.

Once they'd confirmed the black dragon hadn't noticed their movement, they climbed the slope to the stone pillar carved with dwarven runes. Circling around it, they could see a small tower built into the rock wall at the end of a stone walkway behind the pillar.

Jace was very familiar with this place. When raiding the Molten Core — the Firelord Ragnaros's lair — this was the dungeon's entrance.

After completing the quest to find Molten Core's location, the high elf mage here would teleport players deep into the Molten Core. Or you could enter by a more absurd method — jumping into the lava pool next to it.

While waiting to enter the dungeon back then, Alliance and Horde players used to fight massive battles here. That walkway was permanently piled with countless corpses.

Of course, this wasn't just the entrance to Molten Core. The small door beside it led to the bottom of Blackrock Mountain — the entrance to Shadowforge City.

Jace carefully hugged the wall as he walked the walkway. No dwarf guards here, no orcs. And of course, the high elf who gave out the Molten Core attunement quest in the game wasn't here either. Empty, not a soul in sight.

He felt his way forward a bit and found a tunnel opening carved into the rock wall. Stepping through to the platform beyond, he looked out the window at the rolling lava pool below, confirmed there was only this one doorway in and no other entrances, and concluded that this was indeed the entrance to Shadowforge City from the game.

He moved back to the tunnel opening and peered inside. Deep, no end in sight, no light either.

"Go take a look inside first, Morofel. This might be Dark Iron territory ahead. Sora and I need to drop the orc disguises."

"I'll miss your orc form, master," Morofel said plaintively.

Jace gave her a helpless sidelong glance. The succubus smiled and walked into the tunnel, quickly merging with the shadows.

Sora tossed an orcish war blade she'd picked up on the Burning Steppes into the lava pool and said, "I have to admit, Jace, when you were clearing the path ahead and I followed behind you, watching the other orcs cower at the sight of you... I honestly couldn't tell whether I was following that kind-hearted Jace Sesso, or a powerful warlock truly loyal to the Horde."

"Let me tell you a little detail that not many living people know, Sora," Jace said. "When I met the Dragonmaw clan leader back in Grim Batol, I pretended to be an envoy from Blackrock Spire. If I let people casually see through my act, do you think I'd have survived under the Dragonmaw orcs' watch?"

Sora smiled and nodded. Jace grabbed his cloak, intoned "Varastazalnos," and in a rolling cloud of smoke spitting embers, his form shrank, returning to his original appearance.

Then Sora, enveloped by a blur of energy like rippling water, shed the female orc's form. She shrank even more, and when the blur faded, she'd become a female dwarf in a light brown coat.

"Compared to an orc, a dwarf's appearance is easier for me to accept, Jace." She buttoned her collar and said, "But I have to admit, turning into an orc was easier."

"Why easier?" Jace asked, adjusting his belt.

Sora looked at the dark red gem of Anglista at his waist and explained, "The Blackrock orcs' skin isn't pure black or gray. They drank demon blood, so there's still some green in it. Easy for a green dragon to imitate. And the straw-yellow hair isn't uncommon among orcs either, so it was easier for me to adapt."

"So the closer your mortal form is to your natural appearance, the easier the transformation?" Jace asked.

"At least for a young dragon like me." Sora raised her thick arm and made a circling gesture. "But for the more powerful dragons — like Azuregos or Prince Kaelestrasz — they probably have more aesthetic considerations."

"I feel like it's not just Master Marin who needs more education — I could use some more dragon lore too," Jace said.

"Feel free to ask, Jace Sesso." The female dwarf smiled, her red eyes crinkling into slits, then suddenly widened again as if she'd remembered something. She put on a stern face and said to herself, "I need to remember Grede's instructions. Be fiercer. That's more fitting for a Dark Iron dwarf."

Just then, Morofel reappeared at the tunnel entrance. "No orcs ahead, at least. But there are plenty of Dark Iron dwarf sentries watching this passage."

"No orcs is good enough. We'll just go straight through," Jace said. "Stay behind me, Morofel."

"Yes, master."

They walked into the tunnel. Sora asked curiously, "Have you been here before, Jace?"

"No," Jace replied.

Sora said quietly, "But the feeling you give me is like you've been confirming things you already know the whole way." Jace looked at her, thinking: was it that obvious?

Thinking back, yeah, it was pretty obvious. He'd just been so focused on not blowing his cover among the orcs and finding a way into Shadowforge City that he'd overlooked how closely Sora had been watching him from behind.

"I'd been planning a trip to Shadowforge City for a while, so I asked around pretty thoroughly," Jace said. "So I'm gambling — gambling that what those Dark Iron dwarves told me was accurate."

At least half of that statement was true. He was gambling — gambling that the game's content matched this world. There'd been some hiccups along the way, but all things considered, it had worked out.

"Stop right there, human!"

A warning in Common, followed by three pairs of red eyes emerging from the dark tunnel. One of the dwarves lit a torch, and in the firelight, two gun barrels were aimed straight at him.

"Don't shoot," Jace said, raising his hands. "We mean no harm."

"You mean no what?" one of the dwarves holding a gun asked, frowning.

"No... intention to harm anyone," Jace explained in Dwarvish. "I can speak Dwarvish, gentlemen."

"Hmph..." The dwarf muttered to his companion in Dwarvish, "This one's accent stinks of wild dwarf." Jace was glad Grede hadn't come along — otherwise, the hammer would have been flying by now.

Ah, right — Grede didn't have a hammer anymore. What a pity.

Jace was also hoping to find out how to forge the Sulfuron War Hammer while he was in Shadowforge City. He wasn't holding his breath about actually getting his hands on the legendary weapon, but it was worth asking about.

"You there — how did a human and a dwarf end up together? And how did you get here?" the dwarf holding the torch asked. They seemed a bit friendlier now that he was using Dwarvish.

"This is part of an agreement we made," Sora said, glancing at Jace, following their pre-arranged story. "I was suspected of being a spy in the Dwarf District of Stormwind and thrown into a human prison. This warlock was willing to rescue me. In return, I guided him to Shadowforge City. He hopes to learn more about demonic knowledge here."

"But he's already a pretty impressive master of demonology, isn't he?" The dwarf with the gun leaned closer to the torchbearer, staring at Morofel's demonic eyes. "Look at that succubus behind him. Meg, I'll bet you don't see one like that in Shadowforge City in years..."

"I was just fortunate enough to earn this Sayyid lady's favor. It has little to do with my actual knowledge," Jace said.

Morofel squinted happily and wrapped her arms around her master's neck. Jace stroked the demon horn pressed against his cheek and continued, "So I'm well aware I still have much to learn in demonology. Coming to Shadowforge City is an important step in furthering my education. The demonic research in the Kingdom of Stormwind is obviously completely dead in the water."

Hearing Jace praise their city so enthusiastically, the three Dark Iron dwarves visibly relaxed. But one of the dwarves holding a gun still asked, "How did you get through the orc defenses?"

"With the help of shadow magic, of course," Jace said. "But that's one of my specialties. If I can't learn anything worthwhile in Shadowforge City, I'm not about to just give it away..."

The dwarves' faces cooled again.

"Then you're not getting into the city," the torchbearer said. "You can't prove you're not an orc spy. No one would dare let you through. It's not that I disrespect warlocks or have anything against humans. But those are the general's orders, and we have to follow them."

Jace took off his cloak and threw it on the ground in front of the dwarves. "See what this is."

One of the dwarves aiming his gun at Jace and Sora shuffled forward, bent down, felt the cloak's material, then brought it closer for a look.

His brow furrowed at first, then his eyes widened in shock. He quickly stepped back, turned his head, and said to his two companions, "Black dragon..."

"It's black dragon hide! A real black dragon, not a dragonkin!"

"Is that enough to prove I'm not an orc spy, gentlemen?" Jace asked.

"Enough, enough." The dwarf in the middle swallowed. "Come with us."
