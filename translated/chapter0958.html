Chapter 958: Struggle Beneath the Earth

Jace, Sora, and Morofel followed the three Dark Iron dwarves through the deep underground cave passage. The whole way was sweltering and dark, and the deeper they went, the more torturous it became.

The three Dark Iron dwarves had lived here for years and seemed perfectly comfortable. Morofel, being a demon from the Twisting Nether, had little reaction to heat or cold. And Sora, being a living dragon, was naturally unaffected by this temperature too.

Jace — a mortal blessed by dragonfire, a "Firehandler" who could command the elements — had somehow become the most heat-sensitive person in the group.

Come to think of it, since entering Blackrock Mountain, despite the fire everywhere, Jace hadn't heard the voices of any fire elementals.

Not compared to Ironforge — even on the vast plains of Kalimdor, he could occasionally hear elemental voices. Of course, he knew why.

Because here, the fire elementals had a true master: the Firelord Ragnaros.

Most of the elementals willing to communicate with him were free-floating ones who'd broken away from Ragnaros's rule. There wouldn't be many such fire elementals left here.

Thinking about it, Jace even started to doubt whether he could learn anything worthwhile in Shadowforge City. After all, he didn't want to pray to the Firelord like the Dark Iron dwarves just to become stronger. That wouldn't be much different from what the Old God's followers — the Twilight's Hammer cult — would do later.

In the game, Shadowforge City had long since allied with the Twilight's Hammer cult. Jace believed the cult probably hadn't formed that strong an organization yet at this point; most of them were active only in Kalimdor.

Still, he couldn't let his guard down. He remembered Galders — someone, somewhere, had obtained a Silithus obsidian token. That meant the Twilight's Hammer's influence had already shown up here, one way or another.

The walk from the lava lake entrance to Shadowforge City took longer than expected. They'd been going for about half an hour when torchlight appeared ahead in the tunnel. Jace thought they'd finally reached Shadowforge City, but it was just another patrol.

The two groups of dwarves greeted each other, exchanged information about the passage, briefly introduced Jace and his party, then went their separate ways.

Jace could tell the Dark Iron dwarves didn't have any particular hostility toward humans right now. Probably because Shadowforge City was helpless against the orcs occupying Blackrock Spire, and seeing the Alliance army attack Blackrock Spire, they were hoping to use the humans' war with their cousins to reclaim their old territory.

To Jace, this tunnel looked like a natural cave that hadn't been much modified. For Dark Iron dwarves — obsessive underground remodelers — having such a rough and chaotic passage leading to their city was hard to understand.

No sooner had the other patrol left than the dwarf at the front complained, "Damn, this road is a pain in the ass. When will I be done with patrol duty?"

"You can jump into the lava yourself!" the dwarf behind him said irritably.

Jace asked curiously, "After you came to Blackrock Mountain, has this tunnel been your only connection between Blackrock Spire and Shadowforge City?"

"What the hell are you talking about? That corridor back there couldn't even fit my aunt through, let alone an army from Shadowforge!" The dwarf soldier ahead spat. "There used to be plenty of roads leading outside, but the orcs destroyed most of them. The proper passage from the entrance to Shadowforge City collapsed too. This is just a leftover mining tunnel that's still usable. If you'd come two years ago, you wouldn't have had to take this road."

"So the whole city is trapped down below?" Sora asked.

The Dark Iron dwarf was silent for a moment before answering, "Don't underestimate our army. The general is trying to establish contact to the north. The troops at the Cinder Diggings are coordinating from outside... Under the Firelord's protection, one day we'll take back Blackrock Spire..."

"I heard His Majesty the Emperor is negotiating a ceasefire treaty with the orc chieftain," another guard chimed in.

"You actually believe His Majesty would give up Blackrock Spire? You're out of your mind!" The torchbearer turned back. "I didn't say that..." the guard muttered.

The dwarves started arguing about ceasefire versus continued fighting, grumbling and chattering endlessly. The winding mine shafts echoed with vague, muffled voices.

Amid all the echoing noise, Jace picked up on something that didn't sound right.

It was like something scuttling and scraping nearby, but when he listened closely, it sounded far away.

He looked up at the cave ceiling — didn't seem like the sound was coming from there. He turned his head to the side, and suddenly the noise seemed to get clearer.

"Shh."

Jace pressed a finger to his lips. "Quiet."

"What is it, warlock?" One of the gun-toting dwarves turned around. The other two, still bickering, took a couple more exchanges before they shut up. Once it was quiet, even Morofel frowned. Jace said, "I've never been a miner. Is this kind of sound normal here?"

After listening intently for a while, the dwarves' faces grew uglier by the second. Before anyone could say anything, a chunk of rock from a nearby wall suddenly collapsed. The rumbling of the cave-in swallowed everything, followed by a series of sharp chitinous scraping sounds against stone.

The dwarf with the torch swung the light in that direction, illuminating several black bugs charging straight at them through the dust and shadows. Each one was about the size of an Elwynn boar, brandishing massive pincers...

"Scorpions! Scorpions!"

"Open fire! Open fire!"

"Damn it... my bullets..." Bang!

A deafening gunshot exploded right next to Jace. The three Dark Iron dwarves scrambled to draw their weapons, but more and more scorpions kept pouring out of the cracks in the stone. More rocks were being pushed aside all around them. Suddenly, the sound of scraping stingers, chitinous legs, and clattering exoskeletons filled their ears.

Sora's eyes began to glow silver as she looked at the black bugs crawling out of the cracks. Jace shook her shoulder, giving her a look to stop her from casting.

It was obvious that Dark Iron dwarves probably had nothing to do with nature magic. If these soldiers discovered Sora could summon underground plants or talk to beasts in this mine, explaining it would be way too much trouble.

In this narrow, sweltering mine tunnel, Jace naturally couldn't summon fire elementals to burn everything either. So he drew the Azuresong Mageblade, spoke the elemental language to ignite it, and the blade's faint arcane glow erupted into a roaring inferno in an instant.

Even the giant scorpions charging at him with raised pincers faltered at the sight of the blazing magic sword.

Jace charged forward, cleaving through the lead scorpion's carapace with one swing. The splashing flames burned the scorpions behind it, producing a cacophony of hissing and crackling — whether from the fire's burn or the scorpions' pained cries was hard to tell.

But under the blazing Azuresong Mageblade's arc, the beasts crawling from the cave couldn't withstand the assault. They soon gave up attacking from the rear, scattering to avoid the flaming sword's glow. And when the scorpions from the front fully surrounded them, the dwarf soldiers abandoned their guns too, switching to swords and axes, shouting as they fought — hard to tell if it was fear or battle frenzy.

After several minutes of fierce fighting, the scorpions seemed to realize they'd hit a tough target. No more came charging out, and the survivors fled back into the cracks as if following some order, leaving behind a dozen or so stinking, scorched insect carcasses.

Even if they were just bugs, a dozen carcasses filled the tunnel. Jace checked his clothes to make sure nothing was torn or stained by bug fluid.

"Last month you were yelling at me to go underground hunting scorpions, and now these scorpions are hunting us!" the dwarf behind him shouted, still shaken.

"If we make peace with the orcs, we'll still have to keep fighting these scorpions. Endless... and now they're clearly taking revenge."

"Let's get people back here to haul these carcasses away. Scorpion blood, scorpion meat, scorpion shells..."

The torchbearer trailed off as he turned back to look at Jace. "Thanks, human friend. You've got some skill with fire."

"Pfft, it was probably those outsiders' smell that attracted the scorpions..." another Dark Iron dwarf grumbled.

"It was your noisy arguing that drew the scorpions' attention," Sora said.

"You're taking the outsider's side?!" The dwarf's eyes went wide as he raised a finger to start cursing at Sora. Jace sheathed the extinguished Azuresong Mageblade with a harsh, grating sound that cut him off.

The dwarf looked at Jace's less-than-friendly expression, then at the demon beside him, and seemed to remember exactly who he was dealing with. He shut his mouth.

The other gun-toting soldier shoved the dwarf and said, "We need to collect some scorpion meat. Don't waste time. Give some to our guests too — they did plenty of work!"

After that whole ordeal, the dwarf soldiers became much more alert and slowed their pace. Whenever they met other dwarf patrols, they'd warn them about scorpion-dug tunnels and the carcasses back in the passage.

From their conversations, Jace gathered that ever since the orcs had trapped them inside, the dwarves had been forced to hunt spiders, scorpions, and fire lizards for food in the caves.

But it was clearly not enough to feed an entire Shadowforge City. Food stores were dwindling. Everyone was tightening their belts or trying to dig new escape routes, but nothing was going smoothly.

No wonder Emperor Dagran Thaurissan was seeking a temporary truce with the orcs.

Actually, the orcs and the black dragons had reason to want a ceasefire too. They didn't intend to push deep into the Firelord's territory and take over the whole of Blackrock Mountain. They just wanted a safe place to preserve their strength, recover from the losses of the Orc Wars, and put down roots in Azeroth.

If they fought the Dark Iron dwarves to the bitter end, the Alliance would scoop up the spoils from both sides. So even though the orcs seemed to have the upper hand, they might not refuse the Dark Iron dwarves' proposal.

Jace didn't know how long they'd been walking. He was starting to get so tired he could barely keep his eyes open when lights finally appeared ahead. Not torchlight from other dwarves this time — it was a fire basin mounted next to a proper passage.

"Look ahead. We're here," one soldier said. "Finally get to rest a bit. Damn."
