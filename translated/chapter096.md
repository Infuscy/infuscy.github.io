## Chapter 96: Is This the First Spell You Learned?

"Welcome, Duncan's Tailor Shop — how can I help you? Oh… you… I remember you."

The moment Jace stepped inside, before his eyes had even adjusted from the blinding sunlight to the dim shop, he heard the proprietor's loud call.

The shopkeeper was already looking his way. "You're that assistant who wanted to buy a robe from me last time, aren't you? Well, has your master made a decision?"

"My master already ordered a robe from Dalaran," Jace said. "Should arrive before Winter Veil next month. Sorry about that, Mr. Duncan."

"That's business, I suppose. That robe's been sitting here for almost a year already — I don't mind it staying a little longer." The shopkeeper sighed. "So what brings you in today?"

"I wanted you to take a look at something." Jace pulled a single scale from his pocket and set it on the counter. "Can you tell what this is, sir?"

The shopkeeper picked up his spectacles and examined it closely. "Naga scale, lad. You pick this up off the ground?"

Jace didn't answer right away. He found it odd that Ursula hadn't immediately said he'd found the scales, yet this tailor could tell at a glance. Was an armorer more knowledgeable about these materials than an alchemist?

"Mm."

"This kind of scale isn't worth much," the shopkeeper said. "We don't take them here. You might try asking around the Dwarf District — armorers are quite fond of naga scales. Nice color, good for decorating the key parts of a suit of armor, and you can ride the 'naga scale armor' angle to fool folks who don't know any better. But here in the Mage Quarter… a thing is what it is. Those mages sometimes see clearer than we do. It's always us poor little merchants who get swindled, not the other way around."

Strange. They were all naga scales — why was there such a gap between what Ursula offered and what this shopkeeper was saying?

"Why?" Jace asked. "I heard naga scales have magical properties — that they can boost frost magic and water magic. Is that not true?"

"It's true," the shopkeeper said. "But they're not talking about this kind of scale. This type… is fairly easy to come by. Someone who walks the coast their whole life might never see a naga and still stumble across one of these now and then. It's got a striking shape, quite distinctive, so it stands out on the beach. It mixes in with the shells and people pick them up. Some armorers even buy them right off the docks."

"The ones that actually hold magical power are the naga chest scales — the larger ones that cover the back, neck, and chest. Those scales are said to shed very slowly, so they're extraordinarily rare to find. When people say 'naga scales,' that's the stuff they mean."

"Well, I've never seen a naga, so I don't know if those scholars are right or not. All I know is that if you want to make a magic robe, you can only use that kind of scale. These ones are basically good for nothing but decoration." Jace was a bit frustrated. He was completely clueless about this stuff and had just tossed all the scales together. How was he supposed to sort them out now?

At noon he ran to the library and looked up records on naga scales. Fortunately, there were plenty of entries and illustrations. He jotted down the distinguishing features and shapes of scales from different parts of the body onto a sheet of paper, planning to take it home and use it to separate the ordinary scales from the valuable belly-and-back ones that could actually be sold.

After finishing the sketch, he realized the paper at home was nearly gone. Sazeno had almost filled every sheet with writing, and Jace didn't have any spare coin to buy supplies right now.

But payday was only a few days away — his first wage as an assistant. Ten silver. Enough to buy a whole new set of paper and ink in one go.

As for the scales, if there was a market, he'd sell them. If not, he could wait. No rush.

He got through lunch on the free pastries at the Wizard Sanctum, then loitered in the garden by the Sanctum gate waiting for Marin. Regarding the matter of the gnoll alchemy lab — whether the young king was willing to dispatch men to deal it or not — he had to report it to the Wizard Sanctum.

He hadn't waited long when Marin, coming out of the Blue Recluse, spotted Jace from a distance.

"Master Marin." Jace stood up. "I have something important to tell you."

Marin walked over clutching a long loaf of bread. "You're back from Westfall?"

"Yes," Jace said. "And I found the gnolls' alchemy lab. I have enough evidence to prove they're brewing poison inside."

The old mage frowned. "What evidence?"

"Among the herbs they've been collecting, there's deathweed. Could they be planning to poison Moonbrook or Sentinel Hill, something like that?"

"It's not impossible," Marin said. "I'll write to the Royal Mages this afternoon. Though I imagine the king will still need to send men to verify. If there's any follow-up, I'll let you know promptly."

Jace asked, "Master, is it possible there's some other force driving the gnolls to push step by step toward Stormwind?"

Marin replied, "We've been investigating the orc situation lately and have indeed been neglecting the west. But now that you mention it, gnolls across the entire kingdom have been growing restless — there are reports of raids and victims everywhere… However, Jace, the Wizard Sanctum isn't currently responsible for gnoll affairs. Even over at Stonewatch, our focus is solely on providing magical support to the garrison, not solving the gnoll problem."

"So that's not my focus either, and it's hard for me to answer. But if, as you say, we actually investigate and find a poison lab, then we'll have to take responsibility. For now, though, focus on honing your magical skills, Jace. You've only just become an apprentice — aren't you worrying a bit too much?"

Marin was right. Until his strength reached a certain level and people acknowledged it, even knowing about these things wouldn't get him a seat at the table.

"Oh, right." Marin pulled a ring from his robes. "Give me back your assistant's ring and take this. Remember to go find Archmage Andormas within the week to select your courses. He's at the top of the Wizard Sanctum. Though if you're starting classes, it probably won't be until after Winter Veil. Still, pick your courses early and do some preparation, so you're not completely lost when classes begin."

— An Apprentice Ring.

Jace looked at the runes and the capital initials of his own name engraved on it, and felt none of the joy he'd felt when he first became an apprentice.

"By the way, Jace, your Light spell…" Marin said, arms folded. "Any progress these past couple of days?"

Whatever else, he needed to improve himself first.

Jace took a step back, raised his finger, and murmured, "Liyouisa!"

By now he had some experience gathering his focus. The Light spell came together smoothly, a brilliant arcane glow hovering at his fingertip like a light bulb.

The arcane radiance was quite bright, drawing glances from quite a few students in the garden. But Marin wasn't looking at the light — he was staring intently at Jace's eyes and lips.

A full minute passed, but Marin didn't call for him to stop. Jace could feel it: summoning this arcane glow wasn't taxing him much at all. Marin was probably testing just how long he could maintain it.

Two minutes passed… two and a half minutes. Jace was starting to feel the drain on his mana.

Then Marin snapped his fingers — crack!

Jace looked at the old mage. His Light spell flickered, but didn't go out.

"All right, Jace," Marin said. "Let it dissipate!"

Jace clenched his fist and the arcane light faded. He even felt the fingers on his right hand stiffen — he'd concentrated too hard summoning that light.

Jace took a deep breath. "How was it, Master?"

Marin looked into Jace's eyes and asked in return: "Light — is that the first incantation you've ever learned?"
