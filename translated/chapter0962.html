Chapter 962: The Price of a Succubus's Help

Although the Black Iron Bar was called a bar, it was genuinely much larger than any inn Jace had ever been to, whether in Ironforge or Stormwind City.

The dwarves of Shadowforge City lived quite differently from the Bronzebeard dwarves of Thelsamar and Ironforge, or even the Wildhammer dwarves of Aerie Peak.

Dwarven dwellings were all dug underground, sure, but the cave-like rooms of the Bronzebeards and Wildhammers, with their sturdy chairs and stone beds, gave off a feeling of real warmth and safety.

Here, though, the rooms were emotionless and boxy, devoid of decoration. The beds and chairs were made of iron — they felt more like prison cells. And there were no windows.

Jace figured that even without the influence of the Old Gods, living in a place like this every day would probably give a person psychological problems sooner or later.

He stripped off the clothes he'd worn on the journey — thoroughly pickled with sweat and dust — and set them aside, then sat on the hard iron bed and caught his breath. He looked over at Moruphil sitting beside him and at Sazeno, who'd crawled out of the pack and hopped onto the chair, and asked, "What do you think of the demons' strength here in Shadowforge City?"

"There was a succubus outside the bar who left an impression on me," Moruphil said, thinking it over. "She seems to have escaped from the Burning Legion — she still bears the marks of fel tattoos on her body. Namara isn't particularly powerful, but I can tell she's been staying in this world of Azeroth for quite a few years."

"On the whole, the demons summoned by the warlocks here are much stronger than those in Stormwind. Perhaps it's because the demons here can move freely through the streets without restriction, whereas in places like Dalaran or Stormwind, high-ranking demons can only hide..."

"But the demons here can't compare to those summoned by the warlocks at the lighthouse, Sayad," Sazeno said. "You have to admit, the black dragons' shadow blood really does strengthen their connection to the Twisting Nether."

Jace said, "Even without the dragon blood, the warlocks who can go to the lighthouse are all carefully selected — naturally stronger than most warlocks. Next, I need to find a way to get in touch with the warlocks here."

Moruphil gave him a sidelong look. "So, master, you snuck into Shadowforge City under the pretext of helping Lady Katrana Prestor investigate the black dragons in Blackrock Spire, and then, under the pretext of finding a way to deal with the black dragons... came here to study demon magic?"

"Of course I hope to learn something," Jace said. "But more importantly, the Dark Iron warlock community clearly loves collecting all kinds of magic, just like we do — you can tell from the fact that they learned quite a few incantations from the orcs, brought over from the other side of the Dark Portal. So is it really possible that they haven't heard even a whisper of a powerful spell capable of dealing with black dragons? As far as I can see, aside from the person who found the spell directly, the ones most likely to know about it are the warlocks of this city. I plan to start my breakthrough here."

In the game, one of the mysterious spell tablets was in the hands of Golem Lord Argelmach, and the other was held by General Angerforge. Jace had heard the city's patrol soldiers mention a certain general earlier — probably referring to this General Angerforge.

But both of them were deep inside Shadowforge City's military district, or rather the barracks. The barracks were heavily guarded and off-limits to outsiders. Jace had no idea how to get in and find these two for the time being, so he could only start feeling his way around the city.

Sazeno nodded. "That makes sense, master. But how are you going to make contact with the warlocks here? Just find a demon on the street and ask for their master? That seems a little too..."

Moruphil smiled. "I actually agree with you on this one, little thing. No normal warlock would ever trust an approach like that."

"I'll go ask Namara first," Jace said. "When I go, help me check the luggage and see if anything's missing."

"Understood. But, do you remember, master — the second succubus?" Moruphil reminded him with a smile. "Even if you don't care about me, you should care about..."

"You don't need to remind me, Moruphil," Jace said, and headed out the door.

Right then, Namara was walking over from Sola's room. She came up to Jace and raised an eyebrow. "Is there something I can help you with, warlock?"

Honestly, to Jace, Namara's looks definitely couldn't hold a candle to Moruphil's. Her features were more like those of a truly evil demon — more in line with the average human's imagination of a nightmare fiend. And she reeked of a pungent mix of alcohol, urine, and cheap perfume.

But that apparently hit exactly the Dark Iron dwarves' sweet spot, otherwise she wouldn't have had the whole bar wrapped around her finger.

Jace said politely, "I do have a favor to ask, Miss Namara. Could you introduce me to your master?"

"My master?" Namara chuckled softly. "I think you mean my former master — the warlock who summoned me to this world. I'm sorry, dear. He died in Dracodar a few years ago."

"Isn't your master the owner of this bar?" Jace asked.

The succubus lowered her eyes. "Well, we have a purely employment relationship. My former master owed the owner here a large sum of money, so he pawned me to him. But after my master died... I found I was quite suited to working as a barmaid at the Coldshot. The people here are interesting — I enjoy their company. What do you think? Do you think I'm suited for this job?"

Jace clearly remembered that the owner of the Black Iron Bar was a leprous gnome warlock named Plugger, who often wore a big red hat and swaggered around the bar. Miss Namara was precisely his servant — so why had this succubus become a masterless wild demon here?

Recalling his entrance into the bar earlier, Jace also didn't remember seeing that red-hatted gnome warlock. But the moment that thought crossed his mind, he realized a problem.

Leprous gnomes were gnomes who had suffered from radiation sickness. After Gnomeregan was invaded, to kill the invading Rockjaw troggs, the gnomes released radioactive poison gas. But the gas didn't completely wipe out the Rockjaw — it killed a considerable number of gnomish citizens instead, and turned some survivors into monsters afflicted with severe radiation sickness.

Most of them had greenish skin covered in hideous rashes and tumors. Some were even permanently disabled or driven insane. No matter where they went, they couldn't avoid strange looks.

Plugger was the same, of course. But the Dark Iron dwarves didn't seem to care much about the traces of his radiation sickness — after all, this was Shadowforge City. The monsters hiding in every corner and cranny were far more frightening than the rashes on a leprous gnome's face.

In any case, leprous gnomes were a group that only appeared after the destruction of Gnomeregan. And right now, Gnomeregan was still perfectly fine — at least there was no obvious danger, since that unemployed gnome technician had been wanting to go home to find work. Naturally, there wouldn't be a leprous gnome warlock coming to the Black Iron Bar to be the owner.

So what had happened a few years from now?

How did a leprous gnome warlock from Gnomeregan cross the mountains of Dun Morogh and the fierce winds of Searing Gorge to reach the base of Blackrock Mountain, enter Shadowforge City, and become the owner of the Coldshot bar? And how did Miss Namara here suddenly go from a wild demon who'd only recently gained freedom from the Blackrock Mountain war to forming a contract with another warlock and becoming his servant?

Just thinking about it, Jace could almost piece together a brilliant story — though he probably wouldn't get the chance to witness it firsthand.

"You're a perfectly qualified barmaid — it's not my place to judge," Jace said.

"Thank you," Namara said happily.

Jace asked, "Then as a demon, you must know quite a few warlocks in the city, right?"

"Of course." Namara tilted her head with a smile. "You want me to introduce you to other warlocks? No problem, dear — as long as you're willing to pay a tiny, insignificant price..."

"Go on," Jace said.

The succubus leaned close to Jace's shoulder and gently sniffed. "You're quite an unusual warlock — you can tell just from your companion. She once served the Burning Legion, and held a very high rank, didn't she? A Slayer... I've never seen a warrior like her on Azeroth. I think if it weren't for this bar, I wouldn't even have the chance to speak with her alive."

"I had a feeling you were something special the moment I saw you, and now I don't need anything else to confirm it. I think you shouldn't have changed out of those clothes — I like the scent on you. Just breathing a little more of it makes me feel... a little more energized."

She raised her eyes and fixed Jace with those demonic eyes burning with a faint blue glow. "Let me taste you, how about that? I mean quietly, savoring carefully..."

"That won't be necessary," Jace said.

Namara narrowed her eyes. "It's your companion who doesn't want you to do this, right? I've heard some Sayaad are extremely jealous — they treat their master as their property and possession, believing they have the exclusive right to their master. Honestly, do you think they, as servants... have the right to demand that?"

The same old provocation. Jace frowned impatiently. "Is there no other option?"

"Of course there is." Namara straightforwardly grabbed Jace's hand with one arm and pulled open her tight bodice with the other. Her full, purple-tinted breasts bounced right out.

She ran her long tongue over one nipple. "Flick it with your finger — just once, that's all. That shouldn't be a problem, right? Look at it. How many slavers, officers, and nobles in Shadowforge City would give anything to clamp their lips around it and never let go — and here it is, right in front of your eyes, offering itself all on its own... Flick it just once, and I'll help you right away."

Jace grabbed Namara's wrist instead. "Tell me your true name. Then I can consider making a deal with you, demon."

"True name..."

Hearing that demand, Namara's expression changed in an instant.

She yanked her hand back. "That Slayer told you her true name?"

"Of course," Jace said. "Don't warlocks in Shadowforge City have that rule?"

Namara pulled her bodice back up, gave a quick, dismissive laugh, and lowered her eyes. "I hope you enjoy your stay here, human. I'm afraid I can't help you."

With that, she turned the corner of the corridor and left without looking back.

Right then, Sola had just come out of the door. She glanced down the corridor and asked, "What happened?"

"I was going to ask Miss Namara to introduce me to a warlock. Clearly she's not very willing," Jace said. "Are you hungry? Let's go downstairs and get something to eat."

Sola smoothed down her dwarf's coarse braid. "Now that you mention it, I am a little hungry."

"I'll get Moruphil," Jace said.
