## Chapter 963: Starting with the Warlocks

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. By his own sense of time it should have been well past bedtime, but the Black Iron Bar was still lively.

Right now a female dwarf was duking it out with two goblins in the corner — fists and feet flying everywhere. Though both goblins were much smaller in both build and height, they were swinging chairs and mugs around and somehow not falling behind.

The chaotic racket of the fight — now a clatter, now a crash — mixed with the dwarves' raucous toasting, and somehow it all felt like part of the atmosphere rather than an annoyance.

"Miss Namara, over here!"

Jace leaned back in his chair and raised his hand high, but the succubus on the other side completely ignored him, not even glancing this way.

"What did you do to her, master?" Moruphil asked, propping her chin on her hand.

"Who knows." Jace cleared his throat. Moruphil gave him a sidelong look that said she saw right through him, but said nothing.

"The bar's quite crowded right now, sir," a goblins said, approaching and lowering his voice. "Miss Miss Namara probably didn't hear your call, so I've come to serve you instead. For pouring drinks and carrying plates, we're no different from demons, wouldn't you say?"

"What do you recommend to eat?" Jace asked.

"Well." The goblin said, "You probably know the situation in Shadowforge City right now, sir. There definitely won't be any cherries in the signature cherry pie, and no bananas in the banana bread... With the complete fall of Blackrock Spire, the supply of Smoldering Spider Eggs has dried up too. If you really want some, come early in about two days and you might be able to snag a portion."

"Then bring us whatever you're not short on. We're all pretty hungry right now," Jace said.

"Crawler meat, how about that?" The goblin said, bracing himself on the table. "Shadowforge City is actively opening new mining tunnels to deal with the crisis, so there's quite a lot of fresh crawler meat around... You absolutely cannot taste this delicacy anywhere else. A true Blackrock Mountain specialty! You don't even need any spices — just a pinch of salt lets you savor the unique flavor born of the Firelord's own power! Even an outsider who believes in the Holy Light shouldn't miss it."

"We'll have the crawler meat then," Jace said. "Plus a portion of dried black mushrooms, a plate of cheese if you have it, and a Dark Iron ale for everyone here — including the demon."

"No problem!" the goblin said. "That'll be fifteen silver and thirty copper, pay first, sir."

"Fifteen silver?" Jace asked blankly.

The goblin held up two fingers. "If you switch to dried black mushrooms and light ale, it's only two silver."

Just hearing those options, Jace could tell they weren't going to do much to ease the exhaustion of the journey. And with the green dragon next to him — whose red eyes looked so hungry they were practically glowing green — he could only nod and count out the silver from his purse onto the table.

No wonder the Dark Iron dwarves here just drank, or sat around gnawing on bread hard as rock. You hardly saw anyone eating anything else.

"Thank you for bringing me out, master. Truly an honor." Moruphil hugged Jace's arm. "I told you before that I wanted to go out to eat with you — you must have remembered. I'm so happy."

"Ah, right." Jace cleared his throat and nodded. "I remembered."

Moruphil smiled. Sola, sitting across from them, said, "Miss Moruphil has really given me a whole new impression of demons."

"There goes another poor fool, deceived," Sazeno said, squatting on the table. "But honestly, our Miss Moruphil has managed to fool the entire world and still can't fool the master into loving her. Truly pitiful."

Moruphil stared at it. "At least I possess the master's power, while you've only got a few green rocks — handouts from that Kalez..."

"Ugh." Sazeno clutched its neck and choked. "You think I envy you? Ugh!"

"All right, all right, food's coming," Jace said, cutting the two demons off with a sigh.

Just then the goblin brought over a steaming plate of crawler meat. On the gray shells sat translucent, glistening lumps like shrimp paste — the sight of them was genuinely appetizing, completely killing any desire to investigate what a crawler itself actually looked like.

Of course, even Jace, a traveler who'd seen what the hell a crawler looked like in Maraudon, was too hungry right now to care.

"Finally..."

Sola held back too, muttering as she pinched a piece of scalding meat and stuffed it into her mouth, her cheeks bulging as she chewed. Jace took a piece as well, then said, "This might upset you, but I'm really curious... why do dragons hate it so much when humans speak draconic?"

"It's not aimed at humans specifically," Sola said softly. "The eldest of our kind hate anything that isn't a dragon speaking draconic. They consider it a grave desecration — even dragonkin and drakonid who've received dragon blood blessings. In the beginning, those elders wanted all dragonkin to speak the language they had before their elevation — Common, or Kaldorei. But in the end they gradually learned draconic themselves, to make communication easier among dragonkin from different origins. Eventually the elders had no choice but to accept it."

"Actually, I used to think the same way, but after gradually engaging with the outside world, I slowly realized it was just an idea my kin had drilled into me. I don't see why others can't master draconic — let alone the fact that many incantations are written in draconic. Mastering draconic is a prerequisite for learning those magics. Of course, on a subconscious level I still feel shocked and uncomfortable when someone else speaks draconic. But I think that needs to change, little by little — after all, I've held these beliefs for a very, very long time..."

"It's a good thing you think that way, otherwise it would be really hard to live in the Mage Quarter," Jace said. Inwardly he thought Krasus must have gone through a similar shift in thinking — otherwise, with so many humans teaching draconic and learning draconic incantations in Dalaran, having lived there for hundreds of years, if he'd been as hung up on the draconic-language issue as the black dragons, wouldn't he have been driven crazy long ago?

The group drank and chatted, sharing a few observations about the city. When the subject of war drakes and black dragons came up, Sola asked, "How should we go about finding that method for dealing with black dragons next?"

"We can't count on that gnome finding clues first. Right now I plan to start by making a breakthrough with the warlocks here," Jace said. "It's just that the succubus... Namara isn't particularly willing to take us to meet the other warlocks for free."

Hearing this, Moruphil slightly raised an eyebrow, revealing a smile that was subtle but clearly meant for Jace to notice.

"Do you remember what the gnome said?" Sola murmured, taking a sip of ale. "There are many warlocks in the Shadowforge City arena who send their demons into the ring to fight and earn money. Of course, I'm not suggesting you take Moruphil into combat. But I was thinking — that should be an easy place to find all kinds of warlocks. Maybe we could find a good opportunity to strike up a conversation."

"Ah, yes." Jace tapped the table with his finger. "I had a feeling that gnome was trying to trick me into going to the arena — never thought of it that way. After we eat I need to get some sleep. My eyes are about to close on me. When we wake up, let's head to the arena and take a look."

Sola nodded. "I need to rest too, to recover my strength. I might sleep a while longer — remember to come wake me when you're planning to head out."

"Got it," Jace said.

"Then, master." Moruphil leaned slightly closer. "Soon we may be facing the brutal, bloody Shadowforge Arena. Aren't you planning to give your companion enough power to deal with whatever dangers might come?"

"My eyes are barely open," Jace said. "And I never planned to actually send you into a fight." The succubus murmured, "Then you need to stay on guard against possible dangers in that chaotic place at all times..."

"Fine, fine." Jace frowned and took a gulp of ale. "You've got a deal. Let me eat properly and build up some strength first."

Watching Moruphil's triumphant expression, Sola across the table couldn't help but steal a quiet laugh. When she caught Jace glancing her way, she pressed her lips together and pretended nothing had happened, taking a sip of ale.

As if changing the subject, she said, "Come to think of it, Grede would probably love it here."

"After we kill every single Dark Iron dwarf in the city first," Jace added under his breath. Sola couldn't hold back a laugh this time, and got choked by her ale, coughing twice.
