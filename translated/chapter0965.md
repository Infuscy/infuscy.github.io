## Chapter 965: Execution

Jace immediately perked up, involuntarily leaving his seat to lean against the front railing, squeezing in with the many Dark Iron spectators to peer toward the source of the rising clatter of the iron gate.

He cast a Demon Detection glance and saw a tall shadow walking slowly into the inner arena, accompanied by steady, heavy footfalls.

A gaunt, towering demon walked out of the tunnel and raised his head to survey the whole arena. His skin was a faint grayish-purple-blue, his body draped in black, reddish-edged razor-sharp armor. A pair of straight, goat-like demon horns grew out to either side from the top of his head. He held a longsword in each hand, and his eyes burned with fel-green flames, making it impossible to tell where his gaze was focused.

"An Eredar," Moruphil said, leaning close.

Jace noticed that on the opposite gallery and not far to the side, several imps and succubi had appeared, along with the Dark Iron warlocks who were very likely their masters.

So. Only a fight involving a demon could stir these fellows out of the shadows and into the open.

"Warbringer," a Dark Iron dwarf beside them said. "I finally get to see him fight again today. Didn't expect it to be an execution rather than a challenge match."

The Dark Iron dwarf next to him muttered under his breath, "I honestly thought Balreto couldn't handle the Twins!"

"This is the Wrathguard I told you about, master," Sazeno said, swallowing nervously. "They're all completely mad Eredar..."

"So the Dark Iron dwarves know how to summon Wrathguards. These folks have been holding out quite a lot," Jace said under his breath. "If Galders saw this, he'd be furious."

"Pft. Demons." Balreto was clearly not as cocky as before. He glanced at the raptor corpses he'd split open not far away, then dragged the axe out from under the cracked skull — only to find the haft had already snapped.

The Wrathguard tossed one of the swords in his hands to the dwarf. Balreto hefted it and swung it a few times, then leveled it. To him, the demon's one-handed sword was about as long as a two-handed blade.

Still, he smiled confidently and looked up at the Wrathguard. "Let us settle this..."

With that, he roared and charged straight at the demon with the sword. The Wrathguard adjusted his stance slightly and swept his sword across to meet him head-on. With a piercing clash of steel, Balreto was beaten off balance and staggered to one side.

The Wrathguard stepped past him in one motion, using his tall frame and long arms to thrust straight at the Dark Iron dwarf's back. With the enemy's height and reach combined, the dwarf might as well have been fighting a wielder of a pike — but Balreto still snarled and turned to block the Wrathguard's attack.

Jace saw his eyes grow bloodier by the second, his beard practically swelling with some terrible force. Jace frowned. He seemed to catch a familiar scent — one he really didn't care for.

Black dragon blood...

Had this Dark Iron dwarf already undergone the ascension ritual?

As Balreto grew more and more frenzied, his frame seemed even to swell slightly. He rained savage blows down on the Wrathguard with the demon sword, and now even the Wrathguard was having trouble holding his ground.

The demon retreated step by step, while the dwarf only fought more wildly. Between parries he suddenly drove his sword across the demon's belly, leaving a gaping wound that bled burning green blood. The Eredar let out a cold hiss and stepped back. The entire arena's spectators drew a sharp breath.

Jace noticed a succubi approach Adjudicator Griston and whisper something to him. Griston looked displeased at first, but in the end he nodded.

"Pull Samalelas out."

"Coward!" Balreto roared with mad laughter as he watched Samalelas, who had no choice but to retreat at his master's command, gritting his teeth. Jace could understand what these warlocks were thinking — summoning a Wrathguard was no easy matter. If anything happened to it here, that would be a terrible loss.

Clearly, the Dark Iron dwarves who'd organized this execution had also underestimated just how hard Balreto could fight. But Balreto looked as if he'd gotten exactly what he wanted — it seemed from the very start he'd come to shame these people with his actions in the Ring of Law.

Jace hadn't expected to walk right into a show like this. He'd already prepared himself for the possibility of missing the good part.

The Dark Iron dwarf raised the demon sword high. "Look well, Modgud — see what your dark successor has fallen to, daring only to send his pet into the ring, and this demon doesn't even dare fight me to the end. What are you afraid of?!"

As he roared this, the hair on his head actually caught fire. Jace recognized it clearly... this was black flame tinged with shadow. Black dragon magic.

"You demons." He pointed at the succubi and imps in the gallery. "Come down and fight me to the death, you gutless Twisting Nether monsters, you cloven-footed trash!"

Those demons clearly didn't dare go down, and their masters were even more eager to avert their eyes from Balreto's gaze. Moruphil's lips moved, and Jace noticed her tail whipping back and forth.

Jace knew what that meant. She was getting excited.

"Let me go, master."

"Don't make trouble. This one's clearly not ordinary," Jace said.

"A man running with black dragon blood..." Moruphil said, looking at him. "Let me prove myself to you once, master. Otherwise I'll never live down the disgrace of the lighthouse..."

Jace glanced over at Adjudicator Griston. A group of dwarf nobles were conferring about something — it looked like they were planning to resume the fight another day.

"Prove your blood to me, master," Moruphil said. "And isn't this the perfect chance you wanted to meet the warlocks here? Isn't this the best opportunity to get them to tell you... the knowledge and information you want?"

"Don't be impulsive, Moruphil," Sola whispered. "He really is strong."

"Do you really want to go? I don't need you to prove anything to me," Jace said. "Especially not because of the lighthouse."

"I just want to kill him," Moruphil said, lowering her eyes. "Don't worry. I know how dwarves fight, master."

Looking at her eyes, then back at the dwarf below who'd fought himself into a frenzy, Jace could only say in the end, "...All right then."

Moruphil smiled and vanished from in front of Jace in an instant. When she reappeared, she was already in the center of the circular arena. Jace frowned. He didn't care for this kind of fight, but he also knew he could hardly stop Moruphil from going down there.

He could force Moruphil to give up the idea, but he couldn't keep suppressing her pride indefinitely. After all, beyond being a servant, she was also one of Jace's best friends.

"Balreto Shadowsteel..." she called out. "I heard you wanted to challenge a demon?"

"Yes, but not a succubus." Balreto sneered. "Tell your master to go earn gold and fame for you in bars and brothels — that suits you better."

Moruphil held a sword in both hands, lowering her stance into a fighting posture. The fel tattoos across her body blazed with brilliant firelight. "Enough talk, you little dwarf."

By now the nobles and spectators upstairs had noticed what was happening in the circular arena.

"Whose servant is that?"

"Kill him, female demon!"

"Ha... how interesting. This succubus has no master either? She wants to go back to the Twisting Nether?"

"This succubus has no wings — what's going on?"

"Am I seeing this wrong? Is she a Wrathguard too?"

"Kill one more, kill one less — makes no difference either way," Balreto spat, raising the axe in his hand.

The shouting in the gallery grew louder and louder, and the nobles made no move to stop the fight. Moruphil's expression turned cold. She vanished again in a swirl of shadow. Balreto was not particularly surprised — clearly this wasn't his first time dealing with a succubus.

Judging by the look in the afterimage Moruphil left behind, even Jace's heart had skipped a beat. He'd rarely seen Moruphil this serious. She clearly cared deeply about what had happened at the lighthouse.

Just as he pressed his lips together slightly, Moruphil appeared behind Balreto without warning. Her black blade stabbed hard at the back of the dwarf's neck. The dwarf was clearly prepared and turned sharply to swing his sword — and that sword, driven by absurdly powerful force, was even faster than Moruphil's strike. The blade itself bent into an arc as it swept across, cutting straight through Moruphil's midsection from the side, slicing clean through...

Jace felt a chill in his own belly...

But the next instant he realized something was wrong. He hadn't felt the violent reaction of the Soul Link at all. That was just... an afterimage.

Moruphil reappeared not far away and, putting her whole arm into it, hurled the Blade of Eternal Darkness at Balreto, who had already committed to his swing. The blade struck the back of the dwarf's skull with precision and drove deep. The dwarf's whole body went rigid first, then he roared, reaching back to grab the hilt and dropping to his knees.

He kept cursing in Dwarvish, but his limbs had already begun to tremble uncontrollably, and he had no strength left to pull the blade out.

The gallery fell dead silent in an instant. Everyone stared at Balreto's reaction. The Blade of Eternal Darkness radiated a dark light, sucking away all the energy from inside the dwarf's skull pulse by pulse, causing the dwarf's body to convulse in kind.

The curses grew fainter and fainter, until at last Balreto's head and arms drooped. There was no more movement — only a mixture of saliva and blood dripping from his lips.

"The judgment of the Firelord has been carried out," Adjudicator Griston declared, though clearly not with much enthusiasm.

But the citizens of Shadowforge City didn't much care how Balreto died — as long as he was dead. The whole arena instantly erupted in cheers.

The dwarves boiled over, shouting the Firelord's name and titles, excitedly hurling silver coins, copper pieces, and all manner of miscellaneous things toward the center of the arena. An execution had been turned into something as lively as a championship bout.

Moruphil pulled the Blade of Eternal Darkness from the back of the dwarf's skull, looked up toward where Jace was sitting, and smiled, bowing with a kiss blown his way. Jace let out a relieved breath, nodded, and smiled back — then realized quite a few Dark Iron dwarves were curiously looking in his direction.

"Moruphil..."

Jace pulled his hood up, tilted his head toward Sola, and said, "I think we should get out of here."
