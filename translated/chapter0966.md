## Chapter 966: From Booty Bay

"Moruphil's technique was truly spectacular. I really hope that one day in my mortal form I'll be able to fight like her." The two of them were making their way quickly through a deserted corridor. Sola spoke as she walked.

Jace looked back toward the arena exit. He gripped the edge of his hood and glanced in the direction he'd come from. The dwarves' cheers were growing muffled and distant, harder and harder to make out in the endless black corridors and caverns.

"Didn't Essdreth teach you anything about fighting at all?" Jace asked.

Sola shook her head. "In the Emerald Dream I didn't need to fight. I only needed to learn how to influence the Dream's power, how to use my own energy to bring benefit to the Dream and nurture the creatures within it..."

Right then Moruphil appeared from the other side. "What did you think, master? Could I handle Vereesa Windrunner now?"

"If it were Vereesa, that throwing blade would have a hard time hitting her, Moruphil," Jace said, scanning their surroundings to make sure no curious Dark Iron dwarves had followed them.

"Yes, I'll reserve my judgment on that." Moruphil pulled two silver coins from her tightly bound breastplate and pressed them into Jace's palm. "She's a Quel'dorei with very strong magic resistance, yes, master — but I don't think she could fully withstand my magic."

Jace rubbed the coins together and tucked them into his pack. "What magic did you use?"

"I made that dwarf feel... that he was certain to defeat me," Moruphil said. "I don't think he needed my magic to feel that way already," Jace said.

"True. But I helped him bridge the gap between reality and illusion, blurring the line between confidence and arrogance..." Moruphil smiled slightly. "I imagine if Lady Vereesa were subjected to the same influence, she'd probably act even more recklessly and full of herself than this prisoner?"

"You'll never know unless you try," Jace said.

Moruphil asked, "Then will you give me that opportunity, master?"

"Once we finish what we're dealing with right now," Jace said. "Honestly, even if I could stop you, I couldn't hold back her urge to cut your throat."

Moruphil said pitifully, "If she slit my throat, and the demon blood — mixed with your power — flowed down my throat, my neck, my chest, and I collapsed weakly into the dust and begged that ranger for mercy... would you feel sorry for me, master?"

"You like having your skin cut open, don't you?" Sazeno asked. "If you really got beaten that badly, you should be howling with excitement like last night!"

"That's for the master. For her it's different," Moruphil said immediately.

Jace pressed his lips together and said nothing. Sola moved closer to Moruphil and asked, "Did you get hurt in that fight just now? I felt your power weaken considerably — quite a difference from before the battle."

"You should know that casting multiple spells in quick succession is extremely draining," Moruphil explained. "First I covertly influenced that dwarf's perception — against a warrior skilled at dealing with demons, that alone was a very difficult feat. Then I turned invisible, circled behind him, drew his attack with a Shadowstep, and with the shadow still unstable I threw that dagger with everything I had... Even nourished by the master's power, the energy I could expend all at once was still quite limited."

Sola thought about it, then said, "Your control over that dwarf's movements — the angles, the force — was really extraordinary. The afterimage left by that shadow-shift magic, if it had appeared even half a second too early, probably wouldn't have drawn his attack, and a fraction too late you might have been gutted with a single stroke..."

"I have our dwarf friend to thank for that," Moruphil said. "If I hadn't been working alongside him for so long, I wouldn't be so familiar with dwarven footwork and movement. But most importantly, the master's hard work and dedication last night — without your power, how could I possibly have defeated that terrifying madman?"

Sola glanced at Jace with an expression of watching a good show, while the succubus leaned even harder against Jace and said, "I knew the master must love me — he just isn't willing to say it out loud."

"I already regret letting you go down and duel that fellow," Jace said, looking at the corridor opening ahead that was about to lead onto a main thoroughfare, and lowering his voice. "Turn invisible now, Moruphil. Before long, tales of the wingless succubus executing a traitor might be spreading everywhere. We need to keep a low profile for a while."

Moruphil said, somewhat displeased, "Fine, since the master commands it."

Jace was of course worried that warlocks in Shadowforge City who'd heard of him and seen Moruphil would gradually connect this incident to Jace Sesso. If the news reached the black dragons, then his mission to investigate the area around Blackrock Spire would become suspicious.

Of course, on one hand, not everyone knew that the legendary Jace Sesso traveled with a wingless succubus — fortunately, he never used his real name when traveling with a succubus, and when he used his real name he never strolled around town with a succubus. On the other hand, Shadowforge City was blockaded by orcs on all sides, and the Dark Iron dwarves themselves were mortal enemies of the black dragons. In a city where information already didn't flow very smoothly, the chances of an execution gone wrong reaching the black dragons' ears in a short time weren't all that high either.

Jace simply didn't want to be too conspicuous and raise his chances of exposure any further than necessary.

But this matter wasn't without its benefits. Jace had come to the Ring of Law originally to find warlocks and gather information, and he'd been lacking an opportunity to make contact with them. Letting Moruphil make a bit of an impact this way, he might be able to use this fight as a calling card next.

The Black Iron Bar wasn't particularly lively at this hour. There was even a Dark Iron dwarf passed out snoring in the corner. Namara kept glancing at him from time to time, her eyes showing a hint of disgust, but for some reason she didn't seem willing to bother throwing him out.

When Jace and Sola appeared at the doorway, a few people glanced over, then lowered their heads back to their own drinks and food.

Namara looked over this way, gave a slight nod, and seemed to have genuinely turned into a polite barmaid rather than a demon.

The two headed to their quarters. As they reached the foot of the stairs, a Dark Iron dwarf appeared around the corner at the bottom, looking up in their direction. "You, right?" the dwarf asked in Common.

"Who?" Jace asked.

The dwarf glanced at Sola. "This doesn't concern you, ma'am. I have words for this human gentleman."

Jace sized up the Dark Iron dwarf before him. His skin had an unhealthy pale-gray cast, his hair was deep crimson, and his eyes glowed with a bloody light.

Even though every Dark Iron dwarf had shadow power flowing through their veins, the shadow energy radiating from this fellow was noticeably denser.

Looks like he hadn't run fast enough. This dwarf had probably taken a shortcut from the arena and gotten here ahead of him to lie in wait. "Anything we discuss doesn't need to be hidden from this lady. She's the one who brought me to Shadowforge City," Jace said.

"Since you don't mind, then I won't mind either," the Dark Iron dwarf said. "But having a powerful demon servant in Shadowforge City is no disgrace, nor is it strictly forbidden. There's no need for us to hide."

"I've seen you before, in the bar. Accompanying you was a beautiful wingless demon. I haven't lived long, but in these few decades the number of crippled demons I've seen you could count on one hand. To run into a wingless succubus twice in the span of a few days... that can't be a coincidence."

Looks like he didn't need a calling card after all — the man at the door had opened it himself.

"Is there something I can help you with, sir?" Jace asked.

"My name is Molus Blacksear," the dwarf said. "You may not have heard of me, but in Shadowforge City I am no nameless fool. Ever since the Dark Portal opened, I have devoted myself to studying orcish magic and using my findings to advance Shadowforge City's demon research."

"Yesterday when I saw you in the bar I could tell your servant was no ordinary character. This morning's fight only confirmed my judgment..."

Just then a drunken Dark Iron dwarf appeared at the top of the stairs, shuffling down step by step bracing himself on the handrail, then disappearing around the corner.

This Molus warlock's gaze followed the drunk step by step as he descended, then he continued, "I and many of my fellow warlocks have long believed that succubi are far more than assistants, lovers, and serving girls. Their physical capabilities and strength are more than sufficient to make them deadly warriors... But to this day, in Shadowforge City, conservative warlocks have at most used succubi to carry out assassination missions. That isn't combat. What I saw today was a true succubus's fight."

He looked up again. "I've summoned many succubi, and every single one of them has failed to meet my expectations. I want to know, sir — do you have some special method or secret?"

Jace swept his gaze across the dwarf opposite him with Demon Detection, then down the stairs, making sure no demons were lurking in stealth nearby, then said, "You know that's a secret."

"And we're both warlocks." Molus spread his arms, somewhat excited. "You and I both know how irresistible a new spell, a never-before-seen magic, is. So I would never ask you to hand over your most precious secret weapon for nothing, without giving anything in return..."

"You think I don't know what tricks you Dark Iron dwarves like to play?" Jace said, his tone carrying a trace of contempt. "Tweaking incantations here, dropping words there... I didn't come to Shadowforge City to do business with the likes of you, who have no credibility to speak of. I have more important things to do."

"I won't deny that. But at least gold doesn't lie," Molus said. "And, friend, it's possible that for what you came to Shadowforge City to do, my servants and I might be able to help."

Jace paused for a moment, put on an expression of having given it some thought, then smiled. "Jack Slovakia, from Booty Bay."
