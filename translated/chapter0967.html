Chapter 967: Mutual Exchange

By his dress alone, there was nothing to mark Molus Blacksear as a warlock.

He wore no robes imbued with demonic power or etched with void runes, and he carried no staff, no dagger or shortsword for channeling shadow energy. Still, from what Jace had seen and heard in Shadowforge City these past two days, it was clear this dwarf was no pauper. His deep-gray outer garment with crimson flame markings marked him as either wealthy or well-born — at the very least a man of no low social standing — and then there was the Dark Iron walking cane in his right hand.

Dark Iron was not "black iron," and it wasn't even fully black in color. It was a unique metal born of Blackrock Mountain, produced nowhere else. The Dark Iron dwarves believed that Dark Iron, like Saronite, was born of the elemental planes.

Their guess wasn't without reason, because the only anvil capable of forging Dark Iron was the Dark Iron Anvil deep within Shadowforge City — the very sacred relic Jace had nearly laid eyes on earlier. No other anvil, whether in Ironforge or Grim Batol, could successfully forge Dark Iron.

As a result, Dark Iron goods weren't cheap even on Blackrock Mountain, and beyond the mountain they were a rare sight indeed.

Molus led Jace and Sola along several corridors outside the barracks to a remarkably quiet district of Shadowforge City. By his sense of direction, Jace could tell this place was quite far from both the Black Iron Bar and the arena. From here, you could barely even hear the churning of the lava rivers.

Reaching the bottom of a spiral staircase, passing through a short corridor, and arriving at a deep-gray stone door, Molus stepped forward and raised both hands — one overhead, one at his waist — planted his feet, and began to chant an incantation.

As he chanted, Dwarvish runes on the door lit up one by one. What struck Jace as odd was that this wasn't shadow energy — it was some kind of arcane magic.

When all six runes had lit up, Molus lowered his hands, turned, and glanced at Moruphil, who had just revealed herself. He drew a breath. "Please, come in. Welcome. This is my home."

So it was just a residence. Jace had assumed it was some kind of secret vault.

He exchanged a glance with Sola. Without saying a word, Jace could tell she felt the same as him — this Dark Iron dwarf was putting on quite a show.

But Jace wasn't surprised. He'd heard before that Shadowforge City's particular specialty in magic was that mages and warlocks weren't separate camps. The mages here usually knew a fair bit of demon and shadow magic, and unless their talent was truly lacking, the warlocks would never pass up the chance to study the Arcane — after all, this wasn't a place riven by the divide between "dark magic" and "proper magic" the way the outside world was.

Realizing this, Jace even felt a twinge of envy deep down.

Wasn't this exactly the world he and the warlocks of the Dagger Society had always wanted? Demons walking the streets freely and without restraint, shadow magic permeating every corner of life...

The price, though, was a city filled with residents who were volatile, bloodthirsty, and pathologically deceitful — kept in relative check only by the living flames of Ragnaros seeping into every corner of the city, enforcing a brutal, surveillance-like control over this massive powder keg that could blow at any moment.

Compared to the Dark Iron kin Jace had encountered these past two days, this warlock actually seemed the most level-headed. After all, compared to ordinary people, warlocks were more familiar with shadow and had a clearer sense of how their emotions and shadow influenced each other. So the shadow casters who, out in the world, ought to have been the most crazed and paranoid were, in a city of shadow, made to look positively normal by contrast.

Before going in, Jace hadn't had any particular expectations for a dwarf's dwelling, but after passing through the entry corridor, he was still slightly taken aback by the spacious stone hall before him.

Accompanied by the sound of the stone door closing automatically behind him, the place reminded him of the chamber at the bottom of the lighthouse, dug out by some demon or dragonkin — though of course on a much smaller scale. Jace didn't think Resorel could have run through this hall without ducking.

But this place was considerably more refined than that chamber. The stone furnishings and metal decorations arranged around the room — the clothing, crowns, and other details on the dwarf statues — could even stand comparison with the dwarf nobles on the street.

Those high-ranking mages and prominent warlocks were all in the habit of furnishing their quarters with care. He couldn't help but silently resolve, once he was rich, to fix up Ilgath Tower to a similar standard in the future.

But the moment the concept of "being rich" drifted through his mind, it grew hazy and blurred, and his imagination began to fail him...

"Welcome home, Master Molus." A rather tall succubus walked out from inside. Though she called him "master," her tone and bearing were more like those of the lady of the house.

When her eyes fell on Moruphil, a flicker of hesitation crossed them, then she frowned and pressed her wings tightly against her back. "Go prepare some food," Molus said. "Give our guests a proper welcome."

The succubus gave an awkward smile. "There's only wine and some aged cheese in storage right now, Master Molus..."

"Go to Fremal and get some spider legs," Molus said, pointing outside. "Go now."

The succubus avoided Molus's gaze with a hint of displeasure, lowered her head uncomfortably as she passed Moruphil, and left.

She was already about as tall as Moruphil. Even among the Sayaad who'd taken part in assassinating mages during the Dark Portal, there hadn't been any this tall. That alone was enough to show Molus was remarkably skilled at summoning succubi.

After all, Jace had only been able to grow Moruphil to her current height thanks to the blood of the red dragon. Without Alexstrasza's blessing, she'd probably be half a head shorter even now.

After the succubus left, Molus immediately broke into a beaming smile and invited everyone to sit. "Here we can speak freely without worrying about being overheard. These walls may be thicker than an entire building in your surface cities. Come to think of it, may I know this Sayad lady's na—"

Jace tilted his head, signaling for Moruphil to answer for herself.

"Minrin," she replied without a moment's hesitation, then exchanged a polite smile with Jace.

Sola glanced at the two of them with a trace of puzzlement, as if wondering how, given that the two had barely exchanged a word this whole journey, Moruphil could so unhesitatingly know at once that Jace wanted her to give a false name.

Molus nodded respectfully. "I can see that Miss Minrin is a special kind of Sayaad, and very likely once served the great lord of the Burning Legion... I've studied these fel tattoos that strengthen a demon's power."

"As far as I know, there are also Sayaad types who are exceptionally skilled at combat. Their magic is agonizing, and their strength is no less than that of ordinary Mo'arg demons. But for decades now, the warlocks of Shadowforge City have been unable to reliably summon these powerful Sayaad. We've prepared many kinds of ritual materials, used immensely powerful soul gems to establish connections with the Twisting Nether, and discussed incantation problems with numerous demon scholars..."

Jace leaned against the stone armrest beside him. "Have you never thought about strengthening the demons you summon?"

"Of course," Molus said. "But it's not that easy to make a demon grow in strength... and they often don't survive very long in Shadowforge City either."

"They don't survive long in the arena, certainly," Jace said.

"So we'd rather seek a way to directly summon powerful succubi." Molus glanced at Moruphil, who was sitting there looking their way. "She's truly... captivating."

"To attract a powerful Sayaad, the required ritual might be extremely complex," Moruphil explained. "For example, killing an Eredar during the ritual — like the Wrathguard this morning. Slayers, Pain Maidens, and Battle Maidens... are unwilling to serve the weak. They won't be drawn in by simple love tokens. Perhaps you've all been misled by the Dalaran mages of a thousand years ago. But I can't give you very precise information, since every high-ranking demon has different interests. They won't bite as easily as those weak Sayaad, like fish in a river, dwarf."

"Then how did you summon Miss Minrin, Master Slovakia?" Molus asked.

"Luck," Jace said. "And something every warlock must have."

Molus sighed. "Fine. I didn't expect to find a shortcut to exploring demonology so easily... Still, the information Miss Minrin provided is still very valuable. I'll keep my promise, I definitely won't..."

"I haven't told you yet how she won that fight," Jace said.

"Huh?" the dwarf asked. "There's another method?"

"Since you can't manage long-term strengthening, I have a short-term one here," Jace said. "Have you ever heard of the Demonic Empowerment spell?"

Demisette Crowlas had dared to use this spell to trade with him for the incantation and casting method of the Hand of Gul'dan, and she'd stated with certainty that very few people knew it. Jace hadn't been quick to believe her, but he'd also basically never seen any other warlock use this spell.

When he'd executed that Dark Iron servant, he hadn't seen any trace of Demonic Empowerment strengthening on the Wrathguard either, so he was planning to gamble that no one in Shadowforge City knew this spell yet.

If the dwarves were familiar with it, he could use that as an excuse to stall Demisette a while longer when he got back. If the dwarves didn't know it...

"Demonic Empowerment?" Molus's dark-red bushy eyebrows twisted together all at once. "I think I've heard this is an orcish incantation, but... there really is a magic that can temporarily strengthen a demon servant's power?"

A wave of satisfaction rose in Jace's heart, but Moruphil glanced at him somewhat annoyed — after all, the master clearly hadn't used Demonic Empowerment, and now it seemed as if the credit for the fight had been snatched right out from under her.

"What do you intend to trade for it?" Jace asked.

The dwarf thought about it eagerly. "Name your price, Master Slovakia. Gold, or gems..."

"Come to think of it, I've suddenly taken an interest in your collection," Jace said, glancing toward the corridor entrance deeper in the hall. "I heard a human learned an incantation from a Dark Iron warlock here for summoning servants to his side. How about we start by using that as a show of good faith?"

"Ah, for warlocks of our caliber that's not a difficult spell at all." The dwarf stroked his beard, slapped the arm of his seat, and stood. "Follow me to the library. I'll show you the incantation records... Oh, you do read Dwarvish, don't you?"

"No problem," Jace said in Dwarvish.
