Chapter 97: Practically Sophistry

And that's what happened when you showed off — you got struck by lightning.

Is this the first spell I've learned? How the hell did he figure that out?

Was there a safer incantation he could use to brush this off?

Fear… absolutely not.

Summon Imp… don't make me laugh.

Demon Strangulation Curse… ha!

Life Tap… he hadn't even memorized the incantation yet.

Weakness… naga shadow magic?

No, no, no…

Was Marin asking because he'd sensed Jace had learned shadow magic?

It was impossible to detect shadow energy just from appearances. Jace had been coming and going from the Wizard Sanctum all this time, and not even the battle mages on guard had noticed anything off. What were the chances Marin had spotted something?

If he'd noticed earlier, wouldn't he have grabbed Jace and questioned him already?

After all, Jace had learned Fear a month ago. If Marin could have sensed it, he should have sensed it back then.

So Jace judged that Marin must have noticed something off from the way he'd just cast Light.

As for what exactly was off, Jace guessed that Marin could tell he'd learned incantations before — that his prior casting experience had influenced this Light spell, making him not look like someone casting for the first time.

Which meant Marin had no idea what incantation Jace had learned previously… maybe it was arcane?

Thinking of that, Jace realized there was still hope. He wasn't about to get thrown into a Stormwind dungeon just yet.

Find an excuse… find an excuse, Jace. You're the best at talking your way out of things — this is your moment!

What kind of influence could past casting experience have on a current spell? Mana? Focus?

Marin had said that mana could only be improved through sustained training. A month's time, and Jace hadn't been training intensely every day — could there have been a visible difference?

Then it had to be the difference in focus.

Jace figured his focus must have been far stronger than that of a complete novice learning their first incantation, to the point where Marin thought he'd had formal training, which raised his suspicions.

Most young apprentices rarely left the school once they enrolled, let alone had any real combat experience.

And Jace had already clawed his way back from the edge of death several times relying on that single Fear spell. His focus when casting magic was definitely not something those greenhorns could match. Since he'd settled on this being the reason, he also had to come up with an explanation for Marin… aside from having used shadow incantations, why was his focus stronger than everyone else's?

His mind turned another full circle, and Jace suddenly landed on a pretty decent explanation.

Swordsmanship.

This wasn't nonsense. The sword techniques Jace had learned from Grede, and the way he calmly ran through Grede's fencing principles in his head when facing an enemy, had kept him level-headed through multiple battles. That had done wonders for training his focus.

This was it.

Grede, even though you're not here — lend me your strength one more time!

"It's like this, Master," Jace blurted out. "Now that you mention it, I half-wonder if I did learn some magic before, after all — I didn't grow up far from Lordaeron's mage academy, so maybe I was exposed to it as a kid… But try as I might, I genuinely can't remember ever learning any other incantation. What I think really lets me hold my focus the way you described, even attempting energy-efficient casting, is something a dwarf warrior taught me about the mindset of wielding a sword."

Marin stared at Jace with a complicated expression.

"Swordsmanship… can help with casting too?"

"From my own experience, yes," Jace said. "When I was training Light, things went very badly at first. The moment I lost focus for a second, it would slip out of control, or I'd hold it a short while and it would drain all my energy, leaving me exhausted. So I didn't dare keep trying."

"Until that standoff with a boar out in Westfall. I started thinking — why not treat the Light I summoned as an enemy? Picture it as a boar that was about to gore me to death. I'd take it seriously, keep my eyes locked on its state, feel its temperature, learn its temperament…"

By this point, Jace was barely holding onto the logic of his own words. But that was only because he was so nervous. Stay calm, control your emotions, and keep bullshitting with a cool head. Victory or defeat hinged on this.

"That's exactly what that dwarf brother taught me, Master. He told me to keep the point of my sword aimed at the opponent at all times, maintain a deterrent against the enemy, and to always watch the enemy's movements without the slightest slack or slacking off… I think it's precisely that kind of discipline that gave me a better mindset for sustaining Light. Once I decided to do it that way, I could indeed hold Light much longer. With the focus I trained through swordsmanship, I could almost truly feel the temperature and temperament of the arcane energy gathering at my fingertip… It's not definite, but it's helped me cast far more effectively!"

Marin seemed to be getting a little dazed. It was a long moment before he spoke. "I have a feeling this could make for a rather interesting research topic, Mr. Sesso. Many elf mages are formidable swordsmen, for one thing because their lifespans are so long that they have plenty of time to train martial skills. But for another, perhaps it's exactly as you say — the elves know that training their bodies, their reflexes, and so on also provides a supplementary boost to their casting." He paused, then added, "Actually, I'd considered this before, but I never truly committed to spending time learning a martial art, and I've rarely met a student like you who'd actually tried it and could share the experience. Come to think of it, the masters in Dalaran may have already verified this."

Jace said, "This is just my guess, Master. After all, you started by saying I'd learned other arcane incantations… and I was completely lost. I haven't learned any others at all. Light is the first. If I had learned any, I wouldn't have thought I couldn't connect to the mana weave at all."

"Indeed, I was overthinking it." Marin clapped Jace on the shoulder. "Honestly, I was just worried about you. Learning arcane magic from crooked, back-alley sources is extremely dangerous. Some wild mages who know a bit about incantations like to modify them to suit their own tastes and then spread them to their students, inviting disasters no one can control. That's mainly what I was concerned about."

"I understand." Jace nodded heavily.

The back of his head was already drenched in sweat. After fielding Marin's questioning, he even had a fleeting illusion that handling this one problem might have set his life on an entirely different path.

In truth, it really was so. What would be the consequence of having a high-ranking Wizard Sanctum mage squeeze out of him the fact that he knew a whole string of shadow magic?

He'd probably be interrogated and searched, and end up exposing that whole crowd of warlocks at the Slaughtered Lamb. Jace had no confidence he could withstand medieval-style torture.

His future might just turn out to be that of a wild warlock banished from Stormwind, roaming Elwynn Forest with an imp, and after someone killed him they'd get a grimoire: Summon Imp, and learn a warlock's first spell.

In other words, a class-quest NPC that people killed over and over — the Northshire Abbey gatekeeper…

Just as his mind started drifting into boundless fantasy, Marin's voice pulled him back.

"Come with me, Jace. I'll keep my promise — after you mastered Light, I'd teach you a second spell. This time, you get to choose."

Marin turned and headed toward his study.

My choice?

He planned to learn a defensive spell related to the arcane, one that could stay in effect permanently, to patch up his biggest weakness right now — his total inability to deal with ranged magical attacks.

Jace realized that although casters made up an absolute minority of the opponents he'd encountered so far, every time one showed up it seemed to be a lethal threat.

His physical fitness was decent, and he had some combat experience. Against crude, bulky enemies, he could dodge and stall as much as possible, and he could use Fear to control them and buy himself time.

But against a caster?

On the beach, facing that naga sea witch's Arcane Missiles, Jace seemed to have only one counter — pure luck.

The first volley, he'd leaped clear on raw reflexes. The second? Just dumb luck.

If that arcane missile had hit him square in the back… Jace had no idea what taking a direct Arcane Missile would feel like, but it definitely would have been fatal. That female naga wouldn't have shown him the slightest mercy.

His Shadow Protection Potion was down to a single bottle now. The thought of going back to harvest Grave Moss… it made Jace's scalp tingle.

At the same time, he'd witnessed a shaman in Redridge wield both fire and natural elemental magic — wind and thunder.

As for the naga sea witch, she was an all-rounder who commanded water and ice magic, arcane magic, and shadow magic. In other words, just within the Kingdom of Stormwind itself, caster-type enemies were everywhere.

Not to mention that many mages had the ability to interrupt his casting. Fear's effectiveness against them was, at best, questionable. Catching a chance to Fear one of those seasoned mages might be harder than just killing them outright.

Was he supposed to carry a bunch of elemental protection potions every time he went out from now on?

Or stop taking risks altogether, stick to dealing with wolf packs and wild bears, and just coast from here on?

Absolutely not. So there was only one option in front of Jace — Mage Armor.

In the game, Mage Armor was a powerful buff that increased magical resistance across the board while also boosting mana regeneration.

But here… what kind of effect did it actually have?

Considering how the previous spells had performed, Jace guessed that even if there were a difference, it wouldn't be by much.

Just — was this spell hard to learn?

In the game, Mage Armor was a high-level spell you only learned later on.

If he asked to learn the Mage Armor incantation, would Marin laugh at him?

Yet the more complex the magic, the harder the incantation was to master, and the more powerful it was.

Take the Summon Imp incantation, for example. Because he'd used it so little, he still hadn't memorized it — he'd even forgotten how it started. And yet that one incantation had brought him a loyal companion…

His aptitude for arcane magic was far inferior to shadow, so he needed to learn as selectively as possible — put his limited time into training incantations that were truly useful and powerful, rather than wasting energy memorizing a pile of interchangeable, miscellaneous spells.

Mage Armor was unquestionably the best choice among defensive spells.

Marin's words had confirmed that, too — a persistent resistance boost was more universally applicable, protecting you from fatal harm in all kinds of complicated situations, whereas the various elemental barrier spells demanded far more in terms of casting skill and energy.

"All right, two conditions," Marin said at last. "First, learn the Arcane Intellect incantation first. You must practice this spell under the effect of Arcane Intellect — it greatly improves the accuracy of your incantation recitation and raises your mana ceiling, which reduces the danger. Second, you are not to practice it on your own when I'm not present. I have to watch you, so that if something goes wrong you can't control it — or worse, hurt someone else."

"I accept both," Jace said without hesitation.

Not in glossary; appears to be a made-up throwaway spell name in Jace's panicked internal monologue. - 北郡修道院守门员: rendered as "Northshire Abbey gatekeeper." Jace's joke about becoming a repeatedly-killed class-quest NPC. Not in glossary. - 虚弱术: rendered as "Weakness" (the naga's debuff). Not in glossary; later chapters clarify it as 虚弱诅咒/Curse of Weakness.
