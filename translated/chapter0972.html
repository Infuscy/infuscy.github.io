Chapter 972: The Thorium Brotherhood's Trust

Jace pushed aside layer after layer of black, scorched rags reeking of burnt cloth, making his way into the hidden room behind the bar. Behind him, Namara cleared the tables, the clatter of wooden mugs growing ever more muffled, and the sudden burst of dwarven laughter sounded as though it had been sealed away somewhere impossibly distant.

The dim corridor was unexpectedly cold. Old barrels of unknown contents were crammed in everywhere, along with battered crates and tools, piled high with slumbering heaps of dwarven clothing that lay about like filthy sandbags, all of them giving off a collective reek of rot.

If he'd been forced to stay here—a few days, a week, even half a month—perhaps he could have endured it. But he had no idea how anyone was supposed to spend decades in a place like this.

He coughed, alerting whatever residents might lurk here to his presence, then said in Dwarvish, "Mr. Edblocken."

"This way," came a reply in Common from behind him. Jace glanced over and noticed the half-concealed hatch in the corner shadow.

"Good afternoon, Mr. Edblocken." Jace pushed the door open and peered inside. A Dark Iron dwarf who had all but merged with the blackness of the hidden chamber slowly opened blood-red eyes, like a lone wolf lying in wait in the heat of night.

Suddenly an oil lamp flared to life with a whoosh, swaying back and forth as the flame caught. The cramped room brightened at once.

A Dark Iron dwarf with faintly bluish hair and beard was reclining on an iron-mesh bed in a gray vest, watching him. His skin was darker—black as coal—which made his beard look almost light by comparison.

That flame hadn't been kindled by arcane magic. It was a fairly pure elemental flame, quite unlike the Ragnaros fire-power that Adjudicator Griston had summoned to teleport him. But Jace hadn't even heard him speak the words of the elements to call for their help.

The dwarf tilted his head. "That belt buckle is already finished, but you really didn't need to risk getting caught by orcs to come all the way here and find me. I could have found someone to carry it out for you—just a small postage fee."

"You've got the wrong person, Mr. Edblocken," Jace said with a smile.

"Ah… hmm." The dwarf narrowed his eyes. "Well, there aren't that many young humans who come around here, and my eyes and ears aren't what they used to be. Do we have some kind of business between us?"

"We're meeting for the first time," Jace said.

"No one referred you?" the dwarf asked.

"Miss Namara," Jace said.

The dwarf chuckled. "Well then, what can I do for you?"

"I heard in Ironforge about a battle hammer that's quite famous among the Dark Iron clans," Jace said.

"Mm." Edblocken picked up an iron pick from beside the bed and licked it. "A hammer? Are you sure you don't mean two?"

"Two?" Jace asked, intrigued.

"Architect Phornos Darkvale's Ironheart, and another hammer called the Dissenter—without a doubt the two most famous battle hammers in Shadowforge City." Edblocken spoke around the iron pick clenched in his teeth. "The Dissenter isn't in Shadowforge City, and as for Darkvale… you probably won't get a chance to meet him either."

"I'm not talking about those two hammers," Jace said.

"Mm." Edblocken cocked one side of his beard. "Go on, then."

"A hammer connected to the Firelord," Jace said.

"The Firelord?" Edblocken shook his head. "I've never laid eyes on the great Firelord myself. I'm no sage, no priest. Though legend does say he wields a battle hammer—but did you come here to have me tell you fairy tales? You should go to the Lyceum, not me—that is, if they'll let you in."

There was mockery in Edblocken's tone, and his expression appeared relaxed on the surface, but Jace could clearly feel that the moment he'd mentioned the Firelord's hammer, the dwarf's wariness had sharpened steadily, to the point of carrying a hint of hostility.

He definitely knew about the Sulfuron Hammer, and it clearly meant a great deal to him. He was a member of the Thorium Brotherhood—no question about it.

After a few laughs, the dwarf's smile slowly leveled off. He glanced at the silent Jace. "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to say anything to provoke you. But regarding this Firelord's hammer, I truly can't help you much."

"Do you know a Dark Iron blacksmith in Ironforge named Ruige?" Jace asked.

Edblocken didn't answer immediately. He studied Jace for a moment before saying, "I know the name. As far as I know, he left Shadowforge City with the Thorium Brotherhood folk, for disrespecting the Firelord—sought trouble and found it."

If this man really was Roktus Darkcontract, he seemed unwilling to reveal his status as a senior Thorium Brotherhood member.

"I've no idea what you're trying to do here, young sir," Edblocken said. "If there's nothing else, I'd like to get some sleep. I've still got plenty of work left undone."

"Ruige said you could help me, honored sir," Jace came straight out with it. "He's the one who sent me here."

Edblocken laughed and looked at Jace. "I've barely met that man a handful of times. What does he want me to do for you—introduce you to that Firelord's hammer? You've got me confused. Maybe I'm getting old and can't keep up with what you young folks are saying."

Jace took the Elemental Tongue dictionary out of his bag and set it on the iron table beside the bed. "Have a look at this, sir."

Edblocken took the book, opened it, and saw the Thorium Brotherhood's seal inside, along with the book's lending slip signed by Ruige. The vaguely bewildered smile on his face gradually faded.

"What's your name?" he looked up and asked.

"I'm Jace Sesso," Jace said. "Mr. Roktus Darkcontract."

This time he hadn't addressed the dwarf by his Dwarvish clan name—he'd spoken the meaning of his clan name directly in Common.

The dwarf's lips moved slightly. He lowered his head, closed the book, and handed it back to Jace. "Jace Sesso of Grim Batol?"

"You've heard my name?" Jace asked.

"The human wizard who took part in the battle that brought about Deathwing's fall," the dwarf said. "Countless Dark Iron dwarves were heartened by that event—not because Grim Batol finally fell, but because many Shadowforge City citizens felt that Deathwing's demise would make the orcs and black dragons pause their attacks, perhaps even leave Blackrock Mountain. Though in the end the black dragons only grew more frenzied, and Shadowforge City's pressure only mounted—still, at the end of the day, it was a good thing."

As he spoke, the dwarf put on a purple leather jacket—crocodile skin, or some kind of reptile—and picked up a lantern hanging nearby, shining it Jace's way. "Prove that you're Jace Sesso."

Jace drew the demon sword at his waist halfway, letting its arcane light flood the room and drown out the lantern's dim glow. "Song of the Blue Sky," he introduced it.

Accompanied by a smooth, musical note, the dwarf drew a soft breath. His gaze rested on Jace's hand gripping the hilt, and he slowly shook his head. "Its blade looks even more like some kind of jade than the Song of the Blue Sky in the paintings. But even if this is the real thing, what does it have to do with proving you're Jace Sesso?"

Only then did Jace realize that Shadowforge City, cut off from other races by diplomacy and from the orcish wars, was practically sealed off from the world. And this blacksmith had been cooped up here god-knows-how-long—it was already remarkable that he'd even heard Jace's name. Not knowing that Vereesa had given him the Song of the Blue Sky was perfectly understandable.

He had no choice but to sheathe the sword, then pull out his Stormwind royal court mage advisor's sigil and the Lordaeron knight's badge, setting them on the table. Both rings were engraved with his name.

The dwarf picked up the two rings and examined them before saying, "Not bad—these are the genuine articles… All right, Jace Sesso, you really are that human I've been hearing about. I'd imagined you'd be a bit more muscular."

He smiled. "Yes, I am Roktus Darkcontract. The Thorium Brotherhood members outside have probably already told you about me. This Elemental Tongue dictionary is indeed the Thorium Brotherhood's property here in Shadowforge City—that alone is enough to show you've earned their trust."
