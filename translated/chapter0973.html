Chapter 973: Power and Mission

Roktus handed the rings back to Jace. "As you've heard, Ragnaros wields that fearsome battle hammer called Sulfuras, whose eruptions of power were enough to birth Blackrock Mountain—and could just as easily destroy it. Many dwarves who sought to defy his rule believed that only by forging a battle hammer to rival it could they withstand his wrath, though plenty more thought that was a pipe dream. Be that as it may, with that goal in mind, the Sulfuron Hammer was designed. Members of the Thorium Brotherhood, along with fire-tenders who refused to be enslaved by the flame of corruption, all took part in the design. Only…"

"Well, you can probably guess the rest," Roktus continued. "The Sulfuron Hammer is a weapon that was never actually forged, Jace Sesso. The last time the rebels tried to secretly manufacture that weapon, they were caught by Ragnaros's lackey Incendius and dragged before the Ring of Law for trial. They were torn to pieces and tossed into the Broodmother spider caves… Of course, back then the lower reaches of Blackrock Spire still belonged to Thaurissan."

"Incendius," Jace said. "So the rulers of Shadowforge City don't want to see this weapon come into being. But to forge it, they'd have to use the Dark Anvil—the sacred relic here in Shadowforge City."

"Summarized nicely," Roktus said. "That last attempt was romantic and genuinely absurd—and yet it suits this hammer, which is both contradictory and perfectly logical. You can't admit it truly exists, and yet it shapes the fate of countless mortals, even gods themselves. I'm curious, Jace Sesso—why are you interested in this weapon? As far as I know, you're a warlock. Now that I've seen you, you don't exactly look like a blacksmith or a warrior who'd be any good swinging a battle hammer. Or is this just part of a Stormwind mage's research tour?"

"Forgive me, sir," Jace said with an apologetic smile. "Before coming to Shadowforge City, I had no real concept of what this weapon was or how difficult it would be to make. I'd originally only planned to find a suitable weapon for a friend who'd lost his. Now I can see that idea was truly naive."

"The lord of Aerie Peak?" Roktus asked.

"You've heard of him," Jace said.

"Of course." Roktus folded his arms and propped his feet up on the bed. "A human wizard, a dwarf gryphon rider, and a Windrunner noblewoman. I've no love for Wildhammer dwarves, but I can't help admiring those gryphon riders' might. Back then I kept hoping to hear the good news that the witch Modgud had been killed by gryphon riders in Grim Batol—and in the end she did die there, but it didn't change our fate."

Jace blinked. "You were born during the War of the Three Hammers?"

"The War of the Three Hammers? My name was already famous throughout Ironforge before the dwarves split," Roktus said.

Jace asked, "So you're nearly three hundred years old?"

"That's nothing," Roktus said. "If you're on good terms with the elves, my age shouldn't surprise you."

"Mm." Jace nodded. "Maybe it's because ages in the thousands feel too unreal to someone like me—a human—lacking any real sense of scale. Three hundred years feels a great deal more concrete."

Roktus laughed. "You're a good lad, Jace Sesso."

"But have you been hiding here in Shadowforge City like this for all three hundred years?" Jace asked.

Roktus took a deep breath, and his blood-red eyes lost their focus for a moment.

"At first, the Thorium Brotherhood was admired by Ragnaros for their exquisite craftsmanship. But not all the brothers were willing to mass-produce weapons for his armies—many saw it as a waste of talented artisans' gifts. When we raised our concerns to the Firelord, we came to realize that this so-called ruler's presence over all of Shadowforge City was no blessing at all, but a searing, flesh-corroding yoke. And so the Brotherhood's members gradually left Shadowforge City."

"By then I was already quite old, so I volunteered to stay behind as a hostage, to win Thaurissan's trust—to make him believe we wouldn't aid Ironforge and Aerie Peak in attacking Blackrock Mountain in return—so he'd stop hunting down my students and colleagues. But so many years have passed now, and Shadowforge City has changed rulers several times over. I doubt anyone here still remembers I'm a hostage."

"I shouldn't have let this old man ramble on about so much ancient history. Let's get back to the matter at hand." He sat up. "You want to forge the Sulfuron Hammer for that Wildhammer lord… It's not entirely impossible. But whoever wields that hammer must shoulder the mission that the countless Dark Iron dwarves who designed it poured into it—to shatter Shadowforge City's yoke and end Ragnaros's enslavement of all the Dark Iron. That is the fate of the hammer and its wielder."

Defeating the Firelord Ragnaros? Jace was momentarily stunned.

Before Roktus had said all this, he hadn't realized how heavy this matter truly was.

In the game, this wasn't anything difficult—Molten Core wasn't that hard as a raid.

But setting foot here, gazing out over the whole of the Burning Plains, lifting his eyes to the rolling ridges of Blackrock Mountain beneath the thick clouds, watching the terrifying magma surge and flow. Then descending further in, beholding that magnificent dwarven marvel, the rivers of lava running beneath the floor of Shadowforge City, and the countless flame guardians walking its streets… All of this had come into being with Ragnaros's arrival. Grasping that, one could truly feel the power of a "god" that the dwarves worshipped.

Though Shadowforge City had no shortage of cunning, treacherous folk—swindling them, profiting off them, that carried little psychological weight—Roktus Darkcontract was nothing like them. Jace couldn't bring himself to dupe these Thorium Brotherhood blacksmiths into risking enormous danger to forge a legendary battle hammer for him, then just pretend he'd never heard of the mission the hammer carried.

Besides, would Grede even agree to take on that promise?

Jace knew full well that while Grede was willing to acknowledge the Dark Iron dwarves as his kin, distant relatives, and deeply admired the Thorium Brotherhood's craftsmanship, he wouldn't hesitate for a second to smash those relatives' skulls in with a hammer. After all, the terrible crimes the Dark Iron dwarves had committed against other dwarves, especially in Grim Batol, could never be forgiven.

Saving the Dark Iron dwarves? It was hard to connect that with the name Grede Thunderfist—one could even say the two things were complete opposites.

"This is no ordinary mission, sir," Jace said. "I can't make that decision for him."

"I understand the Wildhammer position." Roktus seemed well aware of it too. "However, I do have a battle hammer here—the hammer of the Wildhammer King, Khadros Wildhammer, recovered from the ruins of Grim Batol. Thaurissan wanted us to use it to find the gryphon riders' weaknesses, so the Thorium Brotherhood kept it."

"A battle hammer with over two hundred years of history?" Jace asked.

"Yes. A long while ago, the Explorers' League sent someone to contact me wanting to buy this hammer, but I refused them—after all, it belongs to the Wildhammer clan." Roktus stood. "Come with me, Jace Sesso. I think it would gladly serve the hero who saved Grim Batol. Perhaps it's been waiting here all this time for this very day."

The old blacksmith hobbled out of the room, leading Jace to another storage chamber deep in the hall. It was thick with dust—the moment they entered, a great cloud of it billowed up, and Jace couldn't help coughing.

"Sorry." Roktus waved a hand, sweeping his lantern across one crate after another.

Though the place was covered in dust, the dust-covered crates looked remarkably fine. Where the dwarves had wiped the dust away, ornate gold and silver engravings and runes showed beneath—perhaps property the Thorium Brotherhood hadn't been able to take with them, kept in Roktus's care.

The old dwarf hauled a hard-leather case out from the back of a cabinet, found a small blackened key on his ring of keys, and opened it.

Lifting the lid revealed a small, square battle hammer. Even someone unfamiliar with the elements could feel the faint traces of lightning still lingering on it, causing the hammer's head to glow with a natural pale light.

The hammer bore many pale-blue decorations. Jace rarely saw a weapon with such a dwarven aesthetic. Roktus stroked a blue patch on the hammer's surface. "Take a look. In the age of the Mountain King, weapons forged by the three hammers together were works of art. But all of it was shattered by endless greed, division, and arrogance…"

"This hammer is called the Skyshatter, Jace Sesso." He placed the hammer back in the case, closed the lid, and wiped the dust from it. "Take it out of Shadowforge City. Let it return to its rightful owner."

"I will." Jace took the case. "I thank you on his behalf, sir."

The hammer of the Wildhammer King, Khadros Wildhammer?

If Grede found out, he'd probably lose his mind with joy.

"…Oh, right, there's one more important thing I wanted to ask."

"Go ahead, Jace Sesso." Roktus leaned against a nearby crate. "Everything else can wait."

Jace smiled. "I came to Shadowforge City partly to learn about the Sulfuron Hammer, but the other part—the more important part—is that I came at the behest of the King of Stormwind, seeking a way to break the black dragons' magic. I'd previously learned that the fire-tenders of Shadowforge City seemed to possess a secret incantation that could be used against the black dragons of Blackrock Spire. But as a human, I really couldn't find any good way to make contact with the fire-tenders and learn more…"

At that, Roktus couldn't help but laugh. "That Ruige fellow told you a bit too much, Jace Sesso. Still, if he knows your true identity the way I do, telling you these things is no great matter. I was indeed a fire-tender of considerable rank in the past, free to come and go from Thaurissan's palace, exchanging techniques of working with flame alongside the other elder fire-tenders. But all that changed when the Thorium Brotherhood left. From that point on, they were even more wary of them than they are of an outsider like you."

Here, he narrowed his eyes. "I have heard of that method for dealing with black dragons, though. Some of the bar's customers mentioned that mysterious thing—said to have been obtained from a group of orcish traitors."

"Yes, that's the one," Jace said.

Roktus shrugged and spread one hand. "I'm sorry, but there's nothing I can do about it now."

"No need to apologize, sir." Jace nodded. "I'll find another way."

"Hold on a moment."

Just as Jace was about to take his leave, Roktus called him back.

"Do you know what's even more respected among fire-tenders than a senior fire-tender?"

"A Thaurissan?" Jace asked.

"Exactly." Roktus smiled. "But you can hardly expect the emperor of Shadowforge City and his family to help you."

"Ah…" Jace suddenly understood. "A fire elemental."

"Yes." Roktus nodded. "More precisely, the 'offspring' of the Firelord Ragnaros. If a high-ranking elemental could introduce you to the fire-tenders, wouldn't that be far more useful than me taking you there?"

He drew an old black iron ring from his waist and handed it to Jace. "Take this and head to the Order Hall east of the arena. There you'll find the warden of the Order Hall—a magma lord named Lokol. He was once one of Ragnaros's lieutenants, but he long ago came to recognize Ragnaros's madness and evil, so he broke away from Molten Core and willingly exiled himself to watch over us from the outside."

"In truth, he's been a secret ally of mine and the Thorium Brotherhood all along, Jace Sesso. Ask for his help. Have him take you to meet the fire-tenders—they certainly won't refuse."
