## Chapter 974: "Mountain King," or Keep Wandering?

Back at the lodgings, Jace set the leather case on the table and stared at the faint embossed pattern on it.

Recalling his conversation with Roktus Darkcontract just now—apart from the Dark Iron dwarf's initial wariness toward him, he'd rarely shown any particularly intense emotion, nor had he been hostile toward humans or Wildhammer dwarves the way other dwarves were.

Unlike the majority of elves, who'd spent centuries or even millennia wallowing in idle luxury in Quel'Thalas and still remained utter children at heart, Roktus Darkcontract's three hundred years had been spent in constant rises and falls.

From being a highly renowned master smith among the three hammers in the beginning, to being forced to pick a side in the war, to witnessing the destruction of Thaurissan's city and the curse of the Firelord falling upon all his kin… to awakening, preparing to break away from Shadowforge City, and ultimately having to stay behind voluntarily as a hostage so Thaurissan would spare the Thorium Brotherhood. From the very pinnacle to the very bottom, from glory to ruin—he hadn't merely witnessed it all, he'd been swept up in it.

And yet even a man like that still lit up at the mention of saving the Dark Iron dwarves. He didn't care what kind of dwarf took up the Sulfuron Hammer—so long as he could liberate Shadowforge City, so long as he could bring hope of peace among the three hammers…

But could Grede Thunderfist shoulder that responsibility? Jace didn't know. Grede had fled Aerie Peak, then tried to flee again to Kalimoth, and had declined the position and responsibilities of a Wildhammer lord—holing up in Stormwind all this time, looking for all the world as if he had no interest in dwarven affairs.

And yet at the same time, he'd always wanted to become a true Mountain King.

For the three hammers, what greater feat was there than liberating the Dark Iron dwarves from the yoke of the fallen flame? If this didn't qualify as a true Mountain King, if this didn't earn a place in all of dwarven history… Jace couldn't imagine anything worthier.

Of course, this fellow's contradictions didn't end there—sometimes he was as brave as a lunatic, and at other times timid enough to give Jace a headache. Jace decided he'd stop agonizing over exactly what outcome the dwarf was trying to achieve. He'd tell him about it and let him figure it out himself.

But what if he was willing? What if he wanted to claim the hand of the Firelord Ragnaros, wanted to become the hero who saved Shadowforge City?

Judging purely from Roktus's description, it sounded as though grasping the means to stand against the Firelord would be enough to shatter his yoke… But in truth, under Ragnaros's command there were not only countless fire elementals, but also a host of Flamewalkers.

These were enormous lizard-like fire creatures resembling naga, with evil, demonic faces and bodies covered in charred horns and tentacles. From a distance they looked like flames squirming their way out of lava. They roamed the elemental plane of fire and the volcanoes and lava flows closely linked to it, carrying out the orders of their "Executor" and his direct superior—Ragnaros.

Ever since Ragnaros had been summoned to Blackrock Mountain and created Molten Core, the vast majority of Dark Iron dwarves had never laid eyes on Ragnaros himself again. The ones passing orders between them were these Flamewalkers, so the Flamewalker leaders had already become something like demigods in the eyes of the Dark Iron dwarves.

Judging from some Dark Iron dwarves' descriptions of Ragnaros, many Shadowforge City citizens had probably come to imagine the Firelord looked like these Flamewalkers. Considering that even Roktus Darkcontract, a fire-tender of such exalted rank, had never seen Ragnaros in person, that was only natural.

Jace still remembered those twisted murals he'd seen in the Thaurissan ruins—the depictions of flame were like abstract fire serpents wielding spears and halberds. Those were probably what the Flamewalkers looked like.

Even in the game, these Flamewalkers had spent two centuries operating in Molten Core, already using powerful lava runes to protect the throne of Ragnaros's Flamewalker lieutenant, the Executor Executus.

Here, the state of those runes was probably far more complicated than in the game.

In the game, the way to deal with those runes was provided by a water elemental lord—Hydraxian Waterlord of Azshara, who was bent on thwarting Ragnaros's plan to return to the world, but lacked the strength alone, so he sought the players' help.

How was Jace supposed to find the Hydraxian Waterlord here?

At the very least, on the coast of Azshara earlier, Jace hadn't seen a single water elemental. The fact that he'd be there a decade from now didn't mean he'd be sitting on the same reef a decade earlier.

Just then, a rumbling roar of magma echoed from who-knows-how-many layers of rock and walls away, reaching the room like the Firelord laughing at the futile efforts of all who defied him.

Jace snapped back to his senses somewhat. He had to admit the allure of an orange weapon was powerful enough—he'd already started secretly plotting how to overthrow an elemental lord right here.

"Miss Solamriel is still waiting for you outside, master," Morophil reminded him.

"Mm. Keep an eye on our things, especially this hammer." Jace felt for the black iron ring in his bag, threw on his cloak, and walked to the door. Morophil waved a hand. "Don't worry."

Stepping outside, Sola was leaning against the end of the corridor, watching his way.

Though she wore a flawless Dark Iron dwarf's face, just as Grede had said, she still lacked a certain dwarven quality—something all the more obvious in Shadowforge City, where nearly everyone had such a distinctive style.

But then again, Shadowforge City had every kind of person imaginable, which gave the place a rather warped tolerance for anything "strange"—so much so that a dull, composed Dark Iron dwarf like Sola didn't stick out at all.

After coming back, Jace had already told Sola in detail about his conversation with Roktus Darkcontract. The next step, then, was to head to the Order Hall he'd mentioned and find that magma elemental.

Jace clearly remembered that the wandering elemental Lokol was an hostile boss—every time he entered Blackrock Depths he'd casually kill him. He hadn't expected this fellow to be a mole hidden within Ragnaros's army.

The two of them left the Black Iron Bar and followed the route they'd taken before toward the Ring of Order arena, since it was fairly close to the arena.

Crossing a black iron bridge, he caught a glimpse of the Dark Iron dwarf barracks on the cliff beneath the lava river—those cold helmets in the windows, and the shadows of soldiers sweeping past in neat formation, accompanied by the sound of orderly footsteps.

In the Blackrock Mountain of today, the black dragons and fire elementals kept the orcs and Dark Iron dwarves locked in a stalemate—neither side could do anything to the other, which was why neither had the energy to expand outward. Even so, the orcs and Dark Iron dwarves still made constant small moves on all sides.

If either side suffered a heavy blow while the other caught its breath, it would grow in strength—and then the ones who'd suffer would be Ironforge and Stormwind. Whatever Grede and the other Dark Iron dwarves thought, this wasn't a decision that could be made in a short time.
