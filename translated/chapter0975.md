## Chapter 975: Which Side Are You On?

The area between the Order Hall and the arena was a roughly excavated stretch—the floor and walls were all crude, dark rock that looked freshly hewn.

Dwarf soldiers stood guard and patrolled here with core hounds, and from time to time groups of miners and transport carts rolled through, their wheels clattering over the uneven stone with a clanging racket that echoed through the whole space, thoroughly irritating.

In the game, this was a great spot for grinding experience—mages liked to bring one person to aggro all the Dark Iron dwarves, lure them to the door, and then wipe them all out with a single Blizzard. Jace glanced toward the cave mouth outside, where a number of battered carts and crates had been piled up, along with a few workers and soldiers standing around talking—that was probably the spot.

After living in Shadowforge City for a while and speaking with some of the soldiers, miners, and blacksmiths, Jace found it a little hard to recall the specific scenes from the game.

Gathering all those people together, luring them around a corner, and then flash-freezing them to death with a Blizzard—just imagining it felt deeply unsettling. Sola walked ahead, Jace followed behind, putting on the air of a Dark Iron dwarf leading a foreign visitor.

Though a dwarf and a human walking among a crowd of Dark Iron dwarves was hardly ordinary, a few wall-gazers waiting to be let into the arena to watch the fun were leaning against the wall not far off—two of them goblins—so it didn't stand out all that much.

"Which way?" Sola asked quietly, looking at the fork ahead.

Jace whispered back, "How about left?"

"Left it is." Sola didn't hesitate or second-guess much, striding straight down the left-hand passage.

Jace knew that the Order Hall was less a hall than an order corridor—it formed a ring around the prison at its center, so no matter which way you walked you'd come out the other side, traversing the entire Order Hall.

Entering the fork brought them to a cell block. Dwarf soldiers guarded the cell doors, and fire elementals overseeing the area passed from time to time alongside trainers leading packs of core hounds, making their rounds to inspect the situation. Occasionally the sound of a distant cell door opening could be heard, followed by shouting—"Don't take me to the arena!" "Damn coward!" and the like.

In truth, there was basically only one punishment for breaking the law in Shadowforge City: being dragged into the Ring of Order arena to fight a few rounds against the demons and monsters dragged in from all over Azeroth. If you died, you died. As for survivors—in the time since Jace had arrived in Shadowforge City, he hadn't heard of a single one.

Because there was no prison sentence, Shadowforge City's cells basically served only two purposes. One was to hold criminals waiting to take the stage—like that Dark Iron dwarf warrior of the Shadowsteel family who'd taken one of Morophil's throwing knives in the back of the head earlier; he'd probably been locked up here for some time before entering the arena.

The second purpose was to hold prisoners of war who still had value, to squeeze more useful intelligence out of them—such as Marshal Reginald Windsor of Stormwind, who'd later been captured and brought here.

If he hadn't held his ground and refused to compromise with the dwarves, if he'd been unable to endure the long torment of imprisonment and revealed everything he knew, he'd probably have been tossed into the Ring of Order arena long ago to fight the beasts to the death.

Just then, a dwarf soldier beside them lifted his helmet's visor and pointed at Sola. "What are you here for? That human can't go any further!" he demanded in Dwarvish.

Sola was just about to say something when an explosion suddenly echoed from the passage ahead, drawing everyone's attention.

Then Jace increasingly felt a tremor under his feet. The sensation grew stronger by the second, and soon a complaining voice came from one of the cells nearby.

"Don't let it get close! Keep it away from me!"

"Shut your mouth, you scum!" the dwarf soldier outside bellowed through the cell door's little window, trying to silence the prisoner inside—but his voice was quickly drowned out by a churning of stone and earth.

The area around the Ring of Order had always been sweltering, but now it grew hotter and hotter. The dwarf soldiers who'd been on patrol wordlessly moved to the wall and stopped to wait, as if they already knew what was about to happen.

"Over there." The dwarf soldier waved a hand at Jace and Sola.

Prompted thus, they sensibly pressed themselves to the side and watched the source of the noise. Before long, a fiery glow grew brighter and brighter down the passage, and a massive earthen monster wreathed in molten red light appeared.

It looked much like the lava creatures Jace had seen besieging Grede in Grim Batol, but far taller. The most obvious difference was the two golden bracers bound to its black stone arms, covered in glowing fire runes.

The creature was made up of a dozen or so enormous rocks piled atop one another, held together by flowing lava and flame at its center. When it walked, the stones beneath its feet rolled in swirling fire-winds that drove its whole body lurching forward, making it hard to judge its exact height.

But no matter how it bobbed, it stood at least three meters tall—far larger than most humans. Seen from here, it rivaled a sturdy tauren.

Compared to its hulking, broad black-stone body, its head was downright comical—only about the size of a human fist, like a chip that had been knocked loose when its body parts collided, floating at the very top. Two flickering flames burned on that "head," sweeping back and forth like eyes.

Both dwarves and fire elementals gave the creature a wide berth. The fire elementals seemed to dim their own brightness considerably, greeting its appearance with an attitude of utter deference.

This had to be Lokol, the warden of Blackrock Depths.

In the game he wasn't much to look at, but here the Dark Iron dwarves called him "Lord Lokol," and judging from the reactions of the other flame guardians, Lokol's status here was extremely high. Just as Roktus had said, his past relationship with the Firelord Ragnaros had probably been genuinely close.

The closer it came, the hotter the whole passage grew. Even the Dark Iron dwarf soldiers couldn't help but pry open their armor. The arena spectators who'd been lingering nearby had all vanished by now.

As waves of intense heat pressed in, Jace also seemed to hear a faint, murmured whispering in the air.

Were these the murmurs of the elements? Since arriving in Shadowforge City, this was the first time Jace had heard the Elemental Tongue. Only these murmurs didn't seem to be directed at him—just incomprehensible, meaningless babbling.

"Is that it?" Sola asked quietly.

"Yes." Jace stepped forward and called out in a loud voice, "Lord Lokol!"

Hearing the shout, Lokol's little head suddenly swiveled two hundred and seventy degrees toward them, and his whole body ground to a rumbling halt. "Speak, mortal."

The magma elemental lord could speak Common, though the bubbling rumble of magma that accompanied it made him a little hard to make out.

The dwarf soldiers were clearly displeased that Jace had stopped Lokol in his tracks—they glared at him angrily—but with the elemental lord right in front of them, they seemed reluctant to make a scene.

This was hardly the place to openly declare he'd been sent by Roktus. Jace pondered for a moment, then said in a low voice, "I have intelligence about a black dragon spy here in Shadowforge City to report to you, Lord Lokol. I can only trust an element—I can't trust the dwarves."

The two nearby dwarf soldiers murmured something to each other. Whether they understood Common was unclear. Lokol was silent for a moment, then said, "Follow me."

Jace let out a slow breath, glanced at Sola, and then followed the elemental lord.

Lokol had clearly reduced his pace—before, he'd been moving so fast that ordinary people would have had trouble keeping up. Still, Jace was hoping they'd reach their destination sooner rather than later, because trailing behind this magma element was brutally hot—even from nearly ten meters away.

Following Lokol into an unoccupied cave, the whole space glowed red from the lava flowing off his body. Lokol's head and body rotated in opposite directions. He lowered his gaze and spoke in a voice so deep it was nearly the rumble of underground magma: "Now you can tell me, human. And tell me where you got this intelligence."

Jace held out Roktus's black iron ring to Lokol. "There is no black dragon intelligence, Lord Lokol. We were hoping you could do us a favor."

The elemental lord fell silent for a moment after noticing the ring's engravings, then asked, "Who are you?"

"I'm Jace Sesso," Jace said. "You may have heard the name."

The elemental lord's head drifted upward in the heat wind. He glanced at Sola beside him, then said, "Roktus Darkcontract is willing to help you, but that doesn't mean I have to, Jace Sesso. I know who you are. Deathwing's fall made the whole earth tremble—every inch of stone and soil in Azeroth whispered his name. Some grieved and raged at his death; some rejoiced and felt relieved. But I know that to torment your enemy, you didn't hesitate to entwine shadow and flame, to make them tear at each other. I've endured enough of that endless whispering torment in Molten Core—I want no part of those dark chains."

"The favor I'm asking has nothing to do with shadow," Jace said. "I came to Shadowforge City to deal with the black dragons, Lord Lokol. I heard the fire-tenders possess an incantation that can break the black dragons' magic…"

Lokol cut him off with a sound like shattering stone. "You already have an inseparable bond with shadow, Jace Sesso. Leave. I'm sure a wizard who could handle Deathwing can find a better way to solve his problems."

This burning rock was maddeningly stubborn. Jace was calculating how to persuade him when Lokol had already turned to leave.

Sola drew a sharp breath, then turned and shouted, "Stop, Lokol."

"Given that you're Jace Sesso's friend, insolent Dark Iron dwarf, I won't throw you into the Executor's lava river—but you get this one chance." The elemental lord's voice was furious.

Jace looked at Sola, puzzled. Sola said, "You want to sever yourself completely from the corruption and decay of the Old Gods, don't you?"

Hearing the words "Old Gods," Lokol's entire temperature visibly dropped—the cave even darkened considerably.

Sola continued, "If you have that desire, then you should know who can truly free you from those dark chains—"

"Who can make you a free element again."

While the elemental lord was clearly struggling to process this, the blood-red color in Sola's eyes gradually faded, rippling into a silver moonlight.

She extended her hand and opened her palm. The dark skin there began to flush green, covered in layer upon layer of dense scales, and her fingertips turned into bright-purple claws.

"You are a child of the Dream Queen," Lokol said. "Why would you leave the Emerald Dream and come here?"

Though Sola showed no outward reaction, Jace could clearly sense a flash of guilt from her.

She steadied herself slightly before saying, "That's none of your concern, Lord Lokol. Deathwing has fallen. Sooner or later a new Dragon King will take the throne—for an ancient element like you, that day will come quickly. And when it does, even if you've managed to break free of Ragnaros's control, you'll only fall back into the black dragons' clutches—unless… you accept the purification of Queen Ysera. You know that better than anyone."

"She would never be willing…" Lokol murmured.

"If you don't try, then no," Sola closed her fist back into blackness. "So help Jace Sesso find a way to break the black dragons' magic. Use your actions to prove you're an ally of the Emerald Dream, not an enemy. To the bronze dragons, refusing to even offer this simple guidance means you're not neutral at all—you're standing squarely on the opposite side."

Jace stared at her for a moment, then looked back at Lokol. Lokol was clearly not as resolute as before. After a long while he said, "Remember my name, green dragon."

"My memory has always been excellent," Sola said.

"Follow me. I'll take you to the Dark Iron fire-tenders."

With that, Lokol's head turned back and he rumbled toward the cave mouth. Jace watched him round the corner, then said softly, "I have to admit, it's like you became a different person back there, Sola."

"Grede told me to act more like a Dark Iron dwarf, didn't he?" Sola exhaled, easing her nerves. "Well, I'm a little out of my element too, but finding an ally like him was no easy thing… I was just giving it a try, seeing if he'd be willing to trust me."

Jace said, "It's a good thing you were here. I'd already started thinking of other approaches. But can Queen Ysera really purify an element like him?"

Sola said, "For thousands of years, every time the elements invaded Kalimoth, my kin fought on the front lines, Jace. Countless enslaved elements were liberated because of it—this Lord Lokol clearly knows that too."
