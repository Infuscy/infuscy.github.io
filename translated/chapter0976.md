## Chapter 976: The Temple of Flame

Lokol led the way up ahead, with Jace and Sola trailing behind as they left the Order Hall and entered a narrow, deserted passage.

"When we get to the fire-tenders, what should I say?" Lokol asked.

"Introduce me as a warlock to help them break the black dragons' incantation," Jace cautioned. "And don't use my real name, Lokol. I don't want word of my being here to reach the black dragons."

"So there really is a dragon spy in Shadowforge City?" Lokol asked.

"I don't know. It's a massive underground city—I haven't had the chance to check every single person," Jace said. "But that traitor Balreto who was tried in the arena earlier was clearly a dwarf bewitched by black dragon magic. You can't say there aren't others like him in this city. I have to be as careful as possible."

"Mm." Lokol rumbled an acknowledgment.

Leaving the rough passage, they entered a corridor again tightly guarded by the Dark Iron dwarves. All along the way, every iron-shuttered door swung open at Lord Lokol's approach.

The dwarf soldiers saluted him respectfully, and even the nobles and high-ranking officers in unusual attire greeted him with humble bows. It was abundantly clear just who ruled this city.

Crossing a broad iron bridge, Jace noticed, far off on the other side of the black canyon, an even larger iron bridge—and on it, a gigantic fire elemental gazing their way.

Beside the elemental, a black anvil that looked almost insignificant by comparison sat at the center of the iron bridge, fused into the black span. Was that the Dark Anvil the Dark Iron dwarves spoke of?

Then that fire elemental must be Flamewalker Lord Incendius.

"Lord Lokol—ah, you rarely come this far,"

From a distance Jace heard Incendius murmur a greeting in the Elemental Tongue, but Lokol gave no response at all, pressing on without a word. Seeing it ignore him, Incendius let out a howling laugh like a hot wind and turned to walk toward the far side of the bridge, where a host of flame guardians awaited him.

Crossing the iron bridge, they passed through a broad, familiar hall. There were many such halls in Shadowforge City—the Lyceum had one before it, and there was another just before reaching the bridge where the Dark Anvil stood. It was at that hall that the gnome had stopped him, refusing to let him trespass into Incendius's domain uninvited.

It seemed they were about somewhere equally important now.

Guided by Lokol, they passed through the hall without obstruction. Terrible carvings of fire serpents coiling around an angry face adorned both walls—not a countenance of dwarves, but a thing with no nose, only churning flame for eyes and a gaping abyss for a mouth.

Jace knew that was Ragnaros.

Beneath that abstract relief sat a burning dark-brown anvil—was this the symbol of Shadowforge City's fire-tenders?

The dwarves guarding the deep gates at the end of the corridor shouted the names of the elements and thrust the iron doors open together. Inside was a rather grand hall, lit by fire basins hanging in midair. Jace noticed that the flames in those basins were all fire elementals looking down—only they lacked the humanoid forms of the flame guardians outside.

With Lokol leading them, the Dark Iron dwarf guards didn't so much as question Sola and Jace behind him—only some of the fire-tenders in bright red robes within cast mildly suspicious glances their way.

Then a female Dark Iron dwarf emerged from a side doorway. She wore a robe that blazed like living flame and a Dark Iron crown, clearly a figure of considerable rank in this temple.

"Lord Lokol." The female dwarf raised a hand and spoke in the Elemental Tongue. "It's been several years since you last came to the Thaurissan Shrine."

"I'm here about that black dragon incantation that can't be cracked," Lokol also replied in the Elemental Tongue. "I've brought a human warlock—perhaps he can help with the decoding."

"I am Jack Slovakia." Jace looked at the female dwarf and spoke in Dwarvish. "It's an honor to honor the Thaurissan Shrine. This is Suomola Furnace-Shadow, my apprentice. I have her to thank for getting me through the orc blockade to Shadowforge City and into contact with the great Lord Lokol."

Sola bowed respectfully. The female dwarf asked hesitantly, "You understand the Elemental Tongue, warlock?"

Jace smiled and offered no explanation, but the dwarf still looked uncertainly toward Lokol. This time she switched to Dwarvish. "I don't mean to question your recommendation, Lord Lokol. But beneath Ragnaros's blessing, I believe we don't need the help of demons to finish the decoding."

"The work on that incantation has been going on for years, Yulda," Lokol said to the female fire-tender.

Hearing "Yulda," Jace felt his heart skip—because in Dwarvish, that name was the feminine form of "Yurke." If a human warlock had entered Shadowforge City under the name Yurke, he'd have been suspected of using a false name more times than he could count.

Lokol continued, "Without an effective means of dealing with Victor Nefarius's pets, your situation will only grow more precarious. This is no time to be picky—the Firelord and the Executor are most displeased with the stalemate."

Judging from the ugly look on the female dwarf's face, Lokol's attitude toward the Firelord was no idle boast.

"Rest assured, lady, this has nothing to do with demons," Jace said. "My apprentice Suomola and I have both studied Draconic, and I have extensive experience fighting black dragons myself—so much so that I've become a target for their hunts. For us, cracking this incantation is an absolute win-win. You needn't worry I'll leak anything to the black dragons."

"In that case." The female dwarf spoke in Drakkic with a thick dwarven accent. "I will take you to the Rune Vault."

"My thanks." Jace replied in Draconic.

He glanced at Sola, his eyes saying they were finally in—but Sola was silent beside him.

A wave of barely suppressible disgust was already rippling through her. If this had been the middle of nowhere, this green dragon would probably have snatched up the dwarf in front of her and gobbled her down like a dried blackberry.

Sola shot Jace a helpless look. Jace could only lift his eyebrows to say there was nothing to be done about it.

She had no idea how much more the Dark Iron dwarves would "profane" their language before this was over—what kind of torment would that be for her? Still, you could call it a form of "worldly" cultivation, couldn't you?
