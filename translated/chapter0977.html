Chapter 977: The Fire-Tenders' Vault

The vault doors of the shrine were two enormous black stones covered in flame reliefs. Beneath the leaping light of the fire basins on either side, those flame reliefs flickered as if they were truly alive.

Jace and Sola stood before them, made to look like two pathetic gnomes by the dwarf stone carvings brandishing flames above.

The fire-tender Yulda had gone to find the vault's keeper, leaving Jace and Sola to wait here. These fire-tenders hadn't fully trusted them just because an elemental lord had brought them—beside the fire basin on the other side of the vault door stood a dwarf flame guardian in bright red armor. Though he was looking the other way at the moment, his most important task right now was undoubtedly to keep these two "warlocks" from tampering with the door.

Sola seemed somewhat nervous now. Her Dark Iron dwarf appearance didn't look any different on the surface, but Jace could clearly sense she was worrying about something.

Jace stared at the distant flame guardian and whispered to her, "Something on your mind?"

Hearing a voice, the dwarf glanced their way—but only for a moment before turning back.

The door was enormous. Jace was by this fire basin and the other dwarf was outside the one on the far side, near the corridor entrance, so making out anything wasn't that easy. Besides, he didn't seem to care what the two of them had to say.

"Maybe I shouldn't add trouble at this critical moment…" Sola said, somewhat guiltily. "But I truly can't calm my nerves. Facing an incantation that could easily deal with a dragon like Onyxia…"

"These dwarves certainly haven't figured out how to use the incantation," Jace said quietly, watching the movement at the distant corridor mouth. "Remember what Lokol said not long ago? He said if they didn't find a way to use that incantation against the black dragons soon, the Dark Iron dwarves in Shadowforge City wouldn't be able to hold on much longer… So if simply reciting it out loud were enough to make it work, these fire-tenders wouldn't have agonized this long without putting it into actual use."

That said, Jace also thought of how effortlessly Marshal Windsor had recited that incantation in the game. Either something had gone wrong in the game, or something had gone wrong here.

Still, the notion flashed through his mind only to be cast aside.

What happened in the game didn't matter anymore. He was already standing right in front of that incantation, closer to the truth than anyone would be for years to come. The time had come for him to unveil everything—not to pin his hopes on events that hadn't happened yet.

After a while, Sola finally nodded, as though convinced.

"Even if your true form as a green dragon is exposed, it's no problem," Jace said, looking at her. "Green dragons and black dragons are mortal enemies. Just admit you deceived a human warlock and snuck into Shadowforge City to find a way to defeat the black dragons. They won't casually kill a human warlock from Booty Bay, and they won't casually kill a green dragon from Kalimoth either, will they?"

"That makes sense." Sola's tone relaxed considerably.

"Worst case," Jace said, half-joking, "if you get captured, you'll just be sent into the Ring of Order. At least then you'll earn a place in Shadowforge City's history."

Sola muttered, "Truly nothing to fear."

"You joined us too late." Jace glanced at two figures swaying into focus down the hall and comforted her. "If you'd gone to Grim Batol with me, Wren, Grede, and those two demons, you wouldn't feel the slightest unease at a scene like this…"

Sola nodded, was about to say something, but only moved her lips before Yulda called from a distance, "You two!"

She'd brought a dwarf in a long robe with lava-like patterns to the door. The male dwarf leaned on a long-handled weapon that was hard to tell was a battle hammer or a staff as he walked up. Unlike the typical mage-like attire of fire-tenders, he also wore a rather heavy suit of red chainmail over his shoulders.

"Master Sorgaden, Anvil Lord in service to His Majesty, guardian of the Flame Temple vault," Yulda introduced him with respectful tones. Jace and Sola both inclined their heads in a simple bow.

"I don't know what trick you used to deceive Lord Lokol." The so-called Anvil Lord opened his mouth with a thoroughly unfriendly tone, his eyes lifting coldly to fix on Jace. "The Dark Iron's history with dragons is far older than you can imagine. I really don't know what kind of confidence brings you here to lecture us."

The fire-tender raised both hands and waved them at the flames on the stone door. "But since this is Lord Lokol's order, and we are all loyal to the Firelord, we have no choice but to show you—otherwise it would be disrespect… Only if anything goes wrong, that's got nothing to do with me."

Though he claimed loyalty to the Firelord, Sorgaden's tone was dripping with sarcasm, making even Yulda beside him look rather uncomfortable.

He seemed deeply dissatisfied with Ragnaros's rule, and having a legitimate chance to complain, he couldn't resist letting it all out. "Come in." Anvil Lord Sorgaden beckoned. "One way or another, I'll show you the writing on the stone tablets."

Stone tablets?

Jace had never heard anyone mention that this incantation was recorded on stone tablets, and he'd never brought it up with anyone either.

It seemed the game content wasn't wrong—this incantation capable of countering dragons was indeed recorded on stone tablets, and it was exactly what the dwarves were working to crack.

Jace felt he was one step closer to his goal. The faint doubt that had crept in because of Sola's worries just now had vanished as well.

Sorgaden came to the door and shouted in Dwarvish, "Vault, open!"

This wasn't some complex password or profound incantation, but the moment the door heard his voice it slowly swung outward on its own, accompanied by a rumbling tremor.

When the doors had opened halfway, Jace noticed it was actually two golem constructs inside pushing them.

But then Sorgaden murmured something under his breath, and every fire basin in the vault flared to life at once. This was no mechanical coincidence—this was the true power to command the elements.

Still, Jace had seen the instant Roktus Darkcontract lit his lantern. Though not as grand a spectacle, the old blacksmith had made the fire elementals serve him without any communication at all—truly an eye-opener.

Perhaps the Dark Iron dwarves truly possessed an unparalleled affinity for flame. Perhaps nearly three centuries of study and training had brought his command of the elements to a state of effortless mastery. But to reach the point where the fire elementals seemed like extensions of his own body…

Last time, being in a hurry to get to the fire-tenders, Jace hadn't had a chance to chat with the old blacksmith in detail. Once he'd sorted out the matter of the incantation on these stone tablets and was preparing to leave Shadowforge City, Jace planned to visit him and ask about fire elemental magic.

As a master blacksmith who'd been renowned among the three hammers since the age of Arathi, his understanding of fire elementals was probably no less than that of Adjudicator Griston he'd met in the Ring of Law.

Jace wondered—could he also command fire elementals to travel between planes? Was it only because he feared that if he disappeared, the emperor would order the Thorium Brotherhood hunted, that he'd willingly refrained from using teleportation magic and stayed in Shadowforge City, scraping by in secret?

The brightly lit vault before him displayed no small number of treasures that looked exceedingly precious. They were dazzling and varied, nearly all of them flame-related, and the fire-tenders didn't mind letting an outsider like Jace admire their collection. When Jace showed his astonishment, the Anvil Lord's face even betrayed a hint of pride.

"I'll fetch the items, sirs and madam." Sorgaden pointed to a stone bench nearby. "You may wait here."

A grimoire whose edges were still burning caught Jace's eye first. He stepped closer curiously—the incomplete characters on its cover seemed to be a language blending Dwarvish and the Elemental Tongue, reading something like "Flame Rune Grimoire of the [???] Envoy."

Hanging on the wall behind the grimoire was a broad, exceedingly magnificent set of flame-colored pauldrons. They didn't look forged for a dwarf—the spiraling sharp spikes thrust upward one after another like the horns of a flame demon.

Though such exaggerated pauldron designs weren't uncommon in the game, growing more absurd with every patch, since truly coming to this world it was still the first time Jace had seen something so wildly fantastical—the kind of thing that looked like it could only appear in a cash shop. It had actually broken his immersion a little.

"What's that?" Jace asked Yulda beside him curiously.

"The pauldrons of Flamewalker Lord Gehennas," Yulda said. "He's a rune guardian of Molten Core and one of the Firelord's most trusted guardians. He gave them as a gift to us believers."

Jace nodded. Gehennas was a boss in Molten Core—he was the leader of those terrifying Flamewalkers who sacrificed themselves to heal others. If memory served, he was the one that applied the healing-reduction debuff.

But that didn't matter anymore. What Yulda's words revealed was that Molten Core was indeed protected by special runes. In the game, Gehennas also guarded a rune—only after killing him could one use the water essence given by Hydraxian to destroy the rune.

If one entered Molten Core without destroying those runes, it would be like trying to attack Quel'Thalas without being able to breach the Ban'threshold—a complete waste of effort. Dealing with the Firelord would be even more out of the question.

At that moment, Sorgaden walked out carrying a scroll and spread it flat on the stone platform before them. Jace glanced at the runes on the scroll. "Is this copied from those stone tablets?"

"Those stone tablets," Sorgaden corrected Jace with the plural, then explained, "The tablets have shattered into several pieces—inconvenient to examine."

"Fine." Jace nodded and began studying what was on the scroll in earnest. In truth, this was all for show—he knew from the first glance that he hadn't the faintest idea what was written here.

Was this really Draconic? Jace felt like his classes at the Wizard Sanctum had been a complete waste. Of course, considering he hadn't properly attended more than a few weeks of classes…

Though he could see quite a few characters that looked like Draconic runes, he couldn't recognize them strung together—and there were even plenty of characters he'd never seen before.

As he studied, Jace even found himself reaching for his knowledge of orcish writing—naturally, that led nowhere either. Orcish was completely different from Draconic, in both script and structure.

He was getting genuinely self-conscious, so he stole a glance at Sola—and found her reading with intense focus, even frowning slightly, as though pondering something as she went. Could she actually read it?
