## Chapter 978: Is It Really Draconic?

"It's perfectly normal that neither of you can make anything of it," Yulda said. "After all, the mages and the shrine's fire-tenders have studied it for so long without result either."

Sorgaden said, "Perhaps we should research it in the direction of another language—ancient giant tongue, for instance? In ancient times, the primeval giants had extremely fierce conflicts with the dragonflights. It would be quite natural for them to have developed an incantation to counter dragons."

"That makes sense, Master Sorgaden," Yulda agreed. "We should bring this hypothesis up at our next discussion. Perhaps the runes on it that resemble Draconic have led us too far astray."

"I'd like to see the originals," Sola said suddenly. "You seem to have copied the order wrong, and there may have been some errors in the transcription…" She spoke with such confidence, such conviction, that the two fire-tenders before her were momentarily taken aback.

"Do you know who copied this, madam?"

Perhaps because Sola's accent made her seem like no dwarf to be trifled with, Sorgaden still maintained basic courtesy.

"I don't," Sola said. "But this is indeed Draconic—and at the same time, not true Draconic, Masters. To be precise, it's a special drakonid tongue—a language used by drakonid and disdained by true dragons. You could think of it as a crude, vulgar tongue that those lesser servants, unable to reach their arrogant masters, gradually remade over millions of years—something unfit for polite company."

"Though perhaps calling it vulgar is a bit much. Among ancient drakonid communities, it was actually… one might even say a noble language."

Sola held forth in an extremely confident, even slightly arrogant dwarven cadence, leaving the dwarves thoroughly bewildered.

Anvil Lord Sorgaden was stunned for a moment before asking, "Lady Suomola, how do you know all this?"

"Every family has its secrets, Master Sorgaden," Jace cut in quickly. "There was a reason I chose to bring this lady through the risk of orc capture to Shadowforge City to find a way to break the black dragons' magic."

Sola glanced at Jace, then nodded. "Family secrets."

"If this is a 'noble' drakonid tongue," Yulda asked curiously, "why don't the drakonid use it today? Because we've studied the black dragons' drakonid too, Lady Suomola—they communicate in Draconic we can understand. There's simply no way to match it to these characters."

Sola shook her head. "That, I don't know."

"I'll bring the stone tablets to you," Sorgaden said, and immediately turned back through the hidden door, completely shedding his "Anvil Lord" airs from before.

Of course, Jace had no idea what status that title carried in Shadowforge City or what kind of airs one was supposed to keep. But drakonid language?

A drakonid magic that could counter black dragon magic—wasn't that like a great fire burning down the Black Dragonflight's own temple?

The drakonid had always been utterly obedient to their masters. Could this have been invented by drakonid of another color to use against the black dragons?

But if that were so, and it was this effective, the masters of those other-colored drakonid should know about it too. It wouldn't be shrouded in this much mystery, leaving Sola completely in the dark.

A possibility surfaced in Jace's mind—could it be that the drakonid who spoke this language had once rebelled against the black dragons, even created a specialized incantation to counter them, leading the black dragons to wipe out all the drakonid who spoke it…

But that didn't add up either. The drakonid's obedience to their masters was fundamental—their very blood was a gift from their masters. How could they possibly mount a rebellion on a scale that would make the black dragons take notice?

Seeing Sola unwilling to elaborate, Jace also knew this wasn't the time to discuss the question. Better to wait for the stone tablets.

Soon, Sorgaden emerged from the hidden door carrying something wrapped in black silk and set the bundle on the stone platform.

Unwrapping it revealed several fractured stone tablets. The tablets weren't large—about the size of two or three pencil cases—covered in densely carved runes.

"How did these tablets break?" Jace asked.

"They were already broken when we got our hands on them," Yulda said.

Sola studied them for a while. "This incantation—even this stone tablet—requires sufficient mana to sustain. Once it loses its mana, it loses its power. That's why it shattered."

Hearing her say that, Jace was even more confused.

If this incantation required mana to sustain, then how had a warrior like Windsor, who had no mana at all, confidently recited it before Onyxia and had it actually work?

He very much wanted to ask, but likewise, now wasn't the time to get to the bottom of these questions with Sola.

"Outside, there are rumors that you obtained these tablets from orcish traitors in Blackrock Spire," Jace said. "Could those orcish traitors know how to use these tablets? For instance, they might have brought a mana source capable of activating the incantation, or they might know another incantation that could trigger it?"

"No." Sorgaden said this with absolute certainty. "They knew nothing. Those two orcs were both warriors who wielded no magic. They'd grown dissatisfied with the black dragons' rule and developed treacherous intent… We know this so clearly because after those two orcs were captured, they were thrown into the cells of the Order Hall, Mr. Slovakia. One of them had already died under interrogation."

Jace was stunned. "Why didn't you say so earlier?"

"Because it's utterly pointless!" Sorgaden said, unable to mask the disappointment on his face. "Even under Shadowforge City's tortures, they revealed not a single thing. We even cooperated with warlock demons, using a succubus to break their will—and it was no use. I imagine they were only responsible for escorting these tablets, not people who could activate them. But I must say, their combat prowess and willpower were among the most formidable even for orcs… which is why they were qualified to escort something so extraordinary."

Hearing him say it this way, Jace could more or less imagine that it was probably for this reason that the warlocks, who'd always had a poor relationship with the fire-tenders, had learned about the orcish traitors and the mysterious incantation.

Sorgaden rubbed his forehead and continued. "I'd originally hoped for a breakthrough this time, only to end up right back at that orc's door. But at least now we know what language this is. Thank you truly, Lady Suomola—our research from here has direction. I wonder if you could help us arrange the tablets in the correct order, and correct the errors if possible. If I get the chance, I'd like to make the acquaintance of the members of the Furnace-Shadow family. Do they live here in Shadowforge City?"

Sola seemed somewhat unprepared for the Anvil Lord's words, so Jace stepped in directly. "I'd like to meet the remaining orcish prisoner, Master Sorgaden."

"A waste of time, if you ask me, human," Sorgaden said with a shake of his head.

Jace said, "If you truly thought it was a waste of time, you'd have sent him into the Ring of Order long ago, wouldn't you?"

Sorgaden said, "That's not for us to decide. Perhaps General Angforge thinks that orc still has value. In my opinion, it's more likely that getting into the Ring of Order requires a queue—they need to ensure the citizens of Shadowforge City have fresh entertainment every day, so they don't forget the predicament we're in right now."

He straightened the collar of his robe and continued. "But since you're interested in that orc, I'll let you see him. After all, before seeing these stone tablets, I hadn't expected the two of you to figure anything out either—but it seems there's been real progress."

"You're a warlock, Mr. Slovakia, so I'm warning you in advance—don't try summoning a succubus to repeat the useless efforts those warlocks made. That orc has been tortured enough. If you want to squeeze anything more out of him, keep him alive. If he dies, not only will there be nothing left to ask—General Angforge will take it out on us, and we'll shift the blame onto you. Even if you were introduced by Lord Lokol, that won't save you."

He looked at the fire-tender beside him. "Yulda, take them to the Order Hall."

"Yes, Master." Yulda said. "Follow me."

Who would have thought that after going in a circle, they'd end up right back where they started—but Jace didn't feel this had been a waste of effort at all. If he could get even a shred of a clue from that orc, even just a trace of magic, he might be able to track down the source of those tablets.

Following the female fire-tender back the way they'd come, though they'd walked this road once before, Jace was already starting to feel it was unfamiliar.

Returning to the Order Hall and arriving at a corner, Yulda said something to the jailers, and they opened the door. The female dwarf turned back to Jace and nodded, signaling he could go in.

"Do I need to come in too?" Sola asked.

"Let me talk to him alone," Jace said, glancing at Yulda and the dwarf soldiers nearby. "With Dark Iron present, communication might be difficult."

Stepping through the hidden door, Jace caught a glimpse of a dark figure inside.

This was practically the thinnest orc Jace had ever seen. He was sleeping at the moment, completely oblivious to the visitor's presence. The iron door slammed shut behind him with a bang, and only then did the orc slowly open his eyes and cast a glance at Jace before him.

"Shadowforge City has tall ones too?" the orc muttered in halting Common, then coughed out a cold laugh.

"What I'm here to discuss today has nothing to do with this dwarven city," Jace said in Orcish.

Hearing this, the sneer on the orc's face vanished. He slowly lowered his head. "What do you want to say, human?"
