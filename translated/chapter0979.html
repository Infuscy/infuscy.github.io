Chapter 979: An Unfinished Task

In the sweltering, stinking cell, the only light came trickling in through the window—but even so, the look of disgust on the orc's face was plain to see.

He fixed his eyes on Jace. "The Dark Iron have run out of ideas, have they? Sending a human like you to work on me. Frankly, I'd rather that succubus from before came in… so long as she's willing to make me feel good, I'll tell you everything I know."

The orc snickered.

"I'm not here to do the Dark Iron dwarves any favors," Jace also replied in Orcish. "I came only to find out where that incantation came from. What's your name?"

"That succubus also claimed she wasn't working for the dwarves." The orc spat. "But her mouth was full of lies… Your Orcish is quite good. Tell me—how much gold did the Dark Iron dwarves pay to hire a fellow like you?"

Jace smiled. "Gold can't buy my loyalty. I work only for myself, orc. If you cooperate honestly, I might even find a way to get you out."

"Mm." The orc said, "Not bad. Interesting, human. Watching you flap your beak like a crow spouting useless nonsense is entertaining. You think because I've decided to defy Blackhand, I still have any intention to go on living, you white-feathered chicken-crested fool? Hurk up your dark magic and stop wasting my time—let me see what you're made of!"

The orc had a point. As a Blackrock orc, walking the path of defying Blackrock Spire meant there wasn't a single place left in the entire Eastern Kingdoms where he could lay his head.

He'd abandoned every viable path and chosen a road to certain death—such was his hatred of serving as slaves beneath the black dragons. Having already cast off the black dragons, he certainly wasn't about to let himself be manipulated by a human like Jace.

Just as Sorgaden had said, this orc had a tenacious will. One could imagine his comrades were probably equally hardened characters—otherwise they wouldn't have died under a succubus's torment without revealing anything useful.

But no matter what kind of person someone was, they had something they treasured. Jace was confident this orc hadn't joined the Blackrock Spire rebels purely to escape the black dragons. If that were all, he could have simply walked away like the warlock Carol had. So long as you enlisted in Blackrock Spire, there were always patrols to the Burning Plains—find an opportunity to slip away and no one would be the wiser.

Short of orcs being hunted down across the entire continent, and with Blackrock Spire's orcs having nowhere else for the Horde to settle after Grim Batol's fall, more orcs would probably have tried to leave.

So he must have his own purpose—cracking the black dragons' magic, then overthrowing the rule of the so-called "Advisor," the black dragon prince Victor Nefarius in truth, and saving the entire Blackrock clan from the Blackhand brothers who'd brought the black dragons here?

It sounded almost like a fantasy. But if you didn't know Nefarius's true strength, having such thoughts was only reasonable. Jace guessed the reason they'd dared to act was probably because they believed this incantation could break Nefarius's magic.

What had given them that confidence?

"Let's talk about your work—transporting this incantation," Jace probed. "You've been locked up here for some time now. Probably two or three years, and the Dark Iron dwarves have had that incantation tablet all along. Which means the people you wanted to help have spent these years watching helplessly as the black dragons deepened their enslavement, able to do nothing…"

The orc, who'd been quite arrogant until now, slowly let his temper drop. Though he'd kept the same lurking-in-the-darkness, peering-outward demeanor all along, Jace could feel he was clearly wavering now.

It seemed Jace had guessed right. The orc didn't care about his own life or death, but he deeply regretted not having delivered the tablet to his comrades.

"I'm curious," Jace went on. "What kind of incantation must be written on a specific stone tablet to work? I'm a warlock myself, and I've learned many incantations. So long as you grasp the method and conditions of casting, memorize the incantation accurately and completely, and then it's a matter of talent and timing. I imagine even if this incantation is hard to memorize, you could still copy it down…"

"You know nothing about that thing," the orc said coldly.

It seemed the tablet really was special. Jace felt he was one step closer to what he wanted to know, so he shook his head. "You're the one who knows nothing, soldier. Are you a warlock? Do you know even a single dark spell? I'd say it's only because orcish warlocks lack the magical talent—without the chance to drink demon blood, they'd be utterly worthless…"

In the shadows, the orc's teeth ground together with a creak. Jace was hoping to feel his surging fury, hoping he'd say something more in his anger. But soon the orc let out a cold laugh, and the boiling rage that had flared up cooled just as quickly.

"I know nothing indeed, warlock." The orc laughed twice. "You and the Dark Iron dwarves exhausted yourselves and only caught a worthless piece of rotten meat like me—laughable. Don't get cocky. Soon you'll starve to death along with everyone else here. Your situation is no better than mine."

"Yes," Jace nodded. "Not just you—the orcs in Blackrock Spire will be buried alongside us before long. You don't think the black dragons appreciate you slaves without scales or horns, do they? Those dragons don't even trust the drakonid legions that share their own blood, let alone you. They won't even let you speak their language… Think about it. If Shadowforge City falls, will those black dragons leave behind you disobedient misfits who still harbor rebellious intentions?"

This time the orc said nothing. Jace continued. "From what I know, the black dragons are raising offspring atop Blackrock Spire, relying on you orcs to guard their dragon nests. Before long, more and more orcs and other mortals will become drakonid, and more and more whelps will hatch from the dragon eggs. Some can grow into young dragons. When that happens, they won't need you people anymore. I'd say ten, twenty years at most. That's the countdown to the extinction of you and your kin."

The word "kin"—Jace had deliberately used an intimate, traditional Orcish term: maga'gha, meaning important family and kin. Sure enough, the moment the word came out, the orc's nostrils flared. His anger could no longer be contained.

"Shut your mouth, you lying human! Even if the Alliance collapses and all of Azeroth ceases to exist, the Horde will endure!"

He nearly roared it. Even the people outside had heard the commotion. Jace noticed a shadow flicker past the cell door's little window—someone outside worrying about what was happening within. Jace walked over and glanced out to see Sola notice his eyes, her expression relaxing only then.

This dragon was actually worried he might get hurt by a weakened orc—how…

He gave the outside a look that everything was fine, then pulled the window's shutter closed and turned back, using a streak of Shadowflame to light the fire basin above the cell.

Purple-red flames danced, lending the already grim cell a whorl of blood-tinged menace. Beneath that fel light, the orc's furious expression looked even more twisted.

"Don't believe me? Let me give you some 'news'—your homeland Draenor has been destroyed," Jace said. "I know Teron Gorefiend once came to Blackrock Spire seeking Blackhand's help, but Blackhand refused him. The black dragons, however, helped him get what they wanted—helped Ner'zhul, your warchief on the other side of the Dark Portal, activate the gateway to other worlds, tearing the entire world apart."

"So the black dragons were the driving force and accomplices behind Draenor's destruction. And now you and your comrades have no choice but to keep serving them… In the end, the black dragons will mercilessly execute you. No—perhaps use you as food, feeding your blood and thunder, your strength and glory, into the disgusting little mouths of those whelps. And it's all because of you and your partner's failure."

"Shut up! Shut up! Shut up!"

The orc howled madly, and Jace just watched him woodenly and continued. "But actually, even if you'd delivered this incantation to its destination, I doubt it would have done any good. Deathwing's single breath of flame destroyed all of Grim Batol—and I don't mean burned. I mean utterly destroyed. A towering, magnificent fortress turned in the blink of an eye into a giant pit of surging lava. Don't forget—the surface structures of Grim Batol were no smaller in scale than Blackrock Spire. As far as I know, Victor Nefarius is very likely his eldest son. A fragile incantation…"

"Shut your face!" the orc snarled, spraying spittle. "That curse is a dagger driven straight into a black dragon's heart! The Horde will rise again—maybe not now… but not long now!"

"All I know is that waiting here won't get you anywhere," Jace said. "Do you want to sit by and watch everything slide beyond saving, or do you intend to do something—at least make up for your failure?"

The orc said nothing. Clearly, he'd realized his anger had spirited away his control just now and was desperately trying to restrain his emotions. But the destruction of Draenor, the enslavement of the black dragons, the future of the Horde, his own failure… a cascade of information pressing down on him left him gasping for air.

He didn't care about his own life or death, but he couldn't be indifferent to the Horde's survival.

Even the air of defiance that had let him look through the human before him had vanished. He'd become again that weak, dejected prisoner, panting heavily, so gaunt he barely looked like an orc—at a glance he might have passed for a forest troll.

"Let's go back to the beginning," Jace said in a low voice. "I'm not here for Shadowforge City. I'm here for Stormwind."

"Stormwind." The orc muttered the name.

"To settle in Blackrock Spire, the black dragons have already begun infiltrating Stormwind to the south. I imagine as a former soldier of Blackrock Spire, you'd know that," Jace said.

The orc swallowed, then nodded.

Jace continued. "I suspect Victor Nefarius has sent black dragons to Stormwind in person, transforming into a human. So I must master that incantation to find his true form."

"A woman," the orc said. "I've seen that human woman."

Jace's eyes widened slightly. "What did she look like?"

"I couldn't see clearly," the orc said, lowering his head. "She sat in the seats high above the arena, very far away. But I know that even Nefarius became less long-winded in her presence."

This orc probably hadn't held high status in Blackrock Spire in the past—he'd likely only had the chance to see her when Onyxia occasionally appeared to watch the arena matches. Expecting him to have seen clearly was unrealistic.

But even this scrap of a clue was enough to establish one thing—that Onyxia also liked to appear in human form here.

"These black dragons like transforming into humans, to fool the Alliance's nobles," Jace said. "We were enemies in the past, but now facing a stronger foe, we've been forced to stand on the same side, haven't we?"

He walked up to the orc, staring into his bloodshot eyes half-hidden behind damp hair. "Let me help you deliver the tablet to where it was meant to go. Let me finish the task you couldn't complete. Other than that, you have no other choice."

Seeing the orc still reluctant to speak, Jace asked again, "What are you worried about? Worried I plan to destroy your stronghold? You think I'd do something that helps the black dragons? The Alliance wouldn't do it—and even the Dark Iron dwarves wouldn't."

The orc lowered his gaze and thought for a long while before saying, "A place on the Burning Plains called Talc. The black dragons may not have found it yet."
