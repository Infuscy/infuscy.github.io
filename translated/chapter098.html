Chapter 98: A Difficult Spell

"I don't have class this afternoon — I can map out where you go from here." Malin spoke as he pulled several books from the bookcase behind his desk and spread them out on the table.

"Do you have any thoughts of your own? Any incantations you'd like to learn, ones you've heard of? We generally believe interest is the best teacher. A lot of novice mages are drawn to magic because of Polymorph — the kids absolutely love that spell. Even though Polymorph is the spell in Azeroth most prone to catastrophic accidents, it's easy to learn but hard to master, and the process of practicing Polymorph is genuinely very helpful for a novice mage's growth."

"What about you — do you want to learn Polymorph too? Or… Blink? Another extremely popular but very error-prone spell."

Jace wasn't particularly keen on Polymorph. He already relied on Fear to control enemies; picking another control spell, and an arcane one at that, would be somewhat redundant.

As for Blink… Jace admitted the spell was quite tempting to him. After all, he'd especially loved using it in the game, let alone actually learning it in real life.

And in that abandoned fishing village on Westfall's north coast, he'd witnessed the sea witch wield Blink's power — suddenly popping from behind him right to a spot in front. Truly intimidating.

"Unfortunately, Blink is currently not open for study by apprentices at the Wizard's Sanctum." Malin's words poured cold water on the idea at once. "There was once a thirteen-year-old apprentice who used Blink and ended up fusing his chest with a crate at the harbor. It took two days just to separate the body. After that horrific accident, the Wizard's Sanctum became very cautious about teaching Blink, and the Blink incantation books in the library have been placed under corresponding restrictions."

Is that so? It was a pity Jace had been too far away while being chased by the naga to hear the incantation clearly back then — otherwise he might have been able to figure it out on his own.

But hearing Malin put it that way, fiddling with Blink on his own was even more dangerous than experimenting with shadow magic.

"Master, I've heard of a spell," Jace said. "Heard of it from Lordaeron. It's called Mage Armor."

Malin nodded. "Why do you want to learn this spell?"

"At the top of Stonewatch…" Jace said. "I took shadow magic damage. The scars on my hands still haven't faded. Thinking back, that really was quite dangerous. I heard Mage Armor can counter shadow magic, so I want to learn it."

Malin said, "Mage Armor doesn't just counter shadow magic — Mage Armor can counter nearly all magical damage. It counts as one of the top-tier incantations in the school of abjuration, Mr. Sesso. Well, except for the Light — though if you called the Holy Light a form of energy here in Stormwind, the priests might drag you off and beat you for it. Anyway, I suggest you go select your courses from High Sorcerer Andromath first. If you're setting Mage Armor as your goal, you can sign the abjuration grimoire and study the relevant magical principles and so on — that will be very helpful for your eventual success in learning Mage Armor."

"So I can't learn it now, is that it?" Jace asked tentatively.

"Unless you're Medivh," Malin said. "When he was a little over ten years old and still an apprentice, his magical level was already high enough to crush a mage like me like an insect without a second thought. Fortunately, he was just an innocent, kind child back then, so he left the tens of thousands of living souls in the entire city unharmed, and in the end only killed his father by accident."

"So innate magical ability and powerful magical talent are extremely dangerous, Mr. Sesso. Learning magic that doesn't match your ability… is danger on top of danger. Although the Order of Tirisfal once placed heavy restrictions on magical study due to arcane abuse, which seems extreme in hindsight, there's no denying their original intentions were absolutely sound."

"But if I'm going to take part in your research in the future — going deep into the Wetlands, for instance," Jace said, "when we run into Dragonmaw orcs, Dark Iron dwarves, even red dragons, I'll need to have both shadow and fire resistance simultaneously, won't I?"

"Are you negotiating with me?" Malin said helplessly. "You might not understand this yet, but I'm not letting you learn Mage Armor for your own good — especially a spell that takes effect on your own body. You need to be even more careful."

Jace asked: "So what could go wrong?"

Malin said: "Mage Armor taking effect is similar to the Frost Armor technique that frost mages favor — both use arcane energy to condense an elemental shell and partially assimilate incoming magic damage of the same type… and the danger is similar too. Frost Armor can easily freeze the caster's own body, especially a caster who isn't proficient enough. An error in the incantation, an error in the gestures, any of it could cause that kind of consequence."

"Mage Armor is far more complex. Its casting process is long, because it weaves a prismatic outer layer containing multiple magical protective effects to shield the caster, and every error in color sets off a chain reaction. The moment you slip up, you could face the combined backlash of five or six different elemental magics at once. Compared to that, the demand for energy is secondary."

"I want to give it a try, Master," Jace said firmly.

Malin didn't refuse again the way Jace had expected. He just sat behind the desk and looked at Jace for a long while before asking: "You'd already made up your mind, hadn't you?"

"Not that long ago," Jace said. "About a day or two, after I felt I'd more or less got the hang of Light. So, Master — is there a rule forbidding apprentices from learning Mage Armor?"

"There's no hard rule like there is for Blink," Malin said.

"Perfect," Jace said. "Then the next spell I want to learn is Mage Armor."

Seeing how resolute Jace was, Malin fell into hesitation once more.

Perhaps he was thinking about the matter with the red dragon. Perhaps he was thinking about the focused look on Jace's face when he'd cast Light earlier. Perhaps he was considering whether this young man would become another poor soul who died a miserable death in the mad pursuit of supreme power.

As for Jace, what he was thinking was simple.

If his affinity for arcane energy wasn't as strong as he'd imagined, then the number of arcane spells available to him would be very limited.

Master one sufficiently versatile defensive spell, one sufficiently useful and irreplaceable utility spell… train them to absolute perfection, rather than wasting energy memorizing a pile of miscellaneous incantations with interchangeable functions.

Mage Armor was unquestionably the best choice among defensive magic.

Malin's words had confirmed that, too — a persistent resistance boost was more universally applicable, protecting you from fatal harm in all kinds of complicated situations, whereas the various elemental barrier spells demanded far more in terms of casting skill and energy.

"All right, two conditions," Malin said at last. "First, learn the Arcane Intellect incantation first. You must practice this spell under the effect of Arcane Intellect — it greatly improves the accuracy of your incantation recitation and raises your mana ceiling, which reduces the danger. Second, you are not to practice it on your own when I'm not present. I have to watch you, so that if something goes wrong you can't control it — or worse, hurt someone else."

"I accept both," Jace said without hesitation.
