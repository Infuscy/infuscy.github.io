## Chapter 20: For a Caster, the Most Important Thing Is the Staff

Talc?

Jace still had an impression of the place. There was a cave there, home to a strange black drakonid who gave players quests. Could that drakonid be the leader of the orcs rebelling against black dragon rule?

No—that drakonid's quest for players was to enter Blackrock Depths and kill Belga, the massive lava giant guarding Shadowforge City—the one Jace had seen at the city gates—to prove his worth to the Black Dragonflight… Which meant he should still be loyal to the black dragons.

And given the importance of that quest NPC, Jace didn't think he'd be connected to an incantation described as "a dagger driven into a black dragon's heart."

Perhaps a decade from now, the black dragons had found a way to destroy the rebels' lair? Still, no matter what, he had to go take a look.

He nodded to the orc, was just about to turn and leave, when the orc suddenly said, "Promise me one thing, human."

"Go on." Jace stopped.

"Send those dwarves to throw me into the Ring of Order. Let me fight with honor until I die!" the orc said through gritted teeth.

"I don't see anything glorious about fighting people to the death for others' amusement in that arena. And losing your life over it makes it even less worth it," Jace said, turning back. "Since you believe the Horde will rise again one day, you might as well stay alive and see if you can wait for that day."

The orc said nothing. Jace knocked on the door and came out. The Dark Iron dwarves were all staring at him.

"You were in there a long time," Yulda said. "Did it yield anything?"

"I know where these orcs meant to deliver the stone tablet," Jace said.

"What good does knowing that do?" Yulda said anxiously. "He didn't tell you how to activate the incantation, or—"

"Find the tablet's destination, and isn't that the same as finding the person who can use it?" Jace said. "I need to go talk to Sorgaden."

Back at the vault, Sorgaden was wiping down a display stand holding a Firelord relic. Hearing Yulda's greeting of "Master Sorgaden" from behind, he turned around with a smile that said he was here for the show. "Well? Is that orc still alive?"

"Alive," Jace said. "And I know where he meant to deliver the tablets. I need those tablets, Master Sorgaden. That orc told me these tablets are to be delivered to a very hidden cave on the eastern side of Blackrock Mountain. I must set out immediately…"

"You want to help the orcs complete their escort? Have you lost your mind?" Sorgaden said angrily.

"What's the alternative?" Jace looked at the wrapped tablets already laid out on the table. "Don't you want to figure out how to use this incantation? How many more years do you want to study it? Your research these past two years has accomplished less than what my student figured out at a single glance. The person who can use it is right in front of you—and you're still hesitating?!"

"There are plenty of scholars in Shadowforge City," Sorgaden said. "General Golem Commander Argan, for instance. In fact, we've already planned to cooperate with the military…"

"If he'd really produced results, then your fire-tenders' research would have been a waste of time. Let me take the tablets, figure out how to use them, and come back and tell you. Don't forget—I want to overthrow the black dragon rule of Blackrock Spire just as much as you do! That way I won't have to live every day in a nightmare of possibly being burned to death by black dragons. Damn it."

Jace finished, gripping his cloak, and leaned down until his face nearly pressed against the fire-tender's.

The dwarf glanced at Yulda beside him, then at the tablets laid out on the table in the distance. He pursed his black lips, mulled it over for a moment, then said, "Leave your staff, warlock. As a guarantee you'll come back."

"Are you out of your mind?!" Jace gripped his Mirror Stone Staff. "Without a staff, you're sending me to my death!"

"And what if you take the tablets and run?" Sorgaden asked.

Jace pulled out the Kirin Tor small box containing the Flowing Ice Crystal from his bag. "You can hold this as collateral. It's a treasure of Dalaran…"

"Leave your staff." The Dark Iron dwarf insisted.

Jace shot a sidelong glance at the tablets, sighed, and said, "The gem on this staff is a treasure of the ancient Highborne, and the staff itself is crafted from the sturdiest wood I could find. Take good care of it."

"I've always been quite good at keeping treasures safe," Sorgaden said.

"Damn it." Jace swore in Common, then handed the staff to Sorgaden.

The Anvil Lord ran a finger over the Soul Mirror Stone, frowned slightly, and sucked in a breath. "It really is an ancient treasure… Give me a day. Let me have someone copy the runes on these stone tablets in the correct order Lady Suomola provided."

He looked to Sola beside him. Sola nodded. Jace said, "No problem."

The next day, carrying the wrapped stone tablets, Jace and Sola set out to leave Shadowforge City with their two demons.

Escorted by the dwarf soldiers to the lava hall where Franclorn's Tomb was located, the two of them transformed back into orcs and climbed back the way they'd come.

This time Jace was especially watchful for that black dragon lurking near the Blackrock Spire entrance. But perhaps because this time they were climbing up from the smoky area below, using the statues for cover, the movements weren't as large or conspicuous as jumping down from above—so they didn't attract the whelp's attention. All things considered, a close call, and they made it out to the other side of Blackrock Mountain.

Finally escaping the hot, suffocating stench within and stepping out into the Burning Plains, Jace even felt a sense of relief—at least he was breathing outdoor air again, even if it still reeked of burning.

Descending Blackrock Mountain and reaching an area with few orcs, Jace said to Sola, "The place we're heading is at the far eastern edge of the Burning Plains, and we're practically at the western edge of the plain here. We don't have time to cross the entire region on foot—it could take a week or more. So I want to go over the mountain, get close to the northern wastes, away from the whelps' patrols… and then you carry me there."

"No problem," Sola said. "If you really find the correct method for activating the incantation at that 'Talc,' are you still planning to go back to Shadowforge City?"

"Of course I'm going back," Jace said. "If this incantation really works as well as that orc claimed, telling the Dark Iron dwarves this method for dealing with black dragons would greatly ease the pressure on the Kingdom of Stormwind and make Blackrock Spire keep a lower profile. And don't forget—that damned rock is still in Shadowforge City. Taking it out and handing it over to the Reliquary Society could earn us a good sum of money."

Sola bared her orcish tusks in displeasure. "Don't call it a damned rock just because it couldn't see through my disguise. I put in a lot of effort to counter its soul-revealing abilities."

"That's not why…" Jace sighed. "It's because it didn't do much good in seeing through that princess's disguise either. Fine, not a damned rock. I was just… speaking off the cuff. Consider it a passing complaint."

Still, that didn't seem to comfort this sensitive green dragon much. Sola remained in a sullen mood—though on that fierce black face, it wasn't all that obvious.

Walking east for a day and a night, Jace and Sola followed a Blackrock patrol at a distance up to the mountaintop, then left the patrol and crossed over the mountain while the whelps were flying far off toward Blackrock Spire's peak.

They didn't rest at all, pushing north as hard as they could. Only when the thick clouds in the sky gradually thinned and the stars became faintly visible did they stop.

Who had invented this fearsome incantation?

Sitting on Solamriel's back, Jace pulled out the stone tablet, traced the runes on it with his fingers, and gave it a sniff.

This wasn't written in black dragon's blood, nor any pigment Jace recognized—but it had a strangely familiar quality. Only, the text had probably been written so absurdly long ago that it was no longer identifiable.

He couldn't wait to meet the mysterious person who could use the incantation.
