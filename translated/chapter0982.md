## Chapter 982: The Abandoned

After sliding down a steep slope and pushing through a stretch of dusty brush, they reached the bottom of the ravine.

Jace couldn't help but admire Sola's powers of observation and her familiarity with the mountains. He'd walked all the way here before finally laying eyes on that unassuming cave fissure tucked deep in the ravine. Several massive boulders lay scattered around the entrance, as if they'd tumbled down the mountain and piled up there, making the already modest opening even more thoroughly concealed.

But the size of the entrance didn't mean much. Earlier, they'd come across a narrow, unassuming crack of a cave that had turned out to be a two-hour trek just to reach the bottom — and another two hours to walk back out. A tremendous waste of time.

As they drew closer to the entrance, Jace caught a faint whiff of rot wafting from inside. It didn't smell like the animal dens around Blackrock Mountain. It was more like the weird stench you'd get from a swamp, or something like it.

Could there be there be water inside? This cave didn't seem right.

By now the sky had darkened. Jace drew Crystal Song and probed the entrance, confirming nothing strange lurked within, then took a few steps inside. He glanced back at the succubus and said, "Watch our backs, Morofel."

"Yes, master." Morofel obeyed, melting into the shadows.

Jace took the lead, with Sola following close behind. The cave was a winding, uneven mess — twisting left and right, climbing and squeezing, the passages alternating between narrow and wide, sometimes blocked by giant boulders. Honestly, it didn't feel like a good place to hide a rebel force at all.

The cave was deathly quiet — quiet enough that every step Jace took became deliberate, the scrape of his boots against stone painfully loud.

Even so, he could hear what seemed to be a rhythmic gurgling coming from deeper inside, like bubbles rising to the surface of water from time to time.

The deeper they went, the heavier the stench of decay became. After a while, Jace stopped thinking it smelled like rotting corpses. It was more like something had been left sealed away for too long… He couldn't put his finger into words, but it felt familiar, like something he'd smelled before.

As they walked on, a hint of sweetness crept into the smell. Even though the fragrance mixed with the foul stench into something deeply unpleasant, Jace managed to place it thanks to that very sweetness.

Ursula's herb room.

That's right. This rotten smell meant there might be plenty of spoiled herbs inside…

Jace hesitated for a moment, wanting to tell Sola that someone might be living in here, but found he could barely speak.

"Uh… uh." Jace turned back and pointed to his mouth. Sola glanced at him, seemed to catch on immediately, and gently pressed a finger to Jace's forehead. A fresh breeze swept through, carrying away every trace of the strange odor and howling through the narrow passage. The heavy sense of oppression that had been building in Jace's chest since who-knows-when suddenly lifted.

"The air here is poisonous," Sola said quietly.

"Poisonous?!" Sazeno squeaked in alarm.

"We don't need to breathe, moron," Morofel murmured from behind.

"Ah… uh, well…" The imp thought it over for a moment, then nodded.

Jace licked his lips and finally felt his tongue, which had seemed glued in place, come loose again. "Are you all right?" he asked.

"Don't forget what I am, Jace," Sola said.

Fair enough. Green dragons were the most venomous of all dragonkin — possibly the most toxic living things in all of Azeroth.

"Sorry. It's precisely because I'm immune to this stuff that I forgot to warn you."

"It's fine."

Jace nodded, about to rummage through his pack for some kind of antidote, though he couldn't remember if he'd brought a Restorative Potion. Sola channeled a natural enchantment and placed it on him.

"This spell will help keep the toxins at bay, but it won't last long. Let me know the moment you start feeling unwell again."

"Got it. Thanks." Jace said, "So do you know what's causing this poison gas?"

Sola carefully sniffed the air, pondered for a moment, then said, "I can detect Witchsbane components, and perhaps something that slows the mind and disrupts a mortal body's functions. Agony Extract — it can be drawn from serpent venom, scorpion venom, or windserpent venom. But the Agony Extract here seems exceptionally potent…"

"Witchsbane — you mean that herb?"

Jace had looked into that herb's properties while poring over alchemy books before. It existed in the game too, but he'd never known its specific effects.

"Yes. A highly lethal poisonous herb, but its scent is quite distinctive… I suspect that combined with the deceptively subtle smell of Agony Extract, it could kill an enemy without them ever realizing what was happening." Sola said softly, "In other words, this poison was deliberately crafted — a toxic gas meant to slow movement and kill, not something released by some wild beast."

Jace glanced down the dark passage and asked, "So the person who made it is hiding in here?"

"It's far too quiet, don't you think?" Sola said. "I'd guess that even if someone did live in here, they've long since been poisoned to death."

"Could it be another green dragon?" Jace asked.

Sola shook her head. "This isn't ours. Completely different."

A few steps deeper into the cave, Jace noticed a dwarf's rotting corpse on the ground. Judging by what was left of his clothing and his withered hair, he was a Dark Iron dwarf.

"Poisoned," Sola said under her breath. "Probably a few months ago."

Jace swallowed hard. No matter what kind of dwarf, they all possessed Earthen blood, giving them an extremely high resistance to toxins. If even a dwarf had been poisoned to death by the time he reached this place, without the blessing of the red dragon flowing through his veins, Jace might well have met the same fate.

What the hell was in here, and what did it have to do with the orc rebels?

If this was a hideout for orc rebels, why would they be releasing this much poison gas? And there were no orc bodies here — only one dwarf corpse, and from the look of him, he didn't seem like a soldier or a scout. More like some reckless adventurer or treasure hunter who'd barged in on his own.

Thinking it over as he pressed forward, Jace carefully descended a slope — and suddenly a faint glimmer of light appeared ahead. His eyes widened slightly.

The light was a deep, bloody red. It wasn't sunlight, and it didn't look like firelight either. It was more like… Jace remembered the time he'd met Razelikh in the stone hall at the base of the lighthouse — the terrifying dark crimson glow of the fel blood ritual that had filled the entire hall. This faint light was exactly the same.

But he couldn't smell any trace of black dragons at all, nor sense any shadow. Still, the visual alone was enough to set his nerves on edge.

At the same time, the glow emanating from Crystal Song dimmed considerably. As its master's tension ratcheted up, the blade seemed to brace itself for a powerful enemy.

They all moved with extreme care, making as little sound as possible. After advancing a little further toward the lit corner, Jace leaned out and stole a glimpse of the source of the red light — and furrowed his brow in confusion.

This wasn't a completely natural cave. Inside was a spacious stone chamber. Several half-filled jars connected by pipes sat around the room, conjuring images of some weird goblin scientist's mad bio-lab.

At the very back of the chamber was something like an iron plate. Beside it sat a rather tall figure with his back to them, lying propped against the thick support frame beneath the iron plate, head lowered, motionless — as if dead.

The figure's body appeared to be connected by those pipes, and there seemed to be metal bracers — or manacles — still clamped around his wrists and ankles.

So was this creature the master of this place, or its prisoner?

The two of them observed quietly for a while, confirming no one else was in this "bio-lab," then crept closer.

Sazeno kept his hands clamped over his mouth, whether from fear of making noise or fear of the poison gas seeping in. Jace kept his eyes locked on the lying figure, watching his movements while studying his appearance as best he could.

Once inside the chamber, Jace noticed that one of the pipes connected to the figure was linked to a slowly operating device that looked like a water pump. The pump was still drawing a pale yellow solution from a jar, operating slowly — and the rhythmic sound they'd heard from outside seemed to be coming from this very device. Though once they entered the chamber, the sound it made was actually less distinct.

Only up close did Jace make out the figure's actual appearance.

First off, he was definitely not a normal "person." The creature's entire body was dark red, its skin thick and rough like a toad's. Its arms were long and its legs stubby, with weird webbing-like growths on its arms. The hand resting on its leg looked like a dragon's claw, complete with jet-black sharp talons at the fingertips.

After circling around, Jace could finally see the head that had been hidden behind the body and the metal platform.

It was an extremely deformed dragon head — and grotesquely oversized for its body, making the whole creature look like a monstrous big-headed infant. On top of that, the figure was already quite tall, apparently over three meters in length, which made the head feel even more uncomfortably bloated.

And unlike the sturdy heads of drake men or dragonkin, this head was visibly soft and flaccid. Only the beak-like snout looked solid enough. The rest was not just sickly soft, but covered in swollen blisters all over.

Two horns grew from the top of its head, but one was long and the other short, and both were riddled with holes, as if worms had been eating through them. Jace glanced back at Sola. Sola was completely stunned, staring at the deformed monster in total shock. Clearly, she had no idea what this thing was either.

Even the worldly Morofel frowned at the sight of it.

The creature's eyes were tightly shut, with not a single sign of life about it. Jace swallowed and reached a hand toward its nostrils. He wasn't expecting anything, though — the creature's nostrils were caked with plenty of dark yellow filth mixed with blood, smeared all the way to the corners of its mouth. Probably pus that had flowed out and coagulated. One could only imagine the wretched state of its insides.

Add to that the poison gas filling the entire space, of course…

As expected, the creature had stopped breathing. But Jace could even sense emotional fluctuations coming from the body. Clearly, judging life or death by breathing alone didn't work here anymore — the machines in this chamber were sustaining its life in some way Jace couldn't begin to understand.

Stepping back to observe the creature, combined with the laboratory-like environment, reminded Jace of a boss from the game: Maloriak.

Maloriak was Nefarian's alchemist, assisting Nefarian in conducting a series of "chromatic dragon" experiments atop Blackrock Spire.

He himself was a half-human, half-drunk freak — but clearly a severe failure, neither quite a drake man nor a dragonkin. Not only did Nefarian hold his clumsiness and deformity in the utmost contempt, Maloriak himself was deeply ashamed of it.

Was this creature Maloriak? Could he be the one the orc had mentioned — the one who could use the incantation?

Thinking of what Sola had said earlier about the drake-tongue that no one used anymore, Jace felt that this creature in front of him might truly be connected to the stone tablet.

"Sola, can you heal it with restoration magic?"

"I… I don't know, Jace." Sola looked at the surrounding equipment and said, "If I tried to heal him with natural magic, it might bring catastrophic consequences."

Jace lowered his head and studied the creature. If they couldn't revive him, how were they supposed to learn about his connection to the tablet?

"Let's see what clues we can find in this room first," he said. "Morofel, Sazeno, help me look around too. See if there's anything unusual."

"Yes, master!"

The imp hopped down from the pack, while Morofel looked toward the equipment on the other side of the room.

This place was exactly what Jace had guessed — a laboratory, or rather an alchemy lab. Most of the herbs stored here had rotted in the terrible conditions. Whoever had controlled this place had long since abandoned it, along with this creature.

He walked up to a table and looked back and forth at the bottles and jars arranged on it. They were an assortment of elaborate healing potions. Whoever had been in charge here had clearly tried their best to keep this fellow alive.

"Jace." Sola called out to him. "Find anything?"

Jace looked over and saw Sola crouching beside the bizarre dragon-creature. "He's dreaming," Sola said.
