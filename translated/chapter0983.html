Chapter 983: Blood Ritual

"Dreams? You can enter his dreams and talk to him?" Jace asked.

"No." Sola said, "I send your consciousness in, Jace. I need to guide the magic from outside."

"Hold on." Jace held up a hand. "I'm not the least bit experienced with dream-walking, especially entering a…" He glanced at the creature. "Well, is there any faster way? Have you ever guided someone into a dream before?"

Sola stared at Jace for a moment, hesitating, then said, "I once sent a scythe-owl's consciousness into a raptor druid's dream to help him master the trick of shapeshifting…"

"An owl, right." Jace coughed. "They've got souls, I've got a soul — shouldn't be much difference. Any pointers?"

"Hard to say." Sola looked at the half-human, half-dragon freak. "I don't know what he's dreaming about. We won't know until we're inside. But in his dream, you need to be careful not to touch anything that might cause you to 'die' — or any event that could do the same. For example, don't go jumping off a cliff just because it's a dream. That could cause irreversible damage to your spirit. And if you find him, try not to agitate him, or the dream connection could become extremely unstable."

"Let's try it. Since you're the expert, I'll trust you, green dragon," Jace said.

Sola nodded gravely, shed her orcish appearance, and returned to the familiar look of the golden-haired elven woman from before — probably easier to cast spells in a form she was comfortable with.

She drew a delicate, polished emerald scale from her emerald-green robe. "Sit down next to him, Jace." Jace acknowledged and leaned against the support frame beneath the iron slab bed, taking a deep breath to steady himself.

"Once you're in the dream, try not to disrupt anything at first. Go along with the rhythm of things in the dream," Sola said, holding her scale.

"I understand," Jace said. "Like how you shouldn't forcibly wake a sleepwalker." Sola smiled slightly. "Something like that."

That smile put Jace a little more at ease. If she'd kept wearing that tense expression from earlier, he honestly wouldn't have trusted this young dragon to pull off such complicated magic.

"I'm going to put you to sleep," the green dragon said. "Ready?"

"Consider me ready," Jace said.

Sola pressed her lips together, placed her hand gently on Jace's forehead. His eyes grew heavy, though he didn't feel drowsy. "Has it started?" he couldn't help but ask.

"Started what?" asked a man nearby.

Jace glanced warily to his side and saw a handsome brown-haired man of about thirty grinding some kind of dark yellow plant powder.

The powder's shape and color looked a lot like Goldthorn, which he knew well, but the entire room reeked of a pungent Stranglethorn smell — like cilantro gone mutant.

The man was wearing a light purple outer robe, emblazoned with the Kirin Tor's Violet Eye symbol. The room was decorated in a typical Dalaran style — cream-colored walls, maroon-red carpets, and through the door, under the harsh sunlight, the faint outline of purple tiles and red brick.

Somehow Jace had also changed into a gray-white apprentice's outfit. A crucible in front of him was bubbling away, and he held a wooden stirring spoon. Waves of steam kept hitting his face and blurring his vision — and it was this thing giving off the Stranglethorn smell.

"Pay attention, Mr. Strick. We'll rest after trying this last combination for today," the man said.

"All right." Jace nodded.

So he was already inside that man's dream?

Or was everything he'd seen before the dream, and this was reality?

The moment that thought surfaced, Jace immediately realized it was some kind of shadow power invading his mind and soul. Suspicion, anxiety, unease, tension — these emotions were the early signs of shadow's gradual erosion.

Jace guessed it was the black dragon blood in this man's body that made his dream look normal on the surface while secretly corrupting everything within it.

But for Jace at this point, it was hardly a threat anymore.

Relying on his experience helping Ursula and Marin brew potions, Jace barely managed to meet the alchemist's requirements and helped him complete the experiment.

During the process, he stole a glance at the notebook hanging from the table drawer. The signature on it read: "Kim Mariels." Clearly, in the dream, Jace was this Mr. Kim Mariels's assistant or apprentice, a fellow named Strick.

Actually, the moment he'd seen the alchemist's surname, Jace had felt something — because it was far too similar to "Maloriak." That couldn't be a coincidence.

So before Maloriak became that monster — or rather, before he received his "ascension" — he'd actually been an alchemist in Dalaran?

The results of this experiment hadn't met expectations at all. When Kim Mariels walked out of the storage room, there was a hint of disappointment on his face.

He looked at Jace and beckoned. "Come out for a walk with me, Mr. Strick."

Jace nodded, tidied up his robe, and said, "Lack of success is perfectly normal, master."

Mariels smiled. "When did you get so easygoing? I thought you'd be secretly mocking me in your heart — after all, from the very start you felt the Catseye Elixir formula was already near-perfect, and there was no way to improve its effects without new materials…"

The streets of Dalaran in the dream were quite different from Jace's memory. The young mages walking about were still wearing all sorts of wizard's wide-brimmed hats, unlike the Dalaran Jace knew, where only the elderly mages liked to walk around in such hats.

"I've felt that something's off about you since just now, Strick," Mariels said. "Come on, what's wrong?"

Jace thought it over, then asked, "If there were a way to boost the Catseye Elixir's effects to a terrifying degree — not just night vision, but seeing details the human eye simply can't perceive, even the flow of energy, seeing through a demon's invisibility — master, how much would you be willing to pay for an opportunity like that? For instance, having to leave Dalaran for it…"

Mariels stopped at the roadside and fell silent for a long time, then asked, "I've already made my choice, haven't I?"

"Yes," Jace said.

For some reason, the sky that had seemed clear just moments ago now looked as though it had been raining for a long time, though Jace no longer had the energy to care about the weather.

Mariels let out a deep sigh and rubbed his eyes. "I shouldn't have done this. I shouldn't have… run from reality."

He looked over and asked, "Who are you? How did you find me? Is this the witchcraft of the Burning Thorn trolls? Did Nefarian send you?"

From his tone, Jace could sense an extraordinarily intense hostility toward Nefarian. "I'm from Stormwind, Mariels," Jace said.

"Stormwind." Mariels frowned. "I don't understand."

"I'll be direct. You must know about Onyxia," Jace said.

"Of course I know," Mariels said. "But she hasn't come to Blackrock Spire since Deathwing died."

Jace asked, "Now I have in my hands a stone tablet inscribed with a drake incantation. It's said this incantation can break the magic of black dragons. Do you have any insight on that?"

When Jace said this, Mariels's eyes practically bulged.

"You found those stone tablets? Impossible… Nefarian must have snatched them back!"

"Actually, they ended up with the Dark Iron dwarves," Jace said. "I managed to get them out of Shadowforge City. Do you have a connection to that incantation?"

"I wrote that incantation," Mariels said.

"You wrote it?" Jace asked in surprise.

"Yes," Mariels said. "Specially crafted for Nefarian and his kin, mage."

"You betrayed the black dragons?" Jace asked.

"Betray?!" Mariels seemed enraged by the word. He gritted his teeth and said, "I… was tricked into the black dragons' lair and subjected to that venomous ritual. For decades — centuries — I don't know how many years — there hasn't been a single day I haven't wanted to break free of all this horror. But the black dragon blood enslaves my spirit and controls my will. The chains I want to throw off exist in every inch of my blood vessels, in my brain. Human, can you imagine this kind of pain?!"

As he said this, his voice gradually grew hoarse, increasingly like some kind of monster's roar. His expression became hideous, his body hunched over, his movements like a deformed gnoll.

"You're human too, Mr. Mariels," Jace said.

Mariels froze, his legs buckled, and he collapsed against the base of the cream-colored wall, sitting on the wet floor. The mages passing by on the roadside paid no mind to this alchemist who'd lost control, turning a blind eye to everything happening here.

The sky had turned a thick, pitch black. The scorching stench of the Burning Plains and Blackrock Spire had unknowingly replaced the smell of baking bread and flowers on Dalaran's streets.

"They wanted me to create a new kind of dragon, mage." Mariels stared up at Jace. "Nefarian wanted me to cultivate a new kind of dragon egg — a dragon that possessed the power of all dragonkind, including the power of dreams. No matter how you entered my dreams… that kind of dragon could do it too! They called this dragon Chromagus, the Ender of All Things. Once such a dragon was born, the other dragons would no longer have any reason to exist."

"Chromatic dragons," Jace said.

"Yes, yes." Mariels's gaze grew increasingly frenzied. He pointed at Jace and said, "That's what the goblins called this kind of dragon. Chromatic dragons. A beautiful…"

At that, it was as if he'd suddenly remembered something. The smile that had been slowly spreading across his face vanished in an instant. His nose scrunched up, vicious as if he were about to devour some living creature.

"No, an ugly, everything-twisting creation! This is Nefarian, that perverted madman! He… he thought I didn't understand dragons well enough, couldn't comprehend the power of dragons. He… believed that only by becoming a dragon could I… But mage, although chromatic dragons are ugly and twisted, they will replace all dragons — including black dragons! Including that maniac who calls himself Victor Nefarius — including him too! That's the source of that incantation. Chromatic dragon blood. I used the essence of dragon blood extracted from I don't know how many deformed stillbirths to write this incantation… He definitely wouldn't have expected this."

Jace suddenly understood why the text on that incantation had felt faintly familiar to the touch. Chromatic dragon blood also carried the power of the red dragons, which resonated with the blessing Alexstrasza had bestowed on him…

As Mariels's emotions destabilized, the world of this dream began growing stranger and stranger.

Many of the mages on the street grew dragon heads. Dark red, foul-smelling dragon blood flowed along the roadside into the drains. Even the bricks and stones on the roadside no longer seemed to be Dalaran's red bricks, but had turned into Blackrock Spire's gray-black stone.

"So how do you activate this incantation?"

Jace tried to ignore all this. He was getting anxious, but his tone didn't dare be too urgent, afraid of shattering this fragile dream.

"You want to use this incantation against Onyxia?" Mariels laughed. "That female dragon is still strutting about in human form, swindling everyone?"

"Yes."

"It can be done. They're all the same bastards. But the incantation alone isn't enough."

"What else is needed?" Jace asked.

Mariels said, "Although my incantation uses drake script, it's been encrypted with blood magic to evade the powerful senses of dragons — otherwise they would have crushed me with their bare hands long ago. Only by wearing a ring I specially forged can you successfully activate its power. If it weren't for this cursed body of mine, I probably couldn't have done any of this at all. Ha — thank you, Lord Nefarian!"

He laughed, then lowered his voice and said, "When Deathwing was searching for a lair on the other side of the Dark Portal, I finally gained a tiny bit of limited freedom. I used this opportunity to begin my plan, cooperating with the orcs willing to resist black dragon rule… because only mortals not controlled by black dragon blood could use this ring to activate the incantation. Mage, I could do absolutely nothing in front of Nefarian.

"It's all that blood and those rituals causing trouble!"

At that, the alchemist sighed again. "Those orcs were brave, truly, but their luck wasn't good. The drakes didn't detect my incantation, but they accidentally discovered the orcs' rebellion plot. The incantation was with them, so before Nefarian returned, I had to flee Blackrock Spire and hide in their caves. But that incantation was lost during the orcs' evacuation process."

"But that ring — the ring is still here. Take it. Go kill the black dragons!"

Mariels pulled a metal ring set with a black gem from his pocket. Jace took it in his hand — it looked utterly unremarkable.

But then he realized a problem. This was a dream. Did a ring from a dream count? But Mariels seemed already immersed in the joy of victory — no matter how Jace shouted at him, he didn't respond.

The fellow lay on the ground, eyes glazed, muttering "someone can do it, someone can do it." It seemed that as long as Jace used his incantation against any black dragon — whether Onyxia or Nefarian — it counted.

The dream began to disintegrate along with the alchemist's convulsing laughter. Jace felt his own body breaking apart with it. He bit his fist hard, nearly tearing up from the pain, but still couldn't wake up. Then he felt a massive dragon shadow suddenly loom over him from behind…

Who?!

"Jace…"

A green dragon claw suddenly reached in front of him and grabbed his entire head. "Get out of here."
