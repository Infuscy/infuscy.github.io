## Chapter 988: Return, and Prepare

The Dragonqueen was sleeping in the Dream, but since they'd come all the way to Grim Batol, it wouldn't be proper to not pay their respects in person.

Right now, the dragons were still debating over who had actually killed Deathwing. Some dragons believed it was the red dragons who had paid the heaviest price — especially that Taranstrasyz's sacrifice to protect the Queen was indelible. Some believed Deathwing had simply died of his own arrogance. And of course, others believed the Grim Batol squad had made the greatest contribution.

But no matter what, all dragons agreed that Jace Sesso and his friends were unquestionably the Dragonqueena's saviors. So when they heard Jace wanted to see the Queen, Korialstrasz immediately agreed.

The Dragonqueeneen dwelled in a purified section of ruins high up in Grim Batol. After the evil flames Deathwing had brought dissipated and the corrupted magma had drained away, all that remained was a stretch of harmless, rugged brown-black rock — which instead made the damaged Wildhammer walls appear even more regular and magnificent.

Beneath the rocks, between the walls, lush plants and vines had grown over everything. But rather than the apocalyptic look of a city ruin being reclaimed by the wild, it more closely resembled a carefully tended plant garden — everything thriving and full of life.

The dwarven statues, which had seemed lifeless under the influence of Modgud's witchcraft and the orcs' dark magic, had actually taken on a gentle, almost comical look once all manner of flowering vines had crawled over them.

Guided by Korialstrasz, Solamere carried Jace over these delightful vistas and arrived beneath a dome brimming with emerald green. Queen Alexstrasza was lying in the midst of a group of whelps.

A body so enormous you couldn't believe it without seeing it with your own eyes, covered in scales, rising and falling with each breath at an extremely slow frequency — it was simply a vast forest of red leaves swaying with the rhythm of the wind.

Countless whelp guards, noticing a green dragon appearing here in the company of the Prince, all looked over with curiosity. Solamere was nearly suffocating by this point. She stared fixedly at the Alexstrasza before her, not daring to look away or even blink.

Jace habitually sensed the emotions in the Dragonqueen's sleeping dreams — there wasn't even a trace of darkness or negativity. The moment someone tried to probe her emotions, it instead purified the visitor's consciousness and soul instead. It made Jace, a warlock accustomed to manipulating shadow, feel as if something had been drawn out of him, and he gasped in discomfort.

But through this little probe, Jace set his mind at ease. The Queen's emotions held no negative fluctuations, which more or less indicated that everything was normal within the Emerald Dream.

Of course, he didn't think the culprit corrupting the Dream — the Satyr demon lord Xavius, hiding heaven-knews-where — could stand against Alexstrasza's power.

"To be able to see Queen Alexstrasza with my own eyes… I am beyond honored, Your Highness," Solamere said softly.

"There's no need for such words, young Solamere," Korialstrasz said. "Ysera's children are always welcome here."

Korialstrasz landed at the Queen's side. The dragon that had resembled a crimson deity in the forest now, set beside the sleeping Dragonqueen, suddenly shrunk into a pitiful pet.

"After the Queen heard the news that I'd met you in Dalaran, she said she'd been looking forward to your next visit, Jace Sesso."

He bowed his head lightly toward the Queen, then said, "Unfortunately, she is occupied at the moment and unable to receive you. But I believe when she wakes and learns you came, she'll be very happy."

"I came in a hurry to ask for help this time — truly unfortunate timing," Jace said. "Next time I'll bring the two of them and come specifically to visit the Queen."

"The Queen will be looking forward to that day," Korialstrasz exhaled softly, as if smiling.

At that moment, a whelp hanging from a broken edge of the dome let out a soft hiss. The Prince of the Red Dragons glanced up at him, then said as if remembering something: "Oh, right — there's something else important, Jace. The purification of that demonic sword has made progress."

Outside the dome, Korialstrasz brought Solamere to a place like a dwarven temple. It had sunk more than halfway into the ground during the great shattering of the earth, but a damaged room still remained above.

In this damaged room, surrounded by thorns teeming with powerful life force, the broken Apocalypse lay quietly on a stone pedestal shaped like a dragon's claw — like an exquisite skeletal work of art in the shape of a sword.

Compared to the last time he'd seen it, the evil aura permeating it had almost disappeared.

"This sword cannot be purified by ordinary magical means, Jace," the red dragon said. "Although it is broken and its power severely damaged, it still violently resists all foreign magic — especially the power of life. But it is, after all, broken… unable to withstand the prolonged immersion of the power of life. Compared to when I first brought it here, several weeks have passed now, and its edge has diminished considerably now."

"Will it still spread plague?" Jace asked.

"It's hard to say." The red dragon said, "At present, it's unclear whether it's merely lying dormant and waiting for an opportunity, or whether it has already submitted to the Queen's power. But I believe that before long, this ill-fated weapon that has brought disaster to countless races will be subdued."

Jace nodded. Honestly, he hadn't really harbored any extravagant hopes that the Apocalypse would be thoroughly purified and reborn in the hands of a friendly new master. He just hoped this sword wouldn't inexplicably fall into an enemy's hands again.

After all, he had personally used Ursolass to break this sword, and when it had been in the Black Knight's possession, it clearly had its own ideas and its own will. If he made an evil thing like this hold a grudge, it would be miserable.

Seeing Stormwind's city walls again was already three days later. To avoid attracting the attention of Lakeshire residents and militia in Redridge, Jace directed Sola to fly over the forest south of Lake Everstill to enter Elwynn Forest.

After reaching the forest south of Eastvale Logging Camp, they switched to traveling on foot — after all, any closer to Stormwind and it would be nearly impossible to avoid being spotted by sentries.

After returning to Stormwind, Jace had Morofel take Sazeno home first, then he and Sola went straight to The Gilded Rose.

It was just past noon. The place wasn't crowded, and the food came quickly. Jace started with a sausage to satisfy his craving, since it had been heaven-knows-how-long since he'd eaten meat this normal.

"What do we do next, Jace?" Sola asked.

Jace gnawed on the greasy sausage and said: "Everything's ready now — the incantation, the ring, the incantation's properties…

"…an incantation guaranteed to hit its mark. Before, I was still worrying about how to aim it, but now it looks much easier than I thought."

Sola glanced at a passerby outside the inn, then lowered her voice: "But that's a… dragon, Jace. You don't want to start a fight with a dragon inside the city walls."

"Of course not." Jace gave the innkeeper a look, tossed a few copper coins onto the table, wiped his mouth, and walked out.

Out on the street, Jace said to Sola: "So we need to find a way to lure her outside the walls, then set an ambush. I've been thinking about this the whole way back."

"You should have mentioned it to me earlier," Sola said.

"It's not that I didn't want to talk about it — it's that ninety percent of the time I was awake, you were asleep, Sola. The moment you opened your eyes you'd grab me and talk about how beautiful and gorgeous the Queen is…"

Looking at Sola's guilty yet somewhat embarrassed expression, Jace smiled and said quietly: "All right, all right. I hadn't figured it out either back then. But now I've got a rough idea…"

The two of them walked through a city gate. Winter had already arrived. A cold wind blew over the fallen leaves on the canal, and the ripples on the water looked pale and stiff.

Aside from a few sentries standing guard on the road leading to the prison in the distance, there were no pedestrians at all all the way to the canal bridge leading to the Mage Quarter.

Jace continued in a low voice: "I'm considering using the pretext of discussing investigation results to lure her outside the city. After all, Stormwind is full of eyes and ears, and Katrana Prestor knows this perfectly well. Plus the Park is full of magic-skilled elves. Finding a quiet, secluded place to discuss sensitive information about black dragons and Lord Prestor seems perfectly reasonable, doesn't it?"

"I know SI:7 has some assets outside the city. I could talk to Mathias and have him lend me a location as the meeting place. I can trust him. But this is just a rough plan — I'm still thinking through where the problems might be. Once everyone's here, we'll discuss it together."

After saying that, Jace scooped some canal water to wash his hands. Sola looked at this dark mage carrying Ursolass on his back, squatting by the river to wash his hands and face, and asked: "What about the Dark Iron dwarves?"

"Dark Iron dwarves?" Jace turned around, stunned for a moment.

"If what the alchemist said in the dream is correct, and Archmage Krasus's judgment is right, then it means the incantation on the stone tablets can only be used once before it's spent," Sola said. "If we return to Shadowforge City in the future, how are we going to explain that to the Rune-Smiths? Don't forget your staff is still with them, Jace."

Sola's words immediately stumped Jace. These past few days from the Burning Plains to Grim Batol and back to Redridge, he hadn't given this matter a single thought.

"That doesn't matter anymore," Jace said. "Let's deal with what we need to deal with first, Sola. The rest can wait."

The two of them walked across the canal. Jace said: "After I take you back to the Mage Quarter, I need to go find my alchemist first. Heaven knows what kind of battle we're going to face — even with a red dragon helping, we can't let our guard down. How are you feeling right now, Sola?"

No matter how powerful her natural magic was or how skilled she was with the power of dreams, Sola was ultimately a young dragon who hadn't experienced much combat. Jace was genuinely worried that she might freeze up when facing Onyxia.

To his surprise, Sola said without hesitation: "I don't know why, but I'm not afraid at all, Jace. In the past…

"…I always did everything I could to avoid any conflict, even when running into ill-tempered beasts in the Emerald Dream."

"Maybe this is what Queen Ysera wants to see too," Jace smiled. "She wanted you to leave the peaceful Emerald Dream and come outside to toughen yourself up a bit. Looks like it's been somewhat effective — which means I've done Queen Ysera a great service."

Sola said: "Remember back in Winterspring, you told me that shadow and emotions are linked, and if you want to quickly throw off the Nightmare and break free from shadow's chains, you need to act more according to your own heart and do more things that make you happy. Back then I was still wondering — how could someone like you, who always chases after difficulty and danger, feel happy? Now it seems I'm understanding you more and more."

"You might still have a bit of a misunderstanding there, Sola," Jace said jokingly. "It's not like we go looking for trouble for no reason. The key is, if I don't go looking for it, it comes looking for me. A lot of the time I'm pretty helpless myself…"

"Lady Vereesa?" Sola suddenly looked ahead.

Jace followed her gaze and saw a slender figure standing in the gateway into the Mage Quarter, wrapped in a cloak and hood.

The other party pushed back her hood, revealing silver hair. She smiled at Jace and Sola, walked over without a word, and threw herself directly into Jace's arms.

"Damn it, you're finally back. Not a single word of news…"

Jace held the elf in his arms tightly and said softly: "A messenger who could cross orc blockades, evade black dragon surveillance, and traverse the Burning Plains probably wouldn't come cheap."

"You could tell him to collect on delivery." Vereesa took a deep breath, then whispered: "I'd pay a bag of gold for even a single word from you, Jace Sesso."

"Better yet, just credit it toward the Ilgal Tower construction funds," Jace whispered.

"That won't do. That tower's mine, but the deed doesn't say 'Windrunner' anywhere on it." Vereesa grinned mischievously, let go, then opened her arms toward Sola. "Sola… thank you. I know protecting this wicked warlock must have been an incredibly difficult task."

"Actually, most of the journey it was this warlock protecting me," Sola said, smiling as she hugged Vereesa.

Jace said from the side: "I couldn't have done it without Sola's help this time. At the start, I was even thinking of going to Shadowforge City alone to figure out this incantation business. Now I think about how if she hadn't been there, I can't imagine how much more time I'd have wasted — I might not even have made it out."

"And you were thinking of going out by yourself again." The elf sighed and said in a low voice: "Grede and I were about ready to come looking for you in Shadowforge City, Jace. I'd even written to the Archmage asking about magical shapeshifting. If you'd been even a week later coming back…"

"Sorry," Jace said. "To make sure… I made another trip to Grim Batol, so it delayed me just under a week."

"Grim Batol?" Vereesa's eyes widened slightly. Just then, a few apprentices from the Wizard Sanctum passed by nearby, and the three of them fell silent in unison.

"This isn't the place to discuss things," Vereesa said.

Jace said: "Let's set up a meeting — the sooner the better — to discuss what comes next."

"I was just on my way to find Grede anyway. I can let him know." Vereesa asked: "How about tomorrow morning?"

"Tonight," Jace said. "At my parents' house."

"All right." Vereesa nodded.
