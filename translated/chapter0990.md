## Chapter 990: Evening Gathering

In the evening, Jace arrived early at his parents' house. To his surprise, there was already a dwarf sitting by the canal in front of the house.

"Hey, Jace!"

Grede bellowed from a distance, then said: "I didn't expect you to get in and out of Shadowforge City in under a month, but these past few weeks, I haven't been able to eat or sleep."

"Can't eat? I think you've actually put on a few pounds," Jace said.

"That's because there's been no work." Grede patted his round belly. "Before, I promised to keep an eye on that princess in the city for you, and I didn't dare leave town casually. Besides, if you'd been in Stormwind the whole time, the two of us going out for a few more meals, I'd have gotten even fatter, right?"

"Right." He leaned in and asked: "That dragon said you had something for me. I asked her what it was and she refused to say a word. What is it?"

"Patience." Jace said. "Goddamn it…"

Grede's voice rose. Jace immediately cut him off: "Is everyone else here?"

The dwarf glared at him, jabbed a thumb toward the back door and said: "Sola's here, and Vereesa is still…" Halfway through, he looked past Jace toward the distant riverbank, froze, and didn't finish.

Jace turned around and saw a tall, slender elven woman walking toward them.

She was wearing a blue robe with gold trim — she looked exactly like one of those Quel'Thalas nobles living in the Park who loved looking down their noses at people, and the kind whose family name could be connected to the sun at that.

Considering Vereesa usually wore simple casual clothes at best, maybe a pretty cloak clearly gifted by her older sister, the contrast made it impossible for Jace to adjust all at once.

"She…"

"Uh." Grede explained: "She's been attending quite a few noble gatherings lately. King Varian and the other human nobles can't exactly pretend they haven't noticed a daughter of House Windrunner arriving in Stormwind, can they? And of course, she's also been helping keep surveillance on Katrana Prestor — she's the one in the open, I'm the one in the shadows."

Then he turned his head again: "Though I didn't expect her to dress like this even with the few of us meeting up tonight."

Jace coughed. Vereesa had already arrived in front of them.

"Why aren't you going inside? It's so cold out here." She glanced at the door behind them.

As soon as she said it, she noticed Jace's expression. Her demeanor faltered slightly. "Someone from the Eberlock family wanted to see me this afternoon, asking for the help of the Farstriders. I haven't had time to change yet."

"What did they want?" Grede asked.

"The undead plague in Duskwood is making the wildlife there increasingly crazy. The Stormwind Church and soldiers are short-staffed, so they came to the Farstriders on the off chance." Vereesa sighed.

"You look lovely, Wen." Jace smiled. "You've just raised the class of our little evening gathering by a notch."

"Are you kidding… rushing all the way here has been pure agony." Vereesa tugged at her collar.

Everyone joked their way into the house. Inside, Sola was sitting in the corner reading a copy of *Tales of Darrowmere*. Jace felt like he'd seen this book at Marin's place.

Seeing that everyone had arrived, Sola slammed the book shut with a "bang" and set it aside. Grede immediately blocked Jace's way, glanced at the cloth bundle he'd brought, and asked: "Quick, what's the good stuff inside? A Dark Iron dwarf's head?"

Jace frowned. This guy really had a hard mouth — and yet he was the one who couldn't eat after seeing Dark Iron dwarf leather in Stranglethorn.

"Harder than that." Jace set the cloth bundle he'd brought on the table and opened it, revealing the box inside.

When he lifted the lid, Grede was puzzled at first, then slowly widened his eyes, opened his mouth, and made a few sounds like a fish bone stuck in his throat — "uh… uh…"

"You… you're not joking, are you?"

Grede looked at Sola, then at Jace, reached out and touched the flat surface of the war hammer, then immediately pulled his hand back.

"Dwalgathis." He murmured, his lips trembling.

"What does that mean?" Jace asked. He'd never heard this Dwarvish term before.

Grede swallowed and said: "This is the weapon of Khadros Wildhammer, Jace Sesso! This is Khadros's war hammer — don't tell me you don't know about this!"

"I've seen the statue of Khadros Wildhammer," Vereesa said. "The war hammer he was holding seemed a bit different from this one — it had relief carvings representing the Wildhammer clan, but this one…"

"That hammer was just a ceremonial piece passed down in the Wildhammer clan — who'd take that thing into battle?!" Grede turned his head and stared at the elf.

He turned back, his hands seemingly wanting to touch the war hammer again, but ultimately he couldn't bring himself to.

"Take a look at this thing, damn it… this is a proper tool for smashing people. Our old chief Khadros used this war hammer to smash the witch Modgud to death!"

"It killed Modgud?" Vereesa asked, eyes widening in surprise.

"Every single gryphon rider in Aerie Peak knows that!" Grede emphasized. "Of course, he couldn't withstand the dark power released after that female monster died, so he never got the chance to take this hammer back — but that's nothing to fault him for, nothing! Because Khadros knew there was no need to sacrifice more gryphon riders' lives just to retrieve this thing. The disaster at Grim Batol had already cost too many lives… But how did this end up here? This can't be a fake, can it? A replica from the Dark Iron? Why would they copy Khadros's weapon?"

"This is the real thing," Jace said. "Have you heard of Rokhtus Darkseal? He's the one who gave it to me. He's a member of the Dark Iron. After the end of Grim Batol, the Dark Iron dwarves found this war hammer. Clearly, Emperor Thaurissan at the time wanted to see what was so impressive about this hammer, so he handed it over to the Dark Iron for research."

"Rokhtus Darkseal?" Grede frowned. "He was the leader of the Dark Iron before the War of the Three Hammers, Jace. If he were alive today he'd be nearly four hundred years old — are you sure you heard that right? Even that old bastard Muradin Bronzebeard isn't even three hundred!"

"Believe it or don't." Jace said: "He helped me a great deal in Shadowforge City, and he specifically instructed me to deliver this war hammer to you, Grede."

"To me?" Grede asked. "Why? You didn't buy this, did you?"

"How much money could buy Khadros Wildhammer's war hammer?" Jace asked. Grede was stunned for a while before saying: "But he's a Dark Iron dwarf…"

"Clearly not all Dark Iron dwarves in Shadowforge City follow the Firelord Ragnaros," Jace said. "I could tell — he wholeheartedly wants the Three Hammers to reforge their alliance and come back together again, just like they did in Ironforge over two hundred years ago."

"Hah." Grede gave a dry laugh. "Then he's a pure-blooded Dark Iron through and through, because only Dark Iron dwarves would be crazy enough to spout such nonsense!"

After saying that, he glanced at the hammer again. "But… that really is Rokhtus Darkseal?"

"I'll tell you what — even the elements in Shadowforge City helped me confirm it." Jace took out the Dark Iron ring of the Dark Iron that he'd been carrying and showed it to Grede. "A lava elemental lord saw this ring and knew it was given to me by Rokhtus Darkseal."

Grede looked at the ring and said: "I don't recognize this."

"I can vouch for it," Sola said. "That lava elemental didn't want to follow the Firelord either, so he'd been secretly helping Rokhtus and the Dark Iron."

Grede couldn't help but frown. He looked at the war hammer in the box again and muttered under his breath: "Could this really be Dwalgathis… This thing is of immense significance to the gryphon riders — no, to the entire Wildhammer clan, Jace. I might need to take it back to Aerie Peak to show everyone."

"It's yours now," Jace said. "You can do whatever you want with it."

Grede said: "I really didn't expect you to give me this. I wouldn't have guessed it in a million years. This is something out of legend — even in the legends it never survived, and yet here it is in the real world. Tell me, Jace — how much would it cost to repair that tower? I'll give you however much I've got."

"You don't need to spend a cent." Jace smiled. "Like I said, this is something Rokhtus gave you, Grede. I'm just the delivery boy, nothing more. I didn't pay anything. It's because he heard you defeated a black dragon at Grim Batol that he was willing to give it to you."

"Anyway, Jace…" Grede gripped Skyshatter in his hands. The faint electric glow on the war hammer immediately brightened, occasionally discharging bolts of lightning — making it hard to believe this was a thing that had slumbered in this box for two hundred years.

"This is real. This is absolutely real." He stared at the blue patterns on the hammerhead and murmured softly in Dwarvish. "Hey, hey, Grede." Jace warned: "Don't test that hammer here, damn it."

"Ah, right, right." Grede nodded, set the hammer back in the box, looked at the surrounding tables, chairs, and furniture, and swallowed. "I can tell, Grede," Vereesa said. "This war hammer likes you too."

"Let's hope so." Grede nodded, licked his lips — clearly struggling to contain his excitement. But after all, this was a weapon of the dwarven ancestors, and he didn't want to look too eager to claim it as his own, so he forced himself to calm down.

He closed the lid, steadied his emotions, and said: "Let me cool down a bit. Let's talk about that princess…" Vereesa smiled at the dwarf, then asked Jace: "Did you get the incantation that can break the black dragon's disguise from Shadowforge City?"

"Not just break the black dragon's disguise." Jace laid out the stone tablets and the ring he'd gotten from Reginald Windsor on the table.

Vereesa picked up the ring and looked at it. "There seems to be some extremely sophisticated arcane magic on this. Did a mage make this?"

Jace couldn't help but marvel at how a pure-blooded High Elf was really something else — he couldn't sense a damn thing.

He and Sola explained the relationship between the ring and the stone tablets to Grede and Vereesa, and also gave a brief account of the story of the drake-man alchemist they'd found in the cave.

After hearing everything, Grede immediately asked: "Since this incantation was made for mortals, and even orc soldiers might be able to use it, does that mean I can cast it too?"

Jace was about to reflexively say "of course," but as the words reached his lips, he thought of another timeline — the endgame after Marshal Windsor recited this incantation and exposed the black dragon's magic a decade and a half from now.

"Maybe. But to guarantee success, it's better if I do it," Jace said. "I was just asking," Grede said.

Jace said: "Judging from all the channels of information we have about this incantation, perhaps breaking the black dragon's magic is just a side effect of this incantation. Its true purpose is to kill black dragons outright. But we can't count on it being that efficient, so we need to be prepared for a fierce fight."

"That means we can't do this inside the city walls…" Vereesa said.

"That's what I think too." Jace laid out the plan he'd described to Sola earlier. Grede asked: "What if she's not willing to go out?"

"She has no reason not to." Vereesa sat to the side and said: "For a woman who's been tormented by black dragon magic for this long, she should suspect more than anyone that a black dragon might be disguised right beside her, and be more vigilant against black dragon infiltration than anyone. Frankly, if you want to discuss investigation results concerning her father, she should want to go to an undisturbed place outside the city more than anyone."

Jace nodded and said: "If she has concerns, I'll persuade her to agree — get her to think more about her father's personal safety. She shouldn't refuse. The rest of you will ambush her at the agreed location. After the incantation forces her to reveal her true form… well, there's nothing more to say after that."

Sola let out a long breath. "Onyxia… it's really hard to imagine I'd be involved in going after an elder like her."

"Her own dad's dead as a doornail!" Grede snorted. "Nothing to be afraid of."

Vereesa raised an eyebrow. "Indeed. I've been looking forward to it for a long time — killing this ancient monster with my own hands…"

"You won't get the chance, little elf." Grede patted the box containing Skyshatter. "I'll use this war hammer to crack that female lizard's skull first."

"Don't rush to claim credit — I've also called Krasus here," Jace said. "To guarantee she doesn't get a chance to slip away." Grede immediately slapped his thigh in displeasure. "Oh my gods, a red dragon stealing the glory again this time?"

Vereesa rolled her eyes, then asked: "Has Archmage Krasus recovered from Deathwing's curse?"

Jace shook his head. "His eye seems to be permanent damage, but he agreed very readily to my request. I think his magic has mostly recovered at the very least. But even with a red dragon's help like him, we can't let our guard down. We need to prepare well during this period."

"No problem." The dwarf slammed the table.

"Easy for you." Jace gritted his teeth and pointed at him. "This isn't the Pig and Whistle."

"Ah, sorry." Grede raised his hand.

"There's one more thing." Jace looked at Vereesa. "You've been socializing with those nobles quite a bit lately, Wen. I heard the Queen and the King seem to have some issues recently because of Prestor. Is there any truth to that?"

Vereesa nodded. "King Varian and Queen Tiffen… have indeed grown somewhat distant lately. Logically, with the Queen pregnant, they should both be looking forward to the arrival of the new child — their relationship should be better. I also suspect it's because of that Prestor, but I have no direct evidence that Varian is having an affair with her. And Varian is very careful to keep his distance from her — at least at several banquets and public events, I've never seen Varian do anything beyond ordinary friendship with that princess."

"But I have to say, Queen Tiffen seems deeply troubled by this matter. She's been looking unwell, and even the Church's priests are powerless. There are even rumors among the nobles that this child might not be saved."

"Could it be that black dragon's dark magic?" Grede asked. "She planning to torture the Queen to death?"

When these words came out, everyone fell silent. Outside, the sound of the dark river water rippling was clearly audible.

After a while, Vereesa said: "I don't have the ability to distinguish between curses and dark magic, Jace. Maybe you should find a chance to meet with the Queen and see what's really going on."

Jace frowned and said: "Ever since that meeting at Stonewatch a few years ago, I've been to the palace so many times without seeing her again. But I truly can't pretend this doesn't exist. When I see Mathias Shaw, I'll ask him if there's anything he can do."

"Now how about you tell us about your underground adventure?" Grede asked impatiently. "Tell me what Shadowforge City is like — is it full of gays everywhere? Do those dwarves even dare to screw fire elementals?"

Vereesa looked at him in shock. Grede spread his hands: "What are you looking at me like that for? That's what everyone says!"

"You tell it, Jace." Sola couldn't help but laugh. Jace wanted to say something, but seemed unable to get the words out.

After all, having made a trip through Shadowforge City, he found that although he couldn't fully agree with what Grede had said, he also couldn't completely refute it.

He told them carefully about the events around Blackrock Mountain this time. Hearing that their underground distant relatives could only survive by eating bugs, Grede laughed his head off. But hearing how tough that Dark Iron dwarf in the arena was, he immediately stopped laughing, shouting that if Morofil hadn't killed him, he'd definitely find a chance to teach him a lesson — and he absolutely wouldn't invoke the Avatar.

Although he kept saying he hated Dark Iron dwarves, when it came to talking about those guys, the most excited one was Grede too — maybe the relationship between the Three Hammers was just that contradictory.

If Emperor Thaurissan and the Seven hadn't summoned Ragnaros and cursed the entire Dark Iron clan, perhaps after two hundred years of stalemate, the relationship between the other two hammers and the Dark Iron wouldn't have come to this.

As for Sola, she couldn't go three sentences without mentioning the Dragonqueen — to the point that Jace already regretted taking her to Grim Batol.

Vereesa, unsurprisingly, mocked the two of them for getting tailed by SI:7 scouts, saying that during her time in Stormwind, she could hear a few insects crouching outside her window. This forced Grede to bring up her glorious deeds in Ashenvale and Feras to dampen her arrogance, which made Vereesa furious — but it made Sola laugh so hard she doubled over.

By late at night, none of them wanted to part ways. But Grede was indeed dozing off from exhaustion, and Jace was feeling tired too. As for Sola, sitting there, no one knew whether she was listening attentively or doing something else — after all, even when this green dragon was talking, you couldn't be sure she wasn't sleeping at the same time. Only Vereesa, an elf, was still as energetic as if she'd just jumped out of bed in the morning, but seeing that everyone's voices had been talked hoarse, she knew it was time to call it a night.

The few of them came outside by the canal. The city had fallen completely quiet — pitch black, with barely any lights left.

"Then I'll head off first," Sola said, looking at everyone. "When Prince Korialstrasz arrives, be sure to let me know right away."

"All right," Jace said.

"I'm going home to sleep too," Grede muttered.

"One more thing, Grede." Jace stopped him. "I must tell you this — and it's also what Rokhtus Darkseal wanted me to relay to you."

"I knew getting Skyshatter wouldn't be that simple." The sleepiness on Grede's face vanished by more than half.

"This has nothing to do with Skyshatter, Grede," Jace said. "There's a group of Dark Iron dwarves who've been hoping to break the Firelord's curse. To that end, they're planning to forge a powerful weapon that can stand against Ragnaros's war hammer, Sulfuras — the Sulfuron Hammer."

"I've heard of that," Grede said, his tone rather calm.

"There are fewer and fewer Dark Iron dwarves willing to defy the Firelord," Jace said. "He wanted me to relay to you…"

"Are you kidding me?" Grede interrupted. "That's the Firelord Ragnaros!"

"The master of the Plane of Fire, Jace…" Vereesa said.

"I know it sounds exaggerated," Jace said. "But Rokhtus Darkseal helped me a great deal. He wanted me to tell you, Grede — and I don't want to hide it."

"It's not that I'm scared of anything, and it's not that I don't respect the Dark Iron, but the Dark Iron dwarves made their own mistake — they'd best fix it themselves. Nothing to do with us 'wild folk,' hah." Grede waved his hand. "Trying to lure me into going up against a god with a hammer that doesn't exist?! I haven't been drinking tonight, goddamn it…"

After saying that, he swaggered off toward the Dwarf District, and his figure quickly disappeared around the corner.

"I have a bad feeling about this, Jace," Vereesa said softly. "I think he'll agree eventually, and you'll definitely help him."

"Not tonight, at least." Jace gently put his arm around the elf. Vereesa whispered: "Come back to the Mage Quarter with me?"

"Let me lock the door first." Jace licked his lips.
