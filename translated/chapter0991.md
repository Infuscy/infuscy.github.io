## Chapter 991: Blood Vial

Opening his eyes, sunlight poured through the window into the bed from the crisp winter air. The dappled shadows of the oak trees along the walkway outside swayed across the sheets.

"Good morning, Jace."

Vereesa, simply draped in a light-colored thin blouse, sat by the window and smiled over at him. It was so cold — the sight of her like that made Jace feel cold on her behalf.

Still, Jace could sense a trace of shadow in her emotions. "Morning. Something on your mind?" he asked, leaning up against the headboard.

"Nothing." Vereesa immediately shook her head, but after thinking it over, said: "Maybe it's the thought of facing that dragon mother soon.

"I don't know if it's the suggestion of this matter, but lately my interactions with Lady Katrana Prestor may have seemed very unnatural. She's a human princess of extraordinary beauty and graceful manners, with an extremely delicate mind — it's really hard to connect her with a ruthless, merciless ancient dragon. Every time I'm around her, these two wildly contrasting impressions make me feel involuntarily disoriented."

Jace's brow furrowed by the slightest degree. Seeing his gaze, Vereesa immediately said: "I haven't been cursed or hit by any dark magic." She pulled the cloak hanging by the window over her shoulders. "I'm just nervous, and worried she'll see through my nervousness — just like you."

"You're afraid she'll realize you know something?" Jace asked. "Can she tell?" Vereesa asked.

Jace shook his head. "I seriously doubt it. Have you said anything to her? Or shown any disgust? I think humans are used to the arrogance and disdainful looks of High Elves. She lives in the Park — she should be deeply familiar with that. Besides, the entire Alliance knows the youngest daughter of House Windrunner doesn't like formal occasions and prefers running through forests and the wilds… so how you behave at those dinners and balls shouldn't matter at all."

"I never thought being uncomfortable at those occasions could count as a strength." Vereesa lowered her head.

"Don't dwell on it," Jace said. "Just focus on what you're best at — fighting."

"Mm." Vereesa smiled and acknowledged, but Jace could see that layer of shadow still hadn't fully lifted.

"Is she really Deathwing's daughter, Onyxia?" Vereesa asked.

"When we first caught on to the problem, you weren't here, Wen." Jace got out of bed, putting on his shirt and trousers. "Even a High Elf like you, raised on arcane energy, generates shadow energy when negative emotions arise — that's unavoidable for all living things. But that Prestor — nothing. It's like… those arcane constructs in Silvermoon that haven't been activated."

Seeing Vereesa still deep in thought, Jace asked: "You two haven't become friends during this time, have you?"

"Of course not," Vereesa said immediately. "Even if you hadn't told me to be on guard around that woman, I wouldn't casually befriend a human."

"You'll know when that incantation takes effect," Jace said.

Vereesa asked: "Yesterday when Grede asked if he could activate the incantation, I noticed you hesitated. What are you worried about?"

"I don't think he can successfully cast the incantation. Nothing more," Jace said.

"Then what about me?" Vereesa said. "Teach me how to cast this incantation, Jace, in case something unexpected happens when the time comes."

"Casting the incantation itself isn't difficult," Jace said. "Hold the stone tablet and recite the incantation written on it to activate it — after all, even those orc soldiers could manage it. The key is that ring. There's only one."

"All right." Vereesa shook her head. "I don't know why I'm overthinking things so much. I've always hated this kind of worrying. When you were at Blackrock Mountain, I felt like I'd lost my soul every day. At first I was a little angry that you hadn't even asked whether I was willing to go with you, but later it completely turned into worry…"

"I thought about you every day too, Wen." Jace walked over and hugged her. "Once, when we passed through a mine tunnel, we got attacked by underground scorpions. I was thinking — if you were here, we wouldn't even have to lift a finger, they'd run away on their own…"

Vereesa smiled. "Seems like aside from that, I wouldn't have been much use in Shadowforge City either. What about Sola? I heard from Grede she traveled the whole time disguised as a Dark Iron dwarf."

Jace scratched his cheek. "Honestly, aside from her appearance, she doesn't look anything like a Dark Iron dwarf. It's just that she wasn't the weirdest one in Shadowforge City either, so she wasn't exposed. In any case, she's gotten much braver than before and is more willing to integrate into our group. But I think against Onyxia, she probably won't be of much use either — she's terrified of those older dragons, so don't count too much on her healing magic."

"Don't underestimate her, Jace. She is a real dragon, after all." Vereesa nodded.

"Of course. But our opponent is no ordinary one either." Jace threw on his cloak. "So to be ready, there's still a lot to do today."

"Until next time, then, Jace." Vereesa waved her hand gently. Since they were in the Mage Quarter, Jace naturally had to check in at The Slaughtered Lamb again.

If they were going to brew any complicated potions, Ursula generally needed a day or two. Krasus was unquestionably a dragon — if he'd settled affairs at Grim Batol, he'd definitely arrive quickly. To avoid wasting time, Jace had to get in touch with Ursula as soon as possible too.

Arriving at The Slaughtered Lamb, the shop wasn't open yet at this hour. Jace knocked on the door. Tharle Mor opened it sleepily, as if he hadn't woken up yet.

"What is it, this early?" Tharle said: "Last night I helped Galen Airs move stuff the entire night and had only just fallen asleep."

"Is Ursula downstairs?" Jace asked.

"Yeah, yeah." Tharle pushed the door open, and Jace immediately ducked into the basement.

The moment he entered the basement, Jace suddenly felt a twisted surge of dark power dart out from the shadows to the side. His relaxed nerves snapped taut in an instant. He turned his head and launched a spell of Asphyxiation at the uninvited guest — only to have it immediately choked off by a powerful counterspell force that severed his connection to the shadow power…

Jace wasn't the least bit panicked, because being counterspelled was routine for him. And since his magic had been countered, that meant he was facing a fel hound — which meant the next thing should be tentacles draining his mana.

He endured the dizziness that came after being counterspelled, reached out and grabbed the swaying shadow darting right up to his face, precisely seizing the tentacle, then kicked at the shadow at the base of the tentacle with his foot. A shriek immediately came from beneath him.

"Stop!"

A command in demonic tongue came from down the passage. Ursula, wrapped in an outer robe, came running out in a flurry. The moment she saw Jace, her expression couldn't decide whether it should be shock, joy, or worry.

The fel hound on the ground was still struggling, trying to break free from Jace's control. After Jace let go, it angrily backed away several steps and crouched into a corner, letting out furious hisses.

"Sorry, Jace, this is our new gatekeeper, Kilruk. It doesn't know you yet…"

Jace caught his breath, checked the spots on his body where the tentacles and claws had gotten him, confirmed nothing had been torn, then looked at the massive fel hound and asked: "Where's Bethadim?"

"You forgot — it's been banished," Ursula said, somewhat helplessly.

"Ah, that's right." Jace nodded. "But I learned the incantation for summoning demonic minions from the Dark Iron dwarves, Ursula. I don't know if I can summon a minion that's already been banished, but at least it works for minions currently bound by a demonic contract."

"The Dark Iron dwarves were actually willing to teach you the incantation for summoning demonic minions?" Ursula said immediately. "Could you teach it to me? Let's see if I can summon Mr. Bethadim back…"

Jace said: "I can't guarantee we can really bring him back. And you need to be careful about how many demons you control, Ursula. Right now you've got an imp and a fel hound. If you add another fel hound, your will is going to get very fragile."

"Galders reminded me of the same thing, but I think it'll be fine," Ursula said. "But it's not just you who's had new gains — come with me, let me show you my results!"

"What results?" Jace asked.

"You forgot — Ursoc's Power, the bear-troll concoction!" Ursula beckoned as she walked into the passage. "I used stonescale oil, tried several weeks of different ratios, spent nearly thirty gold… and finally completed a formula that achieved the expected effects. Tremendous power, terrifying endurance — I'd bet that after drinking this potion, you could tell Prince Arthas he could beat an Alterac snow monster with his bare hands and I'd believe it."

Jace frowned. What the hell kind of comparison was that?

But given that in quite a few Lordaeronians' eyes, Arthas still had the reputation of a vase that looked good but couldn't take a hit, he understood what Ursula was trying to express.

Following Ursula to the alchemy lab, many things were still packed in boxes and hadn't been taken out yet. Ursula opened a box she'd set on the table first and took out a vial of concoction from inside.

Just looking at the liquid in the vial, Jace felt this one was probably the real deal.

This vial of golden-yellow medicinal liquid looked like slightly diluted, faintly translucent gold water — pure and beautiful. Ursula's hand holding it trembled slightly, and the liquid inside swayed along with it, occasionally releasing specks of golden starlight.

Pure and beautiful were secondary. The most crucial thing was that it was almost indistinguishable from the corresponding Flask of the Titans icon in the game. "Did you succeed?" Jace asked. "How are the effects? Did you find someone to test it, or were you waiting for me to test it?"

Ursula could barely keep the proud curl from spreading across her lips. "Of course it succeeded! Did I have the patience to wait for you to test it?"

"Who could've imagined you'd bring back something this miraculous from Kalimdor — and who could've imagined something this miraculous was actually invented by bear trolls! Black Lotus, demon blood herb, and stonescale oil combined can actually produce such a miraculous effect. Sometimes I have to admit, Jace — although not exactly rigorous, when some potions are brewed, you can tell whether they'll work just by looking at them. The color, the transparency, the magical aura — a successful potion should look successful! If you try half a day and all you end up stewing is a pot of rotten meat soup, you don't even need to taste it to know it's a failure!"

"The brave Mr. Erland Dudemoor has already helped me test the potion, Jace. After drinking the entire vial, he could barely match Kilruk in a bare-handed fight without using magic — can you imagine? Do you think this world was arranged by the gods? Just like those Dalaran mages say — the ancient Titans came to this world and created everything — otherwise how could it possibly be so miraculous… I can't even put it into words anymore. Maybe those bear-troll formulas were told to them by the gods. Kalimdor is just that mysterious and charming a place!"

Saying all this in one breath in her excitement, Ursula handed the vial of potion to Jace and said: "I plan to name it the Potion of Ursoc's Power, to honor the bear trolls' achievement. What do you think?"

Jace was speechless. He nodded and took the potion, thinking — if Grede drank this and invoked his Avatar, could he hold his own against an adult black dragon?

"This one's free," Ursula said happily. "Watching the idiotic expression on Jace Sesso's face is absolutely worth the price of twenty gold."

Jace couldn't help but laugh. "That's not all, Ursula." Ursula's proud expression immediately dropped.

"What else do you want?"

Jace said: "Fire Protection Potions. A large number of Fire Protection Potions — and they need to be the best kind, potent ones, brewed as soon as possible. Don't worry, I'm paying."

"If you're paying, then there's no problem," Ursula said. "I still have some elemental fire canisters left over from Galen making robes — should be enough to make about ten bottles."

"I also need an empty vial," Jace said, gesturing a bottle about five to six hundred milliliters in size. "Non-transparent, made of the sturdiest material possible."

Ursula said: "I do have this, but what's it for?"

"Human blood," Jace said. "My own blood."

Ursula's eyes widened, as if something had jammed her throat — for a moment she couldn't say a word.
