## Chapter 994: The Unshakable "Curse"

The sky darkened through the afternoon, and by the time evening approached Jace was hurrying toward Stormwind Keep.

Mathias had confirmed that Katrana wasn't having private meetings with the king, which at least meant he didn't have to worry about running into her on the way.

Still, he hadn't figured out what he'd say to the king once he arrived. He couldn't just come by for a casual greeting — he'd never made a habit of dropping in on the king for no reason, and it wouldn't suit his style.

So, the whereabouts and clues regarding Daval Prestor? He hadn't investigated at all, hadn't even cared. Ask the king to practice swordsmanship? Seemed like slacking off when the investigation was going nowhere.

As Stormwind Keep drew closer and the time neared, he slowed his pace and worried about missing the window — then an excuse suddenly popped into his head.

That Lakeshire noble, Adrian Morgan, had asked him to put in a word with the king about supporting the Morgan family's effort to reclaim their ancestral lands in the Burning Steppes. Since he'd promised, he shouldn't forget. He'd use that as his reason.

Entering the palace and arriving at the Park, the royal guards who'd grown familiar with Jace all bowed their heads in greeting and no longer questioned his reasons for visiting. And so Jace made his way straight to the Park, where, just as Mathias had said, the king and queen were taking an arm-in-arm stroll.

Queen Tiffin wore a plain white cloak, her dark gold hair cascading down her back. Compared to how quickly Varian had matured and steadied over the years, she seemed to be much the same as always, unchanged.

Though she fell slightly short of the Princess Prestor's unforgettable beauty, she was still a rare sight in all of Stormwind.

At the moment, the queen's belly was only slightly rounded, but the king was already being considerate and cautious, holding her hand with care, worried for even the slightest mishap to her or the child she carried.

Varian looked, at that moment, as if he'd suddenly transformed from the brat who'd leaped onto the throne into a still-young but dutifully devoted father.

Just from looking at them, it was hard to imagine the two had developed the kind of rift the rumors described. But even standing at the garden's entrance, Jace could feel a distinct mood of anxiety pervading the entire Park.

The anxiety was so intense that Jace didn't think it could have come from just two or three people.

Besides the king and queen, there was a single guard standing in a distant corridor — Varian seemed reluctant to let too many people disturb their time together. But Jace felt that if he'd walked in here with his eyes closed, he would have thought the Park was full of angry, jealous onlookers, all weaving their negative emotions together to create this atmosphere of shadow.

At the same time, Queen Tiffin's steps were indeed somewhat weak, and her complexion wasn't looking well. Even though Jace wasn't a clergyman of the Cathedral, he could tell at a glance that she looked ill.

But surrounded by such severe negativity, how could anyone be in good health? Even on Earth, psychological suffering affected physical health — to say nothing of here, where the shadow stirred by emotions could literally torment a person.

But Jace couldn't sense any curse inflicted by an outsider on Queen Tiffin, nor any mental-influence magic.

Jace had zero confidence in his judgment regarding the arcane, but his judgment of shadow power was enough to impress even a great mage like Krasus — that ought to count for something.

Not only the queen — Varian was the same. Neither of them showed the slightest trace of dark magic influence. Forget the lighthouse folk who'd drunk dragon blood — they didn't even share any characteristics with the dragon cultists, or that Dark Iron dwarf controlled by the black dragon back in the Order of the Circle.

Then again, just looks, money, and charisma alone were enough to throw all of Stormwind into chaos. Why bother using dark magic and increasing the odds of exposure?

"Jace! Something the matter?"

Jace turned his head. "I'd hate to disturb Your Majesty and Her Highness..."

"It's quite all right." Queen Tiffin managed a faint smile in his direction. "Warian and I have the chance to walk here every day, and the king has been very concerned about Lord Daval Prestor's whereabouts. He's been waiting a long time for news from Master Sesso."

Hearing Daval Prestor's name, the king glanced at the queen a little guiltily. "It won't be much longer, Tiffanie."

"I know." The queen nodded coldly. "Since you two have official business to discuss, I won't intrude."

She walked with weak steps into the corridor, and Varian had no choice but to give the distant guard a look to attend to the queen back to her chambers.

"I'd better have tremendous news for you, Jace." The king turned back, his face looking as exhausted as if he'd just lost a battle. Jace pressed his lips together. "Actually, I came about the Morgan family..."

"Light, Jace, what are you — Morg—?!" Varian said in a low voice, his temper seeming to shoot straight to the top of his head.

But he pulled his emotions back almost instantly and said calmly, "Very well. The kingdom has a duty to extend a hand regarding the outrages of Shadowforge City, a duty... My father, my father's father, my father's father's father — they all said as much."

He fell silent for a moment, then asked, "What about Daval Prestor?"

"I'm afraid the problem no longer lies with the father, Your Majesty," Jace said.

Varian stared at Jace for a moment. "You've found a way to get to the bottom of this, haven't you? Mathias mentioned his suspicions to me — our thinking lines up."

"Indeed," Jace nodded. It seemed the king was fully aware.

"Damn it." Varian pinched the bridge of his nose. "That means a lot of my previous ideas have gone up in smoke — Alterac, Lordaeron, Stromgarde, the whole Alliance. I genuinely hoped that woman had no problems, that she really was exactly what she appeared to be..."

He swept his gaze across the Park, took a deep breath, and said with his hands on his hips, "Then do what you have to do, Jace. I'll do everything in my power to help. Losing a good chance to return to the stage is still better than bringing disaster down on the whole city."

"I'll make sure to resolve this outside Stormwind, Your Majesty," Jace said. "That's better than I expected." Varian nodded.

Jace considered for a moment, then said, "When the time comes, I may need to summon a..."

"No problem," Varian said. "A succubus, a Felguard, whatever helps. I'm not some ignorant kid, Jace. Since I chose you as my court mage advisor, I've already prepared myself mentally. So far, you haven't done anything to make me start doubting you."

"Ahem." Jace cleared his throat, hesitating.

Seeing his expression, Varian frowned and asked in a voice so low even Jace could barely hear it, "A Doomguard? Or were you planning to summon a green fireball like those Eredar? I've heard the soldiers on the Dark Portal front describe those..."

"...Fire giants."

Jace said nothing, just kept looking at the king.

Varian swallowed. "I hope you know what you're doing, Jace."

"I'll be careful, Your Majesty. I won't make that choice unless the situation truly becomes unmanageable," Jace said.

Varian nodded slowly. "As long as it's to resolve the difficulty, and no other tragedies occur, I'll find a way to smooth it over for you. I believe Master Andormas and Master Magino Dumas stand with us too. As long as the Wizard Sanctum is willing to help, it won't be a problem."

Jace relaxed slightly, then asked, "The queen..."

"You couldn't help but ask, could you?" Varian cut him off with a trace of anger, staring at him. "This is my own business, damn it. I'll handle it. You just take care of that Ranger Lord of Quel'Thalas of yours — make sure she doesn't think you're carrying on with that Prestor woman too."

"Sorry," Jace said.

At that, Varian seemed to realize he'd lost his temper again. He sighed, leaned against a nearby tree, and said quietly, "It's been twelve years since the Dark Portal, and the war ended five or six years ago. Why does the curse brought by the Orc Wars seem determined never to let this palace be, without end... We won that war."

"I should be the one apologizing. I shouldn't have said that about you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner — I'm probably just tired." The young king glanced over. "If there's nothing else, I'd like to be alone here for a while."

Jace nodded. "Then good night, Your Majesty."

"That's the most practical blessing I can get right now," Varian nodded. "Good night, Jace. May the Light guide you."

Outside the palace, the sky had already darkened.

A man in a dark cloak stood by the canal ahead. He lifted his hood and looked over — an unfamiliar face, a head of curly dark brown hair showing signs of impending baldness, probably not yet forty.

This guy clearly wasn't simple. Jace ran through the list of possible troubles he might have stirred up in his head, but couldn't think of a thing... when the man suddenly spoke. "The Grim Batol matter is arranged, Jace Sesso. I've come as agreed."

"Ah, damn it." Jace let out a long breath. "You scared the hell out of me, Master Krasus. Must you always make such a dramatic entrance?"

Krasus smiled. "Apologies for appearing before you in this unfamiliar form. She knows my mortal guise — I couldn't show up in Stormwind looking like that."

"Understood," Jace nodded. "Then I'll go to her tomorrow and arrange the meeting."
