## Chapter 996: By Mirror Lake

The next morning, Jace was already at Mirror Lake.

Looking across at that red-tiled house on the far shore, the place wasn't unfamiliar to him — he used to come here often to practice spells. He just hadn't expected it to be under SI:7's control as well.

Mathias and Vereesa had been waiting here for some time. It was raining now, and the forest looked somewhat hazy.

"Looks like even the weather is on our side." Mathias pushed the door open. "You think this rain can counter a black dragon's fire?"

Hard to say whether the downpour was on their side right now, Jace thought — because it meant drawing a summoning circle with his own blood outside was pretty much out of the question.

"Might make this house burn a little slower," Vereesa said.

Mathias glanced up at the ceiling beam with a complicated expression, then handed the key to Jace. "Then I'll take my leave, Sesso. Emotionally, I'd very much like to stay and witness everything, but reason tells me..."

He glanced toward Vereesa in the distance with a smile. "I wish you every success, Lady Windrunner." Vereesa nodded. "Thank you for providing the location, Master Shaw."

"It's nothing."

Mathias bowed slightly and headed down the stairs.

After he closed the door, Vereesa let out a breath. "Can we win this?"

"Without Krasus, I'd have serious doubts." Jace slipped Malorak's spell-breaking ring onto his finger. "But now that we've got the Kirin Tor's most powerful archmage helping us, I don't see how she could possibly beat us."

"I know." Vereesa looked out the window. "I don't know why, but I shouldn't be this nervous. Even at Grim Batol, I never felt this uneasy."

Last time they'd met, Jace had sensed that Vereesa's emotions were off, but he hadn't thought much of it then. This time, connecting it to the self-doubt that had suddenly welled up inside him when he met Katrana Prestor yesterday, he couldn't help but be more vigilant.

"Since when have you felt this way?"

"How should I know." Vereesa lowered her head. "Maybe when Grede told me we might be fighting a real ancient black dragon? Or maybe... after meeting Lady Katrana Prestor at the banquet?"

Jace recalled Varian's words from yesterday and hesitated for a moment. "You wouldn't be because of the rumors between her and me..."

"Nonsense." Vereesa cut him off at once, staring at him. "How could I possibly be jealous of a human?" As she said it, she seemed to remember something and frowned almost imperceptibly.

"Fine, she's a dragon — but that changes nothing."

The half-open door pushed open and Sola entered first. "Good morning."

Grede and Krasus followed behind her. The group exchanged greetings and embraces, like just another casual get-together of friends rather than the prelude to facing some terrifying foe.

Jace set Ursula's iron box on the table. "Everyone take two Fire Protection Potions from inside. I imagine, Master Krasus, for you the time it takes to drink a potion would be better spent casting a few more powerful Fire Protection Wards, so..."

"Indeed, I don't need these," Krasus said.

"Then I'll take yours for safety's sake." Grede gave the red dragon mage a sidelong look. "I'd say your dragon hide is probably a lot tougher than a Fire Protection Potion, isn't it?"

"I can't fight Onyxia in my true form," Krasus said. "I'm actually more accustomed to my mortal guise. If I transformed back into a dragon to fight an ancient black dragon like that, it would be abandoning my advantage to challenge hers on her own terms."

"No wonder Dalaran never saw through your identity for so long," Grede nodded. "You might be more human than humans at this point."

Sola looked at Grede as if he'd committed some tremendous offense. But Krasus simply lowered his gaze. "It's hard to say, really."

Jace said, "In a bit you'll all need to hide in the forest. Vereesa will use Queen Ysera's earring to conceal your presence. As long as you're within the dense woods, that earring's power will take effect, and it's extremely reliable."

Krasus and Sola nodded. Vereesa touched her ear and nodded in confirmation. "It's still raining today — what bad luck." Grede glanced out the window.

"Unfortunate indeed." Jace picked up the bottle containing his own blood. "I originally wanted to set up a summoning circle outside ahead of time, to call my minions over to help. Doesn't look like that's happening now. I'm wondering whether I could set it up indoors, in another room?"

"What's in this bottle? I know warlocks use blood to summon demons," Krasus said. "That's right — it's my own blood," Jace said.

"Best not to," Krasus warned. "Black dragons are masters of blood magic. They're extremely sensitive to ritual blood. I imagine that's why that forcibly promoted alchemist went to such great lengths to craft this ring, to hide the lethal magic on the stone tablets. Even using blood to set up a summoning spell in this forest is already very dangerous, let alone in this poorly ventilated little house. She'd easily realize there's a trap buried nearby, Jace."

Jace hadn't failed to consider this — it was just that Krasus's words felt like a death sentence for the idea, which left him a bit discouraged.

He exchanged a glance with Morophil standing in the corner. Morophil nodded in agreement as well. So he could only stuff the bottle back into his pack, shoving Sazeno aside.

"I've already warned Lord Grede Thunderfist to do everything possible not to touch his necklace before the fighting starts, because although Onyxia is arrogant and conceited, that doesn't mean she'll turn a blind eye to danger set right in front of her," Krasus said. "We must take her by surprise. Once she's prepared, whether to fight or flee will be her call."

Jace nodded. After all, in the game, even when hit by a spell by surprise, Onyxia had still found a chance to escape. This time, after confirming Katrana Prestor's identity, they had to use every possible means to crush her — couldn't give her room to think.

With that, he pulled the golden elixir from his pack and handed it to Grede. "Drink this before we make our move."

"What is this?" Grede asked.

Jace explained, "This is the improved version of the potion from the furbolgs. We call it Ursoc's Strength. Drinking it will give you the strength of a god, Grede. Maybe a bit of an exaggeration, but... you'll know when the time comes."

"The strength of a god?" Grede held the elixir and laughed. "With Kadros's warhammer, the life gem crafted by the black dragons, the Black Dragonfather's necklace, plus this potion that sounds terrifying just hearing about it, I think I could duel that dragon one-on-one—"

"Still, be as careful as possible," Krasus said.

"Hah, just kidding, Archmage." Grede looked back at Vereesa. "But I think there's no more debate over who gets to land the biggest blow, little elf!"

Vereesa glared at him unhappily, then said, "I only care about winning. I'm not interested in competing with you."

"You were so cocky a few days ago!" Grede muttered.

By now Jace was in no mood for jokes. He checked his pocket watch. "It's about time. I'm worried she might come early. Everyone to your positions."
