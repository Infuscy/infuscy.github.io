## Chapter 100: False Wisdom and True Wisdom

The next morning, after picking up six Mana Potions from the Slaughtered Lamb, Jace planned to practice the new spells he'd learned — Arcane Intellect and that Weakness magic he'd stolen from the naga — together with his imp Sazeno.

By Mirror Lake outside Stormwind, in a small clearing where nobody else was around, Jace found an open spot and had the imp stand across from him.

"What are you planning to do to me, master?" Sazeno asked, puffing up his chest.

Jace looked at the little imp wreathed in flames, held out his hand, and said: "Come on. Be my test subject."

The imp's grinning face changed instantly. "No, no, no… master — wait, I could, but… wouldn't it break your heart to feel me in pain? Could you at least tell me what spell you're trying to test on me?"

"You're afraid of pain?" Jace said. "I just want to try a Weakness spell I learned from that naga on you. After I got hit by it, I couldn't even lift my hands or feet — couldn't walk. But I memorized the incantation."

"Anuniji, Durzala, Kana."

Jace pointed at the imp using the exact same gesture he used to cast Fear. The imp yelped in fright — but nothing happened.

"'Kana' isn't demonic, master — that doesn't sound anything like demonic!" the imp said. "If I'm not mistaken, the shadow magic the naga use originates from Sha'thari of the Void realm. This is a Sha'thari curse! It sounds right, too!"

"Sha'thari?" Jace hesitated. He'd heard of this language — but in his past life.

Sha'thari was the language used by the ancient parasitic Old Gods and their dark kin to communicate with one another. In the game, there were only a handful of NPCs who knew it.

And in this world, Jace had never once heard the language spoken from a human mouth. He couldn't find a single relevant textbook in the library either (and of course he couldn't). How was he supposed to figure out its pronunciation?

"Since you're so familiar with it, do you know Sha'thari?" Jace asked.

"If I knew it, I'd be done for!" Sazeno jumped as he answered. "Those who master Sha'thari are either pure shadow beings, or the dark flesh spawned by the parasites, or… shadow faithful who wield powerful shadow energy! And I'm just a weak little wretch! Nobody would teach me Sha'thari, and I certainly don't have the power to master it!"

With no teacher at all, how was he supposed to correct his pronunciation?

Jace gazed out at the lake, recalling the naga's pronunciation while skimming stones across the water.

After who knows how many repetitions of "Anuniji, Durzala, Kana" with various pronunciations, pauses, and stress adjustments, suddenly one rendition felt… off, somehow.

Even the imp looked over. Was that shadow energy gathering?

Jace locked in that pronunciation. "Sazeno, get back over there!"

The imp hopped back to its original spot, stood at attention, and excitedly shouted, "I'm ready!"

Only then remembering that it was standing there as a target for some kind of painful spell, its expression shifted from excitement to misery.

"Try not to say it too perfectly, master."

"How could I possibly pronounce standard Sha'thari?"

Jace cracked a joke, then — using the pronunciation that had seemed to resonate with shadow just now, paired with the gestures he remembered from the naga sea witch… well, one pair of her hands' gestures — pointed at the imp.

"Anuniji, Durzala, Kana."

A current of shadow flowed through his body, making his arm tingle and itch. He watched the imp's expression start to go wrong — it stopped hopping around, and its legs began to tremble.

"This is… this is…" Even the imp's mumbling had grown much weaker.

But Jace felt a thrill. The imp's sudden weakness gave him a rush of superiority, the sadistic pleasure of tormenting a weaker being.

After all, he already dominated this relationship. He was the master, and the imp… was a servant, a minion, an unconditionally obedient tool. Then reason surged back up, pulling him out of that "enjoyment," and the shadow energy that had brought the tingling in his arm dissipated rapidly.

The side effect of shadow magic — Jace was no longer surprised by it.

Still, this was far better than the helplessness he'd felt when he'd first touched shadow magic. Now he could tell which part of his emotions came from the dark power and which part came from himself.

"It worked… *cough*!" The imp could barely even stand now. "This is a shadow curse, master. This is Curse of Weakness."

"I know." Jace spoke as he pulled out the paper and pen he'd already prepared, transcribing the pronunciation of the incantation from his final attempt into Common letters.

He planned to teach this Curse of Weakness to the warlocks in the Slaughtered Lamb… Gajin and Galders definitely had other incantations they hadn't shown yet.

Since the warlocks had contact with the Dark Iron cultivators of shadow, there was no way they'd only heard of a single spell — Fear — from them.

The first time Jace had gone down to that basement and seen Erland and Sulenna, they'd hit him with Curse of Agony. Speaking of which, Jace still hadn't learned the Curse of Agony incantation.

The reason he'd never been interested in Curse of Agony was that he'd initially assumed Sulenna had heard the incantation from some random source.

If an incantation didn't have a standard source and someone to correct you during the learning process, it was bound to get more and more distorted the further you went — just like this Curse of Weakness. He didn't have the genuine Sha'thari original text, didn't know the proper pronunciation, and could only manage a crude imitation. He still remembered when the naga had hit him with the Weakness spell — he couldn't even stand, collapsed right to the ground, and the simple act of pulling a potion bottle from his bag had been unimaginably difficult.

And the imp was the physically weakest creature he'd ever seen. If he couldn't even make this little guy fall over, it was clear how much the incantation had been weakened in the journey from the naga to Jace.

Either the problem was with the incantation, or with the casting gestures — there might even be other conditions involved.

Jace suspected that as servants of the Old Gods, the naga might possess some special advantage in shadow casting that he didn't know about yet.

In any case, this secondhand Curse of Weakness could serve as his first step toward raising his standing in the basement circle.

Especially since one of the conditions Gajin had set for letting him join the group was that he be able to hold his own against the strapping Erland. As a spell that degraded physical capability, Gajin would definitely love the Curse of Weakness.

"Next, the second spell, Sazeno." Jace tucked the note away, then pulled out the *Felice — Fundamentals of Arcane Casting* Marin had given him, flipped it open, and found the chapter on Arcane Intellect.

He flipped a few pages further and discovered that there were so many variants of Arcane Intellect — nearly dozens of pages describing all kinds of Arcane Intellect, duration, lasting effects, even offensive variants that bordered on disrupting the mind, overwhelming the target with so much information at once that their consciousness collapsed.

The one Marin had taught him was the most conventional kind — an introductory version derived from the elven incantation and refined by Dalaran.

"There's a second spell?" the imp, still trembling from head to toe, shouted with effort. "Have mercy, master!"

Jace turned and pointed at Sazeno. "Misthis, Eupori, Hininge!"

A mass of arcane energy instantly enveloped the imp. The imp leaped up in fright — but before it could flee or react any further, the energy dissipated.

The same problem again. Jace had a hard time finding that feeling of converting free mana into an infusion of empowering energy.

"What spell was that, master?" the imp asked.

"Arcane Intellect," Jace said.

"Ah… Arcane Intellect, I know that incantation!" the imp said. "Your method is completely wrong."

"Right, you know Blood Pact," Jace said. "Tell me — how do you convert magical energy into an enhancement-effect spell?"

"I was born knowing it. How am I supposed to teach you?" the imp said helplessly. "But I do know Arcane Intellect shouldn't be that hard!" The imp was right — Arcane Intellect counted as an introductory spell, otherwise it wouldn't be in *Felice* at all.

Jace felt that pushing for Mage Armor immediately after learning Light had been too hasty. He'd always been trying to cut corners, always thinking he could learn faster by relying on his own understanding.

But unlike with shadow, he had no talent whatsoever for the arcane. So following the established method, step by step, was actually the better choice.

He opened *Felice* again and, following the book's explanation of the basic version of Arcane Intellect, compared his pronunciation and gestures point by point, then repeated it over and over searching for the feeling.

By now he was starting to feel tired.

Perhaps the earlier practice of Curse of Weakness had consumed some energy, and then conjuring that botched Arcane Intellect had wasted some more.

Should he drink a Mana Potion?

These potions hadn't come easily. If he wasn't truly exhausted yet, he'd best not waste them.

Jace stood up, did his best to forget all the experience he'd summarized while practicing Light, assumed a proper stance without mixing in any of his own wild ideas, and cast Arcane Intellect by the book.

The moment the casting motion completed, he suddenly heard a crisp chime of arcane energy by his ear, and a strange experience began. His field of vision seemed to widen, as if the angle were broader than before.

But he knew clearly that this was an illusion. It was only because his ability to discern images in his field of vision had grown stronger. His attention was no longer concentrated solely on the circle at the center of his vision — it had expanded outward. This was an improvement in observational ability.

The sound of wind between the leaves, the rippling of the lake water, the calls of birds… no, not quite.

That was the call of a black bunting.

Jace remembered it clearly. He'd casually flipped through the kingdom's geographical records in Marin's study and had taken note of the call characteristics of this common Elwynn bird.

The original text read: three consecutive song-like calls from high to low are the distinctive hallmark of the black bunting.

How could he possibly remember a detail like that?

Was this the power of Arcane Intellect? Could this magic even extract hidden information buried and overlooked in the back of his mind?

Feeling this memory and observational ability far beyond his normal state, Jace truly grasped what Marin had meant — that abusing Arcane Intellect would quickly lead to a split personality, gradually growing to hate the version of yourself that wanted to be plain and real.
