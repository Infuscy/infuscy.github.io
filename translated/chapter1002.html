Chapter 1002: Truth and Falsehood

Crossing the square, Jace arrived at the Cathedral of Light. He glanced up at the towering spire overhead — parts of it seemed to have been damaged by the fire rain, faint wisps of smoke still curling from the heights.

He climbed the front steps and entered the cathedral. Inside the white hall hung the faint stench of black dragonfire. Everywhere murmured the low chanting of Holy Light prayers, punctuated by sobs and the faint groans of the wounded.

As he passed the rows of pews that had been pressed into service as makeshift beds, Jace kept hearing the name "Onyxia" spoken by the injured and the priests alike. It seemed her true identity had already spread all over Stormwind overnight.

"Last night's fire rain — I thought the world was ending."

"If even his daughter is this terrifying, what must he himself look like?"

"He nearly destroyed Dalaran!"

"Did you see that shadow last night? She was ten times bigger than any whelp I've ever seen!"

"Deathwing… thank the Light he's dead!"

Yeah. Thank the Light he's dead. At this moment, Jace couldn't agree more.

But from what the soldiers were saying, it sounded like Onyxia had actually revealed herself above Stormwind the previous night. After the Obsidian Realm collapsed, he and Grede had fallen from the sky — she'd probably seized the opportunity and escaped the same way. Not far off, Jace noticed a mage in a reddish-brown robe kneeling with his back to the room.

He pinched a flame of life from his hand and seared it across the corrosive shadow-flame burn on a soldier's abdomen. The fire licked and licked, the soldier gritting his teeth through the pain for a while — and then the corrosive wound visibly began to fade.

"Master Krasus," Jace said quietly.

"Jace?" The man turned around. Jace could see that Krasus's mortal guise had returned to its usual appearance, but his face was far paler than before.

Hearing Jace's name, the burned soldier and several nearby priests all looked over.

Krasus said a few words to the soldier, then stood and walked over. "Onyxia has been badly wounded, Jace. Unless she patches her wounds with Truegold as recklessly as Deathwing did, it'll take her many, many years to recover."

"And you, Master?" Jace asked.

"The Queen will help me, Jace. No need to worry." Krasus smiled. "The moment Onyxia realized we'd seen through her disguise, she tore apart the curse Deathwing had left on me — as if she'd already planned exactly how to deal with me. I'm sorry, Jace. The outcome should have been better than this."

Jace said, "When we were in Shadowforge City, an orc prisoner told me that Onyxia and Nefarian occasionally met in Blackrock Spire. She must have learned about your injury from Nefarian and prepared herself for the possibility that you'd intervene."

Krasus said, "Onyxia didn't flee back to Blackrock Spire. She headed west, out over the sea. I imagine she understands what kind of creature Nefarian is far better than we do."

Jace said, "Last time I traveled to Kalimdor, I encountered some black dragons and dragonkin who claimed they were protecting the Dragonqueen's brood. Onyxia might have headed in that direction."

"Let's hope so." Krasus nodded. "If that she-dragon still remembers to care for her own children, at least it means she hasn't gone as mad as her father and brother."

"I'd like to see the King, Master," Jace said. "Where is he?"

"He was just inside, discussing relief efforts with the bishop." Krasus said. "Many of the soldiers weren't just burned — they were struck by the black dragons' shadow magic as well."

"I see," Jace said.

After thanking Krasus, Jace continued deeper into the cathedral.

Several paladins of the Silver Hand were here helping out as well. Then someone inside seemed to recognize him. "Jace?"

Jace looked over. A young man about his own age, early twenties, stood up. "You've changed a lot… don't you recognize me?"

Looking at the man's build, his solid muscles, and that kind, friendly smile — even with the stubble on his face, Jace recognized him right away.

"Morgan Ladimore?"

"Ha." Morgan walked over with open arms, then seemed to realize Jace wasn't in great shape and thought better of a hug. He looked Jace over. "Were you hurt last night?"

"Uh, bad luck," Jace said. "But what are you doing in Stormwind?"

"There's hardly anyone left in Raven Hill," Morgan said. "That demon business in Moonbrook a few months ago… it scared off all the remaining residents for good. Those demons only had to cross a river and walk a short distance to reach the great cemetery. They all left, and there was no one left for me to protect. After that, I brought them back to Glen, but things weren't much better there either. People were causing trouble, and I heard some strange strangers had come to town. I couldn't rest easy with them there either. So I figured I might as well move straight to Stormwind."

Strange strangers — wouldn't happen to be that Dagger Society crowd, would it? Jace looked him over but didn't find any Silver Hand insignia. "Did you join the Silver Hand?"

"Not yet." Morgan smiled. "The Church was quite willing to take me on as a priest because of my experience fighting the undead in Raven Hill. At least it's let me and my family settle down in Stormwind. I do want to join the Silver Hand, but I'm still far from proving myself."

Hearing that Morgan Ladimore wanted to join the Silver Hand, Jace found it hard to feel happy for him.

After all, in another timeline, it was because he'd gone north to join the Silver Hand and fight the Scourge that he'd failed to protect his wife and daughter at the most critical moment. They'd tragically died at the hands of the undead in Raven Hill, and he himself had returned as a madman and a monster.

But now that he'd moved into Stormwind, maybe things would turn out differently.

"I'm genuinely happy for you moving to Stormwind, Morgan," Jace said. "I hope you, Liss, and Sarah are happy here." Morgan said in surprise, "You even remember their names, Jace. Thank you… By the way, what brings you here?"

"Ah," Jace said. "I'm looking for His Majesty."

"The King?" Morgan's expression changed. He turned and pointed down a dimly lit corridor. "I did just see the King. He's resting in a side chamber in there, but…"

Seeing his expression, Jace remembered that the last time he'd met Morgan, he hadn't yet captured Teron Gorefiend, who had escaped in spirit form. Morgan probably had no idea about all the mess that had happened since. In the information-starved backwater of Raven Hill, he likely hadn't heard much about Jace either.

"I work for the King now, Morgan," Jace explained briefly. "Let's grab a few drinks at the Pig and Whistle sometime and I'll explain everything. How about that?"

Morgan had still looked a little hesitant, but hearing "the Pig and Whistle" he smiled and nodded. Jace thanked him and walked toward the door to the room he'd just pointed out.

A plainclothes guard stood at the door. When he saw Jace, his eyes widened in surprise, then he said, "Master Sesso. I'm sorry about last night."

"It's all right, sir," Jace said. "How is His Majesty?"

"The King didn't sleep all night, but I don't think he suffered any injuries," the guard said. "But…" Jace knew what he was going to say. The Queen.

"I know. Can I see the King?"

"Of course, of course." The guard said. "The King said that if you woke up, I was to inform him immediately. And, Master, thank you…"

"…You could have resolved things with us in a much easier way."

"Trust me, putting you to sleep was still the easier option," Jace said, half-joking.

The guard nodded with some embarrassment, knocked, opened the door, and said something softly to whoever was inside, then stepped aside. Jace entered and saw Varian Wrynn sitting in a chair, his hands pressed to his forehead.

When Varian saw Jace come in, he drew a heavy breath and looked up. Even in the faint light filtering through the heavy curtains, it was clear he'd aged a decade overnight.

His eyes were no longer red, and the look of desperate resolve he'd worn the night before — the look of a man ready to fight Jace to the death — was completely gone.

He forced a smile on the surface, but to Jace, the pain in his heart was plain enough.

Varian looked over and said, "In the face of that black dragon's magic, I was as fragile as a hare, wasn't I?"

"That was Deathwing's daughter," Jace said. "In front of her, there aren't many people in this world who are much tougher than a hare, Your Majesty."

"Don't comfort me, Jace." Varian shook his head. "But at least now I know just how terrible Deathwing and his children truly are, and I understand deeply why you tried so hard to warn me against the black dragons' infiltration. I almost turned this kingdom into another Grim Batol. I've thrown away all the preparations you made, Jace, and all the work of Mathias Shaw. I'm a complete and utter fool."

Jace sighed softly and said, "Right now, the important thing — have there been any leads on the Queen?"

"No." Varian was silent for a moment before he spoke. "But I can accept the worst outcome, Jace."

"You really think I'm counting on her father still being alive, like Katrana Prestor? A person replaced by a dragon like that won't meet a good end. I just hope… I just hope that if it really came to that, she didn't suffer before she was freed. No matter what, I'll make those black dragons remember all of this, sooner or later…"

"Your Majesty…"

"Believe me, I wasn't completely oblivious to the signs, Jace." The King looked up, clutching his chest, and said quietly, "I was deceived, it's true, but I'm not a piece of scrap iron with no feelings or awareness at all. After all, I lived side by side with Tiffin. There are very few people in this world who know her better than I do. But she was pregnant, and those rumors were floating all over the outside — how was I supposed to raise suspicions? She'd either be evasive, or feign illness to avoid people, or deliberately use cruel words to wound my goodwill — she was practically molding me around her finger!"

Hearing that, Jace suddenly felt something was off. Lived side by side?

"Are you sure you've been living side by side with the Queen ever since she became pregnant?"

"The thought of it makes me sick, Jace." Varian took another deep breath, then said, "Thank goodness she kept finding all sorts of reasons not to let me touch her. Otherwise I'd rather take this knife and slit my throat inch by inch than keep those memories."

"I understand…" Jace said. "But I still want to ask — are you certain she's stayed in the palace the whole time?" Seizing on the implication in Jace's words, the King looked up again, his emotions settling somewhat.

"Well, I can be certain. She was pregnant — could I let her leave? Especially with those demon rumors floating around everywhere, and with you and Mathias constantly worried about dragonkin in the city."

"But as far as I know, Onyxia traveled to and from Blackrock Spire from time to time," Jace said.

Varian thought for a moment. "She could teleport by magic, like she did last night, or sneak out under a magical disguise…"

"Use teleportation to burn down a house or an entire garden, or sneak out and leave you alone in the palace for several nights in a row?" Jace stepped forward, coming right up to him. "I know many dragons, including the green lady who helped me get into the palace that night. She's a green dragon, but with magic she can fly even faster than her peers. But even so, flying from here to Blackrock Mountain would take her at least a day and a night, maybe two days. Considering Onyxia is an immensely powerful dragon, she'd certainly fly faster, but it would still be nearly impossible for her to make a round trip in a single night without you noticing. And when she returned to Blackrock Spire, she'd have to handle some affairs… that would take even more time."

"You mean — when she left, she made a false Queen as a double?" Varian asked.

"Very likely. After all, she didn't use any magic in the palace — no enchantment, no curse, nothing else, at least as far as I could tell," Jace said. "I'd even go so far as to speculate that that one was the real one. She may have just influenced her somehow."

"I see," Varian said. "Every so often, she'd have a day or two where she said she wasn't feeling well and didn't want to see anyone — she didn't even want to see me, Jace. That's exactly how it was!"

"Everything happened so fast last night. She shouldn't have had time. She shouldn't have had time." After muttering to himself for a while, the King practically leaped from his chair, his spirits suddenly multiplied several times over, and bellowed toward the door: "Bring me Mathias Shaw! Now!"
