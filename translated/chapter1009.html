Chapter 1009: Back to Ironforge

Winter Veil was only a few days away, which meant Ruige's deadline was fast approaching.

Jace couldn't delay any longer. Although Rokktus Darkpact knew that the elemental-language dictionary was in his possession, and also knew about Ruige's situation, he himself didn't have the ability to leave Shadowforge City. In such a short time, Jace couldn't count on any member of the Thorium Brotherhood to have the skill to break through the orc blockade into Shadowforge City, communicate with him about this matter, and then run back to Ironforge to explain things to Ruige's superiors.

So, taking advantage of the time Krasus was researching the stone tablet incantations, and with no urgent matters to attend to in Stormwind, Jace had to hurry onto the tram to Ironforge.

After all, the tram ride also took about two days. If Jace remembered correctly, what Ruige had said at the time was "before Winter Veil arrives" — he didn't know whether arriving a day early counted as early enough.

Early that morning, Jace hurried to the cathedral. King Varian was still there helping the clerics look after the wounded.

You could tell that the King felt an immense sense of responsibility for Onyxia's destruction of Stormwind and the injury and death of so many people. So during this time, he had never returned to the palace, instead doing his best to stay at the cathedral to comfort the citizens' emotions and personally participate in the relief efforts.

When Jace saw Varian, he was helping the clerics move chairs. The King was wearing a simple blue outer garment, mixed in among a large group of soldiers and clerics. Aside from being taller and having a head of thick, brown-black hair, there was absolutely no way to tell he had any connection to a king or a noble.

"Your Majesty." Jace called out. Varian straightened up and looked over, leaning on his waist and panting. "Damn it, Jace. It feels like it's been a long time since I've done hard labor. Sword training… is simply not the same as manual labor — it gets you absolutely no exercise. My lower back is already starting to ache."

"Has there been any progress on the Queen?" Jace asked.

Varian pondered for a moment. "Quite a lot of progress. I have to say, although Shaw and his people were stretched thin dealing with the black dragons and demons, when it comes to dealing with their own kind, they really do have their methods…"

He glanced at a group of priests not far away who were discussing purifying the corruption of black dragon magic. "I'll tell you the details when we find a more private opportunity. What did you come to me for this time?"

"I'm planning a trip to Ironforge," Jace said.

"Ah, Ironforge." Varian nodded. "Perfect timing, Jace. If you're going to Ironforge, you could deliver a letter for me to King Magni Bronzebeard."

He said to a guard beside him: "James, go to my room over there… and fetch that letter for me."

The guard nodded and left. Varian continued: "These days all of Stormwind's gryphon riders are patrolling the surrounding areas, guarding against possible black dragon incursions. And the journey to Ironforge might require passing through Blackrock Mountain. I really don't feel comfortable risking our already exceedingly scarce elite gryphon riders with the danger of being intercepted in the air by black dragons. At the same time, I also want to send a sufficiently important envoy to Ironforge to underscore the importance of this matter and my respect for King Magni. Just this morning I was still considering this, and then you showed up on your own initiative. I was also worried you hadn't fully recovered. As my court arcane advisor and also an expert in dark magic, you going to Ironforge to warn about the black dragons… is simply too perfect."

"Is this about Blackrock Spire?" Jace asked.

"Of course," Varian said. "Not just Blackrock Spire — the entire Alliance. I think the chaos Onyxia created in Stormwind… is enough to make the entire Alliance tighten up again."

He lowered his voice. "Actually, Jace, at this moment I'm even a little grateful that the black dragon princess Onyxia survived. If she had died like her father, the trouble would have been permanently resolved, and I'm afraid the other kings, mage-lords, and admirals would have forgotten the threat of the black dragons in an instant. You can't expect those nobles thousands of miles away to have even the slightest interest in the dangers of Blackrock Mountain."

Jace smiled slightly and said nothing. At that moment, the guard brought over the letter, already sealed with the Wrynn lion seal, and handed it to him. Jace glanced at the letter and simply said, "I'll convey your meaning to King Magni, Your Majesty."

"Then don't convey the meaning of that last paragraph," Varian reminded him. "Of course," Jace nodded.

"How is Lady Katrana Prestor doing?" the King asked again.

"She seems to have been in a coma and hasn't woken up," Jace said. "Of course, that was also news from two days ago."

Varian said, "If she wakes up, Mathias should come to inform me. All right, Jace, let's leave it at that."

"Then I'll take my leave, Your Majesty."

After leaving the cathedral, Jace headed straight for the Dwarf District.

The tram from Stormwind to Ironforge hadn't improved in the slightest over the better part of a year, and the journey along the way was still miserable.

Although Jace had the experience from last time and had already mentally prepared himself this time, when he arrived at the station he still felt he never wanted to ride this thing again in his life. He even regretted not going to find Sola to carry him there.

Of course, Sola needed to rest frequently. After all, she still had to carry him to Blackrock Mountain next, and then carry everyone to the Pool of Tears at the far eastern end of the marsh. Which of those two trips wasn't more dangerous than going to Ironforge by an incalculable margin? So for problems he could solve by toughing it out himself, he shouldn't bother her.

By the time he arrived at Ironforge, it was already the evening of the second day. Even with the Dragonqueen's blessing, Jace still felt his legs had gone weak from the violent jolting, and he couldn't walk steadily.

He wasn't the only one. A group of gnomish technicians who'd been traveling with him — clearly regulars on the tram between Ironforge and Stormwind — were also each as dejected as defeated soldiers fresh off a battlefield when they arrived.

In this state, there was no way he could go see the King of Ironforge. Jace planned to first go to that hunters' guild he'd visited before and see if they'd returned from Kalimdor yet.

So much time had passed. He'd already made a round trip to Kalimdor himself, even traveling from Gadgetzan all the way to Winterspring, and there'd been a plane crash along the way. Those experienced dwarf hunters, with clear goals and a precise location, should have produced some results by all rights.

After finding an inn and eating some dwarven cheese to fill his stomach, Jace made his way through the stair channels, squeezing up to the seventh level among a crowd of dwarves going up and down the stairs.

After visiting Shadowforge City once and returning to Ironforge, he could clearly sense a stark contrast.

Before coming to Ironforge for the first time, Jace had held quite a few typical stereotypes about dwarves. Although Grede claimed to be a very unusual dwarf, the vast majority of things he did only reinforced those stereotypes even further. And Ironforge was practically the culmination of those stereotypes — the Bronzebeard dwarves were cheerful, frank, and honest… Even Ruige had been infected to the point where, aside from having a bad temper and being a bit petty, he was practically a Bronzebeard in black skin.

But in Shadowforge City, aside from catching glimpses of some common ground in the Ironforge tavern, the streets and corridors were full of suspicious glances, venomous stares, and even undisguised verbal abuse to one's face.

Only with this contrast could one more deeply feel the warmth and friendliness of the dwarves of Ironforge.

After arriving at the seventh level, Ironforge's intricate network of streets, corridor openings, and the carvings and statues everywhere made him dizzy for a moment. Last time he'd been here, that enthusiastic golden-bearded dwarf had acted as a guide helping him find his way back and forth. A few months later, those memories from back then were no longer quite sufficient.

He had no choice but to find an Ironforge guard standing beside a brazier. This time, however, his Dwarvish was no match for what it had been last time he'd come.

"Good evening, sir." He approached the soldier and said in Dwarvish: "I'm looking for a hunters' guild. Their emblem is a deer with an arrow through its chest…"

"Ah! Hajik's…" The dwarf soldier tilted his helmet back and glanced into the distance, then pointed at an intersection. "Go in from here, count to the second corridor entrance and go in, then after you come out, turn right, keep going, keep going, and you'll find their hunters' guild. Come to think of it, human, their guild has become quite famous these days!"
