Chapter 101: Full Circle

In the afternoon, Jace went down to the docks to ask if anyone was buying naga scales. He managed to offload a handful of the smaller, broken ones to a couple of gnomes working for a dwarf blacksmith, and came away with seven silver.

He'd asked around at several places — those two gnomes had offered the fairest price.

Walking home with the silver in his pocket, he passed a general goods store and figured he ought to ask about the naga pearl string. And that broken pocket watch he'd picked up in Westfall might be worth getting repaired and putting to use.

"Daily Goods, for All Your Needs."

The portly shopkeeper was reading a book and wearing spectacles. He delivered the greeting in a perfectly flat tone the moment Jace walked in.

He glanced up at his customer. "Something I can help you with?"

"Just asking." Jace unlooped the pearl string from his wrist and set it on the counter. "How much could this fetch?"

"We don't buy jewelry, sir." The shopkeeper adjusted his glasses, but took the string anyway.

"Colored pearls — pretty things you can only get from the deep sea. It's just that this string is small and a bit cloudy, like something contaminated it. Where did you come by this? The chain itself… doesn't look like something made in the Kingdom of Stormwind."

The shopkeeper turned the string over a few times, glancing at Jace every so often, before finally speaking. "Might fetch you forty silver, sir, but you'd need to ask at a jewelry store for that. Turn left, find the canal, follow it straight down — you'll see a jewelry store."

Forty silver.

Jace was tempted. Selling it would cover the cost of paper and ink… He was facing the orcish exam, and the more demonic text Sazeno could translate from that bilingual book, the faster he could figure out what the corresponding orcish runes meant. That actually mattered to him.

A while back, he wouldn't have hesitated at all.

But this string was a trophy — especially when he thought about that night of desperate fighting, and the string of nightmares that followed. Forty silver couldn't begin to prove what he'd been through.

"Then, sir, is there any chance this watch could be fixed?" Jace set the watch on the counter.

The shopkeeper picked it up and examined it. "I couldn't say. You'd need to ask at the clock shop on the street outside Cathedral Square."

Jace thanked him and took the watch back. "Come again," the shopkeeper said.

He couldn't shake the feeling that something was off about this shopkeeper. He hadn't bought a single thing, hadn't paid for any consultation — why was the guy being so friendly?

Still, he didn't dwell on it. He followed the directions toward Cathedral Square, looking for the clock shop.

As he walked, he kept feeling like someone was following him. He turned around a few times, but it was just ordinary passersby and guards — nothing suspicious.

Was this what happened when you used too much shadow magic? You slowly turned paranoid?

Just as Jace turned into a small alley, reflecting on whether he'd been abusing shadow magic too much lately, he suddenly heard rapid footsteps behind him.

Jace wasn't carrying any weapons. He whipped around and shouted, "Who's there?!"

A soldier in chainmail and a helmet slammed into him, driving him to the ground. Jace wasn't about to just take it — he thrashed furiously and actually managed to shove the armored soldier aside, roaring and bolting for the alley's exit, but before he'd made it a few steps someone grabbed his satchel.

That satchel… he absolutely could not lose it.

Jace swung back and punched the soldier in the face — except his fist connected squarely with the face guard, and the pain shot through his knuckles so hard his eyes watered.

He looked toward the end of the alley. A guard with a long staff was already blocking his way.

"What the hell is going on?!"

"Come quietly, thief!"

"Who's a thief?!"

"We received a report…"

Listening to the soldiers, Jace suddenly understood why the shopkeeper had been so damn friendly. The man had pegged him for a thief trying to fence stolen goods.

And the thing was — what kind of idiot thief walks right into a shop on a main street to fence stuff? Why not hit up the local thieves' guild in the sewers first?

"I'm innocent! All this stuff is mine!"

"Right, of course it's yours, of course you're innocent. Come explain it to the magistrate!"

Jace knew struggling was pointless now. These soldiers had already decided he was a thief.

"I really am—"

"Shut up!"

A fist cracked across his jaw, and Jace blacked out.

Someone slapped his face. He panicked, eyes flying open, and shouted, "…I'm not a thief! You're arresting me on one person's word?!"

He felt like he hadn't finished his sentence, so he just blurted the whole thing out — but only after he'd said it did it dawn on him that he'd been unconscious for a while now, and this wasn't the alley anymore.

Right after he spoke, Jace looked around. It was a cell-like place, pitch black all around. The Stormwind Stockades?

He was tied to a wooden chair with ropes. So he'd ended up here after all.

"He's awake. Bring the seller in to identify him."

Jace followed the voice and saw a fat man walk in.

"Is it him for sure?"

A bearded man in a blue soldier's shirt looked toward the fat man who'd just entered. Jace recognized him — it was the shopkeeper!

"That's him!" the shopkeeper pointed at Jace. "He's the one who tried to fence goods at my store."

"Fence?" Jace stared at him. "Did you see me steal? Who did I steal from? Who filed the report? Who lost something?"

"Look at that attitude — absolutely vicious," the shopkeeper snarled. "Magistrate Morses, just look at this guy. Skinny and dark, dressed in rags. How could he possibly own something that valuable? That colored pearl string is worth a pretty sum! Not to mention a broken pocket watch. He might not even be a thief — he's a bandit! Beat someone up and robbed them of everything they had!"

"Rags? You should write novels," Jace shouted. "Or is that what you did yourself, so you know the playbook?!"

Morses didn't respond to the shopkeeper right away. He was just turning a ring over in his fingers. Jace noticed it then — that ring was his apprentice sigil!

"That ring is mine!" Jace shouted. "Give it back…"

"Do you know what this is for?" Morses asked.

Jace was about to answer, but the shopkeeper cut in: "Didn't expect he stole a ring too? This guy was afraid I'd suspect him, so he didn't show me everything. Clever little bastard. Good thing I'm one step ahead."

"Enough." Morses looked at Jace. "You tell me."
