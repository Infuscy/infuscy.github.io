Chapter 1012: The Lord of Ironforge

After spending the night at Ruige's place, Jace headed up to the upper levels early the next morning, while Ironforge was still fairly quiet, intending to see King Magni.

Even now, the thought of meeting the king of Ironforge still made him feel a little uneasy.

Not because he'd killed Magni Bronzebeard god-knows-how-many times in the game, but because even after meeting so many kings, generals, and lords, he still wasn't quite used to the whole walking-into-a-palace-and-facing-a-bunch-of-nobles scene.

Although King Varian had deemed him the most suitable person to deliver the message to Ironforge, Jace still felt he was probably the most unreliable envoy in the whole palace.

Just like in the game, Magni's palace stood near the Great Forge — the hottest part of the entire city.

Jace climbed his way up to the fifth level, crossed through Ironforge's streets, and entered the passage leading to the Great Forge. With every step, it felt like the temperature climbed another notch.

Winter Veil was almost upon them, and it was Ironforge's most important holiday. So the higher he went from the lowest level, the more Winter Veil decorations he saw.

Streamers and snowflake patterns hung everywhere he looked. Dwarven carts carrying freshly cut Scots pines trundled through the streets, and quite a few shops had already put up their own decorated little trees by the door.

But staring at all these holiday motifs so closely tied to cold and snow while his body sweltered with heat created a strangely jarring disconnect.

Listening to the hammering echoing from the Great Forge ahead, Jace recalled what Ruige had said yesterday.

Although Grede had flatly rejected Roktus Darktreat's proposal, Jace had to admit that, like Vereesa, he felt the dwarf would eventually say yes.

Right now he was unwilling, of course. For one thing, the idea of going up against the Firelord really did sound too far-fetched. For another, Grede hadn't read the spoilers — of course he'd find it hard to believe some smith could replicate the Firelord's warhammer from the material plane. Even a Thorium Brotherhood smith would struggle to swallow that.

If he truly believed someone could forge the Sulfuron Hammer, Jace could practically picture the guy practically vibrating with temptation, gritting his teeth and itching to make a move — all for that legendary weapon, for the chance to redeem the Dark Iron dwarves' name, for the title of the greatest Mountain King in history.

But just as Ruige had said, forging a weapon to use against Ragnaros right under the Firelord's nose was genuinely dangerous. Maybe that was exactly why the Firelord kept such a tight grip on the Dark Anvil.

Lost in thought as he walked, Jace emerged from the passage, and the world opened up before him — a blazing scene and a wall of heat that snapped his train of thought right in half.

Golden molten iron poured from a massive cauldron high above, accompanied by blinding light, steam, and smoke. Bellows the size of houses pumped up and down with a thunderous roar that swallowed everything.

Dwarves bustled about the bridge at the very center of this magnificent hall. Even these most heat-tolerant of smiths couldn't help but shed their heavy clothes, working in vests and thin shirts. The clang of hammers rose and fell between the hiss of steam and the roar of airflow.

In the middle of the bridge sat Ironforge's great anvil — the "Bronzebeard Anvil," as Ruige called it.

Before the Dark Anvil came into being, Ironforge's great anvil had unquestionably been the pinnacle of the entire dwarven smithing world. But in the end, it was a work the dwarves had forged by their own strength alone, without the tempering power of Ragnaros.

In any case, the Bronzebeards and the Dark Irons each had their own anvil. But what about Aerie Peak?

Before leaving Khaz Modan, the Wildhammer dwarves had been passionate about smithing too — otherwise they wouldn't have forged the "Kadros Anvil" at the heart of Grim Batol.

With the fall of Grim Batol, the Kadros Anvil had gradually faded into obscurity.

Gazing down at the molten iron churning below the bridge like lava, Jace swallowed unconsciously and imagined, entirely against his will, what would happen if he accidentally fell in.

After Deathwing had destroyed Grim Batol, in those hellish flames, Jace had nearly tumbled into the magma several times under the threat of those stone elementals. If Grede hadn't been there to help…

Wait.

Jace froze. A ridiculous question surfaced in his mind. Could Deathwing's fury compare to the Firelord's descent?

Although the surface of the Batol mountain range and Grim Batol had mostly been restored by the red dragons over the past year, the depths of Grim Batol still ran with Deathwing's destructive lava, and the Kadros Anvil lay buried within it.

Thinking this, he found himself seized by an impulse to go in and see what had become of that anvil. More importantly, whether it could be used to forge the Sulfuron Hammer.

"Halt, human."

Jace felt something bump against his stomach. He looked down to find a guard in heavy marble-colored armor, bearing a shield emblazoned with a red hammer insignia, hand outstretched, standing right in front of him.

The guard warned him in fluent Common, "The King's Seat lies ahead. Go no further."

Jace looked up and observed the throne room. The gate, carved with dwarven runes, was larger than most doors in Ironforge, but it still didn't quite match the concept of a "palace."

Ironforge was an enormous underground city, yet its throne room was even simpler than Stormwind's. Its main gate faced the great anvil head-on, bearing the full brunt of the waves of heat blasting from the forge. It couldn't be very comfortable in there.

Jace guessed this design might share some kinship with the Iron Throne from A Song of Ice and Fire — that sword-forged seat that cut anyone who sat on it. Perhaps Ironforge's builders had intended this design to remind every future Mountain King that ruling a nation was never an easy matter.

He held out his hand to show the guard his ring and said, "Jace Sesso of Stormwind, Arcane Advisor to King Varian Wrynn. I come to Ironforge at His Majesty's command to request an audience with King Magni Bronzebeard."

"Ah, Archmage Sesso." The dwarf looked up and immediately thumped his fist against his chest. "The king has already received word of your arrival in Ironforge. He is inside now. Please, enter."

Jace muttered to himself — had Varian sent word ahead to Ironforge? No, that didn't make sense. How would King Magni already know he'd arrived?

The throne room was rather noisy at the moment. Several dwarven nobles were arguing about something. Led by the guard, Jace made his way to the center of the hall. He looked up toward the high platform and caught sight of the throne atop it.

The Bronzebeard throne was quite plain and solemn — a bronze-colored monolith standing in place. Beneath the throne, flowing molten iron surrounded the platform, the heat and smoke turning the whole stone dais into a towering oven.

The dwarven palace also seemed less solemn than Lordaeron's, or even Stormwind's. Soldiers, lords, and minor nobles stood about as casually as if they were at a gathering, while the figure on the throne watched in silence as the nobles below traded arguments back and forth.

This king wore heavy dark armor even when not facing battle, and the crown atop his head looked thick enough to have been carved from stone, pressing heavily down on his golden-red braids.

Looking at him, Jace's first impression was: this guy was probably the most miserable dwarf in all of Ironforge. Just then, the king noticed the human who had just entered the throne room and raised his head slightly to look over.

"The envoy from Stormwind has arrived, Your Majesty," the guard said with a bow.

Hearing the guard's words, the nobles who'd been snapping at each other all turned their heads.

"Jace Sesso?" Magni called out loudly. By now the throne room had fallen completely silent, and the dwarven nobles blocking the middle automatically cleared a path.

"Yes, Your Majesty." Jace raised the letter he'd already been holding and said in Dwarvish, "I come to Ironforge at King Varian's command, bearing his greetings and blessings. This is his personal letter."

The dwarven nobles around him began murmuring at the sound of Jace's name, though whether it was positive or negative was hard to tell.

Jace's Dwarvish was good enough for normal conversation, but picking anything useful out of this chaotic buzz was a real challenge.

"Your name arrived long before you did — I've heard a great deal about you." Magni smiled. "I didn't expect you to speak Dwarvish. Honestly, I'd much rather chat with you in private than have this kind of conversation on the occasion King Varian has provided."

Jace bowed slightly. A dwarven noble came over, took Jace's letter, and climbed the steps of the throne platform to hand it to Magni.

As Magni broke the seal, he said, "I heard word of your arrival in Ironforge last night, Jace Sesso. First, I must apologize for that unpleasantness that should never have happened. And I'm also grateful you showed restraint and spared those arrogant, dishonorable hunters. I hope you understand that this incident does not represent all of Ironforge."

So that was it.

Jace actually felt a little guilty — after all, he'd used dark magic right here in Ironforge. But judging by King Magni's reaction, he didn't seem too hung up on the dark magic business.

Magni Bronzebeard held the letter, and even beneath the crown and the thick beard, Jace could see his expression growing darker and darker.

"Onyxia?" he muttered, looking up. "Onyxia invaded Stormwind not long ago and used magic to disguise herself as Queen Tiffin."

The moment those words left his mouth, the whole throne room erupted into noise again. Magni raised a hand for silence.

Jace said, "Yes, Your Majesty. But the black dragon has fled, and Stormwind is safe for the time being, although the real queen's whereabouts are still unknown…"

Magni nodded and said, "In his letter, King Varian mentioned his hope that the Alliance would join forces once more to attack Blackrock Spire. The black dragons and orcs threaten not only Stormwind — naturally, they threaten Ironforge as well…"

"Then that would be doing those Dark Irons a huge favor." A very young dwarven woman's voice rang out from near the throne platform. "The Alliance army paid for it with countless lives and blood, and in the end it only benefited the Dark Irons. I don't think anyone wants to see that day come!"

Quite a few in the crowd agreed with her. Jace even heard someone say, "The princess speaks true," and he wondered if his Dwarvish was failing him.

Magni looked at the noblewoman and said, "King Varian's proposal cannot be decided on the spot, Moira. He also mentioned in his letter that this matter requires discussion among the Alliance's leaders."

"But what we all agree on is that the black dragons of Blackrock Spire have grown increasingly brazen. First the palace of Lordaeron was infiltrated, and now Stormwind's palace hasn't escaped either. So what's next? The black dragons can take on your appearance or mine — there's nothing special about us. The last nail of the Orc Wars must be pulled out. After all, the siege of Ironforge was less than ten years ago. Every lord and warrior here remembers it vividly."

This time Jace heard clearly. That woman was indeed Moira Thaurissan — no, Moira Bronzebeard. At this point she was still the princess of Ironforge, a young noble who bristled with hatred at the mere mention of Dark Iron dwarves.
