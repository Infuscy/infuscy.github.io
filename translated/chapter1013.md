## Chapter 1013: Dragonblood Jewelry

In another timeline, more than a decade later — after the Battle of Mount Hyjal — the Dark Irons and the Blackrock orcs would eventually reach a de facto ceasefire. Stonewatch would be seized by the orcs, and there were signs of Dark Iron dwarves moving south to harass the shores of Lake Everstill.

By then, Onyxia — disguised as Katrana Prestor — had already paralyzed the defenses of the entire Kingdom of Stormwind. Lakeshire, defended only by local militia and the remnants of Stonewatch's routed garrison, couldn't possibly handle the alternating harassment from both forces. So they had no choice but to send word to Ironforge asking for help, counting on the Bronzebeard dwarves to lend a hand.

As a member of the Alliance and the mortal enemy of the Dark Irons, King Magni was naturally willing to help. But Princess Moira Bronzebeard stepped forward, volunteering to lead soldiers to Lake Everstill to aid the humans against the Dark Irons. King Magni, not wanting to keep the princess locked by his side any longer and further inflame the conflict between father and daughter, granted her request.

But the moment he let his guard down, disaster struck. Princess Moira was captured alive by the Dark Irons during an operation and taken back to Shadowforge City. Emperor Dagran Thaurissan hoped that holding the princess hostage would deter King Magni from going to war against the Dark Iron clan.

But what followed surpassed everyone's expectations: Princess Moira and Dagran actually fell in love, and even had a child…

So watching the Bronzebeard princess shout about how she would destroy Shadowforge City and how the Dark Irons and she were irreconcilable enemies — Jace found it a bit jarring, honestly.

Of course, not everyone in the throne room supported her. By now the nobles were already arguing over the divide between the king and the princess's positions.

As the shouting wore on, Jace could mostly make out that although Ironforge had also been besieged during the Orc Wars, quite a few dwarven nobles weren't all that concerned about the threat of Blackrock Spire.

Grim Batol, which could threaten Khaz Modan directly, had already been destroyed. The stragglers of the Dragonmaw clan had no hope of touching the great walls of Dun Morogh, and the orcs of Blackrock Spire would have a hell of a time crossing the mountains to strike at Khaz Modan.

Though for the Dark Irons at this point it was much the same, the hatred and conflict between Bronzebeard and Dark Iron over the past century was very real.

Jace had assumed that nearly every dwarf civilian and soldier he'd meet in Ironforge would hate orcs to the bone, and that the nobles shouldn't be much different either. He hadn't expected the reality to diverge so far from his assumptions.

Of course, Jace couldn't say they were entirely wrong either, since Ironforge hadn't yet experienced the black dragon disaster firsthand — much like the people of Stormwind today, who had never suffered an invasion by Dark Iron dwarves. Aside from the displaced members of the Ladimore family, most people's impression of the Dark Irons was merely "cunning and treacherous," a far cry from the death-or-deadly hatred reserved for orcs.

Perhaps sensing that arguing further was pointless, or perhaps noticing Jace standing in the middle looking a bit awkward, King Magni bellowed at the red-faced nobles to stop and dismissed that morning's gathering.

The princess, still dissatisfied, went alone to wait near the throne platform for her father to come down. The dwarven king narrowed his eyes with a kindly expression, as if wanting to offer some comfort, but before long the father and daughter were arguing again.

Jace felt his mission was probably complete — he'd delivered the letter and conveyed the message. There shouldn't be anything else to do, so he stood at a distance waiting to take his leave of King Magni.

Not long after, Princess Moira shook her head and left the throne room quickly. King Magni glanced over this way, his eyes widening as if he'd only just noticed Jace.

"My apologies, Jace Sesso, for making you watch our little show."

He swaggered over, his iron boots striking the floor hard enough that everyone nearby could feel the tremor with every step.

Jace shook his head. Magni looked up at him and said in Common, "Go back and tell King Varian that Ironforge supports the Alliance's preparations against Blackrock Spire and the Blackrock orcs. The threat of the black dragons can no longer be ignored like this. Today I'll write to King Terenas II to propose a summit of Alliance leaders, and I hope to see King Varian there so we can discuss concrete plans together."

"Thank you, Your Majesty." Jace nodded, though he knew that once the letter reached the Alliance leadership, it might just die there.

Just when he thought this trip to Ironforge was pretty much over, Magni suddenly called out to him: "Jace Sesso, one moment…

"…I extend a sincere invitation for you to stay and attend the Winter Veil banquet in a week's time. To us, you are far more than just Stormwind's envoy — you are a living hero."

He glanced toward the door and said, "Dun Morogh has long been plagued by Frost Trolls. Just the day before yesterday, more than a dozen residents and soldiers were killed in Karanos. And you may have heard — Moira's mother died at the hands of the Frost Trolls. If you had arrived a little earlier, a hero like you coming through Ironforge's gates, she never would have treated you that way."

"I would truly love to attend Ironforge's Winter Veil banquet, Your Majesty," Jace said. "But Onyxia's whereabouts are still unknown, and there's no telling whether Blackrock Spire might make further moves. As King Varian's dark magic advisor, I must return to Stormwind as soon as possible and stand ready. Not to mention that Queen Tiffin's whereabouts are still unknown…"

King Magni nodded and said, "I understand. A real pity."

"If there's nothing else, Your Majesty, then I'll take my leave," Jace said.

"I do have one more question." King Magni said, "So then — you are not only a researcher of dark magic, but a wielder of it, a dark mage… Are these rumors true."

"They are true." Jace nodded. "I haven't hidden this from King Varian either. I apologize for the unease caused by my use of shadow magic last night. Those hunters had already threatened my life, and I was forced to resort to extreme measures."

"You need not apologize, Jace Sesso." The king waved a hand. "It's just that in Ironforge, people's tolerance for shadow magic may not be as high as in Stormwind. I've already suppressed the news of exactly what magic those dwarves were hit with. While you're in Ironforge, try not to be too forthcoming if anyone asks you about it, or it'll bring you unnecessary trouble."

"I understand, Your Majesty." Jace nodded.

"But I myself have a rather large question." King Magni frowned at Jace and said, "You've heard of the witch Modgud, and you know the dark calamity she brought upon Grim Batol and our entire race. Nearly every dwarf's impression of shadow mages, warlocks, and all practitioners who've turned to the dark side stems from her.

"Modgud was originally a great arcanist, a respected sorceress. But everything changed when she found that dark artifact. Like her, every person who came into contact with dark magic over the next two centuries — whether human, elf, or dwarf — without exception had their nature twisted by the shadow, became someone else entirely, and ultimately met a disastrous end.

"I know many warlocks and shadow mages, Jace Sesso, but very few who are like you… Not that there are none, but those people either exist only in legend, or they vanished long ago and no longer interact with the outside world… How exactly did you manage to resist the corruption that shadow magic brings?"

"Forgive my uncontrollable curiosity." Magni smiled. "First of all, I'm a dwarf — it's unavoidable. Second, I must say, finding a way to counter the shadow's corruption would mean the world to me. You've given me hope."

Jace's eyebrows trembled slightly. He couldn't help but ask, "Why would the king want to counter the shadow?"

"Not me." Magni said. "The Dark Iron clan."

"I lived through the War of the Three Hammers. There are very few dwarves left alive from my era. I personally witnessed the passion, the resolve, and the bravery of the Dark Irons before the schism. Two centuries of division haven't erased those memories. I've always felt that the corruption they suffered could be resisted, even reversed… I've always hoped that one day the Three Hammers could stand together again."

Jace nodded. He'd noticed that every dwarf who'd lived through the War of the Three Hammers harbored a fervent wish for the Three Hammers to reunite — whether it was this king, or the leader of the smiths.

"There's actually no trick to it, Your Majesty." He took a breath and tried to explain. "Probably, before I came into deep contact with the shadow, I already knew that engaging with it would eventually twist my nature, so I've been consciously resisting it all along. The negative emotions that shadow power easily breeds — suspicion, self-doubt, arrogance, contradiction, hatred, depression, fear, cowardice, and so on… I can only fight them consciously. The cost is that sometimes these traits are necessary — you need fear to stay alert against danger, cowardice to protect yourself, suspicion to avoid being deceived, hatred to remember who your enemies are… But none of that is more dangerous than falling completely to the shadow and being twisted into a dark monster."

"So you've been fighting this power all along, rather than having a once-and-for-all method to suppress the shadow's corruption." Magni said.

Jace nodded. "At the start it could be called a fight. By now, I think I've gradually grown accustomed to resisting it, Your Majesty."

King Magni's mood visibly fell. He knew this was no way to save an entire people. "Might I offer my own understanding of the Dark Iron clan's current situation, Your Majesty?" Jace asked. "Please." Magni said.

Jace said, "Even with skin turned pitch-black and eyes turned blood-red… many Dark Iron dwarves are still far more reasonable and friendly than their kin. I've met quite a few such Dark Irons — mainly Thorium Brotherhood members, plus some wanderers living abroad. And they all share one thing in common, Your Majesty: they've rejected their faith in the Firelord Ragnaros."

"You believe the Dark Iron dwarves' corruption stems from the Firelord's curse…" Magni said.

Jace said, "Perhaps once that Firelord is driven back to where he came from, Shadowforge City and the entire clan will be freed. Even if rifts with the Bronzebeard and Wildhammer remain, they'll be rifts between normal people — not the absolute hostility of an extreme cult."

"That sounds simple enough." Magni let out a sound somewhere between a laugh and a cough. "Enter Blackrock Mountain, kill Ragnaros, free the Dark Iron clan… Still, this is ultimately a matter within the dwarven people."

Jace had been on the verge of telling Magni Bronzebeard about Roktus, the Thorium Brotherhood, and the Sulfuron Hammer, but recalling how Roktus had refused to mention the Sulfuron Hammer at all when he couldn't yet trust him, he swallowed the words on his lips.

Once he'd confirmed that the Kadros Anvil deep within Grim Batol truly possessed enough strength and toughness to rival the Dark Anvil, suitable for forging the legendary Sulfuron Hammer — once he'd verified it with Roktus, Ruige, and the other Thorium Brotherhood members — then it wouldn't be too late to tell the dwarves of Ironforge.

And even if Grede still refused to take up the Sulfuron Hammer and shoulder the responsibility a Mountain King ought to carry, then letting this genuine Mountain King before him do it would be a tremendous thing for the dwarven people as a whole.

If the Sulfuron Hammer could truly help the Dark Iron rebels and the Bronzebeard dwarves shatter Ragnaros, that would be a good thing for Stormwind, the Kingdom of Stormwind, and the entire Alliance.

Thinking this, Jace simply nodded and bowed slightly.

"You've taken up too much of my time, Jace Sesso." King Magni said. "Still, there is one last thing."

"Yes, Your Majesty." Jace said.

The king said, "Ever since you and your companions rescued the Dragonqueen and crushed the Dragonmaw orcs in Grim Batol, I've intended to bestow upon you a gift that truly represents Ironforge, as thanks. It's just that in Ironforge, most of our treasures are made for dwarven use — whether armor, helms, weapons, cloaks, or even rings…"

He stroked his thick fingers and continued, "Most of them are hard to fit on a human hand, so I've been quite vexed."

Jace thought to himself that this made sense. If Magni gave him a Mountain King battle axe or some Ironforge war helm… aside from Grede, no one else could use it. But the problem was he'd already given Grede so many things — he really didn't want to lug another dwarven treasure back to Stormwind just for that guy to freeload off it again.

"But King Varian's letter mentioned something unusual." King Magni cupped his hands together into a sphere and said, "The black dragon Onyxia was wounded in Stormwind. A dragon of that size, once injured, sheds a great deal of blood — and dragon's blood can solidify into magical gems of extraordinary power, given the proper forging.

"If we have a sufficient quantity of Onyxia's blood, I can have Ironforge's craftsmen forge you a dragon blood talisman. Such a talisman can grant its wearer dragon-like perception and the power to resist flames. Our craftsmen are no strangers to creating such treasures, but they've never attempted it with the blood of a black dragon as powerful as Onyxia. Even a talisman made from ordinary black dragon's blood lets its wearer work an entire day in the Great Forge without feeling the heat. It's hard to imagine what kind of power Onyxia's blood could bring."

After hearing this, Jace was taken aback. He did know that in the game, after killing Onyxia, there was a quest reward called the Onyxia Blood Talisman, which increased the wearer's fire resistance, dodge chance, and defense rating. But he'd never expected it to be a dwarven creation.

Jace said, "There is indeed black dragon blood left behind in Stormwind, Your Majesty, but I don't know whether that blood is suitable for crafting a talisman…"

"No matter." Magni raised a hand and clapped Jace on the shoulder. "I'll send a jewelcrafter back with you, Jace Sesso. Once it's forged, we'll send it back to you. This is the gratitude of Ironforge and the Bronzebeard clan toward the dragonslayer — naturally, it won't trouble you at all."

"Truly, thank you, Your Majesty." Jace nodded, though it immediately dawned on him that this thing seemed like it was made for a warrior like Grede?

No. He already had Rasharel's Life Gem. If this talisman really could be made, he couldn't let Grede have this one too.
