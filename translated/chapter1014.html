Chapter 1014: Sustainable Development and Cooperation

The next morning, Jace stood at the Deeprun Tram entrance with his bags, waiting for the jewelcrafter King Magni Bronzebeard had sent for.

The appointed time had already passed, and Ironforge was lively by now. Even the sleepiest dwarves should have been up and about by this point, so he couldn't figure out why the jewelcrafter hadn't shown up yet.

Just as Jace was debating whether to head to the throne room and ask about the situation, three Ironforge soldiers approached from not too far off with a scholar-looking dwarf in tow.

The lead soldier stepped forward and said, "Reporting, Archmage Sesso. By order of His Majesty, we will escort you and Master Harnil Stone to Stormwind to retrieve the black dragon's blood! This is an extraordinary mission — it is our honor to work alongside you!"

"It is my honor as well." Jace smiled, then looked at the jewelcrafter who'd been escorted — practically sandwiched — over by the guards.

Even without reaching out to sense the emotions radiating from him, Jace could tell at a glance from the dwarf's face that he was reluctant, even uneasy.

"A pleasure to meet you, Master Harnil Stone." Jace extended his hand. The dwarf hesitated, nodded, and shook it, saying quietly, "Likewise, Archmage Sesso."

The lead soldier said, "Master Harnil probably didn't get enough sleep last night, but no matter — he can sleep on the Tram."

"I seriously doubt that, huh!" another soldier laughed.

All the way onto the Tram, the jewelcrafter still couldn't settle down, and he kept avoiding Jace's gaze. Jace was starting to suspect the fellow knew he was a warlock who dabbled in dark magic.

If that was the case, it was understandable. Demon summoning, shadow magic… for most ordinary folk who'd never had contact with such things, these were naturally terrifying elements. The fact that this jewelcrafter hadn't shown any obvious disgust and was merely worried already exceeded Jace's expectations.

The other soldiers, on the other way, cracked jokes nonchalantly the whole way. Clearly, for them, getting to ride the gnomish Tram — something most people didn't even have the privilege of doing yet — on official business was thrilling enough.

After the Tram set off, the three dwarven soldiers whooped and hollered the whole time, marveling at the power of gnomish engineering. The jewelcrafter sat in the farthest corner from Jace, hugging his box in silence, staring blankly ahead.

The journey went smoothly enough into the evening, when the Tram's noise suddenly started sounding off. If Jace hadn't ridden the Tram back and forth three times already, he probably wouldn't have noticed the change in sound.

Not only him — the jewelcrafter had also started constantly inspecting the track connections above the carriage. Jace looked up too and saw sparks beginning to fly from the point where the carriage suspended from the track above met the rail.

"We've got a bit of a problem, folks." A gnomish engineer shouted from the back, "I'll try to bring the car to the front repair station for maintenance first. Don't worry — you can continue on to Stormwind in a car on the other track. It'll only delay you a few hours!"

"How long until we get there?" Harnil Stone asked.

"About half an hour to the repair station, twenty minutes if we're lucky," the gnomish engineer said.

Harnil said, "But I don't think the wheels up there will last that long. When was the last time you overhauled and replaced them?"

"This car is one of the first batch of carriages to enter service, dwarf." The gnomish engineer put on his mining helmet as he said it. "No need to worry — I've already signaled the colleagues up ahead with the signal lamps!"

"No need to worry, then why are you putting on a helmet?" a dwarf soldier asked.

The gnome said unhappily, "This is simply a regulation of the Deeprun Tram project — I must follow it strictly. There's nothing to be nervous about, relax, gentlemen. The way you're carrying on is making me nervous!"

Even so, the dwarven soldiers immediately lost their earlier cheerful excitement and put on their helmets one after another.

Harnil and Jace, who had no helmets, exchanged a glance. For some reason, Jace felt like he trusted this jewelcrafter's judgment more than the gnome's.

This thought lingered in his mind for about ten seconds before the whole carriage suddenly shook. A few small boxes, apparently not secured all too well, clattered down the tracks and quickly disappeared from view behind them.

The passengers in the carriage were thrown about. One dwarf soldier nearly went over the edge along with them. Jace gripped the nearby railing tightly to keep from sliding off.

Looking up again, the wheels meeting the track above had begun vibrating furiously, emitting an intensely grating "rum-tum-tum-tum" noise, not to mention the sparks flying everywhere.

"Mother of… damn, how's this supposed to last twenty minutes?!"

The dwarven soldiers panicked and shouted. The gnomish engineer's yelling was already being drowned out by their screams and the chaotic noise. Jace stared at those wheels, thinking to himself — he'd escaped the black dragon's claws only to wreck down here?

"I knew there was a reason normal people aren't allowed on this thing!" Harnil roared, gripping a railing with all his might. "Holy Light preserve us!"

Right then, a sharp clang came from above, and the whole carriage suddenly dropped a good ways. Now sparks were flying from below too. For a moment, every sound anyone could produce was swallowed by the echoing metallic cacophony reverberating through the tunnel…

Jace stared hard at the wheels above and shouted at the top of his lungs, "Duk, Heduniluoyinmosi!"

The shadow power summoned by the Asphyxiating Spell lashed out like chains and claws, yanking the carriage — which had already dropped a considerable distance — back up and binding it tightly to the rail above.

But the speeding carriage tore the spell into a vast, thin mist of shadow that scattered downward. Jace had no choice but to concentrate and reinforce the incantation, to make up for the power rapidly dissipating from the strain…

The wheels above popped off with a bang and clattered away into the tracks behind them, vanishing from sight. Even the gnomish engineer screamed now — though with his mouth open and eyes wide, Jace couldn't make out a sound he was making.

Jace felt his energy being dragged away swiftly by the weight of this massive carriage. He couldn't tell how long they'd been hurtling forward amid the screaming of everyone in the carriage and the roaring of the carriage itself, but by the time they reached the repair station, he was already feeling dizzy.

A large crowd of gnomish engineers was already waiting there. The moment the car stopped, they swarmed over it… Jace no longer had the energy to pay attention to their repair work. That stretch of road had taken a lot out of him. All he could do was collapse into a chair nearby and catch his breath.

The still-shaken dwarves gathered around him to express their thanks repeatedly — even the jewelcrafter came over on his own.

"I'm sorry, Archmage Sesso. It was me… I was prejudiced against you and your people. One of my cousins died at the hands of a Dark Iron warlock. I'm sorry I transferred the impression of those evil fiends onto you."

"It's all right, I understand." Jace smiled. "I probably know better than you do exactly what those people look like."

The interesting thing was that now the jewelcrafter had finally let down his guard, it was the soldiers' turn to get scared. While they waited for the next carriage to arrive, the three of them kept quietly discussing the terrifying dark magic from a moment ago.

"Archmage Sesso, are you injured?" Harnil suddenly said from beside him. "What?"

Jace noticed the dwarf looking at the back of his neck. He reached up to touch it and felt a small amount of blood. But it wasn't his blood.

"Ah, probably scraped something somewhere. It's nothing, Master." Jace said.

Seeing the dwarf walk away reassured, he immediately picked up the wrapped bundle of Ussalus he'd set aside. The cloth around it had indeed seeped through with a little blood.

While he'd been casting that spell, this staff had actually been secretly helping him.

"Are you craving the souls prepared for that dark god in that temple, worried I'd die and no one would take you there?" Jace asked softly in Demonic, smiling.

Ussalus gave no response at all, lying there quietly. The blood slowly seeping from it grew viscous.
