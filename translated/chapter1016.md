## Chapter 1016: Feeding

The sky had already been a little overcast at noon when Jace was at the Blue Heron, and now, as luck would have it, it had started to rain, the air growing colder by the minute.

But Jace, having just escaped the Great Forge yesterday, felt refreshed and invigorated, so he made his way to the high elf mage's residence with a light step.

Beneath the eaves by the courtyard fence, the mage guards on watch were sheltering from the rain there.

Ever since the campaign at the Dark Portal, more and more mages from Quel'Thalas had been coming to Stormwind, using it as a base to study the magic of the demons and the Dark Portal.

So at some point without anyone noticing, the guards the Wizard Sanctum had stationed here were replaced by Quel'Thalas sentinels, making this place look more and more like a proper embassy compound.

Jace walked over to the eaves and greeted them in elven. The elf nodded slightly. "Good afternoon, Archmage Sesso. How may I help you?"

"I'm here to see Lady Vereesa Windrunner," Jace said. "Is she upstairs?"

"A few days after Onyxia attacked Stormwind, the Ranger-Lord moved out," the guard said. Jace was a little surprised and asked, "She returned to Quel'Thalas?"

The guard shook his head. "I couldn't say for certain, Archmage Sesso."

Jace looked at the package in his arms wrapped around that pair of crocodile-skin boots, then glanced toward the corner leading to the Park. The rain was coming down harder now.

He'd originally planned to deliver the boots to Vereesa first before going to find Sola, but it looked like he'd have to ask Sola first.

He came to Sola's door and knocked. Sola opened the door, looked him over, and asked, "Jace, you're soaked from the rain outside — are we setting off for Shadowforge City right away?"

"Not yet." Jace looked into her eyes to confirm she was actually awake, then flung his cloak outward to shake off the rain and hung it on the hook by the door. "The problem with that slab hasn't been resolved. There's no confirmed way to replicate it yet, so we can't just hand it back to the Dark Irons like this."

"Is there a problem with my blood?" Sola sat slowly on the bed, lowering her head and speaking softly. "Maybe the power of my blood can't compare to Onyxia's or Prince Korialstrasz's…"

"Krasus got his green dragon blood from you?" Jace sighed. "It's not a problem with your blood. The problem now is that even if we used Queen Ysera's blood, it wouldn't do much good. I'm planning a trip to Booty Bay to ask the merchants there if they have any chromatic dragon blood for sale."

Seeing Sola still looking a little unconvinced, Jace sat in the chair beside them and said, half-joking, "Don't overthink it, Sola. Even if the replication really did fail because your blood isn't as powerful as theirs, that's not your fault. Who made your birth mother have you several thousand years too late?"

This finally coaxed a smile out of Sola. "Don't talk about the mother who bore me like that, even though I have no idea who she is."

"All right, sorry." Jace said. "By the way, could you take me to Booty Bay?"

"How do we get to Booty Bay?" Sola asked.

"Fly south along the coast and you'll see it." Jace pointed out the window.

"Now that you mention it, I remember hearing people talk about that pirate-filled place at The Pig and Whistle Inn." Sola leaned forward a little. "When do we leave?"

"Tomorrow?" Jace asked.

"Then give me some time to rest a little," Sola said.

"No problem." Jace said. "One more thing — do you know where Vereesa went? Did she return to Quel'Thalas?"

"She didn't say anything about returning to Quel'Thalas." Sola thought about it. "But she did move out of the Mage Quarter. I remember she had Grede help her find a new place in Old Town, near The Pig and Whistle Inn."

"What." Jace frowned slightly. "What's she doing over there?"

"She didn't tell me either." Sola said helplessly.

"All right." Jace said. "I'll go take a look over there. I won't disturb you. Rest well, Sola — I'm counting on you next."

Sola hesitated for a moment. "Before we set off, could you take me to get something to eat, Jace?"

"Sure." Jace said. "Where do you want to go? You name it."

"Take advantage of the rain and go to Elwynn Forest?"

The way Sola said it, her eyes practically glowed green. Only then did Jace realize what she meant by "something to eat." "Of… of course." Jace said. "But why take advantage of the rain?"

"When it's raining, there are very few people outside, so the chances of being seen are small," Sola said.

"Mm, that makes sense." Jace nodded and took down his cloak. "Then let's go ask at the Gryphon Roost."

"All right." Sola smiled.

By the afternoon, the rain was coming down harder. The two of them crossed the Trade District and came to the eastern city wall. Before they even climbed the stairs, they could hear the gryphons roaring from atop the wall — noisy and chaotic.

It seemed the weather was the cause. Most of the gryphons had returned to their roosts to rest, and quite a few hadn't calmed down from their excited flights yet. Drenched and dripping, they kept screeching at the dwarf or human gryphon keepers passing by.

"Good afternoon, sir." Jace called out to a dwarf gryphon trainer hurrying past and held out his Court Arcane signet. "I'd like to buy a live animal here to feed a gryphon. I don't mind if the price is a little steep."

"This isn't a market, I—" The dwarf, having been stopped, was a little impatient, but the moment he caught sight of that ring, his eyes widened. "You can go find one directly below the wall, Archmage!"

"No problem, thank you, sir." Jace said.

After thanking the dwarf, Jace brought Sola to the animal pens below the Valley of Heroes. Gorjett was resting in the great gryphon roost here. He seemed to have heard Jace's footsteps — the moment Jace greeted him, the beast lifted his head at once and craned his neck over excitedly.

Stormwind no longer had only his single Alterac gryphon. Right beside him were two or three others only slightly smaller in size. One could clearly tell that his temper had mellowed considerably.

Of course, what had mellowed his excitement wasn't just those fellow gryphons — more likely it was Sola following beside Jace. The moment Gorjett caught sight of Sola, that excitement immediately gained a trace of caution.

Although the mages in Stormwind couldn't see through Sola's disguise, these gryphons could vaguely sense something off about her. The gryphons Sola passed — even the little creatures that had been making a racket — all quieted down in unison, daring only to steal glances at this slender golden-haired woman with a smoldering curiosity they couldn't suppress.

"You know Gorjett." The gryphon keeper in charge of feeding looked over and asked. "Are you a friend of Lord Grede Thunderfist?"

"Yes, I'm Jace Sesso." Jace nodded, showed him the ring, and said, "I'm planning to buy a sheep to test a new spell outside. Put the money on Lord Grede Thunderfist's tab."

Jace deliberately loaded the word "Lord" with extra sarcasm, thinking to himself that he had to find more opportunities to make this guy bleed a little.

"So it's Archmage Soso!" the keeper smiled. "No problem, I'll let Thunderfist know. Pick any sheep you like here."

Sola peered inside for a moment, then pointed at the biggest one. "That one, Jace."

Jace looked at the sheep, then sized up Sola's mouth, and nodded. "Fine, that one." Leading the sheep, the two of them left the wall and came to a quiet patch of forest outside the city.

"This should be about right here." Jace said. "I'll keep watch for you. If anyone comes close, I'll let you know."

"It won't take long," Sola said.

Jace nodded. The golden-haired woman beside him walked to the clearing ahead, and in a natural wind that parted the rain curtain, her body rapidly swelled…

The sheep, witnessing this, let out a stifled bleat. Having been docile the whole way, it suddenly wrenched itself free from Jace's hand and bolted deep into the forest.

This was food for Sola — how dare Jace block it with shadow magic?

He was just about to run after it when a green shadow suddenly darted past his ear. A massive snake-like snout bit into the sheep's hind leg.

The young dragon raised her long neck, tossed the helpless sheep into the air, then swallowed it in one gulp. The large sheep bulged out her neck, and as she lowered her head and swallowed hard, the bulge slid down into her belly and vanished without a trace.

Jace stared blankly at the scene. He'd been planning to witness how Sola tore into her meal, yet the whole process hadn't even produced a single drop of blood.

Amid the drifting leaves, bits of grass, and rain, the young dragon swished her long tail contentedly, then transformed back into that frail girl's form once more in a swirl of mist.

Jace looked at her stomach. He'd been about to ask, "Where'd that whole sheep go?"

But the words died on his lips. When it came to magic, it was better not to ask too many questions.

"Ah, next I need a good long sleep, Jace. I think in a few more days, I could even fly you back to Kalimdor." Sola smiled and patted her stomach lightly, as if even her voice sounded more full of life.
