Chapter 1017: Every Hunter's Dream

The two of them returned to Stormwind and parted ways in the Trade District. It was three or four in the afternoon by now.

Jace had originally wanted to go to Grede's place and ask where Vereesa had moved, but the guy turned out not to be home.

Without seeing Vereesa, or at least knowing where she'd gone, he couldn't feel at ease heading to Booty Bay. So he went to The Pig and Whistle Inn on the off chance.

It wasn't dark yet, so there weren't many people in the inn, and no elves either.

Jace asked the door a group of workers gathered together chatting if there'd been any elves around. One of them said he'd seen an elf woman near the wall yesterday, also with silver hair.

That had to be Vereesa. Silver-haired elves weren't exactly rare in Quel'Thalas, but in Stormwind at least, Jace hadn't seen a second silver-haired elf woman.

From here toward the wall, passing through an Old Town gate would bring you to the SI:7 headquarters. Could Vereesa have business with SI:7?

Even if she did, there was no need to move house all the way over here, and she hadn't said a word to him about it either.

Jace walked quickly toward the SI:7 compound. Before he reached the small gate, he heard a crisp snap of breaking wood from a nearby alley, then another not long after. It was like someone chopping wood, but much clearer and sharper than that.

He glanced over curiously. It turned out to be the place of the warrior guild Grede had joined before. Behind a half-shut door was a courtyard filled with all kinds of training dummies and arrow targets.

In the empty courtyard at the moment, a slender figure in a black-gray rain cloak had her bow drawn, aiming at a target whose center was packed tight with a thick cluster of arrows.

Her fingers released — a whistle-like twang — and the arrow slammed straight into the middle of that cluster, driving deep inside and shattering several arrows that had been crowding it. Broken wooden pieces showered onto the waterlogged ground, joining the heap of snapped shafts and lone arrowheads.

Without a moment's hesitation, the figure nocked another arrow and drew the bow taut again, like a machine re-targeting the center. As if having noticed someone approaching from the side, her attention shifted ever so slightly — and the bowstring suddenly snapped with a twang, startling even herself.

"Damn it!" she cursed under her breath, annoyed, and swapped for another bow from the shed nearby.

"Vee." Jace followed her under the shed and tried calling out.

Vereesa glanced over, startled for a moment, before saying, "Jace… what are you doing here."

"What kind of question is that." Jace smiled. "If anything, I should be asking why you secretly moved here to train by yourself."

Vereesa let out a heavy, stifled breath. "I can't keep going like before, Jace. I must have neglected my training, must have grown slack after becoming Ranger-Lord. I could have entered that rift — it absolutely should have been within my power. If I'd pushed harder, held on a few more times… at least I could have gotten in there and helped you. Onyxia wouldn't have escaped so easily. Even if we still couldn't have killed her, at least… at least you wouldn't have been hurt like that."

"I'm much better now, Vee," Jace said gently.

"Don't comfort me, Jace. Don't let me slack off again. What I need now is more grueling practice." Vereesa grit her teeth, tested the bow in her hands, and stepped back into the rain.

"Then do you need this?" Jace asked, lifting the package in his arms.

Vereesa glanced over curiously, then lowered her bow. "What is this?"

"New boots." Jace said. "A gift I brought back from Ironforge."

"Mm." Vereesa looked at the old boots on her feet and smiled. "Since when do you think of buying me gifts, huh?"

"You might not believe it, but these boots actually didn't cost anything."

As he spoke, Jace set the package down on the rack beside them and opened it. Vereesa leaned in curiously, touched the material of the boots, and her expression suddenly changed, her eyes widening slightly.

"Light above, what kind of hide is this… doesn't seem like dragonhide, but it's not inferior in the slightest. It's not really dragonhide, is it? But this color — white with a greenish tint — won't Sola be angry if she sees it?"

"As if I'd make boots out of green dragon hide, Vee." Jace said. "Remember that time we went to Maraudon, that legendary crocodile I mentioned to Grede?"

"Ah, some impression of it." Vereesa nodded.

Jace continued explaining, "Earlier I sold the information to a hunters' guild in Ironforge. They actually managed to hunt down that giant crocodile, and it made a huge name for them in Ironforge… Here, this is their gift of thanks to me — a pair of white crocodile-skin boots made from that crocodile's hide."

"Light above." Vereesa looked like she wanted to laugh but held it back. She lifted the boots and said softly, "I really thought that legend was just something the dwarves made up…"

"Try them on." Jace said.

"It's raining right now, and I haven't even washed my feet yet, Jace. I'm afraid I'll get the boots dirty." Vereesa said, her brows lowering.

"What's there to worry about?" Jace said. "These are battle boots made for rangers and warriors like you. If a little rain could ruin them, they wouldn't be worth anything at all."

"But these are a gift from you." Vereesa smiled.

"Then it matters even less." Jace said. "I'm the one telling you to try them on, after all."

"All right, all right." Vereesa set her bow aside, and the tense look on her face had already relaxed.

The ranger happily kicked off her old boots and swapped on the new shoes. She strutted back and forth a few times with the same solemn air as those Spellbreakers patrolling back and forth in Quel'Thalas, then puffed out her chest, barely containing her smile. "Ah, there really is a difference with good things. You didn't secretly find a mage to enchant these boots, did they? They feel both light and sturdy — not like a material that could grow naturally on a wild beast."

"I haven't had time to enchant them yet, but these boots can clearly take some very powerful magic. Maybe that's exactly why the dwarves call it the Crocodile God!" Jace said, leaning against the empty weapon rack beside him. "Since you like them, I'll go to the Wizard Sanctum… ah, I just remembered we've got a member of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six staying here. When I get back from Booty Bay, I'll ask him to take a look and see if he can enchant these boots with something useful…"

"You mean Master Krasus." Vereesa gave a wicked smile. "Even though he's injured now and struggling to recover, enchanting something shouldn't take too much out of him!"

"Exactly." Jace nodded vigorously, and the two of them burst out laughing at once. Vereesa threw her arms around Jace and hugged him tight. "Thank you, Jace. Truly, thank you."

"Don't thank me yet," Jace said softly. "There's something else."

"What?" Vereesa asked.

Jace beckoned with a finger. "Come with me. I didn't bring it on me."

Vereesa narrowed her eyes suspiciously, then sighed softly. "I knew the moment I saw you, I wouldn't be able to focus on training."

Jace glanced toward the arrow target. "But you've been training all day, haven't you? The arrows you've smashed could pierce Sargeras full of holes. A little rest won't hurt, will it?"

"All right, all right." Vereesa waved a hand. "Let's go. I want to see what you're hiding."

"Whose arrows and bow are these?" Jace asked.

"The warrior guild's. Don't worry, I paid my dues in full — I can use them as I like." Vereesa stared at him. "I'm not suspecting you of anything…" Jace gave her a sidelong glance.

Back at his rented room, Jace opened the door. Morfile was sitting there, idly tossing a ball of fel-green flame back and forth with Sazeno to pass the time. Seeing Vereesa, she clenched her hand to snuff out the flame and looked over with a smile. "Good afternoon, dear master. Good afternoon, Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

"Good afternoon, Morfile." Vereesa smiled.

"Bring that thing out, Morfile." Jace rubbed his fingers together.

"At once, master. I've been guarding this gift you prepared for the Lady Windrunner with my life." Morfile said as she dragged the bundle out from under the bed.

"What life, you demon?" the imp jeered from the window.

"Then with my… soul." Morfile corrected herself, glaring at him.

"Truly, Stormwind is far too dangerous. You must have put in tremendous effort to protect this." Jace said as he took the bundle. The succubus rolled her eyes at Jace. Vereesa looked on with the expression of someone enjoying the show.

"In any case, this thing… is absolutely precious, Vee." Jace handed the bundle to Vereesa.

"This looks… like a short staff? You're not trying to get me to learn some incantation, are you." Vereesa opened the package skeptically.

When she was halfway through opening it, she twitched her nose — her keen sense of smell had clearly caught that something was off.

"This is animal… no, how could there be such a, uh, what kind of animal's tendon is this?" Vereesa sniffed it and said softly. "Restorative Potion, wait… this can't be…"

"It is." Jace said. "This is Onyxia's tendon, Vee. I was thinking of making it into a bow."

Vereesa's eyes went wide at once. Her lips trembled a couple of times. She looked at Jace, not knowing what to say.

"I don't know who in Quel'Thalas could make a bow out of this, Jace, I…"

For a moment, she became completely at a loss, as if she didn't even know how to hold the thing.

"Sola said the Kaldorei seem to know some legendary method of making one, Vee. Don't worry about it, we'll find a way." Jace looked at the tendon and said. "Once this is made, you probably won't ever have to worry about a snapped bowstring again."

Vereesa bit her lip and threw herself onto Jace, knocking him onto the bed. The ranger's strength was such that it made Jace's old injury ache dully all over again… but seeing the elf before him strip off her garments piece by piece and kiss him eagerly, he endured it without a word.

Morfile, off to the side, propped her chin in her hand and watched the two of them tangle together on the bed, clicking her tongue. "Sigh, I knew it… I knew it…"
