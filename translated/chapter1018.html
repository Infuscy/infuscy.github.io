Chapter 1018: A Taboo Even for Goblins

The green dragon soared freely between the forests and the coast of Stranglethorn Vale. Looking down from high above, one could even catch glimpses of the beasts dwelling in the forest leaping down from the trees in terror and scattering in every direction.

Saomailier seemed to be enjoying herself. When far from those troll ruins, she would even deliberately fly low, skimming over the beaches where basilisks lay basking, forcing those clumsy, heavy creatures to wake up and seek cover from the wrath of this green behemoth. The intermittent, chirping little growls she made at the sight sounded exactly like a young dragon's laughter.

This slightly introverted young dragon playing about so cheerfully was just like a child who'd been cooped up at home for ages and finally had a chance to run wild outdoors.

Jace gripped the horns on the dragon's back and neck tightly to avoid being thrown off by a sudden dive or pull-up. Every time Jace gasped in shock or couldn't help but cry out, the green dragon seemed even more delighted, as if toying with this fragile mortal on her back was part of the fun.

Jace could sense that although Saomailier had grown attached to Stormwind, she was a guardian of the forest through and through. If Queen Ysera hadn't wanted her to risk returning to the Emerald Dream and falling to the Nightmare's corruption and control again, she would probably still prefer to remain in the forest.

Considering the presence of hired gryphon riders in Booty Bay, before approaching Stranglethorn Vale, Saomailier descended into a patch of rainforest not far north of Stranglethorn and let Jace off, transforming back into the high elf mortal form she'd maintained ever since coming to the Eastern Kingdoms.

"Be careful, Sola." Jace observed the forest, looking at Sola as she straightened her emerald outer robe. "Last time I came here, I ran into a…"

"A what?" Sola asked.

Only when he got this far did Jace realize that this fellow was a monster far more terrifying than a devilsaur. If anyone should be afraid, it should be the devilsaur that was afraid of her.

He looked again into the depths of the rainforest. Everything around was quiet to the point of death — he couldn't even hear the sound of insects. Whatever could flee had probably already fled.

"Nothing." He waved a hand. "Let's go."

Jace had been to Booty Bay several times already, so he entered through the northern gate of the town and hurried up to Mitherserd's alchemy lab.

Sola, on the other hand, was quite curious about this town. Especially when she saw trolls, orcs, humans, dwarves of varying skin tones, and gnomes all standing together — and occasionally even catching a glimpse of an elf — she showed considerable fascination.

"Don't you find this surprising, Jace?" Sola kept pace with Jace's footsteps and said. "So many races that hate each other, able to stand together in a place like this… it's truly rare. And I didn't even know this place existed. I always thought this was a place occupied by the Gurubashi trolls."

"It was, once. But the Gurubashi trolls declined long ago, and the goblins took it over," Jace said. "Most of the people who settled here left their own races for one reason or another. Some were driven out, more came to make money — legally or otherwise. In any case, most people here don't have much sense of racial belonging."

Sola said softly, "Just like me."

Jace laughed. "I'm about the same."

Sola shook her head, as if disagreeing with Jace's remark, but said nothing more.

At the very top of Booty Bay, smoke was rising from one of Mitherserd's alchemy lab rooms. A faint scent of Living Root drifted through the air.

"Is Master Mitherserd in?"

Jace shouted toward the alchemy lab. Unexpectedly, a sudden clatter of metal came from the side. A large pile of what he'd taken for a broken machine or a garbage heap suddenly stood up on its own.

After a series of rotations, unfoldings, and combinations of various parts, that pile of junk actually transformed into a robot clad in green armor plating!

"Maximilin System Prototype booting up. Suspected intruders: two. Initiating elimination protocol."

It raised its thick arms and aimed them right at Jace's direction. Sola flicked her fingers, and a mass of vines grew wildly from the soil, yanking at the robot's arms. But the robot reacted with terrifying speed, jets of spinning flame spraying from its body and burning the vines to a crisp — as if it already had a countermeasure prepared against nature magic.

"Hostile action 122 detected. Enemy type diagnosis complete…"

Before it could finish speaking, Jace spotted the obvious "main unit" behind it covered in connected cables and ripped the whole thing off with Bloodgrasp, flinging it to the side. A large cluster of components clattered away with it.

"Critical error, unable to locate critical… critical…"

The entire robot wilted. Its voice grew low and slow, gradually dissolving into meaningless static.

The door of the alchemy lab in the distance banged open with a crash, and a goblin's roar came from inside.

"Hey, what's the meaning of this, Mr. Slovak? Aren't you a mage and alchemist from Stormwind? You should have some culture and upbringing! How come you're even more savage and crude than the Gurubashi trolls?!"

"Damn it, damn it." Mitherserd ran over, holding his head. He inspected the Maximilin System Prototype back and forth, then finally spread his hands. "Congratulations, Mr. Slovak. Even though everything you learned in the first half of your life was herbology and incantations, you're clearly a genius at engineering who understands it better than I do — you found its weak spot in one shot. But now I don't know what to do. All I know is my sixteen gold pieces have gone completely down the drain."

"This piece of junk is worth sixteen gold?" Jace asked.

"Because it's clearly not meant to deal with someone like you!" the goblin said through gritted teeth. "You think if there was someone who could kill demons more easily than slaughtering chickens and was dead set on killing me, I'd count on this thing to dissuade him?!"

"This was self-defense." Jace raised his hands. "It was already initiating its 'elimination protocol.' What else did you expect me to do?"

"Ah, honestly…" Mitherserd flung his hand. "Fine, fine. Consider it a lesson learned. This is still better than senselessly causing a loss of life. I'll send it to the robotics experts and see if they can fix it. They should have plenty of experience with shadow-corroded parts…"

After saying that, the goblin glanced at Sola standing beside Jace, then asked, "So what is it this time? Has this elegant elf lady also been mysteriously struck by some death curse?"

"Speaking of which, thank you for bringing it up." Jace said. "How are Noggfrag's potion sales going? I haven't received the gold you promised me the better part of a year ago in Stormwind."

Mitherserd rubbed his hands together. "Well, this…"

"What's the situation this time?" Jace asked. "Did the goblins carrying the money get tossed into the Twisting Nether by demons?" Mitherserd shook his head. "Fine, since you've come all this way in person."

With that, the goblin trudged off to a distant room. Jace simply sat down in the lounging chair where the goblin sunbathed and rested for a bit, waiting for him to come out.

"What's this money situation about?" Sola asked.

"The whole story…" Jace looked toward the goblin's direction. "It's complicated. Before going to Kalimor, he promised to deliver the money to Stormwind within half a year. Now the better part of a year has passed, and he's acting like it never happened."

"Are you sure this goblin would actually know about chromatic dragon blood, Jace?" Sola asked skeptically. Jace didn't answer. Honestly, he wasn't holding out much hope either.

That Mitherserd had withheld his cut of the potion profits for so long — Jace wasn't even particularly angry about it.

For one thing, he'd never counted on goblins having any decent credibility in the first place. He figured Mitherserd had probably assumed he'd die in Kalimor, or if not Kalimor then somewhere else, so he'd wanted to drag it out as long as possible.

For another, it was related to the real purpose of his visit this time.

Mitherserd knew he was Jace Sesso, and he more or less knew his strength. Yet he was willing to offend a warlock of his ill repute over thirty gold pieces. Who's to say there weren't goblins in Blackwing Lair bold enough to steal and sell chromatic dragon blood?

He knew this line of reasoning was quite a stretch, but deep down, he'd gained a sliver of hope.

"Here it comes, here it comes, the gold is here." The goblin said as he came out, holding a bag and tossing it onto the small wooden table beside the lounging chair.

Jace nodded and counted the gold pieces briefly. There were only seventeen. "I believe you promised thirty-one gold and…"

"Seventy silver." Mitherserd said, embarrassed. "I still owe you a little over fourteen gold. I'm working hard to raise the funds, truly."

Jace frowned and looked toward the distant glittering surface of the sea and the tall ship sails. "Tell me what I want to ask you, Master Mitherserd."

"Go ahead." The goblin nodded dejectedly, his spirit seemingly already half-drained. "Do you know those goblins who serve the black dragons?" Jace asked.

"Ah… hmm?" The goblin glanced suspiciously at Jace, then at Sola. "Why do you ask?"

"There's something I want to make right now that might require materials related to black dragons. I was thinking maybe I could get some here." Jace said.

"Impossible." The goblin shook his head with a smile. "I have no connection to them, and I have nothing related to black dragons here — neither dragonhide nor dragon's blood… You're not the first person to come asking."

"Not at the trade station either?" Jace asked.

"They can't afford to take that risk." Mitherserd pointed into the distance. "You think Booty Bay could withstand a blast of flame from one of those black dragons? Look at this town — it's like a target built specifically to welcome a dragon swooping down and burning everything to the ground."

"What about other dragons, then?" Jace asked.

"Other dragons." The goblin's brows relaxed a little. He lowered his voice. "I have an alchemist friend who has things related to red whelps — small scales, among other things… Frankly, I've never actually participated in hunting whelps in Khaz Modan myself, so judging purely on my own, I don't know if his goods are real or fake."

Jace felt Sola's mood sour instantly beside him, so he gave her a look that said, hold your temper for now.

"Whelps probably won't do." Jace said. "I know black dragons seem to be at odds with all other dragons. Wouldn't those goblins serving the black dragons have a chance to smuggle out some things from other dragons' bodies and bring them here to sell?"

Mitherserd agonized for a moment. "All right, this one… maybe I shouldn't be telling you." He put his hands on his hips and looked at Sola. "Can she be trusted?"

"Completely." Jace said.

"Mm." The goblin lowered his head. "If I tell you, give me another six months on the money, how about that? I really owe a lot of people money for researching Noggfrag's formula…"

"Six months? Then talk." Jace said.

The goblin relaxed at once. He leaned in and said, "Remember Mr. Pucidiaz?"

"Of course. That collector has helped me quite a bit before." Jace said.

"If there's anyone in all of Booty Bay who dares to buy things smuggled from the black dragons, it's probably him," Mitherserd said. "I hear he's been in contact with goblin alchemists serving the black dragons — or those artificers and smiths — to build his collection of dark magic-related items. But this matter… he would absolutely never readily admit his connection to those goblins. One thing you must be careful about — don't go and ask him this question directly. Not that many people know this little secret of his, and he'll immediately trace it back to me. Don't tell him I told you. You know where he lives — go yourself. Just pretend you never came to me."

Jace nodded. "You still owe me money. I wouldn't sell you out."

"Of course, of course." Mitherserd agreed, flashing a wide grin.
