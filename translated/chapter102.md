## Chapter 102: Clothes Make the Man

"Wizard Sanctum apprentice sigil. This is my ring — I'm a Wizard Sanctum apprentice!" Jace said immediately. "Go ask Master Marin in the Mage Quarter, he's my teacher. And Master Andormas too — they can both vouch for me!"

Hearing Jace rattle off all those names, the shopkeeper looked a little nervous.

"He… he's making things up, Magistrate Morses."

Morses said, "I do know Master Andormas, but I can't exactly summon a senior magister down here just to verify your identity. So if you want to clear this up fast, cooperate. Tell me — what do the two letters on this ring stand for?"

"Jace Sesso." Jace said. "The initials of my name."

"You're Jace Sesso?!" Morses's eyes went wide.

The shopkeeper looked even more nervous now. He leaned in and whispered, "You know this man?"

"Jace Sesso is the hero who fought off the gnolls at Stonewatch," Morses said. "I heard the boys at Lakeshire talk about him. Jace Sesso is a big brute who can take on seven or eight gnolls at once, and even after having his hand chopped off he kept fighting — King Wrynn himself received him for it… Lad, you're pretty solid, though not quite as brawny as I pictured. But your hand's still attached. So tell me — when did you steal our hero's things?"

Jace was stunned. What the hell was this? Seven or eight at once? A chopped-off hand? "You don't believe me? Fine — let me see the King!"

"You think you can just waltz in and see the King?" Morses snapped. "Here's what's going to happen. As long as you can prove you're a Wizard Sanctum—"

Before Morses could finish, Jace strained against his bonds, extended his finger, and intoned, "Liyouisa!"

A sudden burst of arcane light flooded the room, turning the two men's faces bone white. Morses squinted against the glare, caught off guard, while the shopkeeper stumbled back several steps.

"Does this prove I'm a mage?!"

Jace roared — and the arcane light kept getting brighter!

"Stop… stop it," Morses said, shielding his eyes.

"Does it prove it?!"

"Yes! Just drop the spell and we'll talk."

When the light faded, the shopkeeper was pressed against the back wall, frozen in place. The prison doorway was packed with soldiers drawn by the glow, crowding together and staring in.

Morses rubbed his eyes and told the soldier next to him, "Someone get down to the Mage Quarter. Find that Marin mage and ask him to come take a look at this man."

"Understood." The soldier left the cell. The shopkeeper edged closer and asked, "Sir, can I go now?"

"You're not going anywhere." Morses's tone toward the shopkeeper was noticeably colder than before.

After a length of time Jace couldn't quite gauge, he kept his eyes locked on the shopkeeper, glaring daggers at him. The shopkeeper went from worried to actually frightened.

Finally, footsteps came from outside the cell. It wasn't Marin who arrived — it was his daughter.

"Oh? Jace, how'd you end up getting arrested as a thief?"

"Ma'am, who are you?" Morses asked.

"I'm Amy Marin, Archmage Marin's daughter."

"He's your father's student."

"Of course he is," Amy said. "He's been working for my father for a month now. We know each other."

"So he's the Sesso who killed gnolls at Stonewatch?"

"Yes. He met the King at Stonewatch."

Hearing that the young man before them was the fighter who'd taken on six or seven gnolls single-handedly atop the Stonewatch tower, the soldiers around them looked surprised. A low murmur rippled outside.

Jace had a feeling this story was going to get more and more ridiculous with every retelling.

"He doesn't look the part… maybe they were exaggerating," Morses muttered.

"You've never even seen me!" Jace said, annoyed. "Let me go. I didn't steal a thing!"

"Seems there's been a misunderstanding." Morses looked at Jace's hands, then back at the shopkeeper. "There's no case here, sir. You can't judge what someone owns just by the way they dress. Don't you think you owe him an apology?"

"I…" The shopkeeper stammered. "I'm sorry, Mr. Sesso."

Two soldiers helped cut Jace loose. He rubbed his numb wrists and said, "An apology and that's it? One word from you and I got knocked out and dragged here. My head's still pounding."

The shopkeeper dug into his pocket and pulled out two silver, holding them out to Jace. "It's all I have on me. My sincere apologies — I shouldn't have judged by appearances, sir."

A punch for two silver. Same rate as the Slaughtered Lamb hiring out living target dummies.

It wasn't much, but Jace pocketed the money and his temper cooled a little.

Led by the guards, he and Amy walked up the stairs and out — sure enough, this was the Stormwind Stockades, right next to the canal. Not far from the Mage Quarter at all.

Amy looked Jace up and down. "How did this even happen?"

Jace sighed. "They took one look at me and decided I was a thief. I genuinely never thought I'd run into something like this."

Amy asked, "Jace, didn't Master Andormas arrange for you to get a set of mage's clothes made at Larson's?"

"Not yet. I only met Master Andormas a few days ago," Jace said.

Honestly, he hadn't been all that motivated to go — but after this whole ordeal, he was starting to realize that getting a set of clothes befitting his current status was actually pretty important.

"When I first entered the Wizard Sanctum, I met Master Andormas in the morning and was already rushing to get clothes by noon," Amy said. "Kid, I don't know how it is in Lordaeron, but people in Stormwind aren't as naive as you. They judge everyone by their clothes. If you walk around in a simple outfit like that, carrying things that only robed mages would have, they're going to assume something's off — either you stole from a mage, or you're planning to use magic for no good."

Jace was a little bothered by Amy calling him "naive," but thinking about it — after arriving in Azeroth, he'd spent most of his time running around with a bunch of kids, stealing fruit and hijacking horses, goofing off all day.

Slowly, his personality and outlook had been shaped by fitting in with that pack of brats.

It was only after leaving Lordaeron on his own that he'd started living like an adult again. Maybe he really was just a clueless young fool stumbling around.

Amy went on, "I heard you're planning a trip to Dalaran? When the time comes, if you want people to know you're Wizard Sanctum, you can't very well show up wearing that, can you?"

"Fine. I'll go take a look at Larson's," Jace said. "Hey, thanks for bailing me out."

"Bail you out?" Amy paused, then laughed. "That's a pretty good way to put it. By the way — what were you carrying that made that merchant think you were a thief?" Jace opened the parcel Morses had returned to him and took out the pearl string and the broken pocket watch. "I just showed him these two things."

Amy glanced at the string first, then examined the watch. "This pearl string is quite pretty. But this watch — how did it get into this state? There's so much sand in it."

"It was already broken when I found it," Jace said. "I picked it up in an abandoned town in Westfall. Think it can be fixed?"

Amy said, "Give it to me. I know a gnome mage who loves tinkering with little gadgets like this. I'll have him take a look."

Jace's eyes lit up at the word "free." "Really? That would be great?"

"No promises he can fix it."

"As long as there's a chance!"
