Chapter 1022: The Green Dragon

In a clearing deep in the Stranglethorn jungle, the distant murmur of the coast's tides still carried.

Pokodiaz paced back and forth in his usual spot, excitement and nervousness written all over his face.

Today the collector—who almost never set foot off his ship—had put on a brand-new set of "adventure gear." A deep-red leather jacket, gloves, and trousers covered in yellow patterns. At first glance it was hard to tell whether the markings came from the beast the hide had been taken from, or whether they'd been tooled in on purpose during the making.

But the more Jace looked at it, the more familiar it seemed.

"Is this devilsaur leather, Mr. Pokodiaz?" Jace asked.

"Ah, you can actually tell," the goblin said with a smile. "The devilsaur is without a doubt the most powerful, most fearsome beast in all of Stranglethorn! If you ask me, it's not much worse than a dragon! A lot of people say that wearing devilsaur leather makes you as strong and savage as a devilsaur itself… I can't say I feel that directly, but I'll admit this outfit's at least given me a bit more confidence for adventuring!"

Jace smiled and said, "Where I come from there's an old saying: the worst students have the fanciest stationery."

Pokodiaz spread his hands. "As an adventurer I really am a rank amateur, but more is always better than less, wouldn't you say?"

"Fair enough," Jace nodded. "Do a lot of wealthy folks in Booty Bay like devilsaur-leather outfits?"

The goblin thought for a moment. "From what I know, plenty of rich men like to buy these for their bodyguards and vault guards, just to look intimidating. And there's no shortage of demand over in Gadgetzan either. But mine's different from theirs. Mine's made from the hide of Tidora, a cunning devilsaur that roamed the ancient Gurubashi ruins. Two orc hunters died bringing that beast down—two orc hunters!"

"On top of that I paid an extra dozen gold in compensation for their families, though I seriously doubt they'd have been able to carry that money back to the other side of the Dark Portal."

He said it proudly, then added, "But I'll admit, when it comes to dealing with devilsaurs, those orcs really know their business. The Gurubashi trolls' experience is no doubt richer, but they're not keen on helping us hunt devilsaurs—something to do with their gods, from what I hear. The orcs don't care about any of that. The problem is, ever since orc mercenaries started showing up in Booty Bay, the devilsaurs have been getting thinner and thinner. Who would've ever thought the terrifying monsters that once ruled the jungle would be hunted to this state by green-skinned folk from another world?"

So that was why, a decade from now, there wouldn't be a single devilsaur left in Stranglethorn—all wiped out by goblin rich boys throwing money around to show off.

By now the jungle had gone noticeably quieter. The goblin glanced around and whispered, "Is there danger about? The animals must be sensing something terrible that I can't."

No. Jace looked up. He knew it was Sola… no, Solamiel, coming.

A shadow swept across the ground overhead, accompanied by a rush of wind, and the surrounding trees shuddered.

Jace was starting to understand why even the most savage beasts would bolt the instant they felt her approach.

He'd almost never experienced Sola's approach from the prey's perspective before. This green dragon had lived a thousand years; even if she'd never learned how to fight, her sheer predatory instinct alone was enough to bring a wave of killing intent.

Beside him, Pokodiaz had frozen in place, his pupils darting frantically across the sky.

Then a rush of wind came from behind them, and bushes, twigs, and dead leaves went flying. The goblin spun around, and there she was—a massive young dragon in shimmering green, slowly beating her enormous wings as she came to a stop in front of them.

The goblin, who'd already been scared stiff, let out a roar right in his face as she landed, making him bite down and stumble back two steps, nearly falling over.

"No, no… no, this, uh…" It was a long while before a few broken, unintelligible sounds tumbled out of his mouth.

Jace looked at him. "You can still back out, sir."

"I'm not backing out," Pokodiaz murmured. "The opportunity is right in front of me. Worst case… I die!"

The green dragon touched down, spread her wings wide, curled her tail, and stretched. Then she slowly lowered her head toward Jace.

"I answer your summons, Jace Sesso," she said, reciting the line they'd rehearsed with perfect form. "Is this the ally who helped us find the blood of the chromatic dragon?"

"Yes!" Pokodiaz chirped happily, then immediately realized something was off and waved his hands. "No no no, wait—I didn't find it. I bought it, bought it from someone else. But I'm absolutely an ally of the dragons, no question about it. During the Orc Wars a lot of idiots in the Trade Cartel did business with the orcs, but I personally never funded Orgrim and his Horde a single copper! I'm nothing like them!"

The green dragon didn't respond to his declaration. Pokodiaz could only laugh awkwardly, while Jace shot him a skeptical glance.

This guy had never exactly said where his money came from. He didn't live inside Booty Bay proper, and his relationship with the bay's ruler, Revilgaz, was… complicated. But given how much bad blood he had with the Bloodsail Buccaneers, he probably had ties to the Trade Cartel—or even the Blackwater Raiders—that he didn't want to talk about.

"Ready?" Jace asked.

"I think so," the goblin said, nodding at the green dragon. "Then let's begin."

Jace climbed up the young dragon's foreleg, then reached back down, grabbed the goblin by the arm, and hauled him up onto his shoulder. Pokodiaz swayed a couple of times, steadied himself, and immediately wrapped his arms tight around Jace's neck.

He looked down at the young dragon as she shifted her body and shuffled forward, and stammered, "I'm not dreaming, am I?"

Solamiel lifted her head, a trace of anger in her voice. "What do you take me for? I assure you I haven't used any magic on you, little one. All of this is perfectly real."

"What do you mean?" the goblin blinked.

"It means it's not a dream. Nothing deeper than that," Jace explained, then pointed at the dragon's back. "See those horns and scales up there? Green dragons are venomously toxic. If you accidentally brush up against them and get scratched and something goes wrong, that's on you."

"What…" Pokodiaz hugged Jace's neck even tighter. "So you came along for my safety? What about you, Mr. Slovaka—aren't you in danger too?"

Jace said, "I already know how to avoid getting hurt. But it's your first time, so be careful."

"Thank you. I'll remember that," the goblin said quickly, nodding.

"Hold on tight," Solamiel said.

"We're ready, Great Green Dragon," Jace said.

Solamiel seemed to take to the name naturally, though she gave Jace a strange sideways glance.

She spread her wings, and Jace saw her shoulder muscles tense—completely different from the gentle lift-off he'd felt before…

…this time he really needed to hold on tight.
