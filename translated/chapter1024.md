## Chapter 1024: Disciples of Old

This remote corner of Booty Bay hadn't changed in years—messy tree roots, weeds, a road paved who-knows-how-many-years-ago now shattered and choked by all manner of plants, and the dilapidated old houses on either side patched so many times they were barely recognizable.

Now that night had fallen, the place looked even more sinister. Jace led Sola through the tree roots one after another, ducked into the cave-like opening that hid the door, pushed aside the roots, and came to the wooden door.

Jace knocked on the sliding panel on the door. Before long, faint footsteps came from inside.

He glanced back at Sola. The green dragon seemed to have no particular objection to seeking help from a troll. After getting used to Grede and Vereesa making a huge fuss about any contact with trolls, her calm was honestly a little jarring.

The sliding panel banged open, revealing a skull mask.

The pale-yellow pupils behind the eye holes swiveled, landing on Jace, then on the woman behind him.

"I don't quite understand, Jace Sesso," the person behind the mask said in a crone-like voice. "What does it mean, you bringing this Quel'dorei to my door?"

"No need to worry, Sylia," Jace said in a low voice. "She's not a Farstrider, and she didn't come here to take your head. Even the Amani Empire has wanderers like you—it's not strange for a few odd souls in dull Quel'Thalas to want to come out and get some air once in a while, is it?"

The troll gave a hmph-laugh and opened the door. When she took off the mask, that feline face was revealed.

"I am Sylia, Quel'dorei. This mage should have introduced me."

"He didn't tell me your name, Lady Sylia, but I know you're a highly skilled herbalist," Sola said with a slight bow. "I am Sola Shade, a botanist of Quel'Thalas. In a sense, we could be considered colleagues."

"Your talent for lying falls far short of this human's, my lady," Sylia said, staring at her. "A botanist of Quel'Thalas has nothing whatsoever to do with us, by any measure. I'd sooner call those folk arcanists who enslave the forces of nature—that would be far more accurate. Even if they could set aside the arcane for just a moment and plant a few more flowers in their gorgeous backyards without the ley lines' involvement, I'd be willing to admit they have the faintest connection to herbalism…"

"This isn't a very good start," she said, looking toward Jace with a hint of arrogance. "If you have business with me, at least don't deceive me so obviously. Show me a little basic respect, both of you."

Sola stared at the troll and raised her right hand. She didn't chant—didn't even move her lips—she simply snapped her fingers, and the tree roots at the doorway behind them went mad, yanking the already-closed wooden door open with a shriek of straining wood.

A great mass of roots even surged to her feet eagerly, coiling and writhing like a pack of hounds crouching at their master's feet awaiting orders. What's more, as these roots approached Sola they visibly sprouted fresh buds.

Not until Sola lowered her hand did this supernatural surge of life finally subside.

Sylia's eyes clearly panicked. She glanced around her own dwelling as though the place no longer belonged to her. Only after confirming that the disturbance had died down did she stare in a daze at the high elf before her.

"Impossible. You're a true druid."

"I am not a druid. As I said, I am simply a botanist who loves the forest and nature, Lady Sylia." Sola lowered her head and said something in the troll tongue.

Sylia nodded slightly as well, hesitated for a long while, then said in Thalassian, "The honor of knowing you is mine as well, Lady Sola."

"You wouldn't come looking for me if you didn't have a problem big enough, Sylia," Jace said.

"Of course." Sylia pointed to a wooden stool nearby. "Please, sit, both of you. Make yourselves at home. We can take our time talking."

Jace cleared his throat uncomfortably and stole a glance at Sola beside him. Sola sat down and gave him a strange look—her eyes probably asking, "What exactly is your relationship with her?" Jace could only frown innocently and sit down in the wicker chair to one side.

The troll alchemist took a strangely shaped clay cup that looked like a little toad, ladled a bowl of hot broth, and held it up to the two of them. "Would you like to try some?"

"I'll pass," Jace waved his hand. Sola also shook her head.

"How was the effect of that last medicinal broth?" Sylia asked, sitting across from them, holding her steaming cup.

"Very good. It helped a great deal, Sylia," Jace nodded. "Except the material of the pouch that held it was a bit of a problem."

Sylia raised her eyes and stared at him. "So you brought a dwarf. But that's fine. I don't think an upright Stormwind mage like yourself would be working with Dark Iron dwarves, hmm? So there shouldn't be any major conflict."

She gave a laugh that sounded like a cough. Jace swallowed and said, "I came today to ask for more of that broth."

"I can be done. The price of materials has actually dropped a bit recently." Sylia held her fingers apart to indicate the size. "A bottle this big, ten silver. Depends on what you plan to use it for."

"I'm planning a trip to Atal'Hakkar," Jace said.

Sylia's clay cup had already reached her lips. At the sound of that name, she slowly set it down.

She glanced at Sola beside him, and her expression seemed to shift. Jace could feel a massive wave of suspicion rising in her heart, along with a trace of fear—but even harder to conceal was a surge of excitement welling up from deep within.

In the dim underground dwelling, the troll's emotional energy was nearly materializing into a cloud of shadowy mist. Even Jace, a warlock accustomed to dark things, found it uncomfortable. Sylia was indeed no fighter, a shaman and priestess who knew little of combat, but Jace could sense that everything she'd experienced in the Hinterlands would be enough to make Stormwind's finest warriors tremble.

Jace had known that any female troll who could escape the Amani's grip and flee to Booty Bay to scrape by, carrying rare herbs and treasures, couldn't possibly be as simple as she appeared on the surface.

"You seem quite familiar with the Atal'ai, Sesso," the troll said. "But I think a mere Spectral Detection Potion is far from enough to deal with the evil spirits within."

"Elaborate, Lady Sylia," Sola said.

"How detailed do you want to know?" Sylia's gaze jumped between the two of them. "You two are touching upon the most forbidden knowledge across all of Zul'Aman to Zul'Gurub. Show me what you can offer, and then I'll consider how much to tell you."

"How much money do you want?" Jace asked.

"Gold is indeed a good thing, but it can't buy everything," Sylia said, staring at him. Jace asked, "You don't want money—then what do you want?"

Sylia gave a mysterious smile. "Don't look at me with that expression, Jace Sesso. Though I do miss what you once gave me, I must admit that's not enough to make me betray my past faith."

Sola looked between the two of them, her expression growing more and more bewildered. Jace just nodded. "Say what's on your mind directly, Sylia."

Sylia gave a hmph-laugh. "I've heard things about you, Jace Sesso. I don't doubt you—or Lady Vereesa Windrunner's strength. Her family's name has echoed across the Amani Empire for hundreds of years. If you truly intend to enter Atal'Hakkar, I won't ask what your goal is, but I hope that if the chance arises, you'll do me a favor."

"Go ahead," Jace said.

"I want a skull of a Son of the Soulflayer," Sylia said.

"Son of the Soulflayer?" Sola asked. "What is that?"

Sylia looked at her and said, "The Sons of the Soulflayer are a group of extremely powerful wind serpents, though nowhere near as powerful as Hakkar himself, or his avatar that descends upon this world. And unlike their father-god, the Sons of the Soulflayer are not immortal. I believe you wouldn't even need to enter the temple—you could find many remains of the Sons of the Soulflayer just in the Black Morass. I once tried hiring people to search, but they only found bone fragments. It's simply far too dangerous there—roaming demons, leftover orcs… so very few people are willing to take the job."

"This request isn't unreasonable," Jace said. "I'll do my best."
