## Chapter 1025: Sacrifice

"Since you've agreed…" Sylia shifted her sitting posture and said, "You should know that although the Atal'ai were once an extremely large clan, the number of exiles driven out of the Gurubashi Empire was not great. And they lost more members in the process of searching for new ritual grounds and building a new temple. Summoning Hakkar requires real sacrifice. You need to sacrifice souls—real, quality souls—not just snatch a few crocodiles or great black cats from the swamp and cut their throats, deluding yourselves that this will satisfy the Soulflayer's needs."

"I participated in rituals of communion with the Soulflayer as well," she said, staring at Jace. "Hakris, high priest of the Witherbark tribe, was obsessed with sacrificing souls to the Soulflayer in order to commune and communicate with it. I've seen those souls. I've seen the Soulflayer's… greed. The Atal'ai are far more fanatical than Hakris. To win the Soulflayer's favor, they would only give more, never less."

"But the Atal'ai's numbers are nowhere near as large as the Witherbark tribe's," Jace said.

"True," Sylia said. "So they tried many rituals that even forest trolls would find horrifying, torturing the believers about to be sacrificed, making them experience death in unspeakable agony, then raising them again and again with necromantic magic to trap their souls, then killing them in even more painful ways, over and over—ultimately forging those souls trapped in shattered husks into abominations so maddened that even the Atal'ai priests were appalled. When quantity isn't enough, raise quality. Only this could satisfy the Soulflayer's appetite… at least that's what they believe."

"But why?" Sola asked. "Why are the Atal'ai so fanatical about summoning Hakkar into this world?"

"Why?" Sylia glared at her. "Haven't you heard the Atal'ai's prophecy? Once the Soulflayer descends upon this world, all Atal'ai will live forever, immortal…"

"Of course, not all the Atal'ai firmly believe in this prophecy. Back in the Hinterlands, I captured some Atal'ai fugitives who'd escaped from within. They knew the Gurubashi Empire would never take them back, so they chose to flee all the way to Zinsalo. These things I learned from their own mouths. If you want to doubt, go doubt them—even a boiled head in a pot can tell lies."

After saying that she gave a dry laugh, unclear whether she was mocking those pitiful Atal'ai or simply finding it amusing to scare the two of them with these voodoo tales.

At least Sola didn't find it amusing. She fell silent for a moment, brows tightly knit. The evil spirits Sylia described—so full of hatred and pain—were naturally far beyond ordinary ghosts. The situation inside that temple seemed far more terrifying and complex than she'd anticipated.

Jace was uneasy too. He was certainly worried about conditions inside the temple, but more importantly, Ulthalesh behind him seemed to be whispering already. To him, Sylia's description was nothing less than vividly painting a picture of an oasis just ahead to a man dying of thirst in the desert.

"But if these evil spirits are meant to be sacrificed to Hakkar…" Sola raised her head and said, "If those trolls are bent on summoning Hakkar, there shouldn't be too many ghosts wandering the temple, I'd think. I imagine—at the very least, they haven't yet succeeded in bringing Hakkar into the world."

"Logical," Sylia nodded. "But this is all old news. Before I left Zinsalo, the Atal'ai priests already had offerings far more powerful than the blood of believers and evil spirits to present to the Soulflayer. Those evil spirits then conveniently became the guardians of that underwater altar. A real pity…"

"What offering?" Sola asked, a premonition already forming in her heart.

In fact, Jace knew this answer too. He'd just been hoping that this was still more than a decade before the game's timeline, and that conditions inside the Temple of Atal'Hakkar hadn't yet deteriorated to that point of no return…

Of course, the moment Sylia brought it up, he understood that all the darkness and depravity within had happened far earlier than he'd imagined.

"Dragons," Sylia said.

"Impossible?" Sola nearly lost control. "Green dragons… could never become accomplices in summoning the Soulflayer. They went there precisely to stop the Atal'ai from summoning the Old God!"

"Your queen clearly underestimated the Atal'ai's power!" Sylia said, pointing at her. "You think a tribe that could survive the bloody Gurubashi civil war, build a massive temple, and struggle to survive a devastating flood… could be destroyed so easily?"

Had Sylia already guessed that Sola was a green dragon? How had she figured it out?

Of course, before Jace could even consider it, Sola had already stood up in fury.

"You were a believer in an Old God yourself, Sylia!" she said, stepping forward. "Of course you'd speak for them! Those trolls are nothing but fools driven mad by deception—they are utterly incapable of resisting the power of the green dragons! Right now they must still be trying, must still be attempting to stop…"

"Yes, yes, yes—this spirit, this courage, this arrogance," Sylia said, looking at her. "It is precisely these 'fine' qualities that buried your kin inside, while advancing Hakkar's return to this world by a great step. Carry on, Sola—or whatever your name is—beautiful and kind dragon of the Emerald Dream. Carry on, follow in the footsteps of your kin, and take these foolish friends who value your life as your own and keep helping the Atal'ai accomplish their great cause."

Sola staggered back a step, glanced at Jace beside her, and for a moment couldn't find words. "I… I apologize to you, Lady Sylia." She lowered her eyes, which were already glistening with tears.

Watching Sola like that, Jace actually felt a pang of guilt in his heart, because he'd long known that the green dragons inside the temple might already be in such a state—he just hadn't said it.

"Hold on, hold on." Sylia suddenly changed her expression. She pointed at Sola and said, "Don't move, dear."

Just as Sola was wondering what was happening, the shaman picked up a small cup and carefully caught a falling tear from Sola, staring wide-eyed. "A green dragon's tear. When you walked through that door, I never imagined I'd get my hands on this from you."

"For the sake of this tear, I'm willing to help you a little more," she said, staring at the cup in her hand. "If you know anything about the Soulflayer's lore, you'll know this is a deity highly skilled at controlling minds. And the Atal'ai—their followers are also highly skilled at this kind of dark magic. I, as a defector, happened to learn a few extra methods of resisting such mental magic, including the recipes for at least two medicinal broths."

The troll looked back at Jace. "I know that for you, Jace Sesso, mental magic is hardly a threat. But for this green dragon lady, it certainly isn't—not with so many precedents already set. One of them I happen to have the means to prepare here, but it will take at least two weeks to make. Consider yourselves hired helpers for now. I still want the skull. As pure as this green dragon's tear is, it can't replace the use of a Son of the Soulflayer's remains."

"Thank you for your help, Lady Sylia," Sola said softly.

"The thanks of a green dragon who knows humility and how to ask for help is worth more than this tear itself," the shaman said with a sigh. "Thank you, Sylia," Jace said, glancing at Sola. "I knew coming to you was the right call."

"I think you know a little too much, Jace Sesso," Sylia said with a smile.
