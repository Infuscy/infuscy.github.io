Chapter 1026: A Successful Sale

At night, Booty Bay was still raucous. From the docks below came the occasional shrill goblin laugh. After leaving the shaman Sylia's dwelling, Sola was silent the whole way, and Jace didn't know how to start comforting her either. Jace knew that the reason she'd clashed with Sylia like that wasn't arrogance, nor blind faith in the green dragon prince Eranikus—it was simply that she didn't want to believe things had reached a point of near-irreversibility, couldn't bring herself to believe that the green dragonflight had already become the Atal'ai's sacrifice. Near the cliff-side railing, Jace glanced at the sailing ship moored below and said, "If you'd like to be alone for a while, Sola, I can take the ship back. Just remember to get the vial of blood to Master Krasus as soon as possible." "No, Jace. We'll go back to Stormwind together," Sola said. Jace nodded. "Then let's set out tomorrow morning. Let's find somewhere to rest tonight—you've had a long day too." He looked down at the buildings below, then gazed toward the Sailor's Home inn, which from here was buried in a sea of lights, and thought for a moment. "The Sailor's Home is probably full. I'll ask over by the elves' side. The Reliquary has a station here—a big house with only one or two people living in it. They'll definitely have space to sleep for the night." Sola kept silent, not responding. After a while she said, "I didn't know about everything that happened in there, Jace. I… including Queen Ysera, and Ysondre—they don't know what exactly happened at Atal'Hakkar. I wasn't trying to trick you in there… to use you." Jace asked, "Why would you think I'd see it that way?" Sola said, "Sylia… she must think I was leading you deep into the swamp to risk your lives for me, to help my kin." As she spoke her voice grew quieter and quieter. Jace said, "All right, Sola, but Sylia meant well too. She only said those things because she was worried about us. She's a Witherbark priestess after all—don't expect her to say anything pleasant. But I could tell she didn't want you walking in there ignorant and getting yourself killed either." "Of course, she might not be doing it purely for your sake. But think about it—she betrayed the Witherbark tribe, betrayed her faith in those gods, and fled to Booty Bay to hide. If Hakkar truly descends upon this world one day, and the Atal'ai regain control of Zul'Gurub, and the Gurubashi Empire rises again on the back of Hakkar's power… it would definitely threaten Booty Bay, since this was troll territory long before the Gurubashi declined." "Think about what would happen to her then—a former priestess who abandoned her faith. Sola, I'd say she's probably one of the trolls who wants Hakkar's descent the least. And you, as a green dragon—if you were trapped inside like your kin, providing power for the summoning of an Old God—that would only accelerate the outcome she doesn't want to see all over again." When Jace mentioned those green dragons imprisoned as sacrifices, Sola's eyes trembled again. Jace couldn't help but press his lips together. "Sorry." "Actually, I wasn't supposed to concern myself with what happened there," Sola said. "But Prince Eranikus and more than half the green dragons in there—I know all their names. They watched me grow up… Jace, they are my fathers, my brothers, and my sisters." "Believe me, I understand that feeling," Jace said, looking at her. "But precisely because they watched you grow up, they'd want you even less to rush over there recklessly because you're emotionally worked up." The green dragon nodded. Jace said, "Wait two weeks. Wait for Sylia to finish making the Spectral Detection Potion and the broth that resists the Atal'ai priests' mental magic, and we'll set out together. And give Vereesa some time too—let her return to Quel'Thalas to get the Holy Wizard Oil. We need to prepare for the possibility of facing a legion of evil spirits… we must be as ready as we can possibly be." "I understand, Jace," Sola said. "I'm truly grateful to all of you for helping me like this. I really don't know what to say." "Don't say that." Jace leaned his arm on the railing. "A fellow like Grede definitely wouldn't thank me for saving his life—he'd blame me for getting in the way of his chance to show off. But that's the kind of crew we are, Sola. We're just that sort of people… never mind that they're your kin and family. Even if you were planning to buy a house in Stormwind and were short on money, and took a job clearing kobolds out of a mine from the Warrior's Guild or Goldshire, we'd help you without hesitation—of course, as long as you split the money properly afterward." Sola, who'd still had some moisture in her eyes, couldn't help but laugh at Jace's words. She said, "I seriously doubt Vereesa would be willing to do that. She definitely wouldn't want to chase kobolds into a mine for money." "A noble lady really is a different breed," Jace said helplessly. Seeing Sola's mood ease up a little, Jace felt relieved too. Of course, he'd actually been a little embarrassed to accept Sola's thanks. If Ulthalesh hadn't paid too great a price in souls during the head-on clash with Onyxia, leaving him no choice but to find ways to make up for it; if not for the Blood of the Chromatic Dragon forcing him to come to Booty Bay to ask around, giving him the chance to drop in on Sylia and learn about Hakkar—he might still have been completely in the dark about what exactly was going on inside the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, and who knows how much longer the promise he'd made Sola would have been delayed. Just then Sola said, "I'm actually a little curious about how Sylia saw through my disguise." "Has nothing to do with your disguise," Jace said. "Just remember who you're playing next time, Sola. Most high elves have very little interest in nature—even the Farstriders. Even if certain botanists do have that kind of intense feeling, they probably wouldn't go around telling everyone about it like it's something to be proud of. I don't know if I'm making myself clear…" "I rarely interact with high elves. That was an oversight on my part…" Sola said guiltily. Jace nodded. "And the one you're most familiar with is the sort who wouldn't be taken for an elf even if she returned to Quel'Thalas. Of course, Sylia probably sensed it too by now—after all, a high elf who commands nature magic and is also itching to get inside an Atal'ai temple to find out what's going on is awfully suspicious… don't forget she lived in the Hinterlands for a long time. She definitely counts as one of the mortals who knows green dragons fairly well." When the two arrived at the Reliquary's station, sure enough these high elves had no intention of resting even at night. In the main hall, an elf woman sat at a desk carefully recording the characteristics of a bluish-green gemstone. "Good evening, my lady," Jace called out. The elf woman glanced toward the door, narrowed her eyes, and said, "Am I seeing things? Is that Archmage Jace Sesso?" "It's me," Jace said. "You are…" "Gelrei of the Lightchasers. A pleasure to meet you, Archmage Sesso." The elf woman stood and came to the door. "Please, come in, both of you. This is…" "Sola Shade," Sola said. "Hello, Lady Sola. A pleasure to meet you." Gelrei smiled and nodded, then looked at Jace and asked, "I wonder if there's anything the Reliquary can help with, Archmage Sesso?" "The Sailor's Home has no vacancies, Lady Gelrei," Jace said. "We plan to head back to Stormwind tomorrow, so we were hoping to borrow two rooms at the Reliquary for the night. Would that be all right?" "Of course, Archmage. You're welcome here anytime," Gelrei said. "I also had the honor of attending Lady Vereesa Windrunner's Ranger-Lord investiture ceremony. If not for the Orc Wars, a scene like that might not come around for decades or even centuries. Truly unforgettable." "My Thalassian was still pretty poor back then," Jace said, a little embarrassed. "So even though I was there from start to finish, I still felt completely lost." "No one would mind, Archmage Sesso." Gelrei smiled. "Then let me show you to your rooms?" Jace glanced at some of the ornaments on the walls, including a few simple magical items and some artifacts scavenged from the Gurubashi ruins, and suddenly thought of something. "Is the Reliquary interested in certain rather special trophies?" "Trophies?" Gelrei asked. "Specifically what do you mean?" "I mean things that can hardly be called 'relics,'" Jace said. "Things unrelated to history, that might carry a touch of magic but aren't exactly magical items." "Souvenirs?" Gelrei said. "That's right, souvenirs," Jace nodded. "Could you be more specific, Archmage?" Gelrei asked. Jace glanced at Sola beside him; her face wore a curious expression too. "A head," Jace said frankly. "A… dragonkin head." Sola's eyes widened. Gelrei immediately asked in surprise, "Could it be the head of Blackwing Lord Reshrel?! The Reliquary heard about the incident at Moonbrook, Archmage Sesso. Is Reshrel's head in your possession?" "That's the one. So you're interested?" Jace said. "Interested, absolutely," Gelrei said at once. "Previously there were researchers from Quel'Thalas who came south, hoping we'd investigate the magical items related to that demon summoning incident… everything related to Reshrel we certainly can't let slip. However, we learned from a Wizard Sanctum mage that this dragon lord also had an artifact that helped him maintain his formidable power and vitality. Might I ask how much you know about that?" "Ah, that thing." Jace pondered for a moment. "That can't be discussed for now. I don't have… authorization. No authorization." Not without the authorization of Wildhammer Thane Grede Thunderfist, he thought to himself. "All right, I understand completely," Gelrei said. "But regarding that dragonkin lord's head, might I ask what sort of price you have in mind, Archmage?" "Two hundred gold." Jace blurted out the price he'd been planning to fleece Pokodiaz with. "I can't make that decision on my own," Gelrei said. "I'll write to headquarters tonight and have them send an examiner to Stormwind to meet with you, Archmage. How does that sound?" "That works," Jace said.
