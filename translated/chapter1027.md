## Chapter 1027: The Four of Us

Back in Stormwind, the hour wasn't late yet after Jace dropped Sola off.

Krasus wasn't in the Mage Quarter. Jace immediately headed to the Dwarf District to Grede's home with the box containing the vial of Blood of the Chromatic Dragon—this needed to get to the red dragon mage as soon as possible.

He should have been living with Grede all this time, after all. He didn't want the Wizard Sanctum or the Kirin Tor to know he was in Stormwind, so he'd been making do there.

Arriving at Grede's house and knocking brought no response. Jace was just about to throw his weight into pounding the door when it banged open. Grede rubbed his eyes and asked, "What's all the yelling for? Oh. You're back."

"Sleeping at this hour in the afternoon?" Jace asked. "This is for your own good," Grede said.

Jace glanced inside and saw no sign of Krasus. "What's it got to do with me?"

"Stay at peak physical condition so that the moment you need me, I can deploy at a hundred percent strength and keep you safe in top form," Grede said with a yawn.

"Sure, sure," Jace said. "Where's Master Krasus?"

"He said yesterday he wanted to stay at the church. Said it would let him help the more severely wounded recover at any time," Grede said. "Just a pretty excuse." Jace said. "You snoring definitely drove him away."

"If you came here asking for help, Jace Sesso, you'd better forget it," Grede said, looking up at him. "Because whatever you ask, I won't agree to it."

"Drinks tomorrow. I'm calling Vereesa too," Jace said.

Grede stared at him, twitching his beard, and bit out: "You bastard… noon or evening?"

"Evening." Jace pulled out the box in his hand. "Are you sure Krasus is staying at the church? I need to give this to him."

"Give it here." Grede held out his hand. "He hasn't moved his luggage out yet—he should be back sometime tomorrow. Who'd have thought a dragon would carry so many clothes to change into? Hmm? Is he even a real red dragon? Why can't he just magic up a fresh set of clothes like Sola?"

"Krasus has been with the Kirin Tor for centuries, Grede," Jace said, handing him the small case. "All those archmages couldn't tell he was a dragon—there must be a reason for that."

"Probably fooled himself," Grede said with a click of his tongue, fiddling with the case. "What is this?"

"Blood of the Chromatic Dragon," Jace said.

"You got it this fast?" Grede asked in surprise. "That red dragon was saying a few days ago he figured your trip to Booty Bay might be a waste."

"So it's extremely precious, Grede," Jace told him. "If you break it, selling yourself wouldn't be enough to cover the damages."

"Got it!" Grede said, grabbing the door handle. "See you tomorrow evening."

This trip to Booty Bay had left Jace, who hadn't fully recovered yet, somewhat worn out too. Letting Grede help deliver the blood to Krasus gave him a chance to get a proper night of sleep.

Time quickly came to the next evening. Winter had arrived, and The Pig and Whistle Inn was packed with customers coming in to warm themselves, making it even more lively.

Jace and Grede had already seated themselves, waiting. Listening to the drinkers nearby gossiping about nobles being arrested, Jace quietly asked the dwarf, "Any news on the queen?"

"I was waiting for you to tell me," Grede said. "By the way, I already gave that vial of blood to Krasus."

"Good."

Just as Jace was considering whether to order some food first, Vereesa appeared at the inn's entrance.

The two of them noticed the light-colored crocodile-skin boots on her feet right away. Grede's eyes widened. "Well, well, Lady Vereesa! Those boots don't look cheap. Did your sister buy them for you? Look like crocodile skin too…"

"Guess where the crocodile's from," Jace said.

"Hmm?" Grede looked at Jace. "Did you give them to her?" Vereesa sat down by the window, leaning against it, and nodded with a smug expression. "Where was I supposed to guess that from?" Grede snorted a laugh.

"Tyramek. Remember that place?" Jace asked.

"Some hunter actually managed to capture Lotgrim?!" Grede asked in surprise. "You were the one who told them he was in Maraudon?"

"That's right," Jace said.

"Pay up!" Grede slapped the table. "We agreed back then—we tell the Ironforge hunters' guild about this, and we split the money! Vereesa and Mosa don't want a share, so it's between the two of us. You don't think you can walk out of that cave alive on your own, do you, Jace Sesso!"

"Give the Life Gem back first," Jace said.

Grede stared at Jace for a moment, then his eyelids slackened and he sat back down. "I'll let you slide this once, given how hard up for cash you are."

Vereesa watched the commotion with amusement, and just then Sola quietly took a seat to one side.

"Ah, Sola." Grede turned his head. "So how'd you two manage to get that… that bottle of the stuff, hmm? I'd never believe this guy paid money for it. You didn't help those goblins find something, or kill someone, did you?"

"Nothing that dramatic, but it wasn't easy either," Jace said.

"What's wrong, Sola?" Vereesa seemed to notice something off about Sola. "I'm fine," Sola said softly. "Just… a little worried."

"Well, this is what we're here to talk about today, Wen." Jace leaned forward. "Atal'Hakkar… the situation in that temple is far more dire than we imagined. I asked that troll friend in Booty Bay this time. She said the green dragons inside have likely already been controlled by the Atal'ai."

"So there's no one in there to help us at all?" Vereesa asked.

Jace thought of the green dragon Itharios, who wanted to find adventurers to go deep into the temple and rescue Eranikus, and shook his head. "Hard to say for sure. She doesn't know the exact situation either—we'll have to see for ourselves. But conditions are much worse than expected, especially the evil spirits inside, which may be stronger than we thought. So, Wen…"

"I understand. I'll go back and ask the magisters for more magical items for dealing with the undead," Vereesa said after a moment. "But… is there enough time for me to go to Quel'Thalas and back? If all those green dragons are trapped, then every day we delay, they suffer more torment."

"No need to rush," Sola said. "Though I hate to say it, they've already been imprisoned in there for God knows how long. A week or two won't make a difference. If we're going, we must be prepared and proceed with caution."

She glanced at Jace as she spoke, and Jace nodded in agreement.

"What do you mean, 'if,'" Grede said. "There is no 'if'!"

"That's right, Sola," Vereesa said. "You're one of us. When your family's in trouble, we're not going to sit here and pretend nothing's happening."

"Perfect timing—we're all here." Jace lowered his voice. "I have to be frank about something. Regarding this trip of ours, I actually have my own selfish motives. To defeat Onyxia I used up all my soul shards and a considerable portion of my staff's power. The insane souls inside that temple, offered up to the Old God, happen to be exactly what I can use—otherwise I wouldn't even have enough casting materials to bring that lord back… after all, we'll still need him later."

Vereesa nodded. Grede said, "You really didn't need to lay it all out so clearly…"

"Since we're already preparing to go there, I don't want to hide anything anymore," Jace said.

"I have selfish motives too," Vereesa said, flicking one long eyebrow and glancing sideways at Sola. "If we really do rescue your family, remember to get them to make me a proper bow, using that unparalleled material. That way this trip won't have been for nothing."

"Absolutely," Sola said. "I'd ask even if you didn't."

"I have selfish motives too," Grede nodded, tapping his fingers on the table and muttering, "Uh… I just had it a moment ago. Ah, right—the warhammer Kadros left me didn't do a damn thing at Onyxia's place. The time to prove its power has arrived, Sola. I'm not about to miss this chance to punish evil. This Jace Sesso, even that little imp of his, they've been mocking me about it all along—the nerve! Don't you want to give me a chance to shut them up?"

Hearing everyone say this, Sola's eyes were already a little red. Jace cleared his throat. "Ah, let's order some food, everyone. I'm starving."

"Same here, and we came here to drink in the first place…" Grede grumbled.

Vereesa rested her chin in her hand, looking at the three people beside her, let out a soft sigh, and couldn't help but smile.
