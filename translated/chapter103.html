Chapter 103: The Value of a Curse of Weakness

In the evening, Jace arrived at Sulenna Kerdon's door. He listened for a moment, confirmed there were no strange sounds coming from inside, and knocked.

"Who's out there?" It was Sulenna's voice.

"Jace Sesso. Remember me?"

Bang!

The door was flung open so hard Jace nearly took it to the face.

Sulenna glared at him, forcing out a single sentence: "You little bastard!"

Jace said, "I didn't come here to fight with you, Sulenna. Where does Jagin live?"

Sulenna's face twisted with anger. "That day you came to my place and humiliated Jagin — after that he took it out on me every single day! This is all your fault, and I have nothing to say to you."

"What's that got to do with me?" Jace said, completely baffled. "I can't be bothered with your stupid drama. I'm looking for Jagin. This concerns the interests of our whole group, so you'd better answer me straight. Otherwise, if you delay us from learning new magic, next time I see Jagin I'll tell him you blocked my way. You know how obsessed Jagin is with magic — he already hits you when he's humiliated, so imagine what he'll do when he finds out you kept him from learning new spells."

"You arrogant little brat." Sulenna raised her hand like she was about to slap him — but Jace caught her wrist.

"Tell me where Jagin lives! It's a simple question. How long are you going to keep this up?"

Caught and held, Sulenna froze for a moment. She could feel that Jace's strength was nothing a warlock woman who'd barely trained her body could match. Her defiance crumbled on the spot.

"He… he lives two streets over, opposite the fur shop. The house with the black tiles. First door down the ground-floor hallway."

Jace flung her hand away and jogged down the stairs.

Following the address Sulenna had given him, Jace arrived at the black-tiled house. A light was on on the ground floor. Passing by the window, he heard two men arguing inside — one of them was Jagin.

"I don't understand. You don't trust me?"

"It's not that I don't trust you," Jagin's voice said. "The main problem right now is that your grasp of incantations is limited, Erland. You can't rush this. You need patience…"

The other man was Erland? Why was Erland at Jagin's house this late? Honestly, Jace had never heard Erland sound this serious before, so he hadn't immediately placed the voice.

Erland's voice said angrily, "It's the incantations you teach that have problems, Jagin."

"So where exactly is the problem, Erland? I don't understand why you're always doubting this, doubting that." Jagin's voice was deeply weary, barely holding back his frustration.

Jace walked up the stairs, into the hallway, following the sound toward the door of the room where the two were.

Erland pressed on aggressively: "You're deceiving us. Deceiving this whole group into working for you — Ursula, me, Sulenna, even that new kid. You think we're all your little lackeys?"

"We're friends…" Jagin said.

Erland cut him off roughly: "Save the pretty talk. You and Galders use us to scrounge up everything related to magic — money, supplies — and then feed us fake incantations to keep us happy. You think I don't know? Sulenna practiced one Curse of Agony for months and the result was pathetic. Honestly, a random knife stab would do better. What kind of magic is that? You think I've never seen real magic? I'm a brotherhood man, not some random farm boy or bricklayer you picked up off the street. Do you look down on me? Think I'm an idiot you can just string along?"

Jagin's voice was ice cold: "If you want to learn real magic, the Wizard Sanctum is right next door."

"You know damn well how the Wizard Sanctum recruits students. You having fun messing with me?" This time Erland was truly furious.

"I never—"

A chaotic crash came from inside. Jagin's words were swallowed mid-sentence.

"Let go!" Jace heard a muffled sound, followed by a clatter of things being knocked over. Jagin had probably been hit.

He knocked on the door.

"Who is it?" Jagin asked.

"Jace. Jace Sesso."

"Ah… Jace, come in. Just push."

Jace pushed the door open and saw Jagin picking himself up off the floor, Erland glaring furiously from across the room.

"Erland. You're here too."

"Jace." Erland nodded, coughed, straightened himself, and brushed off his shirt.

Jagin touched his lip, confirmed he wasn't bleeding, and sat down heavily. "What brings you here?"

"When's our next gathering, Jagin?" Jace asked.

"What's today?"

"The fifteenth."

"This Sunday, then. The twentieth." Jagin said. "We're holding a gathering this Sunday. Everyone needs to be there. I've already notified Ursula Dellin and Galders, and now the gentleman Erland knows too." He glanced at Erland. "You can notify Sulenna for me."

Erland said nothing. He looked at Jace, then at Jagin, then fixed his gaze back on Jace. "You heard us talking outside, didn't you? You came in here to save this liar."

"Erland, don't drag him into this," Jagin said wearily. "What will it take for you to believe me? The more impatient you get, the less you'll be able to master magic."

Erland didn't acknowledge Jagin's words at all. He stared at Jace and said, "Has this bastard taught you any incantations? He waves the banner of sharing and mutual benefit, teaches you completely useless incantations, then has you hand over your money, your treasures, everything you fought tooth and nail to take from gnolls or bandits — telling you the incantations will work if you just practice enough. Am I right?"

"He taught me some summoning thing, and… a Curse of Agony. Not a single one works. No matter how many times I practice, nothing happens. And he told me dark magic accepts anyone willing to walk the path of dark arts. Am I not a person?"

"If you'd mastered any incantations," Jagin said, "you wouldn't have to risk your neck scrounging for those things, Erland. Our group needs to survive, so we need a source of income. Just like Ursula can brew potions for us, and Galders can use his talent to organize our grimoire — plus he comes from a wealthy family and can contribute dues. It's the same for you. Even though you struggle with magic, you have your own connections, your own strength. You can contribute something too…"

"Fuck you." Erland pointed at Jagin's nose. "Then tell me — what has Sulenna contributed?"

Jagin was speechless for a moment. Erland watched his expression and grew more and more smug.

He gave a cold laugh and answered his own question: "Forget it. We both know."

Jace thought that if it were him, he wouldn't have been able to take that.

But Jagin took it. He wiped the blood from his lip and said, "You should go home."

"You really do think I'm an idiot," Erland said. "Two gold. Hand it over and I'm out. We'll have no further dealings, and I won't come looking for you again. Deal?"

"I don't have that kind of money, Erland. Be a little more patient," Jagin said. "I'll teach you properly and help you find the right path to mastering shadow magic. You need to trust me."

"Trust, my ass." Erland grabbed Jagin by the collar and hauled him out of his chair. "I think I've been plenty patient, Jagin."

"Erland." Jace spoke up suddenly. "Calm down. Don't—"

Erland gave a contemptuous laugh. "What, are you his love child or something? What did he give you that you're defending him like this?"

He threw Jagin back into the chair and turned this way, clenching his fists. His knuckles cracked.

"This isn't my problem," Jace said. "I'm just trying to keep you two from making this worse—" Erland spat and stalked over, his mouth twisted with rage.

Jace knew he was about to be the punching bag.

"Alena…"

He'd barely gotten the incantation started when Erland reacted with shocking speed, shoving Jace's foot and slamming him into the wall!

"What incantation is that? Did he teach you that?!"

Jace didn't answer. He drove his fist hard into Erland's arm — a brutal blow that made even Erland cry out in pain, caught completely off guard.

The moment Erland's grip loosened, Jace rattled off the rest of the Curse of Weakness at breakneck speed!

Erland tried to hit back, but suddenly his brow furrowed. His legs gave out and he stumbled backward, barely catching himself on the bookcase before he hit the floor.

He stared at his own feet in disbelief, then at Jagin — but Jagin's expression had frozen solid too.

Jace could feel Jagin's shock. Could feel him forcibly suppressing his reaction and keeping it off his face.

"What kind of coward's spell is this?" Erland grunted, trying to push himself up, but slowly his arms went limp too. He managed to stand again, barely, and could hardly walk.

"What did you do to me…" He forced the words out through gritted teeth, then swung a feeble fist at Jace. Jace simply stepped aside and let Erland crash to the floor.

The strapping Erland lay on the ground groaning, trying several times to push himself up on his arms before finally bracing against the wall to haul himself upright.

"Give us a little more time, Erland," Jagin said, looking down. "If there's still no progress in the end, give me some time to save up compensation for you. It might not be as much as two gold, but I haven't forgotten everything you've contributed to this group for as long as we've been together."

Erland nodded, his face waxen. Not long after, he leaned out the window and vomited, dry-heaving for a long time before turning back.

He shot a glance at Jace, then at Jagin, muttered something under his breath, and pushed out the door.

"You didn't come here just to ask about the gathering time, Jace. This incantation — what do you want for it?" Jagin asked quietly.

Jace said, "First tell me what you've got."
