## Chapter 1030: Testing the Replica

On the road back to Stormwind, walking through the woods, Amy seemed to have Jace's conversation with the water elemental on her mind the whole way.

As they walked she finally said, "Sorry, Jace, I know there are probably things you don't intend to tell me, but Ragnaros…"

Jace explained, "King Varian wants to persuade the entire Alliance to attack Blackrock Spire together, but at present Blackrock Spire and Shadowforge City are keeping each other in check."

Amy nodded. "So if the Alliance defeats Blackrock Spire and the black dragons, the Dark Iron dwarves will definitely turn on us again the moment they regain their strength. And this time, without Blackrock Spire to restrain them, they might be even more rampant than before the Dark Portal."

"As King Varian's arcane advisor, I must do everything in my power to find any ally who can be of help," Jace said, looking at the female mage. "I need to find a way to deal with the Dark Iron dwarves at the same time, Amy—otherwise Stormwind will be the first to suffer. Before the Dark Portal, Lakeshire's biggest headache was the Dark Iron dwarves. And you know that when trouble hits here, over at Dalaran…"

"I was born in Stormwind, Jace," Amy said, giving him a sidelong glance. "And yet you're from Lordaeron. You've completely got it backwards."

"Sorry," Jace said with an embarrassed laugh.

Amy sighed. "A lot of people in Dalaran occasionally make fun of my Stormwind accent, so I rarely leave the Kirin Tor academies and libraries to go outside. The saddest thing about coming back to Stormwind is that you all treat me as a northerner again. Reminds me of when you first arrived at the Wizard Sanctum—including me and my father, plenty of people still thought you were from the north and treated you like a foreigner."

"There's no helping it, Amy. You just get used to it little by little," Jace said.

In fact, Jace had indeed realized that when talking with Amy, the Kirin Tor label on her was becoming more and more important.

When he'd first run into Amy in Stormwind, he'd even considered asking Amy to come along to the Temple of Atal'Hakkar. After all, that temple was flooded by the Pool of Tears, with standing water in many places and the lower levels even largely submerged.

In that kind of environment, a mage skilled at manipulating water elements would have enormous room to shine.

Especially remembering how, at the Dark Portal, Amy had calmly used all sorts of frost spells against those demons, from succubi to shivarras…

…she must have been far more confident and powerful than she was back then.

But for now, there was absolutely no way to let the Kirin Tor know about Ulthalesh and the Doomlord, and Amy didn't even know he'd been working with a succubus all along.

Thinking about how he now couldn't place Amy in the position of a loved one, but instead treated her like a Kirin Tor agent, Jace felt a chill in his heart.

After a silence like this, Amy also lifted her head slightly, gazing at the somewhat sparse treetops above, lost in thought about something.

By the time they returned to Stormwind it was nearly evening. The two arrived at the fountain in the Trade District square. Amy said, "In another two or three days I'll be heading back to Dalaran, Jace. Don't know when we'll meet again."

Jace said, "When things slow down in a bit, I'll go to Lordaeron to bring my parents to Stormwind. When that time comes I'll come find you, before summer."

Amy's expression relaxed quite a bit. She smiled. "Hearing that, leaving Elwynn doesn't feel so hard anymore, Jace. I'll cook a good meal and wait for you."

"Now you mention it, I want to jump straight to summer," Jace said. "Then I'll head off," Amy said. "You still need to go to the Cathedral. Hurry." Jace nodded. "Well then, see you next time."

He glanced at the female mage's departing back, steadied his breathing, and headed north.

Arriving at the Cathedral, it wasn't dark yet. By now the Cathedral wasn't as crowded as it had been a few weeks ago. Most of the wounded still here were soldiers who'd been nearest the palace when Onyxia revealed her true form.

Entering the main hall, Jace saw Krasus using his middle-aged balding-man guise, explaining patient care to the wife of an injured soldier.

Jace waited off to the side for their conversation to end. Krasus seemed to notice the extra person looking over from here. "Krasus." The red dragon mage waved. "I've been waiting for you. How's the recovery?"

"Almost there, thanks to the Dragonqueen's blessing," Jace said.

"Certainly more powerful than my medical skills by who knows how many times," Krasus said with a smile. "But these past few days I haven't been idle trying to replicate that spell either. I've finished replicating the tablet. Do you have time tonight? Come test it with me."

Jace hesitated. "I thought you said that once this spell was cast…"

"The whole point of this spell's power is that it doesn't waste its force—it only strikes a designated target. You forget?" Krasus took out a heavy cloth bundle from his bag and said in a low voice. "But you and I have both experienced activating it without finding a precise target. You can assist me in trying to sense the difference from a shadow magic perspective."

Jace took the cloth bundle and opened it, finding an enchanted metal plate inside. With Jace's geography and mining knowledge, he couldn't quite identify what it was.

But the densely packed characters on it were very familiar—they were exactly the ancient Draconic characters that the alchemist had inscribed on those broken tablets.

Jace could barely tell any difference. Krasus must have replicated it one-to-one with magic.

"When I first saw this thing at Grim Batol, I didn't realize how difficult it was to inscribe these runes," Krasus said, touching the back of his neck. "Perhaps my injuries were simply too severe. The biggest problem with staying in mortal form all this time is that it's very hard for me to accurately sense the extent of my injuries. Perhaps I should find an opportunity to return to my true form and see exactly what Onyxia left me with."

Jace nodded. "If you need any medicines or the like… Master, I can try to help."

"It's not that I don't trust the skill of mortal alchemists, Jace," Krasus said with a smile. "I'm not a whelp. Ordinary medicines simply have no effect on me. But then again, that's a good thing in its own way, isn't it? Or would you rather see Onyxia, who'd already been beaten to the brink of death, recover perfectly just from drinking a potion brewed from Living Root and Kingsblood?"

"Of course I wouldn't," Jace said, unable to help but laugh.

"Speaking of Onyxia." Krasus's smile faded. "I've already arranged for two sufficiently cautious adult red dragons to head to that swamp in Kalimor you mentioned before. I've ordered them not to engage any black dragons or dragonkin—just monitor the movements of the black dragon lair. After the Orc Wars and Grim Batol, there's no way we can launch a war against Nefarian and Blackrock Spire right now—especially after the fall of Prince Tyranastrasz… otherwise things wouldn't be this difficult."

"I understand, Master Krasus," Jace said. "You helping treat the wounded of Stormwind is already a great help. I'm sure His Majesty the King must be very grateful."

"I just hope to make up for my mistakes and salvage what I can," Krasus said, gazing deep into the Cathedral. "Never mind. Let's not talk about that for now. Come with me."

The archmage said a brief farewell to a nearby priest, then left the Cathedral with Jace, heading toward a house to the side of the square. Walking across the square, Jace asked, "Why aren't you staying with Grede anymore?"

"Well, the Dwarf District is still quite a ways from here. A priest helped me find new lodgings, and I didn't want to keep disturbing Grede Thunderfist's rest," Krasus said.

"He misses you quite a bit," Jace said.

"Does he?" Krasus smiled.

The archmage's new lodging was a fairly spacious room, tucked in an inconspicuous corner of the square—a good place to test magic without attracting attention.

"Try activating this tablet using the same method you did at Mirror Lake earlier," Krasus said after closing the door. "I'll make sure that if anything goes wrong, the effects won't spread beyond this room."

"Good," Jace said. He lifted the tablet and began to chant.

The enchanted metal plate was indeed more stable than Malorak's tablet before. Jace clearly felt the power of the Blood of the Chromatic Dragon awakened by the runes, then blocked by another sequence of runes and laid to rest.

With no black dragons nearby and no black dragon magic either, the spell naturally did not activate—but the overall feel of casting it was indeed not much different from his previous experience facing Lady Katrana Prestor.

"How is it?" Krasus asked.

"I think there's no problem," Jace said. "But ultimately we'll still need to find a black dragon to test it properly."

"But we only have one vial of the Blood of the Chromatic Dragon," Krasus said, taking out the empty bottle. "How did those goblins get this vial of blood? Vaelastrasz has been lurking in Blackrock Spire all this time… and he couldn't even get his hands on a single drop, not to mention he never even came into contact with a chromatic whelp's corpse."

"Those goblins can do things for money that exceed your and my imagination," Jace said. "And now that Deathwing is dead, I imagine the goblin technicians in Blackrock Spire might get a bit bolder—whereas the goblin merchants in Booty Bay all talk about black dragons like they're terrified out of their wits."

"Truly unimaginable boldness," Krasus said, looking over. "Jace, there are goblins trying to infiltrate Grim Batol to get close to Deathwing's corpse. This is what the whelts I sent to Kalimor told me. Some mercenaries, hired by goblins, broke into Grim Batol without authorization. Of course, they were burned by the whelts patrolling nearby before they could get close. But I think even if they could get near Deathwing, they'd be corrupted by the dark power still clinging to that corpse into a pile of raving rotten meat."

"What do they want?" Jace asked. "Something from Deathwing? Scales? Teeth?"

"The Demon Soul," Krasus said. "Many people know Deathwing is dead, but don't believe the Demon Soul was destroyed by his own hand. Quite a number of those who know of its existence are still scheming over that thing. The most troubling part is that the people who understand the Demon Soul tend to possess some ability to make trouble. The Queen is still slumbering, so after finishing this tablet I'll be heading back to Grim Batol."

"Though I don't know how much help I can be in my current state."

Jace nodded. "Next I need to bring the dragonkin tablet back to Shadowforge City, Master. So when you leave, I might not be in Stormwind."

"No need for all that trouble," Krasus said with a smile, clapping Jace on the shoulder. "Have a safe journey, Jace. Watch out for orcs."

"I will, Master," Jace said.
