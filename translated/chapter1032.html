Chapter 1032: Matthias Shaw's Order

In the Wizard Sanctum's code of conduct for mages, nearly all spells connected to the Twisting Nether were forbidden — and the Kirin Tor was much the same in that regard. This had nothing to do with demons or shadow magic per se; it was the inherent unpredictability of the Twisting Nether itself. For warlocks, summoning demons from the Twisting Nether was one thing — entering it themselves was something else entirely. Galders, it was plain to see, was caught in a tangled mix of excitement and worry. On one hand, he was eager to see whether the doomguard Jace summoned would be something extraordinary, reaching the level of the legendary demon that had aided Kel'Thuzad. On the other, he had no idea whether this attempt would turn out to be a blessing or a disaster. Of course, Galders had no idea that Jace had already secretly joined a circle of warlocks to summon a doom lord. A doomguard's ability to manipulate dimensional rifts was to a doom lord's what Blink was to a proper portal. If Galders ever found out about this, he'd probably launch into a painsthed, earnest plea for Jace to find a way to summon Kallrez back this very moment. Arriving at the alchemy lab, Ursula was weighing ingredients. When she heard Jace's voice, she glanced over and said, "Were you talking with Galders in the hall just now? What about?" "About our future new hideout. He didn't tell you?" Jace asked. "You mean the Twisting Nether," Ursula said, keeping her head down. "I don't like that idea, Jace. I'd much rather stay in Stormwind. We're working with SI:7 now — there's no need to worry about this place being exposed to the kingdom or the Wizard Sanctum… Isn't that already good enough? Ordinary folks won't go poking around to find out whether what's hidden under their favorite tavern is wine or demons." "Galders hasn't made up his mind either," Jace said, sitting down nearby. "More to the point, we don't actually have the ability to make that decision right now. Still, I think it's worth trying." "Oh, for—" Ursula set down what she was holding and looked at Jace. "You're not actually going along with Galders, are you? If you agree, and Sulenna and Erland don't object, doesn't that mean the matter's as good as settled?" She poured a cup of tea from the table and handed it to Jace, saying, "Galene is definitely looking forward to the day she can enter the Twisting Nether. She's said more than once that some of the orcs' robes have to be crafted near void rifts. If the world's most powerful dark-magic robe needed to become a demon to make, I believe she'd demonize herself without a moment's hesitation!" "There's something I didn't tell Galders just now," Jace said, holding the hot tea and taking a sip. "Entering the Twisting Nether to study shadow magic isn't entirely a bad thing." "It's not entirely bad, of course — it makes it easier to channel shadow, you can learn spells faster, second—" Ursula began counting off on her fingers, clearly a pitch she'd already heard from Galders. "More importantly, it could keep us away from some things on Azeroth that aren't quite right," Jace cut in. "What do you mean?" Ursula paused. Jace stepped out for a look, confirming that Galders had left down the corridor, then came back and said, "You know the incantation for Curse of Weakness isn't in demonic, Ursula? It was the first spell I taught you — you should remember it pretty clearly." "I…" Ursula sat to one side and quietly recited the Curse of Weakness incantation, frowning. "It really isn't demonic. I never noticed before." "Because it's not just demons who manipulate shadow energy on Azeroth — you could even say demons aren't the most skilled shadow manipulators. I learned Curse of Weakness from a naga," Jace said. "And I'm not the only one who's encountered spells from another world. Galders came into contact with something special from Kalimdor back in Ironforge — something that can strengthen a person's shadow affinity to an… inconceivable degree. But the cost is that you gradually become a puppet of whatever existence lies behind it." Ursula looked a little dazed. Jace continued, "If we can settle in the Twisting Nether, we can somewhat isolate ourselves from the influence of these special things on Azeroth and study shadow magic — or demon magic, fel — in a relatively 'safe' way. The cost, of course, is facing the volatile and unstable energies of the Nether. There's just no such thing as a perfect choice." "Galders never mentioned to us what that special thing was," Ursula said. "Some people call it a wind stone — a dark brown, purple-tinged thing that looks like a gem," Jace said. "Galders didn't tell you because he was probably worried you'd fret that he'd already come under its mental influence." "I heard my father tell some… legends about the Tirisfal," Ursula said softly. "Rumor has it that buried deep beneath the Tirisfal Glades is an evil thing that has existed since ancient times, whose whispers can drive people mad. That's why, thousands of years ago, the elves would rather fight the trolls for Quel'Thalas than settle in the desolate Tirisfal. My father said he moved to Ambermill for that very reason — he didn't want to be influenced by those 'things.' He also said that everyone living in the Tirisfal Glades was a hopeless fool… Back then, I thought he just hated the people who looked down on his bloodline." Jace hadn't expected that Ursula's father would actually know about the Old God minion sealed beneath the Tirisfal Glades. Then again, given his half-elf blood, it wasn't surprising that he'd heard some of the terrifying ancient high-elf legends about the Tirisfal. "It's very likely connected," Jace said, leaning back in his chair. "But in the past, these things were nothing more than far-fetched legends. It was only after I actually saw that wind stone and felt its power that this turned from legend into a reality I had to face. I imagine Galders is so eager to move into the Twisting Nether partly because he's worried about this sort of thing." "Fine." Ursula spoke like someone conceding. "I'll think it over properly. Truth be told, I don't really have much of a choice, do I? I just hope the shadow energy flooding the Twisting Nether doesn't interfere with my brewing — especially the ingredients that don't play nicely with shadow." "Oh, right." Jace pulled out a pouch of gold coins. "The money I owed you, Ursula." Ursula took the pouch, untied it, and counted. "At least today isn't all bad news." Jace said, "I heard Galders got some rare ingredients from Ironforge. Does that mean those powerful elixirs might get a little cheaper?" "You think Galders got what?" Ursula shook her head. "Not Black Lotus, Jace, so the price is the price. Actually, the price I give my own people is already much cheaper than selling it outside — and what I sell you is even cheaper!" "Fine." Jace sighed. "I've got another request this time, Ursula. I'm hoping you can help me make some anti-venom medicine." "That's no problem," Ursula said. "Anti-venom is even simpler than Fire Protection or Shadow Protection. What venomous creature are you planning to deal with? You're not going after the spiders in Duskwood, are I? I heard yesterday that Larson, the Wizard Sanctum's robe supplier, is short on spider silk again. You didn't take a job from him, did you?" "Green dragons," Jace said. The alchemist's expression froze on her face. After a long moment she said, "The one you and Sulenna ran into in the swamp last time?" "If it's still there, then probably," Jace said. "So I need the best anti-venom you can make." "Fine." Ursula hissed through clenched teeth. "I'm not sure either — I can only do my best…" Then, as if suddenly remembering something, she said, "Oh, right — if you're going after a green dragon, could you get me some green dragon blood?" "What do you want green dragon blood for?" Jace asked. "It's related to Matthias Shaw's request." The alchemist stood up as she spoke, hauled a large jar out of the storage room, and set it on the table. Inside was soaked a mass of dark green, rotting flesh. Jace leaned in to look. "What… what is this stuff? Don't tell me you're branching out into being a necromancer?" "What nonsense are you spouting?" Ursula swatted away Jace's reaching hand. "This is the 'ingredient' Galders brought me from Ironforge. Rare — it really is rare. This is sea giant tumor tissue. Those dwarven hunters in Ironforge can get their hands on anything." "What what?" This time it was Jace's turn to be shocked. Ursula nodded. "Sea giants are real monsters. They say each one stands two or three of you tall, with dark blue or dark green skin, draped head to toe in clothing — or armor — made of seaweed and shells." Of course, what surprised Jace wasn't whether sea giants existed, but that those dwarven hunters had actually managed to kill one. In fact, sea giants had been prowling the coast they'd passed when leaving Maraudon — it was just that, whether by luck or by misfortune, they hadn't run into any. If this sea giant tumor had also been "casually" acquired by that chest-piercing deer-hunter guild along the way during their hunt for the Crocolisk God, then their nerve to hole up in their room and try to stiff him on ten gold pieces of information fees might actually be somewhat justified. "What does Matthias want this for?" Jace asked. Ursula said, "Earlier, he wanted us to provide SI:7 with a potion that makes people tell the truth. I combed through every book I could find and finally tracked one down in a Dalaran alchemy recipe. But that recipe reads like a joke — sea giant tumor is one of the ingredients." "At first I thought it needed a sea giant with a specific tumor disease, you know — those arcane and alchemical recipes that deliberately call for materials that are impossible to obtain, just so the whole thing can't be debunked too quickly, like a murloc's fourth finger or something. But later I learned from an alchemist that nearly every sea giant grows that kind of tumor. So I got Galders to commission it… and then there's black panther heart, swamp beast fungus — those are comparatively nothing." "Even more outlandish than sea giant tumor is green dragon blood. I thought I could get it through the Kirin Tor, but the friend I sent to Dalaran to inquire came back with a flea in her ear. They seem to have green dragon scales, and even those — no one knows if they're real." "If he wants people to make people talk, why not just have you bring a succubus to handle it?" Jace asked. Ursula shot back, "If I wanted you to tell the truth, could a succubus handle it?" Jace thought about it. "Well, then I'm not drinking some mysterious potion you hand me either." Ursula glanced at the cup of tea Jace had drunk half of on the side table. "You drank that one well enough." Jace pursed his lips, sniffed the tea flavor in his mouth, and said, "So you're expecting that little cup to simultaneously contain brewed sea giant tumor, black panther heart, and green dragon blood, and I wouldn't even be able to smell it?" "Then did you smell the Netherbloom in it?" Ursula asked again. "Uh, I…" Jace wiggled his nose. "Fine. Damn it… you've just destroyed my trust in this alchemy lab." The alchemist stood up. "If I want to know something from you, I'll ask you directly, Jace — and trust your answer unconditionally. I don't need to use any potion on you." Seeing her suddenly turn serious, Jace dropped his joking manner too. "How much blood do you need, and what's the price?" Ursula pulled out a large bottle used for storing Restorative Potions from a lower cabinet. "If you can fill this, I'll trade you two Flasks of Refined Wisdom." "Advance me one," Jace said. "I can't guarantee I'll actually get you green dragon blood. If it doesn't work out in the end, I'll pay you back." "Oh, well, fine." Ursula leaned against the nearby table. "But I don't have a Refined Wisdom flask right now, Jace — I'd have to brew it fresh." "How long do you need?" Jace asked. "At least… five days," Ursula said. "I'll come back in ten days. We'll pick up the anti-venom potions at the same time — how's that sound?" Jace asked. Ursula calculated for a moment. "That should about do it. Then it's settled."
