Chapter 1034: The Inside Man

By the time they returned to Blackrock Mountain, everything was completely different from the first time they'd come here.

The orcs sleeping beneath Blackrock Mountain had been more or less wiped out. There were no more pots and pans scattered everywhere, no moreubiquitous hide tents and trash. Orc patrol soldiers marched in formation across the bridge, each squad accompanied by a few orc warlocks and felhounds. Beyond that, virtually every orc entering or leaving the main gate on their own was subject to inspection.

Passing near those felhounds, Jace couldn't help but feel his heart tighten. Was this because of Onyxia's failure in Stormwind?

Could Nefarian be worried that, having defeated Onyxia, Stormwind would immediately send troops against Blackrock Spire? Was he forced to put the entire Blackrock Mountain clan on high alert? If so, he was vastly overestimating Stormwind's military strength and the Alliance's cohesion.

Or had he learned that someone had infiltrated Blackrock Spire disguised as an orc? Was he tightening the patrols specifically to ferret himself or Sola out?

Of course, Jace had no idea what Nefarian was actually thinking. All he knew was that getting into Shadowforge City this time was probably going to be no simple matter.

Jace swept his gaze across the open ground in front of the Blackrock gate, where quite a few orc soldiers stood, using Detect Demon to make sure there were no invisible succubi or other stealthy demons about. Then he reminded Morophil, who was lying in ambush nearby, through the Soul Link to be ready for a fight at any moment.

"Hey, Sola," he said softly to the female orc standing beside him, staring at the gate guards. "If things go south later, the moment I give the word you shift back to your true form and fly away with everything you've got, understand? I'll climb onto your back as quickly as I can."

He looked up, observing the young dragons circling overhead from time to time, and said, "At your speed, I'm confident the black dragons here can't catch you."

Sola nodded. Jace glanced at her. "Relax a little." Of course, that was easy enough to say, but he couldn't relax either, not really.

The two of them followed a group of Shattered Hand soldiers up to the gate. The lead officer, clad in a suit of heavy armor, underwent inspection, said something to the guards, and led the soldiers behind him in.

Jace had been hoping to try slipping in with this group of Shattered Hand soldiers, much like the other warlocks trailing behind the patrols, but a furious shout stopped him in his tracks.

An orc guard shoved Jace's shoulder and grabbed hold of his cloak. "Hey, you in the robe — don't you know you're supposed to be inspected?"

"Let go." Jace stared into his eyes.

Seemingly unsettled by a warlock staring him down like that, the guard glanced rather unnaturally at the several attendants beside him and released his cloak.

"Salute this officer, scout," Jace said to Sola beside him.

"At your orders, officer." Sola thumped her chest with her fist.

Seeing this warlock's bodyguard show such deference to him, the orc guard's expression seemed to soften a bit. He gave Sola a simple nod.

"It wasn't this strict the last time I was here," Jace said.

"When was your last visit, warlock?" the orc asked.

Jace thought about it. "About two months ago, I'd say."

"All right," the orc said. "The order was issued two weeks ago."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"That's Warchief Rend's order. I'm not in a position to question it." The orc officer beckoned to an orc guard beside him, who handed over a leather scroll.

The officer unrolled it. "State your name, warlock."

Jace didn't know any specific warlock names from Blackrock Spire. Even if he did, he couldn't risk impersonating a famous name — it would be seen through in no time.

Nor could he hesitate too long, so he decided to just make up a common orcish name. "Vul'gol Heartrender."

The officer looked at the scroll and shook his head. "Sorry, Vul'gol — you're not on Blackrock Spire's warlock roster."

"Does the Warchief no longer accept the allegiance of any warlocks?" Jace asked.

"No," the officer shook his head.

"Then no one may question my loyalty to the Horde," Jace said with a trace of anger. "I've been forced to wander the wilderness. In the Black Swamp to the south I encountered Alliance mercenary hunters. On the shores of Lakeshire in Redridge I was hunted by militia. It was only through tremendous hardship that I broke through and made it here… At the very least, give me a chance to see the Warchief in person, so I can give up with a clear conscience."

"Well…"

The orc officer was clearly hesitant, because Jace looked like a shadow caster of considerable power, and he probably didn't dare drive the person away on his own.

After thinking for a moment, he said, "Master Vul'gol, I will assign several soldiers to escort you to Blackrock Spire to see Commander Vaelan, and let him make the decision."

"Fine." Jace nodded, sizing up the several soldiers the officer was pointing to with his hand.

They all looked like ordinary Blackrock orcs. He was already plotting to find an early opportunity to take them all out, then slip away to Shadowforge City.

Vaelan was the dragonkin commander and watcher of the lower levels of Blackrock Spire — a powerful high-ranking dragonkin in his own right. Considering that the Blackwing Watcher Rendan could easily see through Morophil's disguise, who knew whether Vaelan might see through his transformation as well. He didn't want to risk having his spell broken deep inside Blackrock Spire — that would be no different from the mind-controlled Katrana Prestor succeeding by proxy, wouldn't it?

Jace glanced at a felhound trailing alongside an orc warlock not far away. This was absolutely not the time for Morophil to employ her Charm.

Just as he'd figured out how to deal with these several orcs, a Shattered Hand orc in a dark brown robe suddenly walked over from the side.

"Sergeant Major Ogmog, let me take him to see Commander Vaelan. The commander will certainly grant him an audience with Warchief Rend. Because I've heard of him — Vul'gol Heartrender. He's of the Shadowmoon clan, used to serve under Gul'dan, but he was also one of the first clear-headed orcs to realize Gul'dan's treacherous intentions."

Jace stared at him for a moment. Who was this guy? How did he know the background he'd made up on the spot in Grim Batol?

He couldn't be a Dragonmaw orc, because Jace had used a different orcish name during his time with the Dragonmaw clan — he couldn't even remember what it was, since he'd made it up on the fly back then too.

But it was clear the other party had come with good intentions, otherwise he wouldn't have acknowledged a name Jace had invented out of thin air, so he'd better play along.

Because this orc was wearing a robe and a heavy shawl, Jace couldn't judge his rank in the Horde by what he was wearing, but the officer before him was treating him with absolute deference and obedience.

"Yes, sir."

This guard didn't even argue — he thumped his chest and stepped aside, dismissing the soldiers he'd just summoned.

"This way, Master Vul'gol." The brown-robed orc turned and walked through the gate.

Jace glanced at Sola beside him and beckoned for her to follow.

Not only was the outside different — the situation inside the Blackrock gate had changed dramatically as well.

The refugee-like orc soldiers and laborers who used to sleep along both sides of the passageways were gone now too. The place had been cleaned out thoroughly. Quite a few orc soldiers were gathered on the bridges around the lava lake, receiving reprimands or orders from officers. The entire Blackrock Mountain seemed to be preparing for a battle to come.

Who were they getting ready to greet — Dark Iron dwarves, Stormwind, or Ironforge?

The brown-robed orc walked the whole way without a word. Of course, this was hardly the time for conversation. It wasn't until they reached a collapsed tunnel, with only the three of them left around, that the orc actively stopped, turned around, and —

Just as Jace thought he was about to offer some kind of explanation, the orc suddenly drew an enchanted dagger from his belt — not at Jace, but straight at Sola.

Jace lunged and grabbed hard, relying on his orc form's tremendous strength and speed to catch the dagger bare-handed. His other hand clamped down on the brown-robed orc's wrist. He hadn't expected that, despite looking like a caster, this guy was so strong that Jace felt somewhat out of his depth. Right now his hands and feet were bound tight by clumps of black withered vines and thorns bursting from the cracks in the stone, but Sola's summoned plants nearly broke apart the moment he exerted force.

However, seeing Jace block him, and the nature magic summoned by the female orc before him, he seemed to freeze as well and stopped struggling.

"What's going on here, Jace Sesso? Who is this female orc?" the brown-robed orc stared at Jace.

"You first — who are you!" Jace said.

The orc said, "I'm Vaelastrasz. Don't you recognize me?"

Jace let out a deep sigh. No wonder this fellow had seen through his disguise in one glance — after all, this cloak was made by him!

He let go and looked at his palm, which had been cut several times by the dagger. "What were you hoping for, Vaelastrasz? That I'd be able to tell through an adult dragon's disguise? You couldn't even see through this young green dragon's disguise!"

"She… a green dragon?!" Vaelastrasz asked, stunned. "Who is she?"

"My name is Solamiel, Lord Vaelastrasz," Sola said, relaxing slightly as well.

"Lady Solamiel, all right, my apologies… I truly didn't consider that a guardian of the forest would take on the appearance of an orc." Vaelastrasz wiped his dagger and tucked it back into his belt. "If it weren't for Prince Korialstrasz's orders, I'd far rather attend to those annoying whelps than put on this appearance again."

"Why did you come here?" he asked.

"I was originally planning to head to Shadowforge City," Jace said. "I didn't expect security to have tightened up so much all of a sudden. What are these soldiers preparing for?"

"They're worried the red dragons will attack the tower," Vaelastrasz said. "I heard Nefarian's sister ran into trouble in Stormwind. Do you know about that?"

"We don't just know about it," Jace said.

"It was you, wasn't it," Vaelastrasz asked with a smile.

"Not just me — your lord helped too," Jace said.

"The prince was involved as well?" Vaelastrasz said. "No wonder Nefarian would fear a red dragon attack here — I was a little puzzled…"

"Did Onyxia come to Blackrock Spire?" Jace asked.

"No, she hasn't shown up at all," Vaelastrasz said. "That's not strange, Jace. I imagine the two of them already had serious disagreements over how to rule Blackrock Spire. Come to think of it, when I noticed you arriving at Blackrock Mountain today, I was even a little suspicious that you might have already sided with Nefarian — it wasn't until you gave some ridiculous name that I was sure you were still on our side."

"Why would you think Jace was going to side with Nefarian, Lord Vaelastrasz?" Sola asked.

"I heard from some orcs that Nefarian wanted to get you into Blackrock Spire," Vaelastrasz said. "Of course, they didn't say 'Jace Sesso' directly — they said 'the human warlock who destroyed the Dragonmaw Legion,' but that's no different from saying your name outright. Don't tell me you had no idea about this? None of those black dragons tried to make contact with you?"

"Getting me into Blackrock Spire could mean a lot of things," Jace said. "Bringing a corpse counts as 'getting me here' too. But Onyxia did try to lure me into Blackrock Spire."

"It doesn't matter," Vaelastrasz said. "Even if you really had been captured and brought in, since I could get Rhonin and the prince out of here, I could get you out too."

"I'd rather not give you the chance," Jace said with a smile.

"Best not to." Vaelastrasz pointed ahead. "You want to go to Shadowforge City — follow me."
