Chapter 1036: Revisiting the Tavern

Jace wasn't particularly surprised by these Dark Iron double-dealings.

If anything, the fact that he'd gotten by in Shadowforge City all this way without running into a situation like this had already improved his opinion of most Dark Iron dwarves' moral standards.

Still, compared to being pinned down by golems with electrical energy in that vault, what surprised Jace more was that Sorgaden had actually mastered the magic of short-distance flame teleportation. He wondered when he'd be able to command fire to that degree.

Of course, Jace wasn't terribly interested in short-distance teleportation. For the distance Sorgaden had moved, he could easily match it with Shadowstep.

What he truly coveted was the ability to teleport across regions — like the plane Onyxia had torn open when she tried to flee Stormwind Keep. Where had she been trying to go through that elemental plane passage of earth and fire? Blackrock Spire, or the black dragon nest in Dustwallow Marsh?

Even from Stormwind to Blackrock Spire, that distance was already staggering.

The problem was, without the aid of fire elements, Jace had no ability to enter that plane at all, let alone tear a rift open with his own mana.

Of course, this was also one of his goals in coming to Shadowforge City — to find Rokhtus Darkcontract and learn more about fire elements.

After leaving the vault, the two of them made their way straight to the Black Iron Bar. Jace went to the storeroom behind the bar, where Rokhtus lived, but the dark room's door was shut tight.

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. It was already eleven at night. Ever since drawing near to Blackrock Mountain again, he'd basically lost all ability to judge the time. The Black Iron Bar was still quite crowded and noisy, which had fooled him into thinking it was still early.

Rokhtus was already old — old enough that even ancient dwarves like Magni Bronzebeard couldn't figure out his exact age. He probably needed even more sleep than your average snoozy dwarf. And since Jace was coming to apprentice and learn, he naturally couldn't go smashing down the door to wake the old man up. So the two of them decided to stay the night and come pay their respects the next day.

He booked two rooms with Miss Namara, checked their luggage, and then the two of them headed down to get something to eat — after all, from entering Blackrock Mountain all the way through the long underground expedition into Shadowforge City, they still hadn't had a single proper meal.

There wasn't much in the way of proper food in Shadowforge City either, but at least the thin gruddle and the fungi were piping hot. Shadowforge City could run short of anything, but never fire.

The two of them sat down. Jace looked at Sola and asked, "What do you think of that staff?"

"Looks quite nice," Sola said. "It's just that the gem on the very top is an arcane gem, so I might not be able to accurately judge its power."

"Didn't you hear Sorgaden? This staff was used by Sage Vallerel." Jace held the bowl of gruddle in his hands and took a mouthful. This meatless "meat soup" gruddle had only a trace of salt flavor, and the bizarre texture even interrupted his train of thought.

He stared at the black fragments floating in the gruddle — looked like broken insect shells, or some kind of underground fern residue — gathered his thoughts, and continued, "And Vallerel was a priest of the Holy Light, not an arcanist or some other mage who manipulates elements with the arcane. So I'd guess the power of this arcane gem probably serves a function like Arcane Intellect — expanding your spiritual power and making it easier for you to communicate with… whatever you believe in, or whatever you command."

"Is that so? No wonder…" Sola thought about it, then seemed to finally notice something off in Jace's wording. "You're planning to give this staff to me?"

Jace nodded. "I already have a staff, and if I don't want it meddling, I've still got the Song of the Blue Sky. I'm not the Lich Queen — I can't wield four weapons at once. Although I didn't consider that this staff has an arcane gem and might not suit you. But at the time I also didn't want to spend too long in that vault. Besides this staff, everything else was either armor that looked heavy as hell or clumsy weapons clearly only suited for dwarves — obviously not the sort of thing you grab and go."

"I really like this staff, Jace," Sola said. "I thought you'd picked one for yourself suited for casting fire magic."

"There was a fire grimoire there too, remember? That was obviously a better fit." Jace smiled. "But after all, that was the Thor'San Shrine. I was worried that if we stayed too long, that Flamebender would cook up some other scheme to break free, so I didn't want to linger too long."

"Thank you," Sola said.

Jace raised an eyebrow. "If Grede were even half as polite as you, I'd be willing to get him more things. Oh right — that move Sorgaden used, teleporting to higher ground with flame, that was definitely a teleport involving an elemental plane, right, Sola?"

Sola hugged her staff and shrugged. "Sorry, Jace — everything happened too fast just now. I was focused on putting those Flamebenders to sleep, so I wasn't paying attention…"

"At least I didn't sense any arcane involvement, master," Sazeno popped his head out of the bag and said. "Although that fire elemental magic was very different from the plane the black dragon tore open, I suppose there must be certain common points between them!"

"Why didn't you come out and help just now?" Jace asked.

"Hah, that kind of small scene didn't need me at all — it'd be a waste of the master's time!" the imp said with a laugh. "Leaving it to that succubus was more than enough!"

Jace gave him a sidelong look. Sola asked, "Is Morophil all right in that shrine?"

"She's fine, because she wasn't in that shrine at all," Jace said.

"That Flamebender shrine had far too many roaming fire elements, Lady Sola," Morophil suddenly popped out from behind Sola and sat down beside her. "Those fire elements couldn't detect me right away, but their anti-invisibility ability was already good enough that I couldn't stay comfortably by those Flamebenders' sides."

"Then wouldn't that mean we couldn't threaten that Sorgaden?" Sola asked.

"He doesn't know I left the shrine." Morophil smiled. "Let them search. Perfect invisibility that can never be detected simply means never having been there in the first place."

The next morning, Jace went early to the storeroom behind the bar. He leaned against the door of the dark room and waited until there seemed to be some movement inside, then knocked.

After a clatter of chains, the door was pulled open. Rokhtus glanced out, saw Jace, squinted, hesitated for a bit, then said, "You…"

"Jace Sesso," Jace said.

"Hmm, I'm already having some trouble recognizing people these days," Rokhtus patted Jace's waist. "But before I'm a hundred percent certain you're Jace Sesso, I don't dare say your name out loud."

"I'm here to return the ring this time, Master," Jace said, handing the ring to the dwarf. "Though not just for that."

"I can see that," Rokhtus said. "Come in, sit, and talk."

Jace nodded and sat down beside the iron bed. "The most important thing today concerns the Sulfuron Hammer, Master."

"Would that Thunderfist be willing to take up this war hammer?" Rokhtus asked cautiously.

"He… it doesn't have much to do with him," Jace said. "I thought of a place where the Sulfuron Hammer might possibly be forged. I just don't know anything about forging myself, so I came to ask your opinion."

"Where?" Rokhtus asked.

"Grim Batol," Jace said. "The Kadros Anvil, swallowed by Deathwing's lava."

"Deathwing, the Kadros Anvil — that is indeed a…" Rokhtus pondered with his head low, then said, "Do you have a way to enter present-day Grim Batol?"

"The red dragons are guarding it," Jace said. "A leader among the red dragons has already agreed to let me go in and see the situation firsthand. Right now I'm thinking that if we could find a group of Thorium Brotherhood members to go take a look as well, to confirm whether the conditions for forging the Sulfuron Hammer actually exist, that would be best."

"Very necessary," Rokhtus nodded. "It's just that I can't leave Shadowforge City, so I'll write you a letter. Go to Ironforge and find Ruige — ah, I remember you know him. Have him take you, with this letter, to Thorium Point. He knows where it is."

"I didn't expect you'd be willing to help us, Jace Sesso. After all, this is the Dark Iron clan's trouble. I really appreciate it." Even as he spoke, he pulled out a scroll so worn that the ink was nearly illegible, and got out ink and pen to write the letter.

"Shadowforge City's situation is closely tied to Stormwind's," Jace said. "I can't just look the other way, Master. But speaking of Ruige, there's also a commission he gave me."

He pulled out the cloth bundle wrapping the thorium dagger and set it on the table before him. "This is something he… forged with his own hands. He insisted on bringing it for you to take a look."

After Rokhtus finished scribbling the letter, he opened the bundle, took out the dagger, looked it over, and asked, "What am you showing me?" Jace cleared his throat. "He wants Master to appraise it."

Rokhtus glanced at the dagger again. "Doesn't look like Khaz Modan ore — should be thorium from southern Kalimdor, Fire Plume Ridge. I have to say, the material itself is quite good. Beyond that, I don't know what else to say."

Jace swallowed and watched the dwarf wrap the dagger back up, already starting on figuring out how to spin the situation as favorably as possible when he reported back to Ruige.

Of course, that was secondary. Jace glanced toward the crates stacked deep in the room, guessing whether there'd be anything like a fire element grimoire among them, and figured it was about time to bring up the matter of commanding fire elements.
