## Chapter 1037: The Unenslaved

Rokhtus reviewed the letter he'd written to Thorium Point once more, thought for a moment, and asked, "You were there firsthand, Jace Sesso — what exactly did Deathwing do in Grim Batol?"

Jace explained, "He shattered the city's great wall with a single devastating breath, trying to burn everyone inside to death in one fell swoop. The apocalyptic lava brought by that breath sank a vast section of the upper city to the depths below — and the Kadros Anvil was among what was swallowed."

"Just listening to you describe it, it's hard for me to imagine what the situation there actually is. I truly wish I could go see it for myself." Rokhtus spoke quietly. "But no matter what, this may be one of the few forging conditions we have left — after all, forging that war hammer on the Dark Iron Anvil is simply impossible."

He stared at the letter in his hand. "If it really works, just the thought of it is enough to get one excited — a war hammer capable of saving the entire Dark Iron clan being born in an ancient city cursed by the witch Modgud, like some kind of karmic cycle. But we can't place all our hopes on a single hammer. When all's said and done, it's still just a war hammer. All the still-sober Dark Iron must rally together at this crucial moment, along with those elements willing to defy the Firelord Ragnaros."

"I've already met with Ironforge's King Magni Bronzebeard, Master Rokhtus," Jace said. "Like you, he wants the Three Hammers to stand together again. I imagine that if one day a Dark Iron dwarf steps forward to defy the Firelord's enslavement, he'd consider lending a hand too — though first he'd have to convince the people around him."

"Magni Bronzebeard." Rokhtus repeated the name. "He's always been a clever and humble noble, Jace Sesso. Although all I can pick up here are scraps of information about him, I know he, like everyone who lived through that era, grieves over the current state of the three clans."

"I can feel it too — the older dwarves all seem to miss that era," Jace nodded. "If the people who dominated the Three Hammers back then had all been like you or him, the whole string of tragedies that followed never would have happened."

Rokhtus smiled slightly. "Actually, back then I also supported Thaurissan storming Ironforge to carve out a place for the Dark Iron — at least seizing equal status with the Bronzebeard, otherwise the Thorium Brotherhood wouldn't have stood at his side. But no one knew what an enormous price would have to be paid once the blow was struck. So I can't blame Modan, Kadros, and the others who made those decisions either. I know you're just humoring an old codger by going along with what I said, but there's no question — none of us can shirk our responsibility."

"But in the end, that was all two hundred years ago. Come on, Jace Sesso — what other help do you need from me right now? I can tell there's something you haven't said yet." The Dark Iron dwarf leaned against the head of the bed.

"There is," Jace said with an embarrassed smile. "I'd like to study the methods of communicating with fire elements here under you."

"Er." Rokhtus frowned at Jace for a moment, then asked, "You want to be a blacksmith?"

"Not a blacksmith. More precisely, I want to become a Flamebender," Jace said.

Rokhtus smiled. "From what I know, you're already a fairly powerful mage."

"In shadow magic, I've indeed picked up quite a bit," Jace explained. "But for one thing, shadow isn't accepted by most people outside Shadowforge City. And besides, I don't want to rely solely on shadow power to handle difficulties, Master. I imagine having lived in Shadowforge City for so long, you must know what prolonged dealings with shadow can do to a person. I'd like to try introducing — or rather, reaching out to — a completely different kind of power."

"I understand how you feel," the dwarf said.

Jace continued, "I've seen many Flamebenders in Shadowforge City who can use flame to teleport. I've also witnessed the black dragon princess Onyxia using fire to help herself shuttle between planes in Stormwind. I wonder if I could master something similar."

"Sounds like you're already quite familiar with dealing with fire elements." Rokhtus circled a finger in the air. "Otherwise you wouldn't have been able to tell how they were using fire elements to displace themselves — what you mentioned, related to the fire elemental plane."

"Yes," Jace said.

"Ah, I remember now — last time you came to me, you brought an element language dictionary you'd gotten from Ruige." The dwarf said this as he got off the bed and picked up a dust-covered lantern. "Then let me have a look at your skills. Follow me."

Most of the people in the Black Iron Bar didn't recognize Rokhtus, even though he'd lived there for a very long time — longer than most of the people here had been alive. But he rarely showed himself, and basically only met with people he knew.

Only Namara respectfully called him "Mr. Idobloden," and Rokhtus politely responded, addressing her as "Lady Namara" — he seemed to bear no ill will toward this demon either.

Although Rokhtus was a bit muddle-headed about recognizing people, he had no trouble recognizing the road at all. He led Jace out of the bar all the way down below, through several corridors, and arrived at an unhewn natural cave.

It grew hotter and hotter here, and they'd lost the Dark Iron dwarves' road markers, statues, and other guides. This place was even more disorienting than the Maraudon caves. But Rokhtus carried his lantern all the way, walking deeper with steady, unhurried steps — until the path ahead was suddenly lit by firelight. The two of them had already arrived at the bank of a lava river.

Jace looked up. At the highest point of the cliff crevice, he could still faintly make out the shadow of the bridge. This seemed to be a lava river at the very bottom of the Shadowforge City abyss.

At that moment, a patch of shimmering bright-red lava on the opposite bank caught Jace's attention. Rokhtus was gazing over there too.

"Don't mind that, Sesso," he pointed to the west of the lava river. "Let's walk a little further this way, away from the notice of those Jabarmagg. No need to provoke them."

"Jabarmagg?" Jace asked. "What's that?"

Rokhtus explained as he walked, "I don't know how it should be translated into Common. It's a kind of giant worm that lives in lava. They've got lots of searing claws and they spit lava. They rose from the depths of Blackrock Mountain along with the Firelord and have claimed countless dwarves' lives. But worst of all — their flesh is inedible."

"I don't understand, Master," Jace asked. "Those lava worms sound like they came here from the fire elemental plane. So why do they attack the Dark Iron dwarves who worship Ragnaros?"

The moment Ragnaros's name came up, Jace felt the lava river beneath his feet seem to churn. Waves of scorching heat struck his skin and consciousness even more viciously, making him instinctively reflect on whether he should have spoken that name aloud here.

"Ha, that name." Rokhtus smiled. "We're too close to the Firelands here — the loyalty of the fire elements far exceeds what you outsiders imagine. But that doesn't include the Jabarmagg, or rather, the lava worms. They do come from the elemental world of fire, but they're wildly ferocious, untamable beasts. Here they obey only one impossibly enormous lava worm — we call it 'Magmaw.' As for the Dark Iron dwarves, the fire elements, even the Flamewalkers — far from being their masters, they're all on its long, long menu."

"Lava worms are dangerous, but I don't dislike them." The dwarf turned his head as he walked. "You know why?"

Jace shook his head. Rokhtus continued explaining, "Because they represent a possibility that cannot be tamed. Even fire, even with lava flowing through their bodies, deep in the bowels of Shadowforge City, in the place closest to the Firelands, they still do not submit to the will of the Firelord Ragnaros."

These lava worms actually weren't under the Firelord's control?

Jace couldn't help but turn his head to look again at that patch of shimmering red firelight he'd seen earlier.

Not far off, the flowing lava river churned even more furiously, as if refusing to agree with what the old Dark Iron dwarf had said.

Rokhtus ignored the flames and looked at Jace. "You probably can't imagine that even in a world ruled by fire elements, there still exist things that refuse to submit to the elemental lords. I think this is also why, even in Shadowforge City, people like me who don't believe in the Firelord can still summon the aid of flame."

The dwarf patted the lantern in his hand. Jace looked at the flame inside, which seemed rather feeble beneath the glow of the lava river, and seemed to sense the power Rokhtus was hinting at.

"Right here, Jace Sesso." Rokhtus stopped beside a massive black boulder. "Try summoning flame. Let me see where you stand with fire elements right now. Don't worry — the Flameguards of Shadowforge City won't detect this."

Jace nodded, but when he calmed his mind and tried to listen for the whispers of flame, the deathly silence still made him deeply uncomfortable.
