## Chapter 1038: A Common Enemy

Under Rokhtus's gaze, Jace steeled himself and drew the Song of the Blue Sky, chanting the language he'd learned from Ruige, calling upon flame to ignite the magical longsword held in his right hand.

After finishing his prayer to the fire, he felt a ball of flame well up from his left hand, as though being guided to drift toward the blade in his right. But even when he slapped the flame onto the blade, it only produced a brief flare along the blue-dragon-scale blade.

And that was merely arcane force pulling the flame alight — not the fire elements themselves truly obeying his command and igniting.

Jace felt his communication with the fire elements was as if it had been isolated and shrouded by some colormons darkness. Outside Shadowforge City he'd been able to summon flame to scorch the giant scorpions trying to besiege him, Sola, and those dwarf soldiers. But here, it was as though the flame couldn't hear his voice at all.

He couldn't help but grow anxious in his heart. So he chanted the prayer once more, louder this time, hoping the flame would answer his command and ignite the sword. This time he seemed to feel a flicker of response from the fire, even a trace of elemental hesitation — but even this wavering force made him eagerly stretch his consciousness toward it, like finally catching hold of a rope lowered down a well.

Yet when he poured all his effort into focusing his mind to guide the flame and concentrated his attention on the blade, a surging inferno suddenly wrapped around the Song of the Blue Sky. Only this thing that burned wasn't true fire elements — it was a mass of purple-black… shadow.

Jace immediately shook the Song of the Blue Sky to dispel the shadow, leaving behind only an unpleasant, fishy stench in the air.

"You possess a remarkably strong affinity for fire, Jace Sesso," Rokhtus said. "The elements tell me — the Dragonqueen bestowed her blood upon you."

"You can hear the whispers of the elements?" Jace asked.

Rokhtus nodded. "The elements are willing to help you and don't mind your close ties to shadow, because they trust the Dragonqueen's judgment, and because you've done much to help fire purge evil. However, that's still not enough for you to merge with the power of the fire elements as one. And here, this barrier is amplified — not only because of the Firelands' enslavement of flame, but also because the shadow power within you magnifies the fear the still-free fire elements here feel toward you. I imagine you've tried many times to ignite shadow with flame, creating devastating crimson flame — like the black dragons."

"Yes," Jace admitted.

"You can open yourself up to the elements you trust — that's good," Rokhtus said. "The fire elements can see into your heart and perceive your sincerity from within. This way, even if you do indeed possess formidable shadow power, the elements will understand you bear no ill will. But elements who have endured countless wars and enslavement find it very hard to give you the same response. What you need to do next is let them open up to you completely."

"I thought the elements already trusted me enough," Jace said.

"Not enough," Rokhtus said. "This may be a rather long process, requiring bit-by-bit tempering, needing time to prove yourself. Those Flamebenders in the Thor'San Shrine trade their total submission to the Firelord in exchange for the absolute obedience of the fire elements enslaved by the Firelord. All they need to do is constantly prove their fanaticism and express their loyalty to the Firelands. What we need to do is far more complicated than theirs."

He raised both hands and summoned flame to coil around his chest, then continued, "Once you can earn the fire elements' absolute trust, you won't even need prayer-like incantations — you'll only need heart-to-heart communication with the fire elements. This is how Thaurissan, together with the seven Sages, summoned the Firelord and destroyed half of Redridge, re-enslaving the elements that had already broken free from his clutches. So you should understand why the fire elements who are now struggling so hard to maintain their free will here are so reluctant to trust mortals."

The dwarf finished speaking. The flame dispersed, and nearby, silence returned once more.

"Then if I help you break free from bondage," Jace said, looking at the lava river and speaking in the elemental tongue, "could I earn your complete trust?"

The moment these words left his mouth, Jace seemed to finally catch a faint, barely-audible elemental whisper — and Rokhtus's expression grew more and more grave.

At first Jace couldn't make out at all what the whisper was saying. He thought it was some kind of peculiar, hard-to-decipher manner of whispering. But before long, he caught a word he could more or less understand from within the whisper.

"Dark pact, Darkseal."

The fire elements had woven Rokhtus's family name into that long string of whispers.

"I believe in his promise," Rokhtus said, also looking at the lava river and speaking in the elemental tongue. "And you all know who he is." The elemental whisper grew more and more fervent. Jace could tell that much of what was being said was directed at him.

"I think you already know the answer," Rokhtus said. "In a way, I'd say you and we now share a common enemy."

Jace nodded. He chanted the elemental tongue once more, and the Song of the Blue Sky in his hand actually gave off a wisp of true flame.

He tried to further guide this ball of fire with his will to keep burning. He watched as the fire element burned brighter and brighter, eventually covering the entire blade and growing an increasingly deep, bloody red.

He'd never felt fire element power this intense in Shadowforge City — it was almost overwhelming this time.

But right then, the lava river churned up once more, as if dissatisfied with Jace showing off so brazenly right in front of it. "Careful, Jace — your power…" Rokhtus warned, his gaze fixed on something beneath the lava river.

Jace sensed it too — something in the lava river was stirring, and getting closer and closer.

He gripped the hilt of the Song of the Blue Sky tightly. Suddenly a mass of lava exploded before his eyes. A blood-red worm burst up from below, shaking its head — a head encased in armor-like forged-iron carapace — as its body swelled a great mass of glowing red up from its throat…

A deafening rumble swallowed Jace's eardrums, but he felt no panic at all. A surge of battle-frenzy raced from his hands throughout his body along with the flame he'd just summoned, quickly flaring up to his brain.

The worm opened its maw from above its head and spewed a mass of lava at Jace. Jace chanted Shadowstep to dodge aside, while at the same time plucking the burning flame from the Song of the Blue Sky with his hand.

He chanted the elemental tongue and called upon still mightier flame to gather. As the ball of fire in his hand continuously expanded, it grew redder and blacker, finally coalescing into a terrifying core of Shadowflame.

Seeing the lava it had poured out failed to engulf this pitiful mortal before it, the worm no longer gathered more lava from its belly. Instead it lowered its head and charged forward, opening its blade-lined maw to snap!

Jace didn't dodge at all. He swung his arm in full arc and hurled the core of Shadowflame straight into the worm's gaping maw as if casting a Shadow Bolt. The worm clearly didn't fear burning, but the impact of the flame shattered a great section of the worm's carapace and limbs, while the shadow seeped deep into its skin and flesh, tearing open swaths of shadow cracks across its head and neck.

The worm writhed in pain, crashing into the lava with enough force to burst apart into a great mass of burning flesh and shell, disintegrating and exploding in the flowing lava river. Gray and black smoke spewed all the way up to the heights.

As the worm died, an ear-splitting shriek of a giant worm echoed from the depths of the lava river, and shouts came from the Dark Iron dwarves above too — the commotion below seemed to have attracted the attention of quite a few soldiers.

"Let's go." Rokhtus glanced at the lava worm's remains, which were now unrecognizable. "Follow me away from here."

Back in the winding, pitch-black cave, Rokhtus extinguished the light of his lantern. Before long came the sound of noisy footsteps and shouting.

Squads of Dark Iron soldiers passed before their eyes. He led Jace to hide behind a cluster of stone pillars and waited for them to pass before continuing to climb upward.

"Thank you, Master Rokhtus," Jace said, looking at the stout old blacksmith ahead of him. "I know you vouched for me to the elements."

"No." Rokhtus turned his head. "The elements would never casually believe anyone's promise, Jace Sesso. What matters most is your goodwill and sincerity, your strength, and everything you've done."

"That doesn't mean you don't need to improve your skills of communicating with the elements. Your elemental tongue pronunciation still has some issues, and when communicating with fire elements you should try to follow the fire elements' pronunciation habits as much as possible — that'll save you energy too."

"I can tell that every element has its own pronunciation style," Jace said. "Water elements' pronunciation, for instance, is harder for me to understand. I'm more familiar with the language of fire."

"Yes," Rokhtus said. "How long are you planning to stay in Shadowforge City this time?"

"Not long — a week at most," Jace answered.

"A week is enough for me to correct your pronunciation," the dwarf said.

Just then, another group of Dark Iron patrol soldiers walked over from above. Rokhtus led Jace to hide in a quiet fork in the road. After a while, once the footsteps had faded, he said, "When you head back, I'll give you a grimoire devoted specifically to the language of fire elements — Ignandifremous. Once you return to Stormwind, you can continue learning on your own."

Jace nodded. Just from hearing the name of this book, he could tell he was about to step deep into the study of the elemental tongue.
