Chapter 1039: "Senior"

If an entire week were spent just waiting for Ursula and Xilya to finish making their potions, it might have felt a bit dull. But if it was spent learning the elemental tongue, a week was far too rushed.

During this stretch, Jace stayed at the Black Iron Bar. He practically headed out to find Rokhtus for lessons every single morning and didn't come back until evening.

Which meant that, over this period, Sola, Morophil, and Sazeno ended up spending a lot of time together.

The Flamebenders still thought Morophil was hiding inside the Thor'San Shrine, so naturally she couldn't often show her face outside and blow her own cover — she could only stay cooped up in the guest room.

But Sola and Sazeno were a different story. She'd occasionally take the imp out to sightsee around Shadowforge City. As long as they didn't go near the lava river at the bottom of the abyss to pick fights with those lava worms, just strolling around the city was more or less safe enough.

After one of the lava worms was killed, deep rumbling roars of giant worm-kind often echoed from the bottom of the abyss. Jace guessed that was probably the leader of the lava worms — Magmaw. That first boss in Nefarian's rebuilt Blackwing Circle after the fall of Blackrock Spire, the nightmare that once made Dark Iron dwarves tremble at the mere mention of it, had actually become the pet of the black dragon prince.

The constant roaring put Shadowforge City's soldiers on edge. Quite a few more patrols appeared on the bridge spanning the abyssal chasm, and even the fire element guards stepped up their rounds. Those tunnels and passages leading down to the abyss were all guarded by numbers of dwarf soldiers.

Because of this, Jace couldn't go near the lava river again to attempt larger-scale elemental magic. He could only try some small, delicate ways of cooperating with fire elements in the storeroom behind the bar.

But small didn't mean it couldn't show progress. As Rokhtus corrected quite a number of mispronounced pronunciations and bad habits Jace had developed during his solo practice, Jace gradually realized that improvements in these most fundamental skills enhanced even the simple act of igniting a flame in his palm.

From Rokhtus, Jace learned that those fire elements unwilling to submit to the Old Gods and unwilling to be enslaved acknowledged that the language they communicated in was the Kalimdor tongue, or Kalimag. But fire elements loyal to Ragnaros, like Incendius, preferred to call their elemental-tongue "dialect" "Ignan" — to highlight the distinction between fire elements and the other elements.

Although this made learning "Ignan" a required course for every Flamebender in Shadowforge City, it didn't mean shamans and elementalists elsewhere had no need to study it.

Even when communicating with free fire elements, using Ignan was more efficient.

A few days later, Jace tried carefully lighting every candle and oil lamp in the storeroom with flame. Although this was only cantrip-level elemental manipulation, the smoothness of it, the harmony of the fire elements' cooperation, and the improvement in mana efficiency were all crystal clear.

If he tried now to call upon flame to carry him into that elemental rift Onyxia had torn open, he probably wouldn't feel the urge to chug a mana potion the moment he got in.

He had to admit — Rokhtus wasn't just a remarkably skilled Flamebender, he was absolutely a seasoned teacher.

The thing is, no matter what ability you possess, mastery doesn't mean you can teach it to someone else with equal skill. Yet this old dwarf blacksmith was not only patient with Jace — a wild talent who'd only received some guidance from Ruige and had basically relied on self-teaching and personal insight — but could also pinpoint exactly where Jace had gone off track, and his methods of correction were highly effective.

Jace even had the feeling that he might have taught other humans how to communicate with fire elements before. After all, dwarves and humans weren't the same race in the end — there were differences in vocal organs and habits. Even so, Rokhtus could still teach Jace how to reach a dwarf's level as closely as possible with human vocal equipment, and in some areas he could even teach him techniques that humans possessed but dwarves didn't.

Considering that this wasn't the first time Dark Iron dwarves and humans had come into contact — long ago, Shadowforge City's designer and architect, Franclorn Forgewright, had gifted Marshal Windsor's ancestor the sword called The Opposer — Jace guessed he probably wasn't the first human to come study fire magic under Rokhtus.

The week passed quickly. By now, if things had gone smoothly, Xilya's Invisibility Potion — and most importantly, the potion to resist the Ata'lai priests' mind-control magic — should be more or less ready. Ursula's Refined Wisdom Elixir might still need another day or two, but now that they were setting out on the journey back to Stormwind, by the time they reached Elwynn Forest the timing would be about right.

Although at the very beginning Jace had told Sola he hoped she wouldn't rush it — after all, the green dragons had been trapped in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar not for a day or two, not even for a year or two — Jace could still sense, when eating meals with Sola, that there was always something lodged in her heart.

Language was the kind of skill that was easy to pick up but hard to master. Jace certainly couldn't expect himself to learn Ignan to native level before leaving Shadowforge City, so it was about time to say goodbye.

That morning, Jace came to the storeroom behind the bar. Rokhtus had opened the door early and was sitting at the entrance reading a book.

"You've given me the habit of waking up early these days, Jace," the dwarf said with a smile.

Jace raised his eyebrows a little sheepishly. "It's just that the time I can stay here is limited, so I want to learn as much as I can…"

"That's nothing," Rokhtus shook his head. "I wasn't as lazy in the past as I am now. You've helped me recall what life was like when I was young — that's not bad at all. But I remember you mentioned a few days ago that you were planning to leave Shadowforge City today?"

"Yes, Master," Jace nodded. "I've come to say goodbye."

"That pronunciation you never got sorted out yesterday — I think when you get back you can try using arcane magic to assist your pronunciation," Rokhtus said. "Although you mentioned you're somewhat lacking in the arcane, I remember that in the Wizard Sanctum, using the arcane to produce a specific sound you want isn't some advanced course. At any rate, it's certainly simpler than the Arcane Intellect and Mage Armor you've already mastered."

"Mage Armor relies rather heavily on elemental power, Master," Jace said. "But I'll give it a try. Of course, if I can produce it with my own vocal organs, I'd still rather not rely on the arcane…"

Mid-sentence, he suddenly realized something didn't add up, and asked, "Master, you seem quite familiar with the Wizard Sanctum? Have apprentices from there come to Shadowforge City to study fire magic before?"

"They have," Rokhtus nodded. "But not an apprentice — a human mage of considerable arcane talent. Although he didn't display any arcane ability, I could tell."

Jace thought about it. "If he had such strong talent, he should have gone straight into the School of Evocation to study fire magic. Why come to Shadowforge City to learn how to communicate with flame? Of course, Master, I'm not saying learning elemental communication is more troublesome than using the arcane to command elements — it's just that many talented mages hold communication with elements in rather low regard. Actually, the reason I came to the Dark Iron clan was because I hit a bottleneck using the arcane to manipulate fire… and my magic teacher recommended I seek them out."

"I'm not surprised," Rokhtus said. "Even in Ironforge before the War of the Three Hammers there was this attitude. Mages had zero interest in communicating with elements, thinking it was a path only blacksmiths with no arcane talent or wild folk living in the mountains would pursue. That was until the Sages summoned the Firelord here at Blackrock Mountain."

The dwarf paused a moment, then continued, "But that human was different from you, Jace. He was very fond of fire magic, but he felt he was enslaved by the arcane, so he didn't want to use arcane power. According to him, he suffered from a kind of 'mana addiction,' like those elves who'd left Quel'Thalas — but I felt his condition was probably far more serious than that. He was seeking a way to wield greater power while excluding the arcane entirely."

"What was his name?" Jace asked.

"He said his name was Kurelai," Rokhtus said. "I know human mages all love giving themselves false names, so it's probably the same in this case. But he was a very unusual young human, so he left a deep impression on me."

The moment the dwarf said this name, Jace froze.

Rokhtus was undeniably a language master. Centuries of life and communication with different races had made him fluent in all manner of languages. His Common was extremely standard — you couldn't even detect much in the way of dwarven pronunciation habits.

And the "Kurelai" that came out of his mouth was definitely not a Common name — or at least, not a normal Common name. Whether by coincidence or not, it sounded far more like the Eredun spoken by the draenei — especially to someone as sensitive to Eredun as Jace.

Because Eredun was demonic.

In Eredun, Kurelai meant "one who seeks redemption." Thinking this, Jace became more and more certain that this was definitely not a meaningless name chosen at random — that mage must have known the meaning of this name.

"What did he look like?" Jace asked.

"Tall — probably a bit taller than you — and very thin. Beyond that, nothing particularly memorable," Rokhtus shook his head. "That was a very long time ago. A mage like him, if by today he still hadn't made his name known to the world — for good or ill — then he probably met with misfortune and died young."

A mage from Stormwind, fluent in demonic, who believed his terrifying arcane talent was a curse and wanted to break free and seek redemption for past mistakes… Jace could barely think of a second person.

Had Medivh also come to Shadowforge City to study how to communicate with fire elements? If that were true, it would be something Jace had never even heard of in his past life.

Who knew what kind of struggle the young man possessed by the soul of the Dark Titan Sargeras had gone through after killing his own father by mistake.

"On one hand, I hope the allies who launch the assault on the Firelands possess god-slaying might," Rokhtus said. "But I still want to tell you — don't lose yourself in the endless pursuit of power, Jace Sesso. Always remember why you started, and use that to steer your direction… Of course, for someone like you who can control their own shadow power, perhaps I don't have much standing to say anything. But I've seen countless powerful beings destroy themselves and everything they loved. In the beginning, every single one of them had reasonable and sufficient motives."

"I'll remember, Master Rokhtus," Jace said.

Rokhtus said, "I hope that one day you'll truly be able to settle down, Jace Sesso."

Jace had heard too many blessings, too many hopes. This Dark Iron dwarf's words sounded perfectly ordinary, yet they struck right at the deepest, most hidden place in his heart.

On the road out of Shadowforge City, Jace couldn't stop turning over in his mind what Rokhtus had said, all the way to a desolate black plain on a barren platform to the south, where no more orc buildings or dwarven ruins were visible.

This was a good place to take off — no black dragons or orc cavalry patrolling. Go much further south and you'd be near Dracodar, where there'd be more enemies.

"You've been distracted this whole time, Jace." Sola, holding her staff, turned her head to ask. "Everything all right?"

Jace glanced at Morophil beside him, who was watching him with an amused look, and shook his head. "Just thinking about the matter of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar."

Sola lowered her head. "I've been thinking too. If all my kin have lost their sanity, are we really going to have to kill them one by one with our own hands?"

Morophil stood to one side and said, "When the time comes, we should be more concerned with how we're going to survive, Lady Sola. Or do you think we can really fight an entire green dragon flight led by an adult dragon?"

"If we don't go in, we have no idea what the actual situation is," Jace said, chanting a spell to restore his human form within a ball of flame. "Let's not agonize over this right now. Orc patrols can appear here at any time — we'd better leave as soon as possible."

Sola also shed her disguise, reverting to her dragon form. She stretched her wings, lowered her body, and let Morophil and Jace climb on.

Once airborne, she turned her head to look at Jace. "Morophil and Sazeno told me about the green dragon blood matter, Jace. Why didn't you ever mention it to me?"

"It's just that an alchemist heard I was going to the Black Swamp and entrusted me to help find an ingredient for her," Jace said, glancing back at Morophil behind him. Morophil, for her part, put on an innocent look as if she'd said nothing at all.

The green dragon shook her head. "It's just a small bottle. Collecting a bit of blood for casting spells or making medicine is no big deal for us."

"So every time that ingredient is needed, I'm supposed to come get blood from you?" Jace said. "I wasn't planning on relying on you to solve this, Sola. It might be nothing to you, but to me it's just too damn weird — we're good friends. If I can find the remains of an unfortunate dead green dragon, I'll collect what I can and put it to good use. If not, then so be it."

Solamiel slowly nodded, flapped her wings once more, and rode a surge of warm natural power forward. Soon the terrain grew higher and higher, and the cinder-smoke stench of the Burning Plains wasn't so sharp anymore.
