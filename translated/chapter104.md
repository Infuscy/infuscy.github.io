## Chapter 104: A Spell for a Spell

In the dim lamplight of a night-shrouded room, two warlocks faced each other.

The young Jace wore a blank expression. The considerably older Gakin, clearly weakened from the beating he'd just taken and from being rescued by the man in front of him, had lost much of his bluster.

"We both walk the path of dark arts, and I know your understanding of incantations is no less than mine, Jace." Gakin's tone was thoroughly deflated. He said quietly, "I'm quite fond of this new spell you've found. Can you tell me where you came across it?"

"It wasn't from the Dark Iron dwarves, anyway. But you saw what it did, Gakin — even a brawler like Erlan went down. And look at me: the spell didn't cost me much at all. I could probably cast it twice more without trouble."

"A perfect spell for dealing with thugs." Gakin nodded, took a bottle of rum from the nearby shelf, and took a swig. "This spell is worth a fortune, Jace. You asked when the next gathering was — you want to share this spell with the whole group?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Unless you have other ideas."

"I have no other ideas. Anything I get from you, I'll share openly." Gakin took a booklet down from the shelf and flipped through a few pages. "As a reward, look here… Jace, I have three incantations from the Dark Iron dwarves that I've never shared with the group. Because I felt that among those warlocks, only you and Zardeth had the right to receive these incantations — letting the others practice them would be a disgrace to those precious secrets."

Jace watched Gakin get more and more carried away as he spoke. When it came to the pursuit of dark magic, Gakin's goals seemed to differ from Erlan's, Surena's, even Zardeth's.

A lot of people who practiced warlock magic were like Jace — for one reason or another, they'd missed their chance to enter the Wizard's Sanctum, been rejected by the arcane, and still wanted to grow stronger, to master magic, so they sought the warlock's path.

But Gakin was like someone who chased darkness for its own sake. Faced with all manner of spells, he always showed a kind of drunken fascination with dark magic itself.

"The first incantation — the Dark Iron dwarves call it the Eye Spell," Gakin said. "This magic is extremely powerful and extremely difficult to learn, Jace. It demands an exceptionally high command of shadow, and I haven't mastered it well. Specifically, it was created by a very highly-ranked orc warlock. It uses shadow power to create an eye that lurks in the darkness, helping you spy on anything."

The Eye of Kilrogg — Jace knew this warlock spell. In the game, there weren't many situations where you could use it, but here… it was unquestionably quite useful.

Gakin went on: "The second incantation is called Curse of Recklessness. It can turn a person into a complete lunatic. You ever wonder why those orc warriors fight so savagely? I suspect they've all been hit by Curse of Recklessness from the warlocks behind their own lines. This spell is very effective — I've already used it on Surena. You know what I mean… maybe one day we can try it on Erlan… ha!"

Looking at his expression, Jace could more or less guess why Gakin had cast Curse of Recklessness on Surena.

But Jace wasn't interested in either of these spells. The Eye of Kilrogg… what would he need to control an eye for? If you were a rogue, a spy, an intelligence agent, you might need that little eye for covert work.

And Curse of Recklessness… from any angle, he had no use for it right now. Curse of Recklessness made people insanely brave and frenzied while boosting their combat power. In this world, you could cast it on your own friends to push them to their full combat potential.

But Jace would never cast it on Grede — Grede was reckless enough already. Hit him with a Curse of Recklessness and it might as well be a "send yourself to your death" curse.

"What's the third one?" he asked.

Gakin turned a page. "The third one I never managed to learn — I'll admit that. But the dwarves showed me this incantation, Jace. The dwarves call it the Blood Draw curse."

"Blood Draw curse?"

Jace thought: Drain Life?

"What exactly does it do?" he asked.

Gakin said: "This incantation draws a person's blood and converts it into your own blood. Sounds terrifying, right? But the effect isn't as strong as you'd think. I watched that dwarf master cast it on a goat. The spell lasted a while — the goat started shedding fur in patches, its skin went all wrinkled, but after about a minute, the goat still hadn't died. When the spell ended and they untied it, it struggled back to its feet. The dwarf master, on the other hand, was exhausted."

"In other words, the vitality you draw isn't even worth as much as the energy you spend, Jace. The reason I consider this incantation so precious is that it's fundamentally different from the others, isn't it? This one draws from a living person… just like the vampires of legend, utterly captivating. But… what a pity. Maybe its creator was aiming for the legendary vampire ideal and just didn't get the results they wanted."

Though Jace didn't know who had created the Drain Life incantation, he knew that in the game, every single Nathrezim dreadlord was a master of life-draining, and their Drain Life was absurdly powerful.

Which meant the incantation itself wasn't weak at all — Gakin and that dwarf had probably run into the same problem he'd had with his Curse of Weakness: the source of the incantation was inaccurate, so the effect when cast was terrible.

Which meant that even among Dark Iron dwarf warlocks, they were only a more authoritative source than this little warlock circle — not the absolute authority.

No matter. Jace was fairly certain this incantation was a demonic spell. When he got home, he could have Sazeno help proofread it and see where the problem was.

"I'll learn this one," he said.

"You're sure?" Gakin asked, a little skeptical.

"Positive."

"Seems you and I are cut from the same cloth, Jace, my friend. After all, chasing practicality at all costs is a desecration of art — so this Drain Life incantation… is it not the ultimate expression of dark art? So Erlan wasn't entirely wrong — some incantations really don't have enough power."

Jace nodded, though he had absolutely no desire to agree.

What good is magic that doesn't work? Why not just fire a cannon?

Once they'd reached an agreement, Jace wrote out the Curse of Weakness incantation in Common letters and handed it to Gakin, then walked him through the casting gestures and points to watch for when chanting. Gakin watched with intense focus.

After that, he tore the Drain Life page from the booklet and handed it to Jace.

"You're not keeping a copy?"

"It's just a duplicate — you're welcome to keep it," Gakin said. "The other incantations, Jace — if you want them, bring something valuable, or another incantation, just like today. We are, after all, a group that shares."
