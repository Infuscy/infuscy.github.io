Chapter 1041: The Magician on the Wall

Winter Veil had passed, and the time had already turned to a new year.

That morning, on the walls of Stormwind, a crowd of children were gathered around a patch of firelight that flickered to life from time to time, shouting excitedly.

"Why does the fire turn into this?!"

"So pretty!"

"How did you do that, mister? Can you teach me?"

"When you're a little older." Jace smiled at the little boy of maybe three or four years old in front of him, cradling the ball of flame in his hand and pinching it out.

At the same time, he flicked another ball of flame from his other hand into the air. This flame looked like a butterfly — or maybe a starling. The children, whose attention had just been on the flame dying in his palm, were suddenly startled by this and shouted excitedly all over again, drawing the curious gazes of the gryphons not far away.

Of course, Jace hadn't come here specifically to perform tricks for the kids running loose in the streets. Not long from now, he and his friends would be setting out for Booty Bay. By arrangement, Vereesa should be returning from Quel'Thalas soon as well.

These past two days, aside from waiting for Ursula to finish the Refined Wisdom Elixir, there wasn't much else to do in Stormwind. So when he got up in the morning he'd thought he'd come up onto the walls to see if he could wait for her blue dragonhawk.

Naturally, Jace hadn't planned to just kill time here idly. He'd of course brought along the Ignan elemental grimoire Rokhtus had given him.

It was a bleak winter's day. A cold wind swept across the ground covered in withered yellow leaves. Gazing out at the ripples on the lake at the bottom of the Valley of Heroes, accompanied by the expanse of green Elwynn Forest, with gryphons soaring out from atop the walls from time to time, and patrol cavalry crossing the bridge in neat formation with rhythmic hoofbeats…

Reading in this kind of environment was truly the height of comfort.

Of course, while studying, Jace also wanted to communicate with the fire elements all over again using the corrected pronunciation.

After leaving Shadowforge City, the free fire elements had become much warmer. Summoning flame wasn't so strenuous anymore. He barely had to move his lips and a flickering flame would appear at his fingertip, dissipating into the air as he shifted his attention.

He just hadn't expected that after casually practicing a few times, he'd attracted quite a few kids playing on the wall.

The Onyxia affair had already passed some time ago. The black dragons seemed to have no intention of attacking Stormwind again. The dark aura had dispersed under the diligent purification and healing of the priests in the church, and the atmosphere throughout the city had relaxed quite a bit.

Perhaps infected by the carefree manner of these children, Jace had simply set down his book and performed fire "magic" for them, amusing the lot of them into a state of curiosity and terror. Sometimes they got excited and pushed close to look carefully; other times they were frightened by a hint of ferocity leaking from Jace's fire magic and shoved each other backward to get away. Their antics had the guards and gryphon keepers on the wall laughing heartily.

"Mister, mister, I saw a powerful mage over by the Trade District who could summon beautiful stars! Can you do it?" a little girl asked curiously.

"Beautiful stars! I've never seen those," another child said.

"I've seen them, I've seen them, all shiny and bright…" another child chimed in.

"Isn't this fire fun to watch?" Jace asked.

"It's fun, but… we want to see stars!"

"Mister, we want to see stars!"

"Mister the mage, please!"

The children shouted excitedly. Jace glanced at the little girl who'd first brought it up and, helpless, went rummaging through his bag for his spell journal.

He felt like the last time he'd cast Light was in another life. That absurdly short incantation — he wasn't even confident he remembered it accurately.

After all, with the Song of the Blue Sky being such a convenient arcane torch, who'd waste mana casting Light?

But to fulfill the children's wish, there was no helping it — after all, he couldn't very well draw his sword in front of the kids.

Just as he awkwardly realized he didn't seem to have brought that little notebook he hadn't touched in Lord-knows-how-long, he suddenly sensed an arcane mana approaching silently from beside him, and his peripheral vision was already illuminated by a white glow.

He turned his head and saw the children mesmerized by an orb of arcane light that drifted slowly before their eyes, every single one of them stunned.

Looking toward the source of the light orb, a silver-haired elf in a simple green robe was holding up a fingertip wreathed in arcane glimmers, looking at his flustered self with the eyes of someone watching an idiot.

"It's an elf lady!"

Some child suddenly shouted, and the children all looked over, shouting excitedly along with them.

Vereesa, on the contrary, was thrown into a fluster by the children's enthusiasm. The arcane light orb she'd been guiding dissipated into thin air from the commotion. Compared to the "stars" that could be seen regularly near the Wizard Sanctum, the children seemed far more interested in the appearance of an elf.

After finally managing to shoo the children away, only the two of them were left on the wall again. Jace watched the children below the wall chasing after a candy vendor into the distance and said, "You stole all my thunder, Ve. Actually, I'm curious what spell that light orb was — how did you…"

Vereesa shrugged. "An underpowered Arcane Missile."

"So you threw an Arcane Missile at the children." Jace nodded slowly.

"Still a lot better than you being forced into a corner and firing off a Shadow Bolt just to save face," Vereesa said, giving him a sidelong glance. "Besides, I used to pull this trick on Lorlas all the time when I was little. I'm very familiar with it — no problem at all."

"How long were you watching from behind?" Jace narrowed his eyes.

"Long enough," Vereesa said with a smile. "After all, a sinister and terrifying demon warlock amusing children with fire tricks would be quite a rare sight even in Quel'Thalas. Come to think of it, what brought you up to the wall to play with the kids today?"

"Practicing flame mastery, of course." Jace snapped his fingers and a wisp of smoke drifted from his fingertip. "I just got back from Shadowforge City. I received guidance from a remarkably skilled sage of fire there, so I wanted to put it into practice as soon as possible."

"Then you picked a rather strange place for it, Jace Sesso." The ranger spoke softly, both hands coming to rest on the warlock's shoulders as she leaned in bit by bit. "You weren't waiting for me here, were you?"

"Hah." Jace said. "I'm so busy…"

Before he could finish, something slightly cool and warm pressed against his lips.

She lightly pursed her lips and whispered, "Sorry, Jace. The dragonhawk last time caused a small commotion, so the Farstriders reminded me not to casually bring them charging into Stormwind. I landed nearby and came in on foot."

"Well, I came up empty." Jace whispered. "How did you know I was here?"

"I am a Windrunner," Vereesa said, lifting her tone.

"Don't tell me you picked out my trail from tens of thousands of footprints on the streets of Stormwind," Jace said skeptically.

"Fine — Sola told me." Vereesa glanced toward a big tree not far away. "They're the ones who sold you out."

Jace sighed. "Sola's eyes and ears have surrounded the walls of Stormwind." The two of them shared a quiet laugh. Jace asked, "How's your preparation?"

"Don't forget what the Farstriders were founded for, Jace," Vereesa said. "There were Hakkar worshippers in the Amani Empire too, and Hakkar's power didn't help them stop our arrows and blades. I've brought along sacred oils for dealing with the trolls' soul-shamans, as well as some implements that can handle voodoo and blood magic."

Vereesa's words made Jace realize this would probably be their first real battle against trolls.

The forest troll they'd encountered in the underground spider nest in the Hinterlands, and the Gurubashi hunter they'd hunted through the jungles of the Cape of Stranglethorn — those had only been minor skirmishes. And even that minor skirmish had nearly cost Jace an axe to the forehead.

This time, delving deep into the lair of the most extreme and evil kind of trolls, they absolutely couldn't let their guard down — especially with green dragons to deal with at the same time.

"When do we set out?" Vereesa said. "I can't wait to kill a few more trolls." Jace said, "Tomorrow or the day after — be ready at any time. My potions should be finished today, more or less."
