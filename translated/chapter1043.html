Chapter 1043: The Silent Forest

As Solamiel flew over Elwynn Forest, despite trying to fly as high as she could, the flocks of sheep below a farm still panicked and scattered in every direction the moment they sensed her.

"I'm so sorry," she muttered under her breath. Grede, draped over her back, grunted. "Even though I'm not a warlock, I can tell you're enjoying this!"

The green dragon twisted her head around and flicked a serpentine tongue at the dwarf, wearing a what-are-you-going-to-do-about-it expression. Grede glared back and blew out his beard.

"Are you planning to go take a look over there, Jace?" Solamiel asked.

"You mean the Moonwell?" Jace said.

"What Moonwell?" Vereesa asked.

"A Moonwell the Kaldorei built in the mountain forest to the south," Solamiel explained as she flew. "The Kaldorei use the water in those Moonwells to purify things corrupted by demonic energy."

"Are you planning to jump in and take a bath yourself, Jace?" Grede asked.

"Fuck off." Jace shot him a sidelong glance, then asked, "Are you sure it's all right to go there, Sola? I've heard it's green dragon forbidden territory. The herbalists I know all say legend has it that expensive Dreamleaf grows in that forest, but none of them have ever dared go in and verify the rumor."

"My kin are worried that outsiders with impure intentions showing up there would disturb the peace of the woodland," Solamiel explained. "The guardian there will certainly know your names, and with me leading you in, they won't be suspicious. Besides, flying south through South Elwynn from here and continuing on toward Booty Bay isn't much of a detour."

"Then fine, let's go take a look," Jace said.

The green dragon veered slightly eastward, changing their route from the last time — when they'd flown along the Westfall coastline — and instead headed straight toward Raven Hill. From here, they could already see the hills in the center of Duskwood, blanketed by dense, oppressive forest.

Once they entered Duskwood, Solamiel grew noticeably quieter.

As she flew, she kept observing the dead forest below. Without anyone quite noticing, not only the forest but even the sky itself had turned dim and gloomy — as if they'd flown from early morning into dusk in the span of a few dozen minutes.

"I can't hear them. The trees, the flowers and grass here are all like silent strangers. They haven't died, but they might as well have." Grede glanced in the direction of Raven Hill. "Just like the ghouls in the cemetery."

"I don't want to put it that way," Solamiel said, "but this is corruption that no druid could ever hope to repair."

Of course Jace understood this too. The dark energy radiating from Karazhan's twisted Twisting Nether nexus couldn't possibly be reversed so easily.

However, as Solamiel gradually drew closer to the cluster of mountains in the middle of Duskwood, things seemed to shift, if only slightly.

The trees grew denser, and the darkness in the air thinned bit by bit. The suffocating shadow aura from outside was replaced by the damp mist and earthy scent of the forest.

When Solamiel flew past a massive oak tree thicker than a small monastery, Vereesa looked up at the canopy — a cloud of green — and breathed deep, her eyes half-closing as if drowsy, then snapping back open in alertness. She looked at Jace beside her. "This mist…"

"Makes you sleepy, but it doesn't feel like any kind of sleep spell," Jace said.

"That explains it," Grede said, slapping his own face. "I slept perfectly well last night… Say, are you two sure this is where we ought to be?"

"That's normal," Solamiel said. "The mist that protects these places has a sleep-inducing and hallucinogenic effect, especially at high altitude. It's meant to make outsiders turn back. Even if someone does wander in and falls asleep, we carry them out. Don't forget, I once guarded a place like this in Ashenvale too, Grede."

When she mentioned Ashenvale, Jace finally realized just how similar this place was to it. It was enough to make a person forget they were within the borders of the Kingdom of Stormwind, only a dozen or so miles from the Nafeltari River — as if they'd returned to Kaldorei territory.

After the green dragon brought everyone down to land, she exhaled a cloud of pink mist to blow the fog ahead aside, then shifted into her high elf form and beckoned. "Follow me."

Back when Jace had studied the geography around the Kingdom of Stormwind in the Wizard Sanctum, he'd learned that over a century ago, this place — the South Elwynn region, present-day Duskwood — had been an almost untouched primeval forest between the Gurubashi Empire and the Kingdom of Stormwind.

Let alone the area enclosed by forest tall enough to inspire dread — neither humans nor trolls had ever been willing to venture deep into that mysterious, silent place to find out what lay within.

Even years later, after humans had taken control of the region and fought an utterly brutal war against the Gurubashi trolls, not a single army had attempted to enter and see what was inside.

After the Kingdom of Stormwind's victory, the area became part of the kingdom, separating Raven Hill from Gelande. For decades, countless people had traveled the road through the middle, yet no one had ever tried to climb those hills, push through the dense forest, and see what was inside. Everyone seemed to tacitly pretend the forest didn't exist.

Not until the Orc Wars, when the dark energy from the Karazhan Cataclysm swept across all of South Elwynn, and the forest at the center appeared to have escaped the worst of the devastation, did it finally attract the interest of a small number of people. Many began calling it "Dawn Forest," in contrast to "Duskwood," the name the outside used for this place.

Sola walked ahead while the three followed behind. The mist that put mortals to sleep dissipated with each step she took. Grede looked up and couldn't help but gasp. "In the name of my ancestors…"

Jace looked up too, and through the fog he caught a glimpse of the flat rooftops of a structure supported by several enormous stone pillars.

As they drew closer, the pillars, high walls, and terraces began to reveal themselves one by one. They looked something like the ruins of an ancient Kaldorei empire — the kind they'd often seen in Kalimdor. Only these pillars weren't quite so "magnificent"; the style was more understated, and they didn't appear to have suffered any serious damage either. Or perhaps, being overgrown with plants and blanketed in moss and greenery, they'd lost some of their more striking features.

"Did the Highborne live here too?" Grede asked curiously.

"Our ancestors once possessed the whole world," Vereesa said. "It's not strange that they settled here."

Vereesa's statement wasn't exactly rigorous. After all, even at the height of the Kaldorei empire, they hadn't been able to wipe the trolls out entirely. Especially the eastern half of the ancient Kalimdor continent — what was now the Eastern Kingdoms — had still been occupied by trolls across vast stretches of land.

But that didn't mean these structures couldn't possibly be the work of the Highborne. In Jace's view, though, this looked more like the handiwork of the night elves. The place reminded him of Darnassus, the night elf capital in the game.

Of course, at this point in time, neither Darnassus nor Teldrassil, the great tree it stood upon, existed yet. But just as Sola had said earlier, thousands of years ago Kaldorei druids had once tried to plant an Andrassil seedling here. This was probably a temple or something similar built by those druids during that era.

He didn't jump in to contradict Vereesa outright — after all, Sola hadn't said anything, and she probably knew far better than the three of them. "Is that right, Sola?" Vereesa wanted to get Sola's confirmation too.

Hearing Vereesa's voice, Sola slowed her pace and looked back. In the thin, pale mist, the distant shapes beyond were hazy, reduced to little more than shadows.

"My kin should be here," Sola murmured softly. Jace, standing not far from her, heard it, but Grede behind them didn't seem to catch it clearly. He raised his voice and said, "What did you say, Sola?"

The moment those words left his mouth, Jace felt the mist in the distance seem to twist suddenly. A shadow came rushing closer with a whoosh of wind.

Vereesa immediately nocked an arrow. Grede grabbed the handle of the warhammer at his waist. But Sola raised her voice and signaled everyone not to panic, until the shadow broke through the nearby fog and appeared before them all.

It was a slightly smaller green hatchling. It growled warily at Jace's group. Not until Sola continuously spoke to it in draconic did the hatchling finally calm down.

The hatchling circled in midair, keeping its eyes fixed on Sola, while Sola asked it something in draconic.

After a while, Sola said, "The Moonwell here still exists, Jace. But the Dreamwarden here has been in a slumber. He needs that Moonwell to sustain his own strength and keep functioning. We can't use the well water under these circumstances — especially not to try to purify demonic power."

"When will he wake up?" Jace asked.

Sola shook her head. "He's helping Queen Alexstrasza and Queen Ysera cleanse the corruption in the Emerald Dream."

"All right," Jace said. "Looks like we'll have to find another way."

Sola said, "I was thinking — if we could rescue Prince Eranikus…"

Before she could finish, Sola fell silent as if something had just occurred to her. Vereesa raised a finger. "That's exactly right, Sola. If we could rescue the green dragons trapped inside the temple, the corruption in the Emerald Dream would definitely be resolved more easily. I think the green dragons might even be willing to use the power of this Moonwell and this valley to help the humans of Duskwood."

"Assuming they're still alive," Grede muttered.

Vereesa shot the dwarf an unhappy glare, but to her surprise, Sola was the one who said, "If they truly have died, at least they won't become sacrifices used to bring an Old God into the world."

Jace could feel that the lightness Sola had brought when she'd set out with the three of them not long ago had been thoroughly swept away by the current topic.
