Chapter 1044: Atal'ai Hexcraft

After arriving at Booty Bay, Grede and Vereesa took Sola to the Sailor's Home inn to rest, while Jace went straight to Sylia's place.

He knocked on the door and after a long while the witch doctor peered out through the little peephole. Jace met her yellow eye and asked, "How's the potion coming?"

"Finished a few days ago," the troll said with a chuckle as she opened the door. "The brewing process went more smoothly than I expected, Jace Sesso. I think perhaps the gods themselves don't want Hakkar brought into this world, and so they were willing to grant a little extra blessing to help me finish the work ahead of time."

"And you still had the nerve to charge money?" Jace asked.

"I wouldn't say no to other forms of payment, Jace Sesso," Sylia said, leaning against the wall and baring her one small tooth in a smile.

"Vereesa Windrunner is still here, Sylia," Jace said, looking at the troll. "Unless you want the residents of Booty Bay to wake up tomorrow morning and see both our heads hanging over by the outer harbor."

Sylia rubbed her thick fingers across her neck, coughed twice, and said, "I should thank you for not bringing that girl to my place. I remember when she was born, all of Zinsalo was talking about the Windrunner family's new member, hoohoo. Though she herself turned out to be less threatening than I'd imagined. And the whole world has changed its face now, after all."

The witch doctor walked back into her house and brought out two large waterskins, along with several smaller pouches.

"The big skins hold the potion that lets you see ghosts. The small pouches hold the medicine that counteracts the Atal'ai's dark magic. But I must warn you, Jace Sesso — this medicine can counter the spells of the Atal'ai priests, but it cannot counter the power of Hakkar himself."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked. "Those Atal'ai have successfully summoned Hakkar? When?"

"I can't say for certain," the troll said in a low voice. "I mean — what if? After all, they've already captured so many green dragons. Dragon blood is so powerful; what if it suits the Blood God's tastes perfectly…"

She looked at Jace's slightly dazed expression and grinned even wider.

Jace shot her a cold look. "If Hakkar truly descended, would those Atal'ai still be content to stay in their temple playing with the dead? They'd definitely seize the moment and counterattack the Gurubashi. But I clearly haven't heard any news coming out of Zul'Gurub."

"That's natural. I'm just considering every possible scenario for my client," Sylia said with a shrug. "How can I be sure this medicine will work?" Jace asked.

"We can test it on the spot," Sylia said. "I can't chant incantations as easily as an Atal'ai priest with enough power and will, but I should be able to cast it through a few special casting materials. It might just require a small fee — say… two gold."

"Two gold?" Jace asked skeptically. "What are you going to use?"

Sylia counted on her fingers. "Two vials of Greater Fel Essence, a spoonful of wind serpent scale fragments, and a small vial of blood of the sacrificed. Trust me, if you tried to buy these things on the outside, two gold wouldn't be nearly enough. And if you think that's too expensive, Jace Sesso, you should know that I am absolutely the last troll in the world who wants Hakkar brought into this world. I can't possibly trick you in there and hand you to Hakkar's worshippers as fresh sacrifices. So I'm not lying to you."

Jace pulled out two gold coins and handed them to her. "Then it's settled."

"You're not worried I'll use magic to control you and make you do things against your will?" Sylia asked with a smile.

"Not worried," Jace said.

"You really are confident in your willpower," the troll said with a nod.

Jace took off the Chain of Will around his neck and casually handed it to Morophil, who had appeared out of nowhere. "I have confidence in her."

Sylia was startled by the suddenly appearing succubus and swore something in trollish. Her whole body shuddered and she stumbled back several steps before steadying herself. She stared at Morophil for a long while before saying, "Damn it, next time at least warn me that there's a demon hiding near me."

"My apologies, Ms. Sylia," Morophil said, holding the iron chain and leaning to one side. "I thought we were old acquaintances."

"Don't you dare move around, demon. I don't want your stench tainting my things." The troll witch doctor swallowed, pocketed the gold, and turned back inside.

A while later, Sylia came out carrying a basket. She placed a coarse cloth doll on a stone platform, put on a wooden mask, and dipped her fingers in fel essence and thick, vile blood in turn, writing out some incantation runes around the doll.

Jace of course couldn't read a word of the troll script, but he looked on out of curiosity. He hadn't been watching long before the coarse cloth doll seemed to move, and along with it all the scattered casting materials and strange statues in the room seemed to come alive.

Though they sat motionless on the surface, to a warlock like Jace who was sensitive to energy, those shriveled beast heads hanging on the walls and the small, crudely shaped statues on the shelves all seemed to be staring at him — every last one of them.

Just as Jace was getting thoroughly uncomfortable, Sylia raised the cloth doll and began chanting incantations in the troll tongue. After just a few lines, a bizarre, unreal wailing sound came from the doll's mouth. Its lips didn't move, yet the voice was incredibly real — enough to make a person's skin crawl.

As it wailed, even the pair of eyes made from some unknown material began to shed bloody tears. Jace was starting to suspect Sylia was using some kind of hex to create hallucinations for him.

And that wasn't all. For a brief moment, Sylia, who had been at a small stone platform not far away, suddenly flashed right in front of him, just staring at him.

The sensation grew clearer, more real by the degree. Gradually, he even began to feel a floating sensation, as if it weren't the doll Sylia was holding but himself, and she was saying some very comforting things to soothe him.

A surge of intense unease and grief filled his heart, making him frown involuntarily as he tried his best not to think about those things. Sylia's words made him feel a little better — but then he realized he shouldn't have been able to understand the troll tongue at all.

"That's enough, Sylia," Jace said in a low voice. "I want to try that medicine."

Even as he said it, he heard the flat-faced, smooth-skinned, brown-furred alien creature beside him open its mouth and say something in a deep, unintelligible voice.

The moment he made out the creature's appearance, a sharp jolt of alarm shot up his back.

"Now, Morophil — give me the medicine!" he tried to shout, but all that came out was a scream worthy of a small child.

The demon covered in garish tattoos and fel light reached over and fed him some of the potion. A fiery, fishy stench sprayed into his nostrils. He swallowed hard, and immediately a rotting-corpse stench welled back up from his throat. He felt like the smell alone was enough to make his eyes water.

Jace suddenly felt as if he'd plummeted from midair and slammed into the ground with a bang. His backside, his feet, his hands all touched something solid, and his whole body became incredibly heavy.

A purple shadow flame ignited before his eyes, accompanied by a burst of mad, hysterical laughter. Sylia hurled the ball of flame to the ground and stomped it out again and again with her big feet.

When the noise died down, the troll braced her waist and straightened up, laughing twice. "You really are a human with terrifying willpower. You were practically turning into the doll and you still remembered the potion… But with you like this, I seriously doubt you could even feel whether my medicine worked. Maybe you should call a friend over to check."

"I felt it, clearly and without a doubt," Jace said with a nod. "So this is the Atal'ai witch doctors' mind-control magic?"

"It can't be exactly the same," the troll said. "Blood magic can be realized in so many ways. Who knows where the Atal'ai will apply the gifts of the Soulflayer? But I can guarantee that no matter what form the hex takes, this medicine will have some countering effect. Remember to drink it ahead of time — the effects last roughly six to seven hours. Drinking it after you've already been cursed is often too late, because against those priests' tricks, it's very hard to judge when the spell actually starts to take effect, unlike with my ritual…"

"All right," Jace said, glancing at the pouches again. "Anything else I should know?"

"Nothing else," Sylia said. "When it comes to slaughtering trolls, I imagine your dear Vereesa Windrunner is far more professional than I am. In any case, just remember — don't die in there, Jace Sesso. And also, the thing I want — the skull of Hakkar's offspring."

"I haven't forgotten," Jace said with a nod. "Thanks, Sylia."
