Chapter 1045: Revisiting Old Ground

Flying from the Elwynn region to Booty Bay, then setting off northeast toward the Blackmire, actually made for a major detour — the journey more than doubled in length.

But made up for by Solamiel's flying speed, a detour of this magnitude was still acceptable. And it wasn't without its advantages, actually. There was one huge benefit: it allowed them to bypass Karazhan.

For Solamiel, she'd rather cut straight across the Gurubashi Empire's present territory, flying right over the capital of Zul'Gurub, than skirt anywhere near Karazhan — especially after having entered Duskwood and felt the dark energy it was radiating.

Of course, choosing this route didn't necessarily mean they had to cross through Zul'Gurub.

Ever since the bulk of their main force had been blasted away by Medivh's destructive magic beneath the walls of Stormwind, the Gurubashi trolls had shrunk back into Zul'Gurub and the few small towns around it, rarely venturing out.

Apart from the occasional explorer or herb-gathering witch doctor, the trolls no longer sought to expand their influence across all of Stranglethorn. So even in the mountains east of Zul'Gurub that bore the Gurubashi name, there weren't many jungle trolls to be found.

Which meant flying through these mountains right now shouldn't pose any danger.

Solamiel followed the mountain range northward. Looking east, there was a vast stretch of deforested, ravaged wasteland left behind from the orc invasion, forming a stark contrast with the deep green that filled the other side of the mountains.

The Orc Wars had been over for five or six years now, and the Horde had already abandoned this forest during the war's duration. But even after nearly a decade, the natural landscape here showed no signs of recovery at all — it still looked like a freshly burned-over wasteland, all gray and lifeless.

Because the Dark Portal wasn't far off anymore, even though nothing could be seen in the northeast from here, its presence could still be clearly felt.

Morophil beside him kept staring in that direction. For demons like them, that kind of powerful planar gateway — even destroyed — radiated enough influence to make any planar traveler feel awe deep in their bones.

Jace couldn't help but glance east from time to time too, toward the gloomy sky that still seemed to lie beyond the horizon.

A few years ago, every time he'd passed through Redridge for one reason or another, he couldn't resist looking toward the Dark Portal, or toward Karazhan.

Of course, back then, aside from vaguely sensing the dark energy within, the more important thing was that for him, these were important locations he'd seen in his past life and that had become the stuff of legend in this one.

But now the meaning was different.

They would have to head to the other side eventually — or at least make the attempt…

And the most important objective of this trip to the Temple of Atal'Hakkar was to build up the mana needed to summon the Gate-Opener again. Compared to when they'd first set out from Booty Bay, the atmosphere on the dragon's back had grown much quieter.

Vereesa kept her eyes on the green dragon's heading, or occasionally gazed west at the deep green hiding Zul'Gurub, stubbornly refusing to look toward the Dark Portal.

Grede kept his head down and said nothing, his hand rubbing the blue emblem on his warhammer.

Jace didn't need to guess to know they were all thinking the same thing — crossing the Dark Portal.

The scenery split by the Gurubashi Mountains didn't finally start to improve until the second day. When Jace opened his eyes, the horizon where sky and distant earth met was still an indistinguishable blur, but below them it seemed to have turned deep green.

Solamiel kept her head down observing the situation below, so her flying speed had slowed a bit. Grede was still snoring away, and Vereesa had just woken up.

"Where are we?" she asked.

"Just entered the marsh not long ago," Solamiel said.

Jace looked around. Though there was no way to pin down their exact position from here, if they'd just entered the marsh and couldn't see the shadow of Nethergarde Keep from here, then they should have just entered the southwestern edge of the Blackmire.

He still remembered that in the game, the green dragon Itharius had taken the form of a High Elf and stayed in a cave near here, trying to get in touch with anyone who might be able to help — to go to the Temple of Atal'Hakkar and save his brother Eranikus.

Jace had never mentioned this to anyone before, partly because he shouldn't know about it, but also because he felt that if Itharius was still lingering in the Blackmire at this point in time, then Solamiel passing through should have sensed him right away and taken the initiative to make contact.

Whether it was to stop her from heading to the Pool of Tears to throw her life away, or to find more allies.

"Can you sense any green dragons here, Sola?" Jace asked.

"We're still far from the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, Jace. We need to fly east for nearly another day," Solamiel said.

"Maybe we shouldn't fly too close," Vereesa cautioned. "If there really are trolls or green dragons guarding the place, then using the dense marsh forest as cover might be better. Up high we'd alert everyone and conveniently become a target."

"Right," Solamiel agreed. "So it might take even longer, Jace. Have you seen any green dragons around here before?"

"No," Jace said, shaking his head. "I was thinking there might be some that escaped."

Solamiel fell silent for a moment. "I can't feel anything at all. If there really were any, they might be deliberately hiding their presence to avoid being hunted by the trolls."

Vereesa said, "East of here, roughly in the middle, there's a fairly large Alliance outpost called Stonard. Those trolls definitely wouldn't dare cross that outpost to pursue anyone."

Jace agreed with both of their points. It seemed Itharius really wasn't here — the situation was different at this stage.

"We could stop at Stonard for the night first," Jace said. "You've been flying for two straight days from Booty Bay, Sola. We're heading to that temple next — finding a proper place to get a good night's sleep is necessary."

"I agree too," Vereesa said, then glanced at Grede, who was still snoring. "He'd definitely agree."

"All right," Solamiel said.

The green dragon followed the direction Jace indicated, following a stretch of hills until evening, when they could see wisps of smoke rising faintly ahead.

They landed early and switched to walking through the marsh to avoid alarming Stonard's griffin patrols. Before long, they saw blue and white Alliance and Lordaeron banners fluttering above a wooden wall, shrouded in the damp yellow fog.

In that endless, deep, dark marsh, those banners felt incredibly familiar — even more so to a Lordaeronian like Jace.

The guards at Stonard naturally recognized Jace's group, so they were let through without any questions. When the commander stationed at Stonard heard Jace had arrived, he even came out to greet them personally.

"Hello, Master Jace Sesso, Lady Vereesa Windrunner, and Lord Grede Thunderfist. I am Captain Wadmar, stationed at Stonard by order of King Terenas II Menethil. It is an honor to meet you."

The commander was a young officer with light brown hair, thirty years old at most. After he finished speaking, he looked at Sola beside them and said, "And this is…"

"Sola Shade. I'm a botanical consultant for the Wizard Sanctum," Sola said with a smile.

"Hello, Ms. Sola," the captain said with a salute.

"Hello, Captain Wadmar," Jace said, glancing at Vereesa beside him before continuing. "We've come here to investigate the rumors that have been circulating about an evil dragon in the marsh. I don't know if you've heard the related tales."

"Of course, Master Sesso," Wadmar replied. "That there are some manner of terrible monsters deep in the Blackmire — dragons and the like — is a legend that's existed since before the Dark Portal opened. It's been circulating quite widely lately too. But at least the griffin riders and I haven't discovered anything unusual yet — perhaps we haven't explored deeply enough. Does this have anything to do with the black dragon attack in Stormwind earlier?"

"It's unclear at the moment," Jace said. "So there haven't been any dragons around Stonard at all? No hatchlings, drakes, or adult dragons?"

In the game, Itharius was caring for a group of green whelps near his cave, and some of those whelps flew quite far. If Stonard's soldiers patrolled the area regularly, they should have noticed.

But hearing Jace ask with such certainty, Wadmar instead looked a touch uneasy.

After hesitating for a moment, he said, "So the Kingdom of Stormwind has confirmed that there are dragons in the deep marsh that pose a serious threat to us? But I confess, Master, that I know nothing about this. It may be that our reconnaissance work has fallen far short."

"No, you've misunderstood. Nothing's confirmed yet — that's exactly why we're here, to verify it," Jace said.

"I see, Master. You really did give me a scare," the captain said with an awkward laugh.

"What about trolls?" Grede asked. "Have there been any trolls lurking around here?"

Wadmar frowned. "News of trolls appearing here, Lord Grede? I wouldn't know. I haven't seen a single troll since leaving Hillsbrad."

"We were hoping to stay the night here at Stonard, Captain," Vereesa said. "I wonder if you have any spare rooms?"

"Of course," Wadmar said with a nod. "Please, follow me."
