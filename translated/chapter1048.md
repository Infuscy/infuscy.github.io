## Chapter 1048: Among the Mushrooms

The forest was already blanketed in dark-green poison mushrooms. Jace stood beside Sola, and from time to time he'd hear a muffled explosion from one of the mushrooms somewhere, followed by a cloud of yellow fog spreading out.

After one such explosion, a dwarf's curse followed. Jace shouted in that direction, "Over here, Grede!"

A few exchanged words later, two shadows — one tall, one short — appeared through the trees. When Vereesa saw the green dragon lying collapsed in the marsh, she ran over immediately.

"Is Sola hurt?"

"I'm fine, Wen…"

Solamiel murmured in a low voice. For a hatchling this enormous, her voice wasn't even as clear as it was in her mortal form. Jace rummaged in his pack and asked, "I only have a few small vies of Lesser Healing Potion here. Does anyone have something stronger?"

"I've got one," Grede said, pulling a vial of deep-red Greater Healing Potion from his waist and bringing it to the green dragon's mouth.

Solamiel raised her head slightly and opened her mouth. Grede poured the medicine in. Just looking at the stark contrast between that small amount of potion and the massive, python-like head, Jace didn't hold out much hope for the potion's effectiveness.

But after she drank it, it wasn't long before the wounds on the green dragon's body that had been seeped with poison no longer looked like they were worsening, and her breathing became a little smoother.

Still, Grede shook his head and sighed. "If you fed this potion to me, I reckon I could have my head chopped off and grow a new one. Fed to Sola and you can't see much effect at all…"

As he said it, he pulled out another vial, but the green dragon immediately closed her mouth and moved her lips, murmuring, "I… feel much better, Grede. Let me rest a while… and I'll be fine."

She turned her eyes slightly to look at the dwarf and lowered her eyelids, as if giving some kind of affirmation. The dwarf could only sigh.

"Is that dragon dead?" Jace asked.

"Dead," Vereesa said in a low voice. "We pierced her heart, and that fallen undead monster quickly fell apart into a pile of rotting flesh. It nearly buried both me and Grede and corroded us into something just like her."

"That would be you," Grede coughed. "I'd definitely crawl out unscathed."

"Suit yourself." Vereesa pulled a small vial of dark-brown liquid of indeterminate nature from her waist and handed it to Jace. "Look, Jace. This is that green dragon's blood… I remember you mentioned that your alchemist friend needed to green dragons' blood from here. Have a look. If it's usable, I can take you to where the corpse is."

Jace took the vial, opened it, and sniffed. "If I hadn't seen that dragon, I wouldn't believe this was dragon blood. This won't do — she's probably already been corrupted into something else."

Grede stood up and said, "Yeah, just look at the state she was in earlier. Flying through the sky she looked just like a green-headed fly bigger than a house. If she looked like that on the outside, then inside the temple…"

Jace glanced at Solamiel lying beside them. She was silent and hadn't responded.

He remembered that in the game, the green dragons inside the temple didn't seem to have changed much. They'd simply lost their reason, become hostile to friend and foe alike, and were lost in the Nightmare, unable to wake — they hadn't been corrupted into undead creatures the way this dragon had.

Of course, that was no reason for him to let his guard down about what was inside.

"Are either of you hurt?" he asked.

"I'm fine," Grede said.

"Me too," Vereesa said.

Jace rubbed his backside, which had only just started to ache — probably from getting whacked by some tree trunk when he'd fallen from the sky earlier.

Then he noticed that Grede's stance seemed a bit off, and Vereesa was standing with one leg bearing slightly less weight, though both of their faces wore relaxed expressions.

"Let's rest here a while first," Jace said. "Sola needs time to recover, and she needs to eat something."

"All right," Vereesa said with a nod. "Then I'll go see if there's anything edible."

"Better let me and Morophil go," Jace said. "You and Grede stay here and protect Sola, and rest while you're at it. I didn't actually expend much effort after falling down."

Vereesa glanced at the dragon claw, already smashed and lying in pieces some distance away. "So you're going to tell me this dragon's claw wasn't attached properly and fell off on its own?"

"Stop arguing," Grede said, raising both hands. "The bog-green dragon, the limping elf, and the human with a swollen backside can stay here and rest. Let the professional hunter's son come save you all."

He drew a knife from Vereesa's waist. "And I have enough strength to carry a marsh crocodile back on my back. Can you do that, little elf?"

"I could make one walk back with me nicely," Vereesa said.

"More evil than a witch deep in the marsh, and I still don't see what there is to brag about," Grede muttered as he walked deeper into the forest.

"Accompany him, Morophil," Jace said, giving the succubus a meaningful look. "There might still be whelps in the forest. Don't let him walk into an illusion spell alone."

"Yes, master," Morophil said, turning invisible and following.

Solamiel seemed to have recovered somewhat by now. She slowly crawled out of the bog and moved to the bank nearby, settling her body down again against a thick tree trunk.

Throughout the whole process, Jace and Vereesa could only watch — they simply didn't have the strength to move a monster this huge.

"I'm sorry we had to do that, Sola," Vereesa said. "About that dragon. I heard her call your name."

"I'm a green dragon too, Vereesa," Solamiel said slowly. "I know what my brothers and sisters are thinking. If she were still alive as she once was, she would loathe her current state even more than we do, and she would want to kill her present-day self with her own hands. If her soul can feel all of this, she'll be happy. She'll be grateful for your help."

After she finished speaking, she coughed painfully a few times. Jace said, "Rest a little longer, Sola. I'll wake you when Grede and the others get back."

The green dragon's eyes flicked toward Jace. She let out a low "mmmph" and lowered her head.

Vereesa looked back and forth at the wounds on the green dragon's wings and belly. "How did she get hurt?"

"She was tangled up by those vines — she was probably the first to come into contact with that monster," Jace said. "But I think the most serious injuries are from those mushrooms. I have a feeling she crashed into at least four or five of them as she fled over here."

"Good lord," the ranger said with a frown.

But it wasn't long before rumbling snores came from the green dragon's direction. Seeing how soundly she was sleeping, the tension on both of their faces eased a little.

"Almost as loud as Grede," Vereesa said with a smile.

"I can hear you, Vereesa Windrunner," the green dragon rumbled.

Vereesa was startled and froze for a moment. "Uh, sorry, I didn't…"

"She can't hear you," Jace said, sitting to one side. "She's talking in her sleep."
