## Chapter 1049: A Land of Sorrow

Although this marsh was called the Blackmire, to Jace — who had spent several weeks in the Burning Steppes and Shadowforge City — the hazy light cast by the sun and the misty, glowing fog throughout the marsh were already enough for him to call it "a lovely morning."

The previous evening, after Grede came back, the four of them had eaten their fill of crab by the bog.

Of course, a "crab feast" didn't sound as grand as the crocodile feast Grede had promised earlier, but the giant marsh crabs of the Swamp of Sorrows were nothing like the river crabs Jace had seen in his past life. Never mind the massive claws bigger than a person's head and the arms and legs thicker than a person's legs — the shell in the middle was so plump that staring at it made you feel less like you were looking at an arthropod and more like you were looking at an ogre's suit of armor.

When Grede dragged the creature back, Jace watched him from a distance, striding along like one of the "Sons of Lothar" setting out from Stormwind for the Dark Portal, and he knew the dwarf must have hauled back something enormous.

The whole night, Jace gnawed on a single crab leg and still felt stuffed before he'd even finished it. Grede ate one crab claw. Vereesa actually cleaned a few legs down to nothing. The rest was swallowed whole by the green dragon in one gulp — she didn't even spit out the crab shell at the end.

Of course, just watching Solamiel crunch that giant crab up with satisfaction and swallow it down made Jace feel even fuller than the dragon.

After chatting about trivial matters for a while and telling some legends about this marsh — especially the things that had happened here during the Dark Portal campaign — the group one by one lay down beside Solamiel and fell asleep.

Jace looked around. Grede was sleeping soundly, but the massive green dragon beside him had disappeared.

Morophil was keeping watch, hidden in a tree nearby. When Jace woke, he could feel a trace of pleased warmth slowly seeping over through the Soul Link.

That was Morophil's greeting. So Jace smiled and nodded at the blurry shadow high up in his Detect Demon sight in response.

Morophil hadn't warned him of anything, which meant the green dragon shouldn't have gone far, and Vereesa should be nearby too. Jace stood up and glanced around, then heard a softly chanted incantation coming from behind a tree not far away.

He walked over and saw the golden-haired elf holding the Staff of Domination with both hands, head bowed, murmuring something. Vereesa was also watching her from not far off.

Hearing Jace's footsteps, Sola looked this way and pointed directly at him. Jace immediately felt a refreshing current of air flow over him from head to toe, and the last traces of drowsiness from just waking up were swept away completely.

"Did we wake you, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

"Not at all," Jace said. "What are you two doing here…"

"This staff really can strengthen my mental power, Jace," Sola said. "I'm gradually adapting to its unique arcane energy. Can you feel the change in my magic?"

Jace wanted to say something nice to comfort Sola, but he honestly couldn't put his finger on it. He could at least sense arcane energy to some degree, but as for the strength of natural power…

"I'm completely clueless when it comes to green dragon magic…"

Sola laughed. "Regardless, it really is powerful. The Dark Iron dwarf wasn't lying to us about that."

"Jace, didn't you say you went to the Dark Iron dwarves to get back that staff with the mirror stone?" Vereesa said, leaning against a nearby tree and glancing at the Staff of Domination in the green dragon's hands. "Then what's the deal with this one? Even by Quel'Thalas standards this staff is… well, perhaps I still lack understanding of the Magisters, but I've truly never seen many staffs more powerful than this one."

"An apology gift," Jace said, tilting his head slightly and explaining in a natural tone. "Those Dark Iron dwarves felt quite guilty about some of the unfriendly attitudes they'd shown, so they gave this staff as compensation."

Vereesa asked, "Dark Iron dwarves, feeling guilty? I'd rather believe that after my cold war with Sylvanas, she'd be the one to back down first." Sola laughed. "Though Jace's explanation is a bit simplified, that's more or less how it is…"

Vereesa gave her a sidelong glance and could only nod with suspicion.

"Mmm." Jace said, "Though I can't feel how powerful this staff is, at least I can tell you're recovering well, Sola. How do you feel yourself?"

Sola lowered her head and lifted the hem of her green dress. "This morning I tried healing myself. Look — I can already shift back into a high elf form again. Not only that, I think I can fly again. We can leave whenever we want. Thank you for that Greater Healing Potion, Grede. Even though he thinks the effect was poor, it really pulled me back from an extremely dangerous state. Without that potion, I don't know if I could have held on through that corruption, as weak as I was."

"And thank you too, Jace," she added in a low voice.

"If we had to say thank you every time we helped each other, you'd probably lose your voice for the next while," Jace said.

"That's true," Vereesa said with a smile. "Still, I wouldn't recommend flying for the rest of the journey. I know it might not be ideal for your recovery, Sola, but you'd better stay in your current form."

"Are there more things like that deeper in the marsh?" Sola asked softly.

"I don't know. And that's the most critical part — we don't know," Vereesa said.

Jace nodded. "She's right, Sola. You flying up in the sky might make too conspicuous a target. And according to the maps from Stonard, even if we go on foot, it'll take three days at most to reach that pool."

Sola said, "I understand. Actually, I'm basically fine now. Even without shifting back into a dragon, I can recover bit by bit."

Jace let out a long breath and looked around at the gloomy forest. "Then the next problem is getting past these mushrooms."

"Don't worry, Jace," Sola said, walking forward with her staff. "Just follow me."

It turned out that although the corrupted dragon had used her power to make these mushrooms grow at terrifying speed, after she died the mushrooms no longer obeyed her commands to explode or keep growing. They'd become nothing more than a vast field of giant fungi, no different from wild ones.

This way, Sola could communicate with them, calming them down so they were no longer sensitive to things that touched or even approached them, allowing the group to pass through safely.

Of course, at first Jace, Grede, and Vereesa all felt deeply uncomfortable getting close to these mushrooms, as if they might be blown to bits at any moment and splashed with highly corrosive venom to boot. But with Sola leading the way ahead, softly murmuring something as she went, the mushrooms not only didn't explode — some of them even obediently moved their crooked mushroom caps aside to clear a path for the group. Gradually, everyone relaxed.

The Blackmire was full of bogs and streams, most of which flowed east, eventually emptying into the Pool of Tears — where Queen Ysera had submerged the Temple of Atal'Hakkar — or into the sea. So as long as they followed the direction of the streams, they wouldn't get lost.

Leaving the woodland occupied by the corrupted dragon's mushrooms and pressing deeper, by evening Jace had begun to faintly hear whispers drifting from some unknown direction.

The voice sounded like the troll tongue, and there were even occasional teeth-grinding noises mixed in. Jace couldn't understand a word of the specifics.

After a cold wind blew past, the whispers carried in the wind sounded once more. Jace asked in a low voice to Vereesa ahead and behind him, "Can you hear that?"

"I can," Vereesa said with a nod.

"Those are the voices of troll ghosts, Jace. Don't worry," Grede said from ahead, swinging his warhammer as he walked. "I heard sounds like this in the Hinterlands. Trolls sacrificing souls to their gods… Those sounds linger around for all sorts of reasons — like excessively evil methods of sacrifice — for a very, very long time."

"If you ask me," the dwarf said, turning his head, "not only is there nothing to worry about, you should actually be reassured. Because it means we're going the right way, and getting closer to where we need to be."

Vereesa also gave an affirmative look. Having two veterans who'd tangled with trolls for so long in the group was quite reassuring.

But the reassuring feeling lasted two seconds at most. Vereesa suddenly turned her head to look behind them.

Sola sensed something amiss and turned back too. Grede was just about to ask what was wrong when the ranger raised a hand, signaling Grede and Sola not to make any moves or say anything, while giving Morophil beside her a meaningful look.

Morophil exchanged a glance with Jace. Jace nodded too. She then turned invisible and disappeared into the undergrowth.

The group continued forward, chatting about the situation with the trolls in the Hinterlands. A few minutes later, a hoarse screech suddenly came from the forest behind them.

They immediately chased the sound back, and before long they saw Morophil dragging a brown-black monster over.

"Look what I caught, master," Morophil said, glancing back at the monster. "Guess what I sensed on him? He seems to have a very strong connection to the Eredar, yet he isn't a demon."

The brown monster screamed and struggled with all his strength, but couldn't break free from the succubus's grip at all. He didn't look much like an Eredar — more like a small, deformed orc with hooves, his body wrapped in tattered cloth corrupted and ruined by fel energy. The sounds he made were as piercing as a pig about to be slaughtered.

"If my earlier senses weren't wrong, this thing has been following us for a while," Vereesa said.

Jace knew what it was. They were called the "Lost Ones." During the Dark Portal campaign, Alliance soldiers had discovered these Lost Ones living in the marsh. But because no serious conflicts had arisen, the two sides had generally coexisted peacefully enough during that period — after all, they shared a common enemy, the Burning Legion.

These creatures had once been Draenei — that small branch of Eredar who had fled rather than become demons. That was why Morophil could sense the Eredar presence on them.

"They're also monsters that came over from the other side of the Dark Portal, same as the orcs," Grede said. "It's not strange you can sense a demonic aura, Morophil."

"What should we do with him?" Vereesa asked.

Jace looked at him and said in Demonic, "We're not here to violate your territory. We're just passing through, no ill intent. But if you keep following us, don't blame us for not giving you a chance."

Hearing Jace speak the Eredar's language, the Lost One was stunned for a moment, then suddenly went berserk and started howling.

"Dark Portal! Destroyed… Draenor is destroyed! Destroyed! All demons, Draenei, orcs — dead! Dead!" What caught Jace off guard was that he was howling in Common.

Vereesa heard it and a fury shot straight to her head. She grabbed him by the collar, snatched him from Morophil's hands, and pressed a dagger to his throat. "What did you say?!"

"Dead… no one can pass… stay… forever, forever!" the Lost One murmured, then let out a series of sounds that seemed like laughter — "chi chi chi ha ha ha."

His vacant, insect-like, dull eyes, paired with his bizarre locust-like face and that string of noise-like words, made everyone present deeply uncomfortable.

"He doesn't know what he's saying, Wen," Jace said. "These people have been tortured to the point where they've lost their memories and their sanity. Killing him serves no purpose. Let him go."

Vereesa gritted her teeth and hurled the creature aside. The Lost One immediately fled frantically into the forest, crying as he ran, until he disappeared completely into the depths of the woods.

"He wants to go home too," Grede murmured.

Vereesa, who'd still been full of fury, sighed when she heard the dwarf say that.
