Chapter 105: Nathrezim Grammar

Life Drain…

That night, back home, Jace had planned to get a good night's sleep and take his time with it the next day — but his mind kept turning over that piece of paper, the spell's effect, and he couldn't fall asleep no matter what.

He wanted to see whether he could refine this terribly underperforming Life Drain incantation into a real spell. After all, his original dream had been to sort out these half-baked warlock incantations and reconstruct them into their original texts, using his knowledge of demonic and orcish and all the other spell-languages.

He sat up abruptly. "Sazeno, get out here. We've got work to do."

"Why more work?!" Sazeno grumbled, crawling out of the bag. "There's no more paper! Sazeno is so tired!"

"Different work." Jace climbed out of bed and pulled the pages Jagin had given him from under the bed, studying the line of incantation carefully.

"Elamun, Siliento."

"What are you saying?" the little imp asked curiously.

"You don't understand that?"

"Why would I understand that?!" The imp grabbed Jace's paper and looked at it. "Ah, demonic written in Common letters. The pronunciation is completely different — how could anyone read it accurately?" Jace pressed: "So what does this demonic text mean?"

"Is this an incantation? I can't tell." The imp looked it over. "This seems to be… conversion, transfer, vitality or something — but as an incantation the grammar's off. This reads like the Nathrezim's ancient language!"

"Don't tell me you don't know Nathrezim stuff." Hearing that, Jace was already starting to despair. He rubbed the bridge of his nose. This little imp was unreliable too, huh.

"Who says I don't know it?!" The imp looked again. "I don't know a lot of Nathrezim vocabulary, but if you read it their way, there should be a liaison here — there needs to be a liaison between vitality and conversion! So it would be… Elamun, Siliondol! Also, you need to pronounce it like the big-winged Nathrezim do — vibrate your throat, push the sound lower, and channel your shadow energy as hard as you can. After all, those fellows are made of condensed shadow and flesh. They're not ordinary demons!"

"You really are an expert," Jace couldn't help but marvel.

"Only mortals with miserable fates get impressed by this kind of knowledge, my master!" the imp said, bouncing around. "Live long enough and you learn things!"

"Tonight I won't be able to sleep unless I test this out." Jace glanced out the window, estimating how long it would take to get out of the city — but Stormwind's night patrols were strict, especially in the small hours. If the guards caught him and found his bag, his staff, and took a closer look, it'd be a second trip to the Stockades.

"Maybe I'll just practice on you," he said, looking at the imp.

"No… master!" The imp flopped onto the ground. "I don't want to suffer! What if I die?!"

"My mana isn't nearly that strong, Sazeno," Jace said. "If I could drain you to death in one go, would I have struggled that hard fighting that naga? Think about it."

"Fine, master. Do it. Just have a little mercy!"

Jace smiled, picked up Jagin's page, and was just about to begin the casting gestures written on it when a thought struck him.

This spell drained life energy. Did demons even have life energy?

The thought cooled him right down.

"This incantation transfers life energy," Jace said. "If a demon like you doesn't have life energy inside, what happens if I forcibly drain you?"

"Nathrezim, right?" the imp muttered. "I've heard of those demons' Life Drain, master. What they drain isn't life force in the narrow sense — it's 'vitality.' Which means all living creatures, even shadow creatures, even undead — as long as they're beings driven by energy, they possess their own energetic drive, and what the Nathrezim drain is that force."

"They can convert drained life into death-shadow, corrupt drained arcane energy into fel, and digest it for their own use. I imagine Holy Light is the only thing they can't successfully drain, since the energy they use to drain vitality is fundamentally shadow power."

"If this incantation really is the Nathrezim's vitality drain, I'd advise you to choose your targets carefully, master. There's certainly nothing worth draining from me — you could drain me to death and there wouldn't be much vitality to show for it, considering what a tiny, pitiful creature I am!"

"Forget it," Jace said. "I'm too tired tonight. We'll talk about it another day."

The imp finally breathed its way back into the bag, vanishing in a flicker of arcane light.

Lying in bed, Jace was a little relieved he hadn't just started draining without thinking.

What if he'd pulled fel energy into his body?

And if that fel did damage to him, how would he even explain it to a healer? Better to find a chance to leave the city and practice on a mammal first — the way Jagin had been doing all along.

There was too much to do! Just thinking about it gave him a headache.

He needed to get mage robes made, prepare for the orcish exam, attend the warlock gathering — and in a few days, once Grede recovered, sword training couldn't slip either.

But if he wanted to grow stronger, he had to seize every opportunity to improve. No wasting time.

Thinking back to his previous life, slacking off at work and rotting away at home every day — who would've guessed he'd actually find his drive after coming to this world.

After all, in Azeroth, if you didn't find a way to get stronger, you'd actually die.

He'd already gotten his hands on quite a few good things lately. Those most beautiful and precious naga chest-and-belly scales — if each one could sell for upwards of five silver, that was several dozen silver in income. The pearl string, if that merchant's estimate was accurate, could fetch forty silver. As much as it pained him, Jace figured the string might be carrying some kind of strange naga enchantment designed to lure people in — better to sell it.

Leaving that kind of unclean thing around would only bring trouble sooner or later. But silver was clean.

On top of that, the orcish command letter… he needed to find an antique dealer to sell it. If it could fetch a few gold… he could practically buy a privy.

It had only been a little over a month since he'd resolved to earn enough money to buy his parents a house in Stormwind.

Of course, he couldn't count on finding something as valuable as that command letter every month — but this was the benefit of improving his own strength.

Without enough power, luck would come and he still couldn't seize it.

Now when he thought about making money, he was calculating in dozens of silver. At the very beginning, he'd risked his life for two silver.

If he climbed a little higher — say, becoming an apprentice mage, or going a little further down the warlock's path, training his body to be a little stronger, able to handle more dangerous situations — he'd definitely be able to earn even more.

The Scourge catastrophe was still over a decade away. As long as he could hold steady, as long as…

Thinking of that, he felt like this month alone he'd skirted the line between life and death several times over. But if he didn't take risks, there'd be no improvement either. Today at Jagin's place, when Erland had thrown everything into shoving him against the wall to interrupt his incantation, and he'd fought back with all he had — dealing with Erland already didn't feel as draining as it had the first time he'd gone down to the Slaughtered Lamb's basement.
