Chapter 1050: Crossing the Deep Water

The stretch from Stonard to the Swamp of Sorrows was far longer than Jace had expected.

Perhaps it was because he'd gotten used to traveling with Sola as his ride, or because he'd traveled too many main roads and forgotten how hard it was to travel on foot through marshes and forests. A distance that should have taken roughly two days was now the third day, and he still couldn't see any change in the scenery around them.

But the streams beneath their feet always flowed in one direction, so at least they didn't need to worry about walking in circles like they might in Deadwind Pass.

When Jace was busy doubting himself, Vereesa, Grede, and Sola didn't think much of the situation. Races that had lived this long seemed to have more patience even for wilderness travel — even Vereesa, who was considered impatient by her own people's standards.

On the third night, the group was resting in a relatively dry stretch of forest. Because of several days of pushing forward and the tense atmosphere, none of them were particularly talkative, so they sat in silence.

Vereesa suddenly glanced sidelong to the north. Jace knew she'd sensed something. Another Lost One?

The previous two days hadn't been without Lost Ones tracking them — they'd just occasionally felt something off, but the other party had quickly backed off and vanished. But this was the first time something with ill intent had approached while they were resting, and Jace was starting to get annoyed.

He was just calculating that if the other party had brought weapons, he'd make sure they didn't leave alive, when Vereesa, as if sensing what he was thinking, said softly, "It's not a Lost One."

Jace gave Morophil a meaningful look to go take a look, but Vereesa raised a hand to stop her. "There's no time. We should move."

"I didn't hear anything," Grede said, startled.

Sola's expression wasn't much better either, as if she'd sensed something.

Everyone grabbed their things and headed south. After some time, Jace finally faintly heard the faint sound of claws scraping from where they'd come, plus a "clank-clank" noise like the friction of armor pieces rubbing together — the kind sound an orc warrior's armor made when they moved.

Though he hadn't seen the things' forms yet, Jace had already guessed what they were.

"Dragonkin," Grede said as he walked. "Green dragonkin, definitely."

Jace thought so too. Dragonkin, in groups…

"I can vaguely sense their presence, but I can no longer communicate with them," Sola said in a low voice.

Jace said, "I think they no longer acknowledge you as their master, Sola. Perhaps they still believe they're guarding the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, still loyal to your Queen Ysera — but before they realized it, they'd already fallen into the Nightmare."

Sola touched the Chain of Will at her neck. Jace noticed the iron chain had taken on a faint reddish tinge again. Another stretch of marsh, another group of dragonkin — it gave Jace a strange sense of déjà vu.

This time, although they weren't black, they were undoubtedly more dangerous and far harder to deal with.

Not only were they green dragons — allies who'd lost their reason, essentially — and there was a real green dragon in their party. More importantly, the dragonkin had an extremely close connection to the dragons who led them. For green dragons, a race whose power was rooted in the spirit and reliant on the power of the Dream, this was even more the case.

Who knew what kind of connection these dragonkin had with the green dragons of unknown disposition deep inside the temple?

Once these creatures became aware of the intruders' presence, every green dragon in the marsh might receive the word — and the trolls might even be put on immediate alert.

But encountering the dragonkin wasn't all bad, because it also meant the Pool of Tears wasn't far off.

"Looks like there won't be much chance of sleeping tonight," Grede said, reaching a hand toward Jace. "Give me a Mana Potion."

"What do you want a Mana Potion for?" Jace asked.

"To stay awake," Grede said. "You don't want me dozing off while I'm walking, do you?"

That was impossible to argue with. Jace pulled out a Mana Potion and handed it to him. Vereesa pointed east. "Let's go out that way, avoid these dragonkin. I can feel the dragonkin in the forest getting more numerous and closer — they may have learned where we were staying earlier."

Everyone nodded and followed Vereesa east, avoiding the direction of the dragonkin's search. They crossed several streams and pushed through a dense stretch of forest when, without the slightest warning, the view ahead suddenly opened up completely.

Before them lay a calm lake. The surface was mirror-still, without a single ripple. So vast, and yet it looked like a pool of dead water. Looking down, it was as if a bloated, giant, dull-eyed fish head might surge up from the deep, dark-yellow water and stare right at you at any moment.

Grede stood on the bank and gazed for a while, then said in a low voice, "Is that temple at the bottom of the lake?" Vereesa shook her head.

In the game, part of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar had sat above the lake surface. But looking out from here, the center of the lake couldn't be made out at all — it was entirely shrouded in fog. Jace couldn't say for certain what the situation was in the middle either.

It was just that the thick fog obscuring their vision was also their cover. Jace could occasionally hear the clatter of dragonkin armor or the sound of footsteps drifting over the lake from other shores, but because of the fog, neither side could see the other clearly.

"No choice — we'll have to swim in and take a look," Jace said. "I'll put Water Breathing on everyone."

"Wait." Grede glanced at the water. "If that temple has already been completely flooded with water, does that mean we'll need to rely on your Water Breathing for air the entire way?"

"It definitely can't be that extreme," Jace said. "Otherwise how would the Atal'ai trolls perform their summoning rituals? They'd all have drowned in there from the start, and the green dragons wouldn't have needed to come investigate at all."

"Either way, we have to go in and see," Grede said, rubbing his hands together. "Come on, come on."

Jace chanted the incantation and cast Water Breathing on each of the four in turn. "If you start feeling like it's getting harder to breathe, remember to remind me to recast it."

Vereesa and Grede nodded. Sola raised both hands with her staff and channeled a natural magic. A rushing wind blew up a flurry of rustling dust and dead leaves. Jace felt as if he'd heard the low growl of some beast or dragon — but not with threatening or intimidating intent, but a call that made a person feel incredibly safe.

After that sound faded, he immediately felt much more alert. The fatigue from not having rested in the evening had dissipated, and he even felt his hands and feet brimming with more strength.

"What kind of magic is that?" Vereesa asked. "A druid's blessing?"

"The Wild Blessing of the Emerald Dream," Sola said, tossing a withered scale into the water. "It has the effect of lifting the spirits and strengthening the body. It'll let us swim a little faster, and also give us higher resistance to disease and poison."

"Well earned, Sola," Grede said, rolling his neck. "I'll go in first!" So saying, the dwarf waded into the lake. Jace stripped down and followed him in.

The Pool of Tears' water wasn't as murky as he'd imagined, but the lake bottom was still a stretch of darkness. Thick, giant aquatic plants extended up from the dark-yellow shadows of the lake bottom, frozen in place like they'd solidified there, forming a boundless forest beneath them.

The worst part was that even here, those whispers of the ghosts could still be heard. Now they were like voices murmuring softly right by your ear — even clearer than the gurgling of water against your eardrums, making Jace instinctively want to look to the side from time to time.

Grede paddled ahead like a frog. Vereesa swam quickly and caught up to the front before long. Sola, though she'd recovered her human form, still swam with the undulating motion of a giant lizard.

Morophil swam with ease, staying right by Jace's side. Jace noticed the broken wing bones on her back still twitched from time to time. If her wings had still been intact, she'd probably have reached the front in no time.

After swimming for a while, Jace tried to approach the lake surface.

He poked his head out of the water and wiped the face. From here, the shore was no longer visible.

He shook his head to look around, and then caught sight of a dark-green building covered in moss, right in the center of the lake — like a mausoleum rising out of the water.

Jace sank back down and opened his eyes to look carefully beneath that building. Only then did he see clearly the deep shadow that had obscured half of their view, stretching all the way down to the dark-yellow depths of the lake bottom where sight failed.

Before he'd realized this building was there, Jace had thought that mass of something was just an ordinary shadow on the lake bottom.

Grede noticed the movement behind him and looked back. Jace pointed at the shadow and waved his hand. Grede looked over too.

He stopped moving and stared for a while. Now everyone had noticed that thing. The Temple of Atal'Hakkar was right before their eyes.
