## Chapter 1051: The Temple of Death

Everyone quickened their swimming speed toward that colossal shadow, and soon one after another they touched the wall.

This temple was quite similar in structure to the one Jace and Grede had seen in the Hinterlands — the one dedicated to the spider god Skadras. Both resembled the stepped pyramids of South American Mayan civilization.

Only that temple had been buried largely underground, while this one allowed you to take in its full profile at a glance — even if part of it was underwater and impossible to see clearly.

After climbing out of the water, Grede hadn't even gotten his clothes back on before he raised his warhammer and surveyed the area around them.

The temple was unnervingly quiet. Aside from the occasional sound of lake water lapping against the walls, there was nothing — no chirping of insects, no birdsong, no footsteps — making the group's movements as they checked their soaked luggage and got dressed involuntarily lighter.

"I thought there'd be a bunch of troll soldiers waiting by the wall for us," Grede said.

"It doesn't mean we can let our guard down just because there aren't any," Vereesa said, throwing on her cloak and observing the tower tops high above. "But I truly can't sense the presence of any trolls at all. Is this really the place we're looking for?"

Jace examined the uneven wall before them, wiping away the greenery and moss on its surface and feeling along the already-faded totem reliefs carved into it, gradually tracing out the pattern of a coiled cobra.

"Look," he said, patting the design.

The elf walked over, looked it over, and fell silent for a moment. "It's not quite the same as the Amani wind serpent totem, but… it's definitely a wind serpent. This is the right place."

The wall here was tall and slippery, making climbing difficult. The group followed the platform beside them for some distance, and after rounding a corner they saw a staircase ahead.

Though this set of stairs had been severely damaged — pitted and cracked all over — it was still far easier to walk than the slick, towering walls. And the entrance to this tower should have been at the very top of the stairs.

They climbed the stairs all the way to the top of the tower without a single green dragon, dragonkin, or troll appearing.

Here, several thick stone pillars still bearing faded troll totems supported the stone beams high above, covered in foul-smelling plants both dry and damp, of indeterminate species.

Beneath these stone pillars was the entrance to the temple — steps leading straight down toward a stone gate that looked like the entrance to an ancient tomb, making a person feel as if walking down was like voluntarily stepping into the gaping maw of some lake-hearted monster.

It was clear that hundreds or even thousands of years ago, this place had once been magnificent and solemn — the stone carvings and runes must have been exquisite. But by now, it had probably been a very, very long time since anyone had come to maintain it.

Just as Vereesa had doubted, from the outside this place looked like a thoroughly abandoned ruin in the lake — devoid of all life, without even the cobwebs or traces of insects commonly found in other troll ruins.

"This is the main entrance to the temple," Grede said, glancing down. "You're sure we're going in through the main entrance?"

"We can't exactly chisel out a new entrance now, and no one else has seen us," Vereesa said as she walked down the steps. Grede exchanged a glance with Jace and could only follow.

Stepping down the steps and arriving at the stone gate, Jace examined the reliefs on the walls around them. Undulating snake bodies, vivid scales, snake heads appearing between the coils, eyes staring straight at every passerby — their gaze seemed to follow you as you moved.

Jace could tell that the situation outside this tower wasn't much different from how it looked in the game, but walking in here was a completely different experience.

Especially stepping through that stone gate behind Grede. The somewhat dry, deathly silence outside suddenly turned damp and ethereal, and his mind was immediately swallowed up by a buzzing — the sound of some unknown something.

Even so, the sound of water dripping — from who knows the ceiling or the floor — could still be heard crystal clear.

Not only did he tense up in an instant, he noticed that Vereesa, walking at the front, had also pricked up her pointed ears and would turn in response to even the slightest unusual sound.

Lit by the glow of Azurewrath, they walked very far down the steps and through several narrow corridors into the depths of the temple. Throughout, they never saw a single troll — not even the figure of any living creature at all.

"Now I'm actually hoping some monster jumps out and ambushes us," Grede muttered. "Right now it feels like there's an axe hanging over your head and you don't know when it's going to fall."

"Then I hope it stays suspended up there forever," Jace said.

Grede laughed. Vereesa, walking ahead, suddenly stopped in her tracks, staring at the lower area beyond the corner ahead. "What is it?" Jace asked.

"Water's blocking the path," Vereesa said.

The group walked over to look, and sure enough, the steps leading down ahead had already been submerged.

Jace still remembered that in the game, to enter the temple you also had to pass through a flooded passage — so the situation was the same here.

"Looks like we'll have to swim through after all," he said as he began chanting the Water Breathing incantation again. Grede complained, "I should've known I wouldn't get dressed, damn it."

The group efficiently gathered their things and waded into the steps. The moment Sola extended a foot and touched the water, she said in a low voice, "The water here is foul."

Grede, with only his head still above the surface, said, "Feels about the same to me."

"I don't mean whether it's murky. Can't you feel the dense death aura here?" Sola said. "Be careful, everyone."

Jace and Vereesa nodded. The moment he sank beneath the surface, he immediately understood what Sola meant. Within the dull, gurgling sounds of the watery depths, there seemed to be a low chant mixed in — whether through the water or not, it sounded like a troll's voice, yet slightly different.

He held Azurewrath ahead of him to light the way. In this dark, murky water, the glow of Azurewrath couldn't even illuminate the surrounding walls, ceiling, and floor in full.

Every so often, he'd look back to confirm that the companions following behind were all keeping up. But this time when he turned his head, he saw an extra, blurry humanoid figure beside Grede.

Jace's eyes went wide in an instant and he pointed his sword in that direction. Grede also sensed something was wrong and twisted aside. That figure suddenly accelerated and swam closer, reaching out both arms to grab at Grede. Grede swung his warhammer and smashed it hard into the figure's face, instantly caving the thing's head into a pulpy, yellow-tinged slurry that churned through the water.

Grede opened his mouth as if he'd shouted something, but only produced a burst of gurgling bubbles. But the humanoid figures around them suddenly grew more and more numerous — some going for Vereesa, some lunging at Sola. In the murky water, their features were impossible to make out.

Sola braced her staff and stabbed a nearby figure into the depths in one stroke. Then a burst of flame-like golden light suddenly erupted from Vereesa's direction, illuminating the entire corridor. Jace suddenly saw countless twisted, bizarre bodies emerging from a massive crack in the lake bottom — like swarms of gray maggots pouring out of a rotting wound.

He couldn't tell how many strange humanoid things were packed together, trying to swim toward them, only to recoil at the illumination of that golden light. But the more they feared that golden light and frantically crowded to avoid it, the harder the whole scene became to describe with normal language.

Vereesa held a blade that still burned with light even underwater and swam toward them while waving at Jace, signaling him to get out quickly. The group wasted no time and swam forward with all their strength. After who knows how long, they finally saw another set of stairs and swam up to the surface following the steps.

Grede collapsed on the stone steps and spat out a mouthful of water. The ranger also propped herself up and caught her breath several times.

The stairs led upward all the way to the entrance of another corridor. Everyone tacitly said nothing, waiting together for the air to quiet down, waiting until only the sound of lapping water remained around them and there was no other movement at all before they relaxed a little.

"Undead," Vereesa said.

Jace stared at the water's surface, which looked like reflective ink lit by Azurewrath. "Resurrected trolls?"

"No," Grede said. "The one that attacked me was an elf."

"The one that attacked me was a human," Sola said.

Vereesa glanced at the dwarf, then nodded. "I saw a human, and then… it seemed there was a dwarf too, but I'm not certain. But what I am certain of is that there wasn't a single troll."

"If we'd drowned in there, we probably would've become one of them," Grede said.

"They have no souls left," Sola said. "Just a group of empty shells driven by the death power within this tower." Vereesa said, "Perhaps their souls were long ago sacrificed to Hakkar, and only their corpses were discarded here."

"Damn it," Grede said, looking up at where the stairs led. "We shouldn't need to go back into the water from here on, right?"

"I don't think so," Jace said.

"Sounds like you've been here before," Grede said, glancing at him.

"The stairs lead upward — at least the next stretch shouldn't have water," Jace said.

"That's true," Grede said with a nod. He put on a few items of clothing, threw on his cloak, and sniffed his clothes. "Mother of — the water here smells worse than my old man's blankets."

Vereesa sniffed her own blouse and frowned too. But she just gave it a simple wipe and headed upward. Climbing the steps to the higher level, there was another long corridor ahead. The exit on the far side was pitch black — impossible to see what lay within.

"I've been inside a troll ruin like this before," Vereesa said. "Unattended, as if it had been abandoned by everyone. But in reality it was riddled with traps — hexes the trolls had left behind, waiting for outsiders to walk into them."

She walked a few steps forward, reached out, and felt along the wall for a while before catching hold of a hole that Jace hadn't noticed at all — like the eye of a snake.

"This one," she said, releasing it and dusting off her hands. "This one's already broken, but who knows how many working ones are waiting for us up ahead."

Grede hefted his warhammer and stared at the hole. "Then we're counting on you, little elf."
