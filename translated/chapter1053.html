Chapter 1053: The Mask Hall

After rounding a corner, the corridor ahead was already packed with Atal'ai mummies. These hollow shells, driven by the temple's evil power, moaned dully — each one sounding like a poor victim about to be smothered with a pillow.

Vereesa led the others quickly past the mummies. When the mummies were likewise enveloped in the Dream Shroud, they would suddenly turn their heads as if sensing something unusual nearby, but before they could react, the group had already moved on.

Relying on the mummies' sluggish reaction time, Jace's party made it through the narrow corridor and into a small chamber heaped with bones and rotting corpses.

High on the walls, giant serpent statues gazed down upon everything below, their eyes still seeming to hold some dark light of evil power — or perhaps some kind of trick, an illusion.

In any case, deep in this temple steeped in the Soulflayer's dark aura, even Jace — a warlock acutely sensitive to shadow — found it hard to tell what was real.

Fortunately, the space in this chamber was large enough. The rotting, half-dead waterside plants grew fiercely from the cracks and breaches in the stone, giving Vereesa's pendant enough power to let the group pass unharmed through the crowds of broken mummies.

But as they left this room and entered another corridor running vertically in both directions, Jace clearly felt the pendant's power being distorted.

First, things that shouldn't exist began appearing — look closely and nonexistent statues and carvings would materialize out of nowhere after the Dream Shroud produced by the pendant swept over them. Then things that should exist vanished from within the shroud.

The most unsettling part was that the abstract wind serpent totems and sculptures the trolls worshipped actually transformed into images of green dragons under the pendant's power.

"I think..." Grede looked at a headless green dragon carving beside him and whispered, "we'd best stop using that pendant, Vereesa. What do you think?"

Vereesa looked back and forth down both directions, then glanced back the way they'd come to confirm no mummies were following. She nodded and dropped the pendant's shroud.

"So which way do we go next, left or right?" she whispered. Both directions of the tunnel led to downward stairways, so neither end was visible.

"Left," Jace said.

"How can you be so sure?" Grede asked, looking at him.

"Gotta pick a direction. Any longer dicking around and those troll corpses will feel their way over here." Jace stared at him.

"Fine," Grede said. "We go left. If there's water we come back and switch to the right, deal?"

"I'd prefer to have some kind of basis for the choice," Vereesa said softly, looking at the two of them. "Blind trial and error... it's not that it won't work, but I'm worried we'll stumble onto traps and mechanisms we never see coming."

She looked at Sora as if hoping the green dragon might have some input, but Sora only shook her head. "You'd best not listen to me."

Indeed, given that every green dragon who'd entered before Sora had come to no good end.

Vereesa looked to Morophil. Morophil glanced at the imp poking its head out of the bag at Jace's waist and said, "You'd be better off relying on that little lump."

Sora raised her hand. "I don't understand... what troll gods are thinking!"

"All right," Vereesa said. "We go left."

Of course, Jace hadn't just said it randomly. As a veteran, he was fairly familiar with this route.

Having come this far, he looked at the distance from the corridor walls on both sides to the edge of the downward stairway, and his gut said they should head left. In any case, it at least counted as the "bit of basis" Vereesa wanted — it was just hard to explain.

The downward stairway didn't, as Grede had feared, lead all the way back into the water. Compared to the chamber and corridor full of undead just now, this place was dead quiet — empty.

The group advanced carefully, passing the doorways of several small rooms, before catching a faint light ahead.

The light was coming from a larger chamber at the end of the corridor. Vereesa crept up and peeked inside, then immediately pulled her head back.

She turned and mouthed a single word in Common: "Serpent."

The others crept up on tiptoe. Jace tried to steal a glimpse and saw, beneath several burning braziers, a dark-blue giant serpent coiled in the corner of the chamber. The chamber had two exits: one on the left, sealed tight by a stone door, and the other a pitch-black tunnel mouth.

Vereesa turned back and drew a finger across her throat, then widened her eyes in a look that asked for their opinion. Jace shook his head. The serpent looked like a guard. No telling what might trigger if they killed it.

He glanced back at Sora, pressed his hands together beside his face, and raised his eyebrows in a look. Sora nodded, gripped her staff, and stepped to the doorway.

The moment she emerged, the serpent whipped its body around and locked onto them. Sora twirled her fingers, and the serpent thunked to the ground, motionless.

Seeing the serpent asleep, the people and demon behind who'd been ready for a fight immediately lowered their weapons and ran for the tunnel mouth on the right. But halfway there, Jace noticed the mask-like shield — or shield-like mask — hanging on that left stone door.

He'd seen a similar flat, shield-like mask among the clutter of things hanging in Sylia's room, though that one had been much smaller.

At the time, he'd felt like he'd seen that mask somewhere before — maybe in the Hinterlands, maybe in the ruins of Gurubashi. It was only now, coming here, that he finally remembered why that slack expression on the mask was so familiar.

Because it was a common marker for entering the depths of the Sunken Temple in the game.

Seeing this mask and then entering the corridor beside it meant you were on the right path — it was right here in this chamber... this fork in the road! It was just that in the game the door slab had been moved aside, while here it was sealed shut.

"Wait." He called to the others, who were already about to duck into the other passage, and pointed. "We need to go through this door."

"Why?" Vereesa asked.

"Because Sylia told me to look for this mask," Jace said immediately.

"Why didn't you say so earlier." Grede came over and felt up and down the stone door. "So how do we open this thing?"

"Can you push it open?" Jace asked.

Grede cleared his throat at Jace. His eyes began to gleam with lightning, his skin rapidly turned white, he yanked off his clothes and tossed them aside, and swelled into a giant silver dwarf a full head taller than Jace.

He came to the edge of the door, gritted his teeth, and heaved — there was a thunderous boom, and it seemed the whole chamber shook with it.

The sound continued for a while. When the surroundings finally quieted, Grede asked in a low voice, "Keep going? This door isn't easy to push."

"If you can't move it?" Jace asked.

"It's not a matter of strength. I can tell it's been sealed with magic," Grede said, glancing at Vereesa. He added, "Or... sorcery."

"Is it possible one of those troll mummies knows how to open this door?" the dwarf asked, looking at the succubus. "Have our dear Miss Morophil try using some vicious charm spell to enslave one and give us a hand. After all, it's for the righteous cause of rescuing the green dragons..."

"Even the most vicious charm spells have a hard time controlling undead, dwarf," Morophil said, leaning against the wall.

"Er." Grede hesitated. Just as Jace was about to say something, Vereesa suddenly waved for everyone to hide first.

Morophil dissolved from view. The dwarf quickly shed his transformation, grabbed his clothes, and followed Vereesa and Jace in a retreat. The group ducked into the tunnel mouth on the right.

"Someone's coming," Vereesa mouthed.

Before long, the stone slab began to vibrate, rumbling as it lifted halfway up until the mask caught on the top of the doorframe.

Four or five trolls walked out through the door. They were clearly alive. The lead one was a female priestess wearing a brownish-purple robe, gripping a serpent staff.

She looked down at the sleeping giant serpent for a moment and exchanged a few words with several guards carrying war hammers and morning stars who flanked her. There seemed to be a note of puzzlement in their words.

One of the troll guards seemed to notice footprints or some such on the ground and crouched down to examine them. At that moment, the room grew increasingly hazy, the air turning faintly pink.

The priestess seemed to notice something. She twitched her nose, clenched her fist, blinked twice, and then thunked to the ground.

The troll guards were stunned for a moment, then immediately tried to run back through the doorway — but before they could reach it, they dropped one after another.

Sora walked out of the tunnel, dispelled the sleeping mist, and waved. The others followed her, stepping over the trolls' bodies and slipping through the crack of the door.
