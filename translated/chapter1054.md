## Chapter 1054: The Atrium

After entering the door, at the turn of the passage, the long corridor was already visible by the light of braziers. This meant they had entered the "living quarters" of the temple.

From time to time, a rumbling sound came through the corridor — like some distorted shouting. Even after drinking Sylia's potion, Jace wasn't entirely sure whether it was an auditory hallucination or not.

Every time it sounded, he could only rely on Grede's visibly heightened alertness to confirm that everyone else could hear it too.

Jace didn't know what relationship the Atal'ai trolls here had with the mummies outside, or whether that giant door and the bizarre, spike-covered serpent guarding it was there to keep out those zombies, or to keep out outside intruders, or both.

In any case, judging by the corpses in that waterway outside, the Temple of Atal'Hakkar had definitely received more than one batch of reckless adventurers.

Actually, Jace was a bit grateful they hadn't fought their way in from the start, as Grede had suggested. Ever since entering the area guarded by living Atal'ai priestesses, every passage seemed to have a massive stone door that could drop down.

These doors were not only heavy but sealed with sorcery — possibly divine power begged from Hakkar — and could only be easily opened by the incantations of these priestesses.

If the rulers of this underwater temple had realized they couldn't deal with these few intruders and found an opportunity to seal them in with some stone mechanism in these labyrinthine tunnels, there was no telling how much effort and mana it would take to get out again.

After winding through twisting corridors, what lay ahead seemed to be another empty great hall.

A sound of footsteps approached. The group hid in the shadows beside a doorway. After a troll soldier rushing past disappeared around a corner, they came out to get a better look at the place.

This wasn't a great hall, but a hollow atrium-like space — except that looking up you couldn't see the sky, only pitch-black darkness and tangled plants hanging down. And because there was a bit of firelight here, the dark places up high looked even less distinct.

Vereesa stole a glance over the stone railing below, then immediately pulled her head back and frowned. A few seconds later, a hoarse shout suddenly rose from below, followed by the responding voices of a great crowd of trolls.

Was some kind of ritual going on down there?

Jace didn't understand the troll tongue. He could only catch words resembling "Atal'ai" and "Hakkar" here and there. He looked back at Sora, but Sora also shook her head. Could it be that even she couldn't understand this Atal'ai troll speech?

He glanced down. Beneath the dark green hall, the shadow-drenched, wet floor was covered in a large expanse of bloody, sticky matter. Dozens of trolls knelt among it, and countless round things sat on the stone altars before them.

Jace had no interest in figuring out what they were up to down there. Using the cover of the black plants hanging from above, he observed the corridors and terraces around the atrium, cross-referencing his memories to determine their exact location and the path ahead.

In the game, following the previous route all the way here, you'd only need to drop down one more floor to reach the heart of the temple. But here they couldn't replicate the game's maneuvers, because there were simply too many trolls in the middle. Even with the cover of these black vines, there was no way five people could just jump down without attracting any attention.

But it wasn't long before Jace noticed that the troll soldier who'd rushed past earlier had reappeared on the floor below, heading deep into the corridor.

He glanced in the direction the troll soldier had left and pointed at a doorway over there. "Let's go take a look there." Vereesa didn't question Jace's judgment. They immediately crept over.

Though the temple was grand in scale and bewilderingly complex inside, it seemed the number of trolls left here wasn't all that large. Even with braziers lit for illumination, most areas were empty and unoccupied — never mind that dozens of trolls were gathered in the middle performing some bizarre ritual. This undoubtedly gave Jace's party an excellent opportunity to infiltrate.

Entering that doorway, sure enough, was a downward stairway.

All the way down to the next floor, the moment they left the doorway Jace caught sight of the passage at the end of the corridor continuing deeper — exactly the way that troll soldier had gone.

The moment he caught sight of that passage, he even felt the whispers seeping from the depths of Ulthalesh on his back — it too had undoubtedly sensed the countless evil souls and flesh and blood prepared as sacrifices to the Soulflayer Hakkar within this temple, and its hunger had reached a peak.

Jace took a deep breath to ease the killing intent that Ulthalesh's sudden excitement had stirred up in him.

He looked up and pointed at the passage. Grede glanced at it, swallowed, and whispered, "You sure?"

The trolls' loud chanting nearly drowned out his question, but Jace still understood from his lip movement. It wasn't just Ulthalesh — Grede too had sensed the ominous aura pouring out from that passage.

"We came to find the green dragons," Vereesa said, leaning close and keeping her voice low. "If the trolls are using them as sacrifices, then the most evil place would be the easiest to..."

Before she could finish, the trolls' chanting stopped dead. The several of them thought their conversation had drawn attention. After a moment of silence, a priestess's voice rose again, and they relaxed a little.

Grede nodded. Vereesa crouched low and moved toward that passage under the cover of the stone railing.

At that moment, countless trolls shouted loudly again, chanting the Atal'ai sacrificial words. The more of these chants they heard, the more — even without understanding them — they slowly seemed to seep into the marrow of their brains, striking at their ears and souls word by word, making it impossible not to begin fearing things they couldn't even imagine.

For Jace, fear was the emotion he was best at manipulating — others' and his own alike. So he wasn't too anxious about this feeling welling up inside him, stirred by the trolls' prayers.

Sora was naturally fine, protected by the Chain of Will. Morophil was even less of a concern. He was just worried that Vereesa and Grede might be affected.

Judging from the two of their emotions, they did seem somewhat agitated and worried, but overall there was no sign of distortion.

Still, they needed to leave here as soon as possible.
