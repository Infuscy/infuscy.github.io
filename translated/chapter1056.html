Chapter 1056: At Long Last...

The whole temple had descended into chaos, and there was no longer any need for Jace's group to advance quietly or feel their way along. They cut straight through the mist, heading directly toward the source of the troll's voice. Jace didn't know whether this was the leader of the Atal'ai — the high priest Jammal'an who'd given the prophecy of immortality. But at least he knew that as long as he could find this troll giving orders, he could at least roughly determine the temple's layout. That was far better than wandering back and forth in these labyrinthine corridors and halls, staring in circles at the mist. After all, from the moment they'd come down from that atrium until now, he hadn't seen a single room structure resembling anything he'd seen in the game or the instance. Maybe the pervasive mist had misled him, or maybe... there were simply differences to begin with. In the game, to kill the Prophet Jammal'an, you first had to break the magical barrier protecting him, a barrier maintained by six high-ranking priestesses located on the terraces high up in the temple. At every fork in the road, Jace would steal a glance down it to see if this was the passage leading to the terraces, or the passage leading to the open Sacrificial Pool where the green dragon blood was offered, from which these terraces could be seen. But as they advanced in the direction of the voice, it was as if they never managed to escape those complicated tunnels and corridors outside, going in circles in place. Not only that, but even the sound of the troll chanting what sounded like incantations seemed to drift at him from every direction. Vereesa ran forward resolutely, passing through one dark corridor after another, and several times Jace felt the voice seemed to be drifting from behind them. But he didn't intend to question it either. Just as Vereesa, Grede, and Sora wouldn't question his earlier directional choices, having entered a troll temple and heard a troll's voice, he'd already lost his judgment. So he had to trust the professional troll-hunter's opinion. At that moment, Vereesa suddenly rounded a corner. Grede followed Jace, bracing against the wall as he took the turn sharply after her. Once around the corner, a broad, elongated stairway spiraled upward, and suddenly Jace's mind clicked with certain scenes from the game. But even in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar in the game, there was more than one such stairway. Still, at least it was a good sign. Vereesa ran fast. Jace didn't have much time to think either, observing the architectural features behind him and around him as best he could while doing his utmost to keep up. Sora followed close by his side, but she kept glancing in the direction of the walls from time to time. The hideous troll faces and serpent-face reliefs on the walls were eerie and ghastly, but they'd seen God-knows-how-many since entering the temple. She shouldn't still have any interest in them. Jace could more or less guess why she kept looking over. Eranikus was probably communicating with her all along through the green dragons' means of communication, urging her to join him and abandon the people at her side, to "stop" Hakkar's descent. Jace didn't want to say anything more. Since he'd decided to bring Sora, he was willing to trust Sora's choice. Since he'd given her the Chain of Will, there was no need to worry she'd be mentally influenced into acting against her own will. It all depended on her. However, the direction Sora looked toward was also a clue. That was very likely where Eranikus — or rather, his shadow left behind in this temple — was sealed by the troll priestesses. He wondered if they could use this clue to determine the relative positions of the various parts of the temple? But just as he was mulling this over, a sudden clatter of footsteps came from ahead. Although it was still basically pitch black before them, with only the light of braziers from high up and no sign of anyone, Vereesa still slowed her pace and raised the bow in her hand. She chanted an incantation, and the tip of the arrow burst into flames of fire elements gathered by arcane energy. With an angry roar, it tore through the air and flew. Jace could almost understand what the fire was roaring about. On one hand, it raged at being suddenly dragged into existence by the arcane. On the other, it looked forward to unleashing its fury. But the will exuded by these complex elements vanished in an instant as the arrow flew. A figure had just appeared and stepped onto the stairway up high when it was immediately struck by this fire arrow, howling as it burst into flames, crashing to the ground and rolling all the way down. Several more troll soldiers emerged following the first troll. One of them hurled an axe hard in Vereesa's direction. Vereesa tilted her head to let it pass and loosed another arrow in the same breath, piercing through the fellow's skull. The trolls shouted out simultaneously. The howling echoed between the corridors and stairways. Grede had already charged to their front. With a great roar he knocked several trolls to the ground, his hammer shattering one of them's chest. Another, before he'd recovered his balance, smashed a club into his face. But Grede merely tilted his head and swung back with a lightning-charged heavy blow that smashed the troll's arm to pulp. The whole troll flew diagonally into the wall. A little dwarf among a crowd of broad-shouldered, long-legged, tusked trolls was like a weight being flung back and forth — impossible to stop, even comically so. But Jace wasn't relaxed because of it. These minions didn't even have proper armor on them, and their weapons were crudely made. They weren't the temple's real defensive force at all. He summoned flames to ignite these trolls, preventing their limbs from rapidly regenerating and posing a further threat. Watching them howl, lose their lives, feeling the shells gradually lose their souls and the entire corpses dim — he was a bit surprised. Ulthalesh didn't even try to devour these minions' souls, but instead let them dissipate and depart. Vereesa and Grede caught their breath, confirmed that the charred corpses wouldn't stand up like the mummies outside, and continued into the corridor. But when the surroundings quieted, Jace felt those murmurs, those chantings of spirits, seemed to be getting closer and not just by a little. He wasn't the only one who heard it. Grede and Vereesa heard it too. These murmuring voices drew so close it was as if they were muttering right by their ears, and Ulthalesh grew so excited it even began to vibrate. A troll phantom suddenly materialized behind Jace. Jace felt a cold necrotic power behind him — hatred, pain, despair, hopelessness. These emotions accompanied the seepage of necrotic power, stabbing straight into his consciousness like shadow blades... Jace turned around. Urged by Sylia's ghost-detection brew, a specter had already materialized before his eyes. It was simply a delicate, semi-transparent troll skeleton radiating a blue-green necrotic glow, gripping an equally delicate bone-handled long blade. It let out a dying man's last sigh and swung its arms, suddenly slashing at Jace! Perhaps it was the specter's pressing attack and the searing pain of the shadow that made Jace feel as if time itself had been slowed. But in reality, everything had nearly happened in the blink of an eye. The spirit's long blade slashed down violently, and the people up front hadn't even sensed what was happening... However, as the blade approached Jace's neck, it seemed to be dragged by some force, giving Jace the chance to finish chanting the Dark Shield incantation. The next instant, the blade cut into a black film shimmering with dark hues and was immediately flung out. The entire phantom shuddered and retreated several meters, still trailing a cloud of shadow light. It was Morophil... She was some distance away, using Drain Soul to tug at this Atal'ai specter. Otherwise Jace didn't know what would happen if he were struck by such a phantom. The specter let out an angry roar, which interrupted Morophil's channeling of Drain Soul. But it also drew the attention of the people up front. "Jace! Behind you!" Jace glanced diagonally behind him. More and more specters materialized from cracks in the walls and from the corridor. They seemed to have been called by the trolls who'd just died, determined to give everything they had to defend this temple and protect the summoning ritual. Sylia hadn't been bluffing about them. The hatred and pain these spirits, tortured for hundreds of years, carried was enough to transform into tangible shadow winds that could wound. Specter after specter appearing turned this corridor into a black, corrosive prison. The specters closed in from every direction. Grede raised his war hammer and called upon a bolt of lightning, trying to tear a bright rift through the pitch-black chaos. But it was futile — he was only swallowed again by the dark tide released by more spirits. Driven by the shadow, these wraiths raised the weapons that existed only in phantom form in their hands, chanting unintelligible words as they pressed closer and closer... Sora called upon the moonlight in Draconic, trying to drive back the oppression. But Elune seemed not to hear — not the slightest ray of light broke through the shadow to answer her call. Grede and Vereesa were also quickly swallowed by the shadow. Though Sora summoned healing winds with effort, trying to make a last stand against the surrounding shadow, when dark, spirit-dragging evil souls wielding ritual weapons that radiated baleful light pressed forward one by one, those forces of nature seemed pathetically thin. Jace watched the light of the Song of the Azure in his hand be instantly seized by countless black shadow hands and dimmed. At that moment, he actually understood the voices of the spirits closest to him. Shadow walker... sink into... the shadow. This is where you belong... "You've got somewhere to belong too," Jace murmured, summoning a pure blaze of fire to burn away the shadow's restraint on him. Seizing this opportunity, he grabbed Ulthalesh behind him. "This is what you've been craving..." he roared in Demonic. "Eat! Eat!" A blood-red light burst from Ulthalesh's eyes. The skeletal fanged mouth, stiff and dried for God-knows-how-long, actually opened a crack. Jace swung the scythe hard at the spirits behind him, which the fire had singed open. A bloodcurdling scream tore at his eardrums, but was instantly suppressed by Ulthalesh's low chant. The spirit was swallowed whole by the blood light in an instant, vanishing without a trace. "Hung... gry!" Ulthalesh roared frantically, tearing at every evil soul that dared come near. Jace could feel it. It had never shown any interest in those weak souls of troll minions, blind believers, or lost priestesses. From start to finish, it had been attracted by these powerful spirits murmuring in the depths of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar. Now, it finally had its wish granted.
