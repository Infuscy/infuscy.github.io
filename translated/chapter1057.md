## Chapter 1057: The Dragonblood Barrier

The evil souls, which by all rights should have known no fear or retreat, blurred the instant they heard Ulthalesh's roar. Perhaps it was their worship of the Soulflayer, their fanaticism for this sacred ground, that kept them from fleeing immediately — but they were clearly hesitating.

And in that moment, the tide of shadow pervading the entire corridor lost its support, becoming a chaotic mess under Ulthalesh's tearing. Some of it even began to dance along with the scythe's power.

Sora raised her staff, crackling with arcane lightning, high above her shadow oppression and chanted loudly in Draconic. Moonlight shining in through every crack instantly drove back the shadows of death, freeing Grede and Vereesa.

Vereesa drew her gleaming blade coated in Holy Wizard Oil and slashed a spirit behind her until it nearly lost its form. The still-powerful spirits could continue to struggle, but those struck by Vereesa or smashed by Grede's lightning-trailing hammer quickly became unable to resist Ulthalesh's devouring, turning into a powerless glimmer of light and sinking into the demon's maw along with the shadow.

Morophil had been using her twin shadow-flame blades to help Jace fight the surrounding spirits, weakening them to feed Ulthalesh. But every now and then she caught a glimpse of this scythe devouring souls, and this assassin who had served demon lords for God-knows-how-many millennia actually paused for a moment, clearly shaken by the sight.

Under Ulthalesh's suppression, even the spirits that the Vilebranch priests feared had nearly lost their ability to resist. After the last wisp of phantom shadow vanished between Ulthalesh's fangs, the entire corridor was empty — no shadow, and no souls. There wasn't even a trace of necromantic residue left behind, as there would be after spirits were violently destroyed.

Sora summoned a wind of nature around Vereesa and Grede, healing the wounds they'd just been corroded by shadow and cold necrotic energy. It had all happened very fast. The two of them didn't seem to have suffered serious magical corrosion, so Sora looked over toward Jace and Morophil, raising her Domination Staff as well — but hesitated as she was about to chant.

After all, she had just witnessed everything with her own eyes, watching the spirits be eaten clean, down to the last crumb, by the unassuming curved bone in the hands of this black-cloaked warlock.

"Would my nature magic... harm that staff, Jace?" she asked carefully.

"It's fine," Jace said, shaking his head. "I... didn't get hurt, Sora. Thanks."

At this moment, aside from his own echo in the corridor, the distant shouts of trolls, and the constant, directionless chanting of priestesses, all that remained was a faint demonic chuckle that was hard to place.

Jace even felt this laughter probably wasn't like Ulthalesh's other whispers, audible only to him. Chances were everyone could hear it right now.

And this wasn't a satisfied laugh, but the triumph of finally having found its target. The scythe was toying with his emotions, urging him to charge quickly into the abyss ahead, to recklessly harvest all the tortured spirits trying to defend this temple without regard for the cost.

After all, this was Jace's promise. And those souls just now couldn't possibly make up for the deficit brought about by defeating and letting Onyxia escape.

Compared to the ravenous desire that Ulthalesh was releasing, which Jace could feel, the evil exuded by those ancient spirits driven by hatred, pain, and fanatical worship just now barely counted for anything.

"You all right, Jace?" Grede asked.

"I'm fine," Jace said. "Let's get out of here quickly. The Atal'ai definitely know we're here by now."

"Not only that," Sora said in a low voice. "I can sense it... the power of the dream. My kin may have already arrived nearby."

"I can't hear the footsteps of dragonkin, Sora," Vereesa said. "But I agree. We need to hurry no matter what. I can feel we're not far from that troll's voice anymore..."

She continued forward. Morophil followed the group, looking up and down the corridor. "What's coming probably isn't your kin, Sora, but your kin's blood."

"What do you mean?" Sora asked.

"I don't know," Morophil said. "I can sense magic driven by green dragon blood eroding my mind. Maybe it's because I didn't drink that troll witch doctor's brew, so I feel it more strongly..."

Jace thought of Sazeno. It hadn't drunk the witch doctor's brew either. But when he opened the demon bag, he found the little guy had already passed out.

"Damn it," Jace said. "Morophil's feeling is right. We've already been hit by magic. It's just that because of Sylia's mental potion, we're less sensitive to it."

"What's there to worry about then?" Grede said. "That's the same as no magic! At least we didn't pass out on the spot like the imp, ripe for the slaughter!"

What Grede said wasn't exactly wrong, but Jace still felt uneasy. Who was casting the magic, and where? Was it the green dragons? Or Eranikus's own handiwork?

Despite thinking this, the group continued forward. Just after passing through yet another utterly ordinary stone doorway — the kind they'd seen countless times in this temple — and entering the dark corridor behind it, Jace felt as if he'd plunged into a hazy, even deeper fog. Where was this?

"Don't go any further, everyone!" Sora shouted from behind. But Jace felt as if she were calling to him from another wave. An overwhelmingly strong wave of drowsiness struck him. Sora's voice was as irritating as an alarm clock trying to wake you up on a dead-tired morning.

But more irritating than that sound was the foul stench wafting in his mouth. He coughed twice, and the stench instantly jolted him halfway awake. Something was wrong here.

Jace immediately backed up several steps and retreated out of the doorframe. Vereesa and Grede also hurriedly withdrew. Sora raised her staff and chanted in Draconic, drawing a symbol Jace didn't quite recognize on the strange doorway. A layer of green-glowing natural energy suddenly appeared.

"This is a minor Dreamgate," Sora said as she cast. "But it doesn't lead to the Emerald Dream. It leads to... I don't know what, but it's certainly not anything good. In any case, it can stop us from entering the corridor behind it, and it would make us completely lose our way inside."

She raised her staff again, pointing at the doorway and chanting an incantation. The green on the door was quickly torn apart by vines and thorns that had crept up from cracks in the broken ground. The corridor illuminated by small braziers once again appeared clearly before their eyes.

After finishing the spell, Sora took a deep breath. "Let's go."

"Do you need this?" Jace asked, handing her a mana potion.

"It won't do much for me, Jace. You'd best save it for yourselves to drink," Sora said, shaking her head. The group walked into the corridor. Vereesa asked, "Is this green dragon magic?"

"It doesn't seem like it," Sora said, looking back at Morophil. "But this definitely used green dragon blood... defiled blood."

"Then it can only be the trolls' magic," Grede said. "Did that troll boss do it? He's scared of us!"

"I don't know," Sora said, looking up. "It seems to come from... every direction."

That green dream barrier, the troll magic coming from every direction — it reminded Jace of something.

Wasn't this the magical barrier protecting Jammal'an? Or at least, a part of that magical barrier?

He clearly remembered that in the game, before killing the six priestesses maintaining the magic, there would be an unbreakable green barrier in front of Jammal'an's ritual hall. He'd always assumed that was some kind of troll sorcery.

Now it seemed that was probably some twisted dream magic the trolls had learned from the green dragons, which they'd used to protect the ritual summoning Hakkar.

These Atal'ai priestesses were so skilled at using dreams to control the green dragons and manipulate everything in the temple. Even the potions the Vilebranch trolls used to counter Atal'ai dark magic could resist nightmare erosion. God knew how long they'd actually kept this herd of green dragons captive here.

And the green dragons, accustomed to slumbering in the Emerald Dream where time held no meaning, probably knew nothing about it either.

Thinking this, the group quickly followed the sound of the trolls to yet another doorway. This time Jace was already familiar with the feeling. This doorway was undoubtedly sealed by a dream as well.

Sora once again revealed the true appearance of the dream barrier. Just as she was about to dispel it with magic, Grede stopped her. "Don't waste your mana, Sora. Let me try!"

The dwarf raised his lightning-glowing war hammer and smashed it hard at the doorway. Who would've thought the whole man would charge straight in as if plunging into water. Jace immediately grabbed the pack on his back and dragged him back out. Both of them sat on the ground. Grede glared and cursed "damn it" several times over.

From high up and from behind came the sound of troll footsteps. The murmurs of spirits came and went. Vereesa and Morophil raised their bow and blades respectively, aiming behind them and preparing for battle. Sora chanted an incantation again and dispelled this dream barrier, but this time she was clearly more exhausted.

This time, even her clothes had faded somewhat in color. Green scales were even exposed on her skin, and a tail had stretched out from under her robe. The severe drain of mana made it impossible for her to maintain a normal mortal form anymore.

She turned back. Her pupils had taken on a reptilian appearance, and fangs showed in her mouth. "I don't know how many more times I can dispel them, everyone. But we must..."

"We're very close," Vereesa said. "I can sense the source of that voice is right ahead, Sora."

Jace tossed Sora the Refined Wisdom Elixir. "This stuff can let this imp summon Karlez, Sora. Drink it. I guarantee it'll work."

Sora nodded and drank the potion. Sure enough, her whole complexion improved considerably.

The group continued forward. After passing an upward stairway, Jace suddenly called a halt to Vereesa up ahead.

Although this temple did have differences from the game — far too many forks in the road, corridors, and small stone halls — through Eranikus's relative position, and the commands probably coming from the Prophet Jammal'an in the distance, Jace could almost determine the positions of the terraces where the priestesses weaving the dream barriers stood.

"We go this way," he pointed at the stairway.

"The voice is over there," Grede said. "You sure you didn't mishear, Jace?"

"I can sense where these powers of the dream are coming from, Vere," Jace said. "Trust me. As long as we take out the trolls maintaining these barriers, Sora won't have to waste her mana anymore."

"Then we'll follow you, Jace," Vereesa said.
