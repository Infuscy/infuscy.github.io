Chapter 1059: Even Through the Thick Fog

"Son of Hakkar," Vereesa picked up one of the long feathers and said. "It's not the first time I've seen this kind of monster, but one this massive... I've truly never seen before. This means we've gone deep enough." "It's drunk green dragon blood," Sora said, staring at the monster's smashed jaw. "The Atal'ai must have pinned their hopes on using green dragon blood to strengthen it." "Just how many bizarre opponents are there in here?!" Grede asked. At that moment, Vereesa walked a few steps back along the body of the enormous wind serpent, reaching a doorway high up. The serpent's tail extended all the way into the doorway. She looked back down and said, "A Son of Hakkar couldn't have just shown up randomly. It must be guarding something important. Perhaps the priests Jace mentioned are in there." Jace nodded, catching his breath. "Let's go in and see." The group quickly gathered themselves and ducked into the doorway one after another. Inside was a slightly twisting tunnel, but huge feathers shed by the wind serpent could be seen everywhere. As they walked, they faintly heard the sound of boulders grinding and troll incantations chanting from ahead. The entire tunnel began to vibrate. "Something's wrong. Are they trying to seal us inside this corridor?" Vereesa asked. Once she said it, the group immediately quickened their pace. But even so, they were too late when they rounded a corner. A massive stone door was still sealing the way ahead tightly, having just slammed shut with a bang. At this moment, from far back in the corridor behind them, the direction they'd come from, there also seemed to be the sound of boulders shifting. Vereesa stepped forward and felt the stone door. She said in a low voice, "Damn it, this is also a stone door empowered by troll sorcery... I only know some very simple incantations for breaking troll hexes, but I can't open this." "Did you think I couldn't push that earlier stone door because I wasn't strong enough, little elf?" Grede waved his hand. "Stand aside." "What?" Vereesa asked. "That was because I didn't want to make noise. I wanted to move that door as quietly as possible." As the dwarf said this, he swelled into a little giant once more. He shook both arms, hefted his war hammer, and said, "Now everyone in the temple already knows we're here, right? Then it doesn't matter. Cover your ears, you scrawny-armed, scanked-legged..." Jace dutifully covered his ears. He watched Grede take a step back, build up momentum, and slam his lightning-trailing war hammer into the stone door! A thunderous boom. It seemed the entire temple trembled from that one strike. The massive door flashed an eerie green light, then dimmed. Not only the door — rubble fell everywhere in the corridor, and a crack even appeared in the wall. Jace felt he nearly got knocked out by the shockwave even with his ears covered. Lightning crackled everywhere in the corridor. The elements roared in anger, but not like when channeled by the arcane, enraged at being enslaved — rather, they cheered for this stone statue's cathartic release. This even seemed to get Grede riled up. He laughed twice and swung again. The lightning from this strike nearly blinded Jace. He could only cover his eyes and ears, feeling the corridor shake again and again, until suddenly one shake. The magic that had still been barely supporting things just now seemed to have its air let out. What followed was the rumble of boulders collapsing. Jace opened his eyes. Leaping out from behind the collapsed rubble was a troll warrior in brown-black armor, swinging a giant axe violently at Grede. But the dwarf who charged forward blocked it with a one-handed hammer strike that bent the axe blade, then kicked him in the breastplate, sending him back several steps. His foot slipped and he fell who-knew-where. Up ahead was the highest terrace in the entire temple. Jace, Vereesa, and Sora also charged into the flying dust and haze. They saw Grede had already knocked down several troll guards. In the distance, the priest in the middle of the terrace, wearing a purple Atal'ai robe, was still channeling the power of the dream without wavering even as his altar was breached. Vereesa cleanly shot him through the head with an arrow. He flipped over and lay motionless. Jace came to the edge of the terrace. Before him was a deep, wet mist — nothing could be seen clearly. But he knew what this place was. The Sacrificial Pool — the circular great hall at the very center of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, surrounded by six altars channeling dream magic. This was one of them. "The power of the dream has been weakened," Sora said, gazing at the dense fog. "But it's still powerful. We must..." "Kill all the priestesses like this?!" Grede said, his eyes wide. "How many altars are there again? Are we going to run this far for every single one?!" "At least there's one right over there," Vereesa said, pointing into the dense fog beside them. Jace stepped over the Atal'ai priest's body to the edge Vereesa had pointed to. He vaguely glimpsed what seemed to be a faint glow of green dream magic over there. At that moment, Vereesa walked up beside Jace, drew her bow, and took aim. After a moment, she released. With a sound of the bowstring, the arrow dragged a faint arcane glow into the mist. A moment later, that patch of green light in the distance suddenly disappeared. A chorus of howls came from the great hall. The voice of that troll high priest in the distance clearly carried a note of rage. Grede looked at Vereesa in surprise. "You just... shot him dead?" "Now the third one," Vereesa said, walking with light steps to the edge of the other side of the terrace, nocking another arrow and releasing. Soon that tiny bit of green light over there vanished too. From behind, beneath the collapsed rubble in the terrace below, the howls and footsteps of a great crowd of trolls had already risen. Grede immediately said, "I'll hold them off for you, little elf. Shoot them all down!" "Don't need you to tell me." Even as she spoke, Vereesa changed position again, stepping to the edge of the terrace to draw her bow and take aim. But this time, her target required shooting across the entire Sacrificial Pool to the priestesses on the three terraces on the opposite side. This time Jace couldn't see even a trace of green light at all. Through the vast fog came the undulating murmurs of spirits, growing closer and not. At the same time, shadow power quickly permeated the entire terrace — extremely similar to the harbinger of the spirits' assault earlier. "Don't worry, Vere, these spirits are mine to handle," Jace said, gripping the demon staff as he glanced at Sora beside him. "Watch over Grede. Don't worry about me." "I know, Jace," Sora said with a nod. Grede had already charged into the corridor. The sound of howling, troll cursing, and dwarf swearing mixed together. Bolts of lightning sprayed out of the doorway from time to time. Every now and then, shattered bones and rubble flew out. Jace raised Ulthalesh and gazed into the fog. Morophil's blades also ignited with firelight. Before long, a spirit suddenly materialized behind Vereesa, but before it could swing the phantom battle axe in its hand, it was dragged by Morophil's Soul Drain power, barely able to advance a step. It was then cut out of existence by a slash from Jace, leaving only a rapidly dimming cry. One after another, spirits materialized from the fog. Jace chanted an anti-demon ward incantation, raised his Dark Shield, and directly engaged these evil ghosts in close combat with Morophil's help. Vereesa stood steadily at the edge of the terrace, drawing her bow and taking aim. Pus, blood, venom, necrotic energy, and shadow splashed everywhere around her. Nature's power and lightning interwove back and forth. But she turned a deaf ear and a blind eye to it all. She only gazed into the dense fog, drawing her bow. In the end, she even closed her eyes, relying on the innate magical perception of a Quel'dorei and the sensitivity to sound and scent of a Farstrider, slowly drawing the bow to full draw and releasing. Jace had just used his scythe to tear a spirit to pieces. Ulthalesh was like a vicious dog barely on a leash, murmuring and chuckling within his skull. But he still glanced at Vereesa, who had just fired an arrow, at this moment. Before long, a string of even more furious roars came from the depths of the temple. He knew — this arrow had hit its mark too. Vereesa raised her long eyebrows in triumph and easily dodged a flying axe thrown by a troll that flew out of the doorway. She watched it fall into the fog as she walked a few steps to the edge on the other side and raised her bow once more to gaze into the distance. But obviously, Jace noticed the fog pervading the entire temple was no longer as dense as before. He could even vaguely make out the shadow of the neighboring ritual terrace at this point. Even so, seeing the terraces on the opposite side or glimpsing the arcane light of the magic channeled on them was still nearly impossible — at least impossible for Jace's human eyes. But Vereesa still closed her eyes, sensing for a period of time before firing the arrow. The arcane glow then sank into the mist. Not long afterward, even Jace felt the drowsy magic that had been pressing on his mind, that oppressive force that always guided you into producing illusions, loosen a little. At that moment, a low, rumbling utterance came from the depths of the temple. "You don't know what you're doing... You are about to commit a grave crime that will plunge all of Azeroth into terror and darkness, Samelia, my child. It's not too late to stop now." This was Eranikus's voice. He was no longer speaking Draconic, but Common. He wasn't just warning Sora — he was warning Jace and the others too. But it seemed that aside from Jace, who was willing to listen carefully, the others couldn't care less. Grede had already fought himself into a frenzy. He raised his war hammer with both hands, smashing and cursing as he went in the corridor. And though Sora looked somewhat grave, she didn't give any response at all. There was still one last terrace left. Vereesa naturally wanted to complete her task perfectly, so she ignored all distracting sounds. The way she stood at the edge of the terrace with her bow raised reminded Jace of the statue of Alleria Windrunner in front of Stormwind — that resolute and graceful bearing. However, the last terrace was completely diagonal to this one. Although the fog had dispersed somewhat, the interference of the dream power not yet broken throughout the temple, combined with the distance, made the difficulty of Vereesa's final shot increase sharply. She took aim for a while, then still lowered her bow and said in Elvish, "I need your blessing, Sora..." Sora had just guided a gust of Rejuvenating Wind into the channel to accompany the dwarf. Hearing Vereesa's voice, she immediately turned back. She chanted in Draconic and raised her staff toward the elf standing here gazing into the distance. Jace warily watched the spirits that could appear again at any moment in the fog, while catching from the corner of his eye that the arcane light in Vereesa's eyes seemed to brighten considerably. "Good, good, I can feel it. This time..." Vereesa murmured in Elvish, then raised her bow again, took aim for a while, and released. Not long after this arcane light sank into the mist, a thunderous rumble suddenly echoed through the entire great hall. The dense fog dissipated as if blown apart by the wild wind brought by this rumble. "It worked, Vereesa. We did it," Sora said. Though her voice was very soft, it was hard to conceal the excitement in her emotions. Jace walked to the edge of the terrace and looked down. In the broad, circular great hall, countless armored Emerald dragonkin stood in the dragon blood, silently gazing this way. Though Eranikus's voice roared, demanding the green dragons immediately kill all the intruders including Sora, these dragonkin remained unmoved. They only stared. The sharp cries of whelps came from a massive corridor in the distance, echoing all the way here. Sora's excitement, which had filled her heart, seemed to have been halted at once. The troll offensive seemed to have weakened somewhat at the moment. Grede roared from inside the corridor: "Shouldn't we get moving?!" Vereesa pointed to the entrance of another corridor. "That Atal'ai high priest is in there." "It should be right," Jace said, staring in the direction the whelps' cries were coming from. "But we need to leave this terrace first."
