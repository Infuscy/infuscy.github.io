## Chapter 106: A Gift

That afternoon, thanks to the whole getting-caught debacle, Jace had missed his Orcish class. He wasn't about to miss this morning's session.

Since he didn't have to spend the entire morning at Marin's, he could do a quick bit of cleaning, ask if there was anything that needed doing, and then head straight out — which meant he could make the Wednesday morning Orcish lesson.

After class, Jace made a beeline for the Larsson tailor shop.

There were two clothing shops in the Mage Quarter. Jace knew Duncan's place pretty well, having been there several times. The Larsson shop, on the other hand, he'd only stepped into once — and the fellow who'd waited on him had been a Lordaeronian.

The very first time they'd met, the guy had looked down on him for being an outsider laborer. It had pissed Jace off enough that he'd never gone back.

He just hadn't expected this place to be a partner merchant of the Wizard Sanctum — that the mages' robes were all made here.

This time he walked in reluctantly, and the shopkeeper had changed: the young fellow was gone, replaced by a middle-aged man.

"Good day, young sir," the middle-aged shopkeeper said, looking over. "Welcome to Larsson's. James, if you please — the proprietor of this establishment."

James had a head of gray hair and a face full of wrinkles, but judging by his bearing and the pace of his speech, he was probably only in his fifties. And his accent had no trace of Lordaeron — he was clearly a Stormwind local.

Jace glanced around. "Last time I was here, an Ivan Larsson was minding the shop. I thought he was the owner."

James said, "That's my nephew. Larsson's my name too, naturally. This shop was my grandfather's legacy. Ivan was just helping me run it. Do you know him?"

"Not really... just curious."

"Well then, what can I do for you, sir? Most of the garments we make here are for mages."

"I am a mage." Jace showed him his apprentice sigil.

"Right then." James put on his spectacles and gave it a look. "First time buying a robe?"

"Yes?"

"Long or short? Have you given it any thought?"

"Short." Jace's decision was swift.

He wasn't just studying at the academy — he'd be traveling and moving around constantly. Long robes were a pain to pack and got in the way of a fight.

"Not exactly the traditional type, eh?" James Larsson asked again. "What material are you after?"

"I'm on a limited budget — about ten silver, boss," Jace said. "I don't know much about robes either, so recommend me something decent if you would. Quality first, something that won't fall apart easily."

"Ten silver, hmm." James said, "If you want something with a bit of quality, you could go with spider-silk. The disaster up in Southshire wasn't without its silver lining — the giant spiders that live there produce silk that was discovered a few years back to have decent shadow resistance. Makes it perfect for robes. Especially now, with all those gnoll warlocks running rampant and no small number of dark-magic-wielding orcs hiding in the shadows, a spider-silk robe is a solid choice for any mage, regardless of skill level. Lightweight, and tough."

"Ten silver would cover it?" Jace said. "Spider-silk seems more expensive than silk."

"Depends how you look at it." James said, "Even spider-silk isn't pure spider-silk... by the Light, that'd cost a fortune. Our cheapest spider-silk fabric runs about twelve silver for a full set. For an apprentice or even a junior mage, that's a perfectly good option."

Twelve silver.

Once he bought this thing, he'd be wearing it for God knows how long. Might as well get something decent.

"Any preferences on appearance? Color, pattern, that sort of thing."

"Something low-key. Brown, maybe. Plain and simple, nothing fancy." Jace said.

"Noted." Jace watched the shopkeeper scribble away, then ventured carefully, "Is there such a thing as buying on credit?"

"Of course." James pulled out a piece of paper, wrote something on it, and handed it to Jace. "Twelve silver, due within a month of picking up the robe. If it goes past a month, it's thirteen silver, and so on. Drag it into next term and the Wizard Sanctum will come collecting, young sir — and if you still can't pay up then, you risk being expelled."

Jace was about to get paid any day now. There was no way it'd drag into next month, so he signed.

"Sign your name at the bottom here," James reminded him.

After Jace wrote his name, James glanced at it and said, "You're Jace Sesso? The initials on the ring — that'd be Jace Sesso?"

"That's right," Jace said. "I just became an apprentice. My master is Master Marin."

James said, "Mr. Sesso, Master Andormas told me to keep an eye out for you. Your robe is being paid for by the crown, so this one's on the house."

With that, he tore up the bill on the spot.

"What..." Jace stood there for a moment, stunned.

"Can I pick something else, then?" he asked immediately.

"Of course," James said. "Though the royal subsidy caps out at fifteen silver — that's the standard allowance for most new mages' robes. Anything above that, you'd be paying the difference yourself."

So it was a fifteen-silver standard subsidy. Never mind, then — Jace didn't feel like going through all that again. He'd stick with this one. He hadn't expected much from the Stormwind crown given how broke they were, but getting this much for free was already pretty good.

"I won't change my selection," he said, resigned.

"Right, then. There's one more thing I'm supposed to pass along to you. One moment." Larsson tidied up the counter and hurried into the back room.

He was still talking as he walked into the back, leaving Jace standing there by himself.

So the king had actually paid for his clothes. That was... really something.

Then he suddenly recalled the strange look Andormas had given him when he'd mentioned the Larsson shop — probably because there'd been too many people around, and it would've been awkward to say out loud that the king was buying him a new robe. So in the end, Andormas had just reminded him to be sure to stop by.

Jace hadn't caught on at all. He'd only finally been willing to come here after getting arrested for suspected theft.

When Larsson came back out, he was carrying a brownish-red outer cloak.

He laid it out in front of Jace with the utmost care, as if terrified of damaging it in any way.

What the hell is this, Jace thought, getting nervous himself.

"This..."

"A Smoke-Scorched Cloak," James Larsson said.

James held up the garment. "The raw material for this outer cloak came from a giant smoldering fire drake that once roamed the Blasted Lands and the south of Loch Modan. Do you know what a smoldering fire drake is?"

"I do," Jace said. "A monster that lies on the ground like a crocodile, with a big sail growing out of its back."

"Exactly." James said, "That fire drake was first discovered by the Bronzebeard dwarves. It would use its reddish coloring to lie in ambush near the Blasted Lands, preying on travelers — and even at over ten meters long, it still managed to pull off its sneak attacks more often than not. The dwarves called it 'Cunning.' Ironforge was too busy feuding with the Dark Iron dwarves to do anything about it for many years."

"Decades ago, King Baratheon dispatched a hunting party to help the dwarves of Thelsamar slay the beast, restoring normal contact between Ironforge and Stormwind. The dwarves used its scales, hide, and back-sail to craft a set of armor and garments as a gift of thanks to Stormwind — and this cloak is one of them."

"Since it's a cloak that's already decades old, it's picked up some wear and tear. This one's been repaired. Its previous owner fought trolls while wearing it — a troll spear punched a hole right through the shoulder here — but after the repair, it's barely noticeable. Our leatherworker's the best in the kingdom." James turned the cloak over this way and that. "But the fire resistance it inherited from 'Cunning' is still strong, and it can also amplify a mage's fire magic — a real treasure for a fire mage. The biggest downside is it might be a bit warm to wear in the summer, Mr. Jace Sesso."

"This is way too valuable..." Jace stared at the cloak, at a loss for words.

"Valuable, yes, but not unimaginably so," James said, folding the cloak up neatly.

"How much is it worth, roughly?"

"In Dalaran it'd sell for about three gold — though since this one's been repaired, plenty of people figure there's some performance loss compared to the original. That's inevitable."

Jace had already seen a seven-gold robe that had been singed by a red dragon, so his tolerance for sticker shock was high — but three gold for a cloak the king was giving him still exceeded his expectations.

Even if it was a gift, he'd be too scared to wear it out of the house.

Jace tested the waters: "If it gets damaged again, could you repair it?"

"This cloak would have to be repaired with magic, Mr. Sesso," James said. "The exact cost depends on the extent of the damage. Something like a punctured hole would run about thirty silver. But this thing is nowhere near as fragile as you think — it was forged by dwarven runebinders, after all."

Jace stopped hearing anything after "thirty silver."

"Could I use it as a rain cloak?" he asked tentatively.

James laughed. "Unless it's raining raptor acid, maybe it might eat away a touch of color. This is a creature that lived around lava, remember."

He wrapped the cloak carefully in some kind of cloth that looked expensive just to glance at, and handed it to Jace, tucking a letter into the folds.

"That's everything Master Andormas asked me to pass along to you. Once you take it, my job here is done."

Jace thanked him profusely and picked up the package. That's when he noticed the wax seal on the letter — a golden lion's head.

He walked out of the shop and into the Park, found a step to sit on, and tore open the envelope.

"Mr. Jace Sesso,

Fulfilling the wish you expressed — sponsoring your entry into the Wizard Sanctum — is far from sufficient to convey my gratitude, because at the kingdom's darkest hour you brought a moment of inspiration to every soldier at Stonewatch, and indeed to all the people of Lakeshire. And that kind of hope in the dark is beyond any price.

Every mage of the Wizard Sanctum dresses according to their station, and I know that a young craftsman who traveled all the way from Lordaeron to sweat and bleed for the rebuilding of the new Stormwind would have little means to furnish himself with such trappings. So I took it upon myself to present a magical outer cloak, passed down from my grandfather King Baratheon, from the royal mages' vault, as a further token of my thanks. May it help you settle into your new life in this kingdom.

May your hand heal swiftly, in the Light's name.

— King of the Kingdom of Stormwind, Defender of the City, and Lord of Stormwind Keep, Varian Wrynn"
