Chapter 1061: The Abyss of Thick Blood

"Like poking a hornet's nest, damn it." Vereesa walked forward a few steps, feeling her quiver again. "Now no matter where that Atal'ai high priest is, he'll definitely be heavily guarded by trolls and dragonkin. There might even be a few whelps helping him out!"

"Why weren't there any dragonkin before?" Grede hefted his war hammer and followed her. "From the moment we entered this temple until now, I didn't even hear the sound of dragonkin."

As he said this, he tilted his head to look at Sora, as if wanting some answer from her. But Sora only shook her head.

Vereesa said as she walked quickly, "Whoever is ruling this temple, at first they had no idea what kind of people had snuck in. Remember the dead people in that waterway at the very beginning, Grede? Clearly those fellows were all our predecessors. And this temple's guardians also felt that even green dragons couldn't resist their magic — other outsiders, whether adventurers or do-gooders... none of them would come to a good end."

She looked at the dwarf and continued, "Clearly, deploying these dragonkin to participate in defending the temple was a last resort the trolls were forced to take. If we hadn't destroyed the dream magic throughout the entire temple and made them realize our true strength, they definitely wouldn't have thought of sending the dragonkin into the fight."

"That makes sense," Sora said. "So just like Jace said, they can't guarantee they can really control the dragonkin either, right? My kin definitely haven't completely fallen... They can still be saved!"

"It's not impossible, Sora," the elf said.

"Now's not the time to discuss whether they can be saved, you lot. Right now, it's whether we can be saved!" The dwarf looked warily down both sides of the corridor. "Don't you think the situation is getting a bit dire?"

"Grede's right. At this rate we're going to be hard-assing the temple's entire defensive force," Jace said, following the others' pace. "We're only this many people no matter what. We need to find a way to shake off this encirclement and interception. We can't fight head-on. Your arrows, my and Sora's mana..."

Vereesa pinched her earring. "Can we use this?"

"Our enemies aren't black dragons, Vere..." Sora shook her head. "Even Emerald dragonkin can sense anomalies in dream power, let alone those whelps. After all, when Queen Ysera gave this earring to the Life-Binder, she never considered that one day this pendant would need to be used against green dragons."

At that moment, Jace suddenly thought of something. "Is it possible... Sora, that the dragonkin here have already lost their connection to the Emerald Dream under the Soulflayer's corruption? Don't forget this earring still uses true dream power! Don't forget what that dragonkin said to you just now — you're the green dragon truly maintaining your sanity, while they're all degenerates who think they're still guarding the dream. Perhaps they've long since lost the ability to travel between the dream!"

Sora pondered for a moment, then said, "Yes. They've betrayed the Emerald Dream. We can really try. Just be careful..."

"Careful of distorted illusions."

Vereesa said softly, chanting an incantation to activate the earring's power with arcane energy. They were immediately enveloped by a haze flowing down from above.

The troll totems on the walls were quickly replaced by winged serpents, writhing and intertwining with worms, baring fangs and glaring at everything passing through the corridor.

Jace could only do his utmost to ignore these nearly impossible-to-ignore patterns, focusing his attention on the figures outside the haze — especially the massive forms of the dragonkin.

Sure enough, as Jace had expected, those passing dragonkin seemed to sense something, but they truly couldn't see the group's figures. This party actually wove their way right through the green-scaled dragonkin, watching those dragonkin brimming with fury running everywhere, hunting, killing, and asking each other in Draconic where the intruders were.

At that moment, a gong blast exploded from the depths of the temple. Even the dream haze brought by the pendant shuddered with it. Jace almost thought this gong was meant to break this layer of dream. But nothing dramatic happened in the period that followed. It only trembled that once, then stabilized again.

Under the protection of Ysera's earring, they successfully entered the Sacrificial Pool. Before them was green dragon blood, blackened in the shadows, everywhere. Soaked in the blood were chunks of corpses, and mountains of accumulated debris, ribs, and whelp skulls could be seen all over. Gazing at all of this, Sora was already ashen.

The viscous blood slowly flowed toward the lower center, into that hole — a hole that seemed capable of sucking everything into it...

Shadows occasionally flew across the sky. Their massive wingspans seemed to cover the entire Sacrificial Pool, making it hard to believe they were merely whelps guarding Eranikus.

Even Sora was the same. Though she possessed stronger mana than most whelps and a body closer to that of an adult dragon, she too felt dread at the corrupting aura of the nightmares flying overhead.

They advanced along the high walls surrounding the Sacrificial Pool. Jace noticed that here, from time to time, one could see floating, burning purple-blue will-o'-wisps, swaying with the shadows passing overhead. On the terrace earlier, when Jace had looked down, he hadn't noticed these flames at all — be careful of distorted illusions.

Jace could only shake his head and put these dream-distorted impressions behind him. The most important thing now was finding the Atal'ai leader and the patrolling dragonkin.

If one paid too much attention to the dream woven by the earring... perhaps they'd end up like these green dragons, immersed in it, never able to extricate themselves again.

Arriving at the entrance of a grand corridor, sure enough, as Jace had expected, countless weapon-wielding dragonkin crouched here, gazing at the center of the blood pool in the distance and the high terraces, completely unaware that this group had already drawn near.

Hovering above these dragonkin was a giant shadow. Jace looked carefully. Even through the hazy cover of the earring, he could make out that it was a green whelp.

It was muttering something in Draconic — seemed like commands, seemed like angry complaints. Jace could make out some words, such as "Sleeper" and the like, but indeed couldn't hear them completely under the dream cover of the earring.

What surprised Jace was that although its scales were somewhat dim, with dark spots on them like those brought by nightmare corrosion, and its eyes were an unhealthy, cloudy white, compared to that corrupted adult dragon outside, it had no obvious difference from an ordinary green whelp.

Why was this so? The green dragons outside the temple were actually more severely corrupted than those inside? Could it be that the ritual to summon Hakkar required sufficiently pure dragon blood? After all, Jace could sense it too — the corrupted dragon's dragon blood basically didn't contain any green dragon dream power anymore. It could probably only be used to make Shadow Protection Potions or similar things.

Before he could consider it carefully, another gong sounded from the depths of the hall, pulling everyone's attention — including Jace's — back over. The dream cover shuddered again. A dragonkin nearby seemed to sense something amiss and looked over in this direction.

Vereesa waved, signaling everyone to follow. Her pace underfoot began to quicken. The group sped up,绕过 this crowd of corridor-guarding dragonkin and entering inside.

This corridor was both deep and spacious, giving this group enough room that the pendant's power wouldn't envelop the patrollers within. After all, even the soulless wandering mummies outside could sense something amiss then, let alone dragonkin soldiers with green dragon blood.

All the way to the end of the corridor, passing a corner, a cathedral-like great hall appeared before them.

This place instantly jogged Jace's memory. This was where the Prophet Jammal'an was. Many Atal'ai high priests and powerful temple guards were here protecting him.

And this was the same. Through the haze, Jace could see crowds of shadows kneeling below the deepest altar. Standing high on the altar was a figure, raising both hands and shouting something. Perhaps because of the dream cover, it was inaudible.

But now there was no need for any dream power anymore.

Entering the cathedral, Vereesa pinched the earring and lowered the curtain, directly raising her bow and aiming at the tall figure at the highest point.

As long as they killed the highest priest of the Atal'ai, with the host gone, summoning Hakkar to this world would become an empty talk — at least completely impossible in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar. Perhaps the green dragons could even be liberated.

Sensing a group of uninvited guests suddenly appearing out of nowhere at the entrance, the dark shadows in the cathedral also turned their heads one after another. At the same time, the braziers hanging on the walls were one after another ignited by some sorcery, illuminating the place brilliantly.

A flaming arrow trailed a streak of fire over the heads of the rows of figures, flying straight to the top of the altar and accurately hitting the troll's neck. However, the troll only staggered a step, then pulled out the arrow from his neck, pinched off the flames on the arrowhead, and tossed it aside.

On the altar, laid with ancient Gurubashi stone tablets, the troll raised the emerald-green Gurubashi curved blade in his hand, pointing at them and shouting something in the troll tongue. And the trolls below the stage also shouted along with his voice.

"The Atal'ai high priest isn't here," Sora murmured. She didn't need to explain — Jace had realized it too.

The tall troll standing on the altar wasn't a priest. He wasn't even alive.

He was a troll mummy wrapped in rotting cloth wrappings. But unlike the soulless wandering mummies outside, he clearly retained his own consciousness. He had his own soul.

Jace knew who this was. This was "the Wretched" Ogom, Jammal'an's bodyguard and the leader of the Atal'ai warriors. But where was Jammal'an?

"No one... can stop... Hakkar's... descent... Atal'ai... immortal!" the troll mummy roared in Common, then burst into manic laughter.

"Atal, Hakar, Dazi, Zul, Dogo, Hakkari!"

"Atal, Hakar, Dazi, Zul, Dogo, Hakkari!"

"Hakkari!"

The temple echoed with increasingly unified, soul-piercing howls. Immediately after, the whelps and dragonkin outside let out agonizing wails.

The Atal'ai mummy warriors holding giant axes and wearing armor pressed closer and closer before their eyes. Ogom's laughter was drowned out more and more, growing inaudible.

"It's impossible. They can't possibly succeed in summoning the Soulflayer," Sora said in a low voice.

"Indeed impossible," Jace murmured.

Vereesa looked at the two of them hesitantly and said nothing.

"But it doesn't look impossible, master," Morophil said softly. This demon seemed to be the calmest one in the entire temple at the moment.

Of course, aside from the imp still sleeping like the dead in the bag.

But Jace knew that the most important artifact for summoning Hakkar himself — that Ancient Evil Egg — was still hidden deep within Zin'Zarro City by the Amani trolls. That was beyond doubt.

But...

"Whatever they're planning, let's kill these dead men in front of us first!" Grede stared at the Death Walkers before his eyes, raising his war hammer and roaring in Dwarvish, "Whatever 'kar' whatever 'lai', let's see who's got the bigger lungs! You dog-faced bastards!"
