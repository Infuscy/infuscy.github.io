## Chapter 1062: Jammal'an's Defenders

"A' Talak, Kagar, Dath, Zul, Dorg, Hakkari!"

A gong blast that shook the entire hall served as the overture for the Atal'ai assault.

Jammal'an's guards — undead warriors known as Deathwalkers — hefted their massive axes and advanced with heavy, measured strides. Grede raised his hammer and charged straight at them, bellowing "Khaz Modan! Khaz Modan!" at the top of his lungs as his body rapidly swelled in size, until he stood nearly as tall as the reeking, gray-wrapped undead trolls.

Ogom the Wretched was monstrously large. If not for how steady and coordinated his gait was, Jace would have taken him for a dread troll — a creature he'd only ever read about in books. A troll mutated into something ogre-sized but far stronger and far more bloodthirsty.

He strode through the closing ring of Deathwalkers straight at Grede. The two collided, one tall and one short, and the first blow ended in a dead heat.

Ogom jutted his tusks and bore down on the axe blade with all his weight. Grede caught it on his war hammer and held for a moment, clearly rattled that some bastard dug out of a grave could actually take one of his hits.

With a roar he shoved the hammer forward, sending Ogom stumbling back, then threw a punch into the big troll's chest that made him grunt and stagger several steps.

In the time it took for that exchange, the Deathwalkers following Ogom had already surrounded Grede. One behind him was just raising his battle axe to slash at the pursuing dwarf when a blade punched through the back of his skull.

Vereesa arrived in a flash, her blessed-oil-enchanted blades cutting through the Deathwalkers like a whirlwind. Grede shot her a look, snorted as if annoyed she'd stolen some of his kills, and went back to hammering Ogom.

Ogom had barely steadied himself and raised his battle axe when Skybreaker smashed the haft clean in half. The burst of lightning scorched a huge swath of the bandages wrapped around him. Grede wound up for the finishing blow, only to get shoved aside by another Deathwalker.

A few nearby Deathwalkers tried to flank the troublesome dwarf while Vereesa was busy with three axe-wielding attackers of her own — until vines and thorns erupted from the cracks in the floor and coiled around them.

The thorns tightened relentlessly. One Deathwalker's leg was snapped clean off.

Sora gritted her teeth and channeled her fury. The miserable fate of the green dragons and dragonkin outside had her seething, and the terrifying boost from the Refined Wisdom Elixir had made her Entangling Roots — normally used to hinder foes — downright lethal.

She guided the wrangling spell with one hand and used the other to cleanse the vile curses the Deathwalkers kept laying on Vereesa, whose situation was growing more desperate by the second.

Jace, however, held back. Several high priests in the deep end of the hall were doing the same, eyeing each other across the chaos. They stood in sharp contrast to the dwarf, the elf, and the undead troll already locked in combat.

These high priests might have spent God-knows-how-many years cooped up in this temple without stepping outside, but they reminded Jace of battle-hardened mages out of the Wizard Sanctum or the Kirin Tor.

Those mages either struck first from ambush or waited with all manner of bizarre counterspells, trying to seize the advantage by going second.

Jace could feel it — these bastards had clocked how dangerous he was. Maybe even the most dangerous one in the group. So none of them bothered helping the Deathwalkers against the dwarf and the elf. They just stared straight at him, waiting for his move. They didn't even seem interested in the green dragon who'd taken elf form off to the side.

But time was on Jace's side.

With Sora's help, he wasn't worried that Grede and Vereesa would fall to the Deathwalkers. As long as he could keep an eye on these priests and whatever dark arts they might throw, once the fight up front turned in their favor, the priests cowering in the back wouldn't be far behind.

Besides, Jace's side had more than four people.

Finally, one of the high priests couldn't hold back any longer. He glanced at a Deathwalker Grede's hammer had knocked flat, raised his arm, and started chanting some spell to aid him — and the moment his attention shifted, a shadow flashed behind him and a blade cleaved his throat open!

The troll priest's incantation was cut short. But the nearby priests reacted instantly, hurling spells at the figure emerging from the shadows — Morophil. Jace hit one of the high priests with a Suffocation spell, wrapping shadow energy around his throat and hoisting him into the air.

He was about to use Blood Grip to drag another priest in front of him when the mental thread driving the spell snapped. Nothing happened.

Worse, the hand he'd reached out didn't look like his own anymore. It had turned into a slimy, scrawny monster's arm. A wave of confusion and hunger flooded his mind. Time around him seemed to slow severalfold.

Time slowed, but that didn't mean he'd sped up. Jace realized some kind of troll hex he'd never encountered before had taken hold. He immediately forced his emotions under control, purging every irrational, baseless desire that had no business being in his head.

In an instant, his hand snapped back to normal. And with his consciousness restored, the source of the hex became clear. He shifted his arm toward it and channeled Blood Grip to yank the caster over — but a thin red line flickered between him and the troll priest, and the grip couldn't budge him.

Purple arcane sigils shimmered across the troll's robe and even his gray-blue skin. Some kind of incredibly powerful Shadow Ward, drawing on an ethereal, otherworldly energy source.

But clearly resisting Jace's Blood Grip was costing him dearly — the priest's expression was twisted in agony.

Jace simply summoned a flame and set the robe on fire. While the priest panicked at the burning, Jace hurled a massive Shadowflame orb that blew him apart into a charred, skeletal wreck.

Jace glanced at his arm, which had been warped by the hex, and instinctively swore — except it came out as a "ribbit."

The frog croak startled him into a cough. He slapped his own face and looked over at Sora. "I got hexed," he muttered. But that came out normal.

Sora didn't seem to hear him. She was channeling a gust of nature's wind to disperse the corrupted miasma left behind by the dead Deathwalkers, then shouted, "Get clear, Grede!"

Jace didn't press it. That high priest had probably tried to turn him into a frog — he'd just managed to resist the spell.

But his arm was screaming in pain. He noticed a tear in the skin where it had been warped, blood seeping out.

These troll hexes were fucking twisted, he thought.

With the priests down, Jace raised Ursales to harvest the souls of these undead creatures that still clung to their will. He clearly remembered from the game that when these Deathwalkers were defeated, their souls would break free and keep fighting — maybe like the crazed Atal'ai spirits, they could feed Ursales a little more.

By now Grede and Vereesa had cut down nearly all the Deathwalkers. The last one standing, Ogom, had one arm smashed to pulp. He sat slumped on the low steps leading to the altar, glaring at Grede.

He was still smirking, but his undead body was on the verge of total collapse.

Yet not a single soul manifested in the entire hall. Ursales seemed to have claimed the soul of the high priest Jace had personally incinerated with shadow fire — but the rest…

Not only could he not feel Ursales feeding, he couldn't even sense any spirits breaking free or drawing near.

The cries of souls in the hall had gone quiet. By now Jace couldn't hear any troll chanting at all.

"A' Talak, Kagar, Dath, Zul, Dorg, Hakkari!"

Boom.

Another dull gong blast made Jace blink.

He swept his gaze across the hall — the standing candles, the toppled Deathwalker corpses, Ogom smashed to pieces blow by blow by Grede's hammer, Vereesa standing alone some distance away with her bow trained on the wide entrance, ready for dragonkin to come pouring in. Sora was breathing hard, leaning on the Scepter of Domination, staring at the rows of troll stone tablets arranged on the high altar.

Morophil had just pulled her blade from a high priest's chest and looked up toward Jace. Another gong blast rang out, and Sazeno yelped and poked his head out of the pack.

The gong wasn't coming from this hall.
