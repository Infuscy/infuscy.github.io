## Chapter 1065: Amidst the Blood Rain

The shattered bones of Hakkar's collapsed avatar lay scattered across the Sacrificial Pool, and a blood rain fell through the hazy shafts of light above.

The blood was steeped with centuries of hatred, resentment, and unwillingness to rest — countless dead trolls, serpents, and other sacrifices, and finally the green dragons. These emotions were so close to tangible that they pressed down on you like a formless shadow in a nightmare, suffocating.

So Jace's expression wasn't exactly light. Vereesa called for Sora and Grede to help move Jace away from here, at least out of the blood rain.

With the avatar dead, the surviving Hakkar broodlings fled as well. Before long, the entire Temple of Atal'Hakkar was quiet as an ancient, abandoned ruin save for the patter of falling blood.

The green dragon flew to the nearby gallery and carried everyone inside to escape the corrosive rain. Once they'd steadied themselves, Jace wiped the blood from his face and watched Sora shimmer back into her elven form amid a glow of dream light.

"Just hold on a little longer, Jace…"

She closed her eyes, raised the Scepter of Domination, and drove it into the ground. With a flare of arcane enhancement, a radiance like moonlight lit the passage, and Jace immediately felt the suspicion, pain, and sorrow he'd carried out of the dream veil swept clean away.

A cool wind dispersed the rot and the fishy stink. Even the ground no longer felt so slippery and filthy. But what followed was a burning, throbbing pain — belly, legs, arms, wrists…

Jace didn't mind sharp, straightforward pain, though. To him, being able to feel these agonies clearly was usually a good sign — it meant his body was still loyal enough to report damage to his will, which was worlds better than having it all controlled by someone else.

"How are you feeling?" Sora opened her eyes.

"Could not be better." Jace smiled, then winced.

"Any more Nature Protection potions, Vereesa?" Sora asked, looking over at Vereesa sitting nearby. "Another sip would do him good. I can still sense a faint toxicity in the air here."

"No more." Vereesa rummaged through her pack. "We drank a lot earlier — at least, I'm out…"

"I'll rest a bit on my own, that's all," Jace said.

Sora nodded, though she still looked uneasy.

Vereesa raised her head and surveyed the passage. "When Hakkar manifested earlier… I thought the whole temple was going to collapse. It looks pretty solid, actually."

"Even Queen Ysera's magic couldn't bring this temple down — and it's kept most of its structure intact." Sora traced the Gurubashi reliefs on the wall. "These trolls' faith in the Soulflayer ran deeper than you'd think. You can tell just from the scale and craftsmanship of this temple."

"So that's the Soulflayer, Blood God Hakkar those Vilebranch trolls were talking about?" Grede shook his head. "Not to doubt my own strength, but I find it hard to believe…"

"They say Hakkar's true form was covered in brilliant feathers, and his heart shone brighter than a diamond…" Sora said softly. "I think the Atal'ai never truly managed to summon him into this world. This was merely a vessel, an avatar imbued with a fraction of Hakkar's power."

Jace sat to one side and said nothing.

He remembered that summoning Hakkar's true form required an ancient egg, which had to be imbued with exactly this fragment of power before it could be used by Hakkar's worshippers to bring him into the world.

That ancient egg was still far away on the other side of the continent, in the Hinterlands — there was no way it could have been imbued in the wake of Hakkar's avatar's destruction.

And summoning this avatar had already exhausted the temple's power. There'd be no second summoning anytime soon — which meant the ritual to summon Hakkar's true form in Zul'Gurub, the way it happened in the game, was basically impossible now. All that power had ended up in Ursales' belly.

Thinking of that, he felt a little more at ease.

After all, before their decisive battle with the Kingdom of Stormwind, the Gurubashi still retained at least some shadow of that ten-thousand-year empire. They never forgot that blood grudge — and once they summoned Hakkar into this world, the first thing on their minds would surely be to march on Stormwind and sacrifice the entire city's blood and souls to the Soulflayer.

This trip to Atal'Hakkar, he'd considered the possibility of facing the shadow of the green dragon prince Eranikus, but he'd barely given any thought to actually fighting Hakkar's avatar.

And he definitely hadn't expected that after they'd barged in and stirred things up, the avatar summoned by the Prophet Jammal'an himself would turn out to be this terrifying.

Jace glanced at the earring clutched in Vereesa's palm. If he'd been too wary of the distorted dream to use it to evade the dragonkin patrols, he probably never would have noticed the soul flames hidden behind reality.

What would have happened then was anyone's guess.

"What exactly happened in the end?" Grede asked. "Why couldn't we touch him at first, and then he suddenly turned so fragile?"

He looked at Vereesa, then at Jace, then back at Sora.

Sora looked at Jace and said nothing — but Jace knew she'd seen everything. From the moment he'd entered the veil, doused the flames, all the way to Hakkar's avatar being forced into reality — because she was the only one in the temple who could see through the veil right now.

"You'd have to ask Jace," Vereesa said. "I have no idea."

Jace tried to explain. "I sensed that Hakkar's avatar used the green dragons' power to hide its weakness inside a dream plane, sort of like…"

"Like the vault of the Guardian Scaravelle." Sora glanced at Ursales on the ground, finishing his thought. "Just on a much larger scale."

Jace nodded. "So as long as we destroyed those flames, we could shatter its 'immortality.'"

"Soul flames." Grede caught on. "You mean those weird fires we saw while hiding in the veil? Honestly, I never connected them to that monster."

"You heard the gong, right?" Jace explained. "Before we broke through the dream power, those purple flames didn't exist at all. When the gong sounded, the flames appeared with it. After the last gong strike, that avatar manifested too — so I suspected Hakkar's summoning was linked to those flames."

Vereesa looked at Jace in surprise. "How were you even able to think about all that in a situation like that?"

"Maybe because Hakkar's brood didn't take me seriously at all. They all went after Sora." Jace looked over at the green dragon. "Gave me room to think about what to do."

"Maybe because they thought you were one of their own," Grede said, glaring at him.

Jace gave him a cold sidelong look. Grede burst out laughing at the expression. Vereesa couldn't help but chuckle too, then said, "After all, Sora is the only green dragon still alive."

"Maybe it's because I tried to dispel its psychic magic." Sora gripped the Chain of Will around her neck. "I tried to free Jace from its phantasmal suppression… and was immediately swarmed by countless wind serpents. It also said… a lot of things to me."

Jace noticed the chain was glowing red-hot — it must have been scalding right now. Everyone wisely resisted the urge to ask exactly what Hakkar had said. He knew each of them had probably experienced some vision or whisper they'd rather not relive.

"It definitely felt that killing you sooner would let it break our minds faster with magic." Grede shrugged. "Even though it feels like I'm the one who personally smashed that so-called avatar to pieces, listening to you lot, it's like I was in the dark the whole time."

"I just found a way to exploit its weakness. As for what to do with it after breaking through, I had no idea either." Jace leaned back against the wall. "Who'd have thought you'd be arrogant enough to leap right onto its face and smash its skull?"

Grede spread his hands. "That's literally my job — just like dealing with the dark magic is yours."

"Fair enough," Jace said.

He could tell Sora still had plenty of questions on her mind — she just didn't want to come across as doubting anyone, or maybe she trusted everyone enough not to press.

"Even though this temple still looks solid, we should get out of here soon," Sora said. "Jace, can you walk?"

"I can walk," Jace said. "But what about your father? I mean, Prince Eranikus."

"Summoning Hakkar sacrificed every green dragon in this temple, Jace." Sora shook her head. "I can't hear his voice anymore. I can't sense his existence anymore."

"How is that possible? He's Queen Ysera's consort." Vereesa asked in surprise. "Are you sure, Sora?"

"I'll tell Queen Ysera everything that happened here." Sora said softly. "We prevented Hakkar's descent — that's what matters most. And this is exactly what the prince and his entire legion wanted to achieve. The rest…"

"Don't jump to conclusions yet." Jace pushed himself up the wall. "Think about it — if Eranikus had truly let Jammal'an pour his soul and flesh into Hakkar's avatar, we never could have survived. The power at his disposal was far greater than what we saw. He has to be somewhere in this temple still."

"You're right, Sora. Don't lose hope yet." Vereesa looked outside. "The trolls and wind serpents in this temple are disorganized now. The dragonkin who were trying to kill us are all down too. Why not search carefully while we have the chance?"

Grede said, "Besides, we came all this way and put in this much effort, fighting all sorts of bizarre things — you can't expect us to go back empty-handed, can you? These Atal'ai trolls must have stashed away no small number of goodies."

"That's a good point." Jace looked at him. "I'd actually forgotten about that."

"But with the dark power here, your recovery will be difficult, Jace…"

Sora said, hesitating. She started to take off the Chain of Will to return it to Jace, but he stopped her.

"Keep wearing it, Sora. Nothing's gone wrong so far, has it? Give it back when we leave." Jace said. "I have the Dragonqueen's power protecting me. I'm not going to be taken down that easily."

Sora still nodded with some reluctance. Grede wagged his finger. "This green dragon's temperament doesn't fit with our crew at all."

"Not many people are willing to match your temperament," Vereesa said, giving him a sidelong look.

The two of them bickered for a few rounds. Jace laughed, and after checking their gear, the group prepared to press deeper into the temple.

That was when he noticed the succubus behind the dwarf smiling at him. "I believe you should give the knife back now, master?"

Jace nodded and handed over the short blade, still stained with his blood.

Morophil took it, licked the blood from the blade, and smiled mysteriously as her eyes narrowed.

It wasn't the twisted, satisfied expression of someone savoring her master's fluids — it was the smile of someone wanting to see what trick her master was going to pull next. Jace knew what she was suspicious of: before he'd even found Vereesa to get the earring and enter the veil, he'd already asked her for a blade carrying the blood of Hakkar's brood.

She must have been wondering how he'd known, before ever seeing those soul flames, that he'd need this blood to douse something.

But Jace just gave her an innocent look that meant nothing in particular. These two demons had zero interest in exposing him anyway.
