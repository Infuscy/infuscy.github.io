## Chapter 1067: Eranikus's Gift

It was a rather beautiful green gem — lustrous and transparent, its shape square and clean, with almost no impurities or veins inside. It glimmered with a faint light that seemed almost like soul force, like a shard of ice that would never melt.

Sora walked over and picked it up. After a moment, a humming sound came from within the gem. "I'd like to see that earring once more…"

A deep, rumbling voice rippled through Jace's heart. Judging by the others' reactions, they'd all heard it too.

Vereesa brought the earring up next to the gem. The voice within murmured, "I cannot see it, but I can clearly feel its power. Ysera, my queen… why do you possess her treasure, mortals? I remember, long ago, she gave this earring to the Life-Binder Alexstrasza. Is the Dragonqueen well?"

"She's well, Father," Sora said. "And Queen Alexstrasza is in the Emerald Dream helping Queen Ysera fight the Nightmare's corruption. It's all because of these friends who helped us enter this temple. Queen Alexstrasza is grateful to them for freeing her from the orcs' imprisonment…"

"Orcs?" The presence inside the gem clearly didn't know the word.

"Deathwing used the Demon Soul to imprison the Dragonqueen," Jace explained. "The orcs are foreign invaders driven by demons — Deathwing's accomplices. But it's all over now. Deathwing is dead."

"Deathwing… is dead?" The voice in the gem seemed to resonate with something, vibrating. He murmured, "So it's true, then. I thought the sense of relief I felt not long ago was a sign my essence had begun to corrupt. Since the Life-Binder is willing to trust you, I see no reason to doubt. No wonder you were able to thwart Hakkar's return to this world once more. I'm grateful she's willing to travel to the Emerald Dream to help Ysera fight the corruption — because that… is closely tied to my own mistake."

"What happened, Father?" Sora asked, glancing around at the hall, the walls, the Essence Pool.

The voice in the gem answered, "Back then, Ysera's magic didn't completely destroy this temple — which made us overlook the danger lurking here. Not until the Atal'ai successfully completed their summoning ritual, threatening even the Emerald Dream with this evil, did Ysera realize the problem. She had to restore the Emerald Dream as quickly as possible, so she sent me to lead the green dragons here, into this swamp."

"Before coming here, I'd already prepared to fight the Soulflayer to the death. But after defeating many of the Atal'ai's guards and entering the temple, I realized the Atal'ai hadn't actually summoned Hakkar's true form — only his avatar. Still, the avatar was dangerous enough. After all, its descent had even disrupted the peace and balance of the Emerald Dream. And its avatar was the most important step toward his true arrival. But I didn't consider all that at the time. Hakkar's avatar, powerful as it was, couldn't withstand an assault from me and my companions. His immortal form was broken as the souls the trolls sacrificed to him ran out. His body was torn apart, his feathers melted by acid, his bones shattered…"

The voice in the gem fell silent for a long while before continuing. "But we realized this didn't stop the evil here from corroding that pool — and the entire swamp. The avatar's remains weren't losing power the way we'd imagined they would after its soul departed. A deeper cleansing and restoration of the entire region was needed."

"But at the time, I was eager to solve the problem at its root. The breaking of Ahn'idar had brought tremendous devastation to the entire world, the Emerald Dream included, and it still hadn't been fully resolved by the time I left the Emerald Dream. Not to mention the War of the Silithid that came after cost us dearly — the Emerald dragonflight's strength was already stretched thin. These things you should know well, Somaleer."

"I know, Father," Sora nodded.

"At the time, I thought an evil god's husk and a bunch of foolish Gurubashi trolls… didn't warrant too much of my attention. I needed to stay at Ysera's side to face graver crises. So I made a decision that would bury every kinsman loyal to me who'd followed me here."

"You chose to let the green dragons consume Hakkar's avatar, hoping your own power would cleanse this temple quickly," Jace said.

"Yes." The voice in the gem said. "With Ysera's instructions, I shouldn't have taken the problem here so lightly."

Jace now understood why the green dragons here had completely severed their connection to the Emerald Dream — and why the Atal'ai priests had planned to use these dragons' blood to summon Hakkar once more.

"But I can still do what I can to make up for the disaster I caused." The voice asked, "What is the current year?"

"The War of the Silithid ended a thousand years ago," Jace said.

The voice in the gem fell quiet for a time, then asked, "How is the swamp outside? The trolls' kingdoms, and the human kingdom established here?"

"Hakkar's corruption hasn't spread far beyond this pool, Father," Sora said.

"Though the situation outside isn't exactly great, at least it has nothing to do with the filth in this temple, dragon." Grede stepped forward, examining the gem from side to side. "Are you trapped inside this gem? How do we get you out — smash it open? Is this some kind of troll sealing stone?"

"No, no." The voice in the gem said. "This is my own gem. When I realized the corruption of my legion had grown beyond reversing, I sealed the still-lucid part of my consciousness inside this gem. That way, even if my physical body decayed, the cultists couldn't fully claim my power. Still, I couldn't stop my corrupted flesh from trying to devour the will inside the gem — it just never managed it. But this also protected my essence from being exploited by those trolls, which was a blessing in disguise."

"You know what to do, Somaleer. Place this gem in the Essence Pool, and my consciousness can be fully cleansed and restored. Then I can return to the Emerald Dream on my own."

Sora seemed to hesitate. Jace cut in immediately: "No. That Essence Pool has been corrupted by Hakkar's power. There's no other way?"

"Even the Essence Pool is corrupted?" The voice in the gem grew much more subdued. He murmured, "All these years, I'd been hoping that one day I could rally myself with that spring built by Ysera's power. Restoring my strength isn't without other methods, but my essence must leave this temple — and once it's separated from my consciousness, my kin may be lost in this temple forever."

"The green dragons in this temple are gone. Only you and Sora remain," Jace said. "I'm sorry — the blood of those green dragons and dragonkin was all sacrificed by the trolls."

The gem made no reply, but Jace could feel the grief radiating through it. Sora lowered her head and quietly wiped her eyes.

Jace said, "If there's a way to cleanse you outside this temple, then we'd best take you out now."

The voice in the gem said, "But I can still sense Jammal'an… that Atal'ai wretch. He's still alive. Kill him — not just for revenge, but to put a permanent end to the Atal'ai's ambitions. So long as he lives, the prophecy won't be broken, and there will always be mad believers following at his side!"

Yes — Jace guessed that the Atal'ai high priest Jammal'an had summoned Hakkar's avatar while they were tangled up with the green dragons, Ogom the Wretched, and the Deathwalkers, but he himself had never shown his face from start to finish.

"He might still be hiding below," Jace said, looking at Grede beside him. "He summoned the avatar from beneath the Sacrificial Pool just now."

"We can't let him escape," Grede said.

"I don't think he will either," Vereesa said. "He'd never abandon the entire temple he created."

"The depths of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar are a corrupted hell… you must be extremely careful entering them." The voice in the gem said, "I've kept some artifacts in this hall, originally meant to preserve some power to fight Hakkar's corruption. But now I can't use them myself — they should be of great help to you."

"Where?" Grede looked around. "There's nothing in this hall."

"By the steps," the voice in the gem said.

The group walked over to the steps. The voice chanted something in Draconic that Jace couldn't quite follow — but nothing happened.

"My apologies, everyone. With only this gem's power… I can't manage it anymore. This is a magical seal that can only be opened by a pure and healthy member of the Emerald dragonflight." The voice in the gem said.

"Can't Sora do it?" Grede asked anxiously.

"I… I…" Sora hesitated. "Even Father's voice can't do it — how could I?"

"You can, my child." The voice in the gem said. "I can feel that by the time you reached here, you'd already exhausted your strength and endured too much pain. Even if these things can't help you find and kill that evil Hakkar cult leader, they can at least help you leave this temple safely. I don't want any more green dragons trapped here because of me."

"But…"

Sora still hesitated. Jace knew why — because she didn't believe her own power was pure.

Eranikus didn't know she'd once fallen, even more thoroughly than every green dragon in this temple, her form in the Nightmare twisted into something like an apocalyptic abomination forged from blood and Shadow magic.

But after a moment's hesitation, she walked up to the steps, straightened her chest, and was just about to chant the incantation when she seemed to think of something and pressed her lips together.

Sora unclasped the Chain of Will from her neck and handed it to Jace. He understood — she was treating this seal as her own test.

If she wanted to prove she truly had no problem, she couldn't rely on any outside help.

After Jace took the chain, Sora repeated the incantation spoken by the voice in the gem.

In an instant, the steps before them began to shift. Piles of dust cascaded down, revealing a passage leading downward.

Sora gasped in surprise and immediately grabbed Vereesa's hand in excitement. The green dragon lord inside the gem clearly didn't know why she was so thrilled, and said nothing more.

Jace couldn't help but smile watching her excitement, because he knew full well that for Sora, this was far more than simply opening a vault door.

After putting the chain back on, Sora walked down the steps.

Jace could feel that some kind of ward against evil — or a protective field propped up by dream power — still existed inside, so he was a little worried stepping in.

The moment he crossed the threshold, he felt Ursales tremble faintly, reminding him of the ward set by Dar'Khan in Winterspring. But this field didn't trigger any reaction, and Ursales wasn't stirred up either — they passed through safely.

To be safe, he handed the demon pack containing Sazeno to Morophil in advance and had her wait outside for now. As a living demon, she clearly had no intention of barging in either — no fancy collision with Eranikus's magic.

The others followed her to the deepest part. Here, not even the light of the Essence Pool reached. The whole cave reeked of damp earth. Thankfully, there was no trace of evil blood or Shadow in the smell.

Jace drew Song of Azure and swung it around, revealing a place like a hollow within a tree. Layer upon layer of still-living vines had completely enveloped the walls.

Deep within the vines, aside from some emeralds brimming with powerful dream energy, and several dragon claws and fangs magically reforged into daggers and short blades, the most eye-catching thing was a longsword placed right in the center, hoisted up by layers of nearly withered vines.

The longsword, its material seemingly white, lustrous jade, was covered in cyan tree-grain patterns. Jace recognized it at first glance — wasn't this the ultra-rare-drop sword from the Shadow of Eranikus, Dragon's Call?

No wonder it never dropped after so many runs — the bastard had hidden it under the floor.

"It was the sword I carried in my mortal form. It can summon the power of dreams…" The voice in the gem murmured. "My foolishness and arrogance betrayed its power. Everyone — it's yours now."

Grede reached for it, but Jace coughed. The dwarf glanced back at him, licked his lips, and said, "This sword's too long. Yeah. Not for me."

"Vereesa." Sora gave her a meaningful look. "This one's yours."

"But I don't know how to use a sword." Sora picked it up helplessly, waved it, then handed it to Vereesa. "You're the greatest swordswoman I've ever met, Vere — you should be the one to take it. And when you return to Quel'Thalas, you can use it to slay more Hakkar priests. I'm sure this sword would be willing to follow you too."

"I'm more accustomed to the Ranger-Lord's blade." Vereesa smiled sheepishly — but the moment she heard Sora meant to give her the sword, her eyes had already locked onto it.

After all, even for a family like the Windrunner's, a treasure sword wielded by a green dragon lord was an unparalleled masterpiece. And Vereesa herself was a skilled swordswoman — it was hard not to be tempted by a weapon that fit so perfectly.

"Take it, Vereesa." Sora pressed the hilt into the elf's hand.

"All right… thank you, Sora." Vereesa said reluctantly. "Let me hold it for now — we'll discuss it properly once we're outside, because this really is too valuable."

"Did I mishear that?" Grede muttered nearby. "There's actually something you consider too valuable…"
