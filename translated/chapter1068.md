## Chapter 1068: The Depths of Atal'Hakkar

Besides the green-jeweled sword Dragon's Call, the other items in the vault were no less precious — they just seemed a little overshadowed beside it.

For instance, some emeralds and green agates brimming with powerful natural energy. Not only were they rich in mana, pure enough to serve Sora as spell components and a mana source, but they could also be crafted into accessories that allowed the wearer to quickly recover strength and spirit.

What caught Jace's attention was a short dagger that appeared to be made from a green whelp's claw. It not only carried the green dragon's own magic but was clearly highly resistant to Shadow corrosion.

Still, he had no time to carefully test the power of these artifacts here.

Leaving the passage and returning to the Sacrificial Pool, the blood rain brought by Hakkar's avatar's death had already stopped.

Naturally, they couldn't just jump into the temple's lowest levels from the shaft at the very center — and at this point, the opening was blocked by the massive corpse of Hakkar's avatar. Getting past those bones just to take a look would've been difficult enough.

Fortunately, Vereesa still remembered that there had been many branching paths leading deeper on the way in. So the group left the Sacrificial Pool and, following her, entered another narrow passage nearby.

By now, Eranikus's essence gem had fallen silent.

Jace wasn't surprised. He couldn't feel much power from this gem — far less than an adult dragon, at best. Trapping yourself in an artifact like this for the long haul had to be torture. Reluctant to talk much and seizing any chance to drift into slumber was understandable.

Eranikus wasn't the only one exhausted — by now, Jace was getting dead tired too.

He hadn't eaten a proper meal since entering the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, nor had he rested even once. The ghosts that could appear at any moment, the trolls patrolling everywhere, the traps and snares of every kind kept his nerves wound tight around the clock. The endless maze of identical, lightless passages and chambers made his head spin.

He glanced at his pocket watch — the hands pointed to three, though he couldn't tell if it was morning or afternoon. He'd originally planned to use how often he drank the Sylvia potion to roughly estimate how long they'd been inside, but he'd soon realized none of them had followed Sylvia's instructions about the potion's duration — they all just took a sip when they felt it was about right, or when they felt the effects were wearing off.

In any case, Jace was now exhausted and starving to the limit. He noticed Grede in front rubbing his eyes too — though that fellow had at least slept properly the night before entering the temple, whereas Jace hadn't even rested well on his last decent night.

Vereesa, of course, showed no sign of flagging from when they'd first entered. She carried her bow at the front, eyes fixed on the depths of the passage.

Though the murmurs of troll ghosts and the howls of trolls could no longer be heard, the walls still occasionally transmitted the sound of footsteps, or the scrape of metal.

There were still survivors here — not to mention the temple's priests hiding in the depths. They couldn't exactly stroll through like they were sightseeing.

While there was no danger for the moment, Jace pulled a strip of dried meat from his pocket and stuffed it into his mouth. The meat had somehow gotten damp and carried a faint whiff of sewer water — but as long as it wasn't soaked in Hakkar's blood, anything that filled the stomach was better than nothing.

At the same time, he still had Eranikus and the green dragons consuming Hakkar on his mind — after all, he'd also drained some of Hakkar's brood's blood with magic. Who knew if that was dangerous?

But after Sora had cleansed him of toxins and disease with nature magic, he hadn't felt anything unusual about the blood he'd drawn in, at least not so far. Probably the blood of Hakkar's brood simply wasn't as terrifying as the blood of Hakkar's avatar.

Given the blood issue, there was one more thing on his mind right now — the green dragon blood he was supposed to bring back for Ursula.

If what Eranikus said was true, and the green dragons' corruption came from consuming Hakkar's flesh and blood, then could this blood still be brought back to Stormwind?

As for Sylvia's commission — the Sacrificial Pool was full of dead Hakkar brood. Just find one of modest size and lop its head off. That part was easy enough to handle.

As Jace pondered whether there was any way to purify the tainted green dragon blood, a shadow suddenly flinched in the dark passage ahead.

He raised Song of Azure. A crisp, brief melody rang out with his movement, and arcane light flared as his focus snapped forward, illuminating the area. The shadow was suddenly entangled by several vines that had sprung from the wall, stumbling a couple of steps — but after only a slight adjustment, the creature nimbly pulled its feet free from the tangle and fled into the deeper shadows of a side passage.

It had all happened too fast. Sora hadn't managed to summon stronger thorns. She moved to give chase, but Vereesa stopped her. "Just an axeman. Probably only running for his life."

Grede nodded. "Looks like the trolls' faith in Hakkar isn't that unshakeable either — at least it can't hold a candle to the faith those priests in the Cathedral of Light have in the Holy Light."

Vereesa looked at the dwarf and said, "You may not know this, but the Rangers encountered Gurubashi jungle trolls in the Hinterlands too — most likely Atal'ai who'd abandoned their faith and fled here. With the evil god's avatar dead, their years of effort have come to nothing. More and more will want to escape from here. Honestly, that works in our favor — the fewer willing to help that fleeing high priest, the better."

Grede turned back. "Then what about Jammal'an? The souls in the whole temple are spent, the green dragons are all dead. If that high priest thinks this place has lost its value…"

Jace said, "The other Atal'ai might be able to change their names and disappear back into Stranglethorn, but he never could — even the trolls in Booty Bay know him. The Atal'ai are mortal enemies of the Gurubashi Empire. Other than staying here, he has nowhere to go. What I'm more worried about is him playing hide-and-seek with us — say, pretending to be dead, waiting for us to leave, then gathering people to plot anew."

"Rest easy, Jace." Vereesa said softly. "I know his voice. I know his breath. Pretending to death and slipping past us isn't that simple."

Ever since they'd descended into the depths of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, this elf ranger's perception of the entire structure and the trolls had far exceeded what Jace, as a human, considered normal. It was completely unlike how she'd been in Ashenvale and Feralas dealing with the night elves.

Without her, Jace probably would have gotten lost relying solely on his past life's memories of this temple long ago. So when she was this certain, Jace harbored no doubts.

As they descended deeper into the temple, walking the spiral stairs, Jace felt the air growing more and more humid. An indescribable stench — you couldn't tell if it was fishy or rotten — grew thicker with every step. Moss began creeping across the steps and walls in greater and greater patches. After Song of Azure swept its light over them, the air and environment took on a suffocating, dark green hue.

After a while, the sound of water appeared ahead — like a fish suddenly breaking the surface. But looking over from here, you could see neither fish nor water.

"Are we getting in the water again?" Grede looked at Jace beside him. "My clothes only just dried off!"

"Shh." Vereesa pressed a finger to her lips. Grede immediately shut his mouth and looked ahead warily.

Even he, who loved teasing this elf ranger, knew better than to ignore the professional's word at a time like this.

Vereesa signaled for Jace to put away Song of Azure first. After he did, she crept forward alone toward the corner ahead and peeked inside.

"It's clear."

She beckoned. Jace and Grede caught up and looked — the passage ahead, lower down, had been submerged. Judging by how high the water reached on a nearby giant serpent statue, the depth probably came up to about waist height.

Grede cursed under his breath. Jace knew that waist height for him and the elf would mean up to his chest.

"I can carry you across," Jace said. "Don't underestimate my strength."

Grede said in a low voice, "I'd rather drown in this foul water today than spend the rest of my life living down the words 'I carried you through the water in a troll's nest.'"

The two of them traded barbs — when a sudden gurgle churned the water's surface. They fell silent at once. Vereesa said, "A fish. Or… some kind of aquatic beast. But not a troll or a magical creature." Grede frowned. "Something like a frenzy fish in there?"

"I don't think it's a fish," Sora said. "We can still turn back now," Jace said.

"Not a chance." Grede was the first to hoist his pack and war hammer and wade into the water.

Jace ran his hand lightly across the surface. He could clearly feel the water had been tainted by the blood of Hakkar's avatar — possibly because it connected to the Pool of Tears outside somewhere, diluting it somewhat, so the toxicity wasn't quite as severe.

But anyone living in it long-term would be hard avoid being affected by this blood.

Eranikus was right — even if he left here to seek purification, once restored he'd have to come back and continue the cleansing work. Without the green dragons guarding this temple, the corruption would eventually spiral out of control again.

Grede advanced with his pack held high at the front; Vereesa stayed at his side. Fortunately, the ground underfoot was solid stone tile, flat and even, so Grede managed a reasonably steady walk.

Jace watched the water warily at his side. After wading for a while, he kept feeling something off about the current around his feet and legs — as if something kept brushing past his calves.

Grede suddenly let out a muffled "ow" and jumped. Vereesa grabbed his shoulder. "Don't worry, Grede. Let me handle it."

She gazed at the water ahead and spoke an incantation in elvish. That was when Jace noticed a dark, massive shadow materializing beneath the surface.

An unimaginably huge catfish revealed itself in the water — its enormous mouth slightly agape, the dull, vacant white eyes set a little higher on either side of its mouthparts, its long whiskers rolling through the water like worms.

It wasn't some fearsome monster — just a whisker catfish of absolutely ludicrous proportions. Though it wasn't undead, Jace could feel a considerable amount of necrotic energy within it. Yet it not only didn't attack in a frenzy — it strangely maintained a restraint remarkable for a carnivorous fish. Was it because Vereesa could communicate with it?

Vereesa continued her incantation, and the catfish's mouth opened and closed in turn. The two carried on a baffling exchange for a few lines, then the catfish turned around, first exposing its pale, bloated belly, then its broad tail, as the water rippled gently.

"I've never seen a catfish this big in my life." Grede looked back. "Its head's bigger than Sora's!"

Sora rarely showed a hint of displeasure — unsure whether it was because she disliked being told she had a big head, or because she disliked being compared to a catfish.

"Follow me, everyone." Vereesa waded to the front. "It's going to take us to the Prophet."

"What?" Jace asked in surprise. "Why would it help us?"

Vereesa said, "It knows we defeated Hakkar's avatar and broke Hakkar's psychic magic here — which is the same as freeing it."

Grede followed, stepping as he asked. "It's that smart?!"

"I'm surprised too," Vereesa said.

Sora said, "My guess is it probably grew to this size feeding on the corpses at the bottom of the temple. It ate so many dead that it was affected by necrotic energy and gained enough intelligence for simple communication. In any case, as long as it's willing to help."
