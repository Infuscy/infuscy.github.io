Chapter 1069: Guardian of the Temple

Though the catfish moved with excruciatingly lazy undulations, its speed was terrifying. The group pushed against the water in a frantic bid to keep up, nearly losing it around the bends several times over. Add in the fact that Jace was now dead tired and starving, and walking even a little made his head spin.

But they hadn't gone far before the catfish led them to a rather broad corridor. When they reached the middle, Vereesa, who'd been walking up front, suddenly stopped.

Jace looked up and noticed the catfish had vanished without a trace at some point. "Where'd it go?" Grede asked.

"It fled." Vereesa said, a hint of uncertainty in her voice. "I think… there's something ahead it doesn't dare face."

"Coward." Grede asked, "What's up ahead?"

The group advanced a little further. Slowly, a dark, colossal shadow materialized in the depths of the corridor.

The shadow blocked the entire passage. At first glance, you'd think it was a wall. Only when you sensed the vitality thrumming within it, and the occasional leak of a corrupt breath, could you confirm this was a living creature.

Jace raised Song of Azure, illuminating the shadow's brownish-yellow skin. He already had a sinking feeling about what this was. They hadn't found the evil awakening grounds directly beneath the Sacrificial Pool — but what they were meant to meet, they'd met all the same.

"Dread troll." Vereesa clenched her teeth. "Goddamn it… there actually is one of these things here."

No sooner had she spoken than that "wall" suddenly turned its head, its face exposed in Song of Azure's arcane glow. Two tusks as long as scimitars jutted from its mouth. Its head was deformed and swollen, drooping low. Its massive arms were packed with what you couldn't tell was muscle or tumor…

This was a true dread troll — not merely a tall troll. Only when he laid eyes on this creature did Jace realize how laughable and inadequate his previous imagination of dread trolls had been. It towered far above an ogre or a tauren in both size and bulk. It didn't even look like a humanoid — it looked like a pure war machine!

"Dathas… Guzunur, Atal'alarion, Zul, Jintus, Jammal'an!" the dread troll howled in the troll tongue.

"Is that Jammal'an?" Grede asked.

Sora said, "No. His name is Atal'alarion."

Jace remembered this fellow — the temple guardian guarding the pool below the Sacrificial Pool.

This clearly wasn't the waters beneath the Sacrificial Pool; it was just a narrow corridor. Atal'alarion's appearance here could only mean there was nothing left worth protecting in those waters — the thing truly worth protecting lay deep within this corridor.

But before he could think further, Atal'alarion was already bracing against both walls, kicking up sheets of water as he charged straight at them. Vereesa hastily loosed two arrows that buried themselves in his chest and jaw — and seemed to do absolutely nothing.

"Come ugly, try me!"

Grede howled and swelled in size, his glowing lightning hammer in hand as he rushed to meet the dread troll head-on. Even at his fully enlarged stature, the dwarf still pitifully short against the dread troll.

Atal'alarion swung his shackled arm down at the hammer. The war hammer collided with the manacles in a blinding flash — the shackles were smashed clean through, but the troll seemed entirely unaffected.

The troll easily burst free of the vines that had rapidly coiled around both his hands, reached out, and grabbed Grede — grip locked on the war hammer — and hurled the dwarf away, leaving behind only a string of dwarven curses.

After throwing Grede, he turned and took a Shadow Bolt right to the face. The bolt ate into the skin and flesh of his face, corroding it into something black, swollen, and oozing — even exposing the skull beneath. But it didn't deliver a killing blow.

Jace had wanted to channel every last scrap of strength he could muster into that one Shadow Bolt and blast the creature apart. He hadn't expected this thing's vitality to be this tenacious. Atal'alarion was clearly enraged. He roared and charged straight at Jace. Jace barely managed to throw up a Curse of Weakness before his vision swam.

After the battle with Hakkar, he'd only just recovered enough to keep up with the group. And even if Ursales had enough power to hack this troll's head off like cutting tofu, Jace wasn't confident he had the strength and focus left to wield it for a perfect strike.

Vereesa decisively drew her twin blades at her waist and rushed to meet him. The dread troll swung a fist at her; she nimbly ducked under it, then swept two flame-wreathed slashes across his arm. She ducked lower again, taking advantage of the moment his reaction lagged from the Curse of Weakness to dart beneath him and drive her sword into his leg.

Though Vereesa had stabbed or slashed him three times in the blink of an eye, the troll seemed utterly unhindered. He swung back at Vereesa to grab her. She barely dodged the grab — but the massive manacle on his arm caught her, sending her stumbling a few steps into the water.

Clearly, compared to Vereesa, who'd poked several bleeding holes in him, Atal'alarion hated the caster of that Shadow Bolt far more. He didn't even bother going after the ranger, beelining straight for Jace instead — and Jace didn't even have the strength for a single Shadow-step.

At the last instant, a green shadow from behind rammed him into the wall beside him, stars exploding in his vision. Jace steadied himself against the wall and looked over — a green dragon had collided with the dread troll.

The green dragon shrieked, claws raking Atal'alarion's skin, fangs gnawing at his neck. But the dread troll's neck was simply far too thick, and the corridor was too narrow. The two colossal creatures battling in the tight space couldn't spread out. The green dragon adjusted its serpentine neck again and again — blocked by the wall here, blocked by the troll's arm and shoulder there — unable to find the right angle for a lethal bite.

Atal'alarion angrily lifted the green dragon entire, no matter how she tore his skin to shreds, and slammed her into the wall beside him. The impact dazed her so badly she could barely keep her grip.

That was when Vereesa pulled herself from the water. Seizing the opening while the two behemoths grappled, she gripped the radiant Dragon's Call in both hands and drove it deep into the troll's waist. The savage expression on the troll's face suddenly slackened. He released the green dragon and scratched at the spot where he'd been stabbed, as though it itched like mad.

Seizing the moment, lightning flashed from the other end of the corridor. Skybreaker sailed through and cracked squarely against the troll's exposed skull. Filth, brains, and shards of bone scattered across the entire corridor.

The dread troll, missing half his skull, collapsed with a crash, slumping against the wall into the water. He twitched for a while, murmuring something in the troll tongue that no one could understand, then went still.

"Damn it." Grede approached, war hammer in hand, and spat at the massive corpse. "No wonder that catfish was scared. This bastard's strength was no joke…"

Vereesa glanced at Dragon's Call in her hand. The wound where the dread troll had been stabbed by the sword had already swollen badly, black and bulging, oozing a yellow fluid — the pristine-looking sword, it turned out, carried an incredibly potent green dragon venom.

"We can't afford to waste time." She looked to Sora, who'd already reverted to her elven form. "That dread troll said he'd defend Jammal'an to the death. The high priest is most likely right inside."
