## Chapter 107: The Sheep Experiment

Compared to a regular linen or wool cloak, this leather cloak was a bit heavier. After pacing back and forth in front of his place a couple of times, Jace could really feel the heft of the monster hide.

But to an outside observer, it didn't have that rough look typical of animal-skin garments. At a glance, it was hard to distinguish from a normal cloth outer garment — the fire drake's back-sail was far smoother than he'd imagined.

Where the cloak attached to the hood, and at the shoulders, the construction seemed especially sturdy — apparently sewn from the creature's outer shell.

Smoldering fire drakes weren't true dragons. They were giant fire lizards that lived in scorching regions, even around molten lava.

When Larsson had first told him the cloak was made from a fire drake's hide, Jace had figured it would reek of blood and guts. Instead, it carried a faint, pleasant scent of smoke — clearly it had been carefully treated.

Well, something worth three gold had to be worth every copper. Jace pinched his waist and suddenly felt like a man of real stature.

Jace stood on the street for a while, puffing out his chest and watching the passersby, hoping some discerning soul would recognize what he was wearing and be impressed.

After standing there for half an hour, not a single person so much as glanced his way.

Hah. Must be that the people living along this canal were utterly lacking in sophistication.

But that wasn't necessarily a bad thing. If everyone could tell how valuable this cloak was, he'd be stared at every day — and someone would be liable to steal it any moment.

Thinking that, Jace pulled the cloak a bit tighter.

Come to think of it, Larsson had said this cloak had fire resistance and could boost the power of fire magic. Was that even true?

Jace didn't know any fire-related magic at the moment, but his master Marin was a specialist in fire elemental magic, and a warlock's offensive spells were inseparable from fire. This cloak could genuinely help his future development.

Though Varian Wrynn had probably chosen this cloak because of the burn on his hand.

Jace looked at the back of his hand. The mark left by the burning sword guard didn't hurt much anymore, but it was still clearly visible, and touching it still sent a twinge of pain through him.

It'd probably leave a permanent scar.

After lunch, Jace planned to take the imp out of the city and find some animal to test that Drain Life incantation on.

He'd been itching to try it ever since last night. If he didn't get to let it out today, he'd lose his mind.

He'd been distracted all morning. During Orcish class, he'd even been called out by Master Dushan for zoning out.

He knew he had his Orcish exam in less than a month, and passing it determined whether he could travel to Dalaran on public funds. It was just that the temptation of his modified Drain Life incantation was too strong — he had too many questions and theories about this spell.

What form did the so-called "vitality" take once it entered his body?

If he was injured, would draining life energy heal the wound? Would it work the same way as a healing potion?

Come to think of it, Jace hadn't had the chance to brew any healing potions yet, so he'd never drunk one. Maybe the effects of the Drain Life incantation would give him some insight into that.

If he was completely healthy and unharmed, would the life energy make him even healthier and stronger — or would it overflow and cause harm?

Of course, Sazeno knew nothing about any of this, because he was a demon and had no idea how the Nathrezim's terrifying magic worked. Everything was waiting for him to figure it out.

So that afternoon, Jace set out with the imp, a pack containing two mana potions, the Staff of Torgol, a meal's worth of rations, Jagin's notes on the incantation, and the summary and modifications he and the imp had worked out together.

But once he got into Elwynn Forest, he realized a serious problem.

Those deer, hares, birds and whatnot — they ran way too fast.

The harmless little creatures would spot Jace from far off, or even just catch his scent, and bolt. And Jace couldn't exactly kill them — he needed them alive to test the spell.

And the ones that didn't run?

Wolves, bears, boars and the like — Jace hadn't run into any yet, and didn't want to. The last thing he wanted was to be fumbling with a slip of paper and chanting a spell while some predator was tearing a hole in his neck and spraying blood everywhere. Even with Nathrezim power, he couldn't exactly suck that back in, could he?

So how was he supposed to solve this problem?

He'd briefly considered hiring some bold soul at the Slaughtered Lamb to be his test subject. In hindsight, two silver for a safe testing opportunity wasn't bad.

But the biggest problem was that the people being tested there were living, breathing human beings.

If it were a Curse of Weakness, a Curse of Recklessness, or even Fear — those were one thing.

But when it came to life-draining, Curse of Agony, or the Corruption he might learn down the line, he wasn't confident he could control the power, and he couldn't predict the consequences. He really didn't want to test it on a living person.

After thinking it over for a while, it suddenly occurred to him that the Dark Iron dwarves used goats for their testing. Why not use a sheep?

There were a few farms near Goldshire that raised sheep. He could buy one and test the spell on it.

Jace followed the side path off the road toward a small farm south of Crystal Lake, and paid three silver to take away a little sheep.

He'd really bled for this test — that one beating he'd taken yesterday had been for nothing.

He just hoped this modification of the incantation would work.

Jace walked a bit further southwest, away from the farm, and after making sure there was no one around, he tied the sheep to a tree and laid out the incantation, casting conditions, and everything else.

Sazeno leaped out of the bag, bouncing around in anticipation of the show.

Jace looked at the sheep, then at the tree next to it.

This tree — wasn't it alive too? Didn't life force flow through its trunk?

What would happen if he cast Drain Life on it?

Jace decided he'd better not go down that road. If an ordinary tree made a perfect test subject, Jagin and the rest would probably be holding their gatherings in the forest.

Since he'd already bought the sheep, he might as well put it to use.

"Elamon, Silendor!"

Jace spoke the incantation, finger pointed at the sheep. He felt a powerful tugging sensation clench at his heart — and what was tugging him was the shadow power that had gathered at the call. But the spell didn't produce the expected effect. That sudden sensation just... ended.

But the sheep had already been terrified.

It thrashed and convulsed, gnawing at the rope trying to escape, letting out a wretched cry that didn't sound like a sheep at all — more like a woman gone mad.

"Shadow power! Shadow power!" the imp shouted excitedly, treating the whole thing like a spectacle.
