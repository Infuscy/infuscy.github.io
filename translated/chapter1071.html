Chapter 1071: The Path to Godhood

The stonework around them grew older and stranger with every step, the corroded reliefs so worn you could barely make out their shapes — it was almost enough to make you forget this was a troll temple devoted to Hakkar.

Along the way, skeleton soldiers kept crawling out from God-knows-where — soaked through, in badly rusted armor, rotted and blackened rags hanging off them, clutching broken, damaged weapons, as though they'd just climbed out of the water.

Though they were the kind of foes that even an exhausted Grede could flatten with one swing, they still slowed the pace of the pursuit.

Finally, they chased the trail into a dark, deep hall half-flooded with shallow water. Grede caught sight of a towering serpent statue standing off to the side in Song of Azure's glow and hurled his war hammer from a distance, smashing the statue to pieces — lest its eyes suddenly light up with magic and cook up some new Atal'ai trick.

The lightning from the hammer lit up half the hall. Jace used the light to make out a hunched shadow climbing step by step up a staircase in the gloom ahead.

This dark hall was the end of the road. No other branching paths led away — the only exit was the way they'd come in.

They hurried to the foot of the stairs. That was when the figure collapsed on the steps, caught hold of the stone in front of him, and turned his face — a blood-streaked face lit bare by Song of Azure.

For a moment, Jace doubted whether the thing they'd been chasing was really this guy.

It was hard to even call him a troll anymore. His neck had stretched long like a serpent's. His body, too, was taller than an ordinary troll's — especially that waist, thin to a degree that made your skin crawl. Two frames of bloody bone had burst from his back, draped with scraps of feather, making the whole of him look like a chicken half-boiled and leapt straight back out of the pot.

"So close." The creature murmured. "I, almost… was about to accomplish a great feat no Atal'ai had ever achieved. I am the true…"

As it spoke, those rotting wings actually began to vibrate — though there was no hint of it ever getting off the ground, only a series of feeble, whimpering sounds.

"A' Talak, Kagar, Dath, Zul, Dorg, Hakkari!"

He raised a strange hammer in his hand and strained to howl the Atal'ai's prayer — a piercing, desiccated rasp.

The Skybreaker Grede had hurled smashed straight into his back. Blood and rotten flesh spattered across the entire staircase and the ancient stone seat high above.

"He wanted to become a god." Vereesa said. "History doesn't know how many Amani priests tried to steal the power of the deities they worshipped and take their place. Their endings weren't much better than his."

"So he was just using the entire Atal'ai to serve his path to godhood," Grede said.

"Each side took what it needed." Vereesa said. "The Atal'ai looked to Hakkar's arrival to grant them immortality — and they'd already seen hope in this high priest. If he really is Jammal'an, then he's at least lived several hundred years. I've seen that name on ancient troll stone tablets I collected in Quel'Danel myself."

"I can't sense his power anymore, Jammal'an." The gem Sora held emitted a voice. "Is he dead?"

"He's dead," Jace said.

"At last…" Eranikus's voice seemed to deflate all at once. "I thought his death would make me wild with joy, let me savor the pleasure of revenge to the fullest — but all I feel now is… cold. My children, my companions — they all died in this temple. At first, Domoreil fled this temple out of fear of Hakkar's power, and I raged at her as a traitor, a deserter. But looking at it now, our stubbornness was just another kind of foolishness."

"Domoreil is dead outside the temple, Father." Sora said softly. "She never made it back to the Emerald Dream, so none of us knew what had happened at Atal'Hakkar."

"That corrupted adult green dragon outside?" Grede asked.

The voice in the gem said, "I… this is my fault. I thought her fear came from being corrupted by Hakkar's power. To stop her corruption from reaching the Emerald Dream, I severed her connection to the dream on my own authority. I killed her."

So that was it — that was why the green dragons here hadn't been corrupted nearly as badly as the one outside.

They'd been under the protection of Eranikus's dream power within the temple, while the green dragon outside, though she'd escaped, had quickly descended into madness after losing Eranikus's protection. Lost and tormented all these years outside the temple, she'd slowly been twisted into the shape they'd found her in.

Eranikus spoke again. "I will be grateful for your help forever — and I believe Queen Ysera would feel the same. It's just that I'm still in no position to repay you."

"So how do we cleanse you inside this rock?" Grede asked.

"If possible, take me to Moonglade — the druids and moon priests there will know what to do," Eranikus said. "Though I know the journey is very far. Somaleer, if you can get word to a green dragon, you could have them come take me back."

Sora said with some hesitation, "Queen Ysera doesn't want me interfering in the green dragons' affairs anymore, because earlier…"

"She was also driven out of the Emerald Dream by your queen," Grede said.

"That's not the same, Grede," Sora said. "The queen only doesn't want me returning to the Emerald Dream — she didn't cut my connection to the dream."

"I understand." Eranikus's voice said. "The queen must want to protect you, Somaleer. You should understand that."

"I know," Sora said.

"But you and your friends saved my life in this evil god's fallen temple. That alone proves the strength of your power and the purity of your heart — proves you're equipped to face every threat the Emerald Dream holds." Eranikus continued. "When I tell Queen Ysera of this, she will certainly allow you to return to the dream once more. And I believe you've earned the right to become one of her guardians, young as you are."

"Dreamwarden?" Vereesa said in surprise. "I remember Lady Ysera — the one who's always looked after you — is a Dreamwarden, Sora…"

Hearing Eranikus's words, Sora was so startled she immediately said, "I wouldn't dare…"

"I trust I have enough authority to make that judgment, Somaleer." Eranikus said in a low voice.

Jace could feel that Sora wasn't exactly overflowing with joy. She touched the Chain of Will around her neck with a hint of guilt, then glanced at Jace — and he immediately knew what she was thinking.

She must believe that the reason she'd been able to help Jace and the others rescue Eranikus in this temple wasn't because of any strength of will of her own, but because that chain was protecting her.

And honestly… that was more or less the truth.

Jace pressed a finger to his lips, signaling her not to say anything about the Chain of Will for now. Sora's lips moved, but in the end she let out a soft sigh.

"I'll find a way to bring you back to Kalimor, Father," she said. "I don't think I'm ready to return to the dream just yet."

"I'll respect your choice, my child," Eranikus said.

"Let's discuss this after we're out of here." Grede rubbed his eyes. "I can't stand this damn place a second longer."

"See if that priest has anything valuables… anything important on him," Jace said, pointing at the corpse. "Check out that strange hammer of his."

"Almost forgot." Grede perked right up, bounding up the steps three at a time. He pried the staff Jammal'an had been clutching in his dead grip free from those bony fingers, waved it back and forth, and asked, "This… why is this a bat on top instead of a wind serpent?"
