## Chapter 1072: The Lucky One's Blood

"Is this a bat, or some kind of weird-looking wind serpent?" Grede lifted the bloodied scepter he was holding for a closer look.

Vereesa stepped in to examine it. "It's not the shape of a wind serpent totem. They've both got wings and fangs, but this is clearly a bat."

Grede looked up at the blurry scale-like reliefs on the walls around them, then at the decapitated giant serpent statue by the entrance he'd smashed earlier. "So why would the leader of a Hakkar temple be carrying a scepter shaped like a bat?"

"Maybe it has something to do with Shirrik, the bat loa the Gurubashi worship?" Vereesa folded her arms. "Well, I don't know either. I've only read about it in books. At least up north, there aren't many forest trolls who worship a bat deity."

She glanced back at Sola, as if hoping to get the green dragon's input. Sola looked at the green gemstone in her hands. "Father, do you know anything about a bat scepter and Shirrik?"

"A bat-shaped scepter?" the voice from the gem replied. "Before I trapped myself inside this stone, I never saw any trace of Shirrik worship in this temple."

"If even he doesn't know, we've got no hope of figuring it out," Grede shrugged. "This thing gives me the creeps. What do we do with it?"

"If you're all all right with it, I can take it back to Quel'Thalas and hand it over to the Reliquary." Vereesa took the scepter from the dwarf and held it up. "The Society's got plenty of scholars who specialize in trolls. I'm sure they'd have some insight on this."

Jace nodded. "That's a good idea. Of course, we're not handing over research materials for free."

"Of course it's not free," Vereesa said. "When I head back this time, I'll bring that ancient Kaldorei mirrorstone along and settle the fee with them."

"Then I'll leave it to you, lass. Don't forget to send the money back." Grede snapped his fingers. "Let's get out of here. I figured that high priest would've holed up in his stash of gold, but there's not a single valuable thing down here — let alone gold."

"For the Atal'ai, the most valuable things are blood and souls. Or food." Jace spoke as he walked down the steps, scooping up something small and conch-like from a puddle nearby. "Well, it's not like there's no coinage at all. Look at this — an Amani troll coin. You could sell it in Booty Bay for two silver."

"You recognize these?" Grede took the coin. "I've definitely seen this kind of troll currency in the Hinterlands. Why would northern troll money turn up down here?"

"I think it might be tied to the Gurubashi Empire's civil war." Vereesa stared at the little conch. "It's said that when Hakkar was first summoned into this world, even the Amani Empire sent legions to take part. But I don't know much about that period of history — Quel'Thalas was at a disadvantage back then, right up until the founding of the Farstriders."

"So this thing's a thousand-year-old antique?" Grede asked.

"Something that wasn't worth a damn a thousand years ago isn't worth much more now." Jace tossed the coin back into the water. "Besides, nothing in here is exactly clean. The closer we get out of this place, the better. The blood contamination down here is way worse than the upper level."

"Yes, getting out of here soon will help your recovery too, Jace." Vereesa said. "You seem even weaker than you were up above."

"I've got those stone totems upstairs to thank for that." Jace cursed under his breath, then suddenly remembered something and went over to Sola. "Is there any way to cleanse the Hakkar curse from green dragon blood?"

Sola shook her head. "There isn't, Jace — at least, none that we know of. If it could be cleansed, my father wouldn't have had to abandon his flesh, and the green dragons in this whole temple wouldn't have fallen so irreversibly. If it comes down to it, I can help you."

Jace said, "Even if we fall back on that, we'd have time once we're back in Stormwind. It's just that since we're already here…"

"You want to take some pure green dragon blood with you?" the voice from the gem asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

"Hmm…" Eranikus's voice fell silent for a moment. "I remember there was one who was only mentally controlled, but whose blood wasn't completely tainted by Hakkar's blood… When we took over the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, she was still a hatchling, so she didn't take part in devouring Hakkar's corpse. As far as I know, she's the only green dragon who survived from that time with uncursed blood. So I once tried using her blood to cleanse the corruption from the other dragons' blood as well — but that failed too, since she'd only just grown into a juvenile by then."

"What's her name?" Jace asked.

"Her name is…" Eranikus thought for a long while, then said quietly, "My essence is too weak. Recalling something this specific is terribly difficult. But if Somaaril saw his body, he'd clearly sense the difference between him and the other dead green dragons."

"I'll try, Father," Sola said.

"Let me enter my slumber, my child," Eranikus said. "Gamalan is dead. I can rest easy now. Staying conscious in this gem drains what little power I have left."

Sola responded by casting a calming spell over the gem. Its glow faded quickly, and it was no longer bright and translucent — instead becoming a thick, deep green, solemn and heavy.

"Then let's head back upstairs," Jace said. "Besides the green dragon blood, I need to bring a Hakkar spawn's skull to that troll witch doctor. Her potion did us a huge favor — I can't forget what she asked me to do."

"Are you sure she's all right, Jace?" Vereesa asked, somewhat skeptical. "She sounds just like an Atal'ai."

"I went with Jace to meet that troll," Sola said. "She should be fine."

"I hope so," Vereesa shook her head. "Maybe I just can't bring myself to trust any of these forest troll priests."

"I'll try asking her what she wants the wind serpent skull for, Wyn," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded, and everyone set about packing up to leave the lower level.

Before they left, Jace didn't forget to grab a dagger from a nearby dead skeleton, stab it into Gamalan's head, and twist it around — just to make sure he wouldn't get resurrected by some weird necromantic magic. After all, this place was crawling with troll mummies, vengeful spirits, and walking skeletons. Who knew if some lunatic might get the bright idea of dragging this Hakkar worshipper's leader back to life?

They planned to return the way they came, so they'd have to go through the waterway again.

Coming in, that giant catfish had led the way and they'd moved pretty fast. Going back, even though Vereesa remembered the route, they still had to carefully distinguish the forked paths and the features of the walls and reliefs, so the pace slowed down a lot.

At this point, Grede looked up and said, "Is that green dragon lord still awake?"

"He's asleep," Sola said. "I cast a nature spell on that gem so he could get some rest. Why?"

"I was just thinking about how to get him back to Kalimdor." Grede said. "You're thinking about how to go back and see Mosa, aren't you?" Vereesa laughed.

"If I can see her while escorting this green dragon lord back, then all the better," Grede smiled.

"It's only January — the Lunar Festival's still a ways off," Jace said as he walked. "Ilgala Tower's still six months from completion, at least that's what VanCleef told me. If Sola escorts you, I figure six months is plenty of time for you to make the round trip."

"You're not planning to go to Kalimdor?" Grede looked at Jace. "Beautiful grasslands, vast red-leaf forests, towering Mount Hyjal…"

The dwarf talked as he looked up, staring at a giant serpent relief coiled along the ceiling. "Mentioning our journey to the north of Kalimdor here feels like it was centuries ago — like something you'd hear in a tavern tale, not something us Wildhammer actually lived through."

Jace felt the same way, of course. Even though Kalimdor had had its share of hardships — like being chased by quilboar, or waking up in the middle of the night to find a thunder lizard walking right past your face — it was still infinitely better than facing the Soulflayer's corruption head-on.

Of course, Kalimdor had its own evils too — Jace knew that far better than Grede did.

"No," he said. "For one thing, I need to oversee the construction of that tower, make another trip to Grim Batol, and most importantly…"

He looked at the dim, no-longer-glowing green gem in Sola's hands. "Are you sure your father has fallen into slumber? That he can't hear anything outside."

"I'm sure," Sola said. "You heard what he said — his power in that gem is fragile, Jace. Even when he's awake, he can only hear sounds very close by. It's all to resist Hakkar's dark magic, from the mind to the physical form. You should have noticed that already."

"All right." Jace nodded. "When we get back, I need to prepare to summon Kalrez back to Azeroth. This staff has already absorbed enough soul power from this temple. It's time it returned the favor and helped me out. Once Ilgala Tower's finished, I'll take it there. With the power of the ley line nexus, a single Doom Lord should be enough to let us…"

"Take a look at Draenor," Grede finished for him.

Vereesa, walking ahead, said softly, "The other side of the Dark Portal."
