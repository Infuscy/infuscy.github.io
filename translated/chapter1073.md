## Chapter 1073: Who Can Lift That Warhammer

Back near the Pool of Tears, things had grown eerily quiet. The sound of a few people's footsteps echoed through the hall; other than that, there was only the occasional plink of water dripping God-knew-where.

Jace was starting to feel that spending a bit more time in the temple wasn't so bad. Once the Atal'ai had finished fleeing or hiding, and the beasts driven mad and frenzied by Hakkar's magic had calmed down, leaving would be much safer.

But looking at the bloodstains, bones, and shredded corpses all over the floor, he really had no desire to sleep here.

Especially that long-dead Hakkar avatar's skeleton in the middle. Even lying motionless, Jace still had the feeling it might leap up and start thrashing around again at any moment.

Too many strange things had happened in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar. He just wanted to shake this place off and get back to Stonard.

Sola frowned as she examined each piece of bone here. A juvenile dragon's shattered skull lay right next to Hakkar's skeleton, split open like a fish head that had been hacked apart with a cleaver.

Looking at the juvenile dragon's state, Sola pressed her lips together and shook her head. Jace could see it too — its eyeballs had already turned white, which counted as a fairly serious sign of corruption.

What Eranikus had said was simple: find a young juvenile dragon that hadn't yet fed on Hakkar's flesh. But every drake and juvenile dragon in this place had been blown apart by the sacrifice ritual — how the hell were you supposed to pick one out?

Still, Sola quickly found a juvenile dragon's severed arm that very likely belonged to the one Eranikus had mentioned.

Beneath the yellowed scales, the pale white arm showed no sign of blood remaining — it had probably all been drained by Gamalan's sacrifice ritual. Jace and Vereesa spent half the day's effort scraping out a half-bottle of residual, mucus-stained green dragon blood from the arm. Compared to the cursed blood of the other green dragons, this small bottle really was about as clean as it got.

A Hakkar spawn's skull was no trouble at all — they were all over the floor. Most were smashed, but finding an intact one wasn't hard.

He cut off a head of a convenient size, wrapped it in a piece of cloth, and after everyone rested a bit, they set out to leave the temple.

Returning the same way, they found that many of the stone gates that had been shut before were now half-open — probably opened by the fleeing Atal'ai trolls.

Jace's group had benefited from these trolls' trailblazing; the way out was much faster than the way in. Of course, the main reason was that they no longer had to watch out for troll patrols or mummies.

When they reached the courtyard outside, Grede glanced toward the sacrificial site where there'd been so many trolls before. It was completely empty now — nothing left at all.

"We're not going through that waterway again, are we? Are we going to run into those rotting, moving corpses this time?" Grede asked.

"This time we're prepared," Vereesa said. "It won't be as messy as last time."

"So we're still going through there." Grede sighed. "I'm throwing out every last piece of clothing on me the moment we hit Stonard, then I'll find some Bronzebeard brothers and get some clothes that fit. Though those little fellows' builds might not quite keep up with us Wildhammer height."

"Like being taller than the Bronzebeards makes you something to be proud of." Vereesa gave him a sidelong look.

"At least I'm taller than my own kin, huh?" Grede chuckled. "So why don't you tell me why you're so much shorter than your cousins!"

"No reason," Vereesa said.

"That's because the Kaldorei received the blessing of the World Tree, Nordrassil," Sola answered honestly.

Hearing that, the elf's face darkened and she strode on ahead, while Sola watched her back with a blank expression.

Grede snorted a laugh. Jace wasn't in much mood to join in right then. He asked, "You sure you don't want to deal with the Dark Iron dwarf situation, Grede?"

Having just provoked Vereesa into storming off, Grede had still been grinning. When Jace asked this, the smile slowly sank from his face.

"Never mind the Thorium Brotherhood members who are experts with a hammer — even if it's a few Bronzebeard brothers from Ironforge, any of them could lift that legendary warhammer and liberate the Dark Iron clans. That's King Magni's wish, isn't it?"

Jace said, "I'm not trying to pin this responsibility on you, Grede. But you've always wanted a weapon powerful enough to be called great, haven't you? I've been keeping this in mind, so I wanted to ask while we had the chance…"

"Isn't this warhammer great enough, Jace?" Grede held up the hammer in his hand.

"Of course," Jace said. "I'm not saying Skybreaker isn't great. But didn't you mention before that it would be hard to keep this warhammer for yourself? It means something to you, sure, but it's also an important relic of the entire Wildhammer clan."

"Isn't the Sulfuron Hammer the same?" Grede said. "Setting aside whether it can even be forged — even if it could, and it really was handed to me, and I really… really defeated… the Firelord…"

The dwarf coughed uncomfortably, then went on: "The first thing the Dark Iron dwarves would do once they were freed from the Firelord's curse is snatch that hammer back! You don't really think the Dark Iron's greed and arrogance came from Ragnaros, do you? That's just an excuse they found to shuck their responsibilities! No — it's not even an excuse worth shucking. They probably take pride in those lousy qualities, and then 'credit' them to the Firelord they worship!"

As they talked, the group had already reached the waterway. Grede stopped at the water's edge and suddenly raised his hands high.

"Ah, I say, great Firelord!" he said, staring at the pitch-black water ahead. "Burn this goddamn troll temple to a crisp — hell, dry my clothes out while you're at it, seein' as I refused to let the Dark Iron dwarves lift the Sulfuron Hammer."

Jace looked up and observed the temple's corridor walls for a while, confirmed that Ragnaros hadn't answered his prayer, then said, "But that warhammer might be forged in Grim Batol."

"Grim Batol, Grede, Grim…" The dwarf muttered to himself for a moment, then blinked. "What?"

"The Kadros Anvil." Jace said. "Right now, the Kadros Anvil — burned by Deathwing — is the most likely place to forge the Sulfuron Hammer, besides the Dark Iron anvils of Shadowforge City. But so far that's just a guess, Grede. Until we can get the Thorium Brotherhood members to see what that anvil looks like today, nothing's for certain."

"You're serious?" Grede said. "You told the Thorium Brotherhood? So when you said you wanted to make another trip to Grim Batol, it was for this?"

"Right, I told Rokhtos Darkbargainer," Jace said. "He gave me a letter and asked me to bring it to Dun Morogh to discuss with the Brotherhood members there. At least it sounds like he's optimistic about the Kadros Anvil too. If you really don't want to be part of this, I expect the one to finally lift that warhammer will be King Magni, or his brother, Muradin Bronzebeard. When King Warian sent me to Ironforge, I briefly told him about the part of the Dark Iron dwarves planning to rebel against Molten Core's rule. He seemed quite moved by it — especially about the idea that the Three Hammers might one day be reconciled."

"Isn't that a good thing?" Grede said slowly. "The Dark Iron provide the plan, the Wildhammer provide the anvil, and the Bronzebeard lift the warhammer to shatter the curse tormenting all of Khaz Modan — like something out of a prophecy or a legend."

After saying that, he looked at Jace again. "If one day — just if — I somehow picked up the Sulfuron Hammer and charged into Molten Core, just pretend my brain's gone rotten. Will you come help me then?"

"Of course. Why wouldn't I." Jace scratched his beard. "Without me you wouldn't survive."

Grede's eyes went wide. "Tch — with you, I wouldn't either."

"What about with me added in?" Vereesa, who'd finished packing her bundle, asked, eyeing the water.

"Then… maybe I'd leave a full corpse, instead of getting roasted medium-rare?" Grede said.

"What about with me?" Sola asked.

Grede looked at her, then at Morfil, the demon sitting nearby with his demonic bag. The demon only gave him a "what the hell do I care" look, then turned his head away.

"I'm just saying things — why are you all taking it so seriously." Grede waved a hand and plunged into the water with a splash.
