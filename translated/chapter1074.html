Chapter 1074: Lost in the Nether?

Walking up the steps toward the temple's tower-top entrance, Jace could already feel his legs turning to jelly.

He'd spent what was at most a day and a night in this dark, gloomy underground palace, yet it felt like half his life had been left behind in there.

Up the steps, a hazy light shone down, and rushing water cascaded down each level, lapping against the tips of his boots — giving Jace a bad feeling.

Sure enough, the rain grew louder and louder. The welcome that greeted them at the door wasn't the long-missed brilliant sunlight, but a torrential downpour.

It didn't seem like it had been raining long, since the water level in the temple hadn't changed much from when they'd gone in. In other words, this downpour seemed to have fallen specifically to greet them as they came out.

Jace, hair plastered to his head, now looked like an otter that had crawled out of the water. He instinctively spat to the side — couldn't tell if he was trying to get the rainwater that had splashed into his mouth out, or if he was just pissed and wanted to show it in some petty way, since there wasn't much else he could do to express his displeasure with the weather.

Even so, the Swamp of Sorrows in the rain was still far more relaxing than anything inside the temple. The faint dark aura that had lingered over the Pool of Tears before now seemed, by comparison, positively friendly and mild.

The group packed up their things on the steps outside the temple, abandoning the badly contaminated food and the clothes heavily stained by Hakkar's blood. A potato tucked in Grede's bag had even sprouted madly writhing black shoots, which he flattened with a single hammer blow.

At this point, who knew what had become of the green drake-folk that had been wandering around outside the pool. Logically, with Hakkar's avatar dead, the troll spirits haunting the entire Pool of Tears gone, and even the Atal'ai high priest Gamalan slain, those green drake-folk should have long since broken free of the curse, left the Pool of Tears, and gone to find their masters.

But in a place like this, the last thing you should blindly trust was common sense. Fortunately, the rain and the mist over the lake formed layer after layer of cover — you couldn't see the shore from here, and the shore certainly couldn't see here.

The rain showed no sign of stopping, pouring down incessantly. Jace could tell that right now it wasn't just Grede — even Vereesa was starting to feel the fatigue. And most importantly, Sola was already exhausted and drained from multiple injuries and severe mana depletion. Forcing her to carry these people and a demon back to Stonard in flight was really too much to ask of anyone.

Right now, even though she still held the overall appearance of a golden-haired elf on the outside, her eyes had already reverted to a reptilian look.

Vereesa found a reasonably dry sheltered space with a stone roof near the Temple of Atal'Hakkar's entrance — a place where they could rest for the night. No dark corruption of Hakkar's blood, no stench permeating the whole temple, no grotesque groaning of all manner of animals — just the patter of rain, and Grede's snoring.

But Jace was already familiar with all of it. He lay down on a massive stone block that had collapsed from the wall and quickly felt himself drifting off.

But before he knew it, Hakkar's avatar's roar rang out again, crashing against his ears. This was a dream — he could more or less tell. In the blood-red field of vision, ghosts drifted everywhere without needing Sylia's ghost potion to see them. He just wanted to ignore all of it and get as good a sleep as possible, undisturbed by anything…

Until he caught sight of layer upon layer of violent Void forces tearing through the blood-red, exposing the purple stars within. Wandering chaotic dark energy crisscrossed and rolled through the endless abyss, and a handful of pitiful figures that fell into it were shredded in the blink of an eye — gone without a trace, not even their souls left to drift out.

He snapped awake in a fright. Vereesa was sitting right beside him, leaning back against the stone wall in a deep sleep. Grede was curled up in the shadows like a little bear, snoring away. Morfil, standing watch, sat not far off, staring straight at him; in the lightless place, the flames in her eyes were like two flickering will-o'-the-wisps, reminding Jace of the six burning soul-flames in Hakkar's dream realm.

"You were dreaming, master," Morfil said softly in Eredun.

The word "dream" in Eredun — or demonic tongue — was closely related to meanings like omen, subconscious, or things yet to come. Jace stared at the succubus for a while, annoyed at how she, whether overtly or hintingly, connected the things he'd dreamed with "the future."

But considering that even Morfil didn't possess mind-reading magic, this was probably just his own anxiety playing tricks on him.

His neck felt a bit cold. He touched it and found that the Will-Chain had somehow returned to him at some point.

Sola was sleeping right next to Vereesa. Even in mortal form, this green dragon slept like a giant lizard, all curled up.

"Let's talk outside," Jace said quietly in Eredun.

Morfil smiled slightly and stood. Jace rose slowly too, making sure not to wake Vereesa beside him, and walked barefoot out of the stone shelter bit by bit.

The rain showed no sign of stopping, still pouring down. Once you were wet, falling asleep again was hard — but Jace walked right into it anyway. Couldn't tell if he was trying to wash off the indescribable worry the dream had brought him, or if he just wanted to find some sense of reality outside the dream.

But no matter what, he knew that even without the rain, it would be hard for him to fall back asleep.

"What did you dream about?" Morfil asked curiously. "Another succubus? Hmm, was she good to you?" Jace gave her a sidelong look. "Vereesa Windrunner."

"That won't provoke me, master." Morfil chuckled. "At least that elf won't steal mana to feed her arcane addiction while her companions are asleep, like the other elven mages…"

As she spoke, she leaned against Jace's shoulder, her fingers slowly trailing down from his chest.

"In the earlier battle, I expended enormous energy helping master share his wounds and repairing the injuries. Master, you promised you'd make it up to me. Why not do it now… and replay the way you tangled with that Quel'dorei in your dream, right here on me…"

"You mean throwing you into the Twisting Nether and letting the forces there tear you to pieces." Jace stared at the demon in front of him.

The slightly strange smile on Morfil's face didn't fade. She just studied Jace for a moment, then said, "Well, your tastes are getting stranger and stranger."

"Let's be serious." Jace gathered his thoughts, then said, "I just dreamed about something… something I've probably been worried about all along but didn't want to admit."

"Ah." Morfil laughed softly. "You're afraid that when we enter Draenor, you'll fall into the Void and be lost in there forever with them."

There was a hint of contempt in the succubus's tone. Jace said, displeased, "You're not worried about this? Dying in the Twisting Nether means eternal annihilation for a demon like you. Don't treat me like some clueless rookie you can fool."

Morfil's expression grew a little more serious. "There are many names for a warlock who wields Uthales — the Arrogant Destroyer, Enemy of the Legion, Demon Reaper, Mad Shadow-wielder, Sargeras's most wanted… But no matter what, 'rookie' will never be one of them. That said, darling, a demon who wields Uthales worrying about getting lost in the Twisting Nether while crossing a shadow passage has to be said — a rather endearing contrast. I've met Eredun warlocks far more pathetic than you who didn't worry about this at all."

"Because I'm not crossing alone." Jace said. "So it won't happen?"

"Zero chance? I wouldn't say that." Morfil twirled a finger. "Risk definitely always exists. But the one crafting the portal for you is an Ered'ruin lord — they're all manipulators of worlds that Kil'jaedan holds in high regard. You couldn't find many more powerful portal-weavers in this universe than a demon like Kalrez, master."

Seeing Jace still had his doubts, Morfil pursed her lips helplessly and said, "If you really don't believe what I'm saying, maybe you can summon that little imp out and ask its opinion again."

Jace glanced back at the entrance to the stone shelter. "It's too late — its voice is too shrill, it'll wake everyone up. It's not that I don't believe you, Morfil."

"Hmm." The succubus narrowed her eyes. "I'm usually better at killing people than comforting them, but who says I love you so much, Jace Sesso. Here's the thing — there are many factors that go into establishing a teleportation passage. The most important ones are things like the caster's skill and talent, teleportation distance, how abundant and stable the prepared mana is, the accuracy of the coordinates, the number of people being teleported… and of course, their mass. Teleporting Sargeras himself would be harder than teleporting tens or hundreds of thousands of Mo'arg all at once, by an incalculable margin."

"The arcane power of Azeroth is unimaginably strong, and the ley line nexus sitting upon it is even more extraordinary." She went on. "Ilgala Tower was damaged by orcs and gnolls, but after repairs and supplementing it with a sufficiently powerful mana gem, it'll be enough to awaken it. That guarantees the prepared mana, and then there's the caster himself… Lord Kalrez — I think goes without questioning. He's loyal to you enough, and more importantly, he doesn't have much choice besides you. As for the number of people being teleported — hmm, five or six, including a juvenile dragon and Uthales — that's nothing compared to the conditions we have."

"What about the accuracy of the coordinates?" Jace asked.

"For other demons, I wouldn't say." Morfil answered. "But Kalrez is a high-ranking officer of the Burning Legion and an Ered'ruin lord in his own right. I'd think he should have Draenor's teleportation coordinates. If Kil'jaedan planned to corrupt a planet, he'd make all kinds of preparations. On one hand, he'd use fear and suspicion to sow discord among the local inhabitants, the way he did with the Brutallus and me. On the other hand, he'd prepare for a full-scale invasion, keeping coordinates and Legion portals close at hand, ready to deploy at any time."

"Without summoning Kalrez back, none of this is clear." Jace gazed at the lake, hazy and indistinct in the rainy night. "When we get back to Stormwind, I'll start preparing the summoning ritual right away."

"I'd say Draenor's coordinates are the least thing to worry about." Morfil said. "Ever since the Dark Portal was established, right up until it was destroyed by the Expedition, several years passed. Plenty of orc warlocks and Kirin Tor scholars studied it during that time, didn't they? Maybe Dalaran still keeps the coordinate runes."

"You want me to let Dalaran know I'm planning to build a little side-grade Dark Portal?" Jace asked.

Morfil moved closer. "At least tonight you don't have to sit on pins and needles worrying about this. Get a good sleep, my master. If you can't sleep, I can help you with a sleep spell."

Jace recalled what he'd seen in his dream and shook his head. "Thanks, Morfil. I'll keep watch here for a bit."

He glanced at his pocket watch and went on. "I've slept four or five hours already. That's about enough."

"As you wish, master." Morfil said softly, her gaze suddenly shifting to the other side. A figure was standing over by the stone gate. Jace squinted — it was Sola.
