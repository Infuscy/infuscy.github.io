## Chapter 1076: Back to the Real World

When they arrived at Stonard, Jace's group wasn't even recognized as the same party that had left over a week ago. The green dragon had barely touched down and walked any distance before they were surrounded by a group of cavalry out on patrol near a watchpost close to Stonard, suspected of being a coven of evil warlocks who'd ventured deep into the Blackmoor looking for demonic artifacts.

If Jace hadn't produced his court mage token in time, the whole lot of them probably would have come to blows.

Of course, Stonard's watch commander, the Lordaeronian Waldemar, gave them a proper apology and arranged food and lodging. That evening they had a proper hot meal in the barracks — this crew that had just slain the Atal'ai high priest and the god's avatar, and yet didn't have a trace of swagger left in them.

After Grede had a few too many, he even went out to the empty ground beside the barracks and danced with the group of cavalrymen who'd nearly come to blows with him, singing an army song from the Orc Wars that even Jace knew well.

After wiping himself down with stream water, putting on a dry, clean shirt that carried a faint smell of worn cloth, and eating slices of bread and smoked sausage with no stench whatsoever, Jace almost felt this orc-village-turned-garrison surrounded by jungle was practically heaven.

Amid the flickering shadows of the braziers, the smell of ale and smoked meat, the singing, the laughter, the shouting — there was no way he'd be in the mood to dwell on the unpleasantness of the clash with the soldiers just now, let alone the fact that everyone had actually been thinking of the defense of Stonard and the Stormwind Kingdom's border.

Outside the barracks, a few priests from Lordaeron were discussing how to purify the clothes Jace's group had brought out of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar.

The most important of these was Vereesa's cloak, since the worthless things that had been torn up by combat or strenuous movement inside had already been thrown out by Jace and Grede.

And the valuable clothes — like Jace's robes, Angelysta's magic belt, the black dragon-hide cloak, and the like — for the most part hadn't been affected by the poison or corruption, and could be handled with a simple wash.

The priests were a bit worried about the source of all this corruption. Because of this, Jace had already spoken with Waldemar and the mages and priests in Stonard about the Pool of Tears, telling them there was a chance fleeing troll cultists might come near the area, and asking them to keep alert during this time.

That being said, Jace didn't really believe that trolls who'd lost their faith and their leader could form any kind of organization that would pose a threat to an Alliance garrison like Stonard.

Vereesa picked up a reddish fruit that looked like a cranberry and was slightly tart, and said in Thalassian, "Back when I first got to know you,

"Jace, I only wanted to be a Ranger-Lord, but I hadn't thought much about a Ranger-Lord's responsibilities. Those things were like the rules and formulae I had to memorize in magic class — I memorized them, I remembered them, but I never thought about what they meant, until I actually got the title. Maybe back then, the biggest meaning of the title was that it let me be Vereesa Windrunner, instead of Lady Alleria or Lady Sylvanas's little sister."

"Why bring that up all of a sudden?" Jace asked.

"I've found I'm still more suited to being a simple hunter — or, what I'd rather not admit, a mercenary." Vereesa stared at the tall soldiers across the table clapping and humming, speaking in a voice only Jace could hear, still in Thalassian. "I'm not good at dealing with Sunfury Tower, and I'm not suited to making reports to the Silvermoon Council… Compared to that, even the training I used to find dull and tedious has become interesting."

"Do you think I'm being childish?" she asked.

Jace leaned back in his chair and chuckled. "Maybe Sylvanas thinks the same way — it's just that she's got another little sister, so she has to prop herself up and play the big sister."

"I don't believe that. She's hundreds of years older than me," Vereesa said. "Sometimes I can't even imagine what the world looks like in her eyes, what I look like to her. Sometimes I see the teenagers who've just entered the Farstriders' apprentice ranks, and they seem like a bunch of clumsy kittens crawling around on the ground, ridiculous. So what does Sylvanas see when she looks at me? A little worm wriggling on a leaf?"

"Sola's hundreds of years older than your sister." Jace said this while glancing over at the green dragon dozing nearby with her eyes half-closed, making sure she wasn't listening.

Vereesa smiled, looked at her, then muttered softly, "Her situation is different, Jace…" As she spoke, she slowly moved closer, looping her arm through Jace's.

Even though she said nothing, pressed this close, Jace could even smell, in every breath she took, that peculiar emotion — slightly nervous, faintly fearful, yet carrying intense anticipation.

"What do you think the other side of the Dark Portal looks like right now?" she asked, switching to Common. "Maybe your sister's asking Turalyon the exact same question right about now." Jace said.

Vereesa smiled. "You're that sure they'd be so leisurely?"

"Turalyon knocked out the Horde's warchief in a few rounds." Jace said casually. "He was the former ruler of Draenor, and every other leader of the Sons of Lothar was no worse than him. Think about it — how could the disaster of Draenor destroy them?"

"Even I think that reasoning's a stretch." Despite the fact that her mouth seemed to be dismissing Jace's nonsense, she couldn't help but smile.

"Speaking of the Dark Portal." Jace asked in Thalassian, "There's one more thing I need you to do, Wyn. My identity is a bit too sensitive."

He glanced at a Stromgarde red-robed mage standing by the doorway getting some air at the other end of the hall, confirming he couldn't hear them over the hall's clamor, then lowered his voice. "When you get back to Quel'Thalas, if you have the chance, look into information about Draenor's coordinates a little."

Vereesa's eyes widened. "Doesn't that… lord know them?"

"He should know them. I just want to be absolutely sure." Jace looked down at her. "If the records kept by the mages of Quel'Thalas or Dalaran match those of our teleporter, then this trip's safety gets one more guarantee. We're right at the door now — I don't want something to go wrong because we didn't do what we could have done well enough."

"Understood." Vereesa nodded. "But I can't guarantee the magisters really kept this. Anything related to the Dark Portal is an absolute taboo everywhere."

"I don't believe those elves would have zero interest in the Dark Portal." Jace said.

Vereesa gave Jace a sidelong look and muttered, "Those elves."

"Quel'dorei." Jace smiled. "Especially considering the Kirin Tor has probably picked the Dark Portal apart inside and out by now — there's no way Prince Kael'thas and the Silvermoon Council wouldn't be at least a little tempted, right? Of course, Wyn, this is all on the premise that you don't expose us. Investigate carefully. If it really comes down to it, tell Lady Sylvanas — she'd definitely be willing to help."

"I know." Vereesa nodded.

After agreeing, she still seemed to have some reservations, wearing an expression of wanting to speak but stopping herself. "What?" Jace asked curiously. "Something else on your mind?"

"I do have a question," Vereesa said. "What exactly is your relationship with that Vilebranch witch doctor?"

Jace immediately felt his throat go dry. The confidence he'd just had going on about the Kirin Tor and the Silvermoon Council evaporated in an instant. "Why do you suddenly want to ask about that?"

"I feel like you know a little too much about the Temple of Atal'Hakkar." Vereesa glanced at a soldier passing by nearby, paused a moment, then went on. "It's like you planned out from the very beginning how to find Sola's father, Prince Eranikus —

"From the start, you knew this temple was divided into upper and lower levels, who was guarding each level…"

Was it that obvious? Jace felt like he'd already done everything he could to make it seem like he was figuring things out by luck.

And in that temple, he felt like his familiarity with the map from his past life had been almost useless — most of the time he'd been far more lost than Vereesa.

So even like this, to an outsider he still seemed abnormally familiar with that temple? Thank God Vereesa hadn't seen how he'd extinguished those flames in the earring's dream realm, or this would have become even more bizarre.

"She's not an Atal'ai either," Jace said. "At least she didn't describe the inside of the temple to me in any detail. She didn't even think I could get in, so she told me that even around the temple there were some wandering Hakkar spawn, and she hoped I'd hunt those wind serpents and help her find a usable skull."

"All right." Vereesa sighed softly. "Maybe I just feel like an apprentice mage from the Wizard Sanctum shouldn't know more about the Hakkar worshippers' temple than I do…"
