Chapter 1078: Pulling the Fire from Under the Pot

Sylia excitedly lifted the Atal'ai Bloodguard's head to one side, sprinkled some strange powder Jace didn't recognize on it, and before long the bloody stench in the room was covered over. She muttered a few lines in troll tongue, then walked with measured steps into the inner room. Jace glanced at Sola in bewilderment; Sola shook her head too — even though she understood troll tongue, the incantations involved in this troll voodoo and hex-craft were too obscure. Before long, Sylia came back out. "A collaboration that couldn't be more perfect, Jace Sesso, and this Lady Green Dragon. I can't help but want to brag to every tribesperson who walks in here that I've got a client like you. But considering that even outside the Atal'Hakkar, the Soulflayer's followers are still numerous, excitement aside I know I've got to keep a low profile. I don't have the walls of Stormwind protecting me, nor powerful shadow magic to defend myself — breaking into my room would take nothing more than an axe that hasn't lost its edge yet and a few lines of counter-hex incantation…" Jace, sitting there, nodded. The troll witch doctor lowered her gaze and observed him a moment, then asked, "What's wrong? You've got some other question? Even though the wind serpent head you brought back far exceeded my expectations, that doesn't mean I'm paying extra for it, Jace Sesso — that wasn't part of what we originally agreed on." "Do business like this and the surprises your clients and partners bring you will only get smaller," Jace said. Sylia smiled. "Learning of Atal'Hakkar's fall is naturally a good thing, but I'd prefer everything around me to be more controllable. Things that exceed my predictions frighten me. Best if they're good things, but who knows if these good things won't come paired with even worse ones?" "Even though it sounds a lot like an excuse for not wanting to pay more," Jace nodded slowly, "it's actually pretty convincing to me." "People who deal with demons and shadow like you understand this more easily," Sylia said. Jace thought for a moment, then said, "Then tell me more about this head — that won't cost you anything. You said just now you wanted to use a Hakkar spawn's skull to destroy something, to permanently prevent Hakkar's return." "Hmm." The troll witch doctor nodded slowly, then said, "I don't mind talking a bit more with you either, since you destroyed Hakkar's avatar and brought back the Atal'ai Bloodguard's head. I don't have to worry you're some disguised Atal'ai priest." She walked step by step to the shelf nearby, picked up a few thin stone tablet fragments from below, and glanced at them. "Even though the Atal'ai leader — the Gurubashi priest who proposed the Atal'ai prophecy that I mentioned to you before, Gamalan — had been committed to communing with the god through souls and flesh, his summoning methods were complicated and enormously costly." The witch doctor held the tablets against her chest and went on. "It's just that he had numerous followers, and after being driven out of the Gurubashi, these followers had no objections whatsoever to his extreme rituals and doctrines, which allowed him to bring the Soulflayer's avatar into this world again and again. The Soulflayer's avatar possessed Hakkar's own immensely powerful magic, and on that foundation, continuing to sacrifice more souls and blood, sooner or later Hakkar's true form could descend by borrowing the avatar's power. At least, that's what the Gurubashi Empire did a thousand years ago. Gamalan is the successor to the most powerful Atal'ai high priests of that era, and he'd already successfully summoned the avatar at least twice. We have to assume that given enough time, he really could reach the heights his predecessors attained." "But actually, the bloody Gurubashi civil war a thousand years ago wasn't the first time Hakkar descended into our world either," Sylia said. "I know — the Ancient Blood Plague of the Zandalar," Sola said. "Yes." Sylia glanced at Sola, her voice growing lower and lower. "Long before the world was shattered, the ancient Zandalar successfully completed the summoning ritual. That time was so secret, so sudden, because that summoner possessed the means to bring forth the egg laid down by the god." So she finally brought it up — the Ancient Egg. Jace knew this was the thing she hadn't mentioned earlier that she wanted to destroy. "Whether it was misfortune or a blessing," Sylia went on, "Hakkar's egg has always been sealed away by us Amani, so for a long time the fanatical lunatics of Stranglethorn have never been able to replicate that summoner's ritual. But who would've thought that the Gurubashi, when all's said and done, still possess a particular kind of cleverness… and then comes the Atal'ai's story, human. I expect you already know it quite well." "What the Gurubashi priests I know want to achieve is to curse the power of that egg through blood magic, to let it rot away unnoticed within layer after layer of protection. That Hakkar spawn's skull is one of the spell components we suspect can establish a connection with that egg. Of course, right now this is still only a guess — we have plenty of other ideas too. How effective it actually is will have to wait until I make further contact with them and experiment." "Since the Amani trolls hold Hakkar's egg, why don't you try summoning it yourselves?" Jace asked. Sylia spread her somewhat clumsy-looking hands. "Because the rulers of the Amani Empire don't want to be replaced by an uncontrollable evil spirit. It's that simple. Even when we were losing battle after battle in the war against the Kaldorei Empire and their successors, the Quel'dorei…" "But Zul'Aman never considered destroying that Hakkar's egg, did it?" Sola said. "Otherwise it wouldn't have been preserved to this day." Sylia stared at the green dragon. "Even an empire as powerful as the Amani has been on the verge of destruction more than once, green dragon. The most conservative Amani ruler knows he must keep an ace up his sleeve, holding onto the potential power that could shake the magical barrier Bantinorel — even if that power could tear us apart too." Jace said, "But after the Orc Wars, Quel'Thalas has been unable to keep fighting the Amani Empire, hasn't it? Then you forest trolls have even less reason to try summoning Hakkar — mutual destruction." "The Orc Wars changed more than just the Alliance," Sylia said. "Maybe you can guess this, Jace Sesso — the actual resting place of Hakkar's egg is in Zinsaro, ruled by the Vilebranch. Before the Dark Portal opened, this place was under Zul'jin's rule, and no one dared touch anything related to the summoning ritual. But now the Amani Empire is facing dissolution, and the Vilebranch has already become an independent kingdom led by a few Hakkar worshippers… The Gurubashi priests have always worried about the Vilebranch tribe cooperating with the Atal'ai, because many scholars believe that once the avatar's power is poured into that ancient evil egg, Hakkar's true form could be born from it very soon. I have some doubts about that myself, but after all I haven't directly participated in any rituals involving the Soulflayer — I don't have much say." "Then you'd better make this clear to your kin." She looked at Sola again. "Even though the Atal'ai's plan has been thwarted, that temple must be thoroughly purified. Otherwise, after the god's avatar collapses, the evil spirits that escaped from it will still be trapped within the temple, and combined with the corrupted blood the Atal'ai collected… that place could still provide the power for the Soulflayer's evil egg to hatch." "You don't need to remind me of that, priest," Sola said. "I just don't want you to be carried away by a phased victory and repeat the mistakes of the past," the witch doctor said. Sola was momentarily speechless at that — could only be glad that the gem storing Eranikus's essence she was keeping was still in its dormant state. Sylia didn't know that the "evil spirits that might be trapped in the temple" she was worried about were actually trapped in the curved staff this human had casually set down beside him. Years of the Atal'ai's efforts had either dissipated along with Hakkar's avatar or been devoured completely by Uthales. In other words, even if Sylia and the priests she mentioned tried and failed to remotely destroy the Ancient Egg through blood magic rituals, the difficulty for the Vilebranch tribe and the Atal'ai to summon Hakkar again would be far higher than Sylia estimated. Even though Sola had just been shot down by that troll, right now she also shifted her gaze toward Jace — and the staff in his hand. Jace knew she was well aware of this too, so she wasn't just not angry, she was even a little secretly pleased. Have to say, this green dragon also knew what to say and what to keep to herself. Honestly, before coming, Jace had been a little worried Sola might accidentally let slip that Uthales had devoured the souls. That said, Jace could feel that Uthales already possessed quite formidable power by now. Even though it no longer complained in Jace's ear from time to time out of dissatisfaction, giving Jace some peace and quiet and rarely showing a trace of being "harmless" — Jace still felt that in the time to come, at least until Kalrez was successfully summoned back, it was best not to feed it any more powerful souls. At the very least, he wasn't just Uthales's master — more importantly, he was its keeper.
