## Chapter 108: Vitality Rupture

"Shut up! Keep your voice down!"

Jace barked at him.

He flipped through Jagin's casting-condition notes once more, double-checking the gestures, because he was certain he hadn't mispronounced the incantation.

The sheep had worked itself into such a state that its movements and sounds had warped — as if something even more terrifying than death was chasing it. The sudden, grotesque distortion of something so ordinary threw even Jace, who'd seen all manner of horrible creatures, off balance.

There were few warlock spells that weren't wicked. Animals had a purer perception of the natural world, so it made sense that they'd react viscerally to dark, negative magic.

If Jace wanted to keep walking down this path, he couldn't keep being shocked by this sort of thing.

With that in mind, he silently recited the incantation and stepped in front of the sheep again.

The sheep stared at him, its eyes already trembling nonstop. The moment Jace raised his hand, the spooked animal thrashed for its life again. Jace felt like the rope tying it up was about to snap.

"Elamon, Silendor..." This time his voice was much lower, but his focus was sharper.

Concentration... like Marin had said, you have to maintain your concentration. The moment he finished chanting, he suddenly felt a surge of heat flow out of his heart, gradually filling his whole body. He faintly sensed an unfamiliar, tactile connection forming between himself and the animal — but he couldn't make out its true nature.

Which meant Jace could feel that Drain Life had taken effect — but there was no green tether of energy like in the game to show it.

The sheep's screams gradually turned to whimpering. Its frantic movements slowed bit by bit. Jace watched it grow weaker and weaker, more and more feeble — its earlier struggling reduced to feeble twitching against the base of the great tree.

But Jace felt his whole body getting hotter and hotter. His skin swelled, his eyes itched, his heart pounded faster and faster, like a tiny man inside his chest was throwing punches at his ribs.

A wave of violent nausea shattered Jace's rhythm. He doubled over, his right hand still maintaining the casting, but it was getting harder and harder to concentrate.

Suddenly, a flash of bright yellow light flickered between his fingers and the sheep, then quickly faded, leaving behind a slowly dimming green glow.

Jace was stunned by this sudden display of energy. The channeling drain magic cut off with it.

Was that... the color of life? Or... fel?

No, this spell was shadow magic. Those pale yellow and pale green things must have been life force — vitality drawn out of the sheep by the shadow.

The sheep had lost all its earlier fight. It had just been shorn, and its new wool hadn't grown back in yet — but just as Jagin had said, even the remaining short fibers withered and fell out. The skin on its body shriveled and turned gray, drained of color.

Jace went over to examine the sheep and found it could no longer stand on its own. It was so skinny it didn't look like a sheep at all — more like a scrawny stray dog rummaging through a garbage heap.

Its life vitality... had been drained away by him?

Jace stared at his flushed, red hands, feeling unbearably hot all over, his forehead buzzing, his ears ringing in waves.

Another wave of nausea surged up his throat. He vomited a mouthful of blood, wiped it with his hand — his nose and mouth were both bleeding.

He stared at the bright red blood on the ground, a spike of panic hitting him.

"Sazeno! Sazeno!"

The imp scurried over in a flash. Jace pointed at the blood on his hands. "What's happening to me?"

"I reckon you drained too hard, master!" the imp said. "Your body's already bursting with vitality — there's no room left for that sheep's potent life force!"

At that moment, the sheep seemed to rally one last time, struggling to its feet and hobbling away into the distance. The rope had slipped right off — the sheep had gotten so thin that it had broken free!

Jace couldn't spare it another thought. He was desperately thirsty, his lips dry and peeling, his vision blurred. He had to find water, fast.

Crystal Lake was to the northeast. The moment that registered, he grabbed his pack and ran. He didn't know how long he'd been sprinting, how much blood he'd vomited, before he finally reached a stream — he had no idea where it came from. He splashed water on his face and only then saw that his eyes were shot through with blood.

He frantically washed his hands, his face, until he'd wiped all the blood away, until he stopped vomiting blood, stopped coughing blood... until his body more or less felt normal again and the dizziness in his head faded. Only then did he stop.

Jace gulped down several mouthfuls of water, sat on a boulder by the stream and rested for a while. The imp, clutching the pack, had only just caught up.

He wiped the water from the corner of his mouth, but something felt off.

He looked at the back of his hand... the scar had actually faded!

Those burn marks that still ached faintly — now all that was left were some uneven, pale patches of skin.

Was this the effect of Drain Life?

That life energy had healed the wounds on his body, and then overflowed past what he could handle — causing his blood vessels to rupture?

But in reality, despite losing all that blood, Jace hadn't felt much real pain or weakness.

What surprised him most was that he'd managed to drain such potent life energy in such a short time — far more than the Dark Iron dwarves Jagin had described.

Was it because the incantation was more accurate? Or was there something else going on? Jace sat on the stone, quietly feeling the changes within himself. After all, every time he'd used shadow magic before, he'd experienced severe emotional loss of control — getting more manic, more excited, more bloodthirsty...

But this time he hadn't felt many negative emotions. He guessed it might be because the sheep's life force had repaired the damage caused by the shadow. This incantation might actually be usable as a healing ability.

Of course, it wasn't without its problems. The biggest flaw was that after calming down from the shock of the spell's effects, he felt utterly exhausted.

Even doing his utmost to concentrate, doing his utmost to get the form exactly right, it still consumed a massive amount of mental energy. And none of the previous spells — Fear, Light, and the rest — had taken this much out of him.

Jace could more or less guess that this Drain Life magic was probably a fairly high-level spell — one that was already pushing against the upper limits of his stamina.

But he'd gotten excellent results from a single cast. He wanted to head straight back to Stormwind. If he walked fast, he could make it to the city gates by nightfall. No need for further testing.

He needed to find a doctor or a priest to get himself checked out. After all, he'd just vomited blood — that wasn't something to ignore.

As for that sheep... there were plenty of sheep wandering around Elwynn Forest without it. He might as well set it free — compensation for having endured his lethal incantation.
