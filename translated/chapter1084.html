Chapter 1084: A Brief Farewell at the Valley of Heroes

Two days later, at noon, quite a few people were crossing back and forth over the bridge at the Valley of Heroes. Jace and Suola stood by the side of the bridge, waiting for Grede to hurry over.

The essence of Ysondre — no, the essence of the green dragon prince Illanicus — was stored in that gem and, for the time being, counted as safe. But no matter what, if they could try to purify him sooner, it was best not to drag things out.

Besides, the Temple of Atal'Hakkar was currently ownerless. Although most of the Atal'ai had already fled, it was hard to guarantee there weren't some priests hiding in the cracks and corners, waiting for a chance to cook up more schemes and conspiracies. Their threat might not compare to Gamal', but they couldn't be entirely ignored either.

Not to mention the green dragonkin still wandering around the area — who knew what state they were in now, after losing the control of Hakkar's mental magic.

Because of this, Suola couldn't stay in Stormwind for long. In fact, with Suola carrying him, Grede didn't need to prepare too much in the way of luggage and supplies. They weren't heading out this time to hunt some mysterious Highborne warlock who'd turned into a demon, nor to chase down a demonic artifact that had been sealed away in a horrible vault centuries ago — they were just going to meet the Kaldorei druids. By all rights, there shouldn't be any danger.

"Make sure to go through Azshara," Jace said, looking at Suola. "Winterspring has too many crazed chimerae, the Barrens is garrisoned with all sorts of quilboar and centaur tribes — comparatively speaking, Azshara, protected by the blue dragonflight, is actually the safer route. And Azuregos knows you, he won't give you any trouble."

"You're worried I'd get shot down into the grasslands by quilboar and centaur crossbow bolts, Jace?" Suola asked.

Jace explained, "At the very least, be careful of those priests' lightning magic — and of course the thunder lizards' lightning. We heard from the goblins before that a goblin zeppelin had once been shot down by centaur storm-call magic."

"You'd be better off worrying about whether a ghoul might bite your ear off." Grede, carrying and hauling bags of all shapes and sizes, arrived at their side. "Anyway, I'm not casually agreeing to help any priest dig something out of a graveyard. I saw more than enough walking corpses in that temple."

"Morgan's not just any priest," Jace said, glancing at his bags. "Did you bring all the demon cloth?"

"Brought it all," Grede said, squinting. "Got any other friends you need me to help out in Kalimdor? Or bring back some travel souvenirs?"

"Remember to ask about using dragon sinew for bows," Jace said, fixing Grede with a stare. "If we successfully purify Illanicus's essence, don't you dare go full great hero and say something like 'I want nothing in return.'"

"I'll remember," Suola said. "Even if it's not about helping my father, I think the fact that we defeated Lady Onyxia and forced her back into her lair where she doesn't dare come out and cause trouble... that alone is enough for my brothers and sisters to help us figure something out."

"Now that you've said that, I'm relieved," Jace said. "How long until you're back, roughly?"

"I think a month or two should be enough," Suola answered.

"Not long," Jace said.

"Honestly, Jace," Grede lowered his voice. "Dealing with those ghouls was nothing. When we get back from the south, rest up properly for a while, let the injuries from the temple heal completely — then we'll feel better about heading out again next time."

"I understand," Jace said.

"Say goodbye to Molophil and Sazeno for me." Grede lifted the bag at his feet. "I'll miss those two."

"Same here," Suola said.

"I don't know about those two, but I'll miss you," Jace said.

"Don't just say it — when you've got nothing to do, go to the Cathedral of Light and pray for me more often," Grede said.

"You really want an evil warlock praying for you in a church?" Jace asked.

"If it's you, why not?" Grede pointed at the sky. "Unless what a wise man like Alonsus Faol firmly believes in is an idiot, then it definitely knows good from bad."

Saying this, the dwarf walked over and hugged Jace, patting him lightly on the waist a couple of times. Suola looked at Jace, took a deep breath, and nodded. Jace nodded too.

Jace watched the tall and short figures gradually grow distant and slowly let out a sigh.

Vereesa, Grede, Suola — they'd all left Stormwind. Now only he and those two demons remained. The thought of it made the bustling noise of the pedestrians passing by suddenly feel hollow.

He'd kept thinking that after all those near-death escapes, all those gains and losses... he'd be able to face partings like this — especially brief ones — with a little less sentimentality.

But when the moment truly arrived, he realized he hadn't really changed from start to finish. This time the faint sense of loss was even clearer. Jace could even feel the dark mana seeping out from inside him.

Only, he couldn't manipulate this impossibly weak, utterly non-aggressive negative emotion the way he controlled fear or panic — he could only let it spread bit by bit through his body, seeping into every inch of flesh and blood.

Of course, Suola was carrying the gem of their elder's essence, Grede had important people to meet, and Vereesa had gone back to Quel'Thalas with the Soul Mirror Stone and the Atal'ai bat staff, plus she needed to investigate the coordinates of Draenor — they all had their own important missions. And Jace himself still had plenty to do.

So he just stood on the bridge a moment longer in a daze, then quickened his pace toward the gate into the Trade District.

These past two days he'd been busy helping Grede and Suola purchase what they needed for Kalimdor, and still hadn't handed the green dragon's blood over to Ursula.

Now that those two had left, it was about time to pay a visit to The Slaughtered Lamb.

Back at his rented room, he picked up that bottle of blood he'd brought out of the temple and made his way straight to the Mage Quarter.

It was still cold out; the apprentices in the Mage Quarter were all wearing thick robes, and Jace couldn't help but start thinking again about that "armor-defensive-power" robe Jalen had told him about a few days earlier.

In World of Warcraft, threat was a fairly intuitive thing. The team's tank had plenty of attacks that generated extra threat, plus the Taunt ability, making it convenient to draw more of the monsters' attention onto himself.

But in the real world, it wasn't that simple.

Everyone knew exactly where this team's weak point was — and that was him.

Vereesa was an impossibly agile ranger. Forget hurting her — even touching her was difficult. And Grede was a solid chunk of rock, a massive earthen incarnate — unless driven to desperation, nobody wanted to tangle with him.

And Suola went without saying. Although she looked delicate and frail, once she was driven into a corner she not only had Sleep, Entangling Roots and other means of blocking enemies, she could hide herself with dream illusions. And if truly forced to the very end, she could reveal her true form and brawl with anyone...

Unless it was a horrifying troll like Atal'alarion, one of the most powerful the entire continent would ever see, there really weren't many enemies who could go toe-to-toe with a nearly adult young green dragon in a straight fistfight.

The red dragon blood in his own body, while it gave him greater resilience and resistance to blows than ordinary people, when faced against a massive abomination like Hakkar's avatar, the protection of the red dragon blood was, at best, better than nothing.

Add to that the fact that he was always looking for openings to cast spells, and the shadow incantations he unleashed were extremely attention-grabbing — so naturally he frequently became the primary target for most opponents.

The moment his mana ran dry and he couldn't freely deploy Dark Shield and Shadowstep, he'd face the predicament of getting swept away by a single swipe of Hakkar's avatar's tail. The robe Jalen had promised would go some way toward patching up this weak link in their whole team.

Jalen Airuo should have already set off for the north by now. The basement was empty and quiet at the moment, with no sound at all — only the scratching of claws on the floor as Ursula's felhound, upon seeing Jace, turned and slipped away down the passage on the other side.

Jace glanced toward the practice room, confirmed no one was over there, then turned and came over to Ursula's alchemy lab. He hadn't expected that the moment he walked in, he'd find her wrapped in a blanket and fast asleep on the sofa.

Just as he was about to go wait in the hall, Ursula seemed to hear his footsteps. She opened her eyes and lifted a hand, nearly falling off the sofa, and gave a startled cry as she sat bolt upright.

"Sorry, I didn't mean to wake you," Jace said.

"Good heavens, was I asleep just now?" Ursula asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

"All right." Ursula nodded. "I guessed this poison might have sleep-inducing properties, but I didn't expect that just smelling it once would knock me out. Damn it..."

The alchemist stood up holding her head, fumbled around the table for a bit, then muttered to herself: "Could it also erase part of my memory? I don't remember tidying up the table properly before I fell asleep... What's the date today, Jace?"

"The twenty-seventh of January," Jace said. "It's only a few days until the dwarves' Elders' Festival."

"I love the Elders' Festival — I'll be able to make a tidy sum off the fireworks business," Ursula smiled, then seemed to suddenly remember something and her face darkened. "Damn it, I wonder when the king will hold the celebration banquet for the queen's return. Did he tell you?"

"No." Jace shook his head. "The queen's body is still very weak — she definitely can't force a banquet together in a short time. The king hasn't told me a specific date either; he hasn't even brought it up. Why do you sound like you hate this banquet?"

"I don't hate banquets — I hate those damned elves," Ursula said, holding her head. "I really don't get what the elves from Quel'Thalas have to do with the queen finally being found. Is it because the clothes they bought were too expensive, so they have to attend every single banquet in this world as much as possible so they don't sit in a box and grow moths? Of course... I'm not talking about Vereesa Windrunner. She's... not quite like those others. You know what I mean, Jace."

"I know." Jace nodded. "Are there a lot of elves in the city?"

"Didn't you already go to the palace?" Ursula sighed. "Ah, well, actually there are more in the Mage Quarter and the Park. Enough complaining from me — it's probably the effect of the drug making me irritable right now. You know I'm not normally like this."

After saying that, she stared at Jace for a while. Jace, already at a loss for how to deal with the avalanche of rambling, just stood there.

"Right — the green dragon's blood." Ursula raised a finger. "Did you get it?"

"I got it," Jace said. "I came this time specifically to make the delivery."

He pulled the blood bottle out from his clothes and handed it to the alchemist. Ursula's face even carried a hint of disbelief.

She took the bottle, looked it over carefully, drew a sharp breath, and said slowly: "This is real... green dragon's blood? How did you manage to get it?"

Jace thought about it for a moment, then said, "Found a dead green dragon, then extracted its blood."

"All right, you've got your secrets." Ursula shot Jace a glance, then went to pull the stopper. Jace watched her hands move and immediately stopped her. "Hold on."

"What?" Ursula asked.

"I brought this out of a cursed place," Jace explained. "And the cause of this green dragon's death was also because of a troll evil ritual. Although I didn't sense any corruption or dark magic when I extracted it, it's hard to guarantee it's a hundred percent pure. If you're going to use it, you might need to prepare to do some purifying and the like."

"I'll keep that in mind," Ursula said.

"Do it right now," Jace said, staring at the blood bottle. "I'll watch from the side in case anything goes wrong."
