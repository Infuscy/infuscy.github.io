## Chapter 1087: Flying Into Darker Darkness

In the middle of the night, back at his rented room, Jace laid the incantation from the scroll out on the table. "Sazeno, come take a look at this."

The imp climbed out of the bag onto the table, unrolled the scroll, looked it over, and asked, "What incantation is this?"

"You don't know either?" Jace asked.

"Hmm." Sazeno thought about it. "Are you sure this is really an incantation? It doesn't look like something an eredar or any other demon would have come up with..."

Molophil observed it carefully, moved her lips and read it once, then said, "I have to say, I rather agree with his assessment."

Jace took out the elven transliteration record. "This can't possibly have been invented by the elves, can it? Anyway, take a look at what might be wrong with this incantation. As things stand, I can cast it successfully, but it really does drain mana badly, and there are parts that aren't very smooth — it might have something to do with the incantation's quality."

"Understood — at least it's not a very heavy workload," the imp said. "After helping you copy that demonic dictionary, I feel like I still haven't recovered even now!"

"How many years ago was that?" Jace said. "Besides, didn't I give you that kind of stone as a supplement?"

"Do you have any more, master?" the imp asked tentatively.

"Wait until I summon Karlez and have him figure out a way to make them for you," Jace said, taking the bottle and anticoagulant potion out of his bag. "I'm going to start collecting my blood these next couple of days — until I can be sure of summoning Lord Karlez back from whatever plane he's on."

"Does that mean I won't be able to replenish my power, darling?" Molophil asked.

"The trust between us has completely collapsed, Molophil," Jace said. "Think about what you did that night."

Molophil lowered her head plaintively and said softly, "I thought that because I'd given so much and endured such terrible pain, you'd voluntarily treat me better — I mean, better than usual."

"It was more than just 'a little' better," Jace said. "The day after tomorrow I'm heading out to the big graveyard on the south bank of the Nafeltari River. I need to save my mana. We'll talk when I get back."

"The big graveyard, yes, that priest. But you're not planning to take me along, master?" Molophil asked.

"It's just dealing with a few ghouls," Jace said.

Molophil leaned to the side, looking displeased, and cocked a hoof. "I thought ever since you took me to The Pig and Whistle Inn last time, you'd truly started treating me as a member of our team. But it seems I'm just an accessory after all. Of course, I'm not saying there's anything wrong with being Jace Sesso's accessory — I'm perfectly willing, master."

Her plaintive voice didn't make Jace feel guilty, but he did fall silent for a moment.

He felt he was actually getting a bit overconfident. After all, that big graveyard buried no small number of heroes who'd fallen during the troll wars — among them should be quite a few fairly powerful warriors, even mages. After being influenced by the dark power of Karazhan, who knew what kind of undead monsters they'd metamorphose into?

Although the last time he and Grede had encountered that war hero covered in Grave Moss who, because his corpse was severely damaged and he couldn't move easily, hadn't given them much trouble — who knew what might be in the tomb keeping Achaeus?

He'd been telling Grede and the others to be careful going to Kalimdor, but here he was being this overconfident himself. "Then come with me," Jace said.

Molophil's mouth nearly stretched to her ears in a grin. The imp immediately grabbed Jace's sleeve. "You're getting softer, master — don't give in to this sayaad's seductive talk!"

"Has nothing to do with her talk. I'm sleeping now. Let go of me, damn it..." Jace finally managed to tear the imp off to the side and flopped onto the bed with a thump.

On Monday, Jace arrived at the city gate at the time agreed upon with Morgan Ladimore. The sky was somewhat overcast — not exactly good weather for heading out, one way or another.

As he walked over, he tried casting a Light cantrip. Of course, it wasn't to drive away the gloomy sky — it was to test how much he'd recovered, physically and mentally, from the injuries sustained in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar.

Actually, ever since testing the Mana Burn, he'd felt he was mostly better. After another day of rest, his condition in every respect was even better by this point — perfect for exploring a big graveyard teeming with shadow and necrotic miasma.

With a priest coming along, this so-called treasure-hunt expedition was, for Jace, more like tourism and relaxation. So he didn't really care much about what the payoff would be.

Arriving near the gate of the Valley of Heroes, Morgan was already waiting there. And not just him — his wife Liss was there too. The two were conversing in low voices, Liss giving worried instructions while Morgan nodded from time to time.

Beside Jace, a girl of four or five was crouched in front of the plaque beneath the Turalyon statue, reading aloud in a loud voice. "My friend, you are the embodiment of justice and..."

"Virtue," Jace said, looking at her. "The embodiment of justice and virtue, Sarah."

"Virtue... the embodiment of virtue. Uh, you know my name, sir?"

Sarah Ladimore lifted her head, and over there Morgan and Liss also looked over in this direction.

"Sorry, I'm a little late," Jace said.

"I just got here myself," Morgan said.

Liss politely inclined her body and said, "Morgan doesn't know any magic, Mr. Sesso — please take good care of him."

"I'll do what I can, ma'am," Jace said.

Morgan seemed to feel Liss was saying too much and gave her a look of complaint.

Actually, the two of them were about Jace's age — still basically big kids — yet layered together with identities like old married couple and parents of a child, it came off as somewhat comical and jarring.

Still, Jace looked at Sarah, who was hiding behind her mother and still a bit afraid of strangers, and thought of Vereesa. At the same time, he thought of his own parents.

It was about time to bring them to Stormwind.

After saying goodbye to Sarah and Liss, the priest picked up his round shield and long staff and followed Jace along the bridge to the base of the wall below the Valley of Heroes.

Here there was a passage leading to the griffon roost atop the wall. Occasionally griffon keepers would bring the griffons here to stroll and relax — especially when it was raining outside.

Jace had told the griffon keepers in advance that he'd be taking Goret out today, so this enormous gray-white griffon had been led here early.

Seeing his old friend appear, Goret immediately raised his head and let out two sharp shrieks, causing the people on the bridge to look down one after another.

Molophil had already secretly climbed onto the griffon's back. Although Goret couldn't see this demon, he could of course feel something strange climbing onto him. But it wasn't his first time carrying this demon either, so he didn't make any complaints — he just turned his head in some confusion to look.

At this point, Jace nimbly climbed up onto Goret's back, sat behind Molophil, tightly bound the wrapped-up Ulthalesh to Goret's body, then reached back and pulled Morgan up.

Of course, he couldn't let this priest sit next to the demon, or get too close to the Ulthalesh staff — especially considering this fellow had once suspiciously smelled a succubus on him.

However, this time Morgan showed no unusual reaction, because his keen sensitivity to evil had been completely swallowed up by the nervousness of riding a griffon.

Not only that — after he got on, the griffon shook his head somewhat uncomfortably. This wariness toward a stranger made Morgan even more on edge. He had any mind left to suspect the demon sitting next to him?

At this point, Jace comforted the griffon in Dwarvish for a few moments, and only then did Goret lower his head and stop making strange puttering noises.

Hearing him comfort the griffon, the keeper who'd brought the griffon here called out with a smile, "Lord Grede Thunderfist himself must have personally taught you how to communicate with griffons, Master Sesso. You sound just like those Wildhammer instructors from Aerie Peak."

"I'll take that as a compliment, sir," Jace called back loudly.

Grede hadn't been wrong — when it came to professionalism in fighting alongside griffons, there really weren't many in Aerie Peak who could match his level. After all, even the griffon instructors invited to Stormwind might not have more combat and riding experience than he did.

By now Goret was already itching to launch into the sky. Jace looked back and said, "Hold on tight, Ladimore."

"I'm ready, Jace," Morgan said.

"Then let's go, Goret," Jace shouted in Dwarvish. The gray-white monster let out a low growl and lunged into the sky.

Having gotten used to Suola's gentle takeoff, Jace nearly got flung clean off by that burst. It was Morgan, gripping the saddle tightly, who was steadier than him.

Watching the watchtowers and forests rapidly pass beneath them, without the protection of nature's power, cold wind howled past his ears and whipped his hair wild — Jace actually got the faint illusion that he was on vacation.

Even if he was flying toward the most feared dark place in the entire Kingdom of Stormwind...
