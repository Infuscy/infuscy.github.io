## Chapter 1088: Damp Nightfall

From the top of the Wizard Sanctum's high tower you could even see the rooftops of Goldshire and the Nafeltari River — but actually flying over there wasn't as effortless as the naked eye made it seem.

Still, at Goret's speed, they made it to Raven Hill before the day's end.

Of course, Jace was going by his pocket watch — it was only afternoon when they crossed the Nafeltari River, yet the darker the sky flew, the darker it got. Goret seemed to know exactly where Raven Hill was, plunging straight in that direction, skimming the thick canopy of Duskwood, and after a dazed stretch the sky had completely darkened.

Amid the deep blue, unfathomable forest, Raven Hill was quite lively. From far off you could see the fires here, especially near the town center square.

At first Morgan was a little confused — he'd moved to Darkshire precisely because most of Raven Hill's residents had left, and later gone on to Stormwind. How come now it seemed even livelier than before?

After landing, several soldiers gathered around. The cobblestones of the square and the surrounding buildings seemed even more run-down than the last time Jace had come here, but there were indeed quite a lot of people coming and going.

Jace took a glance down one of the alleys into town and noticed that side was completely devoid of people — the liveliness was confined to this small square that the road passed through.

"Afternoon, you two... uh, and a priest too." An officer wearing rusty chainmail called out, craning his neck. "Where are you from? What business do you have here?"

Jace stroked Goret's neck a few times to calm him, then dropped down from the griffon's back, took out his ring, and showed it to the officer. "Jace Sesso, Court Mage advisor to the crown. I'm here to investigate the undead activity in the big graveyard."

The officer glanced at Jace's ring and immediately gave a crisp salute, standing straight. "Master Sesso, my apologies. I'm the sergeant major here, Sergeant Santes. An honor to meet you."

"The honor's mine, Sergeant." Jace shook the sturdy officer's hand. The priest also nodded. "Morgan Ladimore of the Church of the Holy Light. Pleased to meet you, Sergeant Santes."

"Likewise, brother." The officer smiled.

He didn't look like he recognized Morgan, and certainly didn't know Morgan had been a guard here for a few years. Given that Raven Hill hadn't had many residents ever since the dark shroud of Karazhan descended, with the inhabitants mostly knowing each other, this officer probably wasn't from the town.

"Raven Hill seems quite lively," Jace said, looking around. "Has a lot of people been moving back lately, Sergeant?"

"Just soldiers, Master Sesso," the officer answered honestly. "Not many townfolk have come back, actually — that's a good thing. The handful of townfolk willing to stay here, we have to worry about them running into trouble at any moment. After all, we can't exactly force them out."

"How's the situation in the big graveyard?" Morgan asked.

The officer shook his head. "Back when there were rumors of demons appearing in Moonbrook, the Wizard Sanctum and the Church cooperated on a few operations here to clear out the undead, brother — but the results were extremely limited. After the matter of the giant black dragon in Stormwind came up, the operations were suspended entirely. The mages and priests here all went back to Stormwind to help out. So the situation in there right now isn't great. Otherwise, the king wouldn't have temporarily stationed us here to guard the road and stop those... walking corpses inside from slipping out and causing trouble."

Morgan and Jace exchanged a glance. Jace said, "Looks like we'll have to go in and see for ourselves."

"The Raven Hill graveyard is bigger than I imagined, Master Sesso," the officer nodded. "Of course I'm not worried that those walking dead with broken arms and legs in there could hurt a great mage like you — I just want to warn you that it's really easy to get lost in there. It'd be much better to find a guide who knows the graveyard well."

"Much appreciated, Sergeant." Jace smiled. "One more thing — could you take us to the griffon keeper?"

"No problem," the officer said.

After settling Goret, the two of them came to the inn entrance. The inn was still open, but the people going in and out at the moment were mostly soldiers wearing military shirts and cotton robes.

"You didn't join the cooperative operations to clear out the undead that they ran here before?" Jace asked Morgan beside him.

"I didn't even know about that," Morgan shook his head. "It was probably because I'd already gone to Darkshire by that time. If I'd still been staying in Raven Hill then, there's no way I would have missed it. But I more or less understand why even with the Church and the Wizard Sanctum cooperating, they couldn't purify the graveyard."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"Too many forebears of noble and soldier families are buried here, Jace." Morgan looked down the tree-lined road leading to the graveyard gate. "Some of them might even be the grandparents, or parents, of many mages living today. A lot of them were heroes of the Gurubashi Wars, even the gnoll wars — so you can imagine how much resistance there would be to dealing with this graveyard."

He leaned against the doorframe of the inn. "A mage like you can understand that once a person climbs out of their grave, they're nothing like they were when alive — all they bring to this world is plague and suffering. But not everyone can understand that, especially people who've never seen the undead and aren't willing to learn about the current state of this graveyard."

"Honestly," the priest lowered his voice. "Even I would have a hard time accepting that Archmage Majino Dumas and his students would burn the place where my parents and grandparents rested, inside and out, with a single fire for this reason — even though I understand better than most people... that it's the safest way to handle it. Forgive me, Holy Light — I shouldn't be saying this. I sound like I'm sympathizing with the undead."

"Nowhere near that stage, Morgan," Jace said. "Putting that aside — the two of us definitely can't sweep the entire big graveyard from end to end either. Our goal this time is to find your sword. Let's rest here for the night and head into the graveyard early tomorrow morning."

Morgan Ladimore's grandfather's grave wasn't over by the main gate of the graveyard — it was further off to the east. The last time, whether with Grede or with Vereesa, Jace had never come this far.

Ever since the previous night, it had started raining, and by morning the woodland had become a muddy mess. All around the big graveyard there drifted a faint, damp aura carrying a hint of ill-omened rot.

The last time he'd come here, Jace's understanding of shadow power had been far less than it was now — but he still remembered the stench of decay that had hung in the time.

Coming here today and smelling that familiar scent, he could even detect, within it, the emotion — a trace of reluctance, tinged with anger — that pervaded the big graveyard and the surrounding forest.

This was an extremely simple emotion, one that only emanated from the reanimated mindless dead. To Jace, this feeling was like hearing a song in a foreign language long ago, then learning that language and listening to it again.

Morgan Ladimore walked in front. The two advanced through the rain, sticking close to the graveyard's iron fence. Along the way he kept glancing into the graveyard, on guard against crazed ghouls that might appear at any moment.

Jace watched his cautious manner and couldn't help but ask, "I'm very curious, Morgan — if you're worried the undead in this graveyard might hurt you, isn't that a kind of doubt toward the Holy Light?"

"Huh? What do you mean?" Morgan turned his head to ask.

Jace said, "Based on my previous understanding, the more steadfast a person's faith, the more easily they receive the favor of the Holy Light. In the terms we use at the Wizard Sanctum, they'd have a stronger 'Holy Light affinity.'"

"That's more or less right," Morgan said.

"Logically speaking, the undead all fear the Holy Light," Jace continued in a joking tone. "So a person of steadfast faith shouldn't worry about undead in the graveyard rushing out to hurt them — so wouldn't your worrying count as your faith wavering?"

Morgan smiled, thought about it for a moment, and said, "You've really got me there, Jace. This might be exactly why the Silver Hand hasn't given me a reply. But I think no matter what, there's still a line between steadfast faith and recklessness. At the very least, I don't want to die right now. If I lose my life for no good reason, then no matter what, I won't be able to keep taking care of my family, let alone help others, or even serve the Holy Light."

"At least from the war stories I've heard, when facing Gul'dan's death knight legions, the Silver Hand knights were utterly fearless — because they knew the Light would surely drive away the darkness. Judging by that, I've still got quite a ways to go."

"And you hunted down and killed Teron Gorefiend together with Lady Vereesa Windrunner, Jace — I know about that," he suddenly said. "Have you never considered joining the Silver Hand?"

Jace was taken aback for a moment. "Are you making a joke with me?"

"Of course not," Morgan said.

"Uh." Jace coughed self-consciously. He very much wanted to say, "Maybe it's because of this big bone staff on my back that's devoured who knows how many crazed souls from the Twisting Nether."

Of course, he had no intention of arguing to the death with this priest right in front of him. And Molophil, who'd been hiding behind them following along, was already thoroughly enjoying watching the show. Jace could even feel her trembling with laughter from within her shadow — and this kind of joy clearly couldn't be shared through Soul Link.

Probably because he'd spent too much time around folks like Molophil and Grede, who constantly needled and bickered with him in a sarcastic tone, running into someone as straightforward as Morgan left Jace a little unaccustomed.

He pondered for a moment and said, "All right — King Terenas II brought this up to me too. I just never responded. I don't think I'm suited to being a... I don't know, a servant of the divine, or whatever."

Just then, from not far behind them, the iron fence suddenly made a clanging noise — then went quiet, as if something had suddenly bumped into it.

The two of them simultaneously turned to stare in that direction. All that remained now was the patter of rain.

After a while, confirming there was no further disturbance, the priest whispered, "Could be a wild hare, a rat... could be... we've entered the range of those undead's activity."

"How far to the tomb?" Jace asked.

"Less than half an hour's walk, we should be there," Morgan said.
