Chapter 1089: The Debt Collector

The forest at this hour was hard to describe as "early morning" — even though it genuinely was early morning.

Through the dark barrier and gloomy canopy of Duskwood, the sunlight was weaker than the moonlight of night, to say nothing of the faint, shifting thick fog obscuring things in the distance. The thick shadows of the trees and the graveyard's iron fence crisscrossed like dark giants, caged and slumbering.

After walking for a while, they passed a side gate of the graveyard that was locked shut tight. Morgan found a broken hole at the base of the iron fence nearby and nimbly slipped through, then helped Jace squeeze through as well.

This was a place Jace had never come to, never even seen. The big graveyard in the rain — row upon row of grave markers, mostly still intact, just more or less slightly damaged.

Based on past experience, as long as it wasn't night yet and they didn't make a huge commotion here, it shouldn't draw over every undead in the place that could still stand.

Morgan waved for Jace to follow, then quickly walked toward a tomb entrance in the distance.

This was the entrance to an quite enormous underground crypt. The stone nameplate above had already broken and fallen off, and the remaining words were illegible.

This entrance clearly had a gate before, and there were still traces of an iron fence on it — looked like it had been visited by grave robbers many times over.

Jace touched one of the bent, broken iron bars, then looked at Morgan. Morgan shook his head and whispered, "That wasn't me."

"Then you're sure your grandfather's sword is still in there?" Jace asked.

"I..." Morgan frowned. "Last time, I was forced to leave before I reached his tomb."

"All right," Jace said. "Even if we can't find it, at least knowing the sword isn't here counts as something."

"Thank you, Jace," Morgan said.

"It's nothing."

So saying, Jace took the lead down the steps and drew Azure Song. The magic sword's eerie glow illuminated the steps and the damp walkway below, submerged in dark green mud. Morgan, witnessing this scene from the side, drew a quiet breath.

After entering the walkway, Jace realized he'd still underestimated the scale of this underground crypt.

This clearly wasn't a single family's tomb. At every fork in the path, there were signs indicating who was buried at the end of this passage, which family's members, or what they had done in life.

The two had only taken a few steps forward when a wet, squelching noise came from the shadows — like some beast moving its tongue.

In the silence of the tomb, this faint sound rang out extremely loudly. Jace and Morgan both stopped in their tracks at the same time, one of their boots scraping against the mud and gravel with a soft scritch.

That wet sound vanished. The tomb fell silent once more. Morgan stared at the fork in the path for a while, confirmed nothing was following them, then continued looking in the direction they'd originally been heading.

But there was now an extra figure over there — a crouching creature, its entire body a dull reddish-brown, staring this way. Its eyes glinted with the cloudy yellow light of necrotic energy under the radiance of Azure Song.

A ghoul.

The ghoul suddenly opened its bloody maw, baring dark yellow fangs streaked with sinew, as if trying to roar something. Jace immediately used a Suffocation spell to choke its neck tight.

The ghoul, which had been about to shriek and pounce, was forced upright by this force, nearly toppling backward. It grabbed at its own neck as if trying to break free of the dark magic's grip, unable to make any noise except for a series of feeble, dry coughs.

But in the next second its head was blown apart by a burst of exploding flame. Rotting brain matter and withered bone fragments exploded all over that stretch of corridor. The ghoul crashed to the ground, clawed at the now-empty stump of its neck for a while, twitched, then stopped moving.

The boom of the exploding flame reverberated through the tomb, like gust after gust of wild wind — but from start to finish, that ghoul hadn't been able to make a single sound.

Jace clenched his fist, rubbed off a few still-sparking embers from his fingers, glanced back down the passage they'd come from, confirmed there were no followers, and said, "Let's keep going. Let's hope he doesn't have many friends in here."

But Morgan beside him had gone a little stiff. He looked again at the ghoul that had just appeared and, within a few seconds, had turned into a charred, rotting corpse, then whispered, "It's only now that I'm starting to get a bit of a real feel for how you could defeat a dragon like Onyxia, Jace."

Jace snorted a quiet laugh and said in a low voice, "Using fire against a black dragon? Best not think like that, Morgan."

"Then how did you defeat that black dragon princess?" Morgan asked curiously.

Only then did Jace realize he'd accidentally opened up a topic that wasn't great to discuss with a priest, so he could only say, "Hard to explain, Morgan — and we're in a haunted tomb..."

"Understood," Morgan nodded.

The two continued forward. Morgan kept feeling the walls and observing the nameplate at every fork in the path. Until he felt his way to a passage entrance strewn with some collapsed bricks, and pointed inside, nodding to Jace.

Jace took the opportunity to observe what was written on that nameplate. Most of it had been worn illegible, but in the middle there were some words about Mortwick Tower, heroes, the Gurubashi conspiracy, and the like.

Similar to the Ilgalar Tower, Mortwick Tower was an ancient mage tower located to the south of Raven Hill, used to guard the southern border of the Kingdom of Stormwind against the harassment of gnolls and Stranglethorn jungle trolls.

It seemed that during the Gurubashi Wars, the trolls had wanted to seize this tower to steal the power of the ley line nexus — and Morgan's grandfather had probably participated in the defense of this tower and rendered distinguished service.

"The farthest I got last time was here, Jace," Morgan said in a low, wary voice. "Last time I came here I ran into a siege by several walking corpses. This time, it seems a lot quieter... although I didn't encounter a ghoul last time either. If there'd been a ghoul back then, I probably wouldn't have made it out."

"Even if the situation's better this time, we can't let our guard down," Jace said. Morgan nodded, then walked into the passage.

This burial chamber looked quite large, with three stone sarcophagi placed along the two sides — one of which had already been pried open, empty inside.

However, Morgan's grandfather didn't seem to be any of these three. He ignored the three sarcophagi and walked all the way in, as if there were more coffins further inside.

But as they walked, a sound suddenly came from ahead. At the same time, Molophil's Soul Link slowly tightened. A blurry, brief shout of pain filtered out of the darkness. Morgan, walking in front, stopped in his tracks.

Jace tried to raise Azure Song to illuminate what was ahead, but besides a blur there was nothing to see.

"La..."

"Dimore... thief, scoundrel, cheat..."

A low, complaining voice came from the shadows, followed by a dull thud that made the whole floor tremble.

"What's going on?" Jace asked.

"I... I don't know," Morgan was taken aback for a moment.

"Pay up, either... or! Or! Give... give..."

A string of blurry words that couldn't quite be pieced together into logic gushed out of the shadows, accompanied by another series of continuous tremors. A tall, dark figure emerged from the gloom, revealed under the light of Azure Song!

This was a bloated, swollen giant — not only tall and large, but fat and stout to boot, its body covered in festering cracks and dead maggots.

Its thick fingers gripped a blacksmith's hammer. The moment it appeared, it hurled the hammer straight at Morgan. Morgan raised his shield — there was a loud clang, and he was knocked back several steps, stumbling onto a sarcophagus.

Jace had still been wanting to ask just what exactly was going on, but seeing that this monster was already completely devoid of reason, driven only by dark necrotic breath and intense hatred, he could only recite an incantation...

Although this giant was bloated and huge, its reactions were actually quick. Hearing the sound of an incantation being recited from the side, it immediately turned and threw a punch. But this punch smashed hard into an even darker ring of light — the tremendous force rebounding back knocked it several steps backward, crashing into the stone wall with a boom.

Jace called upon flame and the chaotic shadow within the monster's body to merge, instantly igniting its entire form.

Amidst the purple-red flames, the monster howled and pounded at its own burning body and head. Soon it lost the support of energy, knelt on the ground, and dimmed — but until the very end, it still muttered the name "Dimore."

Morgan was clearly thrown by what this monster had been saying, and had no mind to think about what magic Jace had just used to deal with the enemy. He caught his breath several times before saying, "What on earth..."

"I was going to ask you," Jace looked at him. "Did you recognize him?"

"I don't recognize him... and I don't remember owing anyone any money," Morgan swallowed. "Let's see if your grandfather's sword is still here first. We'll talk about the rest outside." Morgan nodded. The two walked together to the very end of the passage and pushed open the lid of that sarcophagus.

Unlike the other sarcophagi, the lid was already covered in brownish-black stains — most likely left by that giant just now. The moment they pushed open a crack, Jace felt a faint glimmer of light reveal itself from below.

Inside the sarcophagus lay a corpse half-buried in mud. Although it was a set of withered bones, looking at his appearance Jace couldn't feel much threat or fear.

Beside him, a bright sword covered in mud still radiated luster. The Holy Light sigils and Church inscriptions on it were still clearly visible — it looked as if it had been freshly forged.

Even as a warlock, in such a gloomy underground, illuminated by this warm beam of light, Jace could feel a trace of comfort.

"Achaeus," Morgan murmured.
