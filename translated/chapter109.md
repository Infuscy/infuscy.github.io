## Chapter 109: The Weekend Underground Gathering

A few days later. Sunday. The Slaughtered Lamb basement.

Jace was sitting in a corner, carefully studying the Orcish Rune Patterns: A Preliminary Inquiry spread out on the table.

Jagin had been holding forth by the campfire for a long time, analyzing the various incantations — or suspected incantations — that everyone had gathered over the past few weeks.

Most of the suspected incantations were in Orcish. Jace could even understand a fair number of them.

Orcish incantations weren't unheard of — they were just rare. Even magic invented or refined by orcs, like the Eye of Kilrogg, was cast in Demonic.

After all, the orcs had only been in contact with shadow magic for about a decade or so. The shadow magic they'd learned during that time was all in Demonic too. The connection between the Orcish language and dark magic wasn't deep enough yet.

So most of the exchanges happening here were probably a waste of time. Jace, using exam prep as an excuse, listened with one ear while reading his own book. Nobody in the room minded.

These past few days, Jace hadn't dared try the Drain Life incantation again. It was unquestionably the most dangerous spell he'd ever cast — or even attempted.

Not only could it inflict horrifying harm on others, but losing control of it could hurt him too. That said, despite vomiting blood that day, Jace hadn't felt sick or come down with any inflammation.

When he'd gotten back to Stormwind that day and had the priests at the church examine him, they hadn't found any external injuries or hidden internal damage either. And the bleeding had stopped quickly.

The imp guessed that the life energy he'd drained had probably ruptured his blood vessels in the first instant, throwing his bodily fluids into disarray. But once the excess life energy his body couldn't handle finished overflowing, the remainder had repaired the vessels.

In other words, it was just a process of expelling excess energy. The damage to Jace's body was limited to that very first instant. Once the energy dissipated into the air and that overflow of life force appeared as light, the whole process was over.

The imp's explanation had eased Jace's worries somewhat. And at the church, not even the priest had detected any sign of dark energy corruption in his body.

Even when the Holy Light was used to probe for hidden injuries, there wasn't the searing, agonizing burn he'd felt when being treated for necrotic poison infection. So the matter was considered settled.

Still, afterward, when Jace recalled the moment he'd used Drain Life to rip the vitality out of that sheep, he always felt there was a kind of addiction in it.

He couldn't tell whether it had to do with the shadow magic or the feeling of wielding power.

All magic was addictive. The end stage was what happened to the elves — a dependence on magic so severe that once they were separated from a magic source for a certain length of time, they'd start to deteriorate physically and mentally. The so-called Wretched: bizarre, withered creatures like dried-out zombies, stripped of their reason, their only goal in life to frantically hunt down the tiniest trace of mana.

To keep himself from dwelling on that craving, he decided that after this gathering he'd go find Grede, see how his injury was healing, and get back to swordsmanship training — use exercise and sweat to shake off those negative, toxic feelings.

At that moment, Jace noticed Erland Dudmoor kept glancing over from time to time. He pretended not to see it.

This guy, while a decent fighter and always pretty blunt in what he said and did, wasn't the type to pull a knife on someone in an alley the way Jagin would.

And right now, he was clearly more interested in the jumble of incantations Jagin was rattling off. Unlike his earlier dismissiveness toward the gatherings and magic study, ever since he'd been hit by Jace's spell that day, he'd gotten noticeably more serious about learning incantations. He was even mouthing along to something Jagin was saying.

Just then, Jace was deep in his study of orcish runes when Erland suddenly called out, "Hey, Jagin, what about that incantation from last time?"

Jace sensed that although the words were directed at Jagin, the voice seemed aimed in his direction.

He looked up — and sure enough, Erland was looking right at him.

Erland was clearly talking about the Curse of Weakness. What, with all the incantations Jagin had covered, he still hadn't gotten to the Curse of Weakness?

"I saw young Jace use it pretty well last time," Erland glanced over here, then back toward Jagin. "A spell that powerful — Jagin, aren't you planning to share it with the rest of us?"

Sulenna, Ursula, and Galders were all looking — some at Jace, some at Jagin. Jagin smiled and said, "No need to rush, Erland Dudmoor. I'm delighted that your desire to learn new knowledge is so strong. But the most powerful and effective spells are naturally saved for last. Jace is willing to share his discoveries with us, and I would never betray his trust."

Actually, Jace had been wondering lately why Jagin was using the "precious" incantations he'd hoarded to trade for these practical spells from Jace — but then not keeping the traded spells for himself, instead sharing them with everyone.

Either Jagin didn't think those so-called "precious" incantations were precious at all — the way Jace saw the Curse of Weakness, for instance — and was just bringing them out as small bargaining chips to trade with others.

After all, when Jagin had produced those three incantations, he'd mentioned that he hadn't learned any of them except the Curse of Recklessness.

And the Curse of Recklessness didn't even sound that useful. After all, who wanted their enemies to go berserk? This wasn't a game where you got bonus loot for adding a debuff.

Maybe he genuinely wanted to see this place grow and thrive, sincerely wanting more people to walk his "path of dark arts."

Just then, Jagin was looking over at Jace as he spoke: "Our new member recently acquired a new incantation while traveling outside. He's named it the Curse of Weakness. This incantation is clearly quite different from what we've studied before — its source may not be the orcish warlocks."

Hearing that, Jace was a little surprised. He hadn't expected Jagin to realize this incantation had nothing to do with orcs. After all, he'd only confirmed it wasn't even Demonic — that it came from the Void — by asking the imp.

At that moment, Ursula crept over from nearby and whispered, "Hey, Jace, you didn't learn this spell while you were in Westfall, did you? You stole it from the murlocs and didn't tell me — you went and told Jagin instead?"

Jace said helplessly, "When I first got back I didn't even know what it was, Ursula. This spell isn't the murlocs'. I found it while scavenging. When I first discovered it, I didn't even know if it was shadow magic. I couldn't read it — for all I know it could've been something from the Wizard Sanctum. Didn't you hear Jagin? There's no way to tell if it's an orcish incantation."

He leaned in close to her ear. "Besides, didn't I give you a naga scale as compensation?"
