Chapter 1091: The Last Chance

"Thank you for your cooperation, Mr. Sesso." Dar'Khan nodded firmly. "The last time we met was in Winterspring — to be more precise, in Frosthammer Canyon. Have you remembered anything?" "I have some recollection," Jace said. Dar'Khan said, "At that time my colleagues and I were tracking a group of undead. You told me then that you hadn't encountered them. But later we found some quite unusual rings among a group of Gadgetzan goblin merchants, and those rings belonged to our targets. Although those goblin merchants wouldn't reveal the source of these items, I ultimately tracked the only remaining lead back to Winterspring... I want you to know, it took a tremendous amount of effort." Jace realized that Dar'Khan didn't know that Ulthalesh, which he was currently carrying on his back, was right there. Because as he said all this, his gaze never once fell on the thing wrapped in cloth behind him. Of course, this wasn't necessarily thanks to the cloth wrapping the staff. Because even Jace, who'd seen Ulthalesh in his past life, hadn't immediately realized it was Ulthalesh the first time he'd laid eyes on the staff's true form. And Dar'Khan, an elven mage who'd only heard of this thing in legends, was even less likely to. "The undead wearing rings," he said. "Why not just tell me what kind of things they were? Do they have something to do with the furbolgs plagued by the necrotic plague in Frosthammer Valley?" "Don't talk to me about furbolgs," Dar'Khan said coldly. "You killed those black knights, Jace Sesso — you and Ranger Lord Vereesa Windrunner killed Eredin! Only you could have done it, no one else could possibly have. Are you trying to make me believe that some amateur mercenaries from Winterspring defeated Medivh's hunters?!" Jace shook his head. "Medivh's hunters, black knights... honestly, Dar'Khan, you know far more than I do. I went to Frosthammer Valley to help a group of furbolgs transport medicine to treat the plague." Dar'Khan stared at Jace for a while, then gave a dry laugh. "Are you trying to convince me that the Court Mage advisor to Varian Wrynn, the Ranger Lord of the High Kingdom, the Highpeak Lord of Aerie Peak — a group of heroes who fought at the Battle of Grim Batol — trekked all the way into the heart of the snowy mountains of northern Kalimdor just to help a bunch of brainless beasts transport a load of medicine brewed from savage witchcraft and crude weeds and animal hides?" "It's related to the green dragons, Magister," Jace said. "Queen Ysera believes there exists a corruption of the dream in that place, perhaps related to the furbolgs. Helping the Timbermaw tribe was along the way." Dar'Khan's smile froze on his face. He bit his lip little by little, the corners of his mouth even twitching slightly. Perhaps he hadn't expected Jace to use the green dragons as a shield, or perhaps it was for some other reason — or maybe it was because... he seemed to actually believe Jace's explanation. Yes. Jace sensed that Dar'Khan had a trace of self-doubt in his heart. For a moment, he really was considering whether Jace truly had nothing to do with this whole affair. Jace was a little surprised, because generally speaking, not many people would be brushed off so easily — unless... unless he was a master of shadow magic, relying on detecting emotions to figure out whether the other party was deceiving him. Dar'Khan was precisely Quel'Thalas's foremost researcher of shadow. And coincidentally, Jace was exactly the kind of expert at manipulating his own emotions and deceiving these mages who relied on shadow "lie detection." After hesitating for a moment, Dar'Khan glanced at the Ladimore beside him, then fixed his gaze on Jace. "You deciphered the secret recorded by that Burning Legion demon, Jace Sesso. I know you understand Eredun. I know... this absolutely cannot be a coincidence." "I admit, I did try to decipher that secret, Magister. But in the end there weren't any concrete results," Jace said calmly. "The Eredun language... even the doomguards themselves have already forgotten it. It's an abandoned language, isn't it? And what does this have to do with those black knights?" Dar'Khan didn't immediately respond to his words. Jace could feel that although he appeared calm on the surface, two forces were constantly tearing at each other within his heart. The other side firmly believed that, as an evil warlock, he would definitely be intrigued by the terrible evil artifact recorded in that demonic rune. At the same time, he clearly trusted his own understanding of shadow — this warlock before him really didn't seem to know much about the matter. That unfamiliar Farstrider glanced at him, also seeming somewhat suspicious. She said something in a low voice. From this distance Jace couldn't hear clearly. But judging from Dar'Khan's impatient reaction, he definitely didn't like what this Farstrider was saying. "Sorry I can't be of more help, Magister," Jace said. Dar'Khan was silent, just staring this way. After a moment, his long eyebrows suddenly jerked up, and his mood shifted abruptly. Even so, he still forcibly suppressed his excitement and asked as calmly as possible: "Could it be that you're not interested in the clues I've grasped, Mr. Sesso?" Damn it. Jace realized he'd appeared too eager to get out of this whole thing. He was a warlock — a character who should have been extremely curious about dark power. After all, he shouldn't have known that the secret recorded by that demon was Ulthalesh. He shouldn't have known that this thing was an extreme taboo artifact to the Kirin Tor. There was no need for him to avoid asking about the related clues. This time, Jace had actually been tripped up by knowing too much. After all, he'd been avoiding mentioning Ulthalesh to outsiders for a long time now... it had almost become a subconscious habit. "Of course I'm interested," he said. "But I know you wouldn't tell me — you'd be making me a powerful competitor. So let's not waste each other's time." "You haven't asked — how do you know I wouldn't tell you?" Dar'Khan asked. He must have sensed something. His emotions were nearly out of control, letting this impatience and the eagerness for power about to be within reach almost overflow into his tone, even his expression. Before Jace could give any response, Dar'Khan gritted his teeth and said anxiously: "We could cooperate in excavating the clues related to that staff, Jace Sesso. I believe in your abilities. If we don't cooperate... in the end it might be nothing for either of us." Jace said skeptically, "You've already confirmed that it's a..." "Silence strangle you, Jace Sesso!" Dar'Khan suddenly cursed furiously in Thalassian, then said, "Do you think you're hiding it well? Hmm? I've seen through it, I've sensed it — stop beating around the bush with me, you self-righteous bastard! Do you think you're clever?!" The rain poured down in torrents. Although the more he said the more hysterical his tone became, his emotions rapidly descending into chaos, anger quickly spiraling out of control — his voice still sounded dry and feeble. Jace could understand. Dar'Khan didn't want to just believe that Jace knew nothing about this. Because he felt he was very close to Ulthalesh. This warlock before him was his last lead. If he let Jace leave like this, he might never find it again. "Tell me," the Magister said in a rigid roar. "Where exactly is the Staff of Sathir? Where exactly is Ulthalesh?!" When that name was shouted so publicly, even Ressaticis and the Farstrider beside him were startled. Actually, it wasn't just these two elves — even Jace was somewhat taken aback. He hadn't expected Dar'Khan to get so agitated that he'd blurt out Ulthalesh's name. Ressaticis moved closer and said something in a low voice. "Shut up!" he cursed in Thalassian, then shouted loudly at Jace: "You must know — you know it inside out!" Jace blinked. By now the situation had rapidly spiraled out of control. At this point Morgan beside him asked, "Ulthalesh — what is that?" The rain grew heavier. The sound of raindrops striking the flagstones and the mud, bang-bang crack-crack, couldn't drown out the priest's voice. Dar'Khan seemed to be jolted awake by this question, suddenly cooling all over. "Then you both know — and you both must shut your mouths. The dead are far more obedient and reliable than the living." "I came here to help you find clues, Dar'Khan." The Farstrider beside him said in dissatisfaction in Thalassian. "You never said anything about hurting Jace Sesso. Don't forget he's Lady Vereesa's lover, damn it!" "If you go back on your word now, Marla, then you'll die too," Dar'Khan said coldly. Hearing these words, the Farstrider's face went white as a sheet. She spat, raised the bow in her hands, and Jace grabbed the staff behind him with one hand and said in a low voice, "Run, Morgan!" The priest hadn't even replied when in an instant he was turned into a jackalope in a cloud of arcane mist, falling into the filthy puddle. Ressaticis raised her staff flickering with arcane light. It was already too late to take any of it back.
