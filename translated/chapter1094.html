Chapter 1094: The Holy Light Answers

Jace tried to pull himself up using the headstone beside him to go find the ranger, but his hand slipped on the wet stone and he went down again, his skull bouncing off the slab. This hurt worse than catching one of Dar'Khan's Arcane Missiles just now. That was when he noticed Morgan again. The priest was staring at Dar'Khan's corpse, already half-buried by splashed rainwater, and still hadn't dared pull the sword free. Jace more or less understood how he felt. From his perspective, it had been a baffling chain of events — an ambush out of nowhere, the situation spiraling out of control, a bunch of dead Quel'dorei no one had ever heard of and no one wanted to talk about, and one of them had been killed by his own hand. His companion wasn't a real mage, but a warlock who fought shoulder to shoulder with multiple demons, wielding shadow magic and fel fire. What's more, he was a follower of the Church of the Holy Light. But this wasn't the time for Jace to put himself in Morgan's shoes and overthink things. He gritted his teeth, braced against the headstone again, and forced himself upright, scanning around. Through the rain, he could make out the other side in a blur. The female ranger was still slumped against the steps below that headstone. She wasn't moving, but it was hard to tell whether she was dead. In the distance, Veras Darkshadow lay at the entrance to the underground crypt where Morgan's grandfather rested. One of his arms was gone. Veras. Jace turned the name over in his mind. It was only now, in the quiet aftermath, that he remembered this guy was a future member of the Illidari Council — one of the "Lord of Outland" Illidan Stormrage's advisors. Looks like if Illidan still planned to flee to Outland someday, things would be a little different. And then it hit him — compared to Dar'Khan's death, Veras Darkshadow's was nothing at all. During the Scourge invasion a decade and a half from now, the death knight Arthas, leading his undead armies, would raze Lordaeron and march straight through the Wilde into Quel'Thalas. It was Dar'Khan — then a high-ranking member of the Magisters' Platform — who, in exchange for the dark power promised by the Lich King, sabotaged the Ban'dinoriel barrier from within, leaving Silvermoon caught completely off guard. The Ban'dinoriel... a defense that even the Dragonmaw clan's red dragon armies couldn't break head-on. Even Gul'dan himself would have had to start scheming at the barrier-anchor stones. And because of this archmage's betrayal, Arthas's undead legions marched into the Eversong Forest without the slightest resistance. Then came the fall of Silvermoon, the collapse of the Sunwell and the rebirth of Kel'Thuzad, the coming of the Defiler Archimonde to Lordaeron... And now he was dead. A sudden jolt of pain snapped Jace from his thoughts. He gasped and sat back down. A wave of dull, heavy pain surged through his leg. Jace tore open his already-ripped trousers to look — there was no visible wound where it hurt, and he didn't recall any of these high elves striking him there. This was probably pain he'd taken for Morfile through the soul link. He just hadn't noticed because he'd been too focused. Morfile had hidden herself away somewhere by now. Even if it weren't for the need to keep Jace's secrets hidden, she probably had no desire to face the priest up ahead. Morgan seemed to snap back to awareness at the sound from Jace's direction. He came running over. "Jace, how are you?" "Is there any way..." Jace looked at him helplessly. "To heal me up, Morgan? You're a priest. You ought to know healing, right?" Morgan anxiously examined Jace's wounds. "I only recently became a clergyman, Jace. I haven't formally learned anything yet. I mean, the power of the Holy Light..." "Impossible." Jace glared. "Then what the hell was that just now?! You just... came charging over like that?!" "I honestly don't know what happened. Everything spun all of a sudden." Morgan hung his head. "But I saw them all ganging up on you, and I couldn't do anything. I couldn't even move. All I could do was beg the Holy Light for help. I begged the Holy Light to free me from that mage's spell, to give me the strength to help you, to give me the strength... I don't even remember what I just said. I might not have said anything at all, because every sound I made turned into some weird chirping. But..." "But the Holy Light answered you anyway," Jace said. "It must have," Morgan said. "For a moment I felt unstoppable. But I knew I couldn't lose myself in that dazed surprise. I had to — I had to take down that elf. Quickly and decisively. Or you'd die, I'd die, and in the end no one would know what the hell had happened here." "Speaking of which, right now I don't know whether to thank you or thank the Holy Light," Jace said, half-joking. Then he suddenly recalled the nonsense Grede had muttered on the bridge in the Valley of Heroes — something about praying in a church that sounded like casual rambling but carried a strangely serious undertone. "So can you try praying again and have the Holy Light heal me?" "I... I'll try." The rain showed no sign of letting up. The whole graveyard was shrouded in water mist. Morgan lowered his head, placed his hand on Jace's shoulder, and intoned softly: "Merciful Holy Light, I beseech you to ease this merciless pain. With your boundless compassion, touch the body of this man before me. Grant him comfort and peace." "Holy Light, you are the shield of the weak, deflecting the arrows of suffering. You are the scepter that shatters the chains of torment. I serve you with reverence. In your name..." He chanted it with absolute sincerity and devotion, but the Holy Light didn't seem to respond much. All Jace could feel was Morgan's palm growing unnaturally hot... "Jace..." Morgan could only sigh at last. "It's fine," Jace coughed. "Maybe the Holy Light just doesn't feel like helping a warlock like me." "No," Morgan said firmly. "The Holy Light guided me to defeat those who wanted to kill you, Jace. I was only following..." At a loss for how to finish, Jace cut in. "What about a healing potion?" "Oh, right." Morgan fished a Lesser Healing Potion out of his pocket and handed it to Jace. Jace drank the whole thing. He didn't feel any obvious recovery, but at least he found the courage to try standing up again. He pulled himself up using the headstone once more. The name and epitaph on this stone were blurry, but after all his back-and-forth he'd almost memorized them. Whatever the case, the potion's effect was clearly visible now. "Let me help you, Jace," Morgan said. Jace adjusted the staff on his back and waved a hand. "I can walk. Keep an eye on this mage. I'm going to check on that ranger." Morgan nodded. Jace made his way along the rows of headstones, bracing against each one. Only now did he notice his hands were soaked with the dark blood of Ulthalesh. Honestly, he'd expected the Dark Shield he channeled with Ulthalesh's blessing to hold up a little better against Dar'Khan's magic. Dar'Khan's magical strength probably had something to do with that extraordinary staff in his hand. Jace glanced that way. Dar'Khan had the staff pinned under his body right now, so it was hard to make out. Jace crossed the cobblestone path to the ranger's front. Sensing danger approaching, the female ranger opened her eyes slightly. When she saw Jace, her already bloodless face drained of what little color remained. Even too weak to move, her whole body shook violently. "Dar'Khan is dead, Lady Maela," Jace said, pointing Azuresong — glowing faintly in his hand — at the ranger. "Don't kill me." The ranger swallowed hard with effort. "Please, Master Sesso. I don't want to die. When I followed them here, I never imagined Dar'Khan wanted to make a move on you. I only heard that Raven Hill was crawling with the undead, and that a magister needing Farstrider escort was perfectly normal." "Is that so?" Jace said. "Dar'Khan told me they'd prepared a lot to deal with me." He glanced back at Dar'Khan's corpse and pressed on. "These people, or this preparation — did it include you?" "I don't know," the ranger shook her head. "But he did bring a lot of Shadow Protection Potions, plus Fear Ward scrolls from the Church of the Holy Light." "But I thought those were just for dealing with the ghouls in this graveyard. I brought some anti-undead things myself, but I absolutely had no motive to go after you! You and Lady Vereesa and those powerful warriors defeated Deathwing and his daughter Onyxia, Master Sesso. And you killed a terrifying dragonkin lord with just Azuresong. If he'd told me beforehand that we were here to deal with you, I never would have agreed..." She didn't seem to be lying. Her fear was intense and pure, with little other motive hidden behind it. But Jace was aware of an extremely powerful force deep in his heart, hoping she was lying — hoping she had a dagger hidden in her hand that could deal him a fatal blow at any moment, giving him a reason to rip out her soul... But he quickly realized this was the shadow brought by Life Tap, stirring and influencing his emotions. And perhaps the suppressed Ulthalesh, quietly pushing and driving as well. Though he was doing his best to restrain this savage bloodlust, the ranger seemed to sense something wrong. The fear in her eyes nearly overflowed. The rain on her face seemed to be mixed with tears. Morgan suddenly spoke from behind in the distance. "Maybe we should leave soon, Jace. There are still plenty of undead in the graveyard. If we wait much longer, this place could get very dangerous..." "I know," Jace answered. The ranger sitting on the ground grabbed Jace's cloak. "Don't leave me here, Master Sesso. I won't survive the night. I can testify that it was Dar'Khan and his lot who struck first, out of sheer greed, with the goal of killing you... and I was one of them, but I was coerced. I'll vouch for you, and you spared my life knowing my plight." "Please, Master Sesso. Lady Vereesa is my superior and my role model, and General Sylvanas Windrunner goes without saying. I already know my mistake and my hopeless stupleness... I'm willing to accept punishment." "All right, all right." Jace said. "Do you know what Dar'Khan's staff was?" Maela seemed to grab a lifeline again. "Yes, yes. The Staff of the Torrent — the staff of the magisters' leader, the Archmagister, Master Sesso. Dar'Khan borrowed it from Archmagister Belor'al, under the pretense of helping the Kingdom of Stormwind clear out remnant demons and the dark influence in this area... If I hadn't seen him carrying that staff, I never would have believed him so easily." Jace looked over at the staff with some surprise — right now only the silver handle was visible — hardly believing that was truly the possession of the guardian of the Sunwell and Silvermoon. Dar'Khan had actually brought that thing out just to kill him?! "Morgan," he called. "Search that magister's body. We need to take his staff back to the elves." Morgan answered. But looking at Dar'Khan's corpse, he still seemed hesitant. Jace even suspected that after fighting the undead in the Great Graveyard all this time, he'd never actually killed a living person.
