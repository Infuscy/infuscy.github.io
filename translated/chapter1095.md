## Chapter 1095: The Magister's Secret Research?

Morgan found half a bottle of Vitality Potion on Veras's body — a potion that restored both life force and mana at once.

In truth, elves like Vereesa who carried healing potions on missions were very few. Most high elves preferred Vitality Potions, or even just Mana Potions. To a lot of Quel'Thalas arcanists, mana was practically the same as life force.

Jace handed the Vitality Potion to Maela. "Drink it."

"Thank you, thank you, Master Sesso." Maela relaxed considerably.

Watching her take the potion and down it in one gulp, Jace said, "Now come with us and move your colleagues' bodies out. I'm sure you don't want to watch them rot bit by bit and join the ghouls' ranks. A Great Graveyard full of undead war heroes with top-tier combat skills is bad enough. I don't feel like adding a few undead mages to the mix."

"Yes, sir." The ranger forced herself up using the headstone, saluted in Farstrider fashion, and couldn't hold back a few more coughs.

After that, the three of them moved the bodies of Veras, Dar'Khan, and Ressathia out of the graveyard. Ressathia's corpse had been mangled beyond recognition by the felhound — head separated from body — and it took some effort to collect all the pieces.

And, of course, the felhound's corpse.

Dazarm IV had said its goodbyes to Jace, but its massive headless body left on Azeroth was still plenty horrifying. It didn't have many sword wounds — only a clean puncture through the back, a wound that must have reached the fel heart.

This was enough to show that Dar'Khan had some experience dealing with felhounds. His decision to cut off its head felt more like venting or making a statement.

Right now, Jace glanced toward the tree line where Morfile was hiding, wanting to see if the succubus had taken any serious injuries. But Morfile seemed unwilling to respond to his command and show herself.

He knew that in front of a paladin — or an aspiring paladin — who'd just called upon the Holy Light, Morfile naturally had no desire to appear. Through the soul link Jace could tell her injuries weren't serious, so he didn't press it.

Still, given that they'd just fought side by side in a very real sense, and Morgan had witnessed the whole thing from a jackalope's-eye view, there was nothing left he hadn't seen — and whatever he shouldn't have seen, he'd seen it all.

Even if he hadn't noticed during the fight, he'd at least seen the felhound's corpse by now.

But ever since the battle ended, Morgan had kept silent about all of it — not asking about the demons, not bringing up Ulthalesh either.

Jace was actually grateful he didn't mention these things, especially that he didn't press about the staff. If he'd asked out of genuine curiosity, Jace would have had to lie to him.

Putting one over on this ranger, or other strangers, was one thing. Putting one over on a friend, especially one who treated him with genuine sincerity, was another.

The cold rain showed no sign of letting up, but at least under the tree cover outside the graveyard, it offered some shelter.

Dar'Khan's expression still carried a trace of the agony of being run through by Acherus, along with the vacant look common to most dead faces.

Jace recalled what he'd said to Dar'Khan back when they'd first walked out of the crypt, talking.

This sensitive, volatile mage must have sensed something off in Jace's emotions, which was why he'd latched onto the idea that Jace definitely knew some secret related to Ulthalesh — and decided to settle it once and for all, damn the consequences.

Dar'Khan's research into Shadow ran deeper than Jace had imagined. He'd picked up on a flicker of unease in Jace's mind before it even reached his lips. Looked like he still had a long way to go on the path of wielding Shadow.

Thinking of it that way, Jace was actually becoming curious about Dar'Khan's secret research — especially that Shadow-dismantling magic.

It was those tentacles that could tear apart a Void Portal that made a knot in Jace's stomach. Did Dar'Khan have connections to the Old Gods?

Of course, it was possible to stumble onto similar powers through studying pure shadow mana in the Twisting Nether without ever touching the Old Gods. But Jace still couldn't let his guard down. His paranoid, twisted temperament was very likely an influence of that sort of research.

But right now, aside from plenty of mana crystals for storing mana, various Shadow Ward scrolls and Fear Ward scrolls prepared specifically to counter him, and an assortment of potions for dealing with warlocks and demons, there was nothing particularly unusual on the mage's body.

What Jace actually cared about wasn't just Dar'Khan's sensitivity to Shadow — it was a thing he'd said right after dropping his mask: the dead are much more obedient and reliable than the living.

Anyone else here might have taken it as Dar'Khan spewing threats, vowing to kill everyone present to shut them up.

But Jace had the feeling he meant something else — especially considering that in the future, Dar'Khan would ally with the death knight Arthas for the sake of necromancy.

Was he planning to kill Jace, raise him as a ghoul, and then interrogate him for clues about Ulthalesh? Was he secretly researching necromancy, just like Kel'Thuzad?

Jace scratched his head and loosened the tangle of thoughts slowly knotting together.

Surrounded by the walking dead and dark forces in a place like this, he found his thinking easily led astray — especially in his current weakened state. And no amount of forcing it would get him answers now.

That was when Morgan handed the Silvermoon Archmagister's Staff of the Torrent to Jace. It was silver throughout, inlaid with several mana gems of pure energy. The head was a cluster of sharp symbols woven into an arcane sigil, with an extremely powerful arcane gem embedded at the center.

This close, Jace could even feel its deep connection to the Sunwell.

Dar'Khan had been far from wielding its full potential — just as Jace knew he couldn't bring out Ulthalesh's full power either.

But that wasn't entirely a bad thing. Though he held this demon staff and sometimes borrowed some of its power to cast, he genuinely didn't want to know what it would look like to unleash its full strength.

He gazed out at the silent, rain-soaked forest. Who would have guessed what had just happened here.

Jace could feel that he'd probably be affected by this baffling encounter for a while to come. The few months of peaceful, idle living he'd finally gotten his hands on were going up in smoke along with it.

"Sorry, Jace," Morgan said quietly. "If it weren't for you helping me, they never would have found an opportunity to ambush you like this."

"It's fine," Jace said, eyeing the ranger sitting silently beside them, too frightened to speak. "These people had been watching me for a long time. Getting it over with on this occasion is actually a good thing."

Back in Raven Hill, the town guards were thoroughly shocked by this elven ranger brought back from the Great Graveyard. Jace walked the chief sergeant through the whole story from start to finish. The officer and the several lieutenants listening exchanged stunned glances. No one dared say a word.

A Silvermoon magister, carrying Quel'Thalas's treasure staff, traveling thousands of miles into Stormwind territory to assassinate King Varian's court mage?

Maela clearly understood the gravity of the situation, just squatting to one side without a word.

Whatever repercussions this might cause down the line, right now the priority was getting those bodies transported back.

Jace was fairly injured, so Morgan led a Stormwind priest and a squad of soldiers into the forest to retrieve the corpses.

Raven Hill didn't have a reliable jail, so the officers discussed whether, by the next morning, they should send the ranger and the elves' bodies on to Duskwood Town first.

Since the soldiers had a plan, Jace volunteered to join the escort with his griffon Gorjet and head to Duskwood Town too. For one thing, the conditions here really were too poor for recovery. For another, he wasn't comfortable leaving the soldiers to escort this elf. She was a Farstrider, after all. If she found a chance to escape, she wouldn't be easy to track down again.
