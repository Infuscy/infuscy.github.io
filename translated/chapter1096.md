## Chapter 1096: Business and Personal Matters

After several days resting in Duskwood Town, news had already been sent to Stormwind City via the town's gryphon courier.

That morning Jace opened the window. When he put force into his arms, he could still feel the lingering weakness from Dar'Khan's mad Life Drain.

He'd planned to recover for a day or two and then leave Duskwood Town to report back to the king in Stormwind. He hadn't expected the pain from the battle with Dar'Khan to refuse to heal quickly with time. The dark magic that shadow mage had cast even weakened the effects of healing — including healing potions, and even the regeneration of his own red dragon blood.

The severe arrow wounds Jace had compensated for with Life Drain had looked healed at the time, but possibly because the magic had been interrupted by the ranger, or because of the tearing effect of Dar'Khan's shadow magic, by the next day the arrow wounds had all worsened slightly.

Fortunately, Duskwood Town's apothecaries and priests had plenty of experience treating shadow damage. They gave him some long-acting potions specifically for repairing shadow corrosion and told him to come by every day. Since the Tranquil Garden Monastery had been overrun by the undead, the priests had all moved into town and set up a temporary clinic, so he didn't have to walk far to find them.

Between the visits, his condition came under decent control.

Right now the street outside was quite lively. Many town residents had come out, and Morgan Ladimore was down there too.

He'd been the one taking care of Jace these past two days. Though the guy didn't know any Holy Light healing techniques, he had first-rate experience looking after the wounded, and he picked up the care instructions from Duskwood's priests quickly enough.

When all was said and done, whether it was Holy Light healing, restoration through nature magic, or saving a life through potions, they were all just aids or emergency measures. In the end, nothing matched the body slowly healing itself — it just took longer.

At that moment, a series of gryphon cries came from the watchtower on the eastern ridge, as if they too had been stirred by the commotion in town.

Jace couldn't help glancing that way. These past few days he'd found that rather than worrying about his own injuries, he was more worried about starving Gorjet. He kept hoping Duskwood's gryphon keepers were taking good care of the big fellow.

Still, at least they'd made it to Duskwood Town. If they were still at Raven Hill, he probably wouldn't have slept a wink.

Morfile sat to one side, eyeing sideways the Staff of the Torrent leaning against the wall, its arcane gem wrapped in linen.

The magical gems on this staff never stopped gleaming. From a distance it even looked as if the whole thing was draped in flowing light. So if he didn't wrap it in a few layers of cloth, having it glowing in the room at night made it impossible to sleep.

Though this demon had been at Jace's side for years and knew how to mask her desires to avoid exposing herself in front of outsiders, she couldn't help herself in the face of a treasure like this. After all, she was a fel entity at heart, and arcane hunger ran in her nature.

"If it were me, I'd take this staff and flee straight into the Twisting Nether," Morfile said, sidling up to Jace.

Jace braced himself against the window frame. "To do that, I'd first need to learn how to slip into the Twisting Nether without breaking a sweat. Unlike you lot, if I cut myself, my soul just goes somewhere else."

"Can you feel this staff's connection to the Sunwell, sah-ard?" the imp said. "Might lose its power if you bring it into the Twisting Nether."

"At least its current appeal is more than enough," Morfile said with a smile.

The imp laughed a few times, then said, "Ha, I thought the battle a few days ago would've been enough to fill your appetite. See, Master? This sah-ard is never satisfied, no matter what you give her..."

"The release of battle impulses might ease my craving for mana, but the two are hard to substitute for each other." Morfile's gaze shifted slowly onto Jace. She narrowed her eyes. "Unless I get to have a proper fight with Vereesa Windrunner."

"Wasn't Veras enough of a challenge for you?" Jace asked.

Morfile's burning eyes shifted back and forth, then she said, "Veras Darkshadow was a worthy opponent — sharp, clever, knew how to seize an opening... If I had to name a weakness, it was that he listened to that mage too much. He never focused on me. His whole mind was on finding an opening on you. So even when he caught his opponent, it felt... incomplete."

"And when someone else catches you?" the imp asked with a grin. "Like at the bottom of that lighthouse?"

Morfile's face went cold. "You'll never see that happen twice."

The imp immediately said, "Now that you mention it, I'm genuinely a little eager to start my next life."

Knock, knock. Two raps at the door.

"Shh." Jace pressed a finger to his lips. Morfile, who'd been about to debate the imp, looked disappointed and slipped into the shadows. The imp dove straight into the bag.

He opened the door to find a palace guard from Stormwind Keep.

Of course, he wasn't wearing his guard armor and uniform — just a plain heavy coat. Still, the moment he saw Jace he saluted. "Master Sesso, His Majesty the king is here."

"What, he came to Duskwood Town?" Jace asked.

"Yes." The guard nodded.

"When?" Jace asked.

"Right now."

Jace was still a bit dazed when the king's voice answered from the hallway outside.

He strode quickly up to Jace. The guard saw him, lowered his head, and stepped aside.

Jace hadn't expected Varian to come all the way to Duskwood Town himself. The king gave Jace a quick once-over. "You look all right, Jace. But I heard from the priests' reports that you were badly injured, and that among those elves there were practitioners of dark magic..."

"I've recovered quite a bit. Thank you for your concern, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"This isn't the Keep. No need to be so stiff, Jace." Varian straightened a hand on his hip. "And I didn't come here in my capacity as king."

He glanced back at the guards following him outside, then added, "Though with this crowd trailing me, it's more than enough to make the townsfolk suspect some big shot has shown up."

After saying that, he looked Jace up and down again. "Are you sure you're all right? When I heard the Church's report, I thought you'd be half-conscious for weeks, like Katrana Prestor. Though she seems to have improved recently — I was planning to find a chance to check on her situation, but then this happened at Raven Hill."

"Making me fight another dragon right now is definitely out of the question," Jace said. "But I think there shouldn't be any lingering effects. It just needs time to heal properly."

Varian still looked concerned. He nodded. "I actually bear some blame for this too. I knew that Magister Dar'Khan Drathir had come to Stormwind, and I knew he was carrying that elven staff."

The king glanced toward the Staff of the Torrent in the corner and continued. "But because of the Onyxia affair, too many soldiers, mages, and priests had returned to Stormwind to help, or to handle security in the surrounding area, so the manpower for clearing the undead at Raven Hill had been short for a while. I figured it was a chance to strengthen alliances — having an ally volunteer to deal with the monsters in the graveyard. I didn't think much of it at the time. After all, Farstriders had been helping the kingdom search for stray orcs. You know there have always been hidden Horde elements lurking around Redridge. There are even ogres out here."

"Even if we'd figured out Dar'Khan's background, it would've been hard to tell what he came here to do, Your Majesty," Jace said, breathing deep.

"So why?" Varian asked. "No one told me why that high elf wanted to assassinate you. Does it have something to do with Quel'Thalas? Could it be they don't want a second human-Windrunner union?"

"It's not that," Jace said. "It's about an ancient evil artifact — a demonic weapon said to be forged by the leader of the Burning Legion. Not long ago, I was commissioned by Quel'Thalas's Reliquary to track down a demon in Kalimdor. I uncovered clues about this artifact and passed them along to the Reliquary. I'm guessing Dar'Khan must have searched high and low based on those clues with nothing to show for it, then decided to find his breakthrough through me."

"Then there's no reason to go as far as trying to kill you, is there?" Varian said.

"He probably figured I knew something but refused to tell him," Jace said.

Varian sighed softly. "Another one who threw his life away for the sake of power."

He sat in a chair to the side and thought for a moment. "I didn't have his background thoroughly investigated when he first came to Stormwind. Only now do I know this Magister Dar'Khan Drathir is a member of the Silvermoon Council and a strong contender for leadership of the Magisters' Platform. It's all too easy to suspect he was acting on Silvermoon's secret orders."

"Now that I've heard what you've said, I can put my mind at ease, Jace. And the most important thing is that you're still alive. Think about it — if you hadn't beaten them, they could have used that evil demon artifact to slander your cause of death however they liked, fabricate crimes against you, and because of the dark magic, hardly anyone would have the ability to defend you. And because of the business with Priest Ladimore, the Church might even have been implicated."

"Do a lot of people know about this already?" Jace asked.

"No." Varian shook his head. "But of course I have to notify Quel'Thalas. Archmagister Belor'al already knows. He should arrive in Duskwood Town within the next few days. I don't know much about elven teleportation magic — it might take some preparation from the magisters — but it won't be long."

Jace sat on the other side of the table. "What's their attitude?"

Varian pulled a Quel'Thalas envelope from inside his coat and handed it to Jace. "There's not much worth reading, but at least they apologized to you and to us. They seem quite shocked — they genuinely had no idea what happened."

Jace glanced at the letter. It was all polite official language, nothing of substance. What surprised him a little, though, was that Duskwood Town had been chosen as the venue for discussing the matter — and that had been Quel'Thalas's suggestion.

"They don't want this to escalate," Jace said.

Varian nodded. "Yes. With the queen's situation, Stormwind happens to have too many people here right now. They don't want the whole Alliance finding out what happened at Raven Hill all at once."

The king drummed his fingers lightly on the table, as if weighing his next words.

"I won't pretend about my reason for coming here, Jace." he said. "That Archmagister is definitely here to get his staff back. And you can't let him take it too easily."

Jace actually had a few ideas in mind. He planned to tell Archmagister Belor'al about Dar'Khan's suspected necromancy research and his private quest to track down an evil staff hidden by the Tirisfal Guardians. Quel'Thalas would then tear Dar'Khan's tower and research sites inside out, and Jace would have reason to get involved and see if there was anything useful he could pick up along the way.

"What are your thoughts?" Jace asked.

"I don't want you to think I'm using your misfortune to profit for myself," Varian said. "But if you could get that Archmagister to make some commitments regarding Alliance relations on behalf of Quel'Thalas... nothing extra. Just a commitment to stand together when facing the Horde. After all, a magister assassinated an ally's court mage advisor under the pretense of helping the Alliance. They should make corresponding compensation."

"You mean getting Quel'Thalas to promise to help us attack Blackrock Spire," Jace said.

"More or less," Varian said. "But if you put it that bluntly, the Archmagister will hide behind the fact that the Sun King isn't here and no commitments can be made."

"I understand," Jace nodded.

"All right. Setting aside all the things that need to be said, there's one more important thing, Jace." Varian cleared his throat and leaned in. "How exactly did you take on four by yourself? And all of them Quel'Thalas elites. And I heard Morgan Ladimore's grandfather was a war hero, but he doesn't seem like..."

Jace rubbed his nose and lowered his voice. "In that situation, I had no choice but to summon demons to help."

"That does explain something." Varian nodded. "Walk me through the whole thing — how you took down those mages, the assassin, and the ranger one by one... try to be detailed, Jace. I've been looking forward to this the whole trip."
