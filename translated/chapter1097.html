Chapter 1097: As a Warrior

Seeing the king in such high spirits, Jace gave Varian a rough account of what had happened that rainy night — two mages, a ranger, and a killer lurking in the shadows.

When he got to the part where one of the mages polymorphed Morgan into a bizarre magical rabbit, Varian asked with curiosity, "So jackalopes are real? A rabbit with antlers and wings? I thought it was just something made up by a bunch of drunks."

"Hard to say," Jace said. "After all, that jackalope was just a construct of arcane magic, Your Majesty."

Next Jace described how he'd had no choice but to summon a felhound the Dark Iron dwarf's way to help deal with the mages, followed by the ranger — who was currently locked in the town's dungeon — seizing every chance to shoot at him from among the crypts and headstones.

Listening along, Varian seemed to catch something off again and asked, "What about Morgan? He was right there the whole time, wasn't he? It sounds like he never did anything."

"You forget — he was that rabbit the whole time," Jace said.

"So you're telling me you basically took on four of them by yourself for most of it?!" Varian asked in surprise.

"Don't forget the felhound." Jace glanced toward the shadow beside the curtain where the demon had been lurking. The emotion radiating from that shadow had turned quite unhappy, so he added quickly, "Ah, and of course my succubus companion..."

That shadow tilted its head in satisfaction. Jace went on. "In the end Morgan stepped up and landed the crucial blow when I was about to be tortured to death by that mage. Otherwise I have no idea how it would have turned out."

Varian nodded. "A succubus — is she the same demon lady who helped me suppress the black dragon magic at the palace?"

"Yes," Jace said. "But she had to deal with Veras Darkshadow — that killer hiding in the shadows I mentioned to you earlier. The felhound helped me defeat one of the mages, the other dead one, Ressathia. But Dar'Khan killed it soon after, forcing me to face him alone."

"Many Quel'Thalas arcanists are excellent swordsmen too," Varian said. "If it were those old men from the Wizard Sanctum, they'd have a hard time dealing with a felhound. I know how dangerous those demons are. But I'm really most interested in how you beat that elven mage. I remember when I was a kid, I actually tried to prove I had some arcane talent. But clearly I don't — at least nowhere near as much as the admiral's daughter Proudmoore."

Jace knew very well that if the felhound hadn't bought him some time, and if Morfile hadn't launched a surprise attack later to disrupt Dar'Khan's rhythm as he peppered Jace with Arcane Missiles, he'd have been in serious trouble.

Just like Dar'Khan had shouted in his final moments — without any demons protecting him, going toe-to-toe in a straight duel really would have been very hard to win.

"Mages love to play reactive when dealing with other casters," Jace said. "Because once you've countered the other person's spell, you've cut off their bridge to mana, putting them at a disadvantage. In the end, though I managed to bait out one of Dar'Khan's Counterspells, I still nearly got overpowered by him channeling that staff's force."

"You don't counter magic?" Varian asked.

Jace gave an awkward shrug. "I can. But I wouldn't try using a Counterspell incantation I'm not very familiar with against an archmage like Dar'Khan. I studied it at the Wizard Sanctum, but my arcane talent is probably not much better than yours."

"If someone like you struggles so much against a single mage, then for warriors who've never studied magic, it must be even harder," Varian said, leaning back in his chair. "They don't even need to think about countering anything. Just use all sorts of strange spells to pin your limbs, then casually conjure a fireball the size of a gnome bomb and blow you to pieces."

"The problem is in the casting," Jace said. "Every spell requires an incantation, hand gestures to channel mana, and casting material — or the caster's focus — all working together. Every spell is essentially a ritual, it's just that some spells require a very short ritual. Counterspell, for example. But no matter what, a ritual can be disrupted in all sorts of ways — silencing the target's voice so they can't speak the incantation, or disrupting their movements and rhythm with a surprise attack, like my succubus companion did to Dar'Khan... Once disrupted, mages fall into a relatively passive position."

He went on. "Maybe you need to prepare a hidden weapon — throwing knives, or... an axe. Throw it at those mages as quickly as possible before they can cast at you. Cause as much disruption as you can, and use that opening to close in... That's why races that regularly deal with casters — dwarves, for instance, or trolls — are all skilled at throwing axes or war hammers. I think they figured this out through long experience fighting casters."

"Or carry two weapons," Varian said, slapping the table lightly and swinging both fists. "One mainly for blocking, one for attacking. When you run into a mage, you can use it for throwing, like you said."

Jace fell silent for a moment after hearing that, wondering to himself whether the reason Varian would eventually obtain a powerful weapon that could split into two war swords was just... destiny.

"A better choice is to prepare in advance," he added. "If I knew I was about to deal with my teacher Marin, I'd definitely drink a few Fire Protection Potions first."

"And then get blown into the sky by Arcane Missiles."

After Varian said it, the two of them burst out laughing.

After a while, Varian sighed. "I know that Lord Edras Blackmoore of Durnholde has built an arena in the castle King Terenas granted him. Have you been there?"

"Once," Jace said.

"Pity," Varian said. "I've always wanted to go see it — even really go in and have a proper fight with those orcs. But of course, that's all just fantasy. As the leader of this country, or as Tiffin's husband, I can't be that irresponsible. If my father were still alive, I might actually go try it. At least I don't believe any random orc could beat me as I am now."

"Tell me, Jace," he suddenly lowered his voice. "If that orc, that female assassin... showed up in front of me right now, could she still take my life as easily as she killed him?"

"Not that easily," Jace said. "You're already one of the fiercest warriors in Stormwind, no question about it. And besides, I'm right next to you, Your Majesty. She'd have an even harder time getting the drop on you."

"Call me Varian, Jace," Varian said impatiently. "I don't want someone constantly reminding me I'm a king when I'm seriously thinking about how to fight an assassin."
