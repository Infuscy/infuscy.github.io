## Chapter 110: What Everyone Has in Common

Ursula rolled her eyes at him, but finally nodded. "Fair enough. That scale was perfect, Jace — even better than I expected. Fine, I'll let you off the hook this time, you brat."

"What did you want to use that scale for, anyway?" Jace whispered.

"Secret." Ursula tilted her head. "You're not honest with me, so don't expect other people to tell you everything."

Jace could only purse his lips in frustration and look back toward Gakin, illuminated by the firelight in the center.

Gakin was chanting the Curse of Weakness incantation, then beginning to explain the casting gestures — all stuff Jace had written down for him, so he wasn't really listening.

By now the people in the room could already smell the aroma of roasting meat drifting up from the tavern kitchen above.

If this was the last incantation, then once they were done it'd be time to eat. Jace had been waiting for this moment from the very beginning.

But Gakin suddenly said, "Our little group has been around for a while now. Zardeth and I would like to give ourselves a name, to strengthen our bonds and carry on the spirit of sharing and mutual aid. What do you all think?"

Erlan shouted, "Agreed!" Ursula and Surena nodded. Jace naturally had no reason to object.

"Wonderful, everyone's in favor," Gakin said. "If any of you have a suitable name in mind, speak up and we'll see what everyone thinks."

Surena said, "Black Rose. Don't you think that sounds cool?"

"Sounds like a pirate crew," Ursula said. "And isn't it a bit too flashy?"

"There is something to that," Gakin said.

"Dark Brotherhood!" Erlan declared. "We are born in darkness, and we grow in darkness!"

Hearing that name, Jace shuddered. That name was way too on the nose — this was Azeroth, not Tamriel!

Ursula said, "Sounds like we're up to no good. We're not some criminal gang plotting arson and murder down here. No need to make ourselves sound so evil."

"That won't work, that won't work — then you come up with one, Lady D!" Erlan said, clearly annoyed.

Ursula hemmed and hawed for a moment. She didn't seem to have any good ideas either.

"I have a suggestion," Zardeth said. "Let's call ourselves the Dagger Society. Each of us, when we joined, used a dagger to slaughter a lamb as part of the sacrifice ritual. I believe the dagger is a symbol that binds us together — what we have in common."

Gakin said, "Low-key enough. But won't people take us for an assassin's guild?"

Zardeth said, "Compared to Dark Brotherhood and Black Rose, those sound way more like an assassin's guild, don't they? The dagger has been an important ceremonial object since ancient times."

"That name's boring as hell," Ursula said.

"Stop running your mouth, Ursula Deline!" Erlan said. "It's the Dagger Society. Does a name really need to be this complicated?"

"What about Surena and Jace? What do you think?" Gakin asked.

"I've got no problem with it," Jace said. "Dagger Society it is."

"Since Zardeth put it that way, I agree too," Surena said.

In truth, Zardeth proposing the name "Dagger Society" had surprised Jace, but it wasn't entirely unexpected.

In the game, twenty-odd years from now, the warlocks of Azeroth would form an organization called the Council of the Black Harvest. And the predecessor of that Council of the Black Harvest was a group of six powerful warlocks who called themselves the "Six Daggers."

Maybe Gakin's Dagger Society was the earliest incarnation of the Six Daggers?

Jace had always assumed the founder of the Six Daggers was the original leader of the Council of the Black Harvest, Kanrethad Ebonlocke. Compared to this bunch of warlocks, Kanrethad was far more well-known to most non-human warlock players.

Only now, in the real Azeroth, Jace had never heard of this person. But he did know that Ebonlocke was a noble surname from Grand Hamlet — which meant Kanrethad was probably in Grand Hamlet right now, the future Darkshire.

Was he there even now, secretly studying dark magic on his own?

"Right then — I now declare the Dagger Society's first feast officially underway!"

The moment Gakin finished speaking, Surena let out a sigh. "Thank the Light, I can finally eat something. My stomach's been growling."

"Thank the Light?!"

"Tch!"

The men all burst out laughing. The basement, which had still been a bit serious, suddenly relaxed quite a bit.

Jace slowly stretched to his feet, packed up his book, and waited for Jarel to bring the food down.

He'd gone the whole day without eating for the sake of this free meal. He'd already gotten past the hunger, but now that someone had mentioned the feast, his stomach started growling again.

"Do you like it here, Jace?" Ursula's voice drifted close to his ear.

"Of course," Jace said. "It's pretty relaxing."

"Not because of Surena?" she asked.

"Surena?" Jace asked, puzzled. "Why? I barely know her."

"You don't know? Really? Every one of those men's slept with her," Ursula whispered. "Gakin, Erlan, even Jarel — that bartender who claims he has nothing to do with the warlock circle has been with her. Zardeth... I don't know about him, but he's definitely not as upright as he looks. So what about you?"

Jace wasn't surprised in the slightest. Surena was exactly the type. But he just shook his head and said, "Maybe we're just not that close yet."

"Be careful, Jace," Ursula said. "The moment anything gets tangled up with that, it gets a thousand times more complicated. I think you're a decent kid, so I don't want to see you dragged into the mud either. When those scumbags eventually make a mess of everything, as long as you haven't gotten involved, you can keep your hands clean. You understand what I'm saying?"

Hearing the word "decent," Jace suddenly thought of that sheep he'd drained until it looked like a stray dog. He wondered how it was doing now — whether it had been eaten by wolves, caught by gnolls, or was still wandering the forest.

"Surena's not my type."

Ursula pressed: "Don't tell me you like men?"

"No," Jace said. "Surena's just not my type, plain and simple."

Ursula whispered, "Don't say 'never.' When the moment actually comes, I hope you remember your own words — don't just go and let your body do the deciding for you."

Jace glanced at Gakin and Erlan, then at Surena.

"If she were fairer-skinned, maybe. Right now if the lights were any dimmer, you wouldn't even be able to make her out."

He didn't say anything else, just smacked his lips and shook his head with a self-deprecating laugh. Ursula chuckled and sat back down.
