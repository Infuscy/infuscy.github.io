## Chapter 1101: Bound to Be Released (Part 1)

That evening, Jace returned to the inn room. Though Vereesa had spent the meal clamoring about letting this succubus share his misfortune, in the end she'd expressed that she could find a way to temporarily disable the soul link.

Though her spoken excuse was that the soul link would make a fight unfair, Jace knew she actually just didn't want him getting hurt along with her.

Honestly, Jace wasn't too sure how the soul link could be disabled either. He'd just agreed to give it a try — after all, even in the game you couldn't just dispel or cancel a link like that on a whim.

"Your wish came true, Morfile," Jace said. "Vereesa wants to fight you."

"I'm not the least bit surprised," Morfile asked, leaning in. "The real question is you, darling — are you willing?"

"Fight, fight," Jace said. "If you die, I'll find a way to summon you back." Morfile gave a provocative smile. "So certain it'll be me dying?"

"What do you think?" Jace stared back at her.

"All right, all right. Why would I deliberately upset you? There's no point. My life's worthless anyway..."

Though her words were laced with passive aggression, Morfile couldn't hide her excitement in the slightest.

She sidled up to Jace. "We need to suppress the soul link for now, or this fight won't be interesting at all."

"Did you two scheme something behind my back?" Jace asked. "Can the soul link actually be suppressed?"

"It can, but..." Morfile said. "It requires your blood to cast."

"My blood, my blood. Everything needs my blood. Damn it."

Jace held out his arm in resignation. "Fine. Go ahead." The succubus handed the dagger to Jace. "Do it yourself."

Jace gave her a look of disgust but could only take the dagger.

The weather that night was quite good. The overcast gloom that had hung around so long had thinned out a good bit. Even Duskwood Town, shrouded in Karazhan's dark energy, could catch a sliver of moonlight.

If it were daytime, you might even catch a rare glimmer of sunlight in the depths of Duskwood.

Jace looked up at that faint ray of light, the bundled staff in his arms, but he was hardly in the mood to appreciate the scenery.

Morfile was leaning against a nearby tree, spinning a blade in her hand. Vereesa stood on the other side, carefully adjusting her belt and clothes, making sure everything was properly fastened and tied down.

She was dead serious. From this distance, Jace couldn't sense even a trace of impulsive emotion leaking from her.

If yesterday in the tavern he'd been able to taste a fighting desire tinged with anger, a trace of jealousy, and a hint of regret and guilt — now there was only calm.

Morfile was the same. She didn't even glance Jace's way — either looking at the knife in her hand or gazing at the elf across from her.

"I'm ready." Vereesa straightened her chest and took a deep breath. "I think we can begin."

Sazeno hopped up to the front. "I'd be happy to act as referee for you two!"

Morfile's eyes followed the little imp's movements, then lifted to lock onto the opponent across from her. "Did you forget to bring your bow?"

"If I used a bow, this match would be mind-numbingly boring," Vereesa said.

"Surprising," Morfile said with a smile.

"All right." Vereesa set Dragon's Call, shimmering with emerald arcane light, to one side. She drew two golden Quel'Thalas war blades and walked to the middle of the grass. "I can't wait any more."

Clearly, though Dragon's Call was a powerful sword, enchanted with terrifying green dragon venom, she hadn't held it long. Though she kept it at her side, she certainly hadn't had much practice with it.

To take on a formidable opponent, she'd rather use the weapon she was most familiar with.

"Then for fairness' sake, I won't try to turn invisible either," Morfile said, raising her head. "We'll fight with dual blades, purely on martial skill — like the O'dachi."

"O'dachi?" Vereesa frowned.

"It's..." Morfile thought for a moment, looked at Sazeno, and said, "You explain."

"What's the point of explaining what the O'dachi are now? They're all dead!" The imp raised his burning hands. "I think we can start!"

"Let me stress this again," Jace said. "Pull your punches, you two. No fatalities."

"Relax. I won't make you weep in front of an exquisite golden shrine, Master," Morfile said with a smile.

"No need to worry indeed," Vereesa said, staring at the succubus. "I kill her, and then you waste the summoning materials for a doomlord to call her back? Not a chance."

Jace pressed his lips together in a helpless expression.

But deep down, he was a little curious about which of these two killers was actually stronger.

He had no doubt the succubus held a massive advantage with her invisibility. And if Vereesa had a bow in hand, Morfile might not even have time to fully vanish into shadow before being shot dead.

So what about now — pure martial skill, like Morfile said?

She'd once been a fearsome demon lieutenant, a succubus who could go toe-to-toe with Brutallus himself. But dying and returning again and again had severely eroded her strength.

Vereesa, on the other hand, had received rigorous Windrunner family training since childhood. Since joining the Farstriders, she'd always been one of the most combat-skilled in the organization — even the Farstrider instructors, and the instructors' instructors, were reluctant to spar with her.

"How about it, how about it, Master?! I can't wait any more."

Sazeno turned to look at Jace. Jace leaned against a nearby tree. "You call it."

"Then — begin!"

The imp shrieked with excitement.

Sazeno bounded over to Jace's feet, going wild with "oohs" and "aahs." But neither Vereesa nor Morfile moved.

Cold wind swept through the depths of Duskwood. The black grass rippled like waves on a night sea, a thick, indistinct blur.

Morfile, true to her promise, did not vanish into shadow. Otherwise, drawing on the shadow power of this dreadful forest, she could have concealed herself to the point where even Jace's demon detection couldn't make her out clearly.

Not only did she not turn invisible — she wreathed herself in a coat of flame. The fire pouring from her eyes streamed upward along her reddish forehead, intertwining with her dark hair.

Fel markings on the succubus's body released their glow. Both her hooves gradually reddened like heated metal. Yet the grass she trod on didn't curl from the burning — it grew even more lush...

Combined with Morfile's demonic body and visage, which ran entirely counter to the forces of life, the scene felt increasingly uncanny.

But Jace knew the grass leaves burning more vigorously under Morfile's presence weren't healthy. Beneath the lushness lay a manic, frenzied vitality — life force severely corrupted by Shadow.

Soon the two black blades in Morfile's hands caught fire as well. She pointed the Endless Darkness blade before her — a short sword that drained mana, carrying considerable deterrent power against a high elf.

Still, Vereesa didn't complain about the unfairness of her opponent wielding such a bizarre, corrupted weapon. After all, as long as it couldn't hurt her, it was hard to trigger the arcane power within her.

What's more, her own blades were also fine Quel'Thalas weapons — gifts from Sylvanas.

Seeming to detect an opening in the succubus's stance, Vereesa suddenly stepped in and swung her blade in a spinning arc. The demon easily withdrew a step to evade the sweep, then blocked the follow-up strike as if she'd foreseen the elf's attack.

The moment the fight opened, the two wove through each other with shifting steps and flickering shadows, clashing blades ringing out in a rapid chain of clear, bright clinks. Jace realized that given how little he knew about swordsmanship, within a few exchanges he'd already lost the thread.

"Pretty! Pretty!" the imp cried joyfully.

Still, knowing both of their usual fighting styles, Jace still detected something different within the shifting rhythm.
