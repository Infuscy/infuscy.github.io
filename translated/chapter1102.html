Chapter 1102: Bound to Be Unleashed (Part 2)

Whenever Vereesa had been forced into close quarters before — whether against the demon guards at the Dark Portal or the trolls of Atal'ai — once she charged into the enemy ranks she'd been a whirlwind of blades, savage and relentless.

But against Morophil, that whirlwind seemed to hit some kind of interference, howling but struggling to build momentum.

The demon's face kept its smile, as though she already believed the fight was hers. Vereesa's expression, meanwhile, had taken on a flicker of anger — as if she took offense at her opponent's insufferably smug look.

A few clashes of steel, and Morophil backed off several steps to weather a furious string of attacks. Just when Jace thought the ranger had been driven to the last blow she could still recover from, she suddenly steadied herself with a strength and agility he could scarcely believe — and launched a kick!

Morophil was clearly caught off guard by the follow-up. She threw up her arm to block, but the kick sent her flying back several steps. The ranger pressed the advantage, stabbing furiously with her blade — only to watch Morophil vanish into a swirl of shadow right before her eyes.

"I thought hiding wasn't allowed!" the imp grouched.

That wasn't hiding. Jace watched the demon's shadow whip past the ranger like a gale. Before the imp had even finished his complaint, the demon had already re-formed from a wreath of burning shadow behind the ranger.

A blade punched through the dark mist, stabbing straight for the ranger's neck — but before Jace's heart could even lurch into his throat, Vereesa had already whipped her sword around, a devastating backhand slash that deflected the demon's dagger aside.

That was no counter a normal body should have been capable of. Morophil was thrown slightly off balance by the blow, and Vereesa pressed again —

A head-on, no-guards clash. A shriek of tortured metal, and the two women separated, each backing off a small distance.

The demon's smile hadn't faded, but it had taken on a dry edge. The elf stared at her troublesome opponent, breathing heavily, and after a few steps she'd snapped back to her usual poise.

Jace realized his mouth had fallen open. The imp beside him had stopped talking too.

He'd trained with Grede. He was better with a blade than most people — better than quite a few soldiers, even. But no matter how he tried to imagine it, he couldn't picture how he'd cope if either of these two she-demons cornered him.

Even if Ursulus's scythe was sharp enough to shear through Onyxia's scales and flesh, it meant nothing if he never got the chance to swing it.

From the exchange he'd just witnessed, he figured he'd have several holes punched through him before he even got the scythe off his back.

In truth, Jace could see it clearly enough: despite Morophil's red dragonfire, tainted by demonic power, her raw physical strength hadn't actually bested Vereesa. It was just that in this particular clash, a ranger who cut through other opponents like an autumn wind through fallen leaves had been nearly outmatched in technique and speed by this demon killer.

Before Jace could think it through any further, Morophil suddenly turned into a black demonic blur and charged. Vereesa's taut nerves made her meet the charge on instinct — but the moment she swung her shortsword, she noticed the shadowy image starting to disperse. It was a feint!

But Jace, through his fel senses, felt the emotion erupting from her — not fury at being toyed with, but a sudden surge of excitement. It was as though Morophil hadn't tricked her so much as handed her an exquisitely wrapped gift.

Through his demonic detection, he watched Morophil's true body arc in a short curve around to one side and strike from the flank. Her Dagger of Endless Darkness drove toward Vereesa's neck — a blow that made Jace want to call a halt to the fight right then and there...

But he couldn't interfere. And Vereesa, as one of the dueling parties, was clearly far sharper than he'd expected.

She didn't try to block the thrust. Instead, she suddenly clenched her fist. Jace's thoughts were snapped short by a hollow, ringing boom — a shockwave of arcane force rippled outward from the elf, slamming into Morophil, who had already closed to point-blank range, and blasting her out of the shadows. She stumbled several more steps and nearly crashed to the ground.

The force of it reached all the way to where Jace stood, shoving him back a step. A harsh tinnitus followed, and every other sound in the space seemed to be smothered.

Arcane Torrent... but far more powerful than the one Mara had hit him with. The demon's body was wreathed in flame, and that single burst of magic had sent it scattering and snuffed out.

As sound quickly began to echo again, the succubus's cry just now seemed to drift in from a distance.

The arcane energy cleared. The elf had already turned, pointing at her still-unsteady opponent. Her lips moved, and shards of ice gathered in the grass, freezing the demon's hooves solid.

Same teacher, same lessons — but Morophil didn't get trapped the way Grede would have. She wrenched her hoof free, shattering the ice. But Vereesa had already accomplished her goal, and at the same time she'd already hurled a shortsword...

The thrown blade spun straight toward its target. The demon's eyes went wide, and she barely managed a step to the side — but she'd already lost her balance.

The ranger closed the distance at terrifying speed, swinging her blade in a stabbing arc, and the tip even blazed with a flare of arcane light!

Morophil barely managed to parry the blow, but the arcense force sent her reeling back at an angle — and the arm she'd blocked with was already slick with demonic blood.

Jace clenched his left hand and felt a wave of phantom pain crash over him. The Soul Link had been temporarily suppressed, but the pain didn't seem to have eased much.

But he sensed no defeat in Morophil's heart. On the contrary — though the demon had been battered ragged by that string of blows, her heart had ignited with an unprecedented fervor, a wild excitement.

She laughed, then tossed the Dagger of Endless Darkness at the ranger in a casual throw, which Vereesa swatted aside without effort. More than an attack, it felt like a deliberate gesture — keeping herself on equal footing by hanging onto one weapon.

At this point, Jace couldn't see even a trace of a succubus's coyness, her gentleness, her veiled wiles, her deceptions. She had become something else entirely — an assassin? No, not quite an assassin either. More like a fanatic of the Burning Legion. A gladiator standing in the sand with a shortsword in hand.

"The fight isn't over yet," the demon said low.

The ranger stared at her opponent and licked her lips. Her silver hair was soaked with sweat. Across from her, the demon's tattoos were glowing bright again. Before Morophil could recover from that Arcane Torrent, Vereesa had no intention of giving her a chance to breathe. She attacked again.

She drove her shortsword forward in a relentless series of thrusts. Jace noticed that whenever Morophil blocked or looked for an opening, whenever she gave ground or shifted position, the bone spines on her back swayed almost imperceptibly.

Jace rarely saw her try to flick wings she no longer had, even in the heat of battle — and even then, not often.

Not only that, but Morophil's fighting style had shifted noticeably as well. From the sharp, evasive, counter-punch style of an assassin at the start, she'd become aggressively direct.

She wasn't much taller than Vereesa, but her presence completely overwhelmed the ranger's — even her strength was greater. The arcane power coursing through her burned hotter and hotter, and she even began ignoring some of Vereesa's strikes, especially the ones that wouldn't pierce her demonic heart.

So... was this what she'd always been like?

Vereesa carved deep wounds into her from time to time, but Morophil roared and battered her back into disadvantage, driving her back several steps in a row as she tried to weather the storm and find her rhythm again.

But Morophil gave her no chance at all. She hurled her only black blade — her arm glowing so bright it shone translucent red!

The ranger had just gotten her footing back and barely deflected the throw. The black shortsword ricocheted away — only to be swallowed by an even darker shadow!

Morophil caught the blade as she burst from that shadow, her black hooves slamming into a thick tree trunk as she leaped into the air, bringing the sword crashing down onto Vereesa!

It all happened in the blink of an eye. Jace hadn't even been able to track the demon's shadow.

The ranger's reaction had been fast enough — she raised her blade to block, but the impact drove a grunt out of her and sent her crashing to the ground. Her sword flew from her hand and stuck in the dirt a short distance away.

She watched the demon approach step by step. Vereesa sat up, tilting her chin slightly — almost as if she'd already accepted the execution that was coming.

"I yield," she said with a cough.

Jace frowned. He was about to speak when Morophil tossed her sword aside and extended a hand.

Vereesa stared blankly at the demon in front of her. After a long moment, she nodded and took Morophil's hand, pulling herself to her feet. She swallowed hard, then asked quietly, "You defeated Velethus. How do you think I compare to him?"

"In terms of shortsword technique, you're not inferior in the slightest," Morophil said. "And he liked to rely on shadow magic, whereas you were reluctant to use magic against me until the very last moment. I could tell."

Vereesa nodded in silence. The succubus turned to Jace and said, "Considering that in the graveyard I was threatened by the mage and your lieutenant, and Velethus's attention was on him... that fight wasn't nearly as satisfying as this one."

"You might not think so, but I had a wonderful time, Vereesa Windrunner. I... it's been a long time since I've enjoyed a fight this much. We should do this more often — have bouts that have nothing to do with my master at all. Of course, preferably without bows..."

"This fight had nothing to do with him either," Vereesa stressed. But she still nodded. "But you're right, I... I feel like there's a great deal I could learn from you. I mean that sincerely."

"What, Lady Windrunner, are you planning to learn demonic incantations?" the imp said with a grin.

"You can shut up," Jace said in a low voice.

More than Vereesa's voluntary surrender, what surprised him was Morophil's state at this moment. She'd become somewhat unfamiliar — but not entirely unfamiliar.

He'd seen Morophil like this before: the triumph when she'd emerged from the shadows to seize Velethus, and in that canyon at the Storm Altar, when she'd received the enhancement and raged at him — for one fleeting instant, she'd looked exactly like this.

At the end of the day, she wasn't a shapeshifter, wasn't a plaything, wasn't a spy. She was a killer utterly devoted to combat.

In any case, it looked like this bout hadn't deepened the conflict between the two of them — if anything, it had completely released the long-simmering resentment and mutual disdain that had built up between them.

Jace let out a small breath of relief. He'd half-expected things to go in a much worse direction.
