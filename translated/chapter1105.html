Chapter 1105: Stormwind Bank

The Royal Bank of Stormwind was an extremely familiar place in the game, but to Jace it was still quite foreign.

Though the street leading to the bank sat just south of the Trade District plaza and had been completed the second year after Jace arrived in Stormwind, several years had passed and he still hadn't actually set foot inside.

Past the Gilded Rose, around the fountain, all the way to the bank's entrance — the Wrynn lion banner and the Lordaeron Alliance white flag fluttered before the coin symbol on the bank's sign.

It was evening, and the bank was still fairly busy. The hall looked brand-new and spacious, almost giving one the illusion of stepping into a modern bank with a retro-style interior.

Though this hall was in Stormwind, in both overall style and structure it looked as if it had been designed by dwarves — it just wasn't immediately obvious because it used gray-white marble rather than dark stone or the mountain stone of Dun Morogh.

Jace clutched the ring in his hand and looked around as he walked in, when a greeting suddenly came from the side. "Master Sesso."

The speaker was a rather ordinary-looking man. Jace stared at him for a moment before recognizing him — it was Neden Hutt, the SI:7 spy who'd been captured by orcs and whom Jace had found a way to release.

He'd just put on a bit of weight these past few months apart. "Neden..."

"It's me." Neden gave a slight bow. "Many thanks for the rescue back then, Master. If I hadn't run into you by luck, once I'd been thrown into Blackrock Spire, who knows what fate would've awaited me."

"The arena," Jace said. "The master of Blackrock Spire's favorite."

"The arena... I didn't understand much orcish either, but I could tell from the excitement in those scoundrels' eyes." Neden said with some tension, still looking a bit shaken.

"Right." Jace frowned. "How'd you know it was me?"

"You and that mage from the Wizard Sanctum borrowed two orcish warg saddles from Stonewatch, and then..." Neden smiled. "There aren't many wingless succubi in this world, Master. And the odds of an orc warlock willing to let a human spy escape, while also traveling with a rather kind-hearted wingless succubus, are even slimmer."

"Kind-hearted." Jace couldn't help a short laugh. "That's the first time I've heard anyone describe Morophil that way. You're looking well. How's the recovery been these past months?"

"Better than expected, Master." Neden nodded. "After coming back from the orc side, Mr. Shaw didn't assign me to any more dangerous operations, so I've been working at headquarters. Oh, right — there's something I have to inform you about."

"What is it?" Jace asked.

Neden said, "Dazarum suddenly vanished a while ago. The agents guarding the cell had no idea what happened. After Mr. Shaw communicated with Lady Sulenna of the Dagger Society, he's been searching for the past few days, but with nothing to show for it."

Jace caught on at once. "I was going to bring this up with you too. Dazarum was 'requisitioned' by me on a temporary basis. He's dead — you don't need to worry about him anymore."

Neden blinked. Jace explained, "It was because of the great graveyard incident."

"So that's how it was." The agent nodded. "Mr. Shaw actually had that suspicion. I'll report it to him when I get back."

"Good," Jace said. "Thanks for letting me know. I was planning to head over to your side to confirm it anyway."

"Uh, confirm?" Neden asked.

"I mean, confirm whether you still needed another hellhound," Jace explained quickly.

"There are no more signs of dragon-kin causing trouble in Stormwind, Master," Neden said. "What we need to watch out for now are just a few rogue warlocks who still won't give up after the Lighthouse incident. The Dagger Society's cooperation with us on that front has been very smooth and efficient. We won't need to trouble you further."

"Sounds good," Jace said. Of course he wasn't about to admit that he himself couldn't tell these hellhounds apart. After bidding Neden farewell, he went into the hall, where a well-dressed gnome spotted him and scurried over in quick little steps. "Mage sir, is there anything I can help you with?"

Jace glanced at the service window not far off, which was surrounded by several people, and said, "I'm here to withdraw money. Should I go to that window?"

"The Wizard Sanctum has a partnership with us, sir, so every formally recognized mage is a VIP client. We can handle things specially — just follow me," the gnome said.

"Fine," Jace said. "But strictly speaking, I'm not with the Wizard Sanctum, and I'm not a formally recognized mage."

"Uh." The gnome hesitated. "No problem. Just show me the withdrawal token, sir."

Jace handed him the green-eyed stone ring. The gnome put on a purple enchanted monocle and studied it for a while, then narrowed his eyes, looked up, and asked in a hushed voice, "You're Jace Sesso?"

"Yes. Does this ring have my name on it?" Jace asked as he handed over the court mage insignia. The glanced at it and immediately handed it back. "Damn it — no, I mean... apologies, Master Sesso. I'll take you to get your things right away. Follow me."

The gnome led Jace through a door that looked like it belonged in a prison, into a brightly lit underground passage.

He remembered that in the game, the vault door of Stormwind Bank seemed to be right off to the side. He hadn't expected there to be a complex underground treasury here too.

The gnome had a word with a tall man inside who was presumably the head of security. Jace could tell from his accent that he was from the north.

As they walked deeper, passing one heavy door after another in the corridor, the gnome glanced back and said, "You rarely do business at Stormwind Bank, Master Sesso."

"This is my first time," Jace admitted.

"A mage of your stature should be partnering with us more," the gnome smiled. "Whether it's precious materials, treasures..."

"...you can entrust them all to the Royal Bank of Stormwind with confidence. By the Light, there hasn't been a single theft in this vault since its reconstruction — thanks to our sufficiently professional team of magical consultants, and the professional security force we recruited from Alterac."

Alterac's professional security force? It sounded like some kind of sick joke.

Jace couldn't help but ask, "Did you know that the Book of Medivh was stolen from the Kingdom of Alterac?"

The gnome smiled. "You're not the first mage to question our bank with that heinous crime committed by the Horde. You should know that when the Book of Medivh was stolen, the Kingdom of Alterac had already been taken over by Lordaeron and Stromgarde. Alterac's fall was due to Perenolde's betrayal of the honorable people of the mountain — that doesn't diminish the professionalism of Alterac's mercenaries."

He wasn't exactly wrong. Before the Dark Portal opened, the soldiers of the Kingdom of Alterac had always been renowned across the seven kingdoms for being few but elite. So much so that Terenas had even believed there was no way the orcs could break through Alterac's defenses to strike at his own rear.

Thanks to Perenolde's sudden betrayal, the caught-off-guard Lordaeron had nearly had its capital besieged by orcs.

Jace still remembered those days — the chronic pall of gloom and death hanging over the tall city walls, war horns and horse hooves everywhere, every face wearing the look of someone who'd been terrified out of their wits, until word of victory from Hillsbrade arrived.

The gnome kept going on and on, rattling off the vault's magical protections and what kind of thieves it had dealt with as if reciting a menu. Jace, whose mind had already wandered back several years, wasn't even listening to a word he said.

But Jace didn't care much either, because he knew that even if the Wizard Sanctum's own treasury had holes in it that even Master Marin didn't trust, and the mages there refused to store their most precious treasures in Stormwind Bank — that said more than enough.

No matter how hard the gnome tried to pitch it, Jace ultimately didn't "open an account" at the Royal Bank. Compared to their so-called team of magical consultants or Alterac's mercenaries, he trusted Morophil and the hellhound security team at Ilgala Tower more.

Since Dazarum IV, raised from the graveyard, had been confirmed as SI:7's, the two hellhounds at Ilgala Tower should still be there — so there was no need to worry about the security strength there being weakened.

Carrying the box back to his rented room, he pushed open the door to find the imp scribbling away with a quill on a piece of scrap paper. "What are you doing?" Jace asked.

Sazeno asked, "Does Master not find Dar'Khan Drathir's incantations interesting? They don't sound like demonic."

Morophil was lounging to one side. She glanced at the imp, then looked at Jace. Jace said, "In a couple of days I'm going to Quel'Thalas to get to the bottom of this. You two watch the house well — especially this box. There are two hundred gold coins in here. Two hundred!"

"What?" Morophil sat up with a displeased expression. "I thought Master was taking me to Quel'Thalas. I wanted to experience that 'radiance of the Sunwell' too."

"No," Jace said.

Morophil leaned over and grabbed Jace's hand. "I won. I clearly won against Vereesa Windrunner. Why do I feel like I'm the one who lost now? Where's my reward?"

"Isn't the satisfaction of defeating a Ranger Lord reward enough?" Jace asked.

Morophil smiled and gave a little flick of her brow. "Our Lady Windrunner's humble submission was indeed delicious and worth savoring. Now that you mention it... hmm. But no matter what, that delicacy isn't enough to fill the loss I've suffered since the great graveyard..."

As she spoke, the succubus grabbed Angellista's belt. Jace grabbed one of the demon's horns and held her back. "Wait."

"What?" Morophil asked.

"I'm curious — for a sayaad hunter, are demonic wings important?" Jace asked.

"Of course they are," Morophil said. "If I still had my wings, the Windrunner would've lost even more badly."

Suddenly her mood seemed to drop, though outwardly she showed no change — but Jace caught the inner stirring.

"You saw?" she asked in a low voice.

"Hard to miss," Jace said.

"I'll fully adapt to a fighting style without demonic wings," the demon smiled. "Don't dwell on that, Master."

"Is there really no way to make your wings grow back?" Jace asked.

"There isn't. But the fact that you'd even think that way moves me more than enough." Morophil smiled and wrapped her arm around Jace's shoulder, nudging his ear with his nose as she whispered in demonic: "Still, I'm at least confident I can return to my most powerful state, darling. That depends on how much you're willing to give every night..."
