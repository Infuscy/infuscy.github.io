Chapter 1106: Teleportation

That morning, Jace put on his robe and staff and tidied up his appearance by the canal.

Because today he was heading to Quel'Thalas. He hadn't planned on buying any fancy clothes, but at least he didn't want to look too scruffy.

While shaving earlier, he'd accidentally nicked the skin on his face — that sensation of the cold blade dragging across had made him certain it was going to bleed. But after examining himself by the river for a while, he hadn't noticed anything wrong. The river water was just too blurry, that's all.

Although in Stormwind, just about everyone who knew Jace probably already thought of him as a terrifying hexer, deep down he always felt like he was still that young man who'd ridden a carriage from Lordaeron to Southshore to catch a boat.

Especially with twenty extra years of past-life experience on top, this lifetime had flown by so fast that it was genuinely difficult to adapt to the change in identity in the blink of an eye.

"Jace." Vereesa waved at him from a distance.

Jace walked over, craning his neck and pointing to his cheek. "Is there a cut here?"

"No." Vereesa asked, puzzled. "What's wrong?"

"Worried I'd cut my face shaving." Jace rubbed his chin. "It's fine now. Let's go." So the blessing of Queen Alexstrasza had at least made his skin a lot tougher than before.

The arcane light in Vereesa's eyes flickered, flaring like a flame. She glanced to the side, then asked, "Is Morophil following us?"

"If she's hidden herself, you can see her with just your eyes?" Jace asked.

"Sometimes I can sense it," Vereesa said. "After all, when the Horde invaded Quel'Thalas, sayaad assassins were also a serious problem. Afterward, we more or less all trained in techniques for detecting demonic stealth. But to be honest, trying to pick out Morophil really isn't easy. If she'd actually slipped into the shadows and lain in ambush against me, I'd have had to rely on reflexes alone to deal with it. I have to remind you — allowing a sayaad into the runestone's range is far less simple than letting a human in, Jace. If she just walked straight through the portal, it could have catastrophic consequences. I mean, catastrophic for her personally..."

"She's not that brazen," Jace said. "But hearing you say it this way — we're being teleported directly to the north side of the Eldarath River this time?"

"That's right," Vereesa said. "The archmages told me we'll arrive directly at Sunstrider Isle."

"Sunstrider Isle?" Jace was a little surprised.

Sunstrider Isle lay to the northwest of Silvermoon — the heart of the heart of Quel'Thalas. It housed the shrine of Dath'Remar Sunstrider, the first Sun King who had led the original Highborne from Kalimdor to the shores of Lordaeron and founded Silvermoon and Quel'Thalas, and the island had taken its name from him.

"Near the Faltherien Academy," Vereesa said. "It's very close to the Sunwell. The magical currents are extremely stable, making it well-suited for maintaining a teleportation corridor. And Faltherien itself possesses quite a few magic energy stones. If we were to open a teleportation gate in the south of Quel'Thalas, or even outside the main gates, it wouldn't be nearly as effortless."

"The more you say, the more I'm looking forward to it," Jace said.

Vereesa walked along, giving him a sidelong glance, then asked, "It sounds like you've known about Sunstrider Isle for a while."

"An island named after the Sunstrider royal line — it sounds extraordinary just from the name," Jace smiled.

Vereesa nodded slowly. Jace thought, naturally, Sunstrider Isle...

For most non-quel'dorei outsiders, this island naturally held no special meaning. But for a World of Warcraft player like Jace, it was an entirely different matter.

In the game, this was the birthplace of every blood elf character.

In that timeline, after the blood elves joined the Horde, they quickly rose to become the most populous race thanks to their relatively attractive appearances. So it could be said that Sunstrider Isle was the earliest memory of quite a few Horde players.

And so, upon hearing the name Sunstrider Isle, this sheep-milking trip had taken on an extra layer of meaning.

They made their way to Stormwind Keep, turning through a doorway beside the ramp leading up to the Throne Room into a corridor leading to the Quel'Thalas embassy, and before long entered a garden full of vine-carved wooden walls and bright copper-colored railings.

Several stern-looking high elven arcanists in blue runed robes were already standing in the garden. Upon seeing Vereesa, they bowed one by one, greeting her in Thalassian, then paid their respects to Jace as well.

Vereesa looked at one of the arcanists — a female mage with a head of thick, silver curls. "We can begin, Lady Aelose."

"Yes, Lady Vereesa."

This female mage walked to the center of the group of arcanists, raised her hand, and chanted an incantation. The others cooperated, chanting in unison.

The impact of it was so great that even someone as arcane-insensitive as Jace felt as though everything from deep underground to the entire palace had been stirred by a supernatural force.

The ritual looked quite arduous — even with several professional portal mages constructing it. No wonder even a great mage like Krasus could hardly guarantee he could send Jace straight to Booty Bay in one go.

If teleporting to Sunstrider Isle near the Sunwell was already this intensive, it made him think of the "portal" that would need to be opened later to travel to Draenor — that would undoubtedly be several orders of magnitude harder.

Seen this way, Atramedes's group teleportation spell, which had looked effortless and finished in the blink of an eye, was probably the most absurd magic Jace had witnessed with his own eyes since coming to this world.

But he couldn't ask a blue dragon to help stabilize a portal to Draenor. Doing that would be less efficient than just killing himself.

Thinking of this, he glanced sideways at Vereesa. The ranger, who'd still been tinged with excitement and anticipation, was now frowning as she stared at the arcane circle forming beneath the mages' feet.

She was worrying about the future. Jace could sense she was worried about the same thing he was.

"If there really are uncertain risks, we'll have to scrap the plan, Wen," Jace said in a low voice.

"I know," Vereesa said softly.

A fierce whirlwind spun through the garden. Every tree and flower shook violently. A bright, illusory image slowly unfolded from the center of the mages with arcane force. Every elf gazing at this portal widened their eyes wider and wider.

In the mirage, Jace caught a hazy glimpse of golden buildings by the sea, with pale blue arcane energy crystals floating in between. "Please pass through as soon as possible, Lady Vereesa Windrunner, Master Jace Sesso!" the female archmage called out.

"You heard her. Don't think about those things for now. Jace, let's go." Vereesa took Jace's hand and said softly. "Mm," Jace replied.

The arcane hum grew more intense with each step closer to the portal. The radiance of the Sunwell slowly enveloped their entire bodies...
