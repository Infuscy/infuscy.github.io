## Chapter 1107: Sunstrider Isle

If teleporting from the Wizard Sanctum to Stonard was like being flung by an arcane conduit from the top of a mage tower into a damp, cold, dark trap — then stepping through the portal held open by the high elves to arrive at Sunstrider Isle was like surfacing from a clean, comfortably warm pool, facing a gentle breeze and brilliant sunlight.

Fully emerging from the portal, Jace's boots landed on a smooth magenta-colored floor. Golden phoenix-like motifs paved the area around the square, and together with the red floor tiles they framed an elegant fountain carved with High Elven-style runic decorations.

The water blossoms spraying from the fountain splashed with arcane sparkles. The source of its power was the crystal-blue magic sphere at the very top of the tall, ornate stone pillar at its center.

Jace stared blankly at the sphere for a moment, momentarily unable to tell whether the energy source for the portal behind him was that sphere, or the magic crystals floating in the white courtyard not far away, or the sunrise-like radiance over the sea beyond the lush golden forest to the north.

Amid the scent of flowers and the moist freshness of clear springs, the indistinct sound of harps drifted from a distance that was hard to place. Following the sound, he saw a group of young high elves in white robes who'd gathered curiously and were peering this way.

This place was a little different from Jace's memory of Sunstrider Isle. What exactly was different? There were no mana wyrms to be seen everywhere, no mana panthers strolling among the ruins, and none of those dark green fel crystals standing between the houses and temples.

The Sunwell hadn't been destroyed yet, and everything was still in good order. Aside from the somewhat sparse forests, Sunstrider Isle wasn't much different from the rest of Quel'Thalas.

Beneath a pure, water-blue sky, the high walls and white towers of Silvermoon lay veiled in a layer of pale blue light at the far southern edge of vision. As the eye moved closer, gardens of varying sizes were arranged like plump blocks of color, dotted with all manner of shrines and courtyards in between, with every color of flower stretching across the bright woodland meadows.

The moment Vereesa's feet touched the ground, she ran over and threw her arms around a woman in a thin purple dress in the distance. Jace still remembered her face, even though it had been a while since he'd seen her.

"General Sylvanas," he said with a slight bow. "The honor of seeing you again."

"The honor is mine as well. But, Jace, you don't need to address me that way." The Ranger-General smiled as she walked over. "I remember we were already quite familiar last time, and it hasn't been that long."

Vereesa stole a glance at Jace. "See? She's not as terrifying as you imagined." Sylvanas drew in a slow breath and glanced at Jace as well.

Jace couldn't help but wonder what on earth this woman had reported to her sister — she wouldn't have repeated all his private gossip about the Ranger-General, would she?

"First, I still must apologize to you on behalf of the Farstriders, Jace," Sylvanas said in a solemn tone. "Mara is a capable Farstrider, but she truly lacks judgment and courage, and is easily misled and manipulated by others. At first I thought she could gradually temper herself and fill in her shortcomings through field training, but I never expected her to be incited into participating in that terrible crime against you. Vereesa should have told you of our decision regarding her — she's been removed from the Farstrider ranks, and once the investigation is complete, she'll be exiled to Loch Modan to serve as a guard at Quel'Thalas's garrison there."

Jace nodded, though in his view Loch Modan was nothing more than a scenic tourist destination surrounded by beautiful mountains and forests, where you could enjoy both woodland and lake views. Being exiled there could hardly be called a punishment.

But since Sylvanas was being so serious about it, and Vereesa beside her wore an expression of complete vindication, he couldn't very well question it from every angle.

Perhaps to the quel'dorei, the mountains of Loch Modan were an existence akin to the Wall in A Song of Ice and Fire. He was more curious about how the dwarves of Thelsamar would feel about elves treating their homeland as a place of exile.

"You can rest on Sunstrider Isle tonight." Sylvanas walked toward the edge of the square, pointing to a village composed of low white towers and pretty white houses not far away. "Right over there."

"Since I have a meeting in Silvermoon, I won't be able to join you for dinner tonight. I'm truly sorry, Jace." Hearing this, Vereesa, following behind her, drooped her ears in disappointment and kicked a small pebble far away.

"There'll be plenty of other chances," Sylvanas comforted her in Thalassian, then put on an apologetic expression.

Vereesa looked into the distance without responding. As long as she was by the Ranger-General's side, this executioner who cut down anyone in her path seemed to instantly turn into a girl acting coy and throwing tantrums.

Sylvanas, finding her difficult to handle for the moment, could only sigh softly and look at Jace. "What do you think of Sunstrider Isle?"

"I'm not much for flowery words, so all I can say is..." Jace gazed toward the sea reflecting the brilliant radiance of the Sunwell to the north. "It's like a world out of a dream."

"It is for me too," Sylvanas said. "When I first came to Sunstrider Isle, I had the same feeling. This place is nothing short of a sacred land — and unlike Quel'Danas Isle, it's not so solemn and off-limits. Sunstrider Isle is like the cradle of all quel'dorei."

"I didn't expect the archmages to be willing to teleport us directly to Sunstrider Isle this time," Jace said as he walked. "I thought we'd enter through the Gate of Quel'Thalas again like last time."

"Last time was because the Council of Archmages had blocked nearly all teleportation magic within the Ban'dinoriel," Sylvanas explained. "You may not know this, but there are still some orcs wandering the forests from Wilder to Kel'Daron, and the Farstriders haven't confirmed the orcs have lost the capacity to infiltrate Quel'Thalas for sabotage — the vigilance here can't be relaxed yet."

"Are there still orcs in human lands?" Vereesa asked.

"East of Hillsbrade — more precisely, the Arathi Highlands." Sylvanas counted on her fingers. "Hillsbrade, north of Darrowshire... recently there have been reports of encounters with wandering orc bands in these places."

She tilted her head and glanced at her sister beside her. "You should learn more about Lordaeron's situation, my little moon. You can't just ignore everything because King Terenas II treated you poorly. You're a real Ranger Lord now."

Jace smiled. Vereesa said, "We didn't come back this time for a lecture, General Sylvanas Windrunner."

"My mistake."

Sylvanas put away her smile, then looked at the Farstriders following nearby and told them in Thalassian that they could leave. Soon, the long cobblestone road was left with only the two Windrunners and Jace.

The surroundings quieted down, and Jace sensed the atmosphere shift slightly. Vereesa's expression cooled as well.

"I consulted with both the archmages and the Silvermoon Council, Jace," Sylvanas said in a low voice. "But unfortunately, there truly is no information about Draenor's teleportation coordinates. Rest assured, my sources are absolutely reliable — they won't breathe a word to any other quel'dorei. Of course, even these friends of mine don't know themselves why I'm consulting on this matter, since neither the Archmage Platform nor the Sunfury Spire knows how to open a portal to Draenor, let alone my Farstriders."

As she spoke, the Ranger-General turned to look at Jace, even tossing her golden hair into disarray. The arcane radiance in her eyes flickered unstably too, making it hard for a moment to tell where her gaze was focused.

"Without coordinates, there's no way we can reactivate the Dark Portal, is there?" she asked, coming to a stop. "We're not trying to reactivate *that* Dark Portal, sister," Vereesa stressed.

"Mm, I mean... you know what I'm referring to." Sylvanas looked at her.

Jace cleared his throat, swept a quick glance at Vereesa, and said, "I haven't summoned my associate back yet — he used to be in charge of teleportation matters for the Burning Legion, so I believe he has a very good chance of knowing Draenor's coordinates. It's just that I want to find a second source of information so I can cross-check against what he knows, to avoid problems."

He could tell his reassurance hadn't had much effect. Sylvanas was still frowning tightly. "Perhaps I can also think of something through other channels — from Dalaran," she said.

"Would Prince Kael'thas help?" Vereesa asked carefully.

"Are you out of your mind?" Sylvanas glared at her sharply. "Don't tell this to a single Sunstrider — or anyone from the Sunfury, the Dusk, the Sunreaver, any... any quel'dorei whose family has anything to do with the sun or the morning. We can't afford the risk."

"I'll remember," Vereesa said in a low voice.

"There's no need to rush yet, Lady Sylvanas," Jace said. "Once we've prepared all the other conditions, it won't be too late to think about this step."

Sylvanas's gaze flickered. She relaxed a little and said, "I know, Jace. I just want to let you know... whatever you and Vereesa need, I'll help however I can."
