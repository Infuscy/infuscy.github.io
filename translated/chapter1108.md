## Chapter 1108: Who Wants to Go Home the Most

"The dying sun sinks, a boat sails through the mist..."

"Years pass like a sword, plucking at harp strings..."

Twilight seemed to stand still. Bathed in the radiance of the Sunwell, the deep blue of night could advance not a single shade further into the sky.

Jace leaned against the golden filigree entwined like vines and gazed down at the elves chatting and laughing in the small square below, listening to the slightly mournful melody and lyrics trading back and forth. Light and shadow picked out their sharp, sculpted features and elegant, slender silhouettes, interweaving with the shadows of the trees, the fountain's magic, and the transparent glow of the magic stones, making one feel all the more that this was no place in the real world.

Among them, a female elf had been pulled into a lover's lap on a stone bench by the fountain. She giggled, her drifting gaze seeming to catch sight of the gaze from above. When she noticed it was a human, her expression stiffened slightly — but she said nothing.

Jace had no intention of intruding on the elves' amusement any longer. He breathed in the slightly cool air and turned back inside. Behind layers of violet curtains, Vereesa was reclining in a chair, watching this way.

She'd already changed into a rather light, thin pale-yellow dress that showed off a figure that was lean but quite strong. "How's your injury, Wen?" Jace asked.

"I feel like it's basically healed," Vereesa smiled, half-jokering. "Fighting the Amani witch doctors for so long gave me a powerful resistance to shadow."

Male servants with graceful bearing and carriage brought out all manner of pastries and a bottle of mead in a vase-like ornate jar, arranging them on the table one by one. Before long, the entire room was filled with a slightly cloying compound sweetness.

She stood up, poured a glass, and handed it over. "Try it, Jace. I don't know much about brewing, but Sylvanas told me this was carefully brewed by the quel'dorei from an estate to the south... even she can't bring herself to drink it casually."

Jace sniffed the liquid in the glass. There wasn't much of an alcoholic smell or fermented feel to it — it was more like smelling flowers. This made him somewhat doubt whether the amber substance inside was actually wine at all.

He took a small sip. It reminded him of a certain white wine, but the flavor was richer, with no sourness or astringency to speak of. What surprised him most was that it wasn't all that sweet.

Jace felt this didn't quite match the mead in his mind, so he gave a sheepish laugh. "Grede probably understands this better than me. If this stuff is really that precious, having me taste-test it would be a waste."

Vereesa shook her head. "In Quel'Thalas, Grede only drinks magically made wine. He can't bring himself to believe quel'dorei could brew anything better than a dwarf without using magic."

"There's no magic involved in this?" Jace asked in surprise, then took another sip.

"When all of Quel'Thalas is full of magic brooms and arcane constructs, the nobles at the very top of Silvermoon prefer to have quel'dorei do this kind of work instead." Vereesa leaned back in her chair. "It's the same with wine — many nobles pursue high-quality rarities made without any magic involved. So here, not all nobles are keen on becoming archmages guarding the Sunwell. There are also those who enjoy growing flowers and managing estates with their own hands. There's a Dusk-family garden on Sunstrider Isle, not far from here — sounds rather strange, doesn't it? Completely at odds with the stern style of the hawkstrider knights."

When she mentioned the Dusk, she glanced around at the male servants in the room, then said something in Thalassian to one who'd just set down several slices of ham-like meat. The servants nearby nodded and left the room as well.

Once everyone had left, the ranger got up and paced by the door for a while, then looked out the window again. Confirming no one was near the room, she walked back and sat down in her chair, supporting her forehead. "Sylvanas is right — we simply can't get help from the Sunfury Spire in this matter, Jace. But without the support of Prince Kael'thas and the Sunreaver, it's nearly impossible to obtain Draenor's teleportation coordinates from the Kirin Tor..."

Jace picked up a rather bland-tasting savory biscuit, scooped up the sauce-like warm red wine-flavored cheese, and stuffed it into his mouth, chewing as he spoke. "Maybe Dalaran doesn't know either."

"It was Alleria who joined the Sons of Lothar, not Belvar, or Rommath, or Ethas and those people," Vereesa said. "But Dalaran sent Khadgar — that mage would definitely find a way to record Draenor's coordinates and transmit them back to the Kirin Tor."

"There were also archmages among Alleria's forces, Wen," Jace reminded her.

Vereesa asked, "Don't you know anyone else in the Kirin Tor you can trust, Jace?"

"I absolutely can't let Krasus know what I'm planning," Jace said.

After he spoke, Jace noticed the ranger had been staring at him the whole time — and only then did realize who Vereesa meant. "I can't ask Amy Marin to take that risk for us," he said immediately.

Vereesa let out a deep sigh, rubbed her eyes hard, and said softly, "You're right."

"What's more, she's unlikely to have access to anything that classified. She's only been in the Kirin Tor for two or three years, Wen." Jace leaned back in his chair.

The two of them fell silent for a while. Vereesa asked quietly, "Actually, you don't think much of that Ereduin knowing Draenor's coordinates either, do you?" Jace looked at her, hesitated a moment, and had to admit she'd seen right through him.

Back when he'd just gotten Karlez fully under control and asked the demon about Draenor, the other had only vaguely mentioned that Magtheridon might have already begun attacking Draenor.

There was more than one doom lord in the Burning Legion, and Karlez wasn't even among the highest-ranking. He truly had nothing to do with the Legion's plan to invade Draenor.

So he really might have no idea about this information at all. "Would the orcs of Blackrock Spire know?" Vereesa asked.

"Hard to say," Jace said. "Gul'dan probably knows. The members of the Shadow Council under him might not all know. And the remaining Shadow Council members in Blackrock Spire have been purged more than once."

At the mention of Gul'dan, Jace couldn't help but take another sip of mead.

He had to say, though this wine couldn't quite pin down any obvious distinctive feature at first taste, the more he drank the more character it seemed to have — each sip appeared to relax his mood a fair bit.

After a sip, he picked up a slice of savory raw ham and stuffed it into his mouth, chewing it together with a bit of the wine still lingering on his tongue.

"Warchief Rend over there has already decided to settle in Azeroth, which is why he's opposed by the Bleeding Hollow, the Shattered Hand, and the other orc clans that still want to return through the Dark Portal. I very much doubt he's kept hold of information like this."

Vereesa thought quietly for a moment, then asked, "What about Teron'Gorefiend?"

Seeing her staring at him like that, Jace's heart sank. "You're not hoping some of Teron'Gorefiend's soul's memories are still lingering in my head, are you, Vereesa Windrunner? You watched with your own eyes that night as I... struggled my way back from that state."

Vereesa's gaze flickered. She nodded. "I'm sorry, Jace, I know. Maybe Sylvanas and I are both a little... overly fixated on this matter."

"I'd say she's a bit calmer about it than you," Jace said.

"I don't deny it." Vereesa lifted her glass and took a large gulp. "Besides these places, is there no other source of information? To be honest, even if that doom lord is certain of Draenor's coordinates, even if he has no intention of harming us, I wouldn't dare put all my trust in a demon whose head is full of fel energy..."

"Same here," Jace nodded. "If we can't get it from Quel'Thalas or Dalaran..."

"Orgrim Doomhammer?" Vereosa said. "The Farstriders are still cooperating with Lordaeron to track down this 'real' orc warchief. Would he have kept Draenor's coordinate information?"

"It's not impossible," Jace said.

At that moment, Jace thought of the Frostwolf clan hiding in the Alterac Mountains. Considering this orc clan had been exiled through the Dark Portal, and that there were no demons, mages, or warlocks among them, they truly were unlikely to possess information of this kind. But they probably already knew Orgrim's whereabouts, given they were one of the few organized orc groups left on the continent of Lordaeron.

On the way to Lordaeron, should they venture into the Alterac mountains to find this Frostwolf clan?

Jace felt it was necessary, but at the same time he was a bit unsure how that clan would react upon learning he intended to go to Draenor. Were there any other avenues?

Actually, the ones who knew Draenor's coordinates most clearly probably weren't orcs, or human and elven mages, or even demons — but the Eredar. Or, to be more precise, the "fugitives" among them — the Draenei.

Long ago, the Draenei had piloted a naaru vessel through the stars and landed on Draenor — the planet had even been named by them.

Later, after Illidan's demon forces and Kael'thas's Sunfury legion formed the Illidari coalition and raided that fleet, the leader of the Draenei, Prophet Velen, had been forced to pilot one of the ships, the Exodar, and flee from the shattered Draenor, crash-landing north of Kalimdor.

But all of that was still too early. There was simply no way to find any Draenei in Azeroth right now... Wait.

Thinking of this, Jace pondered a little deeper. Was it really impossible to find any Draenei?

Hadn't they been ambushed by "Draenei" not far from the Pool of Tears?

Of course, those so-called Draenei were just monsters withered and degraded by dark energy, having lost their former wisdom and power. But Jace still clearly remembered the words shrieked at him by the captured Lost One.

"Can't go back... none of us can go back to Draenor."

Though they'd learned from some unknown source that Draenor had been destroyed and claimed none of them could return, even Grede had been able to feel that intense, near-mad longing to return home — let alone him, a warlock.

Could these outsiders still be holding Draenor's teleportation coordinates?

The chances were probably not much higher than with the Frostwolf orcs, Orgrim Doomhammer, or the Kirin Tor — but it was genuinely an important lead.

Perhaps they should take the opportunity to look for those Lost Ones near the Pool of Tears. But considering how, on the way there, Sylvanas had been anxiously wanting to know the situation in the temple, and on the way back, everyone had been physically and mentally exhausted... they truly didn't have the energy to go making trouble for those little monsters hiding deep in the swamp.

Right now they were heading north across the continent of Lordaeron. Jace was just about to open his mouth to tell Vereesa it would be best to find a chance to visit the Alterac mountains and ask those Frostwolves. But before the words came out, it occurred to him that besides the Frostwolf clan themselves, seemingly few people in this world knew their exact hiding spot. Otherwise, they couldn't have survived for over a decade under the joint encirclement of humans and Farstriders.

And the ranger before him was one of the key leaders of those encirclers.

She absolutely wouldn't care whether those Frostwolves had anything to do with the Burning Legion, or whether they were exiled "traitors." To her, orcs just damn well needed to be wiped out. But considering they now needed to obtain intelligence from the orcs that might be relevant to reaching Draenor, she could also compromise a little.

Jace organized his words and said, "When we go to Lordaeron, maybe we can stop near Alterac City or Durnholde Keep for a bit. See if there's any news about Orgrim Doomhammer."

Vereesa nodded slowly, and her slightly furrowed brows eased a little.

She whispered, "Maybe that fellow has already been captured by the human army. When the time comes, we could just interrogate him directly, or his lieutenants, subordinates, and so on."

"Let's hope so," Jace continued. "Once we get this magic crystal and mount it on the renovated Ilgala Tower, I'll immediately set about summoning the doom lord. If Karlez really can't figure it out, we can head to the Blackwater Moar and look for those Lost Ones. Remember them?"

"I remember," Vereesa said. "Are you sure those corrupted monsters could grasp something as complex as teleportation coordinates?"

Jace thought about it and said, "I know the Burning Legion uses some kind of magic gems or extremely sturdy enchanted materials as carriers for teleportation coordinates. If they truly are desperate to return to Draenor, they might well be storing something like that. But that's best-case scenario."

He wasn't talking out of his hat. The coordinates for the Eredar homeworld of Argus were preserved in a disc known as the Seal of Argus, kept by the Nathrezim dreadlords in the vaults of Nathreza.

After listening to Jace, Vereesa glared at the items on the table, then casually picked up a cake with little berries and stuffed the whole thing into her mouth, swallowing it in a few chews.

"Hearing my sister say they found nothing today..." she licked her lips, "I thought we might really have to take enormous risks to reach Draenor, or that we'd have to abandon the plan entirely. But listening to your analysis, Jace, hope has welled up in my heart all over again."

"Don't get optimistic yet," Jace said in a casual tone. "If the time comes and we have to deal with the orc clans roaming outside, remember to hold back from picking fights with them for now."

"I can restrain myself. But can those brutes restrain themselves from throwing a punch when they see me?" Vereesa asked.

By now it had grown much quieter outside. Though the sky didn't seem to have darkened yet, with the afterglow still spilling in through the curtains and windows, the singing and laughter in the square had already faded from hearing.

"Then let me go meet them alone. After all, I still have... that black... uh."

Jace held his glass and scratched behind him, only then remembering he wasn't wearing that cloak. His mind had already begun involuntarily imagining the scene of meeting the Frostwolf clan, but his consciousness was gradually drifting...

He glanced at the golden liquid in his glass. Probably because he couldn't taste much alcohol, and coupled with the fact that this wine really was mellow and fragrant, he'd unknowingly drunk several rounds. By now the drunkenness had stealthily climbed into his head, making him woozy.

"Damn it, I'm drunk, Wen," he said, looking up.

His vision blurred for a moment. Vereesa had already gotten up and come over, pressing a kiss to his lips.

The damp, slightly warm lips brought him back to alertness. The clumsy effort he made to stiffen his body made the elf giggle.

"Aren't you drunk?" Jace asked. "I drank quite a bit too."

"Don't forget, even Grede can't outdrink me." Vereesa threw herself onto Jace and said softly. "Tonight you're mine..."
