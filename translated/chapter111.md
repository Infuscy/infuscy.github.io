## Chapter 111: The Path of Faith Off to a Bad Start

The afternoon had turned quite cold, and the streets were deserted.

Jace emerged from the basement. After spending hours huddled around the bonfire, the sudden blast of cold air hit him the moment he stepped outside, and he quickly threw his cloak on and wrapped it tight around himself.

He parted ways with Ursula and Erland as they came out together, and Jace made a point of studying Erland's expression — checking whether the man was still dwelling on what had happened that night.

On the surface there still seemed to be some distance between them, but whether that counted as holding a grudge, Jace couldn't really say.

After the experience of getting cornered in an alley by Jagin out of nowhere last time, he wasn't willing to jump to conclusions about anyone anymore.

He wandered home, and as he passed along the canal he thought how nice it would be if there were something like headphones here so he could listen to music.

Even if there were no smartphones or MP3 players, a portable music box would do — who knew, maybe there was some kind of magic that could play music inside your own head.

Under a clear sky, he watched the sun slowly sink toward the sea. The upright masts of the sailboats sliced the sun into a few hazy, blurred pieces, like a pumpkin pie cut at random.

The surface of the canal at his feet reflected gold, shimmering with the light.

If there were some light music playing right now, he could almost imagine he was the male lead in a TV drama, walking through the ending-credit montage. Then again, listening to the sound of little boats cutting through the water, the oars churning up the waves, and the harsh, sudden cries of those cold-hardy birds flying in from the gardens of the Mage Quarter had a charm all their own.

For some reason, Jace felt a little lonely inside, as if something was missing.

Maybe it was just coming away from the bonfire gathering, maybe it was the night scene stirring up memories.

Just as he was thinking of finding some amusement to shake off the uncomfortable feeling, he spotted a round little shadow sitting on the edge of the canal right by his front door, staring off into the reddening sunset just like him.

"Grede?"

Hearing the voice, the shadow whipped its head around.

"There you are! I thought you didn't work afternoons?"

"There was a get-together this afternoon." Jace explained as he sat down beside Grede. "What brings you here?"

Grede asked, "Haven't we gone almost a week without seeing each other? Seems like we haven't gotten together once since we split up after coming back from Redridge. I've been cooped up at home these past couple of days, haven't gone to the Fighters' Guild either — I feel like my arms and legs are going soft!"

"Don't tell me you found another job?!" Jace felt his neck tighten.

Nowadays, whenever he heard Grede say things like "I've got nothing to do, figured I'd stretch my limbs," or "oh it's a simple job, there's money in it," he started getting nervous.

"Are you sure you've recovered?" he asked.

"Should be fine," Grede said. "So I want to find a job and test whether I'm really better. Only live combat can ultimately test my recovery level!"

"Count me out, I'm not really up for it," Jace said. "I'm preparing for the Mage Quarter exam right now. If I pass, they'll pretty much reimburse my travel expenses. I'm broke as it is — if I fail, I probably won't be able to afford the ship ticket."

Grede turned his head and asked, "That much money you made gathering herbs last time, and you spent all of it? And the wolf hunting…"

"Go on, keep talking," Jace said. "And then what?"

Grede understood what Jace was getting at, gave a dry cough, and said, "Fine. Then I'll just have to find someone else."

"Right," Jace asked. "Last time you said you wanted to join the Church, didn't you? How'd that go — will they take you?"

"Don't get me started," Grede said. "They're not willing to accept me. I said I wanted to join the Order of the Silver Hand, and they said they wanted priests or warriors the Church trusts. I said I wanted to become a priest, and then they wanted some kind of trial or other, saying I don't have faith in the Holy Light and am not qualified to be a priest."

Jace said, "You don't even know them, and the first thing you say is you want to become a priest — of course they're not willing. And honestly, setting all that aside, do you actually believe in the Holy Light?"

"So what counts as believing in the Holy Light?" Grede shot back. That one stumped Jace. What did count as having faith in the Holy Light?

"Don't you know a lot about the Church of the Holy Light? Last time you told me something about the Church making torches with holy power for the Raven Hill cemetery — I'd never even heard of the Church of the Holy Light making torches for tombs."

Grede explained, "That was all hearsay! I only got to know the Church of the Holy Light in Southshore. The cemetery in Southshore had some torches about as bright as those ones in Raven Hill. There aren't many who believe in the Holy Light up on Aerie Peak — for a lot of dwarves who never leave the Hinterlands, the Holy Light is something new that only showed up after the Orc Wars."

Jace hesitated a moment, then asked, "Do you believe the Holy Light can guide your life? Like, if you do evil the Light will punish you, and if you do good, the Light will answer your call when you're in trouble, that sort of thing."

"I don't even know which counts and which doesn't," Grede said. "For example, does what happened at Sentinel Hill count as the Holy Light descending? That wagon — it was full of Church people. Ever since someone in Southshore told me what the Holy Light was, I'd never felt anything so real and solid."

Jace asked, "Isn't that a good thing? Didn't you tell the Church people about it?"

"I did," Grede said. "But that priest said only believing when the Light brings you benefits is using the Light, not true devotion — that kind of thing won't let you become a priest."

"What a pain in the ass," Jace scratched his head. "The Church people are probably just prejudiced against you Wildhammer dwarves."

"Why the hell should they be…" Grede muttered under his breath.

Jace also knew that before the Scourge invasion, joining the Order of the Silver Hand was genuinely difficult.

Every member of the Silver Hand was either a valorous priest, a devout warrior, or a noble knight.

It wasn't until the outbreak of the Scourge War that the Silver Hand began recruiting ordinary soldiers and knights on a large scale, bringing them into the Church to become paladins to fight the undead legion that was about to sweep across all of Lordaeron in the name of the Holy Light — that's when the Alliance saw the huge explosion in paladin numbers.

Back then, converting a warrior who'd never felt anything from the Holy Light wasn't actually hard.

Facing mountains of skeletons and zombies, in a moment of death-like despair, a true paladin would stand between you and absolute horror, blade and warhammer blazing with golden light, shouting "For the Holy Light!" as a single mighty blow swept away countless evils — no matter how much you'd disliked the Holy Light before, you'd believe then.

A fighter like Grede, if he'd tried to join the Silver Hand during the Scourge invasion, almost certainly wouldn't have been turned away. Right now, though? A lot harder.
