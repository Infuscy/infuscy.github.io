## Chapter 1112: The Crystal's Balance

Jace didn't just know about the Violet Hold — he'd come damn close to being thrown in there on more than one occasion.

When he'd called upon the souls for help in front of countless human, elf, and dwarf soldiers outside Nethergarde Keep, unleashing the Hand of Gul'dan on the pit lord Azgoth, he'd already made his peace with the idea of ending up in the Violet Hold.

But no matter what, he'd never actually set foot inside, so he had no idea what it was really like in there.

"When the Kingdom of Dalaran was first founded, it was a rough-and-tumble city that attracted a great many untrustworthy sorcerers," Belovarl said, gazing toward a mage tower whose silhouette was barely visible in the distance. "Back then, many young and talented human mages modified spaces, shifted planes, and summoned otherworldly beings without a second thought — and never once considered the consequences of controlling such forces."

"At the time, I proposed to the Sun King and the Silvermoon Council that we gift Dalaran some magic crystals capable of assisting in the stabilization of magical forces, to help you rein in magic that was on the verge of spiraling out of control. After all, it was Quel'Thalas that first brought magic to Arathi. Among these crystals was the powerful Taralvethin Crystal. Before the establishment of Bandinoriel — roughly six thousand eight hundred years ago — artifacts like the Taralvethin Crystal had helped the early settlers of Quel'Thalas weather several crises involving the Twisting Nether. Naturally, we assumed these would be of help to you as well."

The founding of Dalaran was two thousand years ago — which meant this Archmage was at least two thousand years old?

Two-thousand-plus-year-old High Elves couldn't be called exceedingly rare in Quel'Thalas; Prince Kael'thas, for instance, was over two thousand years old. But coming face to face with an elder mage who had personally witnessed the founding of Dalaran, and listening to him recount stories that only turned up in the old books of the library as firsthand experience — Jace found it a little unreal. It was as if simply standing beside him would drag you through time itself.

Never mind Jace — even Vereesa, listening at his side, was a bit dazed, then stole a glance over at Jace. After all, to her, a five- or six-hundred-year-old Sylvanas was already incomprehensibly ancient. Just how much had this two-thousand-something-year-old monster seen and done? She hadn't the faintest concept.

"But things did not go as smoothly as we had imagined," Belovarl continued. "Dalaran was nearly destroyed by demons in the end. The Taralvethin Crystal — an artifact that was supposed to be used to control demons and seal away demonic magic — was treated by countless mages as a converter for drawing dark power from demons, becoming an accomplice to Dalaran's catastrophe. Unsurprisingly, it was shattered into several pieces during the battle to liberate Dalaran that followed."

From the Archmage's tone, Jace could still detect a trace of regret, as though two thousand years hadn't been enough to let it go.

"After that came the founding of the Order of Tirisfal," Belovarl went on. "One of the pieces remained in Dalaran, used to imprison otherworldly creatures captured by the Order of Tirisfal, demons included. That demon prison was the predecessor of what is now the Violet Hold. The other fragments were all taken back to the Isle of Quel'Danas. Eight hundred years ago, when the Burning Legion attempted to enter Azeroth from Northrend, one of the pieces was also sent to Northrend before the Guardian Aegwynn's departure, to aid the Blue Dragonflight in fighting the demons."

"Can I take one of the pieces?" Jace asked.

"That is precisely what I mean," Belovarl said. "However, it may not quite meet your requirements, since it really cannot be considered the same category as that magic crystal at Nethergarde Keep."

"Then could I take a look at it with my own eyes, Archmage?" Jace asked.

"The nearest one is to the south, on the other side of those hills. It has been kept in the town of Tranquilliin ever since the end of the orc invasion, in preparation for any renewal of hostilities," Belovarl said, pointing toward a highland to the south.

"Can we go take a look now?" Vereesa asked.

Belovarl turned to the Vereesa beside him, seemingly a little curious as to why this Ranger-Lord seemed even more eager to get her hands on the crystal than Jace Sesso himself.

But that flicker of doubt passed quickly. He nodded and said, "The ship can take a tributary of the Elrendar River to get there in a while. It won't delay us too long."

"Then let's go to Tranquilliin first, Archmage. Thank you for your trouble," Jace said.

"It is no trouble." Belovarl nodded slightly, then gave a few instructions in Thalassian to a male attendant at his side.

Around midday, the sailboat docked at a broad river fork. Belovarl led Jace, Vereesa, and several attendants onto a smaller vessel, which turned southward.

From here, the rows of white towers and elegant elven buildings atop the southern hills were already visible. Jace even had some recollection of this place — it seemed that the first time he'd come to Quel'Thalas, entering from the south, the first thing he'd seen gazing out from the high mountains in the distance was this very town.

Tranquilliin, in the game, was a core Horde player hub, situated roughly at the center of Quel'Thalas south of the Elrendar River.

But at this point the Scourge had not yet turned this entire region into the Ghostlands. Set against the backdrop of Goldenmist Village, Windrunner Village, and various other estates and hamlets, the town didn't stand out all that much.

Docking at Tranquilliin's harbor, Jace immediately sensed the difference between this town and Goldenmist Village.

This place was more of a military outpost, home to a considerable number of soldiers and mages. Those buildings that looked quite magnificent and imposing from afar were actually mage towers and watchtowers holding aloft magic crystals, one after another — the style distinctly more austere.

Led by a Magister of Tranquilliin, the Archmage brought Jace and Vereesa off the town's main street and into a temple shaded by trees.

From the outside, the temple was rather unassuming, but it was guarded by a considerable number of elven Spellbreakers clad in light armor, carrying bladestaves and great shields.

As Jace walked past, he could feel these elven warriors weren't looking at the Archmage, weren't glancing at Vereesa — they just kept their eyes locked on him. It was as though he wasn't a guest invited by the Archmage, nor the hero who had defeated Deathwing, but a thief who might try any trick at any moment.

But given his reputation, and given the sensitive nature of this place, the Spellbreakers' vigilance was easy enough to understand.

The temple didn't have a proper main gate — it looked more like a pavilion that wasn't quite open enough. Stepping in between the pillars carved with elven runes, one could immediately see an irregularly shaped magic crystal resting on a pedestal at the very center.

The crystal was a dim purple, and within it one could discern suppressed, chaotic power — Arcane, Shadow, turbulent Fel…

What surprised Jace was that while the coexistence of Arcane and Shadow wasn't all that unimaginable, Fel and Arcane were two forces that were nearly impossible to reconcile. How could this crystal mix both types of power within the same space?

But as he drew closer and closer, Jace felt his ability to sense the emotions of the elves around him growing weaker and weaker. It was as though the crystal possessed some kind of suppressive force, pressing down on his connection to Shadow.

But after adjusting for a moment, Jace realized this wasn't suppression — it was sedation.

This crystal was suppressing all dark and chaotic forces within its radius, making Jace feel as though he could no longer sense Shadow energy at all. But Shadow was all around him as always; it had merely become somewhat unfamiliar due to the crystal's influence.

Jace found it strange — why had these things never appeared in the game in any direct way?

But then, considering the impact of the Third War… Dalaran destroyed by the defiler Archimonde, the Sunwell exploding, half of Quel'Thalas falling into deathly silence — it stood to reason that these powerful crystals capable of countering dark magic would have been destroyed after those defeats.

"This is a fragment of the Taralvethin Crystal?" Vereesa murmured in Thalassian.

"Yes, Lady Vereesa," Belovarl replied.

It was called a fragment, but in truth the crystal was quite large — probably a size smaller than the one at the Nethergarde Keep mage tower, but at least Jace felt certain he couldn't move it by himself. He'd probably need to get Grede to help.

By now the temple had fallen quiet. Jace walked up to the crystal and asked, "May I try to sense the power of this crystal, Archmage?"

Hearing those words, the Spellbreakers and Magisters around them all tensed up.

Belovarl, however, didn't seem particularly concerned. He nodded and said, "You may. But please exercise as much caution as possible, Mr. Jace Sesso."

Jace nodded. He approached the crystal and reached out his hand, stopping just before actually touching its surface. He closed his eyes and felt the chaotic magical power flowing in a regular pattern deep within the crystal.

He tried to stir these forces and activate the crystal. When he opened his eyes again, the crystal fragment had begun to glow, rolling with violet-blue flames.

In an instant, Jace felt the Shadow power throughout the entire space — including the magic within his own body — being drawn into flow by the crystal.

Instinctively he wanted to resist this force and regain control over his own power. The confrontation between the two quickly caused the crystal to release layer after layer of magical shockwaves, bathing the entire hall in a purple light.

The Magisters and Spellbreakers in the temple looked at their faces all wearing expressions of worry. Only Belovarl kept his gaze fixed on the human warlock before him. Vereesa wanted to say something, but seeing the Archmage's expression, she swallowed her words.

Jace gritted his teeth. In this back-and-forth struggle, he felt more and more that his own swelling, arrogant desire to enslave the crystal would only feed the crystal's power and cause it to control him in return.

He immediately pulled his runaway emotions back under control. Although the Shadow power diminished along with them, the crystal's resisting force seemed to drop as well.

After shifting his emotions a few times, Jace cast every influencing factor from his mind — forgetting the Magisters, forgetting Belovarl Saronar… forgetting all of Quel'Thalas, and most importantly, forgetting Vereesa. Then, within the negative emotions of loneliness and despair, he found a somewhat strange point of balance that could stably hold against the crystal's power.

As the confrontation gradually settled and the shockwaves dissipated, Jace slowly opened his eyes, confirming that his consciousness had pulled free.

"Are you all right, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

Jace took a deliberate deep breath, extracting himself from the emotions of a moment ago, before saying, "I'm fine. The power of this crystal fragment is very formidable."

"Impressive, Master Sesso," a strange Magister beside him couldn't help but remark. Though his voice was soft, it was quite clear in the quiet magic temple.

"Indeed it is," Belovarl nodded and said. "If you feel it meets your needs, Mr. Jace Sesso, then I can decide to gift it to you."

"Then this is the one," Jace said, glancing at the crystal once more. "Thank you, Archmage Belovarl."
