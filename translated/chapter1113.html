Chapter 1113: The Sunstar Tower

Coming out of the temple, Jace was still thinking about how the hell he was going to haul this enormous crystal back.

And that wasn't even the end of it — he had no idea how to install the thing on top of a mage tower either. He wondered if the Stonemasons' guild brothers knew anything about this kind of engineering. He was honestly pretty skeptical, because back at Nethergarde Keep it had been the High Elf Magisters who'd helped the Kirin Tor hoist that magic crystal up to the tower's peak.

"Archmage," Jace cleared his throat. "I'm not yet familiar with the properties of this magic crystal, so I'm not quite sure how to transport it back to Ilgalar Tower…"

Belovarl said, "Once the renovation work on Ilgalar Tower is complete, I will send several apprentices and Magisters to deliver this magic crystal to Stonewatch together. After all, this crystal represents more than just an apology from the Magisters — it is also a testament to the friendship between Silvermoon and Stormwind. With its support, I hope that no demon or dark force of any kind will ever threaten Lake Everstill or Lakeshire."

"I believe this crystal's power will certainly accomplish that," Jace said with a nod, a weight lifting from his mind.

Although Belovarl's tone was sincere, Jace couldn't shake the feeling that he was hinting at something. Especially that deliberate emphasis on "demon or dark force of any kind" — it sounded almost like a reminder: "You're one of them too, you know."

Still, the Archmage's emotions were well controlled. He didn't show the slightest trace of sarcasm or reluctance.

Belovarl said, "I'm afraid we won't reach the Sunstar Tower before tonight, Mr. Jace Sesso. Considering that humans require ample sleep every night, would you prefer we push through the night, or rest in Tranquilliin for the evening?"

"If the Magisters are willing, the sooner the better — I have no issue with it," Jace said. "After all, the Magisters are still waiting at the river fork."

"Very well," Belovarl said. "Then we depart before dusk."

Belovarl went off with the other Magisters toward the mage towers, probably to pay a visit to the Magisters' garrison here. Jace and Vereesa returned to the harbor side and waited for a while. The lines of strider riders passing by all bowed their heads to Vereesa in greeting, and also hailed Jace.

But these knights in silver armor and lances carried themselves with clear arrogance. Even their salute to the Ranger-Lord seemed like a perfunctory afterthought, and Vereesa couldn't be bothered to acknowledge them at all — she didn't return a single salute.

"Who are they?" Jace asked, eyeing the knights' dark-gold cloaks.

"The Sunfury Corps," Vereesa said coldly. "Those fellows just now are Sunstriker knights. A bunch of bastards who don't even know what their own fathers look like."

That was a lot to take in. Jace needed a moment to process it, but in the end he didn't have the nerve to ask where their fathers had gone. Given the tangled love lives of Quel'Thalas nobility, it probably wouldn't have mattered even if he asked.

"But they are indeed a group of remarkably brave warriors," Vereesa said, her tone softening slightly. "Relying on Magisters and Farstriders alone couldn't have held the line against the forest troll armies behind the Amani Mountains for this long."

The click of strider claws scraping against stone came and went in waves, mingled with the tread of High Elf guards' war boots. There was no familiar music, no chatter or laughter, not even much birdsong.

Tranquilliin was truly a military stronghold — perhaps this was precisely why it had once become the last bastion of southern Quel'Thalas to resist the Scourge, holding out long after every other village and town had fallen to wraiths and the undead.

The bright sky had grown slightly dim. Jace, Vereesa, Belovarl, and the attendants had all returned to the river fork.

They transferred back to the original sailboat and continued eastward from the harbor at the river fork. All the while, Jace was deep in an intensive Thalassian lesson under Vereesa's tutelage in the cabin, the perpetually gloomy scenery outside the window making him lose all track of time.

Just as he was getting drowsy, a Magister on the bow suddenly shouted in Thalassian, "I can see the Zendor Runestone — we'll reach Lake Elrendar soon, Archmage!"

Hearing the shout, Jace snapped to attention and came outside, just in time to catch sight of a blue magical rune flickering in and out of view on a distant hillside to the north. Only after the ship drew closer could he make out the legendary runestone, standing all alone beneath a red tree that already looked ancient.

Belovarl nodded from his side. The rune seemed to flicker, as if conveying something to Jace the outsider. He glanced at his pocket watch — it was past three in the morning.

Beyond the runestone, once it had completely disappeared from view, a pale golden statue emerged from the woodland on the southern riverbank — an elven warrior holding a sword high above his head along with a shield.

Behind the statue stood a short white tower with golden trim, its peak glowing faintly with Arcane energy, like a sapphire set in a crown.

"Is that the Sunstar Tower?" Jace asked.

"No," Vereesa said. "This is Corona Village. The Sunstar Tower is up ahead, where we enter Lake Elrendar and reach the eastern shore."

As she said this, Vereesa wore a "just you wait" expression, as though the next sight would be something truly stunning. In truth, Jace's impression of the Sunstar Tower from the game wasn't that strong — he vaguely remembered there being such a place on the map.

The sailboat didn't dock at Corona Village but sailed straight past the harbor and continued on. Before long, just as the sky was beginning to show the first signs of brightening, they sailed into a broad lake.

From here, the steep mountains covered in dark green dense forest were visible at the far end of the lake's horizon. These were the Amani Mountains — even the elves called them that, because they didn't want to forget that beyond this range lay the capital of the Amani Empire: Zul'Aman.

After the sailboat reached the eastern shore and docked near a small pier, Jace couldn't wait to get off. He first sized up the green mountains behind the forest, then lowered his gaze and peered down the path deep into the woods ahead.

From here he couldn't see any tall tower, nor much elven architecture at all.

Vereesa patted Jace on the shoulder. "Stop looking everywhere. Over there."

"What?" Jace looked south in the direction Vereesa pointed, following the lakeshore all the way to where the morning mist made things a little hazy. "Up higher," Vereesa prompted.

Jace raised his eyes and noticed that at the edge of his vision, high up in the lake mist, something seemed to be slowly turning. Wait… that was a small house — more precisely, that was a tower floating high in the sky.

Soon the tower turned back and was swallowed by the mist again, leaving Jace wanting more. But as the fog seemed to thin a bit in the morning breeze, a massive, almost terrifying white silhouette emerged beside the vanished little tower.

Because it appeared so suddenly, the sheer scale of this white shadow made the little boats beneath it and the trees along the shore look pathetically small by comparison, like a pile of carelessly scattered toy blocks.

He stared dazedly for another moment, watching as the shadow of another, smaller tower emerged from the mist, circled once, and blurred out of view again.

Only then did Jace realize — those solitary little towers were revolving around the main body of the great tower by the power of magic.

"That's the Sunstar Tower. It's not very clear from here," Vereesa said. "So? What do you think?"

"Rich," Jace nodded.
