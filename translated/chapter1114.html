Chapter 1114: Everyone on Edge

Following the path along the lake, the Sunstar Tower's appearance gradually came into focus.

This tall tower, resembling a silver-white staff, was ringed at its summit by beautiful turrets lifted aloft by magic stones. Its base was encircled by golden and red forests, situated on the shimmering shores of Lake Elrendar — truly shining like a morning star.

Perhaps the Sunfury Spire in Silvermoon was even taller and more magnificent, but set among the gorgeous and resplendent buildings between Silvermoon's streets and plazas, even the Sunwell's sanctuary paled in comparison, never mind the Sunfury Spire.

The Sunstar Tower, on the other hand, stood amidst meadows and mountain forests on the high ground by the lakeshore. The stark contrast made it especially prominent — the closer one got, the more one felt an almost suffocating sense of oppression.

Drawing a little closer, one could catch wave upon faint wave of a metallic Arcane scent mingling with the natural fragrance of forest and lake water. Jace glanced at the tall mountains beside him and thought that if he were an Amani scout, seeing this tower he absolutely wouldn't dare take another step forward.

Arriving at the foot of the steps leading into the Sunstar Tower, an elven guard spotted the group and immediately saluted before going into the tower to announce them. Before long, a Magister and a Farstrider appeared together at the main gate.

The Farstrider was dressed in a set of deep green casual clothes. Judging by his quite exquisite ornamental shawl and outer garment, this was likely a Ranger noble of considerably high status.

He smiled at Vereesa and lowered his head slightly. Vereesa smiled back politely in return.

Then the Ranger turned his gaze to Jace and gave a slight nod of acknowledgment, though Jace could sense a certain wariness and suspicion beneath his rather courteous exterior.

Actually, this Farstrider looked somewhat familiar, but Jace couldn't recall where he'd seen him before.

"Archmage Belovarl," the Magister beside the Ranger stepped forward and bowed deeply. "I didn't expect you would come to the Sunstar Tower in person."

"Magister Alionnis," Belovarl said, bowing as well. "I am not here to participate in the investigation of the Sunstar Tower and Drahelis's Tower. This time I am guiding Mr. Jace Sesso and Lady Vereesa Windrunner. After this I must return to the Datharvis Academy — I have a lecture there tomorrow, so I'm afraid I cannot stay long."

"Yes, Archmage," Alionnis said respectfully.

Jace listened to the two of them exchange a few words at his side and felt somewhat relieved.

Belovarl still seemed a little preoccupied with his Shadow magic. Though it wasn't overtly apparent, there were always hints between the lines. If he stood there watching Jace search through Dar'kan's belongings, Jace felt like he'd be uncomfortable all over, as if the man were waiting for him to slip up at any moment.

Earlier, in that temple, Jace had already gotten the feeling that Belovarl had allowed him to make contact with the Taralvethin Crystal so readily in order to force him to "reveal his true colors."

Of course, this might just be petty thinking. After all, given Jace's understanding and command of Shadow at this point, his still-decent state of health, and standing as he was on the resplendent banks of the Elrendar River, it was really difficult to pin these petty suspicions and misgivings on Shadow corruption.

The two Magisters exchanged a few pleasantries. The blond Farstrider at the side lowered his head and said, "It is an honor to meet you, Archmage."

"Good morning, Captain Halduron Brightwing," Belovarl said.

So this was Halduron Brightwing? Jace couldn't help but give the Ranger another look.

After the Scourge invaded Quel'Thalas, Sylvanas had died in the battle to defend Silvermoon. Vereesa, because Kael'thas Sunstrider intended to use Fel energy found in Outland to replace the Sunwell to alleviate magical addiction, refused to call herself a Sin'dorei — a Blood Elf — and remained in Dalaran.

And so Halduron Brightwing had taken up the mantle of Ranger-General, becoming one of Silvermoon's leaders after Kael'thas's expedition to Outland.

No wonder Vereesa, a Ranger-Lord herself, still showed such respect to this Ranger-Captain. Even though he was only a Ranger-Captain for now, his seniority and standing among the Farstriders were probably unmatched by all but a few.

Belovarl continued, "With you here, along with Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner and His Majesty King Wrynn's magical advisor Mr. Jace Sesso, I believe the investigation into Dar'kan Drahelis should proceed smoothly."

"That goes without saying," Alionnis chimed in.

"Then I must take my leave. If I depart any later, I'm afraid I might offend the head of the Datharvis Academy — he's far more petty than you'd imagine."

With that, Belovarl smiled, then turned and nodded to Jace and Vereesa in turn before walking down the steps.

Jace had expected the Archmage to vanish in a burst of resplendent Arcane light, the way powerful mages stereotypically abused their teleportation spells. But he simply walked away with measured strides, heading toward the pier.

"I believe this is our first formal meeting, Master Sesso." The Captain saluted in the Farstrider military fashion, then said, "Actually, we did meet in person at Lady Vereesa's promotion ceremony, but I imagine you wouldn't have recognized me then."

"That's true. My apologies," Jace said.

"Call me Vereesa, Master Halduron," Vereesa said with a smile. "You making it so formal makes me very uncomfortable."

"You'll have to try to get used to it from now on, my lady," Halduron said with a smile. "Please, come in, both of you."

The Ranger-Captain and the Magister led the two into the Sunstar Tower. The tower's interior was equally magnificent and imposing — looking up from the ground floor, one could see layer upon layer of spiral staircases extending dizzyingly into the heights, with crisscrossing walkways in between that made one's head spin the more one looked.

Though standing in the open wilderness by the lake, the entire tower was astonishingly clean. Jace wondered whether the tower's owner relied on magic brooms for cleaning or hired servants.

"I am outraged by that grave crime committed in Elwynn, and I must also offer an apology," the Magister said as they walked, with considerable deference. "I'm very glad to see you safe and well, Mr. Jace Sesso. The souls of Dar'kan Drahelis and his accomplices will certainly regret committing such a foolish act."

"He stopped at nothing to find that demonic artifact," Jace said. "His lust for power twisted his will, and his Shadow research accelerated the process."

Hearing Jace say this, Halduron raised his eyebrows meaningfully, as if somewhat surprised that a Shadow mage of all people — and one with such a reputation — would say something like that.

"Marla has already given us some information about that artifact, Master Sesso," he said, folding his arms. "It was something Dar'kan called 'Uthros,' though she wasn't entirely clear what it was specifically used for."

"Based on that name, combined with those unsettling clues we found in the library of Drahelis's Tower," the Magister said, turning his head, "we believe Dar'kan's objective is likely connected to a terrible thing that disappeared centuries ago."

This was genuinely unsettling. Jace could already feel the back of his neck tingling.

Alionnis continued, "We found the researcher in the Reliquary — Kaela Sunseeker — who gave the Dasmora demonic scroll to Dar'kan. Dar'kan traded her certain artifact leads and information that the Magisters still couldn't verify."

"I imagine what they exchanged was probably more than just 'information,' Magister," Halduron said with a smile.

"Combined with Marla's statements, the leads from this Lady Sunseeker," Alionnis said, glancing sideways at the Ranger, "along with some of Dar'kan's correspondence with the Violet Eye, we believe the thing he was actually searching for is called 'Utharus' — the source of the calamity in Deadwind Pass. Marla probably misheard the name. Moreover, it's something we had long believed didn't exist — not something an ordinary person would connect to a name they heard in reality."

"The artifact said to have slaughtered hundreds of jungle trolls in a single night," Halduron Brightwing said, scratching the golden hair at his temple. "I truly wish Quel'Thalas had one too."

"Trust me, Ranger-Captain," Alionnis said, staring at him with a grave expression. "When that day comes, you'll regret it."

The two of them absolutely did not expect that the very artifact they were worried about was currently strapped to the back of the man standing right in front of them — and Vereesa also gave an imperceptible swallow.

Although neither Halduron nor Alionnis detected anything, her guilt was practically spilling out of her eyes in Jace's perception.

Judging it this way, if Vereesa had truly been present at the great cemetery in Raven Hill back then, the moment Dar'kan said the name "Utharus," he would have immediately sensed something was off from Vereesa, and the fight would have broken out right then and there.

Of course, if she had been there, that battle would have started fast and ended fast, no doubt about it.

"If Utharus still exists, then the Order of Tirisfal has been covering up the truth," Alionnis said. "I still remember how the assistant to Guardian Skavar swore in Dalaran that he had destroyed the source of the Deadwind calamity and eliminated that demonic mage who sought to create more death."

"Perhaps it's all a misunderstanding," Halduron said. "Perhaps Dar'kan was just chasing leads in the wrong direction pointlessly. Don't forget he made several wasted trips to Kalimdor already. So, Mr. Jace Sesso — since Dar'kan was so convinced you knew something, even to the risk of damaging relations between our factions, I imagine you must have some other leads."

"The demon scroll I handed over to the Reliquary — the one written by Dasmora — was composed in Eredun," Jace said. "And even in the Grand Library of Dalaran, there aren't many Eredun dictionaries available for reference. So I think Dar'kan made contact with the Violet Eye to gain access to Karazhan, seeking a way to decipher Eredun runes from Medivh's collection."

Halduron stroked his chin and frowned. "Can't we just capture a Doomguard for interrogation? I imagine someone like Dar'kan must have learned some methods of enslaving demons from the orcs."

"According to research by the Order of Tirisfal from long ago, after the Eredun joined the Burning Legion they abandoned their own language, Captain Halduron," Alionnis explained. Then he said in Thalassian, "We may truly need to go to Dalaran and seek the cooperation of Archmage Otrus of the Violet Eye for this investigation."

"You want to get the Violet Eye involved?" Halduron also switched to Thalassian, his tone laced with suspicion. "Don't forget the Sun King doesn't want this matter to spread throughout the whole Alliance — though I imagine they've probably heard the news by now by this point."

"I just want to get to the bottom of these things sooner," Alionnis said.

Vereesa asked, "Didn't you find his 'Eredun dictionary' in Drahelis's mage tower?"

Jace also nodded and said, "Dar'kan must have successfully deciphered that scroll — at least, I'm already fairly familiar with demonic languages, but even from that scroll I couldn't tell what Dasmora was searching for, the thing you mentioned as Utharus. If Dar'kan knew more than me, then either he had already deciphered that scroll, or he had another source of information we don't know about. Either way, it could be connected to Karazhan and the Violet Eye."

"I find it hard to conclude whether or not a dictionary of that sort is in there," Alionnis said, looking at Vereesa, then at Jace, and shaking his head. "Although Dar'kan's research was quite controversial in Quel'Thalas, no one denies his standing as one of the most senior Magisters, Master Sesso, Lady Vereesa. His mage tower housed a vast quantity of magic tomes and research records — there may even be magic-concealed mechanisms and hidden chambers inside. Merely cataloguing the books and scrolls on the surface is already an enormously taxing undertaking."

"We'd better take you up to see for ourselves," Halduron said.
