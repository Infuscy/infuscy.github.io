Chapter 1115: There’s Another Secret

Magister Alionnis and Ranger-Captain Halduron Brightwing led Jace and Vereesa through the great hall of Dawnstar Spire to a gate on the other side. Beyond it was a courtyard filled with flowers and unnamed, plump fruits.

The path through the courtyard passed through another manor further out, then stretched eastward all the way to the forest at the foot of the mountain, where its shadow and traces finally disappeared among the trees.

But following this direction and looking up past the forest, one could eventually spot another, smaller white tower atop a high cliff — that must be Drathir’s Tower.

The group came to the manor’s outer stables and each mounted a hawkstrider. Jace had some experience from the last time he’d come to Quel’Thalas, when he’d ridden a hawkstrider to Farstrider Retreat, so he had a bit of familiarity. Plus, the hawkstriders bred at Dawnstar Spire were clearly more docile, so nothing went wrong.

It was the several elven servants nearby who set down their work and watched, as if waiting for this human to embarrass himself — though in the end they just resumed their tasks, somewhat disappointed.

Quel’Thalas hawkstriders were nothing like Earth’s ostriches. These beasts, which looked fragile and gaudy, were actually terrifyingly powerful in strength and physical toughness — otherwise their cousins wouldn’t have been able to contest territory with the tauren in Kalimdor.

After galloping through the woods for a while, even climbing uphill the entire way, Jace didn’t sense the slightest fatigue from the hawkstrider beneath him. In roughly half an hour they arrived at the cliffside white tower that had seemed quite far away when viewed from Dawnstar Spire.

Viewing it from the outside, no ordinary person would ever imagine this was the residence of a sorcerer who studied the Twisting Nether and Shadow-corruption magic. Bright and fresh — it was practically a saint’s manor. If this weren’t in Quel’Thalas but instead in the Kingdom of Stormwind, the residents of Elwynn would definitely take it for the dwelling of a benevolent divine emissary.

Passing through a courtyard where weeds had already begun to grow and entering the tower, the first thing that came into view was a blue Arcane golem in the corner. It didn’t look like it had been shut down normally — more like it had been dismantled by magic and toppled against the wall.

The Magisters had probably had a fight with the tower’s defenders when they came to search it.

The ground-floor hall was piled high with stacks upon stacks of all kinds of books and scrolls, along with all manner of stone tablets — elven, trollish, even human, you name it. Jace even spotted some tablets inscribed with a certain ancient giant tongue rarely seen even in Dalaran — a language possibly used by the legendary Keepers, as well as the vrykul.

A group of arcanists were hunched together, flipping through these mountains of books one by one, searching for anything suspicious. Summoned Arcane Aberrations used Telekinesis to help them organize the tomes they’d already examined and those they hadn’t yet gotten to.

Several Farstriders stood to the side, unable to be of much help — they could only watch. Seeing this, Halduron rubbed his brow and let out a deep sigh.

“That’s… quite a job,” Vereesa murmured.

“This is nothing,” Alionnis said. “After obtaining the authorization of the Silvermoon Council and the Magisters to enter this tower, dismantling the Arcane mechanisms here and the various forms of magic locks — it felt like being back in the academy again. And the layered puzzles inside Drathir’s mage tower are far more troublesome than any academy exam. To this day we still haven’t broken open quite a few of the magic seals — they’re stored in the warehouse on the left side of the second floor.”

“If only the Grand Magister would be willing to help,” Vereesa said softly.

Alionnis shook his head, then explained, “The hardest seals to crack aren’t exactly that difficult — they’re just layered one inside another, extremely taxing on the mind. Even if the Grand Magister came, he’d have to carefully undo each seal one by one, like peeling an onion. And of course, you can’t recklessly use some destructive spell to just cut the onion open — that would likely destroy the contents inside along the way.”

“I don’t think there’d be anything too important inside a seal like that — at least nothing related to Ulthalesh,” Jace said, watching the busy arcanists. “Otherwise it would be too inconvenient to consult at any time.”

“That’s what I think too, Master Sesso,” Alionnis said. “But we still have to find a way to open them one by one — after all, we can’t ignore even the slimmest possibility. Just like the search work here: until every single book has been flipped through, it’s hard to say we won’t miss something — letters, cipher spells, keys, casting materials…”

Jace surveyed the tower’s ground-floor hall, then asked, “Has every room in this tower been searched?”

“Of course,” Halduron said. “From the magic crystal platform at the very top to the basement.”

“Can I take a look at the basement?” Jace asked.

“Of course. Please, follow me,” Alionnis said.

The two of them followed the Magister and the Ranger-Captain to a side stairwell, descending a narrow spiral staircase to the deepest level. The basement foyer had clearly been sealed by magic, but it had already been broken open — though some Arcane residue still lingered on its surface.

Passing through a short corridor, they entered the basement of Drathir’s Tower. Several arcanists were already here conducting inspections and organizing materials. Though there were four or five rooms and two corridors, Jace genuinely couldn’t sense anything unusual.

He even tried using Sense Demons to look around, wondering whether Dar’Khan might have secretly kept a succubus hidden away somewhere — but that turned up nothing too.

Trying to hide a demon within the coverage of Ban’dinoriel probably wasn’t all that realistic to begin with.

And Jace remembered that the first time he’d come to Quel’Thalas, the mages at the gate had immediately detected Sazeno hiding in his bag. These mages’ eyesight was probably no worse than his own.

Could it be that there was truly nothing to be found in this mage tower?

Jace clearly remembered that Dar’Khan’s Shadow research was anything but superficial. He hadn’t just made deep contact with certain entities within the Twisting Nether — he’d even relied on those contacts and connections to search out a dark plane hidden in the deepest reaches of the Twisting Nether. In the future, the Blood Elf Magister Umbric and his followers had relied on Dar’Khan’s leftover research to find that place, and been transformed by the dark power there into semi-void beings — the Ren’dorei, void elves who later joined the Alliance.

Clearly, neither the Magisters nor the Farstriders had yet found this research of Dar’Khan’s.

Perhaps it was because Quel’Thalas at this time still rejected Shadow — unlike after the Sunwell’s explosion, when countless Blood Elves threw themselves into the study of Shadow and Fel, making it harder to detect this tower’s secrets. Or perhaps it was because the plague of the Scourge had damaged some of Dar’Khan’s seals, making it easier for the Magisters who came later to dig out his results.

In any case, this tower couldn’t possibly be as simple as it appeared now. Dar’Khan had very likely stored his research on Ulthalesh — and even necromancy, or more things that had to be kept secret — together with the information about that void space, in the same place.

“I’d like to take another look at the top floor,” Jace said.

“No problem,” Halduron said, glancing at Alionnis. The Magister also nodded.

Vereesa seemed to detect a shift in Jace’s tone and leaned in to ask quietly, “Did you notice something?”

“It’s too early to say,” Jace said softly.
