Chapter 1119: The Shores of Caer Darrow

The dragonhawks, escorted by the Gryphon Riders, descended near the pier beneath the high walls of Caer Darrow. Vereesa briefly spoke with a local sheriff, and a soldier rode his horse along the wall toward the southern gate.

While waiting for a response from inside the walls, Vereesa and Aeradhnia learned a few things about the orcs and the runestone. Jace raised his head to size up the guards on the ramparts and quickly surveyed his surroundings.

Caer Darrow was unlike most of the Eastern Weald — there was little greenery to speak of on the entire island. It was bare all over, giving it a somewhat shabby and dirty appearance.

In the distance, a patrol of cavalry rode past, their horses kicking up muddy water that sent the residents crossing the street scrambling to the sides in a miserable display.

A decade and a half from now in another timeline, Caer Darrow would be betrayed by its ruler, Lord Alexei Barov, and offered to the resurrected lich Kel'Thuzad.

That terrible sacrifice would turn Caer Darrow into an island of ghosts, transforming Barov's castle and residence into the "Scholomance" — where members of the Cult of the Damned and newly risen corrupted dead studied necromancy. And Alexei Barov himself would fall into darkness as a death knight, becoming the Lich King's slave, wandering day and night between crypts and halls.

Before coming to Caer Darrow, Jace had assumed that with the civilization-destroying Scourge invasion still over a decade away, this place should be lush and green, vibrant and alive, with every resident's face beaming with hope for a happy future.

But in reality, it wasn't like that at all. Some of the houses below the walls were still habitable; a small portion had become nothing but ruins.

The usable parts of those ruins had long since been carried away by other residents. All that remained were charred cinders and fragments of stone.

Yes, during the Orc Wars, Caer Darrow was one of the fiercest battlegrounds outside of Hillsbrad. Even Gul'dan himself had come here personally to oversee the construction of the Stormsummoning Altar.

Jace noticed that the condition of the wall itself seemed to have been repaired quite well, and there were still some craftsmen up there doing minor patchwork.

Over at the pier, the lakeshore situation also seemed rather decent. A group of children had gathered there to play in the water, their laughter and shouting more or less diluting some of the desolation and bleakness the orcs had left behind.

"Good afternoon, Master Mage."

A young woman's voice came from the side. Jace looked over and saw a woman approaching with several armfuls of scrolls and a heavy cloth bag carrying he knew not what.

She had short golden-brown hair, and was wearing a green linen shirt and trousers patterned with decorative motifs, topped with a thick fur outer garment. From her appearance and dress, one could tell she wasn't a fisherman or a farm laboring woman — her living conditions were probably quite comfortable.

"Good afternoon, my lady," Jace said. "You knew I was a mage?"

"Your ring," the woman said, dropping her gaze. "That's a ring of the Wizard Sanctum of Stormwind, isn't it? I'm not mistaken? During the Orc Wars I saw some mages wearing that ring."

As she said this, she frowned and tilted her eyes slightly. "But you sound like you're from the capital."

"That's right. I'm Lordaeroni," Jace said. "But I studied magic in Stormwind."

"You look like a mage of considerable accomplishment," the woman said after a moment's thought. "Actually, what I'm more interested in — do you also find the scenery here beautiful? If one looked through a mage's eyes from here, would one see something different?"

Jace looked carefully in the direction she was pointing and said, "Beautiful — certainly. But… I don't see much that's different."

Perhaps a real mage could see the surging power of the ley lines, Jace thought to himself.

She gestured toward the lake surface and mountains from the west to the south. "I've always wanted to reproduce this unique beautiful scenery on paper, but ever since the orc armies passed through here, I've felt the scenery here is different. I can no longer capture that feeling accurately… But in reality, that lakeshore and those hills weren't affected at all, Master Mage. I mean, the orcs didn't destroy that side, nor did they camp there. Compared to Caer Darrow, that place is almost exactly as it was ten years ago."

"Perhaps it's you who has changed," Jace said.

"Me?" the woman asked, puzzled.

"A person's emotions change many things," Jace said. "The Horde's armies trampled your homeland, forcing you to flee Caer Darrow to take shelter elsewhere. When you returned, facing your already-destroyed homeland and looking again at the once-familiar scenery, you would feel differently too."

As he finished, Jace looked into the distance — the pier, the hazy lakeshore at twilight — and was struck by a inexplicable sense of familiarity.

But this was his first time in Caer Darrow. Even in the game in his past life, he'd never come to this spot.

"Yes," the woman nodded. "Yes, I understand why now."

"What?" Jace asked.

"Nothing," the woman said with a shaking head and a smile. "Not long ago I painted a portrait here for a very good friend and his family. The family had come here on a trip and wanted to leave behind some memories of Caer Darrow, and as it happened I was someone who could paint, so I suggested it. Only, Caer Darrow had just gone through the war and was in chaos — really not suitable as a backdrop — so I chose this place."

The smile on her face faded a little. She continued, "While painting for the family, I felt as though I'd found the scenery of the past again — the Darrowmere of my memories. But ever since they left, it's as if I've lost that feeling again. Can you understand that feeling, Master Mage?"

Jace said distractedly, "At a particular moment, when you pour out all your emotions at once, it's as if you've exhausted everything you have, body and soul. Some time later, when you try to reproduce what you poured out, you feel that nothing is quite right — as if the emotions and inspiration you poured out belonged to someone else, and you never possessed them at all."

"Yes, yes, exactly," the woman nodded.

"Perhaps you should seek out some new emotions and inspiration to help you, my lady," Jace said.

The woman nodded, looking back into the distance. "Yes, you're right… I was too focused on every flower, every blade of grass, every stone in these scenery, and overlooked the factors beyond the scenery."

"Right — I haven't introduced myself yet," the woman said. "I'm Rifrev, from the city of Stratholme."

"Jace Sesso of Brill," Jace said with a smile.

"Jace Sesso." The woman immediately froze upon hearing the name. She asked, "The Lordaeroni mage of Stormwind — you're that Jace Sesso?"

"Yes," Jace said with a nod.

"By the Holy Light, I never imagined you'd be such a tall young man. Forgive my stereotypes of great mages…" Rifrev looked Jace up and down, unable to contain her excitement. "I heard you killed countless orcs in Grim Batol, and slew hundreds of demons near the Dark Portal… Is that true?"

"Not quite that many," Jace said, somewhat sheepishly. "But I have indeed defeated quite a few demons and orcs."

"If only I could have witnessed you fighting with magic…" Rifrev licked her lips, but then seemed to think of something and sighed. "I'd probably have been struck by a stray arrow, or burned to ashes by the flames of a red dragon or an orc warlock."

"Did you come to Darrowmere this time to clear out the orcs wandering here?" she asked. "I know there are still scattered orc tribes wandering all the way from here to Darrowmere Forest. Caer Darrow has these high walls for protection and the lake all around, so there's no worry — but from Corin's Crossing to Tir's Hand and New Avalon, many people are still living in fear."

Jace said, "I'm here investigating a case related to dark magic — in any case, it definitely has something to do with demons and orcs. Do you know anything about the elven runestone and the altar, Lady Rifrev?"

"You mean the one in the woods on the island to the north…" Rifrev said. "I'm sorry, Master Sesso. Mayor Maud'uk has explicitly forbidden anyone from approaching that island. So I'm not very clear on the situation there."

"Anyone?" Jace thought for a moment. "All right, my lady. I understand. I'll go have a word with that mayor."

"Be careful, Master Sesso," Rifrev said. "I only recently learned the news — that friend of mine was ambushed by a treacherous orc near his castle and severely wounded. He's an extremely formidable warrior and a member of the Silver Hand, and even he ran into something like this. The Weald right now is more dangerous than we imagine."

"A Silver Hand paladin?" Jace asked.

"Yes — that's what they call the members of the Silver Hand nowadays," Rifrev said.

"Your friend is Lord Tirion Fordring of Hearthglen?" Jace asked.

"Ah, yes — do you know him?" Rifrev asked.

"I… certainly know the Lord of Hearthglen," Jace said with a smile. "But he probably doesn't know me. I just, uh, I believe I recently heard he was wounded. I hope he recovers soon."

"Jace!" Vereesa waved from somewhere behind him. She and Magister Aeradhnia were now joined by several Lordaeroni-military-looking officers.

Jace waved back. "I have to go, Lady Rifrev. I hope you manage to recapture that feeling."

Rifrev nodded. "Thank you, Master Sesso. May the Holy Light bless your investigation with success."

As he left the lakeside, Jace looked back once more at the artist picking up her scrolls and continuing toward the pier. This scene, combined with the lake water in the distance, the pine-studded lakeshore, and the Alterac snow mountains glowing faintly blue in the distance, reminded Jace of why this felt so familiar.

This woman was the artist who had painted "Love and Family" for Tirion Fordring, one of the founders of the Knights of the Silver Hand.

Although this painting had poured boundless emotion from this celebrated artist, it wasn't a particularly famous painting in Lordaeron itself — at least nowhere near as well-known as it was in another world.

Because a few years from now, the Lord of Hearthglen, Tirion Fordring, would be tried for treason. The "orc" who had "ambushed" him was in fact the very person who had saved him from a collapsing tower.

This incident had caused this hero of the Orc Wars to gradually begin to wonder whether orcs were really a bunch of brainless otherworldly devils. But his sympathy for orcs had ultimately landed him in the courtroom of Stratholme.

From that point on, he became a name that couldn't be spoken in Stratholme, or in all of Lordaeron. To protect this painting, Rifrev had hidden "Love and Family" away in a secret place in Stratholme.

All the way until the Scourge had destroyed everything, and she herself had become a wraith wandering Caer Darrow, yet still thinking of giving adventurers who came here a chance to rescue the painting and bring it back into the light.

Since the end of the Orc Wars, Jace hadn't heard any news of Tirion Fordring and had assumed he'd already been tried. So he'd just returned to his own life, to accompany his family.

Perhaps it was misfortune, perhaps it was fate — in any case, all manner of accidents wouldn't allow him to hide in such peaceful happiness. Trouble and enemies would eventually come looking for him, and he'd have no choice but to deal with them in haste, until the Scourge descended and brought the end of the world.

Thinking of this, Jace couldn't help but pull his cloak tighter. A cold lake breeze blew, and he felt a chill.
