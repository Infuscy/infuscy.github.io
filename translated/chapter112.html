Chapter 112: Misery Loves Company

"To say something a little harsh," Jace said, glancing around to make sure there were no passersby, "even if you don't really believe in the Holy Light all that much, you could still try calling on the Holy Light's power for your own use. What's the harm in trying? It costs nothing."

Grede sighed and said, "If that worked, would I need to go begging the Church? I'm thinking about going back to Aerie Peak to find a mystic and apprentice under them."

Jace couldn't help glancing sideways at him. "You want to become a shaman?"

Grede muttered, "Shaman, shaman — it even sounds like troll talk. I mean learning from those mystics how to communicate with heaven and earth, how to summon wind and thunder. Actually I thought about it before the war even ended, but I just didn't have the talent."

He scooped up a stone and tossed it into the water. "That was right when I'd just gotten hurt, before they'd persuaded me to leave the order, but I'd already more or less seen how it was going to go. I tried what the mystics said, reaching out to the spirits, but I couldn't hear any voice of nature or words of the water. That feeling of frustration, you know what I mean. Plus I was still holding onto a sliver of hope that I might recover, so I didn't keep at it. After it was completely over, I never thought about becoming a mystic again — I just left the Hinterlands…"

Here, Grede's words came to an abrupt halt.

Jace noticed Grede sniffing constantly, his nose twitching, glancing at the river one moment, the road the next, and finally at Jace himself.

"Hold on, why do I smell burnt sootfire drake?" Jace asked, surprised. "You can actually smell that?"

As he said it, he pulled his hood up to his face.

"It's made from a sootfire drake's sail."

"Good lord." Grede stared wide-eyed at the brownish-red material. "Did you save up to buy this?"

"Of course not. I'm so broke right now I have to think twice before eating bread."

Jace tilted his head back — finally, someone to show off to.

"This is a gift from the King of Stormwind, the Defender of the City and Lord of Stormwind Keep — His Majesty Varian Wrynn."

"For real? What are you talking about?" Grede felt Jace's shoulder and sucked in a breath. "It really is sootfire drake hide! This is the real thing!"

"You sound so sure — does Aerie Peak have sootfire drakes too?" Jace asked, curious.

"Aerie Peak doesn't have sootfire drakes. Sootfire drakes are monsters of Khaz Modan," Grede said. "Back in the Grim Batol era, our ancestors used the leather of sootfire drakes that lived deep underground to make a lot of crafts, clothes, armor, little pieces of furniture and such. Some of those got brought to Aerie Peak. I practically grew up around those expensive little antiques. That smell… it's too distinctive."

"On Aerie Peak, a cloak made from sootfire drake sail would sell for a gold coin."

"Only a gold coin?"

"Only?" Grede felt Jace's forehead. "Are your brains fried?"

"But the tailor who handed this coat over to me said this thing could sell for three gold," Jace said.

Grede laughed and said, "Did you think it was Cunning's hide? Sell for three gold… Have you heard of Cunning? A giant sootfire drake that lived for over a hundred years, hunted by the dwarves of Ironforge. They say even real dragons feared its power. Its giant sail and scales and hide were made into over a dozen sets of clothes and armor, and each set could sell for a good dozen or even several dozen gold!"

"What a coincidence," Jace said. "That's exactly the same name as the sootfire drake that Larsson the tailor mentioned. You don't think it's the same beast, do you?"

"Huh?" Grede said. "Let me have another look."

He ran his hand over the coat again, thought for a moment, then said, "I'm not a professional sootfire drake hunter or leatherworker myself, so I can't say for sure whether this is the hide of some special sootfire drake. But I did hear that those clothes were all sent to Stormwind — that part's true. The king really gave this to you? You're not just bullshitting?"

"Of course he did. You want proof? I've even got a letter. Want me to show you?"

Grede waved his hands quickly. "Stop, stop, just keep it to yourself. I don't need any more shocks."

"Then it's most likely really Cunning's hide. I'm honestly jealous," he said, cupping his chin. "If Chief Falstad ever gave me a warhammer, even a cracked one, it'd be worth it. It's just a pity that even if they gave me a hammer, I wouldn't be able to use it anymore, would I."

"Why do you always talk like that?" Jace shoved him, and Grede's foot slipped and he nearly fell into the canal.

"The Holy Light Church won't take me, and I've got no talent for being a mystic. What am I supposed to do? Go learn tricks at the Wizard Sanctum?" Grede gestured with his hands as he laughed a couple of times. Jace couldn't even tell if he was mocking him or laughing at himself.

"So now you understand why I'm learning all that magic?" Jace asked.

Grede's smile froze, and his curled black beard drooped.

He pulled his coat tighter and said, "But dark magic still hurts the body, Jace Sesso. Keep using it and problems will come sooner or later. If you asked me to learn it, I absolutely wouldn't accept."

Jace thought to himself that he wouldn't let Grede learn shadow magic or fel magic either, he just didn't say it out loud.

He said, "Maybe once we get to Dalaran, you could try the Church in Southshore or the Lordaeron capital. They won't take you here, but maybe the requirements at the Church there are a bit lower? And watch what you say this time — ask the priests here more about the Light's teachings before you go. They won't let you be a priest, but if you actively ask to convert to the Church and follow the Holy Light, how could they refuse?"

"Too much hassle," Grede scratched his beard. "From what the priests here say, becoming a priest requires going to some school and going through training. I reckon getting recognized as a warrior would involve a whole messy process too."

"What about Ironforge?" Jace asked. "Aren't a lot of Bronzebeard dwarves followers of the Holy Light?"

"You want me to go to Ironforge?" Grede asked.

"Yeah, why not? Aren't they your kinfolk?"

"Kinfolk… I suppose they are," Grede scratched his beard. "Let's not talk about this now — it's too far anyway. Since you're not looking for work, how about we go practice swordsmanship outside the city tonight? Resume our regular lessons."

"Sure," Jace stood up and dusted off his pants. "I've been pretty cooped up these days too. Practicing swords will get me moving and clear my head."

Grede said, "Good thing I got a couple of practice wooden swords from the dwarves the other day. We can go wild with them."
