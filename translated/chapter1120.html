Chapter 120: The Curse of the Runestone

Seeing Jace walk over lost in thought, Vereesa glanced toward the pier and asked, "What's wrong, Jace? Do you know that person?"

"No," Jace said. "I heard from her that quite a few orcs are still roaming the area, and they're causing a lot of trouble."

"Yes, Master Sesso," the Caer Darrow officer before them said. "There have been frequent reports of orcs appearing from here all the way to the eastern coast. Fortunately, things have calmed down considerably around Andorhal."

"Is it connected to the runestone?" Jace asked. "Or rather, that Stormsummoning Altar built by the orcs."

"You mean those orcs are trying to take the Stormsummoning Altar back?" The officer shook his head. "That altar was destroyed by a group of elven mages — blown into a huge pile of rubble by powerful magic. No matter what, there's no way orcs could use those wrecked stones to summon demons."

"I still remember it — I've never seen such terrifying magic in my life," a soldier at the side whispered.

The officer looked at the Ranger and Magister beside him. "These two ladies should know about it too."

"I know," Aeradhnia said. "After the Orc Wars ended, many Magisters searched for and destroyed ancient elven relics left behind south of the Gate of Quel'Thalas — especially those that had been tainted and exploited by the orcs — and salvaged artifacts that hadn't yet been corrupted."

"Was Dar'kan Drahelis among them?" Vereesa asked. "Since he was so interested in Shadow magic, he certainly wouldn't have missed that opportunity, given how closely the orcs were tied to demons and the Twisting Nether."

"Very likely," Aeradhnia replied. "But the Magisters don't have a specific list of participants, because the situation was quite chaotic at the time. The area south of the Gate of Quel'Thalas was under the command of Lady Alleria Windrunner…"

"Then does Caer Darrow have records from that time?" Jace asked.

"I can take you to see Mayor Maud'uk, everyone," the officer said. "He was in the Darrowmere area throughout the war. He should know very well what happened at the time."

"We appreciate it, everyone," Vereesa said.

Led by several soldiers, the three guests from Quel'Thalas followed the road between the wall and the lakeshore heading south.

The townspeople living beneath the wall showed absolutely no curiosity about the visiting elves. On the contrary, among the children playing in the streets, there was even one half-elf whose appearance was noticeably a bit different.

The children chased and played while shouting at the half-elf: "Cat ears! Cat ears!" Aeradhnia glanced over from time to time, harboring a trace of anger imperceptible to the naked eye. Vereesa, on the other hand, didn't seem to mind at all — she even kindly greeted the children who tried to say hello to her in simple elven from time to time.

Probably she'd gotten used to living in Stormwind and didn't feel there was anything insulting about the nickname "cat ears" — it was even kind of cute.

In the game, Caer Darrow Island was connected to the southern shore of Lake Darrowmere by a large bridge, so rather than an island it was more like a peninsula.

But here, the south was still quite a long distance from the lakeshore, so this was a true lake-island cluster.

At this hour, some small boats with yellow and white sails were setting out from the lower harbor toward the south, or heading north — in the direction of the northeastern shore, the more prosperous Darrowshire.

Led by the soldiers, the group followed a wagon train through the city gate. Looking up, they could take in the entire town of Caer Darrow within the walls at a glance.

The road intertwined with crooked, uneven houses all the way up to the highest point of the hill, where atop the cliff reinforced with stone and wood, several barracks and towers stood alongside an imposing dark castle — that must be the castle of the Barov family, the future site of the Scholomance.

Inside the walls, the traces of the Orc Wars weren't as obvious as outside.

Though still somewhat chaotic, all the residents, travelers, and soldiers seemed to have recovered from the war and returned to normal life. On the other side of the street, between several houses, a tall man in a white shirt yielded the road to the passing wagon and waved toward them.

"Neither the elven kingdom nor Darrowshire notified me of your visit, ladies and gentlemen," the man said rather excitedly. "It's an honor to meet you, Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner, Master Sesso, and…"

"Aeradhnia," the Magister introduced herself.

"Respected Lady Aeradhnia," the man said with a nod. "I am Maud'uk, the mayor of Caer Darrow."

"Maud'uk?" Vereesa looked the mayor up and down. "I heard there was also a human lord skilled in swordsmanship named Maud'uk — is that you, Mayor?"

"No, no — I absolutely wouldn't call myself 'skilled' with a sword," the mayor said quickly, shaking his head. "You're probably thinking of Lord Marduke Blackpool of Stratholme. He's a renowned master swordsman. He once stayed in Caer Darrow when he went to Durnholde to watch the arena matches."

"All right, I apologize," Vereesa said.

"It's no trouble — Maud'uk is a very common name in the Eastern Weald, very easy to mix up for someone just arriving here," the mayor said with a smile. "I heard you've come about the matter of that altar. Please, come talk at my place — looks like it's going to rain outside in a bit. The rain this time of year probably comes mixed with hail. You're used to living in Quel'Thalas, so you might find it hard to take."

Following the mayor down a muddy street, Jace recalled the person Vereesa had mentioned earlier — that was most likely the death knight lord who would make the entire Weald tremble in terror in the future: "Blackhand Marduke."

He couldn't help but whisper to Vereesa at his side, "Why would you care so much about a human swordsman?"

"As a warrior, of course one should understand every potential opponent," Vereesa said. "It's the same reason you're interested in what Dar'kan left behind…"

Jace nodded. The group arrived at a two-story house in an alley. The mayor opened the door, and the moment they stepped in they were immediately enveloped by the warmth of the fireplace.

"You can hang your cloaks over there, sirs and ladies," Mayor Maud'uk said.

"We won't disturb you here for too long, Mayor," Jace said, glancing at the two elves beside him. "We intend to go to the altar on the northern island with our own eyes to see the situation there. But I heard Caer Darrow has sealed off that island. Why is that?"

"That's correct," Maud'uk said. "After the war, Caer Darrow suffered severe damage. Before Lord Alexei had returned, many townsfolk were eager to repair their own homes, so quite a few people went to that island and carried away stones from the ruins of that orc altar to patch up the damage to their houses…"

"But those stones used to summon demons themselves possessed terrifying Void-demon power," Aeradhnia said.

"Yes, my lady," Maud'uk continued. "Haunted disturbances began appearing in town at first — initially dismissed as rumors. Then people fell ill… Even the Silver Hand and the Church couldn't resolve the problem. Not until the mages from Dalaran came did they help us understand the source of these strange problems. In the end it was the Kirin Tor who paid to help those residents rebuild their houses and deal with those eerie stones before the matter was resolved. Since then, that island has been off-limits to anyone."

"If you're willing to take a look, I can apply to His Lordship's magic advisor. I'm sure he'd welcome mages and elven emissaries like you helping solve the problems on that island."

"We also need to understand the situation of Quel'Thalas helping to deal with that altar at the time, Mayor," Vereesa said. "Are there any related records?"

"There should be — also at His Lordship's residence," the mayor said. "But it's getting late now, everyone. Tomorrow morning I'll take you up to see him."
