## Chapter 1121: The Enchanter

Mayor Maud'uk warmly invited the three guests from Quel'Thalas to stay in his house and set out for the castle the next day.

Jace wasn't sure if this was the mayor's own residence or property, but in any case it was not only spacious with several bedrooms, but also quite nicely appointed in terms of furnishings and decor. Certainly not comparable to the residences of the elves in Quel'Thalas, but by the standards of a human town, these were remarkably fine conditions.

Just as the mayor had said, by the time the group walked from the gate to this alley and discussed what time to go to the castle to see the lord tomorrow, the weather outside had already turned gloomy.

Jace reached out the second-floor window and could already feel the cold raindrops mixed in the chilly wind. The sound of wagon wheels, voices, and livestock outside was swallowed by the rain.

The mayor's shadow hurried away wrapped in a rain-cloak. Vereesa leaned against the wall, her gaze following him all the way until he disappeared around the corner.

She looked back and said, "What a waste of time. Perhaps we should have brought more Farstriders along — that way we could make this Alexei Barov take me, a Ranger-Lord, a little more seriously, instead of making us sit around here doing nothing for a night."

The Ranger glanced again toward the castle up high. From this alley at the moment, one could only make out the watchtower beside the castle. "It's not even late yet. Even for a human, their time for sleeping and resting is a bit too early."

"Because of the Sun King's passive policy during the Orc Wars, the attitude of the entire Eastern Weald toward Quel'Thalas isn't exactly warm right now, Lady Vereesa," Aeradhnia said at the side. "When Caer Darrow came under orc attack, despite having a runestone that had stood for thousands of years, Quel'Thalas still didn't lend a hand, allowing Caer Darrow to be occupied and ravaged by the orc armies. Perhaps for this reason, the human lord of Caer Darrow doesn't wish to show too much friendliness either."

"This was already evident when we discussed how to handle the runestone. The Barov family believed the altar should be entirely under their control, while Quel'Thalas believed it should be completely destroyed to prevent the orcs from exploiting it again. If not for the harassment by small orc tribes that truly made the rulers here recognize the danger, forcing them to accept our suggestion, I'm afraid this matter would still be stuck at the negotiation table."

Vereesa drew a deep breath, then slowly let it out. She frowned and glanced out the window again. "If we delay another night, who knows where the people who helped Dar'kan transfer important evidence might use this time to run off to."

"Is this mayor trying to stall for time?" she asked, looking at Jace and the Magister at the side. "Dragging us here, then running back to the castle to report to the lord here."

"You mean the person who helped Dar'kan transfer evidence is Lord Alexei Barov himself," Aeradhnia said.

Jace certainly had this suspicion too — he just didn't want to let future events cloud his judgment of the present. After all, Caer Darrow hadn't yet become a city of wraiths, and Alexei hadn't yet offered his soul to the Lich King.

Still, Vereesa's suspicion was well-founded, especially that mayor's hasty departing figure.

Jace just hadn't sensed any intent to deceive from the mayor. He wasn't lying — at least he genuinely believed it wasn't very suitable to see His Lordship this evening.

But then again, either the whole of Caer Darrow truly had nothing to do with Dar'kan, or this mayor himself didn't know the full picture of what was going on.

"If the runestone of Caer Darrow has been controlled," Vereesa said, "then wouldn't the most suitable place to teleport Dar'kan's evidence be right inside that castle? Unfortunately, even if we could get inside that castle, we couldn't conduct a thorough search. This place is far too suspicious."

"We don't need to search that castle thoroughly, Lady Vereesa. We just need to look at the island where the altar is tomorrow," Aeradhnia said. "If that altar has been reactivated as a runestone to deceive the power of Bandinoriel, I'll be able to tell at a single glance. Then everything will naturally have its answer."

"What if they secretly tamper with things tonight?" Vereesa asked. "Using the time we're sitting around here doing nothing. For example… using magic to shut down those stones or something."

"Uh, that's not so simple a matter — even for a Sin'dorei, never mind the mages here," Aeradhnia said.

"Difficult, but feasible — am I right?" Jace asked. Aeradhnia blinked, then reluctantly nodded.

"Then we'd best go take a look tonight," Jace said.

"That's exactly what I think," Vereesa said with a smile.

"You mean…" Aeradhnia said softly. "You mean sneaking onto the island without Alexei Barov's permission, Master Sesso? That could bring some unnecessary trouble — I mean political trouble."

"As long as we're not discovered, there won't be any kind of trouble," Jace said.

Aeradhnia glanced in the direction of the castle. She was clearly worried that a leak would intensify the tensions between Caer Darrow and Quel'Thalas, so she hesitated.

"If you're not willing, the two of us will go," Vereesa said.

"I was ordered by Master Alionnis to come here to help you, Lady Vereesa, and the importance of this matter to all of Quel'Thalas…" Aeradhnia frowned. "If you truly intend to do this, I can't fail to help. It's just…"

"I'll take responsibility, Aeradhnia," Vereesa said. "If we really get caught by those Caer Darrow soldiers, just say you were coerced by a lawless Ranger-Lord and a corrupt, degenerate human warlock…"

Jace muttered, "Warlock is fine, but must you add those weird adjectives?"

Vereesa glared at him, as if to say you know perfectly well what you've done. Jace couldn't help but cough and lowered his head.

"All right, you don't need to take responsibility for my decision, Lady Vereesa," Aeradhnia said in Thalassian. "This is for Quel'Thalas after all."

"Very well, dear Aeradhnia. When we get back to the Archmage, I'll put in a good word for you," Vereesa whispered.

"Best not mention this at all, Lady Vereesa," the Magister said softly.

"I know what you mean," Vereesa said.

Jace took down his cloak and staff. Aeradhnia pinned up her long hair. Vereesa peered down below the window. The mayor had assigned two guards to this house — one was standing dazed at the front door, the other was on patrol and had just rounded the corner.

If they were simply staying here, they'd feel the mayor had thoughtfully provided security for his guests. But the moment one suspected they were planning to sneak to that island tonight, these two men looked exactly like jailers guarding prisoners.

"What if they notice there's no one inside?" Jace asked.

"I can leave a phantom duplicate at the window here," the Magister said. "The people here know Sin'dorei don't need to sleep every night, so they shouldn't be suspicious."

"How long can it last?" Jace asked.

Aeradhnia said, "If I don't let this duplicate cast spells or converse, just walk back and forth from time to time to deceive the ordinary people here, it won't consume much mana. It can last several hours."

"That's enough," Vereesa said. She went to the window on the other side, secretly watching the patrolling man disappear around the corner. "Let's go."

The moment she finished speaking, she jumped down, making almost no sound upon landing. Jace followed her out the window, nimbly grabbing the eaves and window ledges to climb down. That was when he saw Aeradhnia leap straight out, then drift slowly to the ground.

Was that Slow Fall? Jace hadn't even clearly heard the incantation.

As she landed, she glanced at Jace with a trace of surprise, as if puzzled why the renowned Jace Sesso would be climbing down by hand.

"Let's hurry," Jace said, looking at her as he dusted off his clothes.

The three of them threw on their cloaks and pulled up their hoods, blending into the crowds hurrying to take shelter from the rain under the curtain of rain and passing through the gate. The lake water was pitch black at this hour, save for some lights on the distant pier.

"How are we getting across?" Aeradhnia asked.

"There are some fishing boats over there," Jace said, pointing to a cove beside the pier. "We can 'borrow' one."

Leaving the main road, the three trudged through the mud to a spot not far from the lakeshore. Jace observed the soldiers standing at the pier, then looked back at the watchposts on the wall behind them — one of the soldiers seemed to be looking this way. Stealing a boat directly like this would be quite difficult.

Just then, Vereesa patted him and pointed at the northern lake surface. "Look over there, Jace."

Jace looked in the direction she pointed and could make out a dark, hazy island shadow there — that should be the island where the altar was. Even in the cold rain, two patrolling boats with lantern lights could be seen cruising near that island, with possibly more further out.

"Patrol boats. It won't be easy to get across now," Vereesa said through gritted teeth. "Damn it. Makes me more and more suspicious about what's actually up there."

Aeradhnia walked to the shore, crouched down, and touched the water's surface, then rubbed her fingers together. "Perhaps there's another way to get across, Lady Vereesa."

"What?" Vereesa asked.

The Magister's lips moved faintly, then she dipped her fingers into the lake again. When her fingers touched the water's surface, a layer of ice rapidly formed.

"Just… walk across?" Jace asked in surprise. "Can you cast this kind of magic on the two of us as well?"

"I'm an enchanter, Master Sesso," she said, raising her long eyebrows as she looked over. She stood up and observed the lights at the pier and the gazes of the soldiers on the wall. "Let's go to the northern part of the wall — it looks less crowded there. We'll try entering the water over there."
