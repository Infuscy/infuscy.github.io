Chapter 1122: The Dark Island

By the time the three of them reached a stretch where few houses stood, where no guards patrolled, and where darkness and silence pressed in from all sides, Aeridania pinched a thread of arcane energy between her fingers, gathering more and more frost power until a white cold glow flushed her faintly reddish cheeks purple.

Once the incantation was complete, she pointed at Sylvanas, and the cold light dimmed and dissipated.

Sylvanas tested the water with her boot, and when she shifted her full weight onto it, a sheet of ice formed right beneath her foot, letting her stand steadily on the surface.

Aeridania immediately cast again, applying the same spell to Jace. He felt a chill run through his whole body, and his hands and feet went numb and weak from the cold.

But just like with Sylvanas, when he stepped toward the water, the cold beneath his feet rapidly condensed, letting him walk on the surface. Still, he could tell this thin sheet of ice wasn't as solid as it looked — especially when a ripple rolled over, the moment his boot left the water the ice shattered instantly.

Sylvanas was just too good at balancing her own body, which was why it looked as easy as walking on flat ground. For him it was a completely different story. At the very least, fighting on this lake surface was pretty much impossible.

"Even with the cold rain, this spell won't last until dawn, you two," Aeridania said. "So let's get moving quickly. But I think this should be at least a little more discreet than taking a boat."

Sylvanas nodded and set off at a run. Jace watched her back and couldn't help but feel the whole walking-on-water business was a bit over the top. Still, he took a breath and followed her in, and to his surprise — someone totally unfamiliar with this kind of magic — he could just about manage to keep steady footing on the water.

After they'd run a good distance out onto the lake, Jace glanced back at the island of Caer Darrow, its lights twinkling everywhere, then looked ahead toward that unknown isle cloaked in darkness. In the distance, patrol boats drifted like ghost ships, sometimes near, sometimes far. A faint sense of loneliness slowly crept into his chest, along with a vague dread of being surrounded by boundless dark.

But he quickly realized this fear hadn't come naturally. The closer they drew to that island, the more a shadow power — so faint it was barely perceptible — settled over them bit by bit.

Was this the power of Gul'dan's Storm Altar? Or the power of the corrupted runestones? Jace couldn't quite tell. But one thing was clear: the mayor's decision to seal off this island wasn't without good reason.

After walking for several dozen minutes, dodging the patrol lights from Caer Darrow's soldiers all the while, the three of them finally set foot on the little island.

Now that he was actually on it, though, Jace didn't feel any stronger shadow power. The pines and the grass here looked withered and dim, but they hadn't been corrupted by demonic power into the look of the Blasted Lands, or to the degree of the land around that Storm Altar in the Burning Steppes. The Quel'Thalas magisters' decision to destroy this Storm Altar had probably saved all of Darrowmere.

The three followed the lakeshore for a while, looking for a path that would let it them push deeper into the island's interior. After walking a bit, they noticed a boat moored at the shore in the distance, its lantern still lit, with a single man standing guard beside it.

"A night this late, cold rain like this, and they show up right when we were planning to come ashore tomorrow…" Sylvanas murmured. "That can't possibly be a coincidence."

"Let's slip in through this stretch of woods and have a look," Jace said. "I can more or less feel where the source of this island's shadow power is. I'll lead the way."

The two elves nodded, and Jace took the lead into the trees.

The woods weren't as extensive as he'd imagined. Before long they came to a clearing full of broken stumps, and in the distance they could see a stretch of collapsed ruins. From the remaining broken walls and timber, it was still possible to make out that these were orcish structures.

Yet on the far side of the camp, torchlight flickered. "Orcs?" Aeridania sucked in a breath.

"No." Sylvanas pointed at a mass of shifting dark shadows beside the orcish ruins. "Humans. They're tearing down this camp. What are these people doing here?"

"Well, that's something we'd have to ask Lord Alexei Barov," Jace said.

He looked toward the lower ground beyond. In the middle of it was a fairly large pile of ruined stone — that should be where the Storm Altar was.

There were also a few human figures over by the altar, picking things up. Everything they dismantled was being hauled away to the woods on the island's far northern side.

"Let's get a little closer," Jace said.

The magister, the ranger, and the warlock used the curtain of darkness and rain to creep closer, hiding among the stumps and the remains of the orcish ruins. As they went, they could even faintly hear the people on the other side talking. But with the rain it was hard to make out the specifics — at most they caught the occasional "hurry up, before daybreak" and the like.

"Are they Dath'remar's helpers?" Sylvanas asked.

Jace swept the people with demon detection, then scanned the surrounding woods. No sign of any demons.

Aside from the darkness radiating from the altar itself, he couldn't feel any shadow magic from those people. "Not sure," he said. "Even if they are, they probably would have destroyed the evidence."

As he spoke, he glanced over at Aeridania. "Can you feel the power of the runestones from here, magister?" Aeridania shook her head. "We might need to get closer and try."

Jace gave an "mm" and stood up, continuing to creep forward.

This Storm Altar was far smaller than the one in the Burning Steppes. A standing humanoid statue had lost its upper half — its original face, like the Dark Portal and that altar, should have been a faceless warrior in a hood holding a longsword. The other statues had simply vanished altogether.

Perhaps this altar had once been large, but after being destroyed and having many of its stones carried away, it had ended up in its current state.

The three of them hid behind this side of the rubble pile. Some of the people on the other side had already left with their haul, and things had quieted down quite a bit.

Aeridania climbed up onto the rubble pile, onto a platform riddled with cracks and broken walls. She examined the damaged statue carefully, then looked around, her expression growing uglier and uglier.

"What is it?" Jace asked.

The magister hesitated for a moment. "I can't feel the power of the runestones at all, whether corrupted or normal…"

"…In short, if someone wanted to set up a summoning array on this island or near Darrowmere and secretly transfer something out of Bantinore without anyone knowing, it would be absolutely impossible. At the very least, this altar couldn't do it."

"So we're still one step too late, and this altar's power has already been shut down?" Jace asked.

"It's not like that." Aeridania climbed down from the platform and explained in a low voice. "There simply isn't enough here that could ever be 'opened,' let alone shut down. All that's left of this altar is a pile of shadow-tainted broken rocks. It's completely lost the power a runestone ought to have."

"So did I misjudge the situation?" Jace frowned. "That array isn't meant for teleportation?"

"If this altar is already completely useless, then what are these people doing here?" Sylvanas asked.

"Maybe the same thing we are — planning to see if there's anything useful left," Aeridania said.

"So what is the useful stuff?" Jace asked.

"Perhaps the runes," Aeridania said. "I mean the parts of the runestones where the runes are carved — they're all gone here. The power of those ancient runes is extremely resilient; it's very difficult to completely erase their power through simple destruction. After all, their original purpose was to sustain the magical barriers protecting Quel'Thalas."

Jace still remembered the patterns shimmering on that runestone he'd seen by the banks of the Elrendar River. Even from a distance, he could feel the sheer density of arcane power.

At that, the magister seemed to recall something and turned back.

"Wait… Gul'dan once borrowed those runes as the main conduits for a Storm Altar to channel ley line power. He even tried to use the runes to seize the Sunwell's power. He clearly understood this too, you two."

"What do you mean?" Sylvanas asked.

Aeridania explained, "If someone moved those runes elsewhere, they might be able to activate the runestones' power and create a small arcane barrier to fool Bantinore — to mimic the runestones' power. Given Dath'remar Drathir's level of understanding of this magic, it's not entirely impossible, especially considering Gul'dan nearly managed the same thing himself…"

"So we need to track down where those runes were moved to," Sylvanas said.

Jace put his hands on his hips. "Maybe tomorrow the lord of Caer Darrow will be willing to give us a few leads." Sylvanas sighed. "I'm not very optimistic about that."
