## Chapter 1123: The Barovs

The above-ground portion of Caer Darrow castle looked quite impressive from the outside, but from the inside the space felt somewhat cramped and confined.

However, following the lead of a Barov servant, walking down a broad staircase and deep into the castle's underground levels, was when you truly reached the "main body" of the fortress.

This was the place where the Scourge would one day establish Scholomance. Although Jace had "toured" this vast underground palace more times than he could count, and had already steeled himself mentally before entering, seeing its original state — before it was occupied by ghosts, shambling corpses, and necromancers — still shook him a little.

The candelabras in the corridors that stood like bronze trees, the expensive carpets so clean you couldn't feel a speck of dust or a stain, the paintings hanging on the walls — filled with mysterious imagery, or painted with a fineness that even laymen found a little suffocating, depicting figures and landscapes.

There were several paintings Jace was certain he'd seen near-identical copies of in Lordaeron, Dalaran, and elsewhere — some he'd even seen multiple times. As to which ones were the originals, that was self-evident. What surprised Jace instead was that such precious paintings weren't stored away in a vault but were displayed so brazenly out in the open. God only knew what kind of rarities were kept in the depths of this castle.

If not for the walls, which were slightly darker in color due to the stone, giving the place a more subdued and aged feel, Jace would even have said the scale and splendor here already rivaled the residences of Lordaeron's royal house. At the very least, it was leagues beyond Varian Wrynn's garden and his palace of only a few corridors.

Before the Dark Portal — back when Jace was a child — the Barov family had enjoyed extraordinary prominence. But years of the Orc Wars and the rampaging remnant Horde forces had laid waste to the bulk of the Barov family's property and lands, forcing them to keep a much lower profile in recent years.

Yet walking into this underground palace, Jace could immediately sense, beneath the slight air of decay, the past glory and wealth of this family.

He wasn't the only one. Aeridania also looked somewhat taken aback. Even Sylvanas, a high elf noble who'd grown up in Quel'Thalas, wore a rather astonished expression on her face, eyeing the incomprehensible paintings on the walls with curiosity.

Jace also knew that, prepared as he was, Sylvanas had probably never imagined another world like this hidden beneath this castle.

Still, maintaining these palaces and treasures required enormous expenditure. Although Caer Darrow looked as though it had more or less recovered from the war, the whole of the Eastern Weald still lived under the threat of the orcs. The Barov family's other properties and lands — in places like Hillsbrad Foothills and Tirisfal Glades — weren't much better off.

Warchief Orgrim Doomhammer was still at large, Warsong chieftain Grom Hellscream's whereabouts were unknown, and reports and rumors of orcs appearing in the mountains and forests were everywhere… Under these circumstances, the Barov family could hardly maintain their pre-war scale of operations by going about business as they had before. They definitely needed some extra income.

Jace looked at the guards lining the corridors, the courteous servants passing by in a hurry, the burning candles, the carpet beneath his feet, and felt that with every step he took, with every second that passed, gold coins jingled.

Thinking it over, it wasn't hard to guess what those people they'd seen hastily dismantling the camp on the northern island last night had been up to.

After crossing a corridor that stretched like a wooden bridge, entering the passage at its end, and descending another stretch of stairs, they came to a fairly spacious reception hall. A long dining table was already set with decorative candlesticks, and on the far side of the table sat a middle-aged man who looked to be in his forties or fifties.

He was leaning against the arm of his chair, looking quite exhausted. Even from this distance, Jace could see plenty of gray streaking his dark brown hair.

Jace, Sylvanas, and Aeridania had already entered the hall, but the man merely raised his head slightly to glance their way, then gestured at the seats beside the table. "Please, sit wherever you like, Ranger Lord Sylvanas Windrunner, Magister Jace Sesso, and Magister Aeridania. I only just learned of the news of your arrival — I haven't had time to prepare any gifts."

Although he hadn't introduced himself, judging by his age and tone of voice, this should be Lord Alexei Barov himself.

This hall felt rather empty — the lord of Caer Darrow's voice even carried an echo when he spoke.

But not everyone standing around was a servant or a guard. There was also a young black-haired man who, though he appeared low-key, stood out rather starkly among the retainers.

The man looked only a few years older than Jace, probably about Varian Wrynn's age. He was tall and thin, and the shirt, trousers, and gold-trimmed waistcoat he wore all looked a touch too snug on that lanky frame.

He adjusted the monocle on his eye, and his gaze undisguised its impatience toward Jace and the other outsiders. And Jace could clearly feel disgust, even some anger, emanating from his heart. This was also the reason Jace had been able to pick him out from a crowd of indifferent, impassive servants at a glance.

"I thought the matter of that altar had already been settled with the Quel'Thalas magisters," Lord Alexei said in a measured tone. "Not every landowner cares for others casting such terrible destructive magic on their territory."

"This matter is connected to Quel'Thalas's most recent involvement in crimes related to shadow magic, my lord," Sylvanas said. "The magisters and the Farstriders suspect it may be connected to an uncontrolled runestone."

"Quel'Thalas has over a dozen runestones sitting in it," Lord Alexei said with a smile. "And the runestones on that northern island have almost no power left — that's what your magisters guaranteed."

"Do you remember which magister gave that guarantee, my lord?" Aeridania asked.

"You'd have to ask my adviser, Mr. Purkett." The lord looked toward the magister and coughed twice. "I'm not so familiar with the arcane, altars, orc magic, and the like, Aeridania. After all, a human's lifespan is limited. We must choose which knowledge we wish to pursue."

"Then could we speak with this Mr. Purkett?" Jace asked.

Lord Alexei said slowly, "He should be in Tarren Mill right now, investigating a troll-related case with several mages from the Kirin Tor. Most unfortunate timing. I rely heavily on Mr. Purkett. Every matter great or small within my territory that pertains to magic requires his assistance. As a court mage adviser to King Varian Wrynn, I imagine you understand this sort of situation quite well, Magister Jace Sesso."

Jace could only nod. Sylvanas pressed her lips together impatiently — the dismissive intent here didn't even bother to pretend otherwise.

But then again, last night on that island they hadn't uncovered any direct evidence linking the Barovs to Dath'remar, so there was no good reason to make a scene outright.

"If you'd like to go to Tarren Mill to find him, I can assign several gryphon riders to take you there." Lord Alexei pointed toward the door. "I imagine he could give you a sufficiently professional and detailed account of his discussions with the elven magisters at the time. If you'd prefer to wait for him to return and discuss the ruins on that island together, you're welcome to stay in my castle during that time. Though these are hard times for everyone, I have not forgotten the duties of hospitality — I will not treat you poorly. The people of Lordaeron will never forget the aid Quel'Thalas rendered during the Orc Wars."

Jace leaned back in his chair and listened to him drawl out these words, laced with not a little passive aggression, then glanced at the gloomy, sinister decorations and furnishings of this underground palace.

Being underground, with no windows or natural light of any kind, made the place seem even more eerie. Add to that the hostile gaze of the young man beside them, and being invited to live here felt an awful lot like being invited to sit in a prison cell.

It was easy to foresee that even if they were sent to Tarren Mill, they'd probably have to wait there who knew how long before they could meet the "investigating" Barov family magic adviser, this Purkett.

And even if they did meet him, they'd only get some perfunctory, noncommittal answers.

Those people on the island were most likely "miners" hired by the Barov family, tasked with excavating and collecting everything the orcs had left behind that was connected to dark magic, then reselling it — squeezing every last drop of profit out of that place.

But if word got out, Dalaran and Lordaeron would never permit it, so they absolutely couldn't let any outsider catch even a whiff of it.

"That won't be necessary," Sylvanas said. "We'll have a look at the situation on that island and be on our way. Is that acceptable?"

"Of course," Lord Alexei said readily. "That island has been abandoned for a long time. The only reason it's sealed off is the Kirin Tor's recommendation. I wouldn't want some farmers and fishermen who know nothing about dark magic spreading whatever mysterious infectious disease is up there to Caer Darrow, or anywhere farther afield. You're welcome to stay for lunch, and I'll arrange for you to go ashore this afternoon."

"Thank you for your kindness, my lord," Sylvanas said. "We've already caused you quite enough trouble here. It's still early — we don't plan on delaying too long."

"Very well then, Alexei, you've been in charge of watching over that island all along." The elder Barov beckoned to the young man who'd been standing to the side. "Take our guests up to have a look at those rotten rocks."

"Yes, Father." The young man nodded. "Please follow me, everyone."

So this was young Alexei Barov — Lord Alexei Barov's son.

The reason Jace hadn't recognized the fellow right away was that after Caer Darrow was destroyed by the Scourge's magic, young Alexei hadn't escaped the catastrophe and had been raised into a shambling corpse.

But later, after the Lich King's power had been checked, he regained his will. Together with the other awakened corpses, he joined the ranks of the Dark Lady Sylvanas and became one of the Forsaken.

By then he'd become a hunched undead monster with a head of dark green, rotted hair — naturally, it was very hard to connect that appearance to what he'd looked like alive.

By morning the weather had cleared and the sunlight was a bit harsh. Stepping onto the island again, Jace and the two elves were completely unsurprised by everything before them.

Every trace the humans of Caer Darrow had left on the island had been swept clean. Last night's footprints had been more or rain and hail, and the retinue that had followed young Alexei Barov onto the island had trampled whatever traces of footprints remained into an indistinguishable mess.

Though for a Farstrider ranger like Sylvanas, telling old footprints from new was effortless, she couldn't be bothered to puzzle over these deliberately concealed tracks.

The ruins of the Storm Altar in the sunlight looked even more broken and squat than they had last night, and even the shadow energy hanging over it seemed to shrink back quite a bit under the bright clear sky.

Aeridania and Sylvanas were both a little dispirited and had already started discussing, once they left, going to Darrowshire first to ask around.

Young Alexei stood not far off, staring at the pile of rocks before him, glancing from time to time toward the ruins of the orcish lumber mill in the distance. He'd been silent the whole way, showing no interest in providing any leads and no interest in having any exchange with the three of them over here. Coming here was simply a matter of carrying out the task his father had given him.

"I'm going to have a quick chat with that Alexei, Syl," Jace murmured.

"About what?" Sylvanas asked.

"Business." Jace spoke as he walked toward the woods. Sylvanas frowned, originally meaning to lift a hand to point in young Alexei Barov's direction, but in the end lowered it.

Though she didn't know what Jace was planning, Sylvanas also understood that he probably knew what he was doing.
