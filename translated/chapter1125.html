Chapter 1125: The Jewel in the Crown

Jace walked out of the small woods examining his black cloak as he headed over toward Vereesa.

Malicia — he didn’t have that deep an impression of the name, but once it was linked to Caer Darrow and Scholomance, it wasn’t so hard to recall.

She was one of the bosses in Scholomance. In the game she was called Instructor Malicia.

The reason Jace’s impression of her was so distinct was that she was the high elf leader in Scholomance, standing out quite remarkably among a group of human necromancer leaders.

Jace had once thought that the high elves there had only joined the Scourge and come to Caer Darrow to study at Scholomance after Quel’Thalas was destroyed by the Scourge.

He hadn’t expected that as early as ten years ago, or even earlier, they’d already gotten tangled up with the Barov family?

Perhaps, considering the reputations of quite a few shadow mages in Quel’Thalas, plus the Barov family’s murky relationship with these orcish ruins, the future fate of Caer Darrow might have already had its seeds planted at this very time.

Right now Aeridania was pacing back and forth in the distance, carefully inspecting every piece of rubble that looked as though it had been blown off the Altar of Storms. Vereesa sat on a tree stump to one side watching her, seemingly considering other angles.

Hearing Jace’s footsteps, Vereesa turned her head. Just as she was about to speak, she twitched her nose as if she’d caught some strange smell. “What’s this? This is the stench of demon blood. Did you run into trouble?”

Hearing that, Jace immediately sniffed his own clothes guiltily. There shouldn’t be any succubus smell on him — after all, when he’d just finished dealing with that succubus using Ulthalesh, he shouldn’t have gotten too much on him.

Actually, if that succubus hadn’t recognized the scythe, he definitely would have kept his promise and sent her back to the Twisting Nether. But things being what they were, Jace couldn’t take that risk anymore.

By now, it had become something of a vicious cycle.

The shadow power Jace now possessed, combined with Ulthalesh’s extraordinary dark energy, attracted the most elite wild demons in the Twisting Nether.

Even if they couldn’t compare to a huntress like Morophil, they were all standouts with powerful psychic control or exceptional strength.

It was just that most of these demons had heard of Ulthalesh’s power — some hadn’t just heard of it, but could figure it out on sight, like the sayaad he’d just dealt with. Jace hadn’t even gotten the chance to ask her name.

So in the future, aside from low-intelligence loyal companions like felhounds, Jace would still need to summon other demons as cautiously as possible.

Though Jace felt he’d handled it quite cleanly, Vereesa’s sense of smell was far sharper than an ordinary person’s. Just because he couldn’t smell it didn’t mean she couldn’t.

He sniffed his sleeve again, then said, “It might have something to do with the smell from the orc ruins over there. I’m not sure. I went over and had a quick chat with that young Alexi just now — quite fruitful, Vere.”

Vereesa nodded with half a doubt, but she was clearly more interested in what Alexi Barov had said, so she called out toward the magister in a low voice, “Magister Aeridania, could you come over here?”

Jace glanced over toward young Alexi again. The young man was staring at him right now, his emotions a bit complicated.

Maybe he didn’t understand why he’d told this outsider about the shady dealings the Barovs were secretly carrying out here. Maybe he was worried Jace would take this information straight to the Farstriders and the magisters.

After Aeridania came over, Jace said in a low voice, “Just as I thought — the Barov family secretly sold off runestones that still had residual power.”

Vereesa said quietly, “Damn it. So where are we supposed to look? Start at Tirisfal Keep and go all the way to Tyra Port, asking our way through every town in the Eastweald one by one?”

“The Barov family’s business probably extends beyond just the whole Eastweald, Vereesa,” Aeridania said. “In fact, it might be even more troublesome than that.”

“Don’t worry, I’ve already found out,” Jace said. “Most of the runestones that still had power were destroyed by the Kirin Tor. A considerable portion of the remainder was bought by a Quel’dorei named Malicia. Do any of you know this person?”

“Malicia…” Aeridania thought for a moment, then said, “I remember that name. She’s a magister in Goldenmist Village, and also a senior researcher with the Reliquary.”

“Why would a magister join the Reliquary?” Vereesa asked. “I thought most of you looked down on those archaeologists and collectors.”

“It might be related to her research,” Aeridania said. “Though this sort of thing is rare, it’s not entirely unheard of. It’s just that I hadn’t heard that the Reliquary was purchasing runestone fragments from Caer Darrow…”

Vereesa said, “After all, these islands are the Barov family’s territory, and officially this place has already been destroyed in turns by Quel’Thalas and the Kirin Tor.”

Hearing this, Jace couldn’t help but frown. “Just now young Alexi said the Kirin Tor mage who helped oversee purifying the contaminated runestones here was Kel’Thuzad.”

Aeridania said, “That’s normal. Archmage Kel’Thuzad was mainly in charge of dark magic defense and monitoring in Dalaran.”

“Did you know he was locked up in the Violet Hold for cooperating with black dragons?” Jace asked.

“I knew he’d gone into the Violet Hold, but I didn’t know it actually had something to do with black dragons…” Aeridania said blankly.

“You think Dar’Khan, Malicia, and Kel’Thuzad are connected, Jace?” Vereesa said. “That they interfered together in the purification and destruction of this evil runestone?”

Jace exhaled hard, trying to sort out the thoughts that were gradually tangling in his head. Still, being so close to the contaminated Altar of Storms, the air wasn’t exactly fresh and did nothing to help him think.

“Maybe we’ll find out once we get to Stratholme, Vere,” Jace said.

“Did Alexi Barov tell you all of this?” As she spoke, the ranger started to glance toward the young Barov out of habit. Jace immediately warned her, “Don’t look over there. I promised him I wouldn’t tell you Quel’Thalas people about our dealings.”

Vereesa nodded. “So what did you say to him?”

“I told him I could buy some of the magical implements left behind by orcs and demons,” Jace said. “So he let slip a few things related to the Barov family’s business.”

“So you actually plan to work with those Barov people?” Vereesa asked.

“I don’t have any money,” Jace said. “I was just expressing some interest. I didn’t expect that young Alexi to trust me enough to tell me so much.”

“Though the Orc Wars hit the Barov family hard, they’re not lacking sources of gold, Master Sesso.” Aeridania glanced back at the rubble ruins of the altar. “They’re probably valuing your identity more — wanting to get something far more precious than gold from you.”

Jace knew what the elven magister was talking about.

He could feel it — the greed that could be sensed on both young Alexi and old Alexei. More precisely, it was a desire to survive. Especially on young Alexi, it was even more obvious.

To Jace, it was hard to feel that kind of intense survival instinct from a young man who lived in comfort, was physically and mentally healthy, and faced no threat to his life whatsoever.

Unless what he sought was “immortality.”

Unfortunately, even a dark mage like Jace had no way to grant someone eternal life. If anything, a warlock like Gul’dan, who had studied so much necromancy from Kil’jaeden, would be better able to “help.”

“How do we find Malicia?” Jace looked at Aeridania. “Do you know where she lives in Stratholme?”

“I didn’t even know she’d gone to Stratholme.” Aeridania shook her head. “I’m not familiar with her, Master Sesso. Before, we only had a very brief exchange at an enchanting materials research conference.”

“Let’s get there first and figure it out from there.” Jace spoke as he looked over toward young Alexi and the several Barov servants, and called out, “Gentlemen, I think we’ve seen enough. We can head back south!”

There were two relatively fast ways to get from Caer Darrow to Stratholme. The first was to take a boat directly up the Thondroril River into Darrowmere Bay — Stratholme’s port sat right near the river mouth.

The second was to fly straight there on the Barov family’s gryphons.

Though gryphons were much faster, having just exchanged words with young Alexi about Malicia being in Stratholme, if they immediately had the Barov people send gryphons to carry him and the two high elves to Stratholme, it really would have been a bit too disrespectful.

Jace didn’t want to offend the Barov family — especially didn’t want them to feel he was going to tell Quel’Thalas about their secretly selling runestones — so he had Caer Darrow’s gryphons take them to Darrowshire on the lake’s northeastern shore.

Darrowshire also had a gryphon roost, but as in the Kingdom of Stormwind, unless it was official business, or a privately raised gryphon from a great family, or a hired gryphon rider and so on, generally even having money it was hard to rent a gryphon.

Still, shipping in the Eastweald was quite developed. Getting on a boat here and going north along the Thondroril River straight to Stratholme was probably a two-day, two-night journey — acceptable.

Of course, there was another advantage to taking a passenger ship that couldn’t be matched.

Going to Stratholme by gryphon would be an extremely conspicuous move even within the Kingdom of Lordaeron. The registration at the gryphon keepers’ offices along the way, plus how eye-catching a gryphon crossing the sky was, combined with the special identities of Jace, Vereesa, and Aeridania — probably within a few days of arriving in Stratholme, the whole city’s officials, plus the elven envoys and so on, would all know about it.

If Magister Malicia was still living in the city of Stratholme, as an extremely likely accomplice of Dar’Khan and a dark magic researcher, she might get the intelligence even sooner than these officials.

Dar’Khan had just died by Jace’s hand, and the whole of Quel’Thalas was investigating Dar’Khan’s legacy. If Malicia was the mysterious mage who had transferred the loot from Dar’Khan’s tower, then the moment she learned that Jace, Vereesa, and a magister had suddenly arrived in Stratholme, she would definitely find a way to grab her things and run before Jace’s group could find her hiding place — and they’d be off on another wild goose chase.

Going by boat along with the other passengers heading to Stratholme from Darrowshire was much more discreet. After all, the number of elves moving about in the Eastweald wasn’t small either — a human and two elves traveling together wouldn’t draw too much attention.

Darrowshire itself was about the same size as Goldshire. Looking into the distance, one could make out a small monastery and a few buildings sitting on either side of the main road. Farther off, beyond the pine forested hills, there were a few towers.

By contrast, the port over here in Darrowshire actually looked even more bustling, with travelers, porters, and merchants in heavy coats and outer robes coming and going — quite lively.

Although this was also part of Darrowshire, a stretch of pine forest separated the two, connected by a main road with a handful of small farms in between, feeling like two neighboring villages.

Though Jace had grown up in Tirisfal, he’d always had a bit of trouble understanding the accent of the people here in Darrowmere — especially when they spoke too fast. Lordaeron’s thousands of years of rule had never managed to spread the capital city’s way of speaking over here.

The kingdom’s capital was in Tirisfal, but the people east of the Barrier River seemed to carry a certain pride handed down from the days of the Arathorian Empire to this day.

They considered themselves the descendants of the forebears whose bloodline and culture were closer to the Alterac and Arathi heritage. They believed the ancient royal houses that had established the royal city in Lordaeron — the Menethils, for example — were nothing more than descendants of tribal chieftains and herdsmen marginalized by the empire, no different than the founders of Dalaran.

So they were more inclined to preserve some of their own ways of speaking, or unique cultural symbols — such as ancient names like Alexei and Marduk.

But even setting aside these arcane and abstruse senses of superiority, this really was the richest and most bustling place among the seven human kingdoms.

Stepping aboard this passenger ship, they were about to sail toward the jewel in the crown of this exquisite tiara of the Eastweald — Stratholme.
