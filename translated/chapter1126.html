Chapter 1126: Stratholme

The farther north they traveled, away from the hills and lakeshore at the foot of Alterac, the flatter the broad surface of the Thondroril River became on both sides, and the boundary between the river and the fields grew blurred. It became hard to tell exactly where the water was being held back by the dikes.

The last time he'd come through here, Jace had been riding Grede's gryphon over these farmlands and forests. Back then the light seemed rather dim, and from high up he could only see swaths of dark green and light green — the impression wasn't vivid enough.

Only after getting used to the uneven wheat fields of Westfall and then coming to the Eastweald, where the vast flat plain stretched without a single rise or slope in sight, could one feel the shock brought by the breadbasket of the Kingdom of Lordaeron.

The sky was a little gray. Amid the splashing of the ship cutting through waves at the bow, the passengers on board gathered at the front, belting out songs at the top of their lungs to the accompaniment of a lute with a dry, raspy voice.

Some voices were rough and booming, others shrill and thin — now high, now low, now fast, now slow. It really couldn't be called pleasant to the ear. Aeridania seemed to be reaching her limit. She pulled her hood down hard and hid to one side — if the cabin hadn't been stuffy and foul-smelling, she probably would have already gone inside.

Vereesa, on the other hand, was lifting an eyebrow, watching the Lordaeronians howling into the early spring chill with an expression that was both a little disgusted and a little curious.

"Magister Aeridania." Jace looked at the magister sitting lower down. "If Dar'Khan and his accomplices created a 'magical domain' similar to Ban'dinoriel somewhere in the city of Stratholme, would you be able to detect it when we get close?"

"What?" Aeridania raised her head with some impatience, not forgetting to shoot those humans a vicious glare.

Jace cleared his throat and was about to raise his voice and shout it again, but worried the passengers farther off might overhear. At that moment Vereesa lowered her head and repeated it once in Thalassian. Aeridania's annoyed expression immediately dissipated, and she nodded seriously.

"Of course I could," the magister said. "But I'm afraid they'll shut down the controlled runes to hide their tracks. If they do, it'll be much more troublesome to find them."

"If they only shut them down, that's still fine," Jace said. "If they find a way to take the rune fragments out of Stratholme to somewhere else, then we really won't know where to look."

"To teleport a fragment of a runestone that possesses magical power to another location requires executing a ritual of considerable scale," Aeridania said. "If Malicia intends to organize such a ritual in Stratholme, it should attract the attention of the Kirin Tor or the Darrowmere Arcane Society. After we arrive in Stratholme, I'll go to the magical organizations there and ask around among the Quel'dorei mages I know."

"Don't expose our purpose, ma'am," Jace reminded her.

"I'll be careful, Master Sesso," Aeridania said.

"Then what about us?" Vereesa asked.

"If they want to employ a summoning ritual, they'll need soul shards, Vere," Jace said. "If Dar'Khan, or Malicia, really set up a 'sanctuary' in Stratholme that can transfer those dark magic research results, then they'd definitely need a considerable amount of casting materials for shadow magic or necromancy. So we could go to the alchemy labs, or the herbalist shops, or the casting-materials stores and ask around."

"Don't you have friends, or fellow practitioners you know, in Stratholme?" Vereesa asked.

"No friends, but the friends of friends — I've got plenty of those." Jace shook his head with a smile. "But friends all start out as strangers, don't they? I think I got along quite well with that young Alexi. Maybe the dark-magic enthusiasts of Stratholme will welcome the arrival of a demon worshipper like me too."

Vereesa said, "Be serious for once, Jace Sesso."

"All right, all right." Jace coughed. "What I mean is, I'll go look for a breakthrough in this area — but on the condition that you don't come with me, Vere."

"I know," Vereesa said in a low voice. "Since Malicia doesn't have the ability to teleport the runestone fragments away, I'll contact the nearby Farstriders and have them keep a close eye on the flow of goods connected to high elves, or any other helpful leads… damn it, Stratholme is a port city even bigger than Stormwind. Just thinking about searching for a few stones that are about to lose their magic in a place like this gives me a headache."

"Think of it this way. If we'd actually found all of Dar'Khan's secrets in one go — in his bedroom, or in some storage cabinet in Caer Darrow — then I'd have to doubt whether those were really things Dar'Khan valued." Jace leaned against the railing, staring at the distant sunset that was about to sink into the gray forest. "The more Dar'Khan valued something, the deeper his accomplices would hide it — not even hesitating to use Quel'Thalas's runestones to help. Only that would be enough to show how important it is, Vere."

"Master Sesso is quite right," Aeridania said.

"All right, that's true," Vereesa said.

"I have some magic biscuits here — would you two like to try one? I dotted them with bits of preserved fruit, just for a touch of sweetness," the magister asked.

"Uh, I'd still rather eat something solid." Vereesa tilted her head.

The magister made a helpless expression, took a biscuit from her silk pouch, and popped it into her mouth.

The next day, the passenger boat docked for a while at the estuary leading to Hearthglen for a brief respite. In that short amount of time, Jace had already seen cavalry and foot patrols passing near the port time and time again. The docks were packed with wagons and porters preparing to transport military supplies. It looked as if the Scourge invasion was about to hit at any moment.

It seemed the attack on Lord Tirion Fordring that the painter had mentioned really had put the sentries and armies in this area on high alert.

After all, orcs occasionally sneaking into farms or villages to steal things was one thing. Directly assaulting a town lord was a threat of a completely different magnitude.

After another night, by the next morning the sailing ship entered a broad expanse of river, with colorful sails swaying everywhere in the distance.

The woods on the right bank grew sparser and sparser. Flocks of seabirds flew in and out. Dock after dock, towering sails, and gleaming white stone walls emerged from afar.

Jace stood at the bow, unwilling to miss a single sight or detail in his field of vision. Stratholme's harbor was on an even larger scale than Stormwind's — like a great white mother beast crouching quietly on one side of the bay, gazing at every passerby with tenderness.

"Six years. I'm finally back." A man who looked like a battle-hardened soldier said to his friend, "I swear to you, I'll never leave this place again for the rest of my life."

Jace pressed his lips together and said nothing.
