Chapter 1128: The Grey Cloak

The decor of the Laurel Inn's rooms showed some imitation of the Quel'linns and Quel'Dani Cottage style. It was far from the flamboyant splendor of the real thing in Quel'Thalas, but it could still let wealthy guests indulge their stereotypical "elven fantasy."

Of course, Jace wasn't at all used to staying in places like this. To him, the Gilded Rose Inn was already quite a luxurious place to stay, and that little run-down inn in Raven Hill with cobwebs still in the rooms was just right.

For him, coming in here felt a bit out of place, but for the two elves choosing accommodation in Stratholme, this was actually the conformist, low-key choice.

After putting down their luggage, the three of them had a simple lunch. When they returned to the corridor, Jace glanced at his pocket watch. "It's already afternoon now. Let's not waste too much time — let's head out and ask around now. Meet back at the inn in the evening?"

"Good." The two elves said in unison.

"Are there any magic shops in the city, Magister Aeridania?" Jace asked.

"Chilton's Magic Trick Shop," the magister thought for a moment. "And Matthew's Magic Shop. Both are quite professional casting-materials stores in this city. The mage Matthew's shop also sells some casting materials."

The two shops the magister mentioned were magic stores with some reputation not only in Stratholme but even in Stormwind's Mage Quarter. Jace was quite certain he'd heard both these names — Chilton and Matthew — before at the Wizard Sanctum. He even knew Chilton was a Stormwind citizen who'd opened a shop in Stratholme.

He very much doubted a place this high-end would risk secretly selling shadow-magic casting materials — things like demon limbs and entrails, or… the most infamous of all, soul shards.

After all, Stratholme's tolerance for demons and dark magic was far lower than present-day Stormwind's — even stricter than the royal capital. Still, he said, "Thank you, ma'am."

Aeridania lowered her head slightly. "Until this evening, then, Sylvanas. Magister Sesso."

Jace watched the magister leave down the corridor, then said, "If you get in touch with the Farstriders, Syl, remember to have them watch the harbor too."

"I'll remind them." Sylvanas stood on her toes and kissed Jace on the lips. "Stay safe."

"Don't worry," Jace said.

Sylvanas said in a low voice, "I'm not just talking about the warlocks here. Also… the Church. Don't get on their bad side. I know the Church wouldn't show you no mercy if they knew who you were, but it would be hard to avoid exposing our presence then."

"I'll remember," Jace said.

Sylvanas nodded, checked the bandages binding the Dragon's Call sword sheath on her back, and quickly left down the corridor.

Downstairs in the Laurel Inn's lobby, Aeridania and Sylvanas had already left. The half-elf attendant was behind the counter chatting and laughing about something with another female attendant. At that moment the half-elf noticed Jace, who'd just come down the stairs, and his gaze paused for a beat.

Jace waved at him. The half-elf nodded politely, and after Jace walked over, asked, "Is there something you need, sir?"

"I don't come to Stratholme often. Can you recommend any magic-materials merchants in the city to me?" Jace asked.

"There are many, sir." The attendant smiled. "That elven mage lady who was just with you probably knows more clearly than I do."

Jace frowned. "Mages coming from Quel'Thalas tend to pursue the quality of casting materials, don't care about price at all, and look down on casting methods like us hedge mages. You understand what I mean, right, sir?"

After hearing this, the half-elf let down a trace of the resistance in his heart and said, "I know there's a small shop on Festival Walk called 'the Grey Cloak,' sir. Some mages planning to go to Dalaran, or who got rejected by Dalaran, like to frequent there."

"That sounds just right for me." Jace nodded.

"Then may the Holy Light bless you to begin your studies at Dalaran soon." The half-elf bowed slightly. Jace barely resisted the urge to retort about how exactly the Holy Light was going to bless him into Dalaran, thanked him, and left the Laurel Inn.

Crossing the plaza and passing through the southern gate, then going down a broad street, the road ahead became much narrower. Layer upon layer of houses, high walls, and eaves on both sides nearly blocked out the sky.

Compared to that plaza where the Laurel Inn sat, this district looked considerably more chaotic overall. This should be the "Warrens" controlled by several Scourge plague towers in the game.

Following the road through, passing through a tiny, pitiful plaza, going around the small fountain in the middle and continuing south, squeezing through yet another city gate with the crowd, the area ahead opened up.

A metal placard by the city gate bore the words "Elder Square" in a spelling of Common that few people used nowadays. Jace looked at the not-very-noticeable chapel to the left of his field of vision, then ahead at the barred gate beneath the high wall, and confirmed this was the place where one entered the Stratholme instance through the back gate.

But winding all the way through the Warrens and back had already consumed over an hour, and by now the sky was already showing signs of dimming. Jace headed straight for the city gate on the right, because once he passed through here he'd arrive at the Festival Walk the attendant had mentioned.

If not for the memories of his past life, he definitely wouldn't have been able to navigate this city so smoothly. Though the city was many times more complex than in the game, and he'd taken quite a few wrong turns, by relying on his grasp of the overall orientation and asking for directions now and then, he'd managed to walk in the right direction.

But the moment he arrived at Festival Walk, he froze on the spot.

The long, broad walk was covered layer upon layer with tents and vendor stalls. Crowds clustered around every market area. Just threading through the middle to get to another district looked difficult.

Where was "the Grey Cloak"?

Judging from what the attendant said, it should be a storefront located on Festival Walk. But this was clearly a massive street market. The buildings on both sides looked like ordinary residential neighborhoods — not shops.

Could "the Grey Cloak" be a stall within the market? Though magic materials ranged from expensive to cheap, running a stall in the middle of a market would take some nerve.

This place was chaotic as hell — shouts and hawking voices everywhere. At the far end of the broad walk, from what looked like a hastily erected circus tent, came a roar like a thunder lizard. Was there actually a thunder lizard in there?

That roar, plus the rumbling thunder that followed, startled Jace's eyes wide. But the pedestrians and guards nearby seemed quite used to it. Only one soldier sitting on a ledge to rest and gnawing on a fruit looked up and glanced over with a bored expression.

"What was that noise, sir?" Jace asked.

"Go see for yourself at the zoo," the soldier said while chewing. "Three silver to get in. Way cheaper than the arena fights in Durnholde, and I think it's more interesting than watching orcs throw punches."

"You've been to the Durnholde arena, pauper?" another guard said, laughing as he eyed him.

"Go eat pig shit." The soldier cursed as he tossed the fruit pit aside, picked his shield, and headed toward the market.

The guard who'd mocked the poor soldier watched him go with an amused look, then smiled at Jace. "Don't let our 'Great Fireball' Timmy ruin your good impression of Stratholme, mage brother from Tirisfal. And yes, there really is a thunder lizard over there. No joke. Go look for yourself — a red-skinned monster dragon from across the sea, one that can actually throw lightning."

Jace glanced over at the "zoo" again. It was quiet now.

"See if there's anything I can help with — you don't look like you came here for the zoo," the guard took off his helmet.

"I'm looking for 'the Grey Cloak,' sir." Jace nodded. "Do you know where that shop is?"

"Ah, the Grey Cloak." The guard pointed at a small alley at the foot of the city wall beside them. "Go in here straight, turn left when you hit a left turn, then not far ahead at the second intersection turn right. You'll see a somewhat grubby gray mage-hat sign. A bit hard to find, but you'll find it."

"Thank you very much."

Jace inclined his body slightly, then ducked into the alley beside him. The chaotic noise of the market outside quickly became muffled, and once the surroundings quieted down, the arguing and nagging drifting from the residences on both sides became clearly audible.

After walking for about half an hour, the sky was already getting a little dim. Jace glanced at his watch — it was only a little past five.

Second intersection, turn right. Jace muttered under his breath as he approached the alley on the right, and at a glance saw the gray mage-hat sign deep in the alley. Stepping up the stairs and pushing open the shop door, a man who looked about fifty-something was sitting behind the counter carefully examining a wooden branch in his hands.

He glanced at Jace, then returned his attention to the branch. "Something you need, young sir?"

Jace didn't answer right away. He twitched his nose and sniffed the smell in the shop. There was no aura of shadow here, or scent of demons.

Had he come all this way for nothing?

"I need some demon horn powder, sir. Ground from a succubus's horn would be best," Jace said. "The kind that can be used for shadow resistance enchanting."

The man put down the branch in his hand and sized Jace up. "You'd best go to Darrowshire to buy things like that, young sir. If you don't mind the trouble, you'd best go to Elwynn. The demon materials there are much better than in the Eastern Weald."

"Someone told me you could buy these special casting materials here." Jace looked at the faintly magical colored stones on the shelf behind the counter. "I don't need very high quality — my time is a bit urgent."

The man mulled it over for a moment, then stood up and nodded. He pushed aside the baffle beside the counter. "Then come with me. See if there's anything that catches your eye."

Jace could feel a wariness rise in the man's heart, tinged even with a trace of fear. What was he afraid of? Could he have recognized Jace?

Jace didn't think the people of Stratholme could recognize him, and in Stratholme there were no shortage of people with a Brill accent. Perhaps this man simply had the normal wariness toward an unfamiliar demon-materials buyer.

He followed the man through the door behind the counter into the corridor, thinking all the while how to bring up the matter of soul shards. As he walked down the stairs, he suddenly felt the shadows behind him stir.

The interesting thing was, this feeling was all too familiar — because… he got this exact feeling every time he went down into the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb.

Jace swiftly drew Song of the Azure and slashed back. A piercing musical note pierced through the corridor, and the pale blue radiance it drew cleanly split in two the head of the felhound lunging out of the shadows!

The felhound shrieked and crashed to the ground. Jace thrust out his hand and drove a Blood Grip into the demon's split-open wound, viciously tearing out a black-purple heart, ripping it open, and tossing it aside. The felhound thrashed twice, then curled up motionless.

The whole process had taken maybe a second or two. The shopkeeper, bracing against the wall, his face ashen, stared at the demon heart spraying fel blood as it rolled to the bottom of the stairs. He could barely stay standing.

Jace pointed the tip of his sword at the man. The radiance of Song of the Azure gradually dimmed. This felhound had really been hidden surprisingly well.

He swept deeper with demon detection, confirmed no other demons were approaching, and said, "You'd better explain yourself, sir. I don't know you — I don't think we have any grudges between us, do we?"

"This… it's a misunderstanding, sir. Truly a misunderstanding," the man said with a tremor.
