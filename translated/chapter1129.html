Chapter 1129: The Lighthouse's Influence

"It had to be a very serious, very convincing misunderstanding," Jace said, holding Song of the Azure leveled. "That way I can consider letting you off."

The man swallowed, lowered his eyes to glance at the sword tip, hesitated for a moment, then said, "I thought you were a killer from Darrowmere, sir. Whether it was the way you spoke, or your sensitivity to dark magic…"

"A killer from Darrowmere?" Jace frowned.

"A few years ago I got inexplicably dragged into a vendetta dispute, because the casting materials used by a shape-shifting killer were traced back to me. So I was considered the killer's accomplice." The man caught his breath in agitation and continued, "I only had a private collaboration with some warlocks in the Eastern Weald, but I couldn't predict who they'd hand the things over to. And just because of that, I became the target of a vendetta by some lunatics living on the banks of Darrowmere Lake…"

A shape-shifting killer. A vendetta by residents on the banks of Darrowmere Lake. Stratholme. The Eastern Weald… was this related to Ursula Delling?

Jace probed a look deeper into the corridor and continued asking, "So you've killed other killers who came to your door before?"

"At first someone warned me, but I didn't take it seriously. Then one really came, and I nearly died at that man's hands." The man lowered his head and looked at the fel blood left by the felhound. "Still, I'm not even sure if he came because of those Darrowmere people. I can't be certain even now."

"So you're not a warlock." The man raised his gaze to look at Jace. "You're one of those demon hunters? Or the Silver Hand?"

After asking, he couldn't help but steal a glance at the staff on Jace's back. "Are you a priest? Or a battle mage of the Kirin Tor? Your sword skills are stronger than a lot of sword-carriers I've seen."

"No," Jace said. "I'm just a hedge mage. With Lordaeron crawling with warlocks who use dark magic these days, I wanted to buy some things to prepare for self-defense."

The man nodded vigorously. Jace asked again, "Does anyone else live here?"

"No," the man said with composure.

Jace frowned. When this man started lying and concealing things, he couldn't hide the shifts in his emotions at all — even if he wore a face of fearless composure on the surface.

"Answer carefully, sir. I'll ask you again — do any other warlocks live in your basement?"

"No other warlocks," the man answered.

What was interesting was that this time he didn't seem to be lying. No warlocks — then who else was there?

"Then who else is in here?" Jace flicked his other hand and conjured a flame balanced on his fingers. "You keep a felhound in an alley off Festival Walk, sir. As long as I find evidence of you summoning demons and your casting materials, even if I burn this shop of yours together with the basement to the ground, the Church won't say a word against me."

The man said with agitation, "This isn't a warlock hideout! I only had someone help summon this felhound to protect myself, sir. You're not church personnel, and you're not the Silver Hand. I beg you, spare my life. Don't harm my family…"

"Your whole family lives with a demon?" Jace asked.

"My whole family lives upstairs. My wife knows about this demon, but my two children don't know of its existence." The man lowered his head.

"Whatever." Jace moved the sword tip. "Do you have soul shards here?"

"Soul shards?" The man raised his head and widened his eyes. "You need those? Those aren't things you can gather easily. They can only be torn from living people…"

"Other things with powerful souls work too. Demons, for instance." Jace glanced again at the felhound's heart he'd shredded with his Blood Grip, then sheathed his sword. "Then do you know who in this city is selling soul shards?"

"There were, right after the Orc Wars ended. Some Dark Iron dwarves and warlocks from the south would come into the city to sell them on the sly," the man said. "But ever since the Grim Batol incident, the mages of Lordaeron and Dalaran cooperated for a while to track down any trace of dark magic in the cities. At the time I didn't even dare sell shadow stone fragments, let alone something like soul shards."

The man didn't seem to be hiding anything. Jace couldn't help but frown. He'd originally wanted to track the leads on the evidence Dath'remar had transferred by following the soul shards that summoning rituals required. He hadn't expected Stratholme to actually be cleaner than other places at this moment.

Could it be that after Marysia got the runestones, she never returned to Stratholme at all, but fled straight to some other place? Some remote mountain or forest, like the snowy peaks of Dun Morogh or Loch Modan.

But without walls and guards on the scale of Stratholme to protect them, Marysia or Dath'remar's accomplices simply couldn't hold on to those important materials — plus the runestone fragments themselves.

Besides, the magisters had said that the runestones' power was based on the Sunwell. In Darrowmere, the Sunwell's influence was already weak to the point of being imperceptible. In places even farther away, the runestones couldn't be activated at all — so fooling Bantinore to teleport evidence was out of the question.

Could Marysia be murdering citizens in Stratholme to seize souls? Though Stratholme had tens of thousands of residents, it would be normal for a few people to go missing in the back alleys and side streets from time to time.

"Can you help me get in touch with the local warlocks?" Jace asked.

"Haven't you heard of the Lighthouse Massacre, sir?" the man said. "Ever since that happened, even I am about to lose contact with the local warlocks."

"Isn't that a matter of the Stormwind Kingdom?" Jace asked.

"But plenty of Lordaeron's warlocks were in on it too," the man said. "Like I told you before, you'd best go ask in Darrowshire. I really can't help you. But as far as the information I have on hand goes, let alone here in Stratholme — even in Darrowshire's underground market, nobody dares sell soul shards."

As if he felt Jace was somewhat skeptical, the man added, "You're not church personnel or the Silver Hand, and you're not a mage of the Kirin Tor. Frankly, I don't care whether those warlocks live or die, mage sir. My business can go on without them. And after the events of these past few years, I've already decided I won't do business with those warlocks anymore. So I have absolutely no reason to lie to you."

Jace nodded. "Thank you for the information, sir." Stepping outside the Grey Cloak, night had already fallen.

Actually, Jace hadn't expected to find leads on Marysia directly from this small magic-materials shop. But judging from the information the shopkeeper had provided, it had been very difficult to obtain soul shards in Stratholme these past two years — unless those high elf shadow mages knew how to tear souls directly from people, having learned the demon's "Drain Soul" incantation.

Should he go to Stratholme's magistrate and captain of the guard and ask about missing persons? If he was going to ask, it would have to be tomorrow.

The nights in the city of Stratholme were much more lively than Stormwind's — especially over here on Festival Walk. Jace bought a smoked sausage and a breadstick at the market, and sat beside a nearby fountain to munch on it.

Though it felt a bit like a wasted trip, maybe Aeridania and Sylvanas would have different gains on their end.

Marysia was a magister of no low status. At this moment, besides the Barov family and these three people of his, no one else knew she'd purchased the runestone fragments. Right now she probably still felt she hadn't exposed this connection to Dath'remar and was still mingling in high elf social circles. If that was the case, Aeridania would definitely be able to learn something from the other elves.

As for the Farstriders, they were investigators by trade. The intelligence they knew locally about the Quel'dorei and dark magic wouldn't be less than the owner of a small magic-materials shop. And Marysia might not look favorably on the local warlock gangs at all — she might have not the slightest connection to them.

So maybe Jace was, rather, the one among the three least likely to gather intelligence.

The zoo over there was still quite lively right now. Jace listened to the cooing sounds some kind of flightless bird kept making from inside, and wondered — if soul shards couldn't be bought on the underground market of Stratholme, and they didn't dare murder and loot souls openly to attract the attention of the Silver Hand and the Church, would those high elves try their luck with animals?

Here, ordinary animals like sheep and cattle would have a hard time being crafted into soul shards that could be preserved and traded for long. But sacrificing the souls and flesh of animals while they were alive would be enough to summon some relatively weak demons. The summoned demons could then extract soul shards through various methods.

During that process, what was sacrificed were other casting materials and the caster's own mana — but it was still a method of collecting the rare soul shards.

Though here, they definitely couldn't summon as casually as in all the time in the game. All sorts of backlash and accidents could arise in between. Losing your life in the summoning process would be a regular occurrence — either dying to void power or to a rampaging demon. But for a dark-magic researcher of Marysia's caliber, the whole process might be much easier to pull off. The biggest problem was how to evade the detection of the Kirin Tor and the Church.

Jace stared at the gate of the circus with its colorful flags and slowly realized — this was just for ordinary beasts like cattle, sheep, and horses. But what about magical creatures like a thunder lizard?

He had no doubt that from a thunder lizard — a giant born of ancient elementals, magic, and the Loa of Beasts — one could extract a considerably powerful soul shard.

Still, making off with a thunder lizard from the zoo to extract its soul would be far more conspicuous than carrying out a kidnapping in some back alley.

But Jace just kept watching the crowds going in and out of the gate, enticed by the light of the braziers to either side, and felt more and more that he couldn't just abandon this possible breakthrough.

He washed his hands, which had been handling the smoked sausage, in the fountain pool, then stood up and headed for the circus gate.
