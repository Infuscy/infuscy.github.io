## Chapter 113: Still a Long Way to Go

A small grove at night, with the refugee camp's scattered fires twinkling in the distance and the low murmur of voices rising and falling.

Grede looked up at a moon visible between the treetops. "Let's do it here. The moonlight's still bright enough. Everywhere else is pitch black."

Jace nodded, took off his cloak and set it carefully to the side, leaving just a thin shirt. The wind cut right through it — a little chilly.

"That new coat from the king — you can't bear to mess it up?" Grede asked.

Hearing that, Jace shot Grede a look, unsure whether the dwarf was making an "Emperor's New Clothes" reference or just asking literally.

Did Azeroth even have that famous story? He realized that after living here for nearly twenty years, he was starting to mix up the allusions of two worlds.

"Of course I'm reluctant," he said, picking up the wooden sword leaning against the tree trunk. "One repair job costs dozens of silver. That'd straight-up bankrupt me."

Grede grinned and said, "Then we'll come here and practice every day, and slowly train you into a master swordsman. The small fries won't be able to lay a hand on you, and then you won't have to worry about your clothes getting wrecked. See? Practicing swordsmanship has too many benefits — it can even save you money."

"There's always someone better. If the small fries can't touch me, plenty of others can," Jace said. "But the benefits of sword training are plenty for sure. Just dealing with mage stuff all day is exhausting enough, plus all this other annoying crap — coming out to move around with you is a lot more relaxing."

Grede nodded. "Me too, Jace."

The dwarf also picked up his sword. "We haven't seen each other in so long — let's have a real bout first and see if you've gotten worse."

With that, he raised his sword with both hands, slowly bringing the tip to bear on Jace.

The playful look on his face gradually cooled. Watching him like this, Jace realized that even holding a wooden sword, the dwarf was still plenty intimidating.

Those were eyes that had killed God knew how many enemies.

Jace instinctively averted his gaze, rolled his shoulders, and steadied his wooden sword with both hands, ready.

Grede said, "Come on, attack!"

Jace remembered the techniques Grede had taught him, kept his sword steady on his opponent, and closed in slowly. The other man didn't retreat or adjust his stance, just kept his eyes locked on him.

He suddenly raised his sword, lunged forward, and brought it down in a chop. The dwarf flicked his wrist and turned the blade, easily deflecting the downward strike.

The deflection didn't disrupt Jace's rhythm at all. He reversed his hand and delivered a quick slash… but Grede was faster. His sword thrust forward, winding around Jace's blade a few times along its path, and actually canceled the fast slash while carrying its momentum straight at Jace's midsection!

Jace hastily swung his sword to block while retreating. Amid the clatter of strikes, the dwarf's sword tip had already slid along his blade and guard and smashed into his hand.

The whole exchange had lasted maybe two or three seconds.

"Ngh…"

Jace twisted his mouth, switched his sword to the other hand, and flexed his right hand, which throbbed with pain.

If this had been a real fight, his hand would've been done for. He'd thought his movements were fast and fierce enough, but against a dwarf they were barely a threat.

Grede didn't press the attack. He pulled back, his expression completely unchanged, and said nothing.

Jace took two steps back, thinking about how to break through the other man's guard, but the dwarf's confident — even faintly contemptuous — gaze kept him from concentrating on any tactic.

To be honest, the life-or-death struggle with that naga had given him a lot more confidence in close combat. He hadn't felt much fear even facing a thug like Erland.

But all the confidence he'd built up over these past weeks had been cut down quite a bit by Grede's gaze just now.

The strangest part was that the dwarf didn't say a word, didn't make a sound — just stood there in silence, watching. The further this went, the more his image drifted from the Grede Jace knew, the one who loved drinking and eating, and closer to a complete stranger — a Wildhammer dwarf.

In the end, he wasn't Grede anymore. He was a true elite of Aerie Peak, a meat grinder that had fought recklessly through Amani troll formations, a reaper in the sky who rode a gryphon through the pine forests of the Hinterlands and made trolls and orcs lose their courage at the mere sound.

All he'd lost was one eye.

Jace tried his best to push aside these increasingly scattered thoughts and focus his attention on the sword.

Stay calm. Stay patient. Stay focused.

He told himself this as he adjusted the tip of his sword, preparing for the next attack.

Jace closed in step by step. When he was just about in striking distance, his muscles tensed again for a thrust. This time he'd leave Grede nothing to deflect, force him into a clumsy retreat, and then press the attack into the dwarf's arm…

But then, without warning, Grede let out a fierce shout. Jace's rhythm was instantly shattered — his thrust wasn't hard enough or fast enough, and even his footwork fell apart. Grede easily sidestepped the messy thrust and kicked Jace in the waist, sending him sprawling.

Grede laughed. "Get up. Again."

Jace spat out the dirt and grass from his mouth, stretched his waist, and stood back up, looking at Grede once more.

So that was some kind of Demoralizing Shout, huh?

But while getting back up, he'd already thought up another strategy.

Attack while using Light to blind his remaining eye, then charge straight in — let's see if he'd see it coming. Jace took a skipping step forward, raised his single-handed sword from still pretty far away. Grede glanced at his sword tip, then at his arm, and his expression shifted.

"Liyouisa!"

Jace spread his left palm wide and suddenly released a burst of light. But in the pitch-black night, the light didn't just hit the other guy — it hit him too.

His vision went completely white. He could only guess at where Grede was and swing his sword down hard — and sliced right through empty air.

The Light faded. Jace felt a strange force slam into his back and send him flying forward, tumbling across the dirt for a distance.

Before he could even get up, his waist and backside started hurting.

"Were you trying to use those little tricks on me, huh?" Grede said with a smile. "But honestly, you've improved quite a bit, Jace. We only started training a month ago, maybe a month and a half at most. I'd say you're already better than the vast majority of sword-swinging folks I've seen in Stormwind. Your attacks are genuinely threatening — I have to take them seriously, can't relax the way I did at the beginning."

"So don't get discouraged," he said, pulling Jace to his feet. "You're fighting an expert, after all!"

Jace wiped his mouth. "How'd you guess I was going to use magic?"

Grede patted his belly. "Ha! Compared to those elven mages and trolls, you might as well write 'I'm about to use magic' on your forehead. You think I was joking when I told you back in Redridge that I've got plenty of experience dealing with mages?"

"But speaking of which, if you'd tried using your Fear on me, I might not have had a good counter, Jace Sesso. Still, since we're using practice swords, let's not use overly destructive magic on each other either. The point is to improve our skills, not kill each other, right?"

"I wasn't planning to anyway."

Jace patted the dry grass off himself, thinking about that naga he'd killed on the shore of Westfall, and how it was a pity Grede hadn't seen it.

Come to think of it, Grede had probably killed way more powerful monsters than that little naga. If he'd been on that beach, the creature probably wouldn't have finished casting her first Curse of Weakness before he smashed her brains in.

He still had a long, long way to go in sword training.
