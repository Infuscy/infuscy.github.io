## Chapter 1130: The Westvale Circus

"Welcome to the Westvale Circus, sir."

A fellow dressed in yellow and black, wearing a pointed black hat that made him look like a fat bee, shouted at Jace. "The circus before you can provide you with the most novel, most entertaining performances on the whole continent of Lordaeron…"

"More interesting than the Darkmoon Faire?" Jace asked.

"Of course not." The fat bee flapped his hand and bowed slightly. "But our performances have one unique advantage over the Darkmoon Faire — and that is, they don't exist only in people's imaginations, but are real and genuine reality!"

Jace smiled, took out three silver coins, and tossed them to the man. The fat bee immediately tore a ticket slip for Jace. "Here you have it, sir. Enjoy yourself!"

"Thank you." Jace spoke as he stepped through the gate, and immediately caught a whiff of the grassy, manure-laden scent of the Barrens plains — though following that came the salty, sweet, and milky smells of various snacks.

This circus's zoo actually had some substance. Right after entering, on this small plaza, one could see a swamp raptor lying in a cage by the road, plus a plains flightless bird brought over from another continent.

On a wooden placard on a pillar not far off, crooked lettering read, "The Red Lightning Tyrant of Kalimoth lies ahead!"

Jace looked in the direction the arrow pointed. That broad gravel path covered with ribbons above should be the road leading to the thunder lizard's enclosure.

Following a group of children led by an older mage along this road for a while, Jace finally laid eyes on that thunder lizard. Set off by these tiny mortals before him, this beast was like a rumbling blood-red steam tank.

Still, among thunder lizards, its size wasn't actually that large. At least a full size smaller than the thunder lizard he, Grede, Sylvanas, and Mosha had hunted in the Barrens. And what with the exhaustion and hunger, it seemed to have only enough strength left to whimper from time to time.

The circus had somehow rendered it incapable of unleashing a chain lightning at the curious citizens of Stratholme outside, though Jace guessed it probably still could — otherwise the circus wouldn't use "Lightning Tyrant" as a gimmick.

He approached a circus worker keeping watch over the thunder lizard. This fellow was dressed up as a mage, wearing an extremely exaggerated and comical yellow wide-brimmed wizard hat, but the yellow robe he wore was genuinely an enchanted robe, and he wore a mage ring on his hand too — this man should be a real mage.

"Good evening, sir," Jace said. "When does the 'Lightning Tyrant' perform its lightning?"

"You're too late, sir." The yellow-hat mage said, "Come back next Monday, half past six in the evening."

"Noted." Jace asked, "What method do you use to control whether it discharges or not?"

"Trade secret," the yellow-hat mage said with a smile.

Jace nodded slowly, then asked, "Do you only have this one thunder lizard? He looks a little lonely."

"Thunder lizards aren't gazelles. They don't mind living alone," the yellow-hat mage said. "Don't impose human emotions on these beasts, sir. So long as they have food and drink, they're happy. In Kalimoth they face threats from every direction — centaur, tauren hunting, terrifying wind serpents — but here they live in sufficient peace."

"I'm not looking to accuse you of anything. I'm not one of the Humane Society people," Jace said.

"The Humane Society?" The yellow-hat mage frowned.

Jace waved his hand. "Never mind. I just mean to say — for a circus showcasing Kalimoth beasts, isn't having only one thunder lizard a bit too monotonous?"

"Walk a little farther east over there and you can see the proud hyenas and the plains lions. We even have a wind rider," the yellow-hat mage said.

"A wind rider. Interesting." Jace nodded. "The circus had a dragon before, didn't it?"

"What, what…" Jace was startled. "You mean a real dragon? Not just a bigger thunder lizard or a mutated kodo?"

"No, a real dragon, sir. With two wings, four limbs, and a long tail — a mana wyrm," the mage said. "And it was bigger than any mana wyrm you've ever heard of. Even compared to the red dragons that attacked Hillsbrad, it wasn't much smaller. But it's already been bought by a Quel'Thalas noble."

"All right, so a mana wyrm." Jace breathed a sigh of relief.

After all, getting a real dragon into a cage and charging admission to show it off was something even orcs couldn't pull off… wait. A Quel'Thalas noble?

"You mean an elven noble bought that dragon from you?" Jace asked.

"Yes," the mage said. "It was not long ago."

A mana wyrm possessed a powerful enough soul to travel between the dream and reality. Using it as a provider of soul power couldn't have been more suitable.

An elf buying a magical beast like this not long ago was absolutely not a simple coincidence. "How much did it go for? Who bought it?"

"Trade secret," the yellow-hat mage smiled.

Jace smiled back at him, then took out the silver ring King Terenas had given him and placed it before his eyes.

"The Lordaeron Knight's Medal…" The yellow-hat mage's smile vanished on the spot. He bowed slightly, glanced at the other spectators, then said in a low voice, "I'm sorry, my lord. But I'm not very clear on the specific details of this transaction either. But I can take you to see our boss."

"After you, mage sir." Jace extended his hand.

The yellow-hat mage led Jace away from the thunder lizard's enclosure, through a stretch of tents, and arrived in front of a large tent. He had a quiet word with a dwarf standing guard at the entrance. The dwarf looked at Jace in surprise, then said, "Follow me, my lord. I'll take you in."

Jace nodded. The dwarf walked to the tent entrance and said, "A messenger of the King wishes to see you, respected Mr. Philemon!"

"Let his lordship straight in, then!" came the reply from inside.

The dwarf nodded, Jace lifted the tent flap and walked in. Inside was a refined room filled with ornaments of all kinds in exotic and different racial styles — less like the room of a zoo owner, more like the mysterious parlor of a circus fortune-telling crone.

This Philemon was not tall, but wore an expensive shirt, jacket, and trousers, and had a straight-backed, confident bearing. Seeing Jace, he immediately walked over and extended a hand. "Rubidi Philemon."

Jace shook his hand. "Jace Sesso."

"Jace Sesso?" The circus boss's eyes widened in surprise, then glanced at the ring on Jace's hand, and nervously rubbed his palms together. "I'm sorry, magister. No one told me you were coming to visit my circus today… please, sit down."

"And I didn't tell anyone either, Mr. Philemon," Jace said. "I won't sit. Make it quick — I heard your circus sold a mana wyrm to an elven noble before. Is that true?"

"There is such a matter," Philemon said with some embarrassment. "But I can't reveal too many specifics, magister."

"This involves a very serious crime connected to dark magic, Mr. Philemon," Jace said. "If you insist on citing trade secrets to me again, I'll fly to Dalaran and apply to Archmage Antonidas for a higher-level search warrant."

"I… I didn't mention trade secrets," Philemon said innocently.

Still, at the mention of Dalaran and Antonidas, Philemon was clearly cowed quite a bit. He lowered his head. "That elven noble was quite mysterious. His name was Thalaldis something — some family name. You know how easy it is to mix up these elves' surnames."

"Is he in Stratholme?" Jace asked.

"He should be in Stratholme, but I don't know his exact address, magister." Philemon's tone was already somewhat panicked. After all, a merchant like him had no desire whatsoever to get dragged into dark magic.

"How could you not know the address? How did you deliver that mana wyrm to him?" Jace asked.

Philemon said immediately, "They used some magic to shrink that dragon, then took it away. How am I supposed to make sense of elven business…"

Jace took a breath, thought it over, then suddenly seemed to think of something. "Is there a contract? A transaction contract? With his signature on it?"

"Of course." Philemon nodded.

"Bring it to me. I need to borrow it for a few days," Jace said.

"Uh." Philemon was stunned for a moment.

"In the name of King Terenas Menethil II," Jace said with a hard stare.

"Yes, magister." Philemon said at once.

He rummaged out a scroll from a crate behind him and handed it to Jace. Jace unrolled it and saw, at the very end of the contract, written in Thalassian: "Thalaldis Dawnrose."

He wondered whether this line of writing could be used for magical sympathetic tracking.
