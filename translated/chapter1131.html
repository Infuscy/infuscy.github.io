Chapter 1131: Once and a Thousand Times

By the time Jace returned to the Laurel Inn, it was already the middle of the night. Still, Stratholme’s nightlife seemed richer than Stormwind’s — or perhaps it was the unique “Quel’Thalas culture” of the Laurel Inn that made its guests prefer staying up a little later, since there were still quite a few people in the lobby drinking and conversing.

Aeridania and Vereesa were sitting at a table by the window. Vereesa’s expression was full of worry. Seeing Jace come in, she immediately stood up and waved.

“Good evening.” Jace spoke as he sat down.

“I thought you’d run into trouble,” Vereesa said.

“What trouble could I run into? I was just investigating leads,” Jace murmured.

Vereesa said, “I thought you might have found Malicia’s trail and gone tracking her on your own. The two of us were in here discussing that if you still weren’t back in another half hour, we’d go check those two magic shops.”

“I wouldn’t say I ran into no trouble.” Jace smiled slightly. “A few hours ago I nearly had my throat torn open by a felhound — though luckily my blade was a whole lot sturdier than its teeth.”

“Damn it, don’t joke about things that almost get you killed, Jace.” Vereesa sucked in a breath. “I was worried about you.”

Jace said quietly, “Sorry. But when you’re dealing with shady magic-materials merchants, danger is unavoidable.”

“A felhound hiding in Stratholme,” Aeridania asked. “Is it connected to Malicia?”

Jace shook his head. “Doesn’t seem to be, for now. But the felhound’s owner did let a few things slip. Let’s go upstairs and talk.”

The two elves nodded. The three of them returned to the upstairs room belonging to Vereesa and Jace, made sure there were no busybodies or followers in the corridor, and shut the door tight.

“What did each of you find?” Jace asked.

“The Farstriders don’t know that Magister Malicia has moved into Stratholme either,” Vereesa shook her head. “Logically, moving runestone fragments into Stratholme shouldn’t be so completely unnoticed.”

“I’m not surprised in the slightest,” Aeridania said. “The magisters living in this city told me that Dar’Khan Drathir had been traveling back and forth between Quel’Thalas and Stratholme ever since the end of the Orc Wars. If he helped transport the runestone fragments, it would have been perfectly normal to use some silencing methods or anti-magic barriers to mask the fragments’ power and fool the Quel’dorei’s magical senses.”

Jace asked, “Do either of you know a magister named Theraldri Dawnrose?”

“Theraldri Dawnrose?” Vereesa thought for a moment. “I think I’ve heard that name.”

Aeridania shook her head. “He’s an artist and a collector. He counts as a mage, but he’s not a member of the magisters, Master Sesso. I’m not familiar with him. But what does this have to do with the matter?”

Jace laid out in detail to the two elves everything he’d seen and heard at the zoo, plus his guess that Malicia might be powering her magic by draining a faerie dragon’s soul. After hearing it all, both Vereesa and Aeridania seemed somewhat hesitant.

“I think buying a magical exotic beast to keep wouldn’t be unusual for a Quel’dorei like him,” Aeridania said. “Maybe he’s just seeking some of the vanity that Stratholme can offer, the kind missing from Quel’Thalas…”

Vereesa said nothing, just shrugged, probably feeling Aeridania had a point.

“Then he should have put that faerie dragon on open display, instead of using magic to cover it up and keep it hidden, not wanting anyone to know where he planned to take the beast,” Jace said.

“A summoning ritual isn’t a teleportation spell — it needs enough soul power to fuel it,” he continued. “We don’t have any other clear leads right now. At least help me rule out this lead first. Do you know where he lives?”

Both Aeridania and Vereesa shook their heads. Jace said “mm” under his breath and muttered, “Good thing I kept an ace up my sleeve.”

He spread the circus transaction contract out and pressed it flat on the table. “There’s Theraldri Dawnrose’s signature here. Can we use this signature to perform magical sympathetic tracking?”

“This is blatant criminal behavior, whether in Quel’Thalas or Stratholme, Master Sesso.” Aeridania looked at the writing on the paper. “We don’t have an arrest and search warrant for Theraldri issued by the Silvermoon Council. He just… appeared on this trade contract. But that proves nothing at all.”

“Plotting inside this room hardly counts as ‘blatant,’ does it?” Jace rubbed his eyebrows. “Fine, then don’t concern yourself, magister. Vere, can you help?”

“Of course,” Vereesa said. “But don’t blame her, Jace. She’s already helped us a great deal.”

“I don’t think that way.” Jace shook his head. Aeridania simply turned around to face the wall, pretending not to see.

Jace definitely hadn’t expected that just anyone who came along would be willing to take risks for him without reservation the way Amy had. That this elven magister was willing to turn a blind eye to criminal behavior like this already filled him with gratitude.

Vereesa walked up to the contract and softly chanted an incantation. The signature on the scroll seemed to glow faintly — but also seemed not to. Jace rubbed his eyes, trying to see more clearly, but all he saw was the somewhat embarrassed expression on Vereesa’s face.

The ranger tried again. This time it seemed to improve only marginally over the last attempt.

“Is the residual magical power in this signature not enough?” Jace asked.

“I don’t know. Logically it should be…” Vereesa muttered under her breath. “All right. The sympathetic power I’ve dealt with before has all been very strong, like that Dasmora Highborne dagger… A simple signature like this, even written by a Quel’dorei, is still too difficult for me. The duration of magical contact is too brief. I can’t accurately capture the signature, Jace.”

“It’s all right. At least we know his name now.”

Jace actually felt that tracking a person through nothing but a signature was far too difficult, so it was hard to call himself disappointed.

Aeridania turned back around. “Theraldri Dawnrose very likely lives on the banks of the Greenwood — the valley behind that hill in the old city. That’s Stratholme’s wealthy district, where quite a few Quel’dorei live. Many Quel’dorei like that place, because the source of the Greenwood River is Lake Elrendar. It carries the Sunwell-blessed waters of the Eversong Woods through the forests of Quel’Thalas, passes through Westvale, and flows all the way into Darrowmere Bay.”

Jace had heard his father tell of the nobles in those valley districts when he was a child, of course, and had also seen the rough city map of Stratholme. “That area is full of villas, cottages, and gardens. It’s about half the size of this whole side of the city. At best, that only narrows the search range down to two-fifths the size.”

“When investigating, you must be careful,” Vereesa said. “If Malicia or Theraldri really are connected to Dar’Khan, the moment they recognize us they’ll definitely be on guard and find a way to respond. Then it’ll be even harder for us to root out what Dar’Khan left behind.”

Aeridania pinched the bridge of her nose and sighed deeply in Thalassian. “Damn it, my heart is pounding harder now than when I took the Falthrien Academy senior enchanter exam a hundred and ten years ago.”

“How so?” Jace asked.

“Let me have a try.” Aeridania pressed her hand on the contract on the table. “Let me look at this name. I’d hate for the day to come when Ban’dinoriel gets torn open by some inexplicable dark magic connected to Dar’Khan, and the war axes of orcs and demons are swinging right in front of my eyes, only to regret today having nobly and resolutely upheld the laws of Quel’Thalas…”

“Then thank you, Magister Aeridania.” Vereesa stepped back with her hands behind her back, looking well-behaved. Jace also pulled back his hand and rubbed his fingers reflexively, exchanging a glance with the ranger. Sheepish expressions that couldn’t quite hide a tinge of private delight surfaced on both their faces at the same time.

Aeridania fixed her eyes on that line of text, narrowed her eyes slightly, and moved her lips gently, her fingers pressing on the scroll trembling faintly. Neither Vereesa nor Jace dared make a sound, holding their breath as they watched closely.

After several minutes, Aeridania straightened up, took a deep breath, and smiled. “It was a little harder than I thought, but no problem. I’ve already memorized this signature.”

“Brilliant.” Vereesa clenched her fist. “Can we set out, then?”

Aeridania closed her eyes and chanted another incantation, then said, “There are no clear magical traces connected to this signature nearby.”

“Not even in the Laurel Inn?” Jace asked.

“Indeed none. I imagine if Theraldri has his own estate in Stratholme, then he wouldn’t need to come to the Laurel Inn,” Aeridania said. “This actually increases the likelihood that he lives in the valley district, Master Sesso.”

“Well said,” Jace said. “Then let’s not waste time — let’s head straight to the other side of the hill.”

“Are you sure you don’t need to get some sleep?” Vereesa asked.

“If this isn’t resolved, I won’t be able to sleep, Vere,” Jace said. “If this lead on Theraldri runs dry, we’ll figure out the next step then. We already lost an extra day on the Thondroril River. However much we can make up, we make up. But before we go, I need to eat more to replenish my strength. I’m not like you lot, able to get by on just a ray of the Sunwell’s light.”
