## Chapter 1132: The River Valley

Midnight in Stratholme, and there were still lights burning in the buildings lining the streets. Looking up at the sky here, you couldn't get that dense, spectacular canopy of stars you'd see over Stormwind at night — probably thanks to a little "light pollution" drifting down from Quel'Thalas.

Climb to the top of the low hill in the Old Town and gaze out north of the harbor toward Lordamere Bay, and at the edge of your vision, in the deep black, you could still make out a faint thread of dark blue — that had to be the last glow of the Sunwell.

From the square where the Laurel Inn stood, you circled the statue and kept heading north; the broad avenue soon gave way to a narrow gate, and past that, the road started uphill.

The slope was steep enough to be ridiculous, punctuated by flights of stairs that popped out of nowhere at the switchbacks. The streets of the hilltop Old Town were narrow, winding, and tangled — the houses on either side staggered at different heights, so old and worn it felt like a good rain could flatten them. It was a completely different world from the districts down below.

There was no Old Town in the game. Behind Stratholme's game map there were only impassable mountains and cliffs; farther north lay Quel'Thalas.

Jace had always suspected that the Scourge's undead city in the far south of Quel'Thalas — Dathomar — might have some special connection to the inaccessible northern part of Stratholme. The names and relative positions were both a little suspicious.

After traveling through northern Lordaeron these past few days and studying several versions of the maps from both the human and elven regions, Jace had roughly estimated the distance between Stratholme and the southern tip of Quel'Thalas and figured there were probably still several hundred miles between them. So in reality, there was no connection at all.

The hill wasn't all that high. After a short climb they reached the small monastery at the summit. Looking north, they could see scattered points of light in the deep, forested river valley below — that was the river valley district, where the wealthy lived.

The villas and houses nestled among those lights looked quite lovely and refined. Jace almost regretted not coming by in the daytime to get a proper look.

Then Aradnia said, "I think I can feel something."

She quickened her pace toward a high stone railing, took out the contract scroll, closed her eyes, and began to chant.

When she opened her eyes again, she gazed down at the valley lights for a moment, then pointed toward a small estate on higher ground to the east. "It seems to be in that direction."

"Looks like we're on the right track," Jace said.

Vereesa was the first to bound down the nearby stairs. Jace and Aradnia followed, and the three of them picked up their pace, heading toward that estate.

Even though it was well past midnight, the river valley district wasn't as quiet as it looked. Laughter drifted out of the passing villas and townhouses now and then, and there were even human nobles walking the streets in groups, talking loudly.

They passed through a shaded lane, crossed a stone bridge to the far side of the river, and Jace glanced at the wall of the estate up the street, where a few lights still burned.

When no one was around to see, Aradnia tried the magical tracking spell again, then nodded. "It's over there. I'm certain."

"What do we do next?" Vereesa asked.

"Confirm the evidence first," Jace said. "Best case, we can confirm that Theraldri is connected to Malicia, or that there are traces of demons and shadow magic in that estate."

"I still think relying on a single faerie dragon—" Vereesa clicked her tongue, as if reluctant to challenge Jace's theory again, and left the thought unfinished.

But now that they'd gotten this close, staring at an elven estate that was lovely but otherwise unremarkable, Jace was starting to have his own doubts.

The three of them walked closer along the cobblestone path. As they passed the wall of another estate, Aradnia suddenly stopped dead in her tracks.

They were still some distance from the estate on the low slope they were heading toward. The area was dead silent — not a sound anywhere. The courtyard and house inside the nearby iron fence were pitch-black, with only a sliver of moonlight casting messy shadows.

Now the arcane glow in both elves' eyes had dimmed with caution and wariness until it was barely visible. Aradnia stared at the cobblestones underfoot, her expression grave, and was silent for a long moment.

"What's wrong?" Jace couldn't help asking. "Something off with the magical trail?"

"No," the magister said. "I can feel the runestone's power."

"Where?" Vereesa said.

"Right beneath our feet." Aradnia frowned. "But... the runestone's energy feels off. It's like... I can't quite describe it. Weak. And getting weaker."

As she spoke, her eyes suddenly flashed, and she looked around in a panic. "Damn it — it's gone."

"Gone?" Jace asked. "Someone shut down its power? Like that silencing technique you mentioned before?"

"It doesn't feel like it was shut off." Aradnia said thoughtfully. "More like... it moved away."

Before the other two could react, she walked straight over to a weed-choked path by the roadside and looked around, but clearly found nothing.

"The runestone... walks?" Vereesa followed her. "Are you sure, Magister Aradnia? Could you be mistaken?"

Aradnia said at once, "No. I grew up near the Falithas runestone, and I've taken part in its maintenance. I know exactly what this feels like. There's no mistake — but the sensation has genuinely disappeared."

"At least the runestone's power was underground, right, magister?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," Aradnia said.

"So we're still dealing with a basement in the end." Jace clicked his tongue.

He scanned the courtyards on both sides of the road, then looked up at the lit estate on the hill. "We're still a long way from where we're going. Would a tunnel from over there really stretch all the way here?"

He pointed at the metal fence beside them. "What do you think — could the entrance to the basement be in this courtyard?"

Aradnia approached the railing and looked into the dark grass and trees beyond. By the faint moonlight, it looked like no one had tended to the place in ages.

"The runestone's energy vanished completely here... I can't be sure why, or whether it has anything to do with this courtyard. It's just too strange."

Vereesa did a quick survey of the area. "No one's here. Should we go in and have a look?"

"Let's go." Jace nodded and was the first to climb over the fence.
