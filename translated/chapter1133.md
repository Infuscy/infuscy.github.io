## Chapter 1133: Darkness Beneath the Light

The river valley district reminded Jace of the Park in Stormwind, though here, nestled along the riverbank, it wasn't quite as compact, so each villa had a fairly spacious courtyard.

Jace, Vereesa, and Aradnia observed the courtyard for a while, confirming no one was coming in or out or patrolling, before they started searching everywhere for a hidden trapdoor leading underground, or a well mechanism or something similar.

The courtyard wasn't in great shape — it hadn't quite reached the point of being overgrown, but it looked like it had been neglected for a while.

From here, they could even make out guards or servants passing through the shadows of the doorway lights at the estate up the hill, where the elven noble Theraldri probably lived. By comparison, this place didn't look like it could hide anything important at all.

After searching every corner of the courtyard, Vereesa glanced up at the moon's position, then over at Jace and shook her head. Aradnia had come up empty too.

"You haven't sensed any more of the runestone's energy?" Jace whispered.

Aradnia didn't answer; the certainty she'd felt earlier wasn't as firm anymore. Vereesa pointed toward the building at the end of the road. "Let's finish searching inside first."

The front door of the building was wrapped in several heavy chains, but of course none of them had any intention of going in that way. They worked their way along the edge of the courtyard to a wall whose lower half was already crawling with wild vines, and felt their way to a window.

Vereesa easily opened the window and climbed in — no traps, no guards.

Jace and Aradnia climbed in after her. Inside was an empty corridor, pitch-black and dead silent except for what sounded like rats scurrying inside the walls.

Vereesa took the lead. The other two followed her carefully down the corridor and into an open doorway.

It was a rather luxurious bedroom. Paintings, carpets, a vanity that probably held jewelry and cosmetics, a wardrobe — all sitting neatly in place, as if the owner had only just stepped out.

Moonlight spilled in through the window, accompanied by the faint, ethereal sound of someone playing a stringed instrument from somewhere beyond the courtyard... Jace was increasingly sure they'd come up empty.

He glanced at Aradnia. The magister's face was expressionless too.

Vereesa, for her part, paid little mind to the irrelevant contents of the room. After checking under the carpet and under the bed, she moved toward another door. They hadn't searched the whole house yet — it was too early to get discouraged.

She opened the other door, observed quietly for a moment, then stepped through. It was an even longer corridor, with several doors on either side leading to different rooms.

None of the doors seemed to be locked; some were left ajar. Vereesa moved along opening them one after another, watching for traps in the floor or hatches leading to a basement.

When she reached the second door on the right and reached for the handle, Jace caught her wrist and stopped her. "Wait."

He spoke barely above a breath. Something about this door felt wrong. "Shadow magic, Vere. Careful — I can barely see it."

Vereesa looked at him in surprise and pulled her hand back, while Aradnia didn't seem to have noticed anything either. A gloom invisible to the naked eye seeped from the crack around the door, as if the whole thing were coated in malice. For a moment Jace couldn't quite pin down what was wrong with it.

"Can you try to dispel any enchantment on this door?" Vereesa asked.

"I don't know shadow enchantments well enough, my lady," Aradnia said helplessly.

Then both of them looked at Jace.

Jace glanced at the door. He certainly didn't know any spell to dispel shadow magic, but he could call in someone who did.

"Then allow me to call in some help, Magister Aradnia," Jace said.

"Now?" the magister asked.

"Right now." Jace gripped the staff on his back, closed his eyes, and began chanting the summoning spell for a felhunter. Aradnia stared in shock as the air before them was torn open by the power of the Twisting Nether, but she didn't try to stop it.

A felhunter "dropped" onto the floor from the rift in the void. Jace said softly in the pit lord's tongue, "Dazarm IV."

The felhunter let out a responsive "cough-cough-cough." Jace continued in the demonic language, "Stay as quiet as you can. Devour the magic on this door."

Dazarm swished its spiked tail and walked up to the door. Aradnia backed up several steps, pressing herself against the wall, eyes wide and locked on the felhunter, her hands clenched involuntarily.

Dazarm's tentacles rose and swayed back and forth. It let out a low "hrrrrm" and opened its blood-red mouth lined with rows of spiked teeth. Jace felt the gloom on the door begin to take form as tangible shadow magic.

The door darkened for an instant. Dazarm lunged forward and swallowed the shadowy darkness in one gulp. In a heartbeat, the oppressive gloom hanging over all of them vanished completely.

After confirming there was nothing else wrong with the door, Jace pushed it open.

Inside was another, smaller bedroom, with an elegant bookshelf and desk. On the wall above the bed hung several portraits of human-bodied figures with animal heads. Though the animals' features had been beautified by the painter into human-like smiles, that only made them more unsettling.

Jace looked behind the door and found a rune of magical ward painted on it in some unknown pigment. Vereesa studied it for a moment. "I recognize this."

"I didn't sense it at all," Aradnia said.

"Because this isn't made with the Arcane. This is a Zul'Aman hexxer's Shadow Despair ward, Aradnia — a terrifying magical trap." Vereesa kept her voice low. "If I'd accidentally touched it just now, the powerful shadow magic might have driven us to kill each other."

"A hexxer?" Jace asked. "There are Amani trolls here?"

Vereesa said, "But judging from my assessment... it's more likely the owner of this place deliberately set a trap the magisters wouldn't recognize, aimed specifically at you, Aradnia. The Farstriders have dealt with renegade Quel'dorei like this before."

"Damn it... was this Malicia's idea?" Aradnia frowned. "I don't like being outmaneuvered like this."

"It's not just that you don't recognize it — if Jace hadn't warned me, I wouldn't have sensed it either," Vereesa said. "But at least this proves... there's something here worth protecting."

Jace nodded. Just then Dazarm IV let out a low "thrum-thrum" and paced back and forth around the bed, as if there was something underneath.

The three of them noticed its odd behavior, exchanged a glance, and pushed the bed aside together, revealing a trapdoor underneath.

Aradnia murmured an unlock spell and opened the simple metal lock on the door. Jace lifted the hatch, and Vereesa drew her short blade and dropped in.

For a villa of this size, the basement beneath it was astonishingly narrow and cramped — just a single gloomy corridor. Vereesa took the lead, carefully feeling for traps or mechanisms, but the place seemed relatively calm.

They followed the corridor down and down to God-knew-what depth, then suddenly opened into a wide chamber. But it was pitch-black in here too, with no lighting at all.

After confirming there didn't seem to be anything else alive in the space, the two elves slowly brightened the arcane glow in their eyes and surveyed the chamber carefully.

Even with his vision adjusted to the dark, Jace couldn't make out a damn thing in here — and it was about the same even with the elves' eyes providing illumination.

He drew Azure Song. From what he could see, this appeared to be a natural cave, with few signs of artificial construction. How could a place like this exist beneath Stratholme's wealthy district?

"Here," Aradnia said suddenly.

Vereesa and Jace both went over. Aradnia conjured a Light in her hand and illuminated the wall in front of her. Before them appeared a stone gate of a very distinctive style.

The gate had a grotesque half-insect, half-beast face. The stone pillars on either side were carved with unidentifiable patterns like rib-spikes or the legs of an arthropod.

Even though it was just a single gate, Jace immediately recognized where this style came from.

Naxxramas — or rather, the style of the Scourge's floating citadels that wouldn't appear for many years to come.
