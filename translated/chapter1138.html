Chapter 1138: The Silver Hand

By the time they returned to the surface, the sky had already begun to lighten.

The verdant river valley was tranquil and serene. Neat rows of white-walled, blue-tiled villas lined the heights on both sides of the river, facing the morning light — bright and clean.

Members of the Church of the Holy Light had already gathered around the courtyard. Several priests stared in shock at the undead orc remains brought up from underground.

A bishop nearby was conversing with Magister Vorethal about something, and his expression was hard to call pleasant.

After all, a Quel'Thalas noble had bought a house in Stratholme's wealthy district only to use it for necromantic and demonic experiments — the elves would probably need to have a good long talk with Stratholme's administrators about this.

The cleanup of this cellar was handed over to the Church and the elven envoys. The magisters prepared to set out immediately, taking the charred runestone fragment, along with Theraldri and his accomplices, back to Quel'Thalas — no point taking any chances.

The elves and half-elves, mages and thugs who served Theraldri and lived in the Dawnrose estate up on the hill were numerous — by now, the Farstriders and magisters had already rounded up several dozen of them. Although most of them appeared to know nothing whatsoever about the cellar beneath the other estate or Dar'Khan's plans, the whole lot of them was in a state of bewilderment and panic.

Though he hadn't slept all night, and the tension that had kept him going had now given way to fatigue, his mind full of wanting to find somewhere to sleep, he couldn't afford to waste much more time in Stratholme.

After all, he still needed to go to Lordaeron to see his parents, and both he and Vereesa had important materials seized from Dar'Khan's underground vault in their packs right now. Until he could secure these materials somewhere safe, he had no hope of sleeping peacefully.

On the other hand, the growing crowd of Church of the Holy Light and Silver Hand members gathering in Stratholme also made Jace feel rather uneasy.

Not just him — Vereesa, who was briefing the Farstriders at the moment, was also nervous as hell.

She wasn't worried about the Church business. She was worried that the metal small box in her satchel was bulging rather conspicuously.

However, the attention of the crowd of elven mages was all on that large group of Dawnrose subordinates, so for the moment no one cared what Vereesa was carrying on her.

And Jace could also tell that Vereesa wasn't just nervous right now — there was also a near-manic anticipation in her.

What she carried in her satchel was very likely a key — a key to Draenor. And more importantly, standing beyond that door was her own elder sister.

Though Jace also knew clearly that even if that box truly contained a Draenei crystal recording the coordinates to Draenor, he couldn't rely entirely on this source of questionable origin. Especially since the source most likely traced back to orcs — which made it all the more dangerous.

He'd need to find another set of coordinates to cross-reference it with. Otherwise he'd end up teleporting into some bizarre demon prison and getting locked up at lightspeed, or lifting his head the moment he walked through the portal only to find himself surrounded by a huge crowd of orcs.

Just then, a very tall lord with grey-brown hair appeared in the crowd, and the priests all made way for him. Though this fellow looked to be in his fifties or sixties, he was extraordinarily tall.

Jace estimated his own height to be over one meter eighty, nearing one meter ninety — already considered tall among humans and High Elves. But this man was clearly a full head taller than him, probably two meters, and because he was so tall he appeared slightly gaunt. Though Jace knew full well that this fellow was built solid as a wild bear.

So the moment he appeared at the street corner, he drew quite a bit of attention.

Although this man was wearing a grey-blue linen shirt and a linen cloak of the sort one saw everywhere in the river valley district, he wore a gem-studded silver circlet on his head, and as he walked, a heavy libram occasionally peeked out from beneath his cloak. This was a paladin leader of the Order of the Silver Hand.

Seeing him approach, Vorethal inclined his head slightly. "Lord Saidan Dathoren." The tall man also placed a hand over his chest. "Master Vorethal."

He was Saidan Dathoren?

Dathoren, like Turalyon and Tirion Fordring, was one of the founders of the Order of the Silver Hand. In the future, after the Scourge destroyed Lordaeron, he would lead the remnants of the Order of the Silver Hand in continued resistance, only to be killed and possessed by the dreadlord Balnazzar, who used his name and prestige to found the Scarlet Crusade.

After Vorethal conversed with this paladin lord for a while, he introduced Jace beside him.

When Dathoren looked over, Jace always felt that their interaction seemed less like introducing a friend and more like identifying a suspect.

"Mage Jace Sesso." Dathoren offered a salute. "I am Saidan Dathoren, commander of the Order of the Silver Hand in Stratholme. I've heard a great deal about you."

"Likewise, my lord," Jace said.

"Long ago, before the Arathi Empire split, the humans living here once described the threat of spider demons. There are also records of bizarre, partially collapsed underground chambers discovered beneath Stratholme in the past, with nerubian bones left inside." Dathoren looked toward the villa. "But in the last hundred years, this is the first time a place like this has actually been excavated."

Vorethal said, "It's hard to say whether Theraldri happened to buy a villa built over such a cellar, or bought this place specifically for the cellar."

"I think before this villa was built, the owner of this land at the time — whoever it was — should already have known there was a nerubian cellar underneath," Jace said.

"How so?" Dathoren asked.

"This villa has no normal basement." Jace said. "For a villa of this not-inconsiderable size with a courtyard, that seems rather strange. And that underground entrance wasn't in a warehouse, wasn't in the courtyard — it was inside a small bedroom. I think the original owner intended to keep things hidden from the start."

"It sounds less like a spur-of-the-moment decision and more like activities that have been going on under our noses for many years." The paladin nodded. "The various troubles that followed the orc war caused the Church to more than neglect some matters within the city. Although this crime was committed by elves, the Silver Hand also bears an inescapable responsibility."

"Quel'Thalas will do its utmost to investigate this matter jointly with the Kingdom of Lordaeron, my lord," Vorethal said. The three of them discussed the details of the cellar a while longer, as well as that strange black statue.

By the time the Magisters' Assembly was preparing to set out on their return to Quel'Thalas, Dathoren still hadn't brought up King Terenas's intention to have Jace "further his studies" with the Silver Hand, and Jace was perfectly cooperative in not opening his mouth about it either.

Sending a black mage famous throughout the entire Alliance to the Silver Hand was, more or less, a joke. Perhaps King Terenas saw it as some kind of "redemption" or "prodigal son returns," but the paladins and priests were unwilling to let a sorcerer wear the Silver Hand's sigil, and Jace himself had no desire to stride into an abbey or cathedral and chant scriptures alongside them.

Dathoren, as the leader of Stratholme's paladins, definitely knew about the king's earlier decree, but he clearly also sensed the awkwardness implicit between the two of them, and probably just chose to pretend he'd forgotten the whole thing — and Jace was more than happy not to bring it up.
