## Chapter 114: The Alliance Internment Act

For the next two or three weeks, Jace practiced swordsmanship at night and continued reinforcing his Arcane Intellect casting during the day, extending its duration more and more.

Malin's requirement was to reach the standard of assisting with Mage Armor practice — it had to last at least three to four hours. When Jace had first successfully cast Arcane Intellect that day, it had only lasted a few minutes before fading.

So to meet that standard, he had to practice even more diligently, precisely grasping the technique of manipulating arcane energy.

But to avoid gradually losing his mind under Arcane Intellect's influence, the way Malin had warned about, he always used Sazeno as his test subject. After all, whether the little guy was smart for a while or dumb for a while, he couldn't tell the difference himself — as long as he felt like he could see more clearly, that counted as it working.

Of course, besides sword training and practicing Arcane Intellect, the most important thing was continuing to learn Orcish and practicing his essay writing.

At the end of October, Master Dushan announced the exam topic, pretty much what Malin had guessed: use Orcish to discuss the main conflicts and common interests among the various kingdoms within the Alliance right now.

Jace planned to pick a topic that, compared to the red-hot Alliance Expedition theme, had cooled off a bit recently — the Alliance Internment Act.

It wasn't that he didn't want to ride the hype, it was that he knew too much about the Alliance Expedition's future. The fate of the "Sons of Lothar," the missing General Turalyon and Alleria Windrunner, and the Thousand-Year War that followed, the struggle on Argus, the battle between the Burning Legion and the Army of the Light…

He was worried he'd unconsciously reveal too much in his essay or the Q&A, exposing things that shouldn't be exposed.

Caro Dushan himself was not only an Orcish teacher but also proficient in many languages, had lived in many places, and understood the cultures of different races and kingdoms.

His knowledge of history and geography ran deep. If Jace accidentally let slip something he shouldn't know, Dushan would definitely catch it.

The "Alliance Internment Act," on the other hand, was much safer. After all, this act had been dead in the water ever since the fall of Lordaeron, and Jace didn't know any more about it than the locals here did.

The "Alliance Internment Act," simply put, was a bill concerning the handling of orcish prisoners of war, passed by the Alliance under the leadership of the Kingdom of Lordaeron.

It required all members of the Alliance to concentrate their orcish prisoners in a newly built internment camp below Alterac Valley and to jointly fund its operation.

Recently, many kingdoms or factions holding orcish prisoners had discovered a problem — the orcs were no longer as savage and bloodthirsty as they'd been during the war. Instead, they seemed to be suffering from something like a collective epidemic of weakness and drowsiness.

The leader of the Kirin Tor, Antonidas, believed this was because the orcs' demonic influence was fading. So he supported Terenas II's position: continue to detain these orcs, find a way to cure the demonic influence, and seek coexistence with them, with an eye toward putting them to work someday to make up for the losses of the war.

But the views of Dalaran and Lordaeron met with fierce opposition from other kingdoms. After the internment camps were established, the argument for coexisting with orcs was no longer the focus of the discussion.

Because the maintenance costs of the internment camps were shared by the entire Alliance, the kingdoms that had suffered heavy losses in the war could barely afford to feed and house all those orcs after the war, let alone fund the very people who had killed their citizens and burned their lands.

The main thing was that every country had already put out no small sum for the reconstruction of the new Stormwind, and supporting the internment camps made their finances even more stretched.

What was supposed to showcase the Alliance's level of civilization — the internment camps, which had initially flourished — came to an abrupt halt when the treasuries of the major kingdoms ran dry, turning into a thorn in the side of relations among the Alliance's internal kingdoms.

To make matters worse, the original administrator of the internment camps was the renowned Danath Trollbane, who had joined the Alliance Expedition and could no longer serve in that role. So politicians and nobles who openly opposed the Alliance Internment Act grew more and more numerous.

Because Stormwind had been mostly destroyed in the war, the entire kingdom had also been the most valiant in resistance and the most severely damaged. So in the various Alliance bills after the war, Stormwind was basically always the beneficiary.

The various kingdoms opposing the bill all supported funding Stormwind's reconstruction, and Stormwind itself had received enormous help from Lordaeron and Dalaran. So King Wrynn didn't want to offend either side, and the politicians knew better than to lose their composure in discussions of this act. This instead made Stormwind a place where the topic could be discussed relatively objectively.

Study magic and Orcish during the day, and don't let up on the sword training with Grede at night.

For one thing, like Jace said, relaxation was part of it. But more importantly, he was feeling more and more how important power was in this world.

He'd be leaving Stormwind soon, returning to Lordaeron, returning to Hillsbrad. Jace didn't know what even more dangerous things he'd encounter on that land.

He had to prepare for it, as fully prepared as possible in every respect — body and mind alike.

Once he boarded the ship to Southshore, there'd be no turning back.

Actually… practicing swordsmanship and magic was even more important than practicing Orcish.

If his Orcish wasn't up to par, he'd just have to pay his own way to Lordaeron. At that point he could just sell the naga pearl strand in his hand. Even if he couldn't get the forty silver the merchant had quoted, it could at least cover part of the ship ticket.

But both his physical fitness and his magic level would determine his odds of surviving after leaving Stormwind.

So whether by day or by night, he was incredibly serious and hardworking. After getting his ten-silver salary from Malin, which secured his living expenses for these two months, he grounded himself even more in self-improvement.

The intensity of his nighttime sword training sometimes even surprised Grede.

After all, Grede didn't know what Jace had encountered on the shore of Westfall. The more Jace thought about how weak that naga might have been, the more he thought about his own weakness, and then he'd feel just how powerless he was in this world.

Once you set a goal and work toward it, time flies. Before he knew it, November 12th had arrived — the day of the Orcish exam.

During this time, besides helping Malin deliver two more packages to Lakeshire and Stonewatch, Jace had been improving himself nonstop.

By this day, he'd finished his essay and revised it several times, and prepared possible answers in Orcish to handle Master Dushan's exam.
