## Chapter 1141: Swings of Mood

As he left the range of the Sunwell's light with that pack full of things, Jace felt a wave of comfort and relaxation.

The blue wind hawk flapped its great wings, soaring over the undulating pine forests of the northern Eastweald mountains. It even gave Jace the thrill of a bank robber in a movie who'd just missed the wailing police cars and was bolting into the distant river of traffic.

The cloudless deep-blue sky, the gently swaying green sea of trees, the snow crowning the peaks — with his brown hair whipping wildly in the wind, Jace even wanted to roar out across the vast expanse of Lordaeron.

He'd actually been planning to preserve a little dignity, but he hadn't expected Vereesa to shout out first, then laugh happily.

Although Vereesa had been born in Quel'Thalas, her heart truly had always leaned toward the broader world beyond. No wonder so many Quel'dorei felt she didn't act like an elf at all, but more like a human.

A single day wasn't enough to fly from Quel'Thalas to Alterac Valley. Wind hawks didn't need to sleep every night, but after flying all day they'd still get tired.

The two of them landed in a dense red pine forest east of Andorhal. It was still rather chilly here, so Vereesa gathered branches, cleared a patch of ground, and started a fire. She took off her cloak and boots and moved close to warm herself by the flames.

The wind hawk found a sturdy pine tree on its own, clung to it as a temporary roost, tucked its head into its wings, and quickly fell asleep. Jace found a tree trunk to lean against, threw his cloak over himself, and eagerly pulled out that orc warlock's box.

An orc warlock had been able to seal it — so as a warlock himself, why not try opening it? Besides, there was nothing else to do right now; he might as well give it a shot.

The iron box wasn't large — even smaller than a shoebox. Jace carefully examined it from all angles while sensing the shadow power contained within, studying the orcish and demonic script on its surface.

As he pondered this sinister seal, he felt a strangely icy, desolate taste seep out from deep in his heart — like standing on an empty, deserted street, waiting in the cold night wind for someone who didn't know when they'd return, unsure whether to stay or whether to leave.

This feeling quickly struck Jace's consciousness, even briefly tugging at his shadow power. But as Jace took a deep breath and steadied his emotions, the mutual pulling sensation vanished.

He raised his head. A trace of that sensation — both lonely and somewhat lost — still lingered faintly in his chest. Though he knew this was the shadow seal on the box launching an "attack" to try to stop his interference, he still let out a soft sigh.

A flash of light from not far away pulled him out of his immersion in shadow. Jace thought Vereesa had thrown something into the fire, but it turned out she was waving the two warblades forged from the Apocalypse.

She seemed to have gotten a sudden impulse, rolled up her sleeves, and swung them back and forth a few times, barefoot on the soft, spongy humus soil, as if dancing among the trees.

Noticing Jace's gaze, Vereesa coughed, crisply sheathed the two blades one after another, and put her hands behind her back. "Um — I was just trying them out."

"Carry on." Jace waved a hand and turned his attention back to the box before him, planning to try a different emotion.

He figured that if he could influence the emotion of the shadow seal on the box in reverse — interfering it into an emotion unsuitable for the seal's continued existence — he might be able to weaken it, or even make it fail.

It's just that he'd never done anything like this before, so he didn't know if it would work.

Jace decided to use the rather forceful emotion of persistence to launch an attack against the "loneliness" and loss emanating from the box. Although he wasn't exactly familiar with the feeling of "persistence" — after all, even when he'd been saving up to buy a house for his parents before, he'd been far from persistent, and there'd been moments in between when he couldn't resist spending lavishly on drinks and meat with Grede.

After failing once more, he sighed and lay back against the pine trunk. He hadn't expected that this little exercise would drain more mana than a few Shadowsteps.

The warlock who'd cast this seal was no ordinary one. Considering his connection to Kel'Thuzad probably tied back to Deathwing in Outland at the time, its source was most likely a high-ranking member of the Shadow Council.

If what was really stored inside this box, as that letter scroll had said, was a Draenei crystal containing Draenor's coordinates, then its destination might be even more dangerous.

"How's it going, Jace?" Vereesa asked as she placed the two swords under the tree.

"I don't know much about shadow seals. Probably best to go back and find Sazeno — he'd be more reliable," Jace said. "That imp knows a great deal about the Twisting Nether and demons," Vereesa said.

Jace said, "His master wanted to defect to the Burning Legion in the past, so he forced him to learn a lot of techniques for communicating with demons, plus all sorts of other miscellaneous knowledge about the Twisting Nether... Of course, that's just what he claims."

"The demons following you all seem ridiculously powerful, Jace." the ranger asked as she walked over. "Why is that?"

"The elves by my side are the same," Jace said.

"Smooth talker." Vereesa walked over with light steps. "Tell me, Jace — if it had been Morophil instead of me helping you fight that statue in the spider monster's underground palace, would the battle have been easier? After all, she's so good at..."

"...melee combat."

"You two really love asking these damn questions," Jace said helplessly. "Answer honestly," Vereesa said.

Jace scratched his earlobe. "Since you're asking — it probably would have been a bit easier."

Vereesa frowned. Jace immediately said, "If that statue had gone chasing after her, I wouldn't have cared about her life or death the way I cared about yours. I could've just focused on chanting spells and attacking the enemy... After all, if she dies she can always be found again in the Twisting Nether."

"Don't make it sound so nice. I don't buy for a second that you don't care about her." Though Vereesa's tone was disdainful, she was already barely holding back a smile.

As she said this, she walked over with the air of someone about to pick a fight and lifted her foot, pressing it against Jace's chest.

That kick was quite heavy. Jace was jolted into a cough, his eyes instinctively dropping to her toes, which were smudged with a bit of black dirt. Even as Vereesa slightly eased her foot, he was already starting to feel short of breath and tight-chested.

Jace slowly caught his breath, steadying his breathing. The faint, tart scent of pine needles seeped into his nostrils.

By now, Vereesa seemed to have noticed something off about the atmosphere too. Her gaze shifted cautiously downward, landing below, and her emotions gradually grew hazy as well, her lips moving soundlessly as if unsure what to say.

In the deepening silence of the forest, neither of them spoke for a moment.

The elf, relying on her inhuman balance, tentatively slid her foot downward little by little, hooking the sash of Angrista — a gift from the king — and carefully tugging his trousers down bit by bit. Then, pursing her lips like a child about to play a prank, she pressed her foot there.

The moment the ice-cold, smooth sole of her foot touched that hottest, most sensitive part of him, Jace couldn't help but suck in a breath, bracing his hand against the tree trunk behind him.

Reacting to his sound, Vereesa glanced up at his eyes as if she'd done something wrong, nervously lifting her foot. "Don't." Jace said, catching her ankle.

Vereesa mumbled hesitantly, "Actually, I don't really know how to..."

"Like this," Jace said softly.
