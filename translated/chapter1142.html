Chapter 1142: Departing Strahnbrad

Leaving the red pine forest behind and flying over the Andorhal valley, the Alterac Mountains at the far edge of the horizon grew sharper and sharper. The wyverns touched down at Strahnbrad, the town at the foot of the mountains, because the Farstriders had a post there that could help look after the beasts. Strahnbrad had once been the largest and most prosperous town in the Kingdom of Alterac apart from the capital, Alterac City. After the capital was destroyed by the allied multinational forces, many refugees fled north to the shores of Lake Lordamere and Tirisfal Glades, or settled here. The valley where the town sat was a shortcut for the people of Andorhal heading south to Durnholde, or further east to the Arathi Highlands. Thanks to its convenient roads and the influx of many Alterac refugees, Strahnbrad had shot up to become the liveliest town in the whole region in just a few years. Of course, liveliness brought more than just prosperity — it brought chaos too. From the woodland cabin the Farstrider rangers had rented outside town, looking along the river and the road toward the town, one could see makeshift shacks thrown up all along the way, smoke rising from cooking fires everywhere, and the clamor of voices audible even from this far off. The people coming and going — whether townsfolk or travelers passing through — all wore expressions that could hardly be called positive or optimistic. Vereesa was talking with a ranger; she was the leader of the Farstrider force in Strahnbrad at the moment. "Leader" was a generous term — she was in charge of exactly three rangers, including herself, responsible for gathering intelligence on rogued orcs at the foot of the Alterac Mountains. And right now the other two rangers were both out on missions; she was the only one watching what looked like a hunting cabin. This whole trip to the Alterac Mountains tracking down the Frostwolf orcs — Vereesa hadn't been sure the whole way whether they'd actually find anything. After all, in Eastweald, the Silver Hand paladins and the Farstriders had cooperated for years and still hadn't caught the leaders of more than a handful of rogued orc clans. Whether she, a single ranger, plus Jace, a warlock, could find the base of an orc clan was anyone's guess. Let alone this wasn't the forests and great plains of Eastweald — it was the most mysterious and treacherous great snow mountain on the whole continent of Lordaeron. Still, Jace had been turning something over in his mind. In the game, the battleground "Alterac Valley" pitted the Alliance's Stormpike dwarves, based out of the town of Dun Baldar, against the Frostwolf clan to the south, fighting for control of the entire valley. The town of Dun Baldar sat on a high cliff among the mountains north of Tarren Mill. Jace had known that not long after the war, and had even met a few Bronzebeard dwarves from Dun Baldar. So as long as they located Dun Baldar and headed straight south through Alterac Valley, wouldn't they find where the Frostwolf orcs were? Even if the Frostwolf clan wasn't as powerful as it would become after the New Horde was established in the future, their hideout at this time probably couldn't be too far from that place. But when he saw the Alterac Mountains map that the ranger Kreya produced, with its marked towns, fortresses, and suspected orc sightings, Jace fell silent all at once. The Alterac Valley near Dun Baldar was far more than a single stretch. The Stonehearth Outpost that in the game sat in the lowlands south of Dun Baldar had been shoved way over to the west on this map. And in this area, there were no reports of orc activity at all. The deviation between the map and the game wiped out half his confidence in one stroke. After all, the in-game battleground had to account for balance — equal numbers of outposts and fortresses on the north and south sides, mirrored base positions and terrain. Reality was never that neat. Still, Jace noticed that further west from the Stonehearth Outpost marking on the map was a vast, unoccupied valley. No outposts marked there, and no trace of orc activity. He pointed at the area. "Is there nothing here?" Kreya explained, "It's a frozen lake, Mr. Sesso. The humans of Alterac call it Iceblood Valley. The environment inside is extremely harsh — uninhabitable. It's a no-man's-land." "Iceblood Valley. A frightening name." Vereesa thought for a moment. "Orcs are much larger than us humans, with thicker skin and sturdier bodies. Could they survive in a harsher environment than we could?" She looked at Jace. "If I were an orc, I'd consider hiding in a place where the enemy simply refused to come." Jace nodded. The fact that Vereesa had figured this out on her own meant he didn't even need to come up with a reason to lead her there. Because on the Alterac Valley battlefield, the Frostwolf army's frontmost defensive structure was called the Iceblood Watchtower — probably an orc outpost that would later be established near this frozen lake. "It's not impossible," Kreya said. "But we can't provide any further intelligence, Lady Vereesa. Right now, both the Arathi-controlled territories and the Lordaeron-occupied areas of the Kingdom of Alterac are focused on stabilizing the situation and restoring production." She pointed at the towering snow peaks behind the town. "Because there have been very few orc attacks or injuries in this mountain range — at most sightings and encounters. Compared to Dalremar up north, it's far more peaceful. So the rulers of the nearby towns have little inclination to spend manpower and resources organizing armies and hunters to enter these harsh areas. These unoccupied places are almost entirely unexplored." "I see," Vereesa said. "Could we borrow this map?" "You're planning to enter this area to look for orcs, Lady Vereesa?" Kreya said. "You could wait for the other two to return and accompany you and Master Sesso into the area." "No need," Vereesa said. "We just want to do a quick reconnaissance." "Very well, Lady Vereesa. A safe journey to you," Kreya said. Rubbing shoulders with orcs was obviously something they couldn't let the Farstriders tag along for, otherwise Tirion Fordring wouldn't have gotten captured first — Jace himself might have been dragged to Stratholme for a public trial, and running into Lord Dathrohan a few days later would have been awkward. The streets of Strahnbrad were packed. Refugees, travelers, and soldiers from all across the continent of Lordaeron had gathered here, shouting and cursing in waves. Many refugees in ragged clothes stood on both sides of the street watching the passersby. Jace and Vereesa, two outsiders wearing cloaks and hoods, attracted particular attention. The two of them walked all the way into an inn on the town's central square. Even after they'd gone inside, a group of people sitting on the wooden railing by the fountain in the middle of the square kept staring at them without blinking. There were quite a few people in the inn. Two groups were in the middle of arguments, and the whole place was thick with a fierce anger, a sense of grievance, and unwillingness — it made Jace feel uneasy. He and Vereesa picked a spot far from the arguments and sat down to eat something, though even there they could still hear what they were fighting about. The Alterac people had intensely bitter grudges against the people of two other kingdoms, and because the House of Perenolde had surrendered to the Horde, they could hardly mount open resistance against foreign rule. This pressure kept Strahnbrad perpetually on the edge of erupting into conflict, which was why patrolling cavalry flying the Lordaeron royal banner could be seen everywhere, barely maintaining a surface calm. After a simple night's rest at the inn, the two of them set out heading southwest, following a stream upstream. According to the map, partway up the slope ahead sat Falkenbrad Tower, a watchtower controlled by Stromgarde. This was close to the road Jace had traveled before with Grede, coming down from Alterac City on the way to Durnholde. Once they left the area under the actual control of the Kingdom of Lordaeron, red flags could be seen on the mountains above and below. But after passing Falkenbrad Tower and continuing southwest, even those red Arathi banners disappeared. The terrain grew higher and higher, the snow on the road thicker and thicker. Pushing further forward, the vast unoccupied zone on the map drew closer and closer. That night, Jace and Vereesa rested in an Alterac Kingdom watchtower that had been destroyed during the Orc Wars, managing to start a small fire in a slightly cleaner wind-sheltered room. The wind outside howled like the low growl of a beast. The cold drafts that gusted in from time to time made the already-struggling fire shudder and flicker, making an whooshing sound. Jace finished checking the luggage and looked up to survey their surroundings. The upper half of the tower had been torn clean off, and what remained had been thoroughly scorched. Blackened rubble was scattered everywhere. There was no question — this watchtower had been destroyed by the orcs' red dragon riders. "Will we reach Dun Baldar tomorrow?" Jace asked. "I don't know." Vereesa studied the map by firelight. "At our previous pace, if the map is drawn accurately, we should reach Dun Baldar by nightfall tomorrow. But the road's getting harder, and there seems to be a canyon in between that we know nothing about." She sighed. "Right now I almost wish some orcs would suddenly pop out and ambush this tower, so we wouldn't have to keep searching blindly through these snow mountains…" Before she'd finished speaking, amid the pauses in the shrieking cold wind outside, there suddenly came a rustling sound — like boots or feet rubbing against snow. Vereesa glanced warily toward the outside. Jace shot a look at the fire. It was probably too late to try to put it out now. If there was something out there, it had most likely been drawn by the light inside the tower. After a long stretch with no second round of sounds, Jace whispered, "What was that?" "Last night in Strahnbrad I heard some people say there are a lot of mountain bandits in the snow mountains made up of Alterac people, who specialize in robbing Lordaeron travelers." Vereesa looked at Jace. "I wonder if they rob people from Stormwind too?" "I am a Lordaeron person," Jace said, eyes wide. "Ah. Right." Vereesa shrugged, the corner of her mouth twisting into a wicked little smile. "Should we go take a look?" Jace asked. "Otherwise I won't be able to sleep." Vereesa nodded. The two of them crawled out from under a collapsed pillar and came outside the tower's high wall. The night in Alterac was pitch black, and the wind mixed with snow made it impossible to even see the stars in the sky. As Vereesa concentrated, looking toward the slopes and snow around the tower, and the forests in the canyon further off, the arcane glow in her eyes grew brighter and brighter. Suddenly Jace felt an intense emotion rushing in from somewhere. Vereesa sensed something wrong too, whipping around to grab a short blade at her waist and raising her hand to aim into the darkness ahead. A single-horned ogre with yellowish-brown skin charged out of the shadows, sprinting forward with a spiked wooden club held in both hands — yet he'd made the whole approach without a sound. A giant monster like this had rushed up to them as inconspicuously as a ghost! Seeing that Vereesa and Jace had already noticed him, the ogre roared and raised his club — and then took the short blade Vereesa hurled straight into his chin. He gurgled once and collapsed into the snow, tumbling off the cliff. "Some kind of footstep-silencing spell." Vereesa nocked an arrow to her bow. "Damn it. There's a wizard scheming against us." "Though they've really underestimated me." The elf turned and loosed an arrow into the darkness. A crude howl came from that direction. Facing this kind of blackness, Jace couldn't see a thing. He asked, "Is the wizard nearby?" "I don't know." Vereesa breathed out a cloud of white vapor. "I can't feel how strong the spell's magic is, but it's clearly fading fast. The spell on the ogre that just fell has already worn off, which means it's not a particularly sophisticated enchantment. It probably needs to be replenished frequently. Maybe the sound we just heard was…" Before she could finish, several more shouts rang out from the distance, cutting her off. An ogre came charging up while yelling, "Fresh, tender meat! Battle, charge! For the taste!" "You can't hear my voice, ahahaha! You can't hear my voice — scared ya, didn't it!" Another ogre came scrambling up the slope. By now the shouts of ogres were everywhere in the wind and snow. Vereesa shot an arrow into the ogre that had just climbed up, knocking him back down. Jace said, "Cover me for a bit. I'm going to… summon something to help us find the wizard." "All right." Vereesa nodded. Jace raised Ulthalesh and began chanting incantations. Before long, the tower's high wall and the snow were both lit purple by the tearing rift of the Twisting Nether that opened up. Several ogres that had just charged up were all frightened by the sight, hesitating and backing away. "Dazarum IV," Jace finished the naming in ered'un, then said in the Eredar tongue, "Hunt down the wizard hiding in the back." The felhunter had barely touched down before it let out a low growl toward a snow-covered thicket in the distance. A loud shout came from inside. "Don't let that wolf-demon come over here!" a deep voice roared from within. "I was wrong! Mighty demon lord, I was wrong! I surrender!"
