## Chapter 1143: A Simple Question

An ogre with a dark blue sash tied around his waist crawled out from inside. His horns had been broken off by someone, and his rather gaunt frame was covered in wounds.

After this ogre shouted his surrender, the other ogres hiding in the shadows clearly lost their will to fight — whether from obeying their leader's order or from fear of the felhunter Jace had summoned.

Vereesa conjured a Light cantrip, and the ogre obediently walked into the lit area, raising his hands high. "I don't want to fight a wolf-demon. We didn't mean to provoke a summoner of demons. I'm sorry."

"Where's the wizard? Send him out," Vereesa called.

"No wizard, elf!" the ogre shook his head. "There's only a few of us, no two-brained magician! I just wanted to help the folks of the Rockbiter clan get something to eat, that's all!"

The Rockbiter clan?

Jace didn't recall there being a Rockbiter clan in the Alterac Mountains. Rockbiter was considered one of the larger of the ogre clans, but in the game, the ogres who permanently occupied the ruins of Alterac City called themselves the "Broken Ridge ogres."

"So what's the deal with that silence spell?" Jace asked.

"I don't know." The Rockbiter ogre pointed toward a lowland not far off. "We ambush unlucky folks passing by, but they always spot us and run away. I learned from the orcs to make a wish to the snow mountain, asking the mountain to let us walk without making noise — and then we walked without noise! The great mountain covers us with the wind!"

"What…" Vereesa stared at the ogre in shock, then looked at Jace with utter disbelief.

"Some kind of elemental magic," Jace explained. "The elements here are probably communicating with this idiot for some reason, and they're willing to help him out."

"I'm not an idiot!" the ogre roared angrily. "Even if you're a clever demon lord, you need to correct yourself. Kongge saved the wanderers that Lord Ocalar abandoned. Kongge can feed them! Kongge is a sage!"

Dazarum IV seemed displeased at this ogre being so cocky toward his master, and let out a vicious snarl that made the Rockbiter shaman take several steps back and nearly slip down the slope.

"All right, all right, you're an ogre sage. So you're saying you learned this wish-to-the-mountain method from the orcs?" Jace asked.

"Yes, orcs." The ogre who called himself Kongge raised his hands. "We followed those little people here. They said we could live here, we could make a life here… but it's more dangerous than Frostfire Ridge! Those damn little liars, I'm going to skin them and use it to wipe my ass sooner or later!"

"Do you know where they are?" Jace asked.

"I don't know," Kongge said.

"If you don't know where they are, how are you going to skin them to wipe your ass?" Jace asked.

Hearing that, Vereesa shot Jace a sideways look. Jace shrugged.

Kongge said, "Those orcs have bad tempers. They always like picking fights with all kinds of people, so they got into a war with the hot-headed lads of Broken Ridge. And we look like those hot-headed lads, so they don't trust us, so they stopped talking to us long ago!"

"So where was the last time you saw them?" Vereesa asked.

"Uh." Kongge looked west, then looked north, hesitating for a long while.

The ranger simply went back to the tower, got the map, and held it in front of the ogre. "Can you read a map?"

"Of course I can read a map." Kongge took the map, turned it around a couple of times, then pointed at an unoccupied mountainous area east of Iceblood Valley on the map. "About… um, should be… here."

The ogre's thick finger jabbed at a huge swath of territory, but this area was far from Dun Baldar, Stonehearth Outpost, and everywhere else — the direction was way off from the Frostwolf camp in the game.

Jace frowned. At first he was a bit suspicious, but considering how honest this ogre was being, and that he'd actually met the orcs' shaman here, his information should be more reliable than Jace's, who was working off simple memories from another timeline.

"Even though I don't know where the orcs are, I can help you find them," the ogre said.

"Just ask the great snow mountain!" he declared, raising his hands high. "The mountain will tell me many things… really, I'm not lying to you. As long as you're willing to give us your food."

Jace laughed. "You're negotiating terms with the two of us?"

The felhunter beside him let out a low, demonic growl. Kongge was startled and said, "Then, uh, since you're a clever warlock, then answer Kongge's question. As long as you can answer it, I'll help you!"

"That simple?" Vereesa seemed intrigued. She put her bow away. "What's the question?"

Jace had been thinking about whether he could ask the fire elements, but in such a cold valley the fire elements' power wasn't strong, and he didn't know if he could pile up a massive, ceremonial bonfire like the Frosthummer yetis in a short time.

Since this ogre believed so strongly in the mountain, and these few lone ogres had managed to survive this long under the Farstriders' and human hunters' pursuit, maybe they really did have something.

Kongge lowered his eyes, stared wide, and said, "Then I'm asking — where is my home?"

"Home?" Vereesa blinked.

"Nagrand," Jace answered confidently, then smiled at the elf. "The orcs mentioned the ogres' homeland to me."

"We can't go back to Nagrand!" Unexpectedly, Kongge roared in anger. "A home you can't return to is no home at all. We don't know where home is!"

"Don't rush." Jace said quickly, "You mentioned that Lord Ocalar earlier — where is he? Where's the Rockbiter clan? Isn't that your home?"

"We hate Lord Ocalar! He left us here to die!" Kongge gritted his teeth. "We don't want to go to his home! That's wrong! That's wrong too!"

"So you don't have a place to live right now?" Jace asked again. "A cave or something."

"There is, there's a cave!" Kongge covered his belly and laughed twice. "I can't tell you where, but that place is warm and safe!"

"Warm and safe — sounds like you really like it. That's home." Jace explained, a little helplessly. "So that's home?"

"Is that home?" Kongge asked.

"What else would it be?" Jace asked.

"Home." Kongge lowered his head and mused for a while, murmuring, "We have a home."

He turned and ran toward the other ogres watching from the side, shouting something in a language Jace couldn't understand. Those ogres all shouted excitedly along with him.

"What does that mean?" Vereesa looked at Jace.

"I… don't know," Jace said. "Does that count as answering correctly?"

"The mountain will guide me to help you find the orcs, clever demon lord!" Kongge said excitedly. "Because you helped us find our home!"

"That simple?" Now Vereesa was completely lost. "I guess so."

Jace stood with his hands on his waist, watching the excited ogres, and for some reason, a small place in his heart felt faintly uncomfortable.
