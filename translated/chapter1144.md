## Chapter 1144: Flame in the Cold Wind

Atop the snow-piled cliff, Kongge, leader of the Rockbiter wanderers, stepped through the snow to the highest point.

There were no offerings, no elaborate ritual. He simply faced the stretch of dense pine valleys in the distance, the sheer black cliffs, the snow peaks even higher beyond, and raised his hands high.

"Snow mountain!" the ogre roared not in the elemental tongue, but in Common. "Please guide me to find those orcs!"

Jace seriously doubted whether this ogre's request in anything but the elemental tongue would actually get a mountain to respond — but then, he'd already asked the snow mountain to cover the ogres' footsteps earlier, so he didn't raise any objections.

After a stretch of waiting, aside from a bit of empty echo, Jace heard nothing at all. No element was trying to answer the ogre's roar. The Alterac Mountains were as silent as any ordinary dawn.

But before long, Kongge suddenly turned his head back and said, "All right, I know."

"The mountain told you?" Jace asked.

"Yes," Kongge said. "I know the mountain told me."

Jace looked at Vereesa. Vereesa was looking back at him. Both their eyes were full of suspicion.

The ogre said something to his companions in the incomprehensible language, then beckoned to a slightly shorter but very stout ogre.

The other ogres all left heading north. Kongge explained in Common again, "Odunt and I will take you there. Odunt can take care of me, and I can take care of Odunt. Now follow us, you two clever, capable little people."

"Interesting — not many people call me clever," Vereesa said.

"Not many call me clever either," Jace whispered. "But being called clever by an ogre isn't exactly something to be proud of…"

Regardless of whether Kongge had actually received some kind of revelation from the "god of Alterac" he believed in, at least he was heading in the direction of Iceblood Valley from here.

With two ogres serving as guards and guides, whether or not they could help Jace and Vereesa find the Frostwolf orcs directly, at least they had two more helpers in the snow mountains. Not bad.

Walking along the snow-covered ridge, Jace gazed off to the northwest, deeper into the mountains. According to the map, that was the direction of Dun Baldar, but from here he couldn't find any trace of a dwarf fortress.

"Maybe we should head to Dun Baldar first, then come back to get these ogres' help, Jace." Vereesa stared at the two giant monsters swaying back and forth not far ahead. "That way we could cross-reference with the dwarves' intelligence. Maybe those Stormpike dwarves have leads on the Frostwolf clan."

"I don't think the chances are good," Jace shook his head. "The Stormpike clan are members of the Alliance. If they found leads on the Frostwolf clan, they'd definitely share them with the Kirin Tor, Lordaeron, the Farstriders, and the magistrates of every Alterac town at the first opportunity. But right now the Farstriders know nothing about it, and the magistrate of Strahnbrad is clueless too. There's no reason for the Stormpike to keep it secret."

"You're right, but these fellows are really…" Vereesa walked along, blowing a breath of white vapor into her hands. "Though I admit they do know the orcs better."

Following the ogres deeper and deeper into the snow mountains, gazing at the boundless white peaks and forests all around, one could almost forget they were walking in the heartland of the continent of Lordaeron.

The Frostwolf clan's decision to hide in Alterac Valley was quite wise. This primitive, violent, high-altitude wasteland had never been fully conquered by the humans who'd walked out of Arathi in thousands of years. And now that a group of villains who excelled at hunting and fighting in pure white snow had been added to it, the surrounding humans were even less daring enough to probe inside.

By the next noon, the ogres led Jace, Vereesa, and Dazarum over a high slope. Borrowing the clear sunlight to gaze into the distance, Jace saw a frozen lake sheltered by ancient pine forests, sitting right in the valley between the mountains.

Was that the so-called "Iceblood Valley"?

Though it was within sight, setting out from here — down the mountain first, then across a large stretch of pine forest on the hills to reach that frozen lake — would probably take another full day of walking.

In the game, the pine trees around the frozen lake in the center of Alterac Valley had either been burned to cinders or felled by the Frostwolf and Stormpike armies as war resources.

For a long time, dwarves and orcs had been fighting over the several outposts and fortresses around this frozen lake, so the place had been turned into a bare wasteland by the fires of war.

But here, the pine forests still densely covered the hills and slopes around the frozen lake. There were even scattered pine trees growing on what was probably an island in the middle of the lake.

No wrecked catapults or steel war machines, no fallen war banners or wooden walls burning and belching thick smoke. Everything was still in its most primitive state.

Jace looked at the map in his hand, roughly estimating their location against the direction of the sun. He felt they'd already strayed far from the "Frostwolf camp" position he'd originally anticipated.

The two ogres still walked with resolute strides forward. Jace couldn't help but call out loudly, "Kongge! Did the snow mountain tell you how much further we have to go?"

He'd barely gotten half the sentence out when a gust of cold wind carrying snowflakes blew in from the distance, swallowing his shout. Kongge up ahead turned his head to glance back this way, his figure obscured by the snow haze.

Jace felt this gust of wind was unnaturally cold. Vereesa couldn't help but wrap her cloak tighter and shiver.

After a while, when the snow wind died down, Kongge said, "I've been moving forward according to the guidance the mountain gives me, demon lord."

He pointed at the deep valley to the south of the slope. "It told me the orcs are hiding in that direction, so I walk in that direction."

"I want to know how it tells you," Jace asked. "Does it keep giving you some kind of guidance the whole time — a sound, for instance, or some kind of image pulling you along — or does it tell you a direction at intervals?"

"Uh." The ogre shook his head. "I don't quite understand what you mean… the mountain's guidance is just guidance." Jace stared at the ogre for a while. He wasn't even sure this shaman had understood his question. But just thinking about how to explain his doubts to this fellow gave him a headache.

Instinctively, he glanced at the slightly shorter ogre beside him. Odunt clutched his pine staff in his hand and grinned stupidly. Jace couldn't help but let out a long breath of white vapor. This one was even less reliable.

"If you don't believe me, I can summon the mountain again," Kongge said. Jace nodded.

The ogre shaman found another rock and climbed up, coming to the highest point of this stretch of snow. He raised his hands high and shouted toward a snow peak in the distance to the north, "Snow mountain! Tell me again! Orcs! We mean to find those orcs!"

Vereesa shielded her eyes from the sun and watched the ogre work, looking like she was enjoying the show.

Even knowing he might be able to communicate with the snow mountain and truly possessed some elemental power, watching this slightly ridiculous scene, even Jace — who had some understanding of the shamanistic path — couldn't take it seriously.

He'd expected the ogre to jump down from the rock as confidently as before, then keep striding forward with equal confidence. But this time Kongge stood on the rock for a long while without saying a word.

Kongge's bodyguard Odunt sensed something was wrong too. He stepped forward to check and said, "Kongge, chief, what's the matter?"

"Snow mountain!" Kongge suddenly roared, giving Odunt a fright.

"I can't hear your voice… snow mountain!"

The ogre shaman forced his hands even higher. This time he even rose up on tiptoes slightly.

The snow-piled pine trees around them began to shake. Another gust of cold wind howled in, and the wind and snow suddenly picked up ferocity in the short time the group stayed there.

Snowflake after snowflake clung to Vereesa's silver hair and cloak. She couldn't help but brush her hair back, steadying herself, and called out loudly, "Are you summoning a blizzard?"

"No…"

The ogre murmured, but the wind grew stronger and stronger, the trees shook more and more violently, and soon the sky, the low-lying valleys, and the distant mountain peaks were all obscured by a white haze of snow.

Soon even Kongge couldn't stand steady. He went "Oof!" tumbling off the rock into the snow, clambered up awkwardly, and roared, "A blizzard is really coming! We can't stay here! Down into the forest!"

Jace pulled up his hood and followed Vereesa and the ogres down the slope. He glanced toward the snow peak on the horizon one more time. By now there was nothing to be seen there at all. The snowflakes blown by the wind kept striking his face.

In the white chaos, he felt like he heard some low sounds — like the wind, like snowflakes buzzing past, and also like…

"Deception…"

"Darkness…"

The four people and one dog charged into the pine forest. The wind and snow made the pines shudder. The howling cold wind pouring through the forest merged with the murmurs of elements, as if every tree, every rock, was issuing a warning at Jace.

"You must not go any further, demon."

"Leave here, leave Alterac, let them go!"

The whispers grew angrier and clearer by the degree. The two ogres led Jace and Vereesa into a narrow cave squeezed between boulders halfway down the mountain slope. After burrowing deep inside, Jace raised his right hand and said in a low voice in the elemental tongue, "Answer my call, flame."

A burst of fire erupted from his palm, illuminating the cave chamber. The low, dark whispers from the snow mountain were blasted away as if by a shockwave, leaving only empty echoes.

After the fire dissipated, Kongge said with a somewhat panicked tone, "The snow mountain isn't willing to help me anymore. This rarely happens."

"Help me find some firewood first," Jace said.

The group gathered pine branches and rotten leaves piled at the cave entrance and set them alight. After warmth replaced the cold and the firelight in the chamber flickered and stabilized, the lingering whispers were driven out too, leaving only the crackle of burning wood.

"Someone is calling on the power of the Frost King to stop you from going deeper, Jace Sesso. The ice told them of your existence, just as I told you their voices," the flame said.

"Where are they?" Jace asked in the elemental tongue.

"I cannot probe anything here while going against the master of this mountain. But not far now, friend of Alexstrasza. You are not far at all."

"Are you talking to the firewood, human?" the ogre asked.

"Stay in the cave with Odunt, Kongge. Come back after the blizzard weakens. Thank you both very much for your help." Jace picked up his staff and stood, looking at the ranger. "From here on, we're on our own, Vereesa."
