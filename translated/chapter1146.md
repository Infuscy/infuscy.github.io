## Chapter 1146: Guidance of the Frost

Sheltered by this primordial forest, the two of them caught a rare breather.

Jace checked that both their luggage was intact. Vereesa, not far off, stood with her bow raised, watching carefully ahead.

This snow mountain reminded Jace of Shadowforge City — not in terms of how it felt temperature-wise, but from the angle of elemental power. This was also a place extremely close to the elemental plane — only it was the domain of frost.

They'd already experienced the pain of being suppressed by violent elementals fighting on their home ground just now. Vereesa glanced sideways at Jace, seeming to have a lingering touch of fear herself.

A constant humming sound echoed through the forest right now. Was it the murmuring of the elemental tongue? Or still just chaotic wind? Even with Jace's understanding of the elemental tongue, he couldn't tell whether this was a sound with actual meaning.

Still, the blizzard did seem to have weakened a lot. After walking through the pine forest for a while, the trees weren't swaying as violently, and the wind pouring through the gaps was no longer so terrifying.

"Did we weather it?" Vereesa asked.

"I don't know. I can't hear the elements' voices," Jace said.

Vereesa suddenly turned her head back. "Could it be that we walked in the wrong direction, and this mountain took it as us admitting defeat and trying to run away, so it let us off?"

Jace looked around everywhere, found a broken tree trunk, and set it alight.

The fire barely managed to burn, but still struggled to persist in the residual whirlwind. The elements' voices also sounded now loud now weak, like the labors of a dying person — impossible to understand at all. Soon this fire too was blown out, struggling the whole time.

Ice elements answered to the Tidehunter Neptulon, making them the ultimate enemies of fire elements. And ice and frost were far more stubborn than water elements. The frost power drifting about here seemed to be actively hunting down every form of "warmth" everywhere.

In any case, this was still the snow mountains of Alterac — frost's territory. They probably wouldn't be able to summon fire elements and listen to their voices again unless they found a cave like before that could completely block the howling wind.

"Keep heading south for now and see how things look once we're out of this forest," Jace said.

"All right," Vereesa replied.

The easing of the blizzard at least allowed the two of them to quicken their pace a little. Walking downhill the whole way, by the time the sky darkened they'd finally reached a place where the trees were sparse and the slope was gentle. They might be leaving the forest soon.

Jace's stomach was already growling with hunger. As he walked he pulled out a piece of elven biscuit he'd brought from Quel'Thalas and stuffed it in his mouth.

This kind of Farstrider emergency ration that could replenish both mana and stamina had no particular taste to it, but in a situation like this it was undoubtedly the perfect food.

"Jace." Vereesa rubbed the pine trunk beside her with her fingers, turned her head back, and said, "There are orcs… around here."

Jace stepped closer to look at the spot on the trunk she was pointing at — there wasn't a very obvious scrape mark.

"Was this cut by an orc's axe?" Jace asked.

"No," Vereesa said. "This is the scrape of an arrow that grazed it. It looks like the serrated arrows the orcs use. We may have found the orcs' hunting ground. Don't forget — neither the Lordaeron people nor the Arathi are willing to come explore this place."

"So we're not far from the orc camp?" Jace said.

"Hard to say." Vereesa shook her head and looked around, her gaze suddenly fixing on a direction to the southwest.

Jace followed where she was looking. An enormous gray dire wolf had appeared there at some point, staring at the two of them.

After noticing the two of them paying attention to it, the gray wolf turned and walked deeper into the forest. Vereesa said in a low voice, "It wants us to follow. Should we go after it?"

Jace nodded. On the snow mountains of Draenor, the Frostwolf clan had long been companions with the dire wolves of those snow mountains. After coming to the Alterac Mountains, they'd also quickly formed an alliance with the frost wolves here — and there must have been the tacit consent of the Frost King Lok'holar in this.

Jace guessed that the appearance of this frost wolf probably meant some kind of reconciliation. Perhaps the Frost King had finally come to trust them.

Following the giant wolf at a distance of several dozen meters through the forest, by the time darkness fell they'd reached the area near a cliff slope. Below the cliff, firelight and wooden fences could be faintly seen. That should be the Frostwolf clan's village.

This place sat in an area that had absolutely no record on the map — it was even far from Iceblood Valley. No matter which direction you approached from, there were nearly impassable deep valleys, cliffs, and layer upon layer of towering mountains.

Not to mention dealing with ogres, violent weather, wild snowland animals lying in wait, and even the malice of the snow mountain itself along the way. It wasn't so strange that the Frostwolves had been able to hide here for more than a decade.

When they approached the fences, several burly orcs were already standing at the village entrance waiting. The gray wolf had disappeared into the forest.

"You forgot to use your cloak to turn yourself into an orc, Jace," Vereesa reminded him.

"It's probably not necessary anymore," Jace said.

"Come a little closer, two," the lead orc called out loudly in not-very-fluent Common. "There will be no fighting or conflict between us, and no harm. Both I and this snow mountain have seen your arrival."

This orc wore a heavy wolf-pelt cloak against the cold. His hair and beard had gone gray and white. He was very tall, but his frame was lean. Spending all these years grinding away in the snow mountains couldn't have been easy.

"'Seen'? Don't you find that a weird thing to say?" Vereesa tilted her head and whispered. "And he's blind."

Jace looked more carefully. The ranger was right — this lead orc wore a black eye patch and looked rather fierce. But Jace could sense that his emotions were extraordinarily calm, almost perfectly fused with this great mountain.

The old orc took a step forward. "I am Drek'Thar, chief of the Frostwolf clan, and the 'chief' of this village, if you put it in your terms."

It was him, then. Drek'Thar, that famous blind shaman, the Horde general of the Alterac Valley battleground.

Durotan — the father of Thrall, the future Warchief of the Horde — had been the previous Frostwolf chief. After his death, Drek'Thar, the highest-ranking member of the clan, had succeeded him.

In the decade and a half that followed, Drek'Thar led the Frostwolf clan through war and exile, surviving the Farstriders' and the Kingdom of Lordaeron's pursuit, ultimately becoming the foundation upon which Thrall would build the New Horde.

"Pleased to meet you, Chief Drek'Thar," Vereesa said. "I am Vereesa Windrunner of Quel'Thalas, Ranger-Lord of the Farstrider force."

"I am Jace Sesso. You've probably already guessed — I'm a warlock," Jace said.

After the two of them reported their identities, the orcs beside Drek'Thar all looked at each other and started murmuring.

It was precisely because the previous chief Durotan had challenged Warchief Blackhand and died that the Frostwolf clan had been labeled traitors by Gul'dan, leader of the warlocks of the Shadow Council, and exiled here to suffer.

On the other hand, their greatest opponents here were the Farstrider rangers, who excelled at tracking and hunting orcs… the two of them really had gathered every problematic element in one place.

Still, Drek'Thar clearly wasn't surprised in the slightest. His expression was extremely calm.

"Was it you who called the blizzard to stop us from coming here, chief?" Jace asked.

"It was me." Drek'Thar raised his head. "But it was also the blizzard that persuaded me to trust you. This mountain shelters my people, and I must listen to its counsel."

Hearing the orc chief say this, Jace was a little surprised.

So the mountain itself had helped out? Then why had it whipped up such a huge snowstorm to block the two of them earlier? Or was that some kind of test?

To beat back those ice constructs, Jace had used no small amount of shadow magic. Could that still earn the frost king's trust?

Still, the thoughts of elements had always been hard to fathom. Since the frost here had laid down its hostility, at least it wasn't a bad thing. At that moment, the orc stepped aside and said, "The wind and snow outside haven't stopped yet. Please, come into the village and talk, two guests."

"I'm a little unaccustomed to an orc talking like this," Vereesa whispered.

"At least it gives you a reason not to put an arrow through his head this very second," Jace said.

"That's true," the ranger raised an eyebrow.
