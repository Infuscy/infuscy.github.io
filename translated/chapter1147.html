Chapter 1147: The Frostwolf Village

The Frostwolf village was far more wretched than Jace had imagined. There were almost no buildings built from piled-up stone. Compared to this, Stonenadder would have been a super fortress armored to the teeth.

Within the crudely erected wooden walls, there were only a few shelters supported by animal hides and timber. Aside from a few orc soldiers and hunters wearing thick leather garments and carrying woodcutter's axes or longswords, most of the orcs were hiding at the edge of the cave at the base of the cliff, peering over this way.

This place reminded Jace of the Frosthummer yetis' village, though it looked even more rudimentary than that.

The group Drek'Thar led wasn't a band of elite wilderness survivors — it was a battered remnant that God knew how many torments had ground down.

On the positive side, though, the camp seemed to be able to support quite a few young people. Aside from the orc children hiding in the cave and peeking out, the orcs on the streets and the square's edge who were glaring at Vereesa with vicious, hostile eyes — though they looked burly and strapping, they were probably only eleven or twelve years old, maybe even seven or eight. They were very likely the younger generation born on Azeroth.

Jace knew that back on Draenor, the Frostwolf chief Durotan had detected the conspiracy of the Burning Legion's demons and refused to drink the blood of Mannoroth, refusing to become a bloodthirsty demonic slave. And it was precisely for this reason that he and his entire clan had grown estranged from the rest of the Horde and fallen under Gul'dan's suspicion.

Still, the skin of most Frostwolf orcs was green, or yellowish-green, a dull brownish-green. These were the sinister colors that only appeared on orc skin after drinking the blood of Mannoroth.

One theory held that although the Frostwolf clan refused to drink the demon blood, they had been contaminated by fel energy from staying on Draenor for so long, so they quickly came to look much the same as the other orcs who had drunk the demon blood.

When Jace looked toward the cave, an orc beside him waved his weapon threateningly and said in the orcish tongue, "Stop looking around everywhere, pink-skin!"

"Just curious, soldier." Jace reached out, pinched the orc's blade between his fingers, and smiled as he spoke in the orcish tongue. "I'm just very curious how you've managed to survive in an environment like this, especially under such difficult conditions."

Hearing Jace speak the orcish tongue, this soldier pulled his weapon free, startled for a moment. Drek'Thar up ahead also turned his head and said, "You speak our language?"

"Quite a few academies in various countries have opened courses for learning the orcish language," Jace said in Common. "But to express my meaning accurately and avoid any misunderstandings, I think it's better to speak Common, Chief Drek'Thar."

"Why?" Drek'Thar asked. "Why do you learn the language of invaders?"

"Many in the Alliance believe you were deluded by the Burning Legion, rather than being another demon race," Vereesa said in a low voice. "Though I'd prefer the latter."

"Thank you for your honesty, Windrunner," Drek'Thar said.

The old orc led the group toward a large tent not far off. As he walked, he said, "Even if the snow mountains of Lordaeron are willing to grant my people shelter and blessing, this woodland truly is no place to settle long-term. We've already made plans to move from here to a place more suitable for survival. We'll be setting out again before long."

So it wasn't Jace's memory that had gone faulty — the Frostwolf clan hadn't settled in just one place after entering Alterac Valley. Perhaps they had only moved later to the valley south of the frozen lake with its many caves, sheltered by pine forests, and built Frostwolf Keep there.

"We won't tell anyone the location of this place," Vereesa said.

"I'm very grateful." Drek'Thar lifted the hide door flap. "Still, white gryphons occasionally appear near the cliffs to the east — sometimes wild, sometimes with soldiers riding them. But even wild gryphons will leak news of our hunters' movements to the Wildhammer dwarves. Those enormous beasts are clever. Staying here, there will come a day when we're discovered by them."

Drek'Thar's Common wasn't very good — his accent and pronunciation were both odd, and his sentence structures had something off about them. But he spoke very patiently, choosing his words as carefully as possible to express his meaning as completely as he could.

"Indeed, many of our clansmen disagreed about meeting with you. They believed your arrival would put this village in grave danger. Setting out to move will put them a little more at ease."

Drek'Thar sat down on a wooden stump wrapped in cracked clefthoof hide, then pointed at the wooden stump beside him. "Sit wherever you like, you two."

Jace and Vereesa each sat down. The orc soldiers inside the tent glared with fury, looking as though the moment Drek'Thar tossed the wooden bowl on the table in the middle onto the ground, they could charge up with their axes and cleavers and hack the two of them to pieces.

The tent erected by the Frostwolf clan appeared to be wrapped in layer upon layer of cloth and animal hides, and the method of construction was quite meticulous. So even though it faced the cold wind head-on and was tucked into a snow hollow like this, it was still fairly warm inside.

"I cannot see what they can see, but neither can they see the things I perceive." Drek'Thar lowered his head. "I know you are no ordinary scouts or rangers. You would find this place very quickly. After meeting, reckless conflict and hostility would bring disaster down upon the entire Frostwolf clan."

Drek'Thar was worried that he and Vereesa would destroy the entire Frostwolf village? Just what exactly had the ice and snow lord of Alterac told this shaman?

Jace had been a little surprised that he would think this way at first, but putting himself in those shoes — a demon summoner who had killed God knew how many black dragons and demons, who had slaughtered Dragonmaw soldiers in droves inside Grim Batol, and a Windrunner ranger-lord who had cut through demon guards at the Dark Portal as easily as slaughtering chickens had entered the snow mountain not long ago, led by a group of ogres straight for this hiding place…

Even if Drek'Thar was an old soldier who had weathered countless hardships and bloody battles, he had to think of the continuation of the entire clan.

The old orc continued, "Like many of my kin who drank the demon blood, I committed far too many mistakes in the first half of my life — prejudice, stubbornness, twisted hatred, a blind lust for power… So I decided to trust the mountain's judgment. I sought the counsel of the Frost King, hoping it would block your steps and make you turn back in the face of difficulty. But the blizzard told me that you did not come here bearing malice, but questions."

"We did indeed come seeking help," Jace said.

"What help can a blind, old orc offer?" Drek'Thar asked.

Vereesa looked at Jace. After Jace nodded, she said, "We want to find the teleportation coordinates for Draenor."

"The Frostwolf clan is an exiled clan, Windrunner ranger." Drek'Thar shook his head. "And even within the Shadow Council, only Gul'dan and his most trusted confidants had access to the magic crystals that recorded the teleportation coordinates of Draenor. Not even the Blackrock clan had the chance to touch it, let alone us. Of course, teleportation coordinates would be of no use in the hands of someone like Warchief Blackhand either — to anyone who doesn't command void magic and teleportation incantations, those crystals would be completely meaningless."

"So there's no teleportation coordinates for Draenor even in Blackrock Spire?" Vereesa asked.

"The chances are slim. As far as I know, Blackrock Spire is now ruled by Blackhand's two sons, and they are far from trusting the Shadow Council," Drek'Thar said. "If there's any place that might have them, I imagine the Burning Legion demons near the Dark Portal… would be more likely to be carrying that sort of thing. The demon lords who guard the Legion's portals — those great demons called the High Erdanin — control the channels for deploying the Legion's soldiers. Since they came here from Draenor through the Dark Portal, they must command the Burning Legion's method of deploying soldiers to Draenor."

Jace nodded. Though Drek'Thar had been deep in the snow mountains for many years, he sounded quite familiar with the Shadow Council, the Burning Legion, and the Dark Portal.

At that moment Drek'Thar asked again, "You want the teleportation coordinates — you intend to travel to Draenor?"

"Yes," Vereesa said.

The orc said in a low voice, "Unfortunately, the teleportation coordinates made by the Burning Legion can only be used by demons — and not ordinary demons either. If returning to Draenor were that easy, I imagine the chiefs of the other clans wouldn't be considering staying on Azeroth permanently."

"We have our own methods, chief," Jace said.

Drek'Thar slowly nodded. "I'm sorry I can't be of any help at all, and the clues I've given you seem rather vague. But this is truly everything I know about such things."

"Are there any other warlocks in the Frostwolf camp?" Jace asked.

"Not anymore," Drek'Thar said.

Jace nodded. It looked like the warlock Karoz still hadn't managed to enter Alterac Valley and find the Frostwolf village. Still, this didn't surprise him.

After all, traveling north from the Burning Steppes, crossing mountains and ridges, passing through the territories of Ironforge and the Kingdom of Stromgarde, evading the pursuit of gryphon riders and red-robed patrol cavalry, then crossing the territory of Durnholde Keep to avoid the hunters' pursuit, finally entering Alterac Valley — and then having to match wits and courage against the countless powerful elements protecting this snow mountain before finally reaching the Frostwolf village… succeeding in reaching here would have been an extremely unlikely event.

Then what about that orc shaman by Lake Everstill?

When Jace had entered the Frostwolf village earlier, he hadn't seen him. But perhaps after all these years, the orc's face had already grown blurry in his memory.

And he didn't know the other's name either, so he didn't know how to bring it up with Drek'Thar.

Still, considering all the hardships along the way, it wouldn't be strange for that shaman to have disappeared on the road.

Although there were no warlocks in the Frostwolf camp, the elemental power of the shamans could also purify certain evil magics. Considering that Drek'Thar was a senior shaman respected across the entire Horde, he might just be able to open that box sealed by an orc warlock with dark power.
