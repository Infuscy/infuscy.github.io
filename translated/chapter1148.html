Chapter 1148: Old Matters That Must Be Raised

"I have something here, chief." Jace pointed at the ranger's pack, and Vereesa took the metal box out of the bag. Drek'Thar sat up and reached his hand over to feel it for a while, then Vereesa placed the box in his hand.

The orc sat back down and felt the box, his expression growing slightly more grave. Jace asked, "Could you help us open it?"

"This is demon magic." Drek'Thar shook his head. "If even you are powerless, Jace Sesso, then I'm even less capable."

Wait — though the traces weren't obvious, Drek'Thar really was hiding something. Could it be that he had a way to open this box but was unwilling to help?

Jace didn't quite understand his motivation. After all, the owner of this box, the Shadow Council, was also an enemy of the Frostwolf clan right now. Even if Drek'Thar didn't trust the two of them, he wouldn't want to help the Shadow Council, would he?

Or maybe he wanted to maintain neutrality and felt it was better to avoid trouble? That seemed more likely.

Vereesa beside him looked somewhat dejected. After all, they'd trekked all the way here from Strahnbrad over four or five days of climbing over mountains, and they didn't know how much more hardship awaited them on the way back.

If they could get a clue related to the Draenor teleportation coordinates, this trip wouldn't have been in vain. But it looked like they'd probably come back for nothing — the whole thing seemed like pointless trouble.

Still, since that reclusive shaman by Lake Everstill hadn't made it to the Frostwolf village, Jace had one more piece of intelligence he could use — news of Thrall.

Back then, Jace had earned that shaman's trust and escaped death by revealing the news that Durotan's son Thrall was still alive, plus telling him that Drek'Thar was hiding in Alterac Valley.

If that shaman had successfully reached Alterac Valley and told Drek'Thar right away that Durotan's son was still alive, then Jace wouldn't have had much bargaining power to exchange with the Frostwolf clan.

"Chief Drek'Thar." Jace looked at the old orc's hand holding the metal box. "I know some clues that the Frostwolf clan would very much like to know — concerning your previous chief, Durotan."

"What?" Drek'Thar was clearly shaken. He gripped the box tighter. "What news?"

"This box is of orcish make," Jace said. "Inside it might very well contain a Draenor teleportation coordinate recording crystal. So opening this box is of great significance to us. As long as you're willing to help, I'm willing to share this news with you."

Drek'Thar dragged his sharp nails lightly across the box, making a squeaking sound. But in the end he still said, "I truly am powerless, Jace Sesso. And Durotan is already dead. No matter what rumors concern him, they are meaningless."

"What about his son?" Jace asked.

What surprised Jace was that upon hearing about Durotan's son, Drek'Thar didn't show any particularly agitated reaction.

The old orc sighed deeply and said, "That's nothing but a rumor spread by people on the outside to trick us into leaving Alterac Valley."

He'd already heard that Durotan's son was still alive? "Where did you hear it from?" Jace asked.

"A wandering clansman. He brought that news all the way here, adventuring from a place near the Dark Portal." Drek'Thar said in a cold tone, "He told me that a human wizard informed him that Durotan's son was still alive and being held in a human castle. There's no question — the spreader of this rumor wanted us to empty out and rescue Durotan's son based on such an unfounded message, then take the opportunity to wipe us out in one stroke. Otherwise, why would he be kind enough to tell a wandering orc to come here to us for help?"

After hearing the orc's words, Jace could almost certainly determine that this wandering orc was the very shaman he'd met.

"That so-called human wizard is me, chief." Jace stood up. "Where is that wanderer? I want to see him."

Drek'Thar raised his head in surprise, then said to the orc soldiers beside him in the orcish tongue, "Call Bazuen here." Vereesa stared at Jace. "What's going on? I have absolutely no idea."

"We didn't know each other yet back then," Jace said.

"So long ago?" Vereesa said. "Then…"

She'd just opened her mouth, full of curiosity, but then realized this wasn't the time to ask a bunch of questions, so she swallowed her words back down.

Jace watched the entrance to the tent. Before long, a tall monster with dark green skin and a head of black-brown long hair walked in.

The moment he saw him, Jace recognized this fellow immediately. It was him — he was the violent lunatic who'd nearly sent both Jace and Grede off to their deaths by Lake Everstill back then.

Right now this orc had a battle axe hanging at his waist, and his body was wrapped in layer upon layer of leather and cloaks. He looked even more burly and solid than he had back then.

"Do you know this human warlock, Bazuen?" Drek'Thar asked in the orcish tongue.

The orc sized Jace up, then said in the orcish tongue, "I don't know him. He claims to be the wizard who told me about Chief Durotan's son's news? That fellow was a skinny, scrawny little tender-skin monkey, and he didn't have much strength either. This human is nearly as tall as an orc kid, and he's sturdy and strong, with boiling blood flowing through his veins… They're completely not the same person, respected chief."

Jace said in the orcish tongue, "Humans grow taller too."

Bazuen frowned. "You speak the orcish tongue? That wizard's orcish was terrible."

"I've been studying," Jace said. "That's nothing strange. And I wasn't the only one dealing with you back then. There was also a dwarf. Do you remember? He was knocked unconscious by a lightning spell you hit him with."

"By the ancestors." Bazuen's eyes widened. "You really are that fellow — at least your voice is very similar!"

He walked over a few steps and swept Jace up into a hug with a laugh. "You saved my life, human. If you hadn't told me the news about Chief Drek'Thar, I would probably have already died at the hands of those human hunters in Redridge. These two years here have been very pleasant. I never thought that after leaving Draenor, I could still live such a peaceful life."

"Most importantly, it meant breaking free from the enslavement of those demons and dark magic," Drek'Thar said with a smile.

"Yes," Bazuen nodded. "That's the most important thing… though I still don't know your name, human."

"Jace Sesso," Jace said. "Glad to see you alive, Bazuen. I didn't expect you to actually make it this far either." Vereesa sat to the side and said helplessly in a low voice, "I should have learned more of the orcish tongue."

"I've learned some Common too, the elf companion of Jace Sesso," Bazuen looked at Jace and said. "You have to admit, warlock — back then you definitely wished I'd get killed by just anyone on the road as soon as possible. I also suspected you'd made up some place for me based on bits and pieces of news you knew, just trying to get me killed. But back then I didn't have anywhere else to go either."

"I really did wish you'd die sooner back then," Jace smiled. "But I really wasn't lying to you."

"You see, chief?" Bazuen said excitedly. "He really does know about Durotan's son's situation."

"I must be cautious about this kind of news, you two." Drek'Thar asked in a low voice, "How did you hear about Durotan's son's situation, Jace Sesso?"

This really touched on Jace's blind spot. Back then he didn't have any channels of information that could corroborate this clue.

Still, based on the story he'd learned in his previous life, he tweaked it a bit and said, "The ruler of Durnholde Keep, Blackmoore, believed that the orc infant he'd picked up was the child of a chief. The blanket wrapped around that child still bore the crest belonging to your clan. That's why he planned to raise that child as a noble."

After hearing Jace's account, Drek'Thar fell silent for a long while before saying, "I think you also suspect the truth of this story, Jace Sesso."

Jace just felt his heart tighten. For a moment he even felt that Drek'Thar had seen through his cover.

Of course he suspected this story — because it was something he'd made up on the spot. But how could Drek'Thar, like Dar'Khan Drathir, detect something off in his words?

Wait.

Long ago, Drek'Thar had also been a high-ranking member of the Shadow Council, and a ferociously cruel shadow manipulator. It was only after being exiled to the Alterac Mountains together with the Frostwolf clan that he resolved to cast aside the dark and return to the shamanistic path — at most only a few years ago.

So he probably understood the principles of this dark sealing even better than the warlock who'd forged this iron box… Was this what he'd been trying to conceal?

"I do. But I think Blackmoore is probably exaggerating and boasting," Jace said. "But that doesn't mean the things that orc infant was carrying aren't worth suspecting, chief of the Frostwolves. I'm the dark magic advisor to King Varian Wrynn of Stormwind. You can trust my sources of intelligence."

This time, Drek'Thar fell into deep thought. Clearly, when Jace got serious, even this former veteran warlock couldn't find anything off in his emotions.
