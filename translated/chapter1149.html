Chapter 1149: The Old Chief's Old Craft

"We should go to that human castle and rescue Durotan's son, chief." An old orc warrior holding a long-handled blade said in a rough voice. "Chief Durotan lost his life to keep us from being enslaved by demons. Now his son is being kept as a pet by humans. We cannot stand by and do nothing!"

"With the people in our clan who can still hold weapons, could we breach the walls of Durnholde, Galvan?" Drek'Thar turned his head slightly and asked.

This orc was Galvan?

For every player who loved the battleground, Galvan, a Frostwolf clan captain, was as familiar an orc as Drek'Thar.

Because on the Alterac Valley battleground of the future, Captain Galvan would be guarding Iceblood Garrison. If you wanted to break through Iceblood Gap and enter Tower Point with any ease, you'd best take out this garrison and kill Captain Galvan first.

Because he corresponded with the Alliance commander responsible for defending Stonehearth Keep, the mage Barinda Stonehearth, the two were also referred to by the Alliance and the Horde as "the man" and "the woman."

Although Captain Galvan's beard had already gone gray and his face was covered in wrinkles — he didn't look much younger than Drek'Thar — right now he took a step forward, and that momentum and force made his body seem a whole size larger than the two shamans combined.

This made Jace feel that even among the elite of the Dragonmaw soldiers in Grim Batol, there probably weren't many who could match him.

Galvan said in a low voice, "I can lead several elite clansmen out of the snow mountain and sneak in. Humans think they've defeated the Horde and are swelling with pride right now. They'll definitely be off their guard."

Drek'Thar moved his lips but didn't respond to Galvan's words.

Though he wore a black eye patch, right now Jace still felt as though his attention was all on himself — that feeling of inexplicably sensing someone nearby was staring at you even though you hadn't seen them with your own eyes.

"What are they saying?" Vereesa whispered. "Rescuing Durotan's son," Jace said.

The two of them went back and forth like this, and Galvan also caught on to the problem.

After all, right now in the tent there was also an advisor to an Alliance kingdom and a Ranger-Lord of the Farstriders. Wasn't loudly plotting to infiltrate Durnholde Keep a little too brazen?

Vereesa stood up in shock, staring at Drek'Thar and warning, "As a Farstrider, agreeing to conceal your hiding place is already borderline betrayal, orc. I have no intention of taking one step further."

Drek'Thar nodded slightly and said in Common, "I understand, Ranger-Lord. Rest assured, I have no intention of involving the Frostwolf clan in such a conflict."

"Did I fail to learn Common?" Galvan said in the orcish tongue. "Are you guaranteeing the ranger that we'll stand by and do nothing? That's the Frostwolf's son!"

"It's been many years since we last saw that castle. Right now we know nothing about it." Drek'Thar said in the orcish tongue. "And right now we don't have any conditions to conduct reconnaissance in human territory. In this snow mountain, just surviving has already exhausted all our energy."

"That castle is much taller and bigger than the Bleeding Hollow Keep that Kilrogg Deadeye took such pride in, Galvan." The shaman Bazuen said in the orcish tongue. "And human cavalry and hunters are tightly guarding the perimeter, from morning to night… When I passed through there a few years ago, I nearly got caught several times. They hunt orcish traces not just for the sake of national hatred, but also for money. That place has already been turned into an arena, used to make orc prisoners fight beasts for entertainment. If you sneak in, you'd be walking right into a trap — unless you've grown tired of fighting the kobolds and troggs in the valley."

"You're not a Frostwolf, Bazuen!" Galvan pointed at the axe-wielding shaman and said coldly.

"But he cares more about the fate of the entire clan than you do." Drek'Thar said, bracing his knee. "Trust me — I care more about Durotan's son than anyone, and I would never stand by and do nothing. But right now the most important thing for us is surviving. Think about it — if Durotan were here, what decision would he make? Would he risk exposing all of us just to rescue his own son?"

Galvan snorted coldly and didn't respond immediately.

Vereesa had picked up a few simple sentences of the orcish tongue during the war, but the orcish spoken by the Frostwolf side clearly differed in accent and usage from Gul'dan's and the death knights' orcish. Not to mention such a dense, tense exchange — even Jace wouldn't have found it easy to fully catch every detail.

But she could sense these orcs were discussing something involving a raid on an Alliance fortress, so she looked anxious, yet didn't dare interrogate them again. She also didn't want to further escalate the conflict.

Jace calmed her emotions with a look, then said, "Most importantly, he might not be willing to come here either, chief."

The three orcs arguing in the tent all turned to look at Jace's direction. Galvan asked, "Why?"

"Blackmoore raised him as a human," Jace said. "He's twelve years old now. For an orc, that's already old enough to go on the battlefield. His views and perceptions are already approaching maturity. But he thinks he's a human, not an orc."

Drek'Thar was pondering something and didn't speak, while Galvan said anxiously, "He carries the blood of the Frostwolf ancestors in his veins. He has the same eyes, ears, and fangs as us. He has the same… skin color as us. He will never be a human, Jace Sesso!"

"I agree. But he needs time to understand that," Jace said. "If at this time you snatch him into the Alterac Mountains, then to him it would actually confirm what the lord of Durnholde taught him from childhood — that orcs are all barbaric, crude robbers and bandits with no civilization to speak of."

Galvan glared. Many complicated emotions collided in his heart, but were ultimately suppressed by reason. They didn't erupt.

Perhaps because he understood that Jace was right.

Vereesa noticed the sudden silence throughout the tent, then looked at Jace, seeming surprised that he could dispel these orcs' impulse to raid Durnholde Keep with just a few sentences.

She still held the stereotype of "barbaric, crude, and uncivilized" toward every orc, but Jace knew that since Galvan could calm down, then Drek'Thar and Bazuen understood this even more clearly.

He still remembered the moment he'd seen Durotan's son in the arena of Durnholde. By now he was called Thrall — "slave" in Common.

But Thrall didn't care. He took pride in this name, took pride in being able to fight for his foster father. Right now he wasn't some Frostwolf child, wasn't some member of the Horde — he was the Alliance's arena star, an excellent gladiator bathed in cheers and applause.

Those humans who reviled his kin would go wild for his performances, would shed tears for his injuries. This showed he was completely different from his other kin.

He still needed to mature a little more, experience a little more, to understand what lay beneath all that madness, cheering, applause, and tears.

Galvan lifted the tent flap and walked out. Drek'Thar didn't stop him. Bazuen beside him, looking at the old shaman, also said nothing.

"Now I understand why the snow mountain wanted me to meet you, Jace Sesso." Drek'Thar nodded.

He braced the iron box. "Perhaps you've already sensed that I was hiding something from you — that I do indeed have the ability to open this box. It's just that for me right now, trying to open this box might come at a great cost. But I believe I haven't misjudged people, and I haven't made the wrong decision. I believe the ice and snow guided me to meet you, to listen to your request for help. The ice and snow sent this name, 'Shadow Council,' which I'd nearly forgotten, back here for a reason."

"You're willing to help, chief?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," the old orc said. "You brought news of Durotan's son. And more importantly, Windrunner Ranger-Lord, your companion brought a powerful helper to the Frostwolf clan — Bazuen. Without his joining, our struggle in the snow mountain would have been several times more difficult. God knows how many newborn children would have perished."

Drek'Thar stood up and said to the orcs in the orcish tongue, "All of you leave. Apologize to Galvan for me first, Bazuen. I'll see him shortly."

"Yes, chief," Bazuen said.

The orc soldiers left the tent one by one. Only Jace, Vereesa, and Drek'Thar remained inside.

The old orc removed his black eye patch, revealing eyes that were vacant and half-closed. He picked up the iron box, murmured something in a language Jace couldn't understand, and a dark purple magic light actually flowed from the cracks of his eyes.

At the same time, the tent shook slightly, and outside the camp the wind and snow suddenly picked up violently.

The howling cold wind crept in through the gaps in the door flap, blowing everyone's cloaks and clothes this way and that. From time to time, orc shouts outside could still be heard.

Jace knew that the magic Drek'Thar was casting right now was far from enough to bring about such terrifying changes. It must have been that by reactivating shadow magic, he was making the entire snow mountain grow more and more restless.

But the old shaman wasn't shaken by these changes. He calmly moved his fingers, murmured incantations in the demonic tongue, and the runes on the box flashed in unison with them.

Before long, with a crisp bang, the box suddenly opened, and the shadow incantations on it failed as well.

Drek'Thar gently placed the box on the table and tied his eye patch back on. He sat on the wooden stool, lowered his head, and murmured for a while — as if conversing with someone, as if confessing to himself.

Until the wind gradually died down and the outside returned to calm, he said in a low voice, "It's done, two guests."

Inside the box was something wrapped in a demon hide, giving off a faint smell of fel stench. Vereesa unwrapped the hide, revealing a dim purple crystal inside.

When this crystal was exposed to the air, Drek'Thar, though blind, clearly sensed something and even tried to recoil.

"This is a draenei artifact," he said. "A draenei crystal, isn't it?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Just as the owner of this box recorded."

"It does indeed contain teleportation coordinates I'm not familiar with, Jace." Vereesa could barely contain her excitement. Her fingers holding the crystal were already trembling.

This crystal didn't possess such powerful force as to make a seasoned, well-traveled old shaman panic. Jace knew that Drek'Thar was afraid of his own past, afraid of the memories that surfaced along with this crystal.

As a young orc, Drek'Thar had once believed the propaganda Kil'jaeden spread to the orcs — that the draenei intended to go to war against the orcs — and launched the first strike.

Countless draenei had died by his hand. Even when the elements abandoned him for it, he didn't waver in the slightest. Instead, he turned to believing in fel and shadow and joined the Shadow Council led by Gul'dan.

Draenei crystals were no rare thing on Draenor — they could be seen everywhere in draenei cities. Perhaps a thread of magical power radiating from this thing made Drek'Thar think back to those scenes, to all that blood, those wails, those lies.

"I never expected these corrupted skills could do some good one day," Drek'Thar said. "Stay for dinner, two guests. Though the conditions of the Frostwolf village may not look good to you, hosting you properly won't be a problem."

"We won't stand on ceremony, then — after all we'll need the strength to leave this snow mountain too, chief," Jace said.
