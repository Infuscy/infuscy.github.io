Chapter 115: Exam Day

That morning, Jace came to Malin’s study as usual to do a little cleaning and put a book he’d borrowed back on the shelf.

Just as he was wondering whether to take another book with him, Malin walked into the room.

“Haven’t seen you in a few days, Mr. Sesso. But your room-cleaning skills have gotten better and better. Every time I come into the study it feels fresh — at least more thorough than my dear Amy.”

“Practice makes perfect, Master.” Jace tidied up his things and was about to leave when Malin stopped him. “Right, there’s something I need to tell you.”

“Do you remember what you told me before about the gnolls secretly making poison in the alchemy lab? You asked me to mention it to the Royal Mages. I did, and we just got the latest news yesterday.”

“The King sent investigators to look, and they confirmed that the gnolls were indeed manufacturing poison. Those murloc corpses weren’t them experimenting on murlocs — they were using murloc eyeballs as part of the poison’s ingredients, and the corpses were just dumped there as emergency food. Those gnolls got hold of a recipe from somewhere and were planning to unleash a plague across the entire kingdom. But Stormwind has already sent a squad to destroy that alchemy lab and destroy all the materials.”

Jace nodded. Actually, he’d pretty much forgotten about this matter over the past while, especially after Malin told him not to meddle so much.

Still, he was a little curious about how this was resolved in the game. After all, the game didn’t have someone like him. If he hadn’t told the Wizard’s Sanctum about this, the matter might have been resolved months or even years later. So did the gnolls’ plague end up getting released?

Well, there was no way to know. He couldn’t exactly go back and check the forums or game groups to see if there was anything about this in the game.

“Did they figure out who was behind it?” Jace asked.

“No,” Malin said. “But besides that gnoll chieftain who’d been attacked, who else could it be? He’s the undisputed chief of the Riverpaw tribe now. We have no sign that the gnolls are working with any other enemies — whether Blackrock orcs, Dark Iron dwarves, or even Gurubashi trolls. Then again, the proud Gurubashi trolls would absolutely never cooperate with gnolls.”

“The only sign right now is that the gnolls learned some magic from the orcs. Even so, during the war they couldn’t even be called allies — they even had a few clashes.”

Jace asked, “Is the King planning to send troops to Westbrook Garrison to wipe out the Riverpaw tribe?”

Malin shook his head. “The King has only raised the bounty again. Another band of mercenaries went missing earlier. I imagine the bounty price will go up again after a while.”

Hogger was still running rampant around Elwynn Forest more than a decade later. No wonder Dughan, Goldshire’s marshal in the game, felt the trouble Hogger caused was almost on par with Blackrock Spire.

After all, terrifying as the Blackrock orcs were, they only ran wild in the northern part of Redridge. Meanwhile Hogger and his gang of underlings hung around Stormwind’s doorstep every day, ambushing passing travelers and merchant caravans for years.

Add to that the Stonemasons’ rebellion a few years later, and Moonbrook gradually being abandoned as bandits and thieves ran amok, the whole of Westfall completely declining — Elwynn Forest’s security forces lost their coordinated support, and wiping out Hogger became harder and harder.

“You have your exam this afternoon, don’t you? Orcish, with Master Dushan,” Malin asked.

“Yes,” Jace answered.

“How’s the preparation? Confident you can take first place?” Malin smiled, as if he was just teasing.

“Probably not,” Jace rubbed his head. “After all, I’ve only been cramming for a month and a half.”

Malin said, “Then just do your best. Even if you don’t end up with a free ship ticket to Dalaran, it’s still good if it motivates you to learn more.”

“Master Dushan hasn’t brought this up at all,” Jace said. “He only mentioned the exam, not the year-end Orcish symposium in Dalaran. I wonder if that’s going to fall through?”

“It won’t,” Malin explained. “Because there’s no need to bring it up. He’s only responsible for selecting the students. Master Andromath has already prepared the travel expenses in advance, just waiting for Master Dushan to find students to go with him to Dalaran. Given the current situation, Dalaran is quite eager to cultivate a group of Orcish specialists sooner rather than later — to strengthen communication with those orcs in the internment camps and prove Master Antonidas’s hypothesis sooner.”

“If you really want to talk about a possibility of cancellation, it’s not like there isn’t one — like a few black dragons suddenly appearing over Dalaran, something like that. But rather than worry about that, why not just study hard for the exam, hmm?”

Jace nodded.

When he headed to the Orcish classroom that afternoon, he specifically wore the light brownish-yellow mage’s short coat he’d picked up from Larson a few days ago, to make himself look more like the average Mage Quarter type. Maybe it’d even earn him a few impression points on the exam.

That said, when he’d ordered the coat he’d specifically asked for something simple and plain, but this piece was more refined than any shirt or jacket he’d ever worn — after all, it was worth more than ten silver.

And this was made at Larson’s shop, which was contracted to make robes at a discount for the Wizard’s Sanctum. If you bought a finished product directly outside, the price would probably jump by a huge margin.

When he arrived at the classroom, there were already a few students sitting outside waiting for the exam.

“Nice coat, Jace,” one of the younger classmates greeted him.

“Good afternoon,” Jace replied. “Good luck on the exam.”

“You too.”

Everyone looked pretty relaxed, because the Orcish class was an elective. Even at the University or the Wizard’s Sanctum, it wouldn’t count toward assessments or grades or anything.

It was basically just an interest course. He was the only one all wound up. Whether the students going in or coming out, they all looked about the same as a normal class. This end-of-term exam didn’t seem that important to them either — after coming out they didn’t discuss how they’d done, they just headed straight to the Blue Recluse to drink.

Jace thought, this is bad. Not only was he probably the one who’d studied the shortest amount of time out of all the students, now he’d also become the worst of all the test-takers mentally.

Actually, he should’ve had about the same mindset as everyone else. The only difference was that he’d learned from Malin what this exam really meant.

If the other students knew that doing well could earn them a trip to Dalaran on the government’s dime, who knows how cutthroat this exam would get. That was the city that every mage… no, every student, every scholar dreamed of.

Which meant there was opportunity within the crisis. This was a disadvantage, sure, but it was also his advantage.
