## Chapter 1150: Return to the Mountains

The sky had already darkened, and the sky over the Alterac Mountains was spread with stars. Two moons — one large, one small, one bright, one dim — hung at the two ends, mother and child separated across the banks of the Milky Way, too far apart to see each other.

Orc men and women were bustling about, working together to load an impossibly sturdy Alterac mountain goat onto a wooden frame. Jace had just been watching them slaughter that goat — it hadn't looked like butchering a goat so much as several orcs ganging up on a horned white demon.

Everything in the Alterac Mountains was enormous, whether snow beasts or wild animals. Even the kobolds hiding in caves were a head taller than their relatives down in Elwynn Forest.

Buried in this snow mountain were treasures no outsider could truly perceive. Only things favored by the snow mountain could truly live here.

Drek'Thar, sitting not far off, gazed at all this. Unlike those laughing, noisy Frostwolf orcs, his emotions remained serious and heavy, unable to relax.

Perhaps it was because of the draenei crystal clutched tightly in Vereesa's hand. Perhaps he was still worrying about this fragile clan. Or perhaps it was worrying about Thrall, Durotan's son.

Bazuen and the other strong orcs together lifted that mountain goat, whose build could rival a wild ox, onto a grill set up from thick pine logs. Beside them, orc children along with several thin, disabled orcs gathered around a boiling large pot.

The children danced and shouted. The adults stirred a meat stew in the pot, filled with chunks of goat offal and carrots. Two giant wolves, grayish-white from head to tail, paced back and forth not far away, now standing, now sitting, looking for a chance to poke their heads in and snatch a piece or two of offal.

"Did you see that child?" Vereesa asked.

"Huh?" Jace snapped out of his daze, then looked in the direction the ranger was pointing.

She was pointing at an orc child. Jace couldn't even tell boy or girl at a glance — after all, children weren't as easy to distinguish as adult orcs, and there was absolutely no difference in how they dressed.

But Jace still realized something was off about that orc child. He looked a bit dull-witted, and his reactions were a bit slow. You could tell when he turned around to argue with the other children, fighting over something that looked like a sweet potato.

"Alleria told me that orcs abandon children who are weak or underdeveloped," Vereesa said. "But here, whether injured, disabled, or born with problems — all the orcs live together."

"I mean, these orcs look no different from us." She turned back to look at Jace. "So why… is it simply being driven by demonic power, like Antonidas said?"

"I don't know," Jace said.

From countless divided small clans to a rebuilt Horde. From a group of clan chiefs to a single Warchief. From Nagrand to Shadowmoon Valley, to the Dark Portal, and finally all the way to Lordaeron, Quel'Thalas…

Jace wasn't hiding anything. He just didn't know how to answer Vereesa's question accurately either.

Vereesa didn't continue pressing. The charcoal fire flickered, swaying back and forth with the gusts of cold wind that swept over. Oil sizzled and seared on the cut, browned skin of the goat.

Right now Jace could hear the voices of the flames. The elements were singing, singing strange sentences that Jace couldn't make sense of either. And the cold wind, the blowing snow, were also singing in rhythm with the flames — like a conversation, like a confession.

The orcs divided up the goat meat and stew. Both Jace and Vereesa received a huge, incredibly tender and fatty portion of leg meat with skin on, and the soup they were served was also packed full of chunks of meat and sides.

After getting the leg meat in hand, Vereesa threw all those doubts and musings behind her and tore into it with big mouthfuls. The way she ate, she didn't look like she was from Quel'Thalas — more like she'd grown up in the Frostwolf village.

Watching her like this, some of the orc children also seemed to let their guard down and came over of their own accord to ask her questions.

At first Vereesa was unwilling to pay those children any mind, but when the conversation turned to fighting, Farstrider combat techniques, and so on, the ranger couldn't help but get interested. She started earnestly explaining things to these orc children.

Things got lively enough that an orc child who looked about a meter fifty or sixty tall even proposed a mak'gora with her.

Though he was immediately scolded by an orc soldier who overheard, the soldier only emphasized the sacred meaning of mak'gora — he wasn't opposed to them testing their skills against this elf ranger.

Seeing that Vereesa was also getting eager to give it a try, Jace had no intention of spoiling her fun by saying something like "you might be teaching our enemies how to deal with us."

After all, this was someone who could settle things by dueling an actual Twisting Nether demon.

The meat didn't have much salt, but it was undoubtedly full of flavor. The goats living in Alterac were covered in thick fat and firm muscle, with distinct layers.

Jace had no idea what kind of spice these Frostwolf orcs had smeared on the goat meat. He'd never eaten anything like it. It seemed to have a hint of cinnamon fragrance, but was also slightly sour — definitely not the disgusting taste of spoiled meat. More like it had been spread with a thin layer of yogurt.

Was this a spice brought from Draenor? Jace was a little curious, but he estimated it wouldn't be long before he could explore the answer himself.

The coordinates for traveling to Draenor were already in hand. Once he returned to Elwyn, he could begin preparing to resummon Karlez. The moment the repairs on Ilgala Tower were complete, that would be the moment they opened the portal to Draenor.

At that moment, Bazuen came over and sat down beside Jace. He stared at the crystal in Vereesa's hand and said in the orcish tongue, "You're looking for those Alliance soldiers trapped on the other side of the Dark Portal."

"You know about the Alliance Expedition?" Jace asked.

"I was just hiding out in Redridge, not unconscious." Bazuen said this as he tossed a chunk of boiled radish into his mouth. Between the orc's thick palm and lips, a radish that big was like him flipping a red jelly bean.

Jace nodded. "Do you want to go back to Draenor?"

The shaman answered, "All orcs want to go back to Draenor, without exception, Jace Sesso. We were all deceived by the demons' lies. Even the cruel madmen in Blackrock Spire know this deeply, not to mention the Bleeding Hollow wandering in the forests, the Dragonmaw hiding in the swamps. As for the orcs locked up in the prison beneath the Alterac Mountains, it goes even more without saying."

"But I know that what they truly want to return to is the Draenor of the past. I mean the one before it was corrupted by demonic power, not the scorched earth that Guldan and his 'master' tortured."

"Before coming to Alterac Valley, I always thought the Frostwolf orcs' skin had kept its original appearance." He looked at Jace. "You might not know — before we drank the blood of the demon lords, our skin was all brownish-yellow, just like the color of Redridge. The Frostwolf chief Durotan refused the demon blood. The last time I saw him on Draenor, his skin was still dark brown. But Drek'Thar told me that by the time he died, his skin was already green like ours."

"It was the scorched earth standing at the Dark Portal that turned him and the entire Frostwolf clan into that. In the past we called that place Tanaan Jungle, but not a single tree is left in that jungle. For orcs trying to escape the power of demons, not drinking the demon blood doesn't mean the power of demons won't find you. As long as you stand on the land of Draenor, you'll be corrupted together with that land, just like those withered, blackened trees, the curled-up shrubs — no one can escape."

Bazuen's words had actually reminded Jace of something. In the game, walking through the shattered Outland that had been thoroughly corrupted by demonic power was actually no different from walking through Elwynn Forest or the Winterspring snow mountains, after all — that place was a game.

But in reality, the situation on Draenor might be far worse than he'd imagined.

"I can feel it, Jace Sesso — the snow mountain and the flames here are both willing to communicate with you, to listen to you," Bazuen said.

Jace smiled slightly. "The flames, maybe. But the snow mountain's voices, without exception, are all telling me to get out."

"Even anger is a form of communication. At least it means they haven't given up on you." Bazuen pulled out a section of carved stone covered in many orcish runes from his bosom. "If you really intend to go over there, at least take this little totem with you."

"What does this do?" Jace asked.

Bazuen said, "This is a token that helps me communicate with the elements and the orcish ancestors. After coming to Azeroth, it's already been of no use, but perhaps it can be of help to you."

Jace took the stone block, which was about the size of a drinking cup. "Thank you, Bazuen."

"If it doesn't help you, if you get the chance, throw it into the mountains of Draenor," Bazuen said. "It's a relic of the Thunderlord clan. Its home is in the mountains."

"I'll remember," Jace said.
