## Chapter 1151: Next Stop, Lordamere

The next morning, Jace and Vereesa prepared to leave. Drek'Thar, Galvan, and several other leaders of the Frostwolf village all came to the village entrance to see them off.

The wind and snow hadn't picked up again the entire previous night. The cold breeze blowing gently outside the cave had actually let Jace sleep more soundly. And this morning the weather was even clearer — the sunlight spilled through the pine forest, the snow reflecting the light, glittering and bright, making a person feel that retiring to a place like this wouldn't be so bad either. Of course, the premise was no lack of food and clothing.

Bazuen walked over and punched Jace's palm with his fist. "You're much sturdier than before, Jace Sesso. If you weren't in such a hurry to leave, I'd really like to have another bout with you. Your Fear left a deep impression on me. When I crossed the Badlands, I ran into a wandering warlock. If it weren't for that fight with you giving me a mental preparation for Fear, I probably would have died right there in that sand."

"There'll be opportunities in the future." Jace smiled slightly, then looked at Drek'Thar and slowly put his smile away. "But I want to say this upfront, Chief Drek'Thar. If the Frostwolves plan to join up with the orcs on the outside, I will never stand on your side and help. At that moment, we'll be enemies on the battlefield."

"So will I," Vereesa said.

"The Frostwolves and our kin on the outside are no longer friends," Drek'Thar said. "Still, if we must become enemies in the future, you will be worthy opponents, Jace Sesso, Vereesa Windrunner."

The two nodded. The Frostwolf chief continued, "I'll send two excellent hunters familiar with this valley to lead you out by a safer route. Where you came from, there are kobolds about at night, and ogres in the canyons further out. Of course, I don't think the two of you would care about the threat of those bandits and thugs, but I'm afraid they'll follow the growing number of tracks and eventually find this place."

"Thank you for your help, chief," Jace said.

Led by the Frostwolf hunters, they walked for two days and one night. After crossing a mountain ridge, they saw the town of Strahnbrad in the valley from afar.

Jace observed the roads in the valley, figured out the route ahead, and the two of them said goodbye to the hunters. After the Frostwolf orcs left, Jace took out the stone shaman totem Bazuen had given him to look at.

The Thunderlord clan, like the Frostwolf clan, had lived in the mountains of Frostfire Ridge in the north of Draenor before Kil'jaeden made contact with Gul'dan and began meddling with the various orc clans.

But living together didn't mean peaceful coexistence. For a long time, the Thunderlord clan and the Frostwolf clan had been hostile toward each other, until the Bladespire ogres began launching attacks against the orcs of Frostfire Ridge. Only then did the two clans turn their spears together against their common enemy.

However, even after defeating the Bladespire ogres, the two clans never truly became friends. They maintained a cold relationship until being unified into a single great Horde by Gul'dan.

This was probably also the reason why, back by Lake Everstill, Bazuen had hesitated slightly upon first hearing the names of the Frostwolf clan and Drek'Thar. He was too familiar with the Frostwolf clan, but he probably also cared about the rift between the two.

Jace didn't know whether Bazuen had revealed his true identity to Drek'Thar and the other Frostwolf clan members. But judging from Galvan's hurtful words and Bazuen's silent reaction, it was likely he'd already come clean.

Considering that the real Azeroth had such massive differences from his memories of the game, Draenor might also look completely nothing like what Jace remembered. If he could use this stone to get in touch with the ancestors of the Thunderlord, to get in touch with the elements of Draenor, then he wouldn't be so likely to lose his way in unfamiliar territory.

Still, the homeland of the Thunderlords and the Frostwolf clan, Frostfire Ridge, had probably been reduced to ashes along with the destruction of Draenor. How was he supposed to throw this stone into the "mountains"?

If there was any part of the surviving Draenor that might have some connection to Frostfire Ridge in its former life, it would probably be the Blade's Edge Mountains — after all, there was still a fortress village of the Thunderlord clan remaining there.

Although Bazuen hadn't specified in detail which mountains to throw the stone into, perhaps it was because he felt that as long as it was on Draenor, the exact location didn't matter. Or perhaps he didn't want to add unnecessary risk to Jace's journey.

Still, Jace felt that if he had the chance to pass through the Blade's Edge Mountains or nearby areas, he'd throw it as close as possible. Close is close.

A while after descending the mountain, they gradually left the snowline. The wind in the mountains was no longer so biting. Among the green pine forests, you could even hear the sound of streams.

"This journey reshaped my impression of orcs." Vereesa walked ahead. "Before, I thought they were no better than trolls, maybe even worse — after all, even trolls didn't burn down an entire forest just to win a war."

"Isn't eating people enough?" Jace asked.

"Do you really want me to compare the specific merits of these two barbaric races one by one with you, Jace Sesso?" Vereesa turned back unhappily. "Even saying this, I could never be friends with an orc — not even those of the Frostwolf clan."

"Sorry," Jace said.

Vereesa looked at Jace. "In that tent, for some reason, I almost thought you were going to go rescue that Frostwolf chief's son. If I hadn't been there, would you have agreed to help those orcs?"

"Why would I help them?" Jace said. "What can they give me — a second Alterac-style roasted whole goat?"

"Out of sympathy for orcs," Vereesa said.

"Retract your accusation, Ranger-Lord. We're about to enter the territory of the Kingdom of Lordaeron." Jace said this as he walked to the front.

"I'm not trying to accuse you of anything." Vereesa quickened her pace to catch up with Jace. "Do you think an orc raised inside a human castle could build peace between humans and orcs?"

"What do you take me for — a prophet, an archmage, or a Guardian of Tirisfal?"

Jace smiled. "There's no peace even between humans, Vereesa. With all of Aegwynn's power, she couldn't even keep the Seven Kingdoms at peace."

Vereesa opened her mouth to speak but didn't say anything. Jace looked at her and continued.

"Yes, compared to Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider, maybe I count as an 'orc sympathizer.' In any case, I don't agree with blasting that Alliance internment camp to pieces with a single Pyroblast. But I also don't intend to meddle in these orcs' problems — especially the Frostwolves. If they can really survive in the snow mountains, that's fine too. After all, the vast Alterac Mountains isn't short of one more bunch of strange folk. Harpies, kobolds, troggs, wargs, yeti… all of them have more influence than the Frostwolves."

The ranger didn't say anything more, just followed behind Jace, watching the road while observing his expression, an elusive smile surfacing on her face.

"Compared to these orcs' survival problems, I'm actually more concerned about my old man and old lady." Jace said this as he quickened his pace, hopping down a stone step. "This trip to Alterac Valley went much faster than we expected. But from here we also need to hurry on to Lake Lordamere. Oh right — I asked you to write to me earlier about my parents moving. Has there been a reply?"

"Of course. Because of all those unexpected incidents investigating Dar'Khan… I completely forgot about this." Vereesa pulled a letter out of her pocket and handed it to Jace. "Mr. and Mrs. Sesso are already prepared. They can leave at any time."

Jace took the letter and opened it to look. Perhaps because this letter had been written to an elf, although his father's handwriting was very familiar, the tone was extremely polite.

Looking at the words on it, he just felt his heart thumping hard.

The goal of buying a house in Stormwind and bringing his parents there was almost complete.

So what would be next — finding a chair to sit in at the harbor of Stormwind, sipping Goldthorn tea while watching all of Lordaeron explode?

Thinking about it, he realized he was dodging this topic deep in his heart.

After all, the Jace Sesso of right now was no longer that day-laborer who'd just docked at the harbor and had to use everything he had to deal with a single Elwynn Forest boar.

No matter what, he had to find the Expedition first.

If the day came when the return of the Sons of Lothar still couldn't stop the Scourge — if an immensely powerful Alliance of Lordaeron, at least nominally undivided, was still headed toward an unstoppable fate — if even Dar'Khan's death still couldn't save Banthinorel… at least he'd tried his best.

Back at the Farstrider post in Strahnbrad, the blue wyvern perched on its roost. Sensing its master's approach from afar, it raised its slender head to peer this way, letting out a hoarse cry.

"Good afternoon, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. Glad to see you both returned safely." The ranger Kreya came out and smiled, giving the Ranger-Lord a salute, then asked with concern, "Did you find anything, lady? Are there new traces of orc activity in the snow mountain?"

"There are," Vereesa said.

Jace straightened his cloak, stealing a glance at her sideways. Vereesa continued, "We didn't see any orcs, Kreya, but we found traces of what looked like a battle between an orc warlock and ogres."

She pointed in the direction of Falkenbrad Tower, which was about to disappear from view from here, and said, "The ruined watchtower at the highest point in that direction. It looked like a place destroyed by red dragon riders. There were signs of black magic being cast."

Kreya nodded earnestly. After a while, she returned to the cabin. Vereesa looked sideways at Jace. "What, do you think I'm a liar?"

Jace touched his nose guiltily. "No."
