## Chapter 1152: Bad News from Silverpine Forest

Traveling quickly through different nations, cultures, and towns… Jace could increasingly sense the distinct flavor of each city.

It might be the scent of some flower, or the smell of smoke and soot, or the aroma of bread mingled with roasted meat, or the fragrance of coffee or floral tea.

When they descended on the royal city along the shores of Lake Lordamere, seeing the green-tiled yellow walls shrouded in morning mist, the towering palaces that had stood for a thousand years — aside from the faint smell of aquatic plants along the waterfront, the whole city was pervaded by a scent Jace could hardly describe accurately.

He used to say it smelled like "childhood," but now he wasn't so sure either.

The wyvern landed at the Farstriders' temporary encampment on the eastern wall. A silver-white wyvern crouched below, and when Vereesa's blue wyvern spotted the others, it immediately let out a low, excited cry. The other wyverns responded enthusiastically, as if the two beasts hadn't seen their own kind in a very long time.

Jace came to the edge of the wall and looked down. A golden Quel'Thalas sailboat was docked on Lake Lordamere, quite conspicuous among the merchant ships and Dalaran ferries.

"Is that the one?" Jace pointed below and asked.

"Yes." Vereesa brushed back a strand of hair. "The Sunlight Voyage. After we set sail, we'll pass by Dalaran. Do you still plan to go into the city, Jace?"

"What for?" Jace asked.

"You said we can't rely on just one Draenor teleportation coordinate." Vereesa reminded him softly. Jace nodded. "True, but it sounds like you're not in such a hurry anymore."

"After getting my hands on a teleportation coordinate… I've calmed down a lot," Vereesa said. "The expression on that orc chieftain Drek'Thar's face when he looked at this 'Draenei crystal' made my skin crawl. It makes me worry that this is really a teleportation stone leading to some demon fortress."

"If there really are no other sources to find, then we'll go to Dalaran," Jace said.

"Alright," Vereesa said.

After a trip to Stratholme, Jace clearly felt that Lordaeron's royal city, by comparison, seemed somewhat lacking in vitality.

Although the royal city was noticeably larger in scale than Stratholme, and it was even a few hundred years younger than Stratholme, it couldn't mask the overall sense of senescence it exuded.

Jace really couldn't find any excuse and could only suppose that this might be the reason the Quel'dorei had chosen to leave Tirisfal seven thousand years ago and head northeast of Lordaeron to establish their kingdom. No matter what stood in Tirisfal, it seemed as if it would be cursed.

After descending from the wall and walking all the way to the Old Quarter, having just passed through the old city gate not long ago, Vereesa patted his shoulder and pointed toward another street intersection in the distance. Jace looked over for a while before noticing his mother walking over from among a crowd.

He raised his hand and waved vigorously. The small figure noticed the movement, stopped to look, and then seemed as if it had been suddenly injected with some force, straightening up entirely.

"Hey! Jace! I see you!"

Henny lifted her skirt and ran across the muddy street, her smile growing brighter. She first gave the elf beside her a tight hug, even making Vereesa lower her head somewhat shyly, then threw herself into Jace's arms.

"I really can't get used to how tall you've grown, I say…" She tidied her hair with effort. "I saw the letter sent from the elves. Honestly, being able to help us find a ship was a huge help. Your father has been asking around the shores of Lake Lordamere recently, seeing if there's anyone who could help move luggage to Stormwind, but there was never a suitable price. An elf sailboat, I say, I never in my life thought I'd get to ride on an elf's boat."

It was obvious Henny was truly excited, talking all the way as she walked, and even gesticulating.

Her mother had always loved novel things. Jace knew this very well. Sometimes he even felt that his own birth had cost his mother the chance to go out and explore. Otherwise, she might have become an assassin or rogue like Pasonia Shaw.

But the bigger possibility was that she would have gotten lost in some unknown corner of Lordaeron, dying under the blade of a gnoll or naga hunter, just like other reckless travelers.

"It was only a few days ago that I rode on an elf sailboat for the first time," Jace glanced at the ranger. "But since we're going to ride their boat, we'll have to learn their customs a bit."

"What customs?" Vereesa asked.

"Start by calling them Quel'dorei instead of elves," Jace said.

"Quel'dorei, oh, I forgot," Henny said with a smile. "Your father did tell me — the King ordered that in front of guests from Quel'Thalas, they mustn't casually say the word 'elf,' they must remember to use Quel'dorei. But forgive me, after all, Victor said even those nobles can't remember."

"It's fine, ma'am, that's just…" Vereesa's expression was extremely flustered. Even when facing Hakkar in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, she hadn't been this nervous. After pondering for a while, she finally stared at Jace and whispered, "What are you saying this crap for at a time like this!"

Jace couldn't help but chuckle mischievously. His mother also laughed lightly, leaving Vereesa behind them, not knowing what expression to make.

Rounding a street corner, Henny said, "The letter mentioned you'd arrive in about two weeks. You're this late… if it weren't for those rangers on the wall telling me you had some business to attend to, Victor and I would have been worried to death. What have you two been doing all this time?"

"Went to Stratholme, and then went to Alterac," Jace said.

"Alterac?" Henny's face clearly showed some worry. She asked, "What did you go there for?"

"Something to do with orcs," Jace said.

Vereesa immediately made a frightened expression that said "why are you telling her everything," and Henny was clearly also a bit surprised.

Jace continued explaining: "You should know there are some orcs hiding in the Alterac Mountains. Vereesa is the Ranger-General of Quel'Thalas. The Farstriders have been cooperating with the region to hunt orcs, so she went to Strahnbrad to understand the situation. That's what delayed us."

"Strahnbrad is already a place teeming with thieves and murderers," Henny pointed east. "Might as well let them fight the orcs. Good for everyone."

After saying that, she looked at the silent Jace and asked, "You're not trying to use the orcs hiding in those snowy mountains to scare me and your father into going to Stormwind, are you?"

"I didn't mean that," Jace said quickly.

"You definitely meant that." Henny narrowed her eyes, gestured with her hand a short-knife's length, and said, "Your father and I have been outsmarting you, this little liar, since you were this big. The moment you open your mouth, I know what you're going to say."

"Don't put it so harshly…" Jace said helplessly. Now it was Vereesa's turn to stifle a laugh beside him.

"Believe me, this is already putting it nicely," Henny said, walking while looking at the street ahead. "And I don't need you to use the orcs in the Alterac Mountains to scare me, Jace. The green-skinned villains in the forests around here are enough to keep me awake at night."

"What happened, ma'am?" Vereesa asked immediately. Jace also frowned slightly.

Henny said, "Some time ago, in the Great Graveyard of Silverpine Forest, west of Lordamere Harbor, a patrol ran into a group of orcs. Many orcs died, and we also lost many people there, including that Wade of the Blumfield family. Remember him? Little Wade's father."

"I remember," Jace said.

The "little Wade" his mother mentioned was a childhood friend who had climbed city walls with him and teased the town cavalry's horses. He hadn't seen much of him since the Dark Portal.

Henny continued: "He should have stood on the wall with your father, but someone in his family died in the war, so he wasn't about to let those orcs off easy. As a result, an orc chopped his head off too."

Saying it that way, a lot of past events surfaced in his mind again. That old Wade was his father's friend. Although he liked to curse, he was a good person. It was hard to imagine he'd die tragically at the hands of orcs.

"Your father said the orcs appearing around Tirisfal recently have rarely been this aggressive," she continued. "Supposedly it's because that group of orcs happened to have one of their chieftains."

"Chieftain? Orgrim Doomhammer?" Vereesa asked. "Have Lordaeron's soldiers found the escaped chieftain?"

"No," Henny said. "It's another one. You know these orc names are all similar, grrr-grrr, that one should be Grog, Grumash…"

"Grom," Vereesa said.

"Yes," Henny nodded. "That's the name."

"That's a very powerful orc chieftain, ma'am," Vereesa said. "He leads an orc clan called the Warsong. Although he's technically Orgrim Doomhammer's subordinate, right now…"

The ranger introduced the details of this Warsong chieftain, while Jace remained silent beside her.

Grom Hellscream. In another timeline, he was a fearsome warrior known to countless people. The orcs regarded him as a savior; while humans and night elves acknowledged his feat of fighting demons, they also regarded him as a destroyer more terrible than demons themselves.

The demonic blood herb Jace had previously searched for was later named Gromsblood after Grom.

When searching for that herb, Jace hadn't felt the reality of that familiar name. But when the name came from his mother's lips and had even killed someone he knew, the impact and pressure were completely different.

Even without the Scourge plague, even without the destruction of the Kingdom of Lordaeron, Grom Hellscream alone was reason enough to leave Lake Lordamere. After all, his parents couldn't stay behind the royal city's thick walls forever.

Counting on the Kingdom of Lordaeron and the Farstriders to hunt down Grom was impossible. Orgrim survived ten years later by keeping a low profile, while Grom survived all the way to later times purely through combat prowess and powerful wilderness survival skills.

Even after experiencing so many dangers, even wielding Ursales, Jace dared not say he could handle this orc warrior.

No matter how skilled he was at demon magic, no matter how skilled he was at shadow and fel power, no matter how powerful Ursales was, could it raise him to the level of the Archdemon Mannoroth? Could it raise him to the level of the Forest Lord Cenarius?

Even if they encountered Grom, it would be just one axe swing.
