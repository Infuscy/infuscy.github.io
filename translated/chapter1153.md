## Chapter 1153: Is Stormwind Really Okay?

By the time they got home it was already afternoon. To Jace's surprise, his father was inside packing things up. The moment he walked in, Vik, who'd been frowning with exhaustion, immediately brightened up at the sight of Jace.

"You're late, Jace. Though the stuff in this house was harder to sort than I expected. I was hoping you'd come back early to give me a hand."

"Sorry, got held up along the way," Jace said.

His mother pressed her cheek against his father's, took the things from his hands, and said, "You should hear our son out about what happened in Alterac, Victor. The orcs wandering around there."

"It's not that I'm not interested in your trip, Jace." Vik raised his eyebrows and drew a breath. "It's just that I've been in no mood to hear anything about orcs lately. Nothing good ever comes of anything involving orcs. By the way, did you tell him about Wade, Henny?"

"I heard," Jace nodded.

Vereesa unfastened her cloak and said curiously, "Grom showing up is definitely major news, but the Farstriders don't have a report on it yet."

"Because it really is recent — probably within the last month. I think the Silverpine side still isn't sure that's the Warsong chieftain," Vik shook his head. "Anyway, none of that concerns me anymore."

"You quit the guard corps?" Jace set his staff by the door and asked.

"More or less, but His Majesty kept my post on the books." Vik patted the sword hanging on the wall — the one King Terenas had given him. "Probably because of you, Jace. The army even gave me a sum of money."

"How much?" Jace asked.

"A pouch of gold coins, maybe a dozen or so. I haven't counted carefully yet," Vik said. "Not a lot, but definitely not a little either. Enough for us to live carefree down there for a while."

"Mm, that's indeed not a little." Jace hung his cloak on the hook on the wall beside him. "But it's far from enough compared to what I've prepared for you over there, so you can both relax."

"Impressive," his father nodded with an admiring look. Beside them, his mother seemed to want to say something and gave her husband a meaningful glance, but he acted as if he hadn't seen it.

That evening, Henny simmered a rich chicken soup with potatoes and cheese, and the whole living room soaked in a sweet, cloying milky fragrance all night.

Vereesa tucked in heartily without a shred of politeness, and Vik and Henny were even happier watching her eat with such relish. But Jace always felt that beneath this cozy dinner there lingered a faint atmosphere of worry.

Of course, he could more or less guess why.

Even if his parents weren't particularly fond of Lake Lordaeron, they'd lived there all these years. Uprooting and leaving all at once wasn't something they could do without a single attachment.

Even when he himself had first gotten on the carriage to Southshore a few years ago, knowing full well that in less than twenty years this place would be destroyed by a horde of ghouls and skeletons, he'd still felt a pang of reluctance.

"We're planning to hold off on selling this house for now," Vik said. "Mainly because we haven't found a suitable buyer. Everyone we know is broke, and I'm not about to sell this place for a handful of gold. What do you think, Jace?"

"Of course." Jace nodded while holding a piece of smoked sausage.

He understood well enough. His parents wanted to keep a fallback here — in case things didn't work out down south, they'd have a place to come back to. And back then his father had mentioned selling the house to support him, thinking he might not earn enough in Stormwind. Now that they'd bought the place, there was no need.

Then Henny gave her husband another look. Vik clearly noticed but still hesitated to speak. "What?" Jace couldn't help asking this time.

"Uh." Vik bit into a piece of bread and chewed a few times. Vereesa also looked up curiously.

His father said in a low voice, "We heard about the black dragon in Stormwind, Jace. Are you sure it's really safe there now?"

"When did I ask you to ask about that?" Henny glared. "Don't you care whether Jace got hurt at all?"

"You were the one going on about the black dragon. How was I supposed to know whether you were worried about whether the place is even livable?" Vik asked.

Henny said angrily, "I thought you'd at least put Jace's safety first, but all you care about is whether you yourself live or die."

"Quit fighting." Jace rapped his fingers on the table. "I'm right here, perfectly fine, aren't I?"

"I knew it." Vik looked at his wife. "I may not have taught him much about swordsmanship, but at least I taught him plenty of ways to stay alive."

"Did you teach him how to survive inside a black dragon's mouth?" Henny said coldly.

Vik smacked his lips and fell silent, lifting his bowl to drink the soup instead.

As for his father's worry, Jace honestly didn't know how to respond right away.

Lake Lordaeron's surroundings had a pack of orcs at most — a threat level that no one would ever compare to Onyxia sweeping across the skies over Stormwind.

"I am the magical advisor to the King of Stormwind." Jace looked across the table at his father. "In my judgment, that black dragon was seriously wounded in that battle and won't be able to come back for a long time. If you don't believe your own son, a member of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six has expressed a similar view."

Vik nodded, and the worry between his mother's brows eased a bit.

So even though she'd said she brought up the black dragon out of concern for her son, Henny was actually terrified of the black dragon herself…

Vereesa beside them had been quiet the whole time, silently eating. Probably even now she still couldn't let go of the fact that during the fight against Onyxia, she hadn't been able to get into that elemental rift to help.

"Besides, Stormwind has proven that not even a black dragon can breach her defenses," Jace said. "So all the smaller players ought to think twice."

"You've got a point," Vik said. "Stormwind just recovered from the Orc Wars. It's only natural that some wicked characters would try to make a move. After this, things should settle down."

"Some of the black dragon's followers had infiltrated Stormwind before. After this, they all vanished — they're genuinely scared," Jace said.

After laying all this out, the atmosphere at the table seemed to loosen up quite a bit.

The family discussed plenty about Stormwind's climate and scenery. Vik even wanted to visit Lake Everstill, the scenic spot famous across all seven kingdoms.

But Jace specifically warned him about the gnolls roaming Redridge, telling him not to treat places where gnolls showed up like his own backyard just because he was an old cavalryman.

When Redridge came up, Jace thought of another thing: Ilgalar Tower.

The mages and nobles of Stormwind all knew he was the master of Ilgalar Tower, so there was no way this could be hidden from his parents. Once they got to Stormwind, how was Jace going to stop his curiosity-driven mother from visiting Ilgalar Tower?

It wouldn't be a problem to take a look during construction, before Karlez got installed — as long as the two hellhounds wandering around the tower were sent far enough away.

But after that?

Jace realized he'd never actually thought this through. Right now Vereesa was chatting animatedly with the two of them, blowing Quel'Thalas and the Farstriders up to the sky, leaving Jace here worrying himself to the point of pulling his hair out.

Even though Jace wanted to protect his parents and didn't intend to spill everything about the demons and dark magic to them all at once, Vik and Henny weren't fools. These things might be hidden across a Baradin Bay, but once they were in Stormwind, they'd leak out sooner or later.

Better to let them know gradually on his own terms when the time was right, rather than letting them find out passively and breed suspicion and doubt.

Except for Morofiel.

Thinking of that, Jace pinched the bridge of his nose and let out a deep sigh.

That night, lying in bed, smelling the faint trace of milk fragrance still lingering in the room, he thought that these next few days were probably his last days in Lordaeron.

The next time he came back here, what would Tirisfal look like? What would this house look like?
