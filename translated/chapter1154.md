## Chapter 1154: The "Cover-Up" Plan

The next morning, Jace got up and pushed open the window. The sky was gloomy and oppressive.

Before leaving Lordaeron, his mother wanted to go to the Grand Cathedral one last time to pray for the Holy Light's blessing, so the whole family was dressed formally and neatly today. Jace had also changed into the clean shirt and jacket his mother had prepared. Now he looked much more like an idle citizen of the royal city than a hedge wizard.

Vereesa's simple travel clothes were already more exquisite and beautiful than what many human nobles wore. To avoid drawing too much attention, she put on a cloak to cover up a bit.

After all, Jace hadn't wanted people who didn't know him well to hear about his return to Lordaeron this time. Vereesa's subordinates could keep a secret and not leak their superior's movements, but the other elves in the city who knew her were another story.

The Grand Cathedral's scale was slightly inferior to the newly built Cathedral of Light in Stormwind, but "ancient" was part of "scale" in its own way. Beneath the high towers shrouded in thick mist, the fine cracks and slight flaking on the gray-white outer wall — the marks left by time — had already become a texture, merging into the architectural style of the building.

Today wasn't the church's day of worship, so there weren't many people in the cathedral. They could get in without queuing.

A few priests were talking warmly with the believers at the entrance. Jace stayed to the side, trying his best not to interact with them, after all he was still carrying a fallen object completely at odds with this church.

He wasn't sure if it was because he'd entered the enemy's dwelling, but right now he could even hear some faint murmuring from Ursales.

He couldn't make out what the staff was saying, but it sounded like complaining — as if it wanted him to leave this place as soon as possible. Every now and then there were even a few grating sounds in the murmuring that felt like threats.

Of course Jace wanted to simply leave the cathedral and wait outside, but doing that in front of his parents would only make him look guilty.

After all, last time he'd come along and sat right up in front. This time he wasn't sneaking in like before, yet he didn't dare walk right up to the front in broad daylight — instead running outside the cathedral to wait. For a mother who was a devout believer in the Holy Light, that really would be quite disrespectful.

"I'm positive this cathedral's Holy Light can't hurt you one bit, so knock it off, damn it." Jace finally muttered a complaint under his breath, and only then did the staff's nagging quiet down.

"What's wrong, Jace?"

Vereesa came over and asked curiously, as if she'd noticed something off about his expression.

"Nothing," Jace said.

"It's not really like those priests say, 'evil and darkness cannot draw near'?" Vereesa asked with a sidelong glance, her tone joking.

Jace said in a low voice, "Don't talk nonsense. the Holy Light has treated me pretty well. If it weren't for the Holy Light watching over me in Raven Hill, I really would have had to move into that fake crypt in Brill by now."

"You mean that priest, Ladimore?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," Jace said. "When Dar'Khan nearly overwhelmed me, he prayed to the Holy Light hoping he could help me — and he actually did. Though I don't know whether the Holy Light was willing to help me, or willing to help him."

"I've had contact with the Holy Light in Quel'Thalas too, Jace." Vereesa moved closer and said in a low voice. "Although I'm not like some old diehards who believe the Light is a concrete god or 'Creator,' I too believe it possesses some kind of discernment. Since it grants its followers the power to save your life, that means it's willing to acknowledge you — not judge you as a demon's apostle or a servant of shadow, like so many people do."

"Let's hope so," Jace said.

He looked toward the pulpit ahead, where an elderly bishop was loudly reciting an ancient creation story about the Holy Light's emissaries saving Tirisfal and Alterac under a radiant glow.

Jace had never heard these stories in his past life. He'd always treated them as humanity's mythologized records of memories from that era related to the Titanic Watchers or the vrykul giants.

His mother was sitting in the very front pew listening to the bishop's sermon, while his father beside her was propping his chin, looking like he was about to fall asleep.

"If not that, then what are you worried about?" Vereesa asked again.

"Actually, I'm thinking about how to tell them about Ilgalar Tower," Jace said.

"A mage tower… There are plenty of mage towers in Lordaeron too. That's nothing strange," Vereesa said.

Jace muttered under his breath, "But not every mage tower has a doomlord inside planning to use the Ley Lines' power to open an otherworldly portal."

"You don't intend to tell them everything?" Vereesa asked.

Jace said, "I can't have them arrive in Stormwind only a few days before they see a whole welcome party of demons waiting for them. There has to be a gradual process, from light to heavy, to let them slowly get used to it. Especially that doomlord — if I hadn't personally taken part in Karlez's summoning ritual, even now, asking me to immediately accept forming an alliance with a high-ranking Ered'ruin would give me pause too."

"Hmm, all right." Vereesa pressed a finger to her lower lip and thought for a moment. "You could use an arcane illusion to shroud the entire tower. During the Orc Wars, Windrunner Tower was severely damaged, and it took the magisters several months to repair it. During that process, they used magic to mask its damaged appearance."

"Why use magic to mask that tower?" Jace asked.

"To, uh, make it look better," Vereesa said. "Isn't that perfectly normal? Windrunner Tower can be seen from very far away. If it stayed in that burned state all the time, it would fill travelers in Eversong Wood with a sense of decay."

Jace stared at Vereesa sidelong. Vereesa sighed and said, "Fine, it's a bit of overkill, but that's what we did. I just wanted to remind you that you could try using arcane magic."

"Can you do it?" Jace asked.

"How could I possibly!" Vereesa said, eyes wide.

"The key is whether we can find a mage who can keep a secret," Jace said.

"Amy Marin?" Vereesa asked.

Jace hesitated for a moment, then slowly nodded. "I don't know if I should drag her into this… It's not that I don't trust her arcane talent, Vere. But she's only been in the Kirin Tor for two or three years, and most of that time she's been studying evocation magic."

"Go ask and find out," Vereesa said. "If she can link with and resonate with the Thalarax magic crystal that the Magisters' Guild gave you, she might actually be able to weave a large enough illusion to shroud the entire Ilgalar Tower by manipulating the power of the Ley Line nexus. Besides, you're not trying to deceive Archmage Antonidas — just keep it from Mr. and Mrs. Sesso…"

"All right, looks like we're going to Dalaran after all," Jace said. "Hope Amy's there and not out on some mission."

In the afternoon, back at the house, Jace and Vereesa helped his parents do a final round of packing for the things they'd be taking. In the evening they rented a carriage and hauled the luggage and crates down to the harbor all at once. They'd set out first thing the next morning.

The original plan was to depart from the royal city's harbor, sail along the western shore of Lake Lordamere to Lordamere Harbor, then enter the Aliwas River and head south from the river mouth around the Kirghiz Peninsula into Baradin Bay.

Now that they were going to Dalaran, they'd leave via a tributary of the Aliwas River west of Dalaran, pass through Burnwood Swamp, and enter Gilneas Bay. The whole journey would be two days longer, but compared to the ten-plus days of sailing ahead, that was nothing.

In the early morning, the surface of Lake Lordamere was shrouded in a thick layer of fog. Jace was already used to this.

Vereesa was explaining to the elven crew that they needed to head to Dalaran first. Jace noticed several Farstriders on board observing the Silverpine Forest to the west. From here, the forest over there was barely visible through the thick fog.

Actually, the detour via Dalaran wasn't a bad thing at all, especially considering the orc-related skirmish his parents had mentioned.

The Great Graveyard they'd mentioned wasn't simply a graveyard, as the name suggested. It was actually a town not unlike Raven Hill. Though tucked deep in the Silverpine Forest, because of the graveyard it always had plenty of people coming and going, and the town's no small place either.

It had a name that a gamer like Jace was more familiar with — Sepulcher.

Sepulcher itself meant "graveyard." The locals sometimes called it Graveyard Village, sometimes Tombstone — all referring to the same place.

The Aliwas River passed through the valley near that town, so if they really went that way, they might still run into orcs entrenched in the forests along both banks of the river.

Now that they were taking the Dalaran route, the likelihood of encountering danger dropped significantly.

When he traveled alone, of course he'd go however was fastest and cheapest. But now that his parents were with him, he had to ensure safety and avoid mistakes as much as possible.

"Teach me a greeting in elven, Jace." Henny came over to his side and said.

Jace nodded, looked at the golden sailboat ahead, and said "Good day" in Thalassian.

"I think I've heard that before." Henny nodded, repeated it softly a few times under her breath, then walked toward the sailboat with confidence.

Vik was still at the dock talking with some old soldiers. They shook hands and embraced each other, then his father picked up his bundle and sword and came aboard.

"Stormwind, here I come," he mumbled as he passed Jace. "Let me see with my own eyes what this 'City of Hope' looks like, and find out just what kind of ability she has that makes her better at taking care of my son than I am, huh…"
