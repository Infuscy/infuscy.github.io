## Chapter 1159: The Dragon Blood Pendant

The next morning, Jace walked toward Stormwind Keep. Halfway there, as he passed the canal, he could see from a distance that his parents' house already had clothes hanging out to dry.

With just this one small "touch," the house no longer looked like a warehouse or a waystation where friends met to discuss things. It had truly become a lived-in, vibrant place, just like the other residences and shops along the canal.

Thinking of this, he couldn't help but smile faintly.

After waking up this morning, he'd drunk a Lesser Healing Potion first, to ease the wound on his leg left by that succubus's tail the day before. But even now his leg still ached faintly, making him walk with a slight limp.

He made his way to Stormwind Keep, and sure enough he was a bit late. A sizable crowd had already gathered in front of the palace. Led by a palace guard, he slipped in through the side, bypassing the citizens, townsmen, and farmers who'd come to see Varian. He went up the ramp, and the noise from the throne room was almost audible from here.

"Soon the darkness will swallow my farm whole. You people living in Elwynn have no idea how hard it is for us farmers who grew up south of the Nafeltarion River. I just hope the King will make some kind of gesture and tell me Stormwind hasn't forgotten us…"

"Stormwind has never forgotten South Elwynn, sir. Currently we are cooperating with Quel'Thalas to cleanse the evil in Duskwood. I believe there will be visible change within a few years…"

"It's not that I'm unwilling to believe you, Your Majesty, but it's obvious none of you have figured out who exactly caused this whole region to become what it is. It was the darkness spreading from the Walsh family's orchard estate to the south that devoured everything. I don't know how much money they've spent in Stormwind lobbying nobles and officials to speak for them — for all I know, some of the people in this throne room right now might be…"

Varian on the throne seemed to want to say something but stopped himself. Finally, he tilted his head and whispered something to a court mage beside him, having him explain to this Duskwood farmer exactly what was going on.

After finally getting rid of this fellow who wanted to blame the darkness of Duskwood on his neighbor's orchard, although it wasn't even noon yet, Varian already looked as if he'd exhausted a full day's energy.

He waved his hand to signal a pause, then stepped down from the throne. When he saw Jace waiting for him in the back, he forced his eyes open, drew a breath, and pulled himself together.

"Jace."

"Your Majesty," Jace said.

Varian glanced at the nobles not far away whispering about the situation below the throne, smiled slightly, and said: "How are things on the Quel'Thalas side? They're not trying to push the blame off on us further, are they?"

"Everything went smoothly." Jace handed over a scroll. "Dar'Khan's accomplices have mostly been caught. The matter turned out to be a bit more complicated than expected — it involves some leftover issues from the Orc Wars, plus some historical problems in Stratholme. I've written it all up in this report, Your Majesty."

"Sounds complicated enough. I'll read it carefully later." Varian skimmed the scroll roughly, then asked: "I heard you brought your parents to Stormwind?"

"Yes," Jace said. "I'm just about to entrust Arna to my father's care."

"I heard your Mr. Victor Sesso served as a cavalryman at Durnholde Keep and fought against the Arathi," Varian said. "That's true, so he's definitely better with horses than I am."

"Glad you're willing to let Mr. and Mrs. Sesso settle in Stormwind, Jace." Varian said in a low voice. "Although this city still has all sorts of troubles and some issues haven't been sorted out yet, I'll work hard to make it a more livable, safer place. I won't let you and your parents down. You might think I'm putting on an act, but this is genuinely what I'm thinking right now."

"I know, Your Majesty," Jace said. "And I'll do everything I can to help you."

"With those words I'm much more at ease," Varian said. "I can't talk with you much longer. I still have some people to see later — they're nowhere near as patient as you."

"I have one more question," Jace said. "Where is the envoy from Ironforge staying?"

"Right. He's been waiting for you for several days. He's in the second room down the ramp, at the end of the corridor to the right." Varian pointed below.

"Much obliged." Jace nodded. Varian smiled, drew a breath, put on the stern expression a King ought to have, and turned back toward the throne.

Following the direction Varian had pointed, Jace came to the corridor on the ramp side. Seeing the dwarf soldier and a dwarf noble standing at the end, he knew he'd come to the right place.

Seeing Jace walking over, the dwarf noble asked, "Who's there?"

"Jace Sesso," Jace replied.

"You're finally here, Archmage Sesso." The dwarf noble said, "I am Kurthalos of Dun Morogh, envoy of His Majesty King Magni, here to escort the dragon-blood pendant Ironforge forged to thank you for defeating that wicked she-black dragon. Please, follow me — I've kept it in the bedchamber."

Jace nodded. The name Kurthalos felt familiar to him, but he couldn't recall where he'd seen it for a moment.

This dwarf noble was quite sturdy even by dwarf standards. And as one of the shorter Bronzebeard dwarves of the Three Hammers, he seemed even taller than Grede — taller than any dwarf Jace had ever seen, including King Magni. In Ironforge, this absolutely counted as a small giant.

"Sorry to have kept you waiting so long, Lord Kurthalos," Jace said.

"It's no trouble," Kurthalos said. "I don't have any other missions on hand lately. If I hadn't come to Stormwind for a look around, the most I could do is watch a bunch of idiots argue at King Magni's side."

Following Kurthalos into the guest room, Jace noticed his heavy armor and weapons still hanging here. His armor was bright copper-colored, and beside it hung a one-handed three-sided war hammer.

What stood out was that both this war hammer and the thick armor were engraved with Holy Light runes, and on the tabard beside them there was even the emblem of the Order of the Silver Hand.

This dwarf was actually a paladin?

But the moment Jace made the connection between this dwarf and the Order of the Silver Hand, the name Kurthalos came back to him at once.

He was one of the "Four Horsemen" who would one day guard the Scourge floating necropolis of Naxxramas, the dwarf death knight — Lord Kurthalos.

Recalling the fallen lord's sharp howls and vicious curses in that future Scourge city, Jace really had a hard time connecting that image with the paladin before him. After all, death could change far too many things.

Lord Kurthalos picked up a silver treasure chest on the nightstand and handed it to Jace. "Open it and have a look yourself. King Magni said he wanted to see you open it with your own hands, so I'll do exactly as His Majesty wishes."

"All right."

Jace took the treasure chest and slowly opened it. Inside lay a massive, plump brownish-red gem that almost immediately dragged all his attention and gaze onto it. The magical aura it exuded seemed to yank Jace right back into that bizarre elemental plane mixed with flame and earth.

Kurthalos stood with his hands on his hips and pointed at the large round gem. "A treasure that lets you backstroke in a lava pool."

"Really?" Jace asked, incredulous.

"Well, I'm exaggerating a bit," Kurthalos said with a laugh. "Someone said that, of course I haven't tried it either. And don't actually try it just because of what I said."

Jace nodded half-believingly. The dwarf added: "I just meant that this pendant can provide its wearer with unimaginable fire resistance and vitality, after all it's the blood of the black dragon princess Onyxia… Quite a few craftsmen over in Ironforge, after seeing this treasure, have run to Stormwind to buy up dragon blood. But most of the blood that can be found nowadays is mixed with too many impurities for all sorts of reasons. The raw material for this treasure is a rare piece of the purest quality."

After all, the Wizard Sanctum's mages had found ways to collect it — their methods and channels were definitely more sophisticated.

Logically, this dragon-blood gem should have gone to Grede. As the team's core, how long Grede could stand in front of the enemy determined how long the rest of them could stay alive.

If Grede had had this dragon-blood ornament when facing Onyxia, he probably wouldn't have been knocked down so quickly. Facing that she-black dragon's furious dragonfire, he might have held out a lot longer, and there would have been no need to force the little gremlin to burn through all its resources summoning Karlez.

But so many benefits had already been given to Grede — not taking anything from him and just giving him this on top of it all felt like too much of a loss. Wait until he came back from Kalimdor.

"Thank you for me, Lord Kurthalos," Jace said with a slight bow. "And thank you for making the trip."

"Don't thank me, Archmage Sesso." The dwarf laughed a few times. "For a warlock, you're really quite polite."

Jace glanced at the three-sided hammer hanging in the corridor outside, then glanced at the dwarf paladin before him, half suspecting this fellow intended to judge him on the spot.

But soon Kurthalos started talking about what he'd seen and heard on his recent trip to Lake Everstill, then kept complaining to Jace about some of the Lakeshire locals' habits that rubbed him the wrong way. Only then did Jace realize this dwarf was genuinely straightforward and not hinting at anything, and he finally set his mind at ease.
