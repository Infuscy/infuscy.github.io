## Chapter 116: A Sneak Attack

Jace hadn't shown up early, so he ended up being one of the last to be examined. When his turn came, he stepped into the room to find Dushan sipping tea, a trace of fatigue already creasing his face.

"Jace Sesso." Dushan looked over and gestured at the chair across from him. "Please, have a seat."

"Your topic is…" Dushan flipped open his notebook. "The Alliance Internment Act, correct? That's quite a complex subject. Whenever you're ready, let's hear what you've written."

Jace nodded, pulled out his manuscript, and began to read.

In Orcish, he roughly laid out the end of the Orc Wars and the Alliance's subsequent debate over how to handle the prisoners of war. He laid out the various kingdoms' stances on the captives and the difficulties they each faced — in short, an objective, dispassionate recounting of the whole process.

If this had been on Earth — an English exam, or any other language test — then in Azeroth that would correspond to a Dwarvish or Elvish exam. An essay like this would be utterly elementary, practically the equivalent of copying out a dull, dry narrative and reading it aloud in all its bland glory.

But for an Orcish exam, things were entirely different. Jace had spent ten whole days just managing to compose this short article in Orcish runes, and getting each character's pronunciation right had cost him enormous effort. And when it came to Dushan, there were even runes whose readings and meanings the teacher himself couldn't quite recall, forcing him to interrupt Jace several times to look up words on the spot.

The truth was, learning Orcish in the current human world felt less like studying a mature language and more like archaeology, or maybe detective work. You absolutely could not apply the methods used for learning any other language.

Being able to complete the writing and reading of such an essay already counted as a very high-level examination.

By the time Jace reached the end, he was nearly falling apart. After all, he had no Orcish pronunciation guide to consult, and the way these runes were pronounced bore no resemblance to any language he'd ever encountered — not even Chinese. Gradually, he'd started to feel like he no longer understood what he himself had written.

Even after a month of cramming, he was still falling short in a lot of ways.

Still, he managed to finish reading it through, and the teacher had basically grasped the content of his essay.

Dushan nodded, pondered for a moment, then asked, "What is your view of the Alliance Internment Act? Remember to answer in Orcish."

Jace had prepared for this question. He said in Orcish, "A very courageous decision. But it looks good and is not beneficial in the long term."

Dushan said in Orcish, "Explain the long-term drawbacks."

Jace said, "This decision means all the kingdoms remain in a state of coin shortage, making all the kingdoms very fragile."

He thought for a moment, organized his thoughts, recalled what he'd prepared, confirmed the Orcish vocabulary, then continued in Orcish, "If war or accident happens again, and the orc laborers are not put to use in a short time, all the kingdoms will not gain enough profit and will face disastrous consequences."

"Disastrous consequences." Dushan nodded slightly and switched to Common: "Very good phrasing, Jace. Very good."

Then he asked again in Orcish, "List possible wars or other accidents."

Actually, what Jace had wanted to say in that last passage was the Scourge invasion. Of course, there was no way right now to guess that a massive undead army would come sweeping in, but he had done some brief preparation on this topic.

"Demon invasion, the Troll Empire, lurking ogres, black dragons and red dragons, the orc legions of Blackrock Spire."

Dushan didn't catch two of the words and double-checked the pronunciation and meaning with Jace. Jace even wondered if he'd been mispronouncing them — by now his head was stuffed full of tenses, stress patterns, rune positions, and all sorts of chaotic nonsense.

Once everything was cleared up, Dushan nodded and asked in Common, "What did you have for lunch today?"

"Dry bread, an egg, and a glass of juice from the Blue Heron, Master. I don't usually eat this well, but with the final exam coming up I wanted a little extra sugar." Jace answered offhandedly in Common.

He'd thought the exam was over, so he'd relaxed on the spot.

"Repeat that sentence in Orcish," Dushan suddenly said.

What?!

That caught Jace completely off guard. He'd prepared for a dozen possible questions, and this old bastard Dushan was fighting dirty?

Of course, he couldn't voice his displeasure directly. For this kind of conversational language exam, the teacher could test however he damn well pleased.

Jace rubbed his forehead and could only rack his brain for every Orcish word he still remembered, doing his best to cobble sentences together.

"Morning. Dry bird food. Bird eggs. Fruit water." After saying that, he looked at Dushan with a pleading gaze, hoping the man would let him off the hook.

"There are still two more sentences," Dushan said.

Are you screwing with me?

Jace gritted his teeth and forced out in Orcish: "Normally, my food is not as good as today. But for the final trial, I must obtain more… more… sweet… sweet things."

Dushan nodded and scribbled something quickly in his notebook. The rather relaxed expression from before had now grown much more solemn.

Jace felt like he was doomed. The things he'd just said were basically kindergarten-level — a kindergartner could've put together smoother sentences than that.

Since everyone took the exam alone in the classroom, Jace had no idea how the others had done either. During regular lessons, everyone used Common to communicate knowledge and understanding, so opportunities to hear others string together continuous Orcish sentences were few and far between, making it impossible to gauge everyone's level.

But he still felt doomed. Nervousness, plus only a month of preparation time, plus Dushan pulling tricks like this — the trip fare was definitely coming out of his own pocket this time.

Chances are he'd get mocked by Marin again when he came out, never mind Grede.

From the moment he'd arrived in Azeroth to now, almost nothing had gone smoothly for him… The one thing he felt heaven had blessed him with was that no matter how unlucky he got, he hadn't lost his life. That counted for something, at least.

He'd always thought he had a bit of a gift for languages, but here he'd gone and crashed and burned on a language exam.

Crashing and burning at the thing you thought you were good at, only to finally confront the cold, hard reality — that you never had any gift to begin with — was just utterly miserable.

The more he thought about it, the more pointless it all felt, until Dushan looked up and asked, "Hey? Why are you still here?"

"Ah, sorry, Master Dushan." He gathered up his things, preparing to leave.

"Right, Jace — you're from Lordaeron, aren't you?" Dushan asked. "Any plans to go home for Winter Veil?"
