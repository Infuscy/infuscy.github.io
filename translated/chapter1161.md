## Chapter 1161: The Sixth Statue

After a big meal with Sola at midday, Jace parted ways with her at the city gate by the canal in Old Town and headed west along the canal toward his parents' house.

Jace could tell that after freeing Prince Ysondre from Hakkar's corruption, a thick layer of gloom seemed to have lifted from the bottom of Sola's heart.

She was still a bit shy, carrying that air of self-doubt, but he could feel she'd become much more cheerful than when she'd first arrived in Stormwind.

Although she was a green dragon who'd lived for a thousand years, considering she'd hardly ever left Ashenvale and the Emerald Dream, and that most of that thousand years had been spent sleeping under her elders' care — she could even fall asleep while flying — the remaining time she was awake could hardly be called fully conscious.

So to this day, she was still a young person searching for her true self. Layering the guilt of being corrupted by the Nightmare on top of that made it all too easy for her to blame herself excessively and become timid in everything she did.

Being able to help Ysondre break free from his torment and pain meant far more to her than just lifting the gloom — it also proved her value to her entire kind.

Without a doubt, this was a massive driving force in helping Sola break free completely from the shadow corruption.

The midday meal cost four silver and twenty copper. After that, Sola had ordered several glasses of milk. Jace felt as if she wanted to use this single meal to replenish all the energy she'd spent flying back from Kalimdor.

Four silver was already the price of a pretty lavish feast for a meal at the Pig and Whistle Inn. Although Jace had winced a bit listening to Sola order, after the meal, watching her happily head back to the Mage Quarter, he felt the money had been well spent.

Whether it was him and Vereesa, or Grede and Mosa, they'd forged a bond with this "press-ganged" green dragon that could never be severed. No one could sit by and watch her grow more and more unhappy, more and more gloomy, and gradually walk back into that terrifying crimson nightmare.

Being able to help her face each new day and each new adventure with sunshine — compared to that, money was hardly anything that mattered.

Arriving at his parents' house, Jace knocked on the door. Henny answered from inside and came to open it.

She'd already changed out of the pretty long dress and outer robe she'd worn for visiting Stormwind, and put on a simple, light set of everyday clothes. She was rolling up her sleeves and drenched in sweat — clearly in the middle of housework, tidying up the new home.

"Jace." Henny smiled. "Vik's already gotten dressed and is waiting to go with you."

"You're not coming?" Jace asked.

"To the stables?" Henny asked.

"I'll take you around the Trade District too," Jace said. "The afternoon's still long."

"Then I'll go change." Henny nodded happily, then shouted toward the inside, "Jace is waiting for you!"

"Coming, coming." Vik answered from inside, then walked to the front door with confident strides.

Today Vik was wearing a handsome brown vest, with tight-fitting breeches on his lower half paired with a pair of quite exquisite tall leather boots with carved patterns. If it were this time of year in Tirisfal, this outfit might be a bit cold, but in Stormwind it was just right.

He straightened the pumpkin-shaped round flat cap tilted on his head, looked at Jace with a smile, and asked, "Well?"

Although he'd put on a bit of weight compared to his younger days, Vik's build clearly marked him as a fairly robust hunter or soldier. Broad shoulders and the muscles on his arms didn't lie.

Still, Jace wasn't about to flatter this old man who'd been boasting to him since childhood. In the end he just pointed at the cap and said, "The moment people see this cap, they'll know you're from Lordaeron."

"Do the folks here look down on northerners?" Vik asked, patting his cap lightly.

"Not exactly," Jace smacked his lips, raised his hand, and said, "We can set out, sir."

"Let's go!"

Vik puffed out his chest and trotted down the steps. These few strides and his current bearing suddenly reminded Jace of those noble officers leading patrols through the royal city. The canal-side street felt oddly out of place with Vik in it.

By now Henny had also changed into a light set of travel clothes and come to the door. She shouted at her husband, who was facing the canal with his small belly sticking out: "Stop putting on airs! You look like a wild pig that just ate its fill and is swaggering around showing off!"

Vik turned his head and tipped his cap. "Thank you for the compliment, my lady." Watching his reaction, Henny couldn't help bursting out laughing.

If you entered the Trade District from beside the canal, cut through the new city to the Mage Quarter, then wound your way through the alleys near the Wizard Sanctum all the way to the stables outside the wall, it actually wasn't as straightforward as simply exiting the city and following the wall all the way east to the seaside cliff.

So Jace planned to take his father and mother through the Trade District square straight to the Valley of Heroes, then exit the city gate — taking the more direct route to the stables. After all, his father would often be taking Arna to the training ground there to walk her around. Wandering back and forth in the Mage Quarter, where it was hard to find your way if you didn't know it, would only get them lost.

Moreover, an ordinary Stormwind citizen who frequently hung around the Mage Quarter was already enough to arouse suspicion. Outsiders rarely entered the Mage Quarter. Let alone a Lordaeron immigrant riding a horse in circles there.

After familiarizing themselves with the locations of the various shops, the bank, and the market in the Trade District, Jace brought Vik and Henny to the registry at Stormwind's main gate — the future Census and Guild Registration Office.

Newly arrived immigrants to Stormwind mostly had to make several trips here to handle all sorts of procedures. When Jace and Grede had first settled in Stormwind, they'd also made several trips — though back then the registry's location had still been in Old Town.

Vik and Henny certainly couldn't be exceptions either. All sorts of registrations, plus the transfer of the house contract and so on — though not as troublesome as on Earth, they were still indispensable.

Coming out of the registry, they passed through Stormwind's main gate into the Valley of Heroes. The familiar statues, the distant cries of gryphons — these were all elements Jace knew like the back of his hand. But to Vik and Henny they were still quite novel and rather awe-inspiring.

Lordaeron's royal city gates were also magnificent, but they simply looked like an enormously heavy, exaggerated massive fortress. Besides grandeur and solemnity, there weren't many other nuanced adjectives you could find.

But the bridge of the Valley of Heroes was something else entirely, especially appearing alongside the five statues of the Sons of Lothar — the sense of awe it gave was something special.

Both Vik and Henny looked up, carefully admiring these statues. When they reached the statue of Turalyon, Henny looked up and said with a soft sigh: "It's hard to imagine that shy boy one day standing here, having become a hero who guards the entire world."

"You know Marshal Turalyon?" Jace asked.

"Hard to say I know him — we only exchanged a few words," Henny said. "He looked very honest and simple back then, staying in the monastery or the cathedral every day, so knowing him wasn't anything to brag about, Jace. It's just hard to imagine — a child of a noble family acting like an ordinary little cleric, squatting in the monastery every day, personally helping citizens in need. But I'm not being wise after the fact — even then I had the faint feeling he was no ordinary person, and that one day he'd accomplish something great."

Vik nodded and said: "When the war reached its most desperate point, many people didn't believe in him. But facts proved that some people only burst into their true brilliance when crisis struck."

"Archmage Antonidas's statue looks a lot younger than the real man," Henny said.

"You've even met Antonidas, Henny?"

"You haven't seen his portrait?"

"No, I don't concern myself with those mages' affairs. Even if I'd seen him I might not have known it was him," Vik shook his head.

"That's Archmage Khadgar," Jace quickly corrected.

"Uh?" Both of them were stunned for a moment.

The family walked to the old mage's statue and only confirmed, by reading the nameplate, that this was really Khadgar and not Antonidas. Vik even muttered to himself: "I was wondering why the Lord of Dalaran, who clearly didn't join the Expeditionary Force, would have a statue here. But I heard Khadgar is a good friend the same age as Turalyon — why does this statue look like he's in his sixties or seventies?"

"I heard he was cursed by the dark mage Medivh," Henny said.

"So that's why," Vik nodded. "If I were the designer of these statues, I'd put how he looked when he was still young here. Even Archmage Khadgar himself probably wouldn't want his cursed, haggish appearance put on display for the whole world to see, right?"

"Who knows," Henny said. "Maybe those mages prefer looking older — makes people think their magic is profound."

Their discussion actually took Jace quite by surprise. He really hadn't considered why people had put Khadgar's post-curse appearance here.

Just as he was turning this over in his mind, his father and mother had already run to the statue of Alleria Windrunner.

Standing beneath this statue, their tone was clearly not as relaxed as when they'd been discussing Khadgar and Turalyon.

After all, Vereesa was already practically family to them, which made Alleria a relative of sorts. And the members of the Sons of Lothar had been officially declared "fallen in battle."

Neither parent knew much about Kurdran Wildhammer. As for the last one, Danath Trollbane, to his father — an old soldier who'd garrisoned Durnholde in his youth — he was still the leader of a hostile nation. Naturally there wasn't anything nice to say.

But when they reached the city gate at the far end of the Valley of Heroes, Vik suddenly said: "I'm not just saying this because he's my son, Jace, but judging by what I heard about the Battle of the Dark Portal back in Lordaeron… you ought to be remembered by people just like these leaders of the Sons of Lothar."

"What are you talking about?" Henny asked angrily. "You're hoping your own son loses his life?"

"You forgot that cemetery in Brill?" Vik shot back.

That shut Henny up at once. Jace smiled and said: "The Valley of Heroes clearly can't fit a sixth statue." He lowered his voice and whispered: "Mine isn't here. It's at Nethergarde Keep."

Henny was startled at once and asked immediately: "You mean your statue?! Stormwind erected a statue of you at Nethergarde Keep?"

Jace nodded. Vik was clearly shocked too. He glanced at the passersby around them before asking in a low voice: "How come we've never heard you mention this, Jace?"

Perhaps it was that period after he'd cast that shadow meteor at the Dark Portal — it had been too agonizing. Whether it was Grede, whose emotions had nearly collapsed, or Vereesa, who, even after seeing Jace recover, had still been so frightened that she couldn't pull herself out of the sadness for a long time…

Every time he thought of that statue that wasn't yet fully completed, seeing himself with both hands raised, one gripping a staff and the other a sword, and recalled the perpetually gray, dry sky from the Dark Portal's malevolent energy radiation, Jace's heart held little sense of honor or pride. Rather, the feelings were quite complex — on the whole, negative.

"When can we go to Nethergarde Keep to take a look?" Henny said.

"There — this is exactly why I didn't want to bring it up," Jace said at once. "Nethergarde Keep is the front line where the Alliance army faces off against demons, Mom. It's full of dark magic everywhere, full of demon blood that can burn a person's bones through!"

He pointed at the forest to the southeast. "So, you think I'd let you go there and run into a bunch of monsters crawling out of the Twisting Nether to see whose fangs are longer?"

"All right, all right, don't worry, Jace." Vik said quickly. "We won't go. But once things are over, once the situation over there improves, you can't stop us from going to see our son."

"Fine," Jace said reluctantly.
