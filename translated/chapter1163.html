Chapter 1163: The Strange Pattern

That evening, back at the rented house, Morophil frowned the moment she saw Jace.

Jace glanced at the Apocalypse twin blades in her hands. He hadn't expected the two swords to stop burning with flame — now they were as quiet as a pair of tamed kittens. The bone material, the curved blades, and the straighter edge of the other sword were all clearly visible.

"Something wrong?" Jace asked.

"Master has encountered something happy," Morophil said. "And it's an unfamiliar kind of excitement. Highly suspicious."

"What, you're going to start hunting down my happy moods now?" Jace asked.

"Of course not." Morophil said, "I just want to know what Master's—"

Jace raised both hands. "There's no second succubus. Fine — a while back I summoned a succubus to deal with a human, but she moved into Ulthalesh not long after."

He pointed toward the staff leaning against the bedhead as he spoke. "Don't believe me, ask him."

"Lord Ulthalesh and I don't have much to say to each other." Morophil shook her head. "But I believe Master's words. You say that sayaad is dead, then dead she is — what else can I do? As long as you can fool Lady Vereesa."

Jace snorted and looked at the imp sitting at the table poring over those scrolls. "How's your reading going? Those materials I got from Dar'Khan — what do you make of them?"

"Not much at all, Master." Sazeno looked up. "A lot of the stuff in these materials — I don't recognize any of it."

"What do you mean you don't recognize it?" Jace took off his coat, hung it to the side, and sat down at the table to look at the scroll spread out under the imp's hands.

The imp was pointing at a strange pattern. It looked like some kind of magic circle, but it didn't quite match one either — there were no power-conducting lines linking the arcane, no runes or incantations at key points to guide it. The whole thing looked chaotic and disorderly.

To Jace's eyes nowadays, even if he wasn't familiar with arcane circles and had little experience drawing elemental magic circles, he could still more or less make out what he was looking at when he encountered those two types.

And this definitely wasn't any kind of demonic or shadow circle he recognized. It didn't connect to any dark magic circle he was familiar with.

In the nerubians' underground crypt, he'd been in too much of a hurry searching, and most of his attention had been on that metal box. On top of that, he'd soon been interrupted by the thugs that noble elf had brought along. So the materials and scrolls he'd found relating to shadow magic and the Twisting Nether rift Telogrus hadn't gotten enough time for careful inspection.

All he remembered was seeing what seemed like a lot of magic-circle-like things at the time. He'd figured that once he was back, he could rely on his demon minions to figure out what those circles did — crack them bit by bit, the way he'd done with the Burning Legion scrolls of Dasmora.

So it turned out all those circles he'd glimpsed were actually these things that defied explanation.

Still, Jace wouldn't claim to be learned and well-read either. He couldn't jump to conclusions based solely on his own judgment.

"Are there others?"

"Yes." The imp said, "That whole stack over there is full of these patterns too, Master. If anything, I haven't seen a single recognizable magic circle or casting rune…"

Jace looked back and forth over the several sheets of paper Sazeno had brought over bearing these patterns. They were all more or less the same, but he could vaguely sense some kind of connection among them.

As he stared at them, he actually started to feel like these patterns resembled orcish runes. Orcish runes were quite different from most scripts in Azeroth — a single rune could contain an enormous amount of meaning. A word, a phrase, sometimes even a whole sentence could be expressed in one composite rune.

So could these patterns be some kind of writing?

Considering that many of these materials were related to shadow and the Void, and might even record the means of reaching the Twisting Nether rift Telogrus, Jace naturally thought of the possibility that they were the script of some Void creature.

"Shath'Yar?" Jace asked.

"That's not the tongue of those parasites." Morophil shook her head. "If it were Shath'Yar… I should be able to sense it. The Burning Legion's demons are all fairly familiar with that language."

"Ha, so you admit you're a lackey of the Burning Legion now, sayaad." The imp pointed at the succubus gleefully.

Morophil smiled. "Wanting to know what excrement smells like doesn't mean you have to crawl out of a brood mother's back end yourself. But I believe you vile imps probably don't understand that principle, which is why you spout such nonsense."

"Pfft! You you…"

Sazeno spat, but then stood there dumbfounded, unable to say anything at all. For a moment he couldn't come up with a comeback. Jace leaned back in his chair and slowly let his thoughts drift elsewhere amid the two demons' bickering.

Seemed like he'd hit a dead end with the two of them. Still, Jace couldn't very well take these materials back to Quel'Thalas to consult with the people Dar'Khan had left behind either.

For one thing, he couldn't reveal that he'd secretly held back so many materials — that would sell out Vereesa. Magister Aeridania, the one who'd helped him search all the way to Stratholme, might end up taking the blame for it too.

Even if he managed it flawlessly and met with those people in secret — thinking back to the interrogation room in Tranquillien, that female shadow-witch Malicia's slightly smug look, as if withholding the clues to Ulthalesh was her revenge for Jace wiping out their whole operation…

If she found out that what he wanted wasn't the clues to Ulthalesh but the secrets hidden in these patterns, that would be practically handing her a lever to use against him.

But at least in Stormwind there was still a group of warlocks who'd studied the Twisting Nether and shadow magic in depth who could help — especially Zardeth, who was researching how to carve out a new hidden space in the Twisting Nether… Jace wondered whether Zardeth and his lot could actually seize this opportunity.

Still, since there was already Telogrus — a known hidden space in the Twisting Nether, free from interference by the Burning Legion or any other powerful faction — it was more reliable by comparison than letting Zardeth and his crew try opening doors everywhere.

After putting the scrolls back into his pack, Jace said, "All right, stop arguing. I've been busy all day and I'm going to sleep. Morophil, a sleep spell."

"Understood, Master."

Morophil smiled and kissed Jace's forehead. She touched the slightly damp kiss mark with a finger, and Jace's head tilted to the side — and he knew nothing more.

At this time of year, Stormwind seemed to get warmer with every passing day. When Jace arrived at the Mage Quarter, the apprentices and mages around the Wizard's Sanctum had all changed into remarkably light, thin outer garments. It had only been a few days since he'd arrived at Stormwind's harbor with his parents, when everyone in the city had still been bundled up quite heavily.

Jace came to Sola's residence and knocked on the door. Sola opened it, saw Jace, and immediately smiled. "Come in, sit down, Jace. I hope you can properly recover the energy you've been spending lately while you're here."

Sola was absolutely telling the truth. Her living quarters had, through her deliberate decorating and influence, become a remarkably beautiful and exquisite "nature's dwelling." Staying there, you wouldn't feel cluttered by foreign objects everywhere — it was like the dwellings of those Kaldorei druids deep in the heart of Ashenvale.

Of course, thanks to Jace's earlier reminder, there was no way to tell from outside.

Jace set the paper-wrapped pastries he'd bought at the Gilded Rose on the table. "Bought these for you, Sola. Dalaran-style almond chocolate cookies. Don't know if you've had them."

"Thank you so much, Jace." Sola narrowed her eyes. "Grede complained about you being stingy all the way here, but I never thought that."

Jace gave Sola a complicated sidelong glance as she couldn't wait to unwrap the package. If these words hadn't come from this green dragon's mouth, he would have suspected she was trying to drive a wedge between him and Grede.

But precisely because it was Sola who'd said it, he believed it was definitely the truth. Over these past few months, that bastard Grede had said God knows how many bad things about him.

He raised his arm and gestured casually out the window. "The last person in this world who should call me stingy is him. It's simply… By the way, did Grede say when he'd be back?"

"He didn't mention that." Sola thought for a moment. "But he did say he'd be staying in Goldshire at night these days."

"It's not that I don't care about Goldshire's economic livelihood and mining safety." Jace rested his chin on his hand. "But I'd like him to be around when I summon demons…"

"Will there be any danger then?" Sola asked.

"In theory there shouldn't be any danger, but I want the summoning process to go as smoothly as possible," Jace said. "For now I've decided to perform the summoning in Deadwind Pass. For one thing, Kallrez had been hiding there before he was summoned to Stormwind, and nothing went wrong. Second, we can also borrow Karazhan's shadow magic power to make the summoning ritual go more smoothly. But you know Deadwind Pass isn't exactly a peaceful place — the more people, the safer."

"Then when do we leave? I can run to Goldshire and look for him today, Jace," Sola said. "He also said he wanted to be there when Vereesa got her bow."

"Then how about we set out for Duskwood the day after tomorrow?" Jace said. "I'll get Vereesa. If there are any other issues, I'll come find you again."

"All right." The green dragon nodded, then seemed to remember something and stood up, walking to the cabinet inside the room. "Oh, right — you came for the mooncloth, didn't you?"

"Ah, yes." Jace rubbed his head. "Chatting about Grede and I completely forgot. Thanks for keeping all this safe for me along the way, Sola."

"It's nothing." Sola smiled.
