Chapter 1165: The “Decoding” Difficulty

“Can you read what’s on here, Ditoktu?” Surena leaned in and asked.

The voidwalker fell silent for a moment, then extended a black palm — or rather, a claw formed from a mass of shadow — and slowly brushed it across the tangle of runes that could barely be called orderly.

Its pair of flame-like eyes began to tremble faintly. Low, indistinct sounds rose from within its body — like groans of pain, or like the soaring chants that echoed from the Cathedral of Light from time to time.

The runes on the scroll began to glow with a purple arcane light together with it. That light burned like an ominous fire, flickering in tandem with the voidwalker’s flame-like eyes — bright, then dim.

At this point, Jace was even a little worried that its “reading” would cause the scroll to burn up together with it.

Still, the commotion from this reading process was clearly a bit too much. Surena couldn’t help but glance up at the staircase behind her, her expression growing increasingly uneasy.

“Better… stop,” she murmured.

“Yes.” The voidwalker answered in Common, yet the rumbling whisper within its body still reverberated for several seconds before finally dying down.

The three warlocks fell silent. After the basement went completely quiet and they’d confirmed there was no reaction from outside, they all let out relieved sighs.

“You just need to tell us what this says, Ditoktu.” Erlan lowered his voice. “You don’t need to read it out the way you do. Do you understand my Common?”

“I have not yet finished reading, mortal. I cannot tell you the precise meaning,” the voidwalker replied.

“Damn.” Erlan put his hands on his hips and lowered his head.

“Jace has a lot of scrolls here. If he’s going to make this kind of fuss every time he wants a single rune decoded, we can’t be doing this in Old Town,” Surena said. “Sorry, this is just a place I use to store things. And while Shaw and SI:7 know about our situation and are willing to leave my house alone, that’s on the condition that we don’t cause a disturbance in the city… My basement here isn’t nearly as secure as the one Zardeth has built under the tavern.”

“But there’s no way you can move this black monster over there either,” Erlan said.

Surena sighed. “It’s not that the summoning incantation you got from the Dark Iron dwarves doesn’t work, Jace. I haven’t learned it yet, but Zardeth has already managed to summon Lady Senlora from his house to the basement without incident. But summoning a voidwalker doesn’t work. I suspect that blood magic ritual must be based on the demonic contract itself…”

“Let me try.” Jace took the scroll and, imitating the voidwalker’s manner, pressed his hand against it, then carefully sensed the shadow power within.

Regrettably, he couldn’t feel anything at all — only a faint residue of magical fluctuation left behind from the voidwalker’s reading.

After all, he wasn’t a voidwalker. He hadn’t mastered the use of these runes with “pure shadow.”

In fact, he felt that Dar’Khan, as well as Malicia, or Ressathia, or all the other members of his gang, wouldn’t have had the ability to decipher what was actually written on the scrolls.

After all, in another timeline, even after Dar’Khan betrayed Quel’Thalas and joined the Scourge, becoming a half-dead lich-wizard, he still never found a way to truly enter the Telogrus rift.

At least not in his lifetime, all the way up to the point when Arthas’s army reached the city gates. But later, another elven mage — Magister Umbric, who successfully led his own apprentices into the Telogrus rift — had actually found a way to decipher these complex runes quite quickly.

From the previous exchanges with the magisters and rangers in Quel’Thalas, Jace could tell that although Umbric was also a fairly well-known shadow researcher in the elven kingdom, his status and knowledge clearly didn’t measure up to Dar’Khan’s.

And Dar’Khan also had an entire gang working for him, and had managed to find a nerubian underground palace beneath Stratholme to serve as a research lab… If Umbric could decipher the runes easily later on, there was no way Dar’Khan could have produced no results for such a long time.

Jace scratched at his stubble. He felt it wasn’t hard to guess the reason — it was very likely because of Draenor, or rather, because of the influence of the Draenor that had been destroyed.

After the Sunwell was destroyed by the Scourge, all the high elves either died in despair, degenerated into mindless ones, or began seeking new sources of power to replace the Sunwell as a solution to the elves’ magic addiction.

Shadow researchers like Umbric naturally hoped the Twisting Nether could replace the Sunwell. So although the things Malicia had said in the interrogation room were quite mad, they weren’t entirely illogical.

Prince Kael’thas, on the other hand, had seized the opportunity to follow Illidan Stormrage to Outland. There in the shattered Draenor, he’d found countless powerful fel crystals from the Burning Legion and brought them back to Quel’Thalas, allowing the thirsty people remaining in Quel’Thalas to ease their addiction. This was why the blood elves’ eyes would later all turn the green of fel energy.

What mattered even more was that what Kael’thas brought back from Illidan and the Burning Legion was far more than just fel energy — there were countless magical items, incantations, and methods of communicating with the Twisting Nether and demonic power, and so on.

Through this process, the blood elves rapidly absorbed the dark magic from the shattered Draenor. Not to mention that the fel-corrupted elves had simultaneously developed a powerful affinity with demons and corrupt magic.

In the space of a few years, demon researchers, warlocks, and shadow mages in Quel’Thalas sprang up like mushrooms after rain.

By then, Umbric would have had conditions and an environment infinitely better than Dar’Khan’s to research the secret materials that Dar’Khan had only managed to obtain by exhausting every trick in the book — the very things in Jace’s hands right now.

Still, what couldn’t be overlooked was that the secrets contained in these materials didn’t just include the method of entering the Telogrus rift. They also included the sources of Dar’Khan’s secret shadow magic.

Could it be that Dar’Khan had partially mastered this “Abyssal tongue,” or deciphered a portion of the scrolls, which was why he could so freely interfere with the shadow energy Jace released?

But judging from the way the voidwalker deciphered these Abyssal runes, using the Abyssal tongue at the very least couldn’t be as simple as speaking Kalimag, the elemental tongue.

“We’ll have to go back to the Slaughtered Lamb and summon another voidwalker,” Surena said. “I already have one here, Erlan. Zardeth won’t allow me to summon a second voidwalker. So next it’s your turn.”

“Zardeth said at least four people need to be present for summoning rituals other than imps and felhounds,” Erlan said. “Considering the terrifying problems we’ve run into before, I intend to follow that rule honestly.”

“Jace is here — that makes three of us. Jalane is right in the Mage Quarter,” Surena said.

Erlan stood to the side with his arms folded, one hand lightly brushing his lips, then looked toward Jace.

Jace knew they wanted him to summon a voidwalker.

Everyone in the Dagger Society knew that the demons Zardeth summoned were always a bit stronger than what others could summon. And Jace was on another level entirely. The succubus Senlora that Zardeth summoned was extremely haughty, but when she saw Morophil, she was as obedient as a maid.

Of course, Jace was also full of interest and curiosity about deciphering the secrets on these materials — he wanted to know exactly where Dar’Khan had learned those spells. But he also had some reservations about the idea of summoning a voidwalker.

He said, “I’m going to summon a demon in the next couple of days — I’ve been planning it for a while. If I perform an unfamiliar summoning ritual right now, it might interfere with the plan that comes next.”

“What demon are you planning to summon?” Erlan asked.

“To summon back a demon I summoned before. Because of the black dragon’s attack on Stormwind a while back, I lost contact with him.”

Jace spoke offhandedly, while thinking to himself: Please don’t ask any further, Mr. Drudgemoor.

“Then good luck, Jace.” Erlan nodded. “Then how about you come find us at the Dagger Society after you complete the ritual? Surena and I will set up a time with Zardeth to call a meeting of everyone. As it happens, Jalane and Ursula are both in Stormwind right now… I’m getting more curious by the day about what’s recorded in the materials you brought. I’m quite sure Zardeth feels the same.”

“Maybe Zardeth won’t be able to wait for Jace to come back and will summon a voidwalker himself to decode these runes,” Surena said. “He mentioned a couple days ago that he planned to try again soon.”

“He also mentioned he was looking forward to seeing what caliber of voidwalker Jace could summon…” Erlan said.

“I can’t wait to crack what’s inside here either,” Jace said. “My business will take about two weeks. What time works for you?”

Erlan rubbed his hands together. “I think we can schedule it for the days before Noblegarden.”

Noblegarden was actually the in-game Easter. Jace had only learned the festival’s original name after coming here. This year’s Noblegarden was at the end of April — just over two weeks away. Enough time for Jace and the others to make the round trip to Deadwind Pass and summon Kallrez.

“Will enslaving a voidwalker take a lot of energy?” Jace asked Surena. “Is your Mr. Succubus still alive?”

“Gakres is upstairs.” Surena said. “But voidwalkers don’t need a ‘contract’ like other demons do, Jace. The method of enslaving them is different. If you need to leave the Slaughtered Lamb from time to time and don’t want to worry about a voidwalker the whole time, you can simply try summoning one out and then let Zardeth or Jalane complete the enslavement.”

Erlan nodded. “Jalane has always wanted a voidwalker to help her refine shadow enchanting, but she hasn’t been able to successfully summon one herself.”
