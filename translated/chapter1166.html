Chapter 1166: Sitting Down Together Again

That evening, the Pig and Whistle Inn. Jace sat with Vereesa. The elf's fingers tapped nervously on the table without pause — she'd lost all the composure a Ranger-Lord ought to have. "You look awfully nervous," Jace asked, leaning back in his chair. "A legendary bow, Jace…" Vereesa lowered her voice, as if plotting some crime. "Considering the wondrous magic of the green dragons, this time tomorrow I might already have it in my hands." "Who knows — maybe they're sleeping right now. Tomorrow when we deliver the stuff, they'll wait until they wake up to make it for us. Might be a few years before that happens." Jace pretended not to care much, glancing toward the tavern hall's door. "You know how dragons… think about time." "Damn." Vereesa sighed, her tapping fingers slapping flat on the table. "That could actually happen." Just then, despite the raucous noise filling the hall, Jace still heard a familiar, hoarse, low-pitched voice muttering something outside. He couldn't help but curl the corner of his mouth. A dwarf with a head of black hair walked in from outside, looking around. "Where are you?" "Over here!" Jace raised his voice. "Grede!" "Ha!" Grede pointed at them. His legs went drumstick-fast, his short and stocky body barreling through the crowd to deliver him to Jace's front, then he threw himself straight into Jace's arms. Jace was slammed by this hundred-plus-kilo wrecking ball. He had to brace the table behind him to keep from toppling over. "Good to see you mostly intact." Grede let go and gave Jace's shoulder a hearty slap. "Mostly intact?" Jace asked. "What's missing?" Grede stroked his big beard. "This. You shaved your beard off and look like a kid who hasn't properly grown in yet." "Not everyone has to follow the aesthetics of your mountain, Grede." Vereesa folded her arms and gave him a sidelong look. "'Hello' in Elvish sure is a long word." The dwarf looked at the elf. "But I'll try to take its original meaning in the best light, Lady Vereesa." Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "Make a little room — you're blocking the seat and Solia's got nowhere to sit." The dwarf looked up. Solia stood to the side, smiling at him. "Sorry, miss." Grede grinned and moved aside. "But you can understand how excited I am to see these two, right?" "Of course." Solia nodded and sat inside. After Grede sat down, he slapped the table and said in a low voice, "When we set out, I thought this green dragon couldn't wait to get back to her home in Ashenvale. But we hadn't been flying a few days before she started going on about how Jace was doing, what Vereesa must be thinking, what Jace would do if he were here, we should go back and ask…" "I don't think I said it that many times." Solia cut him off immediately. "Well, well — at least enough to make this old friend of theirs, who knows them inside and out, wear out his ears." Grede raised both hands. "Makes it seem like I'm completely useless without the two of them." "Solia understands very well how important a ranger is to an adventuring party," Vereesa emphasized. "Important indeed." Grede looked around with an air of seriousness, then said, "So where's our ranger? All I know is there's an elf maniac in our party…" Jace, propping his cheek to the side, shook with barely suppressed laughter. Vereesa was about to unleash on the dwarf, but Jace's stifled laughing drew her attention instead. "You're still laughing?" Jace forced his laugh back down, but ended up swallowing too much air and hiccuping instead. That set Grede and Solia off laughing together, and even Vereesa herself ended up laughing along. The cheerful laughter blended the ease of this table with the atmosphere of the whole tavern. Grede shouted happily, "Quick, come on — get a serving wench for this table with an extremely high level of racial diversity!" "Coming right away, Mister Dwarf!" a voice called back from the crowd, but there was no idea who was shouting. Grede choked on his own saliva and coughed. After he sat down, he looked at Jace and said in a low voice, "I heard from Solia that you finally brought your parents over to Stormwind." "Yes," Jace nodded. "They're staying in my place." "Congratulations, Jace Sesso." Grede patted Jace's hand. "Not just because you earned enough money to buy a house of your own — but because you have family members who understand you and accept you." Jace nodded. Vereesa said from the side, "And friends like him." "No need to tell me." Grede laughed and spread his hands. "That's common knowledge! I heard your father used to be a cavalryman in Hillsbrad — I bet we'll have plenty in common. When we get back from this trip, I'm definitely going to pay them both a visit. You'll be responsible for the introduction, Jace." "I'll take you to meet them," Jace said. "Just remember to go easy on the drink the first day." Just then the serving wench arrived. Grede gave her a sidelong look. "We're not going tomorrow, are we, hmm? Then bring us a round of Ironforge pale ale. Everyone here…" "I'll pass," Vereesa said. "Didn't Solia tell you we're heading south tomorrow?" "I thought you'd already joined our team and absorbed our habits!" Grede complained. "Fine — add a cup of even more boring goat milk to the boring Bronzebeard beer, tall sir." The serving wench nodded. Grede then casually ordered some grilled ribs and roasted fruit. After she left, Vereesa leaned in eagerly and whispered, "I heard you met the leader of Mount Hyjal in Astranaar." "Yes." Grede nodded. "High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind — by the gods, I heard she's lived even longer than that sentinel general at Feathermoon Stronghold." "At least that's the legend," Vereesa murmured. "They've both lived at least ten thousand years. At least in the memories of Dath'Remar Sunstrider, they were already the leaders of the Kaldorei. So what did she look like?" Grede looked up at the ceiling and muttered, "Let me think how to describe her…" Jace curiously leaned in too. Last time they'd gone to Kalimdor to find Ulthalesh, he'd been so on edge carrying that evil thing that he hadn't dared set foot in the night elf territories, leaving straight from Azshara instead. Still, he was quite curious about this moon priestess who'd ruled Mount Hyjal and Ashenvale ever since the end of the War of the Ancients. "She was like a pale pink gem — a finely wrought, living statue." Grede smacked his lips. "Her hair was a blue like water, and she wore an extremely simple, plain white dress. Just standing beside her made the whole world feel less and less real." Vereesa frowned and said nothing. Solia slowly nodded. "I felt that way too." "But she didn't go around lifting her nose to the sky all the time like the elves on your side do," Grede added. "If she was just being very respectful toward Solia, that would be one thing. But she was courteous and friendly even to an outsider like me." Hearing that, Vereesa snorted in displeasure. Grede took another gulp of the wooden mug the serving wench had brought, licked the foam off, and said, "Of course I'm only praising the High Priestess herself — not trying to compare you two groups. After all, most of the dark-skinned elves over there are plenty arrogant too. Before they knew I was Grede Thunderfist, they nearly wanted to grab me like a stunted black bear, string me up, and drag me back to Astranaar." "I really wish I'd been there," Jace said. "Envious of my exciting Kalimdor journey?" Grede laughed. "Don't get me wrong — I just wanted to see what you looked like tied up like a black bear," Jace said. Vereesa burst out laughing. Grede blew at his beard and glared at Jace, but immediately held up a finger. "But the High Priestess did say she'd very much like to meet you in person. She knows about many of the things you've done, Jace." "We'll have a chance in the future," Jace said. "When we go visit Motha in Kalimdor again." Grede chuckled. "No need to make a special trip just to take me to see Motha." "We want to see Motha too," Jace said, and Vereesa nodded. "She misses you very much too, Jace." Grede drained the beer from his mug in one gulp and let out a breath. "Misses you both very much." Jace hummed an acknowledgment, realizing he probably shouldn't have brought up a topic that made Grede sad in this kind of atmosphere. He looked at Solia and asked, "Oh, right — do your kin know we're going there tomorrow?" "They're already waiting for us there, Jace," Solia said. "I told them in my sleep." "He?" Vereesa asked. "Who?" "Thalar." Solia looked at the elf. "He's something of an older brother of mine, and one of the Dream Wardens of the Queen. He'll be the one to help make that bow."
