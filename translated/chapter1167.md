## Chapter 1167: The Dream Lord's Gift of Gratitude

The green dragon circled far around Goldshire and the farmsteads to the south, crossing the broad Nafeltari River and flying toward the tall mountain in the middle of southern Elwynn. Jace could feel Vereesa gripping his hand harder and harder, sweat practically wrung out of her palm.

Back when Jace first mentioned making this bow, she'd been quite excited, but he could tell she still had her doubts. After all, just as the dwarves knew how impossibly difficult it was to craft a gem amulet from dragon's blood, the elves surely knew exactly how tough a mature black dragon's sinew was — and what an absurd craft it would be to twist it into a bowstring.

And this wasn't just any mature black dragon. The gap between an ordinary mature black dragon and Princess Onyxia… was probably even larger than the gap between them and a whelp.

Jace of course knew it was possible, but Vereesa had truly never seen anything like this.

Even when Solia mentioned she'd heard of such a thing, Vereesa felt it might take a very long time to complete.

Perhaps it was precisely for this reason that she'd seemed so nonchalant along the way, as if she didn't have that strong a desire for this bow. Even when she brought it up occasionally, it seemed like a passing remark, or she treated it as an excuse to help Solia rescue her kin from the troll temple…

But ever since Solia and Grede had brought the good news back from Kalimdor — that the green dragons had actually agreed to help make this bow — Vereesa's attitude had shifted considerably.

After all, this legendary bow… was already right before her eyes.

Grede held the black dragon's sinew, wrapped in cloth, in his arms. His rough right hand would stroke it from time to time, while his eyes stayed fixed on the distant, towering forest.

Jace knew that as a hill dwarf, a descendant of an old hunter, Grede was quite skilled with a bow himself. Naturally he was full of curiosity about this legendary bow. His insistence on witnessing Vereesa getting this weapon with his own eyes definitely wasn't just about seeing a friend's dream come true.

This time, Somaelir didn't go looking for chances to romp and play around like she had on previous trips. She honestly made straight for the location where the Dawnwood lay.

She dropped altitude, slipping in among the canopy. The dim sky was quickly replaced by the quiet shadows of the deep forest. The Highborne ruins hidden deep in this secluded valley — those silver-white pillars tinged with green, the collapsed palaces and towers — appeared in stretches.

The green dragon let out call after call, and from time to time a young dragon's low cry echoed back from within the forest. Then Somaelir descended onto the grass, setting everyone on her back down.

"Where are they?" Grede looked around, holding the black dragon's sinew.

Vereesa shook her head. Jace gazed deeper into the temple, but here too he couldn't see the shadow of any other dragon. Solia also looked around, then called out in the dragon tongue to her brother and her other kin.

Her voice seemed to pierce through some kind of imperceptible veil, layer after layer. Jace realized they had already stepped into some boundary between dream and reality. Solia's voice was loud and clear, but as it rippled outward in waves, listening to the forest's echo made one feel drowsy.

After a while, Jace felt a pressing weight that made his eyelids heavy drift over from the forest. A shadow like a great black cloud floated before them. Before this shadow, Jace felt as if he'd been crushed into a tiny insect — utterly pitiful.

"Thalar." Solia smiled toward the direction the shadow came from. But Vereesa and Grede couldn't smile at all. Like Jace, they both felt this suffocating pressure.

Even when facing the Shadow of Eranikus at the bottom of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, Jace hadn't felt a power that made him want to shrink back this badly. Even knowing the being ahead was friendly, he couldn't help but grow instinctively wary.

After all, this was a place where dream and reality interpenetrated. They had stepped into the domain of the green dragonflight. Here… Ysera and her children held absolute dominion… at least, that was what they believed.

Perhaps it was precisely because of this, because they possessed such vast power and authority within the dreams, that the green dragons had underestimated that hand of corruption that had dragged them bit by bit into the abyss since ancient times.

Still, the thing casting this shadow did not make a grand, imposing entrance as Jace, Grede, and Vereesa had expected. What walked out of that shadow, instead, was a figure that was short by comparison.

Though "short by comparison" only meant compared to what the several of them had expected. "I'm here, Somaelir. No rush."

The speaker was a night elf with long, dark cyan hair. He stood a full head taller than any human Jace had ever seen — even taller than the night elf men they'd encountered in Ashenvale and at Feathermoon Stronghold.

He wore a long robe woven from leaves and vines. Vines were tattooed across his bare chest. He held a gold-green staff, his bearing that of a king of the forest.

This must be the Thalar that Solia had mentioned — one of Ysera's four most powerful Dream Wardens, guardian of the Dawnwood's Dream Gate at this moment.

Seeing Jace's group, he said, "Hello, Jace Sesso, Vereesa Windrunner, and Grede Thunderfist. I am Thalar, child and guardian of the Dream Queen."

"I can feel you're still not quite accustomed to this forest. My apologies — this sensation is unavoidable when drawing close to the Emerald Dream. I'm not able to remedy it through my own power. But please rest assured this force won't cause any harm to your physical health."

"It's all right, esteemed dragon," Vereesa said. "I feel fine."

"We've been here once before, so we have some mental preparation," Jace said too.

Thalar smiled slightly. "Then I'm relieved. Father instructed me regarding the bow. It would be my honor to help you complete this work with my own hands."

He raised the staff in his hand. "This is Father's staff. I'll use it to craft the bow's body. So please hand over the sinew that Lady Onyxia left behind."

Thalar shifted his gaze to the two beside them. Jace felt something was off too. Grede, who was holding the sinew, hadn't made a sound since they landed.

He looked over toward the dwarf and found that he had already lain down on the ground and fallen asleep. What's more, there was no snoring at all — he was quiet as a baby. This actually made the people beside him feel unaccustomed.

Jace walked over and patted his face. "Weren't you going to watch the whole process?"

Grede frowned, moved his lips, rolled over, then went still again.

Jace glanced awkwardly at Thalar in the distance, then at Vereesa. He had no choice but to grab the black dragon's sinew that the dwarf was clutching tightly. Grede gripped it hard and through gritted teeth muttered, "You can't… take it away, damn demon!"

It took quite a bit of effort to pull it out. Jace was even a little worried the bastard might go demigod-mode in his dream and match his strength. Jace handed the black dragon's sinew over. Thalar received it, gazing at it for a long while, then said, "Please follow me, everyone.

"Leave Grede Thunderfist here to rest in peace. My children will look after him."

With that, the Dream Warden turned and walked toward the deeper part of the temple ruins.

Seeing Grede sleeping so soundly — a rare sight — Jace couldn't bear to wake him. So he followed after Thalar's footsteps along with Vereesa and Solia.

The closer they drew to the depths of the ruins, the deeper the entire forest became. Although the mist wasn't as thick this time in the Dawnwood as last time, under this faint haze the high canopy above was still hard to distinguish from the night sky.

Thalar led Jace's group to a great altar. A grand white ramp led up to a giant door that resembled a round mirror at the high place. The world inside the door flickered between light and dark, hazy and indistinct — like a mirage covered by a dreamlike veil.

Jace knew this was the Dream Gate.

Because it bore quite a resemblance to the sight in the game, Jace had some mental preparation and wasn't overly startled. But Vereesa was drawn to that phantom reflection, staring as if she couldn't believe it.

After all, who would have thought you could actually encounter a door in the real world that let you walk right into your dreams?

Solia gazed at the things within the door, her expression rather complex. Perhaps she wasn't ready to return to the Emerald Dream. Perhaps the experiences and adventures with Jace and the others these days had changed the way she viewed her homeland.

Who knew how many thoughts were hidden in the heart of this seemingly innocent, kind green dragon? Even a warlock like Jace couldn't easily see through.

Thalar turned around before the ramp leading to the Dream Gate, raised his left hand, and intoned whispers that were hard to make out at this distance.

Solia's gaze, fixed on the Dream Gate, flickered. Jace also noticed that the images within the Dream Gate gradually cleared under the Dream Warden's guidance of energy, giving off an illusory arcane light interwoven with pale pink and green.

The arcane light illuminated the fog, making the entire forest even hazier. One vine after another stretched out from the base of the ramp and from the nearby temple ruins. Passing through the fog, they converged before Thalar, lifting the black dragon's sinew he'd placed on the ground.

As the arcane light appeared, Thalar's voice grew clearer. This voice wasn't the speech of a night elf — it was the voice of a dragon.

The voice was a command, and also like an instruction. It not only drew more and more robust vines over, but also brought many young dragons flying closer, circling near Jace's group and the edge of the fog, watching with curiosity.

From behind the fog came a shout: "Hey! Am I missing something…"

Grede came jogging up to Jace's side, then was immediately stunned to wide-eyed shock by the scene before him, his jaw nearly hitting the ground.

The scene right now gave Jace a sense of déjà vu. He remembered that time at the Quel'Dani Cottage in the Hinterlands, when that botanist summoned the power of nature in the forest to cure Grede's blindness caused by poisoning.

But he knew that botanist was manipulating arcane energy to forcibly enslave the forest's power of nature, draining their life energy like a vampire and channeling it onto Grede to eliminate his disability.

So back then the forest had been filled with unease and agitation — as if they wanted to resist the elf botanist's magic but were powerless to do so. Only this time it was completely the reverse.

At Thalar's call, the world within the Dream Gate, the entire forest, seemed to be falling over itself to contribute its strength to the master of this forest, as if afraid that falling behind would disappoint him.

These vines and natural forces wrapped the sinew in their midst, constantly tearing, rolling, and twisting — and the sinew was gradually being shaped into a bowstring in the process.

And Thalar's guidance, more than a call for help, was an attempt to calm the fervent forest as much as possible, so the work could be completed as flawlessly as Jace could hope.

Jace pressed his lips together and observed the scene. Vereesa was already so tense she was nearly suffocating. Only now did Grede remember to close his mouth.

When that delicate, pale-yellow bowstring emerged from between the vines, Jace had almost forgotten what it had looked like when it was first taken out of the Wizard Sanctum.

Thalar now lowered his arm and reached out to pluck one end of the bowstring from between the vines — gently and gracefully, as if performing some sacred ceremony.

In fact, this already had more atmosphere than most ceremonies Jace had ever seen. It was just that the participants in this ceremony weren't orcs, humans, or mages and warlocks — they were every tree and every vine in the entire forest.

Just when Jace thought Thalar was about to set about stringing the bow, the Dream Warden didn't hurry to start. Instead, he slowly leveled Eranikus's staff before him.

At the same time, two figures similar to Thalar in build — even dressed almost identically — approached from different directions to his two sides.

These two night elves grabbed the two ends of the staff and pulled simultaneously, bending it to produce a suitable arc. As they exerted force, Jace sensed something wrong.

The two figures, including Thalar in the middle — the magical aura emanating from them was actually identical, right down to the degree of emotional calm. Which meant all three of these people were Thalar!

Jace suddenly remembered how another Dream Warden, Emeriss, was nicknamed the "Mushroom Dragon" for being able to make explosive mushrooms grow — Thalar was nicknamed the "Clone Dragon" by players because he could split into three phantom clones in battle.

The Thalar in the middle held one end of the bowstring against the two ends of the staff with one hand while continuing to guide dream power with the other.

When he brought the bowstring into contact with the bow body, the moment it was bound, more vines immediately grew from the staff under the radiance of dream arcane light, further securing the bowstring and bow body together.

When both sides were bound, white flowers and yellow flowers bloomed one after another on the parts of the bow body where the bowstring attached — as if declaring that this ceremony representing the power of the Emerald Dream had successfully purified the black dragon's corrupt power.

Thalar lifted the bow with both hands. The vines wandered back into the fog one by one. The arcane light dimmed along with them. The forest gradually returned to calm.

In the end, the light within the Dream Gate also dissipated, and the images inside grew harder and harder to make out.

"Is it done?" Vereesa couldn't help but ask.

"Yes," Thalar said.

Vereesa couldn't help but walk up quickly. She took the bow from Thalar's hands and cradled it, stroking it gently — as if what she was touching wasn't a weapon but some expensive piece of artwork.

But judging by the way the entire forest had participated in making this bow, this thing absolutely couldn't be that fragile. "You can try it," Thalar said.

Vereesa nodded, drew an arrow from the quiver she'd brought, and nocked it on the bow.

Truth be told, although arrows made in Quel'Thalas were beautiful and of high quality, they seemed a bit mismatched with this legendary bow in terms of artistic style. Jace felt the night elf arrows they'd gotten at the Silverwing Stronghold in Kalimdor would have been more harmonious with it.

The ranger raised the bow. As her movement gradually exerted force, the vines on the bow body also twisted in an unobtrusive way, as if wanting to lend her a hand.

Vereesa loosed her hand. The arrow whistled and vanished into the distant temple ruins and forest.

Truth be told, given Jace's knowledge of archery, he couldn't tell much from it. But as the arrow was loosed, Grede gave a startled "Ow!" and Vereesa's eyes went wide — that basically said everything.

"You can try it without an arrow, Vereesa." Thalar held out his hand. "Let me show you."

"Without an arrow?" Vereesa said in surprise. "Isn't that a bit…"

Though she seemed not quite sure what was going on, she still handed the bow back to Thalar.

Thalar took the bow, murmured something under his breath, then drew the bow empty at the same time. The flower buds on the bow's vines shook. A beam of arcane light interwoven with pale pink and emerald green suddenly appeared between the bowstring and the bow body.

He released the bowstring. With a "bong" of magical sound, that arcane light was flung outward, condensing into a sharp spike in mid-air that pierced through the air and blasted into a stone pillar, echoing with a hollow, booming explosion.

"A magic arrow?" Grede asked in surprise.

"Don't forget — this is made from the Dream Lord's staff." Thalar set the bow down to explain. "But for you, this will take further familiarity and practice, young quel'dorei. First of all, remember this summoning incantation."

Vereesa took the bow back in astonishment. "Incredible…"

Thalar added, "It's just that the power of dreams may be restricted in certain places. I think you've already noticed — the dream power summoned by the staff releases a hard-to-conceal illusory light. If you're planning to ambush an enemy in the dark, this will expose your position. Also, dream arrows can't be enchanted. I know the quel'dorei rangers are quite skilled at this. So you'll still need to keep a supply of arrows handy for all sorts of situations."

"Thank you for the reminder, esteemed dragon." Vereesa nodded. "I'll remember."

"You're welcome." Thalar said. "You defeated Deathwing, and you helped save the Emerald Dream from its crisis — rescuing the Lord from the dark domain of the Shadowy One. And you saved Somaelir, my young sister, keeping her from being lost to the Nightmare and degenerating into something beyond saving."

Somalir lowered her head shyly. Thalar continued, "Compared to these, this counts as only a simple favor. It can hardly repay the kindness you've shown. If you run into other trouble in the future, you can come find me in this forest. I should be guarding near this Dream Gate for a very long time to come, everyone."

Jace nodded. At this point, the dragons' sense of time wasn't such a bad thing anymore. "A very long time" might truly mean a very long time…

"Give this dream bow a name, Vereesa Windrunner," Thalar said. "Father's staff was originally a living, ancient branch, and now it's been granted even more powerful dream power…"

"You mean this bow… is alive?" Vereesa said hesitantly. "Ah, name it, I… it…"

In the game, the ancient bow crafted from Onyxia's sinew was called "Rhok'delar," but clearly that name was given by the ancient tree that crafted the bow.

Here, although the crafting process might come from the same principle, the crafter was different too. The bow body had changed from that ancient rune staff into Eranikus's staff, so it naturally couldn't bear the same name.

"What about Belanur Ilanicatis?" Thalar asked.

Jace knew this was a ceremonial dragon tongue rarely used in daily life. It made him think of Nefarian's staff, Lok'amir il Romathis — the "Hand of the Black Prince."

And the meaning of this phrase was actually quite straightforward. Roughly, it was "Eranikus's Gift of Gratitude."

"Fitting." Vereesa nodded excitedly, murmuring, "Belanur…"
