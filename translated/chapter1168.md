## Chapter 1168: The Wildhammer's Duty

"Mighty power of the Dream, Queen Ysera, Elune… some Lady, please grant this bow your strength!" In the clearing outside Goldshire, Grede stood in the middle of the grass holding Belanur.

After shouting this, he tried drawing the bow, but no dream arcane light appeared between the bowstring and the bow body.

"What was that incantation again, Vereesa?" The dwarf fiddled with the long bow back and forth. "Remind me."

"Dream grant blessing," Vereesa answered, leaning against a tree. "But it has to be said in the green dragons' language, so it's Bangannis…"

"Forget it, forget it, what a mouthful." Grede asked, "So do you have to chant something like this every time you loose an arrow? Like a mage hurling a fireball or Jace flinging a shadow bolt? That's too much trouble."

"It's not that much trouble once you get used to it," Jace called from where he sat at the base of the tree by Vereesa's feet. "Don't you gryphon riders like to shout 'For Khaz Modan' when you hurl your warhammers?"

Grede said, "That's not the same thing at all. We pledge loyalty to the great white mountain, but we ask absolutely nothing in return."

"All right, all right — that comparison really wasn't appropriate." Jace said, nibbling on a pastry as he spoke. "But I was comparing them from the perspective of whether it's troublesome or not."

Grede snorted, reached to his waist, and drew an arrow from the quiver he'd borrowed from Vereesa, nocking it on the bow.

This bow was indeed a bit too long for a dwarf like him, forcing him to nearly level the bow and draw it at an angle — like those troll hunters who liked to ambush from between tree branches.

But even though the posture was a bit awkward, when he seemingly casually loosed his hand, the arrow suddenly vanished from sight and instantly struck into a distant tree trunk. The force of it made Jace blink too.

The dwarf licked his lips, not quite satisfied, and fired two more arrows in quick succession, then drew a deep breath.

"I watched that green dragon's crafting process and it looked pretty straightforward and brutal. Of course I know dream power isn't something a bowyer on Aerie Peak can compare to, but its accuracy and power still far exceeded my imagination!"

Grede looked back over, curling the edges of his beard upward in a huge grin.

"Look, Lady Vereesa Windrunner — a superb archer like you has already reached the very peak of the entire Alliance in marksmanship, so the improvement this bow brings isn't that dramatic. Whereas me, a green apprentice hunter who hasn't seen much of the world, I'm exactly the kind of person who needs a…"

Vereesa blew the leaf she'd been holding in her mouth away. "I can give you my old bow, Grede. I wonder if Lady Valindres ever mentioned to you the value of a Windrunner's bow."

"I know how powerful that bow is… wouldn't dream of speaking its name." Grede said this while lifting Belanur, which from a distance looked like a staff, aiming it at the distant trees and testing a slight draw.

"Give it back to me." Vereesa propped herself up and walked over. "I've barely had a few tries with it in my hands and you've played with it the whole time."

"All right, all right." Grede handed the bow back to the ranger with a look of regret.

Watching from afar as Vereesa drew the bow, chanted the incantation, and loosed a dream-tinged arrow trailing a wisp of illusory light…

In the Elwynn Forest under the sunlight, it was simply like a beautiful painting rendered straight from legend — until that wobbing, dark-brown fatty blocked the view, then plopped down right beside him.

"If you use the bow, what about your warhammer?" Jace asked.

"That strange demon staff of yours doesn't stop you from swinging Songs of the Azure either." Grede swung his arm through the air a couple of times. "What's more, if I run into Onyxia again and get dragged back into that chaotic elemental plane where wind and lightning can't help me pull my thrown warhammer back, a good bow would come in handy. I believe that Bilabo, Bubabonolis something… could pierce through Onyxia's scales without difficulty."

"What surprises me is that you'd actually think Vereesa would be willing to give it to you," Solia said from nearby.

"Just joking." Grede laughed. "And don't forget — I did my part in getting that sinew too, little green dragon. What's wrong with borrowing it for a bit? You don't think your black aunt's sinew and bone are that easy to break, do you?"

"Um." Solia pursed her lips uncomfortably and hugged her knees, scooting aside. Still, there was no refuting what Grede said.

Had he not charged headfirst into that fissure of flame at the critical moment, if Onyxia had broken free of Krasus's magic and escaped back to Dustwallow Marsh or Blackrock Spire intact, then Jace absolutely wouldn't have dared make plans to go to Draenor, and wouldn't have dared bring his parents to Stormwind either. Who knew whether Onyxia might come back and cause trouble from some other angle at any time.

"Oh right, Jace." Grede said then, "I heard from the King's men a few days ago that Lord Kurtaz of Ironforge came to Stormwind specifically looking for you. Is it that King Magni wants you to talk me into fighting Ragnaros?"

"He came specifically to find me," Jace said.

"To find you for what?" Grede lowered his voice. "Is there anything I can help with? Playing hide-and-seek with those kobolds is really boring. I'd rather go fight the gnolls in Redridge — at least those fellows qualify as proper warriors."

"Wait till I get back to Windstorm and call those two demons," Jace said. "We'll rest one night in Goldshire first, then set out toward Duskwood tomorrow. You'll be responsible for protecting me while I try to bring back the benefactor who saved both our lives. Watch out for the unfriendly living and dead in Deadwind Pass."

"Benefactor?" Grede was taken aback.

"Don't forget who blocked that blast of flame for you at the critical moment," Jace explained.

"Ah, you mean that… I know, I know." Grede nodded. "But you still haven't told me what Kurtaz talked to you about."

Jace really didn't want to tell Grede about that dragon-blood pendant. After all, this Wildhammer warrior who could charge bare-chested into a fissure of flame clearly didn't look like the type who needed a fire-resistance amulet.

And right now the dwarf was staring at him with a curious, greedy look. Although Jace knew he was hoping for some new job to do and a chance to earn some money off the Bronzebeard dwarves.

"Sigh, fine." He finally took out the dragon-blood pendant hanging from its mithril chain. "He came to give me this."

Solia glanced over curiously too. Grede examined it carefully back and forth, then asked, "This isn't made from Onyxia's blood, is it?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Lord Kurtaz told me this amulet would let you dwarves jump into lava and take a bath."

Grede burst out laughing, slapping his knee hard. After a while, seeing that Jace's face was expressionless, he slowly reined in his laughter. "Real or not?"

"That's what he said." Jace asked, "Wanna go try it at the bottom of Blackrock Mountain?"

"Isn't this still asking me to fight Ragnaros?" Grede said. "Did you two plan this together?"

"This is all you dwarves' business, nothing to do with me. You're making it sound like I'm awfully enthusiastic." Jace clenched the amulet. "And back in that troll temple you asked about it like you'd already made up your mind."

"I said 'if' back then!" Grede stressed, his gaze following Jace's hand as he stuffed the amulet back into the bag, muttering under his breath. "Besides, that warhammer hasn't been forged yet. If I agree to them before it's even made, wouldn't that make me look cheap?"

"I'll give you this amulet after you agree to them," Jace said, patting his pouch.

Grede moved his narrowed his eyes, and sat to the side. Solia beside them was trying hard not to laugh.

"Speaking of that warhammer, want to come to Grim Batol with me afterward?" Jace asked. "Krasus has already sent word — he said he's found the exact location of that anvil underground."

"The Cadros Anvil…" The dwarf nodded. "When do we leave?"

"In a few weeks, May," Jace said. "We'll go find the Thorium Brotherhood first, then head to Grim Batol together."

"Damn." Grede muttered. "I really don't want to deal with those Dark Iron dwarves. But if those Dark Iron dwarves want to crawl back into our Wildhammer homeland again, a proper Wildhammer needs to be there watching to keep them from pulling anything. Don't you think?"

"Makes sense," Jace said.
