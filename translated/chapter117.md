## Chapter 117: Setting the Date

"Yes, I've already planned to go back."

Jace's answer was calm enough, but inside he was a roiling sea.

"I need to bring a student to Dalaran at the end of the year," Dushan said. "For an Orcish-language symposium organized by the Kirin Tor. I'm thinking the topics we'll be discussing there align quite well with the subject you chose."

So it really was this. But it had been because of his essay topic?

For some reason, Jace suddenly didn't feel quite as thrilled. Getting this opportunity was just a matter of luck?

He realized he wasn't happy about saving a few dozen silver at all. He'd thought he'd beaten out all those other students who'd been studying Orcish for heaven knows how long, through his own effort and talent.

"I've only been studying Orcish for less than two months, Master," Jace blurted out before he could stop himself.

Damn it, he cursed inwardly.

Just say yes, won't you? Are you really going to throw away the money for the sake of your pride?

Dushan nodded, glanced at his notebook, and said, "But your level is unquestionably the best in this class, Jace Sesso. I think sending you to Dalaran would certainly demonstrate our Stormwind Kingdom's Orcish-language research… You don't want to go?"

"I do." Jace said. "Of course I want to go, Master." The best in the class? Jace snickered to himself. Could've just told me I was this good from the start.

"Then I'll put your name forward, then," Dushan said. "When are you planning to head back to Lordaeron? The symposium is being held before Winter Veil — the news I received says around the tenth or eleventh of December. You know how long it takes from Stormwind Harbor to Southshore, don't you?"

"About half a month," Jace said.

Dushan nodded. "Yes, about half a month, Jace. So you'd want to set out within the last ten days — that gives you a more comfortable margin, since there's still a ways to go from Southshore to Dalaran."

"I need to ask the friend I'm going back with and settle on a time," Jace said.

"How many friends?"

"Just one."

Dushan stroked his rough chin and say, "Let me know as soon as you've discussed it. Who knows, I might be able to secure two spots from Archmage Andormas! In any case, the travel budget to Dalaran is only so much — might as well use it. Have your friend give me his details — preferably on a piece of paper. Name… and race, if your friend isn't human."

"Thank you so much, Teacher."

"Don't thank me — thank our Archmage Andormas," Dushan said with a smile. "After all, I'm only going because he's the one footing the bill!"

"Master Dushan, are you going with us?" Jace asked.

"No," Dushan said. "I'm submitting the grades tomorrow and setting out three days later. My itinerary was arranged long ago, since I need to make a stop at Durnholde Keep and spend some time there. We'll rendezvous in Dalaran."

That afternoon, after finishing the exam, he sprinted through the drizzle all the way to the Dwarf District, arrived at Grede's door, and pounded on it — no answer. He bolted straight for the Pig and Whistle Inn.

He pushed inside and threw back his hood, and sure enough there was Grede, sitting in the corner drinking.

He dropped into the seat and slapped the table. "Hey, guess what, Grede."

"You're marrying an elf?" Grede asked flatly.

"What elf?" Jace said. "Guess again."

"You punched a red dragon to death?"

"Tch, what dragon?! Guess again!"

"You're inheriting a noble title?"

"You're really boring, Grede."

"You're the one who told me to guess." Grede grinned and said, "Go on, then. What did you come here to brag about this time?"

"Forget it." Jace stood up. "I thought you'd be grateful if I scored you a free boat ticket."

"Hey!" Grede vaulted over the table and threw his arms around Jace. "Don't leave… my elder brother of seventeen years, I need it, I really need this free boat ticket! I'm so grateful."

"That's more like it." Jace said. "Buy me a pot of wine! Let's talk over when we're setting out."

Grede called over the barmaid, then leaned in close and asked in a low voice, "So you passed that Orcish exam of yours?"

"Of course," Jace said. "I'm someone who's actually conversed with a native speaker — way better than those types who can only write but not speak."

"Don't bring up that native speaker," Grede said, shaking his head. "But I'll hand it to you — the people at the Wizard Sanctum really are loaded!"

"Did you find out? How much is the ticket?" Jace asked.

"Fifty-eight silver per person, if we book passage on the seventeenth aboard the great Baradin Conqueror," Grede said. "It's a wartime troop transport that the Steamwheedle Cartel bought and refitted for a while. It's a merchant ship now, but it might be even slower than the one we came here on."

"The great Baradin Conqueror — sounds impressive," Jace said.

"It is impressive," Grede said. "But also old and beat to hell. If the seas are good, we should reach Southshore sometime between the first and third of December."

"I have to be in Dalaran before the tenth of December," Jace said. "When are we heading to the Hinterlands?"

"No rush to discuss that now, Jace," Grede said. "Winter Veil lasts for many days. No need for me to show up at Aerie Peak on the very first day, like I can't stand to be apart from them."

"All right," Jace said. "Are there other options, then? I mean for ships."

"This is the only passenger-friendly ship with a suitable schedule," Grede said helplessly. "The rest are either too late, too expensive, or fully booked. You know how it is right now with Winter Veil — too many people trying to go home."

"Tickets this expensive and still that many people going home?" Jace said with a scornful snort. "Stormwind's got plenty of rich folks, huh."

"A lot of people work their passage on the ships to cover the fare," Grede said. "I was going to ask if there was an opportunity like that, but they didn't leave me any opening. Sigh. I never thought about going home before — this is the first time I've asked this year, and I've got no experience at all. We got taken for a ride. If you hadn't figured out a way to get us free tickets, this trip would've cost us a fortune. Might've had to sell some belongings, or take on one hell of a job just to afford the fare. Come to think of it, a few days ago I was actually thinking about going after that gnoll chieftain."

The moment Grede said that, Jace was reminded of what Marin had said about more mercenaries getting themselves killed at Hogger's hands.

"There'll be plenty of chances later."

"Plenty of chances to do what?" the dwarf asked.

"To kill that gnoll, obviously!"

Grede's eyes went wide. "I was just talking! You're serious."

Jace said, "By the time we get back from Dalaran, the bounty might've gone up again. You get a few brothers from the fighters' guild to back you up, and I don't believe we can't take that thing down."

Grede took a drink and slowly nodded. "That day I crossed blades with that tall monster, I got the feeling it really wasn't impossible to take him down. I think the biggest problem is actually his lackeys."

Jace said, "In Dalaran, we might find a way to cure whatever aftereffects you've got. By the time you come back, you'll be able to fight them at full strength."

Grede stared at the rim of his half-raised mug and said, "I hope so."
