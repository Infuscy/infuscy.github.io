## Chapter 1172: Arcane Glow Deep in the Swamp

Heading into the Swamp of Sorrows to find the Lost Ones meant they definitely couldn't bring Kallrez along.

The very thing that had caused the Lost Ones to degenerate from tall, sturdy draenei into what they were now was demons. And Kallrez couldn't turn invisible like Morophil, nor could he shift into a more suitable form. If the Lost Ones saw him, it would only traumatize them further — there'd be no way they'd cooperate with Jace and the group.

So he could only stay behind in this valley for now, continuing to hole up with the ogres.

Somel carried the group away from the forest, flying north for a stretch until they were far from Karazhan's shroud of darkness, then pressing east into open hill country before finally flying into the swamp.

Grede looked down at the dung-yellow muck and the twisted, gnarled wetland trees — the kind of foliage and dense canopies that looked almost *viscous* — and shook his head.

"Why does it feel like this place has gotten even more goddamn fucked since we were here a few months back?" he said.

"I feel the same way, Grede." The green dragon tilted her head and answered in a hoarse voice. "Endless darkness and evil are devouring this swamp. Slowly, but they truly have not stopped."

Wynneza asked, "Didn't we already destroy Hakkar's avatar and drive the trolls out of that temple? Why hasn't this swamp been saved?"

"The corruption of the Swamp of Sorrows didn't come from Hakkar," Jace said. "Or at least, a hundred miles from the Pool of Tears, Hakkar's influence was already quite thin."

"I've read some survey records in the Stormwind library. Before Deadwind Pass was destroyed by Karazhan, adventurers and hunters from the villages and towns near Karazhan had approached the Pool of Tears and described sightings of jungle trolls. Back then they didn't think of that temple as a place that worshipped Hakkar — they figured it was probably just another Gurubashi village. At least from their records, the Swamp of Sorrows as a whole was still fairly normal back then..."

"So it was actually the Dark Portal that turned this place into what it is now?" Grede said quietly. "But the Dark Portal only appeared there less than thirteen years ago."

"The Expedition closed the Dark Portal from the other side," Wynneza said. "Isn't that enough to stop this swamp from being corrupted any further?"

"Think about what the Frostwolf chieftain told us back in Alterac, Wen," Jace reminded her. "The Dark Portal turned the jungle on the other side into scorched earth during its construction. Closing it didn't change that."

The group fell silent for a moment. Grede couldn't help asking, "What's this about a Frostwolf chieftain?"

Jace filled Grede in on his and Wynneza's travels in the Alterac Mountains, then talked about the Stratholme investigation, and from there the trip to the great cemetery...

When he got to the part about how Dar'Khan and his accomplices had been waiting outside when he came out of that cemetery, Grede was so furious he practically launched himself upward, nearly sliding right off the dragon's back.

"You didn't kill them all?!" Grede clenched his fists and gritted his teeth. "If I'd been there, not a single one of them would've been left with a whole corpse!"

"I absolutely believe that," Jace said with a slight smile. "Though if you'd been there, keeping a whole corpse would've been the harder option."

"He knows me well." Grede pointed at Jace and turned to Wynneza and Morophil. "He understands exactly how I'd make those elves feel the power of a Wildhammer dwarf — they'd learn this warhammer can shatter the heavens, and smashing their skulls and ribcages would be just as easy!"

"But you took on four of them by yourself." The dwarf turned back and wagged a finger. "I know Dar'Khan Drathir. Even before I met you, I'd heard his name..."

"Morophil helped, of course," Jace said.

The dwarf raised an eyebrow, looking at the smiling succubus. "Then I underestimated your loyalty to Jace Sesso."

"You see? Even a dwarf who doesn't understand demons can tell how deeply loyal I am to you, master," Morophil said with a wounded tone.

Jace shot her a look. Wynneza, sitting beside him, cleared her throat and said, "We're already deep into the swamp. Where to next, Sola? Do you remember where we encountered that Lost One last time?"

"We haven't reached Stonard yet," the green dragon replied. "That creature was farther east."

"Do you remember which direction he fled, Wynneza?" Morophil raised an arm and pointed northeast. "He ran north. Maybe we should turn northeast from here — that might make it easier to find where the Lost Ones are hiding."

"I remember, that's right," Wynneza said.

The Lost Ones' village was indeed in the northern part of the swamp. Jace still had some memory of it, so he nodded in agreement. Having heard everyone's input, the green dragon banked slightly and flew toward the northeast.

The farther they got from the Dark Portal to the south, the more the state of the swamp forest visibly improved. Still, they couldn't fly too far north — if they kept going, they'd leave the Swamp of Sorrows, reach the Redridge Mountains, and fly into the primeval forest where gnoll tribes and exiled Bloodmaul orcs were hiding.

The Lost Ones no longer had the strength to compete with orcs and gnoll for territory. Their numbers were few and their bodies weak — they could only prey on innocent travelers, so charging farther north would've been asking for trouble.

They spent the night in a relatively dry patch of woodland among the swamps. The next day, Jace directed Somel to circle back and forth over a stretch of hills in the northern swamp, searching for Lost Ones that might be hiding in the deep valleys.

Still, Jace didn't have much hope for this area. The last time he'd come here with those mercenaries, they'd found a dead doomguard corpse in this very spot and made a bundle off it. He worried the Lost Ones might've fled this far away out of fear of demons escaping from near the Dark Portal.

But just as he was gazing out toward the swamp forest to the east, picking out the next search area, Wynneza noticed something.

"Sola, fly a little more steadily. I think I heard something," the ranger said softly.

The green dragon immediately leveled her wings and glided for a stretch. Wynneza stood up, squinted toward the northwest for a moment, then pointed at a ravine and said, "Over there. I'm sensing an unusual surge of arcane energy."

Grede also leaned over in that direction for a moment, then asked, "Arcane energy? You mean there's a mage?"

"Possibly," Wynneza said.

"Can those little monsters cast spells?" Grede turned to Jace.

"Maybe," Jace said. "Even with their degeneration, they might've retained the ability to cast. Don't forget — they were originally eredar, after all."

Wynneza lay down and patted the dragon's back. "Head over there, Sola. Fly lower."

Somel slowly swayed her long neck, then turned and flew toward the direction the ranger had pointed.

She approached a ravine shrouded by dense trees. They were already close to leaving the swamp, and Jace was starting to suspect Wynneza had detected some kind of outpost set up by the Kirin Tor or the Wizard Sanctum.

After they got closer, though, Wynneza became less certain. She looked back and forth for a while but couldn't see any visible source of magical power.

"I could sense arcane energy from far away, but up close it's gotten fuzzy. Damn it."

"Should we go down and take a look?" Grede asked. "Can't see a thing from up here."

"Let's check it out. We've been searching all day with nothing to show for it anyway," Jace said.

Hearing Jace's words, Somel descended again, blew a breath to push aside some of the tangled branches, and slowly settled into the dense trees.

From the sky it had looked like an unending, rolling dark green, but once they were inside it was so deep and gloomy that nothing was clearly visible.

"Lady Wynneza's senses are correct — there is indeed a weak arcane energy source nearby," the imp popped his head out to say. "And this is where the professional tools come in, master," Morophil reminded him.

Jace nodded and raised his staff. "We'll have to let the felhound do the work."

Whether it was a reclusive mage hiding in the deep forest, or a demon that had escaped the Alliance's and mercenaries' repeated sweeps — whatever it was, dragging it out from the depths of the swamp wouldn't be a bad thing.

After the felhound darted out, Jace had barely given its orders before it bolted northwest with almost no hesitation.

Wynneza watched the felhound charge off and bit her lip reluctantly. "It's hard to admit that this demon's sensitivity to arcane energy far surpasses ours."

"Before this world was even born, the ereduins — the Annihilans — trained these magical beasts to hunt for arcane sources in the Twisting Nether, Lady Wynneza," Morophil said with a smile. "There's really no need to feel inferior over that."

Wynneza smiled and nodded, quickening her pace to keep up with the felhound.

The felhound led the group into a deep, forested ravine. Shrouded by dense thickets and sitting in a low-lying area, it felt like a damp, hidden pocket plane. Without the felhound leading them, just finding the entrance would've been difficult.

Just as they approached the entrance, Jace caught a glimpse of a faint purple glow from within.

At first he thought it was an illusion, but when he spotted that faint light again through the branches, he immediately recognized what it was.

"Draenei crystal," Wynneza said in surprise. "There are draenei crystals in here too."

"Morophil, hide," Jace said, looking at the succubus. Then he began chanting a banishment spell at the felhound.
