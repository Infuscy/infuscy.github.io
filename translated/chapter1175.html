Chapter 1175: Where the Crystal Points

The Broken's dwelling was crude — it looked like it had been built not long ago, yet gave the impression it might collapse at any moment.

Many tools, weapons, and articles of clothing that clearly didn't belong to Azeroth were sized and proportioned all wrong for the Broken. It was easy to guess that these were items created and used by true draenei.

Of course, Jace wasn't trying to imply that the Broken had stolen anything from the draenei. Though that possibility existed, the truth was probably that the horrific "degeneration" from draenei to Broken might have occurred in just a few short years.

They had to deal with orc pursuit while simultaneously adapting to drastic changes in the environment, leaving no time to shift their mindset. Of course, part of it was also an attachment to their former identity.

From the way the Broken spoke, it was clear that most of them had retained their past memories. But the cities and culture the draenei had created in Draenor were brilliant and magnificent. If they had been the ones to come here and build this dwelling, it definitely wouldn't have looked like something built by miniature ogres.

So perhaps it was precisely because the change had been so abrupt that the Broken hadn't even been able to adapt to their new bodies. Whether it was the way they held their crossbows or the way they'd reacted to Sola's thorn magic earlier, everything had been clumsy and stiff — to say nothing of building a house like this.

Inside the dwelling, in a roughly excavated underground cave, Jace saw the Broken they had mentioned earlier — the one who slept without waking, Magtheridon.

His appearance was extremely frail. His face was sunken, his body emaciated, covered only by a silver-white robe serving as a blanket, lying on a cold stone bed surrounded by draenei.

This bed appeared to have been modified by magic — probably used by these Broken to restore their bodies and cure diseases — and its energy source was the mana crystals they'd brought over from the other side of the Dark Portal.

The Broken clearly didn't appreciate Jace observing Magtheridon and the purple crystals around him for so long, and Jace had no choice but to pull his gaze back.

So these crystals could help them resist the erosion of evil energy, and even heal part of the corruption. That was probably why Noburu had said earlier that they had to rely on these mana crystals to carry on.

At that moment, Noburu approached Jace carrying a small gray leather pouch and said, "Take this."

Jace took it and opened it to look. Inside lay a rather small, pale-purple draenei crystal fragment — about the size of a pendant on a necklace, something that could be held steady in the palm of the hand.

Judging from the surface of this fragment, there were no visible signs of damage. But the mana energy contained within was extremely weak compared to the coordinate record carried by that Shadow Council orc.

"Where exactly does this coordinate teleport to?" Jace asked.

"A draenei holy site," Noburu said. "A place called Auchindoun."

Auchindoun? Jace was momentarily stunned.

He of course knew Auchindoun was the draenei sacred mausoleum, where their greatest sages and warriors lay in eternal rest, and which itself was a holy structure of unimaginably vast scale.

The problem was that Auchindoun had been destroyed long before Ner'zhul used the three artifacts to shatter all of Draenor and flee into the Twisting Nether.

Tarothor — the Teron Gorefiend who would later die at the hands of Jace and Wynneza — had presided over a summoning ritual within Auchindoun at that time, attempting to use the power of an otherworldly being to help the Horde attack the draenei army making its last stand at Auchindoun.

But the summoned creature had gone even more "thorough" than Tarothor's plan. The massive shockwave from its appearance alone destroyed the entire Auchindoun mausoleum, killing more than ninety percent of the living things within and turning the forest around Auchindoun into a gray wasteland of death... achieving the Shadow Council's goal in a way none of its members had anticipated.

Teleporting to a place like that — what difference was there between that and teleporting straight into a Burning Legion fortress?

Still, Jace clearly remembered hearing at Nethergarde Keep that the Sons of Lothar army had captured Auchindoun. And he also remembered the story of Danas Trollbane of Stromgarde killing the Bloodmaul chieftain Kilrogg Deadeye within Auchindoun.

Although in the game Auchindoun had already been occupied in pieces by the Shadow Council and various other Outland factions, that had been nearly twenty years later, when the Dark Portal had been reopened by the doomlord Kazzak, Illidan had led his army to defeat Magtheridon and proclaim himself King of Outland. The shattered Outland of that time was vastly different from today.

Still, even if the Alliance had already captured Auchindoun, it was still an extremely eerie and terrifying place. Not to mention that mysterious otherworldly entity... the bizarre sound-elemental being known as Murmur still existed in the depths of that labyrinthine mausoleum.

Considering all this, Jace couldn't help asking one more question: "I've heard Auchindoun has been through multiple wars. Won't the situation there be chaotic?"

"This coordinate isn't for teleporting directly into the depths of the holy site, but to a village outside the main gate," Noburu said. "But I don't know the situation there at present."

If it was outside Auchindoun, then the situation was much better. If they could get away from the field of death created by the Auchindoun explosion, they might even find the Expedition fortress Alleria had established nearby.

Jace felt much more at ease and glanced at Wynneza beside him. She, on the other hand, was frowning tightly, still seeming somewhat worried about the situation at Auchindoun.

And Noburu, standing before them, seemed to have been pulled into past memories by the matter of Auchindoun. He fell silent, and his mood sank all at once.

After a while, he raised his head and stared at the coordinate in Jace's hand and said, "This is our last hope of returning to Draenor. If you find a way to activate it but decide to abandon us... with the strength of us refugees as we are now, there's little we can do."

"I won't do that," Jace said.

"Of course you won't." Noburu suddenly gritted his teeth, and Jace noticed that this Broken's teeth were a jumbled mess, like a pile of crushed bones crammed together, ugly enough to turn the stomach.

He suddenly grabbed Jace's shoulder, his face twisted savagely, and said, "Because if you really do that, I will curse you — in the name of all things — I will curse you to perish in that destroyed prison, Jace of Stormwind... and all the rest of you, every one of you!"

"Let him go."

The Druidic Call's blade, patterned with tree grain, was already pressed against his neck. Noburu was stunned for a moment, then recoiled as if electrocuted, making two muffled sounds and coughing in pain.

The movements of the several Broken beside them were no slower than Wynneza's right next to them. They seemed to have trained for this — they rushed forward in unison to pin their leader to the ground while forcing a bottle of some indeterminate potion down his throat.

After drinking the potion, Noburu was clearly much calmer. He coughed several more times and said, "I am no longer who I once was, just like those kin hiding in the muck and sewage. No matter how noble the soul and will we once possessed, in the end we cannot exist independently of these bodies twisted by demonic power."

Jace straightened his cloak and said, "Perhaps you really should leave this place, Noburu. Head south to Stonard — that's Alliance military territory. I imagine they'd be willing to take you farther away, just as my friend said, far from the Dark Portal's ongoing corruption."

"That way, once I activate this coordinate, I can notify you more quickly," Jace said, raising his head. "This place took even a skilled Quel'dorei ranger quite a bit of effort to find you. I'm not sure I'd be able to find you again if I came back."

Noburu hesitated for a moment, then asked, "Won't humans see us as enemies?"

"The Alliance doesn't even see those Lost Ones as enemies," Wynneza said. "Every soldier who's fought at the Dark Portal knows those Lost Ones are pitiful creatures enslaved by orcs in another world. As long as they don't actively rob our supply caravans, the Alliance won't launch operations to clear them out."

"You are enemies of the orcs, and you're willing to learn our languages, while also not actively causing destruction and chaos, and you're wholeheartedly determined to return home. There's no reason for us to bear you any ill will."

Noburu nodded and said, "The roving mercenaries in the forest who cooperate with orcs make us afraid to initiate contact with you. After all, there are only a handful of us left. If anything goes wrong, it's the risk of total extinction. And on the Draenor side... nobody knows what it looks like now."

"Even so, you're still willing to go back?" Grede asked.

"Yes," Noburu said. "We, like you, have things on the other side we cannot bring ourselves to abandon." When the leader of the Broken said this, Grede was left speechless.

He looked at Wynneza, then at Jace. Wynneza looked back at the dwarf with an apologetic expression, and the dwarf merely curled his beard and smiled.

Noburu looked back at the other Broken soldiers. They didn't converse or exchange opinions. Finally, Noburu glanced once more at the frail sage lying on the bed, sighed, and said, "We cannot go to Alliance territory. I'm sorry."

"I understand your kindness, Jace, and I understand this choice might be better. But this is a decision of too great a significance, and our leader is still sleeping."

"Are you sure you can protect yourselves?" Jace asked.

"Seizing the teleportation coordinate is the most important goal of our kin," Noburu said. "If they realize the teleportation coordinate is no longer in our possession, perhaps they'll calm down and find something else to do. If things ultimately don't go smoothly, and our fate really is to return to the depths of this dark swamp, then it means the Holy Light has truly forsaken us... and I'll have nothing more to say."

"Since this is your choice, I won't force the issue," Jace said with a nod. "Then we'll take our leave."

"May the road ahead treat you kindly," Noburu said respectfully.
