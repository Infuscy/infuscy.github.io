## Chapter 1177: The Unavoidable Key Figure

Sola couldn't fly directly over the walls in her dragon form. In the years since Onyxia's night raid on Stormwind, it was best not to touch the raw nerves of Stormwind's citizens in broad daylight.

What's more, even here, distinguishing the color of a dragon flying high in the sky wasn't so easy on a clear, sunny day. So every time Sola carried the group near Stormwind, she did her best to avoid all watchtowers and towns, landing in the forest before they walked into the city.

Unfortunately, today it started raining before they reached the city gates, so the group pulled up their hoods and ran to take shelter in a tavern near the gates by the Trade District.

Since the passersby were all taking shelter from the rain here just like them, quite a few people had gathered in the tavern. The floor was wet and covered in dirt, and the whole place reeked of damp, sour sweat.

A few years ago, when Jace and Grede had set out from here to go to Raven Hill looking for Grave Moss, this tavern hadn't even had a name. Now it had an old sign hanging by the door reading "The Broken Copper" — quite fitting for the overall character of this tavern.

Here, Jace was cloaked, clutching the pouch the Broken had given him containing the fragment, silently blending into that very character.

Perhaps it was because this trip hadn't gone as smoothly as imagined. Perhaps it was because everyone had been rained on all the way from Elwynn Forest to the Valley of Heroes. Sola, Wynneza, and Grede were all sitting quietly, saying much of anything.

"Whatever we do next, at least tell the barkeep what we plan to drink first." Grede cleared his throat, raised his hand high, and shouted toward the bar a couple of times.

His shouting livened the atmosphere a bit. Sola said softly, "I wonder when Lady Marin will be able to come to Stormwind..."

"Frankly, she's not studying the school of Transmutation in Dalaran," Jace said quietly, weighing the leather pouch in his hand. "It's very likely that even if she comes to Stormwind and has no objections to our plan, she'd have a hard time helping us repair this coordinate."

"She's not from the school of Illusion either, Jace," Wynneza said. "But she agreed to help us 'decorate' Eldre'Thassil, didn't she? I got the impression she was quite confident."

"I don't really know Lady Marin," Grede said, looking at Jace. "She's your teacher's daughter, right?"

"Yes," Jace said.

Grede looked back at the elf and said, "But you seem to know her quite well — especially since even Jace isn't sure whether she can or can't..."

"Of course I know her better than you," Wynneza said. "I've been in contact with her because of monitoring Dar'Khan, and I've met her many times in Dalaran. Otherwise, how would I have known Dar'Khan went to Karazhan?"

After saying this, she looked at Jace with a hint of guilt and sighed. "Of course, that monitoring could hardly be called successful, since I had no idea he was planning to teleport so many mages and rangers to Winterspring, and then everything that followed. Ever since we found what we were looking for in Winterspring, I let all my surveillance in Dalaran slack off at once. That was my mistake."

"Even counting Morophil, I'm the member of this team most familiar with the arcane," she said, looking at the people at the table. "So I know exactly how talented Amy is. Even setting aside my own assessment... if she weren't outstanding enough, Archmaster Antonidas wouldn't have allowed her to assist in tutoring the princess of Kul Tiras."

"Fine, when it comes to arcane matters, the elf has the final say. I won't argue with you." Grede raised his hands in surrender. Jace could tell that the reasons Wynneza was giving were actually secondary.

Despite repeatedly emphasizing that she wouldn't be so rash as to want to go to Draenor immediately, the closer she actually got to that teleportation gate, the less calm Wynneza could be.

For this, she could trust a demon lord several stories tall. Of course she was also willing to place her hopes on Amy, since Amy was already the most trustworthy Kirin Tor member and mage there was.

"So why don't we just go straight to Antonidas to repair this thing?" the dwarf said, pointing at the fragment in Jace's hand. "According to what that Broken said, give him that orc warlock's coordinate and have him use it as reference to repair this fragment... He's the lord-mage and headmaster of Dalaran!"

"You might as well walk straight into the Violet Hold yourself," Wynneza said. "Save all of us some time."

"We just won't tell him what this thing is," Grede said.

Jace said quietly, "The problem right now is we don't know whether the Kirin Tor even possesses teleportation coordinates to the other side. We can't take that risk. If they do, then precisely because Antonidas is the leader of Dalaran, he'd probably be able to see through this at a glance and guess what we're planning on the spot."

"Fine." Grede nodded.

Actually, Jace had a feeling the Kirin Tor might truly hold teleportation coordinates to Draenor.

After Dalaran's destruction, Illidan Stormrage had most likely obtained a teleportation coordinate in Dalaran, which allowed him to easily open a portal to Outland using the coordinate, then leave Azeroth with the blood elves and naga.

Even if Illidan had teleported by his own methods, before the Dark Portal was destroyed, the Expedition's gryphon riders had recovered many stolen Dalaran treasures, including the Book of Medivh — and among them were very likely the Draenor teleportation coordinates sent back by the Sons of Lothar.

"Regardless, I must return to Quel'Thalas as soon as possible, Jace," Wynneza said. "I need to brief my sister on everything that's happened during this time."

"You're going to take that bow back to show off, aren't you?" Grede asked with a sly grin.

"You think everyone's like you?" Wynneza shot him a look and continued. "I also need to arrange the defense work in Elegos Forest before we begin our plan. I can already imagine that Halduron Brightwing fellow sitting in his chair being all passive-aggressive."

"Remember to write to me once Amy arrives in Stormwind, Jace," she added.

Jace smiled and said, "All right. I'll ask a mage to send the letter to you."

"Honestly, I don't really like these moments where we can't take the initiative and control the direction ourselves," Grede said, tracing circles on the table with his finger. "So in other words, there's nothing to do but wait? And what we're waiting for isn't even something with great odds of..."

"I actually prefer moments like this a bit more," Jace said, tilting his head back and rolling his neck. "We can rest a little and prepare for what's coming next. And it's not like we have nothing to do either — the two of us and Sola still need to take the Thorium Brotherhood people to your hometown later, Grede."

"Of course I remember," Grede said impatiently, scratching the back of his head. "But I think more importantly, Sola needs to be prepared, don't you think?"

"Sola? I'd nearly forgotten about you since you haven't said a word." The dwarf turned to look at Sola beside him and found her sitting in thought, head lowered.

"She's asleep," Jace said, looking at the golden-haired young woman leaning silently against the bench. "She's been flying back and forth so many times..."

"...Trust me, Grede, flying around in a hellhole like Deadwind Pass is no easy work. Let her rest."

"Fine." The dwarf nodded.

Wynneza looked at the green dragon and said, "After going to Ashenvale with you, Sola has grown a lot. Although for a Dream-born who's older than both my older sisters... that statement might sound a bit strange. Remember when those Broken suddenly wanted to attack over the teleportation coordinate — Sola's magic settled it for us right away, without bloodshed, without anyone losing their life."

She looked at Grede and asked, "I wonder if it's because you, a dwarf, can't sense any danger at all, forcing her to shoulder the responsibility?"

"What's that got to do with me?" Grede said with a "hah." "Besides, you didn't sense those Broken lying in ambush around us either."

This left Wynneza completely speechless. Jace explained, "The Broken are different from the Lost Ones, Grede. Don't underestimate them. Setting aside their physical condition, these people aren't that different spiritually from the eredar. They're quite skilled at using all sorts of strange magical forces to help themselves, whether it's concealing their tracks or fighting. So we need to be prepared in this regard before going over there — not all of that group are this friendly and neutral."

"I sensed that," Grede said, pointing at his head. "Those fellows are more than a little mentally unstable."

"About going over there... there's another very important issue, Jace," Wynneza asked quietly. "Are you planning to tell the King about this? I don't know about your relationship with King Varian, how much he trusts you. But I feel like if we're going to Draenor, we probably can't get around him. Eldre'Thassil is in his kingdom, and you're his court mage — if you're absent for too long..."

"I'm also considering how to explain this to him," Jace said. "But I think he probably wants to make contact with the Sons of Lothar even more than you or I do."

"You mean because of the black dragon," Wynneza said.

"Yes," Jace said. "Onyxia caused too much chaos, so Varian is bent on attacking Blackrock Spire — partly to avenge the suffering inflicted on the Queen, and more importantly, to stabilize the kingdom's northern border, which would also draw a definitive end to the Orc Wars."

He paused, then continued, "But relying on the army of the Kingdom of Stormwind alone probably isn't enough to launch a siege under the current circumstances. Aside from Ironforge, the other kingdoms aren't directly threatened by Blackrock Spire and don't have as strong an incentive to help. I'm not optimistic he'll get any promises at the Lordaeron Alliance conference. So he might be even more eager than we are for the Sons of Lothar to return, because the Sons of Lothar would definitely be willing to help him attack the remaining orcs. And right now, if there's anyone in this kingdom best suited to go to Draenor to find them..."

"Then it's you and no one else," Grede said.

Jace folded his arms and raised an eyebrow. "More or less. But specifically what he's thinking, I still need to find a chance to go to the palace and see how things stand."

"I hope he's willing to support us," Wynneza said, resting her chin on her hand on the table. "As a king, even without any material support at all it would be enough — all our actions would be much safer."
