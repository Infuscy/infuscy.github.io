## Chapter 1178: The Festival of the Garden Draws Near

Between now and the Festival of the Garden, Jace had gotten a bit of time to rest.

After sending Wynneza back to Quel'Thalas, Jace took Grede to visit his parents as a guest.

What surprised him somewhat was that Grede and his old man didn't really get along. The more they talked, the more the conversation tended to veer toward the conflicts and misunderstandings between the Dun Hold soldiers and the Wildhammer warriors, and their exchanges often became pointed, forcing Jace to step in and smooth things over again and again.

But really, he'd been too optimistic. His father, as a Lordaeron veteran, didn't have a particularly good attitude even toward his Alterac neighbors — never mind a Wildhammer dwarf. That earlier talk about wanting to meet this dwarf warrior had probably just been a nice thing to say to his son.

Before the Dark Portal, the Wildhammer and the humans of the surrounding nations had been quite suspicious of each other.Vick had enlisted and served in exactly that kind of environment, so it was naturally hard for him to let go of his prejudices.

And Grede's racial prejudice was even more serious. Come to think of it, his attitude toward all humans aside from Jace was fairly lukewarm.

Still, the two did share common ground on how to guard against troll traps in the forest — they finally managed to find a topic they could talk about, albeit under Jace's management. If not for that, Henny was afraid the two of them would've ended up fighting by the canal after all.

Over the next few days, he accompanied his parents on a stroll through Old Town and the Dwarf District, and took them on a tour of Goldshire.

Although the Orc Wars had passed by now and Elwynn Forest had generally cast off the shadow of war — there hadn't been any reports of orc sightings from Moonbrook to Goldshire, and from there all the way to Lakeshire and Stonewatch, for a long time now.

Still, Jace reminded them that unless it was something extremely important, it was best not to leave Stormwind, and especially not to travel alone. Whether it were the kobolds that had been making a comeback recently, or the lone gnolls still prowling Elwynn Forest — both were still dangerous.

Jace knew that with his father's fighting ability in his youth, giving him a sword to deal with a few kobolds or even gnolls shouldn't be a problem. But as the saying goes, many of those who drown are the ones who can swim. Jace also worried that his father's personality might cause him to overlook the dangers of this area, so he warned them as thoroughly as he could.

Fortunately, althoughVick always liked to brag and was always yelling about wanting to duel some famous figure or other, he wasn't the type to actually go looking for trouble for no reason — otherwise he wouldn't have spent these years safely and peacefully in Tirisfal with orcs and gnoll frequenting the area.

The biggest problem right now, ironically, was Alna. Two weeks had passed, and Jace felt like this Alterac mare had gotten a whole circle fatter. It was as if his father wasn't keeping Alna as a mount to ride, but feeding her like a granddaughter.

But no matter what little habits they couldn't get used to,Vick and Henny were ultimately slowly adapting to life in Stormwind. It was plain to see they quite liked this place.

The weather was getting warmer. The Festival of the Garden hadn't arrived yet, but Stormwind already had a festive atmosphere.

Every household had already hung up wreaths, neighbors and relatives were exchanging holiday pastries and boiled eggs, and the more careful ones would paint patterns on them to give to the children.

The Festival of the Garden wasn't very popular in Tirisfal, so Jace's parents weren't familiar with the holiday customs here. Having learned about such a festival just these past couple of days, they still had to ask around and learn on the spot.

When they asked Jace about it, it turned out that aside from the painted eggs, Jace didn't know much either, and got laughed at by his parents for having lived in Stormwind all these years for nothing.

Still, Jace wasn't particularly enthusiastic about holiday affairs. He'd been preoccupied with the matter of summoning a voidwalker at the Slaughtered Lamb.

Voidwalkers weren't demons — that much was certain. But their connection to shadow, and to that mysterious, unknown Void plane, made Jace consistently somewhat nervous about this summoning ritual.

So, after getting the spell scroll for summoning a voidwalker and the dagger-circle's compiled notes and precautions from Erland, Jace had spent this whole time studying these materials whenever he wasn't with his parents or drinking with Grede and Sola.

That day, when he arrived at the gathering in the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb, Galders, Ursula, Erland, and Sulenna were all already seated there.

Ursula was discussing something with Galders. When she appeared Jace at the entrance, she immediately waved.

"Jace!" The alchemist walked over quickly and gave Jace a hug. "I heard you came back a while ago but I was out — I was upset for days."

"Could it be that you had some hard-to-handle work waiting for me to walk right into it, and you missed your chance?" Jace asked.

Ursula frowned and put her hands on her hips. "You're so cold. Can't I care whether my little brother's alive or dead?" Jace rubbed his chin and said, "I'm right here in one piece, aren't I?"

Ursula smiled. A burst of hurried footsteps suddenly came from the basement entrance. "Jace is here? Jace, goodness, I've been looking for you these past couple of days..."

Jaylene walked quickly from the passageway in the back and circled around in front of Jace. "Tell me — how exactly did you purify that demon cloth? How were those Kalimdor elves willing to help you?"

Jace cleared his throat and said, "How was the quality of that cloth?"

"Even better than I imagined," Jaylene said, suppressing her excitement and doing her best to appear calm. "I feel like what you left me seems like even more cloth than what I gave you at the time?"

"I'm not sure how my friend managed it either," Jace said with a smile.

"Speaking of which," Jaylene leaned in a little and asked, "Could you introduce me to that friend of yours?"

Jace pressed his lips together. Galders glanced over and said to Jaylene, "Forget the agreement between us all? Don't go digging into each other's channels, Jaylene. If Jace could tell you, he would."

"Ah, right, right. Forgive my ill-timed curiosity, Jace," Jaylene lowered her head apologetically.

"It's all right, I understand," Jace said with a smile. "So, can you start making that dream robe of yours now?"

"The work on the robe has long since started — as if she could restrain herself," Galders said with a laugh.

"Of course!" Jaylene rubbed her hands together and looked at the two of them. "In about two more days, the first robe will be finished, Jace. But the first one is just an experiment to test the compatibility of enchantment and materials, because after actually getting my hands on this cloth you brought back, I discovered I'm still not familiar enough with its properties. So I'll make an experimental robe first. The second one I'll use the highest-quality materials I've prepared — and that one's yours. At the earliest, it'll be done in less than two weeks."

"The more you say, the more I look forward to it," Jace said. "The robe you gave me before is still intact... even after all these battles and dangers, it's only a bit damaged at the collar and cuffs."

"If possible, once you have the new robe, you could give that one back to me," Jaylene said expectantly. "Studying a battle robe that's been through countless fights is important work for a magical tailor too... not to mention what you're giving me is the 'Robe of Jace'!"

"Of course."

Though he appeared perfectly calm on the surface, Jaylene's words still made Jace's heart tremble slightly. A robe named after him?

What's more, none of the members of the Dagger Circle thought there was anything strange about it — as if the robe Jace wore was truly some kind of rare treasure.

Still, a robe that had been through Grim Batol, the Scarlet Vault, the Obsidian Plane battle, and even Hakkar's nightmares could absolutely be called a legendary robe.

And what would this new robe — sewn from mooncloth, meticulously crafted by the most skilled shadow tailors of the Kingdom of Alterac and Dalaran — go through next?

Draenor, the Molten Core, even...

The members of the Dagger Circle planned to have dinner first before performing the summoning ritual. Right now they were discussing the powerful abilities the new robe might possess, especially the defense far beyond ordinary enchanted robes once Mage Armor was applied.

Listening to their discussion, Jace sat there, and his thoughts drifted farther and farther away.
