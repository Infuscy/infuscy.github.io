## Chapter 118: Mage Armor (Part 1)

The next morning, Jace came to Marin's room, quickly tidied up, and sat down — only to feel a hollow emptiness settle over him.

He'd spent these past two weeks doing nothing but drilling Orcish nonstop, and now that he'd suddenly passed, it felt like a piece of his life had gone missing.

The door opened then, but it wasn't Marin who walked in — it was Amy.

"Jace, come look at this." Amy came in holding a watch by its chain. "You gave me this last time, and the gnome fixed it for you."

"That fast?" Jace got up and took a look. The glass cover was still cracked, but the hands inside were moving, and the time had apparently been set already — it was roughly the current time.

"He couldn't find a proper replacement for the case, but it doesn't affect telling the time, does it?" Amy said.

"It doesn't," Jace said. "This is already way better than I imagined. I won't have to keep squinting at the sun to tell the time anymore. How can I ever thank you and that gnome master of mechanisms?"

"We help each other out," Amy said. "No need for thanks. My father said you're going to Dalaran — maybe you could bring me back a gift? Something they only have in Dalaran."

"Absolutely." Jace nodded.

"I'll be holding you to that." Amy smiled. "Off to class, then." Jace leaned against the table behind him, looking at this beat-up pocket watch with utter fondness — the more he looked, the more he liked it. Before, when things were free, he hadn't treasured them at all. Now this watch that hadn't cost him a single coin start to finish, he couldn't prize enough.

Before long, Marin walked in and caught Jace wiping the watch in his hands. "You got that watch fixed?"

"Even you know about this, Master?" Jace stuffed the watch into his pocket.

Marin set his bag down. "Amy went on about that broken watch for days, worried that gnome from the School of Evocation couldn't fix it. Is it working now? Honestly, I didn't hold out much hope for something that badly damaged."

"It works. It works wonderfully." Jace couldn't hold back his smile — after all, this had saved him not just a few dozen silver, but even more than that.

He had no need to show off with a pocket watch. As long as it told the time, that was enough. Especially considering the upcoming trip to Lordaeron, being able to check the time anytime would probably matter even more.

Marin said, "Seems the gnomes do have their skills. If you'd left it with that watchmaker by the Cathedral Square, he'd have taken one look and given up. My own pocket watch broke a few years ago — you couldn't even tell from the outside — and he refused to fix it for me."

"Right then — your exam. Aren't we going to talk about it?" the mage said, sitting behind his desk. "My interest in you is starting to outweigh my interest in these books, Jace Sesso."

Jace cleared his throat and said, "Master Dushan has already agreed to try to secure our boat tickets. I'll wait until class time and go find him at the Wizard Sanctum to tell him the date I've decided on."

"When are you planning to leave?"

"For now I'm thinking the seventeenth," Jace said. "I hear it takes fifteen to twenty days to reach Southshore, and there's still travel after that, so I want to head out a little early."

"As early as possible is the right call. After all, the war only just ended, and a lot of things haven't settled into the new rhythm yet," Marin said. "I knew he'd pick you to go to Dalaran. He's thought for a long time that you have a gift for languages, and I agree, Mr. Sesso."

"I was wondering — have you ever considered doing language-related work, besides studying magic? Things like excavating lost ancient languages, for instance. You might be able to help the Wizard Sanctum recover a lot of lost spells, ancient secrets, and the like."

Is he implying I have no arcane talent?

Jace thought for a moment and said, "Those kinds of jobs are probably the sort that children of nobles and wealthy merchants pursue. I still want to walk the path of magic."

Marin pulled open a drawer, took out a small box of biscuits, and handed it to Jace. "Without money, the path of magic is hard to walk too. Fortunately, mages always have ways of scraping together coin on their own."

Jace chewed on the slightly salty snack and said, "Maybe studying magic also means being better able to protect myself. And protect my family."

"There's a saying," Marin said in a teasing tone. "Words sometimes cut deeper than swords. Language is a weapon too — no less powerful than magic."

"Ask the person who said that whether they'd rather be attacked by words or have a blade stabbed into their heart," Jace said, then picked up a novel to kill time.

Marin burst out laughing. "Such confidence, my Mr. Sesso."

Of course Jace understood the times when words cut deeper than blades. He'd been cut by a blade in this life, and wounded by words in his last one. The blade wound hurt like hell for the first few days, but at most two weeks later it was nothing to speak of. But the words from his last life — he still remembered them in this one, and thinking of them still made it hard to bear.

But he'd decided that in this life, he would never be hurt by words again. Except for curses, of course — that was beyond his control.

"Master, my Arcane Intellect can last over two hours now. Is it possible for me to start learning the Mage Armor incantation?"

Marin turned around, surprised. "You practiced Arcane Intellect up to two hours in just two or three weeks? How many times a day do you cast it on yourself?"

Actually, the target had been his own — not a few times.

Jace had, of course, been practicing entirely on Sazeno, and to this day he hadn't noticed the little imp showing any signs of split personality or mental confusion. Then again, given an imp's mental state, it was hard to tell whether they were split or not.

He said, somewhat sheepishly, "I've been practicing on the sheep in Elwynn Forest. On myself… I've only tried it a few times to test the results. Not many times on myself at all."

Marin narrowed his eyes. "You didn't sneak into someone's farm and cast spells on other people's sheep, did you?"

"Absolutely not. Everything I do is open and aboveboard." Jace pressed a hand to his chest. "I can reach over two hours now. Last time I figured I was close, so I cast Arcane Intellect on myself and sensed the duration myself. I think it should be about right."

Marin said helplessly, "Fine, then. Let me see how well you've got Arcane Intellect down."

Jace stepped away from the desk and fluently recited the Arcane Intellect incantation. He'd chanted this spell so many times over the past two weeks that it had practically become second nature, so he recited it extremely fast, extremely smoothly, and extremely accurately.

That feeling of having a rush of clarity filled the space between his ears again. Jace took a deep breath and looked at Marin. "How is it, Master?"

"Not bad," Marin said. "However you learned it, it looks quite normal. Now sit down properly and watch me demonstrate Mage Armor once."

"Yes, Master!"
