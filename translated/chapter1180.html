Chapter 1180: A Living Bait

Generally speaking, Void beings were rather straightforward and didn't spout nonsense the way demons did, so Jace had no intention of doubting this mass of shadow chaos's verbal acknowledgment.

Moreover, as its power diminished, the Void Binding ward had already firmly restrained it, so this chaos could be considered settled.

Still, this was a bit different from what he'd agreed on with Sulenna.

Jace's gaze swept across the warlocks beside him and asked, "Don't you have a way to enslave it with an incantation? Why does it still treat me as its master?"

"These kinds of Void beings require pure shadow power to be suppressed to complete the enslavement..." Galders pushed aside the sweat-damp black strands of hair on his forehead and explained. "And obviously, Jace, under those circumstances, only you could manage that."

Jace looked at Sulenna. She could only give an apologetic smile at the moment.

"So you'll have to bear with it a little, Jace, and help the Dagger Circle control this voidwalker," Sulenna said, leaning against the wall.

"This probably isn't a simple voidwalker, Sulenna. Look at the shadow armor on its body," Jaylene said, catching her breath and checking the damage to her short staff.

Jaylene was right. Although this creature looked very similar to a voidwalker, it was clearly much closer to a physical entity, and it was draped in layer upon layer of shadow crystal armor, like a towering elemental warrior.

Although it was currently weakened to the extreme by Jace's magic, the dark power it contained still wasn't something an ordinary voidwalker could compare to...

More importantly, this fellow didn't have the magical binding bracers on its arms that ordinary voidwalkers or elemental beings needed to exist in the physical plane. In other words, it could stably walk in places outside the Twisting Nether like Azeroth purely by relying on its own power.

Jace still remembered what these armored Void beings were — they were called Voidlords, a kind of minion that warlocks in the game could only successfully summon at a very late stage. Most beginners in shadow and the Void didn't even know of their existence.

Still, it was obvious this Voidlord hadn't been summoned by Jace — it was more like it had tried to hunt Jace and failed, and ended up trapped here instead.

"What exactly went wrong with the summoning ritual earlier?" Ursula asked. "Jace, what did you see?"

"I can't say for sure." Jace turned around and looked at the now-silent Voidlord, asking in Eredar, "Tell me — what is your name?"

"Lord of the Biraatu Shards, the Drowsy Gaothoros," the Voidlord said in Eredar.

"That's way too long," Jace said. "I'm guessing Gaothoros should be your name in the Void. Tell me, Gaothoros — why, when I was already planning to flee, didn't you let me go and kept chasing me all the way here?"

"Must... devour your shadow power, master." Gaothoros slowly raised one hand and clenched a bright-purple fist tightly. "I cannot refuse, cannot resist, must... grow, must transcend."

"So, Jace," Erland said from the side. "Sounds like you just served as living bait in the flesh."

"I don't like that metaphor," Jace said, scratching his scorched-red shoulder. "Damn it, help me find a new shirt first."

"You should change your pants too," Ursula reminded him.

Ursula's words made Jace break out in a cold sweat. He thought he hadn't been scared to that extent, had he?

But when he lowered his head, he realized it was the right thigh area of his pants that had been burned away in a large patch. This set of clothes was something he'd bought these past couple of days specifically to accompany his parents around Stormwind — and now it was completely ruined.

Originally, he'd worried that the magical enhancement runes on his robe might amplify the power of the summoning ritual and attract something hard to control, which was why he'd worn ordinary clothes. He hadn't expected that in the end, what determined the summon wouldn't be how well he performed the ritual, but his own magical power.

Just then, a burst of hurried footsteps came from outside. "What's going on?"

Tharle muttered in bewilderment as he ran to the door, then caught sight of this terrifying otherworldly creature and his eyes went wide. "Did the commotion just now have something to do with this monster?" he asked.

"Did it sound very loud outside?" Galders asked.

"Broke a couple of glasses, luckily no customers were hurt." Tharle wiped his forehead. "But it's nighttime and there are many guests — there's no way to brush this over, Galders! All we can hope is that the mages didn't notice the situation here... but I wouldn't hold my breath. Actually, when was the last time we made a commotion this big?"

"I don't know," Jaylene shook her head.

"Don't say you don't — I can't remember one either..." Ursula said quietly.

Erland said, "Then I'll go explain the situation to Mathias Shaw, so he can communicate with the Wizard Sanctum before they investigate our tavern."

"You think this counts as 'making trouble'?" Sulenna asked with some worry. "Will he help us smooth it over? Or will he have all of us thrown in the canal first?"

"I'll say it was Jace Sesso who made the 'trouble,'" Erland spread his hands toward Jace. "He'll definitely be willing to help."

Then he looked at Jace and asked, "This is the truth, isn't it?"

Jace looked at Gaothoros, who had fallen silent and had already drained all the drifting shadow power in the room, and sighed. "Tell him that. In a few days I'll go apologize to Shaw myself."

Erland nodded and left the basement with Tharle with him.

Next, it was a matter of seeing whether this Voidlord could interpret those documents unearthed from the underground palace in Stratholme.

Having been through that battle, Ursula and Jaylene — two warlocks who weren't very skilled in combat — were quite exhausted. Sulenna seemed to have been cut on her leg by the armor fragments that had erupted at the start and was sitting to the side examining the wound.

Her peripheral vision noticed the staff in Jace's hand that had been soaked through with strange arcane blood. She was about to ask something but didn't open her mouth. After all, since Gajin's death, the Dagger Circle had basically established a rule of not investigating sources.

Aside from occasionally being truly unable to control their curiosity, or caring as friends, everyone no longer tangled with each other over such things. Even Galders no longer tried to dig out the story behind the things Jace brought back.

But if not for this unspoken understanding, Jace might not have made the decision to pull out Ursulus in front of so many people just now to quickly suppress this Voidlord.

"It looks like the situation here has stabilized. I need to go check the magical runes in various parts of the basement, Jace," Galders said. "The magical vibrations just now might have caused problems."

Jace nodded. After a while, Jaylene brought Jace some simple, clean clothes to put on for now.

He looked at the Voidlord before him. Its terrifying black magic shadow had enveloped half the room in a darkness as gloomy as complete night — the light of the candles on this side couldn't penetrate it at all.

And its main body was like an abrupt burst of purple fel light amidst the gloom, made somewhat glaring by the contrast of the black.

"A few rules." Jace straightened his sleeves and said in Eredar, "First — you are not to leave this room."

Gaothoros nodded its small head swaying with two purple flames and let out a deep "mm" in an ethereal voice.

Jace looked at the Void Binding ward trapping this Voidlord here, then looked at Sulenna and said, "I'm a little worried this might not be secure enough, Sulenna. Tomorrow we have to find a way to reinforce it."

"I'll tell Erland," Sulenna nodded.

"Second." Jace continued in Eredar, "You are not to harm any mortal of this world — whether here or outside."

Gaothoros agreed without the slightest hesitation.

"Third." Jace held out three fingers, then finally sighed and said, "I'll think of tomorrow morning."
