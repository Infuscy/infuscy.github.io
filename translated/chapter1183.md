## Chapter 1183: A World Without Dreams?

Arriving at the house where Sola lived, Jace couldn't help feeling that the flower beds and green trees planted around here seemed to be growing even more lush than elsewhere.

Sola was already waiting downstairs. The moment she spotted Jace appear by the flower bed, she narrowed her eyes and smiled.

In the depths of autumn and winter, Sola's light, fluttering green robes had always looked painfully cold no matter how you saw it. Only once spring and summer rolled around did they stop looking so jarring.

Of course, to her it was nothing more than an illusion sustained by the power of her scales, so she herself had no clue anything was off.

"Sorry, I ran into Master Marin and we talked a bit longer than I meant to," Jace said. "Did you wait long?"

"Not long at all, Jace." Sola walked over. "About today… I've actually been quite curious about the Bloodtooth boars. After all, if the Stormwind chefs can cook those animals' innards into something that delicious, then the Bloodtooth boars themselves must be pretty tasty too, right? At least in Kalimoth I could never find boars this plump. The black boars living in the coastal wastelands seem skinnier than the wolves here — nothing but skin and bones, honestly."

"Our Bloodtooth boars grow fat sneaking west to steal crops and poultry, and every now and then they'll snack on a lone traveler. Of course they're not the same," Jace said. "In the Kalimoth grasslands there's not even anyone growing grain. What, you're not planning on having lamb today — you want to try Bloodtooth boar for a change?"

Sola clasped both hands together. "Then take me west to look for some this time."

"No problem," Jace said.

They went to an unpopulated spot not far from Mirror Lake, where Sola resumed her true form as a green dragon and carried Jace flying westward.

Ever since she'd returned from Kalimoth this time, Jace had felt that her body seemed considerably larger than before. The last time, when she'd carried Grede, Vereesa, Morophil, and himself all together to the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, her back had managed to seat all four of them, but it had still been a bit cramped during rest stops.

But now her shoulders seemed much broader, the scales on her back sturdier and wider, and the horns on her neck noticeably more pronounced.

Honestly, ever since coming back from Kalimoth, Jace felt he could no longer describe her head as "snake-like."

She was, after all, a "semi-young" dragon. Now she seemed to have moved a little further from a juvenile's appearance and taken a step toward an adult dragon's form.

"Did you accept Prince Eranikus's offer?" Jace asked. "To become one of Queen Ysera's Dream Wardens."

Solamriel tilted her head and said in a husky voice, "No. I expressed to the Queen my wish to remain here — around the Forest of Elwynn — so that I could also help Brother Thalar whenever needed. Still, the Queen granted me her blessing. I haven't truly felt the benefits the blessing has brought me yet, but I seem to have grown at least a little because of it."

"I think it's pretty obvious," Jace nodded.

"Really?" The green dragon turned her head back, displeased. "Grede said there wasn't the slightest difference."

"He was probably in a bad mood from leaving Musa and was just humoring you off the cuff." Jace said this, then glanced back at the green dragon's tail. Aside from the difference in body size and horn shape, the most obvious distinction between a juvenile and an adult dragon was the tail.

A juvenile dragon's tail tip was a club-like growth studded with bony knobs similar in color to the horns all over its body — it looked just like a cob of corn.

But once they reached adulthood, these knobs would grow into spikes, and most adult dragons' tails would gradually become a spiked mace during this stage.

By now, Solamriel's tail was already a little different from when they'd first met in Winterspring. Those knobs had indeed grown longer and shaken off their corn-like appearance.

So this young dragon who had slumbered in the Dream for a thousand years was finally setting out on the path to adulthood?

"How about it — have I gotten prettier, Jace?" Solamriel asked.

"Of course you've gotten prettier," Jace answered. "Which means you need to eat a few more boars and keep growing."

The green dragon seemed encouraged. She wagged her tail left and right, flapped her broad wings — webbed and shimmering with a golden glow — and excitedly soared toward the river plains in the distance.

Jace had never imagined that the discussions about Bloodtooth boars he'd heard from hunters while killing time in the taverns of Moonbrook and Sentinel Hill would actually come in handy one day.

Following the boar habitats those hunters had mentioned, he guided Sola toward a stretch of barren high ground east of Moonbrook.

Although from the surface it appeared to be nothing but a yellowish-green wilderness of rolling hills where not a living thing could be seen, Jace could still feel a fierce, undisguised primal fear hiding beneath the grass clusters and bushes.

"That way." He pointed toward where the fear was most intense, and Solamriel dipped lower and dove in that direction.

Sure enough, as the green dragon approached the ground, the boars lying low and hiding under the vegetation finally couldn't hold out any longer and bolted. Though screaming in terror, the boars still fled as a tightly packed group — which gave the green dragon her opening.

She flew in slowly and unhurriedly, closing on the boars, and exhaled a cloud of pale pink dream-mist. Jace felt the mist instantly "douse" the boars' fear. The whole family of tawny beasts, big and small, toppled to the ground in a neat row, peacefully drifting into sweet dreams.

The green dragon flew out, banked back around, and landed beside the little boars lying scattered across the tall grass. She picked one up in her mouth, tossed it into the sky, and swallowed it whole.

Jace stood watching this scene, about to ask whether she could actually taste anything gulping it down like that, then glanced at the rows of white, dagger-like fangs in her mouth and decided he'd better mind his own business when it came to dragons.

One boar, clearly the leader of the herd, was noticeably too large — it looked like a damn bear.

Solamriel couldn't swallow it whole, so she pinned it down with a claw, grabbed its lower half, and tore it clean in two. The boar was jolted awake by the sudden ripping, but all it managed was a hoarse, gurgling screech before its life ended.

Jace glanced at the other boars still lying there, a little worried they might be startled awake all at once. But the green dragon's dream magic was clearly on a different level from ordinary hypnosis — despite the terrified howl of their companion being torn to pieces, the other boars slept as soundly as if nothing had happened.

"Delicious… mm." The green dragon licked the blood from the corners of her mouth with her long tongue, then said, "I wonder if there'll be similar beasts on Draenor."

"I hear there's a kind of massive clefthoof over there. The orcs love eating their meat and use their hides to make tents, saddles, even armor," Jace said, half-joking. "Though I don't know if you could take one."

"Ah, uh." The green dragon lowered her head, less than confident.

Watching a dragon worry about not being able to beat a cow was a novel experience, and Jace couldn't help but laugh. Of course, clefthoofs weren't ordinary cattle either — those hill-sized monsters might genuinely not be pushover for a sufficiently weak young dragon.

But at the same time, a thought occurred to Jace.

"Will your magic even work on Draenor, Sola? I mean — Draenor doesn't have the Emerald Dream."

"Most of my magic comes from the power of nature and my own mana, Jace, and isn't directly tied to the Dream," Solamriel said. "But… there is indeed a portion of my magic that relies on the Dream."

"Won't even the dream-earrings the Dragonqueen gave Vereesa have problems then?" Jace said. "And that bow."

"The power of that bow comes from my father's staff, Jace — it was taken directly from an ancient tree within the Emerald Dream. It is itself a part of the Dream," Solamriel explained. "Vereesa isn't a druid; she couldn't possibly channel the Dream's power directly with a simple incantation."

"As for those earrings." The green dragon thought for a moment. "That is one of Queen Ysera's treasures. I never examined it closely before, so I can't say for certain. But I imagine the Queen's treasure might be like that staff — carrying the power of the Dream, perhaps a small fragment of it… Perhaps we won't know whether they'll work until we actually set foot on Draenor."

"Right. Should I go ask Thalar?"

"Better not, Sola." Jace shook his head. "I'm worried he'd guess what we're planning."
