## Chapter 1184: The Dream of a Super Easter Egg

Whether or not Draenor could still make use of the power of the Dream, here in Azeroth Solamriel's magic still packed a considerable punch.

The green dragon's dream power held back the coke and ash of the Burning Steppes, allowing Jace and Grede to sit on her back and stare straight through the thick volcanic smoke while breathing air that didn't taste much of cinders. Otherwise, Jace truly couldn't imagine how they were supposed to cross those mountains from the sky.

Still, forcing Solamriel to contend with Blackrock Mountain for too long would wear her down. She pushed through the Burning Steppes as quickly as possible and entered the Badlands to the north, where she could catch her breath a little over the dry, sandy terrain.

After reaching the Badlands, Grede and Jace — who hadn't managed to eat a thing since leaving Elwynn — hastily pulled out their own provisions to fill their stomachs.

Jace took out a chunk of cheese that had already crumbled into several pieces in his pocket, pinched off a bit, and stuffed it into his mouth. Just then, Grede passed him a colorful egg.

At a glance, Jace thought it was a leftover painted egg from the Garden Festival a few days back, but when he took it he realized it was actually a peeled pickled egg. He gave it a sniff — it even smelled of garlic.

"Why is it red?" Jace asked.

"Juice from a kind of red Khaz Modan berry." Grede nodded. "Pretty, right?"

Jace bit into it with the anticipation of a tea egg, and the damn thing absolutely wrecked him with a blast of heat.

He stuck his tongue out and asked, "Sweet, salty, spicy, plus garlic and fruit — whose recipe is this?"

"An old woman in the Dwarf District gave it to me for the road," Grede said, tilting his head. "I'll have you know, this is a traditional food in Grim Batol too, Jace Sesso. Otherwise, with raptor eggs that big, what are you supposed to do when you can't finish one in a single meal? You've got to find a way to pickle them in a jar and eat them slowly."

Jace painfully swallowed the whole egg, thinking to himself that if only he knew how to make century eggs, he'd give this clueless dwarf a little transmigrator shock.

"There's an egg-painting contest in the Dwarf District and Old Town, held during the Garden Festival," Grede said, grinning at the look on his face. "That old woman's son brought out a gorgeous raptor egg with a painting of Loch Modan's snowy mountains on it — really beautiful. Everyone else's eggs were either fairly large turkey eggs or vulture eggs — not exactly enough surface to paint anything on."

"What about your egg?" Jace asked.

"I didn't have an egg. What egg would I have?" Grede said loudly. "I was originally planning to make an unprecedented mega-egg and crush the entire Stormwind egg-painting scene with sheer, overwhelming force…"

"If you bring up asking me to steal eggs one more time, I'll eat you raw, Thunderfist!" the green dragon roared, twisting her head around.

"I wasn't asking you to get a live dragon egg from Thalar!" Grede protested, raising his hands. "Not even broken shells? I could piece them back together and glue them up a bit… Apart from our little death-seeking crew, no one would recognize what a green dragon egg originally looked like!"

The green dragon shrieked, "I said no, no, no!"

Jace slapped a hand over his forehead and sighed. "Aren't you taking this a bit too far?"

Grede made a round shape with his hands. "You don't know the look on those people's faces when they brought out their little eggs — prouder than if they'd beaten back the demon legions at the Dark Portal! I'd love to find a real dragon egg and scare the hell out of them."

"I wonder if there are any broken black dragon egg shells left in Grim Batol," Jace said, scratching his head. "But those eggs have surfaces rougher than volcanic rock, plus horns that could run a person through — I doubt you'd be able to paint any decent design on them."

"I still remember — we were just about to smash those eggs to pieces when Deathwing came charging over in a rage." Grede lay back on the dragon's back. "Though by the time we find those eggs, the egg-painting contest will probably be long over."

"Don't lie on my back!" Solamriel shouted angrily.

Grede sprang back to his feet and muttered, "Sorry, sorry… I won't bring it up again…"

As they bickered, the dark air around them thickened once more, and a wave of cinder-stench struck them — which meant they'd drawn near the north side of Blackrock Mountain.

Conditions north of Blackrock were a little better than the south, but only just. After all, the place called Searing Gorge was about as straightforward as its name suggested.

Sensing the drastic change in climate, Jace and Grede immediately shut their mouths and quickly finished the food in their hands.

Though Solamriel tried to veer further east to buy them a little more time, not plunging straight into the gorge, the hot wind laced with cinders soon caught up and enveloped them. She could only brace her dream power to shield them.

"Where are the Dark Iron dwarves?" Grede asked.

"North," Jace said. "Fly north into the mountains of Khaz Modan and keep an eye out, Sola. The place is called 'Thorium Watch' by the Thorium Brotherhood — there should be a tower there."

The green dragon observed the mountains in the distance and gave a slight nod. "Don't worry, I'll find them, Jace."

Grede still looked a little uneasy. "Didn't Roktusk give you a marked map or anything?"

"No. He only mentioned that Thorium Watch is north of Searing Gorge, west of Dustfire Valley," Jace said. "West of the mountain pass leading to Loch Modan. Nothing more detailed than that."

Roktusk Darkvow certainly wasn't that unreliable. He'd mentioned at the time that if Jace couldn't find Thorium Watch, he should go to Ironforge and have Ruige show him the way.

But Jace thought about Ruige's succubus… and then having to explain to Sola and Grede why he'd taught a Dark Iron dwarf how to summon a succubus, and it all felt like it was getting way too complicated.

Besides, making a detour to Ironforge would probably burn through several more days.

In any case, he had Roktusk Darkvow's letter of introduction to the leaders of the Thorium Brotherhood. There was no need to worry about the Thorium Brotherhood mistaking them for enemies. If even a green dragon couldn't find where that watchtower was, then they could think about heading north to Ironforge.

As it turned out, Jace's worry was entirely unnecessary. For a green dragon like Solamriel, finding a group of dwarves who hadn't hidden themselves with any special magical means in a valley like this was no challenge at all.

The further they got from Blackrock Mountain, the more plants stubbornly clinging to life appeared on the mountainsides. Sola communed with the pine trees along the way and quickly picked up word of the Dark Iron dwarves' movements.

And so, before the sky had fully darkened, Jace could already see an iron tower poking out from the valley floor in the distance.
