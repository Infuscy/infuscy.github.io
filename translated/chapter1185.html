Chapter 1185: Thorium Watch

Solamriel circled above the watchtower for a while, and Jace took the opportunity to observe the environment of the valley below.

Beneath the watchtower were some simple tents supported by steel frames, a number of stacked supply crates, and quite a few furnaces and metal tools presumably related to smelting and forging.

This should have been Thorium Watch, all right — after all, in this vast stretch of mountains, the only watchtower that still looked intact enough to be habitable was this one.

Then again, you could probably pass this off as just an ordinary Dark Iron dwarf camp if you tried hard enough.

After all, Jace had heard in Shadowforge City that a fairly large number of Dark Iron dwarves were still stationed within Searing Gorge, relying on the mine pits and the bases and forts built around them to hold out against the orcs of Blackrock Mountain, searching for a chance to reestablish contact with Shadowforge City. It would make perfect sense for these Dark Iron dwarves to build watchtowers in the northern mountains to keep an eye on Ironforge's movements.

The most critical problem was that there wasn't a single dwarf in sight from up here. Although the facilities looked old and worn, they didn't appear to have been completely abandoned.

"Fly lower, Sola," Jace said. "If there's anyone here, I'll be able to sense them."

"All right." The green dragon slowed the beat of her wings and gradually descended toward the watchtower. Jace closed his eyes and carefully felt for the shadow energy emanating from the camp and its surroundings, trying to detect the emotions of any living things within.

When the green dragon drew a little closer to the top of the watchtower, Jace suddenly felt a surge of intense emotion… A roar erupted from the watchtower, followed by a large net launched from some device, flung straight at them.

The nets were enchanted and flew with terrifying speed. Solamriel immediately flapped her wings to dodge backward, but even so she couldn't quite keep up with the net's velocity. Jace had just drawn Skysong when a bolt of lightning suddenly streaked over and exploded right in the middle of the flying net, instantly blowing it to shreds that scattered across the sky.

The thunderous blast lit the gloomy valley white as daylight, then slowly faded back to darkness as the arcs of lightning dissipated. Grede used lightning to drag the Shattered Skysong back into his hand and bellowed down in Dwarvish, "You dare cast a net at the Lord Gryphon Rider of Aerie Peak, you black-skinned bastards? Have you forgotten how we taught you a lesson in Grim Batol?"

"What the hell? A Wildhammer?" A Dark Iron dwarf poked his head out from the top of the watchtower and looked around. One by one, more dwarves emerged from under the tents, from behind the crates, and from between the rocks.

"How the hell are you riding on the back of a black dragon? Did Rendal Blackhand capture you and drag you up Blackrock Mountain to be his slave?"

"Yeah, and I share a cell with your wife!" Grede roared back.

The two groups of dwarves immediately started trading insults, the content utterly vile. Jace leaned close to Grede and reminded him, "They think Sola's a black dragon."

Grede glanced at Jace, cleared his throat, and shouted down at the top of his voice, "Open your swollen, blind rabbit eyes and take a good look — what color are these scales?"

At that, the commotion below suddenly died down.

"Right, uh… that's not a black dragon," one of the Dark Iron dwarves said. "Look, the scale color's off."

Solamriel's mouth had already begun leaking a pale pink slumber-mist. One blast of this and the whole camp would be laid out flat — especially a race like dwarves, who had particular trouble resisting the lure of sleep. Probably not a single one would be left standing.

But the dwarves below clearly had no idea what this lethal stuff was. One of them shouted, "Whatever that weird dragon's about to spit out, don't let it, or I'll open fire! I've shot down fifteen black dragons with this gun!"

The moment those words left his mouth, Grede let out a cold laugh, and Jace clearly heard a muttered "Isn't that a bit too much bragging?" from below.

At least this group didn't look much like soldiers of the Dark Iron legion. Jace urged Sola not to be so on guard, then tried saying, "We've come looking for the Thorium Brotherhood."

"What do you want with the Thorium Brotherhood?" a dwarf carrying a musket shouted from under a tent.

"Are you the Thorium Brotherhood?" Jace asked.

No one below answered immediately. But after a moment, a Dark Iron dwarf dressed in a red suit of clothes and wearing a flame-enchanted metal crown stepped forward.

"Yes." He looked elderly, and his voice was quite deep. "We are the Thorium Brotherhood. I am Overseer Ofester. State your purpose, human. Are you from Stormwind, or Stromgarde?"

"I'm from Stormwind, Overseer," Jace said, taking out the letter in his hand. "Actually, I've brought a very important letter for you."

"This letter concerns a matter of great importance. It may affect the fate of your Brotherhood, the Dark Iron, the entire dwarven race, and even every inhabitant from Khaz Modan to Elwynn."

The dwarves immediately began murmuring amongst themselves, many of them skeptical. Even so, Jace felt he couldn't just blurt out Roktus Darkcontract's name in front of everyone.

After all, Roktus was currently in Shadowforge City in at least nominal "hostage" status. If this watchtower had any informants with close ties to Shadowforge City, and word got back that Roktus was colluding with the Thorium Brotherhood outside to overthrow Ragnaros's rule, Roktus would be in serious trouble.

Overseer Ofester nodded and raised his hand high. "Lower your weapons. Let our guests from Stormwind land, brothers!"

The riflemen on the watchtower and in the tents raised their gun barrels. The green dragon then landed on the open ground before the watchtower. The giant gusts from her wings made the tents flap wildly, forcing the dwarves to desperately grab onto ropes and steel frames to keep their dwellings from being blown clean away. Even Grede was a bit taken aback by the display — it seemed he was finally feeling her growth in size too.

Jace hopped off the dragon's back and, together with Grede, was led by the Overseer to a tent.

After reading the letter from beginning to end, despite the fact that the lower half of his face was completely covered by a white beard and his skin was black as coal, Jace could read utter shock straight from the Overseer's eyes — and that was without needing the shadow power stirred by sensing his emotions.

He passed the letter to an elderly dwarf beside him wearing bright chainmail, who had an equally white beard. After that dwarf's equally shocked eyes met his, the Overseer tossed the letter into the burning furnace.

"Only a few years ago we lost many of our finest smiths over this very matter, Master Jace Sesso." Overseer Ofester looked up, glanced at Grede beside him, and said, "But that hasn't broken our resolve or our spirit of adventure."

"We heard about what happened in Grim Batol too, and learned of the fall of the Dragon King and the destruction of the Dragonmaw clan. That matter had an enormous impact on Blackrock Mountain. But the Kadros Anvil…"

He glanced at the elderly dwarf beside him. "It's certainly a very bold idea. But since Master Roktus Darkcontract himself is willing to give it a try, I see no reason for us to refuse this possibility. Honestly, I never imagined that the humans of the Kingdom of Stormwind would be willing to help the Dark Iron dwarves."

"As I said just now," Jace said, "ending Ragnaros's rule benefits everyone living in the Redridge Mountains, Overseer. King Varian Wrynn has always cared deeply about this matter as well."

"You really think…" Grede stepped forward and looked at the two Dark Iron dwarves before him. "A warhammer forged from a volcano can utterly defeat the one who created that volcano? I mean… think about it — a fire warhammer that defeats the Firelord. It sounds pretty damn far-fetched if you ask me."

"I don't know whether the Sulfuron Hammer can shatter the Firelord's body, Lord Grede Thunderfist of Aerie Peak," the Overseer said. "After all, none of us have ever wielded this warhammer with our own hands, much less had the chance to bring it before Ragnaros. But we know this: ever since this concept was proposed, ever since the design was completed, countless corrupted fire servants — from the fire elemental cultists in the Thaurissan Shrine to the flame serpents hiding deep within the Molten Core — have tried by every means possible to destroy it, whether the materials to forge it or the craftsmen who can forge it…"

"Every dwarf who has taken part this mission can feel the Firelord's anxiety and the fear of his shameless lackeys, Lord Thunderfist. Tell me — as a gryphon rider, do you fear the sky?"

Grede stared at Overseer Ofester and, for a moment, found himself speechless. After Ofester finished speaking, he simply nodded and exchanged a few words with his old companion.

A while later, Overseer Ofester said to Jace, "Let me have a meeting with our Master Smith and the other brothers tonight, Master Jace Sesso. We need to find the right men to accompany you to Grim Batol tomorrow to inspect the Kadros Anvil. In the meantime, you can rest and have something to eat."

Jace nodded and watched the two dwarves leave. Not long after, a dull, hoarse bell rang from the watchtower, summoning everyone to the open space beside the tent for the meeting.

Grede sat to the side and, ever since hearing what Ofester had said, hadn't spoken much. He just kept gnawing on the salty bread the Dark Iron dwarves had brought over.

"You all right?" Jace asked, looking at him.

"What that Overseer said makes a lot of sense." The dwarf looked up at Jace. "What do you think, Jace? Do you think that warhammer…"

"You want to hear what I really think?" Jace asked.

"Of course," Grede said.

"If you ask me," Jace said, "at the very least, King Magni Bronzebeard is very keen on this idea. I believe many Wildhammer dwarves will be won over by it too. And this has been a legend circulating in Shadowforge City for God knows how many decades, even centuries — even a sage like Roktus, who has lived since the era of the War of the Three Hammers, believes in it deeply… Think about it, Grede. What was the last concept that united the Three Hammers like this?"

"Ironforge's a damn fine place," Grede replied in Dwarvish.

"Yes." Jace gazed toward Blackrock Mountain. "Perhaps it truly can destroy Ragnaros's power. Perhaps it can't. But right now it at least already possesses the power to give the Three Hammers some kind of shared purpose. Isn't that enough? Next, someone just needs to take it and swing it into Ragnaros's face. As for whether it'll actually work… when the combined armies of all three dwarven clans storm the depths of the Molten Core and have backed the Firelord into a corner where he must show himself, does it really matter how much that warhammer can accomplish?"

Jace turned back and continued, "Even if the warrior wielding that warhammer falls before Ragnaros, every other dwarf will fight with everything they have to avenge him. I don't believe that kind of power can't destroy that false god."

"False god?" Grede blinked.

Jace looked at him and said, "If he really is a genuine deity like you and those fire-tenders claim, then he should at least crush Onyxia's brother with his own hands to prove it — instead of letting him blockade Shadowforge City and starve his own followers to death without lifting a finger."

"And we've already dealt Onyxia a heavy blow." Grede nodded slowly. "You and I, uh, and that big demon — we've already dealt Onyxia a heavy blow… You make a lot of sense, Jace. You really do. At least that's what I think."

"But there's no way I could be knocked down so easily." Grede laughed. "I think at least a lot of dwarves already consider me a genuine Mountain King, Jace. A Mountain King doesn't die that easily!"

Sola smiled and said, "Then may I take that to mean you've already decided to take up that warhammer, Grede?"

Grede froze. "Wait — no, that warhammer hasn't even been forged yet! I was just hypothesizing, I was just… trying to get Jace to stop saying such unlucky things. Can't he say something nice for once? Can't it be some warrior wielding the Sulfuron Hammer leading the Three Hammers to total victory?"

"Of course it can. I was just making a hypothesis," Jace said. "And there's no rush about this warhammer either. Once we go with those dwarves to the Kadros Anvil, we'll find out whether that warhammer can actually be forged."

At the mention of bringing dwarves to Grim Batol, Sola couldn't help but press her lips together a little tighter.

Though she didn't say a word, Jace knew exactly what she was thinking. Apart from their own group, Sola was very particular about carrying anyone else on her back.

"Don't worry, Sola." Jace glanced at her. "Hm?" Sola blinked.

"I mean, you don't have to carry those dwarves to Grim Batol on your back," Jace said. "You've seen goblin zeppelins, right?"

As if her thoughts had been easily seen through, Sola nodded, a little embarrassed.

"I have."

Jace jabbed a thumb over his shoulder. "See those mine carts they've got lying over there by the watchtower? Have those dwarves attach a rope to a mine cart, and you just carry the rope and fly them over."

Grede couldn't help but laugh. "Good idea. Anyway, I don't want to sit in the same cart with these bastards!"
