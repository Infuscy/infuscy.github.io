Chapter 1186: A Long-Held Wish

The Brotherhood dwarves' eagerness to travel to Grim Batol far exceeded Jace's expectations. He'd originally thought the Overseer might have to strong-arm a few people into going to inspect the Kadros Anvil, but the reality was that he had to weed out the young smiths whose skills and experience he felt were still lacking, leaving only the most capable.

In the end, a team of five Brotherhood smiths was formed, including the "Master Smith" Burnhart, the smith chief of Thorium Watch, all preparing to set out. Overseer Ofester, considering his advanced age, his less-than-robous health, and the need to manage affairs at the watchtower, decided not to force himself along.

Faced with these smiths, Jace naturally raised the point that there were too many of them to all squeeze onto a dragon's back together, and that they'd have to find another solution — like removing the wheels from a mine cart and turning it into a "basket."

They got straight to it. These craftsmen had incredible hands, and in a single evening they'd used netting and metal railings to retrofit a mine cart into a simple basket large enough to hold every Brotherhood member making the trip to Grim Batol.

Solamriel tested lifting the basket first to confirm the structure's strength was sufficient for flight, and then the dwarves scrambled to jump in, pushing and shoving each other for a spot.

Judging by their expressions, it was hard to say whether they were desperate to rush to Grim Batol to see the legendary anvil seared by the Dragon-Father's fire, or simply desperate to experience what it was like to be carried into the sky by a dragon.

But once the green dragon spread her wings and lifted the mine cart off the ground, even though they couldn't see the expressions of the Dark Iron smiths below, just from the whoops and cheers Jace became more and more convinced it was probably the latter.

Grede, sitting to the side, wore a look of utter disdain and muttered from time to time, "Honestly, they've never seen a damn thing."

Thorium Watch was already situated at the very northern end of Searing Gorge, so heading north from here, they quickly broke free of the cinder-laced scorching winds and plunged into the snowy mountains of Dun Morogh.

Although from the game map the distance between Thorium Watch and Grim Batol didn't look all too far, in reality this was essentially a straight shot clear across Khaz Modan, requiring them to cross countless snowy peaks and ridges along the way — decidedly no easy journey.

As they passed over the expanse of Lake Loch, the Dark Iron dwarves had grown too cold to make much sound of excitement anymore, huddling in the mine cart under thick clothes and shivering.

Solamriel's dream power indeed had a hard time fully covering the basket below, but it was enough to show that these underground dwarves, who had lived in a volcano for two hundred years and were supremely heat-resistant, had nearly lost the cold-bearing constitution they'd brought out of Dun Morogh.

Bitterly cold as it was, the scenery of snowy mountains, pine forests, and lake surfaces was a delight to the soul — certainly nothing comparable to yellow sand and black scorched earth.

As they flew over Lake Loch, Jace vaguely felt that he was about to fulfill a wish that had lingered with him for a long time but that he'd never actually gotten around to completing.

But just gazing at the boundless, mirror-like surface of the lake, he couldn't quite recall what it was.

After flying for a full day and half a night, the group rested for the night in a quiet pine forest on the shore of Lake Loch. Early the next morning, after setting out again, it wasn't long before some murmuring came from the mine cart below.

The voices grew more and more excited, as if they were approaching somewhere special. And just then Grede nudged Jace's shoulder and pointed into the distance.

"Look, Jace — remember the last time the two of us came looking for that ancient ruin? You said you'd always wanted to see…"

"What?" Jace looked in the direction the dwarf was pointing. When he saw that colossal structure jutting up abruptly from the far end of the lake, like a giant wall, his whole body jolted.

"The Greatstone Dam."

Jace finished the thought in a low voice. He'd nearly forgotten about this!

The first time he'd come to Loch Modan, Jace and Grede had spent nearly ten days trudging through the Wetlands to reach Dun Algaz, their minds set on getting to Loch Modan as quickly as possible. They'd neither the energy nor the mood to make a detour to see the Greatstone Dam.

That regret hadn't been made up for after the Battle of Grim Batol either. Although one of Alexstrasza's handmaidens had flown Jace over the Greatstone Dam on the way back, she'd flown way too high that time, and the lake surface had been shrouded in thick mist — he couldn't see a thing. Since there was always the chance of being spotted by gryphon riders, Jace hadn't had the nerve to ask the red dragon to fly lower so he could get a good look at the dam.

This time, he finally got his wish.

"Let's get a little closer, Sola." Jace leaned forward. "I want to see it clearly."

"All right, Jace."

Solamriel banked slightly toward the northwest, then flew straight over the Greatstone Dam. When they reached the other side of the dam, Jace immediately turned to look back.

Set against the grey, colossally massive dam body, the towering pine forests surrounding it looked like nothing more than tufts of thick green beard.

In the center of the dam, a sculpture of three dwarf lords — one large and two small — gazed down over the entire Wetlands like rulers, water spewing from their mouths and pouring into the lake below, the sound deafening.

Yet even that thunderous roar couldn't drown out the excited shouts from the Dark Iron dwarves in the basket.

"Francroon! Great Francroon!"

Yes — few people knew that this super-sized dam that had reshaped the northern geography of Khaz Modan was actually built by the Dark Iron dwarves.

But it was also the Dark Iron dwarves who, in the wars that followed, were the most eager to blow it up, attempting to destroy the homes of all the Bronzebeard dwarves throughout the Wetlands.

Still, no matter how many times the Dark Iron tried to blow it up, their plans ended in failure every single time. In another timeline, the one who finally brought it down was the Cataclysm wrought personally by Deathwing, twenty years later.

So in a sense, Jace and his group were the saviors of this architectural marvel — even though not a single person in this world knew it.

Thinking of this, Jace let out a quiet sigh.

Reaching the Greatstone Dam meant the Barthor Mountains weren't far off. When the grey dam body disappeared at the edge of his vision and the scenery below was entirely replaced by what seemed like endless marsh and rivers, the shadow of the northern mountains gradually grew clearer.

Having been here once before, Solamriel had already memorized the location of Grim Batol, so she flew straight in that direction.

Not long after, in the light of the setting sun, Jace could already see the silhouettes of young red dragons flying toward them from a distance.
