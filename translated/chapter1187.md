## Chapter 1187: The Kadros Anvil

The Barthor Mountains were already covered in unbroken dense forest. Compared to the last time they'd come here, the scars of destruction Deathwing had left behind had faded even further — one could say they were now almost completely imperceptible.

The destroyed fortress of Grim Batol now resembled a ruin reclaimed by nature, a post-apocalyptic garden. The arrival of spring had added even more breath of renewal to this reborn hillside.

The young dragons flew ahead as guides, and Grede couldn't help crawling forward to gaze upon this ancient home of the Wildhammer. At this moment, he couldn't utter a word — only swallowing unnaturally from time to time.

Just then, a slightly older young red dragon flew over and said in Common with a fairly heavy accent, "Prince Korialstrasz was informed of your arrival just now, Jace Sesso, Grede Thunderfist, and Lady Solamriel. He is waiting for you in the deepest part of Grim Batol. I will lead you to meet him."

"Much appreciated." Jace called out.

The red dragon nodded slightly, lowered his height, and flew toward a collapsed wall. Solamriel immediately followed on his tail.

The crumbling ancient stone wall, built by the Wildhammer clan, was thickly covered with climbing vines. The Dark Iron smiths climbing out of the "basket" were extremely curious about the scene, sniffing here and commenting there, occasionally critiquing in the thick Shadowforge City dialect. Even with Jace's Dwarvish, at this distance on the dragon's back he still couldn't quite make it out.

After jumping down from the green dragon's back, Grede squinted at the group of Thorium Brotherhood smiths and whispered, "Wait until me and the brothers from Aerie Peak come back here someday, and we'll rebuild Grim Batol into a city even more breathtaking than the Greatstone Dam, and show these black dwarves a thing or two…"

"I thought you'd long since forgotten about that," Jace said.

"How could I?" Grede said. "Once we find our boss Kurdran Wildhammer on the other side of the Dark Portal, I'll tell him about this — Grim Batol must be rebuilt, whether the Three Hammers unite or not."

He raised both hands, gazing at the broken towers in the distance. "Ah, a city that has endured the corrupt witch Modgud's fell magic, the occupation of the Dragonmaw clan, Jace Sesso's infiltration, and the dragonfire of Deathwing… By the time its reconstruction is complete, Grim Batol will unquestionably become a place even more legendary than Ironforge, which has stood for thousands of years!"

Jace laughed. "My name gets to stand alongside Modgud and Deathwing now?"

"Why wouldn't it?" Grede patted Jace on the waist. "Don't sell yourself short, lad."

"I think so too." Sola said, walking up beside Jace and nodding seriously.

Grede's offhand boasting Jace could brush off, but once Sola said something like that, he couldn't take it lightly. He didn't say anything, but at that moment he felt his whole body grow uncomfortable, his curved staff clutched in his arms.

Was a character who got written into stories supposed to be someone like him — someone awkward from start to finish, standing here without knowing where to put his hands?

Maybe there was nothing wrong with that after all.

"By the way, does Aerie Peak still regard Kurdran Wildhammer as its grand lord to this day?" he asked.

"Yes." Grede nodded. "A lot of people think he's done for, but obviously Falstad doesn't see it that way. He insists his good brother is still over there figuring a way back. As for me — if Captain Alleria is still alive, then Kurdran won't die so easily. That's obvious. Dying in battle is an honor, but dying before an elf would be a bit embarrassing."

"So your basis for thinking Kurdran isn't dead is that Vereesa believes Alleria is still alive," Jace said.

"Exactly." Grede said matter-of-factly. "All right…" Jace smiled.

The red dragon led the group into a cave beneath a wall root propped up by tree roots. It looked like a tunnel covered in solidified grey-black lava, but before long, after passing through several more layers of sturdy tree roots, they entered a dwarf-carved corridor once more.

The corridor was patrolled by a number of armored red dragonmen, huge in stature and marching with firm steps.

From the rough excavation traces among these dwarf ruins, it could be seen that the underground sections of Grim Batol had been modified by the dragonmen, to allow people to pass through smoothly.

When the red dragon and Jace's group approached, the dragonmen lowered their heads in greeting. But when they saw the group of Thorium Brotherhood smiths behind them, they raised their heads again, putting on a posture of utter distrust — as if these Dark Iron dwarves had no business being in this place.

Most of the Dark Iron dwarves were long accustomed to the hostile attitudes of other races, and when this happened they often returned it with even worse hostility multiplied.

But this was Grim Batol after all. The target of their hostility wasn't the humans of Stormwind or their Bronzebeard neighbors to the north, but genuine dragon-kin.

So the Dark Iron dwarves stuck closely behind without daring to fall a single step back, without daring to make eye contact with those dragonmen guards. Seeing these Dark Iron dwarves scared witless, Grede was thoroughly amused, his mouth cocked to one side the whole way.

Of course, not all the Dark Iron were equally frightened. The black-bearded "Master Smith" Burnhart clearly looked like a man who'd seen the world. Even when the dragonmen treated him poorly, he nodded and returned their greetings quite naturally.

And rather than observing the red dragons and their draconic servants here, he was more interested in the traces of Deathwing's dragonfire and lava deep within Grim Batol.

Whenever he came across those lingering black lava formations, he would walk over and touch them, knock on them, his eyes widening now and then as he muttered something to himself.

Krasus had mentioned earlier that the red dragons had found a way to purify the fire deep within Grim Batol, but the deeper they went, the more Jace felt a dense shadow gradually creeping upward.

Even from the increasingly solemn expression of the red dragon leading the way, it was clear he wasn't the only one sensing this change.

However, this shadow energy had already parted with the frantic, berserk fury it carried when under Deathwing's control — it was more like disordered darkness drifting in every direction. Scorched by the red dragons' power, fleeing in every direction, unable to gather, barely able to struggle on by clinging to the magical traces Deathwing had left behind.

After descending a spiraling staircase, they arrived at a bridge that had lost its railing. Jace looked to the side and immediately saw the familiar hall below.

That was where the Dragonmaw orcs had once imprisoned the Dragonqueen, and also where Jace had confronted Deathwing in his mind.

Alexstrasza had broken free of the Demon Soul's power and gained her freedom, then used her magic to shield Jace's group, preventing them from being incinerated in an instant by the first blast of Deathwing's furious flames. And although this hall had slowly sunk to such a deep place, thanks to the Dragonqueen's magic it had actually survived relatively intact from that apocalyptic fury.

Even now Jace could still see the spot where he had tried to pierce that golden disc with Skysong.

"I've killed more orcs in there than I've ever seen from outside, Sola." Grede laughed, pointing at the completely blasted-open gate. "You believe that? Back then, heavily armed orc soldiers poured through that gate in droves… and the few of us had no choice but to charge headlong and fight."

Sola looked toward Jace. Jace nodded and said, "He's not exaggerating this time. I watched him charge into the pile of orcs, swaying back and forth like a tumbler, hacking at their knees…"

"Pah!" Grede glared. The three of them immediately burst out laughing, and even the red dragon leading the way couldn't help but smile.

Crossing the great bridge, they entered another ruined corridor. Here, the Dark Iron dwarves who had been discussing Grim Batol and the black dragonfire all along the way also fell silent.

Because like Jace, they too could feel the power brimming with anger and vitality erupting toward them from the end of the corridor in wave after wave.

The vision ahead grew increasingly red and bright, reminding Jace of the lava giant guarding the city gate he'd seen in Shadowforge City. The Thorium Brotherhood smiths couldn't help but cry out in surprise.

The entire hall had been swallowed by black lava. Although the lava here had cooled to black, most of it still held all sorts of bizarre, jutting shapes — as if it had been suddenly frozen while surging and churning like fierce waves.

Some of the lava looked like struggling madmen, some looked like leaping beasts, some looked like spreading-winged evil dragons. They had devoured the windows, doorways, and every other structure and defense built by the Wildhammer dwarves and Dragonmaw orcs on the surrounding walls of the hall, turning the great circular chamber at the center of Grim Batol into a black-grey, viscous vortex of coke.

And at the center of this vortex, on a semi-circular platform of dark brick like the scales of some monstrous evil, sat a slightly warped and deformed anvil.

Half of it was still intact, but the other half appeared somewhat collapsed. The shape of the collapse resembled both a dragon's claw and a human hand — as if it had been gripped fiercely by some terrifyingly powerful force. Lit crimson by the firelight reflected from the lava river behind, it looked like a bloody handprint.

Master Smith Burnhart stared at the anvil, nearly dumbfounded. A Dark Iron craftsman behind him murmured, "It's the Kadros Anvil."

"This is practically a real hell…" another dwarf craftsman behind him said, looking up at the severely collapsed ceiling, nearly toppling backward.

In this black, fire-lit tidal wave stood Prince Krasus, dressed in red. Upon hearing the dragonmen's announcement behind him, he turned around.

"Good evening, Jace. Aquestrastrz told me it might be some time before you could make it — I didn't expect you to arrive so quickly."
