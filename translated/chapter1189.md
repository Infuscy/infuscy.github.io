## Chapter 1189: Thunderfist's Decision

In the game, crafting the "Sulfuron Hammer" required collecting a large quantity of expensive materials… not only a considerable amount of Dark Iron, but also a massive quantity of Arcanite.

Arcanite was something Jace had never even seen in this lifetime. It was an enchanted thorium, typically smelted from thorium and a type of magic crystal containing arcane energy. Never mind Arcanite — Jace had rarely even laid eyes on thorium products.

Then there were the various elemental essences obtained from fire and earth elemental monsters. Compared to these, the flask of flame essence Jialene had used to craft her robe earlier was nothing at all.

Yet none of these were the most outrageous materials needed for forging the Sulfuron Hammer.

To successfully replicate this fearsome warhammer of the Firelord, one had to seize lava metal chunks from the powerful elemental lords that grew from the flames of Ragnaros's very body — the most important component of the Sulfuron Hammer: Sulfuron iron. It was a terrifying metal that burned madly, and was likely the main reason an ordinary anvil couldn't successfully forge this warhammer.

In the game, gathering all these materials together required either a fat wallet or the help of an entire guild's worth of people from every walk of life.

Clearly, the Thorium Brotherhood had once managed to collect these materials and attempted to secretly forge it on the Dark Anvil of Shadowforge City — only to be caught by the fire elemental wardens, who interrupted the process and seized the unfinished warhammer.

Which meant that here, these Dark Iron dwarves were capable of sourcing the materials on their own… Or perhaps, just like in the world of the game, they would gather the needed materials from mercenaries and adventurers all over the world who wanted to stay on the good side of the Thorium Brotherhood.

It's just that this time, all these materials would no longer be heading to Shadowforge City, but to Grim Batol instead.

Two of the Thorium Brotherhood smiths remained beside the Kadros Anvil to learn more about the situation here from Krasus, while Master Smith Burnhart and two other apprentices bid farewell to the red dragons and returned to Thorium Watch with Jace's group.

After the Master Smith recounted what he had seen and heard in Grim Batol to the watchtower's Overseer Ofester, the Brotherhood members at Thorium Watch all looked visibly excited.

Although the Dark Iron dwarves of the Thorium Brotherhood had brought along plenty of unpleasant personality traits from Shadowforge City — such as a deep hostility toward outsiders — at the very least, just as Roktus had said, they never stopped wanting to free their brethren in Shadowforge City from Ragnaros's curse and enslavement.

And after traveling for a stretch with a few Dark Iron cousins, Jace could feel that Grede was slowly letting go of his hostility toward them too.

On the flight back to Stormwind, Grede was also much more quiet than on the way there — no longer bickering with the green dragon, too lazy to boast the whole way.

Right now Solamriel was gliding south along the spine of the Redridge Mountains. The restless black winds from the Burning Steppes hadn't started blowing yet. Jace looked at the dwarf. "After this trip, have you changed your mind at all?"

Grede thought for a moment. "I still think King Magni Bronzebeard, or his brothers, would be more suited to taking up that warhammer than me."

"What, you're afraid of the Firelord?" Jace asked.

"Afraid?" Grede turned his head and pointed into the distance. "When the black dragon princess tried to escape, who was it that charged in without hesitation and smashed her escape to pieces with a hammer? I can march into Shadowforge City with the Bronzebeard's army. I can charge shoulder to shoulder with Dark Iron soldiers, go into that volcano and duel Ragnaros to the death, and see exactly what this self-proclaimed deity is made of. None of a that's a problem!"

After saying all this in agitation, he lowered his head and continued, "But… I don't think I can lift that warhammer, Jace. Especially after I saw Burnhart shed tears. You don't often see a Dark Iron dwarf cry. Even saying the words in our own language feels like your tongue is tripping over your teeth."

"Because it involves too much beyond fighting, killing, and the glory of the battlefield." Grede counted on his fingers as he spoke. "You know, Jace Sesso, when I went with you to face Deathwing in Grim Batol, it wasn't for the title of Mountain King. Of course, I definitely want to be recognized by all of Khaz Modan as Mountain King — but at the time, I simply didn't want you to die, that's all. Like Vereesa Windrunner, I was thinking either I'd pull you out of that mess, or we'd have to die together. That was it."

"But everything I've experienced since coming out of there… it's made me feel uncomfortable. I think you and Vereesa went through something similar in Lordaeron and Quel'Thalas. You should understand."

He sighed and continued, "All right, about being Mountain King — I don't even know what I want anymore. But at least I know for certain that I don't want to be the Wildhammer Lord Thunderfist. I don't want to be involved in Thunderfist family affairs. I don't want to be stuck at Aerie Peak sitting around with Falstad Wildhammer dealing with all that messy business. Maybe Falstad is good at that stuff, but I think I'm more like Grand Lord Kurdran — better at riding a mighty gryphon brother soaring between the sky and the mountains, gleefully hunting down the villains who get on my nerves. He has his Skarrel, I've got my Gorjet…"

"You feel like it's gotten mixed up with too much politics," Jace said.

Grede turned to look at Jace in silence for a moment, twitched his mustache, and raised a finger. "That's exactly it, Jace. Politics. In other words — if I really got that warhammer, if I really defeated the Firelord and his lackeys in front of King Magni, Falstad, Thaurissan, and all those lords and big shots, honestly, I'd rather die on the spot."

He swallowed and said, "At the very beginning, when you first mentioned the Sulfuron Hammer to me… I really didn't think too much about it. I just wanted a weapon of my own — not a Wildhammer ancestor's relic, not plunder snatched from someone else's hands, but one that truly belonged to me. I was thinking, if I defeat the Firelord wielding it, that ought to prove something, right? But looking at it now, I was still thinking too simplistically."

"But if you make me give up this opportunity… damn it, that's a warhammer that can defeat the Firelord!"

Grede growled this out with his head lowered. Jace couldn't help but laugh, and even Solamriel turned her head to glance at the dwarf.

"A lot of people want you to take up that warhammer, Grede," Jace said. "The true leader of the Thorium Brotherhood, Roktus Darkcontract, is the one who most wants you to do it — and he doesn't expect you to do anything else after it's done."

"What do you mean?" Grede asked.

"If you ask me, when that day really comes…" Jace gazed toward the wilderness in the distance. "Take it and run. Run straight to Kalimoth. Use the Sulfuron Hammer to smash the centaurs who want to bully Musa's family and let them feel some passion from another continent. No one will say a word. No one will dare say a word. That warhammer will be yours, without dispute. As for how the other dwarves carry on — to hell with that, it's not your problem either. If anyone wants to make trouble for you, they'll first have to buy a ticket to Booty Bay and then sit on a sailing ship for two months. Gryphons can't fly across the Great Sea."

Grede listened and couldn't stop a snorting laugh. He said nothing, just kept laughing. It was a long while before he cocked his mustache and looked ahead. "You won't help them out then, will you, Sola?"

"Of course I won't, Grede," the green dragon said, then chuckled softly too.
