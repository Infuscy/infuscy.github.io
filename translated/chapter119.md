## Chapter 119: Mage Armor (Part 2)

Jace obediently sat off to the side and watched Marin rise and straighten his collar.

So it had finally come to this. He was finally going to get his hands on one of the human world's top-tier School of Abjuration spells.

"This incantation is a dragon tongue spell, and a very long one. Listen to it once — don't expect to memorize it all at once. Just get the feel of the recitation right, and roughly remember the rhythm between it and the hand gestures, to form an initial impression."

As Marin spoke, he took out a dust-covered tome, flipped it open, and deftly found the page he wanted. He hoisted the heavy book and walked to the center of the room.

"Lokpotoria, Arkanoa…"

As Marin chanted, Jace could already feel arcane energy drifting through the air, swirling in a rhythm like a flock of singing birds. Ethereal arcane starlight gradually gathered over his body.

Jace had never seen spellcasting on this scale and couldn't help but stare, transfixed. Just as Jace thought the incantation was about to reach its conclusion, Marin kept going.

"Zlodatu, Unibelsoalun, Inguluku, Izikutu…"

This long?

Jace gradually started to feel like those mages in movies and games who needed to carry a spellbook around with them, to flip open whenever they needed to cast. Especially for a spell like Mage Armor — he couldn't rely on memory alone to cast an incantation this long. After all, if he suddenly got a line wrong one day, he might very well set himself on fire. Literally set himself on fire.

By this point, Marin's chanting had become almost a song. His voice grew rougher, and Jace could feel the arcane energy joining into the vocalization.

The ambient arcane energy had tinted the entire room a very faint pale violet, while the arcane energy on Marin's body had scattered in every direction — some of it even flying out the window and under the door.

Jace knew this was the process of the arcane seeking out elements and reshaping them. Driven by Marin's incantation, the arcane energy scattered like a pack of hunters, ranging everywhere to hunt down the elemental forces Marin desired.

"Elementarak, Itakxi, Bedyinkatu, Nazazunsa!"

There's still more!

But this time it finally ended. After Marin spoke the last line of the incantation, he clenched his fist sharply, and all the arcane energy instantly converged from every direction. Jace saw light like a rainbow suddenly contract, gathering all at once onto Marin's body. Colored light — a mirror-like layer of arcane radiance — covered his robes, his cheeks, and his hair, which trembled in the magical wind.

Jace grabbed the table and braced his feet to keep from getting blown into Marin by the gust.

"Mage Armor." Marin took a deep breath and looked at Jace. "It's done. It's been a very, very long time since I last cast this spell — I even thought I might fail this time." Jace could only describe the casting as spectacular. The entire room had practically become an ethereal ballroom during the process of Marin guiding the arcane.

Think about how in the game this is an instant-cast buff — you just press a hotkey and you're done. Simple and brutal.

"Would you like to give it a try?" Marin asked.

"Can I?"

Jace took the grimoire and walked to where Marin had stood. From the corner of his eye he saw Marin had already drawn his wand, ready to snuff out any rogue elements that might break loose at any moment.

"With Arcane Intellect's help, you should be able to attempt it," Marin said, pointing to the line of incantation in the book.

It was a line of dragon tongue with a Common phonetic transcription alongside it. Jace could tell that the Common transcription had several subtle differences from the incantation Marin had just recited.

Perhaps it was because Marin had pronounced the real dragon tongue, while this transcription was only meant to aid memory — much like what Jagin had done when recording the Life Drain incantation.

Which meant that following this transcription definitely wouldn't achieve Marin's level of effect.

Still, the good news was that Jace had listened to Marin's recitation under the effect of Arcane Intellect, and he'd concentrated intensely. With the hints from this transcription, plus imitating Marin's intonation, he felt confident he could reproduce the Mage Armor incantation in full.

Including the hand gestures…

"I'm ready," Jace said.

Marin nodded. "I'm ready too."

Jace glanced at Marin, swallowed, and began to chant.

"Lokpotoria, Arkanoa… Modolatu."

As he spoke, he waved his hand through the air the way Marin had, guiding the free-floating arcane energy to his side to receive his commands.

At first it went smoothly. From the corner of his eye, Jace caught sight of arcane starlight beginning to gather in the air.

But as the energy gradually accumulated, Jace felt his mental grip on it growing strained. The arcane energy was like a whole school of fish darting wildly through a lake, while Jace was holding seven or eight fishing rods in his hands, having to haul in these immensely powerful fish that desperately wanted to escape.

Marin's attention was also drawn awake by the free-floating arcane energy Jace had stirred up. He didn't notice that the faintly shimmering arcane energy on his own body was also peeling away bit by bit under the pull of Jace's chanting, like dissolving pixels.

Jace coughed, and Marin's gaze snapped back to Jace almost instantly.

"Don't stop halfway at this stage, Jace. Keep going."

Jace nodded, looked toward the window, and said, "Unibelsoalun, Inguluku, Zhongzikutu…"

By the time he reached this point, his fatigue had begun to accelerate and weigh him down. Was his energy reaching its limit? Was his casting not standard, or was the incantation not standard?

Maybe it was because he wasn't focused enough… No, Jace knew he couldn't let his mind wander anymore. He had to focus and finish the casting!

The arcane energy was already flying and swirling under his guidance. Everything was going smoothly — unexpectedly smoothly. Even Marin had stood up.

He seemed to have noticed that Jace was showing signs of exhaustion. Several times he looked like he wanted to open his mouth, seemingly to stop Jace from continuing — but each time he was about to speak, he slowly closed his mouth again, only watching those arcane stars remake the entire room into a corridor of magic.

Just hold on one more line…

Jace told himself: just hold on one more line.

"Elementarak!"

He gasped for air and forced out the next words: "Itakxi, Bedyinkatu, Nazazunsa!"

Jace clenched his fist the way Marin had, and his field of vision seemed to narrow drastically — so much so that everything around his clenched fist had gone dark.

A wild elemental whirlwind wrapped around him. Dazzling light poured in from every direction, struggling to illuminate the darkness in his vision — but the darkness swiftly devoured the last sliver of his sight, and the last shred of feeling along with it.

Jace felt a violent ringing in his ears overwhelm the last vestige of his senses. His legs went soft, and then he knew nothing at all.
