## Chapter 1192: The Name That Cannot Be Mentioned

That Morophil's defeat had been that impressive was a pleasant surprise, but Jace hadn't forgotten to ask about those Dar'Khan scrolls.

As it turned out, though, just as Jace had expected, the Dagger Society hadn't managed to glean anything useful from what they'd deciphered from the Void Lord in less than two weeks.

Judging from the notes and records Galene Ailuo had compiled, the information read like someone's transcript of conversations with the Void — some of it expressing worship, some issuing summons, recorded down to the last detail.

A few passages mentioned Void rifts, which more or less proved a connection between the scrolls and the Tygos Rift. But proving a connection was a long way from helping the Dagger Society actually pinpoint a set of Twisting Nether coordinates they could teleport to.

Still, the scrolls hadn't been fully translated yet, and there was plenty of unexplored content left to mine. Besides, Jace hadn't been counting on the Dagger Society to outpace Dar'Khan's team anyway.

It was just that, compared to the Tygos Rift, he'd actually been hoping to find something about manipulating shadow power… after all, the technique Dar'Khan had used to tear apart his shadow magic with his bare hands had been damn impressive.

They'd be heading to Draenor soon. If the teleport coordinates panned out, it meant they could be facing hordes of hostile Void creatures and swarms of Burning Legion demons.

With that in mind, Jace was always hoping he could take his shadow magic skills to the next level, and these scrolls were the most promising lead he had.

Of course, rushing things was usually the first step toward getting controlled by power instead — and that went for arcanists just as much as anyone, let alone warlocks, who were especially vulnerable to being consumed by their own emotions. Jace understood that well enough.

After finishing Galayn's notes, Jace was getting drowsy too. Morophil was sitting across from Gathosius, the two of them locked in a staring contest, and neither seemed bored yet.

"I heard from the master that your armor was shattered in the previous battle," Sazeno asked in demonic. "How is it whole again? Don't tell me there's someone in the Dagger Society who can forge Void armor?"

"My armor was forged by myself," the Void Lord said in a low voice.

"Well, that's a first," the imp muttered.

"I need to get some sleep — I've got to head to the Wizard Sanctum tomorrow," Jace said with a stretch and a wave. "You two keep it company here."

"Yes, master," Sazeno said. "I've still got so many things I want to ask it!"

Catching the look Morophil was giving Sazeno, Jace added, "No killing demons tonight."

"Tonight," Morophil nodded.

Sazeno laughed. "She might not even beat Lord Gathosius, master!" Jace pressed his lips together in exasperation and shut the practice room door.

He walked into the alchemy lab and collapsed into the lounger beside it. "I'm crashing here tonight, Ursula…"

"No problem." The alchemist tidied up her things, then said, "Jace, are you planning on going somewhere far again?"

"Not exactly. Tomorrow I might head over to Ilgara's Tower to check on the progress. Why?" Jace asked, leaning back in the chair.

"I don't mean that." Ursula said, "These past few weeks, your mood's been off. It's the same way you get every time you're about to go off and get yourself killed… I hope it's just my imagination."

Ursula was a warlock too, and as an alchemist who could brew all kinds of shadow-related potions — or emotion-manipulating concoctions like the Rage Potion — her sensitivity to moods was naturally sharper than your average warlock's.

Jace hadn't tried to hide anything here, but he hadn't expected her to pick up on it so quickly.

After all, every time Jace had been about to go somewhere to risk his life, he'd come here to prepare properly. Ursula was probably the person who knew his moods and state of mind best at times like this.

"Something like that," Jace said. "But I can't tell you where I'm going, Ursala. This isn't just about me."

"Fine." Ursula nodded, pressing a hand to her forehead. "Then at least tell me if there's anything I can do to help. That way, if I suddenly hear you've died out there somewhere, I won't regret not doing anything."

Jace couldn't help but laugh. Still, heading to Draenor definitely wasn't something you could do with a whole baggage train of potions — this wasn't a one-way trip, it was a long journey. So whatever he brought either had to last a long time or be something to save his life in a critical moment.

"Three Restorative Potions," Jace said after a moment's thought. "Two Elixirs of Brilliance would be ideal. And I'll also need one Elixir of the Ursol'khen."

Ursula leaned against the cabinet and studied him for a while before saying, "The things you're asking for don't exactly put me at ease, Jace. But I'll do my best to prepare them."

"I'll pay you," Jace said. "I've still got some gold on me. And don't mention this to anyone — best not to, even within the Dagger Society."

"Of course," the alchemist said. "Just come tell me before you actually set out, if you do."

"I will," Jace said. "Though I can promise you that if I come across any strange, unfamiliar herbs along the way, I'll bring some back for you."

"At least you've got a conscience," Ursula said, tilting her head.

The next morning, Jace arrived at Marin's study and knocked. An apprentice who looked barely more than ten or so opened the door.

The kid took one look at Jace in his black cloak and flinched. It took him a moment to remember to ask, "Who… who are you?"

"Jace Sesso. I'm one of Master Marin's students," Jace said. "Is the Archmage in?"

"You're — uh, master, good morning, I'm — Master Marin, he, she…"

Hearing the name, the apprentice looked completely dazed. He glanced back and forth, mumbling something incoherent, and Jace couldn't make heads or tails of it either.

Until the door to Marin's alchemy lab opened and Amy poked her head out from a cloud of drifting steam. She looked at the floundering apprentice and said, "Go back to your work, Mister Ceril."

"Yes, ma'am!" The apprentice nodded vigorously and practically fled back into the study.

"So I've become 'You-Know-Who' at the Wizard Sanctum?" Jace asked.

Amy laughed at the odd phrasing, then said, "Not quite. Kids just get spooked easily — it doesn't mean they don't respect you, Jace. If anything, the Wizard Sanctum has been cracking down hard on anyone secretly studying shadow magic, to keep people from getting ideas after hearing your story."

"So Master Magino Dumas finally has work to do again," Jace said with a nod.

"That's true enough." Amy raised an eyebrow and glanced at the clothes under Jace's cloak. "Nice new outfit. The reddish-brown suits you. Did you buy it specially for the Garden Festival?"

"You could say that," Jace said. "But it's a robe, Amy. Can't you tell?"

The smile vanished from Amy's face. She looked down in surprise. "Oh my god, wait…"

"It's May now. Even though Master VanCleef hasn't notified me yet, I'm guessing the reconstruction of Ilgara's Tower should be about done," Jace said. "Are you free today?"

Amy nodded. "Of course. Just let me finish brewing this potion for my dad."

Jace said, "Let's head to Stonewatch together. I'll tell you about this robe on the way — and I'd like you, as a member of the Kirin Tor, to test its quality for me."

"Now that you mention it… I'm curious too," Amy said. "Shall we ride gryphons?"

Sola had just gotten back from Grim Batol, and Jace hadn't planned on dragging her out of bed again. But he was a little surprised that Amy had volunteered to ride a gryphon.

"You can ride gryphons?" Jace asked.

"You seem to be underestimating me a bit." Amy crossed her arms. "Tell you what — I'll ride Old Marin's gryphon Womaji, you ride yours, and we'll race. Valley of Heroes to Lakeshire."

Jace laughed. "I think you might be misunderstanding the situation, Lady Amy Marin."
