Chapter 1193: A Mage's Riding Skills

The blue-tiled roof of the Zatar'a Tower, shimmering with a faint arcane glow, flashed beneath them and vanished. The whole forest surged toward them like a churning sea. Gorjett beat his wings furiously, dive-bombing toward a fading green speck ahead — as if it weren't a rival, but prey. Tiny, pitifully small, yet impossibly cunning…

"Grrrrrrrr…" He let out a furious low growl, his muscles coiled like steel beneath Jace, writhing with every beat.

Jace gripped the gryphon's neck and turned his head to watch the situation ahead. He knew full well he'd lost control of the beast.

He wasn't sure whether Old Marin's Stratholme gryphon was just that outstanding, or whether Amy had really learned, as she claimed, how to communicate with gryphons using arcane magic back in Dalaran. That little green monster flew like it had a gnome motor strapped to its back — launching off the ground as if it had been catapulted into the sky.

By the time Gorjett shook his head and caught sight of it, the thing had already gone "fwip-fwip-fwip" and disappeared.

But as a massive Alterac-bred gryphon, Gorjett proved the strength of his bloodline after all. Before they'd even caught up to Eastvale Logging Camp, Jace could already see Amy and her gryphon up ahead.

All along the way, splintered leaves and broken branches whipped up by the lead gryphon's wake flew through the air — and before they could fall, the larger, grey-white gryphon barreled through them, blasting them even further away.

Big talk was big talk, but if he couldn't catch up before Lakeshire… Jace clenched his right hand, half-surprised to find himself actually considering the Blood Grip.

Of course, if he really started playing dirty in this regard, he had no chance of outdoing a Kirin Tor mage. And besides, now that Gorjett's fighting spirit had been roused, it was anyone's race.

Womaji and Gorjett swept over Eastvale Logging Camp and the nearby barracks one after the other, drawing looks up from workers, soldiers, and passing travelers alike. Up ahead, Amy glanced back, and Jace knew — she was feeling the pressure now.

If Fel Empowerment would've worked on Gorjett, Jace would've been muttering it under his breath already. But he had no other tricks up his sleeve, so all he could do was "pray" haltingly in Dwarvish.

"Come on, Gorjett!"

"Catch him, mighty Gorjett…"

He stumbled through the Dwarvish: "If you catch that little runt up ahead, when we get back I'll talk to your keeper about improving your feed. Bloodfang Boar — even green dragons love the stuff!"

Unexpectedly, Gorjett seemed to understand — he cocked his head and let out a piercing shriek, the feathers around his neck growing hotter and hotter. "Bloodfang Boar," Jace repeated the key phrase. "Better food!"

Gorjett lowered his head and blasted a couple of bursts of hot air from his nostrils, then smashed through a cluster of high-reaching branches and charged at the rapidly closing Womaji once more. This time even Jace couldn't help but lean forward and bellow in Dwarvish: "For Khaz Modan!"

The yellow-tinged mountains had already come into view. Here, Jace could practically smell the lake. Terrified howls of wolves fleeing through the forest rang out now and then, but none of that mattered anymore…

Flying hard all the way from Elwynn Forest to the shores of Lake Everstill, Womaji was clearly running out of steam. When the red rooftops of Lakeshire finally appeared at the edge of sight, Gorjett made his final push, surging in from overhead. The blast of wind he kicked up not only blew the Stormwind lion banners fluttering on the tents along the shore, but threw Womaji's flight rhythm into such turmoil that the poor gryphon wobbled back and forth several times before finally managing to steady himself — by which point Gorjett had already reached the destination.

The grey-white gryphon charged, wild-eyed, toward the gryphon roost. Jace barely managed to haul him off course into a nearby clearing, but even so he sent every living thing within sight — keepers, gryphons, horses, and passersby alike — into a frenzy of panicked shouts and cries.

Gorjett's talons gouged a long trail of claw marks into the ground before he finally came to a stop. He turned and walked into the roost, spreading his wings and letting out a defiant, roaring challenge at the newly arrived Womaji overhead.

The green gryphon could only lower his head and touch down lightly on the ground, clearly wanting to veer away. It was only under the female mage's stern orders that he reluctantly allowed himself to be led closer by the gryphon keeper.

Here on his side, Gorjett looked like he wanted to frighten off anyone who came near. Jace kept patting his neck, calling for him to calm down, and it took a good long while before the gryphon abandoned the idea of pecking the nearest gryphon keeper's head off and the battle frenzy fully-fired up inside him was finally quelled.

After finally managing to settle him down, Amy had already walked up.

The female mage smiled at Gorjett, who was still steaming from his sharp beak. "I have to admit, you won, Jace."

"He's the one who won," Jace said, wiping the sweat from his hairline, and looked at Amy. "Your gridphon-handling skills caught me off guard, Amy."

"Kirin Tor members all need to learn how to get along with gryphons," Amy explained. "We even hired a troop of dwarven gryphon riders to teach us in Dalaran. After all, it's not realistic to set up a teleportation network linking all the mage towers, outposts, and cities across Lordaeron and Stromgarde."

"That works out perfectly, then," Jace said. "You can ride a gryphon between Ilgara's Tower and Stormwind in the future. That'll make it easier to find me when something comes up."

"Are you going to live at Ilgara's Tower from now on?" Amy asked, then slapped her forehead gently. "Ah, I almost forgot — you've already moved your parents to Stormwind."

The two chatted as they walked onto the Lake Everstill Bridge. Jace looked at Amy, hesitating for a moment on whether to tell her about Draenor right here. But with all the people coming and going on this bridge, he couldn't quite bring himself to say it. Better to wait until they'd seen the tower.

"There's going to be a lot of work in the coming days that'll rely on that tower's power," Jace explained. "The Taralfsin Crystal." Amy said. "That's why you asked Quel'Thalas for it?"

"More or less," Jace replied.

It was just past noon. The two grabbed a simple bite at the Lakeshire tavern. Once seated, Amy kept staring at the mithril chain across the chest of Jace's robe, looking deeply interested.

"Want to take a look?" Jace asked. Amy nodded. "Of course."

Jace took off his outer robe and handed it over. Amy held the short robe up to examine it, even casting a simple spell with one hand while holding it with the other.

After a faint flash of arcane light, she couldn't help but widen her eyes and let out a soft breath.

"Whose masterwork is this robe?" Amy had barely asked the catch before something clicked and she held up a hand. "Never mind, you don't have to tell me. This robe uses quite a few demonic materials — I can feel it. I'm guessing the creator wouldn't want their name known, especially not to a Kirin Tor member."

Jace smiled and nodded. Amy continued, "I may not know tailoring, but I've seen plenty of powerful robes in the Kirin Tor, Jace. This one really is extraordinary. It must've cost a fortune, right?"

"It took a great deal of effort," Jace said.

"Still." Amy carefully folded the robe and handed it back. "Strictly speaking, this robe wouldn't be legal in the Kirin Tor. Two illusion spells have been layered onto it to mask arcane radiation, I'd guess to conceal several extremely powerful demonic runes runes… the enchantment work is truly exceptional, to the point that I didn't even detect any magic on this garment at first. But if you're going to wear it to Dalaran, don't go showing it off to others on purpose. Just because I can't spot anything off doesn't mean someone else can't, Jace."

"I'm definitely not going to go looking for trouble with your Kirin Tor for no reason," Jace said.

Amy, who'd been sipping her juice, choked and coughed at that. Jace put his robe back on, thinking to himself that Galene Ailuo really wasn't messing around.

No wonder Galders held her in such high regard, even though she was at best half a mage and half a warlock, and her command of either school of magic was far from exceptional.
