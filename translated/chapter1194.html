Chapter 1194: The Stonemasons' Achievement

In the afternoon, the two continued on toward Stonewatch, where they both received a rather warm welcome. Jace hardly needed explaining — in this place, it didn't matter whether Jace's reputation in Stormwind City was the King's arcane advisor or some black sorcerer. The soldiers stationed here couldn't care less. All they knew was that Jace was the brave young man who'd ended up surrounded by a pack of gnolls to warn them of the ambush. And as Marin was one of the most respected archmages of the Wizard Sanctum, he was naturally quite well known at Stonewatch. His daughter was welcomed just as warmly. A Stonemasons member living at Stonewatch told Jace that the renovation work on Ilgara's Tower was essentially complete, and they'd entered the final stage of cleaning up and inspecting the interior facilities. It looked like Edwin VanCleef's promise was genuinely reliable — he'd said it would be done before summer, and it was done before summer. Considering that the Lake Everstill Bridge had dragged on from the end of the Orc Wars all the way through the fall of Lordaeron… this was remarkably rare. Leaving Stonewatch and heading into the forest, the various traces of war left behind by orcs and gnolls — the ruined camps, the debris — had all been swept clean over this period. On the whole, it was practically indistinguishable from a virgin forest. Amy walked ahead as they went, saying, "I haven't set foot in this place since the orcs showed up, Jace. The last time I walked this soil, I must've been only twelve or thirteen. Though it was hard to come here back then too — my father always thought there'd be too many gnolls around. I used to sneak in here to play whenever he came to Stonewatch on business." Listening to the female mage recount the amusing incidents she'd had here in the past, they approached Ilgara's Tower, where the arcane starlight floated in the air around it. By now it was nearly evening. The sky darkened quickly, and the forest rapidly turned dim and gloomy. As she chatted with Jace, Amy casually conjured an impossibly brilliant Light spell and tossed it ahead. The orb of light led the way from the front, illuminating vast stretches of the deep, dark forest. Having just heard Amy tell quite a few terrifying stories about gnolls, Jace couldn't help asking, "Aren't you worried about attracting gnolls?" "Attracting gnolls?" Amy turned her head and waved a hand. "No need to worry about me, Jace. I'm not that helpless little kid I used to be." Actually, Jace had been waiting for Amy to say something like "compared to gnolls, you're the scary one" or "I'm walking right here with you — what's there to worry about when gnolls are the least of our problems?" If Grede had been here, he definitely would've gone out of his way to say something like that just to mess with Jace. Jace had even whipped up a few witty comebacks on the spot, ready for exactly that kind of joke. Amy's response caught him off guard, and for a moment he didn't know what to say. But at the same time, Jace felt as though he'd finally let go of that last shred of reservation about her Kirin Tor identity. The last traces of the crimson sunset had vanished from the sky, and before the Blue Child and the White Lady had yet to show their faces through the treetops, Jace and Amy finally caught sight of the mage tower's silhouette up ahead, and the lights beneath it. Below the tower, the craftsmen's temporary camp had replaced the gnoll camp that had been in disarray before. Right now, two militiamen in simple thick coats and holding spears stood guard outside. Ilgara's Tower looked no different from the outside. Like in the past, it squatted on its high slope, surrounded and hidden by the towering ancient oaks around it, shrouded so thoroughly it even looked a little pitiful. "Ma'am! Sir!" the sentry over there called out. "Identify yourselves — you're getting closer and closer to private grounds where trespassing is forbidden!" "Jace Sesso!" Jace shouted back, holding up the Advisor's Ring. "Is Master Edwin VanCleef here?" "Master Sesso," the sentry replied. "He's right in the camp!" Before long, a not-quite-familiar figure walked out of the camp. Edwin VanCleef was dressing more and more like a nobleman these days. He looked to be in slightly better shape than before — or, put another way, he'd put on a bit of weight. Probably having sorted out quite a few internal Stonemasons problems and with work going more smoothly, he'd relaxed considerably in both mind and body. "Master Jace Sesso," VanCleef called out with a smile — you could tell from his voice alone that he was in much better spirits than before. The two shook hands, and he turned his gaze to Amy. "Lady Amy Marin. By the way, regarding the restoration work in the Mage Quarter — especially that apartment near Hafgan's shop — I've already had someone assess it. I'll find a chance to sit down with your father and discuss the next steps." "Wonderful," Amy nodded. "I'll tell him the good news when I get back." "Well then, Master Sesso." VanCleef glanced at his pocket watch. "Shall we go take a look at the inside of Ilgara's Tower now, or tomorrow?" "Let's go now," Jace said. "I've waited long enough getting here — I can't hold back any longer." "All right," VanCleef said. "Please, follow me." Amy was clearly already brimming with anticipation too, immediately quickening her steps to follow. After all, one of the two most powerful mage towers in Stormwind was right in front of her, and she'd have free access to it in the future. Even for a Kirin Tor mage, that was an enormous draw. Though there was no difference to the outside, the interior of Ilgara's Tower had been transformed beyond recognition. Just as he'd instructed, the rickety wooden staircase in the center of Ilgara's Tower had been completely torn out and replaced with brand-new stairs installed along both tower walls, running all the way up to the reinforced library and tower-top section. A faint scent of pine drifted through the interior of the tower, putting one at ease — especially for Jace, who'd lived here for a time before. To be able to purge every last trace of the rot and sour stench the orcs and gnolls had left behind the whole tower was no small feat. He looked up and gauged the open space in the middle. Fitting Kallrez in there shouldn't be a problem, but if he spread his wings, it might be a touch cramped. So if they moved him in, they'd need to give him a specific heads-up about that. They walked all the way up to the topmost tower level, which had also been largely refurbished — including the windows and roof beams. Anything that looked unsafe had been reinforced or had its materials replaced. From the inside, the upper half still followed the old style, but it was as clean and beautiful as if an entirely new tower had been built to the old specifications. "We consulted with people from the Wizard Sanctum, Master Sesso," VanCleef said, standing by the window. "They assessed how this mage tower's connection to the nearby ley line nexus has changed after the renovation, and confirmed there's no difference from before." "You can trust our work," he added, glancing at Amy's lavender cloak. "After all, the Kirin Tor spoke very highly of our mage tower construction at Nethergarde Keep." Amy nodded. VanCleef looked down at the record book in his hands and said, "His Majesty the King has settled all the payments, so in a few more days, once all our work is fully complete and the signing and sealing is done, Ilgara's Tower renovation project will be considered entirely finished." "So please help us spread the word, Master," he said in a low voice. "This Ilgara's Tower project is extremely important to the Stonemasons' reputation." "I'd do it even if you didn't ask, Master VanCleef," Jace said. "If there are other projects down the line, or if any of my friends need something, I'll come to you." "Then thank you very much," VanCleef said. "Well then, you're free to look around the tower on your own, Master, and see if there's anything else. The tower is in a perfectly livable state now — you can rest easy."
