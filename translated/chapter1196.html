Chapter 1196: The Unwitting Accomplice

The Stonemasons needed a few days for their final wrap-up work, and Amy took the two teleportation coordinates back to her apartment to study them. After returning to the Mage Quarter, Jace's first order of business was to write a letter to Wynneza telling her that Amy had come to Stormwind and had agreed to help with opening the Draenor teleportation gate. After that, he'd originally planned to follow Wynneza's suggestion and feel out Varian's opinion — test the waters a bit — but after thinking it over, Jace decided to hold off a little longer. At least wait until Amy had confirmed she could sort out the teleportation coordinate problem. Best to wait until everything was ready. Amy took a preliminary look at the two coordinates and figured it might take a week or two to make any progress. Over the next few days, Jace wasn't willing to just sit around waiting either, so he arranged to head out into the forest outside the city with Grede to get some practice in, preparing for the eventual entry into Draenor. Actually, Grede was more looking forward to going a few rounds with Kallrez than sparring with Jace. And Jace figured that sparring with Kallrez would probably give him a better preview of what Draenor would be like. But it hadn't been long since Sola got back from Grim Batol, and these past few days she'd been so tired that even meeting up for a meal found her drowsy and half-asleep. Dragging her out to fly through Deadwind Pass of all places — neither of them could quite bring themselves to do it. And Jace couldn't very well haul Kallrez to the front gates of Stormwind. So that idea would have to wait until Ilgara's Tower was finished and they could move him over there. Today, a new blacksmith shop in the Dwarf District was opening and had invited Grede to drink. The previous blacksmith shop's owner had done Grede quite a few favors before, so Grede couldn't very well refuse, which was why he hadn't come out with Jace. Perfect timing, then — Jace decided to head to the Mage Quarter and ask Amy about the progress on repairing the teleportation coordinates. She came to the Blue Heron for lunch every day at noon, and that hadn't changed since returning from the Kirin Tor, so she was fairly easy to find. Given that Jace's black dragonhide cloak was becoming somewhat recognizable at the Wizard Sanctum, people would occasionally spot him as he passed through the Mage Quarter. So this time he grabbed a plain light-grey linen cloak and headed to the Blue Heron, blending in among a crowd of apprentice mages and students. It was the lunch rush, and the tavern was quite lively. Young students were discussing a slightly comical Arcane Missiles mishap from the night before. Jace threaded his way through them, looking around — and sure enough, just as he'd expected, Amy was sitting by the window. On her table sat a red juice. She was propping her cheek with her hand, staring at several scrolls on the table while continuously twirling the quill in her fingers. Jace grabbed a cup of coffee and sat down across from her. Amy quickly rolled up the scrolls warily, and only after looking up to see Jace did she relax a little. "Jace… you scared me." "Do you need to be this on edge?" Jace asked with a laugh. "Don't forget what I'm working on…" Amy said, pointing at the writing on the scrolls, then lowered her head and flusteredly brushed her hair back. "Fine, just looking at this stuff probably wouldn't give anything away. I'm just too paranoid." Jace glanced at the densely packed writing on the papers. "What is this?" "Obviously, the task you gave me." Amy organized her thoughts, then said, "Both of those crystals recorded teleportation coordinates, and these two coordinates are very close to each other — at least both aimed at the same plane, right?" "Yes," Jace said. "The holder of the damaged coordinates told you that you need a relatively complete coordinate to repair it because the fragment this damaged coordinate lost is precisely the part that locates the plane, Jace." Amy held the quill between her fingers and gesticulated with both hands as if slicing bread for Jace. "You can think of this coordinate as an incantation. One part of the incantation helps the teleporter come to this world; the other part is responsible for sending the teleporter to a precise corner of this plane." "I more or less guessed that," Jace nodded. Amy made an assenting sound and continued, "If we can use the remaining fragment of the damaged part to replace the 'precise part' of that complete coordinate, we'd get a complete 'safe coordinate.' Sounds simple, isn't it?" Jace said nothing, because he could tell from Amy's tone that a "but" was coming. "But." Amy pressed a hand to her forehead. "After all, this isn't an incantation or a piece of writing that can be crudely cut and pasted, copying a section here and keeping a section there. This is an arcane message — and an arcane message left by the eredar. It's stored in that box like a mass of complex, shifting, bizarre three-dimensional images. Just interpreting what these images are trying to express and writing it down on paper…" Here, the female mage tiredly tilted her head against the wall. "These past few days I've felt like I'm back in the Kirin Tor. Those old mages just love finding problems that the younger generation can't possibly solve to mess with us." "But I shouldn't be this pessimistic," Amy said. "I'm not a Transmutation School mage. Give me some time, and I could probably take this back to the Kirin Tor and study it carefully for about two years — that should be about enough." Then she muttered under her breath, "And pick up a Transmutation School spatial magic mage ring while I'm at it." Hearing her say that, Jace touched the still-scalding coffee cup and fell silent for a moment. Because in reality, even if they went to Draenor now, the chances of encountering Alleria and Turalyon were already slim. Although the exact point at which they'd gone completely missing in Draenor was somewhat vague, what was certain was that it wasn't long before Ner'zhul used the Staff of Sargeras to tear Draenor apart. By now, it had been three or four years since the Dark Portal exploded. If they delayed another two years because of the coordinate issue, that would basically mean cutting off all hope. Of course, Jace would never actually charge into Draenor on a gamble using the orc warlock's coordinates the way Grede had suggested — he couldn't stake the lives of everyone in this crew on that. Seeing Jace lost in thought, Amy said a little sheepishly, "Sorry." "It's all right, Amy." Jace sipped the layer of fat floating on his coffee. "None of us have the slightest clue how to repair this thing — even Wynneza, who studied some arcane magic at the Faltherine Academy. And that big friend of mine can't fix it either, even though he claims he specialized in spatial teleportation at his old unit." Amy let out a guilty little laugh, took a sip of her juice, and said, "At first I really didn't expect it to be this difficult, because I know a lecturer colleague in the Kirin Tor who studies this kind of spatial teleportation coordinate. Maybe it's because I'm fairly familiar with him that I underestimated how complex this magic is." "Could we ask him for help?" Jace asked. "Impossible," Amy said, shaking her head at once. "The number of people in the Kirin Tor who can repair this kind of coordinate isn't exactly one in a million, but every single one of them would be able to tell at a glance what this is — because I know most of them have seen the Draenor teleportation coordinates sent over from the Dark Portal. Quite a few of them may have even participated in in-depth research." "So we'd have to find someone who doesn't recognize Draenor teleportation coordinates but has the skill to repair this?" Jace asked. "There might be mages like that," Amy said, lowering her head with a soft sigh. "But we can't take that risk. And I can't go around asking them one by one whether they've participated in Draenor teleportation coordinate research. Even setting aside whether it would arouse suspicion, those people probably wouldn't admit it to me out of pride even if they really hadn't seen them." As she spoke, Amy suddenly froze, staring at the papers and writing beside her hand for a long moment. Then she said, "I just remembered someone. She definitely hasn't participated in the research, and she can help us." "She?" Jace asked. "Who?" Amy stole a glance at the people beside them, then said in a low voice, "Jaina. Jaina Proudmoore." Hearing the name, Jace choked on his coffee. "What, what — who?" "You've met her," Amy whispered. "I know," Jace nodded. "But are you sure? She's not even fifteen yet." "Yes, which is precisely why she couldn't possibly have had access to Draenor teleportation coordinates," Amy said excitedly. "And when it comes to spatial magic, she's already performing better than many of her teachers, Jace. And she happens to be a solid student who doesn't just focus on casting experience but also emphasizes theoretical learning. Good lord — looking at it this way, this girl is practically the key that fate placed there, just waiting for us to solve our problem!" The female mage adjusted her posture, leaned over the table, and moved closer. "We don't need to hand her the actual crystals. I'll organize the information inside, write down the parts that are giving me headaches, and have her help me interpret them. After that, I can handle the rest myself…" "And there's another advantage to having her help. Judging by her current level, she should be able to enter the Kirin Tor in a few years at most. But she's the daughter of the Kul Tiras admiral. Archmage Antonidas is willing to teach her magic with all his knowledge to build friendly relations with Kul Tiras, but he wouldn't necessarily show her Kirin Tor secrets like the Draenor teleportation coordinates. So it might be a very long time before she figures out exactly what I showed her." "And that way, if things ever leak, she won't be implicated either, since she won't understand the situation," Jace said. "Yes," Amy said. "What do you think of this idea?" "If you think it'll work…" Jace said. "Then I'll get started organizing this stuff right away and do a little disguising…" As she said this, Amy seemed to perk up several times over. And not just Amy — Jace found himself straightening up involuntarily too. At the very least, he couldn't think of a better idea at the moment.
