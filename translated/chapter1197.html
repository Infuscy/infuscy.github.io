Chapter 1197: Time to Meet the Friends

The two grabbed a simple bite to eat, then walked out of the Blue Heron into the gardens. Jace had been turning the teleportation coordinate problem over in his mind the whole time.

Jaina might still be a well-behaved little darling at this point, but she was definitely no fool. So Jace asked with some concern, "How are you planning to tell her, Amy?"

"Hmm." Amy thought about it. "I'm not a Transmutation School teacher. If I just handed this to her as a practice assignment, it might actually arouse suspicion. But I can't ask any other Kirin Tor colleagues in this field for help either, so the only option is to ask for her help in the name of seeking assistance… I'll need to think this through carefully."

"Remember not to mention my name," Jace reminded her. "I'm afraid that the moment she hears it's connected to me, she'll immediately link these teleportation coordinates to the Twisting Nether."

"I know," Amy nodded. "But I'll need to go to Dalaran in person — no teleporting or sending an intermediary to deliver it. You have to understand, the mages who transport letters by magic are all Transmutation School. I'm worried they'd be quite sensitive to what's inside. The odds are small, but if something goes wrong, we'd all go down together."

As she walked, she continued, "And meeting Jaina in person will make her take it more seriously. If she has any questions, I can explain on the spot. That's more controllable."

"Right," Jace said. "Then I'll leave it to you, Amy. Safe travels."

Amy smiled. "Don't say things like that, Jace. It's just that if I leave now, I might need your help. My dad's heading to Stromgarde next month, so I can't ride Womaji. I was wondering if I could borrow Grede's Gorjett? I've never ridden an Alterac gryphon before, but I imagine it can't be that different."

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. "He's probably drinking with his friends right now. Let me go ask him."

"I'll come with you," Amy said. "I've been working on those magic coordinates all morning — I'm getting dizzy looking at them. A walk will clear my head."

The two made their way to the blacksmith shop in the Dwarf District. It was nearly lunchtime, and a crowd of dwarves was gathered at the shop entrance, chatting and laughing — quite the lively scene.

Jace spotted Grede from within. The fellow actually stood out as tall among a crowd of Bronzebeards. And he'd gone and put on a set of traditional Wildhammer leather garb — compared to the stiffly dressed Bronzebeards, he looked like a savage who'd run straight down from the mountains.

"Grede!" Jace called out, raising a hand. "Lord Thunderfist!"

The Wildhammer dwarf looked over, pointed at Jace, and gritted his teeth. "Shut it. Call me 'lord' again and I'll knock your front teeth out."

He stormed over in a huff, only to glance aside and notice the female mage standing there. The murderous expression vanished instantly. "Ah — cough — I didn't see you there, Lady Amy Marin."

"It's quite all right, Mister Grede," Amy said with a polite smile. The two had met before through Jace, but they weren't truly familiar with each other.

Grede turned back to Jace. "What do you want? I'm in the middle of a celebration here — can't you see how dressed up I am?"

"To borrow your Gorjett," Jace said.

Grede tilted his head and squinted sideways at Jace. "Didn't you just borrow him? After that last outing, Gorjett came back like he'd swallowed gunpowder. What's the occasion this time?"

He pointed at a Bronzebeard dwarf behind him. "I just promised that fellow I'd ride Gorjett to Booty Bay for him tomorrow to deliver some important cargo."

"Uh." Jace scratched his head. "So when will you be back?"

"Fastest is a week round-trip. I don't want to linger in Booty Bay." Grede said, hands on his hips. "Who knew you'd want to borrow him again so soon after the last time."

Jace nodded. Amy said, "A week is actually fine too. I can use the time to prepare carefully."

"So it's you who wants Gorjett, Lady Amy?" Grede stroked his beard and said to Jace in a low voice, "Can't you ask Sola? She'd definitely be willing to help."

"Then get back to your business," Jace said.

"So you're shooing me away the moment you're done with me?" Grede asked.

"You expected us to drink with you?" Jace narrowed his eyes. Grede grinned, waved his hand, and turned back toward the crowd of dwarves, shouting as he went.

"You heard him, Amy." Jace turned his head. "We've here got a friend who flies even faster than Gorjett who might be able to help you. But she's been busy these past few days, so I feel a little bad calling on her. But now that you're with us, Amy — whether she agrees to take you or not, you need to meet her."

"A friend who flies 'even faster than Gorjett'?" Amy looked as if she hadn't quite parsed what Jace was saying. She thought about it. "Uh… you don't mean that green dragon lady, do you?"

"Exactly," Jace said. "But don't mention it to anyone when we get back. Right now, in the whole Mage Quarter, besides your dad and us, no one knows there's a dragon living here."

"He didn't even tell me." Amy straightened up and nodded slowly, her breathing quickening. "Honestly, I don't think I'm ready to meet a lady like that yet…"

"Don't imagine her as some terrifying monster," Jace said.

In the afternoon, Jace brought Amy back to the Mage Quarter, through the alleys between the apartments, heading toward Sola's place. Along the way, Amy kept her eyes on the high windows and eaves, as if trying to spot some clue — a dragon head or a dragon wing or something.

They'd reached Sola's door and Jace had already knocked before Amy said in surprise, "She actually lives in this room?"

Before Jace could reply, Sola had already opened the door. She looked at Jace with a smile. "Good morning, Jace, and then…"

Sola jolted as if waking from a shake, her eyes going wide. "This is?"

Amy suddenly seemed at a loss for words too. Jace quickly made the introductions. "Sola, this is Amy. Amy, this is Sola."

"Ah, so you're…" Sola let out a breath. "You're Amy Marin. I've heard a lot about you from your father, Amy. It's a pleasure to meet you."

Amy immediately said, "The pleasure is mine as well, Lady Sola."

Sola stole a glance at Jace. "You told Amy about, uh, me? Everything?"

"Pretty much everything, including Draenor," Jace said, leaning against the doorframe. "We're all in the same boat now. Actually, the reason we're here to ask for your help is related to Draenor."

Sola nodded. Amy stood to the side a little stiffly, practically out of breath. After all, knowing that the blonde girl in front of her, who had something off about her demeanor, might be a green dragon in disguise — even a well-traveled mage couldn't stay calm about that.

"Come in," Sola said, gesturing in a low voice. "Don't talk in the doorway — there are people living at the end of that hallway."

After the two came inside, Amy was quickly drawn to the "decor" of the green dragon's dwelling, which was full of natural vitality, looking around unceasingly. After Sola closed the door and noticed Amy's gaze, she looked a touch pleased with herself.

She walked to the opposite side of the two of them, sat down, and asked, "So how can I help?"

"It's about the teleportation coordinates," Jace said. "Amy can't repair those teleportation coordinates on her own, so she needs to go back to Dalaran to find someone to help. That fellow Grede lent Gorjett out to someone, so we're out of options, Sola… which is why we've come to you."

Amy quickly added, "I want to say I don't mean any disrespect, Lady Sola. I'd be asking you to help take me to Dalaran — it would be an absolute honor for me."

Sola couldn't help but laugh. "Don't say that. I know you're a good friend of Jace's, so I have no reason not to help. And it's not far from here to Dalaran by flight — as long as we don't fly through Blackrock Mountain again, it won't be that exhausting."

"Sorry," Jace said with an embarrassed laugh. "So you're agreeing?"

"Of course," Sola said. "I don't have any ideas about the arcane energy in those draenei crystals. The only way I can help is by taking you on the journey. But once we reach Dalaran, Lady Amy, you have to treat me to a good meal."

"Absolutely," Amy said.
