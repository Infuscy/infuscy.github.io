Chapter 120: The Bishop

"The responsibility is mine. I am his teacher."

"Then what exactly was the spell?"

"Mage Armor?"

"Mage Armor — if I remember correctly, that's a very high-level Abjuration incantation. Honestly, I've been on battlefields and I've still barely seen a handful of mages use it."

"It isn't as practical on the battlefield as you'd imagine. After all, no matter how much energy you spend giving yourself stronger elemental resistance, once the enemy soldiers close in on you, you'll find you'd have been better off hurling as many fireballs and whirlwinds as you could to slow their advance."

"Light above, I've only just noticed this sigil — he's merely an apprentice?"

"Alas, yes. An apprentice who'd barely been one for a month. He was dead set on learning Mage Armor, and I saw he had a somewhat unusual talent, so I thought a difficult spell like that could gradually guide him faster through the gates of the School of Abjuration."

"Plainly, his mana reserves were insufficient."

"His mana reserves… fine, I don't want to get into a deep discussion with you about what exactly happened. I understand what you're trying to say, Bishop, but you didn't see the situation at the time. If you were me, you wouldn't have been able to resist letting him cast it either. That was his first attempt at casting Mage Armor. Honestly, it was stunning. I was hoping he'd pull it off in one go and give me a story I could talk about at the Wizard Sanctum for the next twenty years without getting tired of it. Unfortunately, I just wanted him to succeed too much, and it ended like this."

"That's not like you, Marin — so eager for glory, so reckless. We've known each other a long time, and I've never seen you willing to take a risk like this. Then again, I don't visit the Wizard Sanctum much, so I'm not familiar with your ways. Perhaps every teacher hopes their student surpasses them — thinking of it that way, it's quite normal."

"Maybe you just don't know me well enough."

"I'll admit, after this war sparked by the Dark Portal, I no longer dare claim to understand anything. Too many things have been beyond expectation — too many things have even shattered what I once held to be common sense… Huh? He seems to have woken up. Is it because we've been talking too loudly?"

"You talk as if you were hoping he wouldn't wake up."

Amid the back-and-forth of conversation, Jace forced his eyes open — immediately followed by a headache he could barely endure.

He grimaced and groaned, clutching his head for a while, until a hand came to rest on his chest.

"By the Light, I beseech You to release this young man from this suffering, to set him free and deliver him. For his suffering was born of effort and diligence; his suffering was the setback met in striving toward a nobler ideal. O Light, I know You admire courage, admire hard work, and admire the will to prevail — and he is a follower who walks the path of Your teachings…" Amid the low murmuring, the headache miraculously eased. Jace could even feel how powerless and useless his own hands, pressed against his head, were compared to that warm hand on his chest.

"A good sign," a low voice said beside his head. "He's woken up this quickly — at least you didn't make any grave mistakes, Master Marin."

"A mercy," Marin's voice came from the foot of the bed.

Jace tried opening his eyes again. The light was a bit harsh.

He found himself lying in an extremely bright room — the bricks and stones were all white, making the whole space feel especially clean and sacred. This was most likely the hospital at Cathedral Square.

Standing beside him was a church priest in gray robes, holding a silver mitre in his arms. Marin sat at the foot of the bed. Jace tried to sit up, but the moment he moved too much, his head throbbed with a dull ache again.

"Don't get up so fast, Mr. Sesso," the priest said, steadying him. "What matters most for you right now is rest."

"How long was I out?" Jace asked weakly.

"Not long," Marin said. "A few hours, roughly."

He glanced to the side. "I told you already, Archbishop Benedictus — it's not as serious as you think."

"Which means the Light still favors this young apprentice," the Bishop said with a smile.

"Thanks be to the Light," Jace muttered, coming back to his senses.

The moment he felt his mind beginning to turn under his own will, he suddenly caught the name Marin had just mentioned and turned to stare intently at the priest beside his bed.

This bishop was probably in his fifties, not yet sixty — about Marin's age.

A head of thick, dark-gray hair, with a hairline that hadn't receded too far for his age. A carefully trimmed beard. His forehead and the corners of his eyes were creased with wrinkles.

Though past his prime, he was still powerfully built — broad-chested and broad-shouldered, without having gone soft yet. He looked like an old warrior who'd been through heaven knew how many wars.

Benedictus? The Archbishop of Stormwind himself is personally looking after me?

No — he wasn't the Archbishop yet. The current leader of the Cathedral of Light and the Church of the Holy Light should be Archbishop Alonsus Faol. Benedictus was still Faol's student.

Alonsus Faol would die in the Scourge invasion more than a decade from now, and only then would Benedictus inherit the seat of Archbishop.

It was just that the road Benedictus walked after that was cause for worry.

The Scourge War and the destruction of Lordaeron dealt a devastating blow to Benedictus's faith, making him doubt the Light more and more, until he ultimately walked to the other extreme — falling into the ways of darkness, being exploited by an Old God, and becoming one of the leaders of the Old God-worshipping cult known as the Twilight's Hammer.

By then he'd become a powerful shadow priest, a dark priest. In truth, his communion with the Shadow had already begun while he served as Archbishop of Stormwind — it was only when Deathwing shook the world that his true faith was exposed. But right now, he should still be quite devout toward the Light, shouldn't he?

Thinking all this, Bishop Benedictus exchanged a few pleasantries with Marin and had already left the room. Even if he wasn't the leader of the Cathedral of Light, he was still a busy bishop — he couldn't linger here for long.

As he left, he glanced back at Jace from the doorway. It was a glance that seemed deliberately turned back to look. Given Jace's current state and what he knew about Benedictus, there was no way he could keep his mind from racing.

After all, Jace was a warlock. If the first time he'd gone to a priest for treatment of necrotic poison he could only be counted as a rank amateur warlock who'd barely crossed the threshold, now — able to use Fear with ease and having mastered multiple schools of Shadow magic — he was probably one of the most skilled Shadow practitioners in all of Stormwind. At least, that was the case at the Slaughtered Lamb.

He didn't know what kind of treatment he'd undergone while unconscious, or whether Benedictus had noticed something. But Jace was certain that even if the Bishop had sensed the Shadow, he hadn't said anything to Marin.

Jace could only pray that that look had merely been a doctor's concern for his patient.

Marin said, "I don't know whether to say you were lucky, Mr. Sesso, or whether to credit the Bishop's devotion."

"I passed out from mana overexertion?" Jace asked.

"Your mana wasn't enough to sustain the arcane forces you summoned, Jace — their activity exceeded expectations," Marin said. "In short, you ignored my warnings and forced a high-level spell, but your own energy was insufficient and you overloaded your willpower. But losing consciousness wasn't the most severe consequence — that's why I say you were lucky enough."

"When did you ever warn me, Master Marin?" Jace said. "When I was casting, I remember you being especially eager for me to succeed."

Marin stroked his chin. "You can tell people that, and that's fine. An apprentice can make mistakes — no one will blame you."

Jace had an accident while learning a spell, and as the supervising instructor, Marin definitely had to take the bulk of the blame. Even if Jace said otherwise, Marin still bore responsibility for teaching him the incantation, didn't he?

Fortunately, there were no serious consequences this time, and probably not many people knew about it, so distancing himself from responsibility as much as possible likely wouldn't draw any investigation.

Still, after this, would Marin even continue teaching him the Mage Armor incantation?

"Did I almost pull it off, Master?" Jace asked. "I really felt I was just a little bit away. It's such a shame — I was already close to completing the spell at the time, wasn't I?"

Marin neither confirmed nor denied it. He only said, "No one knows whether, if your energy had reached the level needed to cast Mage Armor in full, you could have succeeded in the end. Everything will have to be verified by the next attempt. If you still want to make progress before you leave for Dalaran, come to me in the mornings. I won't be handing the incantation over for you to memorize for now. I hope you remember our agreement."

"Practice Mage Armor only in your presence," Jace repeated.

"Exactly. Practice it only under my supervision," Marin said. "If I were to tell you the casting method for Mage Armor and you practiced it in secret, then passed out just like this time with no one around to tend to you or treat you — think about what the consequences would be."

That was true. Especially if he practiced at home and set the house on fire, or passed out in his room and got discovered — then everything hidden in his house would get the grandest of grand exposures.

Marin continued, "Next time we'll start with the fundamentals of the School of Abjuration, to prepare you for when you enter the Wizard Sanctum to study. Can't run before you can walk — the consequence of this accident was nothing but the bitter fruit of being too aggressive."

"But there are only two or three days left before we leave Stormwind," Jace said.

"Then we'll talk about it when we get back," the mage said.

When we get back? Jace could hardly wait as it was.

"Understood, Master."

He'd wanted to leave, but Marin forced him to stay and rest at the hospital for a full day, making sure he'd fully recovered before he could go.

Though Jace didn't know exactly where he was within Cathedral Square, at night he could hear the choir singing drifting over from who knew how many walls away.

Lying in bed, Jace still couldn't get the incantation Marin had taught him that afternoon out of his head.

"Lokpotoria, Arkanoa…" Just reciting the first couple of lines from memory, and Jace could already feel that headache creeping back. Recalling anything beyond that was utterly impossible.

Marin was right — he was still far from fully recovering. Best not to tangle with the arcane ley lines for now. He'd get a good night's sleep first.

But there was no way he was going to spend the two or three months away from Stormwind practicing no spells at all. He'd already learned half of Mage Armor — how could he just stop midway?
