Chapter 1200: Getting Along with the Mage Tower

When Magister Rommath left, he left Jace with a large stack of books on how to utilize a mage tower's magic crystal, to help him quickly figure out how to get along harmoniously with the Taralfsin Crystal.

However, these books had several rather serious problems. For one, they were all written in rather obscure and difficult Thalassian. Although Jace had dabbled a bit in this "field" of Thalassian, on the whole he was more familiar with everyday conversation. For his current Thalassian level, he still needed to read with a dictionary at his side.

The comforting part was that Rommath had actually left him a Thalassian-Common bilingual dictionary. However, this dictionary had been compiled during the founding era of Dalaran. Aside from some profound and obscure specialized vocabulary covering natural geography and magic, most of the word usage was completely different from today's.

Of course, looking at it from the bright side, this happened to make up for Jace's weakness of being familiar with everyday conversation but not understanding specialized vocabulary.

The second problem was more serious: the books on maintaining mage towers and employing the power of magic crystals made no mention of the Taralfsin Crystal. The several other types of crystals the Grand Magister had introduced to him on the Eldarath River, on the other hand, all had roughly corresponding content.

Since the Taralfsin Crystal wasn't a typical elven mage tower energy crystal, this was quite normal too. Of course, Rommath had also taken this into account and specifically left some relatively easy-to-understand articles researching the Taralfsin Crystal. But these didn't teach a mage how to use a mage tower equipped with this kind of crystal either.

In other words, aside from the basic knowledge about the mage tower and the crystal itself, exactly how to utilize the combination of the two was something Jace would have to figure out on his own.

Fortunately, this crystal didn't need to be adjusted by hand for every single parameter like a gnomish mechanical product, with every function switched on and off personally. The moment it was activated at the tower's peak, Jace had already felt it beginning to stabilize the ley line energies that had become extremely unbalanced due to the Karazhan Cataclysm, while also controlling the level of demonic energy within the tower.

As long as it could do this, it was enough for the needs of Jace's crew going forward. After all, he didn't need to use this mage tower to deal with orcs and the Black Dragonflight, or gnoll invasions, Dark Iron dwarf uprisings, and the like.

By evening, as the sky grew darker, the crystal atop Ilgara Tower naturally shone even more brightly. After reading all day, Jace looked out the window — everything in his vision seemed to be covered in a layer of dark purple-blue.

Jace went downstairs and came outside. Grede had already been sitting under the tree early on, staring up at the top.

Keeping him company in staring were the two felhounds crouching not far away. They too raised their heavy heads and stared straight at the slowly rotating magic crystal, as if staring at it a bit longer would let them absorb some of the residual mana radiating from it.

"Staring at it for too long makes you feel like your soul's being sucked in, Jace," Grede muttered. "You can try it too."

Jace glanced at the Ursolus placed at his side, then asked the dwarf, "Do you know what it feels like to have your soul drained?"

"Don't put on that innocent look, you warlock who messes around with succubi every day," Grede said, pointing up high. "Just stare at it carefully and you'll know."

So that's what he'd meant. Jace gave him a sidelong look, but still did as he said and stared intently at the rotating, shimmering Taralfsin Crystal.

Compared to Grede, Jace could naturally perceive much more from the crystal. After all, he'd already had a fairly deep exchange with it back at Tranquillien, and here too he'd activated it with his own hands.

Gradually, he could even sense the rhythm of this entire forest from within the crystal. It was just that to Jace at this moment, these were still some indecipherable noise and chaotic undulations. But Jace believed that as long as he continued to learn and familiarize himself, and after enough training, he might be able to use this crystal to monitor the Stonewatch Forest — and perhaps even farther.

Just as he was gradually immersing himself in the crystal, Grede suddenly let out a low exclamation. "Damn it, did you do something?"

"What?" Jace asked, snapping back to attention.

"It's changed color a bit," Grede said.

Jace looked over at the crystal. It had indeed grown redder, though right now it was returning to its original slightly bluish purple-red.

Was this related to the process of him making contact with it? Jace wondered.

One of the books sent from Quel'Thalas mentioned that a mage establishing a connection with his own mage tower was roughly a process of mutual adaptation to achieve a final harmony. Many mages would use specific incantations and implements to accelerate this process.

"Is it my imagination?" Grede asked again.

Jace didn't answer immediately, because he wasn't entirely sure either.

He looked at the dwarf, then turned his attention back to the crystal once more, immersing his consciousness in the crystal's mana just as he had before.

"Yulaxinka, Bistanta…"

Jace chanted the incantation. Grede immediately widened his eyes and took several steps back. "What are you doing? Why are you chanting the Shadow Bolt incantation?"

Jace ignored him. He wasn't simply drawing on shadow power from his surroundings and his own body — he wanted to use the incantation to channel the crystal to extract balanced, pure shadow power through it.

Of course, it didn't go smoothly at first, because he'd never done this before.

His incantation and the shadow power he subconsciously called from other sources spiraled and gathered in his hand, forming an increasingly large shadow sphere — but this wasn't what he wanted.

He interrupted the casting, scattered these forces, and focused his attention on the crystal once more, chanting the incantation.

"Shh, don't talk."

Jace pressed a finger to his lips, glanced at Morophil, who was resting her chin on her hand and watching him from the high window, and tried to shut out the fervent desire and suppressed longing burning in her eyes. Instead, he groped for the loneliness and bitterness of being alone, searching for the rigid feeling of punching a metal wall with all your might when facing an insurmountable hardship…

As these emotions were summoned from the void and swelled and burned more and more fiercely under Jace's skilled manipulation, he finally felt as though he'd grabbed hold of something in the flowing river of mana.

The Taralfsin Crystal blazed with purple-red once more following this sensation, even rotating more and more slowly, eventually coming to a near stop. The entire forest was dyed red by the blood-purple radiance emanating from the mage tower.

Grede looked at the crystal again. Morophil by the high window couldn't help but raise her head to look at the tower top. The flames in her eyes had even turned the exact same color as the crystal, pulsing with the same frequency.

This was the feeling.

"Yulaxinka, Bistanta." Jace chanted the incantation once more. This time he drew power directly from the crystal, slowly gathering an extremely unstable shadow core in his hand.

This somewhat "fragile" shadow sphere was far less powerful than the Shadow Bolts he conjured when casting on his own, let alone the Shadow Bolts he summoned using the Ursolus, which were comparable to some kind of soul bomb.

But the most important thing was that summoning this mass of shadow had cost him almost no mana.

At that moment, a thought suddenly popped into Jace's mind.

Of all the magic he'd mastered, the most mana-intensive was undoubtedly Shadowstep — or, to be more precise, casting Shadowstep in rapid succession.

The Shadowstep he'd learned from Morophil used his own consciousness and energy to drag the surrounding dark energy around him, wrapping himself in shadow and suddenly flashing a great distance away.

Because the spell had to be cast quickly, Shadowstep didn't have the dedicated conditions for channeling and gathering power that Shadow Bolt or Hand of Gul'dan had. After one Shadowstep grabbed the shadow energy floating nearby, a consecutive second one could only drag the shadow that was harder to channel — thus consuming several times more mana than the first Shadowstep.

For demons, this was much simpler. Demons like the sayaad were practically lumps of shadow and fel energy condensed into flesh. But mortals couldn't move the way Morophil did, so in the game, the rogue's Shadowstep was designed as a long-cooldown ability for a reason.

?

But what about now? Jace looked at the Taralfsin Crystal, thinking — what if he used the crystal to channel shadow mana to fuel Shadowstep?

He set his sights on Morophil at the window, once again establishing a connection with the crystal through his consciousness, driving the shadow power within to surge over and wrap around him…

A powerful dark wind dragged him straight toward the tower. Grede gasped in alarm, his eyes barely tracking the direction of the dark force's charge. In the next instant, Jace was already clinging to the middle of the mage tower's wall.

Jace immediately drove the next Shadowstep. This time, empowered by the crystal, he didn't even feel as though he'd consumed any mana before being carried by another surge of shadow power upward. Morophil's shadow was already appearing almost at his fingertips…

Suddenly, Jace felt as though this force had lost its momentum all at once. He was simply "thrown" out of the dark current into midair, on the verge of losing all support…

In his peripheral vision, Morophil at the window had already grabbed the window frame, as if she wanted to leap out and catch Jace directly. But in that instant, Jace directly used his own mana to drive Shadowstep and flashed behind the succubus — except his body still carried the inertia of being suspended in midair and hadn't found solid footing, so he inevitably stumbled several steps forward and crashed into an empty storage rack.

Morophil turned her head to look at Jace, smiling as if looking at a clumsy student. "I thought the master of the Ursolus was going to fall to death outside his own mage tower, darling."

Jace gave an embarrassed laugh. From below came Grede's roar.

"Jace, are you all right?!"

Jace put his hand on the railing beside the stairs and waved. "Still alive."
