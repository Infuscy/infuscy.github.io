Chapter 1201: Absolutely Not a Prison

With the crystal installed and in place, the next step was to perform the summoning ritual. The next morning, a troop of cavalry arrived from Stonewatch — probably because the unusual light released by the Taralfsin Crystal last night had caught the attention of the watchtower sentinels. The Stonewatch garrison had always been highly vigilant about strange lights in the forest, since it was quite likely to be gnoll sorcerers up to no good. Considering that the last time Ilgara's Tower had been activated was ten years ago during the orc invasion, it was only natural that such a light appearing now would worry them. Jace vaguely remembered that the first time he'd come to Stonewatch, it had been a gnoll warlock who'd drawn the cavalry away with shadow magic that had found the opportunity to launch a surprise attack on the walls. This time, only three cavalrymen had come, probably having noticed that this phenomenon was related to Ilgara's Tower. After explaining the matter of the magic crystal to the Stonewatch soldiers, they left contentedly. After seeing the soldiers off, Jace began drawing the circle at the base of the tower according to the blueprint he'd designed earlier. Because it involved many modifications and other circles combined together, this summoning circle took a very long time to draw — from morning all the way into the afternoon. In the middle of it, Grede had even started dozing off watching. As the sky grew somewhat dim, Jace ignited the portions of the circle drawn with his own blood with shadow flame in one go. The entire great hall of the tower's ground floor was illuminated purple-red by this flame. "Feels quite good, master," Sazeno said from the side. "The power of that mighty elven crystal is stabilizing the mana of the summoning ritual. I'm certain that performing the summoning here will be simpler and more reliable than summoning in that ogre circle!" "Let's hope so," Jace said, nodding. At this time, the burning flames grew increasingly shrill with the sharp, distinctive sound of shadow, and Grede beside him couldn't help but scratch his ears and open his eyes. "Uh-huh?" the dwarf said, looking over. "We starting now?" "Do you have any other questions?" Jace asked. "Will that demon throw a tantrum because he's trapped here?" Grede asked. "You're not worried?" "Isn't that exactly the reason you're here keeping watch?" Jace said, looking at the shadow flames gradually spreading across the circle. "If things go wrong, then like you said — we'll just have to fight." Grede said nothing, just hefted his warhammer and muttered something under his breath in Dwarvish. It sounded like he was hoping everything would go smoothly and hoping that demon wouldn't have too bad a temper. It seemed the scene of Kallrez fighting Onyxia head-on in that burning elemental plane had indeed made quite an impression on this dwarf — especially when compared to the time he'd been beaten black and blue by the Black Dragon Princess earlier. This wasn't the first time Jace had summoned Kallrez, so he had a lot more confidence this time too. He walked to the summoner's position in the center of the circle, raised the Ursolus high, closed his eyes, and began chanting the incantation. "Velinka, Toxintani, Tostesta, Subinas Ereduin, Kallrez Ma'ali…" Although the distance to the being he was summoning wasn't that great, this was still the summoning of a demon lord. The interior of Ilgara's Tower began to tremble faintly. Jace felt the ley lines become unstable as well — but all of this was alleviated by the response of the Taralfsin Crystal. The things the Kirin Tor set their sights on were good things indeed. Otherwise, how could they have kept the Violet Hold calm for hundreds of years. As the crystal's energy activated, the mana quickly returned to calm, and Jace gained an extra layer of confidence in his heart. He thought back to when he'd originally wanted to borrow the power of the ley line nexus beneath Ilgara's Tower to summon Kallrez back from the depths of the lost Twisting Nether. Back then, he hadn't even thought about getting a mage tower crystal to stabilize the mana from anyone. Referencing the summoning altar built by those ogres in Deadwind Valley — if not for the magic crystal, and with Kallrez being so far away, even if the summoning ritual could be completed successfully, the power of the Twisting Nether would probably be enough to tear the tower apart and collapse it directly… He continued the summoning ritual. Soon, a crack in the Twisting Nether unfolded from the center, and the murmuring mutters of Kallrez came from within. Void winds poured out from it. A considerable portion was soothed by the crystal's power, but plenty still whipped up a continuous whirlwind within the tower. Grede held his hood steady. Morophil also grabbed the handrail of the nearby stairs — and took the opportunity to seize Sazeno's tail. Jace ignored the "flap-flap-flap" of pages turning at the top of the tower, paid no mind to the sounds of unknown objects shattering from time to time… Soon, the crack grew larger and larger. Kallrez reached out a hand from within — one claw grabbing the edge of the crack, the other reaching out to press down on the center of the circle. "Mmm, so this is the 'new environment' you promised, Jace Sesso," the doom lord muttered in Demonic as he poked his head out. He turned his head back and forth, surveying the surroundings. His long horns scraped against the wall — fortunately, the mage tower's outer wall was solid enough that it only left a scratch. "Watch it — this tower is my property!" Jace shouted in Demonic against the howling gale. "Your tower? Your property?" As Kallrez spoke, he stepped onto the ground. The foundations and ground floor of Ilgara's Tower were far sturdier than the altar the ogres had thrown together. Protected by the circle empowered by the ley lines, only the few bricks where he'd planted his feet solidly had been cracked. Jace frowned at those few bricks. At this moment, the great demon crawled fully out of the crack and tried to straighten up and spread his enormous wings — Jace immediately roared again. The doom lord was so startled by the roar that he stopped moving at once, as if frozen in place. The demonic shadows pouring in from the crack in the Twisting Nether scattered and spun away. As the vision within the tower cleared, Kallrez slowly turned his head and finally realized where he was. He raised a finger and touched the window not far from the tip of his horn. Then undisguised fury surged into his mind. His hair even ignited in flames on the spot, and fire spurted from those small eyes squeezed fleshless. "You've found me a cage?! Jace Sesso!" The doom lord extended a sharp nail that nearly poked Jace on the forehead, cursing viciously in Demonic: "Anuuk — Amil… Just you wait, idiot! Wait until Kazoth dies, wait until this accursed contract between us is fulfilled — I will kill every wretched maggot writhing on Azeroth, including you! I'll personally smash these toy-like towers and walls of yours to pieces, and hang you and your friends, everyone you know, from my horns as decorations!" "You mean, after the contract is fulfilled?" Jace said, pointing at the succubus beside him. "Fine. When the time comes, you can make me and the Lady Morophil here into belt buckles." "Really, master?" Morophil practically leaped up in excitement. "Is that really allowed? Then the rotting flesh of us two could be forever tightly entwined under the tempering flames of fel fire…" "Uh…" Jace's gaze shifted toward the succubus. He coughed to cover his embarrassment. After all, he'd only said it offhand, and Morophil's description had dragged this joke somewhere completely bizarre. Seeing the seemingly enraged doom lord and the inexplicably delighted succubus, Grede felt the atmosphere was a bit off too, but he didn't understand Demonic and could only look back and forth in bewilderment. "Shut up, sayaad." Kallrez snarled angrily. Morophil shrugged with a "how boring" expression. The great demon turned back to Jace and said in Demonic, "Don't think I don't know what tricks you're playing. Don't think you can stall for time like this forever. Let me out — I'm only warning you once." His nail was practically a black broadsword to Jace, and several times, as this demon raged, it nearly pierced his chest. "What the hell are you two saying, Jace?" Grede asked in a low voice. Jace patted Grede's shoulder, stared at the stiff, warg-like face of the doom lord pressing right up against him, and said in Common: "Before you fly into a rage, feel the power of this tower, Kallrez. Karazhan's shadow power is, after all, the aftershock of an explosion. The true power held by that ancient tower — you could never extract even a trace of it. But here is a completely different story." Kallrez said nothing, because he knew Jace was right. This great demon straightened up and looked around once more, then quickly raised his head to look toward the tower top. Jace knew — he'd sensed the presence of the Taralfsin Crystal. Kallrez was clearly larger than when he'd first descended at the Storm Altar, or to be more precise, he'd recovered somewhat. It was probably because he'd been rather well taken care of by the ogres who worshipped demons and dark magic in Deadwind Valley during this time. So right now, as he straightened up and raised his head, the space within the mage tower was clearly more cramped than Jace had anticipated. However, his power hadn't seriously exceeded the carrying limit of the Taralfsin Crystal. This same model of crystal, capable of controlling all the demons within the Violet Hold, had experienced a period of turbulence while summoning the demon, but had now returned to normal — and had smoothly brought the great demon confined within the shadow-binding circle into the scope of its stabilizing mana. By now, the doom lord's fury had clearly been doused considerably by the mana of this tower. He lowered his head and looked at Jace. "This is a ley line nexus of this world…" "Yes," Jace said, raising both hands. "Think about it, Kallrez. For a hunter, what could be more comfortable than prey delivering itself to your door? How could this be considered a prison? It's practically a buffet! And all you need to do is use the power of this tower to send us to hunt down your enemy Kazoth… this is practically a sure-win deal." The great demon fell silent, and the fel fire sprouting from his head quieted. Although he said nothing, Jace knew he'd probably been convinced for the time being. After all, as long as mana was supplied continuously, demons were generally quite easy to deal with. "Not bad," Kallrez said in a low voice in Demonic, slowly lowering his head as his horns scraped a shallow mark on the stairs beside him. "Damn it," Jace sighed in exasperation.
