## Chapter 1204: The House of Wryn's Psychological Baggage

The cries of soldiers announcing his arrival echoed through Stormwind Keep, mixed with his full name and the title of Court Mage Advisor. Before long, Varian came hurrying over from the throne hall and gave Jace a meaningful look toward a door beside him.

Once the two of them were inside, Varian casually pulled up a chair and sat down. He glanced at Jace and couldn't help but smile. "Did you just come from your parents' place?"

Jace blinked, then touched his face and glanced down at his clothes and the cloak hanging beside him. "Does Mathias have to report every last detail like that?"

"Without that guy, I don't suddenly go blind." Varian traced a finger along Jace's shoulder and face. "You never used to look quite right when you came here — either your collar was tucked into your neck somewhere, or your shirt-tail had gotten stuffed who-knows-where. Today you look perfectly normal, like someone's been taking care of you."

Varian laughed. Jace couldn't help it either and laughed along. But he wasn't like Varian — Varian couldn't feel the secret he was carrying right now, while Jace could sense Varian's intense displeasure. Though that probably had nothing to do with him.

"How did the trip to Lordaeron go?" Jace asked.

Varian immediately wiped the smile off his face, frowning. "Did you come to see me today because of Lordaeron?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Fine." The king rubbed one side of his forehead. "You know about the Lord of Hearthglen, I take it?"

"Lord Tirion Fordring," Jace said. "I heard he was ambushed by an orc near a watchtower on his own territory a few months ago and badly wounded. Is that what this is about?"

"You actually knew about that," Varian said in surprise. But he immediately seemed to remember something. "Ah, right — you went up north before, so it makes sense you'd know."

He stared at a portrait of a king high on the wall behind Jace. "I was actually quite worried about Lord Tirion Fordring's injuries and planned to visit him in the Eastern Plaguelands on my way to Lordaeron, considering everything he gave during the Orc Wars. I never expected Lordaeron and Stromgarde to use his misfortune as a pretext, claiming the orcs hiding in the north still had the strength to assault fortresses, and flatly refused to commit to supporting the Blackrock Spire campaign!"

"But as far as I know — and of course I mean no disrespect to Lord Tirion Fordring — that was just some orc lurking in a dark corner who came out of nowhere and had a proper brawl with the watchtower's lord! But in their mouths, it was as if the orcs had already battered down Hearthglen's walls with siege engines!"

The more Varian spoke, the angrier he became. He glanced toward the door, lowered his voice, and continued, "There are even nobles down there who think Stormwind hasn't sent troops to help Lordaeron and Stromgarde attack the rogues hiding in the forests and snowy mountains, so they have no obligation to march south and join the siege of Blackrock Spire. Completely forgetting how many times over the years Stormwind has thwarted orcs and demons sneaking north out of the Dark Portal."

"Shaw warned me I might run into this sort of thing," he said, pointing at the wall to the north. "But I never expected them to just lay it all out in my face like that. It really is a perfect 'noble's garden' down there! Don't the Lordaeron people not celebrate this goddamn holiday?!"

"Indeed, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"Indeed what?" Varian's words still carried a trace of lingering anger.

"They really don't celebrate Garden Festival much," Jace said, pursing his lips.

Varian just stared at Jace in silence for a moment. He might have just remembered that Jace was also a Lordaeron man, or he might have realized his emotions had gotten seriously out of hand.

But since he was facing Jace right now rather than his privy council or the kingdom's citizens, there wasn't much need to restrain his temper.

For a moment, the king who had to keep up appearances and the good friend who didn't need to care so much collided, leaving him genuinely torn.

In the end, he dismissed the guards at the door and closed it, then let all that tangled emotion dissolve into a long sigh.

Still, even though this had been a deeply frustrating diplomatic trip for Varian, for Jace it was like being handed a pillow when he was already sleepy.

"If only the Sons of Lothar were still around, we wouldn't have to agonize over this at all with those people," Jace said, folding his arms.

"I spill my heart to you about the problems I've been having, and you hit me with this useless stuff?" Varian said, lowering his head. "I'm not expecting you to charge into Blackrock Spire alone and kill that evil dragon, Jace."

"Because I've been thinking about something lately, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"What?" Sensing Jace's rather serious tone, Varian immediately shifted back to a formal voice.

"Attempting to make contact with the Sons of Lothar," Jace said.

"Are you serious?" Varian asked.

The situation really had changed.

Before Onyxia had wreaked havoc on Stormwind, if Varian had heard Jace talk about contacting the Sons of Lothar, his first reaction definitely would have been to doubt they were still alive, or to worry that Jace would use dark magic to conjure up another Dark Portal and plague Stormwind — just like the reaction of pretty much every other ordinary person.

But by now Varian understood clearly that closing the Dark Portal didn't mean the end of the Orc Wars at all. If they wanted to secure a more stable future, the Kingdom of Stormwind's strength was far from enough.

Detecting that Varian indeed harbored hope about this, just as the others had expected, Jace pressed further. "I want to at least find out whether the orcs' homeland really has been completely destroyed, and figure out how many Alliance soldiers survived over there, lost and unable to find their way home."

"But the Kirin Tor is still guarding the Dark Portal at Nethergarde Keep to the death, on Archmage Khadgar's orders before he left, Jace," Varian said. "I don't know much about magic, but I know you can't make a move right under their noses."

"Even if Nethergarde Keep weren't watching the Dark Portal, I wouldn't have any way to restart it," Jace said. "I plan to probe Draenor's situation through a different method."

"Something like a scrying crystal ball?" Varian asked tentatively.

"Closer to a demon-summoning ritual, actually," Jace said. "At present… of course, nothing's set in stone yet. But if we get word that the Sons of Lothar are still alive, I believe the Kirin Tor would soften their current stance at least a little."

Varian fell silent for a moment, then said, "I'm afraid even if the Kirin Tor learned the Sons of Lothar are still alive, they wouldn't soften their stance, Jace. If this is going to happen, we'll have to do it ourselves. You might not want to believe it, but right now in the royal courts of every kingdom, including the Violet Citadel, the two of us are probably among the very few who genuinely want the Sons of Lothar to come back."

Jace knew this, of course. As the situation gradually stabilized and everyone could muddle through well enough, there weren't many rulers who wanted an elite corps that had once saved the world to suddenly drop back in from the sky.

Varian said in a low voice, "You're my court mage, and more importantly, you're my dark magic advisor. So as long as you know what you're doing, I don't care what methods you use. If you ever do find word of them, contact me as soon as possible."

"I'll be conducting my work at Ilgalar Tower, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"I'll cover for you, Jace," Varian nodded, then asked, "Still, there won't be any sudden… hard-to-explain 'weather phenomena,' will there?"

"There shouldn't be," Jace said.

He knew what Varian was hinting at. Decades ago, Varian's grandfather, King Baratheon Wrynn, had gone to great lengths to explain why a sudden lightning storm had annihilated an entire Gurubashi troll army. Of course, in the end he still couldn't put to rest the fears Stormwind's citizens held toward Medivh amid all the rumors.

Just opening a small teleportation portal shouldn't cause that kind of terrible fallout. The years had passed, Draenor's great explosion had settled, and the extent of Draenor's collapse wasn't as thorough as Khadgar had predicted. Judging by the various connections between Azeroth and Outland afterward, the magical turbulence on that side shouldn't reach over here anymore.

Of course, everything would depend on the situation after Kallrez opened the teleportation channel.
