## Chapter 1206: A Different World, Similar Troubles

Since the situation on the other side was completely unknown, Jace had Kallrez temporarily close the rift to prevent anything from squeezing through — or noticing the existence of the teleportation rift. Even though that side was silent as the dead.

Right now, there still hung in the air a trace of otherworldly breath that clearly didn't belong to Ilgalar Tower.

A group of them sat on the library level looking at each other. Jace had just described what he'd seen on the other side, and none of them quite knew how to react.

"So we still crawled into an orc fortress?" Grede asked, looking at Jace.

Jace didn't know how to answer the dwarf's question. After all, he couldn't tell everyone here that he knew exactly who might be living in Auchindoun right now, and who might potentially move in later.

Morophil sat nearby, watching her master's dilemma with great interest. She could clearly tell Jace was trying to cover something up. Jace could only warn her through their soul link to keep a low profile.

Just as he was thinking about how to properly explain the possible situation on the other side to everyone, Vereesa spoke up first. "At least based on the last news I heard from Draenor, Auchindoun had already been stormed by the Expedition. The orc commander defending the fortress, Kilrogg Deadeye, should have been captured or killed in action."

"Who? How do you know all that in such detail?" Grede asked.

"Probably because I'm Lady Sylvanas Windrunner's sister," Vereesa said, displeased.

"Ah, right." The dwarf stroked his chin and thought for a moment. "So the Broken hiding in the forest lied to us? I distinctly remember them saying the teleportation coordinate's endpoint was in a small village."

He glanced at Amy. By now Amy had been silent for quite a while — she was definitely suspecting that something had gone wrong with her repair work.

Jace looked at the doom lord. "Do you have any insight, Kallrez?"

The big demon was just about to lift his head toward them when Jace said quickly, "Don't move! Just talk from there."

"Ugh…" Kallrez exhaled a puff of fel smoke in resignation and said, "I can feel that Draenor's entire ley line network has been torn apart along with the world's collapse, Jace Sesso — my master. That's probably what caused the teleportation magic to drift. This doesn't surprise me. The Legion's vanguard frequently had to deal with situations like this, considering that those soldiers often arrived a bit later than Lord Archimonde. So facing a world riddled with holes and on the verge of collapse was quite common."

Jace nodded. Beside him, Amy also breathed a sigh of relief.

"So the Broken weren't trying to trick us," Sola said. "It's just that by the time they came here, Draenor hadn't suffered that disaster yet."

"Then they've still got some conscience," Grede said. "Still, at least we know Draenor hasn't been blown to powder. If places like palaces or basements can still exist over there, even cellars quiet enough to hear nothing at all — that means the chances of the Sons of Lothar surviving are very high."

"Really," Vereesa said, barely containing her excitement. "Much more optimistic than I expected, Jace."

Though everyone present was quite excited, Jace — someone who'd known about this since the day he was born — naturally wouldn't have too great an emotional reaction.

He smiled along with everyone on the surface, but in his mind he was pondering what the situation inside Auchindoun should be like right now.

If, as the Broken had said, they'd been teleported to a small village outside Auchindoun before, they could have scouted Auchindoun or the surrounding Terokkar Forest first, then slowly gathered information and pieced together Auchindoun's condition.

But now that wouldn't work. Before everyone formally entered the portal as a group, they had to roughly figure out what they might encounter.

At that moment Grede asked excitedly, "Since this portal allows such quick back-and-forth trips, can we just send the entire Expedition back through it?"

"No way. Medivh having the orcs build a portal that massive wasn't just for show," Amy said, looking around the interior of the mage tower. "The Expedition had tens of thousands of soldiers. No matter how you limited the frequency, it could trigger a magical disaster. Even if it didn't reach the Dark Portal's level of devastation to the Black Morass, it would at least affect Lake Everstill. And considering the ley lines here are already fragile, even if the Taralfsin Crystal could hold up, the ley lines themselves might not last."

"It's not just a magic problem, Grede," Vereesa said. "Remember, Draenor used to be a world as vast as Azeroth. Even if this portal could send everyone back one by one, you'd have to consider the difficulty of assembling them all beside the portal. Forget about finding every single Expedition member in that shattered land… just think about the difficulty of calling every Thunderfist in the world back to Aerie Peak within six months."

"Fair point," Grede shrugged. "At least I wouldn't want to go back."

Vereesa continued, "When that world was still intact, retreating through the Dark Portal was already that difficult, let alone now. So in the end, we'll definitely need the Kirin Tor involved…"

"I agree," Amy said. "Bring Archmage Khadgar back. With his influence, he could definitely persuade Dalaran to get involved in this."

At this she lowered her eyes and said, "Assuming he's still alive."

Grede laughed. "Don't worry, Miss Amy Marin. I think rather than worrying about him getting killed by demons and the Twisting Nether, you'd better worry about him dying of old age! Jace can walk back and forth alive — how could Khadgar have any problems?!"

Amy was still smiling, but when the dwarf suddenly brought Jace into it like that, she glanced over here a little sheepishly. But Jace wasn't paying much attention to their conversation. He'd been keeping his head down, thinking about Auchindoun the whole time.

Of the most important cities the draenei had built on Draenor, aside from the Temple of Karabor and Shattrath City, Auchindoun should be among them. Of course, as the draenei's sacred burial ground, Auchindoun's status was no lower than Karabor or Shattrath. So a massive number of elite troops had gathered here to resist the orc and demon offensive.

Although the Shadow Council had summoned the otherworldly elemental lord "M'muru," triggering a massive explosion that destroyed Auchindoun and conquered the city, countless draenei defenders had survived and hidden in the ruins. Some of these defenders even survived until the Dark Portal was reopened twenty years later.

But by then, their final leader, Maladaar — a powerful follower of the Holy Light — had been driven insane by uncontrolled emotions of corruption from Auchindoun's failure and the deaths of countless comrades, turning to Shadow and necromantic magic.

If he were still sane enough, he might actually be able to help this group. After all, the draenei should have attacked the orcs in Auchindoun alongside the Expedition soldiers. Unfortunately, that had been before Draenor's great explosion. Jace had no way of gauging his current state, so they'd have to be as careful as possible.

Another clue was that Jace hadn't sensed any traces of demons on the other side of the portal.

Twenty years later, Burning Legion demons had entered the crypts where Maladaar and his organization, the Auchenai, were stationed. Similarly, the Shadow Council had also summoned many demons to protect their attempts to continue controlling M'muru deep within another section of the crypts.

The absence of demons probably meant they hadn't teleported to where the Shadow Council warlocks were hiding. At the same time, the Burning Legion's demons hadn't made contact here yet — all things considered, that counted as a blessing within a misfortune.

As for the other forces that would want to enter Auchindoun in the future, seeking soul power or mana — the arakkoa living in Terokkar Forest, or the "etheric" travelers "the Voidspawn" who'd come here from another world — even twenty years later it had seemed as though they'd only recently arrived at Auchindoun. So Jace guessed that at this moment, when Draenor had just experienced an unprecedented catastrophe, even if they'd already made contact with Auchindoun, they probably hadn't brazenly forced their way in and established a stronghold at the risk of engaging draenei and orcs.

But at least one enemy could be confirmed — ghosts.

The Auchindoun explosion had killed countless soldiers guarding the sacred burial ground, while also awakening countless ancient souls hidden deep within. These souls, influenced by Void power and the horrific explosion, had become twisted and maddened, crazily seeking revenge and hostile to any living thing that tried to enter Auchindoun.

The Burning Legion demons and the arakkoa who pursued dark magic had been drawn into Auchindoun largely because of these souls. Maladaar's corruption was also closely tied to these souls.

"Jace?" Vereesa asked. "What are you thinking about?"

"Auchindoun is the ruins of the draenei sacred burial ground, Wen," Jace said. "Just think about the orc army and their dark magic that stormed and destroyed that place…"

"So we could be surrounded by countless vengeful spirits," Vereesa said.

Having experienced the dark energy from Karazhan spreading through Duskwood and raising the entire Raven Hill, the farstriders familiar with this region had a very clear idea of what happened when dark magic got mixed up with graveyards.

"Damn it," Grede frowned and let out a long sigh. "Why do we have to deal with ghosts in another world too? Are you serious, Jace? I still have nightmares about that ogre temple! The winding passageways were designed specifically to piss me off — like you'd never find the exit."

Yes, this was also an important danger. The destroyed Auchindoun was also an extremely chaotic place.

Since they didn't know exactly where the portal had landed, and considering the complexity deep within that burial ground… the Broken who'd been willing to guide the way had gone from "passing refugees willing to lend a hand" to indispensable companions.
