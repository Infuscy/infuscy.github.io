## Chapter 121: The Goblins' Crimes

By noon the next day, Jace couldn't feel anything wrong with his body anymore, but it still took a hell of a lot of convincing — and even a round of calisthenics performed right there on the spot — before the watching priest finally agreed to let him go.

Yesterday he'd been meaning to find Caro Dushan and sort out the ship tickets, but then practicing magic had eaten up the whole day in one go.

Jace dashed over to the Blue Recluse, planning to grab a bite and then head straight to the Wizard Sanctum to find him — only to run into him right there in the tavern.

Dushan was sitting by the window reading a book with a cup of coffee. Plain white shirt on top, a pair of gray linen trousers below, and combined with that slightly anachronistic short stubble of hair and beard, Jace almost thought he was looking at a middle-aged office worker sitting at a street café in twentieth-century Italy — if not for the crushingly medieval décor all around him.

"Master Dushan," Jace went over and asked, "mind if I sit?"

"Ah, Jace, of course." Dushan moved his coffee cup aside. "So? Did you and your friend settle on a time?"

"Settled." Jace sat down and handed over the note Grede had written. "We're planning to take the seventeenth — that sailing of the *Great Baradin Conqueror* to Southshore."

"I know that ship belongs to the Steamwheedle Cartel." Dushan tucked the note into his pocket. "That's fine, that's fine — but do you know what the goblins did during the Orc Wars?"

Uh-oh.

That had landed squarely in Jace's blind spot. He'd never paid much attention to the goblins as a race, let alone what they'd been up to before the game's timeline even started.

Generally speaking, goblins in the game leaned closer to the Horde overall, but aside from the Bilgewater Cartel, most of them stayed more or less neutral — and they did business in Alliance cities too.

Seeing Jace's blank look, Dushan said, "Decades ago, the Steamwheedle Cartel seized Blackwater Bay from the trolls and renamed it Booty Bay. It had originally been a human bay, lost to a troll raid, and now it belonged to the Steamwheedle Cartel and the Blackwater Raiders. The Cartel made it their new operational base, looking to muscle in on trade across half the continent. In any case, during the Orc Wars, the Steamwheedle Cartel used Booty Bay's port to help the orcs transport supplies, provided warship technology, handed over the entire Stranglethorn Vale map — all sorts of dealings — in exchange for gold. And that gold was everything Orgrim Doomhammer had scraped together from the vaults of Stormwind Keep."

"In other words, the wealth the Kingdom of Stormwind had accumulated since the Troll Wars helped the Steamwheedle Cartel expand at lightning speed. They called it business, but in the Alliance's eyes it was nothing short of a hostile act. If not for the massive sum they volunteered in reparations after the war to aid reconstruction, and their explicit promise to assist Stormwind with maritime travel and cargo transport between the kingdom and the others, once the kingdom recovered it would've blown Booty Bay to smithereens. But what of it? That money was Stormwind's property to begin with — no matter the figure, those reparations could never make up for the crimes they'd committed. The Cartel would have to prove itself going forward, show how much effort it was willing to put into upholding its reputation for neutrality."

Jace really didn't know much about any of this. When he'd first arrived in Stormwind, he'd seen the Steamwheedle Cartel's flag more than once and had assumed the goblins and the Alliance had a pretty cozy trading relationship. And they did, actually — just on the economic side.

Jace said with a helpless shrug, "My friend spent days asking around the port. He didn't want to take a goblin ship either, but there really wasn't another choice."

Dushan nodded. "I'm not objecting to your decision. Right now, any ship you can get on is the best option. I'm just telling you to be careful, Jace Sesso — those goblins will do anything for a profit. But I understand your situation. I'll handle it for you and deliver the tickets to your teacher, Master Marin. Don't worry."

"Thank you, Master," Jace said.

"Oh, and Jace — I haven't asked you yet." Dushan took a sip of coffee. "Is there anything… special about the way you study Orcish? I know some wealthy noble families in Dalaran have been buying up orcish books, texts, letters — anything they can get their hands on — to help themselves master Orcish as fast as possible. But you… I imagine your family doesn't have that kind of wealth? No offense meant."

Wait — rich Dalaran nobles buying up orcish writing?

Then he wouldn't have to risk taking that orcish letter to the Booty Bay auction after all. He could just sell it on the side while he was in Dalaran.

And that fragment book… Jace thought. He could copy down everything in it and then sell the book itself to one of those Dalaran buyers looking for orcish materials.

Except that fragment contained Life Tap. If he sold the book, wouldn't that be the same as publishing the spell for the whole world to see? He had no idea whether Dalaran's warlocks had already learned it. If they had, then fine, selling it was no big deal. But if they hadn't… wouldn't that be handing his trump card straight to the competition?

Thinking it over, Jace decided the fragment book couldn't be sold directly.

The thing was, before the Scourge invasion, warlocks on Azeroth — human warlocks especially — had developed at an incredibly slow pace. What really kicked human dark magic into high gear was the Burning Legion demons the Scourge had summoned, scattered across the world after their defeat.

Which meant that right now, Jace needed to be careful not to go spreading around the good stuff he'd learned. That was the only way to stay ahead of every other warlock as fast as possible.

What a beautiful spirit of sharing… screw that.

"My family doesn't have much money — just enough to cover day-to-day expenses. There's no way we could afford those expensive books," Jace explained. "My parents are ordinary people. I've been learning Orcish mostly from the library, plus my own understanding. I think a lot of the students in class treat Orcish as just a hobby, but I'm genuinely hoping to use it someday to land a decently paying job or something. Maybe that's my edge."

As he finished, Jace swallowed involuntarily. He still couldn't quite manage to lie through his teeth with a perfectly straight face.

"So you're actually optimistic that the orcs left behind on Azeroth will one day coexist peacefully with humanity?" Dushan asked casually.

"Not optimistic at all, ha!" Jace gave a dry laugh. "If humans and orcs are going to coexist peacefully in the future, then what good would learning Orcish be for making good money, Master Dushan?" Caro Dushan smiled. "Fair point."

Leaving the Blue Recluse, Jace felt like he'd finally put one major problem completely to rest.

Standing in the gardens of the Wizard Sanctum, Jace looked up at the white tower and thought about the Mage Armor problem.

The single biggest obstacle to learning Mage Armor right now was that his mana pool — his spiritual stamina — wasn't high enough. He had no idea what the final result would look like if his limits were sufficient, but one thing was clear: without breaking through that ceiling, he wouldn't even be qualified to find out.

Recasting yesterday's attempt in his mind, Jace realized he'd started feeling fatigued about a third of the way through the incantation. Past the two-thirds mark, when he was doing high-density communication with the arcane energies, he'd basically been running on fumes.

By the standards he'd followed when practicing Arcane Intellect, he should've taken a break at the two-thirds point, or downed a mana potion to steady himself.

Instead he'd gotten impatient, overextended his mana, and paid for it by blacking out.

Thankfully there'd been no permanent damage. Marin had told him there were plenty of cases where that kind of overexertion led to consequences you could never take back.

Then there was casting focus, too. Jace knew that during yesterday's attempt his mind had wandered more than once, thanks to nerves and self-doubt. That had definitely taken a heavy toll on the spell.

But if he just pushed through with better focus, would he be able to complete it cleanly? Not necessarily. Jace didn't think trying again anytime soon would go much better, let alone account for the risk. Thinking it over, since he was already at the base of the Wizard Sanctum, he might as well hit the library and look into whether there were other ways to raise his mana ceiling besides just practicing spells.
