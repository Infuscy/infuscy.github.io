Chapter 1210: The Only Means of Contact

After May passed, Midsummer Fire Festival drew closer and closer. This year the festival was coming a few days earlier than usual. Jace hadn't asked about the specific rules for setting the festival date, only that the Trade District was already showing some Fire Festival atmosphere. Decorations on shop windows and doors, people on the street starting to choose brightly colored clothing, young men and women who could perform the "Dance of the Flame" trying out moves and sharing tips — much like how it always was around this time of year. The Fire Festival was one of the most important holidays of the year. Unlike Garden Festival or Winter Veil, which were more regional, every human kingdom from north to south — even the dwarves and elves, noble and common alike — celebrated this holiday. But Jace would probably miss it this year. He didn't feel too bad about it, though. He'd never had a particularly good impression of this holiday anyway. Beside the Wizard Sanctum, an apprentice mage in a red wizard hat was showing off fancy fire tricks to a young female apprentice. Watching this scene, Jace was reminded of a Fire Festival from a few years ago… the day when a sky of fel fire, coming from the other side of the Dark Portal, had engulfed all of Stormwind. When the news from Nethergarde Keep arrived, countless young mages and Stormwind soldiers had rushed south. Jace had been among them. After that, his life had seemed to suddenly veer in a direction he couldn't quite keep a firm grip on. And what about this time? Jace didn't like this kind of coincidence. Another Fire Festival, and another teleportation portal leading to Draenor. He just hoped he was overthinking it, or that his habit of always expecting the worst was kicking in again. By the time he arrived in Stormwind, it was already the middle of the night. Grede had definitely gone to sleep, and the elven embassy had already closed its doors. Vereesa wasn't staying at the Gilded Rose, so she might have gone to the elven residence in the Mage Quarter. Jace went to the Mage Quarter, but after asking the elven guards on duty, Vereesa wasn't there either. Looks like he'd have to wait until tomorrow morning and check the elven embassy. Walking out of the elven residence alley and not too far down the road brought him to the entrance of the Slaughtered Lamb. It was quite lively here in the middle of the night. Nobody paid any attention to an unremarkable traveler in a black cloak like Jace. Jace came to the counter and knocked on the table. The dark-faced bartender who'd been writing something in a ledger looked up and said, "Ursula's been waiting for you, Jace. She told me that if I saw you, I should tell you everything you asked for is downstairs." "Is she downstairs too?" Jace asked. "She went to Sulenna's to pick up some things. She'll be back in a bit," Tharle nodded. "Besides, I can't quite remember the names of all those potions. You can ask her directly when she gets back." "Fine," Jace said. "I might be going on a long trip, Tharle. So go ahead and cancel my rented room for me." "Don't worry about it," Tharle closed his ledger and pressed his hand on it. "You're sure you've packed everything? Don't leave anything behind that has anything to do with our people here." "Mm," Jace acknowledged. "Sorry to trouble you." Coming down to the basement, he closed the door. The bang echoed through the hall and passage. Only the felhound guarding the place lightly scraped a claw against the ground. Aside from that, there was almost no sound at all. Jace came to the room where Gaothoros was bound. The Voidlord slowly raised his head as Jace entered. An ethereal Shadow howl gradually filled the room with his movement. The Void power emanating from this powerful, pure Void being didn't carry much of the slightly fishy stench of a black dragon's flames. Even at this close range, it was the same. "Gaothoros," Jace called its name. "Master…" Gaothoros's purple body flickered faintly in response. "If I go to another place — say, another world within the dark Void — is there some way we can communicate?" Jace asked in Eredun. "You are a mortal," Gaothoros said. "So there is no way." "Can't I communicate with you through a Void being's means?" Jace asked. "I cannot," Gaothoros said. "Unless you use the same ritual to summon me to that world. Then I will be able to communicate with you face to face and hear your voice." Jace thought, "Well, that's fucking useless." He'd originally been hoping that if he could establish some kind of long-distance mental communication with this Voidlord, he'd be able to stay in contact with the Dagger Society even while on Draenor. Of course, he hadn't held out much hope to begin with. After all, even in the game, aside from the Old Gods — beings capable of shaking the entire world — there weren't many living creatures who could communicate mentally across the Void and between planes with mortals… well, except for player whispers. But he still had one last card to play — Kallrez. Though Void beings couldn't communicate with mortals, the demons had at least found rituals and incantations for cross-plane communication, in order to receive orders from their lords at all times. Jace still had the ritual circle Dasmora had used when attempting to communicate with her superior — Kallrez himself — in Eldre'Thalas. Although this ritual circle was somewhat complex, and he had no idea how much mana it would cost to cast from Draenor to Azeroth, it was already the only means of long-distance communication Jace had at his disposal. Jace looked up, gazing at the Voidlord before him. Come to think of it, summoning him into this world seemed to have few uses besides helping the Dagger Society train in Shadow magic, crafting Void-related magical items, providing some knowledge about the Twisting Nether, and helping him interpret Dar'Khan's records. And even interpreting Dar'Khan's records had only amounted to translating the contents on the page. How to actually use them — Galders and the others were still completely in the dark. Of course, it was possible that the Dagger Society's understanding of the Void Walker wasn't thorough enough. This would require further development. Just then, the sound of a lock being undone came from outside the passage. Jace left the room. Ursula had just walked into the hall. Seeing Jace appear before her, she set down the things she was carrying, took a deep breath, and let it out. "You're about to set out, aren't you?" "Yes. Everything that needs preparing is ready. It's time to go," Jace said. "I know I shouldn't ask too much," Ursula raised both hands. "Come with me." Coming to the alchemy lab, Ursula handed Jace the bag of potions. Jace set the coin purse on the table. Ursula didn't even count it — just took it and stuffed it in a box. "Where's Galders?" Jace asked. "He and Erland went to Booty Bay," Ursula said. "Looking for a way to decipher those scrolls you brought back from Stratholme." "Hold on," Jace said. "They're not showing the contents to outsiders, are they?" "Relax, Jace. Even if you asked them to show outsiders, they wouldn't agree," Ursula said with a smile. "They know what they're doing." "Fine. I thought I might be able to help a little," Jace said. "There should be plenty of downtime on this trip. I was planning to see if I could study the things Gaothoros translated myself." "Is it far?" Ursula asked. Jace hesitated, unsure how to explain. Ursula raised both hands. "Sorry. I asked something I shouldn't have again." She took a cloth-wrapped package down from her bookshelf. "But about those scrolls — Galders left a set here for me. He said if I couldn't figure them out, I should give them to you. This is a copy transcribed by Lady Sola with Lord Gaothoros's help. It's written in the 'Abyssal tongue' recorded with Eredun pronunciation. Some of it Gaothoros translated the meaning into Eredun in the margins, but most of it is incantations or used in some ritual — it doesn't have much concrete meaning… anyway, it's quite a headache." Jace nodded and took the package. After thinking for a moment, he said, "This time it really is far, and I don't know roughly when I'll be back. If the Dagger Society runs into any problems, or if you run into any trouble, go to Ilgalar Tower and find a Kirin Tor mage named Amy Marin." Jace handed Ursula his Wizard Sanctum apprentice ring. "Show this to her when you go. She'll trust you. Tell her whatever's happened to you or the Dagger Society. She'll find a way to notify me." "Amy Marin." The alchemist took the ring and nodded. "I'll remember that name." "Wherever you're going, whatever you're doing, be careful, Jace. Have you heard of Gul'dan's skull? A lot of orcs say the Warchief used his skull as a focus to reopen the Dark Portal, which had already lost its power…" "I've heard of it. What about it?" Jace asked. "If you die, I won't pass up that opportunity," the alchemist said with a wicked grin. "You wouldn't want your head being grabbed by everyone and fought over as a treasure, would you?" Jace touched his neck and smiled, but Ursula's smile quickly turned cold. This joke wasn't really that funny to her after all.
