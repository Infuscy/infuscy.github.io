Chapter 1212: The Ghosts of Auchindoun

The vast, dark hall fell silent once more, as though that low hum had never sounded at all.

Everyone waited in silence for a moment before Grede asked, "What kind of creature was that? That M'mu—"

"M'mur," Noboru corrected, leaning on his spear. "I've never seen it either, Lord Thunderfist. But the moment it manifested from the orcish summoning ritual, it blew apart all of Auchindoun, killing many of the orcish leaders and warlords — and just as many of our own people."

"So it's still here?" Grede asked, glancing toward Vereesa and Sola. "Meaning we've fallen into the hands of something even worse than the Burning Legion?"

"The source of that sound is still far from us, Grede," Vereesa said. "Perhaps it hasn't even noticed our arrival. If it were truly powerful enough to wipe an entire stretch of land off the map with a single strike, it would have dealt with us already — not let out one inexplicable... cry like that."

Noboru spoke softly in Eredun, explaining the group's discussion to Mag'tour, who said, "From what I understand, although M'mur is an otherworldly creature summoned by the Shadow Council, they themselves cannot control it. The orcish leaders could only use Auchindoun's soul energy to keep it contained, to keep it from shattering all of Draenor."

"It won't come deal with us, and it won't notice anything. According to the Broken who escaped, M'mur has no consciousness whatsoever and no ability to communicate. It's just a mass of chaotic... element."

After Noboru relayed Mag'tour's words to everyone, Grede's eyes went wide. "'Shatter' all of Draenor? So if those Shadow Council orcs lose their grip and let it out, we all get wiped out for nothing, right? How absolutely thrilling."

Jace didn't believe M'mur's power was enough to destroy all of Draenor, but he also knew very well that now was not the time to go poking that thing.

It seemed neither Mag'tour's group of Broken nor the Shadow Council had any real idea what the hell M'mur actually was. The orcs hadn't been able to take Auchindoun, so following a mysterious summoning tome believed to be a Burning Legion grimoire, they'd drawn upon Auchindoun's immense soul energy to perform a summoning ritual.

Their original intention had been to bring forth a sufficiently powerful Burning Legion lord to help them break the Draenei defenses. What they hadn't expected was that this summoning tome had actually been written by a cult of followers who had been secretly siphoning soul energy to commune with the otherworldly M'mur — and worshipped it as a god. The result had been catastrophe.

If not for the near-inexhaustible soul energy within Auchindoun, the Shadow Council never would have found a way to seal it.

Twenty years from now, the draenei and orcs hiding here would learn that it was actually a "sound" element — and quite possibly the most powerful sound element lord to have ever existed in the Void.

How did one destroy a sound element? Could shadow magic harm it? Could dream arrows, war hammers, or lightning move it? What about the venom spewed by green dragons? Neither the Shadow Council nor the draenei knew, and Jace certainly had no clue.

But at this point, M'mur wasn't even an enemy. If it weren't for the fact that it existed deep within Auchindoun — and that containing it forced the entire Shadow Council to pour enormous manpower and resources into that prison — Auchindoun would have long since been completely overrun by the orc warlocks, and Jace's group never would have had this opening to slip in through.

"At least it hasn't managed to destroy Draenor in all these years, Grede," Jace said. "And this land probably has no shortage of things capable of ending the world. The Burning Legion's demonic invasion, the energy pouring in from the Twisting Nether, the orcs' Scepter of Sargeras — and now there's M'mur on top of all that..."

Vereesa and Sola beside him listened with deepening frowns, but Grede, who'd been wearing a look of worry just moments before, let out a laugh. "Well, that's something, at least! Kurdran would like you, Jace."

Just then, a deep hum sounded again from the distance — like the rumble of something collapsing, rising up from deep underground, the vibration tickling the soles of their feet.

Once that muffled rumble had passed, Jace looked at Vereesa and said, "Go check out that fissure over there, Wen. I need to stay here and try the magical ritual to commune with Karlez."

Vereesa nodded and stepped over a fallen stone pillar, heading in that direction. Jace took out the vial of demon blood he'd prepared and, following the diagram on the scroll, drew the ritual circle.

He'd always used animal blood for this diagram before. Demon blood was more effective, more potent — it should make communion easier in theory.

But when he stood before the circle, chanting the incantation and Karlez's name and channeling shadow energy to activate it, he didn't feel that sense of connection with something distant that he'd felt the time he'd successfully activated this circle.

The thing his incantation called to was so far away it was nearly beyond reach, no matter how far he stretched out his "hand." The circle wasn't the problem, and the incantation wasn't the problem — after all, Jace had succeeded with it before.

If Dathmora had been able to commune with Karlez, who was still deep within the Twisting Nether at the time, from Eldre'Thalas, then that meant this circle could just as easily reach a demon on Azeroth from Draenor. Which left only one possibility... insufficient power.

Dathmora controlled an arcane tower built by the Highborne in Dire Maul, which allowed her to draw upon the magical source that sustained the entire city. Of course she could activate that ritual with ease.

Jace tossed two small Soul Shards onto several key runes and magical lines in the circle, then called out the incantation again. This time he clearly felt that "hand" stretch further, pushing deeper into the Twisting Nether.

The Soul Shards rapidly dimmed and crumbled to dust. Then the lines and all the characters on the circle ignited with fire as expected. The blood symbol at the center rose into shadowflame, and with the flickering of that flame, a rough, murmuring voice came through.

"Is that you, Karlez? Answer my voice," Jace said.

"Yes, Jace Sesso, my master. Most fortunate that you haven't forgotten how to use this circle, heh." The circle vibrated with sounds transmitted from another world.

The people nearby all sensed the presence of that voice, though aside from Jace and Morophil, who'd been gazing up at the vaulted ceiling of the upper hall all along, no one could make out what exactly the voice was saying.

"A surge of otherworldly energy came through the portal to Yllagala Tower a while ago, my master. But the soul contract still holds, so I assumed you were still alive. Though Amy Marin has been worried sick about you — her tiresome questions and distrust of me have been most unpleasant."

"Then you'd better be patient," Jace said. "Cooperate with her and keep her safe, Karlez." He took a deep breath, because communicating over such a vast distance through a circle consumed an enormous amount of power.

"How's my tower?"

"Not particularly terrible," Karlez replied. "At least not much damage, if that's what you were asking..." With that, the demon let out a mocking, cold laugh.

Jace wanted to fire back a few barbs, but considering the magical expenditure, he suppressed the urge. "You can close the portal. Wait for my next order," Jace said.

"Yes, sir," Karlez muttered.

The shadow rift behind them vanished, and the hall fell completely silent — they could even hear Sola's breathing clearly from nearby.

Though everyone had been prepared to be left behind in Draenor, watching that portal actually close — meaning they had completely severed their ties with Azeroth and entered this alien world — the expressions on everyone's faces were still far from cheerful.

"If we want to return to Azeroth, we'll have to come back here and tell that demon to open the door for us again, won't we?" Grede asked.

"Seems that way," Jace said. "But if we can find the Expedition, we should be able to get other portal coordinates. Don't forget — the Kirin Tor's portal coordinates were given to the Expedition and carried back by a gryphon rider before the Dark Portal closed."

"Something's approaching, everyone," Vereesa suddenly called softly from over there.

Everyone immediately turned to look in that direction. Before long, an unnatural ghostly light was flickering from the collapsed fissure where Vereesa was.

Jace had encountered countless spirits in Deadwind Pass and deep within the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, so he immediately recognized the signs of ghosts drawing near.

After everyone took cover, the souls appeared. They weren't like the twisted fiends in the troll temple — they still bore the appearances of their living selves, much like the spirits in the villages of Deadwind Pass.

They were Eredar — or more precisely, draenei. Each of them stood over two meters tall, their horns, tendrils, and even their glowing demon eyes still clearly visible.

Chests bare and powerful hooves carrying them forward with elegant strides, the men and women walked in from the depths of the corridor, chatting and laughing as they went — as though this were still that sacred, solemn temple.

Watching them, both Grede and Vereesa were nearly struck dumb. After all, all they'd ever seen near the Dark Portal were those proud, cold-eyed Eredar demon warlocks. They'd almost never seen "Eredar" with such relaxed, friendly expressions.

Especially since they'd never seen a female Eredar before. Compared to the males, female draenei features were closer to humanoid — and with the slightly blurred, translucent magical visages of their spirits, they carried a kind of hazy beauty.

Of course, curiosity aside, everyone was still ready for a fight. Vereesa had already been planning to coat her Dragon's Call blade with Wizard Oil to counter an attack from these ghosts — but to her surprise, the spirits emerging from the fissure showed no intention of attacking at all.

They walked straight past Vereesa heading in this direction, then ignored Grede and Sola, and every single Broken nearby, walking straight toward Jace.

Noboru watched these approaching draenei ghosts with complicated eyes, gripping his spear tightly. One of the Broken even turned his head away, unwilling to look at them.

The ghosts walked right up to Jace's side. Jace gripped Uthales tightly, forcibly suppressing its savage urge to devour all these souls right in front of the Broken.

The spirits peered around the ritual circle, then exchanged words in voices too faint to make out clearly, wearing rather bewildered expressions — as if they didn't know who had summoned them here.

After a while, they turned and left, until the ghostly light faded completely.

Jace had a rough idea. The demon-communion circle hadn't just consumed the power of the Soul Shards — it had probably also drawn upon the soul energy within Auchindoun.

These draenei souls had likely come here unconsciously, intending to throw themselves in as "fuel."

So from here on out, any soul-related magic used within Auchindoun would have to be handled carefully. After all, you never knew how many things would take notice of the magical disturbance, or whether the attention you drew would be as unconscious and friendly as this — or hostile.

"We can follow these souls for now," Mag'tour reminded softly in Eredun. "Perhaps they'll lead us to an important landmark, Sesso."

Jace nodded and said to Grede and Sola, not far ahead, "Let's follow them."
