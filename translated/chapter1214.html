Chapter 1214: A Rare Bit of Solid Intel

The junction held by these two draenei "necromancers" was a grisly sight, heaped with bones everywhere — it looked less like the hideout of some powerful mage and more like the lair of a man-eating beast.

Jace glanced over the bone piles and the cages stacked on the platforms along both sides of the passage, confirmed there was nothing living left among them, and caught up with Mag'tour and the other Broken.

"I still remember this place," Mag'tour said, looking back at Jace. "A priest once gave me a tour here, Sesso. The demonic plague hasn't yet stripped away that part of my memory, at least."

Noboru relayed Mag'tour's words to the others. Vereesa nodded, bow in hand, while Grede at the rear examined the necromancers' corpses. "Don't we want to see if they've got anything good on them?"

"Didn't you hear what they said?" Jace replied. "They've got an 'Exarch,' and those souls already sensed what we did here. God knows when their reinforcements will show up — we can't afford to waste any more time. Besides, there's nobody here who's going to pay you gold for loot!"

Grede nodded reluctantly, hefted his hammer, and caught up, muttering, "Not like I'm a mage anyway." The others fell in behind Mag'tour. Though he limped along with his cane, he moved at a fair clip.

The Broken sage hobbled ahead with his walking stick, leading the way, while the others watched the flanks and rear for any Auchenai priests that might come after them, as well as the draenei spirits peeping from the shadows.

The existence of these necromancers very likely meant that Exarch Maladaar had long since twisted his mind and become the leader of a cabal of fallen draenei.

The Eredar demons were cruel, bloodthirsty butchers — but that didn't mean their counterparts, the draenei, were paragons of virtue by comparison.

The doom of Auchindoun and the catastrophe that engulfed all of Draenor had struck one after another, profoundly affecting the last surviving draenei garrison trapped deep within.

Hatred of the orcs, the grief and despair of watching their kin die one by one had driven them. Irreversible negative energies, combined with the power of the Twisting Nether pouring into this world, had brewed a shadow that gripped them — causing these guardians of a sacred site to begin exploiting the souls of their own kind and the honored ancestors entombed in the holy mausoleum to strengthen themselves... not only to exact vengeance on the orcs, but to purge every unclean outsider.

After rounding several corridors and walkways, Jace could even smell a change in the air. The previous chill, slightly damp-rot scent had gradually given way to the smell of ash and cinders. Drifting in from outside, perhaps?

The great hall of Auchindoun hadn't echoed with M'mur's "murmur" again for a while now, but every so often, shouts of humanoid creatures could be heard. Probably because they were too far away — by the time the sounds reached here they'd grown extremely faint — but it wasn't hard to guess that the Auchenai members had likely caught wind of what had happened to their comrades.

After climbing a long, tall flight of steps behind the Broken and turning through a doorway, Jace finally felt a flicker of familiarity.

Dark blue walls, arched narrow doorways, rubble and bones scattered everywhere, stone fire basins, and interlocking floor tiles bearing a passing resemblance to Eredar runes — it all called to mind the Auchindoun dungeons from the game.

The group passed warily through the gallery and was just about to enter the next narrow doorway when a shout rang out from a nearby ruin. "Wait..."

The speaker was using Common, but the cadence was uncannily like that of the draenei priests from before. Having already been burned once, Vereesa spun around almost instantly, an arrow already nocked on the string, its tip shimmering with the eerie, many-colored light of a dream arrow.

The arrow's glow made even the dim hall shimmer with a faint radiance. In that pale light, a bare-chested draenei beside the rubble raised a hand and said softly in Common, "Don't hurt me. I'm not an enemy. Please, help me..."

Jace sized up the draenei. He looked tall — even taller than those two priests from before, nearly matching Mosa's height. But he was terribly thin, as little more than a skeleton wrapped in skin, the ridges of the bone crest beneath his scalp clearly visible.

Still, his waist-wrap and trousers bore the same patterns and ornamentation as those Auchenai priests. Jace couldn't help but draw Azure Song and point it at him. "You're Auchenai?"

"Yes," the draenei nodded. "But I do not follow Maladaar, if that's what you're asking."

Jace could tell this draenei wasn't lying, at least. And though his gaze toward the Broken held a measure of wariness, it wasn't the same look those priests had worn — the kind that wanted them dead on the spot.

"Who is Maladaar?" Grede asked.

"You don't know Maladaar?" The draenei looked blankly at the dwarf, then over at the Broken behind him. "Wait — you're not Alliance Expedition soldiers?"

Jace lowered the tip of his sword slightly. "We came here looking for the Expedition. We got separated from them, priest."

Hearing that, the draenei's tension eased a little. "Maladaar... is the leader of the Auchindoun defense force."

"This Maladaar — he'd be the so-called Exarch?" Sola asked.

"Yes," Noboru nodded. "Exarch Maladaar was once the most revered among us."

"Not anymore," the draenei said. "His hatred of the Horde has turned him... into something even more terrible than the Horde. Now he intends to seize control of all the soul energy within Auchindoun, to wipe out every outsider. The Horde, that otherworldly creature, the Expedition — even those of us who refuse to obey him. All of them."

Jace noticed this fellow spoke Common far more fluently than that last priest, even wielding grammatical constructions that Vereesa herself wouldn't have used with ease.

"We'll help you, priest. Come with us," Vereesa said. "We've already crossed blades with some of the priests who follow Exarch Maladaar. They may well be on their way here."

"I'm not asking you to take me out," the draenei said. "I have to find my father first. He and a group of Alliance soldiers were captured by the orcs. You're not the Expedition, but since you came here looking for them — please, help me go..."

"Liar," Grede cut in, leveling his war hammer at the draenei as he turned to Jace. "This one's even more cunning and treacherous than those two Eredar. He's definitely trying to lure us with a bunch of fancy lies straight into some necromancer's trap."

Saying that, Grede looked half-ready to cave the draenei's skull in then and there. Jace held him back. "Wait. Let me ask him first."

Grede's fury was barely contained by Jace's intervention. He wound up and kicked an orc skull flying, then went and sat down off to the side.

No need to guess — this draenei wanted to use Jace's group to find his father. But he really didn't seem to be deceiving them, either. At least Jace couldn't sense any deliberate concealment or falseness from him.

If there really were Expedition soldiers trapped somewhere nearby, Jace couldn't just look the other way. "What's your name?" Jace asked.

"Levithus." The draenei coughed violently, then pressed a hand to his chest. "I am Levithus. As you can see, my father and I are both members of the Auchenai. A few years ago, we helped the Expedition breach Auchindoun to rescue our brethren trapped inside by the Shadow Council. So I suppose you could call us allies."

Levithus?

Jace frowned slightly. The man really wasn't lying, but that name felt strangely familiar — and not in a good way.

Vereesa asked at once, "So what you're saying is, Alliance soldiers are inside Auchindoun too? That part of it is held by our forces?"

The priest named Levithus shook his head. "It sounds like you've been separated from the Expedition for some time?"

"Actually, we only just got here," Jace admitted. "We came from Azeroth looking for word of the Expedition."

Levithus said in astonishment, "How is that possible? These days, only the Void Gate maintained by Magtheridon and his demon lieutenants can get anyone in or out... Even the most powerful arcanists can hardly work teleportation magic on present-day Draenor, whether near or far, let alone cross dimensions."

Everyone looked at Jace. Jace coughed. "We have our own methods, priest."

Levithus looked skeptical, but he didn't dwell on it long. "The Expedition left Auchindoun years ago. They destroyed the Shadow Council's defenses back then, but after the Horde's warchief initiated that ritual, the great catastrophe of Draenor shattered several of the Expedition's key strongholds and fortresses. They could no longer sustain the fight here and had to withdraw to the north and east."

Suddenly, a piercing shriek rang out from some indeterminate distance. The entire chamber shook with it, rubble and debris raining down in clouds of dust.

Once they'd recovered from the tremor, Levithus brushed the dust from his shoulders and continued. "That catastrophe caught everyone off guard — not just us and you, but the Horde too. And the tearing and shaking went on for a long time, so the Expedition's withdrawal was extremely chaotic. Many of your soldiers were killed or captured by the Shadow Council in the process. My father and I had intended to help the Expedition enter Auchindoun to search for those left behind, and that's how we were captured. The Shadow Council is fighting Maladaar's followers for control of this shattered holy city — but it's precisely because of that that I found the gap between their battlegrounds and managed to survive."

With that, Levithus drew a short sword from behind his back and held it out. "If you still don't believe me, this should go some way toward proving it."

"A Farstrider corps blade!" Vereesa recognized it almost instantly. She could barely contain herself as she asked, "Do you know Alleria Windrunner?"

"I know her. She's one of the Expedition's leaders. But I haven't seen her with my own eyes since the great catastrophe, my lady," Levithus said. "But I'm certain she survived it. I heard she personally took part in several survivor-search operations."

"She's alive." Vereesa bit her lip, staring at Jace. "You were right, Jace. She really is alive... It's only now, at this moment, that I... I..."

By this point she could barely form a complete sentence. She just leaned on Jace's shoulder, head bowed, as if on the verge of breaking down completely — but unable to let herself go entirely given how dangerous this place was.

And Grede, sitting off to the side, actually trembled his beard and wiped at his eyes, his nose turning red for once over something other than drink.

That this dwarf, who always had a sharp tongue for Vereesa, would be moved to tears by news of her sister — Jace had found it surprising at first, but thinking it over, maybe it wasn't so unexpected after all.

As for himself, he'd long since confirmed that Alleria and the rest of the Sons of Lothar had survived. And yet, right now, he still felt a prickling in his nose. It was as though all the anticipation, the preparation, the risk — had finally paid off for real.

Though what Levithus had said — "haven't seen her with my own eyes since the great catastrophe" — still sat like a knot in Jace's chest.
