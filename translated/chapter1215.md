## Chapter 1215: Which Way Is the Shadow Council?

While the others were questioning this lone draenei priest, Noboru had been off to the side translating the conversation for Mag'tour.

After a while, Mag'tour asked in Eredun, "You said your name is Levithus?"

"Yes," Levithus replied in Eredun.

"Is your father's name Nithlin?" Mag'tour asked.

The light in Levithus's eyes flared up all at once. "You know him?"

"I met your father in Telaar, Levithus." Mag'tour lowered his hand and placed it over his heart. "I am Mag'tour. Like your father, I was once a scholar... Perhaps Draenor isn't as large as we thought."

Telaar was a town in Nagrand. Levithus, the scholar Nithlin, plus Nagrand... Jace seemed to be piecing together fragments of his memory concerning that whole chain of quests.

Levithus — couldn't he be that Eredar leader at the top of Auchindoun who'd tried to use a necromantic ritual to gather all the soul energy unto himself?

Twenty years from now, he and his father would find a necromantic codex in Shattrath City, and under the demons' sway, attempt to use that codex to twist every spirit wandering Auchindoun...

Who would have thought he'd once worked so wholeheartedly to aid the Alliance Expedition, even at the cost of his own life.

Of course, Maladaar had once been an Exarch whom even Prophet Velen held in high regard, and he'd ended up like this. So a young draenei undergoing such a transformation in the future was even less surprising.

At least right now he was still a reliable friend, and Jace had no intention of holding against the present-day him something that wouldn't happen for another twenty years. "Do you know where your father and the Expedition soldiers are being held?" Jace asked.

Levithus shook his head. "I can't be certain, because I lost contact with them about a month or two ago. I'm sorry — it's very hard to perceive the passage of time accurately in here. I was injured and could only hide. For dozens of days I've had nothing but arcane conjured food to eat, and I could barely do anything else..."

Arcane conjured food had no nutritional value whatsoever — it could only barely sustain life by filling the stomach with arcane energy. This young draenei was an Auchenai priest and likely not a professional mage, so the fact that he'd managed to keep himself alive in these ruins for over a month, starving as he was rather than starving to death, was already no small feat.

That went some way toward corroborating his sincerity, too. After all, a priest loyal to Maladaar wouldn't need to spend dozens of days making himself into this much of a wreck just to con a band of outsiders who had almost no business being in Auchindoun at all.

Grede said bluntly, "Tell me which direction the Shadow Council warlocks are holed up in, and we'll go straight there."

Levithus pointed to a doorway half-blocked by stones. "Enter through the doorway on the right. That winding path leads to a place your language calls the 'Arcane Sanctum' — an enormously grand, pillar-filled hall, and the most important area in all of Auchindoun. It's currently occupied by the Shadow Council and used as a site for their demon summoning rituals. I can take you close to it."

"Then let's go," Grede said, hefting his hammer and looking back at Vereesa and Sola. Both nodded.

Vereesa especially — if she hadn't spent all this time not knowing which direction to go, she'd probably have charged straight into a pile of orcs already.

Jace glanced back and exchanged a look with Morophil. Morophil nodded too — it seemed the succubus saw nothing amiss with this Levithus, same as him.

But the moment Levithus caught sight of the demon behind the group, his expression tensed and went rigid.

He almost instinctively raised the staff in his hand, a faint glow just beginning to kindle at its tip, when Jace seized the hammer-head of the staff and snuffed out that wisp of golden light.

"Don't be too hasty to judge, Levithus," Jace said in Eredun. "You should know — there's a reason I learned your language. And the reason I could come here when no friendly forces can enter Draenor is the same."

The arcane demon-light in Levithus's eye-sockets flickered for a while before dimming. "That's a demon you summoned."

"Yes," Jace said.

The priest said calmly, "You are walking an exceedingly dangerous path, friend. And that path is rife with corruption and depravity. I have never seen a single soul who walked that path meet a good end."

"I'm not here to argue with you, and I don't expect you to understand what I do." Jace stared into his eyes. "Lower your staff and let me and my friends figure out a way to rescue your father. Or stay here and wait to die alongside your Light and the spirits of your ancestors."

Levithus nodded. Jace let go and watched him lower the staff.

For a draenei, Levithus was a touch too tall — even now, starved as he was, his silhouette was starting to resemble a troll puffing out its chest.

Walking through the deep, dim corridors, Jace kept worrying that his height would draw attention and blow their cover.

Still, although this stretch of Auchindoun was littered with bones and thick with the foul stench of shadow, there were few undead about, and no living souls passing through.

This must be the contested zone between the Shadow Council and Maladaar — quite likely part of what would one day be the Sethekk Halls held by the arakkoa, or the Mana-Tombs occupied by the ethereals.

After walking for roughly two or three hours, Jace habitually glanced at his pocket watch — but here, telling time by watch wasn't reliable either. After all, they hadn't seen the sky once, and had no way to reckon when it was day and when it was night.

Just then, Vereesa said suddenly, "Orcs."

"Where?" Grede asked.

"Use your nose," Vereesa said.

Grede twitched his nose and his eyes went wide. "That's warg scent."

"They're orcs," Vereesa said firmly.

"Wargs!" Grede said through his teeth.

"Shh." Sola pressed a finger to his lips. "Keep it down."

"Whatever it is, it means we're getting close to the orcs," Jace said.

"I don't sense anything at all," Levithus said, lifting his head and sniffing the air. "We're still a long way from the Arcane Sanctum."

"I don't sense anything either, but on this you'd best trust a ranger's judgment," Jace said.

"And a dwarf hunter's," Grede added immediately.

Jace sighed faintly. Vereesa said suddenly, "Hide."

The group and the Broken quickly retreated and tucked themselves behind a corner. The tall draenei clopped forward on his hooves, cracking who knew how many stones with every step, the sharp clatter trailing behind him. It took him a while to get into position, and every step made Vereesa's eyes narrow involuntarily.

But by then it was already too late. From the pitch-black corridor ahead, unseen, came an orc's roar — and then the barking and pounding of wargs, growing closer by the second.

Vereesa placed two arrows on her bow and edged toward the corner, ready to greet the oncoming wargs with a surprise. But just as she was about to step out, she heard the sounds of more and more orcs talking in the distance.

A whole patrol?

That ruled out acting carelessly. They'd come to rescue captives, not to spring-clean the place.

Vereesa hesitated for a moment, but the wargs sounded closer with every heartbeat...

Just then, a figure in a black cloak and hood sauntered into view right beside her, completely startling everyone. It patted the ranger on the shoulder, stepped out of the shadow they were hiding in, and walked to the front of the corridor.

"Get back here, you worthless dogs," Jace barked in Orcish.

The two wargs were so frightened by Jace that they slammed on the brakes, nearly skidding to the ground. Hearing the command, they whimpered and turned tail. Jace planted Uthales on the ground with one hand and waved his thick, brown-green fingers at the patrol.

"Follow me."

"Right away, darling." Morophil grinned, trotting up on her hooves — and casually blew a kiss toward Vereesa, who stood there dumbfounded, too stunned to make a sound.
