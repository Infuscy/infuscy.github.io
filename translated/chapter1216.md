## Chapter 1216: A Suitable Identity

Hearing the commotion from the wargs, the distant patrol stopped talking and curiously looked over in ones and twos.

Jace observed the figures half-illuminated by torchlight up ahead while walking forward at an unhurried, unhurried pace over the rubble-strewn ground.

Thanks to his previous experiences with transformation, combined with Vaelastrasz's powerful transmutation magic, Jace had adapted to orcish mannerisms and bearing very quickly despite having only just transformed.

He shielded his eyes from the torchlight and walked toward them, leaning on Uthales with every step.

Jace noticed that this group wasn't composed entirely of orcs. It was an orc warlock, two orc kennelmasters, and four Broken servants in tattered clothing.

He wasn't surprised, of course. After all, the draenei priest they'd killed earlier hadn't been entirely grasping at straws when he'd called the Broken traitors.

The Broken had indeed helped Targonor and his Shadow Council summon M'mur, and the stronghold the Shadow Council called the "Shadow Labyrinth" in the game was full of Broken members.

Aside from these seven individuals and the two wargs, the deep corridor held no one else. Jace felt that he and Morophil should be able to deal with them quickly without exposing the rest of their companions to the entire Shadow Council.

But doing so still carried a risk — and that was the warlock.

The spellcasters in the Shadow Council were unquestionably experts, not the sort of warlock hacks you found in Moonbrook. This warlock very likely had her own summoned minions, quite possibly a succubus with a Soul Link.

Judging from his own Soul Link with Morophil, if he struck and killed this warlock, her demon minion would probably become immediately aware of many of his magical signatures. If her minion was right there at her side, ironically, that would actually make things less troublesome.

Jace sized up the group; they were sizing him up too.

He might as well try infiltrating the Shadow Council this way and see if he could find out where the captives were being kept. So as he walked, he quickly set about weaving a suitable identity for himself.

There was no question that the Shadow Council's loyalty to the Burning Legion ran deeper than their loyalty to the Horde. By now, the Horde on Draenor had fallen to pieces. Warchief Ner'zhul had taken the Horde's most elite members and vanished into the Twisting Nether. The remaining Horde members had either become Magtheridon's slaves or were fighting on their own in dire straits...

So showing up under a Horde identity at this point would carry little weight. After all, even when they'd been loyal to the Horde, the Shadow Council had looked down their noses at other Horde members with haughty contempt. He might as well go all the way and join the Burning Legion.

"Halt there, warlock." The female orc raised her torch, shining it in his direction. "I've never seen you, and I've never seen your succubus companion."

She gave Morophil a particularly careful look and said with considerable deference, "If I'd encountered a succubus warrior this powerful before, I certainly would have remembered it."

"I'm sure you would," Morophil said coldly.

The female orc nodded respectfully once more. The demon's coldness didn't provoke her at all, because her gaze had never left Morophil's fel markings. After all, those were the fel markings of Veriltain, a high-ranking satyr lord — a genuine Burning Legion sigil.

Jace hadn't expected that these fel markings, which had led no small number of people to mistake him and Morophil for Burning Legion lackeys, would come in handy for something like this one day.

"I am Yurok, dispatched by the great Lord Magtheridon to come here and meet with your master, Grandmaster Vorpil," Jace introduced himself.

Grandmaster Vorpil was one of the bosses within the Shadow Labyrinth. Twenty years from now, he would become the leader of the Shadow Council.

But at this point, with Gul'dan and Teron Gorefiend dead, and the ogre warlock Cho'gall having broken from the Horde and fled to Kalimdor, Vorpil should have been the highest-ranking member of the Shadow Council.

Of course, Jace was gambling on the memories of his past life here. If anything went wrong, aside from killing every living thing in this place as quickly as possible, he had one last card to play — Morophil's mental influence magic.

After hearing Jace's self-introduction, the warlock frowned and asked, "You broke through the Alliance encirclement and the black dragons' fire on your own, then slipped past the Auchenai blockade to get here?"

That was a lot of information packed into one sentence. It meant the Alliance Expedition currently controlled Terokkar Forest — or at least could stop demons from infiltrating here from the north.

But what was all this about black dragon fire? Why would black dragons attack the Horde? Hadn't Deathwing cooperated with the Horde to transport dragon eggs from Azeroth here? Why would the black dragons have turned on the Shadow Council?

Jace had the faint, unsettling feeling that this change might have something to do with him. After all, in another timeline, Teron Gorefiend had returned to Draenor alongside Deathwing, and had led the Shadow Council alongside the black dragons in the defense of Hellfire Citadel and Karabor Temple — until Turalyon killed him with his own hands.

But in this timeline, Teron Gorefiend had died an ignoble death — twice in a row, in the ruins of Dalaran and Ironforge City — and his soul had dissipated entirely within Jace's own consciousness, leaving behind only a few dark spells as his legacy. He'd never made it back through the Dark Portal.

And those artifacts had all been stolen by Kel'Thuzad and handed to Deathwing, then carried to Draenor by the black dragons. Presumably for that very reason, the Shadow Council orcs had wanted to get in touch with Kel'Thuzad, who had taken an enormous risk on their behalf — to give him a Draenor portal coordinate, to leave him an escape route in case he got thrown into the Violet Hold.

Which brought to mind what Galders had mentioned before — that the warlocks captured at the lighthouse by the Wizard Sanctum had revealed secrets concerning Kel'Thuzad. He'd hidden many of the secrets Deathwing had left him inside a small pocket dimension in the Twisting Nether, using a doomguard.

Back then, Galders and Jace had been somewhat skeptical — would Kel'Thuzad really summon a doomguard alongside the warlocks just to hide things?

Now that they knew about the portal coordinates, and connected it to the business of fleeing to the Dark Portal and the Black Dragonflight, it wasn't hard to guess: this doomguard had most likely been summoned by the orc warlocks on Kel'Thuzad's behalf, used to activate that portal coordinate and escape Dalaran.

Far, far too many connections flooded into Jace's mind all at once. He was beginning to realize that the impact of his being forced to kill Teron Gorefiend alongside Vereesa on the streets of Dalaran all those years ago... might have rippled all the way to Draenor. The situation here now might not be quite what he knew.

And the Dark Portal?

He'd executed one of Kil'jaeden's jailers in front of the gates of Nethergarde Keep. What had that brought to Draenor?

Of course, Jace couldn't ask too many questions. After all, as an emissary of the Burning Legion, an orc warlock loyal to Lord Magtheridon, he was supposed to have a crystal-clear understanding of all these matters.

Morophil slowly wrapped her arm around Jace's neck. "My master certainly didn't come alone, my lady. With my help, he was able to evade those Auchenai and fool Auchindoun's soul-watchers to get here."

"Yes." The warlock nodded respectfully, then said, "However, I know that Lord Magtheridon has already blessed Hellfire Citadel with his sacred blood. The envoys from Hellfire Citadel who came here before have all become... imbued with the sacred blood..."

She was referring to "fel orcs" — terrifying fel creatures that resulted from drinking a pit lord's blood twice. They were already very close to demons compared to ordinary orcs. Their skin had turned from fel-green to blood-red, their eyes spewed flame like fresh blood, demonic spikes covered their bodies, their frames were larger, and their tempers were more volatile and impulsive. But in return, they followed their demon masters' commands with even greater fervor.

After arriving in Outland and taking over the Horde that Ner'zhul had abandoned, Magtheridon had quickly made the orcs there drink his blood, to better enslave them into his service and more savagely attack the Expedition and the draenei, helping him conquer Outland completely.

The Shadow Council members inside Auchindoun, cut off from communication with the outside world, had never had the chance to drink Magtheridon's blood and become fel orcs. So how was Jace, an orc arriving from outside, supposed to explain himself?

This actually stumped Jace, because he could hardly use "hadn't gotten around to drinking it yet" as an excuse.

Although Magtheridon had probably only been on Draenor for a short two or three years, Hellfire Citadel might indeed still have some orcs who hadn't drunk the blood yet. But as a "special envoy," he ought to be fairly high-ranking — even if he hadn't drunk it yet, it would make more sense for him to drink it first before being dispatched.

It really didn't add up. Jace used the Soul Link to give Morophil a subtle hint, intending to have her deploy her mental magic.

Unexpectedly, Morophil let out a cold laugh. "You ignorant fool. Not every blessed one who drinks an Annihilan's blood twice turns into one of those brain-dead red-skinned madmen. If you ask me, those types are the ones squandering that precious fel-fire blood. Read a few more Eredar tomes and educate yourself."

The warlock was clearly cowed by Morophil's overbearing, razor-edged attitude. After all, who wanted to argue the properties of demon blood with a high-ranking succubus who looked like she'd be unpleasant to cross?

Morophil's persuasiveness wasn't just in her bearing, either — Jace's mind was jogged by her words too.

Because this wasn't just the succubus talking out of her ass. The powerful Hellfire Wardens who guarded the imprisoned Magtheridon in the future were a group of high-ranking warlocks who hadn't been twisted into red-skinned madmen by the fel blood.

Not only had they not turned red-skinned, they'd also been able to resist the compulsion in the blood to obey Magtheridon's commands — which was precisely why they'd earned Illidan Stormrage's trust.

And in another parallel-timeline Draenor in the future, the fel orcs who drank Mannoroth's blood twice were all black-skinned, wreathed in green fel light — fairly close to Jace's current brown-green skin. So while pushing it this far was a stretch, it couldn't be called wildly far-fetched.

Truly, as a former lieutenant of Overlord Brutallus, Morophil's level of knowledge about pit lords was convincing. Jace realized he still had some catching up to do in this department.

This was also the reason Jace hadn't brought Sola, who could also turn into an orc. The Shadow Council was nothing like the chaotic Burning Steppes, and still less like Shadowforge City. Every orc here was either a fanatical Burning Legion devotee or a warlock steeped in dark magic. Sola really couldn't blend in here — only a demon like Morophil could manage it.

"I have no further questions, Envoy Yurok." The warlock tilted her head and looked at one of the kennelmasters beside her, clad in dark leather. "Take the envoy to the Arcane Sanctum. The rest of you, follow me — we've still got tonight's patrol."
