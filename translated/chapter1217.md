## Chapter 1217: The Arcane Sanctum

Deep in the ruined halls of Auchindoun, the corridors were shattered and hollow. The fire basins the orcs used for lighting were actually burning the souls of draenei or the Broken — lean in close and you could still hear the faint, miserable whimpering of those souls.

The pale blue-white flames those souls gave off were as hopeless and feeble as the spirits themselves, illuminating the walls and doorways but unable to light the vaulted ceiling.

Though Jace was a shadow manipulator capable of reading emotions from those flames, he found it hard to completely ignore their pain and struggle the way those orc warlocks did. So every time he passed one of those fire basins, he felt deeply uncomfortable.

But right now he could only try to block it all out, because he'd soon be arriving at a place full of high-ranking warlocks. The slightest emotional slip could arouse their suspicion.

This wasn't the first time Jace had infiltrated Horde territory using an orc transformation. Vereesa and Grede were no fools — they'd probably figure out soon enough why he'd turned into an orc and followed the patrol toward the Shadow Council's territory.

Right now, he watched the kennelmaster and a warg ahead of him, stroking the scalding fel flames on Sazeno's head where the imp perched on his shoulder.

"You'll be a great help to me, Sazeno," he murmured in Demonic. "You can help me more than Morophil can. You'll speak with our master for me when the time comes, won't you?"

"Yes, my master, I understand. For the Burning Legion!" Sazeno said with a grin.

Morophil shot Sazeno a cold look. Jace could tell that posing as a member of the Burning Legion was more painful for her than pretending to be in love with him.

Hearing Sazeno's confirmation, Jace nodded. Once he found where the captives were being held in here, Sazeno could use Phase Shift to slip out and find Vereesa and the others, so they could coordinate an inside-outside pincer.

Now, first he needed to be on guard against whether the Shadow Council had any floating eyes — demons that could easily detect invisibility. From his memory, although a fairly powerful floating eye had entered the Auchenai Crypts to harvest souls in the future, there shouldn't be any where the Shadow Council was based.

But after all, this was twenty years earlier, and there was always a gap between the game and reality. Jace couldn't let his guard down just like that.

After following the kennelmaster for about half an hour, running into several patrols along the way, Jace realized that the Shadow Council's presence here was far larger than he'd imagined.

Although purple banners identical to the Shadow Council's on Azeroth sometimes hung on the walls and doorways, when the orcs saluted each other they rarely mentioned the Shadow Council. Instead, they called themselves by another name — something that roughly translated to "the Secret Cult" in Orcish.

The Shadow Council here was no longer the "elite warlock cabal" of Gul'dan's era. It was more like an orc clan devoted to the demons of the Burning Legion.

Before long, rounding a corridor, a towering guard demon appeared before them. Jace had no particular wariness toward these demons — enormous and fearsome as they were, they weren't exactly blessed with sharp wits. He gave it a glance and kept walking.

Still, seeing a guard like this meant they were close to the Shadow Council's real stronghold.

Sure enough, passing behind the guard and entering a short tunnel, Jace could already hear a constant rise and fall of murmurs, shouts, and voices.

At the tunnel's end, passing through a doorway, he entered an enormous, dimly lit hall. This was the Arcane Sanctum, then?

The grand, draenei-style hall was draped with tattered purple Shadow Council banners. Leather tents and battered wooden crates were scattered everywhere.

Jace looked at the members of the "Secret Cult" lying or standing about and couldn't help frowning. Whether it was the Shadow Council, the Blackrock clan, or the Dragonmaw orcs, they all seemed to be cut from the same cloth.

Whether they occupied a dwarf stronghold, a human city, or a draenei sanctum, they always ended up propping up shacks inside these grand buildings and forcing themselves through a miserable semi-nomadic existence.

Unlike Grim Batol or Blackrock Mountain, the entire Arcane Sanctum reeked of depraved shadow-rot. On top of that, the crash of weapons in brawls and the sound of cursing, the obscene panting of orcs coupling wildly with succubi, the wet slap of flesh against mucus... the stench of sweat and rotting food, mingled with cloying, nauseating perfume, crept into the nostrils in waves.

The kennelmaster and his warg picked their way over twitching black-green backsides, bare rumps and legs. Jace followed behind, reining in his reactions as best he could, trying not to let the chaos get to him.

Because he'd noticed several orc warlocks sitting against the wall, watching him, their gazes tracking Jace's footsteps slowly.

They might not care who this unfamiliar warlock was or why he'd come. But if Jace's emotions showed the slightest irregularity, they'd catch it for certain.

Jace glanced back at Morophil. She narrowed her eyes at him, the tip of her pointed tongue peeking out slightly from the corner of her mouth. Jace took a deep breath, recalled a few of the wild nights back at Yllagala Tower, and used that to fully immerse himself in the chaos around him.

Just then, one of the orc warlocks seemed to lose interest in observing him. He lowered his head idly and buried his face between the thighs of a succubus beside him. But that succubus kept her eyes fixed on Morophil, never looking away.

Fortunately, the emotion radiating from that succubus wasn't wariness or tension — it was tinged with jealousy amid faint fear. But whether she was jealous of Morophil's figure, her looks, or her power, Jace couldn't read that finely.

The kennelmaster led Jace straight across the hall to a breach formed by a collapsed wall. Inside the breach, an orc in dark yellow armor was sitting and tearing into the raw meat of some kind of fowl.

Seeing the two approach, he stopped chewing and looked up. The kennelmaster said, "A warlock from Hellfire Citadel. An envoy. He wishes to see Grandmaster Vorpil."

The armored orc looked suspicious. He eyed the kennelmaster. "Did you question him? He doesn't look like one of theirs."

"Lady Taka confirmed he was dispatched by Lord Magtheridon..." the kennelmaster said.

Hearing Magtheridon's name, several nearby orcs who'd been dozing all looked over.

"Dispatched by Lord Magtheridon, hmm. How did Taka confirm that?" The armored orc put the meat aside, wiped the grease from his hands, and stood up. "You just walked across that stretch of wilderness watched by the black dragons, did you, warlock?"

"At Hellfire Citadel, even Shatterer Krytan addresses me as 'Grandmaster,'" Jace said coldly.

Shatterer Krytan was another warlock of considerable standing within the Horde. Jace wasn't sure whether he'd once been a member of the Shadow Council, but he was at least of the Shadowmoon clan, stationed at the Blood Furnace of Hellfire Citadel alongside a host of Shadowmoon orcs — so he'd very likely joined the Shadow Council.

The orcs here were sealed tight inside Auchindoun, cut off from the outside world. They'd hardly have the means to go check with Krytan whether he knew a warlock named Yurok.

Jace had taken a risk dragging Krytan's name out to boost his own standing, mainly because he didn't want to explain how he'd made his way here from Hellfire Citadel. After all, he didn't know the current situation on Draenor — the more he explained, the more holes would show up.

Still, dragging Krytan's name out proved remarkably effective. The orc swallowed and nodded. "Grandmaster."

Then he turned to Morophil and thumped his chest with a fist, saying in Demonic — which sounded almost indistinguishable from Orcish — "Noble lady."

With that, he said awkwardly, "It's not that Grandmaster Vorpil disrespects an envoy of the Legion come from Hellfire Citadel, but he never sees anyone this late. He's very old and needs his rest."

"I can wait," Jace said. "I'll see him tomorrow."

"Then tomorrow I'll take you to him, Grandmaster. You... ah, can rest wherever you like." The orc glanced around, his tone growing distinctly flustered.

"Much obliged," Jace said.

The orc straightened up. "The Legion will reign."

"For the Legion," Jace said.

Truth be told, not getting to see Vorpil was actually a good thing for Jace. He had no interest in matching wits with a Shadowmaster. If he could find where the Shadow Council was holding prisoners tonight and bust the captives out while the warlocks were resting, they'd be gone.

But looking at those soul-fire basins and the Soul Shards scattered all over the ground, Jace had already lost most of his hope. A captive locked up in a place like this for over a month — there probably wouldn't even be scum left of him by now.

Yet just as he was calculating where to start looking, a rough shout suddenly rang out from behind him. "Hey! Fresh face!"

Jace turned. An enormously tall, powerfully built ogre with two heads was leaning against a stone pillar, watching him.

He wore enchanted metal pauldrons and a dull crimson-purple robe draped across his body. His bare chest and arms were covered in thick black fel markings.

Jace could tell almost at a glance who this was: "Inciter" Blac'kat, one of the leaders of the Shadow Council inside Auchindoun.

"I can see your cloak is made from black dragon hide! Ha! Impressive." Blac'kat, the head bearing the black horn, mumbled with a crooked grin. "Come over here and chat with me. Where'd you get your hands on a treasure like that?"
