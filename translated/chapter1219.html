Chapter 1219: We're Already Friends

"You don't seem too sure." Seeing Jace's momentary hesitation, the single-horned head of Blac'kat curled the corner of its mouth. "The 'Book of the Fel Names' — it's a grimoire. I'm not certain of its true name; perhaps it has no name at all, Envoy Yurok. But this much I know: it records the true name of every single demon in the Twisting Nether... Does that ring a bell?"

Jace didn't answer immediately, because Blac'kat's tone made it sound as though he ought to know about this "Book of the Fel Names."

He carefully masked his emotions while thinking it over — Jace Sesso, who'd come all the way from Brill, certainly wouldn't know. But should the Legion envoy Yurok know?

Blac'kat's description wasn't far off. By the accounts of many in the game, the Grimoire of the Fel Names did indeed record the true name of every demon.

Twenty years from now, one of Illidan Stormrage's subordinates — Varedis the Felborne, the first of the blood elf demon hunters — would find this grimoire in Blac'kat's sanctum and absorb its power. Not only would he gain command over the fatal weaknesses of countless demons, he would also gain a body that was all but impossible to kill.

However, Varedis hadn't managed to break the Shadow Council's seal and take the grimoire. But he'd believed that after stealing the grimoire's power, the physical book itself would be useless, so he'd left it where it was.

And that became the key to defeating Varedis as a player — take this book from Blac'kat here in the Shadow Labyrinth, and burn it while Varedis was channeling the grimoire's power, and you could destroy his undying body.

Jace was almost certain that at this very moment — more than a decade before Varedis would absorb the power of the Grimoire of the Fel Names — the Shadow Council shouldn't have obtained this book yet.

Otherwise, given how important this grimoire was, Blac'kat — as a clever ogre warlock, a high-ranking member of the Secret Cult, even one of its leaders — would never have so casually revealed the Shadow Council's treasure to an outsider warlock who'd been here less than half a day.

So why did Blac'kat have the feeling that Jace ought to know the whereabouts of this book?

The answer was probably obvious: the book was currently in the hands of the Burning Legion, and most likely in Magtheridon's personal possession.

That made perfect sense too. If there was anyone in the Twisting Nether who naturally ought to possess a tome "recording the true names of all demons," it would be the Burning Legion — not the Shadow Council hiding in Auchindoun.

A decade and a half later, Illidan Stormrage would lead Kael'thas Sunstrider's Sunfury forces and Vashj's naga into Outland, storm Magtheridon's Black Temple, and imprison him beneath the Hellfire Ramparts. The Shadow Council had probably seized the chaos of that opportunity to filch the Legion's Grimoire of the Fel Names for themselves...

So Blac'kat's purpose in bringing up the Grimoire of the Fel Names right now was obvious. The fellow had somehow caught wind of this grimoire's existence and was trying to sound Jace out.

The bastard really had more tricks than an ogre had any right to. Talking about trading stories for treasure locations, putting on that naive, wide-eyed ogre act — when in reality he'd known all along where Jace had come from. All that chatter about the black dragon cloak, the legend of Khadgar — it had all been to fish for clues about the Grimoire of the Fel Names.

Having straightened all this out, Jace had a pretty clear picture now.

A Grimoire of the Fel Names? Something that held the lifeblood of Magtheridon's entire legion in its hands? How could a mere orc envoy like himself possibly know about a thing like that?

"Every demon's true name?" Jace glanced at a patrolling guard demon passing outside the door. "Including his?"

"Including him, certainly." Blac'kat's one-eyed head said with certainty. Then he raised a hand and traced a small circle in the air, lowering his voice. "All of them. Every demon in the Twisting Nether... It may even bear the name of Kil'jaeden himself, Envoy Yurok. Just think — this treasure's power is no less than the Skull of Gul'dan. You could even say that the Scepter of Sargeras itself pales beside it. To possess it is to possess the means to stand against the Legion itself! And you've truly never heard of it?"

"The topic of Lord Kil'jaeden... makes me feel we should speak no more of this, Blac'kat," Jace said quietly.

Even as he said it, Jace tried to gather the yearning for boundless power churning in his heart and set it ablaze like a fire.

He wished it would light up this ogre's face a little, let the fellow see it nice and clear.

By now, both of Blac'kat's heads were smiling. Jace knew he'd already sensed that searing desire with perfect clarity.

"Fear not, esteemed envoy. No third party will learn of this conversation. I trust you'll also keep our little agreement. I rather feel we can call ourselves friends by now." As he spoke, the ogre gently stroked a raised bulge on the arm of his abstract stone seat with one thick finger.

"If the Legion truly considers the Book of the Fel Names precious enough, they shouldn't be leaving it in the hands of a demon lord who guards a portal, don't you think, envoy? A thing like that should be placed in the hands of someone truly capable of protecting it."

He laughed without a care in the world. After all, Auchindoun was surrounded by black dragons and Alliance armies, and the main force of the Burning Legion was still far away. Even if Magtheridon learned that Blac'kat wasn't so loyal to the Legion and had his eye on the Legion's treasures, his retribution wouldn't be falling on Blac'kat's head anytime soon.

But for the Legion envoy Yurok, the situation was entirely different.

Seeing Jace remain silent, the single-horned head continued. "There is far more to the secrets of that grimoire than this. If you could help me and Grandmaster Vorpil learn the name of that lord, we wouldn't mind sharing more of what we know. For us demon summoners, it would be the highest honor imaginable just to catch a glimpse of the thing's face — we don't aspire to open and read it."

"Indeed," Jace nodded. "You speak truly."

"Easy, my envoy." Blac'kat raised a hand. "This is Auchindoun, not the fortress of Kargath Bladefist. You can say whatever you like..."

"I can't guarantee anything, Blac'kat," Jace said.

The ogre couldn't help but laugh again. He wiped the blood from his robe, reached over, and patted Jace on the shoulder. "This is an enormously important step forward for the alliance between the Shadow Council and Lord Magtheridon, envoy. I believe Grandmaster Vorpil will be most delighted to meet with you tomorrow. Most, most delighted."

"Let's hope so," Jace said. Blac'kat's single-horned head laughed so hard it broke into violent coughing.

The one-eyed head, gasping for breath, said, "Ah, I think I've kept you far too long, Envoy Yurok — my most treasured friend. Hmm, I imagine your lady companion is already burning with impatience back in your den. Go on, go satisfy her."

Jace nodded, braced himself against the cave wall, and left the ogre's den, returning to the unspeakably debauched, chaotic hall. Morophil was waiting for him not far away.

Set against the orcs and demons who had utterly lost themselves to carnal desire, Morophil actually stood out as somewhat distinctive.

But tonight was no night to stand out. Jace walked over, pulled her into his arms with one hand, and pressed her to the ground as forcefully as if he were grabbing a rabbit. Morophil cried out in surprise, then couldn't help trembling with laughter.

"So, my master, you finally willing to let me taste your true power?" she asked, eyes narrowing. "I'm serious," Jace said in a low voice.

"Don't expect too much, darling. The situation is bad."

Blac'kat's laughter echoed through the entire Arcane Hall. Jace bent down and pressed a light kiss between Morophil's breasts, breathing in her maddening demon-fel scent. "Just bad? That's already infinitely better than I'd feared."

"How worth celebrating, that is," Morophil said excitedly, cupping Jace's face. "Quick, give up your power, my envoy husband — prepare fully for what we're about to do... I feel you're more excited than usual. What is it?"

Jace smiled enigmatically and pinched Morophil's chin hard. "Don't worry, I'll tell you..."
