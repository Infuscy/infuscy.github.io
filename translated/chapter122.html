Chapter 122: Mana and Ley-Line Nodes

Jace slipped into the Wizard Sanctum library like he owned the place and ended up right by the language stacks, the way he always did — he'd been spending so much time here studying Orcish that his feet carried him there on autopilot.

He turned back and asked the librarian for books on mana, spirit, and stamina. The section the librarian pointed him to, though, required checking his apprentice sigil before he could get in. Turns out if you weren't an apprentice, you couldn't lay hands on those books at all.

Everything he'd looked up before had been auxiliary subjects — alchemy, herbalism, that sort of thing — or languages. This was the first time he'd gone looking for something actually magical.

Maybe the Wizard Sanctum library followed some Order of Tirisfal common rule against arcane abuse after all.

There were a hell of a lot of books on human mana. Row after row of shelves, most of it dedicated to the subject. Over the past few thousand years, humans had run God knew how many experiments trying to figure out exactly where mana came from. The surprising part wasn't the research — it was how little elven scholarship there was on the subject.

After skimming a few books on the origins of mana, Jace realized that the high elves didn't seem to care much about things like mana capacity or spiritual stamina at all.

Because they had the Sunwell.

The Sunwell was a magical well on the island of Quel'Danas, north of Quel'Thalas. It was the holy site of the entire elven kingdom and the pillar of their civilization.

Ten thousand years ago, the War of the Ancients had destroyed the Highborne's Well of Eternity, leveled the Kaldorei capital of Zin-Azshari, and shattered the Highborne's dream of eternal rule.

The fallen aristocrats were captured by their former inferiors — the night elves, as they'd come to be known — and forced to give up the arcane and submit to a more natural way of life. But some of the Highborne refused to accept that.

The ancestor of Quel'Thalas's royal house, Dath'Remar Sunstrider, had stolen one of the Well of Eternity's vials from the night elves' watch, crossed the great ocean, and arrived on the Eastern Kingdoms, a land split between two massive troll empires. Those vials were the last remnants of the destroyed Well of Eternity.

That vial of eternal waters was poured into a magical spring in Quel'Thalas, forming the mighty Sunwell — and it became the second home of these Highborne exiles.

For thousands of years, these elves who considered themselves a superior people had poured out countless lives in defense of the Sunwell. Because it was the power of these magical waters that allowed the elves to thrive and grow — from the first time the Highborne had walked the ancient lands of Kalimdor, right up to the present day.

Whether the Highborne of old or the elves of Quel'Thalas now, whenever they wanted to wield magic, the boundless energy of the Well of Eternity or the Sunwell was right there at their disposal.

These elves and the magical wells that accompanied them lived intertwined, symbiotic. You could say those wells had given birth to them, nurtured them. The elves could skip food and water, but they couldn't stray too far from a magical well for long.

In return, those wells looked after them, cared for them, even indulged their every need… For many elves who never left the wells' reach, mana was like air — there when you called it, gone when you waved it away. Only uncivilized races on the fringes of the world would rack their brains scraping together a scrap of mana.

Jace realized he was staring at the brain-scrapings of one such uncivilized race: humanity.

By observing the elves and the world they lived in, humans had reached a conclusion: if you wanted to strengthen your mana, you had to get close to a source of it, and reinforce your spiritual power through long-term exposure.

Some people strengthened themselves by constantly casting spells, actively drawing arcane energy to swirl around them, building an affinity between their bodies and magic through repeated contact and communication — raising their spiritual power that way.

Marin hadn't told him that some people chose to go straight to nature's mana sources — the ley lines, the most energy-rich channels that ran beneath the surface of Azeroth.

Or the most powerful mana sources of all: ley-line nodes.

Ley-line nodes were places where countless ley lines converged. Jace knew the most powerful ley-line node on Azeroth was up north, in Northrend — the home of the blue dragons. It was called the Nexus, and in the dragon tongue, "the Nexus" and "ley-line node" were the same word.

Another one sat to the south, and quite close to Stormwind — the place where Karazhan stood. Even the blue dragons believed Karazhan sat at the intersection of nearly every major ley line.

Like the Nexus and Karazhan, the other, weaker ley-line nodes had long since been claimed by various temples and capitals. The magical spring where the Sunwell had been born, for instance, was itself a ley-line node no less powerful than Karazhan or the Nexus.

Jace was starting to understand why Marin had never mentioned this approach. An apprentice like him simply didn't have the means to get close to any major mana source and live some kind of training-retreat lifestyle.

Every ley-line node was either guarded by heavy forces, or just approaching it was dangerous enough on its own. The Karazhan Cataclysm hadn't just destroyed the whole of Deadwind Pass — it had blackened half of Elwynn on top of it, and that wasn't even getting into the horrors at the bottom of the tower.

As for the Sunwell, just the thought of it made Jace let out a bitter laugh.

At this point in time, there were barely any humans who could even cross the threshold into Quel'Thalas, let alone make it past the runestones and the indestructible magical barrier of Ban'dinoriel into Silvermoon City.

And the Sunwell lay even farther north, hidden behind Silvermoon's white walls and golden tiles — you had to cross a strait to reach it. Legend held that every single elf guarding the Sunwell was scion of a noble house, a scion of a great clan, the cream of the crop among an already elite force.

Forget humans — even an elf whose bloodline wasn't pure enough wasn't allowed anywhere near it.

A headache. A real headache.

Knowing too much was actually making Jace feel worse and harder about the whole business of improving his mana. If he didn't know all this messy stuff, maybe he could've clung to a little blind optimism.

Jace kept flipping through records of other ley-line nodes, trying to find one he could actually reach. He even found accounts of mages investigating frequent elemental activity in Westfall, complete with notes on the mages' attempts to communicate with the elements in Kalimdorian.

Didn't expect a Wizard Sanctum mage to try and pick up shamanism on the side just to get stronger — more desperate than he was, honestly. At least warlocks and mages had something in common. That was brutal.

In a book on the geography of Hillsbrad, he found a record of a ley line slumbering beneath Ambermill, running straight to Dalaran.

Right — Dalaran itself was a ley-line node. No wonder the elven and human mages had chosen to build their city there, sheltering spellcasters who'd fallen out of favor in the various kingdoms.

With the ship to Dalaran leaving in just two days, Jace couldn't help feeling a little excited.

Even though he'd been born in Lordaeron and grown up by the shores of Lake Lordamere under the Order of Tirisfal's watch, he'd never once entered — or even gotten close to — that city of magic. He'd only ever gazed at it from across the lake, at the faint violet towers shimmering in the distance.

He hoped the journey would go smoothly, and that nothing else would go wrong.
