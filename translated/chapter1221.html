Chapter 1221: The Floating Demon

This floating eye was many times larger than that odd floating eye "ticket-taker" Jace had encountered in Booty Bay — the difference between a calf and a bull. Yet even with its enormous body, its countless tentacles fumbling and flailing as it slowly descended from above, it didn't make the faintest sound.

Was this the floating eye boss from the Auchenai Crypts? Jace didn't know, and there was no point in finding out.

The appearance of a floating eye meant that neither Invisibility Potions nor Phase Shift had any meaning here. Back in Booty Bay, that little floating eye had been able to spot Morophil trying to sneak aboard a ship to skip on her ticket from half a bay away — to say nothing of this powerful floating eye "lord" before him now.

In his demon-detecting vision, the blue-green flesh-ball's cluster of small eyes lining the top and the great central pupil radiated an arcane glare that nearly swallowed every other demonic silhouette, flashing like a giant light bulb. It was enough to make Jace's consciousness feel almost "dazzled."

Jace even worried whether Sazeno had already been spotted by this floating eye. But the imp had left some time ago, and this floating eye was drifting over slowly from a distance, its great pupil fixed solely on Jace's direction rather than the way the imp had left — so it probably hadn't caught anything unusual yet.

It could also be that the Arcane Sanctum was full of all kinds of imps — visible ones, invisible ones, lone ones, ones in swarms — and Jace's little imp didn't stand out that much among them.

Jace's mind raced. The appearance of the floating eye made it nearly impossible for Vereesa and the others to rely on the imp's guidance to sneak in. At the same time, it meant that rescuing the Auchenai priest and those two unknown human Expedition soldiers peacefully, without anyone the wiser, was no longer realistic.

But even if they had to resort to force, at the very least they couldn't let Vereesa and the others be driven into a corner.

He was an orc warlock, an envoy dispatched by the Burning Legion — whatever happened, he still had room to maneuver. As for the others, aside from Sola, who might still find a chance to slip away on her own, what about Grede and Vereesa? They'd have no choice but to fight their way out.

Jace also didn't know what methods Blac'kat and Vorpil had at their disposal to deal with his group. In the game, Blac'kat wielded mental magic capable of turning friends against each other, and considering the confident, cunning air he'd just displayed, the more Jace thought about it, the more uneasy he felt.

And once fighting broke out, it was very likely that the weakened captives — drained of power and soul like living batteries — would accidentally die in the crossfire. Then all their effort would have been for nothing...

Wait. Their methods of invisibility were useless now — but was his own disguise still good?

Morophil hadn't tried to turn invisible at all, and Jace could only press himself against the wall. He quickly scanned his surroundings, looking for a fissure or side passage to duck into, but the nearest intersection was too far away. There was no way to get there without drawing attention.

What's more, the floating eye had clearly already spotted him. Drifting closer and closer from above, it kept that enormous central eye fixed on Jace almost the entire time, paying virtually no notice to every other warlock and patrolling soldier that passed.

But whatever it had seen, it didn't say a word. It simply floated slowly, silently past from overhead, swiveled its great pupil to look ahead, and turned into another corridor, leaving.

Jace looked at Morophil beside him. At the moment, she was gazing at him full of guilt and said softly, "I'm sorry, my master."

Jace shook his head. He had no intention of blaming her for failing to detect this fiend. This sort of demon was among the strangest and most peculiar members of the Burning Legion, and rarely appeared on the battlefield in any real sense. What's more, floating eyes were natural counters to magic-users who specialized in invisibility, like Morophil.

Of course, it was precisely because floating eyes possessed a stronger will than most demons of the Legion that even after joining the Legion, they tended to be less loyal and more self-serving.

Was there any possibility of turning it through some means? If he revealed Uthales, it should frighten it into cooperating. But that would mean exposing the fact that he possessed Uthales to the entire Burning Legion.

Any other ideas? "Jace Sesso!"

A rough demonic voice suddenly roared inside his head, making Jace snap around toward the direction the floating eye had just vanished — before he realized it was Sazeno's voice. Damn it, he'd almost thought that floating eye had even seen through his thoughts and memories.

The imp's Void Call was only one cry — meaning he'd already found Vereesa and the others and they were preparing to move. Which meant Jace had to coordinate with them now.

An idea surfaced in his mind: he could try to forcibly enslave this floating eye, and after Vereesa rescued everyone, withdraw together to a safe distance and then release it, avoiding having this immensely powerful demon occupy his mental strength for too long.

As for whether it would expose him after being released, Jace figured that by then they'd have long since left Auchindoun and found the Expedition. Who cared whether it exposed him or not?

Jace immediately turned and followed the floating eye that had just passed by.

Rounding the corner of the corridor, the demon was drifting along leisurely at a height, and by now it had turned into another corridor's corner.

Jace glanced at the folds of demon-flesh on its back, calculating just how much power it might possess.

Forcibly enslaving a demon in a healthy state — before it had been weakened or its will broken — consumed an enormous amount of power and was difficult to control.

But Jace had neither the leisure nor the time to fight this floating eye, let alone the kind that could easily slip between planes — far more adept at it than an imp. If Jace didn't finish it in one blow, it would find a chance to flee deep into the palace and tell everything that had happened here to the members of the Shadow Council. In which case, Vereesa and the others might not have drawn any attention yet, and he'd be the one tripping the alarm himself.

So Jace had to try to forcibly enslave this fellow. In fact, Jace had never used a demon enslavement spell to forcibly control a fully healthy demon before. He pulled out a Refined Elixir of Wisdom and gripped it tight.

When he thought about it, every time he'd used a Refined Elixir of Wisdom before had been for grand occasions — the Black Dragonflight, the avatar of Hakkar, the black dragon princess, even Deathwing himself. He really didn't want to waste a bottle of potion on some floating eye that had popped out of god-knew-where.

But if he didn't use it this time, he didn't know if he'd even make it through safely, to say nothing of relying on the draenei to find Expedition members later... He simply downed the entire little bottle of Refined Elixir of Wisdom in one gulp and quickened his pace, leaning on his staff.

Jace didn't dare follow the floating eye too closely, lest he arouse its wariness. Right now he was lurking in a doorway with a view of the great hall of the Arcane Sanctum, pacing back and forth slightly with his spellbook in hand, waiting for the floating eye to drift a bit further while watching everyone else's movements.

From here, all he could see was a guard demon standing spear in hand before a pillar, and the orcs all dead asleep in their bunks.

Judging by Sazeno's scurrying speed, plus the time he'd shouted Jace's name, and roughly estimating the distance between himself and Grede's group — by now the imp had probably already brought the rescue party into the Shadow Council's territory.

Of course, Jace had no way of telling whether Vereesa had slipped in, or where she'd gotten to — because if he could spot her at a glance, the orc patrols would be able to see her too.

"Find them," Jace murmured in Demonic into the succubus's ear. "Don't worry about me here."

"If that's your order, my master." Morophil nodded.

Jace patted her cheek lightly, watched her walk away and vanish at the end of the corridor, then quickened his pace again in the direction the floating eye had left.

The floating eye drifted slowly, surveying the surroundings as it moved further and further from the Arcane Sanctum and the prison where the captives were held.

Jace watched it drift away from a distance down the corridor, his nerves winding tighter and tighter — because through the Soul Link with Morophil, he could sense something he couldn't quite put his finger on. Perhaps they'd already made contact. Perhaps they'd already begun cooperating. Perhaps they'd already entered that prison...

Morophil had already scouted this place thoroughly. She knew exactly where to avoid the patrols and where to take cover. As long as she coordinated well with Vereesa, nothing would go wrong.

Honestly, Jace had no need to worry. Back in Maraudon, she'd already coordinated with Vereesa...

Suddenly, the floating eye spun around in midair and locked onto this direction — no, behind Jace. Had it sensed something?

At almost the same instant, Jace felt Morophil's soul suddenly seized by something and tighten with fear. Had she also detected the floating eye's wariness over there?

The floating eye's speed abruptly increased. Above the deep vaulted ceiling, it gathered a cloud of shadow-mist, carrying an ethereal humming as it flew toward them.

It was heading straight for the corridor that led to the prison. The flesh-ball, which had been drifting along slowly, covered the distance to Jace's side in mere seconds.

Now. "Verata..."
