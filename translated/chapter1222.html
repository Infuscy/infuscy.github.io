Chapter 1222: The Shortest Time, the Greatest Space

Jace had barely spoken the first word of the incantation when the observer's smaller eyes suddenly swiveled onto him!

"Tahotusqi, Milon..."

"I knew it, that unnatural blaze!" the observer roared in Demonic, fixing its largest eye straight on him. Prismatic light gathered in pulses from its pupil, but Jace still rattled off the incantation as fast as he could, then thrust his left hand high toward the source of that iridescent glow.

"Tahototani! Hear my command, demon!"

Jace bellowed in Demonic. A burning sigil of shadow blazed to life on the observer's orb. But the creature wobbled its fleshy body and muttered, "No mortal can enslave... Lord Shirrak... you are all my servants, all my slaves, all the souls of Auchindoun will surely..."

The demon-enslaving shadow sigil burned with crimson bloodfire under Jace's direction, and the force of it slammed straight into the eye.

"Arrogant mortal! Arrogant! Arrogant!"

The moment he seized control of the observer, Jace felt the world go black. The entire corridor plunged into darkness so thick he couldn't see his hand in front of his face, and only a single thing remained in his vision... Shirrak's enormous eye.

The blood vessels on the eye spread like rivers; the pupil held what looked like malevolent green stars. For a moment Jace couldn't tell whether he had enslaved the demon or the demon had enslaved him.

"You dare... enslave... a Watcher! Soon you will pay for your folly, you will..."

"Luhravenkta, Luarakipta..."

Jace channeled Life Tap, and the shadow power he'd bought with his own blood crushed the observer's will in an instant. Every one of Shirrak's eyes flickered as if stabbed, and Jace wrenched himself free from that darkness.

"Rage! This is... powerlessness! This is... acceptance! This is... anguish! This is... no!"

The observer began to mutter under its breath: "I... no, soon I... cannot obey... I can only obey... no, soon I will, I will... you're dead, so dead..."

Shirrak wasn't wrong. Jace had barely overpowered the observer's mental suppression, but he realized immediately that the enslavement was unstable.

The demon was powerful enough to throw off the bonds of enslavement at any moment — maybe in a few minutes, maybe even less... So he had to use those few minutes, use this observer to carve out as much room to move as possible for Vereesa and the others.

"Kill every orc, every ogre, every member of the Shadow Council here," Jace said coldly. "Kill!"

The observer drifted back toward the ceiling in a daze. Its dense rows of filthy fangs ground against each other like battle lines, and from between its lips it spat a few words: "Kill... kill... hah-hu, soon... I'll come kill you..."

Shirrak turned and flew toward the Arcane Sanctum. The whole palace had erupted into chaos — shouting everywhere. Shirrak's roars had probably already drawn the attention of plenty of orcs.

Jace glanced back toward the direction of the prison. By now he couldn't feel the tension in Morophil's soul anymore.

"Hurry, hurry..." Jace murmured, chasing after Shirrak, when a brilliant eye beam suddenly flashed out of the corridor and nearly took him square in the face.

In the great hall, orcs howled with rage. The chanting of spells and the roaring of demons rose and fell. Jace stole a glance that way and saw the observer Shirrak unleashing brilliant prismatic eye beams into the crowd. Flesh, torn tent canvas, splinters, and rubble flew everywhere. Shadow Bolts, Arcane Missiles, and fel fireballs crisscrossed the sky...

Shirrak was more powerful than Jace had imagined. As a demon species that wasn't particularly known for frontline combat, this creature ignored the shadow magic being hurled straight at it, letting it splat against its soft flesh without taking any serious damage.

Not only that — whoever attacked it got a beam right back in return, blasting the caster beyond all recognition.

Orc warriors scrambled for crossbows and arrows. Packs of worgs and their handlers could only roar helplessly nearby. The Broken fled in panic. Any living thing might be swept by an eye beam and killed the next second.

In just a few minutes, the Arcane Sanctum, which had been quiet and still moments ago, was now filled with screams and furious roaring. The stench of charred meat and the reek of shadow filled every corner.

"What the hell is going on?! What the hell is going on?!"

The furious Blackheart charged into the hall with footsteps that shook the ground, kicking aside several innocent orc soldiers along the way.

"Lord Shirrak!" Blackheart raised his staff at the observer — only for the observer to fire a beam straight at the tip of the staff. The shadow gem shattered on the spot.

The ogre took the eye beam full on his heavy runic pauldron and staggered back two steps. The observer was about to fire another beam of demonic light straight at him when a fel fireball — launched by some warlock — caught it square in the back of the head, throwing its beam off target. It swept across a stone pillar and the wall beside it instead, leaving a long, deep scorch mark.

"Kill! Kill!"

The observer howled at the top of its lungs while locking onto a Shadow Bolt flying straight at it. The bolt actually slowed down, curved, and flew off somewhere else.

By now Blackheart had touched the blood seeping through his pauldron. He stared at the observer that was slaughtering everything alive in a frenzy, and the fear overflowing from his heart was laced with a sharp thread of suspicion.

He raised his right hand, grabbed a handful of soul shards, and quietly began channeling some complex spell. Jace immediately sensed that the spell had something to do with mental influence — it was aimed at the demon-enslavement bond between himself and Shirrak.

Jace raised Ulthalesh, drawing in the chaotic shadow power of the entire hall, gathering every scrap of force he could muster and shaping a shadow mass. As the Shadow Bolt swelled, its ominous hum grew louder. By now not only the nearby orcs and demons dodging the beams, but even Blackheart channeling his magic and Shirrak slaughtering everything in its path had noticed the disturbance in the corner.

When the observer turned this way and locked onto Jace, the power of the demon enslavement was nearing its limit.

He swung Ulthalesh straight at Shirrak. A terrifying shadow mass, shaped almost into the form of a demonic skull, tore open its convulsing jaws and hurtled forward with burning magenta demon-light in its twin eyes. Before Shirrak could roar out a single sound, in the blink of an eye it and its great orb were completely consumed by the energy, exploding into a shower of demonic flesh that scattered as a rain of purple blood, splattering across a wide stretch of floor and corridor pillars.

Shirrak's demonic soul didn't escape into the Twisting Nether. Instead, it struggled to coalesce from that rain of blood, spinning through the hall together with the souls of who-knows-how-many orcs who'd died by its hand, all of them swirling around the staff Jace held high, coiling, howling, and finally sinking into the blood-soaked wrappings.

Within the wrappings, Ulthalesh began to chuckle softly, as if it had foreseen this outcome from the very start.

Every orc who witnessed this was too terrified to move. Blackheart lowered his arms and abandoned the now-pointless ritual.

The ogre was drenched head to toe in Shirrak's flesh and blood. He stared this way in a daze, his lips beginning to tremble, his emotions a chaotic mess — like a foul stew earnestly cooked to mush by the dumbest idiot in the camp.

"Go... go to the Sanctum of Shadows, inform Master Vorpil of what's happened here, go! Now!"

An orc officer came back to his senses after a good while, shouting toward the depths of the palace. A few soldiers nodded frantically and ran inside.

"What happened?" Blackheart's one-eyed head asked in a muffled voice. "Why did Shirrak do this? Who provoked him?"

The orcs looked at each other, none daring to speak. Then a warlock jumped out and shouted, "Someone enslaved Shirrak, master! Someone must have used magic to enslave him!"

Both of Blackheart's heads turned to the warlock at once. He murmured an incantation, and a Shadowburn engulfed the warlock in an instant...

The orc howled in pain, and soon collapsed powerless to his knees. Both of Blackheart's heads roared at his charred corpse: "No one can enslave an observer, you fool!"

Furious as he was, every warlock in the hall still watched his hysteria with deeply suspicious eyes.

Every member of the cult knew that Blackheart the Inciter was the Shadow Council's most skilled practitioner of mental control. His dark rituals with Cho'gall and the ogres of Nagrand had always been murky, and no one knew who the "dark master" he kept muttering about really was.

Now that Cho'gall had betrayed the Horde and the Shadow Council on Azeroth, who knew when Blackheart would stir up his own disaster?

The cult members all knew this. Blackheart himself knew it well.

"What magic did you use to take our souls, Envoy Yurk?" He turned both his heads this way.

"Watch your words, Blackheart. These souls are not my property." Jace walked to the largest piece of Shirrak's remains and said, "They will be used to power the master's portal, to summon more soldiers and lords to this world and utterly crush everything that stands in the Legion's way."

He looked up at the towering ogre and continued, "I only hope... that Lord Shirrak's betrayal was an isolated incident. Otherwise the master and Lord Kil'jaeden will question the loyalty of the Shadow Council and the demons summoned here."

The ogre was about to say something when a clatter of footsteps came from a side corridor, drawing his attention.

"Inciter, a prisoner has taken advantage of the chaos to escape — the draenei has escaped!" An orc warrior stumbled out of the corridor, panting.

Blackheart merely nodded with both heads indifferently. Compared to the situation in the hall, those half-dead soul shards in waiting really weren't worth much.

By now, the arrogance and self-assurance he'd shown when discussing the demonic registry had completely vanished from his emotions. All that remained was a trace of suspicion and boundless fear.
