Chapter 1222: The Shortest Time, the Greatest Space

Jace had barely spoken the first word of the incantation when the eye demon's smaller eyes suddenly swiveled onto him! "Tahoatusi, Miloon..." "I knew it, that unnatural blaze!" the eye demon roared in Demonic, fixing its largest eye straight on him. Prismatic light gathered in pulses from its pupil, but Jace still rattled off the incantation as fast as he could, then thrust his left hand high toward the source of that iridescent glow. "Tahoatani! Hear my command, demon!" Jace bellowed in Demonic. A blazing sigil of shadow blazed to life on the eye demon's orb. But the creature wobbled its fleshy body and muttered, "No mortal can enslave... Lord Hillrak... you are all my servants, all my slaves, all the souls of Auchindoun will surely..." The demon-enslaving shadow sigil burned with crimson bloodfire under Jace's direction, and the force of it slammed straight into the eye. "Arrogant mortal! Arrogant! Arrogant!" The moment he seized control of the eye demon, Jace felt the world go black. The entire corridor plunged into darkness so thick he couldn't see his hand in front of his face, and only a single thing remained in his vision... Hillrak's enormous eye. The blood vessels on the eye spread like rivers; the pupil held what looked like malevolent green stars. For a moment Jace couldn't tell whether he had enslaved the demon or the demon had enslaved him. "You dare... enslave... a Watcher! Soon you will pay for your folly, you will..." "Luhalawenkta, Lu'alakepta..." Jace channeled Life Tap, and the shadow power he'd bought with his own blood crushed the eye demon's will in an instant. Every one of Hillrak's eyes flickered as if stabbed, and Jace wrenched himself free from that darkness. "Rage! This is... powerlessness! This is... acceptance! This is... anguish! This is... no!" The eye demon began to mutter under its breath: "I... no, soon I... cannot obey... I can only obey... no, soon I will, I will... you're dead, so dead..." Hillrak wasn't wrong. Jace had barely overpowered the eye demon's mental suppression, but he realized immediately that the enslavement was unstable. The demon was powerful enough to throw off the bonds of enslavement at any moment — maybe in a few minutes, maybe even less... So he had to use those few minutes, use this eye demon to carve out as much room to move as possible for Vereesa and the others. "Kill every orc, every ogre, every member of the Shadow Council here," Jace said coldly. "Kill!" The eye demon drifted back toward the ceiling in a daze. Its dense rows of filthy fangs ground against each other like battle lines, and from between its lips it spat a few words: "Kill... kill... hah-hu, soon... I'll come kill you..." Hillrak turned and flew toward the Arcane Sanctum. The whole palace had erupted into chaos — shouting everywhere. Hillrak's roars had probably already drawn the attention of plenty of orcs. Jace glanced back toward the direction of the prison. By now he couldn't feel the tension in Morophil's soul anymore. "Hurry, hurry..." Jace murmured, chasing after Hillrak's direction, when a brilliant eye beam suddenly flashed out of the corridor and nearly took him square in the face. In the great hall, orcs howled with rage. The chanting of spells and the roaring of demons rose and fell. Jace stole a glance that way and saw the eye demon Hillrak unleashing brilliant prismatic eye beams into the crowd. Flesh, torn tent canvas, splinters, and rubble flew everywhere. Shadow Bolts, Arcane Missiles, and fel fireballs crisscrossed the sky... Hillrak was more powerful than Jace had imagined. As a demon species that wasn't particularly known for frontline combat, this creature ignored the shadow magic being hurled straight at it, letting it splat against its soft flesh without taking any serious damage. Not only that — whoever attacked it got a beam right back in return, blasting the caster so badly they were barely recognizable as human. Orc warriors scrambled for crossbows and arrows. Packs of wargs and their handlers could only roar helplessly nearby. The Broken fled in panic. Any living thing might be swept by an eye beam and killed the next second. In just a few minutes, the Arcane Sanctum, which had been quiet and still moments ago, was now filled with screams and furious roaring. The stench of charred meat and the reek of shadow filled every corner. "What the hell is going on?! What the hell is going on?!" The furious Bleakart charged into the hall with footsteps that shook the ground, kicking aside several innocent orc soldiers along the way. "Lord Hillrak!" Bleakart raised his staff at the eye demon — only for the eye demon to fire a beam straight at the tip of the staff. The shadow gem shattered on the spot. The ogre took the eye beam full on his heavy runic pauldron and staggered back two steps. The eye demon was about to fire another beam of demonic light straight at him when a fel fireball — launched by some warlock — caught it square in the back of the head, throwing its beam off target. It swept across a stone pillar and the wall beside it instead, leaving a long, deep scorch mark. "Kill! Kill!" The eye demon howled at the top of its lungs while locking onto a Shadow Bolt flying straight at it. The bolt actually slowed down, curved, and flew off somewhere else. By now Bleakart had touched the blood seeping through his pauldron. He stared at the eye demon that was slaughtering everything alive in a frenzy, and the fear overflowing from his heart was laced with a sharp thread of suspicion. He raised his right hand, grabbed a handful of Soul Shards, and quietly began channeling some complex spell. Jace immediately sensed that the spell had something to do with mental influence — it was aimed at the demon-enslavement bond between himself and Hillrak. Jace raised Ulthalesh, drawing in the chaotic shadow power of the entire hall, gathering every scrap of force he could muster and shaping a shadow mass. As the Shadow Bolt swelled, its ominous hum grew louder. By now not only the nearby orcs and demons dodging the beams, but even Bleakart channeling his magic and Hillrak slaughtering everything in its path had noticed the disturbance in the corner. When the eye demon turned this way and locked onto Jace, the power of the demon enslavement was nearing its limit. He swung Ulthalesh straight at Hillrak. A terrifying shadow mass, shaped almost into the form of a demonic skull, tore open its convulsing jaws and hurtled forward with burning magenta demon-light in its twin eyes. Before Hillrak could roar out a single sound, in the blink of an eye it and its great orb were completely consumed by the energy, exploding into a shower of demonic flesh that scattered as a rain of purple blood, splattering across a wide stretch of floor and corridor pillars. Hillrak's demonic soul didn't escape into the Twisting Nether. Instead, it struggled to coalesce from that rain of blood, spinning through the hall together with the souls of who-knows-how-many orcs who'd died by its hand, all of them swirling around the staff Jace held high, coiling, howling, and finally sinking into the blood-soaked wrappings. Within the wrappings, Ulthalesh began to chuckle softly, as if it had foreseen this outcome from the very start. Every orc who witnessed this was too terrified to move. Bleakart lowered his arms and abandoned the now-pointless ritual. The ogre was drenched head to toe in Hillrak's flesh and blood. He stared this way in a daze, his lips beginning to tremble, his emotions a chaotic mess — like a foul stew earnestly cooked to mush by the dumbest idiot in the camp. "Go... go to the Shadow Sanctum, inform Master Vorpil of what's happened here, go! Now!" An orc officer came back to his senses after a good while, shouting toward the depths of the palace. A few soldiers nodded frantically and ran inside. "What happened?" Bleakart's single-eyed head asked in a muffled voice. "Why did Hillrak do this? Who provoked him?" The orcs looked at each other, none daring to speak. Then a warlock jumped out and shouted, "Someone enslaved Hillrak, master! Someone must have used magic to enslave him!" Both of Bleakart's heads turned to the warlock at once. He murmured an incantation, and a Shadowburn engulfed the warlock in an instant... The orc howled in pain, and soon collapsed powerless to his knees. Both of Bleakart's heads roared at his charred corpse: "No one can enslave an eye demon, you fool!" Furious as he was, every warlock in the hall still watched his hysteria with deeply suspicious eyes. Every member of the cult knew that "Instigator" Bleakart was the Shadow Council's most skilled practitioner of mental control. His dark rituals with Cho'gall and the ogres of Nagrand had always been murky, and no one knew who the "dark master" he kept muttering about really was. Now that Cho'gall had betrayed the Horde and the Shadow Council on Azeroth, who knew when Bleakart would stir up his own disaster? The cult members all knew this. Bleakart himself knew it well. "What magic did you use to take our souls, Ambassador Yuruk?" He turned both his heads this way. "Watch your words, Bleakart. These souls are not my property." Jace walked to the largest piece of Hillrak's remains and said, "They will be used to power the master's portal, to summon more soldiers and lords to this world and utterly crush everything that stands in the Legion's way." He looked up at the towering ogre and continued, "I only hope... that Lord Hillrak's betrayal was an isolated incident. Otherwise the master and Lord Kil'jaeden will question the loyalty of the Shadow Council and the demons summoned here." The ogre was about to say something when a clatter of footsteps came from a side corridor, drawing his attention. "Instructor Bleakart, a prisoner has taken advantage of the chaos to escape — the draenei has escaped!" An orc warrior stumbled out of the corridor, panting. Bleakart merely nodded with both heads indifferently. Compared to the situation in the hall, those half-dead reserve soul fragments really weren't worth much. By now, the arrogance and self-assurance he'd shown when discussing the demonic registry had completely vanished from his emotions. All that remained was a trace of suspicion and boundless fear.
