## Chapter 1223: An Ambassador's Got to Have a Mission

Jace was certainly no born orc, nor was he one of those cruel, ruthless Shadow Council high-ups like Talgore or "Shattered" Krithak who'd learned dark magic from Gul'dan.

Even with the boost of an Arcane Brilliance potion, the spell he'd used to enslave Hillrak held far weaker and shorter than he'd expected. To cover up his enslavement magic, he'd been forced to blast Hillrak into paste with everything he had before the demon could regain its free will — otherwise one slip of the tongue would've exposed him and brought the entire cult down on him.

But that impulsive decision had, of course, been followed by a chain of uncontrolled chaos. Fortunately, Jace had barely managed to steady the situation, though he couldn't deny there'd been a fair bit of luck and no small amount of guilt involved.

Right now, Jace was sitting firmly in an orc-hide chair, observing the Arcane Sanctum from a corner — the floor strewn with mangled flesh and scorch marks of shadowburn, orcs rushing about trying to clean up the mess, the ogre seething with rage and nowhere to vent it.

He tapped one hand on the armrest and propped his head up with the other, putting on an air of detached indifference on the surface while doing his utmost to mask his real feelings.

Just then, an aged orc appeared at the mouth of the corridor leading deeper into the Sanctum. He said something to the several orc guards standing beside him, blades and shields in hand, then looked straight toward Jace.

The hunched, wizened old orc had white hair and beard, and his loose black robe made him look like an old raven that could no longer fly. He walked unsteadily, like a creature still getting used to land.

As he slowly drew nearer, Jace noticed he appeared to have undergone some kind of self-blinding ritual — a hideous scar over each eye socket, and the pair of nearly grey-white eyeballs within didn't seem to be of much use anymore.

"Ambassador Yuruk." Vorpil cleared his throat and spoke in a hoarse voice. "I apologize for meeting you so late. I am Vorpil, the Shadow Council's instructor here."

"Master Vorpil." Jace nodded.

"I felt that unparalleled surge of shadow power from deep within the Shadow Sanctum... even if my subordinates hadn't told me, I would have known you are a warlock of remarkable power. And so young, so... full of vigor." Vorpil sat down in a chair beside him and sighed deeply. "But I don't believe I've ever seen you, Ambassador. Did you once join the Shadow Council? Follow Gul'dan?"

"No," Jace said. "I was once a shaman apprentice. Then I came to realize the elements' fickleness... but by the time I truly found how to embrace the Shadow, the war was nearly over, and the Shadow Council had already ceased to exist on Azeroth. Fortunately, though — Talgore, or rather, Lord Gorefiend, taught me how to master the power within myself. He shaped who I am today."

Jace had already rehearsed his backstory thoroughly, so no matter how this warlock pressed him, he could talk his way around it. Which was why his self-introduction came across as confident and at ease.

"So it was him." One of Vorpil's eyebrows twitched. "No wonder I can sense Gul'dan's legacy in the way you channel shadow power. It seems that Talgore, in the end, wasn't a complete failure — at least before his final annihilation, he cultivated a true warlock, a true master of shadow."

At that, the old orc let out a couple of mocking snorts.

He spat forcefully, then glanced toward the eye demon's remains scattered across the floor. "I knew Hillrak was never satisfied with the offerings we gave it. Sooner or later it would find a way to seize more souls to sate its appetite — either leave here and delve deeper into Auchindoun for more draenei souls, or sacrifice our own."

He paused, then continued, "But I did not expect that the emissary sent by Lord Magtheridon would put to death a Legion-appointed Watcher... in order to save the lives of insignificant wretches like us."

Hearing this, Jace kept his outward expression and emotions level, but inwardly he was starting to waver.

Indeed, with the war on Azeroth already lost and the Horde of Draenor essentially disbanded, all orcs who still followed Magtheridon and the Burning Legion — people like "Ambassador Yuruk" — had precious little honor left to speak of.

Most fel orcs and warlocks now regarded demons as their masters. Even the demon servants summoned by warlocks held a high station; plenty of cult orcs treated Morophil with more respect than they treated an emissary like Jace.

Killing an eye demon lord to save your own skin was at least logical coming from an ogre like Bleakart, who held the Burning Legion in scant regard. But for a character like Ambassador Yuruk — someone cast as a bootlicker to demons — it didn't quite add up.

"If Lord Hillrak seized Auchindoun's souls to satisfy his own desires, then he became the insignificant one, Master Vorpil," Jace said with a natural expression. "And that is precisely why the master sent me here."

Although the words were already out, Jace hadn't actually figured out a good reason for killing Hillrak yet. But after saying it, the offhand remark he'd tossed out to keep Vorpil from sensing his guilt had, in turn, given him an idea.

So Jace continued along the same thread: "Those Alliance scum occupy Terokkar, supporting the draenei of Shattrath. Legion soldiers can hardly advance in this direction — Auchindoun must contribute, Master Vorpil. Of course, the master also knows that your cult faces threats from both draenei remnants and Alliance soldiers, so..."

He slowly placed his hand on the armrest and said, "So the Shadow Council will assist the Legion in using Auchindoun's soul power to raise a new fel forge — as the foundation for a new portal. Before long, the master will find a way to send an Eredar here to the Arcane Sanctum to help you complete this task. Once the portal is open, Legion soldiers will help you swiftly wipe out every last resister in Auchindoun, and together with the Legion of Hellfire Citadel, form a pincer around the broken remnants hiding in Terokkar..."

Jace snorted coldly. "Anyone who attempts to obstruct or delay this plan is an enemy of the Legion, an enemy of Lord Kil'jaeden. Whether he's one of the Sons of Lothar or some demon lord."

"I understand." Vorpil said quietly. "Excellent, Master Vorpil. Very good."

Jace snorted with laughter, then threw his head back and laughed loudly and triumphantly, drawing the gazes of more than a few orcs.

He didn't bother hiding his emotions in the slightest, because he genuinely was delighted — that he'd managed to cook up such a jumbled, half-baked excuse to fob off Vorpil's suspicions in such a short time.

Considering Magtheridon was a dim-witted brute to begin with, Jace didn't need to dream up much in the way of a reasonable explanation for this spur-of-the-moment story. As long as it looked vaguely logical, he could force-feed it to this old orc on the grounds that "Lord Magtheridon wants it done this way."

Suddenly, a deafening hum cut off Jace's laughter, rattling him so hard he sat there coughing with his mouth hanging open, and he had to steady himself again.

"Find a way to send this monster away, Master Vorpil. Since you've already found a way to control it, there must be a way to send it back to the Twisting Nether. Don't let it go on wasting Auchindoun's souls." He lowered his voice.

"I'm afraid that won't be possible, Ambassador." The old orc waved a hand. "The only reason the Shadow Council can still exist in Auchindoun and remain loyal to the Legion is almost entirely on account of Murmur's presence."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

"As long as Murmur is still in our hands, Maladaar and his Auchenai don't dare make a move." Vorpil explained, "The destruction caused when Murmur descended already terrified them. So long as they believe Murmur still possesses the power to flatten Auchindoun all over again — even destroy all of Draenor — they won't dare launch an offensive against us. This is the most valuable trump card the Shadow Council, and indeed the Legion, holds in these wastes, Ambassador."

So the tale that Murmur could destroy the world, the one that circulated in the game later on, had actually been a rumor spread by the Shadow Council? Of course, Vorpil hadn't come right out and told Jace exactly what kind of power Murmur possessed, so Jace couldn't just make a judgment offhand.

"Very well. I understand your difficulty. But my master certainly won't want you delaying his plans on that account," Jace said.

"Lord Magtheridon may rest assured," Vorpil said.

"Then my mission here is complete." Jace cast an idle glance at the corpse of a dead demon guard in the distance. "I don't suppose there's anything to eat? I'd like to fill my stomach a little before I head out."

"There is, Ambassador Yuruk." one of the orc guards said. "I hope at least it's cooked," Jace reminded him.

The orc guard nodded. Vorpil said, "Well then, Ambassador, I still need to help my subordinates deal with the chaos here — I won't keep you any longer."

"This is your domain, Master Vorpil." Jace nodded.

Soon, an orc laborer brought over a handful of raisins, a bread ring, some rank cheese, and a bowl of meat broth. After a whole night without sleep, Jace was already so hungry he was getting lightheaded. He didn't care what any of it tasted like — he tore off a piece of the greasy bread ring and stuffed it into his mouth, only to accidentally jab his hand with one of his own tusks.

Thank the Light Vorpil had already left for the warlocks' side. If he'd seen Jace being this clumsy at eating like an orc, that would've been the dumbest way to blow his cover.

Not long after, Jace felt a wave of rather warm and excited emotion slowly approaching from behind. A few slightly cool, crimson-colored fingers brushed across his cheek.

Morophil climbed onto the orc-hide chair and sat on the armrest, draping her legs across Jace's lap. "Aren't you going to praise me, darling?" Jace kissed the scale-like demon skin on her lower leg, then drank a bowl of broth. "How did it go over there?"

"With my help... no one was hurt. They're already waiting for you." Morophil glanced sideways at the spot Jace had kissed, then casually popped a raisin into his mouth. "And I have to admit, the commotion you made in here really did draw attention more effectively than my panting... By the way, how did you manage to enslave him?"

"I enslaved you, didn't I?" Jace said with a smile. "You don't think you're weaker than that eye demon, do you?"

Morophil flashed a smug grin, then threw her arms tightly around Jace's thick neck. Jace choked, reached up and grabbed her horns. "Let go, damn it... I haven't finished eating."
