Chapter 1224: So Close?

After eating a solid breakfast — by Auchindoun's standards, at least — Jace figured it was about time they left.

This meal had been about more than just filling his stomach. More than anything, he wanted Vorpil and Bleakart to see him sitting there in broad daylight, perfectly at ease even after creating such a bloody scene, and in no hurry to leave — thereby cementing his identity as a "Legion ambassador."

Otherwise, throwing a mess like that and immediately bolting would've looked suspicious as hell.

Two cultists were assigned to escort Jace out of the Arcane Sanctum. Once they reached the corridor outside and left the cult's patrol range, Morophil gave her master a hint through the Soul Link.

Jace caught it, turned around, and looked at the two orc soldiers. "This is far enough, you two. I can manage on my own from here."

"Yes, Ambassador."

Both sword-warming soldiers thumped their chests in salute. Their attitude was a far cry from how the orcs had treated Jace when he'd first entered the Arcane Sanctum.

For these demon-power-worshipping fanatics, putting on a proper display of demonic power was the surest way to earn their respect. Just as Jace was about to turn and leave, one of the soldiers spoke up. "Ambassador, Master Vorpil also asked me to give you this." Jace turned back and took the dark-yellow leather pouch the soldier handed him. Inside were four potion vials.

He pulled out a stopper and peered inside. The liquid was thick as glue or some kind of oil, and it gave off an overpoweringly fishy stench. On Azeroth, Jace had handled plenty of fish helping Ursula brew potions, but this fishy smell was unlike any fish he knew.

Then again, of course it was — this was Draenor.

"What is this?" Jace asked. "I'm not an alchemist."

"Master Vorpil said it's an Invisibility Potion," the soldier said. "To help you cross the wastes outside Auchindoun."

Jace nodded, then asked, "I took the long way around from the east when I came, and I kept a low profile and moved carefully the whole way, so I wasn't attacked by any black dragons. Can you tell me exactly how many black dragons are out there?"

"I'm not sure either, Ambassador," the soldier admitted. "But they're led by a massive adult black dragon. We don't know his name, but the arakkoa gave him a nickname in their own language — 'Tyrannus'."

Tyrannus. The name clicked in Jace's mind all at once.

Yes — in the game, there really was an enormous dragon circling over the Bone Wastes. That dragon was called Tyrannus. If you wanted him to land, you had to summon him using the bones of dead draenei.

But that dragon was an undead skeletal dragon. Jace still remembered how, when he'd first arrived in the Bone Wastes in the game, he'd wondered why there was a skeletal dragon — a sight unique to Azeroth — here in Draenor. Turned out it was a black dragon that had died and been resurrected by some unknown magic.

Since the orcs kept bringing up the black dragons circling Auchindoun, Jace figured that leaving here and crossing the wastes to the north without being noticed by the dragons was probably impossible.

If he had Solia fly them out, it'd be faster but would definitely draw the dragons' attention more easily. Traveling on foot across the wastes would be easier to hide, but far too slow.

What to do, exactly, they'd have to figure out once they actually left Auchindoun and saw what it was like outside. After all, up to this point, their little band of Azeroth visitors hadn't even seen Draenor's sky yet.

Morophil led Jace down a few empty corridors, and before long, a faint green shadow popped out from behind a pile of rubble ahead — Vereesa.

Jace immediately grabbed his cloak and pulled off the disguise, then embraced Vereesa tightly as she ran over, neither of them willing to let go for a long while.

"Next time you plan something this complicated... remember to discuss it with us first. At least give us a heads-up," Vereesa said into his chest.

"It was already a pack of wargs right in front of our faces," Jace said. "Emergency situation. But you lot moved fast..."

"Rescuing captives from an evil nest is something both the Farstriders and we Gryphon Riders have absolute, extensive experience with," Grede said with a grin as he walked up. "And those orcs' eyesight is nothing compared to the forest trolls'!"

"Of course." Vereesa said proudly in Thalassian.

Solia had come over too. She glanced at Jace's face and said, "Is that... blood, Jace?"

"What?" Jace touched his cheek. Grede frowned, walked around behind Jace, and said, "Demon blood. It's on your cloak too."

"What happened?" Vereesa asked, worried, watching Solia's fingers.

"It was that demon's blood," Jace said. "I killed an eye demon in there."

"Killed an eye demon?!" Grede ran back over, stunned. "So that's the chaos you caused? You killed a demon right in front of those orcs?"

Sazeno came bouncing over at last, climbed up Jace's leg, and poked his head out of the satchel at his waist. "If our operation hadn't gone so smoothly, my master would've been forced to stall in there a little longer — he probably would've ended up becoming the chief of those orcs!"

"Close enough, little guy," Morophil said with a smile.

"What, what..." Grede looked back and forth between the two demons.

"It's hard to explain all at once." Jace cleared his throat. "Let's get out of here first, and I'll tell you everything that happened inside slowly. Where are the draenei? Magtur and our draenei priest."

Grede pointed behind him. "Noberu is tending to the three we just rescued. They're in the corridor outside. We kept them a bit farther off, so if any orcs came by it'd be easier to move the wounded."

"How are the rescued prisoners doing?" Jace asked.

"I've given them emergency treatment..." Solia said. "It's just that the natural power here is so thin it barely exists, so I could only rely on my own mana. I'm afraid the results aren't particularly good."

"Don't talk like that, Solia." Grede waved a hand. "I saw that young fellow after your magical treatment — he could practically leap straight out of Auchindoun."

Whatever the truth of it, at least those two humans didn't look like they'd die anytime soon, and Jace could relax a little.

Then Vereesa said, "I can't wait to tell you... The rescued sergeant major said that Alleria was still in the camp before he came into the Bone Wastes to search for survivors!"

"How long ago?" Jace asked immediately.

"Half a month before he got captured by the orcs near Auchindoun." Vereesa bit her lip. "I feel like... I was almost touching her golden hair, Jace."

Jace looked at Morophil. "How long were those two kept in the Arcane Sanctum?"

"Not long." Grede cut in to answer. "At least they felt it was only a week or two. The soldiers who came in with Lirath and his father have already been taken away. I imagine... there's not much chance they're alive. They were only brought in recently."

"So." Jace said. "Alleria is still here..."

"Of course she is," Vereesa said happily. "What I'm trying to say is we might not have to wait much longer to find her. As soon as we can get out of this big black tomb!"

"Don't be so hasty, little elf!" Greminded her as he caught up. "We're in a completely unknown land here. Have you forgotten the Farstriders' training?!"

"I don't need you to remind me, Wildhammer," Vereesa said sharply.

The two of them bickered their way ahead, but Jace had grown a bit dazed, his steps slowing. Could it be that Alleria Windrunner and Turalyon were still in Draenor — still not missing?

"What's wrong, Jace?" Solia beside him asked. "Is something off?" Jace snapped back to his senses. "I was thinking about the black dragons."

"Black dragons?" Solia asked. "There are black dragons nearby?"

"Yes," Jace said. "The orcs mentioned several times that black dragons circle around Auchindoun. Once we head outside, we'll have to be careful."
