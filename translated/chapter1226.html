Chapter 1226: A Sky of Blood

With five Broken, two draenei, and two Alliance soldiers added to the party, their group now numbered twelve mortals, a dragon, and two demons.

Before setting out, Solia and Amy had helped the Broken procure some dried rations and pickled vegetables from the fortress. Relying on the Broken's strength and stamina — still impressive despite their degeneration — they'd carried quite a bit more, and combined with what Grede and the others had brought, it still wasn't enough to feed this big crowd for ten days.

Though they now had the Flowing Ice Crystal and both draenei had ample mana to conjure food, water was obviously more important than magically created "fake food." Auchindoun sat at the heart of a dead wasteland. Collecting real water with the Arcane here wouldn't be easy — let alone supplying a dozen people, and one of them a living green dragon.

So their time was tight. They had to leave Auchindoun within ten days and find an Alliance expeditionary force or draenei camp... at the very least they had to get out of the wastes and reach a forest. At least Terokkar Forest still had rivers and trees, no shortage of fruit, flowers, wild vegetables, and surviving wildlife.

Of course, all while risking discovery by roving black dragons.

If it had just been the few of them, Jace wouldn't have worried much about a single adult black dragon. After all, their party now included a half-grown green dragon, plus Vereesa's Dream Arrows.

And Grede's war hammer, even without the backing of lightning, could give even Onyxia herself more than she could handle — never mind some no-name black dragon haunting these parts.

But with this many Broken and draenei, plus the two human soldiers, Jace had to consider their safety and couldn't afford to be reckless anymore.

That noon, everyone packed up and prepared to set out. It looked like the scholar Nitlin and the two soldiers were in decent shape after Solia's treatment and could keep up with the group. The Broken leader Magtur had been recovering quickly ever since leaving the Black Morass. Right now, although he wasn't as sturdy and solid as Noboru, he could basically take care of himself.

Grede and Vereesa walked at the front, handling reconnaissance and pathfinding. Jace brought up the rear with Sazeno, Solia, and Morophil. Led by the two draenei and the Broken, they advanced toward the surface. After walking for a while, even the rumbling Murmur let out from time to time seemed less deafening.

Jace looked at the whole party trudging along ahead of him, then finally couldn't help but glance at the green dragon beside him. "Solia, that... hmm, do you think you can carry this whole crowd?"

Solia curled her lips slightly and carefully sized up the cloaked, luggage-laden figures huffing and puffing their way up the stairs ahead. "If it were just the people, I think I could give it a try. But with all these bags and food on top of that, I really don't know, Jace. Even if I could get airborne, it'd be a real struggle. Sorry."

"Mm." Jace said. "Let's get outside first and see how things look. Maybe traveling on foot will actually be safer."

The smell of dust in the air grew heavier, the fishy reek of shadow faded bit by bit, and the signs of destruction on the corridors and walls became more severe — often they'd be walking along and have to find a gap to squeeze through a collapsed section of rubble.

Right now the path was blocked, with no visible opening. But from between the cracks in the rubble, a draft could still be felt, so the draenei and Broken judged it wasn't completely sealed — they just needed to clear a way through by hand.

Once the draenei and Broken set to work, the job turned out to be less troublesome than it sounded. These powerful creatures weren't just skilled at magic — they were far stronger than humans too.

And of course Grede, a descendant of the Earthen, was even stronger than them. Right now he hauled a boulder out of the dirt pile and flung it aside, and with a crash a whole heap of rubble collapsed — revealing a thread of grey, hazy light ahead.

Ever since they'd left the Shadow Council and Auchenai's territory, there hadn't been any torches or braziers around. This light was dim and faint — definitely not the glow of shadow or arcane magic. It was sunlight. Draenor's sunlight.

"We've arrived." Nitlin shielded his hands lightly against the dust and murmured, "Lak, Kar, Rukmar..."

"What language is that?" Jace asked.

"The arakkoa tongue — it means 'praise the Sun Goddess.'" Nitlin smiled and switched to Common. "I always feel that no matter how you put it in the draenei language, there's no way to express passionate feeling for the natural world as the natives do."

Jace nodded. With Nitlin's explanation, the name Rukmar came back to him.

Long before the draenei had come to this world, the arakkoa had already built a glorious civilization in Arak. And Rukmar was the arakkoa's sun god — and their creator.

But that was history from before the war between orcs and draenei. With the invasion of the Burning Legion and the destruction of Draenor, all of Arak was dragged into the Twisting Nether by the boundless, terrible power of the Staff of Sargeras. The arakkoa civilization was devastated as well, leaving only scattered arakkoa roosts clinging to survival on the remnant of Outland, and the deity they'd believed in back then was quickly forgotten.

The crowd used the opening to clear a path through the rubble, squeezed through, and stepped up the stairs. Following the light out through the corridor, a desolate expanse of ashen grey appeared before their eyes.

The sky was a dust-choked dull red, and the sun soaked within it like a black, bleeding stain. Auchindoun's remaining high walls stood before them like the skeleton of some colossal beast.

Along the twisted, broken edges of the ruined walls, metal supports corrupted to black by fallen magic jutted out like claws. Dim crystals were inlaid in the corpse-like reliefs like the clouded eyes of the blind.

The wild wind of the wasteland blew past the ruins with a sound like a coarse wail — genuinely miserable, yet somehow hard to muster much sympathy for.

On the heels of that wind, a blast of sand and dust came rushing in. The two draenei and the Alliance soldiers had somehow already put on their face wraps and scarves. Jace and his bunch of dimension-traveling tourists from Azeroth all caught it full in the face — coughing, rubbing their eyes.

Soon enough, they each learned from the locals and wrapped up their cloaks, hoods, and cloth coverings.

"Is anyone here?" Grede asked, raising his head.

"You mean living people or dead people?" Livius asked. The two voices sounded bone-dry echoing between the parched stone walls. Grede stared at him blankly, then said, "If the dead people can stand up, then yeah, count them in."

"Sometimes." Livius stared at two tall skeletons leaning against each other in the distance, dressed in tattered grey-blue robes. "When the catastrophe of Draenor had just passed, some death knights came here and tried to raise the dead to form an undead army."

"Though this undead army was swiftly destroyed by the Alliance and Auchenai's combined forces, there are still some undead lurking nearby. These undead all carry the foul plagues laid by those necromancers, so we have to be careful — especially... you."

Livius looked toward the Broken, who had already wrapped their faces up tight. Noboru gave a stiff nod and made no sound.

"Livius." Jace pulled out the leather pouch Vorpil had given him, took a vial of potion from it, and handed it to him. "Do you know what this is?"

The priest pulled out the stopper. "Smells like some kind of sea fish."

He passed the potion to the Nitlin beside him. "Father, do you know what this is?"

Nitlin took it, shook it, and sniffed. "Ah, this is an Invisibility Potion. Because it uses fish paste brewed from the flesh of the Crescent Sabertooth, it has a fishy smell. This potion isn't easy to brew — this one's completed quite well. I'd estimate this vial can keep a person invisible for at least two to three minutes, provided they don't use magic and break the invisibility. Where did you find it?"

"I got it from the Shadow Council," Jace said honestly.

Nitlin nodded. "It seems they've learned more in Auchindoun than just refining souls."

"There are four vials," Jace said. "I'd like to give them to the three of you who just escaped from the prison. Your physical condition isn't great — if you run into an emergency, it's best to have a life-saving trump card up your sleeve."

"I know the Invisibility spell, Master Sesso," Nitlin said. "It might not be as potent as what this potion provides, but it's a serviceable substitute."

He looked toward the Broken and said in Eredar, "Magtur, perhaps you should take it. I think you haven't recovered well enough yet."

"Thank you, partner Nitlin." Magtur took the Invisibility Potion and bowed deeply. Jace distributed the potions to the two Alliance soldiers and Livius, then looked up at the sky.

Draenor's sky was more than just dust and gales. The dark power of the Twisting Nether had poured in, and boundless shadow power ceaselessly tore at this fragile dimension.

Gazing at Auchindoun's shattered dome, ripped wide open by the explosion, Jace could almost directly feel the fear, depression, and struggle howling between those rolling clouds.

He couldn't risk Solia carrying so many people and so much luggage, racing dragons who'd long since adapted to this environment through weather like this. From here on out, they'd have to rely on their own two feet.
