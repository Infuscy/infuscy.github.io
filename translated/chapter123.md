## Chapter 123: The Mysterious Former Member

The next morning, Jace walked into the Slaughtered Lamb, greeted Tharle Mor, and headed down the steps as usual.

He was here today to explain the situation to Jagin or whoever else was around and say his goodbyes. After all, he'd be gone for nearly two months — he couldn't just disappear without a word.

He passed through the cell door into the main hall. Galders was there as always, the guy who never seemed to have anything better to do — five days out of seven he was camped out in the basement reading and drinking tea.

"Galders, let Jagin know for me — I'm heading back to Lordaeron for Winter Veil. Probably won't be around for a while."

"You coming back?" Galders asked.

"Of course I'm coming back," Jace said. "Why wouldn't I? I've still got plenty going on in the Mage Quarter."

"We had a member before who said the same thing — that he was going on a long trip. Left, and never came back." Galders said, "He was the most talented of any of us. I don't think Jagin or myself could've matched him. His leaving hit Jagin hard. And Jace… I see his shadow in you."

"I've never heard a single word about this person," Jace said.

Galders said, "Because there was something mysterious about him through and through. Honestly, from the very beginning I had the feeling he'd leave someday — though back then I thought it was just my imagination. By the time he left, Ursula had only just joined us, and Erland was fairly new too. As for the rest of them… they probably don't want to bring him up."

"Can I ask his name?" Jace said.

"It's no secret. Though I doubt that was his real name." Galders said, "He went by Mosh'ul. Judging by his accent, he wasn't from the Kingdom of Stormwind either. He said he'd come to Stormwind to work and earn money, same as you."

Mosh'ul? If it was the Mosh'ul Jace was thinking of, then of course he knew who that was — though only from the game.

In the level-60 era, Mosh'ul the Bloodcaller was the warlock NPC for the epic mount quest, standing at the Altar of Storms in the Burning Steppes waiting for someone to help him gather materials and complete the demonic ritual that reached into the Twisting Nether.

A deeply mysterious NPC — just standing there in a black robe, with no past and no future to speak of.

But Jace wasn't the least bit surprised that he had ties to the underground warlock circle beneath Stormwind. The Altar of Storms the orcs had built wasn't far from Stormwind, as the crow flew — you just had to cross some brutal mountain terrain to reach it.

"Right — the Altar of Storms itself is a ritual site that gathers mana. The orcs built the Altar of Storms on Azeroth specifically to draw in the ley-line energy here, to help Gul'dan and the other powerful orc warlocks channel their power, summon demons, or cast the kind of devastating spells that could turn the tide of a war." Jace could probably guess why Mosh'ul had wanted to leave this place. In his eyes, this little outfit probably didn't have much of a future. Staying any longer would've been a pure waste of time — there was no way to make real progress in shadow magic with a bunch of clock-watchers.

So he'd left in search of a more powerful mana source, especially one steeped in shadow, chasing greater strength and deeper, more terrifying magic.

The Burning Steppes' Altar of Storms had been the perfect destination. Back when the orcs of Blackrock Mountain and the Dark Iron dwarves were still locked in their endless feud, before the black dragons had even descended on Blackrock, most of the orcish camps and structures across the Burning Steppes had been completely abandoned. Nothing guarded them but the native fire elements and wild beasts.

Mosh'ul had probably spotted that window of opportunity and gambled on claiming the entire Altar of Storms for himself.

And that was probably why, even a decade and a half later — long after the Scourge War had ended and the timeline had reached the World of Warcraft level-60 era — the warlocks of Stormwind were still a bunch of mediocre types who could only teach the most superficial magic, while Mosh'ul was already communicating with worlds deep in the Twisting Nether and breaking in the fearsome Xorothian Dreadsteed. They weren't even in the same league.

Come to think of it, would it be possible to summon a dreadsteed here?

"I don't trust that man." Galders's words snapped Jace out of his thoughts. "I think he's a liar through and through. Him leaving here was probably a blessing for us. Jagin thinks he's some kind of artist. I think he'd have brought trouble to the Slaughtered Lamb sooner or later — or rather, to our 'Dagger Circle.'" Then his gaze turned deadly serious. "Jace, let me say this while I'm at it. Wherever you go, whatever you're planning to do, don't talk about anything related to the 'Dagger Circle.' That's our understanding. Our rule. My important job down here is making sure our activities stay hidden from the outside world. And believe me, Jace — if anyone out there learns about our existence or our aims, I'll find out fast who leaked it. I have the ability to make that person disappear for good. Whether that's Jagin, Sulenna, Ursula, or you."

"Mosh'ul too, of course. Though ever since he left us, everything's stayed quiet. I figure it's because his own dirty secrets are a hell of a lot more troublesome than ours, so he can only keep his head down and do whatever he wants to do, without ever getting tangled up with us again."

Jace nodded. "I know that much, Galders."

"Good, then." Grede nodded. "I like you, Jace. I'd be very happy if you stayed. If you don't come back, I'll only feel regret — I won't stand in your way. Just don't let things between us reach a point where there's no coming back."

When Galders said "I like you," Jace didn't feel an ounce of appreciation. It just made his skin crawl.

For all he knew, the guy was some SI:7 agent, or an underground assassin from some killer's guild, slumming it at the Slaughtered Lamb for fun as a hobby.

He had the feeling that Jagin only came to the Slaughtered Lamb out of a love for forbidden magic, looking for a sense of belonging — but this man was the one who actually treated this whole thing like a career.

"Don't be so tense, Jace," Galders said. "I'll let the others know you're taking a leave. Fair winds wherever you go."

How the hell was Jace not supposed to be tense? Some guy with an unclear background had just told him to his face that he had the power to make people vanish at will — if he could stay calm in the face of a death threat like that…

Walking out of the tavern, Jace felt like something had tethered him to the Slaughtered Lamb. Had Galders secretly laid some kind of sealing curse on him?
