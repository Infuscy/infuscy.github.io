## Chapter 1231: Black Dragons and the Horde

At the makeshift overnight camp, Alleria met with the draenei, the Broken, and the Alliance soldiers. Both soldiers were over the moon — the First Sergeant even said excitedly that he'd thought he'd never see a Farstrider again in this lifetime, let alone Alleria.

The Sons of Lothar had something that the Alliance on Azeroth had completely lost... the way different races got along so harmoniously, without any barriers. It really was something rare.

In his past life's game, he probably wouldn't have treated this as anything special. After all, players could cooperate without obstacle whether they were orcs, undead, or blood elves — and even when conflicts arose, they had nothing to do with race.

But here, communication between many races felt like being separated by a mountain. Even among Jace's own group, it would be hard to say the barriers had been completely eliminated.

But here with the Sons of Lothar, the human soldiers seemed to find absolutely nothing unusual about an elven commander. A proud high elf captain could treat a Wildhammer dwarf with courtesy the very first time they met, with no trace of looking down on him or distaste...

Jace of course also knew this wasn't just because they were elites who'd come together of their own accord for the same great and difficult goal. It was also because the apocalyptic environment of Draenor and the ever-present threat of attack from the Burning Legion and the Horde forced them to rely on one another without distinction.

In the days to come, Azeroth would face a similar situation. On the slopes of Mount Hyjal, where the Defiler could annihilate a legion with a wave of his hand; at the peak of the Frozen Throne, where the Lich King's will drove tens of thousands of skeletons and walking corpses in a great southward offensive — only then...

Jace rubbed his temples and tossed his gradually wandering thoughts to the back of his mind. After all, Alleria Windrunner was right before his eyes now. Everything was already different.

At that moment, Solia came walking around from the other side of the hillside, her arms full of yellow, glowing flowers. Seeing Alleria standing close to Vereesa, chatting intimately, she'd probably guessed her identity at once.

"Ah, you must be Lady Alleria Windrunner." She greeted her in Common.

As she spoke, Solia carefully set the flowers beside the shelter the Broken had put up. Hearing her greeting, Alleria and the others all looked that way. The ranger captain's gaze seemed somewhat unusual.

When she wasn't in Stormwind, Solia generally appeared in her elven form, since she wasn't all that familiar with human features yet. In such situations, when meeting other elves she'd normally greet them in Thalassian.

But probably because everyone in the camp, including Grede, completely understood the elven language, she'd opened with Common instead.

But to Alleria, this was very strange indeed. Given her status and position in Quel'Thalas, an elven companion who'd come to Draenor with Vereesa greeting her in Common instead of Thalassian — and guessing her identity on top of that...

Alleria quickly sized up Solia's appearance and said quietly, "You're a dragon."

This time it was Solia's turn to be startled. She glanced behind herself nervously, confirmed that her tail wasn't sticking out, and asked, "How did you know?"

Vereesa let out a long breath and whispered, "That makes me feel a whole lot better."

Jace smiled slightly. Actually, being sniffed out of the forest by a ranger like Alleria was nothing to be ashamed of — she'd been a Farstrider for hundreds of years, after all.

But that wasn't important, because right now he actually felt something was off. When Alleria guessed Solia was a dragon, there was barely any reverence or surprise at all — just wariness and tension.

But seeing that the others in the camp weren't surprised that Solia had been exposed as a dragon, Alleria didn't immediately escalate her hostility further.

"Let me introduce you." Grede walked right up between the two. "Solia, this is the beautiful Lady Alleria — I think no introduction is needed. Lady Alleria, this is Solia. She is indeed a green dragon."

"A green dragon?" Alleria reacted as if she'd never expected it. "You thought she might be a dragon disguised as a black dragon, didn't you?" Jace asked.

Alleria nodded. "You truly are a remarkable warlock, Jace. You must have sensed my tension."

"Sorry, Captain." Jace said, a little embarrassed.

"I'm glad it was you who smelled my tension this time, and not an orc." The ranger captain said, "Yes, I thought she was a black dragon, or a red dragon... but the red dragons in Outland were mostly whelps captured by the orcs — they wouldn't take our form. But black dragons were a completely different story."

"Have black dragons tried to infiltrate the expeditionary force?" Vereesa asked.

"Not just black dragons — demons have tried too." Alleria said. "Which is also why we carefully inspect every stranger of unknown identity."

She pulled out the goggles she'd worn while riding the void ray and gave them a shake. "The gnomish engineers crafted goggles that can detect demonic aura, which has greatly increased the difficulty of demons infiltrating our ranks. But for dragons... there really isn't much to do besides guess."

"Still, I've never met a green dragon..." Alleria said. "Not in Draenor, nor on Azeroth."

"Because this young lady green dragon also ran away from home, same as your sister." Grede laughed.

"You're not much different." Vereesa stared at him. "And unlike us, you don't even dare go back." Grede paused, ground his lips together, spread his hands, and said nothing.

"Well, it's a pleasure to meet you, Miss Solia." Alleria nodded. Solia inclined her head in return. "Likewise, Lady Alleria. But this black dragon infiltration... what's the story there?"

"When we assaulted Hellfire Citadel, one of Deathwing's sons once tried to take the form of a mage to sabotage our plans from within." The ranger captain said, "Fortunately his disguise was seen through by Khadgar, or the coordination between our front and rear lines would likely have been thrown into chaos and we'd have been put on the back foot. He almost pulled it off. If Hellfire Citadel hadn't fallen quickly, and the Horde had received reinforcements from Shadowmoon Valley and Auchindoun, our expedition might have suffered a heavy setback from the very start."

"One of Deathwing's sons — was it Nefarian?" Grede asked.

"I don't know, but that black dragon called himself Baron Sablemane and claimed to be a mage from Alterac." Alleria said. "I have to say, that was a rather good angle. We'd only just entered Draenor at the time, facing countless unfamiliar and chaotic circumstances, and the situation in the Kingdom of Alterac was a complete mess — perfect cover for him to fish in troubled waters. If it were now, he'd never get inside our walls with such a clumsy lie again."

"It's Prince Saberion," Solia said. "He's always liked that peculiar title."

"Doesn't he worry about using it too much and being recognized?" Grede asked.

"Clearly there's no one in the expeditionary force who knows him like Solia does," Vereesa said. "These damned black dragons have always liked playing games on the edge of danger like this, showing off their abilities."

"Indeed." Jace thought for a moment, then looked back at Alleria. "So, Captain Alleria — at least when the expeditionary force assaulted Hellfire Citadel, the Horde and the black dragons were still fighting side by side?"

He looked toward the sky over the Bone Wastes, then asked, "Then why did the black dragons later turn against the orcs?"

"Deathwing stole three powerful treasures from our world — the Eye of Dalaran, the Book of Medivh, and that..."

"...the mysterious Staff of Sargeras, whose appearance we never caught sight of." Alleria explained, "We originally thought these three treasures were weapons Ner'zhul used to plunder other worlds, the same way he ravaged ours. But in the end we learned that these were merely his means of escaping the Burning Legion's control."

She continued, "Deathwing believed that with his power and that of his brood, he could control all of Outland. He could also help Ner'zhul resist our attacks while simultaneously countering the Burning Legion's control. So there was no need to use these artifacts to flee to another world — he only needed to pledge his Blackwing Legion in service. So instead of handing these artifacts over to Ner'zhul, he seized the Skull of Gul'dan from his hands, intending to use its immense power to strengthen the countless dragon eggs he'd brought from Azeroth."

"But Ner'zhul ultimately found a way to get hold of these artifacts," Vereesa said. "And successfully activated the ritual."

"Yes." Alleria gazed at the gloomy sky and sighed deeply. "Deathwing gave the three artifacts to his son — that Baron Sablemane, or rather, Saberion — to keep safe, putting him in command of the orc tribes in Shadowmoon Valley to fight against us. He himself took the Skull of Gul'dan and the Blackwing Legion north, far from the Dark Portal and the expeditionary force, to the mountains of Gorgrond, intending to hatch the dragon eggs there."

"However, Ner'zhul soon found a way to take control of Saberion in turn. Khadgar believes the master of the Burning Legion helped that orc warchief, because the demons could never tolerate their slaves being controlled by black dragons... and later events proved Khadgar's guess right. Not long after Ner'zhul's ritual began, countless demons poured into this world through the power of that terrible staff, as if they'd arranged it in advance. They swiftly seized control of the Dark Portal and entered Azeroth, cutting off our contact with you."

"So you took the Skull of Gul'dan from Deathwing's hands, and ultimately used its power to close the Dark Portal?" Vereesa asked in surprise.

"Yes." Alleria nodded. "Though at the time we had no idea that was Deathwing himself. Back then, no one knew he'd truly come to Draenor — only that the Skull of Gul'dan had been taken north. When I saw that devil whose outspread wings could blot out the sky, I thought I was dead... but now, he is the one who is dead."

"Hah, sounds like Vereesa already told you about our heroic deeds at Grim Batol, my lady!" Grede clapped his chest happily.

"You took part in the Grim Batol operation too?" Alleria asked in surprise.

Grede's face darkened and he pointed at Vereesa. "You goddamn — you didn't mention me?! You shoved all the credit onto this warlock, didn't you!"

"There's still time to mention it now!" Vereesa shot back.

The two immediately started bickering again. Jace quickly raised both hands. "Without you, we'd all have died in the lava of Grim Batol, Grede. If not for you, we never could have made it back alive. I must admit it!"

"That's more like it." Grede coughed. Vereesa still gave him a disgruntled sidelong glance.

"At any rate, Khadgar will be very eager to meet you." Alleria smiled. "If not for him back then, all of us — even every living creature in Draenor — might have had no escape from Deathwing's wrath."

"Is Archmage Khadgar that powerful?" Grede asked. "He defeated Deathwing head-on!"

"Truly and honestly." Alleria said seriously.

"No wonder Jace was dead set on finding him, always telling me that as long as he could get the Kirin Tor on our side, he could definitely bring everyone back," Grede said.

"Even though he's one of the Kirin Tor and so young, all the mages of the Wizard Sanctum hold him in high regard — there must be a reason for that," Jace said.

After he finished speaking, he looked back at Alleria and asked, "I have one more question, Captain... What about that Saberion? Are the black dragons here all following him?"

"Perhaps, but frankly we know nothing." Alleria said, "We haven't had any news of him ever since he was defeated by Ner'zhul. Perhaps he's already dead. Now the black dragons are doing everything they can to hunt orcs and attack orc strongholds — we suspect it's also very likely to search for his whereabouts."
