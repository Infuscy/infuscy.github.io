## Chapter 1232: Alleria's Fortress

Jace and Vereesa hadn't brought back any game, but Alleria and her fellow ranger on patrol had each brought along a supply of elven rations—enough to distribute to everyone in the camp who needed the energy, a little something to tide them over so they wouldn't go to bed hungry.

Since they'd run into one of the expedition's leaders, there was no need to worry about getting lost anymore. The next morning, once it had brightened up a bit and they'd gathered their things, everyone set off with the two expedition rangers, heading down the mountain into the endless Terokkar Forest.

Terokkar Forest in the daytime, despite the silver-tinged mist drifting through it, still carried an air of desolation. The seemingly dense undergrowth, the sturdy trunks and towering canopies, all looked as though they'd been draped in a gray shroud—nothing more than a shell barely clinging to life.

Of course, if Jace did his best to ignore his sensitivity to shadow energy and simply observed the place through the eyes of an ordinary young man—the way he'd done walking in with Vereesa last night—this really could be considered a lush, vibrant place, especially with the Bone Wastes they'd just escaped as a backdrop.

"You just agreed to let them name that fortress after you?" Vereesa's voice came from up ahead.

"I wasn't actually here when they named it," Alleria's voice replied. "But I don't think it's a big deal. The orcs burned our Quel'Thalas to the ground, so we ought to take something back from them, one way or another. Let my name pierce into this place like an arrow, and let those orcs know that there's only one fate that awaits anyone who crosses the Sin'dorei—and that's death."

When Alleria mentioned the word "Sin'dorei," Jace couldn't help but glance ahead. At this point, "Sin'dorei"—or "children of the blood"—was still nothing more than an aggressive, vengeful piece of rhetoric to the high elves, not yet the true name of a people who would be forever cast out by another race.

"Jace." Sora held a bundle of purplish-red grass blades. "You feel it too."

"What?" Jace asked.

"This forest isn't as full of life as it appears on the surface," Sora said. "There are many beasts here—enormous insects, wild animals, and birds. They lived here even before Draenor was torn apart; this is their home. But they wander through the forest like refugees, unable to find what was once theirs, living in constant fear and hesitation."

Jace nodded. "Yeah, I feel it too."

Sora went on, "Those wastes will eventually spread here and swallow every living thing, and then reach even farther. Because Draenor is already dead—and this place is nothing but her slowly rotting corpse. Was all of this really caused by that twisted staff?"

"Ner'zhul thought he could control the power of that staff," Jace said.

Sora murmured, "I thought Auchindoun, as a destroyed tomb, must have been filled with death and despair. But coming here, even though I can feel a powerful life force, all I'm really touching is their agonizing struggle. I'm sorry, Jace… maybe it's just… their grief and sorrow are overwhelming me, and there's nothing I can do about it."

Grede, walking alongside them, muttered, "Kill one more Burning Legion demon, and the suffering of Draenor's creatures eases just a little. That's at least something, isn't it, Sora?"

"Yes." Sora managed a faint smile. "You're right, Grede."

Still, she only relaxed slightly and never fully shook off the sadness. After all, even Jace could feel the gloom hanging over Terokkar Forest—Sora, as a green dragon with deep ties to the forests and nature, could hardly ignore it.

The group followed Alleria for the better part of the day, reaching a broad river by afternoon. Following the riverbank north, as the sky darkened a little, Jace spotted a watchtower ahead. It was an exact replica of a Lordaeron watchtower, even flying Lordaeron's white banner.

A long horn blast sounded from farther off, and everyone picked up their pace.

"We're finally here." The sergeant up front couldn't help but sigh. "We're home."

Perhaps to Jace and his group, who had only just arrived in Draenor from Azeroth, the expedition soldiers' idea of "home" sounded a bit jarring. But looking toward the riverbank by the watchtower, Jace could already see neat farmland, a mill, and a waterwheel.

Alleria's Fortress sat at a bend in the river in the distance. The walls weren't very high; the tops of many towers behind them were clearly visible, and Jace could even make out a golden-roofed elven mage tower.

When he saw that mage tower, Jace could picture how this fairly large fortress—or perhaps it would be more accurate to call it a fortress-town—had been built in just a few short years.

It was as if someone had taken a town like Darrowshire or Tirisfal, where humans and elves lived side by side, and transplanted it wholesale into this alien dimension full of bizarre trees, with glowing seed-pods that looked like stars hanging from the heights, all set against a sky stained dark red by the crimson heavens. By any measure, it was jarringly out of place, yet the people living there seemed not to care about the dissonance at all.

The narrow gate saw a steady flow of wagons, cavalry, and patrol squads coming and going; at this hour in the evening, it was even rather lively. Jace knew the expedition had prepared to hold their ground long-term in this apocalyptic world teeming with demons, black dragons, and dark magic. This place had truly become their "home."

As the horn calls echoed back from the direction of the fortress, people in the fields, at the mill, and on the gate all turned to look over. Just then, a four- or five-year-old child suddenly charged up from the field below the road.

He held a long, straight wooden stick leveled at Jace and shouted in crisp, clear tones: "Surrender, orc! The Light will punish your arrogance!"

Jace flicked the stick away with a finger and asked with a smile, "And who's the arrogant one now, huh?"

"Uh." The boy froze for a moment, and then Jace got a good look at him.

He was a half-elf boy with a head of golden hair, and he immediately reminded Jace of that half-elf youth who'd been bullied by a bunch of human boys back in Lordaeron.

"Arator," Alleria said from the side. "Apologize to Uncle Jace."

"Sorry, Uncle Jace." The boy bowed deeply and earnestly.

"It's fine," Jace said.

"Arator…" Vereesa murmured softly. "He's gotten so big."

Unlike Vereesa, Jace was more surprised that the paladin "Redeemer Arator"—who, a decade and a half from now, would be standing guard at Honor Hold clad in armor of judgment and carrying a crimson greatsword—was still just a little kid this small right now.
