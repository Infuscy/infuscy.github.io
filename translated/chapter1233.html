Chapter 1233: Long Live the Alliance

Alleria's Fortress leaned against a cliff face, built along the shore of a small lake at a river bend. Plenty of children were still playing in and around the water at this hour. Judging by their ages, they had probably all been born after the expedition had crossed through the Dark Portal—most of them had likely come into the world only after Draenor was torn apart by Ner'zhul's out-of-control Sargeras' Staff. Alleria and Turalyon's child Arator was even one of the older ones among them.

When they crossed the bridge into the fortress, a ranger stood by a low tree on the riverbank watching the children at play, though her main attention wasn't on the kids. She was keeping a wary eye on the far shore and the slope beyond. She might have been watching for orcs, or for black dragons, or for some other creature that posed a danger on Draenor. In any case, Jace could sense from her emotions that this place wasn't as peaceful and idyllic as it appeared on the surface—danger was always lurking.

More than a simple military stronghold, this fortress really did look like an ordinary human town. Even the central square and the fountain sculpture under construction bore a strong resemblance, and next to the square there was a clock tower being repaired and a town hall. But all around, the low walls and watchtowers that blocked off most of the view remained a constant reminder to the people living here that this was still a place that needed to guard against enemy attack.

The moment the two Draenei entered town, they joined up with two other Draenei and clasped hands tightly, exchanging words of relief with excitement. These tall, gray-blue-skinned outsiders stood out conspicuously and imposingly among the crowd of short humans and elves.

Scholar Nitlin looked at Jace and his companions. "I want to thank you once again… for your help, sirs and ladies. The Draenei will fight bravely against any race that seeks to re-enslave and control us, and we do not fear death. But that does not mean we would waste our lives meaninglessly, or willingly let our lives become fuel for the Burning Legion. After this, I and Livius and the others will find a way across Talador and return to Shattrath City, to tell all of the Sha'tar and the refugees of Auchindoun about the help you gave us."

"Watch yourselves on the road, Scholar Nitlin, Priest Livius," Vereesa said.

"We will, Lady Vereesa Windrunner." Livius and the Draenei bowed deeply. Then Noboru asked, "So Shattrath City is still inhabited?"

"Yes," Livius answered. "Many refugees from Auchindoun and K'aresh have gathered there, Noboru."

"Could we follow you back to Shattrath?" Noboru asked in Eredun. "Does that city still welcome us?"

"Of course it welcomes you." Nitlin smiled and replied in Eredun. "And having more companions on the journey will make it safer too. If you're willing to come back with us, that would be all the better."

"It would be our honor, old friend Nitlin," Magtur said. Having been a rather unflappable old scholar the entire journey, he finally let out a quiet sob. He turned around and reached out his three-fingered hand—swollen, demon-like, with sharp claws—and clasped Jace's hand tightly. "Back in the Black Morass, I never imagined that one day I would have the chance to set out for Shattrath again, Jace Sesso. Nothing can express the gratitude that I and my companions feel."

"We couldn't have made it here to meet the expedition without your help either, Magtur," Jace said in Eredun. "And you and Noboru were willing to trust us in that situation, willing to show us how to repair the teleportation coordinates. I'd say it's more like you fought for your own chance to return to Draenor."

"You are an admirable person, Jace Sesso," Magtur said quietly. "May your path always be clear."

"And to you as well. Safe travels, Magtur," Jace said.

The Broken came up one by one to Jace and his friends to bow their farewells, exchanging blessings among themselves, then left with the Draenei.

After they had gone, Grede asked curiously, "What kind of place is Shattrath City?"

Alleria explained, "Shattrath City… is almost a complete ruin by now. But in the past, it was the greatest city the Draenei ever built in this world—filled with light and warmth, beautiful temples and towers everywhere. Many Draenei have told me that before the war between the Draenei and the orcs, Shattrath City was like a seat of crystal surrounded by mountains and sea—a silver crown studded with amethyst gems."

She gazed at the retreating figures of the Broken and said, "But before we came to Draenor, Shattrath City had already endured multiple sieges and destructions. Its walls and streets were corrupted, many of its towers had collapsed, and it had become a terrible place filled with death and despair."

"Even the orcs couldn't stomach the scars that war and dark magic had inflicted on the city. They looted the treasures and artifacts inside and left. But precisely because of that, many Draenei were able to return after the abandoned it, hiding in the districts or buildings that hadn't been completely tainted yet."

After listening, Grede sighed. "I'm tired of condemning the atrocities of orcs and demons. These unforgivable bastards—from Lordaeron to Stormwind, to Auchindoun and Shattrath… I'd wager the orcs who followed Ner'zhul to other places made just as much of a mess over there. That's their nature!"

"That is precisely why the Sons of Lothar came to this world, Lord Grede Thunderfist." Alleria nodded. "Although we ultimately failed to stop Ner'zhul's insane magic, at least we forced him to end up taking only a handful of diehard loyalists with him. I think we've done what we could to minimize the destruction he and his magic could inflict on other worlds."

Just then, the bell at the top of the town hall began to clang, and before long more and more townsfolk and soldiers gathered in the square out front. The guards on the walls and watchtowers also looked over with curiosity.

When the ringing stopped, Alleria looked up at the top of the clock tower and raised both hands high. "'Sons of Lothar,' today is an extraordinary day. I have brought news that will thrill every single warrior holding the line here in Draenor…"

By the time the ranger captain said this, the crowd had already begun to murmur among themselves, and some people were shouting excitedly.

Because they had already seen this dusty, weary-looking group standing beside Alleria, as if they had only just arrived after untold hardships. And the ranger captain was looking at these travelers with an expression full of respect and joy—that alone said enough.

"Is that Lady Vereesa Windrunner, Captain Alleria?" someone suddenly shouted.

"Yes." Alleria smiled and nodded. "The heroes beside me, including my dear sister, have been entrusted by King Varian Wrynn of Stormwind and have come here to find us, having endured countless terrible perils and dangers!"

When she spoke Varian Wrynn's name, the crowd erupted… everyone hurled whatever they were holding high into the air, and their shouts completely drowned out the second half of Alleria's sentence.

Quite a few people were so excited they couldn't even stay on their feet, getting jostled by the jubilant crowd until their legs gave out and they sat on the ground, quietly wiping away their tears. Many children, not understanding what was happening, were frightened into wailing—and their parents didn't even think to comfort the kids right away, too busy kissing and embracing each other.

At this moment, nothing mattered more than being able to return to Azeroth. If they could go back, their children wouldn't have to live in constant fear anymore, wouldn't have to scrape by cautiously in this terrifying alien realm teeming with demons and dark magic…

The soldiers on the wall saw the crowd suddenly erupt and craned their heads to ask each other what was going on, while people outside the gate curiously peered in. Before long, the news Alleria had announced reached the walls, and even beyond the walls, and joyful shouts carried farther and farther away.

Of course, Jace and his group were naturally moved to see the expedition soldiers so happy, but their feelings were also especially complicated—particularly when considering the difficulty of transporting such a large group of people all the way back. Jace even privately felt that this might not be the best time to reveal their identities.

But he also understood Alleria's choice. After all, this wasn't twenty years from now—the Sons of Lothar would have settled down by then, grown accustomed to life in Outland, and become true Outland natives.

Not to mention that the expedition was facing an even more difficult situation than they would on that timeline—roaming black dragons scattered across all of Draenor, and an even stronger demon army pouring in to fight Deathwing.

Most importantly, many of them had not yet fully accepted the reality that they would be trapped in Draenor for the rest of their lives after just a few short years. They were still lost. Telling them at this moment that Azeroth and the Alliance hadn't forgotten them could greatly boost their morale.

"My brothers and sisters!"

After the crowd had calmed down a little, Alleria said, "But just as Archmaster Khadgar said, although we have achieved a breakthrough, traveling between Azeroth and Draenor remains an extraordinarily difficult and dangerous undertaking. At the very least, however, we can already see the dawn of victory."

"And what makes all of this possible is the bravery and resolve of our comrades fighting in every corner of Draenor. Without the tenacious fight we've waged all along, we wouldn't live to see the day they arrived here. And if we don't keep fighting tenaciously, we won't be able to let ourselves, our loved ones, and our friends see the day we can go home…"

"Well said, Captain Alleria! For the Alliance! For the Sons of Lothar!"

"Well said! For the Alliance!"

"We should spread this news to Honor Hold and Kirin'Var Village as quickly as possible!"

"Most importantly, take it to Shadowmoon Valley! Tell the Wildhammer brothers! Tell those paladins! Archmaster Khadgar has to know about this right away!"

"That's right—by the Light, long live the Alliance! Long live the Sons of Lothar!"

"Mommy…"

"Blessed be the Light…"

The warriors shouted, and even those normally proud and composed elven rangers lost control of themselves, celebrating alongside their human and dwarven comrades.

A few gnomes standing by the stables and the paddock held hands and happily sang some song Jace had never heard before, the dwarves joined in shouting lyrics that had no melody whatsoever, and the human mages and soldiers beside them—even without knowing the words—cheerfully sang along to the beat.

Before long, the square in front of the town hall had almost turned into an impromptu carnival.

Jace let go of the reservations in his heart. The expedition's strength in Draenor was already stretched thin enough. If they didn't pull together at a time like this, if they kept second-guessing everything, it would only cause this already-thin strength to waver further.

"This might be a bit of a mood-killer right now, esteemed Captain Alleria." Grede sidled up to the ranger captain and asked in a low voice, "But, uh… when's dinner?"
