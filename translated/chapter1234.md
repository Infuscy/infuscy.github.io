## Chapter 1234: A Gloomy Night

Actually, Grede didn't need to be the one thinking about it—the residents of Allerian Stronghold had already taken the initiative to bring out the food they had stored at home, eagerly joining in the celebration that had no preparation at all.

Before long, the ground was covered in baskets filled with fruits of all different colors. The most numerous were those pale-blue, giant-cone-like fruits that hung from the canopy and glowed softly. The residents of Allerian Stronghold had given them the simple, blunt name "Terokkar fruit." As the most common fruit in Terokkar Forest, that was perfectly reasonable.

Grede really was about to starve. He immediately ran to the basket by the fountain and grabbed one, breaking it open without even bothering to ask what it tasted like. But judging by the way he kept eating with increasing, almost desperate enthusiasm, the Terokkar fruit couldn't have tasted all that bad.

Sola was starving too. She hadn't expended the energy to fly, but healing her companions and purifying the shadow corruption along the way had consumed a massive amount of her mana. At this point she had abandoned any pretense of the poise expected of an elegant elven maiden, pulling out a particularly plump fruit and stretching her neck to take a bite straight in.

Vereesa, on the other hand, seemed rather stiff in the face of the many soldiers and mages who had already recognized her, not to mention the crowd of people pressing in to ask her about conditions in Azeroth, which made it impossible for her to slip away. Facing all these soldiers' curiosity about her homeland, she couldn't bring herself to abandon them for food and drink, and could only do her best to answer their questions.

Jace noticed that a lot of the food that had been brought out was fried—fried fish, fried meat—as if this place in Terokkar Forest had no shortage of oil at all. These fried foods had a rather unfamiliar taste when bitten into, something like the flavor of kerosene. This oil surely hadn't been brought over from Azeroth.

"It's pressed from Olemba tree seeds," Alleria explained. "So it has a strange taste. This oil isn't exactly delicious, but the good thing is it's everywhere in the forest."

Jace looked at the ranger captain walking up beside him and nodded. He took another bite of an extremely tough fried meat slice, then looked out at the crowd happily sharing food.

"Is the road between here and Wildhammer Stronghold safe, Captain Alleria?"

Alleria nodded. "In the years since the cataclysm, this stretch of road has been relatively safe. That's largely thanks to the black dragons guarding Auchindoun so closely—otherwise I'm certain the remnants of the Shadow Council would never have let us communicate with the Wildhammer clan so easily."

"I'd like to head to Wildhammer Stronghold as soon as possible to meet with Archmage Khadgar," Jace said. "I heard from Nitrin that the Burning Legion is preparing a second offensive in Shadowmoon Valley. Every day more people might die."

"I think so too." Alleria said, "I'll brief the gryphon riders right away. Rest well tonight, and we'll set out first thing tomorrow morning."

"Before we leave, could I take a look at a map of Draenor?" Jace asked.

"Come with me, Jace," the ranger captain said.

The two of them made their way through the celebrating crowd into the town hall. Alleria unrolled a scroll across the table and said, "This is a map we did everything we could to draw after the cataclysm. The dashed lines in these areas represent… well, places that've been torn away. It might be a bit hard for you to grasp, but in any case, these are areas you absolutely cannot approach. We've already lost several gryphon riders here—if you try to get close, you'll be sucked in by the power of the Void in an instant, and there's no escaping it no matter what."

She sighed and continued explaining, "It's not just the gryphons—even the landmasses that were less firmly connected to the ground, the buildings on top of them, all got pulled in. At first we thought this consumption would keep going until the entire world was torn to shreds, but fortunately it only lasted a few months before gradually reaching some kind of equilibrium. That doesn't mean those places aren't dangerous anymore, though."

As Alleria explained how they had explored the post-cataclysm world, Jace studied the map of Draenor drawn by the expedition. It was clear that they had drawn heavily on the pre-cataclysm topography of Draenor, but what surprised Jace was that many of the confirmed "landmarks" didn't quite match the Outland of the future either.

Take, for example, the capital of the ogre empire—Highmaul, a landmass that in another timeline had been completely torn away, but which now was still firmly situated on the Nagrand landmass.

Perhaps after a while longer these places would fail to hold on and fall into the Twisting Nether—or perhaps the great sundering caused by the Scepter of Sargeras was simply that uncontrollable, and no one could predict what results it would produce.

Looking at the map marked with the locations of fortresses and settlements, Jace asked, "How many people does the expedition have left in total?"

"Turalyon said there might be eight to ten thousand who survived that catastrophe, but I think… he's being too optimistic." The ranger captain brushed a strand of golden hair back with one hand, then pointed to Allerian Stronghold's position on the map. "Currently we have just under two thousand here, including the wounded, children, and the draenei staying with us."

"Wildhammer Stronghold has two thousand defenders. They're our finest and most excellent soldiers. Honor Hold up north is led by Danath—according to the messages he's sent, there are still over a thousand stationed there. They should be able to hold out a while longer. The orcs there have been routed by us, and without demon support they currently have no intention of launching an attack. They themselves were hit hard by the cataclysm too."

"As for Kirin'Var Village… well, if the news is accurate, Farahlon was probably the place hit hardest by this disaster, even though it's the farthest from Shadowmoon Valley. Although there are no longer any orcs in Gorgrond or Farahlon capable of besieging it, in the best-case scenario they probably have fewer than four hundred left. Maybe fewer."

"Less than six thousand in total," Jace said.

"About that," Alleria said.

Jace didn't know how many people Kallrez's power could allow to pass through that teleportation portal, but given the capacity of the Tower of Ilgalar, a few dozen people per day was probably already the limit. He still remembered the violent tremors that had occurred when the few of them had passed through the portal, even with the support of the Taralfsin Crystal.

There was a reason why even someone of Khadgar's ability couldn't open a stable teleportation portal from Draenor to Azeroth.

They needed a stronger source of magical power to give the portal greater stability, greater strength. If such a source of power existed in Draenor, Khadgar would have found it long ago, so they'd probably have to look to Azeroth—for example, the Sunwell, or the Well of Eternity…

…but the elves guarding these two wells would never allow a demon to defile their sacred grounds.

So they still needed Khadgar and the Kirin Tor to think of a more suitable solution.

"Besides us, there are also the draenei," Alleria said. "They've helped us a great deal in the war against the Burning Legion, Jace. They've already expressed their wish to leave Draenor together with us."

"Can't they leave on their own ships?" Jace asked.

"Their ship has already crashed in Nagrand, Jace. It can't be used anymore," Alleria said.

What Jace actually wanted to say wasn't the ship Oshu'gun that had crashed in Nagrand, but the naaru fleet—the one that would later be seized by Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider and the Sunfury, the "Tempest Keep."

But Jace immediately realized that Alleria clearly knew nothing about the naaru fleet. In other words, the leader who would one day lead Shattrath City and the Sha'tar—A'dal and the naaru under his command—had not yet come to Outland to shelter the survivors, and there was no telling when they would arrive.

Jace had the feeling that they might never come. Alleria and Turalyon had not encountered another naaru fleet during the cataclysm—the "Army of the Light"—and followed them to join the "Thousand-Year War." Plus, it seemed that the only passage between all of Draenor and the outside world that could be opened was by a Burning Legion demon like Kallrez…

Jace gazed out the night window. The bonfire's light flickered on the glass, the laughter, the shouts.

Before long, these sounds grew blurry under his stare. He raised his eyes, and high above there seemed to be a vast dark net enshrouding everything. The expedition soldiers outside, the companions who had come here with him, and even the countless black dragons and orcs—all were like fish struggling helplessly within it, unable to escape.
