## Chapter 1235: Limited Choices

Back in the barracks, the soldiers who had wrapped up the celebration trickled back to rest, while some others headed out to relieve their comrades on night watch.

Alleria had arranged quarters for the group in a rather out-of-the-way spot, basically avoiding any overlap with the coming and going soldiers. Jace suspected that if it wasn't to give them a quiet enough space to rest properly, it was probably because of the demon Morophil.

Housing space in the fortress was fairly tight—perhaps because wounded soldiers were sent here from Shadowmoon Valley from time to time—so Jace and the others could barely get a single large room for everyone to stay in together.

But for a group of companions who'd often been forced to sleep in a dirt pit out in the wild, having a big room with beds and a roof to sleep in already counted as extremely luxurious conditions.

After exchanging news about Azeroth and Draenor with Alleria, Jace came back to find Grede already snoring away. Sora was curled up peacefully on the bed by the window, fast asleep. Morophil sat invisible by the window watching outside. Vereesa sat across from Sora's side, and when she saw Jace come in, she looked over.

Jace glanced at the two sleeping figures and said softly, "We're heading into Shadowmoon Valley tomorrow, Wen. It might get dangerous out there. You should try to get some sleep too."

"I've heard the soldiers here say that Shadowmoon Valley is practically a hell on earth," Vereesa said.

Jace nodded. "Those orcs told me Shadowmoon Valley is full of rivers of fel fire, the ground scorched black…" Vereesa looked at Sora beside her, then said quietly, "Shall we talk outside?"

Jace nodded. The two of them left the room under Morophil's gaze and came to the window in the corridor outside. "You think I'm afraid of Shadowmoon Valley, afraid of fighting demons, Jace?" Vereesa said.

"Of course not," Jace said. "We went through the Dark Portal together, faced a demon army that size."

Vereesa said in a low voice, "I thought I could help my sister, share some of her workload. But I've been here and… it seems I'm not just a child in her words—I actually am a child. From start to finish I've been completely out of my depth, couldn't even help the soldiers ladle out the gruel properly."

"Does ranger training include that too?" Jace teased. "Or is this part of the job description of a Ranger-Lord?"

"Don't make jokes, Jace." Vereesa pursed one corner of her mouth. "And don't bring up 'Ranger-Lord.'"

"What, you think that as a Ranger-Lord you should be doing a better job than Captain Alleria? You don't think Sylvanas, as Ranger-General, could manage an entire fortress her very first night on the job, do you?"

The elf thought about it, then shook her head.

"Let alone you—you're so much younger than her," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded slightly, as if in agreement. But Jace could tell that his words hadn't actually comforted her. She still had a considerable tangle of heavy thoughts churning in her heart—worries, anxiety—as if what had just been mentioned didn't even count as anything important.

Jace also knew that Vereesa wouldn't be worrying herself sick just over a matter of pride. And of course, he didn't like deliberately probing what the ranger was thinking. He quickly pulled his attention away and tried asking, "So, Wen, is there anything else you want to tell me?"

Vereesa turned her head. "Maybe that question should be directed at you, Jace."

"At me?" Jace was taken aback.

Sometime without him noticing, it had started to rain outside, pattering against the window frame. The lamps in the courtyard and the paths between the buildings grew blurrier and blurrier.

With that glance outside, Jace momentarily felt as if he were still in Azeroth—aside from those alien fruits hanging high in the sky that still looked like stars or moons even in the rain, living in this fortress really wasn't much different from living in some small Lordaeron town.

There were hurried footsteps passing outside, accompanied by a soft mutter of "When's this rain going to stop?" The two of them looked at each other for a while, until the sounds had completely faded.

"Ever since we came to Draenor, I've felt like the things weighing on your heart have been getting heavier and heavier." Vereesa stared at Jace and continued, "Don't give me that stuff about warlocks, shadow power, reading emotions to brush me off. I'm a ranger, I have my own methods of observation. And it's not just me who thinks this—never mind Grede, even Sora's felt it. She's worried about you too. Hasn't she told you?"

"So, is there anything about what's worrying you that you can tell us? Don't forget, this doesn't just concern our own lives anymore—it concerns the expedition, and Alleria too."

Jace realized there was no way to tell Vereesa everything he knew about Draenor. Even if he straight-up told her he was some kind of prophet, there was no way to explain away future events like Alleria and Turalyon's disappearance, the naaru fleet's arrival in Netherstorm, and so on.

What's more, these were just his guesses too. He was sensing that something was off.

Seeing Jace hesitate, Vereesa let out a soft sigh. "Whatever it is, don't carry everything on your own, Jace. Right now you're reminding me of Menethil Harbor… ah, damn, who would've thought that was already three years ago. The final prove was that you can't solve every problem yourself—even if you're unwilling to admit it, without me and Grede, even with Morophil and Sazeno on your side, you'd most likely have lost your life in Grim Batol."

"Not 'most likely,'" Jace said. "Definitely."

"And now we have Solamriel," Vereesa said. "I think you know very well that although she says she's not good at fighting, she's no weaker than any of us—especially in willpower… I don't think I could have stood back up and walked out of everything she experienced in the Nightmare. We're all willing to share your burden, Jace Sesso—never mind that dwarf."

"I know, Wen. It's just that the current state of Draenor might be much worse than I expected, plus there's no good way to get everyone back right now…" Jace smiled slightly. "At least I can assure you of this: right now there isn't an old black dragon patriarch secretly muttering in the back of my head."

Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "Best not. Promise me—if you really run into some difficulty, or a problem you can't solve, tell us. At least tell those two demons. Don't go stewing over things alone."

Jace raised his hand. "I promise. I swear on the Light."

"You swear on the Light?" Vereesa said. "If you don't promise, when we get back I'm going to introduce Miss Morophil to Mr. and Mrs. Sesso."

"Hey, that's not necessary, come on." Jace nodded helplessly. "Fine, fine."

The ranger grabbed Jace's hand and kissed it immediately, then wrinkled her neck back and tasted the tip of her tongue. "You've eaten too much Oleba oil—it's like the tycoons of Gadgetzan opened up another factory in your mouth."

"I didn't have much of a choice," Jace said innocently.

"Forget it." Vereesa tilted her head and stared at Jace's lips. "After all, I don't have much of a choice either."
