Chapter 1236: Into Shadowmoon Valley

Early the next morning, outside the fortress walls, several gryphons had been made ready.

Alleria mounted one with practiced ease, then waved and said, "If you don't know how to ride a gryphon you can sit behind me, Commander Little Moon!"

"Don't underestimate me—I've learned a lot since you've been gone." Vereesa shot the ranger captain a sidelong look, swiftly climbing onto a gray-black gryphon and grabbing the reins.

Vereesa climbed fast, all right, but she clearly made the gryphon uncomfortable—it wobbled its body to adjust its balance. Fortunately, out of professional pride if nothing else, it at least didn't throw its rider off its back.

She was that much of a mess, but Alleria was a completely different story. She sat on her gryphon's back as steady as if she'd grown there, with none of the awkwardness you'd expect between an elf and a gryphon.

But then again, neither could compare to how natural the dwarf looked. Grede was chatting with two Wildhammer knights about something, and when he noticed the elves up here starting to mount their gryphons, he raised a hand and ran over.

He excitedly climbed onto a beautiful yellow-and-white gryphon and shouted, "Ha, I never even dreamed that coming to Draenor I'd still get to ride a real Hinterlands gryphon! Riding on Solamriel was like sitting in a prison in Aerie Peak—there's absolutely none of the thrill of travel!"

Jace had just mounted a yellow gryphon. Hearing Grede's voice, he turned to look back at Sora.

Over there, Sora couldn't care less about what Grede was saying, but she still had some reservations about the crowd of people watching them off from the walls and the gate.

Jace had already discussed it with Alleria—it would be best for Sora to reveal her true form publicly, to let the expedition know that in Azeroth, it wasn't just the Kingdom of Stormwind, not just Quel'Thalas or Aerie Peak, not just the Alliance as a whole—even the dragons stood with them.

She looked over at Jace's side one last time, as if seeking confirmation. After all, except for the time Krasus had chased Onyxia and flown straight into the keep from outside the city, she had never revealed her true form in front of such a huge crowd of humans.

But Jace nodded, and Sora finally set her reservations aside.

She raised her arm, and amidst a warm wind and dust that blew up without any warning, her body suddenly expanded… emerald dragon wings lifted high, her long tail swept across the surface of the river, churning up a spray of drifting leaves and water. Quite a few soldiers on the wall couldn't help but take a step back.

The soldiers' first reaction was, of course, a start. Considering that most of them were veterans who had survived the Orc Wars and had spent some time in Outland fighting black dragons, suddenly seeing another green dragon certainly wouldn't fill them with much reassurance.

But when the natural wind that accompanied the green dragon's wingbeats blew past, when the warm breeze carrying a trace of healing and comfort wrapped around the scent of the homeland of Azeroth and swept across the river's surface, every soldier who felt this gust of wind—no matter how deep their hatred for black dragons or red dragons had once been—found it impossible to regard the beautiful Solamriel as an enemy.

Compared to the slightly turbulent emotions of the expedition soldiers, the gryphons were remarkably unflappable—at most they were buffeted by the wind the green dragon stirred up, tilting their heads to avoid the blades of grass and dust. They were elite war mounts who had tangled with black dragons, after all.

Even Alleria was a little stunned looking at her silhouette, until the green dragon lowered her head and said to her in a slightly husky voice, "I'm ready, Captain Alleria. We can set out at any time."

"Good." Alleria nodded as if snapping out of a daze. "We can set off."

Alleria and the two Wildhammer gryphon riders took the lead up high, with Grede following close behind. Vereesa checked her luggage and went up after them. The green dragon brought up the rear.

The gryphon Jace was riding lifted its wings to take off, but then felt that the weight on its back seemed a little off. It glanced back, and saw nothing.

Jace squeezed Morophil's invisible wrist, which was gripping his waist, and said in Dwarvish, "I'm probably a bit heavier than expected, Mr. Gryphon. My sincerest apologies."

The gryphon twisted its head—whether expressing understanding or displeasure was unclear—but in the end it flapped hard and took off, catching up to the long golden tail-spike that Solamriel was swinging.

It was early morning now. Above Terokkar Forest was a sheet of gray-white drifting mist, and the blood-red sunlight that filtered through had somewhat lost its color.

Looking north, the deep-green forest stretched endlessly, with nothing else in sight.

If this were the game, around now you'd be able to see the Pillar of Light that pierced the very sky in the center of Shattrath City. That Pillar of Light was the radiance released by the naaru leader Ad'aal, a beacon guiding every wanderer and survivor of Outland to Shattrath to seek shelter and resist the evil and madness spread by the Burning Legion.

But over there, there was nothing but an undifferentiated mass of dense gray, not a trace of radiance to be seen.

If Ad'aal had not descended upon Outland, then the leader of the Draenei, the Prophet Velen, might still be leading the survivors of Shattrath. Jace knew he might be taking refuge at the Draenei settlement "Telredor" deep in Zangarmarsh at this moment, or he might have already returned to Shattrath, or he might still be on the run. Since Alleria hadn't mentioned his whereabouts, that meant he probably hadn't been able to make contact with the expedition yet at this point. Perhaps blocked by demons, or trapped by the shattered terrain…

Although the Prophet Velen was absolutely a reliable ally to have in the fight against the Burning Legion, that was on the premise of being able to find him quickly.

Jace steadied the gryphon's sway and took out the simple Outland topographic map that Alleria had given him from his waist pouch, and saw that Zangarmarsh on it was still an ocean. He didn't know if the expedition had forgotten to update the map, or if the waters of the Zangar Sea really hadn't all drained into the Twisting Nether yet.

In any case, the topography of Draenor really was quite different from what he was familiar with. Finding Velen as quickly as possible had become a pipe dream.

After resting by the river for a night, the next day the gryphons flew into the depths of Terokkar Forest, until even the cliff face that Alleria's Fortress leaned against was no longer visible.

In the distant sky, what appeared to be a vast field of shattered bouldons seemed to be floating. After flying a bit closer, Jace could even make out remnants of broken walls on some of the boulders, along with collapsed and split-open ogre huts.

"Is that 'the Edge'?" Vereesa shouted toward the front.

"Yes, but don't worry—it just looks close from high altitude. It's actually still very far away!" Alleria shouted back. "But we can't go any farther south! The collapse of the Arak Peaks tore away the forests of Talador and the mountains of Shadowmoon Valley, so the situation there is extremely, extremely dangerous and unstable!"

Vereesa pulled her hood tight and stared upward with an anxious nod. Lightning flickered now and then among those shattered boulders, accompanied by the continuous rumble of thunder—truly terrifying. No matter how bizarre things got back in Azeroth, at least he'd never seen the sight of an entire swath of earth blown into the sky and held aloft by the suction of the Twisting Nether and the gravitational pull of Draenor's wreckage.

"Careful, everyone." One of the Wildhammer knights in the middle shouted in an accent so thick it nearly swallowed the Common words. "Goddamn Shadowmoon Valley is almost here!"

Actually, even without his reminder, Jace could already feel the corruption and darkness spreading from ahead.

The gryphons flew extremely fast. As the group crossed ridge after ridge, the forest below gradually began to blacken, wither, and grow sparser.

A stench of demonic fel filled the air. In the sky where black and red interwove through the thick clouds, there were now and then piercing, screeching sounds—the noise produced by the friction and explosions of shadow power. Jace was all too familiar with it.

Unlike the boundary between the Bone Wastes and Terokkar Forest, where the trees had been blown apart outright and then slowly been consumed by desertification, their corpses irreversibly dried up and buried.

The forest here hadn't completely vanished and died. Instead, in its struggle against demonic power, it had grown more and more twisted and deformed. Every single thing that had managed to survive this catastrophe had become a maddened monster, making you feel as if they were trying to lift their heads to scream and curse at you.

The green dragon couldn't help but fly higher and higher, as if trying to escape this madness. Until suddenly they crossed a split-open, deep ravine and the trees thinned out considerably, the phantom shadow roars emanating from deep within that black forest finally quieted down a little.

It was obvious that this place was no longer suitable for spending the night in peace. The gryphon riders decided to pick up the pace and push through the night to reach Wildhammer Fortress as quickly as possible.

When passing through these ravines that looked as if they'd been crudely hacked out by a blade, there would often be dark-blue crawling beasts about ten to twenty meters long on the cliffs.

They hung from their long tails, their demon-faced heads raised, their corners of their mouths curled into what looked like strange smiles as they stared upward—as if lying in wait to hunt these visitors from another world.

What was most unsettling was that these creatures would suddenly vanish, then reappear in an indistinct flash on a farther cliff ahead, continuing to fix their eyes on the gryphons flying past high above, as if planning to find the right angle to launch an assault.

Although he couldn't make out their full appearance from high up, Jace could more or less guess that these were a unique species of Draenor—Phase Wyrms.

A Phase Wyrm suddenly appeared on a tree branch that Grede was passing and stuck its head out to let out a hiss, startling Grede so badly that he yanked the reins and flew a good distance higher in a panic.

"This is what you call 'relatively safe,' Captain Alleria?!" Grede shouted.

"We had their meat in our dinner at the fortress the other night, Lord Thunderfist." Alleria laughed. "Compared to what we're about to see next… these little creatures really don't amount to much!"

"What?! Their meat—no, wait, what else is there…"

Grede shouted in surprise, but before he could finish his sentence, the gryphons suddenly flew past a cliff, and a sheet of black immediately spread out before their eyes.

The dwarf still had his mouth wide open. Like a machine, his head turned to look at this boundless plain. Rivers of fel-green lava were densely packed across it like torn, festering wounds, releasing blinding flames of light.

The forest that had stretched all the way here from Terokkar had been reduced to little more than debris, looking like the curled, damaged hair left after severe inflammation had ravaged skin.

"Welcome to Shadowmoon Valley, heroes!" the Wildhammer knight at the very front shouted with what sounded like gleeful schadenfreude. "The Burning Legion and the orcs' favorite playground! Hooray!"
