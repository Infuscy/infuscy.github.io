## Chapter 1237: Not Only Old, But Young

The gryphons swept low over the scorched lowland plains of Shadowmoon Valley. Grede and Vereesa both kept their eyes locked on every detail of the ground below, and neither of their expressions looked good.

Because in Azeroth, even the Blasted Lands—the place closest to the Dark Portal—had never suffered such terrifying corruption from both shadow and fel.

Even the Burning Steppes, compared to this place, seemed to have been reduced to an ordinary fire scene.

The dark clouds in the sky were all cinders from fel fires. What blew them was the shadow wind that poured in violently from the Twisting Nether. Beside the rivers of flowing green fel lava, burning fel elementals and shrieking Voidwrestlers tore at each other, drawing power, then taking turns exploding and annihilating—the struggle endless and unceasing…

Jace was reluctant to admit it, but this hell completely remade by demonic power didn't fill him with dread from the bottom of his heart. After all, for a warlock like him, a place like this was actually where he could bring all of his power into full play.

He could hear the trapped souls of dead Draenei groaning helplessly here, the souls of dead orcs still letting out savage roars. Mixed into these roars were even hollow, ringing sounds like fel energy grinding against metal like flames, and all the other ghosts who had lost their lives here but were bound by fallen magic and unable to pass on… all of them could be used by him, could become part of his power.

Here and now, he even felt capable of turning everything around. Although Jace knew very well that this was nothing more than the extremely abundant shadow power rushing toward his body against the gryphon's flight, mingling with his inner desires and worries and spreading outward—arrogance, conceit… self-righteousness, self-righteousness.

Although Shadowmoon Valley was dark enough to blot out all sunlight, it wasn't shrouded in the thick, unbroken gloom like Terokkar Forest. Here, relying on the light released by fel explosions, one could see very, very far. Before they'd even been in Shadowmoon Valley for half a day, Jace caught sight of that towering, abrupt volcano in the distance—or more accurately, saw the bright fel flames continuously erupting from that volcano. It was like a faceless fel beast raising its head and belching out scalding acid like ceremonial salvos from time to time.

Judging by the traces of that drool flowing down, the mountain appeared so abrupt and steep, as if it had been yanked up straight from flat ground. And that was indeed the case.

This was the "Hand of Gul'dan"—the real Hand of Gul'dan.

Decades ago, Gul'dan had wielded dark magic and unimaginable, terrifying magical power to create this volcano. The immense force he unleashed had brought the entire orcish tribes of Draenor to their knees beneath him and the Shadow Council. But he hadn't created this volcano to show off his power—it was to completely sever the connection between the orcs and the elements with the power of fel.

He had unquestionably succeeded. The already fragile trust between the shamans and the elements was utterly destroyed with the rise of the Hand of Gul'dan. Even the fire elements of Draenor were infected by the fel energy of this volcano, rapidly degenerating into mad, chaotic fel fire, continuing to spread this evil corruption and pollution farther and farther—to Shadowmoon Valley, to Talador or Terokkar, to Terana, to what was today the Hellfire Peninsula.

Jace stared into the brightness of the volcanic crater, and he didn't feel the slightest fear. Because perhaps one day, you could do it too, Jace Sesso.

You were the true dark emissary who had inherited Gul'dan's power and will—you hadn't submitted to the Void, nor had you submitted to the Burning Legion, because you were your own master, and no one could sway you.

He frowned and glanced back at the staff behind him. That curved demon bone emitted no sound at all. Were these his own thoughts?

Just then, he saw the green dragon beside him tilting her head and staring at him.

Her sapphire-blue eyes were like a pure, sweet spring in this chaotic, evil hell—just one glance was enough to cleanse away many filthy, distracting thoughts.

Solamriel's emerald-jade scales, which undulated with the beating of her wings, were similar in color to the fel rivers flowing through all of Shadowmoon Valley, yet completely out of place—as if they existed in two planes separated by millions of miles, forcibly twisted together by some projection, making it impossible to equate them.

Had she noticed something off about him?

Jace didn't know, but he sensed no wariness from her emotions, only… reassurance.

"Jace! Look ahead!" Vereesa, flying up in front, suddenly shouted, yanking Jace's attention over.

Beneath a cliff face ahead, two towering, massive walls sealed off the road leading into the mountain pass. Those walls were covered in bone spikes from demons or some kind of Draenor beast, looking like an orcish stronghold—but flying from them were some familiar white and red banners.

"That must be Wildhammer Fortress, ha! I can see it, I can see my warhammer, can you see it, Jace!" Grede raised Kadros's warhammer high and shouted.

Just as Grede said, Jace couldn't quite make out the design on the white banners from this distance, but at least he recognized the red ones. That was the emblem of the Wildhammer dwarves he'd seen on Aerie Peak—the square, solid Skybreaker, which was the very hammer Grede was waving in his hand right now.

"Damn, you lot have only been here three to five years at most, huh?" Grede's high spirits couldn't help but carry a note of pride.

He shouted toward Alleria and the two gryphon riders leading the way up ahead: "Tell me how you built this fortress in such a short time! Don't tell me it was purely the magic of the Kirin Tor wizards! That completely goes against the spirit of our kind. Overlord Kurdran would never deign to call it 'Wildhammer'!"

"It really wasn't magic, Lord Thunderfist!" Alleria shouted back in response.

"When we came here, this was Ner'zhul's home, Lord Grede—a place called Anguish Fortress!" one of the Wildhammer knights replied. "We tore down their toy-like houses, tore down their laughable walls with zero sense of aesthetics, and then put them in more suitable positions—the ones facing Magtheridon head-on, facing the demon lords of the Burning Legion!"

"That's right, that's how it's done, haha!" Grede laughed. "That's our style!"

"Except for the little Bronzebeard dwarves building bunkers outside the wall, which ruined all the beauty!" another Wildhammer dwarf shouted. "Take a look, Lord Grede—compared to the solemn walls and watchtowers we built, that thing is ugly as a gray pimple that grew out of nowhere. Though I have to admit it's still pretty useful!"

"It is, very good at getting beaten up by Infernals!" another dwarf shouted back.

The place the Wildhammer knight was referring to did indeed have a gray, shabby structure, like the dwarf watchpost bunkers Jace had seen in Loch Modan. Grede looked over and immediately burst out laughing again, laughing so hard he couldn't help but cough violently several times.

Alleria also couldn't help but laugh along. Jace looked at these expedition soldiers up ahead, and from their excited emotions he couldn't sense at all that they were currently pushing deep into a place corrupted by demonic power—it was really as if they were touring a Burning Legion theme park.

Several gryphon riders came flying from the direction of Wildhammer Fortress to meet them. One of the humans shouted, "It's Captain Alleria! Why is there a, there's a… dragon?!"

"Report to Khadgar and Turalyon!" Alleria shouted. "We've found the heroes who came here from Stormwind! This green dragon lady came here with them!"

"Stormwind?" one of the gryphon riders said. "Azeroth?!"

"Yes, Stormwind in Azeroth!" Grede shouted, raising his warhammer. "Tell Overlord Kurdran that a boy from House Thunderfist has come to reinforce you!"

The gryphon riders immediately shouted and turned around, one of them flying at full speed toward the wall. By the time they descended near the fortress and landed on the wall, the soldiers in this whole area were already shouting at the top of their lungs, unable to contain themselves.

Jace remembered that the Shadowmoon clan had built Anguish Fortress here as a magnificent underground city hidden within the mountain. The Wildhammer dwarves had then continued to crudely modify the mountain, turning it into a fortress-town somewhat similar to the Dark Iron city of Dun Modr north of the Wetlands.

Right now, a long, wailing horn sounded from within the fortress. Solamriel's descent drew the attention of every warrior in training and every guard on the wall.

The gryphon landed on the wall. Jace saw the stance and formation of the soldiers training inside the wall, and immediately understood what Alleria had meant by "they are our finest and most excellent."

Ever since coming to this world, whether in Lordaeron or Stormwind, or even at Nethergarde Keep, he had never seen an army with such firm resolve. And the hellish scenery beyond the walls made such will and morale all the more precious.

With these people and their descendants, holding out in a godforsaken place like Outland for twenty years, surrounded by evil on all sides, was no longer so hard to imagine.

"Alleria?"

Just then, a mage in blue robes had just climbed up the wall, accompanied by several soldiers. He glanced over and immediately walked over quickly.

If he hadn't come close enough for people to see his aged, deeply wrinkled face, judging by the two steps he took up the stairs and the rather brisk little jog that followed, it would have been hard to believe he was actually that old.

Or perhaps, he wasn't that old at all.

"Khadgar!" Alleria jumped off the gryphon and walked over, her tone carrying a hint of a challenge. "Maybe you won't believe it, but we really did wait for reinforcements to arrive from Azeroth. Doesn't this count as proving your earlier judgment wrong?"

"You think I care about my own judgment, dear Captain Alleria?" Khadgar shook his head slightly and looked at Jace's group. "If it means getting everyone back to Azeroth, I'd gladly let my colleagues in the Kirin Tor laugh at me for the rest of my life."

"Not that there's much of that left anyway." The archmaster scratched his gray-and-white stubbled beard.
