Chapter 1238: Not Only Old, But Young (II)

Seeing Vereesa and Alleria standing together, Khadgar couldn't help but pat the ranger captain on the shoulder. "You have no idea how much Alleria missed you and General Sylvanas. I can't even remember the last time I saw her this happy—even hearing that Turalyon had survived the plasma of a pit lord wasn't quite like today."

"No need to make me sound so heartless, Khadgar," Alleria said, not amused.

Vereesa smiled and stood at attention, saluting in the ranger fashion. "We meet again, Archmaster. You look much younger than the last time we saw each other."

"You always know just what to say, Vereesa." Khadgar straightened his collar. "But I suspect that has more to do with the length of my beard."

"Before, I figured that since I'd turned into an old man, I might as well enjoy all the perks of being an old man—like having people overlook the serious mistakes I made out of senility, or 'pulling rank as a senior' to make my words carry more weight. But then I realized that having a long beard actually got in the way quite a bit when fighting demons, and caused a lot of inconveniences in daily life too…"

"I won't tell Archmaster Antonidas about this when we get back, don't worry," Alleria said.

"Best not," Khadgar said. "And you, from Aerie Peak…"

"Grede Thunderfist of Aerie Peak." Grede took the hand Khadgar extended and shook it vigorously. "They erected a statue of each of you five heroes at the gates of Stormwind. You look a damn sight better than your statue. I thought they'd deliberately carved you as a scruffy old bastard, or mixed you up with Archmaster Antonidas."

"They put up a statue of me?"

Khadgar looked a little surprised, but wasn't particularly delighted about it. Alleria beside him just watched him with an expression that said she was waiting to see his reaction, clearly enjoying the show.

Jace understood why the Sons of Lothar would have complicated feelings about hearing this. As someone who also had a statue erected at Nethergarde Keep, he understood what a statue meant—people had accepted your sacrifice, and had no intention of revisiting the matter. Khadgar surely caught that message right away.

Of course, the expedition had prepared to sacrifice themselves, to fight every demon and orc in Draenor to the very end to protect Azeroth. But hearing that the Alliance really seemed to have given up on trying to find them couldn't leave their hearts entirely unmoved.

"I wonder where Overlord Kurdran is, Archmaster Khadgar. Is he in the fortress?" Grede asked eagerly.

"He's in the hall just up there." Khadgar pointed to the mountain path behind him.

"Then I'll go see him first." Grede waved the warhammer in his hand. "I have something extremely important to show him, extremely, extremely important."

"Strictly speaking, this is the home of you and all the Wildhammer dwarves, Grede of Thunderfist, so actually…" Before Khadgar could finish, Grede had already zipped past him with a whoosh.

"I'll go on ahead, sorry everyone." The dwarf charged straight down the stairs, and a Wildhammer knight chased after him shouting, "Slow down, slow down, Lord Grede, we're coming with you too…"

"He should be back in a moment, actually." Khadgar watched the backs of the dwarves who had already run out of sight, then finally cleared his throat and looked over at Sora, who was also watching Grede leave. "Well, I considered many possible scenarios for Azeroth reinforcements breaking through the demons' blockade to reach Draenor, but I never expected there'd be a green dragon lady."

"I am Sora Shade." Sora inclined her head. "Archmaster Khadgar, I've long admired your name."

"Given that there's a green dragon, I think I may have already guessed how you got here." Khadgar stroked his chin lightly. "Could it be that the power of the Dream can cross the boundary between Azeroth and Draenor?"

"It actually can't," Sora shook her head. "If it weren't for the precious token that Queen Ysera gave to Vereesa, I wouldn't be able to use even a scrap of the Dream's power here."

Khadgar let out a slight sigh. Sora looked at the Jace beside her, and the mage followed her gaze to the young warlock standing to the side. "You must be Jace Sesso."

Jace was startled for a moment. "Uh, do you know me, Archmaster Khadgar?"

Alleria folded her arms from the side. "It was Khadgar who told Turalyon and us the news—Teron Gorefiend was killed by an unknown young mage in Stormwind."

"But…" She looked at the archmaster. "How did you guess he was Jace Sesso?"

"The reports that came through the other side of the Dark Portal mentioned that the young mage was a Lordaeron man studying magic in Stormwind—that's quite rare, so I was particularly impressed by that detail." Khadgar tapped his temple, then raised his tone slightly and continued. "It wasn't hard to guess. You're so young… and you speak with a Brill accent, and you're wearing a ring of the royal mages of Stormwind—one that Master Nielas Aran used to wear. But the most crucial thing is that you appeared here alongside Lady Vereesa. I know Teron Gorefiend was killed by the two of you."

Khadgar laid all of this out bit by bit like a detective showing off his reasoning. Jace couldn't help but admire him. "I am indeed Jace Sesso, Archmaster Khadgar. It's an honor to meet you."

"The honor is mine, Mr. Jace Sesso," Khadgar said earnestly. "Teron Gorefiend was a troublesome foe for us, no less difficult to deal with than Ner'zhul. Hearing he died beneath Blackrock Spire was an encouragement to all of us at the time. But even after losing such a key figure, Ner'zhul still managed to complete his insane plan under our siege. If it weren't for that self-proclaimed 'Dragonfather' and his self-important idiot son meddling, I don't think things would have come to this pass."

"They also 'coincidentally' brought a piece of good news, Khadgar," Alleria said, leaning against the parapet. "That lunatic you hate so much is dead in Grim Batol."

"Dead?" Khadgar's eyes widened. "You mean Deathwing, Alleria? You're telling me he's dead?"

"Yes, Archmaster," Sora said. "The dragons of Azeroth already know this. It is also why the Life-Binder, Queen Alexstrasza, has been freed from the prison of the Dragonmaw orcs."

"Dragonmaw orcs—that's no surprise." Khadgar rubbed his forehead and murmured as if talking to himself. "So that's how… so that's what it was. Master Krasus will finally be able to piece everything together."

"Actually, it was Master Krasus who found Jace and went to Grim Batol, Archmaster."

Vereesa said this, and her voice still carried a note of complaint. Jace knew that to this day, Vereesa was still extremely displeased about Krasus sending him off to Grim Batol alone.

"You rescued the Dragonqueen, Mr. Sesso?" Khadgar looked at Jace.

"Vereesa and I," Jace said, quickly adding, "And Grede—Lord Grede."

Khadgar glanced back at the dwarves who had already run out of sight, then said, "The air on the wall really is terrible. I'm sure it's even harder for you all, having just come here from Azeroth, to acclimate. Let's continue inside—we have a good chat about Grim Batol… about how you came here from Azeroth. By the way, the food and drink in there are of quite good quality. I'm grateful to be living with the Wildhammer dwarves—you'll be saying the same thing soon enough."

"Besides," he added, looking to Jace's side and switching to Eredun, "Wildhammer Fortress's aerial patrol can detect invisible succubi. They'll be flying over here before long."
