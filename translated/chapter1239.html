Chapter 1239: Where a Warhammer's Value Lies

That Khadgar could see through Morophil didn't surprise Jace in the slightest.

Coming to Draenor this time, he had never planned to hide the fact that he was an evil warlock from the expedition soldiers—especially their leaders—because getting back to Azeroth right now required going through Karlez's power. Since the existence of a doom lord would have to be made public to the Sons of Lothar no matter what, they might as well be upfront about it from the start.

But judging by the emotional tremors coming through the soul link, Morophil was far more panicked than Jace himself.

Of course, Jace knew what Morophil cared about wasn't whether her master had exposed the fact that he'd brought a few demons along. It was that her invisibility had been seen through at a single glance—which had most likely triggered her Rashorel PTSD.

Although most powerful demons had self-esteem that far exceeded their actual strength, especially when facing mortals, the extent of Morophil's embarrassed anger still exceeded Jace's estimate. Then again, she wasn't about to actually pop out of invisibility like an out-of-control imp and pick a fight with Khadgar.

"How did Deathwing die?" Khadgar asked eagerly as they walked down the stairs of the wall. "I saw his body. I genuinely thought that if his god-like appearance and power were going to be the last thing I ever saw in my life, it wouldn't be a bad way to go."

"He swallowed the power of the Demon Soul," Jace said. "I guess he didn't want the imprisoned dragon power to return to all the Dragon Aspects, rendering all his millennia of effort wasted, so…"

"Why?" Khadgar slowed his pace and turned back. "If he planned to swallow that much power, why do it now? Unless the Demon Soul… shattered."

"Yes, Archmaster," Vereesa said. "He accidentally smashed the Demon Soul himself. I guess the humiliation of being toyed with by mortals finally drove that monster out of his mind."

"People can't lose what they never had, Vereesa." Khadgar smiled. "Had he any sanity left, he wouldn't have thought of using the Skull of Gul'dan to accelerate the hatching of dragon eggs—something he'd paid an enormous price to have the orcs haul all the way here from Azeroth. The moment his offspring came into contact with the Skull of Gul'dan's power, they would become half-demon, half-dragon monsters spewing fel fire. That would do nothing but harm to his plan to continue his bloodline."

"But Deathwing knew perfectly well that he himself couldn't touch that thing. So how did you manage it?"

"It's a bit complicated," Jace said. "And a lot of it was luck. In brief, I magically enslaved Nekros, who was trying to turn into a demon to deal with us, and made him swallow the Demon Soul…"

Khadgar narrowed his eyes slightly at first, then suddenly raised his brows.

"I know what comes next, Jace Sesso. Now I'm not at all surprised you were able to come to Draenor."

Walking down from the wall, Jace saw at a glance two… no, two fully-armed steam tanks, parked cold against the base of the wall. Faced with their bulk, Jace really couldn't bring himself to use the lightweight measure word "vehicles" in front of them.

If the bunker the Bronzebeard dwarves had built outside the wall looked a bit comical compared to the wall itself, these steel beasts—with spikes on their wheels, steel shovels mounted on the front, and an enormous-caliber howitzer mounted on their backs—were absolutely no joke.

As the group passed these tanks, a bolt of fel-green lightning rolled through the clouds in the sky, followed by a crash of thunder that shook the earth and the walls. Everyone couldn't help but look up for a moment.

"Where's Turalyon?" Alleria couldn't help asking.

"You've come at an unfortunate time—he went out on patrol with the cavalry this morning." Khadgar pointed to the west. "But he should be back by evening."

The mage walked as he spoke. "The demons seem to have been quite quiet lately, but that doesn't mean we can let our guard down. I'll admit I'm far from matching your husband on that."

"Why?" Vereesa said. "Demons don't need to rest."

"Demons also know better than to line up to die," Khadgar said. "The first offensive launched by the Burning Legion already let Magtheridon, master of Karabor, feel our strength. He knows he needs far more soldiers to shake the defenses of Wildhammer Fortress. If I'm not guessing wrong, he's currently using countless portals to transport demons one by one from the Twisting Nether and all over Draenor to Karabor. The demon army has occupied the Blade's Edge Mountains, occupied Hellfire Citadel, occupied Shadowmoon Valley. They've enslaved every orc and ogre they've laid eyes on. Once he believes he's ready, he'll launch a far more ferocious assault than the last one."

"They've counted on our having no ability to counterattack, only to defend," Alleria said.

"Our manpower is limited, our resources are limited, and the cost of launching an offensive is something we can no longer afford," Khadgar said as he walked toward a tower gate. A roar suddenly came from inside.

"You actually got it back from the Dark Iron dwarves, Grede?"

"Absolutely, my lord!"

Grede slapped the warhammer on the table. The Wildhammer dwarf across from him—with a fiery red beard and a thick braided ponytail tied up at the back of his head—looked dumbfounded. "Those red-eyed apes would never let this treasure go. I don't believe it for a second—they must have tricked you, produced a fake to cheat you out of your money, to get you to work for them. They're always that kind of scum!"

Even so, the dwarf couldn't help but reach out and touch the "Skybreaker" lying on the table. He furrowed his brow, raised his head, and stared at the Wildhammer clan banner hanging on the wall. "But damn it, this Anvilmar-era artwork really does look like the real thing."

"It was the leader of the Thorium Brotherhood, Roktus Darkshadow, who gave this warhammer to Grede, Lord Kurdran," Jace said with a slight bow. "In part to thank him for his contributions against the black dragons in the Battle of Grim Batol—the black dragons currently occupy Blackrock Spire, enslaving the Blackrock orcs there and waging war against Shadowforge City. Second, they also hoped to use the power of the other two clans to free the Dark Iron from the Firelord's curse."

"Roktus Darkshadow." Kurdran Wildhammer scratched the blue war paint on his bald head and asked in a low voice. "Does this person really exist?"

"He does exist, Kurdran," Khadgar said. "Medivh once mentioned him to me, and called him 'the only one in that city who still has his wits about him.'"

Khadgar's words made Jace's heart skip a beat. He remembered the young human who had visited Roktus—the mage who, terrified of arcane power, had come to Shadowforge City to learn how to communicate with the elements. At the time, he had suspected that was very likely Medivh himself. Khadgar's words greatly increased that possibility.

"Called a 'sane one' by Medivh." Kurdran let out a cold snort. "What a terrifying joke."

"At least back then he was mostly normal for the better part of the time." Khadgar took a deep breath and walked to the table. "So I believe this weapon is most likely the true Hammer of Kadros."

"Since you put it that way." Kurdran looked down at the warhammer and muttered. "My ancestors… this is really his Skybreaker? Look, it's not that I don't want to believe it, but this kind of thing really does feel… unbelievable. It survived the dark magic of the witch Modgud, endured all those years beneath Blackrock Spire, and now it's even crossed an impossible boundary between worlds to appear before me."

He lifted the warhammer, raised it high, and held it up to the emblem on the red banner. "Tell me—doesn't this count as some kind of omen of victory?"

Grede's initial joy at sharing the good news had mostly dissipated by now. He stared fixedly at Kurdran's hand, a trace of unwillingness churning in his heart—like an innocent boy whose toy had been snatched away by an adult.

But just as he himself had said, Kadros's warhammer truly represented the heritage of the entire Wildhammer clan. If Kurdran just took it and hung it on the wall on behalf of the whole clan, Jace couldn't think of a good reason to ask for it back right away.

However, just as Jace was figuring out what to say, Kurdran actually swung his arm around and shoved the warhammer back into Grede's hands.

"He was Kadros's warhammer in the past. Now he's Grede's warhammer, my dear brother of Thunderfist." Kurdran clapped Grede on the shoulder. "We're not Bronzebeards—we don't play that museum, souvenir nonsense. The most valuable moment for a warhammer is the moment it smashes an enemy's skull. And even more precious than that is the moment it gets shattered by an enemy's forehead—because that marks the moment it truly met a worthy foe!"

Kurdran looked at all the dwarves in the hall. "We thought in the past that the Skybreaker had died a worthy death, but actually we underestimated its power. Now it's returned, carrying a desire for a truly worthy enemy. Grede, brothers of Thunderfist Hall—take it… find a real enemy, and use your power to hurl it. And aim it well! This is the heritage of Grim Batol!"

"Long live Grim Batol!"

"For Khaz Modan!"

The Wildhammer dwarves in the hall, Grede included, roared with excitement. Kurdran gripped Grede's shoulder, shaking with emotion. "Quick, come outside with me. I'm going to show your warhammer to every brother in the fortress!"

"Ah, I apologize—you must be the Jace Sesso that Grede mentioned." He looked over with some guilt. "Your arrival has thrilled the expedition and all of us beyond measure. Although Draenor is a place full of interesting bastards and rousing battles, it's still a bit harsher than Aerie Peak, and there's no good drink to be had, so we'd still like to go home sooner rather than later. Being trapped in this godforsaken place forever wouldn't be much fun at all. But right now I obviously can't wait to announce this great news to everyone! Give us a moment…"

"I understand completely, my lord," Jace nodded, then watched the Wildhammer dwarves jostle and laugh their way out the door, just like a bunch of schoolchildren rushing to the dining hall after class.

"I wasn't counting on Kurdran having the patience to discuss the portal with us anyway," Khadgar said, watching the backs of the laughing dwarves. "Especially given that this portal might be less than conventional."
