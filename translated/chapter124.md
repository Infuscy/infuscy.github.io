## Chapter 124: Grede's Preparations

Jace got home at noon and started packing. The reason he was starting a full day early was that he had way too much to sort out.

First off, anything warlock-related — books on Demonic and Orcish, the mountain of copies Sazeno had written out for him — all of it had to go, and it had to be packed up tight.

For the sake of secrecy, Jace wasn't planning to open any of it for the entire trip. Best to bring a lockable little chest, seal it up, and carry it there and back exactly as it was. The one goal: leave none of that stuff behind in Stormwind for someone to stumble onto his little secret.

None of those books carried any arcane energy, so they'd never be detected in Dalaran — but there'd definitely be people there who'd recognize the contents. So it had to be sealed shut, no opening it.

The two biggest problems were the imp, and the staff he'd looted from that orc corpse.

The imp problem was obviously the biggest. He'd been carrying that staff stick in and out of Stormwind, even into the Mage Quarter — the one thing he'd never done was bring it into the Wizard Sanctum.

But Jace figured there were warlocks in Dalaran too. Bringing an item with a little shadow energy on it wasn't going to set off any massive alarms — alchemists carried shadow taint on them all the time.

And besides, there was nothing about the surface of that wooden stick that screamed "shadow magic."

The imp was a whole different beast. This was a genuine, bona fide demon. Shoved in a bag like that — was that really not going to be a problem in Dalaran?

If he didn't want to bring the imp, his options were killing it or releasing it.

Killing it was clearly the safer bet. Releasing it was a wildcard — no knowing what trouble it might bring.

But killing it…

The journey ahead was a long one, and there was no telling what might go wrong along the way. Whether Sazeno's benefits outweighed its risks was genuinely hard to say.

In the end, Jace decided to bring the imp along and stash it in the warlock's haversack.

If nothing had gone wrong all this time — if a city the size of Stormwind hadn't produced a single person who could tell there was a demon in that bag, not even Morgas up at Stonewatch, not even the gnolls —

Even so, Dalaran was still a hurdle.

If it came down to it, he could just leave it to wander the forests of Hillsbrad by itself. A demon didn't need to eat or drink — standing at the base of a tree for ten days or so wouldn't kill it, right?

Jace rubbed his eyes, thinking about what would happen when Grede met the imp.

Honestly, Grede's tolerance for his dark magic had gotten a lot higher these past few weeks. Keep slicing the sausage… he didn't know how much further that tactic would hold.

But a demon showing up out of nowhere — that wasn't slicing sausage anymore. That was chopping off a whole leg. He'd need to wait for the right moment to ease Grede into it.

And then there was Aerie Peak…

Maybe at Aerie Peak it really would be better to leave it outside to roam on its own. Bringing a demon into the mountain fortress of the Wildhammer clan was just too dangerous.

Jace had bought a simple lockable wooden chest at the general store earlier. He packed the fragment book, the dictionary, and the copies into it, crammed full, then tucked the Gorefiend's letter into the warlock's haversack and stuffed the imp in there too. That was more or less everything he needed for the road.

As for the leftover naga scales, the materials he'd been holding onto, the extra potions — he'd leave all that in the rented room. His landlord was a fairly reliable sort. Paying three months' rent in advance and asking him to keep an eye on things shouldn't be a problem.

After getting all that squared away, Jace sat on the bed and stared at the mess of bags and chests scattered across the floor, then glanced out the window at the white sky over Stormwind.

More than two years since he'd come here. Finally going back. He wondered what Lordaeron looked like now.

Thinking about it, he couldn't help but laugh a little.

The Kingdom of Lordaeron wasn't exactly posting ten-percent GDP growth every year. How much could it have changed?

If anything, Stormwind's reconstruction was the one you could call "changing by the day." Every time he looked out the window, it seemed like some new building had popped up.

That evening Jace had arranged to meet Grede at the Gilded Rose. When he arrived, Grede was already inside with the drinks ordered.

Jace glanced at the clear liquid in the glass and sniffed it. High-proof sweet liquor.

"Going big this time, huh?"

"Can't drink tomorrow night, lad," Grede said. "Ship boards at six the next morning. You got that pocket watch, don't oversleep."

"Relax," Jace said. "I've been waking up at five every morning for the last two months. Don't forget I've still got to clean that mage's place — if I don't want to get docked pay, I can't sleep in. I've already built myself a solid early-to-bed, early-to-rise routine."

Grede asked, "How's the packing coming?"

Jace said, "I've got a small chest. It's a pain to carry, though — don't you have one of those big woven bags? The one you packed herbs in? Could you fit some of my stuff in there?"

"Sure thing," Grede said. "I don't have much luggage — what I do have I'll keep on me. That bag's still mostly empty, haven't figured out what to put in it. How big's your chest?"

"The book kind." Jace held up his hands to sketch out a small square. "A bit bigger than a book. What's inside probably doesn't weigh much more than the chest itself."

Grede nodded. "Just bring it over to me. I'm guessing it's your…"

"Hey." Jace looked around. "There are people everywhere. Give me a break here, man."

"Always slip my mind. But what's the big deal about some books?" Grede lowered his voice and went on. "Your stuff's too dangerous — I'll pack it at the very bottom of the bag and bury it. Don't go thinking you can pull it out and read it whenever you feel like it."

"I wasn't planning to," Jace said. "I'm locking that chest up, carrying it there locked, and bringing it back locked. Just so it doesn't sit in my rented room where someone else might find it."

"That works. Keeps us both safe," Grede said.

The two of them watched the barmaid bring over a couple of thick spicy wolf chops, glistening under a pour of red chili sauce.

Grede swallowed and asked, "You sure those mages can get the tickets sorted?"

"Why wouldn't I be sure?" Jace said. "Both my teachers said it's no problem — you're the only one saying otherwise. Can you stop raining on my parade and let me eat in peace?"

Grede grinned, tore off a strip of meat, and stuffed it in his mouth, talking around it as he chewed. "I just feel like this happiness came out of nowhere, is all! So — did you find a subletter?"

"Subletter?" Jace said. "What subletter?"

"We're going away for two or three months. You're not going to sublet your place?" Grede said. "Just paying three months' rent for nothing — that doesn't hurt?"

"The stuff in my place is worth a lot more than the rent, and I can't take it with me anyway. Might as well leave it there as storage." Jace took a drink and asked curiously, "Is it easy to find a tenant?"

Grede nodded. "I rented my place out to a mother and daughter. Heard they're refugees from down south. That little girl's thin as a kobold."

Jace frowned. "That's a weird way to put it."

"I'm just saying, is it vivid or not?" Grede went on. "That family camped outside Stormwind's gates for nearly a month before they found a place. I told them to hurry up and find a new spot, otherwise I'm going to feel too guilty to move back in."
