Chapter 1240: The Legion's Whip

"About that portal." The archmaster turned around, leaned against the table, his gaze sweeping across the group, then said, "Go ahead, everyone. If Alleria can accept it, I have no reason to hold any prejudices either."

Since Khadgar could detect Morophil and had even kindly warned Jace, Jace figured he had probably already more or less guessed how these people had gotten here based on that alone.

So he set aside his caution and confessed, "We got here relying on the power of a doom lord, Archmaster. The portal for this doom lord is continuously powered by a…"

"Slow down, slow down, you're going too fast." Khadgar raised a hand. "You're saying, a doom lord?"

"A doom lord?" Alleria looked at Jace, mouth half open.

"You didn't know?" Khadgar looked at her.

"I thought it was just an Ered'ruin," Alleria said, stunned.

"Ered'ruin…" Khadgar rubbed his lower lip with his thumb. "Well, it could also be a doom lord."

"I'm sorry, Captain Alleria, I wasn't clear enough before," Jace said. "But an ordinary Ered'ruin demon doesn't have the power to open a portal stable enough to reach here."

"That's indeed the case," Khadgar agreed. "Currently only high-ranking officers of the Burning Legion can possibly pass through the ruptured realm of Draenor, drawing on the residual power of Sargeras' Staff to enter this world. But even that is quite a feat."

"But that's a doom lord, Jace," Alleria said. "Don't tell me you just left a doom lord inside the borders of the Kingdom of Stormwind!"

Vereesa said anxiously, "Jace accomplished something Dalaran couldn't through that demon, sister, I think…"

"Calm down, both of you." Khadgar looked at Alleria. "Think about it, esteemed Captain. When we planned to use the Skull of Gul'dan to close the Dark Portal, many people thought it was inappropriate too. But in the end, even you had to admit that the Skull of Gul'dan did spare our world from being torn apart by Draenor's explosion—it saved countless residents of Azeroth, strange as that sounds."

"Then I'll assume you have a way to control that doom lord, Jace Sesso…" Alleria said.

"I've taken a lot of precautions," Jace said. "And if we couldn't control him, he wouldn't have brought us here either."

"That does make sense." Khadgar raised his eyes, glanced toward the spot by the door where Morophil stood invisible, then looked back at Jace. "So what's the price? You asked for his help—what did you promise him?"

"To kill a demon lord named Kazzak," Jace said. "Apparently he has some personal grudge against that demon." Khadgar was taken aback for a moment, then let out a laugh. "Well, that sounds like a strange contract that can hold up."

"But the portal we've opened can't transport too many people, Archmaster," Jace said. "One or two, no problem. Consecutive dozens, I think, would already be pushing it."

He looked at Khadgar and continued, "If Archmaster Khadgar could return to Dalaran, he could persuade the archmages of the Kirin Tor to work together and find a sufficiently safe method to establish a stable passage for the expedition to return. I think with the Order of Tirisfal's research on demons and the Void, combined with the power of the Kirin Tor council, this isn't an insurmountable problem."

"Persuade." Khadgar let out a long breath. "I'm starting to regret shaving off my beard. If there's anything more irritating than endless demons, it's those old fossils sitting pretty in the Sky Council."

"Even someone as stubborn as Antonidas wouldn't stand by and ignore thousands of Alliance soldiers trapped in another world," Vereesa said. "Right now they're only able to do whatever they please in their castles and palaces because they've completely written you off as dead here!"

"I was just joking, Ranger-Lord." Khadgar said quickly. "Never mind that they wouldn't ignore the fact that the expedition is still holding on—even if they still intended to resist with every excuse in the book, I wouldn't be deterred."

"Where is that portal?" he asked.

"Inside Auchindoun," Jace said. "Currently we only have one set of coordinates located deep within Auchindoun, Archmaster."

"Ah, I know those coordinates," Khadgar said. "Those were used by Sha'tar forces to reinforce the Auchindoun campaign. But after the Cataclysm, the coordinates shifted to the depths of that holy tomb. The coordinates I sent to Dalaran also included one from that place, so I'd always been a little worried that someone might actually use those coordinates to plunge straight into the arms of the Shadow Council… fortunately, it looks like you've already taken care of the trouble inside."

"Not completely," Jace said. "But we know a safe route to reach that shattered hall, one that avoids the Shadow Council and the Auchenai necromancers."

"We can take you in, Archmaster," Vereesa said. "I still remember the way."

Jace nodded. "After we reach Auchindoun, I can contact that demon through a ritual. I already tried it before coming—the ritual works. Once he receives my summons, he can open that portal again and send you back to Azeroth."

"No." Khadgar said, "You must stay here. Perhaps Vereesa, or Lord Grede, could guide me, but you can't all go to Auchindoun."

"Wildhammer Fortress's defenses look reasonably solid for now, but in the last battle, Magtheridon and his subordinates already set their sights on me. If they notice my leaving, these demons might want to seize the opportunity and launch an immediate attack. Although Magtheridon doesn't recognize any of unfamiliar faces, your presence alone would at least give those demons more to think about."

Khadgar was right. As one of the two or three most powerful spellcasters still alive in the world, Khadgar's role for this fortress and the entire expedition was by no means just symbolic.

He really was the only person in the expedition who could help Dalaran open a "Dark Portal," but he was equally important on this side. Once his position was left vacant, there was no telling how the Burning Legion would react.

Khadgar had actually put it very politely. The real problem was that although Jace's group was skilled at infiltration and raiding, even lumped together they couldn't compare to an archmaster like Khadgar in a castle defense like this.

Alleria had clearly also realized this problem. She stood to the side, opening her mouth as if to speak but hesitating—probably also reluctant to spell it out right away.

But even if it wasn't spelled out now, once Turalyon and Kurdran returned and the four members of the Sons of Lothar sat down together with Jace's group, this issue would definitely have to be faced.

After a spell of silence in the group, Jace said, "When I was hiding within the Shadow Council in Auchindoun, I heard a piece of news, Archmaster Khadgar."

"What?" Khadgar asked.

"A demon lord of the Burning Legion possesses an artifact called the Tome of Blighted Names," Jace said. "My demon minion told me that it's very likely the 'whip' used by the master of the Burning Legion to keep Magtheridon and his legion in line."

"I've never heard of this thing. Are you certain it really exists?" Khadgar asked.

"At least the leader of the Shadow Council, Instigator Bleakheart, said it with absolute conviction," Jace said.

"Bleakheart, that ogre warlock." Khadgar thought for a moment. "You're saying you want to steal this thing? I agree that would indeed be…"

"Impossible," Alleria said. "Wouldn't something like that be layered with protection by the Burning Legion, airtight?"

"Bleakheart wanted to collude with an orc warlock from Hellfire Citadel to steal that treasure and bring it into the Shadow Council's hands," Jace said. "So I'm guessing that thing might not be as difficult to obtain as one would imagine, otherwise they wouldn't have set their sights on it."

Hearing Jace's words, Vereesa and Sora both looked at Jace at the same time. Jace gave them a subtle signal—details like turning himself into an orc warlock and worming his way into the Cabal were trivialities that didn't need to be elaborated on.

"Do you know where it is?" Khadgar asked.

"I only know it might be in the hands of an Eredar demon guarding a portal," Jace said, looking at the two members of the Sons of Lothar. "Does that description ring a bell?"

Alleria raised a finger as if remembering something. "Could it be that Eredar lord who's drawing on the power of the ley lines inside Gorian Fortress? He's the guardian of the Legion portal on the highlands of Nagrand."

"Worth considering, Alleria." Khadgar said in a low voice. "I suspect that cunning ogre warlock learned of this Burning Legion intelligence before we did for a reason—after all, that place is the old capital of the Ogre Empire."
