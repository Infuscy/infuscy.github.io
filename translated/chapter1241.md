## Chapter 1241: Mithril Heart

"Before the ogre traitor Cho'gall led the Horde army to sack Highmaul and executed the 'Imperator,' that place was the capital of the ogres across all of Draenor." Khadgar's finger pointed to Nagrand's position on the map spread across the table. "According to intelligence gathered by the rangers, although Sargeras' Staff didn't ravage most of Nagrand, a spatial channel was still drawn there by the ley line nexus beneath Highmaul, dragging a vast area from the western seabed of Nagrand to the outlet of the Zangar Sea into the Void. Most of Cho'gall's ogre garrison that remained there perished in this catastrophe, giving the demons an opportunity to exploit the opening."

"As far as we know, although a small number of ogres are still plotting to retake that place, that Eredar lord probably won't give them the chance," Alleria said, looking at Jace. "He's seized the ley line nexus of Highmaul, intending to use its power to stabilize that teleportation channel. He's building a new Legion portal there, which, like the other portals, will continuously transport demon soldiers into Nagrand. Once the portal's construction is complete and the ley line nexus is depleted, the remaining parts of Highmaul will definitely be dragged into the Twisting Nether."

Twenty years later on another timeline, when players reached the Twilight Highlands at the far west of Nagrand, a truly imposing Legion portal already stood there. And the western side of Nagrand had already been occupied by Burning Legion forces. Several fel forge camps had been constructed, working in concert with that portal to continuously deliver more demons to this place. Just as Alleria had predicted, Highmaul had also vanished without a trace by then.

Guarding that massive portal was indeed an Eredar lord—Conqueror Resakhel. But in the game, players had driven him back using a demonically-modified soul cannon, making it hard to judge his true strength.

"What do you think, Sesso?" Khadgar asked, sitting against the edge of the table. "You want to steal something right under an Eredar lord's nose? If what you said is accurate… that's something handed to him personally by Kil'jaeden."

"I don't think it could be harder than stealing the gruel he brought for his children right out of Deathwing's own hands," Jace said.

"To be precise, snatched it away," Khadgar said with a sly smile, holding up a finger to emphasize the point. Then he continued, "This Tome of Blighted Names you mentioned has also piqued my intense interest. We know every demon has a true name, and knowing their true name is equivalent to holding the scepter to dispose of them at will. Think about it—if Kil'jaeden intends to use this to keep the demon army in Draenor in line, it very likely records the true names of quite a few demon lords, even Magtheridon himself. If we really get our hands on it…"

"We might be able to charge straight into Karabor and take care of that lord of demons," Alleria said.

Khadgar shook his head. "There's no need to sacrifice more lives, Captain. Even if we killed them all in Draenor, we'd only be sending their souls back to the Twisting Nether—sooner or later they'd make a comeback. Right now the most important thing is getting everyone back to Azeroth. If that artifact really does exist, it means we can build the teleportation passage and evacuate much more methodically."

Alleria caught her breath and said in a low voice, "I know, Khadgar."

"However," Khadgar added, looking at Jace, "if there really is no clue to that book's whereabouts, or if getting it is too difficult and the chances are slim, don't take the risk. Come back and we'll discuss it, or find another way. Our group has held on in this world for years—we're not in a rush for a month or two."

"I understand, Archmaster," Jace said.

"Get some rest for a while first, Jace," Alleria said. "You haven't had a proper sleep for two nights now, not since we got close to Shadowmoon Valley. By the time you wake up, Turalyon and the others should be back too. Then you and Vereesa can join us for a proper meal."

"They can have those rooms up there that were just built and cleaned out. I've been trying to find an excuse to tell Kurdran I wanted to move in." Khadgar pointed behind him.

Listening to Alleria and Khadgu discuss arranging rest for them, Jace only realized that in this hall, he was probably the only one who genuinely needed to sleep as soon as possible.

Sora hadn't needed to fight or cast spells along the way, half-asleep and half-awake following the gryphons in flight. The two elven rangers had already rested at Alleria's Fortress and were full of energy right now.

The moment he thought of this, a wave of drowsiness suddenly hit him.

Following Sora, Vereesa, and Alleria into the cave fortress built by the dwarves, the two rangers chatted and laughed all the way there. Jace couldn't even get a word in. All he felt was that the blackened stone walls and floor seemed to bring him right back to Grim Batol when it had been occupied by orcs.

Vereesa still had plenty to say to Khadgar and Alleria, so she left first. Sora stayed in the next room, and Jace sat alone in this stone chamber built by who knew whether dwarves or orcs.

This room had no windows. In Stormwind that might be a drawback, but in Shadowmoon Valley it was an enormous advantage.

The shouts of soldiers training, the cries of gryphons, the rumbling in the sky—none of it could be heard clearly. The atmosphere went quiet all at once. The memories of Shadowmoon Valley, Terokkar Forest, and Auchindoun all seemed to blur together in his fatigued consciousness.

Jace rubbed his face and was just about to lie down to sleep when Grede and Sora's voices came from the corridor in greeting. Before long, the figure of that black little dwarf flashed past the door.

"Grede, hey, I thought you went to sleep ages ago."

"Huh? So this is where you are." Grede turned his head and said, "Get a good sleep, Jace. I guarantee you won't find a more reassuring room than this in all of Draenor."

"It is," Jace said. "By the way, do you have any idea what we're doing next?"

"Archmaster Khadgar told me just now." Grede waved his hand. "Head to Nagrand… find a demon's treasure, something like that."

He continued, "Doesn't it feel like we've gone back to the old days, huh? Taking jobs from folks who can't solve their own problems, then heading off to a place we've never been to deal with trouble we've never dealt with before, and getting a few silver coins out of it…"

"But the past is the past, and the present is the present. So this time that demon's treasure has to stay in your hands. You can't let Khadgar take it back to Dalaran like the Eye of Medivh or the Book of Dalaran. That fellow may not like the Kirin Tor, but he is one of them at the end of the day! Our stock is different now… we can't be brushed off with a few measly coins. Slaughtering an Eredar lord and getting a magic book as payment is about right."

"Book of Medivh, Eye of Dalaran," Jace corrected, propping his chin on his hand.

"What's the point of remembering so clearly? They're not ours either way." Grede poked his head in and rubbed his fingers together. "Once we get that thing, remember to pay me some gold coins, huh? The little lizard and the little elf probably feel too awkward to ask you for money, but I know…"

"Don't you want that treasure made from Onyxia's blood?" Jace asked.

"That's a separate matter. Alright, I'm going to sleep." Grede finished, glared, and turned to leave. But Jace sensed something indiscernible in his emotions—at least in the span of these few sentences, there was no way to clearly see what he was thinking. "Hold on," he called out.

"What." Grede moved back, brimming with irritation. "Haven't you heard the saying? Don't keep a dwarf from his sleep!"

"Are you all right?" Jace asked.

"Ha." Grede gave a dry laugh. "You think I'm those two, needing a warlock holding a prophecy orb to help untie their emotional knots? I'm Lord Grede Thunderfist of Aerie Peak, son of the greatest hunter of Grim Batol. My heart is made of mithril!"

Morophil, standing to the side, couldn't suppress a couple of laughs. Grede pointed at her and said, "What the hell are you laughing at?"

"Get out first, Morophil," Jace said.

The succubus tilted her head and walked out step by step. As she passed Grede, she gave him a rather mocking sidelong glance. Jace knew this succubus had already seen through the tangle in Grede's heart.

After she left, Jace sighed. "If there's a problem, just say it. I'm not at the point where I'd fall asleep the second I close my eyes. By the time we wake up, we'll probably be preparing to head to Nagrand."

Hearing this, Grede fell silent for a long while, the emotions in his heart growing more and more chaotic.

Finally, he couldn't hold back a huge gasp and sat heavily against the doorframe, muttering in a choked voice, "Fine, fine, I think I'm missing that stupid dad of mine who got himself killed, Jace…"

"I've always blamed myself for breaking his warhammer, Jace. I always felt sorry to that hammer, sorry for what he left me… damn it, the ancestors bear witness, who knew that dead man's demonic sword would be so powerful?! Apocalypse?! What kind of godforsaken name is that?!"

"But I've figured it out now. I think I really am not a normal Wildhammer dwarf. I really am a sentimental, fragile bastard. Lord Kurdran was right. My father would be proud of me, because that warhammer met an enemy truly worth fighting for it. Isn't that right, Jace Sesso? That warhammer died a worthy death. It fulfilled its mission! Isn't that right?!"

Jace was just about to speak when Grede suddenly pointed at him, forcibly choking back his sobs and growling in a hoarse voice, "Don't talk. I don't need your affirmation. I don't need anyone's affirmation. I don't need any damn warlock right now, I don't need anything! So you shut your mouth… don't say a word!"

He staggered to his feet, cursing at something as he charged into the corridor, his voice growing more and more distant.

Not long after, Sora appeared at the door, looking blankly in the direction the dwarf had left, then over at Jace's side. "What's the matter with him…"

Jace wanted to say something, but in the end he just let out a soft breath. "Sleep well, Sora."

"Mm. You too, Jace," Sora said.
