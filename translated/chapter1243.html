Chapter 1243: Gifts from the Sons of Lothar

Compared to the Wildhammer fortress's overall wretched exterior, scarred by battle and shadow-corrosion, the banquet hall's interior was like stumbling through a portal into a cozy tavern — the contrast with the long tables laid out with every kind of food and ale hit all the more sharply.

At Alleria's Watch, Jace had felt the expeditionary force endured on Draenor partly out of their belief in protecting home, and partly because Alleria's Watch was so much like the mixed human-elf towns of Azeroth that after living there long enough, you could start to blur the two worlds together… but the Wildhammer fortress? Not only was it surrounded by demon scorched earth and a fel-green sky, the fortress itself looked like an orc-built dwelling of the apocalypse.

But the moment he saw dishes this lavish — dishes that had absolutely nothing to do with "the apocalypse" — he understood.

Beyond the assorted fruits and fungi from the Zangarmarsh, Nagrand, and Terokkar Forest regions, there were kaliri roasted to the size of super-turkeys, clefthoof steaks laid out on the table as neatly as Dark Iron dwarven armor, and plenty more strange meats and offal that Turalyon and Khadgar hadn't gotten around to introducing.

Jace only truly felt the urgency of surviving in an alien world when he considered what these food varieties revealed about the regions the expeditionary force's soldiers could now safely reach.

Except for the clefthoof, which was so dry and tough to chew that all you could taste was the seasoning mixed with your own saliva, most of the other meats were hard for Jace to get used to right away — the texture and taste both weird as hell, weirder than those Zangarmarsh "little mushrooms," like undercooked frog.

Khadgar had been enthusiastically introducing the out-of-town guests to more normal meats like talbuk and warg, but he and Turalyon had both tactfully sidestepped several types of soft, squishy roasted meats in equally large quantities. Add to that Vereesa tucking into those meats without any regard for appearances while Alleria stood nearby with the look of a prank that had gone exactly as planned…

…Jace suspected there were probably a few of Outland's giant insects in there — like the Terokkar giant moths they'd seen before, or the predator brood insects nesting in the upland mountains between Hellfire Peninsula, Zangarmarsh, and Terokkar.

But even though the texture and flavor were uncomfortable, Jace knew this was the same feeling he'd had the first time he ate proper cheese or authentic Sanguine Isle blood sausage — in other words, a cross-cultural adjustment, not a problem with the food itself. After all, the head chefs here were Wildhammer dwarves. Jace had tremendous faith in their cooking and taste. Of course, that faith didn't come from Grede Thunderfist — it came from that trip to Aerie Peak… and Aerie Peak's finest were all at the Wildhammer fortress now.

What Khadgar had said before, that he'd consider himself lucky to be living alongside the Wildhammer, was absolutely not an empty compliment.

As the banquet wound down, quite a few soldiers who hadn't drunk much the whole time quietly left the hall clutching fruit and meat, reluctant to go. The rest were either passed out drunk or already too far gone to sing straight. Jace knew it was time to head out.

None of them had drunk much, because even the dwarves understood that the ale would taste better after the mission succeeded — so even Grede had stuck to low-strength fruit wine and buttermilk the whole time, and Vereesa even less so.

After feasting their fill, the group got dressed, gathered their gear, and made their way to the wall top under the escort of several Sons of Lothar leaders. Kurdran Wildhammer covered his mouth and coughed. "If we were at Aerie Peak, I could have sent you off with each of you riding a pair of gryphons. But given the state of the Wildhammer fortress right now, I don't even dare send gryphon riders on scouting missions to the east. Maintaining close communication with Honor Hold and Alleria's Watch depends entirely on the gryphon riders' efforts, my tall brothers. So I can only spare the two strongest gryphons to carry you to Nagrand."

"No matter, Lord Kurdran," Sola said. "I can take them to Nagrand. Back on Azeroth, the rest of them are already quite used to traveling on my back."

Hearing Sola say this, the Sons of Lothar all looked mildly surprised, and Khadgar couldn't help but ask, "You're willing to let them ride on your…"

"Ah, we prefer to call it a collaboration, Master Khadgar." Grede cut in immediately. "Because the rest of us haven't sprouted wings like demons, and our legs are clumsy and slow. We need the help of an elegant green dragon lady to cross mountains and seas…"

"Exactly," Vereesa said.

Everyone nodded gravely. Sola smiled, looking satisfied that the dwarf had finally come around a little.

"Well, if you're collaborating like that, I suppose there's nothing I can help with." Kurdran put his hands on his hips and thought for a moment. "Right — I can give you each a pair of demon-detecting goggles. The work of gnomish engineers. Because of the strange magic inside, they'll slightly affect your vision, but in a hellhole like Draenor, what else do you need to see besides the ghostly figures of demons? After all, even orcs have demon blood running through their veins!"

"Bronn, quickly now — take the gryphons back and bring four pairs of goggles!"

"Yes, my lord!"

"Excellent, my lord." Grede rubbed his hands together and looked at Jace. "Now no matter what kind of demon turns invisible and swaggers around in front of me, I'll be able to spot it."

Jace shot him a look. Khadgar watched the two of them and smiled, then pulled a vial of potion from his robes. "Centuries ago, the ogre sorcerer-king established the Ring of Judgment on a nexus of the ley lines along Nagrand's western coast. But after the Great Cataclysm, the ley lines and arcane nexuses across all of Draenor shifted — except for Karabor. That's why our teleport coordinates all ended up misaligned."

He eyed the vial and continued, "After drinking the liquid in this vial, you'll be able to see the locations of the most powerful ley lines and arcane nexuses. If you reach the Ring of Judgment but can't find the nexus's exact location, you can use it to help search. I imagine that eredar sorcerer spends most of his time near the most critical source of arcane power. I'm certain he's not just there to power the portals — these demons' thirst for magic probably surpasses even the quel'dorei."

Both elven rangers simultaneously glanced sidewise at the archmage, and Khadgar quickly added, "Of course, I'm referring to the Magisters' Commission."

He pressed the vial into Jace's hand. "Don't drink it all at once, Sesso. Half a vial's potency lasts a full day. This potion was originally meant to help us find Ner'zhul inside Karabor. In the end, all it did was let me watch the orc warchief disappear into that twisted portal with his cursed scepter. I hope this time it'll truly help you."

"Thank you, Master Khadgar," Jace said.

"I'd hoped our first meeting would be longer, that we'd get to know each other more deeply, Jace." Turalyon stepped forward. "But I think from the choices you've made and the friends you've kept, I already know you well enough — and I understand why an outstanding quel'dorei warrior like Vereesa chose you."

Jace and Vereesa exchanged a glance. The ranger lowered her head shyly, but her hand gripped his all the tighter.

"I don't imagine I can be much help to you on the magical front." The paladin took a small round shield with a red border from a nearby soldier and handed it to Grede. "This shield is called the Defender of Thalas. This mithril shield is enchanted with extremely powerful spells that can restore and sustain the wielder's mental strength. Not only can it help a priest or a knight of the Silver Hand build trust with the Holy Light — it can also help a Thunderfist gryphon rider call down the thunder. A heavy shield isn't suited to a mission like this, but this one's size and enchantment are just right."

Khadgar added, "The first owner of this round shield, Master Thalas, and his twin brother Master Malistar were both battle mages of the Order of Tirisfal at the time. They were famed across the Seven Kingdoms for fighting side by side hunting demons with enchanted swords and enchanted shields."

Turalyon said, "Master Malistar's shield was lost during the Battle of Blackrock Spire. This one has been in my keeping."

"My gods, I can't possibly, Marshal Turalyon — this is your shield," Grede said.

"Honestly, I'm not as skilled with a shield as I'd thought, Lord Grede." Turalyon insisted. "Right now, you need it more than I do."

"Then I'll return it when I come back." Grede took the shield. "It's settled then," Turalyon said.

"You already have the legendary weapon the Dream Wardens entrusted to you, little moon." Alleria spoke with pride and reluctance intertwined. "I don't know what else I could possibly offer."

"Then give me your blessing, sister." Vereesa wrapped her arms around the ranger-captain tightly. "That's enough for me."

"For me, it isn't enough," Alleria murmured, slipping two vials of golden-yellow potion into her sister's hands. "This is a recipe the Auchindoun vindicators taught us. Khadgar refined it to better suit quel'dorei constitutions. I can't remember what the draenei call this potion anymore, but the Farstriders gave it a more fitting name — 'Relentless Death.' It will enhance your strength and focus, making your attacks more precise and more lethal… make sure the demons remember that name well, and carry the painful memories of provoking the expeditionary force on Draenor back to the Twisting Nether to tell their minions and masters."

"I will," Vereesa said.
