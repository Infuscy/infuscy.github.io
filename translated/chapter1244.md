## Chapter 1244: Setting Out for Nagrand

Sola'miel was a green dragon who could fly on the power of the Dream. Other young dragons — even some adults — probably couldn't match her speed.

When she'd followed the gryphons to Shadowmoon Valley before, she'd clearly slowed down to leisurely tag along.

After setting out from the Wildhammer fortress, Sola'miel took less than half a day to bring Jace and the others back to the dead-end remnants of the Terokkar Forest stretching this way. By then Grede was still on her back checking over the traveling rations, arrows, and emergency healing and mana potions the Wildhammer fortress had packed for them.

"They only gave us a few waterskins — that definitely won't be enough to drink." Grede looked up. "I remember a few of the Wildhammer brothers mentioning that the water in some of the Terokkar Forest rivers isn't safe to drink anymore, especially up north. Are they that confident we'll easily find water?"

"Alleria said Nagrand is like a wilderness that evil hasn't touched," Vereesa said. "There should still be plenty of unpolluted rivers and lakes."

Jace knew she was right. In the game, the Nagrand map was crisscrossed with streams and great rivers, and there were several lakes where rivers converged. Considering that the Bone Wastes and Terokkar Forest both turned out to be far vaster than Jace had expected, Nagrand was probably an unimaginably large grassland too. Finding water there shouldn't be hard.

"Let's hope so." Grede lowered his head and rummaged some more, then touched the small shield strapped to his arm. "Marshal Turalyon really thinks of everything. I've wanted a proper shield ever since I left Winterspring. And I mean a shield actually meant for a dwarf, not some misshapen junk made for dragonmen or trolls."

"Why didn't you just buy one back in Stormwind?" Jace asked. "Now I've got one," Grede said.

Jace snorted. Meanwhile Vereesa, sitting to the side, sighed. "Thinking back on how I used to treat Marshal Turalyon, I'm ashamed. But he never seemed to hold my past rudeness against me."

"I remember you always wanting to fight him, just to test yourself," Jace said.

"That was ages ago!" Vereesa stressed, then said, "He's more of a leader than I imagined. I used to think he was just a boring, rigid type who spouted noble principles nonstop."

"I'd say your impression of him wasn't entirely wrong, little elf." Grede tapped the small shield lightly with his warhammer. "But his principles clearly resonate with the soldiers at the Wildhammer fortress. Spouting noble principles in a pretty place like Quel'Thalas naturally sounds hollow. But when tens of thousands of demons are at the gates and he can still use those principles to rally everyone around him — that's a completely different story."

"You're right." Vereesa lowered her head. "When Quel'Thalas burned, I was just an apprentice blinded by hatred. I didn't take part in any of the fighting, and I couldn't begin to understand what they'd all been through together."

"And now you're on the path to saving the entire Sons of Lothar, Vereesa." Jace took her hand. "That's right, Jace," Vereesa said with a smile.

Grede hurriedly pulled on the gnomish goggles and stared at the succubus sitting behind him. Sazeno, leaning against Morophil's back, immediately jumped up with a shriek. "Don't look at me, don't look at me with those, ahhh—"

The imp dove straight into Jace's pack. Morophil heard the commotion and turned around, then saw Grede. "What are you looking at, dwarf? I'm not even invisible yet."

"I just wanted to see if your demon heart was shattered, she-demon," the dwarf snorted.

"If my heart shattered, I'd die and be sent straight back to the Twisting Nether," the succubus said with a smile. "But if a certain dwarf's heart shattered, he'd just weep in the corridors — and claim it was mithril…"

"What?!" Grede bellowed. "Who?! Which dwarf would actually—"

"What, weeping?" Vereesa turned around, shocked.

"WHOA!" the dwarf roared, hard enough that even the green dragon flinched and swayed.

He stumbled up onto the dragon's shoulder and gazed out at a spread of blue twinkling lights ahead. "Beautiful Terokkar Forest — we're finally here!"

"We've been here before," Vereesa said, looking at Jace. "What dwarf? What's going on?" Jace scratched the corner of his mouth. "Uh, I don't know."

"Don't scare people like that, Grede." Sola turned her head to look at the dwarf on her back. "I thought you'd spotted a black dragon."

"Black dragon…" Grede coughed. "I almost wish one would fly out and give us some fun."

"Let's stay away from the Auchindoun wastelands, Sola — head north," Jace said. "We don't yet know how the black dragons there feel about us."

"Understood, Jace." The green dragon responded and slowly beat her wings.

"Back in Auchindoun, you never brought up that Book of Vile Names matter with us," Vereesa said.

Jace looked toward the southwest and explained, "At the time I couldn't care less what the demons were hiding. I only talked with the warlocks in there to buy Morophil time to find where the prisoners were being held. But now it looks like we have to find some way to counter the demons."

"Flying from the Wildhammer fortress to Auchindoun would take days. Even not counting the time Master Khadgar would need to return to Dalaran and consult with the Kirin Tor, just the round trip from Auchindoun alone would take ten days or more. And those ten-odd days… would be enough for the Burning Legion to organize a sizable offensive. That's something I didn't consider at the time."

"I hope that book is as powerful as those warlocks said," Grede said. Vereesa nodded in agreement.

Jace was well aware that this book recorded far more than the demon true names Blac'kat had believed. Just the sheer dark magical energy it contained would be enough to turn a fledgling demon hunter into the nightmare of all Outland.

Now that they'd truly set out on the journey to find this book, Jace had naturally started considering how to use its magic. But he also remembered the fatal weakness that came with this grimoire — when the book was burned, the one wielding its power would be severely weakened, perhaps even stripped back to nothing.

Then again, without this book, he had no confidence in fending off a demon assault without Khadgar. As for its drawbacks — those didn't matter anymore.

Worst case, after absorbing the book's power, he'd have Khadgar take it back and hand it to Amy. Surely Magtheridon wouldn't launch a full-scale invasion of Azeroth just to kill him?

Of course, all of this was speculation based on game lore. As for what power the Book of Vile Names could actually bring, beyond Blac'kat's scattered remarks, Jace had no idea. He'd have to judge when the time came.

Anyway, Jace had no intention of going back into the Shadow Labyrinth to ask around further, because he had a feeling that Blac'kat, an ogre with more than a few tricks up his sleeve, had already sensed something off about him — he just hadn't figured out a good way to deal with him at the time. Having barely escaped, going back now would be walking straight into the trap.

Based on Khadgar's guess, Blac'kat had probably learned about the book from ogres who'd fled the Ring of Judgment. So perhaps the ogres Alleria had mentioned, who were lurking nearby waiting to retake the Ring of Judgment, would know more about it. That was one potential lead.

After entering the Terokkar Forest, without the pervasive fel fires of Shadowmoon Valley, the night grew dark and deep. The blue glow atop the treetops didn't provide much illumination, and looking down from high up, the night dotted with blue specks only looked more eerie.

Grede eyed a dark stream flowing past below and said, "Shall we rest here tonight? I bet there are fish in that river."

"No," Vereesa said. "Let's fly a bit further, Sola. Once we're clear of this dense forest, we'll find a place to rest." The green dragon acknowledged, and the dwarf looked over. "Why?"

"This place is close to a spot called Skettis," Vereesa explained. "That's where the arakkoa hide. My sister specifically warned me not to get tangled up with those arakkoa."

"Then isn't it dangerous for us to fly past here too?" Grede asked.

"The arakkoa can't fly anymore," Jace said. "If they try to hit us with shadow magic, I'll show them exactly what shadow magic is."

Grede jabbed a finger at Jace. "The moment we left the Wildhammer fortress, your true colors showed…"

"…wicked warlock," the two said in unison.
