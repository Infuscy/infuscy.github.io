## Chapter 1245: Where the Light Dwells

In the four or five years since crossing the Dark Portal to Draenor, the expeditionary force had in its early days taken the Hellfire Citadel at top speed, then raced against time to seize Auchindoun through the combined assault of demons, orcs, and black dragons, pushing into Shadowmoon Valley. Along the way they'd established Honor Hold near the Dark Portal in the northeast, the Terokkar Forest in the middle, and the Wildhammer fortress across the southeastern part of the continent.

But before they'd managed to consolidate their gains, the Great Cataclysm of Draenor began — something none of them had foreseen. Then came what everyone knew: the closing of the Dark Portal, the destruction, the shattering, the survival.

To the north of Hellfire Peninsula, beyond Gorgrond, the expeditionary force hadn't conducted deep exploration — only Khadgar's party had set foot there searching for the Skull of Gul'dan. As for Nagrand to the west, beyond the simple surveys carried out by Farstrider squads gathering intelligence on orcs and ogres, they knew virtually nothing about it.

Jace looked at the Draenor map Alleria had given him, the Nagrand section. The parts near Terokkar Forest were depicted in considerable detail, but once you approached central Nagrand, it was almost all guesswork and estimates. It differed from Jace's memory in many places — whether before or after the explosion.

Still, he kept his focus on a village called Talar.

In the game, it was a Broken settlement. From what Magtur and Nitlin had described of their past travels, Talar should have been a small town before the war between the draenei and the orcs. Perhaps they could pick up some intelligence about the demons at the Ring of Judgment there.

Of course, when all of Draenor had just experienced an unprecedented catastrophe and everyone was living day to day, there was no guarantee the residents there would be friendly toward a group of strangers right from the start.

So Jace thought perhaps they should head to Shattrath. It had been over a week since that group of draenei and Broken had set out from Alleria's Watch, and Jace didn't know whether they'd reached Shattrath City.

Jace figured there shouldn't be too many problems. First, that group had mages, warriors, hunters, and seasoned Auchenai priests. Though some were weakened, they weren't the sort that ordinary beasts, Lost Ones, bandits, or roaming demons could handle.

Moreover, by all rights the Terokkar Forest, though desolate and silent at the moment, was still a place the Shadow Council hadn't yet gotten around to causing trouble in the way they did in the game. And Prince Kael'thas's forces weren't conducting magical experiments there either. It should have been a relatively peaceful and tranquil place.

Based on Alleria's description of Shattrath City, most of the orcs who'd taken part in the Siege of Shattrath had probably already fled back to Hellfire Peninsula and Shadowmoon Valley.

If they could re-establish contact with them, they could probably get them to help approach the residents near Talar.

The group spent one night resting in an abandoned orc encampment by a riverbank, then continued flying west the next day. By afternoon, Grede pointed at the forest to the north. "Look… Jace."

Jace followed Grede's gesture and saw, beyond the dark green orei trees and the elegant dark-red plants swaying like dancing ladies, an empty slope. The slope was covered in damaged stumps and felled trunks that hadn't yet been hauled away by the loggers.

As the green dragon continued west, abandoned siege engines scattered across the forest and the logging clearings began to appear. A massive, toppled catapult swept past beneath them, standing among the scattered boulders like a dark mountain troll, its thick launching arm feebly propped up.

"Does that remind you of the siege engines we saw by Mirror Lake?" Grede looked back at the catapult as it disappeared into the shadow of the trees. "Though there's no orcish writing visible on them, I'd stake my hammer that these are all orc work."

"Are we getting close to that 'Shattrath City'?" Sazeno asked in a small voice.

"Quite likely," the dwarf said. "You wouldn't need these big things for a small village. The siege engines in Loch Modan were all built for assaulting Bronzebeard castles."

Before he'd finished speaking, a rapidly approaching orc encampment nearby erupted in a clattering commotion. As the dragon flew overhead, several short, stocky figures in heavy cloaks fled their tents in a panic and bolted north into the forest.

"Broken," Vereesa said, bow raised.

"We can't just fly into Shattrath like this," Jace said. "The expeditionary force's soldiers can barely tell a green dragon from a black one. They'd have even less chance of telling you apart."

"So you're going in for a look?" Grede asked.

"That was the plan. Besides, good luck finding reliable water sources with this map." Jace pointed at the blank stretch in central Nagrand.

"What if there really aren't any?" Grede asked.

"Even if there really aren't, Jace can get us fresh ice water." Vereesa gave him a rather amused look. "Right, Jace? I told Alleria you were a mage trained at the Wizard Sanctum — that's why she didn't object to giving you Song of the Blue Sky."

"Ah… yeah." Jace nodded guiltily. While everyone was looking north, he pulled out his spellbook and flipped to the Conjure Water page to refresh his memory.

Having all decided to check out Shattrath City first, Sola beat her wings hard several times and dove down over the treetops toward the heavily deforested area.

Before long, some utterly destroyed dark walls appeared on the ground, their bare earth exposed. If not for the occasional eredar inscriptions on them, they'd be hard to distinguish from ordinary rock.

Further off in the mist, hardly any tree shadows were visible anymore. Jace could feel a thick current of grief and despair settling out of it.

After flying past a cliff slope, the green dragon landed beside a small thicket. Ahead came a humming like wind, yet they couldn't feel any airflow.

Following a crumbling stone path that appeared and vanished, the group walked north and soon reached a cliff's edge. Jace examined the rock face beneath his feet and realized this wasn't an ordinary stone cliff — it was a broken section of wall.

Peering into the depths of the mist, vague black shapes began to emerge, like a pack of beasts crouching in the fog, or a group of travelers who'd died of cold and hunger, curled up together. But reason told Jace these weren't living things — they were draenei temples and houses burned by orc shadow and flame.

This was Shattrath City. There was no other possibility. Just the stretch of the city facing them, shrouded in mist, was already vast enough to make Grede's eyes widen in disbelief.

Through the mist as it thinned and thickened, everyone could make out more distant, even more indistinct massive temple-spires. That might have been the heart of Shattrath, or merely a larger building in this district. At this moment, none of them could make out the true face and scale of this "City of the Light."

In the game, Shattrath, though bearing the weathered look of a city that had been through war, was home to refugees from all across Draenor. Whether arakkoa, draenei, Broken, or Lost Ones — even orcs, ogres, demons who'd fled the Burning Legion, and members of the expeditionary force — no matter who you were, so long as you abandoned fighting and killing and came here, the naaru and the leader of the draenei, A'dal, would shelter you and let you live on here.

So whether the surrounding slums, the Light's Hope Plaza that gathered all the Shattrathar members, or the elevated terraces where the Astromancers and Aldor priests dwelled, all could be considered quite lively.

But right now, this place… was empty. You couldn't see anything, and there was hardly any sound.

Gazing at the buildings whose dark-blue crystal spires emerged from the mist, Jace even began to wonder whether Levihus and Magtur had made the right choice in trekking all the way here.

Just then, rumbling footsteps came from the path they'd walked up. Vereesa looked back suspiciously, fitting an arrow to her bow as she did.

Before long, a tall black shadow shifted out of the mist. It swayed its trunk, and a pair of tusks as long as longswords lifted high as it raised its head. It seemed to have no fear whatsoever of this group of outsiders made up of hunters and warlocks, stepping closer and closer.
