## Chapter 1246: The Slums

The beast that emerged from the mist was a strangely shaped elephant. Its forehead all the way to its nose was covered in thick, armor-like skin, and above its ears grew a pair of long horns nearly as large as its tusks.

Since the horns grew in the opposite direction from the tusks — one set pointing up, the other down — from a distance it looked like a several-meter-tall armored soldier in plate, slowly approaching while gripping two night elven glaives.

This must be an elekk. Though Jace had only ever seen elekk bones in this world, this beast's appearance was already very close to the one in the game — only far more ferocious.

Vereesa was just about to teach this reckless creature, which had no sense of self-preservation, a lesson, when she spotted a draenei sitting atop the elekk as she raised her bow.

He was a priest in a pale violet robe. Noticing people blocking the road ahead, he sat up and peered forward, then raised his staff and said in fairly practiced Common, "Lower your weapons, Farstrider. Have you forgotten the help this beast gave in building your fortress?"

"Sorry, we mean no harm," Vereesa said, lowering her bow.

Grede looked at the shield-like emblem on the elekk-rider's robe. "Who are you?"

"I am a guardian of the Aldor." The draenei climbed down from the elekk and walked up to the group. "I expect you're lost, followers of the Light."

"What's the Aldor?" Grede asked.

"The Aldor, like the Shattrathar, are the defenders of Shattrath City and servants of the Holy Light," the draenei said. "Most of the Shattrathar vindicators fell defending Auchindoun, so we must shoulder the mission of reviving Shattrath City. We have returned to the City of the Light to serve the Holy Light alongside the draenei gathered here, praying that one day the naaru will descend once more — just as they guided us to safety from the Burning Legion's clutches ten thousand years ago, surviving once again from the demonic fel fire."

"I don't understand," Grede said.

Vereesa frowned. She was probably a bit lost too.

The Shattrathar were the defenders of Shattrath City, the Aldor were something else, and then there were the naaru. For travelers who'd only just arrived from Azeroth, all these eredar terms were genuinely confusing.

But at least Jace knew the Aldor were a group of priests opposed to the Burning Legion's demons. Here on Draenor, so long as that one point of alignment held, they weren't enemies.

"Have a group of draenei and Broken arrived at Shattrath recently to join you?" Jace asked.

"Broken," the draenei elekk-rider said coldly. "You mean the Kurenai? They have been forsaken by the Light. The Aldor cannot accept such unbelievers."

"What? What do you mean, can't accept?" Grede said, displeased. "You cast them out just for not believing in the Holy Light? Leave them to live like savages in this godforsaken forest? What about us — not one of us believes in the Holy Light either. Are you planning to drive us out too? Relying on this tumor-headed boar here?"

"Not accepting the Holy Light doesn't mean you must be our enemies, dwarf." The elekk-rider pointed below. "If you're seeking shelter, you can head to the lower city down there. We've set aside an area there specifically to take in wanderers from all across Draenor. So long as you don't approach the holy temple and disturb our worship, we can coexist in peace."

"Look at what Draenor has become." Even Sola sounded somewhat displeased. "And you still want to sort every surviving life by faith?"

"The naaru have never answered us, Farstrider." The elekk-rider looked at Sola with her golden elven features. "That shows our order is not yet devout enough, our ranks not yet pure enough. Given the present situation, the fact that we're willing to take in any friendly faces at all is already the greatest concession we can make."

"Have you considered the possibility that you might all have a few screws loose?" Grede tapped his own temple with a finger. "If you took in a few more refugees, gave them a bit more food…"

"Most important of all," he said, pounding his chest, "respected them from the bottom of your hearts. Then maybe that naaru of yours would be willing to listen to you. Isn't that what people in your church ought to be doing? Right now, I'd say those bald bastards at the Cathedral of Light have you beat by a mile!"

"Grede." Jace patted the dwarf's shoulder. "Can we enter Shattrath City?"

The elekk-rider shot the dwarf a cold glance. "Of course. You can enter through the entrance ahead. Once inside, walk a short distance to the left and you'll reach the refugee camp. Once you've entered Shattrath, please maintain a grateful heart toward the Holy Light. If there's nothing else, I'll take my leave."

With that, the draenei guardian climbed back onto his elekk and slowly passed in front of the group, vanishing back into the mist.

"You believe I could smash that boar and the boar-rider both to pieces with one swing, Jace?" Grede asked. "I believe it," Jace said.

"I already thought that mage Levihus was insufferably arrogant," Grede said, pointing that way. "Now it seems all draenei probably wear the same face. Cut from the same cloth as those demon warlocks — not a damn bit better!"

Vereesa nodded in agreement.

Jace, for his part, figured that when a people faced annihilation, different groups would react in different ways. That was only natural.

There were those like the Auchenai necromancers, who intended to harness the power of souls to destroy the Burning Legion. There were those like Velen, who led their followers in another mass exodus. And then there were those like the Aldor, who believed the catastrophe had befallen them simply because they weren't devout enough…

If Lordaeron had already fallen, and the surviving crusaders had formed the Scarlet Crusade, occupying several towns across the Lordaeron continent to resist the undead to the death. In the ruins of the Sunwell, Kael'thas led the surviving quel'dorei, calling themselves the "children of blood," using fel energy to ease their magical addiction and making vengeance their people's sole purpose — then Grede and Vereesa might more easily sympathize with the Aldor's fanaticism.

Not to mention that in another timeline, the Aldor truly had summoned the naaru to this city, saving everyone who came here. Only here, they seemed to be running into some difficulties.

"What's a naaru?" Vereesa asked. "Don't know," Grede said.

Jace shook his head too. "Perhaps we'll find out once we meet up with Magtur."

Following the road along the wall south, just as the patrolling knight had said, they quickly reached a gate. A draenei elekk-rider stood guard here as well. Seeing Jace's group, he showed no sign of refusal — he merely said in Eredar, "Welcome to where the Light dwells."

Jace expressed thanks in Eredar, then entered the city gate with the others.

The long passage into the city was dark and damp, lit only by a few flickering braziers. Though Jace could tell some purification solution had been used to scrub away the corruption of dark magic here, he could still catch a faint stench left behind by fel and shadow.

This ominous aura, combined with the fact that the guards hadn't noticed Morophil and Sazeno at all as they mingled among the four, made Jace somewhat concerned about the city's security. Then again, relying on a handful of patrolling soldiers to stop every ill-intended infiltrator from getting into a ruined city wasn't realistic.

Judging from what the patrolling draenei had said, the Aldor probably only cared about security at the "holy temple." As for the lower city — or rather, the refugee camp — they basically left it to its own devices.

After emerging from the passage, what lay before them was row upon row of collapsed dark buildings. The streets had been encroached upon by scattered rubble and ruins, pocked with craters and fisshes left by magic and cannon fire.

Following the patrolling soldier's directions, Jace led the group to the left, through these shattered walls and rubble piles. Before long, a massive dark-red canvas appeared in the mist.

It seemed to be an orcish sail — or maybe a hide the orcs used to drape over the top of a great beast pit as cover. Jace couldn't quite remember. In any case, these cloths were tied firmly to the buildings still standing on both sides of the street, turning the stretch ahead into a giant tent.

Beneath the "tent," small huts and makeshift shelters were packed side by side. As Jace and the others approached, pairs of pale blue eyes slowly emerged from the shadows, peering over curiously.

These were all Broken who'd fled here. Entering this "refuge," Jace could even see quite a few Lost Ones wrapped in rags darting into the gaps between buildings or shelters, too afraid to meet their gaze.

"Do you have crystals, Azerothians?" A hooded and masked Broken approached, speaking in fairly practiced Common. "Draenei crystals, soul shards, even apexis… anything at all. I've got plenty of treasures — things salvaged from the already-destroyed Farhalen, things found in the Sun Temple of Alanka. If you have crystals, I can trade you for any of it."

"We're looking for someone," Jace said. "I want to know if any refugees have arrived here from outside recently?"

"Many." The Broken eyed the demon bag Jace clutched tightly, then pulled down his mask to reveal his fearsome fangs and murmured, "Give me a name…"

"Sesso, you're all here. I didn't expect to see you again so soon." In the distance, a tall shadow stepped out from among the Broken watching them.

Telling draenei faces apart was genuinely not easy for Jace. But Levihus was so absurdly tall, and coupled with the tattered Auchenai priest trousers he was wearing, he was recognizable at a single glance.

"This place isn't that big. Finding someone isn't that hard."

The enormously built Auchenai mage strode up to them with big steps as he said this. The Broken "merchant" paled with fear, backed up two steps, and turned to melt into the crowd.
