Chapter 1247: Another Group of Insiders

Between the tents and the ramshackle hovels, on the damp, cracked streets, there was a stench everywhere like rotting fruit mixed with the foul smell of decaying shrimp and crab.

The diminutive Lost Ones huddled in the dark cracks and beneath the awnings, staring fixedly at every living creature that passed by, like beetles peeking out of a cave. The weakened Broken sat or leaned along both sides of the street, each going about their own business, occasionally murmuring to one another — as if discussing these outsiders who'd come through.

"Why are you in a place like this, Levihus?" Grede couldn't help asking. "I thought you'd be in that holy temple."

"I am not an Aldor priest." Levihus explained as he walked. "They refuse to let me, or any Auchenai member, near that so-called temple."

"So-called temple?" Jace asked. "What exactly is that thing?"

"It's a temple situated on an elevated terrace." Levihus pointed north, where the view was blocked by walls. "Before Shattrath was destroyed, that place was nothing but an obscure little sanctuary. But now the Light's Hope Plaza has been severely damaged and corrupted, beyond restoration… so the Aldor have temporarily adopted that small sanctuary as their chapel."

Vereesa looked around. "You're the only draenei here."

"And my father," Levihus said. "He's resting inside. But you're right. Besides him and me, there are no other draenei here. I mean… draenei who haven't been afflicted by that dreadful disease. I imagine that's what you meant as well."

He looked toward a curled-up Lost One nearby who coughed from time to time. That Lost One looked like a mouse soaked by rain, trembling against the wall.

"Are you two the only ones who escaped from Auchindoun to here?" Vereesa pressed.

"Not just us." Levihus lifted a gray, tattered door-curtain. "But most of the other Auchenai fugitives have left. Many continued north searching for our prophet and leader. The Aldor priests believe we've been corrupted by the evil of Auchindoun and won't allow us near the holy temple. And many draenei feared that staying here among the Broken would lead to infection."

"Infection?" Grede blinked.

As the draenei ducked into the cramped little room, the group saw Scholar Nitlin and Noburu. They were sitting with two Broken hunters who had come to Draenor alongside Jace's group, eating some kind of pastry to stave off hunger.

Outside, the slightly manic whimpering of the Lost Ones wasn't muffled by the walls — it echoed back and forth even more hollowly, setting one's teeth on edge.

"Good afternoon, honored friends." Noburu bowed his head respectfully when he saw them. "Good afternoon, all of you. Where's Magtur?" Jace asked.

"He and one of our hunters went to meet with the refugee camp's leader," Noburu answered.

"I thought you'd all been turned away at Shattrath's gates by those tall eredar," Grede said, relieved. "I couldn't stand by and watch friends we'd gone to the trouble of bringing all the way from Azeroth get shut outside their own doorstep."

"This is no longer our home, Lord Thunderfist," Noburu said.

"What do you mean?" Grede asked. "Has it got something to do with that… infection? They want to quarantine people who've come down with your disease?"

Levihus looked at the dwarf and explained, "The source of this disease is a strange illness called 'Red Pox' in the orcish tongue. The Shadow Council modified this plague with fel magic and loaded it into catapults, hurling it into Shattrath City as a weapon. Every draenei struck by this vicious weapon would undergo physical changes, becoming… like the Broken. Aggravation of the condition from various causes would eventually turn them into those Lost Ones."

"In the period right after the Siege of Shattrath ended, countless draenei were affected by this disease, and those who came into contact with them suffered the same fate. So the notion spread that this disease could be transmitted by contact. But in reality, that was merely residue from the weapon still clinging to clothing, luggage, and skin. The war has been over for many years now, and the residue has long since disappeared. My father and I spent a long time in Auchindoun among countless Broken, and we weren't affected either."

"It's just that many of our kin still don't understand this, or they're simply too terrified of this physical mutation," Noburu said softly. "I understand their fear."

"The rulers of this city — the Aldor — do they also believe this disease is contagious?" Sola asked.

"They know there is no contagion, honored Dream Emissary," Levihus said. "The Aldor priests are Shattrath City's finest scholars. They figured out what was really going on during their flight from Shattrath, which is why they were the first group to dare return to this 'plague city.' Back then, even we Auchenai were terrified."

"The reason they intend to drive us out is that they believe we're interfering with their rituals." Scholar Nitlin looked up. "They don't think these afflicted kin of theirs will infect them, but they do believe the Broken have been cut off from the Holy Light for a reason — and that includes us Auchenai priests who attempted to wield necromantic power in Auchindoun."

The elderly priest continued explaining, "The Aldor returned to Shattrath to summon the naaru, yet they have received no response — from the Holy Light or from the naaru. The Aldor initially took in refugees because they at least still remembered the mercy of the Light. But as things grew more and more difficult, those priests began to feel we were the obstruction between them and the naaru."

"Perhaps the naaru have forgotten us, Father," Levihus said.

"Don't say that, my dearest Levihus." Nitlin shook his head. "We can never know how they will respond to our expectations. Perhaps what we and the Aldor are enduring right now is precisely a trial the Holy Light has set before us — a trial from the naaru. We must keep the Holy Light always in mind, or you will stray further and further from the path."

"I understand, Father," Levihus said. "I sincerely hope the Aldor succeed, only…"

The tall draenei sighed. Grede looked back and forth between the two of them. "So is anyone going to explain to me what a naaru actually is?"

"The naaru…" Nitlin lowered his head. "They are perhaps the purest and most luminous beings in all the dark void. They are emissaries of the Holy Light, and every time their presence appears, it means redemption and hope. They are not creatures of flesh and blood, but beings constituted of the Holy Light. Their existence surpasses my understanding — and the understanding of many Aldor scholars. But that does not diminish our reverence for them, Lord Thunderfist. Many years ago, the naaru led us to escape the Burning Legion's clutches and come to Draenor. So the Aldor believe the naaru will once again lead us through this trial."

Grede stole a glance at Jace. Clearly he still didn't understand, but he didn't press further. Vereesa had been frowning the whole time, as if still trying to imagine just how abstract a naaru's appearance could be.

"Perhaps the naaru still remember you, Scholar Nitlin," Noburu said then. "But they will undoubtedly never favor us cripples again. Even the sage Akama has left Shattrath City. There's no reason for us to place our hopes here either."

Sage Akama — the Broken leader who, in another timeline a decade and a half later, would follow Illidan Stormrage to fight Magtheridon, only to betray Illidan in the end.

Jace remembered that Akama had also been a bishop of considerable standing before the war between the draenei and orcs began. He was probably the highest-ranking draenei in all of Outland to be afflicted into a Broken.

The moment Akama was mentioned, neither draenei knew what to say.

Noburu continued, "If Sage Magtur's discussions with the refugee camp's leader go smoothly, then we will gather all the Broken refugees who've fled here in Shattrath City. When the time is right, when we've gained enough strength, we will head west together into Nagrand to Talar, defeat the ogres occupying it, and take back that old town which has always been ours. If the Aldor and the naaru won't help us, then we will save ourselves."

"At the very least, we will help you when that time comes," Levihus said. "Talar is also the homeland of my father and me, Noburu."

"Then that will be our honor, priest," Noburu said respectfully.

"Talar is occupied by ogres?" Jace asked.

"Yes, Master Sesso," Noburu said. "We only learned this news recently. Those ogres took advantage of the chaos caused by the Great Cataclysm to seize Talar. But sooner or later, we will take it back."

Jace unfolded his map and looked at Talar, still marked as a draenei town. His feelings were complicated. "You're planning to head to Nagrand?" Levihus looked at the group.

"Yes," Vereesa said. "We're planning to head to the Ring of Judgment in western Nagrand, which has been occupied by demons, to carry out a reconnaissance mission."

Jace said, "Originally I thought I'd ask around in Talar for anyone who's been living in Nagrand recently and knows the movements of demons and ogres. I didn't expect Talar to have been occupied by ogres already."

"Talar had already fallen before the demons seized the Ring of Judgment," Levihus looked at the two of them. "I'm afraid none of the draenei who fled from Talar know what's been happening at the Ring of Judgment these past few years. But that doesn't mean there are no 'friendly parties' in Nagrand who understand the situation."

"You can use fire magic to transform into an orc, and you've also mastered the orcish tongue, Jace Sesso," he said. "And in Nagrand there still lives a group of orcs who haven't been controlled by demons, residing in a village named after a dead orcish chieftain. They oppose the demons and despise the ogres. Perhaps you could try talking to them."
