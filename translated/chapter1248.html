Chapter 1248: Nagrand

That night, the Broken helped Jace find an unoccupied room nearby where they could rest. It was clearly a place orcs had once lived in — the ground was still littered with rotting clefthoof hides that nobody had bothered to clear away.

The smell was bad, but who knew whether, once you got those things out, whatever mysterious things they were hiding underneath might give off an even stranger stench you couldn't bear. So they just left it as it was.

In the damp, dark night, hoarse crying and shouting drifted in from outside from time to time. Shadows of unknown origin occasionally flickered through the cracks in the walls.

In the distance, the hollow sound of draenei chanting prayers came and went. Even Jace, who was thoroughly fluent in Eredar, couldn't make out what exactly they were singing.

Though he knew they were praying to the Holy Light, beseeching the naaru's descent, this chanting in the mist-shrouded ruins of Shattrath sounded endlessly desolate, even carrying an indescribable air of despair.

Jace wasn't the only one who felt this way — he could read the emotion out of the shadow itself. Morophil, who normally couldn't stand being anywhere near the Cathedral of Light, was utterly unfazed by this chanting. It was Grede and Sola lying beside her who couldn't get a wink of sleep.

Vereesa lay there wide-eyed, staring at those wall cracks as if ready to put an arrow through the next shadow that emerged. "Come to think of it, are there really orcs in this world who haven't been controlled by demons?" Grede muttered.

"Vereesa and I ran into a band of them in Alterac Valley," Vereesa blinked and looked over. "Meeting them completely overturned everything I thought I knew about the orc race. I wouldn't even dare tell Alleria about it."

"Best not to," Grede said quietly.

"There's a group of orcs sheltering from the plague in Nagrand. They never drank demon blood, and they never joined the Dark Portal expedition," Jace said. "They were abandoned by the Horde and ended up escaping calamity after calamity instead."

In the game, these orcs called themselves the Mag'har — meaning "uncorrupted" in orcish. But Jace hadn't yet heard any orc use that term to describe these forgotten people, nor had he heard it from any of Draenor's natives. The expeditionary force's Nagrand map only marked a few places where orcs had been sighted and possible points of settlement, with no mention of how they differed from other orcs.

As for the village named after an orcish chieftain, Jace suspected it might refer to the Mag'har orc village in the game — Garadar, named after the old Frostwolf chieftain Galard, Durotan's father.

"Doesn't never having drunk demon blood mean their skin isn't green?" Vereesa asked.

"Very likely." Jace stared at the broken wall, thinking. "So even if I transform into an orc, I might not win their trust right away. Vaelastrasz's magic doesn't give me a choice of skin color."

"Then I can't go with you, master?" Morophil said mournfully.

"Of course you can't." Vereesa immediately fixed her gaze on the demon. "Didn't you hear what Levihus said? Those orcs despise demons. You going along this time would only cause more trouble."

The succubus shot the ranger a resentful look. Sola shook her head. "My orcish is still far from fluent. Otherwise I might have been able to keep an eye on things for Jace."

"It's fine, Sola. I'll go myself," Jace said. "This time is safer than infiltrating the heart of the Shadow Council, at least."

"Since you put it that way." Grede adjusted his beard. "Then I'll take it as you already knowing what you're doing. That puts my mind at ease, mm."

With that, the dwarf flopped onto his cloak and was soon snoring away.

"Get a good night's sleep, Jace. I'll keep watch tonight," Vereesa said. "You too, Sola."

"I'll leave it to you, Vereesa." Sola lay down on the ground and, like a playing-dead viper, tilted her body to one side.

After lying down, for some reason, Grede's snoring in the silent, uneasy Shattrath City actually became something that settled the mind.

Jace, who'd been unable to fall asleep and had been about to ask Morophil for a sleep spell, had unconsciously drifted off to the sound.

When he opened his eyes again, the sky was already growing light.

Sola and Grede were still asleep. Morophil glanced beyond the door-curtain — meaning Vereesa was outside. Jace lifted the curtain, and the ranger turned to look at him.

"Morning, Jace."

"Morning, Vereesa."

He raised his head. The mist seemed to have thinned slightly compared to last evening. Looking up and into the distance, he could make out the silhouettes of more of Shatthath's tall buildings. The walls, severely corroded by dark magic and blackened and yellowed by fire, and the massive blue crystals that had lost all their power entirely, looked decrepit in the thin mist.

Looking at this scene, Jace was suddenly reminded of many things from his past life.

In all of the Burning Crusade version of Outland, there was only one main city — Shattrath City.

In the level-70 version, every time you finished a dungeon, every time you completed a quest, exchanged gear, enchanted items, you came back here.

Not to mention that in that version, only Shattrath had portals to every major city and the Sunwell — far more convenient than Ironforge or Stormwind in that regard.

Add in those few years before the level-80 expansion finally opened…

Gazing at the misty, tranquil ruins of Shattrath, with the pinpoints of light from Terokkar Forest in the distance, it was only at this moment that Jace realized just how deeply this city had imprinted on him. Back then, the Aldor, the Shattrathar, the slums, the naaru, the Broken — those names had stood for entirely different things.

Seemingly sensing something, Vereesa asked curiously, "Does this city look very different in a warlock's eyes?"

"A little different," Jace said.

"I'd really like to see what this world looks like through your eyes — what I look like," the ranger said softly. Right after, she quickly added, "Of course, I'd never learn dark magic just for that."

Both of them laughed. Jace let out a long breath. "I'll go wake them up. Time to head out."

"All right," Vereesa said.

They couldn't linger in Shattrath any longer. After all, every extra moment of delay meant more of the expeditionary force's soldiers might be injured or killed in clashes with the demons.

The expeditionary force longed to return to Azeroth as soon as possible. Jace's group was even more anxious than the expedition force to bring them back. Were it not for that, he'd have liked to hole up at the Wildhammer fortress a few extra days and properly eat his fill of all the Draenor-style dishes and drinks before setting out.

But Jace also knew that after sending Khadgar back to Azeror, during the time they spent waiting for news on this side, he'd have plenty of opportunities to taste all the various specialty foods the expeditionary force had developed on Draenor.

There were quite a few ogres around the border between Terokkar Forest and Nagrand, so Jace found a Broken refugee in the slums who frequently ventured out into Terokkar Forest to forage for food, and had him mark several ogre villages in the mountains near Shattrath on the expeditionary force's map.

Leaving the slums and climbing over the collapsed wall into the forest, the group mounted the green dragon's back and continued flying west.

After crossing the stretch of towering mountains behind Shatthath City, Sola flew as high and as fast as she could to avoid being spotted by any ogres that might appear, breaking free of the cover of the dense, dark Terokkar Forest.

Gradually, the dark forest below became obscured by mist. The air around them seemed to grow clearer and clearer, the sky brighter and brighter, the sunlight a bit blinding.

Just as they were discussing whether they might have been spotted by black dragons or ogre hunters, Sola flew over a towering mountaintop. Grede, facing forward, suddenly froze.

"By my ancestors in Khaz Modan…"

Jace and Vereesa turned to look ahead too, and neither of them could find words.

Before them, a sea of emerald-green grassland rippled with the wind, stretching all the way to the farthest reaches of sight.

And there, at the edge of vision, white clouds rolled on like mountains, and the vast sky was so deep it was suffocating… it even made the void-shadows that tore through the world beyond look like pale pink sunset clouds.

Jace stared at a floating island drifting high in the sky, along with a lone tree upon it, and murmured unconsciously, "We've arrived. Nagrand."
