Chapter 1249: The Lonely Wilderness

Although in the game Shattrath City sat right on the border between Terokkar and Nagrand, the suddenness of stepping from that gloomy, misty forest into this vast expanse still caught Jace off guard. It really did feel like crossing a zone boundary in a game — and it brought that same surreal awe.

The last time Jace had felt this way was climbing from the barren sands of the Thousand Needles into the quillboar nests, passing through and suddenly seeing the great grasslands of the Barrens.

Looking back, the forest they'd left behind stood atop a towering cliff, which stretched on and on to a point beyond sight — like a high wall separating Terokkar from Nagrand. Perhaps it was this very wall that completely blocked the dark malice coming from Shadowmoon Valley, protecting this last patch of surviving Draenor and making the two regions look so strikingly different.

Only the Farhalen Plains, Gorgrond, and Shadowmoon Valley weren't any closer to Nagrand, and the mountains and obstacles between them were just as varied. Yet one had been torn apart by the void, and the other had collided and twisted against the towering Frostfire Peaks — while Nagrand had almost entirely preserved its original appearance.

It might have been the power of the elements, or perhaps luck. Some even speculated it might have been the peculiar Red Pox disease, originally from the Ring of Judgment, exerting some indescribable influence on this land. In any case, Nagrand had barely managed to survive.

Nagrand hadn't been entirely untouched. The floating islands drifting high above, the ground-shaking booms that rang out now and then across the empty wilderness for no apparent reason — all of it meant this seemingly vibrant world was slowly decaying, just like everywhere else on Draenor.

You could tell from Sola'miel's reaction too. She just flew ahead indifferently, without uttering a single word of wonder. Remember, this was a little dragon who, back in Westfall and Elwynn Forest, would dive-bomb the sheep and cows on farms just to spook them. Faced with a landscape that left the other three gasping in astonishment, she was silent.

It was probably because, after growing accustomed to the breathtaking natural splendor of the Emerald Dream, Sola was more curious about settlements built by mortal hands. Nagrand couldn't offer her much to be amazed by.

After that initial shock, Jace gradually realized a more practical problem.

The Terokkar Forest had completely vanished from view. Sola'miel flew as high as she could to avoid being glimpsed by ogres or orcs on the great plain. But looking around from such a height, all Jace could see besides more distant plains, hills, and sparse green woodland was… nothing he recognized.

Demon-worshippers' fortresses, ogre dwellings, surviving orc tribal villages — or anything at all that could serve as a landmark. Nothing was visible.

While Vereesa and Grede marveled at the strange sourceless water dripping down from the heights of the floating islands, and the forests that had toppled into crevices like a shark's rotating teeth, Jace kept comparing the map in his hands to the landscape before his eyes.

But in truth, nothing matched up at all.

Nagrand's most distinctive landmark wasn't actually any structure built by the local natives, but rather the mountain Oshu'gun.

Oshu'gun wasn't its real name — that was just what the orcs called it, meaning "Mountain of Souls." It was the crashed naaru vessel Genedar that Alleria had mentioned before. Centuries ago, the draenei had relied on it to flee here from the dark void. The reason the orcs considered it a dwelling place of souls was that the energy released by the dead naaru inside drew the souls of orcs and draenei alike, unsettling even the shamans.

The Genedar, even larger than Auchindoun, had crashed onto an impossibly vast plain. From an extremely far distance, one should have been able to see this "Crystal Mountain," providing guidance to travelers passing nearby.

But even Oshu'gun couldn't be seen at all.

Then Jace remembered a casual remark Khadgar had made before they set out — he'd said the expeditionary force had already held out on Draenor for years; they could afford to take another month or two… Khadgar knew full well how fast a dragon could fly. Relying on Sola's speed, flying from the Wildhammer fortress to Shattrath and crossing all of Terokkar had taken less than three or four days. Had the archmage already anticipated they'd be traveling Nagrand for weeks or even months?

Based on the map's markings of orc sighting locations, Jace judged they should fly northwest — which was also the correct heading for Garadar according to the game map.

He looked up to the northwest… Wait. After searching back and forth for Oshu'gun, the Mountain of Souls, with dusk approaching, looking up at the pale-purple rivers of void-channels interweaving with the shadows of unknown planets in the sky, Jace was getting disoriented.

"Are we flying west right now, Sola?"

"Yes, Jace. Roughly west."

Though the green dragon answered this way, the sun hadn't set in the direction they were facing. Jace hadn't even noticed when the sunlight tearing through Outland had vanished into the void sky. Clearly, the goddess Rukhmar didn't exactly favor an outsider like him.

"Lost your bearings, Jace?" Vereesa reached out a hand. "Or give the map to a professional."

Jace handed the map to the ranger, then pointed to where he roughly judged Garadar's location to be. "We need to head northwest, Vereesa. Looking for the orc village."

"All right. We can find a river first, then follow it." The ranger pointed ahead to the right. "Even on Azeroth, those orcs built their camps and fortresses along rivers. I imagine they'd have the same habit here. No different from us."

"No different from you humans and elves," Grede stressed.

"Underground rivers are rivers too," Vereesa said to the dwarf.

Nightfall in Nagrand came quietly. The void-shadow power painting the sky pale purple didn't make the place pitch-black like the forests of Azeroth. There was still plenty of visibility on the wilderness, enough to see very far.

Sola'miel, who had been flying at full speed the whole way watching for black dragons and ogre movements, was very tired by now. After discussing it with her companions, she landed on a clay cliff, startling a flock of unnamed critters — something like wildcats — that came scurrying out of the brush and bolted into distant ground crevices.

"I just saw some antelope-like beasts hiding under the trees." Grede hopped off the dragon's back and pointed east. "Right over there. I'm going to go have a look. I've been eating rations for two days straight and my mouth's about to crack open. I need something greasy to lubricate the works."

"You sure that's not just your hunger playing tricks on you?" Jace asked as he secured his staff.

"I think I noticed them too, Jace." Vereesa shouldered her bow. "Shall we go take a look together? If we're lucky and run into local hunters here, you could talk to them whether they speak Eredar or orcish, while the two of us couldn't do more than say hello."

"Someone needs to stay behind to watch Sola and the gear."

As he said this, Jace glanced back at the green dragon, who had already flopped onto the ground with her head tilted, dead asleep.

She hadn't even had the energy to shift back into her elven form. Her long purplish-red tongue had flopped out and lay sprawled on the dirt. The faint trace of pale-yellow venom seeping unintentionally from her saliva had already burned a large patch of innocent grass half to death.

"Guardians of the forest, huh. Tsk tsk." Grede looked at the pitiful plants and sighed. "Morophil, this task falls to you."

"Me?" The succubus pointed at herself. "Don't forget, when we went out together before, who was the one who helped you hunt…"

"Then this time I'll go hunt to make it up to you," Jace said.

"The master really does have a way with words," Morophil said sourly, sitting to the side.

"Protect Sola. If anything happens, remember to call my name, Sazeno," Jace said, looking at the imp sitting beside the succubus.

"Got it, my great master!" The imp hopped up. "Who's protecting who with this lady dragon, though — that's still up in the air!"

Morophil snorted dismissively. Jace exhaled, then gazed out from this slightly higher vantage point into the distance. In the darkness, there was no sign of anything moving at all.

Back in his past life, playing the game, he'd been quite curious about exactly how the Mag'har orcs had managed to live peacefully in this chaotic Outland for twenty or thirty years, all the way until the level-70 version when a second wave of expeditionary forces arrived from Azeroth.

Now he was starting to get a sense of it. Nagrand was simply too vast — far larger than in the game, and far vaster than the rough map in his hands indicated. Neither the Burning Legion nor the Illidari could be bothered to plunge deep into this super-sized wilderness, where even orcs rarely set foot, trekking all that way just to conquer a few thousand harmless old, weak, sick, and disabled folk.

Living in a place like this was isolation in the most literal sense.

Grede put his hands on his hips. "There's no river in sight anywhere around here, Jace. Isn't it time for you to step up? Get us and Sola some water to drink. I'm not sure how much water a dragon her size needs to quench her thirst, but I figure a little is better than none."

Jace grunted an acknowledgment and drew Song of the Blue Sky. "Vald, Tiqa, Kaltyr."

This was his first time casting Conjure Water on Draenor. After raising Song of the Blue Sky, he did feel arcane energy gathering toward him — only to be shattered midway by some twisting force.

Jace was already psychologically prepared for Draenor's chaotic ley-line power, so this didn't break his concentration. Even so, the water elementals coalescing in the empty bowl at the sword's tip didn't gather into a standing pillar of ice the way they did on Azeroth. Instead, they scattered into a mass of ice flowers fluttering everywhere, splattering all over the ground.

Grede stuffed one of the larger ice chunks into his mouth, chewing as he asked, "Why does your Conjure Water look like it's gotten worse?"

"It has nothing to do with skill in the incantation. The arcane summoning's been disrupted." Vereesa frowned. "No wonder Master Khadgar doesn't dare use any teleportation spells anymore. In a place like this, even casting Blink carries enormous risk."

In truth, this was already far better than Jace had expected. After all, the fact that this Conjure Water hadn't erupted into a raging, out-of-control inferno in Draenor's wildly chaotic magical energy was already a blessing — and that was thanks to the arcane power within Song of the Blue Sky itself.

He suddenly wondered whether the fire elements here would still be willing to listen to his voice.

Since arriving on Draenor, Jace hadn't heard a single fire element communicate with him willingly. In the Terokkar Forest and at the Wildhammer fortress at the mouth of Shadowmoon Valley, the omnipresent roar of shadow had already crowded into his awareness.

He tried pinching his fingers and softly speaking the language of the elements. A small flame danced naturally up from his fingertips. The flame's dance was slow and feeble, full of curiosity but carrying a trace of unease…

Jace didn't know how the elemental lords upon the Elemental Thrones viewed him as an outsider, but judging from this little flame's behavior, they were at least willing to try building trust first.
