## Chapter 1250: Nothing But Hills

Among the draenei who defended Shattrath City, there was a high-ranking vindicator named Nobuuto. After being infected by the Red Pox — modified by orcish warlocks — he too became a wretched Broken. He had once been one of Shattrath's mightiest paladins, a steadfast believer wielding a crystal warhammer. But after becoming a Broken, he could no longer commune with the Holy Light at all.

Cut off from the Light, Nobuuto had tried repeatedly to make contact with the Aldor in Shattrath, or the draenei who followed Velen, hoping to be accepted — but in the end he was rejected every time.

After all, even Akama, a bishop second only in standing to the Prophet Velen himself, had been cast out. Let alone a mere vindicator like him.

After leaving Shattrath, Nobuuto came to the highest point of Nagrand. There he received the elements' care and began learning to become a shaman. Akama had been at his side then. Countless Broken walked the path of the shaman in this way, and in the days that followed they brought the teachings of shamanism and the elements back among the desperate draenene.

The elements of Nagrand, having endured countless demons, shadows, and disasters, held considerable tolerance and goodwill toward all who wished to fight against the demons. Perhaps that was why, even though Jace couldn't hear their voices, they still accepted him.

Here, with no view of Oshu'gun and no view of the Elemental Throne, no matter how hard Jace peered north, it was nothing but empty slopes.

He squinted as he scanned, following the other two along the cliff edge of the high slope to the east. After leaping across a split ground crevice, they walked into a cluster of earthen mounds rising one after another.

"I feel like we're close to some kind of beast," Vereesa sniffed the air, looking around. "The 'wild goats' we saw earlier may be hiding right here."

"Oh, so you already know what the animals here smell like?" Grede asked.

"Even if I've never been here, I've eaten the meat of several of Lord Kurdran's local animals already," Vereesa said.

"Fair point. But I'm already about to forget what those meats tasted like. There was one — some kind of goat — called a taba-something…" Grede muttered as he walked ahead. He gazed for a moment at the patches of tree shadows cast by the sky's arcane glow in the lowlands in the distance, then said, "Mmm, truly beautiful. You know, I finally understand why those defeated orcs were always muttering about Nagrand, Nagrand."

"But if there's a place this wonderful, why go to our lands at all?" he turned back and asked. "This place could easily hold thirty thousand, even three hundred thousand orcs of all sizes, and every single one could build a house for their kin and children. And this is what it looks like after the whole world has been torn apart… god alone knew what this place was like a decade and a half ago! I even think Masha should pack up her tauren relatives and leave that centaur-plagued land to settle here."

"Don't you remember what Antonidas said?" Vereesa shook her head. "The orcs drank demon blood, which stirred the thirst for bloodlust within them. They wanted to conquer other races, starting with the draenei, then us. If they defeated us, they'd move on to other worlds. No particularly convincing reason needed."

"Well, I never drank demon blood…" Grede had barely gotten the words out when a massive earthen mound nearby suddenly shifted. The earth and rock beneath their feet shook violently, and both Jace and Vereesa nearly got knocked off their feet.

"Damn it, was that an earthquake?"

The dwarf had just turned his head when the "mound" suddenly tore itself from the ground, dragging along a mass of dark-brown "tree roots" and bearing down toward him. Grede barely managed an "Ahh" before he was crushed entirely beneath it, vanishing without a sound.

"Grede!"

Jace shouted and drew Song of the Blue Sky. Behind the "mound," an enormous bestial face suddenly swung around and fixed its gaze squarely on him.

Its dark-brown face was covered in thick armor-hide bristling with coarse hair. From its snout rose a black horn the size of a dwarf pine. When it opened its massive mouth, its blunt, thick teeth were as irregularly spaced as a battlement!

Clefthoof!

Seemingly startled by the blue glow emanating from the demonic blade, the giant beast roared angrily in Jace and Vereesa's direction. The stench of its breath blasted Jace backward several paces in one go, slamming him down onto another mound.

That mound started shifting too. All the mounds began swaying and "standing" up. Some large, some small, they either rumbled in alarm and slowly shuffled away, or turned their heads trying to locate Jace and Vereesa.

Jace looked toward Grede and saw the dwarf shouting, struggling to free himself from the mud he'd sunk into, craning his neck to see through the dust kicked up by the heavy hooves of the many "mounds" — trying to spot the danger that might crush him again at any moment.

Seeing that Grede hadn't been trampled to death outright, Jace finally breathed again.

But he didn't have much time to catch his breath. The scene was chaos — the ground splitting open, rubble and earth heaving and collapsing in tangles.

Through the billowing dust, a massive mound of a grey-brown hue had already locked its blank, small eyes — burning with intense fury — onto him. Its front hooves were poised to strike, ready to launch an unstoppable charge at any moment.

This clefthoof before him was far larger than the thunder lizards and kodo beasts they'd encountered in Kalimdor — bigger even than the steam tanks at the Wildhammer fortress. This must be the leader of the clefthoof herd.

Herbivore though it was, that didn't mean it had no temper or couldn't kill. Especially as the guardian of the herd, when faced with outsiders it had to demonstrate overwhelming momentum and force to prove its standing within the group. And Jace had clearly become the object against which it would prove its strength…

Watching this clefthoof bull lower its head and aim its horns straight at them, bellowing and charging furiously — each stomp of its hooves, black as stone pillars, sending violent tremors through the earth — Jace had no choice but to throw himself into Shadowstep to dodge its path. The clefthoof bull charged savagely forward for several dozen meters, toppling several trees and smashing through a giant boulder like blocks and toys before it finally ground to a halt.

Just as it was laboriously turning around to launch a second assault at the three of them, an unremarkable glimmer of light suddenly slipped into its forehead. The clefthoof bull, still brimming with savage fury moments before, calmed at once. The shadow energy that had been gathering around its extreme emotions also suddenly lost its foothold and dissipated into the air.

It stood there dazed for a long while, before finally its legs gave out under its enormous body and it collapsed back to the ground. The violent shaking rumbled on for quite some time, and the dust it kicked up blanketed a large swath of grassland.

Vereesa, standing to the side, slowly lowered her bow. The second dream arrow on the string faded away as well.

"Isn't this the clefthoof the expeditionary force mentioned? I can see now why those orcish hunters aren't as skilled at tracking and tracing as the Amani trolls," Vereesa said. "A hunter's job here is probably closer to…"

"Fighting giants." Grede walked over to finish the thought, patting the dried dung and hay from the top of his head. "They'd probably have more in common with the giant-hunters in Ironforge. In any case, tonight's dinner is settled."

He drew the short blade at his waist and walked toward the dead clefthoof bull. The descendant of the earthen didn't seem to have been hurt much by that stomp.

Some of the clefthoofs, large and small, in the distance were still fleeing without a second thought, while others were hesitating and looking this way, unsure whether they might charge over at any moment to avenge their old patriarch.

"Did you see that, Jace?" Vereesa pointed at the back of the dead clefthoof bull. "What?" Jace turned his head.

"Those two pairs of tooth marks," Vereesa said.

Jace narrowed his eyes and looked carefully. Under the arcane light spilling from the sky, the back of this enormous clefthoof did indeed bear four large, round, hairless scars at opposing angles — blurred and indistinct.

"Something bit those?" Vereesa said. "If it was some kind of predatory beast… wouldn't its head be a little too big?"

Jace couldn't be sure, but it reminded him of a true giant that walked Nagrand — the gronn, the offspring of the "King of the Blade's Edge Mountains" Inciter Gruul that Blac'kat had mentioned.

"Hey!" Grede shouted over. "You two — Song of the Blue Sky, Dragon's Call, whatever you've got, lend me one. This bastard's hide… hell, even a young dragon's teeth can't cut through it."
