## Chapter 1252: The Younger Generation

The stranger in the black cloak walked out of the woods, followed a dirt path between wide stretches of farmland, and came onto a road churned with warg tracks.

He pressed on along the road, past one farmstead after another as he climbed higher, and bit by bit the sky began to brighten.

The buildings here didn't look like the orcish caves or strongholds one normally saw in Azeroth — they were more like miniature ogre dwellings. Quite a few orcs glanced his way along the road, but none with much wariness or curiosity. Several, in fact, ducked and hid the moment they noticed this dark-green-skinned orc appear — into their homes, or beneath an overhanging ledge.

Jace came to a wooden bridge spanning a narrow gorge and looked north. Spread out below the morning light was a vast lake shimmering with ripples — that had to be Lake Elrendar, beneath the Throne of the Elements. Which meant this village really was Garadar.

But the lake was so enormous here he couldn't make out the far shore. All he could see was a hazy line of pale-purple bluffs standing on the opposite bank, and beyond them the looming silhouettes of still loftier mountain ranges. There was no telling where, exactly, the Throne of the Elements lay.

None of that mattered to him right now. Jace glanced back toward where Vereesa and the others were hidden, confirmed there was nothing amiss, and continued across the bridge.

He'd barely reached the far side when two orc hunters appeared ahead, clad in yellow leather jerkins, carrying spears and bows with long knives belted at their waists.

They were both brown-skinned Mag'har — "uncorrupted" orcs who had never drunk demon blood. Compared to the green-skinned orcs they looked leaner, and their skin was covered in lesions and scars that gave them a somewhat haggard cast.

The two regarded Jace with eyes that held a cold edge of unease, but neither said a word. They lowered their heads and hurried past — no attempt at stopping him, no greeting either.

At first Jace hadn't been quite sure how the orcs here felt about green-skinned orcs. From the emotions these two hunters had shown, at least he could tell the Mag'har still considered the blood-drinkers their kin, and didn't regard them with excessive suspicion. That was somewhat better than he'd expected.

Beyond the suspension bridge he could see, high up in the village, a tent that was crude but impressively large. That would be where the village's leaders were.

By now full morning had arrived, and more and more orcs moved along the village paths. Many of them noticed Jace, but none approached to greet him. Some had been chatting amongst themselves, and the moment they caught sight of him they broke off their conversation and quickly moved out of his line of sight.

Jace was climbing higher, thinking over how the villagers' attitudes toward him might shape the best way to approach them, when a sharp voice rang out ahead.

"Stop, warlock."

Jace looked up the path. A strapping young orc was staring right at him.

"Out of the way, youngster," Jace said.

"You're not that much older than me." The orc studied him and asked, in oddly accented Orcish, "What are you doing here? The war is over."

Jace adjusted to the accent and used the moment to size the fellow up.

He had a shaved head with a topknot not unlike Kurdran Wildhammer's, and he was tall and powerfully built — not much shorter than the tall, elite cult warriors who served Vorpil back in Auchindoun.

But those orcs had been doubly amplified by demon blood and shadow, then hand-picked as elite warriors. This one was a pure, uncorrupted orc.

"I need to speak with your leader. Are you the leader here?" Jace asked.

The orc narrowed his eyes. "Whatever you have to say, you can say it to me. I'm Garrosh."

"You're… Garrosh?" Jace's eyes widened in genuine surprise.

Yes — the Garrosh who would one day succeed Thrall as Warchief of the Horde, son of Warsong chieftain Grommash Hellscream, lived here in Garadar. Years later he would transform Orgrimmar from a rough orcish settlement into a steel fortress, leading countless fanatical followers of the "True Horde" into fierce conflict with the Alliance…

Jace remembered he was the same generation as Thrall, which meant he should still be a teenager at this point. So Jace hadn't expected to cross paths with him at all — much like Arthas back in Lordaeron.

But he'd overlooked how early orcs matured. At six or seven they could already tower over a grown human, and by their teens they were already old enough to take part in the Horde's major decisions.

"I'm Garrosh. So you know my name." The young orc lifted his chin slightly, but uncertainty spread quickly through his bearing.

His Orcish was halting and clumsy — no more fluent than Jace's. But this really was Garrosh, and he was still a long way from being Warchief, so Jace adjusted his mindset almost instantly.

"I am Yuruk, one of the lieutenants and advisors to Shattered Hand chieftain Kargath Bladefist, ruler of Hellfire Citadel. You should know that Ner'zhul has betrayed us, and Chieftain Kargath will soon become Warchief of the entire Horde."

"Surviving the plague doesn't mean we've gone deaf and blind, warlock," Garrosh said. "But Chieftain Kargath has already come here. I don't understand why he's sent you."

"His earlier visit didn't do enough to humiliate us Mag'har, and sending a warlock like you is just asking for more of the same. I'll say it again — we won't join your fight, whether it's against the Alliance or against the black dragons. I won't agree, and Greatmother Geyah will feel the same. You already have an endless supply of demons as your 'helpers.' Isn't that exactly what you wanted?"

When he said "helpers," Garrosh's tone turned mocking, and he cocked his mouth in a smirk. Jace knew he was implying that every orc besides the Mag'har had essentially become a demon's slave — the kind of jab that most easily stung an outsider orc's pride.

"I'm not familiar with the details of Chieftain Kargath's visit," Jace said with an unconcerned honesty. "I only recently returned from the other side of the Dark Portal, Garrosh. I'm not here to pick a fight. The reason I've come is to sincerely ask for help."

"You came back from Azeroth?" Garrosh frowned. "Yes," Jace said.

"Do you know how my father is?" the young orc asked.

"I'd like to speak with Greatmother Geyah as soon as possible — if she's the leader here and you're not," Jace said.

At the word "leader," a surge of fury roared to Garrosh's head and he seized Jace by the collar. Even with the magic of Vaelastrasz and the dragon blood flowing through his veins, Jace had the strength to overpower many blood-drinking orcs — but he was no match for this young giant.

"Who the hell do you think you are, telling me what to do?" Garrosh growled.

"Let go." Jace gripped his arm and warned in a low voice, "I'm not here to start trouble, Garrosh. But you can't help Chieftain Kargath, and he won't care how many of you die or how many are left."

Garrosh's yellowish little eyes flicked to the side. Finally he let out several sharp breaths, spat to one side, and reluctantly released him.

Jace knew the orcs of Garadar had never drunk demon blood and had never fought in the Horde's wars, but many of them knew full well what a high-ranking warlock could do. And even those who didn't had been given a vivid demonstration by the catastrophic destruction of Draenor.

He clenched his fist, then looked at the staff in Jace's hand. "Grandmother hasn't been well lately. Even if I let you past, the people up ahead won't allow you to see her. Whatever you have to say, say it to me. I'll see if there's anything I can help with — as long as you don't harm the villagers here. That wouldn't do you any good either."

"From the last I heard," Jace said, "Chieftain Grommash is fighting the Alliance in a forest no less vast than Nagrand, and the Alliance's king doesn't seem able to do much about him."

Garrosh was taken aback for a moment. "He's alive?"

"Clearly. He can run and jump," Jace said. "Gul'dan is dead. I don't think anyone's going to turn the Warsong chieftain into a death knight now."

"I see. That will do." The orc nodded.
