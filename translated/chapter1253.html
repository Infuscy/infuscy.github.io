Chapter 1253: Serve the Demons, or Stand with Your People

Nagrand's sunlight streamed through a narrow square window into the round hut. Dust motes and bits of dry grass drifted through the cramped space. Garrosh Hellscream sat on a round boulder — when Jace had first walked in, he'd mistaken it for a warg post.

The young orc tidied some odds and ends beside him and muttered something under his breath. Jace said, "What was that?"

Garrosh looked up. "Ah, you can't understand the plains dialect. What clan are you from? I mean before you joined the Shadow Council."

"Shadowmoon clan," Jace said.

"That figures," Garrosh said. "But the way you talk, you sound like a Blackrock. Not just your speech — the way you walk, too."

"I've spent the last few years under Warchief Blackhand, actually had little contact with Gul'dan." Jace sat on a stone bench across from him, adjusted his cloak, leaned back against the wall, and caught his breath. "After that I stayed with them, serving his two sons in a place called the Burning Steppes. That's where I got fluent in Horde Orcish."

"But the Blackhand brothers killed many Shadow Council members. After Gul'dan's betrayal they stopped trusting warlocks." Garrosh said. "They were willing to let you off?"

Jace glanced sideways. "You know a great deal about the other side of the Dark Portal, Garrosh Hellscream."

"Plenty of news reaches here, reaches the village," Garrosh said. "I have to know as much of it as I can, Yuruk. It's vital for our little tribe's survival. We lack soldiers, we lack seasoned veterans, we even lack hunters. If we also lose touch with the outside world, we'll be finished in no time."

Jace nodded slowly. "What you've heard is accurate enough. The chieftains on the other side trust warlocks less and less, and right as Teron Gorefiend delivered Ner'zhul's orders for us to return and assist with his strategic withdrawal, I managed to make it back through the Dark Portal — but I could never reach Shadowmoon Valley. Alliance soldiers at the valley fortress blocked my path. I had no choice but to remain at Hellfire Citadel, serving Chieftain Kargath. Serving the demons."

At the phrase "serving the demons," Garrosh gave Jace a meaningful look. He tilted his head slightly and glanced outside, then said, "So what trouble have you run into?"

"Chieftain Kargath wants me to go to Highmaul and make contact with the demon lord there," Jace said.

"I don't see how we could help with that," Garrosh said with disgust. "That's a problem between demons and demons."

"It's not that simple," Jace said. "Magtheridon doesn't trust that Eredar lord. He believes the lord is holding an artifact that could threaten his rule, so he intends to have Chieftain Kargath send someone to feel out the situation. Naturally the task fell to me."

The young orc frowned. "What does the pit lord intend to do? Kill the Eredar warlock and seize his artifact? Or have you steal it? You're expecting me and our people to do the fighting for you? That would only drag peaceable Garadar into your… vortex. Your plan is no different from Chieftain Kargath's."

"I don't need any of you to lift a finger. I just want to learn the details of the place." Jace held his staff. "Besides, I know the Warsong clan hunts ogres — even gronn. Chieftain Grommash is a renowned giant-killer. Your battle-axes and war hammers have shaken the very walls of Highmaul… I thought the only place I could find a way into Highmaul was from you."

"So you want a guide." The young orc rested his hands on his knees and thought for a moment. "Does Hellfire Citadel have no maps of the place?"

Jace said, "Highmaul was previously occupied by that two-headed ogre and his Twilight's Hammer clan. If you ask me, you'd have a better chance of finding a Highmaul map in the Stormwind that Cho'gall destroyed. You know those ogres have had divided loyalties all along — they'd never hand over all their secrets to us."

"This is beyond my ability, Yuruk." Garrosh stood. "If you're hungry, sick, injured, or lost, Garadar can take you in. But we won't involve ourselves in a squabble between demons."

Jace stood too and looked toward the large tent high up outside. "Then I'll have to speak with Greatmother Geyah." Garrosh said coldly, "You've no right to call her that."

"As far as I know, she's not your grandmother either," Jace said.

"No, she's not. But she and Chieftain Galard saved my life — saved everyone in this village." Garrosh said. "When I was sick, Greatmother Geyah cared for me with tireless devotion, just as she does for every ill person in the village. To us, her kindness is no less than a birth mother's. And you — you're just someone who drank demon blood for a lie and dragged the entire race into hell…"

"Like your father?" Jace asked.

"How dare you—"

Garrosh ground his teeth and stepped forward, but he didn't dare draw the short blade at his waist. Still, he was shaking with fury, his teeth grinding audibly.

"I'm not trying to compare myself to him." Jace watched his hand, then met his eyes. "I'm only saying that everyone makes mistakes — even the greatest warrior in the Horde. And I'm trying to atone for mine."

He stepped closer to Garrosh. "Let me be frank with you, Garrosh. This is a perfect opportunity to sow discord between the two groups of demons in Draenor. Auchindoun has reliable intelligence on why Magtheridon is worried about Highmaul: the thing that Eredar lord controls is precisely the key their masters use to keep Magtheridon in check. Now that pit lord has seized Gul'dan's Black Temple, his ambitions have been stoked by the powerful magic there. If that artifact slips from the Burning Legion's grasp, he'll find a way to break free and make himself king — exactly as his masters fear."

"But what he doesn't know is that while Chieftain Kargath may fear Kil'jaeden's magic, he isn't truly loyal to Magtheridon himself. Once that pit lord cuts off the Legion's support, the chieftain will be free of his leash. When that happens, he'll rise up and found a new Horde, becoming a true Warchief in every sense. Deprived of our support, Magtheridon will be severely weakened — and then not only the Horde, but countless enslaved races of Draenor, draenei, arakkoa, even those Alliance soldiers will seize the chance to take their shots at him. You're the son of the Warsong chieftain, the son of that greatest warrior. You should understand this."

By the time he reached this point, Jace himself was swept up in the fervor, as though he were truly an orc painting a glorious future for the Horde's next Warchief. Even the red dragonfire sustaining his magical appearance burned in sympathy, flushing his skin a shade of crimson.

Morophil, on the village perimeter, clearly felt his surging emotions. Through the soul link Jace could sense her worry. He had to calm himself and send her a reassuring signal — everything was still under control.

But the hotheaded chieftain's son wasn't infected by the power radiating from Jace's emotions as Jace had imagined. Instead he sank into even deeper hesitation. Even the fury Jace had provoked by mentioning his father drinking demon blood had faded by eighty percent.

Jace had actually been hoping to ride that anger and ignite a fierce fighting spirit against the demons.

At the same time, though, he could feel that Garrosh didn't disbelieve him. Judging by the young orc's initial attitude toward him, if he thought Jace was lying he would have mocked him with every word — not fallen silent like this.

The orc was worried. A thick, viscous emotion churned in his heart, tugging at every fiber of his flesh, holding him back, keeping him from taking another step.

He was worried that his choice would drag Garadar into a firestorm, that the villagers would lose their lives and homes because of his decision — just like every orc clan beyond Nagrand.

Jace couldn't help but feel a pang of emotion. This future fanatical leader who would build the most fearsome orc army was, at this moment, the least orc-like orc he'd ever met. And he was in the full flush of youth, not a weathered elder like Drek'Thar who had weathered countless blood-soaked storms.

"Wait here." Garrosh looked up. "I'll relay your words to Greatmother Geyah and see what she decides. Don't you dare walk in on your own, warlock, or I'll drive you out of here at any cost. The demon blood flowing through your veins has already destroyed too many beautiful things. Don't use it to harm our kin."

Jace leaned his staff against the wall and raised his hands. "Relax, Garrosh. I'll wait here for your word." Watching the younger Hellscream disappear toward the tent, Jace was far less composed than he let on.

He was somewhat worried about how the true leader of Garadar — the Greatmother — would view the news he'd brought, how she would weigh this rationale for stirring up conflict between demons.

Greatmother Geyah was no simple old orc hag. Even though Jace hadn't paid her much attention in his previous life, he knew she had an extraordinarily close bond with the elements and ancestral spirits of all Nagrand.

That was one of the key reasons so many orcish strongholds and villages in Nagrand had been destroyed by ogres or seized by demon-worshippers, while Garadar had survived catastrophe after catastrophe of unending chaos.

He'd considered having Sola disguise herself as an orc and enter the tent, healing Greatmother Geyah's ailments to win trust.

But disguised as a Mag'har, in such a tight-knit community, she'd be a stranger wielding absurdly powerful nature magic — she'd certainly be challenged. And disguised as a green-skinned orc, she'd face the same problem as Jace: they wouldn't allow Sola into the tent, or even near any sick person to prove her power.

Factor in Sola's uncertain command of Orcish, plus her shy, introverted nature… things could spiral further and further out of control.

So Jace knew that at this moment he could only place his hopes in the Greatmother's own wisdom.

Jace figured that if she truly possessed some gift of foresight, if she truly guarded Garadar through crisis after crisis like a prophet and watched over the villagers living out their quiet lives, then he should trust her choice — whether she chose to refuse or to help.
