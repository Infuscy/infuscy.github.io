## Chapter 1254: The One in the Village Best Suited to Dealing with Demons

Garrosh had been gone for some time, and the tent area had remained perfectly calm. No orcs had come charging out with ropes and cleavers, and no one had suddenly bellowed "Die, demon slave!" or "Accept your fate, cunning Alliance dog!"

If not for the bands of void energy streaking across the sky, this quiet orc village nestled into the hillside would have been a veritable paradise amid blue skies and green grass, utterly unconnected to the shattered world beyond.

So sitting here idle wasn't so hard to bear after all. Jace was even enjoying this brief spell of peace, just watching the distant clouds drift slowly, drift…

The cry of a windroc in the distance snapped him awake, as if reminding him where he was. He'd nearly dozed off. Then the steady clop of leather boots drew closer from afar — Garrosh had returned.

"What did Greatmother Geyah say?" Jace asked.

"She doesn't want me to act as your guide to Highmaul, Yuruk," Garrosh said. Jace nodded. "I see."

"You don't look angry," Garrosh said.

"Why would I be angry?" Jace said. "I need to save my strength and mana for dealing with those demons. No time to waste it on you Mag'har. With that, I'll take my leave."

"Wait." Garrosh pulled a bone necklace from his chest. "The Greatmother wanted me to give you this."

"What is it?" Jace asked.

"This is the relic of Kashur," Garrosh said. "That name sounds familiar," Jace said.

"You don't know her name? Well, she passed away before our generation was born." Garrosh said. "But you certainly know Drek'Thar — the Frostwolf warrior and shaman who later followed Gul'dan and Ner'zhul into the embrace of demon magic. Back when he still walked the path of spirits and elements, Kashur was his teacher."

At that, Jace remembered exactly who Kashur was.

Many years ago, Kashur had been a shaman of extraordinarily high standing in the Frostwolf clan and across the entire Horde. It was in the guise of her spirit that Kil'jaeden had come to Draenor, attempting to persuade the orcs to abandon the shamanic path and turn to the shadow. Even Ner'zhul had been deceived for a time, precisely because he had once revered this matriarch so deeply.

"I remember now — Ancestress Kashur… but I don't understand," Jace said. "Why would the Greatmother give me such a precious relic?"

"I don't understand either. She said you should take this to a farm at the foot of the cliff." Garrosh shielded his hand against the sun and pointed toward a cluster of squat houses crowded together on the bank of a river below. "Right there. Go find a man named Jorin Death-eye."

"The son of the Bleeding Hollow chieftain?" Jace asked.

"Yes," Garrosh said. "The son of Bleeding Hollow chieftain Kilrogg Deadeye has been in Garadar the last couple of days. You're in luck. The Greatmother said he'd be more help to me, and that I should stay here to protect the village."

"I see. My thanks to the Greatmother."

Jace took the necklace and, shielding his eyes from the sun, glanced up toward the tent. A frail figure seemed to be looking his way from the entrance, but in the bright sunlight he couldn't make it out clearly.

"The Greatmother also wanted me to tell you something," Garrosh said. "When you meet Jorin, don't hold anything back — those were her exact words. She said you'd understand."

I'd understand?

Jace was baffled for a moment. Don't hold anything back — what did that mean? Had Greatmother Geyah already seen through his magical disguise? That was more terrifying than a beholder.

This place was so close to the Throne of the Elements that he'd actually been a little worried from the start that his disguise might fail for some strange reason. That was more or less the same reason he hadn't had Morophil sneak in with him.

But what he couldn't fathom was why he shouldn't hold anything back with Jorin Death-eye either.

"Jorin is a very clever fellow. He's seen and knows more than any of us. It would be better for you to be forthright with him," Garrosh said. "The Greatmother is willing to trust you, so I'm willing to trust that you're doing this so we can break the demons' chains in the future. I hope he sees it that way too."

Jace nodded. "I'll remember. I'll be open with Jorin Death-eye about why I've come."

He'd picked up his staff and taken two steps when Garrosh called out again. "There's one more thing I want to ask you, Yuruk."

"What?" Jace asked.

"My father drank demon blood. He listened to the demons' lies and invaded another world. You witnessed all of that with your own eyes." Garrosh said. "Do you really think he can try to make amends for his mistakes, like you?"

"You care about this a great deal," Jace said.

"I'm his son. Everyone knows that." Garrosh shook his head. "How could I not care?"

Jace turned back and studied his sun-yellowed skin through narrowed eyes. "If you'll take a warlock's advice — forget about your father. Right now you're the guardian of Garadar. Greatmother Geyah believes you should defend this place. To the villagers here, you matter far more than the name Grommash Hellscream, chieftain of the Warsong."

Garrosh fell silent. Jace didn't try to probe his emotions any further.

He simply raised a hand. "Take care, Garrosh. When this is over, I'll come back and return this necklace to her with my own hands."

In another timeline, Grommash had sacrificed his life to cleave the lord of the pit lords Mannoroth in two with a single axe stroke, freeing the orcs enslaved by his blood. But here, Jace could no longer be certain about anything in the future. There was no way to foretell any of it to this young orc.

He left the area and headed back to the woods where Vereesa and the others were hidden. As he walked, it felt as though he'd stepped through a blurry film of water.

He shed his magical orcish appearance, and under the dream-veiling of the Ysera pendant, even the red dragonfire was smothered as though it couldn't properly burn, flickering and dying out. But at least he'd made it back to his human face intact.

"How did it go, Jace?" Vereesa came running out.

Jace held up Greatmother Geyah's necklace. "The chief of Garadar wants me to see the son of Kilrogg Deadeye. And she said…"

"…don't hold anything back."

"She saw through your disguise?" Sola said in astonishment. "That's absolutely impossible. That's Lord Vaelastrasz's magic."

"I don't think she saw through your dragon magic," Jace said. "I believe she figured it out through other means. But she bears me no hostility, and she hasn't exposed me to the other orcs."

"So you're saying we're going to see that orc as members of the Alliance Expedition?" Vereesa looked at the dwarf. "I know Kilrogg Deadeye died at the hands of Commander Danath. The Sons of Lothar are his son's father's killers. How could that possibly work?"

"Maybe your Orcish isn't quite up to snuff and you didn't catch their meaning." Grede stared at the bone necklace and asked Jace. "It's not that," Jace said.

"So what's your plan?" Vereesa said. "We'll follow your lead."

Jace looked at the necklace again. It was utterly unremarkable in itself — no enchantment, no trace of wondrous power. Probably just a deeply significant heirloom passed down through the Frostwolf clan.

If not for that last thing Garrosh had relayed from the Greatmother — "you'll understand" — Jace might actually have thought Geyah was worried about a demon-blooded orc coming to Garadar and playing mind games with the young folk here. But with that added, Jace couldn't shake the feeling that the meaning wasn't that simple at all.

Though he'd resolved to trust Greatmother Geyah's choice, Jace hadn't expected her to give him guidance that was this difficult to follow to the letter.

"Maybe that orc hates the Burning Legion even more than you do," Sola said from a tree branch, looking down at Morophil below. "Considering that even our Lady Succubus here, who despises the Burning Legion to such a degree, exists — Master, I'd say it's not strange at all for a mortal who never drank demon blood to show goodwill toward a temporary enemy for the sake of fighting the Legion…"

"Let's just go, then," Jace nodded. "Just as we are… to see Jorin Death-eye."
