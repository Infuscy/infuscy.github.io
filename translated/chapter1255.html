Chapter 1255: Death-eye

The place Garrosh had pointed to looked as though it lay right at the foot of Garadar, but in reality there was still a considerable distance to walk. Following Jace, the group left the woods and came onto the main road. Vereesa kept her eyes on the high orc village the whole time, watching for anyone who might notice their presence.

Fortunately, this tranquil place didn't have many people keeping high alert on a clear, bright day, and a few cloaked figures crossing the open plains weren't all that conspicuous.

A few orc hunters rode past on wargs not far off. Once they'd gone, Jace scrambled up the dirt embankment and crossed over, heading toward the farm cottages ahead.

That house was no different from the others in Garadar — crude and ordinary. It was hard to connect it with the "Bleeding Hollow clan."

Long before the lord of the pit lords Mannoroth gifted his blood to the orcs, the Bleeding Hollow clan had already been a sufficiently dark and sinister orc clan. They were cunning and shadowy, conducting superstitious divination through ghastly rituals of blood and flesh, organs and venom.

In truth, these people were already far from strangers to Jace. In his time beyond the Dark Portal, Jace had accompanied a mage from the Wizard Sanctum and a band of mercenaries from across the world into the Black Morass in search of demon relics, and in the gnoll-infested forest they'd run into a band of roving Bleeding Hollow orcs. The leader of that band, a warlock, hadn't even been willing to respect one of the orcs' most cherished traditions — Mak'gora, the rules of honorable single combat.

Just as Jace and the others were debating whether to walk straight up, an orc walked out of the house.

He looked quite a bit older than Garrosh, with a slightly shorter topknot, and a dark-brown beard braided into a thick, long plait that hung over his chest.

Vereesa immediately gripped her bow tight, and the flowers and leaves along the Dreambow stirred awake with her battle intent. Jace clenched the sword hilt at his waist, and Grede readied himself to hurl his war hammer at any moment. But when that orc noticed the handful of outsiders, he showed no surprise or panic. He glanced back up toward the village, then looked back again.

"You've got some nerve, coming straight here." The orc actually spoke Common — his tone and accent were practically Gurubashi, but it was unmistakably Common.

All of them were a little astonished that this isolated orc village had an orc who spoke Common. Vereesa and Grede exchanged a glance, but neither dared relax their vigilance. Jace called out, "We're looking for Jorin Death-eye."

The orc pointed to his right eye. "This eye is indeed dead, if that's what you mean. What, killing my father wasn't enough — you've come to wipe out the rest of the line?"

"Who sent you? A warrior like Danath Trollbane wouldn't use such methods to deal with an enemy." The orc snorted a laugh. Vereesa murmured, "Seems he already knows everything."

"Jorin Death-eye." Jace held up the bone necklace. "Greatmother Geyah sent me. She wanted you to look at this."

"You mean Greatmother Geyah?" Jorin muttered in Orcish, and the smile vanished from his face.

He crossed the fields toward them with a half-believing, half-doubting air. As he came closer, Jace noticed that his right eye socket was an empty, black void — nothing there at all, like a wound that had never healed.

The orc took the necklace from Jace's hand, examined it carefully for a long moment, then looked back up toward Garadar. "I don't understand," he murmured to himself.

"Neither do I," Jace said in Orcish. "But Greatmother Geyah wants you to help me."

Hearing that, Jorin Death-eye looked up at Jace with some surprise, then glanced at the dwarf and the two elven women beside him.

He switched back to Common. "I think I'm beginning to understand. Let's go inside, guests. I doubt you want to be seen by any passing wolf-riders. Not everyone in this village can stay as calm as I do around outsiders."

Jace had Morophil turn invisible and keep watch near the farm, then followed the others as Jorn led them across the field ridge to the house. Grede eyed the rooms on either side and asked, "You're the son of Kilrogg Deadeye — doesn't that make you the prince of the Bleeding Hollow clan?"

"Prince." Jorin glanced at the dwarf. "I suppose."

"You live here by yourself, with no guards or anyone to look after you," Grede said.

"I don't need guards, and I don't need looking after." Jorin walked inside and looked around. "There are enough seats. Sit."

Jace dropped onto a stone beside him. Grede sat down next to him. Vereesa still seemed a little confused about whether these random rocks were actually meant for sitting, and only settled in beside Sola after seeing her sit down.

"But if your father's dead, doesn't that mean it's your turn to inherit the chieftain's seat?" Grede pressed on. Vereesa shot him a look, and Jace glanced his way too — why did he keep poking the sore spot?

"It's hard to say whether I'm still truly a member of the Bleeding Hollow clan at this point." Jorin looked out the window. "There's a group of Bleeding Hollow still on the other side of the Dark Portal. There's a group of Bleeding Hollow at Hellfire Citadel, commanded by one of my cousins. They're far more the real Bleeding Hollow. And the Bleeding Hollow who stayed in Nagrand… they'd rather call themselves 'Mag'har' — in the human tongue, 'the Uncorrupted.' So do I. Just like every villager here. Truthfully, those clansmen and I no longer qualify as the same kind of orc."

"No wonder Greatmother Geyah thought you'd accept our arrival," Jace said.

"I don't think that's what she had in mind," Jorin said. "Tell me your troubles. Let me see just how difficult this is."

Jace sensed there was some background here, but for now he had no intention of digging into it with this unfamiliar orc.

Since the Greatmother wanted honesty, he didn't plan to hold anything back. "You should know that the Expedition is currently facing off against the Burning Legion in Shadowmoon Valley, Jorin Death-eye. But to ensure we have a chance to withdraw from there — and to keep the demons from seizing the chance to attack in the process — we need to acquire a powerful artifact, one formidable enough to make Magtheridon think twice. And we have reliable intelligence that this artifact is in Highmaul."

"Highmaul is currently ruled by an Eredar lord named Balthas," Jorin Death-eye said. "After the shattering of Draenor, those demons took advantage of our recovery from the nNagrand coast's devastation and invaded that castle, seizing a powerful magic source there."

Balthas?

Jace leaned against the wall and thought for a moment. He distinctly remembered that in the game, the Eredar lord who controlled the Legion portals in Nagrand was named Razuulai. So who was this Balthas? Wait — could he be the Eredar lord who gave players the quest to kill Razuulai, the one loyal to Illidan Stormrage?

Jace felt like a lot of things were suddenly falling into place.

The Eredar were perhaps the most arrogant race in the universe, and that arrogance had even carried over into the draenei. These kind-hearted, Holy Light-worshipping people, even tens of thousands of years after leaving Argus, still couldn't shed that air of superiority rooted deep in their bones.

So Jace had always found it rather baffling why an Eredar lord would willingly serve under someone like Illidan — a "blind, crude night elf half-breed."

Unless he'd committed some grave offense that had provoked Kil'jaeden, leaving him too terrified to return to the Burning Legion — such as losing the Demonic Codex, the very leash Kil'jaeden had intended to use to control Magtheridon and the Burning Legion in Outland!

In the game, Balthas had once mentioned that Razuhelia was his brother — most likely sent by Kil'jaeden to "clean house" and prove his loyalty, which was why Balthas had been in such a hurry to get players to kill him.

"How do you know so much about what goes on inside Highmaul?" Jace asked.

"Because I personally took part in the assault on Highmaul, human." Jorin looked at Jace. "After the Warsong clan captured Shattrath, Gul'dan ordered Grommash Hellscream — who by then had already drunk demon blood — along with my father, to assist his student, that two-headed ogre Cho'gall, in attacking Highmaul and killing Overlord Margol. He wanted to use the fall of the ogre empire to demonstrate the power of Mannoroth's blood to those of us in the Horde who hadn't yet drunk it — to show that whether it was a great draenei city like Shattrath or a fortress that had threatened us for centuries like Highmaul, both were fragile before the power of demons."

When he said "the power of demons," Jorin laughed with contempt. Then he said, "Regarding that artifact you mentioned, I've heard of it too, human. Many ogres know that Balthas keeps an artifact in the depths of Highmaul as though it were a holy relic, and that artifact records terrifying knowledge and weaknesses concerning countless Burning Legion demons. So you want to steal it from Balthas's hands?"

"Yes," Vereesa said. "Can you help?"

Jorin looked at the staff-like longbow leaning against Vereesa, then at Jace's cloak, then at the staff cradled in Sola's arms radiating a faint arcane glow. "It looks like the Alliance has sent the sharpest warriors they could find to get their hands on that thing."

"Not far off from the leaders of the Sons of Lothar either!" Grede clapped Vereesa on the shoulder. "She's the younger sister of Alleria Windrunner herself!"

Jace had expected Jorin to show displeasure at Vereesa's connection to the Sons of Lothar, but he hesitated little and instead seemed rather excited. "That Windrunner captain? That settles it, then. I can help."

"But in exchange," he added, "you'll have to do something for me too."

"Speak," Jace said at once.

Jorin Death-eye fixed his single eye on Jace, and Jace increasingly had the feeling that the blinded socket was still somehow functional.

The orc studied him, turning Ancestress Kashur's necklace over in his hands a few times, then said, "My clansmen and I don't live in Garadar — we live in a village further south. But that place is under constant threat from the Boulderfist ogres. Those creatures still harbor a grudge over the Bleeding Hollow helping Cho'gall take Highmaul, and Grommash's fine son won't risk his neck for our survival. But clearly Greatmother Geyah still keeps my troubles in mind."

"Boulderfist ogres?" Grede said. "I've heard of that tribe. It's an enormously huge ogre tribe. You want us to clean out their whole lair for you?! How is that possible?"

"Nothing that complicated." Jorin looked at the dwarf. "The Boulderfist ogres of today are ruled by a gronn living in Nagrand. Kill that gronn, and the ogres will be thrown into chaos for a time without their leader. That would buy us enough time to mount a retreat or move somewhere safer."

"I know you outsiders have some stereotypes about gronn, but they're not the masters of the world, and they're certainly not indestructible gods. Not all gronn are as powerful as Gruul. The gronn I'm talking about… is only one of Gruul's many offspring. So I'd call that a very fair trade. What do you think?"
