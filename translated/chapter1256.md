## Chapter 1256: Mark of the Burning Legion

"I know Deathwing wasn't beaten by Gruul." Grede glanced outside at Jorin Death-eye, who was stretching lazily toward the sun over the farmland, and said in a low voice. "But did he fight Gruul at all?"

"You might have been fast asleep at the time, Jace. Captain Alleria and Archmage Khadgar told us they watched Gruul and Deathwing grappling with each other with their own eyes… You know what grappling means? Me smashing a whelp with one hammer blow — that's not grappling. Two monsters locked together, trading punches — that's grappling! Do you really think we need to go provoke a son of that King of Monsters just to help this one-eyed orc?"

"But not only has he been to Highmaul — he may even have lived there for a time." Vereesa folded her arms. "And he knows so much about that book, and understands the demons there far better than we do. I think passing up a guide and helper who's practically perfect… would be downright irresponsible to the mission."

"Now you're willing to trust orcs, Vereesa. I really wonder if those orcs in Alterac Valley have some sort of charm spell." Grede scratched his head. "Damn it, I think this orc is a little strange even by orc standards. Look at him — always smiling, like something's not quite right in the head. He really doesn't resent the Sons of Lothar for killing his father? For all we know he's planning to lead us into the gronn's cave and stab us in the back, using the gronn to wipe us out and avenge Kilrogg Deadeye."

"If you'd been half as careful on the road to Ashenvale, we wouldn't have run into so much trouble," Sola said. "Are there gronn in Ashenvale?" Grede shot back.

Sola said, "I think the orcs here are just different. Or rather, the orcs here are what they really are." Grede sighed. "I'm not afraid of fighting one of Gruul's sons. I just don't want to be constantly worrying about betrayal."

"If you really don't trust him," Jace said, "I'll just ask him directly how he feels about it. I'm a warlock — I can tell if he's hiding something from us."

"Then let's do that," Grede said. "If you think he's fine, I'm fine." Jace nodded, stood, and walked to the door. "There's one thing we want to ask you, Jorin Death-eye." The orc turned, scratched his bare forehead, and ambled back over. "Go ahead."

"You don't seem to resent the Alliance over your father's death," Jace said.

"For the Death-eye, death is more like a play that's been performed countless times over." Jorin said. "We know how it begins, how it ends — we even remember the details. At most we're mildly surprised by the timing of its arrival. But when my father drank demon blood and followed Gul'dan through the Dark Portal, even that last trace of surprise vanished for us. I won't say he brought it on himself, but there isn't a better way to put it."

"You're saying your father knew how he would die?" Grede asked.

"Yes." Jorin said. "Long ago, when I was very small, he told me he would die at the hands of a strange warrior in silver armor, and that blood would stain the pale ground. I think when he led the Bleeding Hollow warriors to the gates of Stormwind and saw the soldiers there, he must have understood what it meant."

"Does that have something to do with your lost eye?" Vereesa asked.

Jorin Death-eye didn't answer the ranger's question. He fixed his single eye on her for a moment, then said, "If you're worried I'll avenge my father… there's really no need. The most important thing to me right now is whether my clansmen and I can survive in Nagrand. Ogres and demons are far greater problems than the Alliance."

Even without a warlock's gift for sensing emotion and shadow, everyone in the room could feel Jorin's indifference to Kilrogg's death.

Jace had expected to feel some suppressed hatred buried deep in his heart, or at least resentment toward his father — but there was nothing. Compared to Garrosh's fiery inner world, this Death-eye before him was like a pool of thick, dead water, without a single ripple.

"I have one last question," Grede said. "Go ahead," the orc said.

"Who did you learn your Common from?" the dwarf asked.

Jorin said, "I was already learning your language before my father died, so that when you killed him and came after me, I wouldn't be completely in the dark."

Grede was taken aback for a moment. Jace glanced at Sola beside him and felt his head spinning a little too. "I've nothing more to say," Grede said.

Jace nodded. "We'll accept. We'll help you deal with that gronn."

"Good." Jorin said. "Jorin Death-eye — clearly you already know who I am."

"Vereesa Windrunner, Ranger of the Farstriders."

"Grede Thunderfist, Gryphon Rider of Aerie Peak."

"Sola Shade, Mage."

"Jace Sesso, Warlock."

"Warlock Jace Sesso?" Jorin frowned and looked over. "Are you the one who killed Targon?"

"Yes. She and I killed that death knight together." Jace glanced at Vereesa. "Will that affect our cooperation?"

"No." The orc looked at the ranger and shook his head. "I was just confirming something I'd heard."

"The gronn we're dealing with is named 'Durn' — yes, Gruul's stupid sons all have names of their own." He turned back, rummaging through a pack in the corner as he spoke. "Before anything begins, I need to give you something first, Jace Sesso. Since we have a warlock among us, a lot of things will be much easier."

The orc pulled a stack of scrolls from a pile of leather-bound booklets and held them out. "Can you summon demons?"

"I can," Jace said.

"Summon a succubus here. She'll help us blend into Highmaul more smoothly." Jorin said. "The demons in Highmaul all bear a symbol called the Mark of Kil'jaeden — a sigil carried by demons loyal to the Burning Legion. The Shadow Council members and demons there use it to recognize their own. But I have a way to forge this Mark of Kil'jaeden as well — it's just a rather troublesome process. So to avoid wasting time, you'd best start learning now. If your imitation is convincing enough, with luck we may be able to sneak all the way into the depths of Highmaul with the help of your demon minion…"

Jace knew this mark. In Outland, killing demons had a chance to drop these marks, and turning them in to the Aldor could increase reputation.

But a problem occurred to him all at once.

"Are you certain every Burning Legion demon carries this mark?" Jace said. "When I brought my demon into Auchindoun disguised as a Shadow Council member, they didn't realize at all that she lacked a Mark of Kil'jaeden."

"Impossible." Jorin said. "Let me see your succubus."

"She's outside," Jace said.

"Morophil, come in for a moment."

"Morophil?"

After a long while, Morophil walked through the door with visible reluctance, fixing Jorin Death-eye with a gaze that could hardly be called friendly. "Your succubus has no demonic wings?" Jorin asked curiously.

"I'm starting to think you don't want that other eye either, orc," Morophil said.

Jorin's curiosity vanished instantly. He swallowed and glanced at Jace. Jace merely cleared his throat and said nothing.

The orc studied the flames burning in Morophil's eyes carefully. After a long, hard look, Morophil said impatiently, "I don't have a Mark of Kil'jaeden. Don't waste your energy looking."

Jorin Death-eye took a step back, his tone grave. "You truly don't have a Mark of Kil'jaeden. What you have is a Mark of Sargeras — a sigil carried only by high-ranking officers of the Burning Legion… except it appears to be broken. I can see that."

"It took me God knows how much effort to break it," Morophil said in a rather provocative tone. "So are you planning to repair it?"

"Why do you have a Mark of Sargeras?" Jace asked.

"The Legion's lapdogs imagined it would make me bow forever to their great lord. Things didn't quite go as planned." The succubus gave a contemptuous laugh. "Does it bother you, Master?"

"I only care whether the demons in Highmaul can tell it's broken," Jace said.

"I don't think ordinary lesser demons would easily notice," Jorin said. "But Balthas himself also bears a Mark of Sargeras, and he should be able to tell the difference. Still, it would be hard to produce a more useful mark than this damaged Mark of Sargeras."
