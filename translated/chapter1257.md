## Chapter 1257: Boulderfist Pass

Before leaving Garadar, Vereesa and Grede spent some time learning to ride wargs. Jace and Sola had already ridden wargs for long stretches on the Burning Steppes, so they were more familiar with it.

Jace, of course, pretended he'd never ridden a wolf in his life, while Sola simply climbed on and rode off without fuss, which surprised Jace a little. Jace could only explain it away by saying she was an elven veteran who'd fought in the Orc Wars.

When introducing herself, Sola hadn't mentioned she was a green dragon — she'd given her cover identity as a mage. Which probably meant she didn't want to reveal the truth to this orc, and certainly wasn't about to let him ride on her back either, demon blood or not.

In truth, even if Sola had been willing to save time by grudgingly carrying this one-eyed orc to find "Durn," Jace would hardly have agreed.

Durn — known in the game as "Durn the Hungerer" — was quite possibly the most terrifying creature walking the Nagrand prairie.

It wasn't just that Grede had felt fighting this gronn was a bad idea from the start; Jace had actually thought it dangerous too, because Durn was the tallest and largest even among Gruul's seven sons.

In another timeline, after Deathwing left Outland, his son Sabellian had led the black dragons of the Blade's Edge Mountains into a prolonged sovereignty war with Gruul's offspring. In the end, most of the adult black dragons had died there — to say nothing of the whelps.

So Jace figured these gronn giants must have picked up some dragon-hunting techniques from fighting the black dragons, Gruul and his brood especially.

If this giant were really as easy to deal with as Jorin Death-eye made it sound, he wouldn't still be alive more than a decade from now.

And Durn wasn't the only gronn in Nagrand. Many smaller hill gronn lived among the hills and gorges, and Jace was also worried that a green dragon flying in the air would be a conspicuous target when they approached Durn's or those gronn's lairs.

To avoid the traveling orcs moving between Garadar and the nearby farms, they chose to cross the open plains, following a river that ran toward the southwest. After riding hard for nearly two days, on the afternoon when the sunlight finally stopped being so harsh, they crested a high ridge and saw, in the low ground ahead, a handful of squat houses with thin smoke curling up from among them.

"My people won't be happy to see me with 'mercenaries' from another world." Jorin eyed those houses. "Our original village, Gul'rok, was destroyed fighting the Alliance and the draenei, and now it's occupied by a band of Warmaul ogres."

He glanced over at Jace. "Even though it was the Shadow Council and the Bleeding Hollow warriors who followed my father into battle who fought, they are still our clansmen."

"You're a very sensible prince, Jorin Death-eye," Vereesa said.

"In most orcs' eyes, that kind of 'sense' is cowardice and compromise," Jorin said. "But I think it's still a good deal better than cooperating with demons."

As he said it, he glanced at the succubus sitting sideways behind Jace. "Of course, I mean the Burning Legion, Lady Succubus."

Morophil inclined her head politely. Jorin pointed to a riverside hut that looked like a guard post and said, "You can rest there today. I'll bring some meat from the village to serve as provisions for tomorrow, when we head to the gorge to track down Durn. A few days ago in Garadar I saw many windrocs flying east — I think another aged clefthoof must have died over there. Today our hunters are likely to bring back a good haul."

"No need, old friend — if you mean the day we arrived in Garadar," Grede said with some pride. "That clefthoof was killed by us, actually. We tracked your Garadar hunters' trail to find your village."

Vereesa rolled her eyes. Jorin looked at Grede in surprise. "You killed a clefthoof?"

"You can drop the plural," Jace said, pointing to the ranger beside him. "Basically, she did it."

"Impressive." Jorin exclaimed in Orcish, then switched back to Common. "Ever since Gul'dan launched the invasion of Azeroth, it's become very difficult for Nagrand to organize clefthoof hunts. Taking down a lone clefthoof is hard enough, let alone killing the leader of a whole herd. I think Durn's really going to shut that foul mouth of his for good this time."

"Then, first thing tomorrow morning when the sky lightens, I'll come back here. I'll bring a hunter we can trust to lead us to the gorge to track down that gronn."

"Very well," Jace said.

Jorin headed off toward the Bleeding Hollow village. Jace's group took the fork in the road toward the hut. Sola said, "The clefthoof hide here is much thicker than that of many whelps — truly hard to deal with. I've never seen such a beast in Azeroth."

Vereesa said with some satisfaction, "I had no idea it was that tough before I took the monster down, Sola. But Azeroth doesn't have beasts that can defeat a Mountain King with a pile of dung."

Sola couldn't help but giggle. Grede jumped in fury and shouted, "That was a hoof caked with mud, not dung! Are you ranger blind?!"

"I'd say it was more like the clefthoof's tail. Of course, I'm not even sure clefthoofs actually have tails…" Before Jace could finish, Grede stalked ahead and barked to cut him off.

"The tail lifts up and squirts dung, is that it?!" Grede glared at Jace, then at the two elven women. "You're all in on it — the demon too!"

"I didn't say a thing," Morophil said immediately.

Sazeno piped up, "Actually, I'm on your side, mighty little lord. If you ask me, that was clearly—"

"What can you see hiding in that bag?!" Grede cut the imp off, took two steps back, and said, "Just wait — after tomorrow, all of Draenor will know who the real Mountain King is!"

He bared his big teeth for emphasis. "Mountain! King!"

The next day, before the sky had fully brightened, while a thin haze of pink still lay over the Nagrand grassland, Jorin Death-eye and an orc hunter arrived at the hut.

Mag'har orcs were all somewhat smaller than the demon-blooded orcs and slightly leaner in build. A fellow like Garrosh, who relied on his ancestors' bloodlines to stand tall even among green-skinned orcs, looked practically like an ogre in Garadar.

So when the two cloaked orcs came riding up on wargs, at first glance they really didn't look like two orcs Jace was familiar with — more like two islanders from Kul Tiras.

Jorin had clearly already explained the situation to the hunter, so though the man was wary of this band of foreign outlanders, he wasn't particularly alarmed.

The Bleeding Hollow orc rode his warg in the lead; the others rode close behind. They crossed a shallow ford to the far side, then entered a gorge that grew deeper and deeper. Tall trees grew at all angles up the high slopes, blocking out the sky and casting the place in gloom. For a moment they could have sworn they weren't in Nagrand at all, but back in Ashenvale.

Of course, Ashenvale was a gorgeous forest steeped in warm arcane energy. Here there was only the faint stench of urine and the rust-sickness reek of old blood drifting through the whole gorge.

Every so often Jace could read the malice drifting through the air. The residents of this gorge had to harbor an extraordinarily high degree of malice, and no small number of them had to be studying shadow magic, for it to reach this level.

The orc hunter stopped near a high slope and led the group off their wargs, then scrambled up a nearby cliff face.

Vereesa was a ranger, Grede was a hillman, and the Bleeding Hollow hunter and Jorin were orcs — needless to say, they all climbed with terrifying speed. Jace hadn't done much climbing lately, but he got by on his old experience scaling city walls and church steeples, and his body was still up to the task.

The one who surprised him was Sola — the green dragon "mage" who'd been quiet the whole way climbed faster than anyone, though she deliberately climbed a bit and then paused, staying right beside Jace at the rear, because the way she clambered really didn't move like a humanoid at all — more like a terrifying giant gecko. At least for now she didn't want to reveal her true identity to the orcs.

Once everyone had climbed up, the Bleeding Hollow hunter signaled them all to keep quiet, then crept forward with Jorin to observe.

They waited there for roughly half an hour, and the mountain gorge slowly grew a little brighter. By the time the sun was probably high overhead, the thin forest mist had thinned somewhat, and a squat but enormously fat blue-grey ogre appeared in the shade of the trees below.

The rotund monster peered in their direction. A few minutes later another ogre walked up and the two exchanged hushed words.

The two Bleeding Hollow withdrew from the cliff edge. After a quiet conference, Jorin said, "This path in leads to the Boulderfist ogres' lair. Our target is inside. Ogres don't dare trespass on a gronn's lair, so ordinarily there won't be too many ogres guarding him inside. We can slip past these outer sentries and go into the cave to deal with that monster. Just stay close to our hunter — he can take us on a side path to get as deep as possible without being spotted by the ogres…"

Before Jorin could finish, a shimmering, water-ripple-like mirror suddenly enveloped the entire group. The two orcs looked around in surprise. Jorin stared at his own hands and murmured, "What… this can't be…"

"This is no dream." Vereesa looked back at Sola, who held the pendant, and explained to the orcs. "This is a dream field, drawn from magic of our world, sustained by abundant life energy. In this gorge forest, it's perfectly suited to draw upon. So long as you remain inside this dream field, those foolish, sluggish ogres will never be able to detect you."

Seeing Jorin still hesitate, Jace said, "Even Gurubashi trolls couldn't detect anything unusual about this field, Jorin. You can set your mind at ease."

At the mention of Gurubashi trolls, Jorin seemed to stop doubting entirely.

After crossing the Dark Portal, Kilrogg Deadeye had led the Death-eye army into the Stranglethorn Vale and fought a fierce battle against the jungle trolls there — and been defeated. As long as Jorin kept up with his clan's movements on Azeroth, he would certainly know they'd come out the worse in a contest of scouting and counter-scouting.
