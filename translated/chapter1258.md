## Chapter 1258: The Fetid Lair

Sola maintained the dream field as she walked to the front of the cliff. When she looked out beyond the field, everything had churned into a blur, and the distant ogres had become two watery blue shadows.

The group followed Sola down the low slope ahead. Jorin watched the movements of the two ogres the whole way, and indeed they never detected this band of people closing in. They slipped right past the cliff face beside the two ogres without either of them reacting.

The gorge was home to quite a few blue-skinned Boulderfist ogres. They were slightly shorter than most ogres, but there were a great many of them, and they appeared to stand together in impressive solidarity.

Deep in the gorge was a fairly spacious valley, with a stream running through the middle. Quite a few ogres sat on the open ground — some squabbling and brawling with each other, others dividing up something to eat.

At the far end of the clearing was a cave — its mouth broad and tall. A two-headed ogre stood near the entrance, directing several ogres carrying beast carcasses inside.

Those slaughtered prey were all long-legged hoofed beasts, their bloodless fur a blue-grey that wasn't far from the ogres' own skin. Jace guessed those must be a herd of unlucky talbuk.

After the ogre ahead had moved off, Jorin said, "That's Durn's dwelling. Are you ready?"

"Ready," Grede said. The others nodded.

Sola held the dream field as she steered around the clusters of ogres on the clearing, past discarded animal bones toward the cave entrance. When the two-headed ogre followed the food-carrying Boulderfists inside, they slipped in after them.

The cave was littered with bones and dried black bloodstains, and before they'd gone very deep, a suffocating wave of foul stench hit them in the face.

Of course, it still fell a little short of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar — the stink here was crudely pure and natural, not enough to conjure associations with anything deeply sinister and terrifying.

Once inside, Jace was reminded of Gruul's Lair in the Blade's Edge Mountains in the game — the inside was likewise a simple, wide passage leading to an even broader, roughly hewn giant chamber.

Several noticeably better-dressed and better-armed ogre chieftains sat there, along with two sorcerers covered in magical ornaments.

But dealing with these Boulderfist leaders shouldn't be difficult. Jace didn't think they were any tougher than the Deadwind ogres of Deadwind Pass, who'd been slaughtered in swathes by the high chieftain's Shadow Bolt storms. The real problem was Durn, deep inside.

Sola looked up and examined the tree roots along the cave ceiling. The life force in this earthen cave was still sufficient to sustain Queen Ysera's earring.

They skirted the chieftains sitting in the middle deep in conversation and followed the Boulderfist laborers hauling meat deeper into the cave. Here the passage narrowed a bit, and before long they came to a place blocked by a wooden gate.

The laborers dumped the carcasses in a muddy pit in front of the gate, then hurried away. Even the two-headed ogre didn't care to linger long. Then a nearby ogre shouted something in a language Jace couldn't understand, and the gate was hauled open.

Vereesa leaned forward a step, meaning to seize the chance to peer inside — but a boulder-like enormous fist, dark brown, thrust out from within with a rushing blast of wind that slammed into them. As they struggled to keep their feet against the gust of rank, bloody air, the carcasses of six or seven talbuk had already been snatched back through the gate.

Now all that remained before them was freshly dug earth, ground-up scraps of flesh, and vast smears of blood.

The entire cave rumbled from the force of that fist strike. The ground shuddered with it. Soil and shattered tree roots rained down the walls, and even the dream field flickered with slight instability.

Before any of them could make out what they were seeing, the gate had already slammed shut with a bang.

The ogre who'd shouted the order and the one who'd pulled the gate fled the passage in terror, whimpering and muttering as they ran, paying no heed to anything vaguely suspicious around them. That left only Jace's group standing there.

When the cave fell quiet, the crunch of bones being mercilessly ground apart echoed from within. "We could wait here… and go in the next time he's fed," Grede said carefully.

"That was my thought too." Jorin exchanged a few words with the hunter beside him. Sola nodded, and Vereesa gave an "mm."

Clearly, after seeing the size of that "hand," the confident, assured looks they'd all worn walking in had mostly evaporated. Everyone's attitude had turned several degrees more serious.

But Jace felt something was off. He looked up at the ceiling again. A small stone trapped in a clump of dirt had just fallen from above and thumped into the ground. It reminded him of some unwelcome memories — memories of fighting Gruul in the game.

The gronn lords inside the cave were undoubtedly fighting on true home ground. When facing enemies, they could make maximum use of the power of earth and stone here — even use that power to replenish themselves — unless they could be killed in a single stroke.

Could Vereesa's arrows pierce a clefthoof's skull? Could they pierce a gronn's rock-like skull? Even if they could, Jace wasn't even sure a gronn's brain was its weak point.

"I think perhaps we should attack when he comes out to forage," Jace said. "Fighting a giant like that inside this cave, we'd be at a severe disadvantage."

"But since the Great Cataclysm, Durn has never once gone outside, Jace Sesso," Jorin said. "As long as the ogres of Nagrand still see him as their leader, as long as the Boulderfist ogres keep feeding him like this… he won't go hungry and wander out. I believe in our strength. Durn can't beat us."

Jace believed it too. But afterward they still had to infiltrate Highmaul, and might have to fight who-knew-what sort of Burning Legion demons. Being injured here would put them at a serious disadvantage. He had to aim for a victory with as little damage as possible.

"Lure him out?" Vereesa asked. She clearly also felt that fighting a gronn lord in this place was a bad idea.

"To force him to come out and fight us on his own, we'd probably have to kill every Boulderfist ogre in the gorge outside," Jorin said. "A gronn rules the ogres, but he doesn't give a damn whether they live or die…"

"I have an idea," Sola said tentatively.

Everyone turned to look at the green-clad elven girl. She so rarely offered opinions — usually she just went along with whatever the others said — that being stared at all at once seemed to make her a little nervous.

"Go ahead, Sola," Jace said.

Sola nodded. "His father styles himself the Dragonkiller. All gronn take pride in hunting dragons — it's what spreads their name across Draenor…"

"Yes," Vereesa said. "That's what Archmage Khadgar said."

"Then let's find him a dragon to hunt," Sola said.

"There are no dragons in Nagrand, Mage," Jorin said. "Unless we go near Auchindoun…" Sola hadn't said "black dragon" — she'd said "dragon." Jace understood almost immediately what she was getting at. "You mean…" Jace murmured. "You're going to…"

"Yes. Let's see for ourselves just how the son of the Dragonkiller goes about his dragon-slaying." A rare flicker of challenge showed on Sola's face. The blue eyes mimicking a high elf's gleam even betrayed a hint of reptilian, yellowish serpent's hue, which in the pitch-black cave was rather fearsome.

"What do you mean?" Jorin looked around at the group in confusion.

"You'll know when we come out, Jorin Death-eye," Sola said softly.

That's right — even the soldiers of the Expedition didn't recognize a green dragon. To ogres and gronn, there was even less chance of telling the difference between a green dragon and a black dragon, neither of which they'd ever seen.

Jace knew that ever since Sola had heard Gruul's name at the Wildhammer stronghold, she'd nursed a long-simmering resentment toward this clan of gronn. Kind and gentle as her nature was, she was a true dragon, and the arrogance buried in her bones wasn't much less than an Eredar's.

One who commanded nature magic wouldn't even call herself a druid — because in the green dragons' eyes, druids were students of the Dream, while the green dragons were part of the Emerald Dream itself. The two could not be mentioned in the same breath.
