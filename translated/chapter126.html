Chapter 126: Casting Force

Jace woke with a slight hangover.

He didn't know the proof on that sweet liquor — heard it was pretty strong — but it hadn't felt like much going down. Chatting with Grede as they drank, it didn't feel like he'd had all that much. The taste didn't pack much of a punch, after all. But by the time he'd stumbled home that night, he could barely walk straight, nearly face-planting into the canal a couple of times and cutting his Azeroth career short.

Jace had bolted downstairs without a stitch on, stripped off his clothes, and plunged into the river for a quick dip. Came up, splashed some water on his face, and felt a whole lot clearer.

He needed to be clear-headed today. He had one critically important task: memorize the Mage Armor incantation.

Last time, even with Arcane Intellect active, when he'd later cast it again trying to transcribe what he'd memorized, he couldn't be sure about the order of two phrases, or the pronunciation of several syllables. The incantation was just way too long, and it was in Dragon tongue — a language Jace didn't understand at all. Even with Arcane Intellect boosting him, he couldn't recite the whole thing from memory.

And now, Marin had specifically told him not to memorize it, and made it clear that he could only practice Mage Armor with Marin present.

But how was Jace supposed to just do everything they told him?

On this trip to the continent of Lordaeron, Jace was determined to learn Mage Armor. And if he couldn't learn it outright, at least he wanted to get somewhere — reach a workable stage on his own. Especially considering that the whole of Lordaeron was far richer in magical energy than the Kingdom of Stormwind. Outside of Karazhan, most of the ley lines and nexuses were scattered north of Thandol Span. Jace had to seize this chance and drill this top-tier Abjuration incantation properly.

After last time's disaster, Jace was going to be a lot more careful. He planned to have Grede keep an eye on him and help handle anything that went wrong — elemental runaways, that sort of thing.

They were leaving tomorrow. Today, during his session with Marin, he had to seize his last chance… memorize the incantation as completely as possible. So he was going to pull out all the stops.

"Sazeno, get out here."

"Yes!" The imp materialized in a flash of light. "What is it, master?"

"How confident are you that you can hide from a mage?" Jace asked.

"One hundred percent confident." Sazeno grinned slyly. "Demons devote their entire existence to hunting arcane users, master. We're born to evade mages' detection, and my skills and knowledge put those lesser imps to shame by a country mile! Even an Eredar… well, depends which one. If Archimonde himself came after me, I'd be done for."

"I'm not asking you to dodge Archimonde," Jace said offhandedly. "Just a single human mage."

"You know who Archimonde is?" Sazeno asked suddenly.

Jace looked up and fixed Sazeno with a stare, realizing he'd said a bit too much.

He coughed, awkwardly. "So what if I do? Archimonde's invaded Azeroth before."

Archimonde, one of the two Eredar masters of the Burning Legion.

He, Kil'jaeden, and Velen had once been the three leaders of the ancient and powerful Eredar civilization, with Archimonde the youngest of the trio. When the dark Titan Sargeras corrupted the Eredar and made them the backbone of the Burning Legion, Velen fled the Eredar homeworld of Argus, and Archimonde and Kil'jaeden became Sargeras's two most formidable lieutenants.

Compared to Kil'jaeden, who preferred to hide behind the scenes and manipulate different races into fighting each other, Archimonde's style was far more direct — drop onto a planet, annihilate everything in his path with his matchless magic, and then drain every scrap of energy he could from the world for his own use.

Archimonde had invaded the Kaldorei Empire, but that was ten thousand years ago. The unstoppable Archimonde had run into the Dragon Soul — the artifact the Dragon Aspects had forged to drive back the Burning Legion, before its corruption into the Demon Soul — and suffered a devastating defeat.

Forced to withdraw and lick his wounds, he'd never forgotten his desire to return to this world and exact his revenge. Ten thousand years later, the torment and destruction visited upon the Kaldorei Empire had become myth and legend, tucked away in the libraries of Quel'Thalas.

A learned human archmage who'd had extensive contact with the elves would know Archimonde's name — that was perfectly normal. But for a lowly apprentice like Jace… being that familiar with Archimonde was a bit odd.

The imp bounced around excitedly. "Your knowledge of the Burning Legion far exceeds what I imagined, master!"

Jace cut off his rambling. "Enough chatter. Here's your job."

"Yes, sir!"

"Stay in my bag. Listen carefully to that old mage's incantation. Memorize the spell. You'd better get every last bit of it right, and when we get back you'll cross-check it with what I remember. Understood? If you dare forget any of it, or come back claiming you couldn't hear clearly, I'll lock you up for two months. And if you expose yourself and let anyone find you…"

"Then the Strangling Curse!" the imp shouted eagerly.

"Relax, little guy," Jace said. "If you get caught, there's no need for me to cast any 'minion strangling curse' on you — you'll suffer a far worse fate. Think you'll just die and pop back to the Twisting Nether? Think again. The mages here will lock you in a special anti-magic prison, study you every day, torture you every day, test what kinds of magic you can withstand, and then develop spells designed to counter and agonize demons. You'll be abused for thousands of years, and by then you'll be crying and begging to go back to the Twisting Nether!"

The imp swallowed. "I guarantee… not a trace for those mages to detect!"

"If you do get caught, don't count on me to help you," Jace said. "I'll do everything I can to distance myself from you. You'll be treated as some fel creature that came with the warlock's bag, and I'll just be a poor, treasure-scavenging junk collector — a stupid, naive apprentice who accidentally picked up this bag in the wilds and brought it to Stormwind. I might get punished, locked up for a few months or years, but you'll be done for. And as long as I'm alive, our contract won't end — you won't even be able to rat me out. Understand your situation, little guy? When I'm in the clear, I'll come visit you in prison."

The imp's voice went wobbly. "Master, have you ever considered throwing your soul into the Twisting Nether upon death and being reborn in a demonic body? The Burning Legion could use a talent like yours!"

"That's a problem for the next life," Jace said, pointing at the bag. "Get in. Now."

"Yes, sir!"

The imp leaped into the warlock's bag in a flash of light. Jace cinched the mouth shut.

It wasn't like he'd never brought the imp into the Mage Quarter before. But bringing it into Marin's house? That was a first.

Outside the Mage Quarter, Jace cast Arcane Intellect on himself, then waited for the arcane traces to fade a bit before heading in. When he reached the garden and looked toward the door of Marin's study from a distance, it felt like someone had suddenly installed several surveillance cameras around the place. But he knew the weirder he acted, the more suspicious it'd look. This wasn't his first time pulling a stunt like this, anyway. He might as well relax and let fate decide.

Jace pushed open the door — only to find Marin already inside.

Marin was tidying his bookshelf. Hearing someone enter, he said, "My dear Miss Marin, remember to brew a pot of tea before you head off to class. That Jace Sesso fellow probably isn't coming."

"Uh… Master?"

Marin turned around, adjusting his glasses. "You're still here?"

"Boat leaves tomorrow morning," Jace said, scratching his head. "I can put in one more day of work."

"Excellent." Marin immediately rolled his sleeves back down and sat behind his desk with a sigh. "A day of rest is a day of rest. Getting old is no easy thing."

Jace set the bag down by the foot of the bookshelf and immediately set to work cleaning with practiced ease. Marin even put his book down to watch him work through every corner with such familiarity.

"You've come today to practice that incantation again, I take it, Mr. Sesso?"

"Yes." Jace gave an embarrassed smile. "It's my last day in Stormwind, Master. I really want to try one more time. Who knows when I'll get another chance — maybe not until next year. If I don't get a decent result, I'm afraid I won't sleep well for months, I'll be stuck thinking about it the whole time."

Marin said, "There's no need to rush. You want to master such a complex incantation in barely a week? You'd be better off taking two months to carefully reflect on the reasons for your last failure, summarize where you fell short, and then when you come back it'll go much more smoothly. If you're willing to stay here this morning, I can go over some of the fundamentals of Abjuration casting for you, and then when you return to the Kingdom of Lordaeron you can recall it at your own pace and digest it slowly. That'll help you grow."

"But…" Jace said. "Last time I was so close, Master."

Marin lowered his brows in resignation. "Since you're so eager to try again, let me go over last time's problem with you in detail. As a matter of fact, I've given some thought to your casting process afterward, Mr. Sesso."

He closed his book, set it aside, and leaned over the desk. "Your mimicry of the incantation was extremely precise — that's your strength. So much so that the arcane energy you summoned at the start was overly enthusiastic. And correspondingly, because your energy was tied up with so much arcane power, they zealously hunted down elements for you, forging the prototype of an elemental armor for you like building a palace. That actually dragged out your casting process, making the Mage Armor you were channeling too powerful — but also too heavy."

"Note that 'powerful' is not a compliment here."

"Compared to barrier magic — things like Frost Armor, or Molten Armor, those elemental shells — Mage Armor is more like an Enchantment-school spell. Enchanting power, that is. Except what's being enchanted isn't an object, but the caster himself. It's a bit like Arcane Intellect. A powerful enchantment also requires the caster to have sufficient capacity to bear it, and that's itself a mentally draining process, Mr. Sesso."

"Arcane magic isn't like the prayers priests offer to the Holy Light, that gentle force that any child or magic-naive patient can withstand. So you see where the problem lies. You have one big difference from the other apprentices: you didn't fail because your casting was imprecise. You failed because you summoned a massive amount of arcane energy that exceeded your current stage's capacity, causing your body to overload prematurely. Even when I was demonstrating for you, I didn't call upon that scale of arcane power."

"Add to that your several moments of hesitation during the casting, which doubled your energy expenditure. Watching you cast Light earlier gave me somewhat of an illusion about your energy levels. The truth is, you still fall a bit short — though that's an easy enough problem to overcome."

After hearing Marin's explanation, Jace thought it over for a moment, then ventured a question: "The spell effect was too strong to handle — so to succeed once, would I have to do it imprecisely and weaken the spell's effect?"

The old mage smiled. "Wouldn't doing it imprecisely increase the cost even more and reduce the spell's effectiveness?"

"Right," Jace said. "So then…"

"That's why I want you to take up Abjuration courses first," Marin emphasized immediately. "Learn how to weaken your spell's intensity while reducing the cost. In other words, deliberately cast a weakened spell, achieving your goal at the lightest possible price. That's a skill every seasoned Abjuration caster must master."

This reminded Jace of those mages in the game who used rank 1 Frostbolt to harass enemies, or opened with rank 1 Blizzard for area slows. Same idea — when resources are tight, you use a cheaper, weaker version of the same spell to get the job done.

Turns out the real world had its own version. Marin went on: "Why do I say Abjuration is the foundation of many schools? If you'd carefully read Master Anasterian Runeweaver's Schools of Arcane Magic — Abjuration, you'd know that a great deal of magical research has to be conducted under enormous risk, and during the process you'll frequently run into minor magical-energy runaways. One of the values of short-term barrier magic is that in situations like this, it quickly and effectively protects the caster from specific types of elemental damage — not just for combat."

"There's no need to cast a full, powerful barrier spell before every bit of research. Sometimes you're only facing minor risks — like the occasional flash, small explosion, or flare-up from combining different alchemical reagents. You'd only need a lightweight fire barrier, not a colossal wall capable of withstanding a red dragon's flame. But sometimes that wall and a thin fire-barrier film can be summoned by the very same incantation. You can think of this as casting intensity — or the force of a cast."

Jace said, "Like when I first learned Arcane Intellect and the effect I summoned only lasted a few minutes — was that a weakened spell?"

"Well," Marin said. "I think that was simply imprecise. The energy you expended was no less — maybe even more — and in the end you only produced a shoddy spell effect. I'm not even sure that qualified as Arcane Intellect. After all, I wasn't the sheep you practiced on."
