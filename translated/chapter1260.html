Chapter 1260: The Hunter, in the End…

Jace Shadowstepped across a rift in the ground, with Morophil's help clearing the cliff edge, which had already begun to crumble and give way.

The gronn ahead was striding forward in pursuit, his swollen eye sweeping left and right between the gorge. Only now did Jace get a full view of the giant. The red-brown monster's hide was covered in tumor-like clumps and rocky horns, and his spine and elbows were sheathed in layer after layer of armor-like earthen skin.

Durn panted furiously like a one-eyed brown gorilla, the countless ogre skulls strung up as ornamentation on his body clattering and swaying in the wind.

Vereesa had no intention of giving him the chance to show off on open ground. A third prismatic beam of light streaked silently straight for that eyeball. The gronn lord instinctively shut his eyes and turned his head. The arrow tore a black wound through his thick eyelid and cheek and flew off into the sky.

Durn spun around and hurled the stalagmite in his hand back toward the cliff where Vereesa stood. The ranger, as if she'd already anticipated his action, had leaped far away almost the instant the stone spear left his hand, vanishing into the treetops. The stone blast pounded that entire cliff face into rubble. Dust billowed.

Having dealt with the ranger, the gronn eagerly raised that single eye again, locking onto the green dragon circling high above. "Dragon hunt!"

Durn roared as he stepped onto the cliff and strode toward the distant slope in her direction. With every step he took, more stalagmites sprouted up around the giant's feet — some fast, some slow — as though the land of Nagrand was answering the gronn's call without restraint, supplying him with weapons to hurl.

Just as Durn reached out his thick arm once more to grope for a stalagmite of the right length, Jace finally closed to a suitable distance. Speaking the Eredun incantation through a Curse of Tongues, he channeled a Curse of Exhaustion. The gronn's wrist was immediately wrapped in sinister green fel energy. He strained hard to lift his arms and throw off the magic's grip, but couldn't break free even once. He only broke into a violent coughing fit, strings of viscous spittle dripping from his mouth.

Jace steadied himself on the shuddering ground, raised Ursula, focused his will, and — drawing on the dark power surging upward from the Shadowflame still burning in the gorge behind him — swiftly gathered a Shadow Bolt aimed at a weak point in the giant's thick hide. The dark energy wailed by his ear, as though it had already merged with Jace's killing intent.

The Shadow Bolt streaked away on a black-violet trail, carrying a sobbing, scraping whisper of arcane force, and struck the gronn squarely under the arm. Chunks of stone-like skin burst outward along with black, viscous, tar-like blood.

The giant wobbled, couldn't resist scratching at the ruptured spot, then let out a low snarl from the pain — still far short of what Jace had expected.

He could feel that the gronn possessed an extremely potent resistance to shadow. Combined with the fact that he'd just been striding calmly through a forest burning with Shadowflame, perhaps this was the very confidence that allowed the gronn to stand toe-to-toe with black dragons.

It wasn't really surprising. In the game, several of Gruul's sons had close ties to the dark magic hidden deep within the Blade's Edge Mountains — more than enough to show that the gronn race wasn't merely bound to the earth, but likely had a powerful affinity with shadow or even the Twisting Nether. Still, no matter how strong their shadow affinity, it couldn't render them completely immune to shadow magic. If it could deal damage, it could wear down his power.

Pummeled continuously by shadow magic and slowed in his movements, the gronn lord could no longer keep pursuing the green dragon in the sky. During this time he'd even been stabbed twice by the stalagmite he himself had summoned beneath his own feet, thanks to his sluggish reactions. Still, the monster braced himself against the thickest, stoutest stalagmite and fixed his single eye firmly on Jace.

The deep, dull regret radiating from this giant was as clear to the warlock as waves of black tide. Durn had realized he'd actually walked into a trap — the hunters' encirclement — and these hunters were clearly far more lethal than he'd imagined.

But at the same time, an uncontrollable hatred burned up from the depths of his heart. He was going to make a final stand.

Jace didn't think his dark shield could stop a stalagmite the size of a stout oak, let alone one conjured with the power of earth elementals — this was no ordinary lump of soil and rock. Just as he was looking for somewhere to take cover, the giant suddenly froze. The hand clutching the boulder had become stuck fast, as though glued in place.

Looking closely, layer after layer of plant roots had burrowed up from the ground and wound tight around both the stone spike and the gronn's arm. The roots creaked and groaned as they tightened, and though an occasional one snapped with a twang, it was already more than enough to give the gronn lord serious trouble.

The gronn heaved and, in fury, glanced up toward the green dragon high above who was channeling nature magic. He let out a hoarse, ragged scream — he meant to grab the bottom of the stalagmite and rip the whole thing out of the earth. But accompanied by an indistinct dwarven bellow, a silver-grey, gleaming earthen creature leaped up and swung a brilliant arc of lightning that smashed hard into the knee of one of his legs.

Amid the crackling of splitting stone, the gronn — his body streaming with black blood, his skin laced with black cracks of shadow corrosion — had no choice but to exhale and abandon his struggle to break free of the roots.

He reached for the dwarf on the ground. Though Jace couldn't see clearly through the billowing dust ahead, he wasn't worried that a Grede in Avatar form couldn't handle one of Durn's grasping hands at this point. Sure enough, after a flurry of wild swipes, one of the gronn's hands was struck hard by a bolt of lightning. He howled and drew the arm back.

Grede gave him no chance to breathe. Without hesitation he brought his hammer down on the gronn's other leg. A flash of lightning later, the gronn lord finally let out a wail of agony and collapsed like a demolished building.

The giant's body toppled toward them and crashed heavily onto the grassy slope. A head the size of a small house landed right in front of Jace.

The violent shaking of the earth made it nearly impossible to stand. The gronn's bloodshot, hideous eyeball rolled and fixed on Jace ahead. He opened his mouth — a cavern full of huge, blunt, yellowed fangs — as though meaning to swallow Jace whole before he died.

Morophil stepped forward to place herself in front of Jace. But in the end the giant only managed to spew out a blast of foul breath. Thick black blood coughed out from within and spattered across the grass.

"Gruul is king, and I am Gru—"

A glowing arrow buried itself in his pupil, cutting off his feeble murmur. Savage nature magic instantly churned the gronn's eyeball into a black, formless mess. The giant only managed a few low, gagging sounds before his upper and lower jaws slammed together with a boom, his mouth clamped shut tight.

"Are you all right, Jace?" Vereesa walked over with light steps, brushing the dust from her clothes as she came. "I'm fine. He didn't touch me," Jace said.

"Your summoned flames…" She raised a long eyebrow. "I really thought we'd actually drawn in a black dragon. Terrified me."

"And you?" Jace asked. "I saw you leap into those thickets…"

Vereesa glanced at Morophil not far away and said softly, "Whatever injuries there were, your concern has already healed them."

Jace looked down and smiled. Behind him, Grede caught up, shedding his transformed appearance as he came.

Finally he returned to his own original form. He walked around the gronn's enormous head, took one look at the eyeball Vereesa had shot through, and shouted in displeasure, "I'm the one who knocked him down!"

"If not for Jace's magic, you'd have been flattened by this monster," Vereesa said.

"Flattened is a stretch." Grede glanced sideways at the warlock, who was flicking the last of the shadow residue from his hands, then said, "But this ogre chieftain really… was tougher than I'd imagined."

The green dragon landed not far away. In a haze of pink-tinged mist she shifted back into humanoid form and came to Jace's side. Though her magical image was clean and tidy, without so much of a bead of sweat, the panic in her eyes and the slight trembling of her hands couldn't be concealed — the aftershock was intense.

"That was close…"

She managed a faint smile and looked at Vereesa. "Thank you, Vereesa. I very nearly got skewered by that stone spear."

"Don't mention it, Sona. It was your idea that saved us," Vereesa said.

Jace nodded. "If this creature hadn't had such a thing about dragons, we'd never have lured him out of that cave."

"Your magic helped too, Jace." Sona said, brow lowered. "That gronn was no simple fool. I believe now that he really could hunt those weaker black dragons."

"I'd say it's not just black dragons he can hunt." Grede patted the corner of the gronn's mouth and said in a low voice. "Good thing we didn't charge in there to fight him. Think about what would've happened going toe-to-toe with him in that cramped cave — I might not necessarily have bought it, but you few with your slender arms and legs…"

This time Vereesa raised no objection. After all, Durn had smashed through those cliff faces and trees as effortlessly as toppling toys and building blocks. It was hard to say what getting hit by those fists and boulders would feel like.

By now, Jorin Death-eye and the Bleeding Hollow hunter had caught up. It was clear they'd had a rough time in the collapsed ravine — covered head to toe in dust and muck.

"You really killed a son of Gruul…" Jorin's single eye went wide as he stared at the dead gronn before him.

"Unbelievable," the orc hunter murmured in Orcish. "I should bring this news to all the Bleeding Hollow at once, Jorin."

"Tell them it was Alliance soldiers who killed Durn and shattered the Boulderfists' will," Jorin reminded him in Orcish.

"Yes." The hunter thumped a fist to his chest and ran back toward the gorge.

"Thank you." Jorin turned back and switched to Common. "You can't imagine what this means for the Bleeding Hollow — for all the Mag'har in Nagrand. This news will reach Garadar, and then Greatmother Geyah and countless villagers of every clan living there will hail you as heroes."

"We don't care about being heroes to the Horde," Vereesa said. "Now you should keep your promise and take us to Highmaul."

"Yes, Lady Vereesa Windrunner." Jorin bowed his head respectfully. "But before that, we need to make a trip to Durn's lair and see what's inside. The Boulderfist chieftains didn't just offer food to the gronn — they offered many artifacts as well. Those artifacts may prove useful when we face the Burning Legion. Best to go in now while the Boulderfists inside are in chaos, before the chieftains who realize Durn is dead grab the best things for themselves. Is anyone injured?"

"No." Grede wiped the black blood from his war hammer, hung it at his waist, and said in a loud voice, "This fellow really was something, but compared to us he still fell a bit short."

Jorin studied the people before him. The last trace of the pride he'd carried as the son of the Bleeding Hollow chieftain had mostly dissipated. He nodded. "Then let's go."
