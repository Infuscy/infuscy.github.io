## Chapter 1261: Relics of Highmaul

Without Jace to guide it, the Shadowflame that had been spreading through the Boulderfist Pass died away quickly. By now only billowing smoke rose ceaselessly from the gorge. The Boulderfist ogres had fled or died — not a single one left standing in the pass.

Jorin Death-eye strode without hesitation past those corpses, severely withered and withered by shadow corruption, and ran toward the cave. The Shadowflame hadn't reached as far as the clearing before the cave, but even here the vegetation had clearly been affected by the shadow radiation — the plants near the gorge entrance had already begun to yellow and wither — and the ogres here had mostly fled as well.

"I thought we'd become the kings of the ogres," Grede said, watching a small blue ogre in the distance scrambling desperately up a high cliff. "After all, we killed their king."

"The gronn are their gods. Even if he's only Gruul's son," Jorin said. "With the intelligence of these single-brained ogres, it's hard for them to grasp the death of a gronn lord, so most of them have been scared out of their wits. Actually, it's the same for many Mag'har. Long, long ago, our ancestors worshipped the gronn as gods too, just like the ogres."

Durn's size and strength had actually taken Jace somewhat by surprise, and now he couldn't stop himself from imagining just how powerful the King of the Gronn, Gruul, must be.

Gruul's strength was more than a full tier above his sons'. Factor in the number of black dragons he'd killed, and Durn didn't even rank among his sons. Thinking back to that nearly unstoppable momentum and brute force Durn had shown at the very beginning, before Jace's Curse of Exhaustion and shadow magic had worn him down…

The "grappling" scene the Sons of Lothar had described between him and Deathwing no longer seemed so hard to imagine.

Up ahead, the entrance to the gronn's lair had been smashed open in a wide gash, broadened by a good margin since they'd first come in, exposing damp, fresh earth.

A two-headed ogre clad in thick hide robes was hugging a bundle and waddling out, his layers of fat trembling. He ran headlong into the group just as they were about to go in. Everyone recognized him at a glance — he was one of the sorcerers who'd been sitting inside conferring with the other ogre chieftains.

The ogre sorcerer saw the people before him and without hesitation thrust out his hands as if to cast some spell. A brilliant arcane sphere blossomed before him — but Vereesa's arcane-glowing arrow pierced it clean through and buried itself in the leather guard over his chest.

The arrow punched through him and slammed into the cliff face behind, exploding in a cloud of dust. The sphere scattered into the air and popped apart. The ogre's two heads stared helplessly at each other. The whole of him sat down on his backside and collapsed onto the belly fat spilling over his lap, the contents of his bundle scattering across the ground.

Vereesa lowered her bow and examined those things with a grimace. "What in the world are those…"

"Gronn-Eye gems." Jorin walked over and picked up one of the yellow-green gemstones.

Grede picked one up in surprise. "Such a tiny gronn? Then how big a gem would Durn's eyeball turn into?"

"The gem is called a Gronn-Eye — it doesn't mean it's actually a gronn's eyeball." Jorin explained as he sifted through the pile. "Ogre sorcerers believe these gems, being intimately connected to the power of earth, grow even stronger when placed beside a gronn and amplified by the gronn's own power."

"So plenty of ogres have been exploiting the gronn too," Vereesa said.

"Yes," Jorin said. "The two-headed ogres understand that gronn are merely a race of naturally powerful giants. There's no such thing as 'divinity' about them."

Grede stowed a few in his pack. "Damn it, we should have gone to Highmaul first and come here later. Now I'm worried these heavy gems might slow us down on the next leg."

"I'll contact Garadar and have them send people together with my Bleeding Hollow to secure this gronn's lair and make sure the ogres don't steal more." Jorin pointed into the cave. "Let's go inside first and find the most valuable things."

The dwarf eyed Death-eye's back with a suspicious sidelong glance. Jace also felt that by the time they returned from Highmaul, God knew how much of the stuff inside would have been hauled away by the orcs.

The group entered the cave. A rich stench of blood wafted out. In the passage, swarmed by flies, lay an ogre who'd been cruelly ground to utter pieces. The corpse had been dragged from deep inside all the way to the entrance in a wet, glistening smear. White bone fragments, blood, and a chaotic smear of blue, yellow, and red were daubed across the cave walls.

This must have been some ogre chieftain inside — when Durn had charged out in fury, he hadn't had time to dodge and had been casually ground into this state. Even Vereesa and Grede, who'd grown accustomed to grisly sights among the Amani trolls, couldn't help but frown at the sight.

They crossed the shattered gate and entered Durn's dwelling, enduring the foul stench. Looking inside, the chamber — quite cramped compared to the gronn lord's frame — was piled high with shattered bones, dried black mud, gleaming gold, gemstones of every color, and magic stones.

The most eye-catching thing in the cave was a desiccated adult black dragon skull hanging from a high point. Generally, being near a black dragon's corpse meant being hit by an acrid reek of shadow-tainted blood, but in this reeking cave Jace could barely detect much of the black dragon's scent. It had probably been dead for a very long time.

Sona gazed at that skull with complicated emotions, as though already picturing herself hanging up there. Grede rushed over excitedly, scooped up a gold coin from the ground, and then sighed.

"Guess what, Jace."

"Ogre Empire 'gold' coins," Jace said.

"Exactly." Grede tossed it back. "Now we know where those strange coins by the River Neltharion came from. This junk isn't worth a damn."

As he spoke, he hefted up an enormous two-handed battle-axe, bigger than Jace's whole body and covered with a hide, and tried to lift it. "Good axe. Even enchanted. Unfortunately not suited for my swinging. Death-eye, can you use something like this?"

"You're asking an orc if he can use a battle-axe?" Jorin said. "If a Warsong heard that, he'd be itching to fight you on the spot. But I'll admit I don't have much experience killing with a greataxe this size. Even an ogre would be better off wielding it with two hands. It's too big for me."

Jorin Death-eye wasn't the least bit shy about admitting his shortcomings in close-combat technique, though he didn't say it with much seriousness either. He ignored most of the ogre treasures and the weapons lying around, his gaze sweeping back and forth over a great pile of shattered bones as though searching deliberately for something.

Grede picked up an ogre cleaver that looked like a chunk of stone, swung it back and forth a couple of times, then chopped it down hard on a massive beast bone at his side with a clanging boom.

The sound bounced rapidly through the cave. Jace immediately felt as though some unusual, terrified emotion had drifted out from a corner.

Terror… that was the easiest emotion for him to pick up on. He glanced back at Morophil behind him and said quietly, "Go take a look."

He turned his attention back to Jorin and Grede, who were rummaging through the bone pile for treasures. Before long, a stream of demonic curses rang out from the corner.

"Come out."

A small two-headed ogre, still shimmered in illusion light and shadow, crawled out from the pile of bones. This fellow had been hiding there with some invisibility magic for that long?

He was one of those ogre chieftains too — very short, but hung with even more sorcerous ornaments. One of his heads even wore an enchanted iron crown, suggesting he probably ranked even higher than the ogre sorcerer Vereesa had shot through at the cave mouth.

He dumped the vessels and magic runes he'd been cradling onto the ground and said in extremely broken, muddled Orcish, "I leave you everything. Let me go."

"Plead your case to the ancestors of the Horde."

With that, Jorin clenched both fists hard, his sharp nails digging into his palms. The ogre sorcerer howled and dropped to his knees. Blood gurgled from both his heads' eyes and ears at once. He clawed frantically at those hollows, and in the end only collapsed with a whimpering sob. His heavy robe was quickly soaked through with blood.

Everyone stared dumbly as this Death-eye carried out his ghastly blood magic, silent, saying nothing.

Jorin stepped forward, rifled through the still-twitching ogre's body, and drew a heavy, stone-pillar-like scepter from beneath his wide robe. At the top of the scepter was an ugly magic gem, emitting a faint, murky, yellowish light.

"It really was here." Jorin turned back and held it up. "Nearly let it slip away again."

"What is that?" Jace asked.

"The Nullifier's Rod," Jorin said. "Highmaul has a very secretive group of ogres who call themselves the Nullifiers, claiming to fear no form of magic whatsoever — arcane, shadow, fel, or even the elemental forces. I'm not certain they truly possess such a strange ability, but the Nullifiers do manipulate the magic source beneath Highmaul, making it serve Overlord Margol and his sorcerous house."

"When we followed Cho'gall in storming Highmaul, several Nullifiers abandoned Margol and fled. The rod of the Nullifiers' leader, Kragg, vanished along with them. Afterward we hunted them across Nagrand. Though we killed every Nullifier we knew of…"

"…the rod was still nowhere to be found. That's when I suspected it had likely been brought by the ogres to this gronn lord. But once Gul'dan led the Horde's elite through the Dark Portal, there was no one left to confirm it. Until now."

He gripped the "rod" and continued. "If that Eredar intends to protect that Burning Legion codex with sufficient magic, the best way would be to use Highmaul's own power to sustain a protective barrier — just as the Nullifiers protected their Overlord. In that case, this rod might just serve as a key, everyone."

"You should have told us about this earlier," Grede said.

"I wasn't certain it was here at all," Jorin said. "If I'd told you first and we didn't find it, the precious trust between us would have been on the verge of collapse before we even entered Highmaul."
