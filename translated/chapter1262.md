## Chapter 1262: The Bleeding Hollow's Gift

By the time they returned to the Bleeding Hollow village, it was already the middle of the night. From a distance Jace could see the reddish glow of fires burning in the village, and hear chanting voices rising and falling with the leaping flames.

The orcs were celebrating Durn's death. The village was safe — Jace knew the Bleeding Hollow hunter had already carried the news of their victory back here.

But the chanting, whether in rhythm or in its shrill pitch, didn't sound like singing. It was more like some kind of cult ritual, and it set his teeth on edge. Add in the shadows dancing beside the bonfires in the dark, and the feeling only got stronger.

Yolin Deadeye led a few men toward the village. They passed by the hut where the group had been staying earlier without slowing down, and Jace understood that the Bleeding Hollow chief's son intended to bring them into the village itself.

"Are you sure this is a good idea, Yolin?" Jace asked, eyeing the village.

"You certainly can't trust a Bleeding Hollow orc to help you become a hero of Nagrand out of the goodness of his heart. And honestly, I do have my own selfish reasons."

As he walked, he continued, "The Mag'har can hardly join the Alliance or the draenei in attacking our blood-drinking brethren. But the Mag'har have absolutely no intention of helping the demons against you either. I want to show you that — and at the same time, let my kin gradually understand the Alliance's position, so there won't be conflicts. I don't want to see that day come."

"You don't seem very confident in your 'kin,'" Vereesa said.

Yolin glanced at the elf. "They won't win. They'll get nothing from the Burning Legion."

"How do you know?" Grede said. "The orcs have assembled thousands at Hellfire Citadel."

The orc said nothing. He looked at the dwarf, then fell silent and kept walking.

Grede raised his eyebrows slightly toward Jace, then glanced at Vereesa, as if he wanted to complain about this guy putting on mysterious airs again. But Jace had a pretty good idea why Yolin Deadeye was so certain.

Like his father, he could see omens of the future through that blind eye.

After they entered the Bleeding Hollow village, the orcs' chanting quieted. They pulled aside to open a path, turning to look over in unison. There was no welcome, but not much suspicion or hostility either — they simply stared.

Up ahead, the altar on the clearing was piled with the innards of creatures Jace couldn't identify. Blood ran down from the platform, looking unnaturally red in the torchlight, which only made the whole atmosphere stranger.

Grede probably hadn't expected this kind of "welcome ceremony," because his expression was a little awkward. The rest of them weren't too unsettled — after all, they'd been in and out of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar enough times. Compared to those Atal'ai, this crew of Bleeding Hollow orcs was small potatoes.

Never mind that Molofier was right here beside them. Dark as these blood-magic-wielding orcs were, they couldn't hold a candle to her and her kin.

Deadeye didn't lead them toward the altar. Instead, he followed the path running down the middle of the village all the way to the end, while the orc villagers fell in behind them as one, arriving together before the door of a dark cave.

"Please, go in, all of you," Yolin said.

"What's this about?" Grede asked. "You're not planning to gouge out our eyes too, are you?"

Yolin couldn't help but laugh twice. "I don't think everyone here could team up to beat you, never mind forcibly digging out your eyes. And most importantly… not just anyone can complete that sacred ritual, Gryphon Rider."

Grede nodded, peered inside, and said, "All right," then walked in.

Jace, Vereesa, and Sola saw the dwarf go in and exchanged glances before following him inside. It was a black underground chamber, trimmed up quite neat and orderly. At the far end was a circular platform used for some kind of ritual, with blood channels carved into it forming a simple magical array. There was already some thick, viscous blood in them.

Just then, an impressively massive orc walked out of the deeper cave within. "Massive" wasn't quite the word — he was deformed, deformed enough that Jace was reminded of the impossibly enormous "dread troll" he'd seen in the pool at the bottom of the Temple of Atal'Hakkar.

This deformed orc's tusks jutted outward like an elekk's, and he waddled even more clumsily than a gronn. His body was painted all over with white war paint, and on his chest was a circular eye symbol. When Vereesa and the others saw this monstrous orc, their eyes went wide.

Yolin took a black bowl covered in runes from the monster's huge hand, then began chanting some kind of orcish dialect incantation that Jace couldn't understand.

The torches and candles in the blood cavern flickered as if blown by a wind, though there was no draft in here. Yolin bent down and used the bowl to catch a measure of blood from the end of one of the channels, then respectfully held it out.

"The Bleeding Hollow's thanks," Yolin said in a low voice.

Vereesa examined the bowl and exchanged a glance with Grede. The ranger tried asking, "You want us to drink it?"

"Yes," Yolin said.

Jace took the bowl. "I'll drink it for them."

Yolin nodded in silence. Vereesa grabbed his wrist. "I can do it, Jace."

"There's nothing wrong with this blood. It's a bit thick, but at least there's nothing demonic in it, Vere." He looked at her and said quietly, "Relax. I'm a warlock — I'm better suited to handling this kind of thing than you are."

The elf hesitated for a moment. Jace had already downed the entire bowl of blood in front of the other companions and all the Bleeding Hollow onlookers.

Honestly, it went down better than Silia's ghost-detection potion. It wasn't the worst thing he'd ever drunk. It was just that when he swallowed, his throat felt dry as if someone had painted it with tar, and it made him cough in spite of himself.

After Jace managed to drink down every last drop, the Bleeding Hollow villagers who had been watching in silence all started shouting. Judging by their emotions, it was probably a gesture of approval and gratitude. But in that gloomy blood corridor, amid the foul stench of blood and the firelight, it was hard to feel at ease.

But Yolin Deadeye's expression didn't relax. He kept his eyes fixed on Jace, until—

Suddenly, a burst of blazing flame devoured his field of vision. Jace couldn't help but blink. But the cheering was still going on, and he could even hear Grede's voice off to the side asking, "How's the taste?" — but besides the burning flames, he couldn't see anything at all.

He knew this was just some kind of vision, so he didn't panic. He just reached back and forth with his hands, trying to grab onto something to steady himself. That was when he felt Vereesa tightly grip his hand, supporting him and helping him regain his balance, which settled his nerves a little. "Jace… what's wrong, Jace?"

Jace shook his head, and sound and sight returned to normal, going from blurry and chaotic to gradually clear.

Yolin was still standing in front of him. The Bleeding Hollow orcs' cheering had turned into another low "chorus." He turned back. Vereesa was looking at him anxiously, calling his name.

"If there's something wrong with that bowl of blood, Yolin Deadeye," Vereesa said, staring at the orc in a low voice, "I will kill every single orc here."

"I swear it on my eyes, Farstrider." Yolin pointed to a small jar placed in the deep center of the blood cavern and said, "That bowl of blood won't harm Jace Sesso in any way."

"It's fine, Vere." Jace gripped the elf's hand tightly. "Just tastes a little strange." Seeing that Jace had recovered, Vereesa finally let out a breath and hugged him tight. "Hey, Yolin," Grede said. "Can we leave now?"

"You may. The ritual is complete."

"What ritual?" Sola asked.

"Why, a ritual of gratitude," Yolin said, looking at the green dragon.

Leaving the blood cavern, Nagrand's pink night suddenly felt crisp and clean. The air was incredibly pure — even the smell of blood from the innards on the altar seemed a good deal fresher.

Sola confirmed that Jace hadn't caught any blood plague or poison; his life signs were perfectly normal. Vereesa and Grede could finally set their minds at ease, since you couldn't argue with a green dragon. But everyone was still a little shaken by what had happened in that blood cavern.

"What the hell kind of thanks was that," Grede muttered. "I thought he was inviting us in for a feast!"

Behind them, Yolin was giving instructions to a few of his trusted men about leaving the village for the west immediately. Since he was speaking some orcish dialect, Jace could only catch the rough idea.

He glanced at Molofier. The sayaad didn't seem to know what had happened either; she was just curiously observing Jace's reaction. The imp was rocking back and forth in his demon bag to the rhythm of their footsteps, not sensing anything amiss.

That fire — only he had seen it. What was this, a Bleeding Hollow divination omen?

That night, the group sat in the orcs' tent. The whole space still reeked of the foul smell of Jace's shadowflame, thoroughly overpowering the stink of everyone's sweat. But after countless adventures, the group had long since grown used to it.

Still, after what had happened in the blood cavern, none of them had much appetite. Vereesa looked at Jace, who was lost in thought, and finally couldn't help asking, "Did you see something?"

"Yes," Jace said. "I'm just thinking about how to tell the others."

"Did you see how you die?" Grede asked. "Like DeadEye?"

"I…" Jace shook his head. "I don't know. I'm not quite sure, but I don't think so. Remember what Yolin said — not just anyone can complete the Deadeye ritual. Don't forget, the chief of the Bleeding Hollow had to sacrifice an eye to see his ultimate fate. I don't think drinking a bowl of blood would produce a similar effect."

"So what did you see, Jace?" Sola asked.

"Fire," Jace said. "Just fire, plain and simple. I felt like I was being burned on a pyre, but I couldn't feel any pain from the heat. What do you make of it?"

"No pain from the heat…" Sola frowned slightly, thinking.

"Nothing else? Just fire?" Grede asked.

"Couldn't see anything besides the fire," Jace said. "Couldn't feel anything either. But I can only say this fire… doesn't seem like anything good."

They looked at each one another, but no one had an answer.

"Whatever this fire is, I'm willing to be burned to death in it with you, master," Molofier said with a smile. The elf looked over at the succubus in shock. Jace immediately grabbed her and said, "I'd actually prefer to stay alive, Molofier."

"Ah!" Grede toppled onto his heavy cloak. "Let's just get to sleep. When we get to Highmaul, we'll need to watch out for anything on fire."
