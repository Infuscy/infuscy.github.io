## Chapter 1263: The Old High Arak City

Jace had expected this night to be full of ominous nightmares — maybe waking up once, maybe lying awake all night, with the vision from the blood cavern flickering past his eyes again and again like a movie.

But no. He slept soundly, as peacefully and comfortably as if Sola had hypnotized him. By the time the next day came and he opened his eyes to watch the figures of Bleeding Hollow orcs who'd risen early to head out to work, drifting through the morning smoke, he even wondered if everything that had happened in that cave last night had just been a dream.

It had truly been Yolin Deadeye's gift — an omen, a warning, brought by the dark ritual of the Bleeding Hollow clan. Not his own ability, so once it passed, it passed.

What exactly this fire foretold, Jace hadn't failed to consider.

Perhaps danger within Highmaul. Perhaps something to do with Karabor or Hellfire Citadel. Or maybe the machinations of the black dragonflight.

Or perhaps it came from within him — elemental fire, the flames of the red dragonflight, demon fire. Not all demons wielded green fel fire; many demons were masters of pure flame too.

Or maybe an even more distant omen — the Molten Core, the Firelord Ragnaros?

For some reason, he always felt none of these quite connected to that surging, crimson fire. This fire carried boundless fury, staring straight into his eyes, yet it seemed hollow, so that he couldn't feel any heat from it, never mind pain.

"When you see your own death, can you feel pain?" Riding on his war wolf, Jace couldn't help asking Yolin Deadeye beside him.

"What?" the orc looked over. "You want to know how I'm going to die?"

"No," Jace said. "I'm not trying to pry into your secrets, Yolin. I'm just curious how much information the Bleeding Hollow visions actually contain — things like smell, touch, sound, even… emotion."

"Ah, you really did see something," Yolin said.

Jace nodded. "Yes, I did."

"I know your human mages and scholars like to study everything to death — want to take a ruler and measure the whole world with it."

The war wolf's gait rocked Yolin's body. He glanced out at the Nagrand plains, bleached yellow by the sun, and said, "I'm not judging the way you do things. But an omen like that… is something too precious to measure, Jace Sesso. Honestly, I wasn't even sure you'd see anything at all after drinking that blood."

Jace swallowed involuntarily. The orc went on: "If you yourself can't make out its meaning, then there's no way anyone else can help you — not me. Even if my father were still alive, he couldn't help you much either."

Yolin was right about that. There truly weren't many people in this world who could foresee the future, and even most of them could hardly tell whether what they saw was a misleading illusion or a genuine omen.

For a transmigrator like Jace, who already knew so much in advance, it was easy to overlook the significance of prophecy. It wasn't until now, when he'd encountered a glimpse that truly qualified as an omen for him, that he understood just how "absurd" it really was.

"So this can't be avoided," Jace said.

"I can't answer that either," Yolin said. "And I can tell — you don't even know whether it's a good thing or a bad thing for you."

"That's true," Jace said.

"Hey." Grede, riding his war wolf at the front, said, "Look over there."

Jace thought something unexpected had happened up ahead, but after following the dwarf's pointing finger, he noticed an abrupt hill-like silhouette, like a white elekk, materializing somewhere within a boundless, flat golden-yellow plain that stretched to the horizon.

"Oshu'gun." Yolin pointed to a lake off to the right of the hill and said, "From here we just follow this road forward. Four or five days' travel and we'll reach Highmaul."

"Still that far?" Vereesa turned around, glancing at the map the Alliance Expedition had given her. "This map says once you spot this gray-white 'mountain,' the ogre city isn't far off."

"The Alliance map isn't wrong, per se. Because before long we'll be able to see their walls," Yolin said. "But Highmaul isn't a lone fortress — it's an entire city. In the past, tens of thousands of ogres gathered here, Farstrider. So even after entering that city, it'll still take us a long time to reach the area occupied by the Burning Legion. And now that the great catastrophe of Draenor has destroyed part of the city, and other parts have been ruined by the Horde's army and the demons, the roads in there are even harder to travel."

"But that also gives us a chance to sneak in," Vereesa said.

"Yes," Yolin said.

"What's that?" Sola asked, pointing at the western sky.

Everyone raised their heads. As the war wolves advanced step by step, leaving a hill behind, the western sky fully revealed itself before them.

Far beyond the countless drifting floating islands, coiled deep within the cloud layers that rose like mountains, was an enormous spiral. It was shadow energy swirling in the void — of that there was no doubt. But even knowing what it was, the uncanny spiral trail covering nearly half the sky still made your skin crawl to look at. It was like a black hole that constantly sucked away your spirit and attention, intent on making you part of the spiral itself — so that you both couldn't bear to keep staring and couldn't help but stare.

"That was left behind by the Staff of Sargeras," Yolin said evenly. "The spatial tearing force released from Karabor — or rather, the Black Temple — reverberates and collides at the very edge of the world it can reach, forming exactly these magical vortices. It was these vortices that shattered the Zangar Sea and the Shielded Sea, destroyed Farlon, and crushed Frostfire Ridge and Gorgrond together into the Blade's Edge Mountains, churning like waves. And those, along with those spatial portals, are merely its aftershocks."

"I hope I never run into that thing in my lifetime," Grede muttered under his breath.

Jace knew very well that after Ner'zhul used that staff to open portals and flee Draenor, he hadn't escaped Kil'jaeden's clutches. Instead, he was caught, killed, his soul forged into an icy prison and hurled into Northrend, laying the groundwork for the Burning Legion's next invasion of Azeroth.

When they'd been carried by Sola from Kalimdor back to Dalaran, they'd passed through Northrend along the way. They'd already encountered a nerubian there claiming that a "dead king" was waging war against them, which confirmed the matter from the side. So the Staff of Sargeras that had left Draenor alongside Ner'zhul had also ended up back in Kil'jaeden's hands.

Sooner or later, this staff would once again become a tool for Kil'jaeden to invade Azeroth… so Grede probably had a hard time avoiding it.

But as long as the Expedition could return to Azeroth, with the help of the Sons of Lothar, the Alliance had no reason to lose the demon invasion that might prove unavoidable in the future. Jace believed that firmly.

Thinking of this, he couldn't help lowering his head slightly and letting out a soft breath. Up ahead, Vereesa was turning to look at Jace, her expression seeming to carry some worry about the power of the Staff of Sargeras. But just then, as if infected by the look of relaxation on Jace's face, she smiled too, flicked her reins, and urged her war wolf to speed up a little.

Over the next two days, whether by day or by night, resting or traveling, the moment they raised their heads they could see the magical vortex swirling in the sky above the direction of Highmaul, like a sinister, unblinking eye watching them advance. So the mood in the group never really lifted.

By noon of the third day, after crossing a river and climbing to the top of a high slope, Jace gazed out at a broad dirt road still clearly visible in the distance, piled with rubble and broken walls. At the road's end, a gray wall cut across the field of vision, and farther beyond the walls, seated deep within like bald old men, a cluster of dilapidated brownish-yellow towers and domed houses sat motionless, devoid of any sign of life at all.

Yolin jumped off the wolf's back, and everyone dismounted from their war wolves as well. Yolin patted the huge war wolf's head and said in Orcish, "Go. Take your brothers and sisters back to the village."

After the war wolves left, Yolin straightened his pack and pointed at the badly damaged short wall and said, "There are still many ogres hiding in this city, right among those ruins. That eredar, Batas, couldn't be bothered to wipe them all out. These weak, wandering ogres don't dare provoke the demons either, but they're still a threat to us. The demons are very likely to realize outsiders have entered Highmaul by tracking their movements, and become alert. That would cause trouble for our operation."

"Besides Batas, how many demons are there in Highmaul?" Vereesa asked, giving Molofier beside Jace a sidelong glance. "Or rather, are there any powerful demon lords — the kind worthy of bearing a Mark of Sargeras?"

"I don't think there are any demons with a Mark of Sargeras anymore," Yolin said. "But among these ruins there may still be succubi, doomguard demons, and roaming packs of imps serving Batas. As we approach Highmaul, we especially need to watch out for succubi — you ought to know better than me why…"

Grede pulled out a pair of goggles from his pack. "So now we have to break out our grand lord's treasures."
