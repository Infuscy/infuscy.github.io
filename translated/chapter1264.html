Chapter 1264: Black Orcs?

Everyone except Jace and his two demons put on the goggles Kurdlan had given them. This time they really did look like a proper special ops squad going deep behind enemy lines.

Yolin Deadeye led the group to a crumbling section of wall. He looked at the brick seams, now overgrown with weeds and vines, and said, "When we entered Highmaul back then, Grommash charged through the main gate with his burning hatred of ogres, while my father led us — the Bleeding Hollow warriors and hunters — in through here. It was hidden enough that it didn't draw much attention. Margok didn't even know where the Bleeding Hollow had come from until his castle fell."

"You said earlier that Grommash's son — that orc chieftain in Garadar — wasn't willing to help you deal with Durn," Grede said. "But now you're saying their clan has a deep hatred of ogres? What's the story there?"

"His mother died at the hands of ogres," Yolin said. "But Garrosh hadn't been born long at the time, so he probably has no memory of his mother. Or maybe his father never talked much about her… But you're right, dwarf — Garrosh really doesn't act like a traditional clan member. At least not like a Warsong."

"If you ask me, he's not even as good as an elf," Grede said, pointing back at Vereesa, who was picking her way through the weeds. "You know how these elves dealt with the trolls who killed their kin and friends? I'll tell you — even you blood-obsessed Bleeding Hollow would have nightmares."

"I experienced that firsthand the other night," Yolin Deadeye said. "I was genuinely worried something would go wrong after Jace Sesso drank the blood, and the Windrunner lady would slaughter the entire village. I could tell she was fully prepared to do it."

"I'm sorry, Deadeye. I didn't mean it like that," Vereesa said.

"No need to apologize, my lady," Yolin turned his head and said. "Before the founding of the Horde, the divide between the Bleeding Hollow and the other clans was bigger than you can imagine. We've long since grown accustomed to the fear and wariness outsiders feel toward our way of life. It's understandable."

Vereesa nodded, and Grede said, "Once we reach Highmaul, what's the next move? You're an orc, two of you can turn into orcs, plus two demons — you can all pass yourselves off as Shadow Council and walk right in. But there's no way me and the little elf can disguise ourselves."

"We can't all go in," Jace said. "If that demonic tome is really being treated as a relic and enshrined in the depths of Highmaul by Batas the way Yolin described, then even if we manage to find it, the moment we steal it, the magic left behind is very likely to alert the entire fortress."

"Just like the vaults in Dalaran?" Grede said. "What about that staff? That Withered Staff — can't it manipulate Highmaul's magic? Can't we use it to slip in quietly, without anyone being the wiser…"

"Of course I hope this staff can help us pull it off without anyone noticing," Yolin said, hefting the Withered Staff, wrapped in hides to block its magical glow. "But first of all, I'm not an ogre warlock, never mind a Withered one. I've read plenty of High Arak magical texts, but that doesn't mean I can wield this staff with precision… So we still need to be ready for a real fight."

Jace said, "So you two will be our outside support — or come in and save our skins if things go wrong. Exactly how we play it will depend on what the inside of Highmaul looks like, how the demons are guarding the place. In the end we might need Sola to fly us out under dream-shroud to slip pursuit."

Sola nodded. Yolin rubbed his stomach and took a nervous deep breath. Jace glanced at him. "What's wrong?"

"I've never been carried by a dragon before," Yolin said, stealing a glance at Sola. "A little worried about getting my belly torn open."

"If it really comes to flying out of here, I'll allow you to climb on my back, orc — but just this once," Sola said. Yolin said, somewhat surprised, "This is my honor, noble green dragon."

After passing through the wall and entering the inner districts, the group felt as if they'd stepped into a giant's country built out of giant stones. Everything was oversized — doors, windows, steps, walls, even the chairs by the roadside.

It was obvious that the rulers of the Higharak Empire had once genuinely run this city as a thriving metropolis. The infrastructure was remarkably complete, even now that it had fallen into such disrepair.

There were also distinctly ogre features — like the iron cages everywhere, and fighting pits of varying sizes dug into the ground like pits. Presumably because the ogres at the bottom of the ladder were both stupid and hard to manage, the troublemakers all had to be locked up as much as possible. Hot-blooded soldiers were regularly shoved into the pits to fight each other and burn off steam, so they wouldn't take it out on the ruling class of the city — the two-headed ogres.

Of course, this wasn't a management style unique to the Higharak ogre empire, but the number of fighting pits and prisons here really was a bit much.

After walking all morning, Jace and the others had spotted at least three of them, and the cages were all over the streets.

But aside from the huge ones clearly meant to hold ogres, there were also many smaller cages — clearly meant to hold the ogres' slaves.

Jace knew that ogres had always hunted orcs, dragging them into the cities as slaves. Even though the Higharak Empire, which had once ruled over half of Draenor, had collapsed because of the orcish rebellion, even now — long after Highmaul itself had fallen — organized ogres all over the world still constantly schemed to capture orcs and other races as slaves.

After crossing a broad street, up ahead at the bottom of a flight of steps lay the skeleton of an orc clad in dark leather armor.

Grede kicked the orc's boot and said, "Your skin isn't green, Yolin. How are you going to pass yourself off as a Shadow Council orc?"

"Hood up and face covered, just like a regular Shadow Council cultist. Nothing will stand out," Yolin answered. "It's dim inside Highmaul. As long as direct sunlight isn't shining on our faces, it won't be easy to make out the details. When I first came here with them over a decade ago, plenty of orcs who hadn't drunk the blood fought alongside those who had."

Just then Vereesa raised a finger. "Listen." Grede whispered, "Ogres or orcs?"

"Demons," Molofier said.

"Hide," Jace said.

The group immediately darted across the street and into a stone house opposite. After a good while, Jace finally heard the muffled sound of something stepping on bricks in the distance.

Everyone fell silent. Molofier went invisible at the door to observe. Soon after she reappeared and beckoned to Jace. He crept over and stole a glance outside, then saw a group of figures passing through the street in the distance.

At the front were several prowling felhounds. When he saw their tentacles whipping back and forth through the air, Jace instinctively held his breath — even though they were so far away he could barely make them out, he was terrified that even a trace of shadow aura would be detected by those keen-brained beasts.

But following right behind those felhounds were several figures of pure black.

Jace rubbed his eyes and looked carefully, but still couldn't make out what those dark silhouettes were. Under the brilliant afternoon sunlight, those dark figures were simply like cut-out blocks of black, with no detail at all to allow anyone to identify them.

Yolin looked carefully and shook his head, puzzled.

Vereesa crept over cautiously, but when she narrowed her eyes and peered closely, the arcane light in her eyes glowed slightly — and one of the felhounds suddenly raised its tentacles in alarm and stopped in its tracks.

Before Grede could even crane his neck in curiosity, the three of them immediately pulled their heads back. Vereesa yanked the dwarf down too, and even Molofier went invisible and ducked behind the wall. When the footsteps resumed, everyone finally relaxed a little.

They'd only just entered Highmaul a short while ago. Going by what Deadeye said, it might still take a day and a night to reach the castle. If they were exposed now, things would get ugly fast.

"What was that black stuff?" Jace asked.

"Orcs," Vereesa said. "At least judging by the way they walk and carry themselves, they're definitely a group of orcs. But I don't know what dark magic is being used to obscure their appearance…"

Hearing Vereesa say this, Jace remembered the orcs he'd encountered when playing the game and entering the Twilight Ridge in Nagrand — the little bit of Highmaul that remained after it collapsed into the void, where the portal sat.

The orc warlocks of Twilight Ridge seemed to use a magic similar to Shadowform, pouring shadow energy through their bodies to become shadow-like existences, in order to strengthen their affinity with the Shadow.

But in the game those warlocks were just a bunch of trash mobs. Jace had never bothered to learn the origins of their magic. "Do you know what this is?" he asked Yolin.

"I'm not sure," Yolin said. "Last time I was here, I hadn't seen the Shadow Council members use this kind of magic. I don't know how they do it."

"Why don't we follow them and see what they're up to," Grede said. "They've got felhounds," Sola said.

"Yes, and just now I used a little arcane magic to try to see more clearly, and I nearly got caught by that felhound," Vereesa said.

"But felhounds can't identify a damaged Mark of Sargeras," Jace said, looking at the succubus. "Back in Auchindoun, the felhounds around those warlocks didn't dare even look at her."

"Go ask them, Molofier. Find out how they do this magic."

"No problem, master," Molofier said, drawing closer. "But walking that whole distance costs a lot of power, and if I have to use mental magic…"

"Stop jabbering and get going," Jace said, pushing the succubus straight out the door. This made Molofier stagger two steps, laugh softly, steady herself, and then look in the direction the orcs and felhounds had left with a feigned, slightly helpless sigh.

The demon brushed aside a strand of black hair and walked forward hoofstep by hoofstep. Jace stole a glance at the group behind him. Yolin was looking at him in surprise, as if curious about exactly how he and this succubus got along. Vereesa had already gone quiet off to one side, staring out the door without a word.
