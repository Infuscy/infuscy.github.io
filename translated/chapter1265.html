Chapter 1265: Dark Form

Nighttime in Highmaul wasn't as quiet as the Bleeding Hollow village. After the cries of windrocs and other birds, the chirping of insects, and the roars of nameless beasts in the high mountains had all died down, a rumbling like distant thunder would sound from time to time.

Void energy scraping and colliding with the edges of shattered land, the pulling force of powerful magic and the arcane energy rampaging across the sky tugging at each other — the ethereal reverberations never ceased…

Sola guided vines and wild grass to seal off most of this doorway. From outside, you couldn't even tell that this building, half-collapsed as it, still had an entrance. Besides blocking sound, it served as camouflage so the group could rest inside for a while with some peace of mind, without being discovered by passing ogre stragglers or Shadow Council patrols.

More importantly, Sola could use the life-force barrier formed by these plants to mask as much as possible the unusual shadow energy being released from inside.

Because Jace and Yolin Deadeye were trying out a new spell that Molofier had tricked out of those orcs — a spell the orcs called "Dark Form." Jace suspected that, just as he'd guessed before, this was actually another form of magic similar to the Shadowform used by shadow priests in the game.

Unlike the strong connection between the Shadowform mastered by priests and void creatures, this spell was written in the ancient language of the Nathrezim dreadlords, recorded by the orcs in Orcish runes on talbuk parchment scrolls for ready study and use.

Since the original core members of the Shadow Council had mostly been orcs of the Shadowmoon clan, a lot of the Orcish used by the Shadow Council to record and study spells also carried the linguistic habits favored by the Shadowmoon clan. This had also let the members of the Dagger Society familiarize themselves with these habits while studying orcish magic.

But the Shadow Council members here had their own system of notation. Probably more than a decade of expansion, destruction, rebuilding, and fracturing had caused many factions to form within the Shadow Council itself. For example, the people in Auchindoun liked to call themselves "Cabal members," while the Shadow Council here, although they left the traditional purple magical sigils of the Shadow Council on their scrolls, called themselves the "Dead Shadow."

The linguistic habits of this "Dead Shadow" sect made Jace's task of understanding their records a little harder. Right now he was sitting there frowning, carefully puzzling over the section on casting technique. Watching him sit there, Grede couldn't help asking, "When we were in Auchindoun, didn't you learn any more Shadow Council magic from those orcs, Jace?"

Jace looked at him. "Are you saying an emissary of the Legion so appreciated by Magtheridon that he didn't need to drink fel blood to turn his skin red still needed to ask the incompetent rats trapped in Auchindoun for pointers?"

"But how long is it going to take you to learn this spell?" Grede asked. "Don't tell me every warlock here knows this magic?"

"Yes, they do, dwarf," Molofier said. "In an ogre military camp off that way there are about a dozen orcs resting. Every one of them who isn't wearing heavy armor or carrying a heavy weapon is under the protection of this form of magic at all times."

"Let me try first," Jace said.

With that, he walked to the middle of the group, closed his eyes, and connected with the shadow power in the ruins. At this moment he could even feel the madness and hatred left behind by the ogres and orcs as they'd fought and killed, echoing through the ruins in the form of Shadow.

Jace first cast Arcane Intellect on himself. After a few deep breaths to adjust, he tried to draw in the dark power drifting all around, chanting at the same time: "Bilolaznina, mieokru jue reqi…"

The chant finished, but Jace didn't feel any resonance between his spell and the shadow energy he'd pulled in with his will. He'd originally thought that a demonic spell which any random warlock apprentice here could learn shouldn't be that hard.

"Humility… intense humility, master," Molofier reminded him. "I can feel what this spell's power is trying to achieve. You need to let the Shadow inside you gradually be transformed by humility, letting more shadow magic seep into your mental essence without reservation and merge with you as one. But right now your emotions are a little too intense."

"Unless… you could cast Life Tap, converting a part of your flesh and blood into shadow power. Then releasing this spell would probably be infinitely easier."

"I'll pass on that," Jace said. "I'll try adjusting my mood."

Yolin Deadeye stopped his own studying and looked over toward Jace. Jace knew he wanted to learn something from his own attempt, considering he'd already failed several times just now.

But putting Jace in a state of humility wasn't that easy. Even the first time he realized he was facing Deathwing, what he'd felt deep down was more fear and despair — and that was different from the humility Molofier had mentioned. At least on the level of seeking union with the Shadow, they were completely different things.

At the same time, the boost from Arcane Intellect and the darkness formed from the madness of war pervading the whole ruin were also interfering with his search for this emotion.

For the Shadow Council, of course, this would have been effortless. After all, most of their members had already accepted the Burning Legion as their masters, completely submitting to the tyranny of those demon lords. Probably even the warlocks in Hellfire Citadel who answered to Kargath Bladefist hadn't humbled themselves quite so thoroughly.

So he could only slightly release the shadow power he'd drawn in from the ruins, while doing his best to ignore the countless associations and profound memories stirred up by Arcane Intellect. He tried to use the broader mental space granted by this arcane magic to imagine something bigger — the shattered Draenor, Azeroth on the other side of the Dark Portal, even the Twisting Nether, the passage of time…

He thought of the Bleeding Hollow's omen. How powerless mortals seemed in the face of an omen that would inevitably come. As a transmigrator, he knew countless stories that hadn't happened yet, and they'd already been rewritten by his actions. But that omen's vision — even a mass of fire too hard to interpret — seemed so unshakable.

"Bilolaza…"

As he chanted the spell, Jace felt his skin grow colder and colder, as if he was about to sink deep into a place the sun had never shone upon.

When he finished the chant and opened his eyes, he could seemingly sense the magical power dragged over by the Staff of Sargeras spinning ceaselessly beyond the sky, and the void rift in the depths of Highmaul that hadn't yet successfully connected to the void passage outside Nagrand but had already started to be subtly charged by the demons.

He looked at his hands, which seemed to be giving off a gloomy dark purple under the swirl of shadow energy.

In the game, Shadowform not only strengthened one's connection to shadow magic but also improved personal defenses. Jace didn't have time to run further experiments here, after all. The most important use of this magic right now was still infiltrating Highmaul. The other effects could be slowly tested later, after they got out.

"Did you pull it off?" Grede stood up and asked.

"At least it looks the part," Jace said.

"Your talent for Shadow magic… surpasses many void researchers I've seen in Quel'Thalas, Jace," Vereesa said, looking him up and down. "I'm afraid even outstanding figures like Dar'Khan would be hard-pressed to match it."

"Maybe that's exactly why Krasus insisted on bringing me to Grim Batol," Jace said.

Vereesa shrugged. "You mean he wanted to put your talent to good use — or that the Kirin Tor planned to use the black dragons to eliminate you, a dangerously unstable factor?"

"Maybe both," Jace said.

The elf smiled helplessly. Yolin Deadeye said, "The ranger lady is right. You really are a born warlock. Although I can't manipulate my innermost feelings to command shadow power the way you can, I am indeed skilled at Life Tap. I can try converting my own flesh and blood into shadow power the way the lady said."

"Are you sure, Yolin?" Jace asked.

The orc nodded, clenched both fists, and chanted: "Luhe lwentaka, luha lajiputa…"

In an instant, the veins across his bare skin became clearly visible, turning purple. Shadow power sprouted, gathered, and swelled inside him at a startling rate. He really was unnervingly good at this magic.

Jace had never seen anyone else use this magic, and every time he cast this spell himself, it was only in the most desperate moments that he resorted to it. After all, using this magic carried a very great risk of void transformation.

But for DeadEye, sacrificing his own flesh and blood didn't seem like a big deal. Sacrificing blood to gain dark power was something the Bleeding Hollow had been doing for thousands of years.

When the shadow power converted from flesh and blood revealed itself, Yolin, as if worried the shadow power would escape at any moment, hurriedly recited the "Dark Form" incantation. The Shadow followed his chanting and bit by bit devoured his skin, which had already turned dark from the Life Tap, allowing him to finally enter Void Form smoothly.

"It worked," Jace said.

"I've heard that some northern ogre warlocks can also walk in the Shadow, just like those Shadow Council warlocks," Yolin said in relief. "If they can all learn it, I certainly can't be worse than them."

"If I need to go in with you, do I need to learn this magic too?" Sola asked.

"Didn't Molofier just say that among those warlocks there are also armed guards carrying weapons?" Jace said. "We'd have a leading succubus, two warlocks who've entered this 'Dark Form,' plus a female guard — isn't that exactly the pattern of those Shadow Council orcs going out on patrol?"

Sola nodded, and Grede asked, "So you're sure that using the red dragon's transformation cloak to turn into a green-skinned orc won't break down in this pitch-black state? Won't it ruin your disguise? Don't you want to try it now?"

"Vaelastrasz told me this transformation can't be broken by magic," Jace said. "But it only lasts two or three days, and it takes over a week to recover the power to use it again. I already used it once back in Garadar, so I should just barely be able to use it again now. We still can't even see Highmaul yet — let's save it for when we reach the bottom of that castle. I think it should be fine."

"You turned into an orc in Garadar?" Yolin asked.

"Not to you," Jace said. "The first time I went in, I was obviously seen through by your Greatmother Geyah. She hoped I wouldn't disguise myself and meet you directly, so I came just like this."

"I always thought that old woman was a bit of a fraud, and that she shouldn't be leading us," Yolin Deadeye said. "But as the years have passed — the failure of the Dark Portal, the destruction of Draenor… I've come to realize more and more how important she is to the Mag'har. She really does understand what kind of person I am better than I do myself, Jace Sesso."
