## Chapter 1266: A Communication the Burning Legion Would Understand

The few who needed sleep caught a quick rest in the ruined building, then set out again before dawn.

Yolin Deadeye took the lead up front. He moved through every corner and fork in the road with composes, almost without hesitation or doubt, which made Jace wonder exactly how long he'd stayed here after helping Gul'dan take this fortress.

At the same time, the city built around Highmaul was far larger in scale than Jace had imagined. It had several distinct districts, divided by artificially built canals, just like Stormwind. Before Draenor shattered, Highmaul had been a port city too.

Near some of the mountain cliffs on the city's outer edge, and in the nearby districts that were still relatively intact, thin trails of cooking smoke could still be seen rising. Those were probably the ogres Yolin had mentioned who were still willing to live here, or the stragglers still scheming to strike back and seize their homeland from the demons.

But at least on the streets of these main districts, no ogres were to be seen. Occasionally, Shadow Council orcs could be seen walking about.

Yolin said they weren't actually here to patrol — they were here to search for treasures. Margok's sorcery lineage and the warlocks under him had preserved countless precious legacies of the Higharak Empire in this city. Although most had been carried off by Gul'dan and Cho'gall, a vast amount of magical wealth still remained throughout the city.

All along the way, Jace had been flipping through his spellbook. As he learned more and more spells, he'd grown to need this record close at hand at all times.

The incantations of shadow magic weren't like the arcane spells mages learned in school. The latter were mostly written in variant Draconic and Thalassian — just like Amy had said, as regular as geometric arithmetic, full of patterns everywhere, and they often corroborated each other.

Shadow magic, on the other hand, came with countless strange races and strange languages. Pronouncing them correctly while needing to channel and even manipulate emotions, plus preparing all sorts of complicated casting materials — soul shards, the blood of demons or other creatures…

Although Ursolus could help Jace brute-force his way through many tedious steps with sufficiently powerful soul energy and achieve even stronger effects, that didn't change the fact that every single spell was still as complicated as an instruction manual. Jace's memory was certainly not poor among the students of the Wizard Sanctum, but at this point he was starting to feel the strain.

Jace felt that although the Shadow Form used by the orcs here was currently just a tool for infiltrating Highmaul, it definitely had a lot of room for improvement. The scroll Molofier had tricked out of those orc warlocks was clearly too crude — it must have been a "fool-proof version" taught by the ruler here, Batas, to quickly raise the overall skill level of the warlocks in this place by a whole tier.

He hadn't forgotten that another important goal of his coming to Draenor was to help the Dagger Society search for more powerful shadow magic, including the method of entering the Telogrus Void Rift. The Shadow Council here was a very promising breakthrough point.

That afternoon, just as the sky was starting to dim, the group crossed a torn-open rift in the ground. This whole area looked as if it had recently experienced a massive earthquake — there was hardly any building still standing. Completely collapsed rubble and timber was piled on both sides of the rift.

The farther they walked, the more something felt off about the atmosphere of Highmaul as a whole. The plants on the ground visibly withered and weakened, and an increasingly distinct demonic stench pervaded the air. Although the view was blocked by walls and buildings so they couldn't see far, Jace knew they were close to Highmaul.

The moment they walked into a narrow alley, the sound of wings beating came from above. Everyone ducked beneath a stone monument nearby until a dark shadow swept across the sky.

It was a doomguard in a hurry, carrying a scimitar longer than a man. It quickly vanished and disappeared to the north.

Molofier watched the demon fly away and said, "There are many demons nearby, master. We're already very close to the Burning Legion's territory."

Jace looked at the Sazeno at his waist. The imp had simply hidden itself in the bag, peeking out with just one eye to steal glances outside. "Are we splitting up here?" Vereesa asked.

Everyone, including Yolin, looked at Jace. Jace said, "Right here, then."

He patted his demon bag and said, "If I need you to sneak out and deliver a message for us later, Sazeno, I hope you'll be braver than you are now."

"I… I'm not scared of anything," Sazeno popped his head out and said. "Just give the orders, master. I'll fight those, those, those demons to the death!" Jace gave it a look, then said to the elf and dwarf in front of him: "Then the two of you need to be careful from here on."

"Trust me, Jace," Grede said. "When it comes to dealing with the Burning Legion's demons, the little elf and I have a bit more experience than you. The demons at the Dark Portal were all their elite."

"He's right, Jace," Vereesa agreed. "Not to mention we now have Lord Kurdlan's goggles — invisible demons won't catch us."

Jace knew that before the battle at the Dark Portal had fully erupted, she'd joined Sylvanas and the Farstriders on a successful mission to raid an eredar-occupied watchtower, so he really wasn't too worried.

Yolin had already taken off his goggles and entered Shadow Form. Jace also hurriedly wrapped himself in his cloak, chanting the spell written from Vaelastrasz's name, and transformed into a tall, dark-green-skinned orc in a burst of flame.

Compared to the orc Jace had turned into, Yolin was a full head shorter. The Bleeding Hollow chief's son looked up at the warlock-turned-orc in some surprise, studying him for a while before saying, "This appearance of yours makes me feel very uncomfortable, Jace Sesso."

"We're even more uncomfortable than you, orc," Grede said.

"I didn't come here to make you comfortable." With that, Jace closed his eyes again, raised his hand to channel the Shadow, lowered his posture, and guided himself into Shadow Form.

When his muscular arms and broad palms were shrouded by a strange, semi-transparent black-purple shadow energy, everyone breathed a slight sigh of relief.

"When we get back to Azeroth, I really need to treat Vaelastrasz to a good meal," he said, turning to Sola. "What do red dragons like to eat?" Sola, now transformed into a green-skinned female orc, picked up an orcish scimitar from the ruins and said, "Uh, I honestly don't know."

"Magic pastries," Grede said. "That Krasus loves eating things he conjures up himself with tricks. Doesn't touch proper food at all."

"Krasus is probably the least red dragon of all red dragons, Grede," Jace said. "Let's talk about it after we get back. Let's move."

Leaving the alley, they soon arrived at a flat, broad road full of cracks and dents. At its end, they could see a flight of steps leading up a high hillside. Here, they finally got a glimpse of Highmaul, perched at the very top of the hillside, surrounded by massive towers and walls.

Although Highmaul had been built by ogres out of impossibly huge stones in a crude fashion, so the style looked a bit childish, like something children had put together.

There were quite a few Shadow Council soldiers standing guard in the watchtowers and walls along the road leading up to Highmaul. High up on Highmaul's towers, the silhouettes of doomguard demons could even be made out.

Considering it had stood here for at least several centuries, combined with the vast number of demons that might be hiding within… there was really no way to take it lightly.

Molofier walked forward calmly, heading straight for the entrance to the steps flying the Shadow Council's banner. Yolin, Jace, and Sola followed behind her, doing their best to appear submissive.

Jace had been mentally rehearsing how he would explain their identity to the Shadow Council at the entrance, but they had no intention of stopping this unfamiliar succubus. And Molofier had no intention of talking to them either — she didn't even look at them, just strode straight up the steps as she pleased.

Things went much more smoothly than Jace had expected. Even Yolin felt a slight surge of surprise in his heart. After all, these Shadow Council orcs didn't even ask questions. Some orc guards didn't even look at them at all before letting them pass.

Jace felt that back in Auchindoun, if he hadn't shown up in front of those Cabal patrolmen with a face full of green skin, and instead appeared with red skin alongside Molofier, they probably really could have walked right in without any exchange at all.

After climbing onto a platform near a damaged gate, a felguard standing at the door finally stepped out and blocked the group's path.

He lowered his head slightly, then said in a low voice in Demonic, "Lord Batas's magical ritual is at a very critical stage, my lady. He does not wish to be disturbed. Unless there is extremely urgent intelligence from Karabor to report."

Just as Jace was racking his brain, dredging up his memories of the Black Temple from his past life and fabricating a piece of urgent intelligence that might get the felguard to let them pass, he didn't expect Molofier to raise her head and lightly spit out a single word.

"Begone."

The felguard stepped aside without a word of complaint, raising his polearm. Molofier walked past this Mo'arg with undisguised disgust.

Yolin and Jace exchanged a glance and quickly followed in her footsteps with Sola, bowing and scraping.
