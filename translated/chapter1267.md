## Chapter 1267: The Spellbreaker's Hall

The fel energy within Highmaul made the interior space feel as if it were inside a furnace, thick with smoke. The disproportionately wide halls, suited to ogres, and the terrifying high walls and pillars built of brown boulders made a person walking through them feel a hint of loneliness, as if abandoned in the center of it all.

Jace had assumed Highmaul had probably been transformed by the Shadow Council into a major base independent of Auchindoun, and that there might be other Shadow Council leaders here he didn't know about.

However, the interior of Highmaul hadn't undergone large-scale renovations like the Arcane Sanctum in Auchindoun. There weren't even many of the Shadow Council's purple banners hanging, and there was hardly any sign of daily life.

This castle was under the absolute rule of the Burning Legion's demons. The demons here had all been dispatched from the Twisting Nether through the Legion's portals, and they had no feelings or regard whatsoever for the orcs.

The orcs within — whether the armed guards or the warlocks — were all in a constant state of high tension. Not tension about possible outside invaders, but tension toward the doomguards and felguards watching over them.

Most of the Shadow Council warlocks did nothing but eat and study the strange magical implements they'd found in Highmaul.

Every hall they passed through had all sorts of runestones, or enchanted ogre weapons, staves, and rods hanging there.

Every room stood one or more floating green fel crystals, just like the fel crystals that would one day spread across all of Quel'Thalas, each imprisoning a bizarre flame that looked like a demonic eye.

The orc warlocks relied on the demonic power brought by the crystals to slowly corrupt those implements, converting them into sustenance that could feed the demons.

After Yolin witnessed this scene, he quietly hid the "Withered Staff" he'd brought deeper under his clothes. He had no idea whether the warlocks or demons here had any special methods for tracking down treasures — especially those felhounds that followed the warlocks around; they really couldn't be ignored.

Fortunately, Molofier walked at the front with considerable confidence, blocking most of the suspicion directed their way. The demons here, whether felguards or felhounds, were all somewhat wary of her gaze and unwilling to meet her eyes.

The occasional visible succubi were even more the same. Those succubi, whether male or female, even those clearly wearing armor or whose bodies were wreathed in green fel fire from fel enhancement, were all unwilling to pass shoulder-to-shoulder with Molofier and preferred to switch paths and avoid her early.

The only ones who didn't fear Molofier much were the doomguards. The Ered'ruin themselves considered themselves an outstanding demon race on par with the eredar, so naturally they didn't want to debase themselves before a sayaad.

But even when they looked this way with a provocative air, it was only to compete for dominance with this sayaad officer who appeared to have come from Karabor. They instead ignored the three Shadow Council orcs walking behind her with their heads lowered.

High up in the corridors of Highmaul, dried ogre heads hung everywhere.

When Jace first saw a group of orcs gathering for a meal and eating meat, he was a bit curious. There was no sign of Shadow Council members hunting outside either. Aside from the flying windrocs and the mountain gronn living in the hills, there were no other large creatures nearby to source meat from. So where exactly was these people's roasted meat coming from?

As the ogre heads hung from above became more numerous, and the Higharak ritual ornaments carelessly piled against the walls grew more and more abundant — even to the point where it made even they, who had been attacked by ogres more times than they could count, feel uncomfortable — Jace more or less understood.

Following Yolin's guidance, Molofier led the group to the entrance of a rather grand hall.

Peering in from here, the stone pillars in the hall were carved with many ogre magical runes. Judging from the residual energy on the runes, they should have been arcane energy runes.

In the center of the hall, a doomguard was drawing out the mana of a two-headed ogre sorcerer even taller than himself, trapped within a binding circle.

Although that ogre sorcerer wasn't much shorter than Blekath, and judging from the ornate dark-purple runic robes on his body, he had likely been a nobleman of the Higharak Empire in the past, right now there was nothing he could do except let out a faint, low moan.

Yolin glanced at the ogre, then turned back and cursed in the Bleeding Hollow clan's local dialect. "Not here. We'll probably have to check around Margok's palace. Jace Sesso, didn't you say you had a potion that could reveal arcane nexuses?"

Jace took the potion bottle Khadgar had given him from his potion pouch and handed it to Yolin. "Drink half."

The orc grunted, checked that no one was patrolling nearby, then poured half the bottle into his mouth.

Jace took it back and poured the remaining half into his mouth. He'd barely put the empty bottle back into his pouch when he felt the area around his vision quickly covered by a hazy, shimmering light-purple magical glow.

Whether it was that Khadgar's potion was too potent or too chaotic, Jace suddenly felt that the power of this arcane glow was about to confuse his normal vision. It took him quite a while to reorient himself to what he'd seen before drinking the potion.

A blaze of purple light to the north, brilliant like the center of the Milky Way, immediately caught his attention. That was probably where the ley line nexus was located.

But now the problem was, this potion could only let a person sense the presence of nearby ley lines, but it didn't draw you a travel route from the "real world." So they still had to rely on Yolin.

"Strange," Yolin muttered. "The ley line nexus isn't beneath Margok's throne, but further out — in the Spellbreaker's Hall."

"The Alliance side guessed that the tearing of Draenor caused a large-scale shift in the ley line nexuses," Jace explained in a low voice.

"So that's how it is." Yolin looked toward a doorway on the other side of the hall and said, "Then we're in luck. That fellow Cho'gall, relying on the supreme authority Gul'dan had given him within the Shadow Council, forbade the Bleeding Hollow clan from approaching the Imperator's Throne of the High Arak, so I'm not very familiar with the palace near there. But the Spellbreaker's Hall where the Withered ones are located — I know it well. Come, my lady Molofier, this way—"

Molofier nodded and walked toward the corridor to the northeast that Yolin had pointed out.

Generally speaking, places occupied by the Burning Legion were always full of all sorts of fel magical installations — black tower pillars, boilers burning with fel fire, used to feed demons or power teleportation devices.

The area around the Dark Portal, the forge camps that later spread across all of Outland, even Hellfire Peninsula occupied by the Burning Legion were all the same. But there were none of these things in Highmaul. The pervasive smell of scorched fel was brought by the excessive number of doomguards and fel crystals living here.

Jace recalled what Alleria had told him: once the ley line nexus here was exhausted, Highmaul would definitely not be able to withstand the suction of the residual magic of the Staff of Sargeras, and would fall into the void along with the shattered parts of Draenor.

Thinking further, in the game, the location of the Twilight Ridge portal in Outland was also among the mountains, not surrounded by stretches of broken walls and rubble… In other words, the Burning Legion's teleportation portal might be in the deep mountains they'd glimpsed before entering this ogre city.

The eredar definitely understood this better than the Alliance Expedition, so they had never intended to run this place as a long-term stronghold like Karabor from the start.

After walking along a long pathway full of cracks and collapsed bricks and stones, Molofier walked up flight after flight of steps under the gaze of groups of imps wandering back and forth, following Yolin's directions into a spiraling long corridor.

By now the sky outside had darkened. Inside the long corridor, only the green firelight from the fel crystals remained. But at the same time, Jace could also catch glimpses of the radiance beneath his feet, brilliant as the Milky Way, growing increasingly dazzling. Clearly, this spiraling long corridor had been built around the so-called Spellbreaker's Hall.

Jace remembered that in the game, entering Highmaul from the outer city, you could quickly reach the Spellbreaker's Hall, and from the Spellbreaker's Hall you could walk up the steps, pass through a hall and a pathway to reach the throne hall of Imperator Margok. But walking it here was much more troublesome, and the internal structure of the castle also made it easier to get lost.

Of course, to reduce the complexity of the maps, the game skipped over many places with loading screens, so it was hard to say for certain what the actual structure was.

Molofier led the three orcs around to the great doors of the Spellbreaker's Hall. From a distance, Jace noticed a thick arcane shield, shimmering with purple magical sparks, floating above the doorway.

That doorway didn't even have felguard guards like the main gate of Highmaul. Even the patrols in this corridor were very sparse. There were only a few succubi and the shadow-form orc warlocks accompanying them. That great door practically stood there completely empty.

Because the person who had woven this arcane shield had reason to be confident. When Sola and Yolin Deadeye saw this magical barrier, their faces fell. Jace could also directly sense the strength of this magical barrier.

When they got close to it, he could even hear a fluctuating humming that reminded Jace of the sound Dalaran's magical shield made when blasted by black dragon flame — wave after wave, almost pounding into his brain.

"We need to get a look inside," Jace whispered.

Molofier nodded, threw out her chest, and strode toward the doorway. The three behind her quickly followed in her footsteps.

Passing through the doorway, Jace stole a glance into the layer of magical barrier. Through the hazy arcane energy, he saw a tall, blurry figure guiding an extremely violent spell at a suspended fel crystal high above.

Was that Batas?

There was almost no doubt. This figure's shadow was about the same size as a draenei's, but a good deal taller, which made his frame seem rather lean.

Beside him was also the demonic shadow of a rather powerful Ered'ruin demon. That seemed to be this eredar sorcerer's assistant, helping him carry out this magical ritual.

The intensity of the spellcasting inside made it impossible for Jace, who had drunk Khadgar's potion, to look directly at it for too long. He could only close his eyes and look away until they passed the doorway and reached the wall on the other side, where it eased up a little.

The group left the corridor and came to a terrace outside, waiting for a patrolling felguard and a squad of heavily armored "Dead Shadow" orc warriors who were gradually approaching to pass.

After they walked far away, Yolin cursed in a low voice. "I saw it. I saw that demonic book. It's right inside the Spellbreaker's Hall. That eredar has placed the book right behind him."
