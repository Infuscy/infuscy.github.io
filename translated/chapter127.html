Chapter 127: Stealing "Secrets"

The concept of casting force was still fairly new to Jace, and it sounded pretty complicated too.

He'd never heard anything about the "force" of casting from the Slaughtered Lamb, or from Jagin. Then again, he'd never even heard the concept of casting technique from those warlocks at all. All their magic was just chanting incantations and flailing through whatever gestures someone had taught you. It worked or it didn't — pure chance.

Clearly, this "force" technique for casting weakened spells wasn't something you could pick up overnight. It definitely involved a deep communion with arcane power or the ley lines — not something Jace could manage just by pronouncing the incantation correctly.

After saying this, Marin didn't continue. He lowered his head back to his book, leaving Jace to think it over.

Truth be told, Jace still had no real sense of communing with ley line energy. He felt like a speaker who'd memorized a script so thoroughly and delivered it with such feeling — the arcane energy was the audience. Jace would rattle away up on stage, speaking with passion and moving the crowd to tears, but he himself had no idea what he was saying or why the audience was so touched. When the audience wanted to ask Jace a question — that was like Jace trying to perform deeper arcane manipulation, like this so-called casting force — Jace was completely lost.

So this was what arcane talent actually meant. When prodigies encountered the audience, it was like meeting brothers and sisters — they shared endless common ground, could accurately and deeply sense their emotions and thoughts, because they'd grown up and lived alongside that audience their whole lives.

Jace? He not only didn't know these audience members, didn't understand their culture and nature — he didn't even speak their language. He wasn't even an outsider. He was a foreigner, plain and simple.

But shadow? That was a completely different story.

Jace resonated with shadow power so intensely that every time he channeled it, he had to fight down the urge to manipulate the shadows — or be manipulated by them. Every throb of shadow power, every emotion the shadows wanted to express toward him — he could read it all with perfect clarity. He wanted to respond, but he didn't dare. Every time he stirred the shadows and got pulled into their influence himself, he'd have to reflect on which parts of him had changed in ways they shouldn't have. That process of reflection was even more exhausting than learning arcane magic, or Orcish, or alchemy, or any of that.

If arcane energy was an audience, then shadow was a woman madly pursuing him. So beautiful, so clingy, a smile that could make you drunk — but wherever she went, she carried a knife, and she kept her eyes fixed on your heart. But she never said what the knife was for, never said why she was staring at your heart. Everything left unsaid. Meanwhile, you'd increasingly ignore the knife in her hand, drawn in by her unique aura, little by little, until the tip pressed against your skin and drew blood. At first the pain would wake you up. But in the end, even the pain itself became part of the pleasure.

Dark magic accepts all who are willing to accept it… Every caster seemed to know that line, whether they were mages in the Wizard Sanctum or warlocks in the Dagger Society. But it seemed only Jace had been accepted quite this thoroughly.

As he mused, Jace glanced at his pocket watch. Ten o'clock already. The Arcane Intellect he'd cast was probably about to wear off.

The morning was slipping away, and this afternoon Marin would be teaching at the Wizard Sanctum. What had he come here for? To listen to Marin's lecture and reflect on himself?

He'd come to memorize the incantation!

Jace glanced at the warlock's bag on the floor. He'd steeled himself to bring this imp in here — how could he waste the opportunity? Marin had already reopened his book and gone back to reading. In a moment he might pick up his pen, and then it'd be too late to ask for anything. Was he really going to accept two months with no magic to practice?

"Master," Jace said.

"Mm." Marin didn't even look up.

"Could you demonstrate it one more time, Master?" Jace said. "Since you can cast weakened spells, one more Mage Armor shouldn't be any trouble, right? I just want to feel one more time how a standard Mage Armor actually forms. Last time I only remembered to observe — I didn't feel the undulations and fluctuations of the arcane energy. I think what I should focus on next is tending to the moods of the ley lines and arcane energy, isn't it? After all, the casting process is the process of communicating with arcane energy — that's what you taught me."

Luring Marin into chanting the incantation like this made Jace feel a little guilty — the man did have his best interests at heart, after all.

But right now, the fierce desire to learn the Mage Armor incantation had become an obsession.

He thought that if Marin refused again and insisted Jace start Abjuration courses after coming back before teaching him, then Jace would just give up and stop dwelling on it. If he agreed and was willing to demonstrate once, that would be an opportunity granted by the Holy Light.

Uh… the Holy Light?

"Good." Marin cut off his drifting thoughts. "You're quite right. The force of casting is the process of deep communion with arcane power, and for you it's something that requires long-term study. But since you're so insistent, I'd hate to crush a student's desire to learn."

Here it comes, here it comes, here it comes.

Jace was practically roaring inside his head. Sazeno, you miserable little bastard, had better be ready! Everything hinged on this!

This time Marin didn't even bring out the spellbook. He walked confidently to the center of the room, pushing the desk back a bit.

He looked almost excited. Maybe he'd just been looking for an excuse to cast it again himself?

The old mage glanced at Jace. "Feel the gathering of arcane energy carefully. Feel the response they make when they answer my call. Listen to them speak, listen to them sing, Mr. Sesso."

With that, he raised his right hand and began to chant.

The swirling magic winds, the arcane energy spiraling in from every direction, the room growing ethereal, the electric glimmers, flames, and natural essences given off by the elements hunted and captured by the arcane — none of it could hold Jace's attention anymore.

Every scrap of his focus was locked onto Marin's lips. Every syllable, every tone, every rise and fall of inflection. He even began to detect patterns in the Dragon tongue pronunciation within the incantation — the breathy plosives, the sibilant fricatives like a snake's trembling tongue, the guttural voiced consonants like a growl from the throat…

Until Marin finished casting. A burst of iridescent light rippled around his body, and crystalline armor sheathed itself against his robes, radiating colors in every direction. A whirlwind scraped across the bookshelf and sent papers rustling on the desk.

But at this moment, Jace could only hear one sound — the incantation repeating and murmuring endlessly in his mind: Lukbaturi, Arkanuo, Molodatu, Unibelsoalun, Inguruku, Izhkutum, Irementarak, Itakhsi, Baidinkatu, Nazazuisa.

After the demonstration, Jace didn't linger. He made an excuse about being hungry and left with his bag.

To avoid arousing Marin's suspicion, he kept up a calm front all the way until he'd left the garden — then he bolted. He sprinted all the way home, reciting the whole way.

He tried to use his short-term memory to keep repeating it in his head, forcing it into his brain. During that run, he couldn't even fathom how he'd managed to memorize so many incantation segments all at once. It had to be the Arcane Intellect's help.

He burst into the house, flung open the warlock's bag, and shouted, "Get out here, Sazeno!"

The imp materialized in a flash. Jace immediately grabbed him by the neck and barked, "Write it out for me now! Transcribe it! In Common phonetics!"

Before the imp could even get a word out, Jace shoved a piece of paper at him — one side already had some prices written on it.

"The other side!" Jace flipped it over and stuffed it back at him, then quickly grabbed a sheet of paper for himself and started transcribing.

"Lukbaturi… damn it…"

He scratched his head furiously as he wrote. He hadn't expected the imp to finish first.

"Done, master!"

It held the paper out to Jace, who tossed it aside. "Don't bother me, wait till I'm finished!"

Jace closed his eyes, carefully recalling the scene of Marin casting, and wrote until the last syllable.

When he looked at the incantation the imp had written, he could feel his heart pounding.

He laid the two side by side and compared them, from the first character to the last. Aside from some differences in how a few syllables were written, the overall pronunciation and the order of the phrases matched perfectly.

It had to be as accurate as possible. This magic was too important.

Jace murmured the incantation. He cast another Arcane Intellect on himself — the last one must have worn off by now.

At this point he couldn't worry about side effects or Marin's warnings. Priorities, after all.

With Arcane Intellect's help, Jace transcribed the incantation Marin had chanted one more time, this time even marking the pronunciation as precisely as he could. Then he checked it again, cross-referencing the imp's transcription to correct a few uncertain spots.

This time there shouldn't be any problems.

Jace copied the finalized incantation onto another sheet of paper, then combined the casting notes he'd picked up from the book last time with Marin's casting process, sketching it out roughly in words and simple diagrams until the page was covered edge to edge.

Finally, he carefully tucked the paper into the warlock's bag. By the time he'd finished everything, it was nearly evening. Mage Armor here was way more complicated than in the game. He hoped its effect lived up to its casting process, rather than just reducing damage by a few percent — in the real world, that'd be next to useless.

What's the difference between a ten-centimeter-wide hole blown in you and an eight-point-seven-five-centimeter one?

But considering that Fear and Drain Life were far more complex and terrifying than in the game, there was no reason Mage Armor should be any less impressive.
