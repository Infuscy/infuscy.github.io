Chapter 1273: We Have to Find Everyone

"So this is the grimoire?!" Grede asked in surprise.

"It is, it's that..." Sola eyed the succubus up and down too, as if she couldn't quite picture how the demon had managed to swallow that book.

Jace picked the Book of Demon Names up off the ground and wiped the leftover blood off its demon-skin cover. The book seemed to have given up — it wasn't struggling anymore.

"Good thing you were here to help, Morophil. I... thank you."

"I thought we'd long since passed the stage of thanking each other with words, master."

Morophil cocked a corner of her mouth. After she'd coughed the thing up, the whole demon looked a lot more relaxed, even if that didn't quite match up with the horrendous wounds all over her body.

The others hadn't caught the demon's meaning right away, but Jace understood perfectly well. Even though he'd given everything he had this time, he definitely wasn't going to dodge what this demon needed.

He turned to look back at the completely shattered Highspire, but after only a second or two the floating island beneath them rumbled and shook.

"We need to get out of here fast, everyone. We're not out of danger yet," Sola said. "I can't keep holding these two islands together... it's too much for me."

"We'll figure the rest out once we're on solid ground, Jace," Grede said. "Can you fly back from here, Sola?"

"Should be fine," Sola said. "Once I shift back, you lot move quick — this place is fragile enough as it is."

"Ready!" Grede shouted.

Jace quickly bandaged his cut arm and tightened the straps on his staff and pack. "I'm ready too."

Sola suddenly resumed her true dragon form amid a mist blowing into the Void, spreading her wings wide. The floating island immediately began to crumble now that it had lost the guidance of the nature magic ritual.

The vine-lashings snapped one by one, and before anyone could climb onto Sola's back they had to grab her claws instead. The moment they had a grip, Sola leaped straight into the air and dove toward the cliff far below.

Jace knew that Sola, having given the Dreamqueen's earring to Vereesa, had lost the protection of the Dream plane. She couldn't rely on dream-power to fly as she had before, either. Right now she could only fight the Void's pull with her own raw strength.

Fortunately the Void energy here wasn't as overwhelming anymore. She didn't have to claw at something with all four legs every second just like when they'd first left the Spellbreak Chamber. Now, flapping her wings hard was enough to fight the drag behind them.

The two floating islands they'd left behind broke apart and drifted slowly toward the vortex high above. Sola's fierce panting roared in Jace's ears like a steam boiler, but he could see her speed building, the ruined city at the cliff's edge drawing closer and closer.

That said, the city's edges were still coming apart bit by bit. Walls and houses kept collapsing, then turning into little floating islands as the earth itself cracked into the sky. Sola had no intention of stopping at the edge. She kept flying deeper, looking for a truly stable spot.

"We need to head over there," Grede said, climbing onto the green dragon's back and pointing at a hill to the southeast. "Vereesa went that way."

Jace glanced back at the Gorian capital, now splitting into a sky full of spinning debris in the sunlight. He could barely believed he'd actually escaped from there.

The arcane magic cloud formed by the ley line nexus's explosion was still being sucked and warped by Void energy, flickering like a thunderstorm and throwing off bolts of lightning.

Any floating islands near that storm had long since been shredded into powder and dust. If Yorlin Deatheye hadn't poured out the last of his mana at the end to rein in the ley line energy with the Felwaste Staff, he, Sola and Morophil would have been torn to pieces by that terrifying surge right from the start.

"What about that orc?" Grede asked.

"We'll go back and find him after we find Vereesa. She's got a hell of a lot better eyesight than us," Jace said.

"Deatheye didn't make it out?" Sola asked.

"No," Jace said.

"Then he's good as dead," the dwarf muttered under his breath, glancing back at the Spellbreak Chamber, which no longer looked anything like it used to.

"He's not dead," Jace said.

Grede gave Jace a sidelong look but didn't question it further.

Down in the streets, quite a few Shadow Council orcs and ogres were fleeing for their lives. In the sky far off, there were even doomguards struggling to fly back toward Nagrand. They had no interest in tangling with this group of people anymore — not even in fighting among themselves. Every last one of them was just running for their lives.

From the city's edge into the hillside road up ahead, you could still see some crude ogre houses on both sides. This was probably Highspire's "suburbs."

Every so often you could see a felhound or an orc corpse on the ground — either skewered clean through or with its head smashed in. Clearly the handiwork of a ranger lord whose combat skills and survival instincts were wildly out of balance.

When they reached the hillside, Jace noticed the charred remains and scattered armor of a doomguard shot straight through the heart. Not far off were the bodies of a felguard and four or five felhounds. Demon blood had splattered everywhere, corroding the local plant life to a withered, dying state.

Demon blood and mangled corpses covered the mountainside. Fleeing imps and half-dead felhounds were everywhere, making it hard for Jace to pick out Sazeno's trail.

But just as he was starting to worry, a weak little fireball shot out of the treeline. The wind sent it veering off toward the magic vortex to the west, where it exploded.

Everyone looked in that direction and spotted Vereesa waving up at them from below.

"You're alive!"

"You too, little elf!" Grede shouted excitedly.

Sola landed in the forest, flattening a whole swath of undergrowth. Vereesa leaped up and threw her arms around Sola's neck, then scrambled up onto the green dragon's lowered back with the imp and threw herself into Jace's arms.

"When I saw Highspire explode... my heart nearly stopped," she said quietly.

"We only had time because of you guys, Wen." Jace pressed his cheek against hers. "If Grede hadn't come up with that idea, I can't imagine how we would've broken through that grimoire's ward with an eredar lord and a whole pack of doomguards bearing down on us."

"Grede's idea? What idea could he possibly have come up with?" Vereesa shot the dwarf a look.

"It really was the little elf's idea," the dwarf said with a grin, looking at the ranger. "But without my warhammer, your little lover boy would be a permanent resident of the Twisting Nether!"

"What?" Vereesa's already-fair face went white as a sheet.

"You don't think the two of us could've made it back from that explosion alive on nothing but Shadow Step, do you?" Grede hefted Skyshatter. "I saved his life with the power of the elements!"

"And not with the necklace the Dragonqueen gave me — with the method I learned on my own at Aerie Peak. Though the power was a lot stronger than I expected. I'm guessing the local elements were pretty willing to help..."

"That's the truth, Wen. But we'll save the details for later," Jace said, looking at Vereesa. "The person who pulled us out first still hasn't come back. We need to go find him."

"Yorlin Deatheye didn't make it out?" Vereesa asked.

"Right," Jace said. "Right now he's missing. We'll need you and Sola to help track him down."

Vereesa nodded and tossed the earring up for Sola to catch in her mouth. Now back in command of the Dreamqueen's power, the green dragon beat her wings, sending up a shimmering mist as she soared back toward the cliff's edge.

With the Dreamqueen's aid, her flight against the Void vortex's pull became a whole lot more effortless this time.

While they had the time, Jace gave them a detailed account of everything from when they'd been held up by Bathas and the demons in Highspire to how Yorlin had helped them escape. When he got to the part where Deatheye handed him the grimoire, both Vereesa and Grede fell silent for a long while.

"In my whole life, I've never wanted one of those orcish weird rituals to actually work as badly as I do right now," Grede muttered. "That eye he lost had better really be worth it."

"He and us aren't close enough that he'd throw his life away for us," Jace said. "So I believe he's definitely still alive."

"Right, that book, Jace?" Vereesa asked. "Let me see what we went through all this trouble to find..."

Jace handed the grimoire over. The ranger took it, wrinkled her nose, then looked back at the succubus sitting behind them. "Why... why does this ledger smell so strongly of demon?"

"It's a demon's ledger, of course it smells like demon," Grede joked.

"To be precise, it's the smell coming off her," Vereesa said, looking at Morophil.

"I dropped it while we were running for our lives, Wen," Jace said, pointing toward the storm cloud in the distance. "Morophil fished it out of there. It nearly got dragged into the Void and torn to shreds along with her."

"To make sure this disobedient thing would come back with my master's summoning ritual, I had to take some rather extreme measures," Morophil said, quite pleased with herself — even a little provocatively. "Like forcing it down my throat, mmh... so what you're smelling is this demon's bodily fluids. How do they strike you, ranger?"

From all the signs, this Book of Demon Names was exactly what Morophil had suspected — an artifact Kil'jaeden had entrusted to a trusted subordinate to keep tabs on Magtheridon and his legion.

If they got their hands on it, Magtheridon probably wouldn't dare launch any more attacks against the Expedition. Which meant the Expedition could withdraw from Draenor back to Azeroth at their leisure.

From that angle, Vereesa might have wanted this book even more than Jace did. After all, it could guarantee the safe return of countless far travelers and elven soldiers — especially Alleria Windrunner and her husband Turalyon.

So right now Vereesa didn't show the slightest disgust at the filth covering the book. If anything, her emotions surged. Her lips moved slightly, as if she wanted to say something and then swallowed it back.

Seeing her like that, Morophil was actually the one who felt a little uncomfortable. "...You're welcome," the demon said.

Vereesa nodded, took a tight breath, and clutched the evil Legion grimoire in her hands.

Still, even though Yorlin Deatheye believed he wouldn't die in there, after Sola searched back and forth across several islands that hadn't immediately broken apart, she still couldn't find any trace of him.

Vereesa kept her eyes on the ground-level ruins, the high castle debris, even the walls and houses spinning through the sky. A handful of surviving Shadow Council soldiers were crying out in despair, but among them there was no sign of Yorlin.

It was getting on toward evening. The sky was darkening. More and more of the city was being dragged into the Void. The survivors of Highspire either fell into it helplessly or had long since fled without a trace. And they still hadn't found him.

"What happens if you fall in?" Grede asked.

He glanced back at Morophil, since of everyone here, she was the one who'd come closest to being dragged into the Twisting Nether.

"A normal orc couldn't withstand that kind of energy," Morophil said. "Even Bathas was torn apart."

"You saw him die," Jace asked.

"Yes, master," Morophil said. "You're not expecting that eredar, after you tortured him with your magic like that, to survive the Void's twisting power..."

"But the staff didn't catch his soul," Jace said.

"Ah, I see. So he recognized Uthales?" Morophil asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

"No wonder he was willing to die together with a mortal warlock," Morophil said. "I was a little puzzled... that's not in their nature. But if he knew he had no chance of winning either way, and that staying in the fight might get his soul devoured on top of it, then going all-in and using the explosion's force to escape makes sense."

"But doesn't that mean the Burning Legion already knows about Uthales?" Sazeno asked.

"Already?" Morophil said. "His demon soul will take some time to return to Argus, and then a good while longer to rebuild a body. But it's a matter of when, not if."

"Unless Kil'jaeden or Archimonde decides he brought back important enough news that they need to learn it from his soul immediately... but that's not something Bathas could decide on his own. It depends on whether those leaders are interested. On the other hand, since he lost the leash Kil'jaeden had fastened around Magtheridon's neck, I imagine Kil'jaeden will want to have a word with him sooner or later no matter what."

"How much time do we have, roughly?" Jace asked.

"Hard to say, master," the succubus said. "But there's an easy way to tell when they've learned the news: watch for when the Legion sends a new demon lord. A pit lord on the same level as Magtheridon — or a higher-ranking overseer."

"Because once this grimoire has been stolen, they'll have to send a new master that it can't influence to take control of Draenor and turn the situation around. The first one I can think of is the Nathrezim lord Tichondrius — the Legion's cleanup expert."

"Lord Tichondrius?" the imp started. "Anyone but him..."

"Whoever it is, by then we might already be back on Azeroth," Grede said.

"Let's hope so," Morophil said. "I don't want to get captured by them again."

Jace stared at the ruined houses slowly spinning in the sky and drew a deep breath.

Ever since he'd gotten his hands on Uthales, he'd been imagining the day this would become known to the Burning Legion. Now it was hard to say whether it had come faster or slower than he'd pictured. It was about the time, more or less.

"I don't think he's here anymore, Jace," Vereesa said, shaking her head. "If he really escaped, he would've seen us in the sky a long time ago and come out to signal us on his own. Don't forget he knows plenty of orc and ogre spells. Getting our attention wouldn't be hard at all."

"Could he have already gone back to that village?" the green dragon asked, tilting her head toward the back. "Using some unknown blood magic teleportation ritual, or an ogre teleportation rune, for instance?"

"Hard to say," Jace said. "Let's go back and check."
