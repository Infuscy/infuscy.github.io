## Chapter 1274: The Perfect Casting Material

Under the clear night sky of Nagrand, the green dragon soared high with wings spread wide. With Highspire gone, there was no longer any reason to worry about being spotted.

Even so, with Deatheye's fate unknown, Jace hadn't been able to sleep a wink all night.

Grede's eyes were wide open too. This guy could fall asleep on his feet the moment there wasn't any fight to fight, but right now he was completely unable to drop off.

Jace knew that even though the dwarf didn't like orcs and found it hard to fully trust Yorlin Deatheye, the truth was the man had saved their lives. Even Vereesa could hardly equate him anymore with the green-skinned orcs who'd burned and pillaged across Azeroth.

Using the silvery Spiritsong spirit-mountain as a guide, Sola first brought everyone to the Boulderfist canyon.

Quite a few Bleeding Hollow warriors had occupied it by now. A banner of the Bleeding Hollow clan's eye sigil, made of animal hide, stood planted on the cliff above.

Sola landed in the open ground in front of the Gronn cave. Several orcs who didn't know the situation raised their spears and bows and leveled them at her — only relaxing a little once Jace jumped down from the dragon's back with his hands raised and identified himself in Orcish.

"Where's your chief?" Jace asked.

"You mean Yorlin?" one of the bare-chested orc blademen asked.

"That's right," Jace said. "Is he back?"

"He left with you guys a few days ago," the orc said.

Jace thought it over and decided not to tell the orcs here about Yorlin's disappearance for now. Yorlin had mentioned he had a cousin in Hellfire Citadel, and that a group of Bleeding Hollow orcs who'd drunk the fel blood were following that Deatheye in service to the new Warchief, Gorekhan Bladefist.

If the Bleeding Hollow orcs here learned that Yorlin was missing, some might go to Garadar for help — but probably more would choose to head to Hellfire Citadel to follow their new chief.

Judging by what this orc said, Jace got the feeling that even the warriors here hadn't fully accepted Yorlin as their chief yet. In the hearts of many Bleeding Hollow, it wasn't at all clear whether that cousin or Yorlin had more right to lead the entire Bleeding Hollow clan after Kilrogg Deatheye.

"We split up for the time being," Jace said.

"He's not here, human," the orc said, pointing east. "Maybe he went back to the village."

They left the Boulderfist canyon and soon flew to the vicinity of the village. Two Bleeding Hollow orcs carrying food at the village entrance looked over with curiosity. Gazing into the village, where the clearings between houses looked less crowded than before, Jace got the impression that Yorlin probably wasn't here either.

"Run into any trouble, giant-killers?" one of the orcs called out.

"Is Yorlin Deatheye here?" Jace asked.

"He's not back yet," the orc said. "Isn't he with the Boulderfist ogres?"

"Maybe," Jace said, sighing as he looked toward the colored glow of the western horizon. "If he comes back, tell..."

Halfway through, he realized he didn't even know who to ask these villagers to notify when Yorlin returned. In the end he could only have them pass the message along to Greatmother Geyah in Garadar to finish the sentence, so it wouldn't be too awkward.

The orcs agreed and walked off. Jace looked back at the companions behind him and shook his head.

"Where do we look next?" Grede asked.

"Alleria said that if you get dragged into those passages, you might end up teleported who-knows-where. It could be somewhere else in Draenor," Vereesa said.

"Or even Azeroth," Grede said. "Should we go back to Stormwind and look for him?" Vereesa gave him an unhappy sidelong glance.

"Let's stop looking, master. Let's hurry back to Wildhammer Stronghold with that thing!" Sazeno said. "What if the Burning Legion is already planning to come after you?!"

"Without him, we never would've gotten that grimoire," Jace said, looking at the imp. "I know you don't want me to die, but that's not how we do things, Sazeno."

Vereesa, Grede and Sola all nodded. Jace said, "But we really can't keep searching like this forever. I just haven't exhausted all my options yet, and leaving now without trying everything would gnaw at it."

"What are you going to do?" Grede asked.

Jace didn't answer him. Instead he turned to look at a nearby brazier, fixing his eyes on the flames inside. "Flame..." he said softly in the tongue of the elements. "Hear my request."

"We want to know where Yorlin Deatheye is... even just a clue, even just... the slightest hint."

The flames dancing in the brazier made a whooshing sound in the wind, mixed with a faint, indistinct murmur, but Jace couldn't make out what it meant.

After a while, Sola tried asking, "Don't the elements know either?"

"At least this means Yorlin isn't in Nagrand anymore," Jace said. "Back in Highspire... the elements in Nagrand helped us a lot. I could feel it. If they knew where Yorlin was, they wouldn't refuse to tell me."

Vereesa stared at the flames, which were flickering more and more weakly. "So that means he really might've been sucked into a Void passage."

"Then I've only got one last option left," Jace said.

"There's still a way?" Grede asked, wide-eyed.

"It really is the last one," Jace said. "If this doesn't work, then I'm out of ideas."

The succubus let out a snorting laugh as if she'd thought of something. "You're not thinking of using..."

"Yes," Jace said, pulling out his personal spell-record book. "A summoning ritual."

"A summoning ritual?!" Grede said. "That Deatheye isn't a succubus!"

"You can summon people too, Grede. It's a Shadow Council method," Jace said, looking at the Bleeding Hollow village ahead. "But this kind of summoning ritual can't be done by one person alone. It needs three warlocks who command shadow magic working together. Right now there's only me. I'm short two."

Grede looked at Vereesa, looking a little lost. Vereesa said, "None of the orcs here have drunk fel blood. You really think you can find two orc warlocks, Jace?"

"Don't forget Drek'Thar in Frostwolf village. He abandoned the shamanistic teachings and switched to studying dark magic before he ever drank the fel blood. It doesn't have much to do with whether they drank or not," Jace said. "Wait here. I'll go in and ask."

Jace walked up to the village center, beside the altar crusted with dried blood, raised his hands and shouted to the orcs in Orcish: "Bleeding Hollow! Gather round, I've got an announcement!"

"Kilrogg's son Yorlin sent me here to find clan members who command shadow magic and are well-versed in it! Who's willing to step forward and help? Or knows anyone who fits the bill..."

"Shak the Evil Axe, who lives down the slope," an orc carrying a shovel said. "He studied shadow magic with a two-headed ogre before!"

"Is there only him?" Jace asked.

"Just him. The rest followed Yorlin's cousin Golkum Deatheye to Hellfire Citadel," another orc said.

"Better than nothing," Jace said, lowering his hands and muttering to himself.

He came to the cave the orcs had pointed out — an orcish burrow dug into the hillside. At the entrance was a brown-skinned orc with slightly darkened skin, weaving a fishing net.

Seeing Jace, he looked up into the sun, squinted, and greeted him in Orcish: "Blood and thunder, giant-killer."

"Good morning, sir," Jace said. "You're Shak the Evil Axe? You know some shadow incantations?"

"Yes," Shak said, putting down his work with a hint of wariness. "But don't do anything rash. I don't study that stuff anymore. After I recovered from the sickness, I made my position clear to Yorlin — I won't serve the demons any longer."

"I'm not here looking for trouble. I need you to use your skills to help me," Jace said. "I'm planning to perform a summoning ritual. Have you heard of it?"

"Summoning a demon, or summoning a person?" the orc asked.

"Summoning Yorlin Deatheye himself," Jace said. "He's somewhere outside Nagrand right now. He might be in danger. I need to get him back urgently, and he definitely wants to return as soon as possible."

The orc nodded immediately. "I've taken part in this kind of ritual before."

"Good," Jace said. "But we need a third person. Do you know anyone else who can perform this ritual?"

"Besides Yorlin, I'm the last warlock here," Shak said. "If you want more warlocks, you'd probably have to look among the Warmaul ogres to the south."

Jace thought it over. "Then we'll go find the ogres."

"Uh, you sure those ogres are willing to help us?" Shak said.

"Even if they're not willing to help me, I imagine they'll think it over for the sake of the Gorian treasures that Gronn lords hoard," Jace said.

The orc laughed twice. "You've got a point."

"To summon Yorlin, we'll also need a token with a strong enough connection to him," Jace said. "He might be very far away, so we need something intimately connected to him — clothes, weapons, that sort of thing won't cut it. A ring he's worn for a long time, a pendant, anything like that..."

"There's one thing, human. No matter how far away he is, it'll pull him back," the orc cut in. "That thing is kept in our burrow — his eye."
