Chapter 1275: The Ogre Warlock's Treasure

Nagrand's daytime was so clear and bright it was easy to forget that less than two days ago, their whole crew had just lived through an apocalyptic Void catastrophe. Jace was still feeling rotten from having drained too much fel life-energy from the eredar.

After Sola's treatment, the fel corrosion had finally eased up a good bit. As for the nasty dizziness, he couldn't tell if it was a side effect of the Refined Wisdom Potion or lingering aftereffects from all those uncontrolled spins in the Void's gravity.

"Why don't we just charge in, kill the Warmaul ogres' king, make ourselves king, and then order a warlock to perform that summoning ritual for us?" Grede asked, walking behind Jace.

"You're really fixated on this king thing, Grede," Vereesa said. "Then why didn't you want to become a lord of House Thunderfist when the dwarves of Aerie Peak wanted you to?"

"Leading a bunch of ogre idiots sounds like a riot. Telling a bunch of dwarves when to eat every day sounds dead boring," Grede said.

"Ogre warlocks aren't idiots," Jace said, rubbing his temples. "But every last one of them is greedier than the last."

"Don't forget, the leader of all the ogres in Draenor in the past wasn't a king — it was a sorcerer-dictator. Even the Gronn lords couldn't make those two-headed ogres genuinely bow their heads," Jace said. "I have to make sure Yorlin gets successfully summoned back here, and that I and that Bleeding Hollow clansman both survive the summoning ritual. So I'll deal with them on terms they can accept, with honest exchange. Otherwise, who knows what kind of trouble they might cook up."

"Shak said the Warmaul and the Boulderfist have had a long-running feud, so they and the Bleeding Hollow orcs aren't in a fight to the death," he said, turning back. "We should have room to negotiate."

"Besides," Vereesa said, pointing to the northwest, "the Farstriders' intelligence says the Warmaul king lives in the mountains farther than Highspire, Grede. Look at the map — that place is practically leaving Nagrand and reaching Zangarmarsh. Even if Sola flew there, it'd take two days, maybe three. And by the time we fly there and find their king... could Yorlin Deatheye last that long in the Void?"

"But isn't he immortal?" Grede said. "The power of prophecy, little elf!"

The ranger hesitated. Jace asked, "What if the prophecy isn't that accurate, or what if the Void passage happened to drop him somewhere that can kill him?"

"If that's the case, then how are we supposed to rewrite his fate?" Grede said, looking smug, as if he'd been the first to solve a math problem.

"Very logical," Jace said, holding up a finger. "But I'm still going to try to get this done fast instead of gambling on how tough the little Deatheye's life is."

"Eyes, prophecies, blood magic... the more we talk about it, the more the whole thing feels utterly bizarre, Jace," Vereesa said under her breath.

"That's probably why the other orcs don't like dealing with the Bleeding Hollow," Jace said with a wry smile, then looked up at the tall wooden racks ahead. "The point is, we're here now."

"So how are you planning to talk to those ogres?" Grede asked. "Turn into an orc?"

"Look there," Sola said, pointing at the human pelts hanging from the racks, dried stiff. "Those pelts aren't brown — they're black-green. These ogres aren't on friendly terms with green-skinned orcs."

"The cloak's power also needs a few days to recover its mana," Jace said. "I'll just go in like this. Ogres aren't rabid wild dogs — they won't attack without a word the moment they see you."

As they drew close, they saw a dark-blue-skinned ogre sitting under the racks, wearing nothing but a Talbuk-skin apron.

When he saw this group of strangers approaching, he sat up and said in slurred Orcish, "You shouldn't come here, little people."

"Then where should we come?" Jace asked.

The ogre pointed east. "You should take the main road, the road to the 'Arena.' Skullsplitter is there, waiting for you, to rob you. I'm the sentry. You should hit me first, and I sound the alarm. That would be right."

"What the hell is he going on about?" Grede leaned in and whispered. Jace glanced at the dwarf and swallowed. "Hard to explain."

Sola couldn't help but laugh. She spoke in Orcish with a heavily ogre-flavored accent: "We're here to do business, Warmaul. We didn't come to fight you."

The fact that Sola could imitate an ogre's voice so perfectly didn't surprise Jace, but her rough, gruff tone really didn't match at all with her slender elven frame.

Unexpectedly, the ogre became very pleased when he heard Sola's words. He clapped his hands. "Business! Our boss, Skullsplitter, loves business! But you don't look much like the people who came to do business last time, mmh!"

"The people who came last time?" Jace asked carefully. "What did they look like?"

"About your height," the ogre stepped forward and held a hand up to measure the human in front of him, then said, "But they were wrapped head to toe in cloth strips. Couldn't see their eyes or mouth. Magic leaked out through the gaps in the strips — really beautiful little people! They walked like they were walking and floating at the same time!"

Sola frowned and looked over at Jace. Jace shook his head.

Of course, he wasn't shaking his head because he didn't know what this was — he was shaking his head because he shouldn't know. The ogre was talking about the ethereals, a spacefaring race that traveled through the Void.

They were the ones who would one day occupy a section of Auchindoun's palace, drawing power from the residual ley line energy beneath it — the place that Alliance and Horde soldiers of the future would call the Mana-Tombs.

Which meant that even though Jace and the others hadn't encountered the ethereals yet, this race had undoubtedly already arrived in Draenor and made contact with many of its inhabitants. They just hadn't started exploring Auchindoun yet.

Among the ethereals, the friendly and neutral factions were in the minority, so not running into them couldn't be counted as a bad thing.

"We don't look like them, but we're here to do business too," Jace said, looking at the ogre. "We're hoping to find a warlock to help us out. There'll definitely be a generous reward!"

"Generous reward!" the ogre said, pointing behind him. "Go down there, to the cave with the Talbuk-skin covering. There are warlocks inside! Go talk to them about magic!"

"Much obliged," Jace said with a nod.

"Obliged?" the ogre held out his hand.

Jace was taken aback for a moment, but he pulled a Gorian coin from his pocket and pressed it into the creature's broad palm. The ogre laughed and bellowed at the top of his lungs: "Little people want to do business!"

Walking up the slope, Sola was looking at him with a grin on her face. Jace said, "That guy was playing dumb with me, wasn't he?"

"If he can squeeze money out of you," Vereesa said, "his IQ's already higher than eighty percent of the Reliquary's membership."

All of them nearly burst out laughing. Jace could only glare at Morophil, who was trailing behind the group, giving her a vicious look that said "are you on my side or not," and only then did she manage to suppress her smile.

Behind the patchwork Talbuk-skin covering the cave entrance rolled thick smoke. Getting close to the entrance, you could smell a rich roasted-meat aroma.

If you smelled this kind of meat-scent anywhere else, it'd make your mouth water, but here at the Warmaul ogres', nobody wanted to imagine what kind of meat it was.

The ogre guards, draped in all kinds of armor plates, didn't stop them from entering the cave. They only warned them not to cause trouble. The inside wasn't a vast giant cave like the Gronn lord's — it was a simple mountain tunnel that went straight to the end. At the very back, a small two-headed ogre was stirring a great cauldron. The entire cramped space had been completely swallowed by the bizarre meat-smelling fumes rising from it.

"Mmh, Alliance," the one-eyed head said, looking over and switching to Common. "Orcish?"

"I speak Orcish," Jace said.

"Good," the one-horned head said in Orcish. "The ogres who attacked your ranger outpost in the east earlier were the Gorduni, not us. I don't know if you understand the difference between us. You should go after Gul'dan's sons' problem, Slag on Barrier Mountain, not us."

"We're here to do business," Jace said.

"Real business?" the two-headed ogre lifted his ladle and licked it, while the one-horned head glanced sideways at the succubus. "I thought this was just an excuse to fool those idiots. People who came through the Dark Portal have more tricks than wild demons."

"We want you to help us with a summoning ritual to bring back a person," Jace said. "Reward's negotiable. We've got plenty of Gorian gold coins."

The one-horned head snorted a laugh. "Now that even Highspire's been occupied by demons, what good are those coins except to Gul'dan's sons? They're the only ones who still care about them."

Jace exchanged a glance with Sola beside him. "Gronn Eye gems."

"Mmh, I'm not exactly short on those gems either," the one-horned head said.

"Gronn Eyes that have been sitting beside Durn the Hungerer for several years," Jace said.

"What?" both of the two-headed ogre's heads said in surprise at once. The one-horned head sized up the few people in the group, observing Morophil especially for a moment longer, then asked: "You can steal gems like that from his lair?"

"That's got nothing to do with you. How many do you need to complete this ritual?" Jace asked.

"Two... no, three," the ogre said, tentatively holding up his fingers in a circle. "And they have to be at least this big. Three of them. And they must all have been by a Gronn's side for more than two years. It's not about the difficulty of the summoning ritual — it's that I'm risking my life cooperating with you outsiders."

"No problem," Jace said.

Now he understood why that ogre warlock who'd burst out of the Gronn lord's cave carrying a whole sack of Gronn Eyes had been so reckless with his life.
