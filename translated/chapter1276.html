Chapter 1276: Return from a "Journey"

The stream running through the canyon where the Warmaul ogres lived was quiet. Aside from the faint chirping of insects, there was no other sound.

Jace held a black clay jar in his hands and set it on the summoning circle where the casting materials went. The circle had been drawn over the course of an entire afternoon by the Bleeding Hollow warlock Shak and himself, using doomguard blood.

The summoning circle Shak was familiar with had almost no differences from the one recorded on the summoning ritual scroll Jace had gotten from Mishun and his crew of warlocks.

After all, the human warlocks on this side of the Dark Portal had all learned their knowledge second- or even third-hand from the Shadow Council. It wasn't surprising that an orc like Shak, who'd studied directly under the Shadow Council's upper ranks, would have a clearer grasp of it.

Taking this eye out of the Bleeding Hollow crypt hadn't been easy. Even after explaining to that absurdly massive orc guard that it was for summoning Yorlin Deatheye himself, he'd been extremely reluctant. It wasn't until Jace produced the relic of Matron K'ashur that Greatmother Geyah had entrusted to him that he finally agreed to let them pass.

Before Gul'dan took control of the entire Horde, the place where the Bleeding Hollow chieftains had kept the eye was a crypt in Tanaan Jungle. To the Bleeding Hollow, it was both their homeland and their holy ground.

After that village had been destroyed by demon energy, every Bleeding Hollow clansman who'd survived the plague and the ruin of Draenor treasured the new home they'd built here. And taking their leader's eye out of it might, to many clansmen, have been an act that invited bad luck — unless it was the will of Greatmother Geyah, who had given everyone her protection.

Of course, compared to letting an ogre charge into the Bleeding Hollow's holy ground and perform demonic rituals inside it — those horrifying acts of desecration — taking the eye out was still the more tolerable option. So it had to be done this way.

"We can begin," Shak said, looking at the clay jar.

"Before we start, I want to see your Gronn Eyes," the two-headed warlock said.

Shak opened the cloth bag beside him for the warlock and the two Warmaul axeguards following him to inspect. The ogre warlock lowered his one-horned head and counted them. "Very good. Beautiful Gronn Eyes. Exactly what's required."

"Stop wasting time," Jace said. "Let's begin."

The two-headed ogre clapped his hands and walked to his position on the circle with practiced ease. "We're ready." The orc Shak walked to the opposite side. "I'm ready too."

Jace took the middle position, set out the soul shards, downed a mana potion to ensure he was in peak condition, and began chanting the incantation.

Even though he'd been through summoning rituals a few times before, summoning a target whose location he didn't know still made him feel a little uneasy. Fortunately, the casting materials were absurdly potent, so the spell got off to an extremely smooth start — after all, this was a part of the person being summoned.

When the circle lit up and shadow energy began to swirl and rise, the expressions of everyone around them grew grave.

Even though Vereesa and the others weren't well-versed in shadow magic, after spending so much time around Jace they could sense the strength of shadow power...

When this summoning ritual, backed by Yorlin's eye, established a connection with Yorlin himself, the circle's power clearly roared to life like a sudden blaze. Even the faces of Shak and the Warmaul warlock shifted slightly.

Of course, they had to focus their minds to help Jace guide the ritual and couldn't ask any questions, but Jace knew what they were thinking — because he was thinking the same thing: where the hell had Yorlin ended up?

The power of the soul shards was quickly consumed by the ritual. Jace had no choice but to raise his staff and draw on its soul-energy to keep feeding the ritual. Uthales, which had devoured god-knows-how many demon souls in Nagrand, didn't resist much.

The grassland, already scorched yellow by the doomguard blood, continued to wither under the ritual's influence. Large swaths of bare earth appeared, gradually turning a purplish-black. Grede, who was standing a bit too far forward, couldn't help but take a step back and look up at the sky.

Jace felt his mana evaporating as fast as boiling water, but he kept his focus, trying to make contact with the "landing point" on the other side of the circle and the shadow energy.

"Answer my call, Yorlin Deatheye," Jace said. "Answer me..."

Suddenly, Jace felt something seize his consciousness. A rift in space tore open in the center of the circle, and a blood-soaked orc lunged out of it, knocking over the clay jar on the ground, then collapsed, retching.

The ogre let out an "Oof!" and sat down hard. Shak also couldn't stay on his feet and dropped to one knee, panting heavily. Jace took a deep breath and felt the world go dark in front of his eyes. He nearly blacked out.

"Yorlin Deatheye, is that you?" Grede was the first to run over and help the naked orc to his feet.

The orc vomited a puddle of blood, face-up, and stared with his one eye. "Tell me this is Nagrand. Quick."

"It's Nagrand. You're back," Jace said.

"You summoned me back," Yorlin said, tilting his head. "You... the book, where's the book?"

"Right here," Jace said, patting the demon-hide pouch at his waist. "We got it out."

"Ha!" Yorlin laughed once and lay flat in the mud beside them. "You lot are something else... Back then I thought I could hold on, but I couldn't take it anymore, Jace Sesso. I had to let go, or I'd have been torn in two. I thought I was going to drag you all to your deaths with me, but you survived — and you even got the book out."

"We owe you, Yorlin," Sola said. "I didn't see what happened in the Spellbreak Chamber, but..."

"Hey, what are you all discussing?!" the ogre warlock barked, cutting Sola off. Both his heads spoke in Orcish at once: "We're leaving. Hand over the Gronn Eyes, quick."

"Ogre?" Yorlin heard the ogre's voice and immediately felt around his waist, but he had nothing on him and nothing on his person. After fumbling for a while, he could only point at the warlock with a finger and say in Orcish: "What are you doing here?"

"Don't worry, little Deatheye," the ogre's one-horned head said. "Without me, you wouldn't be back here. Give me the payment and I'll leave right away. Killing you does the Warmaul no good."

"Warmaul..." Yorlin looked at the circle on the ground, then at Shak sitting beside him, and seemed to understand a little. He nodded.

The three ogres took the bag of gems and immediately waded into the stream, fleeing south — as if terrified these outsiders would burn the bridge after crossing it.

Their worry wasn't entirely unwarranted. Jace had, in fact, already steeled himself to wipe out all the ogres at the first sign of trouble. The warlock could sense the honesty he'd shown, and he could naturally feel the murderous intent he wasn't bothering to hide right now.

Back in the Bleeding Hollow village, Yorlin found a pair of leather pants to put on, drank a bowl of meat broth, and only then started looking a bit more alive. Quite a few villagers who were worried about him gathered at the door and peered inside. It took several shooings before they all went home to sleep.

Jace returned the clay jar containing his eye to the crypt and went in. Yorlin laughed. "Having an ogre help summon me back using my own eye — if my father were still alive, he'd have dug out my other eye too."

"If we hadn't done this, he wouldn't even have had the chance to dig out the other one," Jace said.

"True," Yorlin said, his smile fading. "I was originally planning to use the arcane energy there to activate a Gorian teleportation incantation I'd learned, but that spell simply won't work now... Good thing you didn't abandon me. I have no idea how I would've survived there."

"Where?" Vereesa asked. "Where did you go?"

"I don't know," the orc said, shaking his head. "But it definitely wasn't Draenor."

"It was a very strange place. Everywhere you looked, there was a forest made of things that looked like trees but also like sinew and flesh... When I was thrown in, I used my own blood to summon a protective spell, so my consciousness stayed fairly clear. But my clothes, my weapons — they were either torn to shreds or just gone, including the Felwaste Staff. There, I was like a helpless lamb. Even though I never saw any 'predators,' I didn't know how long that kind of luck would last — until I heard your voice, Jace Sesso."

Jace racked his brains through every memory he had and couldn't think of any place that matched this description. But the Twisting Nether contained far too many unknown places. There were plenty that the Burning Legion hadn't gotten around to invading yet. Heaven knew where those chaotic Void passages would drag a person.

"You saved our lives. Now we're even," Jace said.

Yorlin coughed out a laugh. "More than even. At first I thought Greatmother Geyah had you meet me so you could help me deal with that Gronn lord, but now I understand more."

"You're a rare good chief, Deatheye," Grede said. "I can see how much your clansmen mean to you."

"Thank you, Gryphon Rider," Yorlin said, looking around at everyone in the room. "This was truly... an indescribable experience. When I followed Cho'gall into that castle all those years ago, I never imagined that one day it would blow up in my hands like that. I won't forget you. Never."
