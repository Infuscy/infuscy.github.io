Chapter 1277: A Night on the Steppe

Even though Yorlin Deatheye had taken quite a beating on his mysterious journey through the Twisting Nether, the next day he insisted on escorting Jace back to Garadar to return Matron K'ashur's relic.

The whole party rode wolves, and when they reached the river outside Garadar village escorted by several Bleeding Hollow warriors, two rather strapping young orcs were already standing at the village entrance, watching from a distance under the protection of several Mag'har hunters.

The tall orc with the high ponytail was unquestionably Garrosh Hellscream. But the slightly shorter young warrior with two thick braids — Jace had never seen him before.

Right now Garrosh was staring straight at Jace with a rather complicated look in his eyes. That probably meant he already knew Jace's real identity.

The group dismounted and walked up to the orcs at Garadar. Garrosh sized up Jace's cloak and short robe. "For a human, your Orcish is pretty damn good. And you at least kept your word and personally returned Matron K'ashur's relic instead of making Yorlin do it for you. That's more like a clansman."

"I apologize for the disguise earlier, Garrosh," Jace said, holding out the bone chain in Orcish. "But I didn't have any other choice. So pass my thanks along to Greatmother Geyah. Without her trust, I never would've been able to win your help, and I wouldn't have made it back alive."

"Highspire ultimately couldn't escape the destruction of Draenor," Garrosh said, clutching the chain. "With the death of the Gronn lord Durn on top of that... Garadar's two greatest threats are gone forever. Whatever your purpose in coming to Nagrand, you've saved countless Mag'har refugees in practice. Both I and Greatmother Geyah are grateful."

Yorlin Deatheye looked up at the little Hellscream, who was a full two heads taller than him. "Then for Greatmother Geyah's sake, Garrosh, I'm willing to overlook your cold indifference toward the Bleeding Hollow clan earlier."

"And I'll overlook the abuse you hurled at me and my clan before, and all the Talbuk meat you stole from us," Garrosh Hellscream said, holding out his hand.

Yorlin laughed twice and shook Garrosh's hand firmly, then looked over at the slightly shorter young orc beside them. "And you, little Saurfang?" he asked, holding out his hand.

Jace looked over at the orc. So this was the future commander of Orgrimmar, the son of Overlord Saurfang — Draenosh Saurfang.

Compared to Garrosh's father's terrifying future feat of cleaving the lord of the pit lords, Mannoroth, in two, this young man's uncle would accomplish an even more earth-shattering feat on another timeline.

At the Legion teleportation gate opened by the power of the Well of Eternity, his uncle Broxigar, wielding a demon-slaying axe granted by a demigod, had cut down countless demons and, with one final strike, chopped a wound into the foot of the Dark Titan Sargeras as he stepped through the Legion's gate.

In the instant the Legion's great lord's attention shifted, the Kaldorei finally managed to destroy the teleportation gate and prevent Sargeras from setting foot on Azeroth...

Yes, that had been ten thousand years ago. Years after the crisis of the Mount Hyjal War was resolved, the mage Rhonin, the red dragon Krasus, and Broxigar had traveled back in time ten thousand years under the Bronze Dragonflight's leadership, turning around that world-ending crisis that had nearly been rewritten.

Jace didn't know whether this had happened, or whether it had ended.

He hadn't even seen a single Bronze Dragon with his own eyes — at least, not one he could confirm was a Bronze Dragon.

The young Saurfang said nothing. He simply walked over, took Deatheye's hand, and gripped it. Yorlin nodded and smiled. That probably counted as some kind of reconciliation.

"Tonight I'd like to invite you all to stay in Garadar," Garrosh said, clapping the young Saurfang on the shoulder and looking at Jace's group. "We've prepared plenty of food and milk-ale to celebrate the demons' collapse and Nagrand's new life, and also to express our thanks to you warriors in our own way."

Jace was about to refuse, since the most urgent thing right now was to get back to Wildhammer Stronghold and study the power of the Book of Demon Names.

But he turned to look at his companions. Every single one of them had been so tense for days that they hadn't had a proper meal. They'd come out of Highspire and immediately started desperately searching for a way to bring Yorlin back. Right now they were covered in nothing but demon blood, mud and dust — like a bunch of adventurers who'd barely fled a demon's lair... which wasn't far from the truth.

Sola especially. After the battle of Highspire, fighting the Twisting Nether's power for a full day and night, then rushing straight to the Bleeding Hollow village without rest — right now, the moment Garrosh mentioned food and milk, she, who'd been so exhausted she could barely keep her eyes open, suddenly seemed to light up from within, the dragon's reptilian pupils nearly unable to hide it.

So Jace took a deep breath and spread his hands. "They're treating us to dinner."

"Good!" Grede shouted. "Good, good, good!"

Sola relaxed and nodded. Vereesa smiled happily too. At this point, she didn't even want to think about the problems on the Shadowmoon Valley side anymore.

Garadar at night was ablaze with light. Torches were lit everywhere. The bonfire in the village center crackled and popped, flames shooting straight into the sky. Jace could even hear the song of the flame within it — at least, he didn't believe that fire could burn like that without the support of elemental power.

Draenosh Sallead a group of brown-skinned orc warriors dancing beside the bonfire with neat, measured steps, waving ceremonial war-axes and wooden spears, performing a dance that was like a display of martial prowess.

Not far off came the sharp bleating of Talbuk being slaughtered. The fresh meat, still twitching even after the blood had been drained, was immediately seasoned and set over the fire to roast. Quite a few orc children excitedly chased the hunters carrying meat back and forth. But when they saw Jace and his group of outlanders, they couldn't help but bare their tiny teeth that hadn't grown in yet, mustering their courage to show off their barely concealed fear to this pale-skinned warlock.

Sola had long since been unable to resist joining those orc hunters. Jace believed that if she didn't have to put on airs as a dragon in front of these "lesser beings," she would've already started snatching raw meat.

Compared to the grain-ale and milk-ale the orcs served up, Grede seemed far more interested in scaring these children. He showed off his broken-off buck teeth everywhere, bristling his beard and howling, chasing the poor little orcs all over the place like a fat black furry devil.

Now this was a proper orc village feast. Think back to the night they'd celebrated Durn's death in the Bleeding Hollow village... what a bunch of nonsense that had been.

Gazing at the roaring flames ahead, the heat baking his face and neck until they burned.

In this fire, he once again recalled the vision he'd seen through that bowl of thick blood. That fire had been very different from this one.

"Jace Sesso."

Garrosh approached from the side. He handed Jace a roasted lamb leg with a charred, crispy skin. "How do the Horde on the other side of the Dark Portal live? Do they all hide in the forests like you said?"

"Some are like that. Some are planning to rebuild the Horde. Quite a few are in Alliance prisons," Jace said.

"Rebuild the Horde?" Garrosh asked. "Even so, they still plan to work for those demons?"

"Not everyone can sober up after the power of the fel blood weakens," Jace said.

The orc nodded. "If you get a chance to go back and see any warriors who are unwilling to serve the demons any longer... tell them the Mag'har of Garadar welcome them — even if their skin is still green."

Jace could feel that Garrosh's words still carried some hope that his father, or even the entire Warsong clan, would see the error of their ways.

But he could only be honest. "Right now, returning to Draenor is already nearly impossible. Even we only barely made it here by relying on the demons' magic. This world right now... it's like a cage surrounded by dark magic portals, Garrosh."

"But I'll remember your words. I'll help pass them along."

"Regardless, Grandmother and I are grateful," Garrosh said.

Yorlin Deatheye was sitting quietly in the distance right now, drinking and observing this side. Jace glanced at him, then asked the Hellscream beside him: "If Draenor continues to collapse, what do you plan to do — you, them, and Greatmother Geyah?"

"I don't know what Kilrogg's son thinks, but I and the young Saurfang will stay here," Garrosh said, looking down at the hills below, lit a dark purple by the Shadowmoon's magic-light at night. "The Horde has already paid too much for its so-called 'chance at survival,' human. The Mag'har understand that better than anyone. So Garadar will never again invade any other innocent."

Jace nodded. The emotions in Garrosh churned for a moment, as if he didn't want to face this warlock any longer. He stood up and left with a simple goodbye in Orcish.

"What did he say to you?" Vereesa walked over with a pottery bowl of ale.

"Asking about the other side of the Dark Portal," Jace said.

"What, they want to go to Azeroth too?" Vereesa asked warily.

"The opposite," Jace said. "What, you tired?"

"Uh, not yet," Vereesa glanced at the Hellscream's retreating back. "You?"

Jace swayed, worked his waist a little, and brushed the elf's shoulder. "I've still got it in me."

"Don't get the wrong idea," Vereesa said in a deliberately cold tone. "I have no intention of... with you, in these people's lair."

"That demon's a lot more suited to this kind of foul, decadent, horrifying environment," she said, taking a sip of milk-ale and narrowing her eyes. "Especially considering she loses her fighting strength without your magic. I'm not that delicate. Hmph."
