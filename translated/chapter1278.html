Chapter 1278: The Dark Wizards' Spell

After leaving Garadar, Sola carried the group over the vast Nagrand plains, flying east.

When the distant cliff rose into view in the evening, Jace knew Barrier Mountain had arrived, and that Terokkar Forest lay beyond that high cliff. Looking back at the plains tinged with a faint purple light, he actually felt a little reluctant to leave.

Bleeding Hollow, Garadar... even though it had been less than a month, they'd been through so much together with the natives here.

The ogre clans, now bereft of Durn the Hungerer's leadership, should be a lot more chaotic than on the other timeline — and easier to deal with.

Coupled with Highspire's premature collapse, the demons hadn't been able to successfully use the ley line nexus's power to connect the Void passages floating in the sky with the not-yet-activated Legion teleportation gate. The Burning Legion would probably be even less willing to bother with this wilderness than before.

With the Burning Legion's threat gone from Nagrand and the Gronn lords' patrols no longer a concern, it should be a more peaceful place than the other timeline — one where the Mag'har could live out their days in quiet.

Late at night, the group landed on a riverbank surrounded by a forest of gray-blue trees. Taking advantage of the chance to find a clear, unpolluted water source for once, they decided to take a proper bath and rest here for the night.

Right now the green dragon was soaking in the river with her eyes closed, only her head above the surface. Grede, sitting on a rock washing his hair, kept making "chk-chk-chk" noises, which made her flick her eyelids impatiently from time to time.

"Is that guy really Grommash Hellscream's son?" Grede asked while scrubbing his beard.

"Yes," Jace replied, treading water.

The dwarf paused for a moment, then said, "No wonder he didn't seem the least bit surprised when we told him we'd killed Durn."

"Why would he be?" Vereesa said, skipping stones on the shore. "If he really thought it was no big deal, he should've taken Garadar's wolf riders and charged straight into that canyon himself to kill that Gronn lord."

"He's younger than he looks. He's learning to be a leader, just like Yorlin," Jace said. "If a chief always made a huge fuss over everything, how's he supposed to make his followers trust him? And he's a Warsong on top of that."

"That's exactly what I mean by 'trouble,'" Grede said, holding up a finger. "When a grand mage like Khadgar fights Deathwing head-on and wins, I'm going to express my admiration and praise with total enthusiasm! And when a warlock like Jace Sesso gets his robe set on fire by a black dragon and screams and runs away, I'm going to laugh my head off — that's what a true Wildhammer does. Not sit in a broken chair wearing a lord's circlet, playing it cool."

"What... what?" Jace pointed into the distance, rigid with fear, and said in a shrill voice: "That's a dragon! That's a dragon!"

"Get out of here," Grede cursed, throwing a rock over. "When the hell was that?"

"Southshore," Jace snorted a laugh, dodging the rock, and glanced at the ripples spreading across the river's surface from the ranger's stone-skipping. "Not that long ago, actually."

Vereesa picked up another thin stone. "So our great Lord Thunderfist was that much of a coward too?"

"You should ask Jace Sesso about that dragon's fate," Grede said, clutching his beard.

"Knocked off a guard tower with one hammer blow," Jace nodded. "That's right."

"Ah, so that's the one you were talking about — Mafrael," Vereesa said, lowering her arm and looking over. "It's because you killed that dragon that months of our tracking felt like a complete waste. But I'm also grateful for that accident — it's how I met you two."

"I really wish I'd been there," the green dragon said, raising her head a little and sending up a splash of water, her voice low. "How wonderful it would've been if I'd known you all back then."

"You're not late, Sola," Jace said, looking over. "You should realize by now that we really can't survive without you."

The green dragon made an "mmh" sound and shyly buried her head back in the water.

Her massive body made the whole river surface surge, waves crashing against the rocks on the shore one after another. The river water she'd soaked in — even if it still carried a trace of the shadow energy from Draenor's tearing — had all been purified.

Jace climbed ashore, quickly dried off his body and hair, glanced at the river water, looked at the already-messy stubble on his face, then casually threw on a shirt and sat down on a large boulder.

"Master, do you think I look better with three horns or four?" Morophil asked from not far away, her head tilted slightly off-balance from the weight.

"You'd look better with no horns," Jace said offhandedly.

"I mean, if you like four horns..." Morophil said, "I could sacrifice a bit of the precious mana you've bestowed on me and coax it to slowly grow back."

Jace was rummaging through his pack. Hearing her words, he looked up and glanced at her. This curvaceous succubus's demon markings were burning again, and her scars had all healed.

Right now, the firelight was even reflected in her black hair. She had clearly fully recovered from the weakness caused by being torn free from the Void's pull. She'd even grown a bit stronger than before — you could feel it clearly from the flames bursting from her eyes.

"When can we do it again like last night, master? I want you to ravage me so mercilessly again..." Morophil lightly touched between her legs, licked her lips and said in a low voice: "I'd let myself be smashed to pieces again for that..."

"That's enough, succubus," the ranger in the distance said, gripping a short blade and pointing it at the demon. "From here to Wildhammer Stronghold, that warlock is mine."

The demon glanced sideways over there. Vereesa hooked her foot around the Dreambow, which lay beside her like a staff. "What, you want to fight again? This time we didn't agree on what weapons can or can't be used."

"Fine, fine," Morophil muttered a complaint in demonic, waved her hand and walked off to the side with her barbed tail swishing behind her.

The elf shot Jace a look. In the distance, the green dragon had silently drifted further and further away, while Grede had plunged his whole head underwater, bubbling away.

Jace coughed guiltily and looked down to flip through his spell-record book.

He pulled out a piece of parchment that Yorlin Deatheye had given him, stared at the incantation he'd recorded on it, and read it through softly.

This was the incantation that the eredar lord Bathas had chanted when he'd torn down his dark shield. Back when dealing with Dar'Khan and his cronies, Jace hadn't had time to cast Arcane Intellect, so at the time he'd only had an extremely vague impression of Dar'Khan's incantation.

This time he'd prepared thoroughly before fighting those demons, so he remembered this eredar's incantation very clearly, and had recorded it promptly afterward.

Moreover, the first time he'd heard it, he'd instinctively assumed Dar'Khan's shadow magic was in demonic. But after the explanation of the Void lord Gauzethos he'd summoned, Jace had learned that it was a language called "the Tongue of the Abyss," and he'd already recorded down some of his pronunciations. This time, after hearing it again, the impression was much deeper and the transcription more accurate.

He actually really wanted to learn this incantation that could dismantle shadow magic. Once he learned it, he could easily undo an enemy's shadow-reinforcement... Against other warlocks and demons, the advantage would be huge.

"Sazeno," Jace murmured. "Come here and help me with something."

The imp didn't respond. He turned to look toward the demon-hide pouch and found Sazeno staring intently at the faintly purple-glowing cover of the Book of Demon Names.

"Hey!" he barked.

"Ah!" the imp jumped and looked over. "Master, the mana in this grimoire is terrifying. As a genuine demon, I really can't control my curiosity about this power... Did Kil'jaeden really make this with his own hands?"

"Is there an author's signature on it?" Jace asked.

"Of course not," the imp said, dragging a trail of fel-fire footprints over to Jace's side. "What do you need, master?"

"Last time I fought that magister from Quel'Thalas, Dar'Khan, with you — do you still have any impression of that incantation he used to dismantle shadow magic?" Jace asked.

"Uh, I should..."

The imp hesitated for a moment. Jace took the materials translated by the Void lord out of his pack and laid them on the dry ground, then placed the several guesses he'd recorded on the parchment of the incantation Bathas had chanted on top. "Use these materials. Help me work out this incantation together."

"No rush, master. We can return to Azeroth any day now — can't we do the work after we're back?" the imp asked with a kindly smile.

"You're the one who kept rushing me to leave, saying the Burning Legion was about to come after me," Jace said. "Now I'm preparing properly, aren't I?"

He immediately cast Arcane Intellect on himself. "Come on. Let's get to work!"
