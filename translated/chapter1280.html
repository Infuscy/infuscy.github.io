Chapter 1280: The Source of the Grimoire's Power

The tower Khadgar spoke of was a stocky dwarven tower at the top of the fortress. From the outside it looked like a Blackrock Tower shrunk several times over. No matter how you looked at it, there was no way to connect it with a proper mage tower like Azor or Ilghara.

Following the high-wall walkway at the top of the fortress into the mage tower, then up a past two archives stuffed with jumbled books, they arrived at a room covered in all kinds of arcane runes that reeked of intense metal.

Jace stared at the dense protective arcane runes and had only one question — these were "simple" magic wards? Not only him; even Morophil, who'd followed them in, looked over the room's internal magical structure with some surprise.

"Since the Great Catastrophe of Draenor, I've broken a lot of bad habits," he said while tidying up books and all kinds of runestones and magic crystals. "In Thalassian it would be — the abuse of the Arcane. Actually, I'd already broken quite a few bad habits I'd picked up in Dalaran back when I was at Karazhan. It's just that even there, magical accidents weren't as, well, catastrophic as they are here."

The archmage moved an enchanted lectern over with his bare hands, meant for placing books. "Put it here, Jace."

"Back at Highspire, that eredar warlock didn't use any teleportation magic or invisibility spells either," Jace said as he set the Book of Demon Names on top.

"For eredar and for us, the instability of the Arcane is the same. He definitely didn't want to teleport himself under the floor in the middle of fighting you," Khadgar said, catching his breath with his hands on his waist. "But I believe the reason he didn't use invisibility magic was the arrogance of an eredar lord. You might ask why such a clever race would let that kind of reasoning tie their hands, but plenty of demons that have existed in the Twisting Nether for thousands upon thousands of years are exactly like that. Otherwise, we wouldn't have seized the chance to banish the demon lord possessing Medivh's body back into the Twisting Nether. So sometimes being weak and short-lived is a special advantage of its own."

"Come on, Jace." The archmage snapped his fingers, and the various magic runes in the room lit up one after another like voice-activated lights. For a moment the arcane energy was so abundant that Jace felt like he'd returned to the Spellbreak Chamber.

He looked at the Book of Demon Names. "I know that as a warlock, you should have some command of magic that can draw power, like many members of the Shadow Council. You can start with the simplest one. I'll keep watch for anything unexpected."

"Will the master's magic damage this book?" Morophil asked.

"What are you thinking?" Sazeno said. "The powerful protective wards on this book are almost impossible to shake... Of course, I'm not belittling your great power, my master."

"This imp's right," Khadgar said, glancing up at the pale-blue magic stones hanging from the ceiling. "Worrying about this book is less of a concern than worrying about whether this room can take it. When I return to Azeroth, you can live here and work with the other mages to figure out how to use all the resources in this tower, so do be a little careful."

"You're willing to trust me, Archmage," Jace said.

Khadgar smiled. "Even though my name means 'trust,' I don't trust just anyone easily, Jace Sesso. But I know you're different."

His tone was light and casual, but Jace was still quite taken aback.

After all, the archmage before him was now one of the highest-ranking members of the Kirin Tor. If he hadn't organized the Alliance Expedition and left Dalaran, he would almost certainly have become one of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six, and his status would have been no lower than Antonidas himself.

Perhaps it was because his time studying at Karazhan had exposed him to too much knowledge related to demons and the Twisting Nether, making him more willing to examine the concept of power from a higher level. Or perhaps the difficulties the Expedition faced had forced him to win over every capable person who could lend a hand.

Either way, Jace was deeply moved.

Jace walked up to the Book of Demon Names and felt the mana sealed within the grimoire. Not long after, he even heard the faint murmurs he'd heard back at Highspire — like countless demons crying out in agony in the deep Void.

They seemed to be shouting Jace's name. The voices weren't clear, but they were enough to make it hard for Jace to concentrate. It wasn't until he forcibly controlled his emotions and fought back, letting calm take over his consciousness once more, that he got a grip on the grimoire's energy.

Next came the attempt to draw its power. "Eramanth, Silendor..."

Jace carefully murmured the incantation, trying to connect his shadow magic with the Book of Demon Names. A pale-green arcane light darted from between his fingers, spinning and latching onto the grimoire's cover. Like a vicious giant serpent, it gnawed and sucked greedily.

But no matter how this giant serpent tried to tear open the book's exterior and devour the flesh within, it struggled pointlessly without finding any purchase, its sharp teeth slipping.

The Life Drain magic, having failed to catch any power to draw from back and forth, naturally fell apart. A fierce emptiness and unsatisfied hunger seized his heart. Jace couldn't help but lower his head and catch his breath for a while.

The shadow power he'd expended came back to infect his emotions, forcing him to raise his head and glance at the grimoire sitting firmly on the lectern. At the same time, a fierce frustration and unwillingness, along with a rising craving for even stronger power, gradually began to burn.

But Jace knew very well that this was nothing more than the shadow power answering his summons from across Shadowmoon Valley converging with the grimoire's own dark power, working together to twist his will. He wasn't that kind of person.

Khadgar looked at Jace. Though his brows were furrowed with worry, an unabashed admiration welled up in his heart. "How was it, Jace? Doesn't seem to have gone smoothly."

"This is the only power-drawing magic I know, Archmage," Jace said. "I feel like something might still be off."

He looked at the two demons. "What do you think?"

The imp narrowed his flame-like eyes and thought for a while. "Maybe we should try dismantling the wards on this book first?"

"I think the Life Drain incantation failed to catch the grimoire's energy, rather than being blocked outside by the wards," Morophil said. "If you had to dismantle its wards every time you used its mana, how would Bathas have used it?"

"Obviously Bathas never used it, dear succubus," the imp said. "Of course, I'm not denying your point either. The protective wards on this grimoire itself don't seem to be there to stop people from using its mana."

"We can't succeed on the first try, Jace," Khadgar said. "Tonight I'll pick four trusted elite Gryphon Riders to deliver the news of Highspire's fall and my imminent return to Azeroth to Honor Hold and the Kirinwade Village. While we wait for a reply, we have a few days to study it slowly."

Even though the archmage said this, it didn't mean Jace was willing to just give up.

Varethis Soulflayer, who had successfully drawn power from the demon name ledger, was a demon hunter. Demon hunters also became a playable class in the later stages of the game.

Jace had played this class too and still had some understanding of the abilities it commanded, so right now he carefully recalled those magic skills, wondering if there might be some clue among them.

Demon hunters mostly commanded aggressive attack skills, using fel flames and demonic power to launch swift and savage assaults. Those definitely had nothing to do with drawing power.

And Varethis himself, as one of the first batch of demon hunters Illidan Stormrage had trained among the blood elves, was he really as all-capable as the characters the players controlled later?

Not to mention he was an impatient, arrogant and savage killer. Could he really have figured out the correct method to draw the Book of Demon Names' mana in a short time inside a Shadow Council vault, like a warlock or a mage would?

Right then, an idea gradually surfaced. Could it be that he needed to use Drain Soul?

Demon hunters could hunt demons' souls to fuel themselves. They not only used these demonic essences to strengthen their own flesh and blood, but could even turn those souls into magic bombs and hurl them out. For demon hunters, devouring demon souls was as natural as eating and drinking. It was practically an instinct.

If Varethis saw a grimoire with immense power and wanted to absorb its energy, his first instinct would definitely be to seize its soul power!

Yes, the countless wailing and howling voices in this grimoire very likely came from the countless demon souls Kil'jaeden had plundered. He must have forged this name ledger with souls... to the point that this book practically had its own consciousness, wanting to flee back into the Twisting Nether the moment it got the chance!

The problem was, Jace didn't know Drain Soul. He didn't even know which warlock knew this magic, human or orc. Among their group right now, only Morophil could drain souls to craft soul shards.

Uthales didn't count. Jace had no intention of letting this ancient demon relic, which was always egging him on to become some kind of Outland King, treat this key to the Expedition's fate as a snack and swallow it whole.
