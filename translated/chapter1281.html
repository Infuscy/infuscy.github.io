Chapter 1281: The Auchenai Codex

"I have one more idea I'd like to try with my demon servant, Archmage Khadgar," Jace said. Then he looked at Morophil. "Try using Drain Soul on the name ledger."

"You're not thinking of turning this name ledger into a soul shard, are you, master?" the succubus teased in a joking tone.

"Of course not," Jace said. "I mean drawing on this book's soul power."

"I don't think this book carries any soul, master," Sazeno said, examining the slightly worn cover of the Book of Demon Names from side to side. "It doesn't seem like there's a demon sealed inside, and it doesn't look like a freakish demon itself either. Why would you think you could use Drain Soul on it?"

Hearing that his master was serious, Morophil's expression turned a little doubtful too. But since it was an order, she didn't question it any further.

She raised her hands, fixed her eyes on the grimoire, and moved her lips slightly... Her hands suddenly clenched as if gripping something invisible, then a surge of shadow power enveloped the grimoire. The sound of souls moaning suddenly filled the entire room — even Khadgar's eyes widened slightly.

Clearly the succubus hadn't expected it either. She was startled by the soul impact that erupted from the book under her magic and instinctively pulled her hands back. The grimoire's pages flipped back and forth a few times, revealing the countless demons' true names recorded within in dark eredar runes.

If you only caught a glimpse of those few flipped pages, it would be very easy to take this for a terrifying grimoire that recorded the names of every demon in the Twisting Nether.

The magic gems on the ceiling flickered slightly. The power of the souls spread out and shook the magic runes within the room. Khadgar spread his hands, preparing to brace the runes protecting the tower with his mana and suppress the grimoire's power. Fortunately, the power within didn't spread any further, and the trembling slowly subsided.

A patrolling Gryphon Rider outside the window seemed to glance this way, but didn't show any further suspicion or concern. It looked like they were already quite accustomed to the magic fluctuations that often occurred in this tower.

"This book is indeed forged from demons' souls," Khadgar said, catching his breath and straightening his collar. "But if it weren't for triggering it with this soul-draining magic, I wouldn't have been able to detect what its true nature is at all. I'm curious how you figured it out, Jace."

"Guesswork," Jace said offhandedly, feeling a little guilty. "Guesswork based on the Shadow Council orcs' descriptions and the rumors. It's just that I don't know Drain Soul magic myself and wasn't familiar with this area at all, so I didn't think to look in this direction at first."

"That's normal," Khadgar said. "Besides demons, there are very few mortals who have truly mastered Drain Soul magic. To this day, the ones with clear evidence of having cast this spell are Gul'dan and Ner'zhul, the leader of the guardian spirits of Auchindoun — the deceased former Auchenai prelate and his deputies."

"Because the eredar were already extremely skilled at controlling souls before they joined the Burning Legion," Morophil said, staring at Kil'jaeden's grimoire. "And the draenei... are eredar."

"That's right, Lady Demon," Khadgar said. "And I happen to have an Auchenai priests' soul grimoire right here, Jace. Archbishop Mara'dar gave it to Danath Trollbane at the time, to let him deal carefully with the magic of Kilrogg Deatheye and Ner'zhul. Of course, even though the contents recorded in this book helped us evade the terrifying soul hunts within the Black Temple of Karabor, they didn't allow us to catch up and stop Ner'zhul from escaping here. But this book wasn't thrown away. It's still kept in Wildhammer Stronghold. Recorded inside are the incantations and methods for draining souls, in the draenei way."

"Right here in this tower?" Jace asked.

"In the archive right beneath our feet," the archmage said. "Follow me."

Following Khadgar down the stairs, Jace asked, "You didn't try to learn that incantation, Archmage?"

"Learn Drain Soul?" Khadgar couldn't help but laugh twice. "Are you trying to get me thrown in the Violet Hold, Jace? I may not be that fond of the Kirin Tor, but I'm not that detached. I'd like to keep wearing this ring a few more days."

"Don't forget, Council member Kel'Thuzad has already been warned just for showing a slight interest in soul magic, because it touched on the field of necromancy. Now the Order of Tirisfal is already a name without substance. No one dares to touch the bad luck from that demon disaster in Dalaran two thousand years ago anymore. I'm certainly no different. Even though I learned a lot about what demons, the Shadow Council and the Auchenai can achieve with the power of souls through that book, I never actually tried to do it."

"Kel'Thuzad has already been thrown into the Violet Hold for cooperating with the black dragon, Archmage," Jace said.

"Really?" The smile on Khadgar's face vanished without a trace. He stopped and looked back at Jace.

"The Eye of the Arcane was stolen from the Kirin Tor's vault with Deathwing's help," Jace said. "It was also the night I and Vereesa encountered Teron Gorefiend in Dalaran."

Khadgar drew a deep breath. "It's no surprise Deathwing took an eye to him. He's always been someone who wants to tamper with the 'essence of life.' When Teron Gorefiend led the death knights to attack the Hillsbradal, he showed far more interest than just studying an 'enemy.' Archmage Antonidas warned him many times — after all, he was the most favored student of Dalaran's master."

"He might not be for much longer," Jace said. "What?"

Jade said, "Grand Admiral Daelin Proudmoore's daughter Jaina is studying under Archmage Antonidas. The teleportation coordinates we used to come to Draenor were ones she repaired and completed."

"My heavens, her?" Khadgar said in surprise. "Wait, she's only nine, ten? Mmh, right, she should be fourteen by now."

"You see, Jace. The world never stops producing new generations of geniuses and heroes. In the past it was the inhabitants beside the Well of Eternity, later it was the founders of the Order of Tirisfal, and now it's the warriors of the Sons of Lothar, your few friends, and even that 'young' Lady Dragon."

"And in the future, young Jaina, Prince Arthas of Lordaeron, or men and women who step out of some farmstead, some military camp, or some monastery — some child wailing in a swaddle right now — will take up this responsibility in turn."

"If it weren't like this, Azeroth wouldn't have held on for so long under the gaze of the demon lord." Khadgar's words made Jace feel as if, in a daze, he recalled the very first time he'd started playing this game.

Waking up in a monastery, not knowing which farmstead or military camp it was, facing a completely unknown place and a completely unknown environment. Not thinking about defeating any powerful enemy, not thinking about obtaining any supreme treasure — just wanting to see what kind of valley this on earth was, what kind of people and things were nearby, earn some gold coins, buy a backpack, buy some food. Gradually, naturally, he'd been swept into the depths of the world.

But that game world full of curiosity and joy had already left him. What he faced now were cold dwarven stone walls, dazzling black stairways, anxious air reeking of fel energy, countless demons buried among the abyss and the spikes — and he had already walked a road with no turning back.

"This is the one," Khadgar pulled a book off a shelf and handed it to Jace. Jace could still feel the residual draenei crystal mana on it.

The archmage flipped through the book to the page recording the incantations, glanced at the silver Kirin Tor Ring of Trust on Jace's finger, and said: "When you take this book to the Kirin Tor in the future, don't tell them I studied the contents inside carefully. I really don't want to save the world only to spend the rest of my life adrift in an endless sea of reports and investigations."

Jace smiled and nodded. "I'll study this book carefully tonight, Archmage."

"You should get a good night's sleep tonight. There's plenty of time to study it tomorrow. If you plan to summon a demon to help you try Drain Soul, you can use the basement — there's no anti-summoning circle down there," Khadgar said, pointing up. "Up there, you can't summon a demon out, unless you blow up that magic crystal I brought from Dalaran with shadow fire."

Jace looked at Khadgar's eyes, squeezed narrow by wrinkles, with a hint of suspicion. Even though this guy claimed he didn't know anything, he was an old hand at the various tricks warlocks used to test magic. It really wasn't a loss for the Burning Legion and the Shadow Council to have been defeated by him.
