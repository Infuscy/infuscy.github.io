Chapter 1282: A Demon for the Demons

Over the next few days, the griffin riders Khadgar had dispatched flew off toward the other Alliance Expedition outposts across Draenor to deliver their messages. Whenever he had the chance, Jace headed for the keep's mage tower to study the soul-drain magic in the Auchenai Codex.

Honestly, for a warlock who'd already tangled with souls in every conceivable form, much of it came easily. But what about other warlocks? Especially those living in Azeroth, who rarely even got the chance to lay eyes on a Burning Legion demon… how many of them would ever get the opportunity to learn soul-drain magic?

Even for Khadgar, getting hold of a codex dealing with souls had been no small feat — never mind for them.

So where did their soul shards even come from, then? They couldn't all have gotten lucky enough to summon a succubus like Morphel who knew how to drain souls, or some other demon minion, and just stumbled their way to a solution, right?

That would at least explain why soul shards were so scarce and pricey. Quite a few warlocks claimed they'd figured out how to convert soul shards, but Jace had never actually seen one cast Drain Soul. It seemed those warlocks were just riding their demon minions' coattails and blowing hot air — which, in warlock circles, was perfectly normal.

But that still didn't explain why in the game, warlocks were practically handed Drain Soul the moment they left the starting zone.

Jace knew better than to map game mechanics onto the real world. Take Shadow Bolt, for example — a spell warlocks supposedly learned the instant they created a character. Jace himself hadn't mastered it until long after joining the Dagger Society.

Still, when it came to the rapid spread of Drain Soul, he suspected there was a perfectly reasonable, obvious reason: the Scourge invasion.

Khadgar had mentioned before that Gul'dan and Ner'zhul were among the few orcs in the Shadow Council who'd mastered the art of draining souls. Ner'zhul had been sealed in an ice coffin by Kil'jaeden and hurled to Northrend to become the Lich King — and perhaps the soul-drain magic had spread along with his will to every Cult of the Damned acolyte and necromancer.

By the time Lordaeron fell and the Battle of Mount Hyjal was over, countless Azerothians had no choice but to share their land with roaming demons, ghouls, and undead. Soul-drain going from high-tier forbidden magic to rampant common practice was hardly surprising.

Through reading the Auchenai Codex, Jace also caught on to one of the reasons Drain Soul was so hard to learn: unlike other shadow magic, which drew power from stoking your own emotions, soul-drain required you to capture the emotions of the soul itself.

And a soul's emotions were far harder to grasp than a living being's. Even for someone as skilled at manipulating and sensing emotions as Jace, trying to feel a soul's emotions mostly came across as a shapeless, ever-shifting, twisted chaos. Add the need to wield magic to capture it, and the difficulty climbed another notch.

And given the particular nature of Jace's test subjects, there were some extra complications on top of that.

He had no intention of summoning a demon here to extract souls — not with Vereesa and Morphel right there. Calling a succubus in front of them was obviously out of the question.

As for a felhound… using a loyal, obedient felhound to nobly sacrifice itself for a greater cause was one thing. Ripping its very soul out and robbing it of any chance to return to the Twisting Nether was another. Jace wasn't keen on doing that.

Fortunately, this was Shadowmoon Valley. It lacked nothing except enemies.

Dark, fel winds howled through the forest. Morphel stood off to the side, her burning eyes darting between Jace and the ravine ahead.

"I have a feeling, Master… you're not far from success. I was thinking — once you learn to convert souls into soul shards, will there be even less use for me?"

"Sazeno doesn't even worry about becoming useless, and you're still a long way off," Jace said offhandedly.

"What?! Master?!" The imp popped its head out of the bag. "I'm still helping you organize the incantations!"

"Right, it's here," Jace said, patting the bag. "Go back inside if you don't want to die."

"Mmrgh…" The imp immediately shrank back, pulling the bag's drawstring tight itself.

Suddenly a flash of arcane light streaked past, and the space behind Jace seemed to warp under some grotesque force.

Several meters long, a phase hunter leaped out — its body covered in black-bronze scales tarnished like dull copper, its enormous maw dripping with foul mucus as it snapped at Jace!

Jace turned around coolly and hit it with Asphyxiate, clamping down on its neck — slender compared to its massive head. The beast's throat was seized tight, and its eyes rolled uncontrollably.

Yanked off balance by the spell, the creature crashed stiffly to the ground, its stubby foreclaws raking at its neck, trying to tear away the shadow binding by force.

Jace stepped aside, watching it drag its long, arrow-tipped demon tail around, smashing several blackened trees that were nothing but stumps and broken trunks.

After the beast had rolled around on the ground a few more times, he casually tossed a Curse of Weakness on it to cut down its resistance. Its movements grew feeble — forget fighting the Asphyxiate, it could barely even roll over.

Jace had gotten familiar with these creatures' hunting habits and strength over the past few days. This one wasn't even particularly strong. Seizing the opportunity, Jace raised both hands and began chanting the incantation he'd already memorized cold over the last two days. "Silennita, nashi, kaansani…"

The Twisting Nether, spewing dark power nonstop, was visible in the near distance. Under the call of the incantation, raging darkness quickly gathered in Jace's hands, forming rolling magenta-blue energy.

Jace captured the emotions of the phase hunter before him — resentment, fury, shock — and, like countless times before, used that force to grab hold and yank outward!

Soon, the struggling phase hunter seemed to develop a disconnect between its body's emotions and its soul's. Its flesh still writhed in pain and defiance, but its soul had become abstract and unhinged.

Jace could almost see the soul force pouring from the beast's mouth and nostrils, accompanied by hollow, piercing shrieks.

The soul didn't rage for long. Under Jace's power, it was quickly compressed into a pale purple crystal that dropped to the ground. The massive body stopped thrashing.

"It worked." Jace blinked, then walked over and picked up the shard, murmuring to himself. "Finally worked."

"The phase hunters lurking around here have been getting scarce, Master," Morphel couldn't resist needling him.

"Let's head back to Khadgar's tower," Jace said. "Now."

Though Jace didn't feel entirely confident with the spell yet, he'd succeeded at the very least. He just needed to try casting it on the Tome of Fel Names to see if this could actually work.

Arriving at the gate of Wildhammer Keep, Jace shouted out the location of the dead phase hunter to the dwarven guards at the gatehouse, same as he'd had the past few days, telling them to go retrieve the corpse for meat and hide.

Khadgar, who'd been discussing something with a few griffin riders near the gate, heard the shouting. Seeing Jace running across the yard toward him, beaming, he immediately asked, "You did it?"

"More or less — at least enough to try," Jace said.

"Good," Khadgar said. "Let's go."

The two of them jogged into the mage tower. Morphel dropped her invisibility on the stairs, unwarded the magically locked door, and there sat the Tome of Fel Names, behaving itself.

Khadgar snapped his fingers to awaken the arcane crystal and activate the protective ward. He looked over and nodded, signaling that Jace could begin. Jace rolled his shoulders and stretched his arms, warming up a little — he was prepared for a long haul with this grimoire.

Jace started by casting Arcane Intellect, drank down one of Khadgar's Wisdom Potions, then chanted the incantation to enter Shadowform, bolstering the power of the dark magic he was about to unleash.

Once everything was ready, he raised his hands, steadied his stance, and tried to capture the soul activity of this Kil'jaeden grimoire. When he heard those soul voices filling his ears once more, he began to chant.

"Silennita, nashi, kaansani, nayele, shephetitayanus…"

Jace worked through the incantation with focus, gathering shadow power and reaching deep into the Tome of Fel Names… This time he sensed unfathomable darkness, and with his command over shadow force, he firmly grasped every complex, twisted emotion he felt.

For a moment, he even felt like a bizarre shadow spider weaving a web in a deep soul-hold, every strand tied tightly to the pale hands reaching out from those souls.

But gradually those hands swayed and struggled, making the entire web fragile and unstable — even the weaver itself, the spider, could barely keep its footing.

This wasn't a soul-hold. This was a gloomy lake… This was no place for a spider!

Jace quickly felt like he couldn't breathe, swallowed by countless souls and sinking to the bottom. He gritted his teeth, pulled himself free, and dispersed the countless web-threads he'd already spread, staggering several steps before Morphel caught him.

Khadgar, who'd been holding up runes to block the dark energy radiating from Jace's soul-snatching, steadied things a little before asking, "What happened, Jace? You don't look so good."

"I… I can't quite describe it," Jace said quietly. "This book is like…"

"Why did you give up trying to drain this book's power before?" he asked the succubus beside him.

"This grimoire's power is stronger than I expected, Master. I don't know if I should keep trying," Morphel said.

"You didn't feel suffocated, or… like you were being dragged somewhere?" Jace asked.

"I don't think so," Morphel said gravely.

Was it that his Drain Soul still wasn't proficient enough?

Jace sat in a nearby chair and caught his breath. The book's deep soul energy had scrambled his consciousness, making it nearly impossible to think for a moment.

"Could it be that Kil'jaeden did something to this book, making it usable only by demons?" Khadgar asked. "That's why Bathras could tap its power, and the Lady Morphel here doesn't seem to struggle much, but when it comes to you…"

If only demons could drain its power, then how had the demon hunter Valdris managed it?

Jace was about to shake his head when it suddenly occurred to him… demon hunters weren't ordinary mortals either. They were all half-demonified humans whose souls had been fel-tainted by demons! And when they entered their demon form, they were practically indistinguishable from demons!

So that was the problem. Jace rubbed his eyes hard. Yes, that was it. "Drain it, Morphel," Jace said.

"Me?" Morphel asked in surprise. "You really want me to devour it? This is your trophy, Master."

"No, I lost it. You're the one who fished it out of the Twisting Nether." Jace looked up. "A demon-hunting ledger in the hands of a demon-hunting witch… it couldn't be more fitting. Maybe it was always meant to be yours."
