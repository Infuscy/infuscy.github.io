## Chapter 1283: A Step Toward the Past

The Tome of Fel Names, freshly drained by Jace, still radiated waves of pain and confusion, as if trying to infect everyone in the room with the same shadows.

Hearing Jace's command, Morphel walked up to the dark grimoire and asked again, "Are you sure, Master?"

"There's no more time to figure out how a non-demon could absorb this book's power." Jace glanced at Khadgar, who was watching the pair of them — man and demon — with raised eyebrows and keen interest. "Now that the Burning Legion knows Shattrath has fallen, they're definitely already scheming to snatch this book back. No point courting trouble…"

"You're not worried that with too much power, I'll break free of your control?" Morphel asked.

"Break free of my control, and then what? I'll just summon another succubus. Are you going to immediately join the Burning Legion?" Jace said. "All right, enough talk, Morphel. You're not usually like this. I thought you never hesitated before power laid out right in front of you."

Morphel looked toward the Kil'jaeden grimoire, her expression growing serious and solemn. She slowly raised her hands. "It's just that I've never met a master willing to hand power over to his minion."

"And I've never met a summoner and a demon minion who got along like this," Khadgar said.

"Well, now you've both seen it," Jace said, drawing a breath. "Let's begin."

Khadgar nodded and once again chanted to reinforce the ward, while a blue-purple fel glow began to shimmer in Morphel's hands.

Under the grasping force of coiling, serpentine shadow power, the Tome of Fel Names flipped a few more pages, then was seized fiercely by the succubus's magic.

Her expression was laced with desire and greed. Jace had seen that look on her a few times before, but the previous instances hadn't carried this much venom.

The demon made no effort to hide her emotions. Her black hair curled upward like a whirlwind blown by magical energy. Her hands gradually began to tremble, as if she was struggling to bear the power of this demonic tome itself. Sazeno, who loved to mock Morphel, was now peeking through the gap in Jace's demon bag, too frightened to make a sound.

The soul-shrieks emanating from the demonic tome grew louder and louder. Jace noticed the blazing flames in Morphel's eyes growing dimmer, even weeping crimson-purple tears. The muscles across her body visibly tightened more and more. The fel runes Jace had personally carved into her not only burned but seemed to be extending on their own, sprouting new variations at the ends of their strokes and lines. The horns atop her head grew as well, even cracking the skull.

A sudden, violent headache made Jace instinctively clutch his forehead. He knew this demon must be enduring pain far more intense than his own.

Khadgar drew a deep breath and re-channeled the ritual to strengthen the runes against demonic radiation. Suppressing the dark magical currents at this point seemed to be taking a toll on him.

Morphel had grown taller still. Her appearance now had almost entirely broken free of Jace's mental image of a classic succubus from the game.

More and more soul force poured out from the demonic tome, and Morphel's expression grew ever more savage. The sharp fangs clenched between her lips were already bared past her taut lips.

Watching her like that, Jace even began to wonder if she was about to betray him.

A Morphel who'd absorbed the power of the Tome of Fel Names — by then, no demon in all of Outland would dare stand against her directly. Even the mighty Magtheridon would think twice about that Legion grimoire…

A powerful urge to stop her from continuing to drain the grimoire welled up from within. Jace felt the word of command almost reach his lips…

But something felt off. After all, just before Morphel had started the ritual, Jace hadn't been worried in the slightest.

Because no matter how powerful Morphel became, he could always try to destroy the grimoire to cut off the power she'd gained. Then he could easily defeat her or bring her back under control. Everyone here — and probably even the demons of the Burning Legion — had no idea about that.

Even Valdris Soulflayer hadn't known. It was a secret that one of the Illidari's traitor demon hunters had only uncovered after a long string of covert investigations.

So why was Jace starting to fear her now?

He couldn't help but reach for the Chain of Will around his neck. He worried that the soul storm atop the mage tower had dulled the skin on his neck, leaving him unaware that some mysterious magic was taking control of his mind.

Yet the strange metal against his fingertips was cold and hollow. The Chain of Will hadn't been triggered. No outside force was trying to interfere with his thoughts.

The Chain of Will could only counter mental interference seeping in from outside — it couldn't affect shifts in his own emotions. Jace knew that perfectly well. So had he genuinely sensed, through some clue, that Morphel was about to turn on him?

The succubus before him had grown a little more, but waves of sharp, itching pain were spreading across Jace's whole body — his skull, his chest, and his lower abdomen hurt the most. Jace felt it was time to call a halt to her draining — at least while there was still time…

Wait.

The soul shrieks were growing more intense, and within those howls Jace sensed something unnatural in the emotions flooding his body. Beyond suspicion and unease, more and more anger and helplessness rippled out.

These had no source. They absolutely could not have arisen naturally from his own mind. And he knew this power — when his consciousness had nearly drowned in the Tome of Fel Names's magic moments ago, he'd touched these emotions.

Jace knew where these sudden emotions were coming from now. The soul link!

His bond with Morphel went beyond a contract — their souls were connected too. That was precisely how they could convey simple messages through emotions and share each other's pain. So the terrifying soul force Morphel was devouring was also pouring into his body through the soul link.

Because Jace hadn't considered this at all at first, the emotions had seeped in like an undefended gate. But now…

He immediately began chanting the incantation recorded in the Auchenai soul scripture, guiding the soul energy channeled through the soul link to be absorbed by him, then adjusting with opposing emotions — arrogance, willfulness, the urge to control — to hunt down the depression radiating from the now-unruly demon souls, seizing control of that power.

The troubled look in Morphel's expression eased considerably, leaving only the savage joy of draining power.

Jace could feel the souls absorbed into his own force driving changes in his body. The Eredar dark blood that Sola had barely managed to suppress was surging back to life under the dark turbulence of soul power. The red dragon's flame within him was also fighting it furiously, making the flames in his eyes flash a vivid golden-red one moment and a fierce dark-purple the next…

But all of this power had to belong to him. The dragon blood had long since been converted into his Burning Blood, and the soul force had to become his dark magic as well.

Jace used the clash of the two forces within him to suppress both at once, manipulating emotion and flesh to fuse them into one, until dark-purple flames of Shadowflame reignited in his eyes. The grimoire's souls let out one final shriek, and with the demon winds dispersing, the room atop the tower grew calm once more.

A wave of sharp pain shot from his spine and lower back, forcing Jace to brace himself against the table beside him.

He knew what this clean, decisive pain meant. Ever since receiving the blessing from the Dragonqueen, Jace had felt it often for a while — this was the kind of pain that came from a sudden growth spurt.

Still, judging by the height comparison with Khadgar beside him, this growth spurt probably wasn't as dramatic as the ones from the black dragon blood and the red dragon blessing. Jace knew he'd hit the "diminishing returns" of a Lordaeronian human's height.

Lifting his eyes to look at Morphel before him, the pair of curved brown-black horns atop her head, at a glance, looked exactly like a demon queen's crown — though paired with her alluring, tightly curved half-naked body, they gave off a slightly top-heavy, discordant air.

She was looking at her own hands. These hands were now closer to a demon's than a human's. Though her skin was still smooth, dark-purple sharp nails had grown from her fingertips. She was clearly taller than before — she already seemed about to overtake Jace in height.

In other words, she was probably already past six-foot-three — over a hundred and ninety centimeters.

Without a tailor's measuring tape, Jace couldn't say exactly how tall. The shadow energy flowing across her body made her silhouette less than clear, but she was clearly a full head taller than his current self.

For a former demon lord, this height was still far from enough, but every succubus Jace had ever seen looked pitifully small and childlike next to her now.

What surprised Jace, though, was that her twin wings hadn't fully grown out along with the grimoire's soul force. They'd only naturally extended a few more inches of dark-brown bone spurs as her body grew, looking more like a pair of curved horns on her back.

The Tome of Fel Names sitting on the table had lost most of its power. It now looked like nothing more than an old leather-bound book freshly dug out of an underground storage room. Beyond a sinister, evil air on the surface, its insides had gone somewhat pale.

Now all that remained on its cover were some simple enchantments. Jace felt he could destroy it with a casual Shadowflame version of Flame Shock or a Scorch spell.

But here was the problem: if Jace destroyed it right now, Morphel would undoubtedly be severely weakened. What about him? Would the portion of soul force he shared through the soul link collapse along with it?

Intense curiosity filled the pain brought on by his body's violent changes, but Jace had no intention of actually testing it.

"I need to recharge and repair it immediately," Khadgar said, glancing at the cracked arcane crystal overhead. "Wait a moment — during this time, don't attempt any magical rituals… Mm, my sincere thanks to you both."

With that, the archmage quickly darted down the stairs. The imp hid in the bag, stealing glances at the remaining two people and the demon. Jace looked at Morphel and asked tentatively, "How do you feel?"

"Boundless, abundant power. Thank you, dear…" Morphel said rather proudly.

Her voice hadn't changed much, but it now carried an otherworldly echo from the Twisting Nether. Like the wail of Warden Azshgoth before the Dark Portal, the commands of Lord Bathras atop Shattrath, the warnings of Doom Lord Karlez.

Jace knew she'd taken a big step toward her former self.

Now, in appearance, she resembled less a shivara or succubus of the Burning Legion and more the Diablo franchise's Andariel… Of course, swap that mane of blazing red hair for Morphel's purple-tinged black hair cascading over her shoulders, and it lost that venomous, savage essence. Add the long, thick demon tail curling behind her, and setting those aside, there was indeed some resemblance.

Actually, Jace had been a little worried her back wings would fully grow out — they'd look more like two insectoid limbs than demon wings. After all, no one here knew what Molresphilasariktor, the demoness who'd once served Brutallus, had actually looked like.

"Don't stare at my back," Morphel said, turning around. After a moment she asked again, "Are you disappointed? Is a demon without wings ugly?"

"You've asked that question more times than I can count," Jace said. The demon lowered her eyes, lost in thought, but said nothing.

"Could it be that I took on a portion of the soul force, and that's why?" Jace asked.

"I told you — my soul was torn apart in the Twisting Nether. The fact that it wasn't annihilated on the spot was already the extra luck of a high-ranking demon," Morphel said. "No matter how powerful the demon souls the Deceiver gathered, they couldn't mend my incomplete soul. That's my personal grudge against Overlord Brutallus."

"But I feel I'm ready now, Master. I'm ready to face that brutish beast again. I believe he's waiting for the day of revenge too."

"Taking revenge on you means taking revenge on me," Jace said.

"Yes, after all, we are one now, Master." Morphel gave a familiar smile, but it vanished in an instant. "Still, I'd prefer a one-on-one rematch with him… I've prepared for it for I don't know how many years. Every one of his attacks, every incantation, every single movement — I've recalled and pondered them thousands upon thousands of times. It would be a waste to let all that effort go, don't you think?"
