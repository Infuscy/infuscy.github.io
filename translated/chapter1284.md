## Chapter 1284: Carrying the Expedition's Hope

Three gryphons were already prepped for takeoff atop the wall.

Set against the deep, rumbling booms from the heart of Shadowmoon Valley and the blinding fel flames burning across the dark mountain plains, the soldiers' cries in Wildhammer Stronghold sounded especially desolate. Yet the unwavering faith and hope for the future surging through the countless people here was still as fiery as a red dragon's flame — Jace could feel it clearly.

Now that the puzzle of how to make use of the Book of Fel Names was solved, replies had arrived in quick succession from Honor Hold and Kirin'Var Village over the past two days. Everyone had reported safe and responded to the news that Khadgar was about to return to Dalaran to contact the Kirin Tor. The next step was to send the archmage on his way.

Jace was still adjusting to having absorbed the grimoire's power — whether it was the heightened perception of shadow and soul force, or the aching in his bones. Morophil, who'd absorbed even more soul force, was the same. Her invisibility magic couldn't perfectly cloak her enhanced power yet and needed more practice, so she was still resting atop the mage tower.

At noon, nearly everyone in the keep had noticed the chaotic burst of shadow energy radiating from the mage tower, sending off a purple glow. Many had grown used to Khadgar conducting magical experiments inside, but this time it was a bit much.

Fortunately, the chaos ended quickly and there was no other disturbance in Wildhammer Stronghold, so everyone slowly set their minds at ease. Everyone except Jace.

He glanced back at the mage tower, knowing in his heart that once Morophil adjusted, an even stronger body and more abundant demonic energy meant even more outrageous consumption. When the time came, he'd be the one footing the bill with his own stamina. Just thinking about it gave him a dull, lingering ache in the back of his skull.

Ahead, Grede had already mounted his gryphon. He and another fine Wildhammer knight were tasked with escorting Khadgar to Auchindoun. Sola, Vereesa, and Jace were there on the wall to see them off, along with quite a few Alliance Expedition officers, including Kurdran.

Jace handed the cloth bag containing the imp to the dwarf and said to the archmage beside him, "Once you find that hall deep inside Auchindoun, have the imp let me know — it knows what to do. When the time comes, I'll activate the ritual and have that doom lord open a portal for you to return to Azeroth."

"Clear enough," Khadgar nodded. "Judging from the dispatches Turalyon sent back from the outpost these past two days, the demons are pulling back on a large scale. I think the signal is already quite clear — they know that those eredar lost the Legion's roster, and there's no good way to resolve the problem right now, so they can only pull back for the time being."

"In a few days our paladins will be back, and with your help, plus the Legion grimoire that holds their weakness…"

"I believe that if Magtheridon has even a shred of sense, he won't launch a rash attack. But we still have to be careful — the Burning Legion may have other tricks up its sleeve. This keep still can't let its guard down."

"We'll watch this place well, Archmage," Vereesa said. "When you reach Allerian Stronghold, remember to say hello to my sister and General Turalyon for me."

"Of course." Khadgar smiled and looked over the crowd. "Then I won't waste any more time."

"Oh, right." Jace pulled the Book of Fel Names from his pack and stuffed it into Khadgar's hands. "Take this back to Azeroth for me."

"You mean to hand it over to the Kirin Tor?" Khadgar asked, quite surprised.

"No," Jace said. "To King Varian Wrynn. Make sure he keeps a close eye on this thing before I get back."

"I understand." Khadgar smiled. "Rest assured, I'll deliver it personally to the throne room of Stormwind Keep, Jace."

The archmage tucked the grimoire into his robes, mounted his gryphon, and Kurdran Wildhammer, leaning on the parapet, shouted toward Grede, "Take good care of our gryphons, Thunderfist! Bring them back in one piece!"

"Don't worry, High Thane! I didn't learn a damn thing in the first half of my life except how to take care of gryphons!" Grede waved his hand.

"You'd better!" Kurdran pointed at him, then looked toward the archmage nearby. "Safe travels, Khadgar."

The Wildhammer High Thane spoke as if he'd completely lost his usual devil-may-care attitude, his worry as sincere and heartfelt as that of an old father watching his son march off to war.

Khadgar, on the other hand, replied with a hint of sarcasm, "Don't forget what we've been through together, High Thane. What in this world could possibly make me come to harm? Watch over your keep and keep Jace and the others safe — my end is none of your concern."

"You bastard — next time we charge into a demon horde, I'll forget you straight away," Kurdran said, waving his hand. "All right, mage, that's enough talking. Get out of my sight, now!"

Khadgar laughed a couple of times, ruffled his wind-tousled gray hair, and looked over at Grede. "Then let's be off, Lord Thunderfist."

"Right, Archmage!" Grede patted the gryphon's neck and shouted a command in Dwarvish.

"See you later, Sola!" The dwarf swung his hammer, and Sola waved back with a smile. The dwarf looked toward Jace, about to say his goodbyes, when he suddenly furrowed his brow and asked, "What the hells — did you get taller?!"

Before Jace could answer, Grede's gryphon had already launched into the air with him, leaving behind a trailing "Agh, wait—"

Moments later, Khadgar and his other gryphon rider escort followed into the sky, gradually disappearing into the dark night sky streaked with fel meteors.

"Get back to your posts, don't just stand around here!" Kurdran turned around and roared at the soldiers who'd gathered to watch. "This is no time to slack off! You want to lose your lives before Khadgar and those mages open the portal, huh?! I don't want to have to go back and explain to your wives, children, brothers, sisters, and mothers how stupidly you died!"

He'd just turned around to yell at the other soldiers on the far side of the wall when he found himself immediately blocked by Sola. He instantly switched to a different demeanor and smiled. "Of course I don't mean you, my lady."

Sola had been helping treat the wounded in Wildhammer Stronghold all this time. Many stubborn ailments that Light-wielding priests and paladins couldn't resolve, she could handle — instantly turning the soldiers' perception of dragons around. Even the hot-tempered Kurdran Wildhammer held her in the highest regard, treating her far better than he did Jace and the others.

At that moment, Vereesa, nestled in Jace's arms, turned her head to size him up and said, "Grede was right — you really have gotten taller. Was it that grimoire?"

"Yes," Jace said. "But I didn't absorb all of its power. In the end I had to rely on Morophil to complete the ritual and share the power with me. Kil'jaeden seems to have placed some kind of seal on that grimoire to prevent any race other than demons from using its power — or maybe it was something else. We didn't have time to figure it out."

"That 'Soul Link' between you two, right?" Vereesa snorted softly.

"If it weren't for that link, all of that grimoire's power would've been swallowed up by her," Jace said.

Nearby on the stairs, the Wildhammer High Thane was berating a dwarven guard whose helmet was on crooked. Taking advantage of the fact that Jace's attention had been drawn by their voices, the ranger stole a glance at the top of Jace's head and hesitated, making a small "mm" sound.

"What, you don't like my current height?" Jace asked.

"Uh." Vereesa raised one long eyebrow. "If you'd grown to this height naturally, that would be one thing. But for a demonic tome to push you to this height in just a few days…"

Honestly, Jace had been quietly worried too — if the Book of Fel Names was destroyed and he lost that portion of power, fine, but what if his height shrank along with it? That'd be a disaster.

"What, after being with me this long, you still think I'm some untrustworthy warlock?" Jace teased.

"What are you talking about? I'm worried about your body," Vereesa said seriously. "Don't your bones ache? Or is your mobility impaired?"

She gestured with her fingers. "After all, you shot up so much all at once… I feel like you're not much shorter than Kael'thas now. But don't forget — he's been alive for two thousand years. You're only in your twenties!"

Faced with the elf's concern, Jace felt the apprehension that Morophil's new appearance had stirred up dissipate all at once. He leaned toward her, a little competitively, and said, "The ache is there, sure… As for mobility, today I'll put that concern to rest once and for all, Vereesa Windrunner."

Vereesa immediately caught his meaning. The corners of her mouth curled up slightly, then she quickly pressed her lips together to kill the smile, stealing a glance at the nearby Farstrider rangers before whispering, "Then shall we head back into the keep?"

"Let's go," Jace whispered.

With that, the two of them, under Sola's curious gaze, scurried down the wall stairs like a pair of thieves, shoving each other along the way.
