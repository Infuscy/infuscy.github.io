Chapter 1286: News from Deep in Shadowmoon Valley

"Wake up, Master. Wake up."

The demon's voice called softly by his ear. Jace opened his eyes wearily — last night's chaos had blurred to the point where he could barely tell dream from reality.

Morphel was reclining beside him, her faintly flickering demonic eyes fixed on him. Her pale-pink skin was fully exposed from above, dark-purple evil veins showing through. Jace's heart gave a throb, but below the waist it was already starting to ache.

It was already morning. Beyond the narrow window, the lightning churning in the black clouds in the distance was a bit too glaring.

Last night… perhaps to prove he still deserved the title of "Master," he'd just gone all out.

The sayaad, her demonic power inflated who-knew-how-many times over, had greedily sucked the life force out of him like a madwoman. Jace had watched the dark flames in her eyes gradually turn red, as if what she was drawing out wasn't his power but his blood…

The Morphel of today was no longer an opponent he could easily subdue. Or had she ever truly been subdued? Jace was even starting to doubt himself.

"What is it." Jace rubbed his eyes and muttered.

The demon said, "A dwarven soldier came by earlier and told me that Marshal Turalyon arrived at Wildhammer Keep this morning…"

"This morning?!"

Jace shot upright and started throwing on his clothes in a flurry, asking as he dressed, "Did he come in and see us?"

"No, he spoke from outside the door." Morphel put on a pitiful act. "Rest assured, Master, I won't expose myself and ruin your reputation…"

"In your current state, no one could feel the slightest bit of sympathy for you." Jace buckled his belt, picked up Azuresong from beside him, and hung it at his waist. "Look at those demonic fingernails of yours — even if you dashed into Shadowmoon Valley today and snatched twenty imps for a snack, I wouldn't be surprised in the slightest."

Morphel glanced at her sharp black nails and murmured, "How hurtful. But… there's no going back now."

"Save the act." Jace shoved the door open and charged out, leaving behind only a few light, wicked laughs from the demon.

Vereesa's room was already empty. She must have headed for the wall early on without giving him a second thought.

The more Jace thought about it, the quicker his pace — though right now he could still hear orders being shouted from the wall. Turalyon probably wasn't back yet, so he wasn't late.

Beyond having his power drained dry last night, what had also left a deep impression on Jace was the incantation that had stripped away Morphel's dark invisibility.

He had a vague feeling that the incantation didn't simply strip away the target's shadow energy — it could also channel that shadow energy to replenish his own stamina. Especially after the go-round with Vereesa, the recovery effect had been even more pronounced.

Could that be a felhound's ability — Devour Magic?

Not quite. Felhounds could devour not only shadow magic but all forms of magic, feeding it back to themselves regardless of whether the effects were harmful or beneficial. But whether it was Dar'Khan or Bathras, they'd only used this spell to dismantle dark shields they couldn't crack with direct attack magic — they hadn't stripped away any other shadow.

This spell's capabilities would need further testing, but its bonus effect of using shadow to replenish mana was a pleasant surprise.

Life Tap could massively boost his mana and shadow magic power in an emergency, but the damage it inflicted on himself was too severe — he could hardly activate it unless it was a do-or-die moment. If he had a spell that could devour shadow magic to replenish his own mana, the problem of mana shortage during prolonged combat could be eased quite a bit.

Of course, this ability had its limits — ideally the opponent would be a warlock or demon too. Against an opponent who didn't use any shadow magic at all, it wouldn't be of much use.

But at this very moment, Jace and his companions were facing the threat of the Burning Legion. This spell had come right on time. As for how many chances there'd be to use it after returning to Azeroth, Jace couldn't afford to think that far ahead.

Arriving at the yard, sure enough, the wall was already packed with people, and the sound of horns drew closer and closer.

Jace climbed up the wall and came to Vereesa's side. Sola glanced over and smiled. "Good morning, Jace."

"Morning." Jace straightened his collar. "Didn't expect Marshal Turalyon back so soon. From what Khadgar said yesterday, I thought it'd be a few more days."

"Right," Sola nodded. "The situation at the front must be going more smoothly than expected."

"Let's hope so," Jace said.

Vereesa stared ahead and said flatly, "You're even still alive." Jace cleared his throat and gave an "mm."

"Didn't you say you were going to find Sola?" Vereesa asked.

Sola looked over in confusion and asked, "Weren't you two mating last night?" Both of them turned to stare at the utterly bewildered green dragon at the same time.

"That's… one way to put it," Jace said.

Sola seemed to realize she'd said the wrong thing and nodded.

"I mean, you reek of succubus right now," Vereesa leaned over and said in a low voice. "Turalyon will be back soon — you're not afraid he'll smell it?"

"From that distance, he's hardly going to come shake my hand, is he?" Jace said.

Vereesa stared at him in silence for a while, and Jace felt more and more guilty by the second.

A column of cavalry approached from the distance. The gates were hauled open, and the rumbling of metal hinges yanked Jace's attention back.

"Jace, Vereesa!" The paladin at the front, riding a warhorse and clad head to toe in silver armor, removed his helmet and waved toward them. "Great to see you both back safe and sound!"

Crap, Jace thought.

Vereesa, on the other hand, waved happily at her brother-in-law, looking as if she'd found a savior — the grudge she'd once flaunted in the Mage Quarter, swearing she'd duel this paladin in public, had long since gone who-knows-where.

Given how enthusiastic she was, if Turalyon had personally charged into the keep and smashed Morphel to death with a Hammer of Wrath, Vereesa would probably be wishing Alleria would throw another wedding so she could fight to be the bridesmaid.

"How's the situation up front, Turalyon?" Kurdran shouted, clutching the parapet.

"Overall, very much in our favor!" Turalyon called back. "Has Khadgar already set out?"

"That's right — you're a day late!" The dwarf king glanced down at the gate and shouted, "Widen it up a bit, lads, widen it up a bit! That's a lot of cavalry!"

The paladin had just entered the gate and dismounted when he strode toward Jace.

With every step he took, his war booted feet and his demon-blood-stained armor clanged with each stride, every strike like a Crusader Strike hammering at Jace's heart.

He's not really going to shake my hand, is he?

"Marshal Turalyon," Jace said.

"Truly glad you're all doing well." Turalyon removed his gloves and shook his hand. "I've already heard from the griffin riders about Shattrath — just hearing about it was enough to be stirring. Those demons have gotten a clear picture now of exactly what kind of opponents they're dealing with."

Turalyon sounded overjoyed, but not long after he finished speaking, Jace clearly sensed that the paladin's inner emotions were starting to go off.

Though he was still smiling on the surface, greeting Vereesa and exchanging a few pleasantries about the situation outside and the keep's food conditions, he seemed to be growing wary and guarded.

This was one of the founders of the Order of the Silver Hand, one of the most powerful paladins in the entire Alliance. If even Vereesa could smell the succubus scent clearly, how could he not? His reputation was thoroughly ruined.

"I wonder where Khadgar is," the paladin said, looking over. "If he makes it back to Azeroth, Jace, will we know?"

Jace's mind raced and he seized on it like a lifeline, answering immediately, "Yes. When the time comes, my demon minion will notify me with magic. Then I'll use a ritual circle to contact a demon in Azeroth and have him open the portal. Once Khadgar successfully crosses through the portal, he'll report back to me."

"I'm already preparing that ritual circle. Since it requires animal blood and various demon blood, there might be some rather unpleasant odors. I'm planning to conduct the ritual in the basement of Khadgar's mage tower, to avoid contaminating the keep's food supply."

After hearing this, Turalyon seemed to relax by half. "No need to be overly careful, Jace. In Shadowmoon Valley, how would you even avoid fel and shadow contamination of food? My brothers of the Silver Hand and the Church will help."

Jace nodded earnestly. Vereesa shot him an unhappy look, clearly annoyed that Jace had just slipped right past it.

"Speaking of the Silver Hand and the Church, Turalyon!" Kurdran came over excitedly and said to the paladin, "This lady dragon — you have no idea, since she came to our infirmary… the recovery rate of the wounded and sick has gone up by who knows how much!"

"It's not quite like that," Sola said, embarrassed. "Your priests and paladins are no worse than me at purifying shadow and fel corruption. I'm just better at treating infectious diseases and external injuries."

Kurdran said immediately, "Don't be modest, my lady! Who'd have thought red and black dragons could be so vicious, yet green dragons so kind?"

"Red dragons are kind too, Lord High Thane," Sola said patiently. "They were just threatened by the Dragonmaw clan…"

"Ah, that's right." Kurdran patted his bald head. "I forgot about Grim Batol. Sorry, Lady Sola — we fought too many battles against the red dragons on that side, and it's hard to reverse the impression all at once…"

"Oh, speaking of the Dragonmaw orcs," Turalyon said. "A few days ago we received a report from the outpost — a group of black dragons ambushed Dragonmaw Fortress under cover of shadow. Though the results didn't seem particularly significant, they still killed quite a lot of orcs. You've retrieved that grimoire recording the demons' names, and Magtheridon's made adjustments too — he's planning to gather the numerous Bonechewer and Dragonmaw orcs from Karabor to replace the demons on the front line here. This black dragon ambush should be able to disrupt their troop movements a bit."

"The orcs don't have true names, so they're not worried about being weakened by the ledger, right?" Kurdran laughed. "But orcs of every variety are exactly what we're best at dealing with. Let them come!"

The soldiers nearby, hearing they'd be fighting orcs, roused themselves and cheered along with the Wildhammer high lord, as if they were all addicted. "Black dragons ambushing Dragonmaw Fortress?" Jace asked.

"The Dragonmaw orcs still have a clutch of black dragon eggs in their possession. The black dragons must be trying to take them back," Turalyon explained.

Jace remembered that in the game, the Dragonmaw orcs had been collaborating with Deathwing's mate Sinestra. They planned to use void energy to transform the black dragon eggs into "netherwing dragons," or void dragons — an experiment to create a more powerful new draconic species.

But that was after Nefarian's chromatic dragon project had failed, and Sinestra — or rather, the Dragon Mother Zinsania — had turned to reopening the Dark Portal, coming to Outland to place her hopes on the Illidari under Illidan's rule.

Right now Blackrock Spire still stood tall, and Nefarian's plan was still underway. The black dragon matriarch shouldn't have come yet. So the black dragons in Outland still purely wanted to grow the black dragon population, rather than be controlled by these orcs and turned into test subjects.

In any case, this meant the Expedition and the black dragons were already substantively helping each other.
