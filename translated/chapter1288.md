## Chapter 1288: At Least Tonight Is Not a Time for Seriousness

The news of Khadgar's return to Azeroth quickly spread throughout Wildhammer Keep. Many soldiers had no idea he'd been sent back through a demon portal, but there were plenty of rather dark rumors about the specifics of how he'd left — several of which were clearly linked to the Stormwind warlock Jace Sesso, who'd recently arrived at Wildhammer Keep.

The Expedition soldiers were already used to Archmage Khadgar always finding a way forward in moments of crisis — whether it was outmaneuvering and defeating Deathwing, or using the Skull of Gul'dan to close the Dark Portal and stop Draenor's destruction from reaching Azeroth…

So no matter how dark the method of getting to Azeroth, nobody cared in the least, least of all the Azerothian folks who'd come here carrying hope.

Turalyon had brought news of the demons pulling back and battles breaking out between black dragons and orcs in multiple parts of Shadowmoon Valley. Jace and the others had brought word of Shattrath's fall in Nagrand and the Legion treasure falling into Expedition hands. Now, on top of that, Khadgar had smoothly returned to Azeroth — a celebratory feast was becoming unstoppable.

The Wildhammer high lord, who had always been fond of good food and drink, had no intention of dashing everyone's enthusiasm either. After confirming there were no orc or demon movements near Wildhammer Keep, Kurdran called on everyone to celebrate the string of victories tonight.

Though the scale of this celebration was a little smaller than the last one, when Captain Alleria Windrunner and Archmage Khadgar had both been present, everyone was even more excited and optimistic than last time.

The reason was obvious — watching the plan to return home steadily advance, with the crisis of the Burning Legion's second offensive already resolved, victory was practically right before their eyes.

Humans and dwarves gathered together, and amid their celebratory shouts and crude singing, the rumble of fel fires from distant Shadowmoon Valley could barely be heard.

Under the warm glow of candlelit lamps, Vereesa was holding Sola's hand, eagerly teaching her some popular Silvermoon dance, determined to make her a "true Quel'dorei."

Nearby, quite a few elven men and women — Farstriders and mages alike — were clapping out a rhythm for the two of them, occasionally unable to resist raising their hands or rising on tiptoes, joking with their companions about the pair's clumsy dancing.

Jace sat to the side, holding a glass of dark beer with a somewhat odd flavor, watching them dance. Every so often a soldier or officer would come up to toast him, thanking him for taking such a great risk coming to Draenor and giving his all just so the Sons of Lothar could return home.

He could feel that quite a few of them were still somewhat wary of him. Many knew full well that he was a warlock who'd learned no small amount of dark magic from the orcs of the Shadow Council and had an unsavory relationship with demons. And given that Wildhammer Keep wasn't exactly a big place, any news traveled fast.

But they were still willing to express their sincere gratitude — whether dwarf, human, elf, or warrior, knight, and even priest and member of the Order of the Silver Hand.

The Expedition soldiers, like their leaders, had already cast aside all sorts of dogmas and restrictions amid the blood and fire of Draenor. So long as you could hunt demons, so long as you could be of any help, that was enough to be an ally and a comrade. This was also the main reason the Farstriders of Alleria's Hold had never gone after the black dragons.

Still, not wanting to ruin everyone's mood, Jace couldn't quite let go and relax along with them. Somewhere in his heart, he kept feeling that something wasn't quite right.

Perhaps it was because he'd set foot on a path that no "predecessor" in his memories had ever walked. The stone that had been pressing down on the bottom of his heart ever since birth had, without him realizing it, already sunk away.

Or perhaps it was the sudden growth spurt making the "Morphel's Defeat" that Galeen Ellerian had meticulously tailored for him a bit too snug across the chest. Like a shirt with the top button fastened — it made him, a man used to wearing slightly loose clothes on his adventures, feel uncomfortable the whole time.

"You still seem to have some reservations, Jace?"

At that moment, Turalyon, wearing a light blue military vest, came over with a glass of beer. The two clinked glasses, and the paladin asked with a smile, "Worrying about Lord Thunderfist?"

"How could I possibly worry about him?" Jace gestured with his hand, making a running little man. "Even if all of Auchindoun collapsed, he'd be able to dig his way out while cursing the whole time and run back to Alleria's Hold on foot."

Turalyon couldn't help but laugh, then thought for a moment. "Let me guess — you're not sad that the demon lady from the basement can't attend this banquet, are you?"

Jace laughed along. "Khadgar told me you were a boring, stuffy paladin."

"In his eyes, the whole world is boring and stuffy." Turalyon leaned back against the edge of a nearby table, downed his glass of wine in one gulp, and gently set the glass aside. "Medivh was his mentor. He lived in Karazhan for a few years. After coming out of a place like that, what fun could you possibly expect to find out here?"

Jace also downed his beer in one gulp and nodded in deep agreement. But he didn't quite know how to explain his concerns to this leader of the Order of the Silver Hand.

He thought for a moment, then said, "Karlez — the demon who opened the portal for us — used to be high up in the Burning Legion. He once told me that Draenor was like… something had sealed it off. Many of the teleportation methods that should have worked were no longer effective. He also had a much harder time opening teleportation rifts than expected."

"Khadgar said something similar," Turalyon said. "But you still managed to break through the restrictions in the end."

Jace was momentarily at a loss for how to continue. What he actually wanted to make Turalyon understand was just how wrong things were in Draenor at this moment — the missing Army of the Light, the silent A'dal and Tempest Keep fleet. Compared to Outland of the future, too many glimmers of hope that should have appeared here seemed to have been extinguished.

But at the same time, he didn't quite know how to find the right way to explain all this clearly.

"Before the Dark Portal was closed, we managed to have the griffin riders send the Book of Medivh and the Eye of Dalaran back to the Kirin Tor," Turalyon comforted him. "Don't forget the role those two artifacts played in the destructive power of the Staff of Sargeras, Jace. And of course, the Skull of Gul'dan… Khadgar will surely find a way to use those artifacts to help us return to Azeroth. Archmage Antonidas and the entire Kirin Tor will devote themselves to helping him."

With that, the paladin grabbed a handful of pale-green, translucent-shelled spherical nuts from the plate nearby and handed them to Jace. "On your journey to Nagrand, did you happen to run into a group of, uh… people wrapped in bandages…?"

"I've heard the ogres mention them," Jace nodded.

"Our hunters and pathfinding teams have run into them too," Turalyon said. "They call themselves the Karii, but Khadgar gave them an easier name to remember — the ethereals. Though they're not exactly friendly, they're an intelligent race we can communicate with, and they show no obvious malice. But most importantly, these people only came here in the last few years after Draenor's destruction, just like you."

Hearing the paladin out, Jace's eyes widened slightly too. Right — how had these ethereals slipped through the cracks of the Burning Legion?

Of course, the ethereals' astral travel abilities were something the natives of Azeroth couldn't match, but this did at least show that the Burning Legion's "blockade" was hardly without gaps. If the naaru's ships couldn't get in, that didn't mean others couldn't. Turalyon's words really did open Jace's heart up quite a bit.

If things didn't go smoothly on Khadgar's end, maybe they could go learn from the ethereals. The Expedition knew next to nothing about the ethereals, but he certainly didn't.

Jace was thinking this when he tossed a green-shelled nut into his mouth. Turalyon immediately said, "Uh, you have to shell this first…"

"Oh-uck." Jace quickly spat it out, cracked one open the way Turalyon did following his example, and tossed the white bean-like thing inside into his mouth. A faint sweetness — not bad.

"Oh, right — did the teleportation coordinates Khadgar sent back to Dalaran include Wildhammer Keep's?"

"No," Turalyon answered. "But there's one for a draenei village — Auchena, not far from here. It was previously occupied by a Burning Legion demon named Omor. After the great catastrophe, that coordinate seems to have shifted a bit toward us. We've already occupied that uninhabited highland and stationed a few sentries, in case the Kirin Tor actually figures out a way here but can't find us."

Jace nodded. At that moment, Vereesa suddenly appeared in front of him, reaching out her hand. "Dance with me, Jace?" She glanced at the paladin beside them. "At least tonight is not a time for seriousness, right, gentlemen?"

"Well said." Jace took her hand and looked at the paladin beside him. "Then if you'll excuse me."

"Go on, go on," Turalyon said with a smile.
