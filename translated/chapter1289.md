## Chapter 1289: A Sinister Disturbance

The ritual communicating with Karlez was even more difficult than Jace had anticipated. If he wanted to regularly check in on the situation at Ilgalath Tower, he'd need a lot more casting materials.

It was just that with the Burning Legion pulling back en masse toward Karabor, getting hold of genuine demon blood wasn't so easy anymore. The demon blood Khadgar had stored in the mage tower for research would surely run out soon too, and Jace summoning demons himself was far from enough to sustain the constant consumption.

So Khadgar's successful arrival in Azeroth didn't mean Jace and the others could hole up in Wildhammer Keep and sit around waiting for good news. He had to go out even more frequently than before, hunting the fel-corrupted beasts hiding in the forests and valleys beyond the walls.

An enchanter guide — an elf — led him and Vereesa toward the edges of Shadowmoon Valley. The violent earthquakes were even more frequent than in the Burning Steppes, and fel flames erupted from the ground fissures from time to time, shooting high before being quickly scattered by the shadow winds that ceaselessly howled between the cliffs and hills.

This was where the fel-fire twin-sail drakes lived — creatures so heavily demonized they could survive by devouring nothing but fel lava.

The fel-fire twin-sail drakes were a species of giant reptile similar to the magma drakes dwelling beneath the Searing Gorge. Their bodies were covered in fel-green scales, and they crouched on the banks of lava rivers like crocodiles, even swimming through them with abandon.

These monsters, whose bodies could easily reach ten meters or longer, feared almost nothing in the way of fire. Their thick scales could easily shrug off dwarven gunfire, and even phase hunters didn't dare provoke them lightly — so the hunters of Wildhammer Keep rarely came around to hunt them.

Forget the hunters of Wildhammer Keep — even Vereesa couldn't guarantee she could reliably punch through these fiends' defenses with the Expedition arrows brought out from the keep.

But these creatures' bulk and heavy fel corruption also meant more powerful demon blood and stronger soul shards.

And since these beasts didn't care at all about lurking — swaying the several bright-yellow giant sails on their backs as if afraid others couldn't see them — Vereesa didn't need to prepare ordinary arrows just to conceal her approach either.

Ahead stretched a lava river. Between the blackened scorched earth, the flowing bright-green fel fire was blindingly vivid — but it also illuminated the giant beasts loitering lazily nearby with perfect clarity.

Vereesa picked out a lone, rather sluggish elder fel-fire drake and slowly closed in. The old dragon seemed utterly oblivious to the lethal threat creeping up right before its eyes.

Until Vereesa raised her bow. Before she could even draw the string to summon a glowing dream arrow, her gaze suddenly flickered, aiming further into the distance.

"Someone's over there." She lowered her bow and said quietly.

Jace looked in the direction she was aiming. It was pitch black over there — probably the brightness of the nearby fel-fire river interfering with his observation.

But after staring carefully for a while, he noticed two flickering dark-red shadows approaching the twin-sail drakes on the riverbank from another direction. The two shadows lingered there for a moment, seemingly also looking for an opportunity to hunt.

"Those look like orcs, Ranger-Lieutenant," the mage apprentice beside them said.

"So those are the fel orcs Marshal Turalyon mentioned?" Vereesa frowned slightly. She glanced south. "This is less than an hour's walk from Wildhammer Keep. Didn't the patrolling griffin riders notice these monsters getting this close?"

The mage shook his head. "The last time there was word of orcs was over a month ago, halfway between Alleria's Hold and here. Our patrol teams ran into a squad of fel orcs. There hasn't been any sign of orcs near the keep in a long time."

"Look at them." Jace pointed. "They're not here to hunt fel-fire drake meat."

The two orcs expended quite a bit of effort killing a fel-fire drake. Amid the angry roars of the nearby drakes, those two worked over the drake's head for a moment before hurriedly turning to leave.

"Let's follow them," Jace said. "They want the fel-fire drake's eyes. That's a casting material — there might be an orc warlock of the Shadow Council nearby."

Vereesa nodded. The three of them took a detour, slipping through the blighted woods.

They followed the two orcs all the way to an obviously abandoned orc village. This was quite close to the edge of Shadow valley — terrible, rumbling booms bellowed from the fissures, and fel dust obscured their vision. More than half the village had been destroyed by ground fissures and collapsing boulders. Now all that remained were two or three circular huts of varying sizes that were still barely intact, plus a row of damaged wooden palisades.

Near the palisades, an orc guard was talking with them. These orcs all had red skin. After getting a little closer, Jace noticed black demon spikes exposed all over their faces and shoulders, and the row of spikes along their spines was especially terrifying — so much so that their armor and clothing had been torn open from behind to let these spikes protrude.

Their eyes were a ghastly blood-red, and their tusks curved upward like the massive tusks of a pit lord. Jace was quite certain that at this distance, the tusks of a Mag'har orc would be nearly impossible to make out.

In the game, he'd never observed these fel orcs this closely. Now he understood even more deeply why the Mag'har orcs were unwilling to keep associating with these so-called relatives.

After the two orc hunters entered the compound, Vereesa pointed at a structure behind the broken wall that looked like a stable. "See that? There are several soldiers sitting there with decent equipment, Jace — but I can't quite make out what's going on inside that largest hut."

Jace tried scanning the whole village with demon detection. He pointed at a shadow in a hut next to the stable. "There's a humanoid demon inside — probably a doomguard, but not very strong. Might just be a demon minion summoned by an ordinary warlock."

"Set the stable on fire and make a little trouble, Jace," Vereesa said. "Let's take out this band of orcs and see what they're sneaking around at in there."

"Elements probably won't be much help to me here, Wen," Jace said. "But I have a better idea — take out that gate-guarding orc first."

"There might be more than just this one sentry in this camp, Jace," Vereesa said. "Killing this one will likely expose our position immediately."

"I only need that much time." Jace gripped his staff.

Vereesa nodded, drew an arrow, nocked it, and aimed for a moment. "Where should I aim — the head or the heart?"

"They're not true demons yet, Ranger-Lieutenant," the enchanter said in a low voice. "Even hitting the heart would be hard to kill them instantly." With that, Vereesa put an arrow straight through the fel orc's nose and into its brainstem. The guard let out no sound, slumping against the wall behind him.

His hands twitched and struggled a few times, then went still.

Jace immediately strode out of the woods with Ursales, coming up to the wooden palisades, keeping his eyes locked on the stable behind the broken wall. The orc soldiers sitting inside eating seemed to have noticed nothing yet. Masses of shadow-black mist were already gathering in the air like black serpents.

A curse in Orcish rang out from the ruins nearby. Vereesa immediately condensed a dream arrow and fired it.

The dream energy explosion sent up a shower of rubble. The orc shouted a few times but failed to show himself. By then the orcs in the stable had also sensed something was amiss, grabbing their weapons one after another and trying to get up to see what was going on outside — but it was already too late.

The incessant rifts and the terrifying booms in the canyons had masked the Hand of Gul'dan Jace had already been channeling. A dark meteor dragged shadow fire and the howls of souls under Ursales's guidance, smashing fiercely into the palisade behind the wall. The explosion sent up a burst of purple fel flames and brilliant light, and the shockwave overturned a large swath of wooden palisades and posts.

The wall behind the stable, already on the verge of collapse, had half its wall blown off and caved in on the spot. The fel orc soldiers were blasted into rubble and black blood that scattered in every direction — even their souls had been shattered into fragments of soul debris that scattered into the Void. Ursales couldn't even be bothered to grab them.

Terrifying Shadowflame quickly spread across half the village. But the surviving fel orc showed no fear at all — in the blink of an eye, four or five soldiers still capable of fighting were howling and charging at Jace outside the wall, raising black war axes and spears.

But their fel-iron armor and demon skin were no match for Vereesa's arrows. On the flat slope, they were picked off one by one like live targets.

Jace stepped over the bodies of these orcs, turning into a mass of shadow and darting past the broken wall. Using the Black Dragoncloak as cover, he crossed the wall of shadow fire he'd ignited and reached the front of the hut first.

At the same time, a felhound materialized from a rift in the Void at his summoning, leaping over the open flames to land in front of its master. A clanging of iron boots rang out from ahead — the doomguard was coming.

The doomguard charged at him with a long-handled serrated blade. Jace had just caught its movements with an Asphyxiate when the demon suddenly burned to ashes in a ball of fel fire, leaving only a few pieces of scorched armor and weapons on the ground.

He'd been sacrificed by his master.

The warlock inside clearly already knew how powerful his outside opponent was, and planned to sacrifice his minion to convert it into some powerful demonic force for a desperate last stand?

By then Vereesa had also caught up from behind. She was the first to charge into the hut. Jace and the felhound followed her in. The hut was a typical orc village center — a walk down a curving hallway led to the ruined stone hall in the middle.

In the center of the stone hall, a dark-skinned orc warlock in gray robes stood in the middle of a ritual circle drawn with demon blood, wreathed in fel fire and channeling magic. The moment Vereesa appeared, a ball of green fire lunged at her fiercely.

The Incinerate singe was barely avoided as she ducked behind the wall. The flames crawled up the wall and ignited the roof. The warlock immediately began channeling a second Incinerate — but before the second one could go off, Vereesa was faster than him.

She seized the opportunity to lean out from behind the wall and fire an arrow at the orc. The dream arrow slammed right into the fel flame coils surrounding him, causing a blinding explosion. Though it didn't punch through, it staggered the orc and interrupted his casting.

That shield was probably formed from the doomguard's sacrifice. Jace's felhound darted out of the hallway door and pounced, tearing at the protective flames. The evil power around him visibly decayed at a rate you could see with the naked eye.

The second arrow Vereesa followed up with cleanly pierced the protective magic and shattered the warlock's chest. The orc was smashed into the ground like a rag, his chest almost completely severed, with only a scrap of tattered skin still clinging to it. The whole body twisted and flopped at an angle hard to imagine. His jaw trembled a few times, and he didn't manage to say anything either.

This warlock had died too quickly. Jace had just followed in and hadn't had time to channel Drain Soul and craft a shard yet.

The hall was already ablaze, fel fire spreading everywhere. The felhound gnawed at the spreading flames to no avail — they had to leave quickly.

The enchanter who'd just followed in glanced at the ritual circle on the ground and the various bloody animal eyeballs arranged on the table, gleaming wetly under the fel fire. He frowned. "No mistake — this is the casting ritual for the Eye of Kilrogg."

"Let's get out of here first," Jace said.

The few of them made their way through the gradually burning hallway. Vereesa said, "But we didn't see any floating demon eyes outside."

"That means this orc's ritual wasn't used to protect this village," Jace said.

"That's right, Master," the enchanter said as they walked. "They might be using this magic to scout the keep's situation. We have to go back and report to Kurdran Wildhammer…"

Boommm…

A series of ethereal, enormous booms came from outside, cutting him off. The sound was as if it came from the depths of the Twisting Nether. Then the hallway began to tremble slightly.

Another earthquake?

This orc great hall was about to collapse. Getting buried under the burning wooden and stone roof would be no quick death. They immediately quickened their pace, rushing out of the hallway into the open ground outside.

The abandoned village was already engulfed in shadow flames and fel fire. The ground shook incessantly, and the great hall behind them gradually collapsed. Jace looked out toward the Void — those shattered black floating islands were slowly rotating, and beyond the thick black clouds in the sky, who knew when a hazy, rotating magic dark light had appeared, reminding him of Shattrath.

Was this place starting to collapse too? Jace didn't want another desperate great escape.

Though the trembling of the ground was far less violent than at Shattrath — just a slight shaking — the anomaly in the sky couldn't be ignored either.

The mage also noticed something wrong in the sky. He chanted an incantation, and a faint cyan arcane rune appeared on his forehead. Jace had seen this magic at the Wizard Sanctum — it was a magic amplification incantation to enhance magical perception. He must have wanted to use this incantation to observe exactly what was happening here.

But before the incantation could complete, the rune scattered like shattered glass. The elf mage grimaced in pain and vomited a mouthful of blood.

"What happened?" Vereesa asked.

The enchanter coughed a few times. "No, I don't know…"

"Can you still walk?" Jace asked.

"I can." The enchanter nodded.

At that moment, the pack at Jace's waist began to shake violently. After rummaging through it, Jace noticed it was two flasks of Lesser Mana Potion he'd brought out — the liquid inside was bubbling.

He decisively threw all the mana potions away. "Back to the keep. Now."
