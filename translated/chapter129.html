Chapter 129: The Kul Tiras Ship

The sun rose from the sea, no longer hiding its face. Sunlight raked across the sides of everything in the harbor, splitting every person and ship into light and shadow.

The Great Baradin Conqueror was a black wooden sailing ship built by the Kingdom of Lordaeron. You could tell it had undergone goblin-style modifications — it looked a good deal more aggressive.

Jace knew nothing about sailing, but he could roughly estimate the deck at a hundred and twenty-odd feet, about forty meters long. If his past-life self had seen it, he definitely wouldn't have thought a forty-meter ship was anything impressive — back then, the aircraft carriers he'd heard about were routinely two or three hundred meters. But here, with his own eyes, this ship absolutely qualified as enormous — especially compared to the other vessels at the dock.

The only ship in the whole of Stormwind harbor that seemed longer was a warship flying the lion king's banner in the distance — but only because its forward-raked bowsprit jutted out long, like a lance thrust forward by a charging heavy knight. It had a lot more presence than the tubby transport ship over here.

There were already thirty or forty people bustling about the deck. The whole ship could probably carry two hundred people without trouble, even with a fair amount of cargo aboard. Especially with a good number of goblin crew — that might mean it could carry even more.

Jace sat at the edge of the deck, looking toward the shore. He noticed that unlike the lively crowds seeing off the other ships, this Baradin Conqueror had no well-wishers.

"What kind of people do you think are on this ship?" Jace asked Grede beside him.

Grede was gnawing on a piece of bread. Chewing, he reluctantly turned his head to glance up at the helm, where several goblins sat, also looking their way.

"It's a ship run by goblins of the Trade Cartel. Pay your fare and anyone can board, any cargo can come aboard. If you ask me, I couldn't say for sure. But there are definitely no small number of people like us who simply came too late to book a ticket and had no choice but to get on this ship, Jace."

Jace could only hope the latecomers made up the majority. Otherwise, if the whole ship was full of bandits and thugs, he'd be stuck at sea for half a month — unbearable.

Thinking of that, he sighed. Grede eyed him sideways. "What, you think the passengers on this ship aren't good people?"

"The proportion of good people is probably a bit lower than on other ships," Jace said helplessly.

"And you think you're one of the good ones?"

"What? How am I not a good one?"

"Go ask these passengers — would they rather share a ship with a thief, or share one with a warlock?"

"…"

Seeing Jace's reaction, Grede laughed. "People who travel with goblins regularly might actually prefer sitting with an ogre than traveling alongside a warlock… You, once we set sail, find a nice quiet spot and lie down. Don't cause any trouble, my little Jace. If you expose yourself and someone takes me for a demon worshipper and grabs us both to throw us overboard for the fish, we're done for."

Grede was absolutely right. These days, a warlock's reputation wasn't much better than a goblin's or a thief's — at best, only marginally better than an actual pirate's. And Jace was a genuine warlock. Not only did he wield dark magic, he'd joined an underground warlock circle. Top-grade purity, that.

Thinking it over, the people boarding the ship suddenly looked a good deal more presentable. Turns out the worst one here was him all along.

A moment later, a goblin behind them bellowed, "Is all the cargo aboard?"

"Just tallied it up — not a single piece missing!" came a muffled reply from below.

"Then weigh anchor! Weigh anchor!"

"Any more guests coming?"

"Look at the time. The cargo's aboard, that's what matters. The sun's up! Let the unpunctual ones pay the price!"

"Weigh anchor! Weigh anchor! The Great Baradin Conqueror sets sail!"

"Damn it, tear that plank down, now!"

"Yes, sir!"

"Hurry up!"

A huge crowd of goblins swarmed the capstan, cranking away frantically. A job that should have taken two men now required this whole mob of goblins working together. Jace's earlier thought that the ship might carry extra passengers thanks to its many goblin sailors evaporated on the spot.

The hull gave a light shudder. A clanking, rattling sound of chains sliding came from beneath the ship.

"We're casting off," Grede said, looking into the distance. Jace followed his gaze and saw Stormwind Keep halfway up the hillside. That massive castle leaned against the hill like a shield, while the Cathedral of Light in the distance soared into the heavens like a silver lance.

Jace couldn't help but think about the journey ahead — the troubles they might run into, the sights they might see, from Baradin Bay to Alterac Valley. The further he thought, the vaguer it all became.

"What are you thinking about?" Grede asked.

"What we'll be like when we come back here," Jace said.

"Ah, I've wondered something similar." Grede turned sideways. "The difference is, I've already changed. Spending too much time with you lot, I feel like I'm turning into a human."

"How so?"

The dwarf pointed at the dense cluster of Stormwind's houses in the distance. "Leave behind the mountain caves, the carved gate corridors, the rooms covered in runes… move into these tall buildings and wooden houses, hide behind stone walls. A little change every day, and over time you go bad. Sometimes, walking the streets of Stormwind, I feel like I'm just a very short, very stocky human."

"That reminds me of a story," Jace said.

"Go on, Jace, I could use a story right now." The dwarf turned around with interest.

Jace said, "Long ago, when Kul Tiras was still part of the Arathi Empire, they built a mighty warship. After decades of grinding war, she still hadn't been sunk — but by the time the war ended, every single one of her components had been replaced at least once."

"So, was she still the same warship?"

"Of course," Grede said without hesitation. "No matter how many parts you swap out, she's still called what she's called."

"Then you have your answer, Grede," Jace said.

"So that's what you meant?" Grede's eyes widened in realization. He leaned against the railing, propping his chin in his hand. "There's truth in that, but something about it doesn't quite sit right."

Jace knew this slightly adapted Theseus' ship story was only good for comforting Grede a little. If he, like that ship, had every piece of his body slowly replaced by shadow or fel energy, it might be hard to still call him the original Jace Sesso.

Before they knew it, the Baradin Conqueror was pulling farther and farther from the harbor. Stormwind grew smaller. The things at the dock grew vaguer. The crash of the waves and the sailors' shouts gradually drowned out the noise drifting over from the city.

Translator Notes

Flags

- 舷斜桅 = bowsprit: Nautical term for the forward-raked spar. - 热砂财团 = the Trade Cartel: Same rendering as chapter 128. - 狮子王旗 = the lion king's banner: House of Wrynn royal standard, same as chapter 128. - 忒修斯之船 = Theseus' ship: The philosophical paradox, here adapted with a Kul Tiras warship. - 阿拉索帝国 = the Arathi Empire: 阿拉索 = Arathi in the glossary. - Chapter title 库尔提拉斯之船 = "The Kul Tiras Ship": The title references the Theseus' ship story Jace tells, which is set in Kul Tiras — not the name of the actual ship.
