## Chapter 1291: What Exactly Are We Up Against

The dust of the chaotic magic settled, and the soldiers who hadn't been injured threw themselves into the rescue efforts.

Wildhammer Keep really was a solid fortress, built into the mountainside by the finest dwarves of Aerie Peak. Aside from Khadgar's mage tower, which had exploded from storing too much magical energy, and a few nearby hastily built wooden huts and dwarven houses, the whole structure hadn't sustained much damage from this shock.

But Jace also knew very well that whether or not Kil'jaeden was behind this disaster, the goal hadn't been to destroy these buildings — it was to more thoroughly sever Draenor's connection to the outside world.

Arcane teleportation in the past, and now even Karlez's demon teleportation had been cut off. How on earth had this been accomplished?

Jace didn't know whether Eredar lords of Kil'jaeden or Archimonde's caliber could complete a magical ritual on this scale. But he knew that the shattered Outland of today wasn't capable of sustaining the teleportation of either of them to this world.

Otherwise, when Kil'jaeden suspected Illidan Stormrage and his subordinates were about to betray him and set up their own kingdom in the future, he wouldn't have barely managed to project a shadow over to intimidate them.

As Jace helped the soldiers pull survivors from the rubble, he couldn't help thinking during a moment to catch his breath — why would Kil'jaeden expend so much effort creating a curtain capable of covering this entire shattered world?

First of all, Khadgar had only left three or four days ago, and this blockade had descended right on its heels. This was almost certainly no coincidence.

Perhaps the Burning Legion feared Khadgar's power and worried he might be able to ruin the whole plan, so they'd waited for him to leave before locking up everyone remaining inside.

Second, it had been two or three weeks since the fall of Shattrath. Kil'jaeden had probably already communicated with Bathras's demon soul and learned the news of Ursales — and this curtain might be a forceful response.

But this still didn't explain why, ever since Jace first arrived in Draenor, he'd had the faint feeling that this world had already been cut off.

Even if A'dal and the Tempest Keep fleet might not have arrived in Draenor at this point in another timeline, the Army of the Light should have taken Alleria and Turalyon — the husband-and-wife pair — away at the moment of Draenor's shattering, to join the "Thousand-Year War."

Coupled with the terrifying magical blockade Draenor was facing at this very moment… Jace was very clear that the oppressive pressure from the night sky he'd felt that evening at Alleria's Hold was no illusion, and no fear of the unknown — it was real.

In other words, this magic had already begun before he arrived. It was only now, under the command of some presence peering in from the Twisting Nether, that it had truly taken form.

Was Kil'jaeden waiting for him?

Jace was unwilling to consider this. Thinking it over this way, all the preparation he'd done in Azeroth and all the plans he'd kept hidden for so long seemed literally like walking right into a trap.

But this was also a possibility he couldn't afford to ignore… Could this be Karlez, trying to atone for his crimes, secretly revealing his entire plan to Kil'jaeden well in advance?

Then wouldn't the Burning Legion have known long ago that he possessed Ursales?

That didn't sound very reasonable either. Bathras, the guardian of the Tome of Fel Names, had clearly known nothing about it. It was only halfway through the fight that he'd recognized Ursales's power, and he'd even abandoned Shattrath and the Tome of Fel Names, risking having his flesh torn apart to throw himself into the Twisting Nether rather than have his demon soul drained.

The eye lord in Auchindoun, who'd fed on demon souls through the Shadow Council, hadn't even been able to recognize Ursales — it seemed he still hadn't figured out what had happened even after his demon soul was drained.

Most importantly, Karlez, a high-ranking demon of considerable power, was still firmly under his control, occupying a large portion of his willpower and energy, to the point where summoning demons other than felhounds and imps was quite taxing for him.

Jace believed it was possible that, through some accidentally acquired aid or power, he might suddenly break free of control — but no matter how you looked at it, control was still in place. That demon couldn't forcibly defy orders and pledge allegiance to another "master" while the contract magic still existed.

Demons were just like that — otherwise Jace wouldn't have had zero interest in the rather terrifying demonic transformation of Dragonmaw Warlord Nekros.

In any case, these events seemed to contradict each other, yet also seemed to be faintly connected. Jace couldn't sort out an explanation that made everything clear for the time being, and the more he thought about it, the more muddled his thoughts became.

The most critical thing was that, up to this very moment, he hadn't heard a whisper of wind related to any of these events from the demons of the Burning Legion. Kil'jaeden also hadn't directly warned him, tempted him, or even intimidated him through dreams or visions the way Deathwing had — so much so that even after sorting through all this, Jace couldn't be a hundred percent sure whether this was being orchestrated by Kil'jaeden.

But at least one thing he'd confirmed: the Burning Legion hadn't given up reconnaissance of Wildhammer Keep just because they'd fully pulled back. Those orcs had hidden themselves where the Expedition's patrols rarely went and were secretly using rituals to probe the situation here. Khadgar's departure must have been within their grasp too.

Khadgar had also prepared for this long ago — otherwise he wouldn't have felt at ease leaving only after Jace and the others had obtained the Tome of Fel Names. He'd just assumed the Burning Legion would take advantage of his absence to launch an all-out attack, and hadn't guessed that the demons would actually play this card.

A horn sounded from the wall — Turalyon was back.

Kurdran Wildhammer, covered in dust, pulled himself up from the rubble and looked over. "Jace, Vereesa, come with us to the keep. Lady Sola, please take care of these injured poor bastards for us! I have to get to the bottom of what's going on."

"No problem, Lord High Thane Kurdran," Sola nodded.

The keep's leaders gathered in the keep hall. Turalyon hurried in without even taking off his armor. The moment he saw him, Kurdran anxiously asked, "How are our outposts?"

"Nothing major, just a few mages injured," Turalyon said. "What on earth is this? Can anyone explain it? Why are all things related to magic going haywire?"

"The 'blockade' Archmage Khadgar spoke of seems to have been strengthened," Jace confessed. "After the explosion at the top of the mage tower, I tried contacting the demon on the other side responsible for the portal. He told me the teleportation rift could no longer be opened."

"What? The teleport failed?"

"No way back?"

"How is this possible…"

"Is this the real purpose of this disaster? Were those magical implements breaking just a 'side effect'?"

"They're trying to trap us here for a decisive battle, aren't they? Then the hells with it — let those demons come, let Magtheridon come!"

"That's right, I was already prepared to fight the demons and orcs to the bitter end. At least now we've gotten Archmage Khadgar back — that's much better than before!"

"Everyone quiet down for a moment…"

The hall immediately buzzed with whispered discussions. After it quieted down a little, Turalyon looked over and asked, "So what else are you hiding, Jace? That dinner the other day — did your concern have any real basis? You seem very preoccupied with the barrier between Draenor and the outside world."
