Chapter 1292: This Ally's a Whole Different Weight Class

When the Marshal said that, everyone in the fort turned to look their way at once. It was clear Jace had to come up with something decent right about now.

Jace pinched the bridge of his nose and said, "When we went to Nagrand, we passed through Shattrath City on the way and met a group of priests guarding the place. They called themselves the Aldor."

"I know those Draenei," Kurdran said. "We fought the ogres and the Shadow Council alongside them back then."

"Right. They'd been trying in Shattrath to call upon the Naaru for aid," Jace said. "But the Naaru never answered them, which seemed… unusual. One of the Draenei priests told me they'd always regarded the Naaru as their teachers and guides. If the Naaru stopped answering their pleas, that meant the blockade was far more than a simple arcane-channel shutdown — it was something much more powerful. But back then I just figured I was worrying over nothing."

Kurdran Wildhammer said, "Maybe those gray-blue giants were just superstitious over some legendary nonsense. Even Khadgar doubts whether the Naaru actually exist!"

"If I may, honored Lord Marshal — the Draenei's wisdom and reason surpass what you or I might imagine," a human major standing nearby said. "If one or two Draenei were clinging to delusions, that might be possible. But if every single Draenei believes in something… then it's not entirely baseless. The orcs know the Spirit Mountains of Nagrand carried the Draenei down from the sky centuries ago. Many of the Draenei who came out of that crash are still alive today. They know it was the Naaru's void ship."

"Whether the Naaru exist or not, the Draenei probably do know things we don't," Turalyon said, gazing at the Farstriders in the distance. "Captain Alleria — do you know where that Draenei prophet might be?"

A ranger answered, "After the fall of Shattrath, Prophet Velen appears to have led many of his followers and refugees north to take shelter, Marshal Turalyon. But even the Draenei who remained at Auchindoun don't know his exact whereabouts."

Jace knew this Prophet Velen was actually another reason the blockade had been thrown up.

Ten thousand years ago, before the Eredar were corrupted, Archimonde, Kil'jaeden, and Velen had ruled their homeworld of Argus together. When the other two chose to accept Sargeras's supreme power alongside countless other Eredar, Velen had chosen to flee Argus with a group of followers who refused to be corrupted. They called themselves the Draenei — "the Exiled Ones" — and Kil'jaeden had never stopped hunting them.

Turalyon was right. Prophet Velen likely knew things that even Jace didn't.

"We'll need to send a sufficiently elite squad to the village of Kirin'val in Farahlon to make contact with Magister Wargas, and at the same time find out what they can about that Draenei leader," Turalyon said, staring at Farahlon's position on the map, his eyes full of worry.

The mages here had all been hurt to varying degrees. Khadgar's mage tower had been devastated because of the large number of artifacts and rune-stones stored inside. God only knew what kind of disaster Kirin'val — home to over a hundred mages — had suffered.

"We'll go," Vereesa said at once. "We're willing to take on this mission, Marshal Turalyon."

"No. You must stay here." Turalyon looked at Vereesa. "Wildhammer Keep is on the front line right now. We're facing Karabor — there could be demon soldiers massing hundreds of miles away as we speak. If anything happens to this fort, Alleria's Hold will be left to fight alone, and Honor Hold will soon be caught between two fires… Jace and his minions hold the weaknesses of those demon lords, so they absolutely cannot leave here for the north."

A ranger captain saluted Vereesa and said, "We can accomplish this vital mission, Ranger-Lord. Please trust us."

"I know…" Vereesa looked at the captain, her eyes betraying that she still wasn't quite used to her position as leader among the Farstriders here.

Probably because plenty of the rangers here had once been her elders, even her teachers.

Turalyon added, "Yes. Many of our finest Farstriders have been to Gorgrond and Farahlon. They're better suited than you for carrying out further search and reconnaissance missions, Vereesa. This isn't a matter of doubting your abilities — but Wildhammer Keep needs solid fighting strength right now."

"I understand, Marshal," Vereesa said, glancing at Jace. Jace nodded.

Turalyon was right — they knew nothing about the situation in the Blade's Edge Mountains. Even though Jace was familiar with the in-game map, he wouldn't claim to know the way through that tangled mess of ravines and cliffs for certain, let alone the far more complex reality of it.

And once they reached the Blade's Edge, their group's greatest advantage — Sola's incredible flying speed — would no longer be an advantage. If the gorgers that popped up from time to time in Nagrand were a considerable threat to her, then the Blade's Edge — covered in massive spikes, full of gronn lords, and home to the Gronn himself — would be an absolute hell for her.

Gryphons and hawkstriders, far smaller and more agile, were much better suited for travel there.

"Actually, I don't think things have reached their most desperate point yet," Turalyon said, looking at Kurdran. "Khadgar on the other side hasn't sent word yet. Perhaps he and the Kirin Tor will find a way to rebuild the portal."

"I'm not as optimistic as you are, Turalyon," Kurdran shook his head. "Think about why the Burning Legion waited for Khadgar to return before pulling this stunt! They definitely knew what our archmage was planning. If the Kirin Tor can still easily bring everyone back, then the demons don't deserve to be reverently called 'the Deceiver' by their own masters!"

The dwarf lord slammed the table. "I say we have to prepare to fight the demons here for the long haul. If Kil'jaeden thinks he can close the fence and catch the chicken, then we'll show him just how fierce this rooster really is!"

"No one dares question the Wildhammer's courage and might, Kurdran," Turalyon said. "But we can't give up hope on the other side either."

"That's another reason the two of you can't leave Wildhammer Keep, Vereesa, Jace. Just now I heard Jace say you can still communicate with the Azeroth side through rituals, correct?"

"Yes," Jace said. "It just requires more mana and casting materials."

Turalyon nodded. "We can help you prepare the materials. You need to stay in the fort and not wander too far, so you can maintain regular contact with the other side through the rituals. I imagine Khadgar must already understand just how critical your minion's communication ability is."

"I understand, Marshal," Jace said.

"But Kurdran, you're right about one thing too. Even so, we have to prepare for a long fight against the Burning Legion." Turalyon looked around at everyone. "On one hand, to buy the Kirin Tor time to find a solution. On the other, we have to make the demons understand that they can't even defeat us — so forget about invading Azeroth."

"We all know what the Deceiver is scheming. Draenor is nothing more than a springboard for attacking Azeroth. Our homeland is these demons' ultimate goal. Every extra day we hold out here means our loved ones stay one step further from these fiends."

"Well said, Marshal Turalyon! For Azeroth!"

"For the Alliance!"

The leaders, officers, and mages in the hall all shouted their slogans with fierce determination. Even the elves were resolute, having nearly erased many of the stereotypes Jace held of the quel'dorei.

Turalyon lowered his eyes to the map on the table. "The Draenei each possess extraordinary wisdom and strength, but there are simply too few of them. In a situation like this, we need more allies."

"Perhaps we can win over the orcs of Nagrand," Vereesa said.

"Are you sure about that, Ranger-Lord?" Kurdran said. "I know some orcs who didn't drink the demon blood aren't quite like the orcs we know, but… are you sure?"

"This disaster has sealed off all of Draenor, Lord Marshal — and that includes them. If we fail, the orcs there will suffer too," Vereesa said. "Nagrand can't sit this out. If the Mag'har don't want to help the demons, they'll have to stand with us. They don't have a choice."

Just as Kurdran was hesitating, Jace spoke up. "That young Bleeding Hollow chieftain once told me he wanted to establish friendly relations with the Alliance. He shouldn't refuse the Expedition's envoys coming to discuss this with them, Marshal Turalyon."

"Then I'll send a mage and a Knight of the Silver Hand as envoys to Nagrand," Turalyon said. "Any other ideas, everyone?"

"Perhaps we could get in touch with the black dragons," Jace said.

"Black dragons?" The officers in the hall all started murmuring among themselves.

After all, Jace knew that Outland's black dragons — having broken free of the Old Gods' shadow on Azeroth and been abandoned by Deathwing — had gradually regained their own will. But the Expedition here didn't actually know that.

They only knew that the black dragons didn't seem to have any particular hostility toward the Expedition at the moment — but that was probably just because the threat the demons posed was even greater.

The black dragons hadn't yet endured the bloody, decade-plus war against the gronn lords, and their numbers were still quite large. In the level-70 era, decades from now, the black dragons of Terokkar and Shadowmoon Valley would be nearly extinct, and the adult black dragons left in the Blade's Edge would be few and far between.

The Dragonmaw clan under Illidan's command had stockpiled vast numbers of void-tainted black dragon eggs on the Netherwing Spire and in Dragonmaw Fortress, using them to raise a netherwing drake force as mounts for the Dragonmaw air corps. The Black Dragonqueen herself had even gone to them seeking cooperation.

Compared to that, the fact that the black dragons could still organize a force to assault Dragonmaw Fortress head-on, trying to steal back the eggs inside, was proof that the black dragons were a far more numerous fighting force than the orcs.

If they could be brought to temporarily stand with the Alliance under the pressure of the Burning Legion, things would get a whole lot easier.

"Boy, I was still wondering if teaming up with the Bleeding Hollow clan was a bit off," Kurdran said, folding his arms. "And now you go and make a proposal that makes the orcs all look downright lovable by comparison."

"Will that even work, Jace?" Vereesa said. "Don't forget… we killed their father."

"But the black dragons in Draenor might not know about that." Turalyon glanced at Vereesa, then said, "And right now the demons and orcs are slaughtering and defiling their unborn offspring. Those dragon eggs, brought here at great cost from Azeroth, are the most precious thing in the world to the black dragons — and to Deathwing too. So even if the black dragons already know, I think they understand which side of the scale weighs heavier."

"Hold on — so you're saying you support Jace's idea too, Turalyon?!" Kurdran's eyes went wide. "The hell I just hear?"

"We could ally with the vile gronn against Deathwing, so naturally we can form a temporary alliance with the black dragons against the demons," Turalyon said. "Jace's proposal is pragmatic, Kurdran. The most important thing right now is dealing with the Burning Legion and getting everyone back to Azeroth alive. Everything else, I can compromise on."

"Fine. If the head paladin says so, what else can I say?" Kurdran raised his hands in surrender. "I'll just say this — I'm not the one dealing with the black dragons. Whoever's better at it can go find them! I can only promise that when the fighting with the Burning Legion starts, if my hammer accidentally aims at one of those big flying black lizards, I'll remember to veer it off by a few degrees…"
