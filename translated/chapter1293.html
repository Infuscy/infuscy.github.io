Chapter 1293: The Least Suitable Envoy

The general agreement in the keep's main hall was that a temporary alliance with the black dragons against the Burning Legion was the right call, but who would actually go meet them, how to find them, and how to open a friendly dialogue — those were all still open questions.

Relief work from the magical catastrophe wasn't fully finished, and the outer walls could face demon harassment at any moment, so the two leaders of the Sons of Lothar decided to shelve the unresolved questions for later discussion.

After the meeting, as they walked into the corridor outside the hall, Turalyon called Jace aside. "I think when it comes to negotiating with the black dragons, Jace, it still has to be you."

Not far off, Vereesa was exchanging opinions with the other Farstriders, visibly tense. Though she hadn't insisted on heading north, she clearly wanted to understand the situation as much as possible.

Turalyon's gaze swept across her and the mages and officers who were heading off in conversation, then he lowered his voice to Jace. "Since the collapse of Draenor, we really haven't had any conflict with Deathwing's brood. It was completely different when we first arrived in Outland. Even though everyone here knows the black dragons have fallen out with the demons, the fighting hasn't been over for many years."

"Most importantly, Jace — you're the only one who can figure out what the black dragons actually intend, what they're planning, whether their words match their actions. You're the only warlock here. You have abilities none of us possess."

Jace nodded. He understood that Turalyon wanted him to leverage his advantage in shadow and emotion.

He wasn't surprised Turalyon knew that much about warlocks. After all, they'd been locked in a perpetual game of wits with the Shadow Council from Azeroth all the way to Draenor.

"But facing the black dragons means risking that they've already gotten word from the other side," Turalyon said seriously. "I'm almost certain they have no way of getting news from Azeroth, but Khadgar once told me that dragons have some kind of transcendent connection with their elders that we can't understand. They might have learned something through a mental channel."

"I'm willing to take that risk. This idea was mine," Jace said. "Even if we fail, we don't lose anything. But if we succeed, our chances of victory increase dramatically."

"Your life is already a risk I can barely afford to take." Turalyon's throat tightened slightly. "Khadgar's already cut off out there. We can't lose you too."

Then he added, half-jokingly, "More importantly, I don't want Alleria's little sister to lose the love of her life at such a young age."

When the commander of the Order of the Silver Hand said these words, his emotions were far more complicated than Jace had expected. The transcendent certainty he'd shown in the meeting was mostly gone.

At first, Jace couldn't help wanting to quip — "at such a young age"? What, it'd be fine if she were older? But he quickly realized Turalyon was facing the same problem.

The other timeline's Turalyon had been blessed by the naaru, gaining near-infinite life, but this timeline's Turalyon had never met the Army of the Light. That made him just a mortal, not knowing what the future held for him. And Alleria...

"Trust me, Turalyon," Jace said. "I've dealt with black dragons more than you might think. I'll be careful."

Turalyon's eyebrow twitched slightly. He took a deep breath, shook off the barely perceptible shadow on his face, and managed a smile. "I don't doubt that, Jace. But I can still offer you some advice."

"The scouts from Alleria's Keep often spot young dragons hunting giant insects in the forest near the Skettis peaks when they patrol. You could try making contact with those hunters first, instead of meeting directly with Teribus, the black dragon leader near Auchindoun. Test their attitude first. What do you think?"

"That's a good idea," Jace said. "I'll do exactly that. As soon as Grede Thunderfist returns to the stronghold, I'll set out."

Turalyon patted Jace on the shoulder. "Then I need to head to the northern outpost to assess the damage. Jace, may the Holy Light watch over everyone here."

"Safe journey." Jace nodded.

After Vereesa finished conferring with the Farstriders, the two of them left the keep together. Jace was relaying Turalyon's instructions to Vereesa when a wizard wrapped in a violet Kirin Tor robe walked toward them carrying a small bundle.

"Mr. Sesso, I have here a few soul shards that Archmage Khadgar recovered from the Shadow Council. They've been sitting in the alchemy lab for a long time with no use. I'm not sure if they can help you."

When Jace opened the bundle, he noticed the five soul shards inside were of surprisingly excellent quality. These were likely made from the souls of draenei by the demons during the initial orc invasion of Azeroth.

"These shards are very powerful. Thank you," Jace said, and the wizard gave a slight bow before leaving.

With the soul shards in hand, the two of them headed straight for the basement of Khadgar's tower. By the time they arrived, Morophil had already finished reconstructing the demon blood circle on the floor.

"Good work, Morophil." Jace nodded to the demon before him, who was slightly taller than he was.

"You're welcome." Morophil narrowed her eyes, leaning against the wall as she sized them up. "So, what are you and those mortal soldiers planning?"

"Simple. Keep fighting," Vereesa said. Jace nodded.

"I thought you'd figured it out by now, master," Morophil said. "Do you know what you might be facing?"

"Kil'jaeden the Deceiver, or Archimonde the Defiler." Jace placed one of the soul shards on the ground.

He could feel that just this one shard alone seemed enough to power a ritual like the one he'd barely managed during the earthquake. The draenei souls inside, though fragmented, were immeasurably stronger than any complete fel beast soul.

"It can't be the Defiler." Morophil's voice was cold. "If it were him, this castle would already be flattened, and neither you nor I would exist. Archimonde likes to watch everything that tries to stand in his way crumble to ash in despair. He has no patience for time-wasting 'foreplay.'"

"But Kil'jaeden is a harder enemy to deal with, my dear master. He'd give you a far worse outcome than instant annihilation. I don't think you know what he's capable of."

"Is that so?" Vereesa asked. "Are you afraid?"

"I just don't like blind optimism," Morophil said.

Her face — so translucent you could glimpse the demon blood flowing and the texture of muscle beneath — held a beauty so cold it was unsettling. There was no trace of fear on it either. Just the detachment of a bystander.

"We are a bit 'blind' right now," the ranger said calmly. "That can't be helped, Morophil. We're in the open now, while the enemy's hiding in the dark. But on the other hand, we're nowhere close to optimistic."

"You don't need to worry about us. Just kill as many Burning Legion demons as possible. I don't expect anything more than that."

"Hunting demons, fighting the Legion — that's practically everything to me, Lady Vereesa," Morophil said.

"Good." Vereesa gave a quick smile. "Then we're in agreement."

Morophil didn't reply. She just tilted her head slightly. For her, Jace knew, that was already a serious enough response.

Jace looked at Vereesa and nodded, then stepped into the center of the circle and began his incantation.

Using the power of the draenei soul shards, he groped through the endless void for Karlrez's demon soul. After calming himself, building the bridge of communication was not only not easier — it actually felt harder than before.

That terrifying "Legion web" made Jace feel like a sea beast thrashing in a net, struggling, slowly suffocating...

Then suddenly, the other side seemed to respond. The doom lord grabbed Jace's consciousness with what felt like a demonic claw, yanking him all the way toward the Tower of Il'gara. Jace felt like he could almost see Karlrez's blazing fel eyes staring down at him.

"Karlrez... is Khadgar with you?" He forced out the words in Demonic.

"He has... left here, to Stormwind... Amy Marin, gryphon, report... your news..."

Jace grunted an acknowledgment. "Can you tell what happened to Draenor? With your experience working for the Burning Legion, what kind of magic is this?"

"Very... demonic power. I suspect... only eredar could complete such magic," Karlrez said.

"So you also think it's Kil'jaeden," Jace said.

"Kil'jaeden?" Karlrez hesitated, his voice going fuzzy.

"What do you think?" Jace asked.

"Kil'jaeden could complete... magic like this. Perhaps." Karlrez's voice was so low it was barely audible. "But he is... in the depths of the Twisting Nether. He is on... Argus. Unless he personally descended to Draenor, it would be impossible from... so far away..."

"Draenor is just a pitiful, broken little rock. Even if it once possessed strange magic, now it is... completely spent. It could never... sustain Kil'jaeden's... presence."

"Then besides him, who else could complete spatial magic on this scale?" Jace asked.

"I... don't know," Karlrez answered.

Jace was momentarily at a loss for words. The Book of Demon Names being stolen, the Burning Legion's demons pulling back, the barrier falling just days after Khadgar returned... It all pointed to the Burning Legion orchestrating everything. Otherwise, it was too much of a coincidence.

But if Kil'jaeden hadn't done this himself, how had the Burning Legion managed it?

"Be careful... of Kil'jaeden, Jace Sesso," Karlrez said. "If you fail, if he destroys you completely, I too will be destroyed with you. I was sent to find Ussales, the Devourer of Dark Souls, and now it is in your hands. I will not come out well..."

The link between their souls suddenly broke. Jace's consciousness felt like it had been shoved underwater. He struggled for a moment before surfacing back to reality.

He looked down at the soul shards, now turned to dust, and the completely blackened circle. He thought for a moment, then looked back up at Morophil.

"From the perspective of a demon who knows the Burning Legion very well," he asked, "is it possible that Kil'jaeden is watching us somehow, all the time?"

"Hard to say." Morophil's demon eye flickered. "Hard to do that without me noticing."

"If he really had that ability, he wouldn't have sent Magtheridon to have a few Shadow Council orcs sneak an Eye of Kilrogg near the dwarf stronghold," Vereesa said. "For a Legion commander whose name makes the entire Twisting Nether tremble, that's a pretty crude tactic. Almost laughable."

Jace let out a laugh. "Fair point."

"So, what did Karlrez say?" Vereesa asked.

"He doesn't think this barrier was personally done by Kil'jaeden," Jace said. "Honestly, I've had that feeling too."

"So he's saying the source of this barrier magic is right here on Draenor," Vereesa said.

"That's another problem," Jace said. "There shouldn't be enough power here to support magic on this scale. So we'd better figure out how the Burning Legion actually did it."
