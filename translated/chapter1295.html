Chapter 1295: Victim of Terokkar

Before heading to Skettis, Jace made a point of finding Khadgar's books on the expedition mages' records of the area. But beyond learning that many arakkoa lived there, there wasn't much deeper insight to be had.

Jace knew that Skettis was the only surviving piece of land after the collapse of Arak. And because it was so close to the north, its geography resembled Terokkar Forest more than the peaks of Arak.

The arakkoa that survived were, in truth, the cursed exiles — those who'd been discriminated against in the ancient arakkoa empire.

The "Fallen" exiles had lost their ancestors' beautiful wings and feathers, becoming black, stunted, ragged bird-beaked monsters. Years of flight and persecution had made most of them sensitive and wary.

They hid deep in Skettis, rarely communicating with the outside world, and almost never appeared on the roads between Alleria's Keep and Wildhammer Stronghold. The expedition had never bothered them either.

But to reach the forest Turalyon had mentioned, the Skettis rookeries sat right across the path.

To the south lay the collapsed, shattered remnants of Arak. The powerful suction of the Twisting Nether and the unending Arcane and Shadow lightning made going around that way impossible. They'd have to fly along the northern edge of Skettis, skirting it as closely as they could.

As they swept past the edge of this gloomy forest, relying on his sensitivity to shadow energy, the green dragon did her best to avoid any arakkoa outposts or ritual sites they might encounter, trying not to add unexpected trouble to the journey.

Even so, Jace could feel the intense hostility lurking in the mist-shrouded tree shadows. The emotion was so thick he could sense it without any warlock abilities. He kept his eyes fixed on the darkness the whole time.

According to Khadgar's records, the arakkoa might not be able to fly anymore, but they raised massive numbers of shadow-blessed, ferocious kaliri. No one knew what might trigger these "defenders" of Skettis to attack whoever they considered intruders.

"Jace, allying with the black dragons still sounds weird to me, and that's putting it mildly." The green dragon tilted her head, fixing one of her silver-moonlit eyes on Jace on her back. Her voice was hoarse. "I admit, the way they act here in Draenor isn't what I'm used to..."

"It'd be nice to figure out why they act this way, Sora," Jace said. "If communication really proves impossible, I won't risk further compromises with them. But also — don't attack them on sight. It's one thing if we can't reach an agreement — it's another entirely to provoke them into becoming another bunch of die-hard enemies for the expedition."

"I'll listen to you, Jace." The green dragon answered readily enough, but there was still reluctance in her voice.

In this area, the bare yellow soil was already showing between the trees on the slopes. That meant they were approaching the Arak region. Once, this had been a vibrant, beautiful forest rivaling Nagrand. But a magical explosion from an ancient civil war among the Apexis — the old arakkoa empire — had changed the landscape forever, turning it into a wasteland of barren peaks where only a few patches of woodland still clung to life.

Of course, compared to the horror of the Auchindoun explosion more than a decade ago, the desolation of Arak was just a distant, hazy memory.

The roar of terrible noise drifted faintly across the sky. Though he couldn't hear it clearly over the wind and Sora's breathing, and the gray-white death-waste was still out of sight, Jace knew they were getting closer to their destination.

"Stay alert, Sazeno," Jace said. "I need you to watch for black dragons with me. The shadow energy here is too chaotic. The whole forest has traces of it. My senses might not be sharp enough."

"Do you really think the black dragons can help you against the Deceiver Kil'jaeden, master?!" The imp gripped the edge of the demon bag, staring at the gray-green treetops rushing below. "It's impossible! No matter how many black dragons come, it won't change a thing!"

"Enough with the useless talk," Jace said. "Tell you what — if Kil'jaeden really shows up in front of us, I'll blow you up with a simple spell and send you to reincarnate early in the Twisting Nether. That way Kil'jaeden won't catch your demon soul and torture you for information. Sound good?"

"Sounds pretty good, actually!" The imp's voice was trembling.

Before the imp could finish that short reply, an abnormal rustling suddenly came from the woods below. Jace grabbed the green dragon's spine and looked down. Sora instinctively beat her wings and climbed higher.

A flash of crimson emerged from the shadows beneath the treetop canopy. A cloud of yellow venom sprayed into the sky, shooting straight at Sora. Sora beat her wings hard, dispersing the venom cloud in front of her.

An impossibly long, thin red insect leg lashed out from the forest, taking advantage of her dealing with the venom. Jace noticed the oddity in his peripheral vision and immediately turned to prepare a Suffocation spell to slow it down — but the limb was too fast. He didn't even have time to chant.

The claw tip scraped across the hard scales on the green dragon's back. Though it didn't leave a wound, the impact knocked her off balance. She tumbled forward, crashing through layers of tree canopy. Jace's spellcasting was interrupted, and he fell off her back into the forest, snapping several branches before hitting the ground.

"Jace!"

Sora realized her warlock was gone, but momentum had already carried her away. Her voice warped with distance.

Jace got up, drew Quel'Serrar, and looked toward the strange thing's direction. That blood-red shadow suddenly emerged at an unnaturally fast pace...

It was a massive female spider — so large it made the whole world feel off-scale. Her blood-red body was marked with fel-green patterns. Her two largest eyes, pressed together, looked like amber gems radiating an ominous light.

Jace vaguely remembered this giant red spider that lurked in Terokkar Forest. In the game, it was called the "Terokkar Tarantula." But this spider looked more like a gigantic red black widow, not a thick-limbed tarantula.

Though she was bigger than most of the trees in the forest, she moved with incredible agility thanks to her slender but powerful legs and supple body. She grabbed at every trunk, dodging and weaving as she lunged straight at Jace.

She seemed to have sensed the abundant dark magic within Jace. Her several mismatched eyes all glowed with a hungry, fel light. She even ignored Sora, who'd fallen farther away, and raised a leg like a spear to stab straight at him.

Without any panic, Jace swung Quel'Serrar and sliced clean through the outstretched leg. The mother spider shuddered and stepped back. Her mandibles — curved like demon horns — ground together frantically, making a hissing threat. Rage and shock flooded the entire forest.

Threats meant nothing to Jace now. Taking his chance, he reached out expressionlessly, seized the spider's anger and malice, and twisted them into shadowflame that ignited the spider in reverse. The burst of flame exploded through her chest, tearing apart two of her limbs.

This mother spider had apparently lived so long at the edge of the world, in the company of the Twisting Nether's shadows, that her emotional fluctuations released powerful shadow energy more easily. That was probably one reason she'd mutated to this size. But this time, her own power turned into Jace's advantage.

The mother spider tried desperately to retreat. Her anger quickly cooled into fear, further fueling Jace's flames. Soon, half her body was burned away.

In the end, she couldn't hold out against her body's rapid collapse. She crashed to the ground, her remaining legs twitching and flailing like spasms, shredding the already fragile shrubs and undergrowth, even snapping against the tree trunks themselves. Legs broke, tore away from her body, and soon she was a mangled mess.

"That was spectacular, my master! Ha!" The imp screeched excitedly.

Even though Morophil had taken most of the demon grimoire's power, Jace felt that the soul power he'd kept had still greatly strengthened his control over emotion and shadow.

In the past, he could accurately identify the dark power stirred up by others' emotions, but it was hard to use it so freely — to gather it and amplify his spells with such ease. Maybe the massive shadow energy within this Terokkar giant venom spider had helped him feel the difference more clearly.

Plus, the fact that he'd fallen from the treetops, hit the ground, and gotten right back up... Aside from a slight twinge in his ankle, he felt no real injury.

Thanks to dragon blood, blessings, and the dark soul power that had strengthened his body, his physical condition had clearly improved by leaps and bounds.

Even though the fight looked brutal — the smell of scorched fire, giant insect, and shadow filling the air — it had only lasted a short time because both the spider and Jace had moved so fast.

By the time the green dragon rushed back, the spider was nothing but a large pile of burning, shattered corpse.

Jace clenched his hand, snuffing out the remaining flames. "Fast mover. But picked the wrong opponent."

Sora landed, folding her wings. "But she seemed very good at fighting dragons. I'm not making excuses for my lapse, Jace — but she really knew how to hit my weak points."

"I could feel it too. When she attacked you, there wasn't a moment of hesitation. Like she'd been waiting for us a long time." Jace said.

Neither of them needed to say it out loud. They both understood what had made this giant spider so familiar with fighting dragons.

"Well, then..." The green dragon looked up and murmured.

"Go ahead." Jace smiled. "As long as you don't mind..."

"How could I?" The green dragon eagerly spread her wings, using natural magic to blow away the shadowflame. After a simple purification, she happily pounced on her meal.

The black dragons risked being hunted to come here for food for a reason. This giant spider mother's abdomen looked incredibly delicious and nutritious.

Sora had used up quite a bit of energy flying from Wildhammer Stronghold. She might as well take the chance to replenish. Jace sat down nearby to rest, trying hard not to imagine what horrific sight it would be if she shifted into her golden-haired elven maiden form and started burrowing into the spider's belly, biting and clawing everywhere.

But the green dragon's feast didn't last long. A piercing howl suddenly echoed from deep in the forest. Jace immediately put down the dwarf cheese he was holding and looked up. Sora also pulled her head out and turned toward the sound.

It was a black dragon's roar. They knew it well.

"She's calling for help, Jace," Sora said.
