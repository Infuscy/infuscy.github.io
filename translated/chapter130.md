## Chapter 130: The Dead

For the first few days, the *Great Baradin Conqueror* sailed in waters not far from the coast. Every morning when Jace got up and left his cabin for the open window, he could see a faint green line on the distant horizon.

Contrary to Jace's initial worries, the passengers and sailors aboard weren't as vicious as he'd imagined. There were merchants, workers, plenty of craftsmen and hunters from Stranglethorn Vale, and even a few minor nobles with some name for themselves.

From them he heard that humans had already established some settlements in Stranglethorn — small villages and camps. After the Gurubashi Empire's crushing defeat in the war against the Kingdom of Stormwind, the trolls hardly ventured beyond their own cities, so the two sides left each other alone.

On the whole, they were more or less normal people. Everyone chatting and laughing together, having a drink, sharing some dried rations, getting to know each other in simple ways. For a while, regional and cultural differences were all smoothed over.

This morning when Jace got up, he found that distant streak of green had vanished. The various seabirds that had been accompanying them were gone too. The deck was quiet in a way that felt off.

Jace saw a retired soldier staring blankly at the water. He walked over and asked, "Sir, have we headed out to sea?"

"Yes," the soldier said. "We're probably already approaching Menethil Bay. Next we'll head northwest toward Southshore."

"We're not stopping at a port?"

"Probably not. To save time," the soldier said. "It's just that Baradin Bay is never calm. I've got a bit of a bad feeling."

In reality, that bad feeling arrived much faster than expected. Before long, a bearded passenger nearby was muttering to his friend that they might be sailing into a storm.

At noon, the goblins in the corridor were debating whether to put in at Menethil Harbor, but First Mate Gazzik said the captain wanted to reach Southshore as quickly as possible and didn't want to waste time in Khaz Modan.

By evening, Jace could already see from the bow that the direction the *Great Baradin Conqueror* was heading had turned pitch black.

He asked a goblin sailor, "Are we sure about sailing straight into the storm?"

"What else are we gonna do?" the goblin sailor said. "Turn the ship around now and get caught by the storm from behind? That'd be a rough ride. Relax, human, nothing's gonna go wrong. Go back to your cabin now and by tomorrow it'll be fine."

Sailors were sailors — they had way more experience at sea than he did. Jace simply went back to his cabin and checked over his luggage to make sure nothing would be lost in the storm.

He thought of those scenes in movies where ships encounter storms. In case things got too rough later, he strapped his warlock's bag and staff to his body, made sure he hadn't left anything valuable behind, and lay down on the bed to wait for the ship to meet the storm.

At some point, the ship's swaying had grown more and more erratic. Jace, who'd almost gotten used to life at sea, started feeling violently nauseous again.

He got up from the bed, planning to walk the corridors to take his mind off it, then go topside for some fresh air. But in the corridor, the constant footsteps overhead, the bursts of commotion and startled cries, made it even harder to settle his nerves.

He found the nearest window and looked out at the waves. Black swells rolled past, one after another. Thunder rolled across the sky — terrifying. By comparison, the ship itself was remarkably steady.

"Holy Light… Holy Light…"

A deathly-pale man stumbled all the way toward the stern. Jace watched him run past. He hadn't been all that nervous before, but he found himself catching the man's panic.

With a crash, a wave struck the hull. White spray shot in through the window and spattered him with water. Jace cinched his cloak tight, covered the warlock's bag beneath it, pulled up his hood, and made his way toward the deck.

Just as he was about to exit, a fierce wind howled in, pressing so hard he could barely open his eyes. Cold droplets scraped his face — impossible to tell whether they were rain or sea spray.

"Should we haul the boat up higher, Razvi?!" a human shouted from outside.

"If you can haul it up right now, then do it — what are you asking me for?"

"Trugil, check the situation at the stern, quick!" Jace noticed the sails had been taken down. The thick mast, wrapped in canvas, swayed with the ship beneath the oppressive black clouds.

"Get back to the cabin! There's nothing to see here, human!" a goblin screamed from above.

Jace turned and headed back below. He'd just descended the steps when the hull suddenly lurched violently. He heard a clattering beneath his feet — something seemed to have toppled over.

In the blink of an eye, a huge spray of water came pouring in through the hatch behind him. Jace even thought seawater was flooding in again, but the spray just kept splashing in without any water actually pouring in. It had started raining.

"How is it out there? Everyone inside is scared out of their wits."

Grede suddenly appeared at his side — looked like he'd wanted to go up and take a look too.

Jace said, "Doesn't seem like a problem. Those goblins told us to stay inside."

Before he'd finished speaking, Gazzik came leaping down from above, striding fast and bellowing at someone — "Quick, Norville, check the cargo hold! Is something not tied down? If I find out who was responsible for securing those crates, I'll…"

He raged all the way to the end of the corridor until both his figure and his voice disappeared. Grede said, "In my experience, this storm doesn't count as a big one. Should be fine."

"The big one might still be ahead," Jace said.

"Don't let the sailors hear that," Grede said with a grin. "Otherwise they'll tie you to the bow to appease the sea god's wrath." Grede had barely finished laughing when the hull shuddered violently and then lurched upward. He flailed, trying to grab onto anything within reach, and barely managed to catch a doorframe. Jace wasn't much better off — his shoulder took a hit and he grabbed the other side of the doorframe, wincing in pain.

Before the two of them could get a firm grip, the ship began to list severely. Creaking and groaning filled the entire hull. Clattering came from every direction, along with screams and shouts… until both of them were slammed hard onto the deck.

"How big was that wave?!" Grede asked, pulling himself up.

A flash of lightning from outside lit up the corridor. Both their faces went deathly white for an instant. A hysterical shriek rose from belowdecks — loud enough to go toe-to-toe with the rolling thunder.

"You hear that?" Jace asked.

"Heard it," Grede said.

"What'd he say?"

"Sounds like… dead people."

The two of them nodded simultaneously, then looked together down the corridor leading to the lower hold — the direction Gazzik had disappeared.
