Chapter 1300: Yeah, That Was Me

After that, the group discussed Vereesa and the Farstriders heading to Dragonmaw Fortress in advance to scout and lie in wait, coordinating with the gryphon riders for the attack, and how the stronghold should communicate with the assault force if the Burning Legion made any moves on their end.

Jace, unsurprisingly, would be executing the final step with Morophil — once the fortress's outer defenses were breached, they'd need to get inside with help from the others as quickly as possible and find Sabellian's location.

At the same time, they couldn't overlook Zuluhed.

As the chieftain of the Dragonmaw, Zuluhed had been one of Warchief Blackhand's most trusted lieutenants during the Orc Wars. He'd found the Demon Soul under Deathwing's guidance, and he'd planned the operation to capture Alexstrasza.

Back when Jace had gone to Grim Batol, the Dragonmaw taskmaster Nek'rosh stationed there had been just his subordinate — a lieutenant left in command of the remaining Dragonmaw soldiers after Zuluhed answered Ner'zhul's call and returned to Draenor.

In the game, this guy was a highly ranked warlock in the Fel Horde. He led the plan to cultivate nether drakes on the Netherwing Ledge, producing countless nether drakes for Illidan's riders.

Every time Zuluhed was mentioned, Gorlorio's undisguised disgust and hatred made it clear that the black dragons here extremely rejected nether drakes — those mutated black dragons. That was a far cry from the beliefs held by Deathwing himself and Nefarian.

Those two eccentric father-and-son types always thought black dragons weren't strong enough on their own. They kept trying to breed stronger new dragon breeds through bizarre magic and "scientific" methods — including but not limited to nether drakes, chromatic dragons, and eventually twilight dragons.

After the battle plan was settled, Gorlorio said he'd return to Terokkar to coordinate the attack time with the black dragons under his command. When the meeting ended and everyone stepped outside, the black dragon called Jace back.

Even though they were technically allied, the expedition officers still weren't comfortable staying in the same room with the black dragon any longer than necessary. They hurried off down the corridor. Vereesa hesitated when she saw the black dragon wanted to talk to Jace, looking at him uncertainly. Only after Jace nodded to her did she sigh and leave with Grede and Sora.

Once the corridor was empty, Jace turned to the black-haired man. "What is it? Say your piece."

"How did he die?" Gorlorio asked in the dragon tongue.

"Strictly speaking — suicide," Jace said.

Gorlorio didn't look surprised at all. He tilted his head slightly, then lowered his voice and switched to Common. "And loosely speaking?"

"He tried to absorb the Demon Soul's power and kill the Red Dragon Queen." Jace explained. "But his skin and armor couldn't hold out long enough for him to see it through."

"Absorb the Demon Soul?!" Gorlorio asked in shock. "What do you mean? The Demon Soul broke?"

"He broke it himself." Jace coughed. "Probably wasn't looking closely enough."

"Wasn't looking closely enough? You witnessed the whole thing and survived?" Gorlorio's anger was barely contained. "Or was it you who did it, Jace Sesso? Don't think we don't know about you. He knew your name long ago. Don't treat me like a fool!"

"Yeah, that was me. Kill Jace Sesso of Brill — tell the whole Blackwing flight about it, Gorlorio." Jace stared at him. "If you really want to settle this score right now."

The black dragon's handsome, gray-brown face twisted with intense emotion. But Jace couldn't quite tell if it was hatred, anger at this mortal's insolence, or both. Once a black dragon's emotions boiled over, they were blinding, like molten magma.

But he didn't strike. He couldn't even manage a proper curse. The proud Blackwing could never accept that their honored father had been killed by a mere mortal — or any group of mortals. Even Queen Alexstrasza was beneath their dignity as an opponent. But suicide? That was the most palatable explanation.

So he ground his teeth for a long moment before snarling, "Someone will settle this score with you eventually. But not now. Not us. You won't live much longer — Queen Alexstrasza's blessing won't save you. When you get back to Azeroth, Prince Nefarian will give you a proper welcome."

"I'll take that as a good omen." Jace leaned against the wall. "I'm not even counting on making it back to Azeroth."

Hearing that, Gorlorio squinted suspiciously. "You're a warlock, aren't you?"

"You have demonic power. You could try enslaving a weak Doomguard and have it take you back through a demonic portal. For someone who dared face our honored father face-to-face, that shouldn't be a particularly difficult spell."

"You think I don't know? The magical disaster that just happened was the Burning Legion's way of closing that loophole," Jace said. "Now we're all trapped here together."

"Ha!" Gorlorio let out a bark of laughter. "Serves you right, you little worm."

Jace chuckled. "You don't plan on going back?"

"Why would we?" The black dragon said. "When the Dragon Father left me here to help Sabellian raise the whelps, I thought he'd abandoned me on this pathetic, barren land. But now... well, we're not like you. You annoying lot should just scram already. Leave this place to us. If the prince finds out the Alliance is focused on getting home, he'll be thrilled too. Assuming he's still alive."

"By the way," Jace said. "What if the Dragonmaw warlocks threaten us with his life during the attack on the fortress?"

Gorlorio frowned. "Then we'll see if he can hold out. If he can't, maybe he was never fit to be leader."

"What I'm asking is — if he dies, will you still ally with us?" Jace said.

"If he's dead, that's not 'rescuing' him, is it?" The black dragon's voice was cold.

"Don't misunderstand. I'm just asking." Jace waved a hand. "Anything else?"

Gorlorio folded his arms, irritably licking his fangs. Then he turned and strode silently out of the corridor.

Jace followed him outside. Vereesa immediately walked up. "You okay?"

"I'm fine. He knows we did it," Jace said.

"Uh." Vereesa blinked, watching the retreating black figure. "How'd he take it?"

"He clearly figured it out, but he didn't burn the bridge on the spot," Jace said. "Right now, finding allies to survive is what matters most to us. Finding a leader is what matters most to them. Everything else can wait."

"He also hinted he won't come after me anymore. The guy's all talk — he's actually worried that fighting the Alliance would endanger their numbers. Don't forget, he's been leading the black dragons in Terokkar ever since Sabellian went missing. Which means keeping the black dragons out of conflict with the Alliance has been his decision all along."

"So he's a conflicted, tangled-up character," Vereesa said.

"With a father like Deathwing?" Jace sighed. "It'd be a miracle if he didn't turn out as crazy as Nefarian."

The two of them watched the black dragon's silhouette walk — step by step, under everyone's watchful gaze — all the way across the training ground and out the main gate. Then he vanished. They waited a long time, but never saw him take flight as a dragon again.
