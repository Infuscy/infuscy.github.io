Chapter 1302: An Unusual Bloodline

In the basement, a felboar bound with ropes was letting out a mad roar toward the warlock across from him. The sound wasn't like an ordinary boar's squeal — it was even hoarser and more savage than the felboars outside the fortress walls.

That was because it had fallen victim to Sazeno's Curse of Tongues, and everything coming out of its mouth was fragmented, meaningless demonic utterances it couldn't understand itself, punctuated by shrieks of pain from its burning throat.

In his dark form, looking like a shadowy demon, Jace was trying to strip the Curse of Tongues from the beast with the Devour Shadow incantation — but to no avail.

After all, the Curse of Tongues was ultimately a demonic fel magic. He'd only used Devour Shadow to siphon off some of the Shadow corruption infecting the felboar and convert it into his own mana, but the Curse of Tongues itself hadn't been disturbed in the slightest — practically untouched.

After several days of attempts, Jace had confirmed that this incantation could only dismantle Shadow power. Forget Arcane or elemental blessings — it couldn't even handle fel.

Still, through all this practice he'd gotten much more proficient with the spell. When they faced the warlocks and demons inside Dragonmaw Fortress, it might just prove useful.

The boar's howling was getting more unbearable by the minute, so Jace gestured for the succubus to handle it.

Morophil placed her hand on the felboar's head, and the frenzied, enraged fel-beast immediately looked like it had been smacked with a hammer — it passed out cold, crashing to the ground with drool running from its mouth.

Jace frowned. "Did I look that stupid when you hypnotized me?"

"At least you're much prettier than it is, darling," Morophil said, patting the boar's fat.

"If you've got nothing nice to say, don't say anything."

Jace shot her a look. Just then, footsteps echoed from the corridor behind them.

"Jace?"

"Sora?"

Jace turned around. A green dragon was standing in the doorway.

"You're busy."

"It's fine, I'm just about done."

"I wanted to talk to you — about what happened with the black dragon we encountered in Terokkar the other day." Sora said this with a polite inclination of her head toward the succubus and the imp. Morophil smiled and lowered her head in return.

Jace dispelled his dark form, set his staff aside, and asked, "You mean Gelorius?"

"No, about Needleflame." Sora said, "You remember that little one, don't you?"

"Of course I remember."

Jace led Sora out of the basement and into the corridor before turning back and saying, "That mouthful of smoke she blew in my face choked me half to death. I really hope the rest of the young dragons joining us for the assault on Dragonmaw Fortress aren't all like that."

"I don't want to talk about her combat ability — I want to talk about her 'power.'" Sora said, "I think she's very different from ordinary black dragons. You can sense emotions, so you know what I'm getting at."

"The black dragons here in Draenor have all become quite different, including Gelorius. It probably has something to do with being so far from Azeroth," Jace said.

"Exactly." Sora said, "The dragon leaders more or less all understand what's wrong with the Black Dragonflight... though some are still unwilling to believe it. But Needleflame has differences from Gelorius too, and I think that's why Saberion values her so much."

"Don't forget, the Black Dragonflight is fundamentally different from my kindred. They're a flight that prizes physical strength above all else. A young dragon like me or Needleflame who isn't good at fighting would never be favored among them. That was the way before their betrayal, it's been the way for ten thousand years, and coming to Draenor hasn't changed much. Even Needleflame herself longs to become a warrior."

Jace nodded, and Sora continued, "Something Gelorius mentioned the other day caught my attention — that Needleflame is part of the new generation of black dragons raised here in Draenor after the flight arrived, and that she hasn't been corrupted by an evil artifact like the Skull of Gul'dan. So I was thinking..."

"You're saying Needleflame was likely never corrupted the way her forebears were," Jace said.

"Very likely," Sora said.

"What about her mother?" Jace asked. "Perhaps she was already influenced by her mother."

"Her mother is Queen Alexstrasza herself, Jace!" Sora said. "Don't forget where this whole batch of dragon eggs came from. This was something Deathwing arranged with Teron'Gorefiend — in exchange for stealing three artifacts for him, he had Teron smuggle out a large number of dragon eggs from Azeroth. Most of them came from Grim Batol!"

"The reason she recognizes Saberion as her father isn't because she's the offspring of Saberion and some black dragon mate — it's because she's a whelp who hatched and grew up under Saberion's care. Just like the eggs Queen Alexstrasza entrusted to Malygos that hatched into the blue dragons!"

"These eggs laid by the Dragonqueen were brought to Gorgrond uncorrupted, raised under the black dragons' watch as black dragon eggs, which is why the hatchling turned out so... innocent. So Gelorius was right — this new generation of hatchlings really is enormously significant for the black dragons, and for all dragonkind as well."

So that was how it was. Jace was only just beginning to understand.

In the other timeline, since the black dragon hatchlings born in Draenor had all been killed or gone missing in the most brutal conditions... no one had ever carefully examined exactly how these hatchlings differed from their Azerothian kin.

Sora gathered her excitement a little, then said, "If we really do rescue Saberion, I hope you'll ask him whether we can bring one of these young black dragons like Needleflame back to Azeroth, so we can entrust her to Queen Alexstrasza to raise and care for..."

"You know how much these black dragons value their offspring," Jace said.

"I know." Sora sighed. "But by then we'll have saved his life! Besides, doesn't he want a day when all the black dragons of Azeroth can break free of control and be reborn, just like the black dragons of Draenor? If Saberion truly values these young children the way I suspect, then he shouldn't refuse — we're not asking to sacrifice their lives. They might not live any worse with the red dragons than they do here in Draenor."

"And Queen Alexstrasza isn't just the queen of the red dragons — she's the mother and sister of all dragonkind. That's why even Gelorius speaks her name with reverence. At the same time, I believe this has always been Queen Alexstrasza's wish."

"I promise," Jace said. "I'll bring it up with him."

"Wonderful, Jace." Sora clenched her fists excitedly. "I really didn't expect you to agree so quickly."

"It's nothing," Jace said. "Allying with us was already the black dragons' best chance at survival. We're even helping them rescue their prince — that's practically letting them have it for free. And besides, this isn't a bad proposal for the black dragons either."

"It's not just that," Sora said. "I heard from Grede about what happened at Grim Batol — about how Deathwing invaded your dreams, threatening you with terrifying visions, frightening you, and then, on top of everything in Grim Batol..."

She glanced at the scar on Jace's arm that had already become barely visible and continued, "...that horrible brand. The black dragons have done so many terrible things to you and your people, and yet you're still willing to help them."

"Aren't you the same?" Jace said. "Despite all the crimes the black dragons have committed against your kindred, when you saw a chance that they could be saved, you held on tight and wouldn't let go."

"Whatever else, they are still our cousins... much like the Dark Iron are to the Wildhammer." Sora lowered her gaze. "Regardless, Jace, I'm grateful."

"No matter how I feel about the black dragons, if it's something you, Grede, or Windrunner bring up, I'll do everything in my power to help," Jace said. "Let alone something this meaningful. Hmm — as long as when you stand before Queen Alexstrasza, you put in a good word for me and see if she has any more fine gifts to hand out..."

"Of course," Sora said immediately, nodding. "I'll remember it well."

"And what about Molofir's requests, master?" came a mournful question from the doorway behind them.

Jace coughed a couple of times and said nothing. Sora stifled a laugh and said, "I'll leave you two to it, then, Jace."

"Hey, I wasn't planning on doing anything with her today..."

Jace hurried to explain, but Sora had already fled in a flash.

Well then. If Sora ever became a dreamwarden beneath Ysera and Eranikus, the shining image he'd built up for the green dragons in the Temple of Atal'Hakar would crumble right along with it.
