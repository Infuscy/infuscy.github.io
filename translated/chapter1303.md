## Chapter 1303: Setting Out Like Last Time

The day of the attack arrived, and Shadowmoon Valley was anything but calm. Fel winds howled, and the volcano in the distance — Gul'dan's Hand, its fel glow barely visible — stirred restlessly.

The stench of burning in the air grew unbearably foul. Though the gryphon riders who'd made their preparations laughed and cheered each other on before departure, there was no shortage of curses hurled at the damned weather.

Then again, it was hard to say whether calm or chaos was the more ominous omen — and Shadowmoon Valley had worn this face before.

Standing on the battlements, Jace counted the potion bottles in his pack: Draenor-formula Shadow Protection Potions, Mana Potions, Destruction Potions — all present and accounted for, packed nice and secure.

He remembered that in the game, Zuluhed commanded an immensely powerful rain-of-fire spell. Although his body, along with his robes and cloak, already gave him enough protection that he didn't fear ordinary flames much, he'd still brought a Fire Protection Potion just in case.

Just then, a fully armored Grede walked up. Aside from the cloak he'd brought from Stormwind, the man looked every bit a member of the Alliance Expedition.

"Remember the last time I carried you flying into battle, Jace Sesso?"

"Nethergarde Keep," Jace answered.

"That's right, Nethergarde Keep. Just as pitch-black, just as stinking — you'd think fighting demons had to happen in a shithole like this." Grede gazed toward the dark horizon in the distance, squinting. "Back then you were like a flustered little rooster. Now you actually look like a warrior, though I don't know if that's the height talking."

"Doesn't seem to be the height, from where I'm standing," Jace said.

Grede choked out a laugh. "Where's Morophil? If she's not coming with me and my gryphon, then..."

"She'll be moving with the Farstriders. With the black dragons and your gryphon riders providing cover, Sora will carry her straight to the walls for the assault." Jace pointed to the green dragon sitting calmly on the battlements behind them. "And you'll need to remember to drop me off there too."

"Got it. Lord Kurdran's told me about ten thousand times." Grede adjusted his belt. "What's really strange, Jace — even though we're trapped here in Draenor, facing endless demons, and it looks like we could be done for at any moment..."

He looked up at Jace and said, "I don't feel panicked at all. I actually feel at peace."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"Probably because of what Turalyon said." The dwarf said, "Every demon I kill here, every orc I kill, means my kin back home — especially Mosa and her people — will have one fewer enemy to deal with. I feel like fighting here has real value."

Jace nodded, and Grede continued, "Or maybe it's because deep down, I kind of feel that right now, here and Azeroth... I'm not sure which one is really home."

"Heh." Jace let out a laugh. "Because this place is called Wildhammer Keep?"

"Hah!" Grede slapped Jace on the chest. "Of course it's not that simple. The more important thing is — you, the little elf, and that flying lizard are my family, Jace. Where you are, that's where home is."

"As for that pink horned devil and the smart-mouthed yellow little monster, forget it. Sorry, Jace, I still can't accept demons as family."

The dwarf grumbled on about how Sazeno had made fun of him again these past couple of days, as if trying to shake off the embarrassment of having spoken his heart. Jace couldn't help but pinch his nose, forcing the emotion that was about to get away from him back down into his chest.

"What about Mosa? Does the place she's in count as home too?" he asked.

"Of course it does — after all, even you, a broke bastard in Stormwind, have more than one place to live!" Grede jabbed his finger hard into Jace's chest. Just then, Windrunner came up beside them.

She took a deep breath and asked, "Did I miss something? What were you talking about?"

Jace was about to speak when Grede immediately bellowed, "We're discussing the coming battle, little elf! What about you — got any bright ideas?"

"We won't be moving together, at least not in the early stages of the fight, so it's hard to offer much advice," Windrunner said. "But once we've shattered the outer defenses, I'll come find you in time, Jace."

"No problem." Jace pulled out the rough map of Dragonmaw Fortress drawn by the Farstriders and looked it over again. He'd studied this map carefully over the past while. Because of the difficulty of drawing and reconnaissance, the map wasn't very detailed, but it was enough to show that most of what the game called "Netherwing Ledge" wasn't a ledge yet at this point.

In the game, Dragonmaw Fortress was a few lonely walls plus some lairs, barracks, and watchtowers, with a cliff that plunged into the Twisting Nether nearby, and across from it was the Netherwing Ledge — the daily quest hub for Netherwing reputation.

But here, it was clear that Dragonmaw Fortress also had a land route through the canyon leading to the mines in the southeast and a small town built by the fel orcs that the Expedition didn't know the name of — that was probably what would later become the Netherwing Mine.

The memory of nearly being blown into the Twisting Nether at Auchindoun was still fresh. Jace thought that this time, surely he wouldn't have to face the same disaster again.

But unlike Auchindoun, which had a leyline nexus underground, the leyline nexus in eastern Shadowmoon lay beneath Karabor Temple. So the severing of Netherwing Ledge was probably just natural erosion rather than the result of some major event — meaning the ground should at least be solid for the near future.

More than the Expedition or the Burning Legion, Jace was inclined to think the separation of Netherwing Ledge might be related to the later Illidari conquest of Karabor Temple and the grueling battle in which they defeated Magtheridon.

"Why haven't those black dragons sent word yet? Or are those Dragonmaw orcs just not falling for it?" Grede said impatiently. "If we don't set out soon, my battle fervor's going to fizzle out!"

"If the Dragonmaw orcs aren't falling for it, that means we can just sit back and watch those hatchlings burn and slaughter the orcs' supplies and escorts as they please," Windrunner said. "Isn't that pretty good too?"

"It'd be pretty good if I were the one doing the burning and slaughtering," Grede grumbled.

Just then, a massive black hatchling emerged from the high black clouds and dove rapidly toward Wildhammer Keep. The moment it appeared, it drew fierce, screaming challenges from every gryphon, and the Wildhammer dwarves had to roar to suppress their fury and hatred before the situation finally calmed.

But this hatchling couldn't have cared less about the gryphons. It stared at the green dragon for a moment, then lowered its head respectfully and opened its bloody maw to roar in Common to everyone on the battlements: "An elite force of Dragonmaw Skybreakers has left their fortress heading west to hunt down my brothers and sisters. Lord Gelorius urges you to set out at once, because my kin won't tangle with those Skybreakers for long — the window of opportunity is already upon us."

"Excellent, black dragon!" Kurdran leaped onto his gryphon Skyreaver and roared to everyone on the battlements. "Time to set out, Sons of Lothar! Time to let those fel orcs and demons know our spears are still sharp and our blades still shine! If the demons mean to turn Draenor into a sealed circular arena, then now is the time to let them know just how bloody, how painful, how cruel this arena will be! Let them know with our thunder and fury — it's too late for regrets now!"

"For the Sons of Lothar! For the Alliance!"

The gryphon riders, carrying Kirin Tor mages and Silver Hand knights, shouted as they leaped from the battlements into the sky. Amid the prayers of the priests and the defending knights, Windrunner shared a light kiss with Jace, then turned and climbed up the wall onto Sora's back, leading a company of elite rangers and wyvern riders out together.

By then, Grede and Jace had also leaped onto a dark green gryphon nearby. The dwarf turned his head and said, "Remember Zin-Azshari, Jace? This gryphon might be that crazy beast's cousin!"

"Better not be," Jace muttered.

Grede laughed a few times and urged the gryphon beneath him on in Dwarvish. Along with a familiar sudden feeling of weightlessness, the air and the restless fel stench churned up around them, and everything around them suddenly fell away — they had plunged into the heights of Shadowmoon Valley.

The black, fel-green sky was already full of white, yellow, and dark specks. Though scattered, they maintained a rough formation that Jace couldn't quite define but that had its own logic. And while battle fervor and passion gripped Jace's every sense, everyone's flight speed remained steady and natural.

This truly was the most elite force Azeroth could send forth — even the gryphon riders of the Dark Portal couldn't compare.

But even so, the gryphons and wyverns in the sky at this moment, and the dwarf riders, mages, and elite soldiers on their backs, would probably have a hard time matching the several hundred elite fel orc archers garrisoning Dragonmaw Fortress that Gelorius had described. Even if they could barely fight their way onto the walls and break inside, they'd likely suffer casualties too grieving to accept.

Thinking of how precious Lord Kurdran held every gryphon and gryphon rider, Jace couldn't help but keep worrying.

Not long after, his peripheral vision caught sight of layer upon layer of terrifying black clouds appearing in the lightning-flashing void — and these clouds were clearly much lower than the sky-blanketing smog of Shadowmoon Valley...

Yes, that was Gelorius and his Black Dragonflight.

At the head were three adult black dragons of exceptionally massive build, including Gelorius himself, accompanied by several powerful hatchlings about Sora's size, with countless more hatchlings behind them.

This herd of terrifying monsters wasn't radiating much Shadow energy at all — or perhaps they were simply on the edge of a rift, using the powerful dark forces pouring into Draenor from the Twisting Nether to mask their own magic. Though they were easy to spot, looking like moving thunderclouds, they were chaotic and nearly indistinguishable in the world of detection magic...

These black dragons were flying concealed by the power of the Void. Even Sora in the distance couldn't help but glance that way a few more times — because if she'd been in that position, she probably would have already been sucked completely into the bottomless abyss.

Jace tried to pick out Needleflame among them, but he couldn't even distinguish male from female, let alone tell who was who. In this situation, probably only Sora could make them out.

"When I was little, my old man once told me a story," Grede shouted over the wind, gazing at the black dragons. "Back then he was still in Grim Batol!"

"One day, he was out hunting raptors when he got caught in a rainstorm, got lost, and was trapped in a swamp. Then he came across a plague gnoll struggling in the muck, about to drown. Don't ask me why — probably 'cursed by the dark magic of the swamp,' those were his exact words! Anyway, he had a moment of madness and pulled that gnoll out."

"The gnoll didn't attack him, so the two of them spent over month in that boundless swamp — maybe even longer, he couldn't remember clearly. Up north, the Thador Bridge hadn't even been built yet, and everything west of the Babor Mountains was completely untamed. But the two of them worked together to find food, to hunt, and that's how they survived. In the end, the gnoll slipped away without a word."

"Quite the story, Grede!" Jace shouted.

"After he got back to Grim Batol, he started thinking maybe the Dark Iron dwarves weren't so bad — after all, even a gnoll could cooperate with a Wildhammer to survive!" Grede raised his voice.

"Mm," Jace responded.

The thunder of the Void and the chasms rumbled, and Grede lowered his tone, saying in a muffled voice, "A few years later, the witch Modgud destroyed Grim Batol."

"At least that wasn't the gnoll's doing," Jace said. "That's true," the dwarf said with a nod and a smile.
