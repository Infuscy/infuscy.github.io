Chapter 1309: Consume Destruction

"Morofil!" Jace Shadowstepped back a dozen yards, eyes locked on the dragonkin charging toward him. "Hold that drake — we're swapping!"

At the command, Morofil viciously swept her burning Apocalyptic Flames across the dragon's claw, shoving it aside. As the great beast lost its balance from the blow, she Shadowstepped away.

Jace and his succubus passed each other like two intersecting bolts of shadow. Morofil appeared behind Zuluhed and drove Apocalypse into the small of his back. But the monster acted like nothing had happened, turning to grab the demoness by the arm. Morofil hacked through the dragonkin's arm without hesitation. The Netherwing roared in pain, but the severed limb regrew into a shadowy black hand at visible speed, quickly solidifying.

"I am the Void! I am the Netherwing!" Zuluhed roared triumphantly.

Of course, Jace had already anticipated all of this. He didn't need to worry about what was happening over there, because what came next was the complete annihilation of this Netherwing dragon.

No — not a Netherwing dragon. Strictly speaking, this was pure void energy poured into a shape, held together by a set of dragon bones. In other words, it was more like a dragon-shaped void lord than an actual dragon.

So when he'd called for Morofil to swap with him, he'd already figured out how to deal with this beast. Hope you're really as useful as that gnome said, Annihilation Draught.

Jace seized the first real opening he'd gotten since entering the hall and downed the last finger-sized vial from his pouch.

Instantly, his hands began to tremble. His heart raced. Every emotion surged violently — he could see through everything: the infernal meteors outside the hall, the black dragon's shadow, the swirling void energies, and the massive dragon before him that looked like a constellation of stars...

Only a dozen seconds. No time for awe or deep reflection. "Yog'korani... Morgak, Tam!"

Jace raised Usaalus and chanted the Consume Shadows incantation. His cloak and robe billowed from the raw arcane force. The magical eye in Usaalus's skull-head oozed purple-red fel blood!

The void dragon's body shuddered under the spell's influence, then began to violently crack and dissolve!

She had meant to pursue Morofil to protect her master — the one who was like a father to her — but Jace's terrifying magic changed her mind in an instant.

This puny warlock was going to destroy her!

Rage and shock flooded her mind, twisting the shadow energy Jace was devouring. But Jace's mastery over emotional manipulation let him adapt quickly, keeping the darkness from consuming him...

The void dragon, now smaller than before, lashed out with both claws at him while gathering a breath in her maw...

A void dragon's claw strike wasn't a physical blow — it was like a super-sized Shadow Bolt. To an ordinary soldier or demon, the force would tear and scorch them black; it'd feel no different from a real dragon tearing into them with flesh and claw.

But this was Jace Sesso...

Maybe Onyxia could crush him with one claw. But not you.

Even if I had no shields, no protection potions, and just stood here taking your attack — it wouldn't matter. Because your power is mine now. And my power... can never go back to you!

Jace snorted with satisfaction. The void energy tearing from the dragon had flooded his body, been completely "purified" — forcibly stripped of the concentrated Magtheridon blood it had contained. It rapidly healed the wounds he'd sustained fighting Zuluhed, and even surged his mana back to fullness — fuller than when he'd first landed at the Dragonmaw Fortress!

In stark contrast, despair replaced the rage inside the void dragon, then fear. Her steps faltered. Her attacks grew frantic. Her form suddenly shrank, and the darkened hall brightened along with it!

The Netherwing dragon's mouth had gathered a desperate breath. But Jace showed no fear. He stood his ground, eyes wide open, facing her final gambit!

"Come...!"

The star-like shadow energy crashed over him like a vast sea, blackening the wall behind him. But Jace stood motionless, one hand gripping his staff, the other slowly clenching, frantically unraveling and consuming every shadow he could sense. The Netherwing dragon poured everything into that breath — her body split apart, the star-light snapping...

"Sagrol'ku! My most precious..."

In the distance, Zuluhed howled, barely holding off Morofil's assault. His staff was broken. His wounds no longer healed. Apocalypse's twin blades had burned through his shadow armor...

The void dragon's power crumbled under Jace's Consume Shadows, and she could no longer sustain the orc warlock's injuries.

Morofil seized the opening and drove her blade through his jaw, piercing the tall dragonkin's skull entirely. The tip burst from his forehead. Zuluhed let out a hoarse curse in orcish, then collapsed back into the form of a withered green orc. Morofil cut off his head and tossed it aside.

The void dragon let out a heart-wrenching wail. The soul-link shattered!

Jace felt something go hollow in his chest — like prey he'd already bitten into had dissolved into nothing...

The massive dragon disintegrated mid-air, collapsing into a pile of black bones that clattered to the ground and split apart like coal.

The shadow channel lost its anchor and collapsed. The void portals dissolved one by one. The hall fell silent, the ethereal hum of darkness fading into stillness...

Jace drew a deep breath, his whole body trembling.

The Annihilation Draught's effect had worn off at some point without him even noticing. He could hardly believe it — the dragon had been literally torn apart by his Consume Shadows. The ordeal he'd just endured felt like a nightmare he'd just woken from.

Even Morofil stared at the pile of shattered dragon bones for a long moment.

The others weren't so fortunate. Grede had returned to his normal form, leaning against a stone pillar and coughing painfully. Vereesa collapsed on the spot, and Jace rushed over to catch her.

Her face — and the arms, waists of the other two Farstriders — were covered in crack-like marks of shadow corrosion.

Vereesa forced her eyes open. "Are you alright, Jace?"

"Thanks to your idea, everyone's alive," Jace said quietly, reassuring her.

The ranger managed a relieved smile. Then everyone's attention was drawn to a murmuring voice nearby.

"Light, my radiant Lord I serve. Grant us, your humble believers, in this most difficult moment... to feel your mercy. Thank you for comforting us in our suffering, for watching over us..."

The paladin was kneeling in prayer. Compared to the Church's priests, his recitation was stilted and unpracticed, but it didn't stop a faint glow from spreading across half the hall.

The pain on the Farstriders' faces gradually eased. Vereesa coughed and began to regain her strength.

Morofil moved as far away as she could, barely hiding her discomfort. Jace sighed, doing his best not to show the painful burning caused by the Holy Light warring with the Netherwing shadows he'd consumed.

But as the faint light illuminated the dark hall, everyone noticed the massive door in the distance that had been destroyed by the shadowstorm, faint traces of shadow still flickering within.

They helped each other through the doorway. Another spiral passage led downward.

They were deep enough in the mine now that an infernal probably wouldn't come crashing through the wall and blow everyone up. But Grede still held his shield up as he led the way.

"We should find a good time to spar, Lady Morofil," the dwarf said, glancing back. "That was something, the way you fought. Going toe-to-toe with a dragon. If I spread that story back to Aerie Peak, even knowing you're a she-demon, my people would be singing ballads about your victory. Believe me."

"The Wildhammer clan has always said that anyone who can hunt a dragon is one in a thousand," one of the Farstriders said. "And someone who can challenge a dragon alone — that only happens in legends."

Morofil said nothing. She glanced at the dwarf and nodded slightly. Since the lockdown had fallen, she had changed somehow. Jace could feel it.

Even a demon who specialized in hunting demons couldn't face Kil'jaeden's threat without flinching. Especially when the destruction of her most powerful physical form had been caused by Kil'jaeden's arrival.

"I hope we can go home," Vereesa whispered.

"Even if we can't, Jace can use a magic ritual to tell that demon to spread tales of this battle across Azeroth," Grede said with a grin. "Right?"

"I never thought I'd be saved by a demon," the paladin said, shaking his head. "I hope the Light won't abandon me for thinking that..."

"Well, the Light hasn't run off yet," Grede said.

As they talked, a buzzing magical sound rose ahead. Everyone tensed again. They passed through another door into another hall.

Grede wore an expression of "another one?" impatience. But nothing leaped at them as they entered.

On an altar in the center of the hall sat rows of blue-violet dragon eggs glowing with shadowy light. Atop the eggs lay a dragon, its form blurred by magic.

The dragon looked very similar to Onyxia — the purple wing membranes and underbelly, the crown-like twisted horns rising from its head, curved and majestic. If anything, it seemed even larger than Onyxia.

Three bright purple energy beams fed power into the blurry barrier surrounding the dragon. The beams came from strange gray-brown cubes floating in the air.

Each cube was controlled by an orc warlock. They focused intently on channeling the cubes, not even daring to glance at Jace's group approaching.

"What are these orcs doing?" one Farstrider asked.

No one was sure — but Jace recognized this.

In the game, this was called a "Control Cube." After Illidan Stormrage defeated Magtheridon and imprisoned him in the Hellfire Citadel, the warlocks there used these magical cubes to keep him contained, draining his blood to create more fel orcs.

So this control-cube trick wasn't Illidan's invention. But Jace remembered there being five cubes used to contain Magtheridon, while here there were only three. Maybe Illidan was more worried about Magtheridon escaping and added two for safety?

Didn't matter. What mattered was that Jace knew — once the warlocks channeling the cubes stopped, the prisoner would be freed.

"Kill the warlocks," Jace said.

The rangers loosed arrows. One warlock took an arrow to the neck and fell, still staring at his cube with unblinking eyes as the channel broke. The blur on the dragon faded slightly.

"No... no..."

Another orc, with an arrow in his chest, kept channeling, screaming in despair. A second arrow pierced his skull and dropped him. The dragon's eyes snapped open.

"Finally..."

The black dragon breathed a jet of flame at the third orc. The warlock was instantly reduced to ash that sprayed across the wall behind him. The cube was blasted away, rolling across the floor, its magic extinguished.

Free of the magical restraints, the black dragon lowered his head and fixed his gaze on the exhausted group.

"State your name," Sabellian said, his chain-like lips moving as his raspy voice addressed the warlock at the center of the group.

"I'm Jace Sesso," Jace said. "The Alliance has already promised Gelorior to rescue you, on the condition that the Alliance and the black dragons will face the Burning Legion together. If you don't want to be imprisoned here by demons again, come with us and leave this fortress. Magtheridon's forces are about to arrive. Your children are fighting more and more infernals. There's no time to waste."

"I understand."

The dragon's fury subsided. Then, in a burst of charcoal-colored flame, his massive form shrank and transformed into a strikingly handsome, tall man with black hair and dusky skin. The wings that had wrapped around his body became a magnificent purple-red robe studded with fiery gems.

Jace had a thousand questions. About the dragon named Sagrol'ku. About Zuluhed's extraordinary power. About the relationship between the chieftain and that dragon.

But this really wasn't the time for a detailed discussion. Every second wasted could mean another expedition soldier wounded or killed. "Let's go."

"Lead the way, Jace Sesso," Sabellian said.
