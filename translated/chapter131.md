## Chapter 131: Night Baron

After that violent bout of rocking, the ship was noticeably more stable now. There was still some rolling, but at least you could make your way through the corridors without getting thrown around.

The two men braced against the stairwell wall and made their way down to the bottom of the ship. This was the cargo hold—crates and boxes secured with all manner of ropes, hooks, and wooden planks, though it was clear some of them had come loose.

There was already a scattering of sawdust and glass on the floor. Some crates might've been smashed open.

A few other passengers had made their way down here too, apparently drawn by the sudden commotion earlier.

"Where's Gazick?" Grede asked.

"Who's Gazick?" someone across from them asked.

"The ship's first mate," another passenger answered, then asked, "Why? Was he just here?"

Jace said, "We saw him run down here. Wasn't that him who shouted?"

"If it was him," a passenger said, "then who died?"

Grede surveyed the inside of the hold and asked, "The question is, where's the body?"

The five or six people down here looked at each other, nobody having a clue what to do. Before long, they all found themselves glancing toward the far end of the hold, then looking at each other again. The tall man holding a lantern took a deep breath and started picking his way forward step by step.

Everyone fell in behind him, swaying in rhythm on the heaving deck plates. A few more moments and several more passengers came running down to watch from that side.

As they walked along, Grede suddenly stopped. The people behind him had been examining the cargo a little too closely, and one of them bumped into Jace, nearly sending Jace careening into Grede.

Grede held up a hand, wrinkled his nose, and said, "I smell blood."

Hearing that, a couple near the back instinctively stepped back. A few of the bolder souls around the dwarf tensed up too.

The man in front turned around, lowered his lantern, and swept it across the puddles on the floor. That's when he spotted something—a faint dark tint that was easy to miss.

As the lantern drew close, a vivid scarlet bled through the darkness.

"What is this... wine?"

The dwarf turned and fixed the man with a stare, then dipped a finger in and licked it.

"Sorry, human. You guessed wrong."

At Grede's words, the whole crowd took a step back. A man with a thick Lordaeron accent swore under his breath. Nobody dared move forward anymore.

Grede took the lantern from the man and pressed on. Jace followed close behind. It wasn't blood they were afraid of, or corpses—it was the fact that the killer was still unknown. Or rather, what the killer was.

The seas of Azeroth were never calm. Countless bizarre creatures lurked in their depths, and the most dangerous of all were the naga below the waves. If a pack of naga assassins had boarded this ship, then this vessel was in for a world of hurt.

Jace carefully tested the air. Not a hint of the fishy stink of naga scales. Instead, a strange, acrid reek hung throughout the hold—not the kind of smell you'd ever pick up at sea.

Sniffing away, Jace heard Grede murmur, "You're getting it too, aren't you? That stink."

"Yeah," Jace said. "What do you think it is?"

"I've got a theory, but I'm afraid I'll sound like a damn fool if I say it out loud." The dwarf squinted.

"Just say it," Jace said. "I've got an answer too."

"Then let's say it together."

"Fine."

"Tiger."

Both of them said it at the same time. The dwarf glanced at Jace in surprise. "Where the hell have you smelled a tiger before?"

Obviously Jace couldn't say the zoo. "Don't worry about it. But why the hell is there a tiger on this ship?"

Grede frowned and kept sweeping the lantern forward.

After a while longer, the blood on the ground grew thicker. They were getting closer to the corpse—which meant they were getting closer to the killer too.

Near the bottom of the hull, the lantern finally illuminated the source of all that blood. A goblin lay there, his body torn so badly it was impossible to make out what he'd originally looked like.

"Is that Gazick?" Jace asked.

"I don't think so," Grede said. "The clothes are different. This is the cargo keeper."

"Who killed him?"

Grede swept the lantern back and forth and spotted a strange iron cage not far off, clearly toppled over. The iron lock hanging from the torn-open cage door looked like it had been ripped apart by brute force. That weird smell was coming from the cage.

"There." He jerked his lips toward it.

"Why the hell is there something like this on the ship?" Jace looked at the cage's shape and suddenly remembered the large object wrapped in cloth he'd seen being brought aboard.

"Isn't that the thing several people carried on?"

"Seems like it." By now a few goblin sailors had arrived. When they saw the broken cage, they wore expressions like they'd just bitten into something foul.

"Where's Gazick? Where's your first mate?" Jace asked.

"What are you yelling for? We're looking too!" a goblin sailor shouted.

Just then, a goblin in a black outer robe with a shoulder cape arrived. He glanced at the corpse nearby, looked at the blood on the floor, and sighed. "Quick, everyone up on deck!"

"Yes, Captain Nilazu!"

So this was the legendary captain?

The sailors obeyed and filed out of the corridor one after another. The captain looked at the remaining people. "That includes you. Everyone, out of the hold. Assemble on deck right now!"

A passenger hesitated. "But what about the storm?"

"The storm is nothing compared to the trouble down here. Damn it, get up there this instant if you want to live!"

The captain roared the whole time, his eyes darting to both sides of the hold, his hand never leaving the rapier at his waist—like he was ready to fight something to the death at any second.

"What happened?" Jace asked.

"Up first." The captain pointed toward the corridor entrance.

On deck, several goblin sailors were busy counting the heads coming up from below. Jace did a rough count—about a hundred people, give or take. Everyone was trying to crowd around the mast or grab hold of the railings. That many people packed together looked like a bunch of rain-soaked sausages strung up in bunches.

The rain was heavy and the wind was fierce, but the captain had a point—for a ship this size, the storm was nothing. The occasional roll wasn't about to send anyone overboard.

The real problem was down in the hold. Whatever had killed that goblin. But the captain stood up high listening to the goblins report their headcounts, and Jace had already noticed several sailors picking up guns.

Could it really be a tiger?

Was there a circus on board? The Darkmoon Faire?

Jace scanned the passengers around him. There didn't seem to be anyone resembling the Darkmoon Faire's motley collection of every race under the sun. Mostly humans, with a handful of dwarves and gnomes.

"Listen up, everyone." The captain suddenly called out. "You're all paying passengers on this ship, so no matter what, the Trade Cartel will guarantee your safety. Right now we've got a small problem in the cargo hold. Rest assured, we'll deal with it properly before we let you back to your cabins. Everyone bear with the rain for a bit—you can enjoy the beauty of the ocean storm. Not every ship can take in a sight like this so calmly. Consider yourselves lucky!"

"We just want to know what the problem is!" a male passenger demanded angrily.

"Please calm down, sir!" a goblin sailor shouted. "Whatever the situation, it's under control."

The goblin definition of "under control" was way too broad, Jace thought. If a goblin airship went down and not every single passenger died—as long as one person was still breathing—the goblins would probably classify it as an accident within the controllable range.

"Is it that hard to give us a straight answer?"

"We lost a man too, damn it!" another goblin sailor shouted. "Now stay calm!"

"Shut up." The captain bellowed, then turned to the crowd. "I'm sorry, but to avoid causing further problems, for now we can only—"

"Tell us what's in that cage!" Grede shouted loudly.

Grede had barely gotten the words out when a man in the crowd suddenly shouted in a frenzy, "The cage?! Night Baron got out! It's Night Baron! It has to be! Damn it, I saw it—right in that cage!"

"Night Baron? What's Night Baron?"

"Everyone, don't let your imaginations run wild! Don't believe the rumors! Everything is under control!" the goblin captain shouted, but it was already too late to stop the murmuring.

Commotion rippled through the crowd. Jace quietly asked Grede, "So what exactly is this Night Baron?"

"I have no idea," Grede said.

"It's a black panther." A noble standing nearby answered.

He was wearing a small brimmed hat that barely kept the rain off, and though his clothes were soaked through, the expensive fabric was still obvious.

"Well, I thought it was something serious." Grede snorted. "Just a panther. Watch me go down there later and skin the thing myself."

The noble glanced at Grede. "Wildhammer clan?"

"Indeed. You've got a good eye." Grede's tone climbed with pride.

Rain spattered off the brim of the noble's hat. He blinked and said, "Do you know Night Baron's record at the Cape of Stranglethorn? Your fellow clansman—a Wildhammer gladiator. It killed three."

The gladiators at the Cape of Stranglethorn—this noble could only be referring to one place: the Gurubashi Arena.

The Gurubashi Arena was the only place in Stranglethorn Vale open to outsiders. It had once been a grand arena within the Gurubashi Empire, built specifically to entertain the nobility. After the empire fell into decline, the trolls sold off part of its operating rights to goblins. From that point on, everything from the events to the audience was no longer limited to trolls.

Gradually, more and more businesses sprang up around the Gurubashi Arena. Related industries popped up everywhere, including a number of smaller arenas.

Most importantly, gladiators began appearing like bamboo shoots after a spring rain, and their ultimate goal was to stand on the podium of the great Gurubashi Arena at the Cape of Stranglethorn and claim fame and fortune.

"If that really is Night Baron," the noble said, "think about what that panther could do on this ship. In the enclosed hold, packed with cargo, crates, walls, rooms... that thing is a killing machine even on open ground. And don't forget—back in Stranglethorn Vale, it made its name on stealth and ambush!"
