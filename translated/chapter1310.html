Chapter 1310: Victorious Return

Inside the walls and out, black dragons and red dragons filled the sky, locked in combat with infernals. These fel-fire constructs were nearly immune to flame, forcing the dragons to rely on physical blows and weapon strikes to destroy them. And each one could handle a young dragon on its own.

For now, the expedition and the dragonflight were holding the line. But everyone knew that if the group deep in the fortress couldn't reach the black dragon prince in time, once the doomguards arrived from Karabor... the situation would turn catastrophic in an instant.

Then the Dragonmaw Fortress on the high slope exploded. The entire roof and arrow towers were blown skyward. An impossibly massive black dragon thrust his head out from within, unleashing a terrible blast of Shadowflame into the sky. It caught several falling infernal meteors, turning them purple-red before they hit the ground and burst into clouds of black dust.

"Prince Sabellian!" Gelorior bellowed. The black dragons in the sky shrieked in exhilaration.

"Get all the mortals out of here," Sabellian roared. "Zuluhed and Mor'ghor are dead!"

"Yes, my prince." The black dragons dove into the fortress one after another.

Sabellian fixed his gaze on the red dragons in the sky. "Even if you won't take orders from me, at least live up to the duty Queen Alexstrasza entrusted to you."

"We understand, Prince Sabellian," a nearby red dragon replied.

Kurdran Wildhammer's voice boomed from the sky. "Fall back, lads! Every gryphon rider, take a passenger. One more each means one less brother left to the black dragon's clutches!"

"What about your gryphon?" Jace asked Grede beside him.

"Got hit by fire from one of those infernal giants. Probably won't make it." Grede sighed. "What, you think I'd let Gelorior toss me around otherwise?"

Black dragons, red dragons, and gryphon riders dove into the walls. Fighting off the remaining infernals, they pulled soldiers — fallen and standing alike — from the ruins and battlements, lifting them into the air.

Sora, the lone green dragon, stood on a wall casting nature magic, healing every dragon or mortal she could see. Vereesa wanted to call out and ask her to help carry them all away, but couldn't bring herself to say it.

"Stand closer," Sabellian said, extending his dragon claw. "I haven't been imprisoned that long. I still remember how to fly."

The two rangers and the paladin looked up in surprise. Grede jumped. "You'll let someone ride your back?"

Sabellian lowered his gaze in warning. "One more word and you're walking back on your own, dwarf knight. Don't think rescuing me lets you do whatever you want."

"Forget I said anything." Grede raised his hands. "Thanks, Sabellian," Jace said.

The black prince said nothing. He scooped up the two groups on either side in his claws and rose. His massive black wings, spreading wide, instantly demolished the towers and walls on both sides.

Jace, Grede, Vereesa, and Morofil had been standing close enough to get grabbed in one claw. Grede's face was buried under Jace's cloak, and he grimaced. "What the hell is that smell, Jace? Is it you, or the fel orc blood, or the demon?"

"None of the above. It's the residue from that dragon's magic." Jace glanced down at Grede's head, then noticed the upper portion of his black dragon-hide cloak was nearly burned away.

Not completely destroyed or torn, but the enchantment had been stripped. The surface was ragged, covered in shadow corrosion marks.

In hindsight, while the void Netherwing dragon was barely three years old in real terms, she'd possessed the body and wild power of a full-grown adult dragon. His cloak was made from a young black dragon's hide — it was never going to withstand a full-power blast like that.

What surprised Jace was that Morofil's robe had almost completely absorbed the brunt of that same breath. The decorative elements she'd added based on Garalon Airow's personal taste had been burned off, but the robe's body was almost undamaged.

Clutched in Sabellian's claw, Jace couldn't see the outside world, couldn't lift his arm to check his pocket watch. Everything swayed gently. Vereesa and Grede were both exhausted to the bone, but whether from the lingering shadow pain or the sheer terror of the battle they'd just survived, neither could fall asleep.

Eventually, the black dragon stopped and "spilled" them onto the ground. The roar of voices told him — Wildhammer Stronghold. They were back.

Church priests rushed over to take the wounded. Some looked like they'd been knocked unconscious; others might already be dead. Gryphon riders and Farstriders excitedly recounted the battle, describing the Dragonmaw orcs' miserable fate. Jace's group became the center of attention. From the crowd's shouts, Jace finally learned the paladin's name — a knight from Arathi.

Of course, the one drawing the most attention was Sabellian himself.

Deathwing's son, when he landed in the center of Wildhammer Stronghold, made the walls and towers look comically small — until he shifted back to human form in a swirl of dark wind.

After the wounded were settled and the defenders confirmed no Dragonmaw remnants or doomguard legions from Karabor were in pursuit, the garrison finally relaxed.

After a brief half-night of rest, Turalyon called a meeting in the stronghold's main hall the next morning.

Though Vereesa and Grede hadn't fully recovered, still suffering from the void corrosion, both insisted on attending to hear what the black prince had to say.

Kurdran Wildhammer, the Farstriders, Kirin Tor mages, Church priests, and several officers of the Silver Hand all gathered.

Sabellian, Gelorior, and the female black dragon Gelorior had mentioned — the one who could take mortal form — also came to the hall. Her mortal form was a dusky-skinned woman with light red hair, wearing gray robes. She looked like a volcano witch with no good intentions. But Jace remembered it was her tail-strike that had shattered the infernal meteor falling straight toward him.

None of the black dragons looked particularly friendly, but they all took turns thanking Sora in draconic as they entered the hall. It made Sora so uncomfortable she kept glancing at Jace, as if he could somehow rescue her from the situation.

It made sense. Sora was their cousin, after all — one of Ysera's more favored children, not a mere mortal. More importantly, if Sora hadn't insisted on staying on the walls to heal and bolster the whelps, the damage from the infernal giants would've been far worse.

"You seem to be in good spirits for someone who's been tortured by orcs for years, Baron Sabellian," Kurdran said.

Sabellian wasn't provoked by the dwarf's jab. He nodded slightly. "Lord Kurdran, Marshal Turalyon. It's good to see you both well."

"Before we sort everything out, I want to confirm one thing," Turalyon said. "The promise Lord Gelorior made on your behalf..."

"The alliance between ourselves and the Sons of Lothar," Sabellian said. "Gelorior has told me. I believe this benefits both our kind and the Sons of Lothar. A mutual victory."

"Especially now that the Burning Legion has clearly had its victory?" Kurdran asked. "What do you mean?"

"Don't tell me you can't feel the Legion's barrier sealing all of Draenor," Sabellian said.

"Of course we can," Turalyon said. "It came down as soon as Khadgar left."

"Khadgar left?" Sabellian asked.

"He returned to Azeroth through our portal. Before the barrier fell," Jace said.

Sabellian looked at him, hesitated, then asked, "So who else can wield the Scepter of Sargeras?"
