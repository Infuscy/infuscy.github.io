## Chapter 1317: A Feint

"Kar'lez."

Jace gazed into the void with half-closed eyes, guiding the burning ritual circle under the imp's watchful gaze.

This time, he tried using a few ordinary soul shards as the energy source for the communication ritual, testing whether his growing familiarity with the spell made his mana consumption more efficient.

The soul shards dimmed at a visible rate. Just as he thought he might fail again, a demon on the other side answered his call.

"Master..."

"Your attitude's improved," Jace said.

"I can feel that your power has grown considerably, my master. And not just because of the Eredar's grimoire," Kar'lez said.

"I thought it might be because you're less 'demonic,'" Jace said. "Does Master Khadgar have news?"

"He's beside me now," Kar'lez said.

"He's here?" Jace asked, surprised.

"Yes." The demon lowered his voice. "The Kirin Tor mages are discussing with your king about establishing a branch in Stonewatch Keep that can connect directly to Dalaran, to support close communication with your side. Khadgar wants me to tell you they've set up a teleportation point at Stonewatch, maintained by several human mages from the Wizard Sanctum. High Mage Andormas is also part of the plan."

"So the Kirin Tor knows about Ilgara's Tower? The Council of Six knows an Eredun lord is standing on the shores of Lake Everstill?"

The void was silent for a moment. Then the demon replied. "Khadgar has explained the situation to the Kirin Tor council. He says he used the power of the Tome of Dark Names to learn my true name, then sealed me in the tower using the Talar'fshin Crystal's power, forcing me to act as a communications relay between Azeroth and the expedition."

"Insolence! This self-righteous gray-haired mage... I understand, but this is an insult—"

"Kar'lez!" Jace said. "Do you want to stay in Azeroth or not? Dalaran's got more than one demon sealed away. Some have been locked up for two thousand years. They don't need to kill you to make your life miserable. So do as he says!"

"If that is your command, Jace Sesso." The demon lord's voice still carried a hint of anger.

"Are there other mages nearby?" Jace asked.

"Amy Marin is here, plus two mages from Dalaran," the demon replied.

"Ask those two masters to leave. I need to speak with Master Khadgar alone," Jace said.

A long silence on the other end. Jace felt the soul shards supporting the communication array burn out one by one.

The imp obediently placed new soul shards on the array. Jace tried to reconnect the array to fresh fragments, maintaining the spell. Finally, a voice came through.

"Only Khadgar and Amy Marin are here now, my master." The demon's voice. "He wants you to tell him more about the black dragons. He has many questions about Sabellian's imprisonment by the orcs. About how the Dragonmaw orcs restrained him, and what they were scheming in those crystal-filled mountains."

"That's exactly what I need to tell him." Jace considered for a moment, then said, "The Scepter of Sargeras. The Scepter of Sargeras is still in Draenor."

"What?" Kar'lez's voice lost its confidence instantly. "You mean... the scepter."

"Tell Khadgar exactly what I said," Jace emphasized.

"Yes, master." The demon's voice was low.

After a moment, Kar'lez said, "Khadgar wants me to tell you that a lot of things make sense now. From the Dragonmaw Legion's movements to the demons' actions, to the barrier sealing all of Draenor."

"Yes, Sabellian guessed the same," Jace said. "He thinks the barrier was created by the Scepter of Sargeras too. Nothing else could do it. What do you think, Kar'lez?"

"The Scepter of Sargeras is a terrifying thing capable of twisting space... No one can say what it can or can't do, my master," Kar'lez said.

"Khadgar says all his research on the scepter in Draenor is on the second floor of the mage tower, on the eastern bookshelves marked 'Eredar' — the brown ones. There's also a locked chest next to them. He believes that once you take the scepter from the Burning Legion, no matter who put up the current barrier between Azeroth and Draenor, it won't be able to withstand the scepter's power."

"He says even if the artifact can't send everyone back as smoothly as he'd hoped, once it tears through the barrier, the Kirin Tor can use the Eye of Dalaran and the Book of Medivh to anchor an arcane portal, reconnecting both sides and finishing the job."

As Kar'lez relayed Khadgar's words in demon-tongue, his tone was calm and cold, but Jace could feel Khadgar's excitement and eagerness in the choice of words.

Of course. This had been his plan from the start — from the moment he'd realized Ner'zhul intended to use the Scepter of Sargeras for the ritual that destroyed half of Draenor. Take the scepter. Save everyone.

What could be more thrilling than discovering that an opportunity you thought was lost had suddenly reappeared before you?

"Not to mention that you're a more talented shadow practitioner than even the most gifted warlocks of the Shadow Council, Jace Sesso. That's what he says. He believes you're more suited to take the scepter and complete his research and plan. By the time the Scepter of Sargeras returns to Azeroth, the Burning Legion's eight-hundred-year invasion plan will be foiled once more... It would be perfect, except he won't be there to see it."

Jace was silent for a moment. His attention drifted from the array. With a crack, two soul shards burned to ash. The imp jumped. "Master, don't get distracted! We don't have many soul shards left!"

"Bring that half-burned draenei soul shard," Jace said calmly.

"Yes, master! Yes, master!" The imp hopped onto the table and tossed the remaining fragment over. Jace caught it and placed it on the array. The communication stabilized again.

"So you want us to find the scepter, Master Khadgar?" Jace asked quietly. "I don't dare make that decision myself. The entire expedition is waiting for your opinion."

"Khadgar says the expedition's mages already know what he thinks. Even a human mage named Vargos would support him," Kar'lez said.

"What about you, Kar'lez?" Jace asked.

The demon lord hesitated. "Do you really intend to seize the Scepter of Sargeras, my master? The demon tome of Draenor is in your hands. Before a new demon lord arrives in Draenor, this is indeed a perfect window of opportunity. But I believe Kil'jaeden won't let you win so easily... There may be other traps waiting on the road you can't imagine. Remember my words."

"I know, Kar'lez. I know."

The shadow-link thinned slightly. His vision returning, Jace looked at the burning array ahead. His eyes blinked involuntarily.

He noticed the draenei soul shard was nearly spent too. But he had little left to say.

He cast his consciousness back into the void. "Lady Alleria and General Danath Trollbane should be arriving at Wildhammer Stronghold soon, Master Khadgar. But I think even when they get here, they'll lean toward your opinion. With your confirmation, I can breathe easy. So, let's leave it here. I'll go study the materials on the Scepter of Sargeras."

Just as Jace raised his hand to dismiss the barrier, Kar'lez suddenly spoke again. "Wait, master."

"What?"

"Khadgar says he wants to hear your thoughts. He thinks you have a different opinion."

"Get a few more soul shards," Jace said.

"Oh, master." The imp groaned miserably.

"Hurry up!" Jace snapped.

The imp reluctantly grabbed two soul shards and brought them to the array, making a sour face as he placed them down. The array flared to life again. But the demon blood was almost dry.

"You think this is Kil'jaeden luring you in?" Kar'lez asked coldly.

"Is that your question, or Khadgar's?" Jace asked.

"His question," Kar'lez said. "And now it's mine too, master."

So that was it. That was why Kil'jaeden had lowered the barrier only after Khadgar returned to Azeroth. Khadgar had already sensed something was wrong.

He wasn't a transmigrator. He didn't know about Arthas. But he'd heard countless legends from the Twisting Nether. About how Kil'jaeden used every trick imaginable to deceive the greatest wizards and warriors of countless worlds into becoming his pathfinders, paving the way for the demonic hoard to trample world after world...

For this plan to work, this extremely troublesome man had to be sent away.

So it seemed Kil'jaeden was quite confident in his scheme. But there was still a hole.

He probably knew Jace had established some kind of portal connecting Azeroth and Draenor. But he most likely didn't know Jace could still communicate with Azeroth.

The Bonechewer warlocks Magtheridon had sent could use the Eye of Kilrogg to spy on Wildhammer Stronghold, tracking the expedition's movements to confirm Khadgar had returned to Azeroth. But he didn't know Jace could still talk to Khadgar.

Or maybe he thought that even if Khadgar could barely offer Jace advice, he couldn't steer the Draenor expedition from afar under these circumstances?

And it made sense. After this group had accomplished an almost impossible teleportation, arrived in Draenor, destroyed the Highmaul Arena, defeated the void dragon deep underground, and rescued the black prince — Jace had built enough credibility in the expedition.

Taking up Khadgar's research to find the Scepter of Sargeras next... was practically the natural next step. And with this remaining communication channel — which no one else could hear — whatever Jace said Khadgar had said was what Khadgar had said. No one in the expedition could refute it.

Thinking about it, Jace couldn't say whether Kil'jaeden had underestimated him or overestimated him.

Kar'lez said, "Khadgar thinks you've both arrived at the same conclusion."

"More or less," Jace said coldly.

"He says it's an honor to know you, master," Kar'lez said.

"Tell him the feeling's mutual." Jace took a deep breath. "But I still have to seek the Scepter of Sargeras."

"Yes," Kar'lez said. "He thinks so too. He and the Kirin Tor will do everything possible to support your 'backup' plan. Also, he hopes you'll forgive his earlier loss of composure."

"I didn't see any loss of composure," Jace said gravely. "All I see is darkness."

"He wanted me to convey his laughter. I refused," Kar'lez said.

Jace chuckled too. The imp reminded him, "Laughing costs mana too, master..."

"Alright," Jace said. "That's enough for now, Kar'lez. Thank you for your help."

"At your service, honored master."

Jace dropped his smile and swept his hand across. The array scattered in a burst of flame, leaving only a pile of black ash.
