## Chapter 1318: Father and Daughter

Coming out of the mage tower, it was already late.

Since the Dragonmaw Fortress victory, the relentless daily workload of tending the wounded and reinforcing patrols had pushed everyone in the stronghold to their limits. No matter how much passion everyone brought to the work, the body couldn't lie.

Come nightfall, the whole stronghold was filled with non-stop dwarven snoring, punctuated only by the occasional wingbeats of gryphon riders on night patrol.

After sustaining the void array for so long, Jace was feeling the exhaustion too.

He'd been worried something might go wrong back in Dalaran. Fighting Kil'jaeden and the Burning Legion here while chaos reigned there would've been a nightmare.

But it looked like Khadgar had handled the Kirin Tor. And conveniently pinned the situation on Jace's control of Kar'lez, giving Ilgara's Tower a legitimate "cover story."

Thinking about all the arguments, communications, and back-and-forth between Dalaran, Stormwind, and the Wizard Sanctum during that time... and Amy's awkward position in the middle of it all...

Even though he hadn't had to deal with those problems himself, it was enough to make his scalp tingle.

Thankfully, none of that existed anymore. Now he just needed to focus on Draenor.

Suddenly, a burst of dark breath roaring toward his forehead made Jace instinctively grab for his sword. But in the next second, he realized what the shadow was...

A whelp. A black dragon whelp.

Taking advantage of his moment of hesitation, the creature pounced on his face and knocked him over. The whelp wasn't huge, but still much larger than Jace. It panted heavily, rolled off him, spread its wings, and opened its purple-red maw, flashing pale, sharp teeth that stood out clearly in the dark alley.

"Needleflame." Jace pushed himself up against the wall. "We meet again."

"You recognized me." The black dragon's voice was raspy. "I thought you'd forgotten what I looked like, Jace Sesso."

"A whelp this small and this cocky isn't common in Azeroth either." Jace studied her claws and neck. "What was that ambush for? You didn't think you could hurt me, did you?"

"I wasn't ambushing you."

Needleflame folded her wings, shrinking into the corner like an oversized, snake-headed black cat, and peered cautiously at the sky. Jace could feel her presence slowly contracting, until even he had trouble sensing her.

"I came to thank you." Her voice was low. "For saving my father. You really kept your word."

"That's a... unique way of saying thanks." Jace said. "Most people give money. Buy me a meal. Send a gift. Not that I expected you to be that practical, little one, but you shouldn't—"

He touched his cheek, checking for any bleeding.

"I know," Needleflame admitted. "Lord Gelorior doesn't want whelps who can't shapeshift inside the walls, disturbing the humans. But I wanted to thank you and the dream-sister in person. So I snuck in. Those gryphon riders can't sense me."

"They probably can't be bothered to 'sense' you." Jace smiled. "I haven't heard Marshal Turalyon give any order banning black dragons from inside the walls. We're allies now."

The black dragon snorted out a puff of ash in displeasure. "I heard there was a void-corrupted dragon in the Dragonmaw fortress. About my age. You killed her."

"Yes," Jace said. "I had to. She was keeping your father imprisoned. She was completely the orcs' slave."

"Did the orcs use void magic to make her that big?" Needleflame asked.

Jace understood now why she was asking. "With Sabellian's care, you're already growing faster than most dragons your age, Needleflame. Or would you rather become a puppet with great power but no will of your own? Controlled by someone else?"

Needleflame's head twitched. "No," she rasped. Jace could sense she was holding something back.

"What are you really here for, Needleflame? I don't think it's just to thank me."

"What about the dream-sister?" The black dragon tilted her head.

"She should be over near the dwarf—" Jace gestured, then remembered he hadn't seen Sora when he'd gone back for soul shards. He pointed toward the Church's infirmary instead. "Probably over there. Fly over and find her."

"You take me," the black dragon said.

Jace gave a challenging smile. "Black dragons scared of the dark?"

Needleflame bared her teeth. "I don't know the way! Mortal fortresses are too confusing."

"It's just one field." Jace jabbed his thumb in the direction. "A quick flight gets you there. You'll see the Light symbol. Unless you evil black dragons are all naturally afraid of the Holy Light?"

"My father says you mate with a wingless demon lord every night. He told me to stay away from you." The black dragon snorted. "If you dare walk in there, why wouldn't I?"

"Hah. That ungrateful father of yours really tells you everything, doesn't he?"

Jace turned to leave. Seeing him go, the black dragon spread her wings and blocked his path. "Don't go."

"What do you actually want? Did Sabellian teach you how to annoy people and skip the part about reading the room?"

"You insulted me first."

The black dragon actually sounded a little hurt. Jace almost broke his deliberately angry expression.

"Fine. I apologize, little one." Jace sighed. "Stop beating around the bush. Tell me why you want to see Sora."

"Her name is Sora?" the black dragon asked.

"Somaliel," Jace said. "That's her full name."

"What a beautiful name. Queen Ysera must love her very much, to let one so young learn to shapeshift." Needleflame lowered her head, thinking. "I want to learn how, like her, Jace Sesso. Will you ask her to teach me?"

"I'm no dragon expert, but shouldn't this be your parents' job?" Jace asked.

"Father says Blackwing should be born knowing how to shapeshift. He could change into other beasts as soon as he hatched. Even into mortal forms." Needleflame drooped. "But me — I still don't know how."

"He also said if I couldn't do it naturally, I should just learn to be a good warrior like my other siblings and stop thinking about it."

Sabellian, as Deathwing's direct son, was obviously different from other dragons. Learning to shapeshift as a whelp was nothing special for him. But competing with your own child on bloodline was a bit much. Strictly speaking, that weakness was his fault for not being as powerful as his father.

"Quite a dad." Jace scratched his stubble. "But I'll warn you now — I don't know how dragon stuff works. I've never been a dragon. So I can't guarantee she can 'teach' you to shapeshift, Needleflame."

"Then you'll take me to see Sister Somaliel?" Needleflame's amber eyes lit up with excitement.

"One condition." Jace raised a finger.

"What? Tell me," Needleflame said.

"Never mind. No condition." Jace waved his hand. "Come on, little one."
