## Chapter 132: Forming the Hunting Party

The noble said all that, and Grede wasn't about to let it slide.

"I'm no ordinary Wildhammer," he stressed. "That beast might've killed other warriors, but that doesn't mean it can kill me."

"It killed three at once," the noble said. "I don't doubt your fighting skills, dwarf sir, but a friend of mine watched that fight. Three Sandmar brothers who'd come here from the Highlands, trying to surround it—but Night Baron seized the chance to pick them off one by one. A brutal scene. Though I have to admire your Wildhammer kin's mettle, because I happened to see those three Sandmar dwarves fight too. They once killed a pair of savage Stranglethorn white apes together—no small feat."

Grede stared at the noble. For a long time after hearing the tale, he didn't say a word.

He wiped the rain from his face, glanced at Jace, then at the noble. "Get me a hunting rifle, then."

"Relax, dwarf." The noble shook his head. "The captain isn't about to hand you a gun and send you down there after that panther."

"It's too valuable, is it?" Jace asked.

"Exactly," the noble said. "It's a star of the Gurubashi Arena—its value must be beyond reckoning. You think the captain would transport something this valuable for free? He must have a sky-high shipping contract with the beast's owner, along with an equally sky-high guarantee. The moment that panther gets injured, or dies, or—in short—loses the ability to keep fighting, he's ruined."

"So how much is it actually worth?" Grede asked, curious.

The noble shot Grede a look. "I couldn't say for sure, but there are definitely other passengers here who understand that panther's value just as well as I do. And the captain knows it too. Let's see how much he's willing to put up as a bounty to catch the thing."

The ship's rolling had eased quite a bit compared to before. The wind howled and drove the rain in sheets across the deck. The furled canvas on the mast flapped and snapped.

The worst of the storm seemed to have passed. Only the black clouds pressing low on the horizon still loomed over the lone vessel, as if the sea itself had calmed down and was watching with curiosity to see what these people would do.

The murmuring among the passengers gradually died down. The captain and a group of goblins and a few human sailors were huddled together discussing something, their sharp, rapid voices carrying over the noise of the crowd on the lower deck.

The goblins with hunting rifles were trained on the open hatches, most of which had been firmly locked shut.

Before long, a few goblins dragged out some ropes, forks, and a fishing net from the other side of the hold. A tall human sailor came out last, carrying a long pole.

They were conferring among themselves, and Jace could make out that they were discussing tactics—which hold to search first, which second. It looked like the captain was planning to send his own men to deal with the problem.

Just as the noble had predicted, these fellows had no real weapons to speak of, except for one goblin who carried a harpoon. Even that harpoon was a mooring type—Jace seriously doubted those two prongs could do any real damage.

"They want to take the panther alive," Jace said.

Grede sized up the group and said, "I don't like their chances. A panther that can take on three Wildhammer warriors isn't some ordinary Stranglethorn beast you can compare to the rest. Whether those few could even kill it, let alone capture it alive, is anyone's guess."

"Human." He turned to the noble. "Did your friend happen to tell you what weapons those three dwarves were using against the thing?"

"You have to understand, the arena needs spectacle." The noble looked up at the goblins above. "They wouldn't give those three dwarves any hunting tools. So they just went in with the weapons they knew best—hammers and axes."

Jace had figured as much. For the nobles who came to the Gurubashi Arena for entertainment, hunting with tools was nothing special—for all he knew, they probably got to watch something like that on their own estates once or twice a month.

The real draw was watching short axes and war hammers go toe-to-toe with a beast's claws at close range. Anyone tuning in for a show like that was there for the thrill.

Grede shook his head but said nothing. After all, those gladiators were fighting for fame and glory—of course they prioritized spectacle. He couldn't really say anything on behalf of his clansmen who were killing for coin.

By now the goblins and the two humans had set out. Even though the goblins' skin was green, Jace could tell they looked like death warmed over—every single one of them reluctant as hell.

The two humans wore grim expressions too, even though they had on chainmail that could ward off claws and were carrying various hunting tools. But that equipment clearly wasn't enough to keep the panic and fear at bay.

They knew exactly what they were up against, no matter how much the captain denied it.

After the group went below, a strange silence fell over the deck.

The rain kept falling, a now-loud, now-soft roar driven by the wind. The occasional flash of lightning illuminated everyone's faces—worried and curious all at once. The captain gripped a rope and swayed with the wind, staring into the darkness in the distance.

The goblins with guns did their best to keep their aim on the open hatches, in case the beast suddenly came charging up.

Jace knew there was no way it would charge onto the deck. A panther that had become a star in the arena had to have its own battle strategy—it knew when to hold and when to fold.

Charging onto the deck might let it kill a few more innocent people and pad its "record," but it would quickly get swarmed and beaten to death by the bolder passengers.

Jace noticed that quite a few people present were wearing all kinds of weapons—swords, daggers. He hadn't realized the ship carried this many things capable of killing a person.

Which meant everyone had brought out their means of self-defense, ready to fight to the death at any moment. Only now did the Trade Cartel's passenger profile start to show—not a single easy mark on this whole ship.

Everyone's eyes were deadly serious, locked onto the hatches just like the gun barrels. The panther had to know that coming up here was suicide.

If the hunting party below didn't produce any results, this standoff could last until dawn, or all the way to port.

But once daylight came, the ones on deck would be the ones on the back foot.

Under the direct sun at sea, even if the temperature in late November wasn't that high, being baked that long would be unbearable. Eventually they'd have to retreat back into the panther's hunting ground, and who knew how many more would be killed or wounded.

That goblin who'd been watching the cargo hold was already dead. First mate Gazick was most likely a goner too—otherwise the captain wouldn't have come out personally to take charge. That fellow hadn't shown his face at all during the first few days of the voyage. Grede had assumed he was still back at port "staring at some green-skinned girl's backside" and had missed the departure.

Time ticked by. Some of the people who'd been watching the hatches or pressing themselves to the deck to listen for movement below gradually gave up their vigil. Everyone knew—the hunting party that had just gone down didn't seem to be doing well.

How many more men could the captain afford to lose?

In any case, the ship absolutely could not dock with an uncontrolled panther on board.

The guards at Southshore would never allow a rampaging beast to run loose on shore. Once they got word, they'd be down there in full plate and shields in no time, hacking the thing to pieces.

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. It was the middle of the night by now.

The captain had made his way back to the high point on deck at some point, looking out over the crowd as if wrestling with a decision.

"Two gold coins!" He suddenly held up two fingers and shouted. "Two gold coins to catch that damn—panther! Alive!"

"What?!"

"I told you, it's that black panther!"

"Two gold coins?!"

"That's way too much... two gold coins?!"

"How much can that panther sell for? Does it even sell for two gold coins?"

"Shut up, you pauper... look at you with no vision. That's Night Baron! It'd easily sell for a dozen gold coins at least..."

The deck erupted into an uproar. Even Jace couldn't stay calm.

Since arriving in Stormwind, what job had he ever touched that paid even a single gold coin? Let alone two!

Even taking part in the rescue of an entire castle hadn't come with a cash reward like this. These goblins really were loaded. Though really, it wasn't the goblins who were rich—it was the people running the arena. If the captain was willing to put up two gold coins just to capture that panther alive, then his shipping fee for this trip had to be even higher than that.

But the last few months of earning money in the Kingdom of Stormwind had taught him one thing: bounties were always proportional to the danger. Don't forget that Redridge job hunting orcs—the bounty had only been a dozen silver coins or so.

And these two gold coins was roughly equivalent to killing five or six Hoggers.

Though it wasn't a straight conversion, the idea of having to handle five or six Hoggers at once made Jace's scalp tingle.

But as the saying went, where there's a big reward, there are always brave souls—and sure enough, two people stood up to answer the call.

Grede looked like he was about to burst too. He grabbed Jace's arm. "Wanna go down there and take a shot?"

"I don't want to fight a gladiator celebrity, Grede," Jace said. "Think about it—half the people on this ship know him. Some of them might've even watched his fights. Would you get in the ring with an award-winning boxer from your own homeland?"

Grede laughed. "I'm an award-winning boxer myself! Don't forget what I did to that Torben at the Pig and Whistle! This so-called Baron is famous, sure, but it's still just a beast! And if it's really that impressive, why isn't it called Night Count, Night Duke, Night King? I don't buy it... not if we have the right tools!"

"Look at the guys who just went down there," Jace said. "They were fully armed, weren't they? Why haven't they come back up yet?"

"Just sailors," Grede said. "Those goblins might know more about sailing than I ever will, but they sure as hell don't know more about handling animals. And look at the volunteers who've stepped up—every one of them hungrier for coin than the last. Those look like bounty hunters to me. Four experienced hunters against one beast—isn't that money just there for the taking? Night Baron might be a gladiator, no question, but this isn't the arena!"

"This is a hell of a lot worse than the arena," Jace said, remembering the scene from when they'd followed the blood trail through the hold. "And forget going—if we're going, we wait for daylight, Grede. At least the hold will be brighter. Going down there now is just asking for trouble."

"That's true enough."

Grede was itching for a fight but couldn't do anything about it. He folded his arms and plopped down next to Jace, eyes locked on those two volunteers.

Two people obviously weren't enough to catch that panther. The goblin captain shouted for a bit longer, then pulled a gold coin straight out of his bag and bellowed, "Three! Anyone else! I'm paying three gold coins!"

"Three?" Grede's eyebrows shot up.

Another woman raised her hand to join the hunting party. That was it—Grede couldn't sit still anymore.

"Three gold coins, Jace. What if these people just take the money? And there's a woman in that group."

Jace said, "We weren't planning on earning that money anyway. If they take it, isn't that even better? We get through this crisis safe and sound, the ship sails smoothly to the other side—that'd be the best outcome."

"That was before," Grede said, eyeing the gold coin in the goblin's hand. "Now there's money sitting right there. Three gold coins. If I don't find a way to go for it... I'm just losing out! Jace, five experienced hunters against one beast—you really think we can't handle it? Look at that woman, she's built like a damn bear. I reckon she could punch me flat!"

Jace looked at Grede's insatiable expression, then over at the three people who'd already gathered. They did look like they knew what they were doing, just like Grede said. The way they took the ropes, fishing nets, and long forks from the goblin sailors looked pretty professional.

He glanced at that gleaming gold coin again.

"Ah, to hell with it. This isn't the first time I've done something stupid. Let's go!"

"Hah! Now you're talking!" The dwarf excitedly ran out and shouted, "If five of us go and split three gold coins, how's the split work?"

"The ship's got plenty of silver, Aerie Peak brother." The goblin captain's tone was much calmer than before. "At the current gold coin rate, that's eighty silver each once the job's done. How's that sound? You three in—interested?"

The three looked at each other, exchanged a few words, then nodded in agreement. None of them were the type to fight to the death for a bit more coin. They were probably all here to make some extra money and make the trip a little safer—and more hands made light work.

"I need a shield," Grede said. "Got a shield on this ship?"

"Of course." The captain directed a goblin to bring over a shield with a Lordaeron emblem on it—didn't even have much damage on the surface.

Grede took the shield. The bottom point nearly touched the ground. Clearly not the right size for his height.

"Better than nothing." He took a pole as well, then turned to the crowd and called out, "Anyone else? If not, we're heading down."

The others didn't object. The five-person hunting party had just been matchmade on the spot.
