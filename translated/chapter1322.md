## Chapter 1322: The Paladin's Doubts

At the meeting, the leaders of the Sons of Lothar discussed communications, supply transport, and other matters between Alleria's Keep, Honor Hold, and Wildhammer Keep. From the sound of it, since Draenor shattered, the silithid living between Terokkar Forest and Hellfire Peninsula were an even bigger threat to travel routes than demons and fel orcs.

But Jace's group didn't know much about the situation north of Alleria's Keep, so they couldn't contribute much. For the rest of the discussion, he, Vereesa, and Grede could only act as "observers."

After the meeting, everyone at Wildhammer Keep finally got a chance to talk with the escorts Danath Trollbane had brought. The hallway erupted into a din of voices, each person desperate to know how their friends, family -- even lovers -- living in Hellfire Peninsula were doing.

What surprised Jace was that although, according to Alleria, Honor Hold housed over a thousand soldiers, one of the mages who'd come with Danath seemed to know the situation of nearly every single one. No matter who asked, he could say something.

Jace wondered if Danath had specifically brought this gifted mage to answer the questions everyone in Shadowmoon Valley was dying to ask. When the mage's eyes met Jace's, Jace noticed a faint arcane glow swirling in them, and realized the fellow had cast Arcane Intellect on himself.

Just as he was listening with interest as the mage described the situation at Honor Hold and the supply depot, Turalyon approached. "Jace." He gripped Jace's arm. "Let's talk outside in the corridor."

Jace nodded, patted Grede, who was chatting with a gryphon rider from Honor Hold, and said, "Go find Sora or Vereesa for a drink at noon, Grede."

"I've already found a new drinking buddy. Not as boring as Sora, but can hold his liquor, Jace. It's that little black kid." Grede was chattering excitedly when he noticed Turalyon beside him and remembered he'd arranged to discuss something with Jace. He nodded. "We'll do it another time, then."

Turalyon and Jace left the noisy corridor one after another. With fewer people around now, Turalyon walked ahead and asked, "How's the communication ritual in the basement?"

"It takes a lot of mana," Jace said. "The mages gave me some powerful soul shards, but I still can't say whatever I want. Other than that, no problems."

They reached a stairwell in one of the towers. Turalyon turned and leaned against the cold stone wall. "Did Khadgar really tell you he wants us to focus everything on finding the Scepter of Sargeras?"

Before Jace could respond, the young paladin continued, "Am I compromising too much to fight the Burning Legion, Jace?"

"I think I can trust you. You're Vereesa's beloved, and Khadgar is willing to trust you. You're someone worthy of respect and admiration -- I don't doubt that. And I can temporarily ally with the black dragons, even though they've fought against us and cost us heavy losses in many battles... as long as they don't side with the demons, and as long as they value their children and their kind, enough for me to recognize they're not incomprehensible twisted monsters."

"But the Scepter of Sargeras... I thought Khadgar would share the same doubts I have. Of course, if he truly believes this is feasible, then I choose to trust him. And trust you."

"Khadgar and I both think this is a trap, Turalyon," Jace said.

"A demon's trap?" Turalyon asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

Turalyon let go of the wall he'd been gripping, exhaling as if a weight had lifted. "When the black dragon told us the scepter was in Draenor, even I felt overwhelmed by the mages' enthusiasm. If you hadn't suggested consulting Khadgar first, I might have made a rash decision I'd regret."

"Afterward, the more I thought about it, the more something felt off. But I couldn't put my finger on it. I sought help from the priests, but even the Holy Light couldn't dispel the shadow in my heart. Until now, if you hadn't just told me..."

He paused, as if something suddenly occurred to him. He looked up, meeting Jace's eyes. "By the Holy Light, Jace. I can't imagine what you've been through this entire time."

"Vereesa, Lord Grede Thunderfist, Lady Sora Shadow -- do they know?"

"They know." Jace managed a faint smile. "So as long as you're still willing to trust me, it's not as hard as you think."

Turalyon nodded. "About searching for the scepter -- I plan to send someone to scout Hellfire Citadel first. We just crippled Dragonmaw Fortress, so Magtheridon and his Black Temple will be on high alert. The demons know just as well as we do that black dragons can shapeshift into all kinds of forms to deceive. But are you sure you still want to go? If the demons are using the Scepter of Sargeras as bait to lure you in, you'd practically be walking into a trap."

"What Kil'jaeden needs is a Jace Sesso who can pick up the Scepter of Sargeras, not a dead corpse," Jace said. "Besides, making us actively search for the Scepter of Sargeras is exactly what he wants us to do, Turalyon. Which means that until we find the scepter, Kil'jaeden probably won't order the Legion to launch a full assault on our keeps."

"But they're preparing," Turalyon said. "The Legion's barrier has cut us off from the outside, but the Legion itself still has a steady stream of soldiers coming through their portals into Draenor, compensating for the losses caused by the stolen demon registry."

Jace said, "So I need to act enthusiastic. Let Kil'jaeden think I've taken the bait. The Legion won't be in a hurry to attack. That's the foundation we need to find another way out of this."

"I agree." Turalyon said. "Now I can rest easy."

"One more thing." Jace said. "Has Sabellian mentioned that the black dragons are willing to help the Expedition transport soldiers from Honor Hold and Alleria's Keep?"

"They're willing? He hasn't said a word to me about it," Turalyon said.

"These black dragons..." Jace glanced at the doorway. "They won't offer help on their own, Turalyon. You need to find a time to talk to that Prince. Be diplomatic -- ask for their help. That's the only way they'll agree. He already made that promise to me. I think he's waiting to see how you and Kurdlan approach him."

Turalyon smiled. "I understand. I'll go talk to him."

"Oh, right." The paladin pulled a plump, pale blue fruit from his satchel. "Alleria brought this back. Said to give it to you."

"Thank Alleria for me." Jace took it and bit into it. It was so sweet it hardly seemed wild. Who knew how many rare and precious resources existed here before the demons descended on Draenor?

Leaving the keep, Jace glanced toward the tavern, wondering if he could still catch Grede's drinking session. Just then, rapid footsteps approached from the side. It was Needleflame.

Her black hair was a tangled mess, her clothes a disaster. Jace wondered if she had no idea how humans dressed and had just thrown together something with magic that only looked like clothes.

"Jace Sesso!" Needleflame stopped, pulling a dwarven dagger from her belt. "Father said you're planning to go adventuring in orc territory. I want to go too."

"No," Jace said. "Didn't Grede say he'd take you drinking? Why aren't you there?"

"Don't change the subject. Are you underestimating me?" Needleflame grabbed his belt. "I can shapeshift too. I'm the third black dragon in this keep who can do it, and none of the red ones can! I can learn to turn into an orc. I've seen orcs."

"Let go." Jace pointed at her grubby little hand.

Needleflame released him with obvious displeasure. Angelista's bright red sash now had a dark, greasy stain. Jace frowned, rubbed at it with his thumb, and failed to get it off. "Do you know the orcish language?"

"Lok-tar!"

The little black dragon had practically roared it in full draconic. Jace laughed. "What's the second phrase?"

"That's all I know." Needleflame admitted. "But I learn languages fast. Just like I learned human speech, Jace Sesso. I just didn't bother learning orc before. By the time you're ready to leave, I might have mastered their stupid tongue."

Jace didn't doubt that. Dragons learned mortal languages at an absurdly fast rate -- thoroughly and well -- and age didn't seem to matter.

"But this time your father and I aren't going to fight," Jace said. "That place isn't right for a warrior like you. We'll talk about it another time."

"Fine. But next time you go out to fight, you have to take me," Needleflame said.

"Sure," Jace said offhandedly.

"I know you'll keep your word!"

The black dragon whelp jumped up excitedly, then turned and vanished. By then, Jace was already regretting that promise.
