Chapter 1324: Two Orcs

Riding on Sabellian's back was nothing like riding Sora's. Sora flew with dream magic -- you could sit comfortably up there, even sleep, as long as you found a way to strap yourself to her horns.

Sabellian, however, was much larger than Sora but not at all like a big passenger jet with a smoother ride. Furious currents of shadow energy howled past without pause. Jace had to cling to anything he could hold, and by the end he felt he'd nearly drained all the void dragon mana he'd drawn from deep inside Dragonmaw Fortress.

No matter how much he urged Sabellian to slow down, smooth out the ride, the black dragon wouldn't listen. As if putting up with this was part of the privilege of climbing onto his back.

After holding on for a while, Jace was forced to don Orastrasz's hide. When that thick cloak wrapped around him, the violent black wind on the dragon's back finally subsided.

But even the Black Dragon Prince wasn't stubborn enough to fly directly from Shadowmoon Valley north into the void that the Scepter of Sargeras had torn open, crossing a whole stretch of nothingness to reach Hellfire Peninsula. Instead, he flew northwest into Terokkar Forest first, then turned north, skirting the completely collapsed archipelago of floating islands to conserve strength.

As they entered Terokkar Forest, the air currents eased. The black dragon stopped beating his wings and glided smoothly. Jace finally found a chance to glance downward and see what lay below.

What he saw was utterly unexpected.

A sea.

Yes, this was the area between Terokkar Forest and Hellfire Peninsula. Jace knew that before Draenor shattered, this had been an ocean. But by the time of The Burning Crusade, this area should have completely collapsed into the void. He hadn't expected a remnant sea to remain.

The water, however, was already corroded by shadow and fel, reminding him of the foul, stagnant waters in the Temple of Atal'Hakkar. With the continent constantly trembling and collapsing, monstrous waves piled one atop another. Jace was certain that even the great galleons sailing Baradin Bay, whose crews boasted they could wrestle storms, would be torn to pieces in an instant if they landed here.

"I'm curious. Why didn't you want to bring Needleflame?" Sabellian asked abruptly.

Jace looked up toward the front. "I don't get it. You want your own daughter to go adventuring into orc territory? You want her dead faster?"

"Of course I would never allow it," Sabellian said. "But you didn't even consider it."

"You've been watching me," Jace said.

"I don't need to watch you." The black dragon said. "Needleflame tells me everything. Wouldn't this have been a chance to get closer to her? You could show off your magic and power, or save her life in a critical moment, earn her admiration, turn her into your follower and worshipper, and naturally have her follow you back to Azeroth into the arms of a queen she's never met."

"You're saying it all yourself," Jace said.

Sabellian rumbled a laugh. The vibration in his chest made the ground beneath Jace's feet feel like an earthquake, as if his speech and laughter came from completely different systems in his body.

"You're not like any warlock I've known, Jace Sesso. And you're not what my father judged you to be either."

"How did he judge me?" Jace asked.

"He thought you'd become one of those mortals -- the kind who think they can save the world..." Sabellian trailed off, then fell silent. After a moment, his tone turned cold. "Let's not talk about him anymore."

They flew over the dark, turbulent sea for what felt like an eternity before a range of pitch-black mountains jutted out of nowhere before them.

As far as the eye could see: withered trees, boulders shattered by the crashing waves, chasms spewing shadowy mist, a constant, rumbling roar that never ceased.

The dim, dark sky was crisscrossed with black channels left by the Scepter of Sargeras. Gazing up at those channels, Jace couldn't help wondering how many demons were passing through them at this very moment.

In this harsh wasteland, only the most cunning and resilient things survived. Unfortunately, the Draenor silithid, which the Alliance Expedition called "Stalkers," possessed both qualities in abundance.

As Sabellian flew over these mountains that looked like blackened tumors, crimson figures would occasionally emerge from the massive thorns and fallen black timber, shrieking at the sky as if challenging the black dragon.

In Azeroth, no beast would ever dare roar at a black dragon like that. The beasts of Draenor knew the power of black dragons, of course. They simply understood that this world still had countless beings capable of putting them in their place. Black dragons were still in the "food chain." Sabellian and his kind had a very long road ahead of them, and judging by how it ended in another timeline, that road wasn't easy.

Passing through the thorn-covered mountains at the northern edge of Terokkar Forest, a vast red plain spread out before them.

Massive intersecting chasms scarred the plain like wounds. These weren't caused by the Scepter of Sargeras -- they were the dry beds of dead rivers.

This was Hellfire Peninsula. Before the Scepter of Sargeras tore Draenor apart, this land was called Tanaan Jungle -- a humid jungle surrounded by sea on three sides, very similar to the Cape of Stranglethorn. It was the homeland of the Bleeding Hollow clan.

Here, the orcs had built the Dark Portal, and alongside it, Hellfire Citadel as their base for invading Azeroth. During this time, the orcs felled the forests on a massive scale for building materials. The fel corruption brought by the demonic power quickly turned this place into a "Blasted Lands" mirroring the other side of the Dark Portal -- a horrific crimson wasteland.

If Ner'zhul's ritual had torn the vitality from countless fertile lands including Nagrand, then for Tanaan, it was nothing but a meaningless desecration of an already dead corpse.

Jace was genuinely curious what the orc-built Hellfire Citadel looked like in reality. The image of the game's Highmaul hadn't left much of an impression on him, but the towering, sprawling Hellfire Citadel was a completely different story.

Before he could get a good look at Hellfire Citadel, Sabellian landed among a cluster of hills and set Jace down.

"We're approaching the orc watch posts, Jace Sesso."

The black dragon said this as he transformed in a dark whirlwind into the shape of a fel orc covered in demonic spikes. He retained his blood-red magical robe, and a staff glowing with dark purple magical light appeared in his hand.

"Pick a form you like."

"Anything's fine," Jace said. "As long as it's male."

The orc the black dragon had become laughed, then raised his hand. "Remember: do not attempt to cast any spells. My magic won't be easily torn apart, but Hellfire Citadel is crawling with the Bleeding Hollow clan's Eyes of Kilrogg. That spell can detect the flaws caused by magical disturbance."

"I've got it."

Sabellian nodded. A wisp of shadow curled from his fingertip, and Jace immediately felt himself enveloped in Shadowflame. If his body weren't now far more resistant to shadow and fire than an ordinary person's, this alone might have burned him to death.

But the Shadowflame didn't continue burning. Instead, it rapidly transformed him into a blood-red orc. The spikes covering his body felt incredibly awkward, and the tusks in his mouth were a serious nuisance.

"These teeth are going to mess up my speech." Jace had barely started speaking when he felt one tusk scrape against his lip. His thick skin prevented any real damage, but the constant sensation of "might cut and bleed at any moment" was bad enough.

"Don't worry about it." Sabellian said. "Most fel orcs haven't had Magtheridon's blood for very long either. They're all cut up in the mouth, talking funny. Same as you now."

"Fine." Jace answered carefully.

"Give yourself a name," Sabellian said. "I'll go by Nai'horgar."

"Nai'horgar, Death of Dragons?" Jace asked.

"Yes." Sabellian said. "Dragonmaw orcs love giving themselves stupid, provocative names like that. What about you?"

"Then I'll be Nai'jagg," Jace said.

"If I'm not mistaken, that word means... Pure Black?" Sabellian asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

"A rather ordinary orc name." Sabellian smiled meaningfully, then switched to orcish with a heavy Dragonmaw accent. "But a good name too, Nai'jagg. Hellfire Citadel isn't far now. Let's go."
