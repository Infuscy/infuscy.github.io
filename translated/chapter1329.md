## Chapter 1329: Lost Connection

Having dealt with long-lived races for a while, Jace had developed a certain unease about the concept of "a moment."

He didn't know the exact lifespan of ethereals, but given their mode of existence, the duration of their souls was nothing like that of ordinary mortal races.

The shouts from the Shattered Halls above continued. The gladiatorial exhibition at the War-Bringer's Ring wasn't over yet. The orcs' enthusiasm for watching this kind of sport far exceeded Jace's expectations. After drinking Magtheridon's blood, they probably had even more stamina than when they were brown-skinned -- or even green-skinned -- to sustain that enthusiasm. So he didn't need to rush.

The ethereal Kolez seemed unhurried. He sat at the small table, reading what looked like a spellbook, occasionally checking on the glowing disk beside him.

From his past-life memories, Jace figured the disk was "charging" from the mana crystal lattice next to it. He couldn't see any charging cables or ports, but the energy within the disk was clearly growing more abundant.

After a while, the disk slowly emitted a purple glow, like an upward-projecting lantern. Noticing the change, Kolez set down his tome, turned to face the disk, and adopted a rather respectful posture.

He held that position for what felt to Jace like an interminably long time. Since Jace was a fel orc right now, he didn't dare pull out a pocket watch to check the time, but he guessed it was about twenty minutes, maybe half an hour, or even longer.

Finally, a faint figure materialized above the disk. The communicator actually worked.

The figure flickered, gradually growing clearer. Eventually, the image of a tall ethereal in an elegant black-gray robe appeared on the disk. The runecloth strips binding his body were pale silver, and the arcane magical energy beneath them was as stark white as a blizzard.

Kolez said something in a language Jace didn't understand. The black-gray figure suddenly erupted in fury, as if something had jabbed him, screaming at Kolez in a shrill, frantic voice. Kolez immediately fell silent, head lowered, responding only with very short sentences.

Jace listened to their exchange. He couldn't understand a word, but something about their rhythm and cadence felt oddly familiar. He couldn't place it.

After venting his rage for a while, the ethereal's image seemed to notice the fel orc standing nearby.

Jace could feel the image studying him for a moment. Then it exploded in fury again, shrieking at Kolez even more angrily. Only after Kolez murmured a long stream of words did the figure reluctantly calm down. He turned his attention back to the fel orc.

"My apologies for the misunderstanding, warlock. A minor mix-up." The image spoke in fairly standard orcish. "I am Haramad. The Haramad you're looking for. I hear you want to cooperate with the Consortium. Is that right? You're interested in our communicator? This thing I'm standing on?"

"I am Nai'jagg." Jace said. "Yes, I'm very interested in your communicator."

"We don't normally trade in such items, warlock." Haramad's image said. "But in the current special situation, certain things can be negotiated. Can you make decisions on behalf of your Warchief?"

"What do you mean by 'special situation'?" Jace asked.

"Answer my question. Can you make decisions for your Warchief, Nai'jagg?" Haramad's image asked.

Jace said, "I can speak for my clan..."

"Then I won't waste time with someone like you."

Haramad cut him off bluntly, then started shouting at Kolez again. The image itself flickered from the intensity of his gestures and voice.

"You're trapped in this world. Do you want to know what magic is keeping you here?" Jace asked.

Kolez turned to look at him. Haramad stopped shouting.

"I'm guessing you haven't figured out the source of the problem in these past days." Jace said. "I'll give you clues. Help you find a solution. You give my clan the technology to replicate this communicator -- including how to use it, how to power it..."

He glanced at the mana crystal lattice beside him. "What do you say, Node Prince Haramad?"

"Perhaps you don't know enough about us, Nai'jagg." Haramad said. "The time we've spent traveling between dimensions is beyond the imagination of you flesh-beings. The difficulties we've overcome, the limits we've broken -- they're more varied than you can count. The problems facing this planet are trivial to us. And they have nothing to do with the 'special situation' I mentioned."

"But I am quite interested in the magical lockdown affecting this world. Why don't you tell me what you know..."

"What do you..."

"Then..."

Haramad's image began to flicker violently. The light from the disk below became extremely unstable. Kolez's soul-light within his cloth wrappings started sputtering too.

Kolez said something to the image. Haramad's roars and shrieks came in fragments. Then the entire disk went dark, and the image vanished completely.

"Special situation, huh?" Jace said.

Kolez shook his head. "The Node Prince has not authorized me to discuss this 'situation,' Nai'jagg. But he clearly has an interest in continuing this conversation."

Even if these bandage people were being evasive, even if these arcane energy bundles barely gave off any shadow that Jace could sense, he could still hear that this so-called "special situation" meant they were trapped here.

If Node Prince Haramad had just been upfront and admitted they couldn't get out but were looking for a way to leave Draenor, Jace might have believed they actually had the ability.

As it was, they seemed somewhat desperate. But they couldn't show that vulnerability to others -- especially not to the hostile Fel Horde.

This was the Scepter of Sargeras, after all.

No matter how long you'd traveled through the Twisting Nether, how many kinds of magic and technology you'd seen, how many artifacts more terrifying than this could you possibly encounter?

"So how long does it take to recharge?" Jace stared at the disk. "A few more hours?"

"No," the ethereal said. "Probably a few days. I didn't expect the communication to go this poorly."

"A few days?"

Jace and Sabellian didn't have that kind of time to kill in Hellfire Citadel. He couldn't waste several days here just for a few sentences.

"Can I go see this Node Prince in person?"

The ethereal let out a metallic, grating laugh. "Absolutely not, Nai'jagg. Node Prince Haramad never shows his true appearance to outsiders. But you can go see my colleague Jabal. He's in Blade's Edge Mountains. I can give you the location. As long as the Node Prince still has an interest in talking to you, Jabal should be willing to receive you."

"Then that's settled," Jace said.

The ethereal pulled a scroll from a stack of books nearby, traced a simple map on it with arcane magic, and marked a spot in the middle.

"Here. Northeast Blade's Edge Mountains. Cross-reference with your maps. Look for the terrain -- forests and valleys. Our outpost is fairly conspicuous. Easy to find on a Dragonmaw flying mount."

"Got it," Jace said.

Scroll in hand, he hurried back into the Shattered Halls. Judging by the cheers from deep within, the fel orcs were still at their gladiator matches. He was starting to wonder if the competitions in Hellfire Citadel ever actually ended.

When he reached the War-Bringer's Ring, three heavily armored Shattered Hand orcs were brawling in the arena. The two-headed ogre sat up high, laughing as he ate something. Kargath himself stood near the arena exit, leaning against the wall, silently watching the orcs fight. Sabellian wasn't there.

Just as he was about to ask an orc guard, he vaguely heard some rather odd roaring from a corridor on the other side. Sounded like female orc voices.

Following the sound into the corridor, through a damp, stifling passage thick with the mixed stench of fel and urine, the female orc's roars grew louder.

Reaching the doorway where the sound originated, Jace peered inside. Sabellian was half-crouched behind a prone female orc, holding her head down firmly. Her red demonic eyes were clouded over, her consciousness nearly gone. Several other female orcs nearby had already passed out. Saliva, sweat, and other fluids had soaked a large patch of the floor.

When he saw Jace, Sabellian looked up with displeasure. "You dare interrupt a black dragon's fun? Do you know what happened to the last mortal who tried that?"

"Listen to yourself, Nai'horgar," Jace reminded him.

Sabellian grabbed the female orc's demonic horns and yanked her head back, demanding harshly, "Say it. Am I a black dragon?"

"Black dragon! You're a... mighty black dragon. Powerful Nai'horgar!" the female orc rasped weakly.

Jace rubbed his eyes in exasperation. That orc had no idea what was going on anymore.

The Black Dragon Prince laughed smugly, dropping the female orc to the floor as he stood. Flames engulfed him, transforming back into his crimson fel-robe.

"Had better be important, Nai'jagg." He adjusted his collar and stepped out of the room.

"Take me to Blade's Edge Mountains." Jace glanced back at the female orc who'd just lost consciousness. "I think those ethereals can help us with long-distance communication. I want to go talk to them. And... see what they know about that thing."

"Say please," Sabellian said, staring at Jace.

Jace let out a long breath and held out the map. "Please take me to Blade's Edge Mountains, mighty Lord Nai'horgar."

The Black Dragon Prince took the scroll. "Really. You weak, pathetic mortals. Can't do anything without me."
