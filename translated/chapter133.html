Chapter 133: Ambush and Ambushed

Jace turned down the harpoon the goblin offered him and unwrapped the cloth from the pole he'd been carrying, taking it in hand.

The staff's wrapping was soaked through now, making it a bit heavier than before, but Jace still felt safer holding it.

Staring at the yawning hatch, there was something deeply unpleasant about walking straight into a situation where you knew something was waiting for you in the dark.

The five of them lined up single file and made their way through the passenger cabin corridors one deck below. The three volunteers took point, Grede brought up the rear, and Jace was second to last, sticking close to Grede.

The man in front was clean-shaven, shirtless, with a rope slung across his back. He had no clothing or armor whatsoever, just a long fork leveled straight ahead, every muscle in his body tensed and ready.

Jace held his small notebook—the pages already soaked—and squinted at the spell he'd written down earlier, silently mouthing the words to memorize them, in case he blanked at the critical moment and screwed everything up.

"Hey, look at this." The man in front said.

There was a goblin's corpse on the stairs leading down to the cargo hold. This goblin was one of the men who'd come down earlier to hunt the panther.

He had two deep claw marks across his back. One of them had sliced straight through his abdomen, his innards spilling out. The poor bastard had apparently been badly wounded and crawled this far before dying—just one corridor away from the hatch.

Though judging by his injuries, even if he'd crawled out, he wouldn't have made it.

The others' fates were probably no better. Either dead, or hiding God knows where—otherwise someone would've run back up to the deck to call for help.

The lead man stepped around the corpse, headed down the stairs, and poked his head in to check. Confirming there was no danger close by, he waved the others forward.

Once they'd all made it down into the hold, amid the chaotic sound of waves and rain hammering the hull, a faint murmuring drifted in from somewhere far off.

All five of them changed expressions. They'd all clearly heard it.

Grede looked toward the source of the sound and whispered, "Can anyone make out what that is?"

"Help?" The man with the ponytail, holding a fishing net and a short club, said. "I'm hearing someone shout for help. Sounds like a human."

Now that he mentioned it, Jace thought it sounded like someone shouting for help too.

But not one of them moved. Because they all knew what that sound was for.

There was no way a vicious beast hiding in the hull would let a wounded man go for no reason. It was definitely using his voice to lure others in to rescue him, then ambushing the rescuers.

There were animals on Earth that had mastered this kind of trick—let alone the beasts of Azeroth, nourished by the arcane and twice as cunning and treacherous. Everyone here was an experienced hunter. They weren't about to fall for something like that.

"I'll go check it out," Grede said. "I've got a shield. I can be bait—draw the panther into attacking me, then hold it off... you lot find a chance to grab it!"

"You sure?" Jace asked.

"Of course I'm sure. Why else would I suggest it? Enough talk, Jace." Grede muttered.

"Go ahead, dwarf," the ponytail man said. "We've got your back."

The shirtless man nodded too.

The woman with the club said, "Careful, dwarf. If it's not working, run. Don't lose your life over this."

"Don't jinx it, damn it," the dwarf said unhappily.

"Watch the crates on the sides," Jace warned.

Grede nodded, adjusted his grip on the shield, and started walking toward the source of the sound, checking every step, terrified of getting pounced on out of nowhere.

The others followed and stopped at a distance where they could launch a quick attack, watching Grede disappear into the pitch black.

The ship rolled. The rocking seemed even more violent now than it had been on deck. The woman braced against the cabin wall, eyes locked on the dwarf's movements. The ponytail man pressed himself against a wooden crate.

The shirtless man crouched on the floor against the wall, his long fork at the ready. From here they could see a vague white shape at the bottom of the hold. That sailor they'd seen earlier had been wearing a white shirt under a chainmail vest—looked like the chainmail had been torn off.

Jace was worried about Grede. He had his spells ready for every possible scenario. As for whether these people would notice he was using shadow magic—honestly, in this tense moment, he couldn't bring himself to care.

Grede was almost beside the white-clothed figure now, but the panther still hadn't shown itself.

Jace said, "We need to move closer. If he gets attacked, we won't be able to back him up in time."

"Right." The shirtless man raised himself up and crouched forward. The others followed his lead.

Jace glanced back toward the other end of the hold. Rows upon rows of crates and thoroughly soaked tarps blended together in the dim light. A flash of lightning suddenly lit up the empty walkway as white as a sheet. Jace half-expected to catch a glimpse of some monster lurking in the shadows in classic horror-movie fashion—but there was nothing there.

The ponytail man grabbed the edge of a porthole, his hair whipping in the wind. He looked back in the direction of Jace's gaze and said, "We should watch our backs too. What if it's waiting for us from behind?"

The shirtless man nodded and glanced back as well. Just then, a rumble of thunder rolled in from that last lightning strike, and Grede's voice shouted from up ahead, "This man's beyond saving! He's lost half his body! Damn it all!"

"Holy Light," the ponytail man breathed. "Someone tell me where that panther is?"

"Even the Light probably can't find him now," the shirtless man said, looking around. "Maybe we're overthinking it. The panther wounded this guy, thought he was dead, and left. This hold isn't as big as we thought—if it was in here, there's no way we wouldn't have picked up some trace of it with our experience!"

The whole hold reeked of that panther's distinctive stink, but it was hard to tell where the smell was stronger or weaker. And the cage was still sitting where it had been, unmoved. Maybe it was just the smell of the droppings inside.

The shirtless man was right. This hold wasn't that easy to hide in. Not unless the black panther could turn invisible.

But as far as Jace knew, this world didn't have that "Stealth" skill from the game—where something turned fully invisible and you only noticed a translucent shadow when it was right in front of you.

While the group hesitated over their next move, the hold suddenly lurched without warning. Jace wasn't paying attention and grabbed the cabin wall beside him.

A roar erupted right next to him. A shadow flashed and vanished. He turned to look, but the ship's rocking kept him from whipping around fast enough.

"He... hah!"

The shirtless man shouted something and slammed into the outer hull. He climbed back up and yelled, "There was something just now!"

By the time everyone reacted, the ponytail man was gone. Only his club remained on the ground!

He'd been taken?!

"It's the one with the ponytail who got taken!" The woman raised her club toward behind them. "I didn't see anything... there's nothing behind us either. I was watching the back the whole time!"

"What the hell are you talking about?" Grede ran back to the group. "He just vanished like that?"

"Didn't you see anything?" Jace asked.

"It was pitch black where I was. I was crouching down looking at that man's wounds and got knocked over by the rocking—how the hell was I supposed to see what happened over here?"

"He was taken. And fast. We're no match for this thing. It's waiting down here for us to make a mistake!" the shirtless man said. "We should go back up and wait for daylight. Damn it, this ship is too fucking big! Right now we're not the hunters—we're the prey!"

This vicious beast was more patient than they'd thought, and better at adapting on the fly.

Jace desperately wanted to let Sazeno out right now. This imp was definitely better at sensing hidden danger than any of them—after all, he had demon eyes. No matter how well that animal could hide itself, it was still a living creature, brimming with life force...

But casting a shadow spell could still be passed off as arcane magic—fool these magic-illiterate laymen. A full-on demon running around? How would he explain that?

"Can we make it back alive?" the woman asked.

"Get it together, all of you." Grede banged his club against his shield. "There's one less person to split the money now! You should be more excited, not less! Forget capturing it alive—bring everything you've got. We're killing this son of a bitch!"

"Agreed," the shirtless man said. "I agree with killing it!"

"The most important thing right now is figuring out where that black panther is," the woman said. "You're all experienced hunters, aren't you? Shouldn't you be planning how to surround it?"

"I've only ever hunted in the forests of Stranglethorn Vale. When have I ever done this on a ship?" the shirtless man said. "We definitely shouldn't split up now. If we split up, we're dead for sure!"

"Fine," the woman said. "But finding this thing's location is important too. Let's figure out what just happened."

A harsh noise came from above, like someone fighting—or more like something in the hold had fallen to the ground.

The sea wind howled like a ghost. Everyone was on edge.

The woman looked up. "Should we go up?"

"But what if it's another false alarm?" the shirtless man said helplessly.

Now the remaining four couldn't decide whether to go back up. If they went up and found nothing again, wouldn't that be like being led around by the nose by that beast?

Jace thought: if the panther wanted to pick someone off, why not go after Grede, who was alone? If it had the ability to snatch someone right out from under four people's noses, then even if it had crept right up to Grede in the dark and grabbed him while the ship rocked, the others at this distance would never have had time to react.

So what exactly was stopping it from attacking Grede, and instead going after this group?

Jace looked toward the dark corner where the corpse lay, then over to the area lit by moonlight.

The portholes. It was the portholes!
