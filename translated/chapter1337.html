Chapter 1337: Velen

Since the stronghold meeting days earlier, Jace had been wearing the same shadow robe without changing. Today he was going to meet the draenei leader — he should change into something clean and normal.

Not that anyone at Wildhammer Stronghold cared about dress etiquette. Turalyon wore the same faded light blue shirt day in and day out. The Wildhammer dwarves wore whatever they could find. Even many elves dressed casually, especially the Farstriders.

But Jace felt this occasion deserved a bit of sprucing up. Maybe a shave too. By the time he came out of the passage, Vereesa was waiting outside.

The ranger looked him up and down and said, "Last time I saw you put this much effort in was probably for my promotion ceremony."

Jace smiled a little sheepishly. "You didn't go meet the Farstriders."

"Is Lady Alleria's commendation not enough?" Vereesa brushed her silver hair out of the wind. "I thought you needed me more, Jace."

The stables at Wildhammer Stronghold had acquired several elekks. Every dwarf Jace passed was discussing the draenei who had arrived at the stronghold hall not long ago.

Though Wildhammer Stronghold didn't have as many draenei seeking refuge as Alleria's Stronghold, the soldiers here had still seen plenty of them. They shouldn't have been that surprised by a draenei's appearance. But they were all talking about the prophet's looks — tall, stern, and some rather strange descriptions.

Jace found it hard to focus on the dwarves' chatter. In the back of his mind, he kept feeling that Velen's arrival at Wildhammer Stronghold at this exact moment was almost... scripted.

Logically speaking, the Farstriders had been searching from the Zangar Sea to Blade's Edge for two or three months. They were bound to turn up something eventually. But the fact that Jace had just proposed using Auchindoun's souls as portal energy, and Velen arrived at Wildhammer Stronghold just days later, felt like some kind of reckoning.

Because Velen's title of "prophet" wasn't merely honorary. He was a sage who could genuinely see the future.

But rather than the forced intervention of a prophet, Jace preferred to think of it as a sign from the Holy Light. Or a response.

From his conversations with many priests, Jace knew the Holy Light wouldn't give you clear instructions and explanations like a person you could talk to. But it would always support you in some way — for example, by bringing someone who could help.

But if Velen's arrival really was the Holy Light's response to him... wasn't that a bit too on the nose?

He couldn't help smiling. In his previous life, he'd been something of an atheist. Even after coming to Azeroth, he'd never had much to do with the Holy Light.

He never imagined that one day he'd be interpreting supposed signs like a real zealot. He glanced at the ranger beside him and decided to drop the wild thoughts.

As he entered the stronghold hall, many people inside turned to look at him and Vereesa.

He expected to see scorn, even hatred, for having proposed that earlier plan. But it wasn't what he thought.

Even the priest who had opposed him so firmly, even the female priest who'd left early — they were all here. Their gazes held no hostility. There was even a hint of apology.

Four draenei stood beside Turalyon. Their height and skin color were impossible to miss, even in the dim firelight. Jace could see them clearly as soon as he entered.

The elderly draenei in the middle was the tallest. Jace felt he might be even taller than Livius — practically a giant.

Now Jace understood why every soldier in Wildhammer Stronghold was muttering about his appearance. This old man looked more like a fearsome Eredar lord than a typical draenei. Even Bartas seemed almost normal by comparison.

Yet despite this, he inspired no hostility or wariness.

His hair and beard were pure white, the thick long beard mingling with four sturdy tendrils hanging from his chin. His grey-white robe wasn't complicated in style — much simpler than the ornate priestly vestments he wore in the game. More like a refugee's clothes.

But the magnificent purple crystal staff in his hand couldn't lie. This was the Prophet Velen.

Of course, Jace habitually used his demon-detection sight to take a careful look. Kil'jaeden had disguised himself as an illusion to deceive people more than once.

But the light emanating from Velen made him blink slightly, reminding him of those glaring symbols in the Cathedral. At the same time, Velen seemed to sense something, looked up, and met his eyes.

"Jace Sesso."

Jace was a bit startled. "How do you know my name?"

"Marshal Turalyon told me, of course." Velen smiled. The cracked skin on his face looked like purple bark, especially when he smiled.

Jace relaxed slightly. For a moment he'd thought Velen had foreseen even his name and appearance. After seeing Yorin Deathgaze's abilities, Jace wasn't about to let his guard down around this kind of foresight.

"I'm Jace Sesso," Jace said. "An honor to meet you, Prophet."

"Velen." The draenei leader said. "Likewise, Jace Sesso."

"We were discussing the situation with the Scepter of Sargeras, Jace," Turalyon said. "The draenei also know the scepter is on Draenor. And they've confirmed it's not in Terokkar or Blade's Edge..."

"Then combining our intelligence, there's only one possibility left," Alleria said. "The Throne of Kil'jaeden."

Though this was intelligence Jace and Sabellian had brought back from Hellfire Citadel, the Black Dragon Prince was silent, staring at the map on the table, not participating in the discussion at all.

The Expedition leaders and several draenei vindicators discussed ways to use Wildhammer Stronghold's movements to draw the Burning Legion's attention. Kurdran Wildhammer suggested having Honor Hold draw a group of demon defenders down from the Throne of Kil'jaeden to reinforce Hellfire Citadel. Meanwhile, an elite squad would head for the Throne of Kil'jaeden to seize the Scepter of Sargeras directly from the demons.

When talk turned to seizing the scepter, the Expedition officers all agreed Jace should go. They believed he was the only one capable of wielding it — none of them doubted him because of the Auchindoun matter.

Jace heard his name mentioned more and more frequently. He looked up at the people in the hall, then realized that the only ones who hadn't spoken the whole time were himself, the Black Dragon Prince, and the Prophet himself.

Then the Prophet said, "I'd like to speak with Jace Sesso alone, everyone."

"Of course, Prophet," Turalyon said.

Everyone in the Expedition had heard that the draenei leader was an elder who had lived for tens of thousands of years, who had once refused the Dark Titan's personal offer... So even though many were meeting him for the first time, they were willing to give him due respect.

But after Turalyon spoke, he gave Jace a pointed look, which made Jace feel a bit guilty. So it really is a reckoning after all?

The three draenei vindicators exchanged a few quiet words with Velen, then left the hall with the others. Grede sighed and came up to Jace, whispering, "Don't bring up that matter, Jace."

"I know," Jace said.

Once the hall was quiet, the towering figure of Prophet Velen cast an imposing presence that even Jace, a human standing nearly six-foot-four, found somewhat intimidating.

And not just him — even the raging storms that swept across all of Shadowmoon Valley seemed to quiet down outside, as if afraid to disturb this Holy Light follower's peace.

"My apologies," Velen began in very gentle Eredun. "I had hoped to form an alliance with the Expedition long ago. But it took me a very long time to finally arrive here. The reason isn't just difficulty of travel, or the orc and demon hunts."

"You've arrived in good time, Prophet. I was worried you might have run into trouble." Jace replied in the same Eredun.

He knew his Eredun had a distinctly demonic flavor, but since Velen had initiated the conversation in this language, he wouldn't worry about such details.

"Then what was the reason?"

"The timing was wrong."

"Timing?"

"Kil'jaeden had cast an inescapable net. Even the Naaru stopped answering our calls." The aged draenei prophet looked down at Jace. "At that time, I could see no future, receive no guidance. So I could only gather whatever survivors I could find and keep them as far from the war as possible, using illusions and magic to evade demon pursuit. In the hardest moments, I thought the end had come — that our long wandering would finally end here. Until..."

"You found a way out of here?" Jace looked up.

"I'm sorry to say, I am powerless against his magic, Jace Sesso. You are the one who found the way." Velen said.

"I?" Jace hesitated. "Forgive me, Prophet, but I'm not sure I understand. How do you... well, if you're referring to the Scepter of Sargeras. Once we take it, we can break Kil'jaeden's magic and bring everyone out of Draenor. Just as you and your people originally hoped. Archmage Khadgar thinks so too. You should know him. We've already discussed this."

"But you don't agree with that plan." Velen said. "You know what taking up the Scepter of Sargeras means."

Jace looked at him in surprise. "Did Marshal Turalyon tell you that too?"

Velen said nothing, as if Jace hadn't finished speaking.

Jace instinctively looked back toward the infirmary, but all he saw was a stone wall built by Wildhammer dwarves.

He took a deep breath, organizing his words. "Alright. I'm planning to build a portal. Disguise it as a fel channel to fool Kil'jaeden's magic and take everyone out. But the portal on this side needs enough energy. To some extent, it needs power comparable to those void passages."

He watched Velen's reaction, then added, "Right now, only Auchindoun can provide that."

Hearing Jace's words, Velen's inner emotions sank.

He didn't hide his sadness. Even the floating arcane sigil on his forehead trembled slightly with it. After a long pause, he said quietly, "But you didn't do it."

Jace sighed. "I'm sorry, Prophet. I think I lost my way for a moment."

"Orcs, demons, void travelers from other worlds..." Velen said. "Many have wanted to turn the souls in Auchindoun into their own power. Even some of our finest."

"They refused Sargeras's gift. They saw through Kil'jaeden's schemes. Some even had noble intentions. And yet they still fell into it."

"But it seems none of them ever asked what my departed kin and brave warriors actually wanted."

That's right. Why not ask the souls themselves for help?

Jace looked up. "Why are you willing to help me, Prophet?"

"Before deciding to come here, I saw a vision," Velen said. "A young human sitting in a hall bathed in light, yet shrouded in deep shadow. I often see all kinds of visions, and it's hard to tell which are real and which are not. But this time, I wanted to try to part that haze."

Jace was completely stunned. This draenei had ridden here on an elekk, led by the Farstriders.

From near the Zangar Sea, north of Shattrath City, the journey would take at least ten days. If he'd started from Blade's Edge, probably close to a month. But wasn't he describing what happened just this morning?

"A dear friend once told me that perhaps we too could try to listen for voices from that world, like the wise ones of the tribal clans. Especially in the darkest of times." Velen said. "Go to the Tomb of Light, Jace Sesso. Ask those spirits yourself. Seek your answer."
