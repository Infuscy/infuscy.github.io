## Chapter 1338: Vengeful Spirits

The grey-white wasteland was silent.

Ever since the black dragons allied with the Expedition, the sharp howls that used to erupt frequently were gone. Now only the faint, low rumble of the sound elemental echoed around Auchindoun.

The Tomb of Light that Velen had mentioned stood before them. It was clear that before the Auchindoun explosion, this had been a rather magnificent and delicate crystal sanctuary. But now only a dark grey ruined wall remained, its entrance buried under rubble, pitch black within.

"Are you sure this is a good idea, Jace?" Grede stood beside him, peering warily inside. "Are we really going to talk to these ghosts?"

"Are you saying we should give up on going home just because you're afraid of ghosts?" Vereesa asked.

"I didn't say that." Grede took a breath. "Ghosts it is then. As long as no zombies or ghouls pop out."

"It's raining." Sola looked up.

Vereesa held out her hand, rubbing her fingers together. "It's like even the weather is pushing us inside."

"A sign from the Holy Light. A draenei priest, a prophet..." Grede said. "Now this. I swear Draenor's turning into one giant Cathedral of Light."

"Morophil. You and Sazeno stay here," Jace said. "We'll go in and have a look."

"What," the succubus glanced at him, "are you trying to hide your true nature from these Eredar souls, master?"

"Alright, you're right." Jace took a deep breath. "We'll all go in together."

"I was going to say I'd stay out here and keep watch with these two demons," Grede said.

"What's there to keep watch for?" Vereesa said. "Even the black dragons are on our side now. There's nothing dangerous left in the Bone Wastes."

"Fine, fine. Let's go in!" Grede grumbled.

One by one, they descended the crypt steps, passed collapsed pillars and rubble into the lightless dark.

Sola raised the Dominance Staff with one hand, summoning a gently flickering moonlight that cast a faint glow through the grey-blue corridor.

Vereesa took point, Jace beside her, examining the aged passage as they walked. Looking at the marks clearly made by weapons, he guessed that before the Auchindoun explosion, this place had already suffered through rounds of fighting between orcs and draenei defenders.

The passage wasn't complex — no forks — but it was maddeningly long. Especially as they went deeper, the space ahead and behind was completely swallowed by darkness. The occasional dried draenei and orc corpses only heightened the atmosphere.

Grede looked ahead, then behind. No matter where he looked, he couldn't see clearly. Even his expression had started to look wrong.

He had reason to be cautious. Many of Auchindoun's souls had been tormented beyond reason by endless orcs, demons, and maddened draenei. Some pretended nothing had happened and lived as before. Some wandered aimlessly through the ruins. Others attacked any living thing they saw like wild beasts.

Considering the strength of the draenei souls the orcs had made into soul shards, Jace certainly wasn't about to relax.

Grede wasn't the only one on edge. Even Morophil was tense. Though the demon was now the tallest in the group, her steps were extremely cautious, her goat-like hooves making almost no sound against the stone floor.

Jace couldn't tell if she was wary of possible undead attacks or just uncomfortable entering this temple of the Holy Light.

Just as Grede seemed about to lose patience, he took a deep breath. Suddenly, Sola's moonlight revealed the shape of a doorway ahead, filled with even deeper blackness.

"Should we go in, Jace?" Sola asked.

"Uh..." Sazeno tried to peer inside, but clearly the fel flame on its head couldn't illuminate anything, and it quickly pulled back.

"Let's go in," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded and stepped through first with Sola. Jace, Grede, and Morophil followed close behind.

But as they entered, it was like stepping into a pitch-black, empty night sky. As the doorway receded into the darkness behind them, everything around seemed to vanish completely. Only the mottled, dusty floor tiles beneath their feet — faintly lit by Sola's moonlight — reminded everyone they were still in the real world.

"This reminds me of those nights on the ship to Kalimdor..." Vereesa's voice was startlingly clear. "Do you remember, Jace?"

"I remember," Jace said. "But it's much quieter here."

"Can you make it a bit brighter, Sola?" Grede whispered.

"I'll try," Sola said.

The green dragon closed her eyes. The moonlight above the Dominance Staff began to grow brighter, the pale silver glow pushing back the darkness, revealing more of the hall ahead.

Torn blue curtains, broken chandeliers, rune-etched stained-glass windows blooming like violet flowers, dull draenei crystals scattered everywhere.

Two broken, towering stone statues of draenei sages sat not far ahead, staring straight forward as if whoever came here didn't matter at all.

"And the draenei souls?" the elf asked. "Don't they mind us disturbing their peace?"

"Peace?" Sola sighed softly.

"Maybe we shouldn't have brought these two demons in. The souls probably got scared off," Grede said.

"The souls are here, Lord Grede," Morophil said. "We just can't see them."

"Should we have brought some Ghost Detection Potion?" Grede asked.

"Ghost Detection Potion doesn't mean the ghosts are willing to talk to you," Sola said.

Jace picked up a lantern from the floor. "Let me handle the light, Sola. Thanks."

"Alright, Jace." Sola nodded and lowered her staff. The moonlight faded, and darkness returned. With a murmured elemental word from Jace, a blazing flame ignited in the lantern, casting light once more.

The flame danced. As it flickered, casting Jace and the others' shadows in crisscrossing motion, pale blue figures emerged from the darkness.

They were tall draenei — very similar to those Jace had seen wandering and murmuring through Auchindoun with ghost detection potion. But these seemed more steady in both gaze and awareness.

Grede swallowed, his eyes darting around at the figures. One hand reached for his hammer, but in the end, he only gripped his belt. Vereesa, Sola, Morophil, and Jace found themselves back-to-back, clustered together. They were surrounded by these spirits.

Then a burly draenei stepped out from among the figures. The two horns on his head were practically demonic. His black beard was clearly visible even in spirit form.

He looked like a warrior, wearing armor marked with the Auchenai crest, carrying a heavy three-sided hammer. He fixed his unfocused spectral eyes roughly in their direction.

He radiated burning anger and intense hatred, like countless other draenei souls here — decades of shadow and fel torment had changed them beyond what many former guardians remembered.

"It's strange that fire would trust a demon summoner like you, human." He spoke in fairly standard Common, then suddenly barked, "State your purpose. Give me one reason not to hang you in the Necrotic Depths right now!"

"Honorable Priest..." Jace started, but the draenei cut him off.

"I am the Primate of Auchindoun, mortal. If you intend to defile this holy tomb, you'd best understand who you're dealing with."

*The Primate of Auchindoun is now Maladaar, and he's become the leader of the necromantic priests.* Jace realized he was likely facing the former Primate of Auchindoun... So this was why Velen had sent him to the Tomb of Light instead of directly into Auchindoun to find the souls.

"Primate, Kil'jaeden is on the verge of victory," Jace said. "Soon, every surviving draenei — every living soul still resisting the Burning Legion on this shattered world — will die. Only Auchindoun, only you, can save them all."

"We know." Another draenei spirit chuckled coldly. "You want to use our power to fight the Burning Legion..."

"I only need to open a single portal." Jace looked at the surrounding spirits. "A portal in the heart of Auchindoun, to bring the Expedition and the surviving draenei out of this world. I ask for nothing else."

"The living... the living..." An enraged draenei spirit howled hysterically. "I don't care about them at all!"

"We only want vengeance." Another draenei spirit shrieked almost simultaneously.

"We want to retake the holy tomb, human." The Primate stared at Jace. "Kill Vorpil, the leader of the Shadow Council. Kill Maladaar, the necromancer who calls himself Primate but defiles this holy tomb. Then we will help you hold open that portal."

"That doesn't sound complicated," Grede said, swallowing.

Vereesa took a deep breath and glanced sideways at Jace. Her eyes held some hesitation. It truly didn't sound complicated — but they still needed the Shadow Council orcs to help quietly build the portal. That meant they couldn't conflict with Vorpil at the start... Still, this was already a relatively easy condition to meet. The specifics could be worked out.

"I kill the Shadow Council's Vorpil and the Primate Maladaar. Then you help me open the portal." Jace asked tentatively, "Is that a deal?"

"Yes. A deal." The Primate lifted his head. "But don't walk their path, human. Don't follow their old road. Or the Holy Light will curse you forever. And so will we."
