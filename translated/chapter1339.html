Chapter 1339: At Least We Can Share the Burden

The holy tomb of Auchindoun and its nearby crypts hadn't always been this gloomy. The spirits within hadn't always been this violent.

Though Jace hadn't expected a friendly and cordial conversation — especially with two demons in tow. But he hadn't anticipated that these dead draenei would harbor such hatred and resentment, comparable to the savage emotions of the vengeful spirits around Karazhan's villages.

Auchindoun had stood for centuries. The spirits within were the best of the draenei who had died there over those centuries. But Gul'dan and Teron Gorefiend's dark magic was worse... decades of twisted torment had long since changed them from what they once were.

That was why Gul'dan, the Burning Legion's demons, and Illidan Stormrage had all been easily drawn to the soul energy here. And now, Jace was included in that list.

When they emerged from the tomb, the rain outside had grown heavier than before. Grede held his small round shield with the red trim over his head. "We must be cursed. Every time we come out of a tomb, it pours."

"When was the last time?" Sola asked.

"Don't tell me you've forgotten already?" the dwarf looked at her. "That troll temple!"

"But that wasn't a tomb," the green dragon emphasized.

"It was ten thousand times more rancid than this actual tomb though." Grede grumbled. "We need to find somewhere to get out of this rain."

"We could go back into the passageway," Jace said.

"No." Grede looked around, finally pointing at a ruined draenei building in the dusty haze. "Over there."

The building was much farther away than it looked. By the time they'd sprinted over and ducked under the eaves, everyone was soaked, Jace especially.

"What happened to that fancy new red cloak of yours?" Grede flicked water from his own cloak, looking at Jace. "The day you came back from Hellfire Citadel with that black dragon prince, I saw you wearing it."

"What red cloak?" Sola asked. "I didn't see it."

"You were probably busy in the infirmary then," Grede said. "That knight squad that ran into the Bonereaver patrol..."

"It was a cloak the black dragon gave me. Made me look more like a fel orc officer, better for infiltrating Hellfire Citadel," Jace said, looking at Sola.

Sola nodded. Morophil stood beside them with an expression that was half smile, half something else, watching Jace. Vereesa looked a bit puzzled by the master and servant.

Jace still hadn't figured out how to explain to Sola what that cloak really was, so he'd simply not worn it today. Who knew he'd be unlucky enough to run into rain in a godforsaken place like the Bone Wastes?

"You didn't really have to come along and suffer through this with me," he said, squeezing water from his hair. "It wasn't dangerous, right?"

"Turns out you still need us around to keep an eye on you." Grede sat on the broken section of a stone pillar. "Look what harebrained schemes you started cooking up after two weeks with that black dragon."

Sola frowned at Grede, signaling him to drop it. Vereesa sighed in mild annoyance too. Jace leaned against the wall, silent.

"Gul'dan wielded some truly evil necromantic magic, Jace," Grede said. "The Expedition knows... Those berserk souls in the troll temple who willingly sacrificed themselves for the blood god Hakkar were one thing. The ghosts here are just as scary, sure, but they used to be respected by countless draenei. I don't exactly like draenei — especially that prophet who looks like a demon — but I have to admit they're different from those troll souls."

"Enough, Grede," the ranger said. "Jace only made a suggestion. He's just trying to bring everyone home."

"I know." The dwarf let out a long sigh and slapped his hand on his knee. "You said it by the River Neltharion, Jace. If you ever really fell and became a mad wizard like Mord'gul, I was supposed to kill you."

"Even back then I knew I couldn't do it. Even less now. Truth is, I don't even know how close you are to that line."

"But I do know that if you did it, it wouldn't just affect you alone. The Expedition would split immediately over your decision — the Quel'Thalas elven mages are already imagining how many souls they'd need to feed into an Auchindoun portal, and the Kirin Tor people look like they're ready to compromise too. Marshal Turalyon's been frowning every day because he knows your dilemma, he knows the whole thing..."

Now even Vereesa was quiet. Sola bowed her head too. Jace knew Grede was telling the truth.

The dwarf looked at him. "Whether you want to admit it or not, we're not those two mercenaries scraping for whatever work we could find anymore."

Jace nodded. "I know, Grede."

The dwarf tossed a stone into the curtain of rain. "Does it make more sense now why we came along?"

"It does," Jace said.

"But I didn't come to keep watch on anyone," Vereesa said.

"Neither did I." Sola looked at Grede. "I always knew Jace would make the right decision."

"Then who was it who was crying alone in her room the other night?"

Caught off guard by Grede's question, the green dragon flustered. Jace said, "Sorry, Sola. For letting you down."

"No. I've never been disappointed." Sola said quietly. "I just didn't think you should have to bear all this alone, Jace."

"Soul link!" Sazeno suddenly shrieked. "Whatever my master suffers, this she-demon takes half. That's the wonderful magic of the Eredar!"

"Pity it doesn't cover such intense affection." From the shadows deeper in the ruins, Morophil's voice came faintly.

"Morophil..." Sola laughed and shook her head. Grede looked like he was about to throw up.

Vereesa shot the demon an annoyed glance, but in the end couldn't help laughing along.

The atmosphere suddenly lightened. The three of them and the two demons talked with Sola about their earlier adventures in Kalimdor, stories of sheltering from rain on the vast plains.

The rain over the Bone Wastes went on for a long time without stopping. But no one complained or grew impatient — it was as if everyone was glad for the excuse to just talk here. Even the two demons.

"Do you know how those spirits will help us, Jace?" Vereesa asked, looking out at the grey wasteland.

"Truthfully? I'm not sure either," Jace said.

Sazeno clung to the strap of the bag at Jace's waist with both hands. "The Eredar understand the Twisting Nether. They understand teleportation magic. Even in death, as long as they haven't gone mad, they should know what they're talking about, my master."

"And how much sanity do you think they have left, Mr. Sazeno?" Sola asked. The imp opened its mouth, but ultimately couldn't answer.

"At least they still remember who their enemies are. They know the Expedition can help them," Vereesa said. "Even if they're mad, they're not completely gone."

Jace nodded. He understood what those spirits had meant by specifically warning him not to follow Gul'dan or Teron Gorefiend's old path...

Draining souls. The method he and Ursaleth were best at — turning souls into personal power.

So once he fulfilled his pact with that former Primate of Auchindoun, the spirits in the holy tomb would use their own method to activate the portal?

It felt unreliable to Jace, but he was willing to make this gamble. Just as Sabellian was willing to gamble on him being able to destroy the Scepter of Sargeras for a sliver of hope that his clan could survive.

And where else in all of Draenor was there enough power? More importantly, where would that power be as willing to help the Expedition and the draenei refugees take this final step?

Nowhere. This was almost the last option. And if not for Velen's reminder and tacit permission, who knew when he would have broken free from this cloying craving for dark power?

Still, it did let Jace breathe a little easier. He had never personally manipulated dead souls to activate a portal before. This wasn't some ordinary magic item you could activate with your mind, nor a small shadow barrier you could trigger by calming your emotions.

"We just need to do what we need to do," Jace said. "Hopefully the dead draenei have their own way."

"I wouldn't claim to know much about portal magic," Vereesa said. "But I know we can't just march into Auchindoun for this 'hunt' mission."

"Right. We still need those Shadow Council orcs to help build the portal," Jace said. "The Expedition could probably spare an elite force to take Auchindoun by storm, but it'd be hard to guarantee the Burning Legion wouldn't notice. Don't forget — there are Burning Legion demons inside the Shadow Council too."

"So in the end, you still need to ask that black dragon prince for help," Grede said. "Get him to turn you into a fel orc again?"

"He's the only one who can do it now," Jace said. "I'll try to infiltrate the Shadow Council, help the orcs deal with the Primate Maladaar. With us and the black dragons helping, the Shadow Council shouldn't have too much trouble defeating those fallen Auchenai priests. Then once they've built the portal and expended their strength, while they're waiting for that demon lord to arrive, I'll take the chance to kill Vorpil and fulfill the condition they want me to meet."

"Don't take Prince Sabellian's advice lightly, Jace." Sola looked at him with concern. "That day in the stronghold hall..."

"...He already proved that even freed from Azeroth's influence, those dark things have already become part of his nature. He won't hesitate to enslave and sacrifice others."

"Don't worry, Sola." Jace said. "If he still wants to cooperate with us, it'll be on our terms."

A deep rumble came from deep in the Bone Wastes in the distance, drawing everyone's attention.

"We almost forgot about this thing," Vereesa said.

Murmur.

This summoned sound elemental lord was still bound by the Shadow Council's magic.

Jace didn't believe it would destroy all of Draenor, but a portal couldn't tolerate any deviation. To stabilize the passage from Telogrus to Auchindoun, they had to consider this potential trouble.

On the way back to Wildhammer Stronghold, no one said much more. The stipulations of the spectral Primate hadn't brought much joy at finding a way out.

At this moment, as he calmed down and reconsidered the entire portal process, the thrill of solving a perfect equation was completely gone.

In front of him was only a single strand of spider silk stretched across a chasm, ready to snap at any moment in the howling wind. On one end was tied the hope of the entire Expedition. And the other end was pure chaos.
