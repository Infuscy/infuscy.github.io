Chapter 134: The Cornered Beast

"The panther's outside!" Jace shouted.

Hearing that, the shirtless man immediately climbed onto a porthole and looked out. Jace squeezed through a porthole too, looking up.

Through the dense curtain of rain, a figure leaned halfway out of a porthole above, locked in a physical struggle with a much larger dark shadow!

They were roaring furiously, but the sound was swallowed by the storm, the waves, and the rain. Inside the ship, it just blended in with the creaking and groaning of the whole vessel. You could only hear it faintly if you stuck your head out the porthole.

"It's the one with the ponytail!" the shirtless man shouted.

"You three hold this position! I'm going up and have a go at it!" Grede didn't wait for the others' approval before charging up the stairs. The woman swore under her breath and followed him up. The shirtless man moved to a few different portholes, getting as close as he could to where the ponytail man was fighting, and thrust his harpoon out, trying to stab the panther from below.

Jace watched from this side, locked onto the vague dark shadow. He was just about to speak the Curse of Weakness when the ponytail man suddenly got pounded back into the ship by the panther.

"Shit!"

He cursed under his breath and immediately scrambled back into the hold, sprinting up the stairs. As he climbed, he silently rehearsed the spell to lock it in. Three steps at a time, he charged up to the deck above, just in time to hear Grede roar.

"You yellow-eyed beast! Aaargh!"

The beast let out a savage roar from the next cabin over, sending a shiver straight through Jace's core.

He dove into the passenger cabin and saw Grede slamming his shield into the panther's shoulder. The pointed end of the shield was devastatingly powerful—if Grede put his strength behind a blow like that to a human's face, it probably would've crushed the skull on impact.

But the panther only whimpered and ducked its whole body, slamming hard into the cabin wall. Grede hastily jumped off its back and hit the ground. If he hadn't jumped, he would have been crushed flat.

"Look over here!" The woman leveled her club at the panther. The panther's eyes snapped to her. It snarled and lunged. The woman tried to thrust her club forward, but the panther swatted it aside with one paw. Its other paw swung in a rapid succession of blows that caught her square in the waist... The woman cried out and toppled onto her side. Through gritted teeth she raised her club to fend off the panther's biting. The panther gnawed wildly, about to snap the club in half—when it suddenly noticed Jace, who'd just come charging in.

Jace fixed his gaze on the panther's strange yellow eyes. Calling it a panther was one thing, but this thing was built like a full-grown Siberian tiger. Not only was it solid muscle, it was noticeably more agile—darting back and forth as nimbly as a civet. Its owner must have spent a fortune feeding and training it.

Jace was just about to speak the Curse of Weakness when the panther released the woman and shifted its position, swiping a jar off a nearby crate with blinding speed.

The jar flew straight at Jace's head. A hand grabbed his shoulder and yanked him to the side just in time to dodge it. The shirtless man had rushed up and helped him avoid the throw—otherwise his head would have been cracked open.

This panther clearly had experience dealing with mages. The moment it saw someone casting, it prioritized the attack.

The panther locked onto Jace and was about to pounce when Grede suddenly leaped onto its back, raising his shield to deliver a heavy blow to its spine. But the panther shook its whole body, throwing Grede off and onto the ground. The panther twisted its head to bite at Grede's neck. Grede raised his shield to block. The panther bit the edge of the shield and whipped its neck, flinging the nearly-two-hundred-pound dwarf warrior clean through the air—straight at the two of them!

Jace nimbly rolled to the side. The shirtless man was a beat too slow and got slammed hard against the wall by the dwarf, sliding down with a thud.

Jace pulled himself up from the wet, slippery floor and looked back. Grede's shield had several deep claw marks gouged into it, the edges already cracked and split. That fall had been no joke. The dwarf lay on the ground, clutching his waist and groaning.

The shirtless man was holding his head, mumbling something—he'd been hit hard too.

The fate of every ranged fighter: once your teammates are all dead, you're left alone to face an enemy the whole team should've handled together. It worked that way in games, and reality turned out to be exactly the same.

But in reality, you couldn't wipe and respawn and try again. There was no Feign Death, no Divine Intervention. You just fought to the end.

That dark shadow was coughing violently, head hanging low. It shook its head and looked up, two terrifying will-o'-the-wisp eyes locking onto Jace.

Then it's a duel, then. A flash of lightning lit up the entire cabin, and Jace got a clear look at its full coat. The monster's muscles were twisted together like bundles of rebar. A pair of fangs jutted out like a saber-toothed tiger, long and razor-sharp. Layer upon layer of scars covered its body, so thick that its black fur wasn't even pure anymore.

It launched like a cannonball!

Jace didn't flinch in the slightest. He shouted his spell and raised his staff, pointing straight at the charging panther.

"Alinizhitudur zala, kana!"

The panther roared at the top of its lungs but didn't slow down in the slightest—it crashed straight in!

The Curse of Weakness hadn't worked?!

Jace quickly threw himself to the side, but the panther's shoulder still caught his arm. He spun a full circle and slammed into the cabin wall, seeing stars.

The panther hadn't lost its fighting strength. If he didn't get up, he was done for. Jace forced himself to his feet, then looked back at that panther—and the thing had slammed straight into the wall itself. Probably even dizzier than he was.

The beast might not have been knocked out, but it couldn't get up immediately either. Its four legs struggled against the floor for a while, gouging long claw marks into the wood without managing to stand.

The Curse of Weakness must have worked—it's just that the thing had been charging so fast that even a sudden loss of strength couldn't do much to slow its momentum.

Just when Jace thought the thing didn't have the strength to get up anymore, the panther suddenly let out a low growl and whipped a paw at him with terrifying speed. Jace reacted and backed up immediately, but still got grazed. He sat down hard on the floor.

The panther struggled to stand, trying to take another swipe. Its paw had already swung out when something caught it—the panther roared in frustration and the paw swiped at empty air, stirring up a wind right in front of Jace's eyes.

The panther turned its head. Grede had grabbed its tail and was yelling as he backed up. The two were locked in a tug-of-war!

The panther really was under the Curse of Weakness's effect. Its feet slipped and it whimpered as Grede dragged it away by force.

The panther twisted its body, going for Grede. Jace shouted to the shirtless man, "The net! Use the net!"

He pointed to the ponytail man, who lay unconscious by the porthole, covered in blood. The net was still beside him. The shirtless man yelled and ran over to grab it, then rushed back to where Grede was grappling with the panther and threw the net over it.

"Don't you damn well get me caught in there with—"

Grede yelled and scrambled clear at the same time. The panther tried to pounce again but was already caught in the net, snarling and thrashing wildly.

Grede scrambled up and grabbed the net with the shirtless man, pulling it tight. Jace threw himself over to help hold it. After a short struggle, the panther was trussed up good and tight.

The panther let out a feeble growl—but that final cry trailed off into a whimper. It didn't match this monster's scarred, vicious face at all.

"This net won't hold him forever," Grede panted out in bursts. "Think about it—that cage, he broke the lock! He's got no strength now, but later... who knows. I'm going up to call for help! You two keep watch here! Jace, you alright?"

Jace shook his head. Grede nodded and ran up. Only then did he remember he'd just taken a swipe himself.

He immediately felt around where he'd been hit—his waist, his chest, his legs. No blood, no wounds. Just someone else's blood smeared on him. No injuries, no pain.

Probably because the panther had already been hit by the Curse of Weakness, so even that wild swipe didn't have much force behind it.

Or more likely, because of that cloak.

He pulled the cloak around and looked at the outside. Two white claw marks stood out. So this cloak had blocked the blow for him!

Larsen really hadn't lied to him. This cloak was damn tough. Even a monster like a Smokefire dragon—even a dragon sailcloth wouldn't be torn open by a casual panther swipe.
