Chapter 1340: I Did Promise

When they arrived at Wildhammer Stronghold's walls, Turalyon was uncharacteristically not out on patrol. He was standing on the battlements, looking in their direction.

When the green dragon descended, he didn't dodge the fierce wind kicked up by its wings like the other soldiers. Instead, he walked straight into the draft.

"The ethereal technicians have arrived at our stronghold, everyone!" Turalyon shouted.

"Where are they?" Grede jumped off the dragon's back and asked.

"In the reception rooms next to the main hall, Lord Grede," Turalyon said. "But don't treat it like visiting the Stratholme zoo. They can sense that kind of disrespect."

"I have not a shred of disrespect in my heart, Marshal." Grede straightened his cloak. "I'll go have a look. Meet you in the hall later, Jace."

"Sure." Jace nodded. After the dwarf ran down from the wall, Turalyon looked at him and asked, "How did it go?"

The paladin's tone wasn't asking about the results of the visit — it sounded more like concern. He continued, "Back when we first arrived in Draenor, to quickly break through the orc defenses and stop them from using the artifacts stolen by black dragons and death knights, Khadgar and many Kirin Tor mages came up with some ideas that were... somewhat 'blasphemous,' Jace."

"When facing seemingly hopeless situations, I often have ideas too. Some born of anger, some of anxiety. I think I understand how you felt that day."

"So even if things didn't go well, it's fine. Alleria and I, and everyone at Wildhammer Stronghold, will help you. Whether it's finding another way, or fighting to the end. I think Vereesa feels the same."

"Of course, Marshal." Vereesa said. "And things didn't go poorly."

"The spirits willingly sacrificed themselves?" Turalyon asked in surprise.

"They're not willing to sacrifice," Jace said. "But they did propose a trade. The spirit leader — a Primate who died many years ago — wants me to clean out the Shadow Council and the traitorous draenei priests in Auchindoun in exchange for their help."

"But that means our attack will be detected by the demons." Turalyon said. "There are Burning Legion demon underlings in Auchindoun. They might report our movements to Magtheridon through some ritual. I'm worried Kil'jaeden might figure out our intentions."

"So I still want to follow the original plan," Jace said. "Infiltrate the Shadow Council cult."

"Your original plan had Khadgar leading Dalaran's mages to support you in wiping out the Shadow Council orcs. But if you have to show your hand before opening the portal this time, it means you'll have to face them alone..." Turalyon said. "Are you sure about this, Jace?"

"I definitely can't go alone. That's too risky," Jace said. "I'll go ask Prince Sabellian."

At that moment, Sola — who had just shifted back into human form — walked up. "Let me go with you this time, Jace."

"Don't worry about me being discovered. I've gotten quite good at playing an orc guard. I've learned orcish. Even in Highmaul, the Burning Legion demon guards didn't detect my disguise. One extra person is one extra layer of insurance, Jace. Don't forget — deep in that maze, a powerful alien elemental is still sealed."

Jace knew Sola was more worried about Sabellian than about elemental lords or orc guards. And her concern was justified, so he nodded in agreement.

Besides, Sola had the strongest crowd control ability among all of them — sleep breath. Having her help would indeed be much more reliable.

"I have to say," Jace half-joked to the paladin, "sometimes asking the Holy Light for help actually works."

"Khadgar said the same thing." Turalyon smiled. "Going to talk to the ethereals next about transmitting messages to Azeroth?"

"I want to check Prince Sabellian's thoughts first." Jace asked. "Is he still in the stronghold?"

Turalyon pointed to a slope next to the stronghold. "In the cave up there."

Jace nodded, said goodbye to Vereesa and Sola, and quickly walked down from the wall.

The black dragons and the few surviving red dragons lived on the mountain peak behind Wildhammer Stronghold. As he passed the red dragons' area, they all bowed their heads to Jace, murmuring "Blood of the Dragonqueen" in draconic.

But they lived far from the black dragons. Even though they were now comrades-in-arms, and the black dragons of Draenor were vastly different from those of Azeroth, it couldn't erase the ten-thousand-year rift between the two flights.

Leaving the red dragons' low cliff, Jace passed through a stretch of charred dead woods and arrived at a cave guarded by a massive black-brown whelp. Inside, the mixed stench of magma and shadow wafted out.

Though the black dragons hadn't been living here long, the place already seemed burned by their power into another miniature Obsidian Sanctum.

Sabellian sat lounging on a dark stone throne carved with dragon claws at the back of the cave. He looked at Jace like a duke receiving a vassal.

"Why don't you rest in your true form here?" Jace said.

"Same as you. I like to make simple things extremely complicated and deliberately cause trouble for myself." Sabellian tilted his head. "Do you know why gazelles on the Kalimdor plains often leap back and forth, slowing themselves down when fleeing a lioness?"

"Showing off stamina," Jace said. "Making the pursuer give up."

"Something like that. I'm not one of Ysera's children, so I can't know what those beasts are thinking." Sabellian said. "But at least you can hold a conversation, so I'm curious what's going on. What prophecy did that so-called prophet give you that made you rush out of Wildhammer Stronghold so urgently?"

"We went to Auchindoun," Jace said. "Talked to the souls there."

"How courteous of you." The black dragon chuckled. "Gralorio said the souls in Auchindoun have all gone mad by now. But maybe that means they have common ground with you."

Jace said, "They want me to help them get revenge. Get rid of the Shadow Council leader and the Auchenai leader who defiled the souls."

Hearing this, Sabellian couldn't help laughing loudly. He pointed a finger with a sharp nail at Jace. "So in the end, we've come full circle. You're still going to sacrifice those souls to the portal. I don't understand..."

"I'm not going to sacrifice them. The souls have their own method," Jace said.

"What's the difference?" the black dragon asked with a smile.

"I don't expect you to understand our choice," Jace said.

"I really don't. All I see is a talented, powerful spellcaster being constantly tied down by meaningless, time-wasting, laughable reasons." Sabellian leaned forward, his face fierce. "You suspect the Scepter of Sargeras is Kil'jaeden's bait... Fair enough, I admit that. But those twisted souls in Auchindoun, already corrupted by demons — how are they worth all your hesitation?"

"Think about you and your brother." Jace kept his voice as calm as he could. "Nefarian did what he did for your flight too. You did what you did for your flight's survival too. But you disagreed with his methods. You risked fighting the remaining demons and gronn to stay in Draenor no matter what. Everything the black dragons have done since leaving Azeroth hasn't been for nothing."

As Jace spoke, the smile slowly faded from Sabellian's face. His black fingers tapped restlessly on the dragon-scale-carved armrest. He took a deep breath, the fire in his eyes growing brighter.

"I wouldn't presume to know more about the influence of shadow than you, Prince Sabellian," Jace said. "So you must understand what I'm trying to say."

The black dragon on the throne was silent for a long time. His posture didn't change, but he no longer seemed as lazy and casual as before. Finally, his fingers tapped the armrest twice more. "So what's next, Jace Sesso?"

"Auchindoun," Jace said. "Help me."

"I have no reason to refuse," the black dragon said.

"Then I'll come get you when it's time to leave," Jace said.

The black dragon nodded, then seemed to melt into the dark stone claw throne, leaving only two flickering ghost-fires.

As Jace left the cave entrance, a young black dragon suddenly landed beside him, transforming into a small humanoid figure amid a spray of sand and gravel.

It had to be Needleflame. There was no other black dragon in Wildhammer Stronghold small enough to transform like this.

Sure enough, the scruffy little girl burst out of the dust and grabbed Jace's coat, growling through gritted teeth, "Let me go."

"Go where?" Jace asked.

"Auchindoun. I heard everything." Needleflame looked up with her yellow eyes. "I've wanted to go in there for a long time, Jace Sesso. And you promised me last time. You said you'd take me out this time."

Jace shot a pleading look at the massive whelp beside him, but it just drooped its head as if it had seen nothing. He looked back into the cave. Sabellian clearly wasn't going to interfere either.

"Little one, this time might not be what you think..."

"Is there fighting?" Needleflame interrupted him.

"No..." Jace frowned. "Well, yes, but..."

"Is it 'next time'?" Needleflame pressed.

"Yes," Jace admitted reluctantly.

"I can turn into an orc." Needleflame said, and promptly transformed into a somewhat short female orc with teeth growing every which way, looking none too bright.

The two quick shifts seemed to drain her mana completely. She panted pitifully for a couple of breaths, then said in orcish, "You promised me, Jace Sesso. You said so that day."

This little one wasn't bluffing. Her orcish really was quite passable.

Needleflame might be a young whelp, but she was still more than a match for the average orc soldier. Plus, Morophil was too conspicuous in the Burning Legion demons' eyes now because of the Tome of Infamy's power, making it inconvenient for her to enter the Shadow Labyrinth with them. So the group was short a grunt for miscellaneous work. Small and agile, Needleflame could fill that gap perfectly.

"Go ask your father," Jace said. "I can't make that decision."

"I'll go ask right now!" Needleflame said, sprinting into the cave as if her energy had suddenly been fully recharged.

Watching her steady footsteps and swift speed, Jace couldn't help imagining how hard she must have been training in her mortal form.
