Chapter 1341: Ethereal Technology

Jace had little interest in standing at the mouth of that dark cave waiting for the father-daughter dispute to play out. He knew that if Needleflame managed to convince her father, she'd come flying at his face like an eagle pouncing on a rabbit the moment she could, no matter where he was in Wildhammer Stronghold.

He headed down the cliff toward the stronghold hall. Turalyon had said the ethereal technicians were resting inside. They'd arrived surprisingly fast — it seemed Node Prince Haramad was quite enthusiastic about this matter.

Jace could sense that the ethereals weren't entirely confident about their own twenty-year plan either. After all, they were up against the Burning Legion. Who knew what state Draenor would be in after twenty years of demon occupation? When Jace had proposed a feasible way to escape the Legion's lockdown, investing in this direction was a reasonable choice.

But just as Jace was considering how to get the ethereals to help plan the energy consumption of the communication array, a commotion of beastly cries caught his attention from a distance.

At the stables, two draenei were examining a nervous elekk's condition. One of them was the Prophet Velen. As Jace looked over, Velen almost simultaneously noticed his gaze.

"Prophet."

Jace approached the stables. Velen nodded goodbye to his companion and walked to the stable gate.

His gait wasn't as proud and upright as most draenei. With his robe covering those demon-like goat hooves, he looked more like an elderly human or elf — not the leader of an alien race.

Of course, his height, nearly matching an ogre's, made it hard for Jace to see him as the same type of humanoid.

"You made it back safely." Velen spoke in Common. "I hope the spirits didn't give you too much trouble."

"They want me to kill Primate Maladaar," Jace said. "And the Shadow Council's leader. Then they'll help me."

Whether that counted as "trouble" was debatable. Especially given the hint of distrust and mockery in the Primate's tone — a distinct "I bet you can't" kind of sneer.

But when it came to finding a way out, Jace and the Expedition had been going back and forth for too long. A dilemma that could be solved by simply killing a few enemies was clear enough.

"The priests who escaped from Auchindoun were right," Velen said quietly. "The souls there have long been changed by demonic power. I'm sorry for leading you to a dangerous place."

"It's fine, Prophet. I'm used to danger," Jace said. "Or rather, I don't know what else I could do besides this."

"Nor do I," Velen said. "For me, it's hard to say which is more dangerous — believing in the visions in my dreams, or ignoring them."

"But at least for now, in these uncertain times, we still have our own strength to fight the Burning Legion." The prophet's words lacked conviction, and Jace could feel it.

But this lack of conviction wasn't a desire to deceive — it was a restlessness, like dust that hadn't yet settled.

"It's not over, is it?" Jace said. "What I have to do isn't just getting the draenei souls' promise."

"It's not over, Jace Sesso." Velen said. "But where it ends, only you can know. I can no longer see that far."

Jace nodded. "Thank you, Prophet. For telling me what you did, and for the help you and your people are willing to give."

"We are also saving ourselves," Velen said.

Jace did know where it ended.

The Scepter of Sargeras. The thing he'd been avoiding, yet drawing ever closer.

In a side room of the stronghold hall, Jace could hear conversation as soon as he reached the outer corridor.

When he entered, a group of people were gathered around a pale purple three-dimensional image projected by an ethereal communicator. The image was of a middle-aged human mage.

"Jace, you're a bit late." Vereesa said. "What's wrong? Things not go smoothly with the Black Dragon Prince?"

"No, he's willing to cooperate. I ran into the Prophet," Jace said.

Vereesa took a meaningful breath but said nothing. The three-dimensional image flickered slightly.

"I can see you. I can see you..." the mage's projection said. "This must be Jace Sesso, am I right?"

"This is Archmage Vargoth, Jace. I believe this counts as your first meeting," High Thane Kurdran said to Jace, who had just walked in.

"Archmage." Jace stepped forward. "I heard your name when I was studying at the Wizard Sanctum."

"My honor, Mr. Sesso." The pale purple arcane image smiled, eyes narrowing. Even the wrinkles on his face were clearly visible.

"Incredible, Jace, look at this." Grede pointed at the disc below.

"Indeed incredible, honored Lord Grede." Vargoth's image said. "I've been studying the ethereals' form of existence and their arcane techniques, trying to improve the Kirin Tor's illusion magic. They definitely have things worth learning from."

In the Burning Crusade version of the game, Vargoth always appeared as an image speaking to players, projected by a Dimensional Wand he possessed.

That illusion was indeed similar to the ethereals' three-dimensional projection, though Jace had never connected the two while playing.

Had the mages at Kirin'Var Village exchanged knowledge about this with the ethereals before Kael'thas destroyed the village?

He'd never have the chance to find out about that other timeline. But for now, this unclear echo between the game and reality gave him some relief from the oppressive atmosphere, lightening his mood slightly.

Grede leaned in and whispered, "When we get back, I've got to send one of these to Kalimdor. Then I can talk to Mosa 'face to face' anytime!"

"Mosa better find a moonwell to use as an energy source," Vereesa stared at the mana crystals stacked beside the transmitter. "After all, the tauren probably can't train enough mages for your 'face to face' needs."

"Don't be so smug, elf," Grede muttered.

"The energy is still stable for now, everyone," an ethereal technician said beside him. "But considering Draenor's unstable magic and the long distance between here and Kirin'Var Village, we'd better discuss the important tasks Node Prince Haramad assigned us while the energy is sufficient and the connection is good."

"Of course, ethereal sir." Grede cleared his throat.

"We've already introduced your plan to the Archmage, Jace," Vereesa said. "Using a demonic ritual to transmit the ethereals' method of entering Telogrus to Azeroth. You said you'd need Kirin'Var Village's help for that."

"Yes," Jace said. "Right now, I'm the only one who can perform this demonic ritual, Master Vargoth. To transmit the ethereals' spells, I need to learn their language — at least pronounce it accurately enough. But I don't have enough time to become fluent in a completely foreign language. So I'm hoping to rely on language-related magic to reduce consumption and improve accuracy."

"You mean the modified Abyssal language they use," Vargoth's image said.

"Yes," Jace said.

The Archmage's projection pondered for a long while, then conferred with someone next to him before saying, "It should be feasible. I can try to refine a language potion using the available magical materials in Farahlon. Our new allies can bring it back to Wildhammer Stronghold."

"Glad to hear you're still able to conduct magical experiments, Archmage," Kurdran said. "I was worried the earlier magical disaster might have blown your whole village to bits."

"It hasn't been blown to bits yet, respected High Thane." Vargoth's image smiled slightly. "But things aren't as optimistic as you see either."

"How many soul fragments still remain in Kirin'Var Village, Master?" Jace asked. "This demonic ritual will probably consume quite a lot of mana."

"Very few," the image said. "After all, these fragments were seized from orc warlocks. We don't have much skill in using them. Some of my students and I have been researching ways to use the mana within to save ourselves. We've had some results, but most of it was wasted. If I'd known the black dragons would cooperate with you, we wouldn't have needed to go through all that trouble. Does Archmage Khadgar have any good ideas? Does he know about your plan now?"

"He does, but he still needs to confirm some details." Jace looked at the ethereal technician beside him. "I'll try to communicate with him next, and at the same time test how difficult it really is to transmit the ethereals' spells to the other side."

He looked at the ethereal. "Teach me two words of your language first. Let's see how much mana that consumes."

"You know how to use mana crystals to store arcane energy, so you must know methods for calculating mana consumption too, right?" Vereesa asked.

"Of course." The ethereal said. "We've rarely measured demonic magic consumption, Ranger-Lord. But we can try."

"I wonder if Greeby has any stamina-reducing potions we can use," a Quel'Thalas arcanist said from the doorway.

"Speaking of Greeby." Vargoth's image said. "We've found some more Nethervine recently. Go find Greeby in the alchemy lab, Mr. Sesso. Ask him what this special herb — only found in Farahlon and warped by void energy — can do..."

"You mean that annihilation potion?" Jace asked.

"Yes, you know it. The annihilation potion." Archmage Vargoth's image said seriously. "We have enough Nethervine now for about two doses. When your portal is complete, no matter what kind of mana you pour into it to activate it, the Deceiver won't be fooled. We'll need that potion then."

The Scepter of Sargeras would need a dose too, Jace thought. That thing deserved one.
