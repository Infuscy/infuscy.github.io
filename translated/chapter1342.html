Chapter 1342: Transmission and Infiltration

"Kalerez."

"My master, the mage Khadgar has been waiting for your voice."

"I know," Jace said in Demonic. "And I know what he's worried about. Tell him the Farstriders have found the Draenei prophet, and now we stand together. More and more Draenei are coming to Wildhammer Stronghold and Allerian Stronghold. Tell him not to worry."

"He says it's hard for him not to worry," the demon said.

"Turalyon has already agreed to our plan," Jace said. "I believe that as long as he agrees, the other Sons of Lothar leaders will too."

The other side of the Void was silent for a moment. Kalerez said, "He seems satisfied. But I still don't understand. The Draenei and your plan — are these allies of yours also willing to have their souls drained to power the portal in Auchindoun?"

"Convincing a black dragon to understand our choices is already pushing the limits of my ability, Lord Kalerez," Jace said irritably in Demonic. "I won't bother expecting you to understand either."

"If you all think it's feasible, I don't need to press further," Kalerez said.

The demon was silent for a moment, then suddenly spoke. "Do you think tracking down Kazzak's fate still matters now, mage? Once Jace Sesso is caught by Kil'jaeden, my effective betrayal will be immediately known! I don't know what's so funny about that. Don't think just because you're Medivh's apprentice you can disrespect my power!"

"You two can quarrel after the ritual is done," Jace said. "Now, for the business at hand. I'm going to try transmitting an Ethereal spell to the other side, and the ethereals and mages here will help me calculate the mana consumption. You need to be ready to relay what I say to Khadgar."

"This incompetent mage says that while he can understand the ethereal language, he doesn't have the ability to transcribe the Abyssal runes he hears onto a scroll in such a short time," the demon lord said.

"Tell him it's fine. This is just a test. The purpose is to let the ethereals help me calculate how much mana the entire transmission process will consume," Jace said. "As for how to write it out from your side..."

He adjusted his breathing slightly and continued in Demonic: "Tell Master Khadgar to contact SI:7, Kalerez. They know about the Daggers. It's a warlock organization hidden in Stormwind, currently cooperating with the king, but unknown to most of the citizens."

"You're saying those humans can transcribe Abyssal runes?" Kalerez asked.

"I can't be sure about that right now," Jace said. "But what I can be sure of is this — I summoned a Void Lord in their basement named Gaothaeros. He hasn't grown strong enough to devour the Mage Quarter, let alone all of Stormwind, but he's fully capable of transcribing Abyssal runes. He can definitely help when you're trying to locate the exact position of the Telogrus Rift."

After a brief pause, Kalerez said, "Khadgar wants to ask you — did you already figure out how to get back before you entered Draenor, and that's why you summoned that Void Lord?"

Hearing the archmage's question, Jace couldn't help but think back to that day at the Blue Heron tavern, when Morgan Ladimore had found him and asked him to retrieve his family heirloom, the sword Arcanite Reaper.

Then the Raven Hill crypts — Dar'Khan's questioning and emotional breakdown...

Then the communication with Quel'Thalas, the clues about the runestones, and finally the hidden nerubian crypt beneath Stratholme, and the mad Obsidian Destroyer.

The Telogrus scroll, the Shadow Council teleportation coordinates, summoning the Void Lord...

It seemed like if any single link had been missing, he might never have made it to Draenor at all. And even if he had, he'd never have found a way back.

Khadgar's casual reminder sent a chill down his spine. Even the communication ritual array, which had been perfectly stable thanks to his complete mastery, flickered slightly.

He refocused on the array. "I'm just using what I have available, Master Khadgar. Just like how you used the Skull of Gul'dan to destroy the Dark Portal — it was a choice made under limited circumstances."

"Things have come to this point, so I won't hide it from you anymore. The Telogrus Rift was originally something the Daggers and I found to use as our new sanctuary. I know this violates about a dozen Kirin Tor and Lordaeron Alliance regulations concerning the Twisting Nether. But I hope you and the Kirin Tor can look past it, given the role the Daggers and I have played in all of this. Once we're back, don't pursue it."

"Allowing the Daggers' presence on Telogrus would benefit the Kirin Tor too. Next time you need to summon a demon, you won't have to bring it directly into Azeroth."

The other side of the Void was silent for a long time. Jace cracked his eyes open slightly, checked the nearly spent soul shards in the array, and said, "Add more soul shards, Sazeno. Use the regular kind."

"Take me to that Arcane Sanctum again, my master. The soul shards we lost here have to be replaced from the Shadow Council," the imp said, placing two regular soul shards on the array as he looked up.

"First of all, they're my soul shards, not yours," Jace said. "Second, I'll take you."

"Ha!" The imp turned back and saluted Morofil with an Expedition military gesture. Morofil shot him an annoyed glare.

"That sounds like a threat. Khadgar says so," the demon lord's voice came from the array, pulling Jace back into maintaining the ritual.

"I've never had a good relationship with the Kirin Tor," Jace said into the array in Demonic. "If the Kirin Tor refuses and wants to pursue this to the end, of course I'm not going to doom the entire Expedition over it. But once I get back to Azeroth, I'll have to reconsider my relationship with them."

"He and the Kirin Tor don't care about a fragment in the Void, or a group of recluses studying orcs and shadow magic," the demon relayed faithfully, word for word, following Jace's command. "Khadgar hopes that after the Expedition returns successfully, we destroy the portal in Telogrus. He thinks from your perspective it makes sense too — the Telogrus Rift is your achievement, and the portal will be built by Kirin Tor mages and artifacts from Dalaran's vault. So handing it over to the Kirin Tor to handle is only reasonable."

Jace couldn't feel Khadgar's emotions from Kalerez's relay, but he could read the gravity between the lines. The rift between Jace's group of warlocks and the Kirin Tor couldn't simply be ignored.

But the passage to Telogrus wasn't even open yet. In a crude analogy, the Kirin Tor and the Daggers arguing over their differences right now was like a couple fighting over how to spend lottery winnings they hadn't even won yet. Absurd, and a little helpless.

Still, Jace thought it was ten times better to hash things out now than to keep worrying about the Kirin Tor's hostility through the rest of the journey, only to have the conflict suddenly explode when they finally got back to Azeroth.

"I agree," Jace said. "As long as you can represent the Kirin Tor."

"Can you represent the Daggers? The mage wants to know," the demon said.

"As long as you yield the Telogrus Rift, they can accept the other terms. I'm sure of it," Jace said.

"The mage finds that agreeable," the demon lord said.

Jace let out a small breath. "Then I'll try switching to Abyssal."

"I'm ready to relay," Kalerez said.

For an Ered'ruin demon responsible for Void delivery and watching over portals for the Burning Legion, Jace didn't doubt his ability to relay Void languages. It was his own crash-course level of skill that was more concerning.

More importantly, he'd never spoken anything other than Demonic in a communication ritual — not even Common or Orcish.

Jace shot Morofil a look. Morofil turned to the two ethereal technicians waiting behind them and said, "You can begin."

"Understood, miss."

The ethereals opened a device that looked like a transparent glass cylinder. Arcane energy inside it began to flicker between bright and dim in sync with the burning soul shard.

The imp swapped in another Draenei soul shard per Jace's instructions, and the test officially began.

"Tai, miag," Jace chanted in the unfamiliar language the ethereals had taught him. He quickly felt the dissonance between this language and the demonic ritual. The soul shard's burning grew more intense.

"Ila, iogafus, ar, bek..."

Jace understood clearly that what could cross the Legion's barrier and span the vast distance between Azeroth and Draenor wasn't just sound — it was something that touched the soul, something closely tied to the contract he'd signed with Kalerez.

So switching from Demonic to Abyssal wasn't as simple as changing languages in normal conversation. The Void road ahead felt increasingly muddy, the journey more and more bumpy — even though Abyssal was itself a language from the Void...

"Su, ag, istaeak, du, mar, ong, sil..."

It was a very simple Ethereal phrase, roughly asking whether the other side could hear them. But speaking it within the ritual felt harder than summoning another succubus.

"Repeat what I just said, Kalerez," Jace said.

The demon immediately began chanting Jace's words, mimicking them almost perfectly. At least, that was how it felt to Jace.

The Draenei soul shard dimmed. The imp quickly removed it and set it aside. The ritual suddenly belched black smoke, as if about to collapse.

"That's enough for now, Kalerez," Jace said.

With a series of indistinct grunts from the other side, the ritual cut off. The whole room reeked of burnt demon.

One of the ethereals picked up the transparent tube, looked at the energy fluctuations, and said, "To send all the runes we need to transmit, it would take about thirty-five to forty soul shards of this power level, Jace Sesso."

"Once we have the language potions from Kirin'Var Village, how much will that improve things?" Jace asked.

"Still needs practical testing," another ethereal said. "But based on our understanding of similar spells, it shouldn't reduce it by more than half."

"Then I'll just have to manage as best I can." Jace rubbed the stubble on his chin with his fingers and looked at Morofil. "Come with me into Auchindoun, or near it. Find a hidden spot where the Shadow Council won't detect us. We'll set up a communication array there with demon blood so I can find soul shards for transmission whenever I need."

"Yes, master," Morofil replied.

"Hey, wait a minute." The imp jumped onto the table. "Master, you mean soul shards from the Shadow Council? You just promised me you'd use those orc soul shards to make up for my losses!"

"They're my losses, not yours." Jace pointed at its nose. "Help me make sure the transmission succeeds, Sazeno. When the time comes, I'll use the soul shards I can bring back and have Kalerez make you some candy. If you screw around with me, I'll leave you to Kil'jaeden."

At the mention of Kil'jaeden's name, the imp immediately raised both hands. "Hey, no need for that... I'm already completely satisfied, my great master!"
