Chapter 135: The Star's Destination

Before dawn, the panther was finally dragged up onto the deck.

The goblins and sailors who'd been sent down to help had trussed it up in layer after layer of ropes. By now it had lost the will to fight back, lying on the ground motionless like a mummy. With even its mouth bound shut, the only thing it could move were its eyes.

But Jace was more concerned about his cloak than the panther.

He tried wetting a cloth with sea water and scrubbing at the claw marks. After a while they seemed to fade a little, so he kept at it, trying to rub out the white marks completely.

He scrubbed for a long time, but in the end there were still some traces left. You could notice them if you looked closely.

Aside from those marks, there wasn't any real damage. That panther had been running out of strength at the end, otherwise it could've been a lot worse.

For damage like this, if it could be repaired, it surely wouldn't cost as much as thirty silver, right?

Honestly, it wasn't that noticeable. He'd just let it go.

While Jace was dealing with his cloak over here, the panther—wrapped up like a festival gift over there—kept its eyes fixed on him the whole time, as if it knew exactly what he'd done.

It seemed to know why it had suddenly become so weak and powerless. It knew exactly why it had ultimately been caught.

Jace could even sense this beast's intense hatred of dark magic. Maybe during the Orc Wars it had encountered those warlocks and suffered under dark magic. Otherwise, why would it carry this much hatred?

It was only now that the arena guard who'd been tasked with watching and transporting "Night Baron" finally dared to step forward and take responsibility for the mistake.

He was a powerfully built fellow. Though he had the brown hair and strong, chiseled features typical of a Stormwind resident, his skin had turned dark red from long exposure to the elements.

He addressed the passengers who were just starting to recover from the night's terror: "Next time we'll carefully check the safety measures of the flute. Actually, to be on the safe side, we'd been drugging him to keep him sedated the whole time—the storm probably made the drug wear off early. In any case, a problem like this should never have happened. I'm willing to pay half the bounty as compensation."

"Why the hell didn't you go down and catch him yourself?" the shirtless man stepped forward and demanded. "You should know better than anyone how to get that panther back under control. Even just stepping forward to offer some pointers would've been something!"

"Let's not dwell on it, sir." The goblin captain cut in. "Much as I hate to say it—and I am grateful for your efforts—getting paid means getting the job done. If he'd given you any crucial advice, shouldn't you have split one share of the money with him? After all, this hunting mission was partly about making up for the damage caused by this man and his master. Since you've received the agreed bounty and he's willing to pay a price of his own, I'd like this discussion to end here. Let's not dwell on it."

The shirtless man was fuming but didn't know what to say in response.

Grede and Jace couldn't be bothered with the argument. They'd chosen not to take all silver—they took one gold coin and forty silver, since gold coins held a more stable value.

The two of them didn't need to split the money either, since they were traveling together, so they went for whatever held more value.

Right now Jace was turning the gold coin over and over in his hands. It had been God knows how long since he'd last held a gold coin.

It was a Gurubashi gold coin, stamped with the image of a winged wind serpent.

Because its weight wasn't much different from the various gold coins circulating within the Alliance, and because so many had flowed to every corner of the world during the decline of the vast Gurubashi Empire, it was accepted in most places.

Grede reached over. "Let me see. You hold onto it and you'll probably treat the whole thing as yours."

Jace handed it over. Grede rubbed the serpent emblem and grinned stupidly. As long as the goblin had paid enough, everything else was secondary.

Just then, Jace noticed the man who'd told them about Night Baron earlier, leaning against the railing and watching the panther. He went over and asked, "Sir, do you know where this beast is being sent?"

"Didn't you know?" the man answered. "Durnholde Arena, recently opened by Blackmoore—it's already made a name for itself in the Kingdom of Lordaeron. Plenty of nobles from Alterac and even Arathi have traveled all the way there to watch... because that arena has orcs! Who doesn't want to see how a Gurubashi star beast performs against the orcs of Durnholde?"

So that was it. This black panther was headed to Durnholde Keep to fight orc slaves. No wonder the shipping fee was so high. A star like this—just the appearance fees alone must be astronomical, not to mention the cut from ticket sales.

Jace knew that anyone living on a fixed salary like him could never imagine how much money the entertainment industry could rake in.

"I wouldn't have guessed it, you two," the noble said. "I thought you were going to die down there. Didn't expect all of you to make it back. Though that ponytail fellow... he really got it rough."

Jace had been busy helping move the panther and hadn't noticed the situation with getting the ponytail man back to the cabin. Only now that he'd relaxed did he remember—the last time he'd seen the ponytail man, he was still struggling to breathe.

Right now a few human sailors were having a heated argument with the captain and the goblins about something. They weren't speaking Common, but the human sailors kept pointing to the northeast—toward Menethil Harbor.

Jace could guess they were probably arguing about whether to stop at Menethil Harbor to rest and resupply.

It was clear the captain was still sticking to his own plan—to continue straight on until they reached Southshore. About four days of sailing left, which would get them there before December 1st. That would give Jace and Grede a much more comfortable travel schedule.

That was certainly what Jace was hoping for, because if they diverted to Menethil Harbor, it could delay them by two or three days.

From the murmuring of the people around them, the ponytail man seemed to be out of immediate danger, but at sea, you could never tell what might happen.

Jace was glad he wasn't the captain of this ship, having to make that kind of impossible choice.

Though for the goblin captain, it wasn't really an impossible choice at all. They wouldn't delay the voyage just to treat the wounded.

Just then, the argument seemed to have reached a conclusion.

The captain walked up to the crowd again. He looked out over the deck—quite a few people had already returned to their cabins.

"Is there a priest on board? Right now we have three or four wounded, two of them seriously injured and in need of a professional priest." The captain called out loudly. "If there's no priest, then a druid, a shaman, a witch doctor, a sage, a tide priest—anyone who can slow down death by any means. Step forward, whatever method you have. I'll pay. As long as you can keep these wounded alive until we reach Southshore!"

Watching the people go up to the captain to volunteer, Grede leaned against a crate and muttered, "I had no idea there was a priest on this ship. Why didn't they step up and help earlier? You know, if I'd joined the Church of the Holy Light back in Stormwind, they could've taught me some healing tricks. I could've earned quite a few extra silver coins."

"Maybe that's exactly why you didn't become a priest, Grede," Jace said.

"You should become a priest, Jace," Grede sat up and said. "What you just said sounds exactly like those bald heads at the Cathedral of Light."
