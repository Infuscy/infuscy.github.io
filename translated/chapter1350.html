Chapter 1350: Taking the Bait

The air was thick with the stench of shadowflame. Sabellian's ferocious spell had stunned the gathered orcs into dead silence. Even the warlocks who'd recognized Blackheart's tricks dared not move.

Vorpil sat on the ground, his pale, sickly eyes trembling uncontrollably. Hard to say whether he was more afraid that Blackheart had been influenced by the Old Gods, or terrified of Sabellian's devastating power.

After things had calmed down a bit, Sabellian seemed to sense his reaction had been excessive. He stood there, hesitating.

But Jace understood. After finally making it to Draenor, thinking they'd escaped the Old Gods' influence on Azeroth, only to run into cultists worshipping that same primordial darkness... It would be disheartening for anyone.

The "Pale" one orc warlock mentioned earlier were a type of reclusive orc influenced by the Old Gods.

Their worship of these corrupt beings had turned their skin white and emaciated them to the point of not even looking like orcs. Gul'dan's student, the two-headed ogre Cho'gall, had learned void knowledge from these reclusives.

Blackheart might have taken the same path as Cho'gall — contact with these so-called Pale — or been influenced by Cho'gall directly.

From his earlier sermons, he'd been deliberately trying to distort the faith of these Legion worshippers, reinterpreting the Burning Legion's destruction of countless worlds as part of some master plan by an entity in the deep void, steering them toward worshipping the true "Void."

That also explained why Blackheart was so desperate to get his hands on the demon registry. To let his "god" control Draenor, he'd first need to hold back the Burning Legion's demon offensive.

Back then, when he'd urged Jace — playing the warlock Yulruk — to find the Book of Unholy Names, the rewards and promises he'd offered were probably related to the Old Gods too.

But Jace knew that no matter which Old God had influenced Blackheart, it wasn't N'Zoth — the one who'd corrupted the Earth-Warder Neltharion — or any other being from Azeroth.

Old Gods weren't exclusive to Azeroth. They existed across countless worlds in the dark Void.

It was simply that Azeroth's energy was particularly powerful, blazing brilliantly across the entire cosmos, that had given rise to several such powerful parasites... And Draenor — especially Draenor as it was now — absolutely lacked the same conditions.

But as for whether Sabellian had been too hasty in executing Blackheart... that was debatable.

Who knew how many of the faithful here would have been influenced if Blackheart had been allowed to keep chanting those Shath'yar murmurs? Jace could see that many of the more impressionable cult members had already followed Instigator Blackheart too far down that road.

If they'd delayed any longer, the Arcane Sanctum might have erupted in civil war between Blackheart and Vorpil. On Vorpil's side, aside from a few felguards and some not-yet-fully-convinced warlocks, there was almost no one left.

Sora had recovered from her earlier shock. She warily observed the panicked, confused cult members around them, in case any of them suddenly lashed out.

Most were staring up at Sabellian — or rather, Dragonmaw warlock Naihogar — with complex expressions. Some even gripped their weapons and staves tighter...

Vorpil, helped to his feet by a nearby orc guard, hobbled over to the pile of charred bones. He shouted at the restless crowd below: "Even I didn't expect the ogre Blackheart to have faked Master Magtheridon's orders! The arrival of Karabor's emissaries exposed his scheme!"

He pointed at the bones. "You all saw it! Blackheart was influenced by the Legion's traitor Cho'gall, trying to enslave our cult — even the entire Shadow Council — the same way Cho'gall controls the Twilight's Hammer! Perhaps this is why Lord Kil'jaeden no longer trusts our loyalty! No longer trusts the Shadow Council's ability!"

Seeing the old warlock's attitude, the three fel orcs relaxed a little. Whether he was afraid of Sabellian's power and wanted to save his own skin, or genuinely believed in their emissary disguise — as long as the situation didn't spiral further, it worked in their favor.

Vorpil did his best, in his hoarse, weak voice, to denounce how Blackheart had exploited the cult members' loyalty to the Burning Legion for his own dark power, how he'd planned to betray Magtheridon and Kil'jaeden.

He was passionate and his arguments were solid. But the soldiers below didn't seem entirely convinced. Many — especially the ordinary guards who couldn't see through the ogre's array tricks — still seemed suspicious about Blackheart's sudden change and violent death.

Coming down from the dais, Vorpil's mind still seemed to hold disappointment and anger at Blackheart's deception. But by the time he limped into the back corridor, his eyes were full of exhaustion and worry.

Sabellian looked at him. "Don't worry, Master Vorpil. The cult members need time to accept that Blackheart was a fraud. And I believe he may have been using some kind of mental magic on your students and guards. That also needs time to heal."

"During this period, we'll protect your safety." Jace said with a sincere expression. "Your loyalty to the Legion is beyond question. When reinforcements from the Twisting Nether arrive at Auchindoun, we will explain to that demon lord how you steadfastly defended another of the Legion's portals... You will become the Shadow Council's supreme leader, and together with Warchief Kargath Bladefist, pave the way for the Horde's glorious future."

As Jace spoke, Vorpil watched his eyes intently. Like any warlock, he instinctively tried to read Jace's sincerity or deception through the flow of shadow. But that was precisely why Jace was best at deceiving shadow manipulators.

After hearing his words, the old warlock coughed a couple of times. "I sincerely thank Master Magtheridon and all of you for your help. Before this, I could never be sure whether Blackheart could actually contact Master Magtheridon. Sometimes his actions were suspicious. Your arrival deepened my doubts — it almost seemed to confirm Blackheart's authority... If not for that ritual publicly exposing his trick, I might never have regained control of the cult."

This was a probe into the fel orcs' origin. Jace worked his lips slightly, adjusting his teeth, using the moment to organize his words. It seemed the old warlock wasn't fully convinced just because he couldn't sense any lies.

"We are not the first batch of emissaries Master Magtheridon sent to Auchindoun." Jace said. "Before Dragonmaw fortress fell to the Alliance and the black dragons, Master Magtheridon had already sent several emissaries here. Some who successfully returned brought less than positive feedback. Some reported that the leaders controlling the Shadow Council weren't so loyal. Some doubted the Legion. Some wanted to control Auchindoun's souls for their own use. And some..."

"Some were after Lord Kil'jaeden's demon registry," Sabellian added.

"Yes. Shocking, isn't it?" Jace said. "Master Magtheridon sent us here partly for that reason."

"It's Yulruk — that powerful warlock — and a powerful Sayad lord who was with him." Vorpil said with certainty. "Very few emissaries from Karabor or Hellfire Citadel have been able to reach Auchindorn recently. It must have been him!"

The old warlock let out a dejected sigh, lowering his voice to almost a whisper. "Blackheart insisted that the Hellfire Citadel emissary was a human warlock named Jace Sesso in disguise. He claimed the man was sent by the Alliance Expedition to gather intelligence. Within days, the Bladespire fortress fell, Lord Batas was killed, and the Book of Unholy Names was stolen. Lord Kil'jaeden must have been furious. Fear and worry forced me to believe him..."

"I didn't say who it was. Master Magtheridon didn't tell me to reveal the source of this intelligence." Jace said. "Perhaps the Yulruk you mentioned really was Jace Sesso in disguise. No one can say for sure. But the emissary I mentioned did convey Master Magtheridon's wish to build a portal in Auchindoun. And to this day... I see no progress."

"No, he promised that Master Magtheridon would send more warlocks, even the Legion, to help us build the portal!" Vorpil coughed violently a few times, speaking urgently. "Damn Blackheart. He deceived us all. He thought I was a senile old fool unfit to lead the cult. He thought I would trust anyone... He exploited my weakness! You must believe me, masters! And you too, Lady Sosala!"

"It's not enough for just us to believe you." Sabellian said earnestly. "The point now is to make the whole palace believe you can replace Blackheart — even do better. Let Master Magtheridon and the Legion lord see your capability."

Vorpil's fingers trembled. He looked down the passage to make sure no one else was listening. Then he said, "You're right, Master Naihogar. You're absolutely right."

He swallowed, lowering his voice carefully. "I know the Farstriders at Allerian Stronghold have been attacking Auchindoun's external soul towers recently. It seems they're using those towers to convert the souls of fallen Expedition soldiers, to empower their necromantic artifacts. We should seize the opportunity while they're busy dealing with that — expand our advantage, seize Auchindoun's soul energy, and pave the way for the portal's construction."

Sabellian and Jace exchanged glances. They hadn't expected the old man to jump into the pit himself.

"Are you sure, Master Vorpil?" Jace said. "Blackheart just died. Your cult is in complete chaos. And you think now is a good time to launch an offensive?"

Sora shot Jace a look, as if asking why he was arguing against it when Vorpil had clearly taken the bait.

"With a few powerful warlocks like you assisting me, I'm not worried!" Vorpil said fiercely. "Besides, the cult needs a strong enemy — a significant victory — to reunite. As long as it serves the Legion's rule, the cult does not fear death or sacrifice!"

"We truly have no choice, Naigag," Sabellian said. "This must be Master Magtheridon's will."

Jace ground his teeth. "Indeed."
