## Chapter 1352: The Tomb's Response

Even though Auchindoun was full of collapsed passages, cracks, abandoned corridors and chambers—leftovers from Murmur's summoning explosion that were more than enough for arakkoa, ethereals, or other creatures roaming the wilds of Terokkar to hole up in secretly—Wophil and Brakkat's cult still kept a tight watch on every access point around the Arcane Sanctum.

And ever since the black dragons had allied with the Expedition, the few secret passages that might've let someone slip through had been sealed off completely or had guards posted at them. Sneaking in and out with several people undetected, like last time when they'd rescued the Expedition soldiers, was no longer realistic.

Sazeno led Jace to one passage entrance. Jace had thought the patrols they'd dodged earlier were the last checkpoint, but even from here he could still make out two felhounds, a warlock, and two sword-wielding cult guards in the shadows up ahead.

No more hiding. They'd have to face this head-on.

Of course, if Jace walked in and out as an emissary of the Black Temple, nobody would bat an eye. But the soul shards in his pack would be a lot harder to explain. And there was no hiding those — felhounds could sniff them out from a mile away.

Watching the two cult guards chat, Jace muttered under his breath. "This is the easiest escape route you found?"

"The others are worse, master." The imp let out an awkward chuckle and pointed at a pile of rubble beside a collapsed wall. "Between you and me, you could sneak past from over there. I can turn invisible anyway. You just use your unstoppable Shadowstep..."

"Use Shadowstep in front of felhounds? You might as well have me stand up and yell 'I'm an idiot.'" Jace frowned.

He glanced up and down the passage. Over an hour had passed now. He couldn't waste more time looking for gaps in the cult's defenses — he wouldn't find any he'd missed that his imp hadn't already scoured.

He rummaged through his bag and pulled out a few bottles of Draenor Invisibility Potion that Wophil had given him months ago. He'd stashed them in his potion pouch for backup when he was planning to infiltrate the Spires of Arak. Never thought he'd circle all the way back to needing them here.

Of course, invisibility potions weren't enough to evade felhounds. He'd need to put as much distance as he could.

He studied the rubble pile on the other side of the corridor. The warlock occasionally took his felhounds on a patrol loop over that way, and the two cult guards sometimes did a round too. The moment the felhounds moved farther off, Jace downed an invisibility potion and bolted.

The crunch of his boots on broken bricks drew the orcs' attention. They looked his way, but Jace had already slipped into the warped light, passing out of their sight as he ducked between the rubble and broken walls.

Everything was a blur under the invisibility potion, but this wasn't Jace's first time drinking one. He'd memorized the general layout in advance. He calmly navigated around rocks and stepped over fallen pillars, using the potion's longer duration to push steadily forward...

Just as Jace felt like he was about to clear the felhounds' perimeter using the rubble for cover, a rattling sound like a tractor engine starting up drifted vaguely from the other side of the debris pile... He knew felhounds well enough to understand — the magical ripple from the invisibility potion had been detected. These fel orc warlocks had summoned felhounds far stronger than the ones his colleagues in Azeroth could manage.

If he got caught, he'd have to kill every last fel orc and pin it on the Auchenai priests. But he really didn't want to cause a disturbance outside Auchindoun right when he was trying to slip away. That would only make Wophil more suspicious. So he'd avoid it if he could.

Jace picked up the pace, using his demonic detection to roughly locate the felhounds while relying on the shadow-corrupted rubble and broken walls around him to block as much magical radiation as possible, throwing the hounds off his trail.

But then, the felhounds seemed to lose interest and wandered further off. Was there another magical disturbance here?

Jace wasn't a felhound. Under the invisibility potion, he couldn't even make out ordinary humans or beasts clearly, let alone some other magical disturbance. The potion was wearing thin, and he didn't have time to investigate. He could only glance back quickly.

In that brief look, he caught several tall silhouettes that had appeared out of nowhere, walking in the opposite direction, leading the felhounds away.

The silhouettes vanished as the potion's effect finally gave out. The long corridor fell back into darkness, but Jace had already crossed the cult's defensive line and was on the other side. Deep in the passage behind him, felhound claws scraped against the floor in a chaotic scramble, while somewhere further off, the two cult guards asked each other what they'd seen.

Jace hurried away from the Arcane Sanctum, already guessing what those silhouettes had been... Faint breathing seemed to surround him — a steady inhale and exhale — and mournful cries rose from the gaps in broken walls and the scattered bones around them. "Remember... your promise. Remember, your..."

Eredun whispers rose and fell around him. Jace knew — the souls here were helping him.

Not all the draenei spirits had gone mad and lost their will, then. Auchindoun still had souls willing to help him against the orcs. And those souls must have known about the pact he'd made with the High Priest — to help the spirits reclaim control of Auchindoun.

But how had these souls known he was that warlock? Right. Jace had nearly forgotten.

He looked at his hands. The fel orc disguise had been stripped away by the potion's magic.

He'd been prepared for that. After all, once he found a spot to perform the communion ritual, the disguise would break anyway. It didn't really matter. It was just that when he'd been focused on dodging the felhounds, he'd lost track of that detail.

That must have been how the souls recognized his face and identity.

Both Sabellian and Turalyon had their doubts about whether the spirits of Auchindoun would keep their word. Even Jace couldn't be a hundred percent sure. But their appearance now put his mind somewhat at ease.

Once he left the area the Shadow Council controlled, with no orcs maintaining them, the passages and chambers became even more dilapidated.

Guided by his imp, Jace wound through the ruins until he reached a cave near the open plaza outside. This spot sat between the Auchenai priests' and the Shadow Council's territories. There were hardly any orcs, hostile draenei, or even wandering undead here.

It was far enough from where the orc warlocks operated. Performing a demon communion ritual here wouldn't be detected. A pretty decent spot.

Just then, a demon bearing a familiar magical signature appeared at the cave entrance.

"Traveled a thousand miles, broke through an encirclement, and now we finally meet at the crossroads of death and destruction, my master. Ah, it makes my soul itch with romance."

The tall shivarra stepped into the cave, her silhouette resembling a demonic skeleton wreathed in fel light, her head crowned with terrifying horns. As she drew closer, the alluring face slightly ornamented by demonic skin only made the horror of her wicked body and black demon hooves feel even more unsettling.

"I'm curious, shivarra — when your soul itches, how do you scratch it?" The imp snickered, hopping out of Jace's bag. "You actually brought that booger out of there? Is it really that important?" The succubus said crossly.

"Pretty important." Jace said. "I didn't expect you'd get here so fast, Morofil."

"Fast?" Morofil said. "Don't forget, you walked all the way to Auchindoun with those three dragons."

Jace looked her over. Aside from a key pieces of spiked tight-fitting armor and the two blades of "The Apocalypse" hanging from her demon-forged rune belt, she carried nothing else.

"Did you bring the demon blood?" he asked.

"I did." Morofil flashed a wicked grin and raised a finger. "I just need a container..." Jace seemed to understand. He pointed at an abandoned brazier nearby.

The succubus walked over, bent down, clenched her fists, and suddenly vomited a gushing stream of blackened demon blood! The imp yelped "Whoa!" then watched for a moment before feigning a few dry heaves of his own.

Morofil spit it all out, wiped her lips, and lifted her head, her long black hair flowing back as she looked his way. "Do you think I'm disgusting now, master? Have you lost interest in me?"

"Completely within expectations." Jace cleared his throat. "Hurry up and make the ritual circle. I need to get back before dawn."
