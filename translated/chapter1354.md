## Chapter 1354: The Final Piece of the Puzzle

In the deep corridor, the imp materialized out of a shimmer of light. Sabellian followed behind him, his long cloak dragging as he strode closer. "My thanks," Jace said.

Sabellian grunted, raised a hand, and cast a spell. Jace was instantly enveloped in a swirl of scorching hot sand and stone. When the dust cleared, the figure standing there was a sturdy, burly red fel orc.

Jace stretched his limbs and cleared his throat, adjusting to the voice. The black dragon prince looked at him, baring his blade-like fangs in a grin. "Next time, don't mess up my magic so casually. I can't be everywhere to clean up your messes."

"I'll keep that in mind." Jace said. "Let's head back."

"Your mana is severely depleted." Sabellian walked beside Jace, glancing at him every so often with a frown. "Did something go wrong with the circle?"

"No issues." Jace replied.

"You ran into trouble?" Sabellian said. "Did you fight someone in your human form? What kind of enemy drained you this badly? Did you expose our real identities?"

"I didn't run into any enemies." Jace said. "You worry too much. The circle had Morofil's protection — it's fine. Let's pick up the pace. No more chit-chat. It'll be dawn soon."

"Ah." The black dragon seemed to suddenly understand, looking at the silent imp with a knowing glance. "So you turned down my invitation... because you had other entertainment!"

"There was no 'other entertainment,' and I'm not going to follow you into some orgy with orcs." Jace said, walking faster.

"I don't understand why you have such a prejudice against orcs." Sabellian said.

"Not my type." Jace said.

The black dragon smiled and caught up. "Fit, tall, muscular — add in some annihilan blood infusion... I'd say that's exactly your type."

"Taste is subjective." Jace said.

"But not a single female orc appeals to you." Sabellian said. "How about this — I know a few draenei-orc half-breeds..."

"Not a chance." Jace said flatly.

Sabellian pointed a finger at Jace's nose. "That's undeniable racial discrimination."

"Deathwing's son is calling me racist, huh?" Jace looked at him. "Ah, right. You all don't discriminate. You look down on every race equally — including yourselves."

"Sounds like the conversation's getting heavy." Sabellian sighed with some disappointment. "Let's talk about something lighter. How's Dalaran?"

"Going smoothly." Jace said. "The Kirin Tor is being unusually cooperative."

"That doesn't surprise me." Sabellian said. "The only things that can get those six on the Council to agree quickly are Medivh himself, Medivh's mother, or Medivh's student."

"Or Medivh's old haunt." Jace added.

Sabellian chuckled. "Anything else I need to know?"

"Murmur." Jace said. "Khadgar mentioned a book — the one the Shadow Council used to summon Murmur. He thinks it might contain a way to banish it. Wophil hasn't banished Murmur yet, probably because he still wants to use that otherworldly elemental to intimidate the Auchenai priests, or any other enemy trying to destroy the Shadow Council."

"It's been effective so far." Sabellian said. "At least it's 'intimidated' us into not knowing what to do."

"So the best approach is to convince him to send Murmur away himself." Jace said. "Teron Gorefiend and the Shadow Council originally wanted to summon a demon lord anyway. We can help Wophil summon a real demon lord. Then he won't need to keep this potential hazard around."

Sabellian didn't respond. Jace asked, "Do you disagree?"

"I think Wophil might disagree." The black dragon said.

"Then let's ask him." Jace said.

Back in the depths of the Arcane Sanctum, the sacrificial platform where Brakkat had performed his demon rituals had been cleaned out. The hall had returned to its previous state.

Still, apart from the occasional jarring noise, an atmosphere of anxiety — even fear — hung over the whole hall.

Months of Brakkat's sermons had shaken many cult members'狂热 faith in the Burning Legion. And Wophil's open conflict with Brakkat had only deepened their doubts about the future. That kind of reaction was only natural.

And precisely because of that, they would naturally seek a new, clear goal to dispel that confusion. For now, that goal was destroying Patriarch Maladaar and building the portal.

So for Jace and his group, this situation was actually a good thing.

In Wophil's hall, the old orc was still absorbed in a finely crafted red leather grimoire. Jace guessed it was probably the same book Khadgar had mentioned — the one about summoning Murmur.

He wasn't totally unfamiliar with that book, actually. In the game, it was called the Blood Codex, kept under the old warlock Wophil's altar.

He just hadn't been completely sure before Khadgar brought it up. Warlocks and Burning Legion grimoires used red leather covers so often — demon skin, blood-soaked materials, the usual — that it could've been anything.

Wophil was so deeply absorbed in the grimoire that he only looked up when Sabellian and Jace were right in front of him and a warlock apprentice alerted him. That didn't bode well for Jace. The old warlock was probably exactly what Sabellian had worried — not about to give up Murmur easily.

"Good morning, Master Wophil." Jace said in Orcish. "We have some questions about eliminating Patriarch Maladaar that we'd like your insight on."

Wophil closed the tome carefully and set it on a stone table beside him. "Speak."

"Before Teron Gorefiend left for Azeroth, he summoned an otherworldly elemental called Murmur in Auchindoun." Sabellian said. "According to intelligence from Karabor, this elemental is close to slipping out of the Shadow Council's control."

"Lord Matheridon's concern flatters me." Wophil bowed respectfully to Sabellian, then said, "But as long as I am here, Murmur will not slip from our control. It is securely sealed in the deepest prison of the palace — a place Teron Gorefiend called the Hall of Whispers. If you were to visit now, you would find the name well earned..."

"We are not in the mood for jokes, Wophil." Sabellian said coldly. "If it is truly still under the Shadow Council's control, then it should contribute to the assault on the Auchenai priests. That was, after all, why Teron Gorefiend summoned it — to scour every last draenei from Auchindoun."

Wophil let out a snickering laugh. "You cannot 'command' Murmur to do anything, Master Naihogar."

"Then that is not control." Jace said. "So, on Lord Matheridon's advice... our best option is to nip this risk in the bud early. He believes you have the means to banish it back to its own dimension. Kil'jaeden is having difficulty trusting you and your Shadow Council these days. I imagine it's not just because of Brakkat and his Dark Faction — it's also because of all these unstable, uncontrollable factors here that trouble him..."

"I am certainly capable of destroying it, capable of banishing it!" Wophil waved his withered arm excitedly. "It was easy for Master Teron Gorefiend, and it is just as easy for me!"

He leaned closer to the two fel orc warlocks, lowering his voice. "But if Lord Kil'jaeden could see Murmur with his own eyes... witness this magnificent existence, then he would not see it as merely an unstable, uncontrollable hazard!"

"If you look at the Blood Codex, you will understand — Master Teron Gorefiend poured countless soul energies into Murmur to keep it existing stably in the Draenor plane! It is now like a great soul forge, which will become the Shadow Council's most important asset for returning to its former glory! Lord Matheridon wants to banish it back to the Twisting Nether — that would be throwing away decades of work. I mean no disrespect to the Lord of Draenor, but this would be an unforgivable waste."

"I didn't know..."

Jace muttered under his breath. He genuinely hadn't known that the Shadow Council had been keeping Murmur bound — neither letting it go nor banishing it — for this very reason.

"Of course you didn't!" Wophil cut him off excitedly. "But I do not blame you. This was the Shadow Council's secret, and a great gift I am preparing for the Legion..."

"In that case, Murmur could perhaps contribute to the portal first." Jace said.

"Ha! You are even more ambitious than me, Master Naigag!" Wophil seemed to shed his fatigue in an instant, pointing at the shadow-shrouded giant door behind him. "Teron Gorefiend, myself, and even Brakkat have studied it for ages without finding a proper way to use Murmur's soul, and you think you can use it to power the portal? That is madness even Gul'dan would not dare. Why do you think he never considered using this elemental abomination when he opened the Dark Portal?"

Yes, Wophil was right. Opening a portal required stable soul energy. Something as chaotic as Murmur — whose mere appearance had blown up half of Terokkar — would never work.

But it could still be useful to Jace. In fact, it filled a gap he'd been uncertain about all along.

"But a great demon lord would be different." Wophil puffed up with excitement, pride in his voice. "Once the portal is complete, Murmur and the lingering souls of Auchindoun will provide immense power for the Legion's advance. Those arrogant Alliance scum will be shocked at their own weakness. Though they will not have long to be shocked, will they?"

"I can sense that you are as excited as I am. I know... I know..."

No. Not some demon lord that would never step through the portal. Uuless. Jace thought to himself.

He could almost hear Uuless's low murmur.

Sabellian glanced at the staff wrapped tight in enchanted cloth on Jace's back. Jace knew he and Uuless had arrived at the same thought.

Devour Murmur's soul. Sever the Scepter of Sargeras.
