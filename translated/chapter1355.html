Chapter 1355: The Elemental Soul Theft Plan

“Now I see why you like this human so much.”

In the depths of a chamber with a half-closed stone door, Sabellian sat on a chair made from clefthoof bones, looking at Sola beside him. He tapped the gray-yellow armrest happily. “Can’t bring himself to do the bad stuff that shouldn’t be done, but won’t miss a single bad thing he can do.”

“What did you two talk about with Vorpil?” Sola asked, confused.

“Ask him.” Sabellian said. “From back there, I knew we were on the same page.”

“Draining Murmur’s soul.” Jace took a shelled seed from a handful he was holding and bit into it. “Then we achieve our final goal.”

“No need to be that cryptic.” The black dragon prince raised his black brows smugly. “There’s no Eye of Kilrogg here, and no demons or orcs eavesdropping.”

“Can you… actually do it?” Sola asked carefully.

“Even my father never set his sights on Murmur.” Sabellian leaned back in his chair, twirling an orc skull in his hands. “Because the Shadow Council controls how to bind it. Acting rashly could produce unpredictable results — and that was before Ner’zhul destroyed Draenor. But Murmur’s power is unquestionable. As for severing the Scepter of Sargeras… at least based on my prior assessment of the scepter, there’s no problem.”

“So how do you get at Murmur’s soul?” Sola said. “Or did you already convince Vorpil?”

“That otherworldly elemental is sealed in the deepest part of this palace, a place called the Screaming Hall.” Sabellian said. “We’ll find a way in.”

“Why don’t we just kill Vorpil, Prince, and take over the Shadow Council? That was the plan anyway!” Needleflame’s orc eyes widened. “Once we do that, we can go in whenever we want…”

“For one thing, Blackheart just died, dear. This cult can’t handle another upheaval.” Sabellian looked at his daughter with a faint smile, spinning the skull on his fingertip. “Trying to use mind magic on all few hundred of these people at once… even Onyxia couldn’t do that.”

“For another.” He set the skull down in his palm. “Right now, Vorpil might be the only one who knows how to control Murmur. Until we’ve thoroughly studied the incantations in the Codex of Blood, we’d better not send him to his death.”

“Or at least trick him into dealing with Murmur before he dies.” Jace said.

“On what pretext?” Sabellian asked.

“There are plenty of reasons.” Jace said. “He just convinced us not to banish Murmur on Magtheridon’s orders…”

“Magtheridon ordered Murmur banished?” Sola asked in surprise.

“Of course that was a lie.” Sabellian said. “The vigilance you showed me back in the Arcane Sanctum, madam, has been thoroughly undone by that question.”

“I couldn’t care less about you lot’s good opinion.” Sola said flatly.

“Never mind him.” Jace patted Sola’s wrist, then grabbed another handful of seeds from beside her, nibbling as he continued. “If he wants us to believe the Shadow Council can control Murmur, I think it’s only natural for emissaries from Karabor to take a look inside the Screaming Hall, wouldn’t you say?”

“You’re not wrong.” Sabellian agreed.

Jace nodded. “Then, while we’re in there, we create chaos — while Vorpil still has the ability to bind that elemental — and devour its soul. Of course, that’s assuming we figure out what’s in the Codex of Blood first.”

“Best to do it after we deal with the Auchenai Exarch.” Sabellian said. “We show our strength a little, prove our good faith to Vorpil.”

“Makes sense.” Jace said.

“I want to fight in the assault on the Auchenai too, Prince.” Needleflame said seriously.

“No, you don’t.” Jace said. “While the cult attacks the Auchenai crypts, you need to grab as many soul shards as you can and bring them to the ritual circle Morophil is guarding. The imp knows the way. You go with him.”

“You promised I’d get to be a warrior.” Needleflame said irritably. “And instead I’m stuck doing sneaky thief work!”

“What sneaky thief work?” Jace said. “If you don’t get the soul shards, I can’t transmit the method of connecting Draenor and Azeroth to the other side. Then we can’t go home. The Burning Legion wins. Prince Sabellian and I will be trapped here forever. If that doesn’t make you a great warrior fighting the Burning Legion, I don’t know what does.”

Needleflame licked her lips, glanced at Sola, and asked, “Is that true?”

“Of course it’s true.” Sola said.

Sabellian smiled. “More importantly, you agreed to behave when we came here, Needleflame. Fighting a bunch of necromancers is no big deal. Once the demon army is besieging Auchindoun, you won’t be complaining about a shortage of enemies.”

“I’m not afraid.” Needleflame said. “I hope it comes to that!”

Sola said worriedly, “So the final battle really is unavoidable.”

Jace nodded. “Once the portal opens and the Legion’s wards are triggered, Magtheridon and Kil’jaeden will know we’ve found a way to escape. All our disguises will drop. The Legion’s final frenzy is inevitable. Then we’ll see just how much that Book of Fel Names Morophil absorbed is worth.”

“Look at it the other way — if the Legion doesn’t throw everything at Auchindoun, we’ll have a bigger problem.” Sabellian used his sharp claw to draw a line on the gray stone table before him. “To chase down the Alliance soldiers evacuating from Honor Hold, the demons on the Throne of Kil’jaeden will have to send a large force to work with the fel orcs of Hellfire Citadel.”

The black dragon prince drew another line connecting back to the start of the first one. “That’s when we strike from behind. Like High Thane Kurdran said — drive like a knife straight into the Throne of Kil’jaeden, destroy the Scepter of Sargeras, and cut off the Burning Legion’s ambitions to invade Azeroth once and for all.”

“I was thinking the same thing.” Jace tossed a seed shell onto the table. “And if Kil’jaeden really is waiting for me to pick up the Scepter of Sargeras, he might even leave a path open for me — waiting for me to climb that mountain.”

“Then you cut down all his hopes with one swing. The thought alone is exquisite.” Sabellian rubbed his hands together. “I must witness that final moment, Jace Sesso. There is no way I am missing it.”

“Don’t say my real name.” Jace glanced at the crack in the stone door. “You’re being careless. Don’t forget — Vorpil posted two guards to watch us.”

Sabellian grinned smugly. “He should regret assigning two female orcs to watch us.”

“What?” Jace asked.

“I am using my lower half to weave a tight underground intelligence network in this labyrinth, Master Naigag.” Sabellian said gravely. “This is for the sake of our… group. No — it’s for the Lord of Karabor, the great Magtheridon!”

“There’s no need to dress it up so nicely, Master Naihogar.” Sola said.

“Still a more reasonable excuse than ‘replenishing mana,’ wouldn’t you say?” Sabellian’s eyes slid toward Jace. Sola sighed faintly. Needleflame let out a delighted snicker.

The black dragon prince’s shoulders shook with laughter. He reached for the nuts beside him, but found nothing.

“Where are the delicious Venus flytrap seeds Master Vorpil gifted us? Did you eat them all yourself, Naigag?”

Jace stopped mid-chew and looked at the table. Sure enough, all the shells were piled in front of him. Everyone else had been too caught up in the conversation.

“Your shelling speed is truly astonishing, even with those sharp saber teeth.” The black dragon prince said. “I can’t help but wonder if you’ve got the soul of a kaliri bird living inside you.”

Jace spit out what was left in his mouth and grinned. “I figured you all wouldn’t be interested in these seeds, so I’d save you the trouble.”
