## Chapter 1356: Assault on the Auchenai

Word kept coming in about clashes between the Auchenai priests and the Alliance's Farstriders. Wophil grew more impatient by the day.

Orc scouts brought tales of many draenei necromancers being impaled by terrible magic. From their descriptions, it was all Vereesa's handiwork.

Jace didn't care how many draenei died, or whether the soul-towers were destroyed or captured. He could only silently pray that Vereesa wouldn't get hurt fighting against Patriarch Maladaar's forces. He knew that once Vereesa got blood in her eye, she was even harder to control than Grede. With him not there, the knot in his heart just wouldn't loosen.

But while the Farstriders tangled with the Patriarch out in the wilds of Auchindoun, Jace and his people had equally important work to do in this shadow labyrinth.

That noon, a scout brought word that the second of the six soul-towers had been taken by the Farstriders. Many orc scouts also reported seeing more and more Auchenai necromancers stepping up their efforts to raise the dead and drain souls in response to the Alliance's moves. At the same time, a number of Auchenai vindicators — whom Maladaar trusted deeply — had led acolytes and undead servants to reinforce the soul-towers.

The balance between the cult, the Auchenai, and Alleria's Fortress had been broken. This was the perfect moment for the Shadow Council to seize the entire Necropolis of Auchindoun.

Just as Wophil had predicted, the moment the cult's soldiers and warlocks started preparing to attack the Auchenai priesthood, all the doubt and anxiety from Brakkat's death vanished in an instant.

Even if these people weren't fighting for the Burning Legion anymore, no longer worshipped demons — at least taking Auchindoun meant claiming a near-infinite well of soul energy. Every warrior, warlock, and assassin who distinguished themselves in this battle expected a generous share as their reward.

In the dark corridor, Sabellian watched the groups of frenzied orcs filing past and murmured to Jace beside him. "The more I understand this race, the more I understand why the Legion chose them as their vanguard to invade Azeroth. They will become my help in ruling Draenor someday."

"Orcs don't like being enslaved." Jace said. "Be careful."

"Yes." Sabellian said. "Orcs don't like being enslaved — they especially hate that period of history when they were slaves to ogres and gronn. So in the future, they will rise up and fight back, launching a full assault on the ogre clans of Bladespire and Farahlon. After driving out the demon slavemasters, they will reclaim the shattered Draenor..."

"Let's see how much strength they have left to resist 'enslavement' after their epic battle bringing down Gruul and storming his lair." The black dragon prince smiled. "Or after being epically crushed by Gruul..."

"I hope your plan works." Jace said.

"You sound like you have reservations." Sabellian crossed his arms. "I know you have good relations with the orcs of Nagrand. I promised before — as long as they're willing to stay quietly under the elements' protection and live their lives, I guarantee I won't drag them into this."

Jace nodded, but didn't relax much inside.

If Draenor was freed from the Burning Legion, the Expedition and Velen's draenei fled to Azeroth, and the black dragons, orcs, ogres, and gronn were all tangled up on Draenor again...

And if Sabellian's "use the tiger to eat the wolf" plan took shape — the fel orcs and gronn and ogres tearing each other apart while the black dragons swooped in — then the sons of the orc chieftains at Garadar probably wouldn't sit idly by either.

They hated demons, of course. They also hated the shame of their kin drinking demon blood twice. But if Kargath, as Jace and Sabellian predicted, turned against the demons while the Burning Legion was vulnerable — that might flip the impression many Mag'har orcs had of their red-skinned kin.

Much like how they would react to Grommash Hellscream slaying Mannoroth the Pit Lord in the future.

Sabellian, of course, couldn't see the blood and fire that would likely erupt from all this. His face was full of optimism for the future.

And what Jace found a little ironic was that if someone had told him a few years ago that black dragons and orcs would slaughter each other until mutual destruction, he'd have clapped and cheered. But now, the thought of either side being completely destroyed left a bitter taste in his mouth.

He could only hope that Hellscream's son always remembered his father's shame of drinking demon blood, that Jorin Deadeye wouldn't pin his hopes on Kargath and his fel orc legion again, and that under Greatmother Geyah's guidance and teaching, they would peacefully protect the homeland of Garadar as they'd promised, never again interfering with matters beyond Nagrand... Maybe then everything could end in an acceptable way.

But none of that was his concern now.

The Auchenai priests had cut off all contact with the world beyond the Bone Wastes. Apart from the fleeing priests who feared Maladaar's actions and had made it to Shattrath, no one outside knew what was happening in the Auchenai crypts. And the Auchenai necromancers couldn't care less about the outside world.

But they could never have imagined that the Alliance's Farstriders were harassing and pinning down their priests on the outside — to buy an opportunity for their mortal enemy, the Shadow Council, to attack.

And they certainly couldn't have guessed that Wophil, who'd been locked in a decades-long struggle with Brakkat without daring to attack the Auchenai, had finally found the nerve to make his move.

Because at this point, the cult's only choice was to fight or fall apart. At least Wophil believed that. He had to pin his hopes on these emissaries from Karabor.

The long corridor leading to the Auchenai crypts was dark and sinister, filled with the endless wailing of souls.

Jace knew it was those draenei necromancers desperately siphoning every soul they could find, raising the dead to serve them. The souls of Auchindoun were growing darker and more furious under the defilement of these rituals. The constant screaming in Jace's ears sounded like curses at his inaction.

Not much longer now, Jace thought.

In a sacrificial hall, several draenei necromancers were sifting through towering bone piles for suitable skeletons and mangled remains to piece together undead soldiers for battle.

Swarming ranks of already-raised skeleton soldiers — armed with blades, crystal shields, and spears — guarded the hall's entrance while helping their masters gather more corpses.

The grinding of bone and flesh sounded like a predator chewing on its young. The stench of rot and shadow reached even from the distant corridor outside the hall. Jace, Sabellian, and a group of the cult's orc warriors had already arrived nearby.

A draenei's out-of-control roar drifted from somewhere far off, echoing faintly. Wophil and his people had already made their move from another passage.

The towering necromancer shadows inside the hall raised their heads warily, clearly not understanding what was happening.

A cult warrior captain let out a frenzied war cry in Orcish. Dozens of orc soldiers emerged from the shadows beneath the walls, raising battle axes and longswords as they charged the hall's entrance. Their formation was ragged, but their battle fury was more than enough.

But they weren't facing Westfall militiamen, or the defenseless farm women and children of Elwynn. They were charging something far colder and more merciless.

"Fresh meat..."

"Living souls!"

"Offer them to the Patriarch... offer!"

From the end of the passage, hundreds of clattering skeletons and rotting corpses spoke in halting Eredun, advancing with blades raised under the low murmur of the necromancers' commands.

Many of them still bore the remnants of Shatar and Horde armor, their movements retaining traces of the orcs and draenei they'd once been. But their eyes were filled with the sickly yellow light of necromantic shadow, forever severed from their former lives.

As the decaying yellow light of necromantic energy flooded forth, the muscle-bound orc vanguard crashed into the seemingly slow-moving skeleton warriors... The freshly assembled undead ranks buckled like mud hit by a flood, collapsing backward until they barely held the line.

The undead felt no pain, no fear. They simply held stubbornly against the orc charge. But the orcs only grew fiercer, axes spinning like whirring blades into those lifeless skulls and black hair...

Biting. Screaming. Blades grinding against bone. Overturning boots. Flying weapons and helmets. Beastlike roars. The screech of clashing metal.

On a pile of rubble behind the orcs, two fel orc warlocks watched it all coldly amidst the thick stench of rust and gore.

Jace watched an orc take a sword through the chest — his arm severed, pierced as he fell. Jace tilted his head. "Aren't you going to do something?"

"We don't need this many orcs to build one portal, Naigag." Sabellian glanced at him and smiled. "You're not actually starting to think of yourself as reinforcements sent by Matheridon, are you?"

No sooner had the black dragon prince spoken than the necromancers hiding in the hall made their move.

One after another, mangled bone abominations — hastily assembled — lunged into the orcs and exploded. Bone fragments sprayed everywhere, embedding themselves in orc flesh. The undead soldiers hit by the blast were completely unaffected.

At the same time, several necromancers pressed forward, protected by skeleton guards, chanting spells to manipulate souls and entangle every orc elite fighting at the front. The priests' eyes glowed with a pale, bone-colored light. Their own souls and flesh had likely long been twisted by necromantic curses into undead abominations themselves.

The battle line was starting to waver. Several orcs had already fallen. Sabellian finally changed his expression.

He stepped forward and raised his right hand. Jace felt the dark energy in the entire corridor — gathered from rage, frenzy, and despair — tremble at that single gesture.

This fel orc-shaped black dragon erupted in dark flames. As he swung both hands forward like a tiger striking prey, a rain of shadow bolts scattered forward, slamming into the clustered skeleton warriors and blasting them into flying bone and mangled flesh.

Necromantic energy guttered out like struggling flames in the black downpour. Many shadow bolts flew past them, spiraling into the depths of the hall and catching the necromancers off guard.

The resurrection circles the priests had set up, even the artifacts clutched in their hands and the soul shards scattered on the floor — none could withstand the sudden eruption of terrifying magic. They shattered and collapsed one by one.

Emboldened, the orcs shouted "Naihogar!" as they charged into the hall, throwing themselves at the panicking necromancers.

Those tall, pale, arrogant priests were cut down one by one, hacked into pieces. Without their skeleton protectors, they had almost no means to fight back...

Sabellian admired his handiwork with satisfaction, stepping over the piled bodies to follow the orcs. He was clearly enjoying the feeling of clearing obstacles.

"Teach me that trick sometime." Jace caught up.

"You can't do it?" Sabellian asked in surprise.

Jace lowered his voice sheepishly. "Teach me, and I will."
