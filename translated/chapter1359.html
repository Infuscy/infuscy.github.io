Chapter 1359: Depths of the Crypt

The two fel orcs and Wophil left the library together, emerging at the gate outside the passage. Sabellian looked at the old warlock. "After we return to the cult's palace, we'd like to see Murmur, Master Wophil."

"I must warn you — entering the Hall of Whispers is extremely dangerous." Wophil said.

"Your broken ones and orc warlocks have been working in there for quite some time. I imagine we'll manage." Sabellian said.

"What you don't know, Master Naihogar, is that many talented warlocks and apprentices have died in there." Wophil said.

"We'll study the Blood Codex carefully." Sabellian said. "To make sure we understand everything we need to be aware of when facing Murmur. The rest is none of your concern."

"Yes, Master Naihogar." Wophil said. "Once the remaining draenei priests in Auchindoun are completely purged, we'll return to the Arcane Sanctum. Then I will take you into the Hall of Whispers."

"Excellent." Jace said. "I've been looking forward to this since we left Karabor — to see what kind of otherworldly monster even the traitor Gul'dan didn't know how to handle."

Wophil's face twitched. He was likely one of Gul'dan's students. He hadn't been there for Gul'dan's betrayal of the Horde and the Shadow Council in Azeroth, and probably still felt some gratitude toward Gul'dan. So hearing Jace describe Gul'dan that way sat poorly with him.

Of course, he didn't dare show it. At this point, worshipping Gul'dan — whether in the Burning Legion or the Horde — was a death sentence of a stain.

But Jace didn't much care about the old warlock's feelings anymore. What concerned him more was the growing restlessness and hunger in the staff he held.

Even though Uuless had devoured Maladaar and the souls of who knew how many Auchenai necromancers, it still seemed nowhere near satisfied. From just now until this moment, it had been hammering at Jace's consciousness with that fierce urge — pushing him onward, onward.

Could it be that the infinite soul-energy buried in the Auchenai crypts had whetted its appetite further?

Jace found it strange. He'd been in Auchindoun this long, and Uuless had long known he couldn't forcibly drain the masterless spirits wandering this necropolis — it was part of the deal.

Besides, even during the assault on the crypt gates, it hadn't been this ravenous. Almost... angry.

Angry? Jace could feel the raging demon soul within Uuless — its emotions laid bare. Before, it was mostly arrogance and smugness trying to override Jace's will, or the craving and greed for more souls. But now, the hunger had taken on a note of anger. What was going on?

Uuless kept urging Jace deeper into the Auchenai crypts. Jace was honestly curious what was in there.

"Where are you going, Naigag?" Sabellian asked.

"I'm going to take a look inside." Jace said.

"Go ahead then. I want to see what good stuff the cult's found." Sabellian said. "Master Wophil, take me to wherever your cult is collecting spoils."

"Yes, Master Naihogar." Wophil said respectfully.

Jace nodded. The black dragon was already itching to see what treasures the draenei had kept, impatiently hurrying the old warlock along.

After all, once the Expedition returned and the cult was destroyed, everything the Shadow Council had plundered in Auchindoun would belong to the black dragons.

The orcs had already taken every corner of this crypt. If there'd been any remaining danger, it should have been uncovered by now. So what did Uuless want?

He followed the deep passage further in, crossing another bridge illuminated by shadow-energy's eerie red light. On the other side was a grand hall surrounded by Auchenai sigils and towering draenei statues.

Many draenei acolytes lay dead throughout the hall. Orcs were busy looting anything that looked valuable.

This was probably the Auchenai sanctuary — the place Maladaar had guarded. Jace could feel countless powerful spirits lingering in the dark dome above, looking down at every orc — including himself.

Was Uuless trying to devour these souls?

No. The staff wanted him to go further, closer to the center of the hall.

Jace kept walking until he stood on a draenei emblem shaped like a shield on the floor. Suddenly, a massive blue apparition appeared — walking out of nowhere without warning. Jace stumbled back, gripping his staff tight. "Another soul!"

A shout in Orcish. The orcs all around immediately noticed the apparition. They drew their enchanted weapons. A warlock raised his bone staff.

At the same time, Uuless let out a shriek loud enough to make Jace's ears ring — as if desperate to devour this spirit immediately.

But this ghost was different. Jace couldn't sense the same extremely corrupted dark aura from it that the vengeful spirits summoned by the Auchenai necromancers carried.

It looked very similar to Maladaar. But younger. Fewer wrinkles. Fewer shadow runes. Jace wasn't sensitive enough to distinguish between draenei appearances once those features were missing.

Maladaar had two very distinct characteristics, though — only two thick tendrils on his chin, no hair or horns, just a few indistinct bone plates on his head. The spirit before him had all these same features.

Was this actually Maladaar's soul? Then what had Uuless drained earlier? Or...

A warlock nearby was already beginning to chant a soul-drain spell at the spirit. Jace raised his hand immediately. "Stop!"

The orc warlock broke off his spell, releasing the shadow he'd gathered. Jace looked around at every orc in the hall and added, "Master Wophil has given orders. All souls in the Auchenai crypts are under the Karabor emissaries' authority."

With Jace's command, and the spirit making no threatening moves — standing like a shadow statue in the hall's center — the cult members settled down.

"Jace Sesso."

Jace felt a chill run down his spine. He turned to the source — this Maladaar-like entity.

Out of the corner of his eye, he checked the other orcs. None of them seemed to have heard anything. They remained at their posts, warily watching the spirit, then glancing down at the loot they'd been hauling.

"You may have already guessed. I am Maladaar. Or — not entirely."

The draenei Patriarch's spirit spoke in a cold, quiet voice. But the hall had no echo. And from the orcs' reactions, only Jace could hear this spirit's voice.

"I am a part of Maladaar's soul. The uncorrupted part. The other part has been trying to completely twist and devour me — to absorb me into his power and complete the 'transformation' he's desired. But he never succeeded."

"The countless souls of this necropolis protected me, keeping me from being drained too quickly. The other part realized he couldn't contend with the spirits of the entire necropolis, so he changed his strategy. He hoped to mold me into something even more evil and mad than himself — an avatar, or a dark puppet... to rule Auchindoun alongside him, the necropolis he had already corrupted."

No wonder Uuless was so pissed. It must have sensed it was leaving half uneaten...

Of course, Jace wasn't about to let Uuless eat the other half now. He forcefully suppressed its greed.

Maladaar's spirit continued. "I know — it was you who stopped the other me's plan. You saved... this remnant shade. In return, I will help you and the Expedition complete your mission. When the time comes, every spirit in Auchindoun will awaken at my call."

"You don't resent me for defiling this necropolis not long ago?" Jace said, looking up.

The Patriarch replied, "The Light sees all. And your actions and will confirm the Light's judgment."

"I understand." Jace said.

Under the spirit's gaze, Jace turned and shouted to every orc still in the hall. "Leave this Auchenai Patriarch's soul-shade here. When the Legion's lords descend, they will decide its fate. Everyone out now! Guard the gate!"

"Yes, Master Naigag!"

The orcs answered one by one, taking what they could carry and leaving the hall. Jace descended the steps and walked toward the gate.

"Give my apologies to the Prophet and my people. I failed to protect Auchindoun..."

Jace turned to look at the high platform — the spirit's outline was already hard to make out. He nodded, then turned and left.
