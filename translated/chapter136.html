Chapter 136: After the Battle

This hunt had given the group a real taste of fame on the ship. Wherever they went, people kept coming up to thank them — even the strangers who'd looked downright menacing at first all made a point of expressing their gratitude.

The woman was resting in her cabin with her injuries. The ponytail man, though hurt far worse, stayed conscious the whole time. When Jace and Grede went to check on him, he kept his eyes fixed on the two of them, determined to prove he was still full of fight.

Three of the people who'd gone down into the hold had died, but some had survived after all. The panther hadn't gone out of its way to hunt down anyone who fled and hid — and the first mate, Gazzik, though wounded, had somehow pulled through. Nobody had expected that.

They'd taken a huge risk, no question, but Jace had to admit this was the biggest payday he'd ever had. He'd actually gotten his hands on a gold coin — and half of it was his, not to mention the silver on top of that.

He'd been debating whether to sell the bead bracelet on his wrist, but now it looked like he wouldn't have to. He'd have more than enough left over for the rest of the journey through Lordaeron.

Might as well keep the bracelet as a souvenir.

In the passenger cabin, the shirtless man was holding court, bracing to the people he knew while swigging rum.

"That black panther… let me tell you, it was a star of the Gurubashi Arena for a reason! Every swipe wanted to tear the cabin walls apart — go look down there now, it's covered in claw marks and dents. I was worried the thing was going to claw a hole clean through the hull… No wonder I had such a rough go of it, but we won out in the end! Hah!"

"Of course, the brothers and sisters who fought alongside me were every bit as impressive. Without them, I'd have been torn to pieces — especially that dwarf!"

He raised his cup in their direction. Grede and Jace clinked their glasses back amicably.

Listening to the shirtless man's exaggerated retelling, neither of them felt much urge to jump in with their own commentary.

That was normal enough for Jace — he'd never been one for showing off, and now that he'd picked up these dark magics, he was even more careful about keeping a low profile.

But Grede, who normally loved the spotlight more than anyone, had been awfully quiet this time. That was enough to make Jace curious.

"What's wrong? Something bothering you?"

"I thought you'd use that Fear spell," Grede said, keeping his voice low. "But you used a different spell instead, didn't you? Another shadow magic one? I couldn't say for sure this time, Jace, but… I think I saw your staff glow."

His gaze drifted to the long staff resting on the table. Jace's hand moved to cover it without thinking.

"Right when that panther charged at you, I heard you chanting. Some kind of weakness or stun spell, I'm guessing — something to muddle the beast's head, make it lose its bearings, not know where to attack or how. I could tell. Without you, we might not have gotten paid at all. We'd have just gotten hurt for nothing. I was ready to go all out, anyway." Grede pulled the dagger from his belt and gave it a shake.

"That's about the size of it," Jace said. "But I was worried someone in that group might be able to recognize shadow magic, so I held back a little."

"Don't overthink it," Grede said. "I did some asking around on deck earlier. The long ponytail guy and the one doing all the talking — they're both hunters from Booty Bay. They've never crossed paths with orcs, never gone toe-to-toe with trolls. The woman's a veteran, but she's the only one… she might've been able to recognize your spell, since she fought in the Orc Wars and ran into orc warlocks. But by the time you cast, she could barely stand. She wouldn't have known a thing."

Hearing that put Jace a lot more at ease.

"Speaking of which — you really think you could've killed that panther?" he asked.

"Don't forget I had my shield," Grede said. "I wasn't bluffing this time. That orc in Redridge — I couldn't beat him, and that's the truth. If he hadn't used magic, we could've had a proper match, but that would've been pointless. As for this panther, it was definitely stronger than your average cat. But I get the feeling its reputation had a little help from arena promoters and the crowd's hype. I'm confident I could've taken it one-on-one — as long as I had a blade in my hand, anyway. Bare-handed would've been the end of me. I just cut my nails, after all."

He leaned in suddenly. "What do you think — we should put together a team and go fight in the Gurubashi Arena? Find a reliable third, and we might make a bloody fortune!"

"There's a line between brave and suicidal, Grede," Jace shot him a look. "That's enough out of you… If you want to go, go ahead on your own. I'm not looking for an early grave, and I don't need you blaming me for holding you back."

Jace had no interest in fighting his way through a match only to come up against some team led by a human barbarian, backed by a night elf druid and a blood elf rogue sneaking around the flanks.

Especially if that human barbarian took one look at him and said, "Well, well, isn't that so-and-so? We met at Stonewatch, remember?"

Of course, Wrynn's split barbarian persona wouldn't end up a gladiator for another decade or so. If he and Grede turned pro as arena fighters now, they almost certainly wouldn't last long enough to run into that crew.

Grede laughed. "I'm just kidding. But seriously — if the beast fighters there are all this out of shape, the money's not hard to come by."

Whether the money was easy or not, Grede's opinion wasn't exactly a reliable reference. The man was the kind of fighter who could throw Danath Trollbane's cousin to the ground with his bare hands and have him eating dirt.

Jace had zero doubt about his ability — Grede could definitely rake in coin at an arena.

But that was the kind of work that literally traded blood for gold. The people who went weren't just in it for the money, either. They were either slaves with no choice, or battle fanatics who valued a good fight more than their own lives. Anyone with better options wouldn't go looking for trouble there.

Jace said, "Never mind whether the panther was out of shape or not — I nearly got clawed full of holes. If I'd ended up injured like the ponytail guy, the recovery alone would've eaten up God knows how much time and money."

"But think about the scene back then," he concluded with a note of satisfaction. "Facing down a cat like that and casting my spell calm as you please. I'd say I've got some real skill."

Grede grinned. "I've been telling you, Jace — you're more of a man than you look."

"What?" Jace's smile collapsed. "I don't look like a man to you?"

Grede laughed even harder. He grabbed Jace's face and pinched it. "Look at this smooth little pretty face. Toss you into Shadowforge City and your backside wouldn't last three hours."

"Get your hands off me." Jace swatted his hand away. "Don't touch me, you damn pervert."

"If only I were," Grede sighed. "Hell, these days at sea have been driving me up the wall. The women on this ship are so fragile I'm afraid they'd break a bone if I touched them, and the one who can actually fight got injured."

"Won't be long now. We'll be ashore in Southshore soon enough — go find that valkyrie you've been dreaming about," Jace said.

Grede chuckled a few times, then slowly his expression faded.

"I still want to bring up the arena thing, Jace. Not that I want you to fight in the arena or anything — but you said you'd hold me back…"

He shook his head. "I've got this feeling. I might not be able to beat you anymore."
