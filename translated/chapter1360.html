Chapter 1360: Portal Construction Begins

On the open plaza of Auchindoun's ruins, hundreds of orc laborers were dismantling the surrounding wrecked walls under the cult officers' direction, hauling the stone away for recutting in preparation for the portal's construction at the center.

Two fel orcs stood on the edge of a broken balcony at a higher vantage point, looking down at the orcs moving back and forth below. Jace asked, "Aren't you going to stay and watch over the Auchenai crypts? I'm worried the orc warlocks might get ideas about the draenei souls."

"Don't worry." Sabellian gazed down. "Those orcs aren't you — they won't learn the art of soul-enslavement that fast. Besides, I have trustworthy eyes inside. They'll watch the warlocks for me."

"How trustworthy?" Jace said. "Honestly, I don't believe female orcs can be tamed that way."

"That just means you don't understand orcs — or us." Sabellian's mouth curled, a trace of a black dragon's yellow demon-light flickering in his eyes.

"And so what if word gets out? It's perfectly normal for Karabor emissaries to set up their own networks in the Shadow Council in advance. Makes it easier for the Burning Legion's lords to take over the place later. Honestly, I'm half-hoping those female orcs let something slip — keep the cult's warlocks on their toes, so they don't get any ideas about sneaky business when we're not around."

Jace studied him for a moment, then finally nodded. "Alright. So what did you find in the Auchenai place? I mean with the cult's collected spoils."

"A lot of soul jars and soul shards." The black dragon said. "But most of the rest are Light relics that have lost their power. Turalyon might be more interested in those. All in all — disappointing."

Seeing the thoughtful look on Jace's face, he asked, "Not feeling confident about our final plan?"

"I have to consider the possibility that things won't go smoothly with Murmur." Jace glanced toward the Auchenai crypts. "And even if we do successfully drain Murmur's soul... I'm worried it might not be enough to sever the scepter. So I need to find as much power as I can."

"Your staff can devour souls without limit — that much is certain. Even its creator, the Dark Titan himself, doesn't know its upper limit." Sabellian looked at him. "But you also need to consider your own upper limit — what you can control. I believe you genuinely want more power now to cut through that scepter. But don't forget..."

"Every fall begins with a problem that seems unsolvable." Jace said quietly.

The black dragon stared at him for a long moment. "Yes."

Jace took a deep breath. "Hard to believe that right now it's a black dragon telling me to calm down."

"The feeling's mutual. If it weren't for you — a warlock, no less — pointing it out, I'd have had a hard time realizing what Kil'jaeden was planning." Sabellian's gaze followed a group of laborers as they crossed the plaza. He was silent for a moment before adding, "Or at least, hard to convince myself it really was a trap."

"Besides, even Aegwynn would think twice before facing your staff. Let alone me."

Jace smiled. A group of laborers passed behind them, led by an orc soldier. The two fell silent by unspoken agreement.

The orc laborers were noticeably smaller and frailer than the warriors and warlocks. Even their faces looked a bit different. Smaller eyes. Duller expressions. Somewhat simple-minded.

Mixed in with regular orcs, the visual difference might not have been so stark. But when they were all singled out for manual labor and the soldiers were put on the other side, the contrast became sharp.

Orc clans had always had a tradition of culling the weak. Back in Garadar, Jace had witnessed Mag'har elders performing some kind of ceremony for newborns by the river.

But Jorin Deadeye had mentioned to Jace that in most orc clans — including the Bleeding Hollow — bringing a child to the river after birth was actually a way to conveniently drown the weaker infants.

Only in clans with the harshest living conditions — like the Frostwolves in the snowy mountains, who couldn't afford to lose any newborn — had this ancient tradition become a symbolic ritual.

Garadar, where the Mag'har lived, had been founded by Garad, chieftain of the Frostwolf clan. So they'd carried over that tradition.

But the orcs from other clans in Garadar hadn't objected either — probably because the Mag'har were all the sick and weak who'd been quarantined and abandoned here in the first place. They had no heart for culling anyone else.

Of course, not every flawed child could be identified at birth. Those who grew up smaller and weaker than others either died easily in conflicts or became laborers.

That hadn't fundamentally changed even after Thrall the liberator founded the new Horde. Laborers were still the most marginalized and discriminated-against group in Orgrimmar and all of Durotar.

When Jace was studying Orcish and orc culture at the Wizard Sanctum, the cruel treatment of laborers in the Horde was used as proof of the orcs' evil nature in Alliance propaganda. Back then, he'd focused on the language itself. He hadn't spent much thought on why orcs were so fond of slavery — even Thrall, a freed slave himself, hadn't abolished it completely.

But after coming to Draenor and seeing the ogres of all sizes, then witnessing the crowds of laborers — shorter and frailer than normal orcs — he'd started to realize something. The orcs' choices might be hard to judge by human standards — or the standards of his past life's world.

Orcs had evolved from ogres. The orcs' ancestors were the "deformed" among the ogres. And the ogres had shared a similar fate — their own ancestors were the one-eyed ogoron, a kind of giant between gronn and ogres. The ogoron had themselves "degenerated" from the gronn.

Even today, many ogres still bore traces of gronn and ogoron features — thick, rough stone-like skin, stalactite-like horns on their backs and heads. These ogres who retained gronn-like traits were regarded by their kin as gronn-avatars, honored as "lords."

And the gornn's own ancestors were the now-extinct magnaron — far more powerful than themselves. This evolutionary chain could be traced back hundreds of thousands of years, to a primordial age when mountain-sized giants fought the terrifying flora that ruled Draenor.

Many orc scholars knew this history. The shamans could hear these stories from the elements and the ancestors' spirits. So they might be concerned about one question: would orcs continue to "degenerate"?

Jace guessed that these obviously weaker laborers were seen by many Horde rulers as signs of degeneration. These "inferior" orcs gave them a kind of fear about the future — which bred hostility.

Of course, it was more likely that orcs simply had this kind of culture and temperament.

The laborers working below for the cult weren't just orcs. There were also broken ones, and even draenei.

These draenei weren't Patriarch Maladaar's followers. They were survivors of Auchindoun's destruction, dragged out from every corner of the ruins.

Even now, many draenei still hid in the nooks and crannies of the ruins. They didn't trust the fallen Patriarch Maladaar, but they couldn't abandon this place either — not when venturing into the Bone Wastes meant risking being eaten by black dragons.

Besides, even if they left the Bone Wastes, what then? Everyone who'd left Auchindoun had never been seen again. They always said they'd reach Shattrath and rebuild that city. But not one had returned with news from Shattrath.

Now that the power balance in Auchindoun had tipped toward the cult, these draenei who'd survived in the cracks had lost their last refuge.

A group of laborers and soldiers were dragging a bound, towering draenei out of a passage. The gray-blue-skinned giant was emaciated past recognition, his hooves scraping against the dusty stone floor — like an old ox being dragged to slaughter.

His torn purple robes still bore the faint remains of a Shatar vindicator's insignia. He'd rather die than work on the Legion's portal like his kin had.

Despite his thin, ragged state, the orcs were still being thrown off balance by the former vindicator's resistance.

One officer roared and drew his sword, leveling it at the draenei's heart. The draenei's arcane-infused eyes locked onto the blade at the same time — almost as if he hoped the sword would pierce his chest soon and end his long, painful, miserable fate.

"Stop." Jace called out in Orcish, halting the officer's move. "Didn't Master Wophil remind you not to casually slaughter these draenei, honored Deathsworn? Get some poison, or find a warlock to knock him out and drag him to a cell. Don't waste the portal's fuel."

The orc officer stared at Jace, breathing hard. Finally, he grunted an acknowledgment and rehung his sword, shouting for a warlock to come help.

The draenei had clearly understood Jace's Orcish. His previously resigned expression twisted into uncontrollable fury.

"You devil! Gul'dan's fine apprentice — ha! You'll end up just like that little coward! The Naaru will curse you. The Light will punish you. Your soul will writhe in fel fire... until the end of time. Wait—"

Before he could finish, a flash of violet shadow-light. A warlock who'd just walked over finished his incantation, and the draenei crumpled — as if struck on the back of the head — shaking the floor with his fall.

"Does the draenei language not have swear words?" Sabellian asked curiously, watching the giant being dragged away.
