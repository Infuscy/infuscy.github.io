Chapter 1361: Only Those You Trust

Jace had seen plenty of portals in the game, and a few in person here. He'd seen mages' portals at the Wizard Sanctum, and the Dark Portal from the Azeroth side, glimpsed from a distance in the Blasted Lands.

But designing a portal himself? No chance. He was completely clueless about that.

The Legion portal currently being laid at the center was designed by Wophil and a group of warlocks he trusted. Wophil had told Jace and Sabellian it would stand about forty feet high — roughly twelve meters — which he considered the minimum needed to transport a powerful demon lord.

When the warlocks pointed out the approximate height to Jace, he felt like completing a portal that size in a month — and carving void runes all over it — was a pretty ambitious goal.

But Wophil was confident about the timeline. The laborers and warlocks here were already well-practiced in portal construction. And the stone was sourced directly from Auchindoun itself — cut on-site — eliminating the time-consuming process of transportation. He insisted it could be done within a month. Jace chose to believe him.

Come to think of it, if they had chosen to take this place by force with the Expedition, how would they have gotten a bunch of Alliance paladins, mages, and priests to build an evil gate connected to the Legion's realm? That would've been a real headache.

And on Draenor, apart from Matheridon's Legion, there was probably no construction crew more experienced than the Shadow Council. On that basis alone, they couldn't off Wophil too quickly.

Early that morning, Jace stepped outside Auchindoun for a walk — and to check on the portal's progress. A bunch of orc laborers had already started digging the foundation. Another group was putting up scaffolding and dressing stone. The whole scene looked primitive and rough, but reasonably organized.

As he was estimating what the finished portal would look like, footsteps sounded behind him. He turned. A short figure in a dark brown cloak — a fel orc — rushed up from the shadow of Auchindoun's high wall.

"Lord Naigag!" Needle grinned, skidding to a stop in front of him, holding up a gray leather pouch. "The thing you asked for... check it out!"

Jace took the pouch and opened it. It was already full of faintly glowing draenei crystals. He counted twelve, plus some scattered smaller soul shards.

"You stole this many at once? Won't they notice?"

"I didn't take them all at once." Needle said proudly. "And Lady Sosara put them to sleep with sleep magic easy. Those orcs slept sounder than a clefthoof about to die of old age. They won't even know they were hit with magic!"

"Plus, when the cult attacked the Auchenai priests a few days ago, a lot of warlocks took soul shards from Brakkat's treasury — many of them made from draenei souls. A lot of those warlocks died in the Auchenai catacombs. Who knows how many soul shards got left behind with the bodies?"

"Heh." Jace chuckled. "Your father didn't come up with that explanation for you, did he?"

"I thought of it myself!" Needle frowned irritably. "Why do you ask? Do you think I'm not as smart as him?"

Jace lowered his voice. "He's got a few thousand years on you..."

"I'm not dumber than my father!"

Needle hissed, keeping her voice low. Watching this short female orc throw a tantrum, Jace thought her grubby, mischievous look was actually kind of endearing.

"Then live a few thousand years yourself, yeah?" Jace said. "Maybe you'll be way stronger than him by then."

Hearing that, the young black dragon's face darkened.

She looked at the pouch of soul shards in Jace's hand. "I don't think I'll live that long, J— Lord Naigag."

"That's a normal lifespan for your kind, isn't it?" Jace said. "And you're growing so fast. A few hundred years and you'll be grown."

"But this is Draenor." Needle said. "My father says this world is dying. And we have to struggle on in this dying world, because other worlds are even scarier for us."

Jace pressed his lips together. "Have you ever thought about coming back to Azeroth with us?"

"What?"

Needle's eyes went wide with shock. Jace could see right through her in that instant — she wasn't a fel orc at all. Even though his senses could barely detect any flaw in her appearance.

"Azeroth. 'Going back.'" The young black dragon repeated the phrase, as if unsure what it even meant.

Watching her, Jace suddenly felt reluctant to take her from her father.

When Solara had first found him and suggested bringing this young black dragon back to Azeroth, Jace hadn't really thought about how the just-hatched creature felt. Back then, he figured Sabellian was the only one who could decide whether she left or stayed.

Now that Sabellian had basically agreed, reaching the point of actually asking Needle — Jace realized getting her to want to leave wouldn't be easy at all.

He didn't want to talk about how vast and beautiful Azeroth was. He didn't want to use Solara or the Dragonqueen's promises of teaching her interesting magic or abilities as bait. He didn't want to use those childish, empty tactics on this kid.

He thought for a moment, then said, "Azeroth is a scary place — just like your father said. There are things there even more terrifying than orcs, demons, and gronn lurking... and they hold a lot of malice toward your kind."

"That's why your father decided to stay here with his people. It's a very smart choice for you. Going back to Azeroth — you might face horrors and corruption beyond anything you've ever known."

After a long pause, Needle asked carefully, "What does my father say..."

"He doesn't want to let you go." Jace said. "But I think he does hold out hope that you could live peacefully on the other side. He didn't say it outright — but the fact that he agreed says enough."

"Because if I can survive, it means all our kind could go back." Needle said quietly.

"The Dragonqueen might think so." Jace said. "But I can promise you — your father isn't expecting you to shoulder that burden for the whole flight by yourself. You're different from other black dragons. He might think you'd have a better chance over there. An easier time surviving."

Needle rubbed her eyes, then asked, "And if I go there — will you help me?"

"My power means nothing in that world." Jace smiled. "But there will be beings far mightier than me who want to help you, protect you. Like the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza I mentioned... your father must have spoken of her. She'll take good care of you. And beyond her, there are many wise and powerful Dragon Aspects. Once they see you, they'll know how important you are."

"But I don't trust them." Needle looked up. "I've heard those names... but I've never seen them. Never heard them speak. I only trust Sister Solara. And you."

Jace couldn't help a small laugh. More like an involuntary cover for the stir of emotion inside.

"That's a problem, then." He said. "If you come with us, you'd need to stay in human form most of the time. Don't you look down on humans?"

"I don't care." Needle's eyes grew wider and wider.

"Alright, alright." Jace glanced at a group of orc laborers passing nearby. After they'd moved on, he said, "I'll have to think about it..."

"Think about what?" Needle pressed.

"Well, about what our chances are of surviving this portal business." Jace said.

"Then you're saying yes?" Needle grabbed Jace by the collar.

"Let go!" Jace smacked her hand away, then checked his surroundings to make sure no other orcs had noticed. "You're just a servant, little one. Don't disrespect a Karabor emissary."

"I know, I know." Needle said impatiently. "You said yes."

"So, you're not afraid of the danger on the other side?" Jace asked.

"I'm a warrior, Lord Naigag. A warrior shouldn't fear danger!" The black dragon patted the blades at her hip.

Jace crossed his arms and sighed. He wasn't sure if she was genuinely prepared, or just caught up in the heat of the moment like a kid.

But he couldn't really fault her for that. It wasn't like their group hadn't made terrible decisions on impulse before — more than once. Needle might fit right in with them on that front.
