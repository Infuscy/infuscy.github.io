Chapter 1365: The Shriekhall

The Shriekhall lay at the deepest part of the palace. Past several deep, wide, shadowy corridors, under the watchful eyes of orc guards and warlocks, they finally reached a grand stone gate. And behind this gate was the source of the high-pitched howls that erupted from Auchindoun from time to time. Even though Jace had been in Auchindoun so long he'd nearly tuned out the jarring noise, he still took a moment to adjust his breathing and steel himself for what lay ahead. Wopil raised his staff, aimed it at the crack of the stone gate, and chanted. Streams of purple shadow light crisscrossed the runic lines on the gate's seam, and the grinding sound of tumbling boulders came from all directions at once. After Wopil's spell ended, the two massive doors slid apart on their own, the grinding rumble drowning out everything else. The doors only opened a crack, but it was enough for four people to enter. Even before they got close, Jace could hear a steam-whistle-like shriek from inside. The ground vibrated with the sound, making his feet feel unsteady. Wopil glanced back at the three of them, their hesitation plain on his face, and couldn't hide a flicker of pride. "Don't worry too much, emissaries. Murmur is indeed a terrifying otherworldly being, but it's still under the Shadow Council's control. It hasn't caused any more trouble in the decades since the great explosion." The old warlock looked at Sabellian and gestured. "Follow me." The black dragon prince in fel orc guise strode in first. Jace and Sora exchanged a look and followed. Wopil's eyes flicked briefly to the staff in Jace's hand. A quick glance, but Jace caught it. He was extremely sensitive to anyone who had designs on Ulthalesh. Even the current leader of the Shadow Council couldn't escape his notice. Sabellian's guess was right. The old bastard had bad intentions. The Shriekhall lived up to its name — constantly filled with chaotic roaring. The deeper they went, the more it felt like being inside a high-speed vintage train, or riding the unfinished minecart metro from Ironforge to Stormwind. At least on the minecart metro, the constant grinding noise would eventually get adapted to, becoming background noise. Here, the sound fluctuated wildly, like an unstable, raging maniac who might start screaming at you at any moment, never letting anyone relax or adjust. Yet the passage was populated. Most were Broken and orc warlocks, each looking utterly exhausted, tormented in both body and mind. But they were undoubtedly the elite of the elite in the Shadow Council — why else would they be assigned to guard Murmur? And Jace's plan to bait Wopil into making a move against them, then claim self-defense and take out Wopil and even Murmur, hinged first on convincing these "jailers" of Murmur. Once these elites realized Wopil had been consumed by lust for power, and with Sabellian's "intelligence network" coordinating from outside, getting the entire Shadow Council to forget Wopil and submit to the Karabor emissaries would be only natural. "Master Naigagg." Wopil leaned in as the sound from the Shriekhall's depths momentarily subsided. "Can you feel Murmur's incredibly powerful soul energy?" "Very clearly." Jace said as he walked. "So what I said before wasn't empty talk." Wopil nodded. "Once that lord arrives and sees Murmur, he'll know how much effort and persistence I've put into ruling Draenor for the Burning Legion... Then I must ask you and Master Naihuojiaer to testify before him, before Lord Magtheridon, and especially before Lord Kil'jaeden." Jace said nothing. Sabellian, ahead of him, replied, "That's what we should do. For the Legion." "For the Legion, Master Wopil." Jace nodded. Jace's words were swallowed by a sudden burst of noise. He looked up at the shadowy depths of the hallway ahead, then back at Wopil. He reached inside and summoned a force, mimicking the crushing pressure he'd used in the Auchenai crypts when he'd drained the necromancers' souls — that overwhelming aura that had cowed everyone present. Wopil's expression didn't show much complexity; it stayed respectful. But Jace knew he'd received his "signal." Jace was letting the old bastard feel death closing in, making him desperate to find a way to kill him, panicked and unsure how. Then Sabellian could step in and offer him a lifeline. At that moment, another louder, higher-pitched cry crashed out like a tidal wave. Even Sabellian frowned, and Sora beside him lowered her head and gritted her teeth. This blast would rupture the eardrums of any ordinary mortal lacking mana, make them vomit or even pass out. Jace took a deep breath, suppressing the storm churning in his gut, steadying his rattled mind. When the roar subsided slightly, Jace felt like he could barely make a sound. Silence. This was a very powerful area-of-effect silence. Jace realized the Shriekhall was practically this sound elemental lord's domain, like Ragnaros's Molten Core beneath Blackrock Mountain. Which meant that without the Shadow Council's restraining seals, most of Auchindoun's "sound" would be forcibly enslaved by this elemental creature, making spellcasting impossible. After the noise faded a little, Wopil led the three fel orcs into a hall. Several soldiers were dragging a blood-faced warlock out. The warlock was trembling, eyes rolled back — probably wounded by that last shockwave. Seeing the warlock's state, Jace realized being hit by that sound wave during spellcasting would be far worse than getting Counterspelled. "We're here!" Wopil shouted from nearby, but his voice sounded muffled, like it was coming through several walls. Past the hall and around a corner, a wide corridor appeared. This passage was meticulously decorated with purple tents made from Shadow Council banners. Under each tent, tables held several soul shards, rows of potion vials, piles of shadow tomes, and spell books scattered on and under the tables. This was the Shadow Council's most secret place. At the end lay a purple rotunda lit by shadowfire, where rows of Broken priests and orc warlocks were channeling some kind of soul magic at the empty center. In the air, several pale blue soul energies twisted like serpents, plunging into the rotunda without showing what they'd latched onto. "Lord Terongor's legacy, gentlemen!" Wopil shouted excitedly, striding forward. Jace quickened his pace to keep up, carefully observing the rotunda, trying to sense if something invisible was there. After staring for a while, he found nothing. But getting closer, he noticed the purple rotunda wasn't empty. At the far end, there seemed to be shadows of standing stone pillars. What was that... Just as he craned his neck for a better look, the warlocks simultaneously stopped casting. Then, a blast of mind-bending noise erupted without warning right before their eyes! It was like ten thousand unknown beasts screaming at once, crashing straight into them. For an instant, Jace felt his vision "stutter," tearing apart... He instinctively covered his ears, but it was useless. The powerful sound waves ricocheted through the hall, punching through his hands over and over, battering his brain! And in that moment, as his vision fractured into countless pieces, a silvery-white, bluish magical silhouette appeared... It was a towering, quaking maelstrom, a spinning storm of a monster. It held a black scepter as large as a cathedral's massive chandelier post. Its face was a wide-mouthed, ferocious beast, crowned with an illusory diadem like churning white foam... It made no sound beyond the opening and closing of that massive maw, each motion releasing a more violent blast of sound. More warlocks and priests joined the front ranks of casters, apparently trying to suppress its fury with soul magic, until the swaying silver phantom gradually faded... Murmur... The image in that sound wave just now lingered like a nightmare. Jace almost wondered if he'd hallucinated. He looked toward the rotunda's depths again. This time, the shadows were clear. Two massive stones carved with orc runes, pulsing with dense soul energy. Jace had seen similar totems in Garadar and some abandoned orc villages in Nagrand — including that stone Bazun the Thunderlords shaman had given him. Those were much smaller than these, of course. And unlike the others, these appeared to be heavily corrupted by shadow magic... Sora had retreated several steps, her face terrible. Jace looked down at his hands. His fel orc illusion was already distorting from the repeated sound waves. But Sabellian quickly closed in, repairing his magical disguise during a lull in the noise as the phantom was suppressed. Jace steadied himself, looked up, and recalled the impact. He realized that despite their fury, the sound waves bouncing around the Shriekhall all seemed to orbit those elemental stones... Those were probably the "hitching posts" the Shadow Council used to trap this otherworldly creature.
