Chapter 1368: The Kirin Tor's Little Joke

If the beginning of the ethereal scrolls Jace had been given had some coherence — enough to piece together fragments of meaning — then the following sections were nearly impossible for Jace to interpret alone. He figured these were probably just raw spells, or extremely abstruse casting methods.

Considering this was a method that Dar'Khan, that respected researcher of the Twisting Nether in Quel'Thalas, had spent who knows how many years failing to understand, it was no surprise Jace couldn't make sense of it.

Fortunately, on the other side, they had help from the void lord Gaothros, plus a whole crowd of Kirin Tor mages analyzing and recording. That was bound to be more reliable than Dar'Khan and whatever ragtag underground crew he'd scraped together.

After over an hour of transmission, Jace had expended most of his mana and three Draenei soul shards. But Karlrez seemed completely unbothered, maintaining his highly accurate mimicry the whole time.

Then again, that bastard was running off the Redridge leyline node and Taralf's Crystal for stable power, unlike Jace's rough-and-ready casting conditions over here.

The other side went quiet for a moment — probably briefly discussing the content Jace had just transmitted — before Karlrez spoke. "Khadgar wants me to tell you, master, that this section of scroll matches his expectations. As mentioned in the previous section, to make Dalaran's mortal mages feel the chaotic and elusive mana spread of prophecy magic like the ethereals do, and thereby locate specific coordinates, they need extremely powerful mana sources as support. The kind that could make the Alterac Mountains dance for you..."

"In any case, Khadgar means that the arcane energy intensity mentioned in this scroll is probably approaching the absolute limit of what Dalaran can provide. In other words, they might need an artifact on the level of the Eye of Dalaran. But the Eye of Dalaran still has to power the subsequent Telogrus portal. If they use it to locate Telogrus, there won't be enough energy left for the portal."

"What's with the Alterac dancing?" The demon asked.

"An old joke from Dalaran." Jace said. "If the mana's strong enough, it could make the Alterac Mountains dance."

It was originally a joke students and teachers told to complain about assignments. But if Khadgar was saying it, he might mean it literally — making a mountain dance.

Back in the Blade's Edge Mountains, that ethereal technician Jabr had mentioned that making human wizards locate Telogrus in the vast Twisting Nether like the ethereals would require extremely powerful mana support. Jace had assumed it couldn't be that demanding. Couldn't Dalaran meet those conditions?

Now it seemed the ethereals' concerns weren't unfounded.

But if locating Telogrus was this difficult, how had Magister Umbric, the future leader of the void elves, managed it back then?

No, wait — by then, the doom lord Kazzak, who had descended on Azeroth alongside Archimonde, had already reactivated the Dark Portal in the Blasted Lands. The connection between shattered Draenor and Azeroth had been re-established. Many ethereals had used that to enter Azeroth.

Hell, ethereals had even shown up in Silvermoon working with Prince Kael'thas. So Magister Umbric could have just relied on the ethereals to easily locate the Telogrus Rift, not facing the same difficulties Jace and Khadgar were dealing with now.

"No other solutions?" Jace asked.

"Khadgar says there's one option now." Karlrez said quietly. "Ask King Anasterian to borrow the power of the Sunwell. But no non-high elf has ever set foot on Quel'Danas. And judging by the attitude in the Kirin Tor council, Prince Kael'thas himself seems to be deliberately avoiding any mention of the Sunwell. Khadgar feels that even if King Anasterian really agreed to use the Sunwell, it would take a terribly long debate to finalize..."

Jace felt a surge of anger. He asked, "But Vereesa and her sister Sylvanas's older sister, Quel'Thalas's own hero Alleria Windrunner, is still trapped here! Not to mention all the magisters and Farstriders here beside her. To save Silvermoon's most elite forces, they still can't authorize the use of the Sunwell?!"

"You too have been acknowledged as a hero of Quel'Thalas by Prince Kael'thas and the Sun King." Karlrez relayed. "Khadgar says if you and the other heroes weren't confirmed alive, the Sunwell option wouldn't even be brought up. Those high elves are actually worried that establishing a void connection with the Telogrus Rift might contaminate the Sunwell and destroy the very foundation of Quel'Thalas's survival. Khadgar agrees. He thinks it's a legitimate concern."

"Yes. It is." Jace said quietly.

Actually, in another timeline, the Sunwell did nearly get corrupted by void energy — and it was caused by Alleria herself, after she'd become a void elf. Kael'thas and Silvermoon's magisters fearing this was completely understandable. It affected the safety of tens of thousands of Quel'dorei.

"What about the Well of Eternity, then?" Jace asked. "I mean, the Well of Eternity on Mount Hyjal."

"Khadgar considered that too." Karlrez said. "But he thinks getting that reclusive race, who doesn't even care about their ancient Kaldorei allies, to help a group of soldiers from another continent touch their ten-thousand-year-old sacred site... well, the difficulty might not be much less than convincing the Sunstrider royal family to use the Sunwell."

"And it all has to happen within a month." Jace rubbed his brow.

He'd just finished transmitting the scroll's content. His mana was at a low point. Combined with this distraction, the array even flickered, the void communication channel destabilizing, forcing him to concentrate again to keep the ritual from collapsing.

Breaking it and restarting would cost far more than a little energy.

Then the demon lord said, "Khadgar says he has one more powerful backup mana source, and it doesn't risk contamination." Jace's eyes shifted behind his eyelids as he tested, "Karazhan?"

"Yes, Karazhan." The demon lord said. "But Khadgar also says it's a very risky choice. It could cause an even worse disaster. Considering the last disaster there affected half of Elwynn, the Kirin Tor probably won't approve his plan, and the King of Stormwind might have concerns too."

"So to do it, he'd have to go alone. The upside is he's familiar with Karazhan. The downside is losing Kirin Tor and Stormwind support. The chance of things spiraling out of control is high. So Khadgar himself isn't sure whether to do it."

"He said if Lady Aegwynn were here, none of these problems would exist."

"Lady Aegwynn?" Jace felt a jolt run through him. He looked up. "Why not go find her?"

There was a long silence on the other end. Then Karlrez said, "That human mage was just making a boring joke. He asked me to apologize for cracking wise at such a critical moment."

"Why is that a joke?" Jace said. "Karazhan was built by Aegwynn herself..."

Karlrez relayed, "Khadgar says the Order of Tirisfal searched for Aegwynn until Medivh destroyed it, and never found her. Many mages who tried to locate her had their memories wiped or were killed. 'Finding Aegwynn would solve everything' has practically become a running joke in the Kirin Tor."

"But what if they really could find Aegwynn?" Jace asked.

"Are you serious, master?" Karlrez said. "Do you know where Medivh's mother is?"

"Don't ask questions. Just pass the message to Khadgar. Follow orders!" Jace said irritably.

Karlrez muttered a few curses in obscure Ered'ruin. The other side went quiet again, then the demon said grumpily, "He wants to confirm you're not joking."

"I want to confirm too." Jace said. "If we could really find Aegwynn, would she be willing to help?"

"Khadgar says before the Dark Portal opened, Aegwynn tried to warn the kings that Medivh was attempting something very dangerous. But back then, no one — including him — realized what it meant..." Karlrez relayed word for word. "She also tried to stop her son, but failed, and went into hiding again. So she probably feels some guilt about this whole affair."

"North of Dustwallow Marsh, south of the Ragewater River's mouth, east of the great plains." Jace said slowly in Demonic. "We met Aegwynn there."

"What?" Morophil asked in surprise. "Are you sure that female mage was Aegwynn, master?"

"Shh." Jace closed his eyes, tilted his head toward the other side, and continued, "Is that specific enough? And don't ask me how I know."

"Hey, Master Khadgar?"

"He's already teleported away, master." Karlrez said.

"Fine." Jace sighed. "If there's nothing else, we're done here, Lord Karlrez."

"Yes." The demon said.

As the array burned out, Jace practically collapsed to the floor.

His heart was pounding. Not from mana exhaustion, but from realizing how many people, how many things, this plan might involve... He could only hope Khadgar's trip to Kalimdor went smoothly. Even if he was the one who'd personally run his blade through Medivh's chest.
