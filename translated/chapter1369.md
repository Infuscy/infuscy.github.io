## Chapter 1369: A Fleeting Dream

If Jace had never met Aegwynn, he wouldn't have had the confidence to reveal her location to Khadgar.

After all, he had absolutely no reason to know where Aegwynn was hiding. This secret wouldn't exist in the libraries of Dalaran or Karazhan. It was a gap Jace couldn't paper over no matter what excuse he made.

If Aegwynn ever found out that some random young mage had casually blown her hiding spot, she would not let that slide.

But he had met her... even exchanged a few casual words.

Considering that gray-haired woman in the forested hills north of Dustwallow Marsh looked so much like the legendary Aegwynn — and more importantly, she'd effortlessly seen through Queen Ysera's dream realm, something even black dragons couldn't detect —

Taking her for the legendary former Guardian was a reasonable guess.

From the moment the Expedition crossed the Dark Portal to now, including Jace and his group, everything they'd done was to undo the catastrophe Medivh had brought upon Azeroth.

If Aegwynn truly felt guilty for her son's crimes, this was a perfect chance to make amends. Khadgar was right. She shouldn't refuse.

But if Aegwynn agreed, they faced another problem — what about Ulthalesh?

Even though Ulthalesh had been sealed away by Aegwynn's teacher, she'd never seen the staff herself. Still, with eight hundred years of life giving her endless knowledge of the Burning Legion and demons, expecting her not to recognize the thing was completely unrealistic.

But from the fact that Khadgar had immediately teleported to Kalimdor upon hearing the news, he clearly had no intention of telling the Kirin Tor about contacting Aegwynn.

Because when it came to anything involving that Karazhan family, the Kirin Tor was cautious to a maddening degree. So Jace figured Khadgar planned to privately ask Aegwynn for help, have her use Karazhan's power to open a path from Azeroth into the Telogrus Rift, and keep the whole thing secret from the Kirin Tor.

Aegwynn probably had similar needs. She'd been hiding from the Kirin Tor for a very long time. She probably wouldn't agree to appear publicly in such an operation.

She also wouldn't enter the Telogrus Rift herself to participate in building and opening the portal. Thinking that, Jace relaxed a little.

If the fact that he possessed Ulthalesh was ever dragged out by the Kirin Tor and Khadgar, he could confess, voluntarily hand in the weapon, and maybe — given his identity as a hero who'd saved the Alliance multiple times — find a way to survive. Worst case, he'd end up in the Violet Hold with Kel'Thuzad. At least friends and family could visit.

If Aegwynn ever got wind of it, though, his days would be numbered.

Leaving the demon ritual chamber, Jace avoided the fel orcs guarding the portal construction site at night and headed toward his meeting spot with Sabellian.

He habitually rubbed his temples, as if that could relieve the headache from the intense communication ritual.

The Arcane Intellect had worn off. As Master Marin had once told him, the noticeable dulling of his thoughts and the weakening of his perception made him wonder which state was the real him.

Sabellian was waiting at a corner of the corridor ahead. Seeing Jace's expression, he asked, "Every time you go chat with Khadgar, you come back with a different mood. Let's hope this one's good."

"Absolutely good news for you." Jace said. "At least you don't have to deal with Aegwynn."

"Ah." Sabellian smiled. "So you finally dragged that lady into this. But you don't look like you're worried about Aegwynn."

"Did your father ever tell you about me consuming Teron Gorefiend's soul?" Jace asked.

"I told him." Sabellian said. "Back when we first got to Draenor, he didn't care about what was happening in Azeroth. I was the one lurking in Honor Hold, so I had a better handle on the other side's movements."

Jace said, "When I first swallowed Teron Gorefiend's soul, some blurry images from his memories flashed through my mind. They rarely appeared after that, and when they did, they were faint. But just now... right after I used Arcane Intellect, I suddenly saw a very clear memory image."

"As a close friend of Teron Gorefiend, what do you think? I don't want his soul being so strong it gives me some kind of split personality."

"That 'close friend' comment almost made me think I never learned Common." Sabellian chuckled. "Anyway, don't worry about it, kid. I don't see two souls in you. It's just a side effect of absorbing his soul too thoroughly. You're not an out-and-out soul-devourer, so you wouldn't know about this side effect."

"Then again, can't blame you. Who'd expect him to crawl so completely into your shell? It probably takes a death knight created by Gul'dan himself to pull off such a clean 'fusion.'"

"When you put it that way, I almost hope the side effect gets stronger." Jace said. "Because that was a memory about Murmur. I thought I could find a way to control Murmur through that memory. But the image disappeared instantly and had no sound."

Sabellian's expression grew serious. He thought for a moment. "Why not ask that little one kicked out by Ysera for help? She's more professional than I am."

"The little one under Queen Ysera's special protection." Jace corrected. "But... thanks for the suggestion."

"Don't mention it. I need to go find Wopil. See you." Sabellian said, summoning a cloud of dark, searing dust that engulfed Jace.

Back at their resting spot in the Arcane Sanctum, Sora was curled up in the room sleeping.

Her sleeping posture was close to a normal humanoid's. Considering green dragons were all sleepers, it wasn't easy for her to change the sleeping habits she'd grown up with.

Jace patted her shoulder and gently woke her. Sora's eyes shot open at the sight of Jace, the blue moonlight within flowing for a moment before returning to the red of a fel orc.

"What's wrong?" She looked around warily.

"I need your help." Jace sat down beside her. "Can you hypnotize me and enter my dream? Like you did with that half-dragon alchemist last time."

"Of course..." Sora said. "But what do you want to do?"

"I want to find Teron Gorefiend's memories." Jace said.

"You mean his soul's memories? But didn't they disappear years ago?" Sora asked.

"It seems seeing Murmur triggered something. Some echoes flashed up again." Jace said.

"Lie down, then." Sora said. "Let me look."

Jace obediently lay on the ground. Sora gently breathed out a puff of pink mist that settled over him. He couldn't help asking, "Do you think I could resist your sleep mist with a powerful warlock's willpower?"

But a nearby orc warlock glanced at Jace, frowned, and seemed to say something — Jace couldn't hear clearly.

Because the entire magnificent Eredar-style hall had already been swallowed by silver-white storms and shrieks. The piercing noise was nearly splitting everyone's skulls. Nearby, several weak-willed orcs had already fallen to their knees, blood streaming from their eyes and ears.

Before long, Jace felt like he couldn't hold out either.

He couldn't feel the shadow connection, couldn't sense any spells or magic. He looked around frantically. In his peripheral vision, a familiar figure in the distance was shouting something in his direction, but he still couldn't hear.

It was Wopil. A much younger Wopil.

His hair was still brown-black. He looked in his prime. But the scars on his face and his eyes — clouded from shadow torture like cataracts — were unmistakable.

Jace took a couple steps toward him. He wanted to find Wopil and demand the spell to control Murmur... But as soon as he moved half a step, someone grabbed his hand tightly from behind.

"Don't." A green-skinned female orc stared at him. "Come back. Don't go any further."

The voice was so clear it drowned out all other muffled sounds... Jace sat bolt upright, gasping for air, about to speak. Sora immediately covered his mouth and gestured with her eyes toward the outside.

He checked his pocket watch. It was already morning. Some vague sounds of orc soldiers talking came from outside.

"Did you pull me out?" Jace asked.

"Of course." Sora propped open Jace's eyelid with one hand and examined his fel orc's swollen eyes. "That was a very dangerous dream. Don't fall too deep into it, or the price you pay might far outweigh what you gain."

"Fine, I'll listen to you. It's not like we don't have a backup plan." Jace sighed. "Looks like we're still relying on Morophil to sneak in and use mental magic to solve this."

"Strange, though. If seeing Murmur could stir up Gorefiend's memories, why didn't the same thing happen when I saw Deathwing in Grim Batol? Deathwing's pressure and terror should be much stronger than Murmur's, right?"

"But don't forget." Sora said quietly. "When he attacked Auchindoun, he was still a living orc. When he saw Deathwing, he was already a walking corpse. His memories and emotions as a living being would be far more vivid than after death."

"Makes sense." Jace said.

"Next time you enter the Shriekhall, you might remember more." Sora said. "I'm not just saying that to comfort you. It's very possible, especially now that you're aware of it."

"But the next time I go in will be the showdown." Jace said. "Hopefully it appears 'in time.'"

Sora smiled. "Let me help you get some real sleep. You didn't rest at all tonight."

"Thanks." Jace nodded.
