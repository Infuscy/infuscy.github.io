## Chapter 137: One Warlock Is Trouble Enough

"What do you mean, how is that possible?" Jace said, glancing over at the boisterous crowd. "Beating me would be a cinch for you. Forget it — remember how we sparred outside the city gates?"

"I mean if you went all out," Grede added.

Jace's gaze snapped back. He knew what Grede meant by "all out."

The dwarf went on, "If there's some distance between us when we start, I don't think I could close the gap before you finished your spell. Your casting… look, once you get the words out, I'm either stumbling around like that panther with no idea where I am, or I'm frozen stiff on the spot. Either way, you'd have plenty of time to put a blade in me."

"You could throw your axe or sword while I'm chanting," Jace said.

"Right," Grede said. "I could. But that means betting everything on luck, Jace. I'd be giving up my best shot at rushing you, all for the slim chance a thrown weapon connects. If I miss, that's it — I lose. And even if I hit, that doesn't mean you've lost."

"What does that tell you? It tells you the only way I can beat you now is by gambling. Don't forget I've only got one eye — judging distance is basically guesswork for me at this point. And you've always been slippery as hell, whether it's dodging a blade or anything else, even by dwarven standards. I know your skill. With the gap closing the way it is, I don't like my odds."

Jace studied Grede's eyes. What was that look? He couldn't read it. But he had a nagging feeling — especially considering how Grede had been talking about finding some kind of magic to learn lately. Was he eyeing the shadow forces again? Did he want to turn into a Dark Iron dwarf?

"I'd rather not compare notes on who'd win between us," Jace said. "Want to grab something to eat?"

"Quit changing the subject." Grede kept his voice low. "I'm not saying I'm unhappy about it, Jace. You've grown faster than I ever imagined, and in the two months since you learned shadow magic, I've been watching you — and I haven't noticed any change. You're still the same Jace. Maybe there really are people like you who can resist the corruption shadow magic brings. Maybe the legends about Grim Batol were exaggerated from the start…"

"The legends about Grim Batol weren't exaggerated." Jace cut him off. "I've spent a hell of a lot of time and energy keeping shadow's influence in check, Grede. Don't act like it's nothing. Every single day I spend a long time going back through my old thoughts, my old feelings, asking myself if there's any reason to be angry, any reason to be jealous, whether I'm having desires I don't actually want — because I'm terrified these things aren't coming from me, they're coming from the shadow. If I give in to them, I might never get back to who I used to be. I'd become a Sesso you don't recognize."

He wasn't exaggerating his fears in the slightest. He knew how those cautionary tales ended, and he refused to become one of them.

And it was precisely because of that that he'd been able to grow, bit by bit, through training and self-conditioning — so that even with that panther's eyes locked on him, muscles coiled to strike, he could stay perfectly calm and chant the spell exactly as he'd planned.

He didn't want fear. He didn't want anger. He didn't want impatience or fury. He just wanted desperately to hold on to who he really was.

Grede's eyes flickered. "Listen to you, talking like you know Grim Batol inside and out."

"I probably know a little more than you think," Jace said before he could stop himself.

Right now he was starting to think Grede was genuinely drawn to shadow magic. Or maybe it was just simple envy, something uncomplicated.

Or maybe — and this was the part that scared him — the shadow energy radiating off Jace had been subtly influencing Grede through their long time together, breeding in him an unnatural attraction to the shadow. He hoped to hell that wasn't it.

"Fine," Grede said. "I've never been to Grim Batol either. It's just a legendary mountain city to me. You could go dig through the Stormwind library for history — it wouldn't surprise me if you knew more than I did. This is getting too serious, don't you think? We just beat the hell out of a vicious beast, and neither of us got hurt. This ought to be something to celebrate, and instead we're sitting here talking like somebody died… The booze ran out, by the way. I'm going to find someone to buy some more. Don't you think jawing away like this with nothing to drink is a waste of good conversation?"

The dwarf clapped Jace on the shoulder and stood, heading toward the other end of the cabin. When he came back, Jace cleared his throat and asked, "Do you want to learn dark magic?"

Grede let out a long breath. "I don't know."

"Dammit." Jace leaned across and grabbed Grede's beard, forcing the dwarf to look at him. "The war's barely been over a year or two, Grede. What kind of world is Azeroth? Here, some people die but stay alive. Some people are alive but might as well be dead. Some people can destroy a city of tens of thousands with a snap of their fingers. Some people can make an army of thousands obey their every thought without saying a word. Some people can destroy a whole race — a whole civilization — with a few sentences. Some people can lie dormant at the bottom of the sea or in the heart of a desert for tens of thousands of years. Some people can manipulate everyone's dreams. Some people can split an entire planet in half with a single sword stroke!"

"You've lost one eye. Two years and you're already giving up on ever healing it? Is healing this eye harder than sundering ancient Kalimdor? Maybe there's a mage in Dalaran who could conjure you a brand-new eye with magic. Maybe for him it's easier than baking a crab cake!"

Grede, beard still caught in Jace's fist, forced the words out through clenched teeth: "I didn't realize you hated shadow magic this much."

"Maybe I just know a little more about shadow than you do," Jace pressed on. "Whatever the case, don't go making a life-or-death decision on a whim, Grede. Let's try to fix your problem first. We've got plenty of possibilities we haven't even tested yet — magic, elves, whatever. Let's exhaust every safer option and hit a dead end before we start entertaining whatever half-baked notion crawled into your head. All right?"

"I followed your lead against that panther and nearly got my throat torn out. It's not too much to ask that you listen to me once, risk-free, is it?"

Grede nodded hard.

Jace realized he'd gotten worked up enough. He let go, sat back in his seat, and took a pull of beer. That faint ghost of alcohol cooled him down, just a little.
