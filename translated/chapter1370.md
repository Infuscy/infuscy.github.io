## Chapter 1370: Because Naigagg Must Die

No matter how many complications dragging Aegwynn into this might cause, for Jace right now, it was all academic.

If Khadgar was willing to risk going after that ultimate headache of the Order of Tirisfal and the Kirin Tor for the Expedition, then Jace couldn't afford to slack off on his end.

At this point, personnel were steadily withdrawing along the route from Terokkar Forest to Honor Hold and Wildhammer Stronghold. The entire Expedition had to concentrate at Allerian Stronghold in less than a month. From now until the portal was opened was undoubtedly the most critical and dangerous period. Jace had no energy left to worry about what was happening on the other side.

In the evening, standing in Auchindoun's central plaza, Jace looked at the portal foundation frame that had already been erected, observing every peon and overseer at work.

He found that he'd gone from hoping the project would finish as soon as possible to hoping something would happen to delay it, stretch the timeline a bit.

After all, neither side of the demon ritual — his and Karlrez's — seemed to be going smoothly. One side involved the extremely dangerous Aegwynn, the other involved Murmur, which could potentially shake all of Draenor.

Sabellian had said the last few days that he was plotting with Wopil on how to use Murmur against Jace, but Jace hadn't seen any negotiation between them.

Rather than scheming behind Jace's back, Wopil seemed more enthusiastic about the portal construction.

He'd been personally supervising the demon rune carving on-site from morning to night. With him watching, the builders didn't dare slack off.

At this rate, Jace figured it would be finished in about two more weeks.

As he watched a group of orc warlocks gathered together discussing something, trying to guess what they were talking about, a tall fel orc walked up beside him.

"What's it like knowing a bunch of people are planning your murder behind your back?" Sabellian asked.

"Are you saying there's progress?" Jace asked.

"There is." Sabellian said. "Master Wopil and I have a lot of common ground."

"I haven't noticed you two talking these past couple of days." Jace said. "I thought there was a problem."

"Of course we had to be careful. Couldn't let a paranoid, sensitive bastard like you realize that Lord Magtheridon's emissaries had developed a rift."

Sabellian smiled.

"Fine. But isn't this a bit fast?" Jace said. "You didn't use any mind control magic or illusions to cut corners, did you?"

"I'd prefer to call it 'clicking.'" Sabellian twirled his finger. "And that trip to the Shriekhall scared more than just you. It scared Wopil too."

"What?" Jace asked. "Why would he be scared? I thought he looked pretty smug in there. Totally drunk on Murmur's confidence."

"That trip solidified his resolve to kill you." Sabellian said. "He's now a hundred percent certain you're going to kill him. His exact words were, 'Though he hides it expertly, he can never escape my perception.' I'll admit, you're very skilled at manipulating shadow energy to stir emotions. But crafting such complex, strong feelings that even an old warlock who's spent decades in the Shadow Council is utterly convinced..."

"Maybe I didn't fool him at all." Jace said. "Maybe what he sensed really was my true intention. Wopil isn't Kargath Bladefist."

"Maybe. But I suspect the most important thing is that he's realized whether you want to kill him or not, he might be abandoned by the Legion anyway." Sabellian said. "Gul'dan's betrayal already aroused Kil'jaeden's suspicion. In Wopil's eyes, our 'portal plan' probably isn't just Magtheridon's relief mission — it's essentially the Burning Legion taking over the Shadow Council's rule from him and Bleakheart, to prevent another Gul'dan situation."

"When that demon lord descends on Auchindoun, Wopil will have to hand over control of Murmur. Then he becomes a completely useless old man. So the portal construction is a countdown for us, and for him too. He needs to use this time to maximize his 'value.'"

The black dragon continued. "A few days ago, after I expressed dissatisfaction with you, he immediately started probing about your staff's origin. I told him it wasn't a reward from the Burning Legion, but something you acquired yourself in Azeroth... He latched onto that like a drowning man grasping a lifeline."

"You can see, after Terongor left Auchindoun, under pressure from the Alliance, the Auchenai, and the black dragons, the Shadow Council never made good use of Auchindoun's soul energy. All they could do was barely catch some wandering ghosts to make soul shards. But even those shards were powerful enough."

"He thinks with your staff, Auchindoun's souls could be used more efficiently and quickly. Even the Burning Legion's demon lords would appreciate such efficiency. Maybe they'd let him help take Karabor, tame the raging souls filling it..."

"So that's the new 'value' he's found." Jace said.

"Exactly." Sabellian continued. "And that's not all. I hinted to him about Kargath Bladefist and the current Horde's dissatisfaction with Magtheridon's rule..."

"Wopil didn't state his thoughts outright, but I could feel he believes the Shadow Council could once again become a tool for demons to supervise and rule the Horde. At the very least, when Kargath tries to overthrow the Legion's rule, the Shadow Council could serve as a force to curb his ambition."

"Wopil thinks he can use these few hundred shadow-corrupted black-skinned orcs against tens of thousands of red-skinned kin in Hellfire Citadel?" Jace asked.

"Naturally, my little guidance helped." Sabellian showed a mysterious smile. "I told him that countless warlocks in Hellfire Citadel and Karabor still miss the Shadow Council from Gul'dan's era. They just don't dare openly express their worship of Gul'dan. The Scepter of Sargeras is waiting for someone truly worthy to wield it. Then the Shadow Council could return to center stage, and even Kargath Bladefist would have to bow in worship, just like 'the good old days.'"

"So isn't that 'another Gul'dan situation'?" Jace asked.

Sabellian said, "I had to work pretty hard to convince him that dealing with you was the only way to break the Shadow Council and himself out of their dead end. There was no other option. So don't you go opening your mouth and making that hotheaded old man cool down."

"Of course I won't." Jace said. "So what's the specific plan? Don't forget, we need to use his murder plot against me to control Murmur."

"Not yet." Sabellian looked down at the busy peons. "I think it'd be too obvious if I made the plan."

"Obvious?" Jace asked.

"He was the one who first tried to drive a wedge between us. But I'm the one who helped him decide to kill you. I analyzed the pros and cons and laid out the Shadow Council's future path." Sabellian said. "If I then propose such a clear plan involving the Shriekhall, it'll probably arouse his suspicion..."

"It would feel like I disguised myself as a human noble, trying to seduce some proud Quel'dorei arcanist. But after just a little contact, I find her more enthusiastic than me. She's already picked out which Dalaran inn to spend the night at. I take off her robe and find she's got brand-new enchantments under there... Then I need to think twice."

"At the end of the day, I'd think I was entering a frozen exterior hiding centuries or millennia of burning passion, but instead I'm walking into some dragon's elaborately prepared arcane bomb."

"You didn't have to explain it in that much detail." Jace said. "But you have a point. Let's split this. On your end, you show weakness, tell him you can't handle me, let him think about other helpers on his own. That way Murmur should enter his consideration. He saw clearly how suppressed I was in the Shriekhall. On my end, I'll request another visit to the Shriekhall, letting him naturally choose to use Murmur against me."

"And what reason will you give for requesting another visit to the Shriekhall?" Sabellian asked with narrowed eyes.

Jace thought about it. Doing it directly would be too obvious. He'd already been in once to show the Legion lords Murmur's power. Next came waiting for the demon lord to arrive. Why would he need to go again?

He leaned against a broken wall and thought for a moment, then said, "I'll have Sora do it."

"What?" Sabellian said.

"Emissary Sosala came to Auchindoun as a Dragonmaw dark shaman." Jace said. "I can have Sora request another entry into the ShriekHall from Wopil, on the grounds of conducting in-depth research on this unfamiliar alien elemental's 'elemental language.'"

"Wopil will definitely refuse." Sabellian said. "The old man doesn't trust her. Not just because he doesn't trust shamans, but because her background has almost nothing to do with the Shadow Council or Gul'dan..."

"So you go suggest he accept. But demand I accompany her. Frame it as me walking right into his trap." Jace said. "And come up with a reason why I'd definitely go in with that dark shaman."

"Hmm..." Sabellian nodded slowly. "Now that's a bit more interesting."
