## Chapter 1372: Sorsala's Reason

When Jace and Sora reached the bend in the long corridor before the doors of the Screaming Hall, Sabellian was already waiting there.

"And Master Vopiel?" Jace asked.

"He's inside," Sabellian said. "Probably wrapping things up. He'll come out to receive you shortly. You arrived a bit earlier than agreed — not quite the behavior one expects from an arrogant Karabor emissary."

"I'm not alone." Jace glanced at the female orc beside him, draped in a brownish-green cloak. "So what reason did you come up with for me to come with her?"

"What else could it be? Lovers, of course." Sabellian shot a sidelong glance at Sora.

"Do we really have to make it so complicated?" Jace looked toward the doors and lowered his voice. "I could've just said I cared about Murmur's elemental language, or..."

"Your image is already set in stone," Sabellian said. "The whole of Auchindoun knows you're Gul'dan's loyalist. You wouldn't be interested in elements. Your reason had to be special, didn't it? Otherwise I could enter the Screaming Hall myself. If we'd found another excuse, I'd be going in with her — there wouldn't be a strong enough reason to bring you along. Put yourself in Master Vopiel's shoes."

"Fair enough," Jace said.

"But..." Sora leaned in. "I... I don't know how to..."

"No need to act lovey-dovey like your mother Queen Ysera and that lot headed by Eranikus. Orc couples interact much more normally." Sabellian lowered his voice. "Just treat him like your superior — not that different from how you always act, really."

"R-right." Sora nodded and agreed. Jace had half-expected her to defend her queen. But the corridor saw frequent traffic from other cult orcs, and arguing too loudly risked exposing their problem.

Just then, the doors groaned, followed by a grinding scrape as the crack slowly widened.

Vopiel stood between the doors, waving in their direction. Beside him, a guard bellowed out their names over the elemental reverberations.

"And your real lover?" Sabellian asked.

Jace looked toward the other corridor entrance where Needleflame stood holding her warglaives. "She just said goodbye to your daughter."

Sabellian's eyes swept the wall nearby before he seemed to realize he was embarrassing himself and looked down. "Seems reliable enough."

"She is." Jace beckoned to Sora. "Lead the way then, Lady Sorsala."

"I'm worried my nervousness will make him suspicious," Sora mumbled.

"Don't worry. Be as nervous as you like," Jace said. "Let him see how terrified you are of Murmur. Let him have one last moment of pride over what he thinks he's protecting."

"Didn't know you were a poet," Sabellian snorted, following behind them.

Once through the doors, the familiar roar that had traumatized everyone last time rang out again. But Jace had drunk a Nature Protection Potion in advance this time, which dampened the shockwaves echoing from the Screaming Hall. That, plus experience from last time, kept him from immediately panicking.

Morofel had already slipped in. Even through focused demon detection, Jace could feel her presence through the soul link amid the sonic vibrations.

While Vopiel walked out to greet Jace and the others, she melted in like a patch of shadow, undetected by Vopiel or the orcs guarding the entrance. Jace figured the orcs guarding this place were more alert to Murmur himself — after all, anyone trying to sneak in invisibly would've been caught by the patrolling Eyes of Kilrogg outside.

But with Needleflame guiding and directing, those drifting Eyes of Kilrogg weren't a problem either. She always claimed to be a warrior, but she was better suited as a rogue or stalker — then again, this wasn't a game, so the distinction didn't matter much.

As they walked, Jace observed the orcs' attitudes and emotions toward him.

Sabellian had said Vopiel entered the Screaming Hall early to prepare. Jace suspected he might already be setting a trap. But these orcs looked no different from last time — visibly exhausted, terrified of Murmur's power, and hypervigilant against another potential loss of control. Nobody seemed to have spare energy to pay much attention to the repeat visitors.

Once Vopiel made his move, Jace would need to quickly win these people over and paint Vopiel as the enemy of all.

Given that many of these warlocks and cult guards were high-ranking Shadow Council members, each of them had spent years in this sealed palace with Murmur, possibly knowing the monster better than Vopiel himself. If they couldn't eliminate Murmur in the end, at least they could help contain the situation and keep its power from reaching the portal outside.

"Master Teron made his judgment on this otherworldly elemental before departing for Azeroth," Vopiel said as he walked ahead. "He believed Murmur was a monster beyond any form of communication — fundamentally different from those elements with self-awareness. Perhaps it has its own consciousness, but that consciousness contains nothing beyond the drive to destroy and devour all things orderly..."

He turned to look at Sora. "So I was surprised, Lady Sorsala, that you believed it could respond to your call."

Sabellian tilted his head to watch Sora's reaction. Jace kept walking forward, as though unconcerned with their conversation. Sora glanced at her two fel orc companions, then back at Vopiel, lifting her chin slightly.

"I'm not interested in whether it will respond to me, Master Vopiel."

"Oh?" The old warlock seemed suddenly intrigued.

"We've long mastered the technique of enslaving elements through twisted souls and shadow in both Azeroth and Karabor," Sora explained calmly. "I simply want to see if I can use the same method to enslave this unprecedented type of elemental... After all, its power is about to be sacrificed to the Legion. I need to satisfy my curiosity before the portal opens. This is also the shared desire of all the shaman-priests in Karabor who walk this path."

"Of course, I don't plan to enslave Murmur itself. But the sound element that fills the entire Screaming Hall will be more than enough to verify my hypothesis." After she finished, Sabellian looked at Jace as if to ask, "You taught her that?"

Jace gave no response. It went without saying — of course he'd taught her that. Sora's delivery had been spot-on. He'd specifically convinced her to view these wisdom-corrupted orcs from a dragon's perspective, not to think too highly of these so-called masters. And Sora had done exactly that.

"Dark Shaman." Vopiel savored the title. "Sounds like you're Ner'zhul's student, not Zuluhed's. Zuluhed never liked enslaving elements with dark energy. The last time I saw him, he still prayed to Nagrand's elements — even though those elements no longer answered the Horde."

Sora shot a glance toward Vopiel. Jace's heart tightened.

Vopiel seemed familiar with both Zuluhed and Ner'zhul. Jace's knowledge of these things came largely from the game. Had Ner'zhul trained so-called dark shamans in Karabor? Jace had no idea about any of that.

He hadn't had time to prepare Sora for such detailed questions, either.

He moved his lips, wanting to help, but then thought better of it. As a Karabor warlock who supposedly looked down on the cult, he shouldn't be jumping in to explain elemental matters for a "dark shaman" like Sora.

But after a moment of hesitation, Sora spoke. "The path I walk has nothing to do with Ner'zhul, that betrayer of all. I figured everything out gradually in Grim Batol, and then at Dragonmaw Fortress after returning through the Dark Portal. The one who helped me on this path was Warlord Nekros, not Chieftain Zuluhed, who left Azeroth long ago."

"I recall Nekros was someone who hated magic — elemental power, shadow, all of it. So when did he turn to the shamanic path?" Vopiel asked as they walked.

"From the moment he obtained the black dragon's treasure, the Demon Soul," Sora said, steadying her voice. "That artifact was enough to enslave even the mightiest dragons, let alone the elements that crawl beneath them... Whoever possessed something like that couldn't help but desire the power to control everything."

She actually managed to talk her way out of it?

Jace couldn't help but glance at her twice more. Sabellian also took a deep breath — whether from surprise at Sora's quick thinking or discomfort at the mention of the Demon Soul.

Of course, inside, Sora was already a wreck. Not just emotionally — even her fierce crimson face was trembling slightly.

"But looking at you now, I don't think you can succeed," Vopiel said coldly.

"That's exactly why I had to come," Jace said. "I can't let the finest dark shaman to survive Dragonmaw Fortress throw her life away for nothing."

"Understandable." The old warlock looked at them and nodded, then turned and continued forward.

Jace placed a hand on Sora's shoulder — she was a full head shorter than him — and gave her a look that said "it's fine." Up ahead, Sabellian relaxed his jaw, working his teeth loose.
