Chapter 1373: Who Is the Traitor?

Deep in the Screaming Hall, a row of Broken priests and warlocks channeled soul energy into the two elemental stones. In the center of the circular chamber where Murmur dwelt, nothing was visible except the occasional ripple of light distortion.

The elemental stones were in a dominant phase, temporarily suppressing Murmur's power.

But this was clearly cyclical. Soon enough, unable to bear the suppression any longer, it would struggle to break free, triggering a thunderous roar that would shake all of Auchindoun. It had been this way every cycle since Teron and the Shadow Council bound it here years ago.

Jace kept his attention fixed on Vopiel, whether or not his gaze was directed that way.

Partly he wanted to see if the old bastard was planning to make a move, and partly he wanted to see if Vopiel would chant any incantations toward the elemental stones in the meantime.

But Vopiel remained calm, as though he'd long grown accustomed to the spectacular elemental chaos here. His lips never moved.

Then he turned to look at Sora. "Now is the time, Lady Sorsala. Seize the opportunity."

"Thank you, Master Vopiel." Sora stepped forward.

Her voice still carried a note of panic — obvious enough to a warlock like Jace, and probably to Vopiel as well.

But that didn't matter. Facing an unfamiliar and powerful otherworldly elemental lord, nervousness was normal for any shaman. This was the main reason Jace had dared let Sora accept Vopiel's test.

But he wasn't the only one using emotional fluctuations to deceive Vopiel. Vopiel was deceiving Jace too. Having planned this murder, he betrayed no hostility toward Jace at the scene — not even as much as he'd shown during their last visit.

Still, Jace had to admit that if he hadn't coordinated with Sabellian in advance, he truly wouldn't have been able to tell Vopiel was planning to move against him.

This also made him understand why the surrounding warlocks and orc soldiers didn't know about Vopiel's plan. Any orc subordinate who couldn't control their killing intent might have revealed Vopiel's intentions to Jace in advance. A veteran warlock like Vopiel couldn't possibly be unaware of that.

Besides, a warlock who demonstrated his power with some terrifying magic of unknown origin in the Auchenai crypts just to intimidate the cult members... even if Vopiel hadn't planned a murder, plenty in the cult were already sharpening their blades.

Before long, the warlocks maintaining the soul energy began to struggle.

Sora seemed to sense something too. She took an involuntary step back — not from tension or fear, but because the broad circular chamber began to ripple with an invisible sonic energy from the center. It rotated outward with a humming drone, battering against her body again and again...

Jace knew Murmur was about to use this power to manifest again.

At that moment, Vopiel's heart surged with barely restrained, violent emotion. As the sound rose in pitch and intensity, the echoes in the Screaming Hall soon climbed to a volume that would terrify any mortal into screaming...

The front-row warlocks maintaining the binding struggled to keep pouring in soul energy. Suddenly, the old warlock spun around and pointed at Jace, shouting at the same time. Sabellian moved in tandem, trapping Sora in a cage of burning claws. Behind them, waves of intense emotion surged — from surprise, to anger, to resolve...

It had begun.

Jace looked back. Rows of orc soldiers in black heavy plate drew their blades and hand axes, rising one after another and advancing toward him.

The powerful sonic waves from Murmur's struggle made it impossible for him to chant any incantation. The entire Screaming Hall had become the sound elemental lord's domain — no room for any other noise.

Even for someone as skilled with shadow as Jace, without incantations he could at most push and pull at the chaotic shadow energy, unable to quickly form decisive spells.

This was the opening Vopiel had been waiting for. A powerful spellcaster who couldn't cast was just a fish on the chopping block.

Sabellian smirked as he watched. If he hadn't sealed Sora away in time, keeping the advancing orcs from harming her, Jace might have genuinely worried that he'd switched to Vopiel's side.

Jace gripped his staff with both hands, glancing between the black dragon, Vopiel, and the orc warriors drawing their weapons.

"You've betrayed the Legion! You've betrayed Lord Magtheridon!" he roared fiercely, though he had no idea how far his voice carried.

"You're the one betraying the Legion. You..." The old warlock strained his throat to a scream, his voice already cracking from the sustained high-frequency ringing, but still barely intelligible.

"...You want to destroy the Shadow Council. You want to overthrow Lord Magtheridon. You want to take up the Scepter of Sargeras and become Lord Kil'jaeden's agent in Draenor. Your ambition has been laid bare, Naijag. Accept your—"

Another wave of demonic sound surged higher, drowning out everything he wanted to say next. Behind him, a swelling silver-white torrent of air roared upward, forming an abstract humanoid shape.

In the pitch-black circular hall, amid the colliding currents of air, the hysterical old warlock's crazed shouting made Jace's mind reel for a moment.

Wait...

The sonic roar continued. His disguise was coming apart piece by piece beneath his thick crimson cloak, starting slowly from his hands.

A dozen orc warriors in black enchanted helmets charged through the shaking air and sound like ghosts, closing in. Seeing Jace standing alone, Vopiel could no longer suppress his smugness. Together with Sabellian, he began to laugh triumphantly.

I knew your ambition all along. I knew what you were scheming. And in the end, you walked right here, right into my trap. Now accept your fate, warlock...

Suddenly, a burst of flame erupted from Jace's hand. A demonic rune seared into the ground flared blood-red, and from it emerged a towering demonic shadow.

Blinding fire shot out like serpents, cutting through black pauldrons and helmets as easily as slicing tofu. Morofel emerged from among several smoking, fallen corpses, her demonic runes shimmering like molten gold through the rippling, blurred air...

Everyone in the Screaming Hall was stunned by the sudden appearance of this female demon. The nearest warriors either fell back or froze in place, not daring to advance. On the other side, Vopiel's blackened face instantly drained of all color. The triumphant smile hadn't even had time to fully fade.

Only the warlocks and priests at the very front of the circular chamber remained calm, continuing to channel soul energy in an attempt to suppress Murmur's struggle. They knew that no matter what happened behind them, if they failed, everything would be destroyed along with them.

"He couldn't have summoned—"

A cult warlock's raspy voice was barely audible before it was swallowed by the waves of roaring. Jace seized the moment and shouted at the top of his lungs, "Glory to the Master!"

"Glory to the Master! The Legion shall rule!"

"The traitor shall fall!"

He kept his arm raised, continuing to shout until the people around him could clearly hear his voice. Then he whirled around and pointed at Vopiel. "Kill this second Gul'dan! Prove your loyalty to the Legion!"

"Prove your loyalty to the Legion!" Morofel roared in Demonic. Under her current demonic power, her voice actually shook some of Murmur's echoes apart — even startling Jace.

The cult soldiers instinctively raised their weapons again, but they no longer knew which way to point their blades. Morofel paid them no mind. She simply strode toward Vopiel step by step.

Vopiel was clearly panicking now. He looked at Sabellian beside him, but Sabellian remained motionless, just watching... Jace had been cowed by Murmur's power, and so had Vopiel. When Sabellian and the cult's elite no longer supported him, what did he have left?
