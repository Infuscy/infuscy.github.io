Chapter 1376: The Echo Fades

News of Vopiel's death and Murmur's banishment spread instantly throughout the Shadow Labyrinth without needing to be passed around. The violent earthquake, the thunderous roar that carried across most of the Bone Wastes, the trembling of the elemental stones — none of it could be ignored...

But at the same time, word also spread that Vopiel had tried to use Murmur to kill the Karabor emissary and seize the demonic treasure.

So aside from some chaos stirred up by a few draenei and Broken who tried to rebel or escape, the orcs didn't descend into disorder from the upheaval — not from the Screaming Hall to the Arcane Sanctum, the Pulpit, and the portal beyond.

What Jace had learned during his time in this palace was that as long as the orcs believed what they were doing was for the demon's arrival in Auchindoun, for the Burning Legion and their Lord Magtheridon — and that the enemy they were dealing with was a rival or traitor of the Legion...

...things could always be kept under control.

After all, even Blakart, when preaching about his "Dark Lord," had to start by praising the Legion, subtly hinting that the Burning Legion and that Dark Lord were somehow connected.

Maybe Kil'jaeden, enraged by Gul'dan and Ner'zhul's betrayals and unable to fully trust rebellious orc leaders like Kargath, had grown increasingly suspicious of the surviving Shadow Council.

But his suspicion wasn't necessarily wrong.

Jace could see that the highest-ranking orc warlocks besides Vopiel were actually the ones in the entire cult least loyal to the Burning Legion.

At least their loyalty wasn't based on worship of demons themselves, but on the benefits demons might bring — or more bluntly, on ultimate power.

So the rewards and dark blessings that awaited on the other side of the portal became Jace's reason for persuading them to trust him for now.

As for whether Vopiel had betrayed the Legion, whether greed had consumed his reason, whether Naijag and these fel orcs from Karabor were seizing power or acting in self-defense — the orc warlocks didn't really care much.

Of course, besides all these reasons, there was another equally important factor that made the high-ranking warlocks and cult members of the Shadow Labyrinth bow at Jace's feet.

That was the fact that Jace had, right in front of all the Screaming Hall's keepers, forcibly chanted Murmur out of existence using the elemental stones.

Vopiel had always claimed he had the power to banish Murmur. His subordinates Blakart and the other cult warlocks believed it, of course — but no one had ever actually witnessed him do it.

What's more, the old warlock had always said he was the only one in Draenor since Teron Gorefiend who could banish Murmur — not even Deathwing or Gul'dan could do it. But now a Dragonmaw warlock from Karobar had shown up and demonstrated on the spot how to send this otherworldly evil god packing.

And the one who claimed he could do it got himself smashed to paste by a single hammer blow on the spot...

That was enough for the cult up and down to understand why Magtheridon had sent someone like this to help Vopiel crush High Priest Maladaar and take charge of summoning the new demon lord.

After ordering the orcs to begin cleaning up the Screaming Hall, Jace and Sora returned to the Karabor emissaries' quarters. The Arcane Sanctum was full of orcs running back and forth shouting. Wargs and felhounds barked from every direction.

The succubus servants summoned by warlocks to guard soul fragments and magical artifacts watched Jace and Sora pass with unease, then nervously looked away when their eyes met.

"You should move into Vopiel's hall, my master!" the imp clutching Jace's demonic satchel said. "It's way more comfortable than this dark little nest! Then I wouldn't be just an imp anymore — I'd be the Shadow Council leader's imp!"

"I just want to get back to my tower bedroom and sleep," Jace said.

Not many days left, he thought.

Back at the quarters, he collapsed onto the draenei's hard stone bench. It was hard to believe they'd just been fighting a terrifying elemental lord capable of destroying the world.

Well, he hadn't actually been the one doing the fighting. That had been Sabellian.

If the guy hadn't tried to twist elemental power with dragonfire, he wouldn't have drawn Murmur's wrath — buying Jace enough time to cast.

"The Queen's earring?" Sora across from him suddenly gasped as if startled. Jace tapped her clenched right hand. "It's here."

Sora looked down, mechanically opened her palm, saw the faint green-glowing earring, and finally let out a long breath.

"Now that Vopiel and Maladaar are both dead," she said, staring at the pendant, "those spirits should be ready to help us too."

"Once things settle down here, I'll go ask the spirits right away," Jace said. "Remind them."

Sora nodded, then looked up and asked, "So, how do you think I did this time?"

"Exceeded expectations," Jace said without hesitation.

"Then your expectations must have been ridiculously low," Sora said. "I feel like I was just reacting on instinct."

"Your 'instinct' controlled the dream and helped me complete the ritual," Jace said.

"With a horrible surprise like that, I don't know what else I could have done," Sora said.

"Dealing with an old warlock like that, surprises were unavoidable," Jace said. "If he hadn't been isolated and desperate, I don't think he'd have fallen for it so easily in the first place."

"There are traps for the desperate, and there are traps for the composed. Don't sell yourself short, Master Naijag."

Just then Sabellian appeared at the doorway, looking at the nutshells scattered across the floor. "Pretty sparse victory celebration. Or did you two already finish eating?"

"Haven't even started." Jace asked, "How's the portal outside doing?"

"The damage to the Screaming Hall didn't reach the portal. Thank the draenei for building this necropolis so big and sturdy." Sabellian sat down. "But the work's definitely delayed by a few days. A lot of laborers panicked. Some even tried to escape with the Broken."

"That's not necessarily a bad thing," Jace said. "The other side is still looking for a way into the Terokkar Rift. We can't rush on our end."

Sabellian held a blackened orc skull in his hand, examining it from different angles. "Needleflame has already slipped out of the Arcane Sanctum with your demon lover in the chaos and headed for your 'secret base.' You can rest easy. But word about her has spread among the warlocks. I know some of them are already looking to you for lessons on improving the succubus summoning spell."

"I understand." Jace glanced at the skull in the black dragon's hand and frowned. "What's that?"

"Vopiel's skull." Sabellian grinned. "Gul'dan's head had enough magical power to open and destroy the Dark Portal. I was wondering if this student of his might have similar 'talents.'"

"And?"

"Turns out it's good for one decoration on a necklace I wear in my true form. Don't expect anything more." Sabellian made a gesture like a magic trick, and the skull vanished.

"Speaking of transformations — back in the Screaming Hall, I lost my disguise from casting. A lot of orcs might have seen," Jace said.

"You mean when Murmur blew half the hall to pieces?" The black dragon prince wagged a finger.

"I wouldn't normally worry," Jace said. "But you remember what Blakart said to Vopiel before — about me sneaking in disguised as Yuruk. I'm worried he might not be the only one in this crypt who thinks that."

"I think, you two... if anyone suspects, they could try to explain it as Blakart's dark curse." Sora said. The other two fel orcs both looked at her.

"Go on," Jace said.

Sora swallowed and continued, "That Dark Lord that Blakart worships, that ancient lurking presence... Queen Ysera told us that things buried in the darkness are best at manufacturing lies, sowing discord, breeding suspicion, and turning kin against each other."

As she said this, Sabellian's expression grew complicated.

Sora bit her lip and went on, "That ogre used a dark curse to influence a lot of cult members. So in extreme fear, they might trigger what the curse wanted them to see. And the purpose of these hallucinations would be to sow discord between the Shadow Council and the Legion's emissary. Vopiel could have been a victim of that kind of illusory magic too."

Jace and Sabellian both said nothing. Sora asked cautiously, "Does it sound stupid?"

"No," Jace said. "It sounds very convincing."

He looked at the black dragon. "Could you quietly ask around through those informants you've 'bought' today? See if there are similar rumors?"

"Fine." Sabellian glanced sidelong at the warlock, then looked at the female orc. "Honestly, I think you've been around this warlock too long, Lady Sora. You might have picked up some bad habits."

"Oh? You think this is a 'bad habit,' Master Naihuogar?" Jace asked.

"Compared to you now, I'm practically stuffed to the brim with glorious courage and a powerful sense of responsibility. The Holy Light itself might look on me with favor." Sabellian raised his hands. "Before I came to Auchindoun, I never thought I'd be willing to get injured saving a mortal and a green dragon."

"Are you badly hurt, Master Naihuogar?" Sora asked.

"Hurt? Hardly." The black dragon leaned against the cold wall behind him. "Forgive my exaggeration. It was only two full-force strikes from an otherworldly evil god threatening all of Draenor. What could possibly go wrong? Won't need much rest."

"That was my oversight. Morofel dominating Vopiel so easily made me lower my guard," Jace said.

"Well then, as an apology." Sabellian tapped a finger on the table. "Promise me this: once all this is over and you leave Draenor, never come back. Compared to someone like you, I'd rather deal with characters like Kargath Bladefist, Gruul, or Magtheridon."

"That's no problem." Jace snorted. "I won't come back even if you beg me."
