Chapter 1378: One Step Further

After a few days, the Shadow Labyrinth had more or less returned to its previous calm. Though the old leader Vopiel had been killed, Murmur's banishment also removed the ticking time bomb that had been hanging over every orc's head. Now they could focus their energy on building the portal and preparing its power source. Once the most sensitive and tense few days had passed — with Sabellian and Needleflame helping to handle some "rumors that shouldn't be circulating" about the Screaming Hall chaos — Jace was able to sneak out again at night when no one was watching. He was eager to check on Khadgar's status on the other side, and also to visit the Tomb of Lights to confirm the spirits of Auchindoun's commitment to him. In the dead of night, he crept cautiously toward the demonic ritual chamber. Halfway there, he could already feel the furious, uncontrollable magical power swelling from the staff clutched in his hand. The foundation of Murmur's existence in Draenor had been completely devoured by Ulthalesh. Now it was comfortably digesting that twisted evil energy. And this was bringing its master an unavoidable influence. Every day, Jace felt more familiar with shadow — sensing other people's emotions, perceiving the dark aura drifting through the labyrinth, it all came effortlessly. Once, when he entered the Pulpit, he could clearly read every orc present — warlock and soldier, male and female. Their fear, desire, rage — all laid bare before him. Including, of course, some people's dissatisfaction and suspicion toward him, their shock at Vopiel's death, and that hollow sense of loss after Murmur was banished... At that moment, it seemed the only thing preventing him from strangling these dissatisfied people was Sabellian's disguise magic — fragile enough to be shattered by a spell. It felt like only sacrificing the entire expedition's path back to Azeroth, undoing months of everyone's effort at such a tremendous cost, could stop him from slaughtering these orcs like beasts. Once he entered the empty corridor, far from the cult's patrols, he couldn't wait to reach out and merge with the shadow energy... Soon, the entire broad hallway was shrouded in his dark magic, becoming pitch black. The faint remnants of the night sky barely filtering through the cracked walls were completely consumed. Jace felt he had never been so powerful. Perhaps he could try some spells he'd never dared to imagine before, accomplish some terrible feats he'd never considered attempting. But he also knew this was Ulthalesh's terrifying ambition at work — that sharp hook stirring in his heart, tugging at his savage desires, was pulling tighter and tighter... He drew his hand back. The entire long corridor regained a hint of light and shadow — doorframes, braziers, all taking on faint outlines again. It's not time yet. You can't waste your power like this. Because you know very well that all the magical energy gathered now must be poured into the Scepter of Sargeras. To complete our revenge. To complete your defiance of Kil'jaeden and Sargeras himself. Jace squeezed his eyes shut tight, dispersing the thoughts that were nearly spilling from his mouth. He hated this feeling. Hated the process of his inner goals gradually merging with Ulthalesh's desires. Sometimes he thought it would be nice if this staff and the Scepter of Sargeras could both just die. Two of the most terrifying artifacts in the Burning Legion, destroying each other — that would be two wins for Azeroth. But no one had ever walked away from Ulthalesh alive. He knew that very well. In the hidden chamber where the communication ritual array was concealed, Morofel had already prepared the circle. "Some in the cult have noticed you're different, Morofel," Jace instructed. "Don't try to enter the Arcane Sanctum anymore." "It's not arrogance, master." Morofel lifted her chin slightly. "None of the Shadow Council's current members are worth my personal attention... So I look forward to the day the portal opens, so I can face real enemies at the Throne of Kil'jaeden." Jace watched her for a moment. "Yes. Real enemies. I'll take you there." "I can't wait, darling." The succubus inclined her head. Jace snapped his fingers. The array burst into flames. The incantation had barely finished — he hadn't even closed his eyes to properly feel the void bridge built through the demonic pact — when he sensed Kalrez was already about to respond. Even Morofel's eyes widened in surprise. She looked at Ulthalesh set aside, then at her master, unable to stop herself from licking the corner of her mouth with her long purple tongue. "Master? You feel... different." The voice came from the other side. "Maybe it's the confidence from eliminating an otherworldly evil god." Jace spoke in Demonic to the other side of the array. "Has Master Khadgar returned, Lord Kalrez? Tell him the last trouble in Auchindoun — Murmur — has been dealt with. I'm hoping to hear some good news from the other side." "He's back." Kalrez said. "But he says he's not particularly in the mood to celebrate your great victory." "She refused?" Jace asked. "Quite the opposite. She agreed." The demon said. Jace's heart thumped. So Aegwynn really had come to the Kingdom of Stormwind. "Is there anyone else in the tower?" Jace asked. "No." Kalrez said. "All the Kirin Tor record-keepers here — including Amy Marin — have gone to Stonewatch to organize the ethereal spells you sent over. They have to accurately transcribe everything into a new enchanted codex by tonight. Khadgar will take it to Karazhan and give it to Aegwynn." Kalrez was silent on the other end for a long moment before continuing. "Khadgar told me she lives in what looks like a decent hilltop cottage. No cover around it. Almost no trace of magic detectable either. But Khadgar had to search for a while before he finally spotted the place..." "She wanted to know how Khadgar found this place. Though he didn't intend to reveal anything about you — he wanted to shift the blame to some dead Tirisfal Council member — Aegwynn guessed quickly anyway." "I'm not surprised." Jace said. "She hasn't seen many outsiders recently. Our intrusion caught her off guard." "Khadgar wants to ask you: when you saw her last time, was she performing any magical ritual?" The demon lord relayed. "No. She found us." Jace said. "He wants to know whether you sensed any shadow magic or necromantic activity." The demon lord said again. "No." Jace said. There was silence on the other end. Jace suddenly realized — was Khadgar walking in on Aegwynn trying to resurrect her son Medivh? Resurrecting Medivh was no simple magic. Not a ritual you could complete by chanting some spell and expending some mana. Even Alexstrasza, the Queen of Life, couldn't fully resurrect someone thoroughly dead... It must be a long, painful process. "Did he detect her researching some dark magic?" Jace asked. "Khadgar doesn't know." The other side said. "He's just speculating. And Aegwynn still looks incredibly powerful — her appearance matches those portraits: imposing and strikingly beautiful. Not like someone exhausting great magic on forbidden research." "To get Aegwynn to agree to help, he didn't dare ask any questions. And he feels it's dangerous to discuss a mage like that behind her back." "I agree. And we won't get anywhere discussing it anyway." Jace nodded, then couldn't help glancing back at the dark doorway — as if Aegwynn was already standing there watching him. "She didn't impose any conditions?" Kalrez said, "No. The only condition she gave was that she'd move to another location, and if she met any mage in the future, she'd kill them on sight. Whoever died, it would be Khadgar's fault for not stopping her in time. Khadgar thought that was fair, so he agreed." "Alright." Jace said, wiping his sweat-damp forehead. Though Khadgar didn't know what secret plan Aegwynn was pursuing, from his description, it seemed Aegwynn still hadn't succeeded in resurrecting Medivh. Was the time not right? Or had this been delayed by Jace's own interference? As for precisely when Aegwynn successfully resurrected Medivh — even in the other timeline, there was no detailed timestamp. But it was definitely before the Scourge invasion. After all, Medivh had first appeared as a crow, warning the kingdoms of the coming disaster... Though not many believed his absurd prophecy at the time. Now, there were only a few years left before the Scourge invasion. Krasus and the Kirin Tor had already begun focusing on Northrend. If the expedition could successfully return to Azeroth... Jace didn't believe they'd repeat that timeline's mistakes. Maybe this world didn't need another prophet after all.
