Chapter 1379: The Spirits’ Promise

Jace didn’t know just how much revulsion — or even hatred — Aegwynn held for the Order of Tirisfal or the Kirin Tor. Nor did he know how she truly felt about her son’s death.

But since she’d accepted Khadgar’s invitation without imposing overly harsh conditions, it meant she was willing to help bring the Sons of Lothar home.

If Aegwynn was willing to help, then the ethereals’ prophetic magic incantation had a real chance of working. In all of Azeroth, it would be hard to find a more formidable combination of power than Aegwynn herself plus Karazhan.

Thinking back to when they’d first snuck out of the Tower of Ilgalar, thinking they could bring the whole expedition back on their own… it was almost laughably naive.

The next night, Jace prepared to slip out of Auchindoun under cover of darkness and head for the Tomb of Lights.

Though the Tomb of Lights was in the Bone Wastes, the distance was too far to make a round trip on foot in one night. Especially considering the trek across the vast, pale Bone Wastes. He’d need Sola to give him a ride if he wanted to return to Auchindoun before Draenor’s sun rose.

Before the great explosion Murmur had caused, the Tomb of Lights had been a temple, surrounded by a small village of about a hundred draenei — mostly Auchenai priests.

Now the entire village had been nearly flattened. Even the temple was nothing but piles of rubble and broken walls.

Last time he’d come here, it had been raining. Besides Sola, Jace had had Vereesa, Grede, and Morophil with him. They’d walked together discussing the area’s situation, talking about the dangers ahead — too distracted to pay much attention to the desolation around them.

Now, Jace stood alone at the edge of the tomb entrance, among piles of broken stone. He swept his gaze across the grey-white plains. A cold wind carrying dust blew past, and a chill seized his chest.

Enough chill to power a pretty strong Corruption spell that could take down a talbuk, he thought.

“What are you thinking about, Jace?” Sola approached, pulling her green long cloak tight. She’d transformed back to the golden-haired elf form she liked best.

“I thought I’d see Vereesa, or the other Farstriders.” Jace turned around. “Haven’t they been attacking the necromancers near Auchindoun lately?”

Sola looked north into the distance. “Needleflame told me that since the Auchenai crypts fell, the Farstriders have focused on helping Honor Hold soldiers retreat. Meanwhile, some of the draenei and Broken who escaped Shattrath — rather than following the Aldor in continuing to call upon the Holy Light — feel that following Velen out of Draenor is the better choice.”

“She’s a formidable warrior and tracker. But more importantly, she’s a Ranger Lord, Jace. Among the Quel’dorei currently with the expedition, at least nominally, she holds the highest rank.” The green dragon said. “I know you miss her. But you know how badly Vereesa wants to prove she’s worthy of that title, don’t you?”

Jace nodded. “Without her and the Farstriders handling reconnaissance, the soldiers at Honor Hold never could have slipped away right under the Burning Legion’s and the orcs’ noses. Of course I understand how important that is. Same with Grede — he’s on the front lines at Wildhammer Stronghold, standing with his kin. If they and Turalyon withdrew early, the demons would immediately realize we’re planning to run.”

He let out a soft laugh. “Just like how I’m stuck here with a bunch of angry spirits, hostile demons, and warlocks. Everyone’s in exactly the right place.”

“Not all of us are demons or warlocks.” Sola exhaled. “I’m not one of you.”

Jace smiled. “Want to come down with me to see those ghosts?”

“Let’s go.”

Sola’s reply was stiff, and her expression tightened. She was still haunted by the vicious, twisted spirits they’d encountered here last time.

Entering the Tomb of Lights, the wind outside grew fainter, and Jace sensed something different.

Last time they’d come here, dark corridors, an empty road ahead, a gloomy atmosphere… Shadow, corruption, decay seemed to fill this forsaken place.

But this time, though it was still too dark to see anything — and before long Sola had to raise her staff to summon moonlight to light the way — Jace felt the threatening atmosphere that had once enveloped travelers here was gone.

They passed through a long corridor to the deepest part, arriving at a spacious temple hall. Sola raised her staff high, illuminating the massive statue ahead.

Jace looked at the faces of the two draenei saint statues. They didn’t seem as stern and frightening as before. Instead, they felt reassuring. Though Jace knew full well their appearance hadn’t changed at all.

“I’m going to wake them, Sola.” Jace looked at the green dragon, who was glancing around.

“Okay.” Sola nodded.

Jace found a lantern and raised his other hand, calling on the power of flame.

A tongue of fire leaped from his palm, shooting into the lantern and igniting it. Sustained by Jace’s mana, it burned brightly even without oil or other fuel, illuminating half the chamber.

He stared at the flame, letting out a small sigh of relief. He’d actually been worried that letting Ulthalesh completely devour the soul energy sustaining Murmur might affect Draenor’s elements’ trust in him.

A moment later, the spirit of the former draenei Exarch stepped out from the firelight.

Just like the saint statues in this temple, his soul looked unchanged from last time. Tall and robust, demonic horns, stern face, heavy — even cold — armor, and a tri-pronged hammer in his hand…

But as Jace watched him approach, along with the many draenei spirits appearing behind him — he couldn’t muster the same sharp vigilance he’d felt before, as if facing evil undead.

“I’ve returned, Exarch.” Jace spoke in Eredun. “The orc warlock Vorpil. Exarch Maladaar. They’re dead.”

“I am no longer the Exarch of Auchindoun, Jace Sesso.” The former Exarch’s voice echoed through the chamber. “I apologize for my rudeness before… I thought I could steadfastly maintain my will, preserve my past, amid the filth and corruption brought by demons, orcs, and my fallen kin. But I didn’t expect this persistence to drag me into another unfamiliar abyss.”

“Fortunately, you destroyed Maladaar’s palace. You interrupted his horrific ritual of torturing both us and himself. The spirits of Auchindoun were liberated because of this. Every soul seeking shelter here is grateful for your good deeds. I am merely one among them.”

Jace nodded. “To be honest, my intention wasn’t to do good. I’ve completed our task. I’ve helped you achieve vengeance. Now it’s your turn.”

“I understand your concern. Shaking off the shadows of the past doesn’t mean we forget our promises.” The former Exarch said quietly. “Besides, countless draenei lives depend on your plan… The demons cannot be allowed to think they can destroy the last true eredar.”

“Return to Auchindoun, Jace Sesso.” A white-robed draenei spirit beside him said. “When the time comes, call for our aid. We will appear to help you complete the final step of the portal. And those Shadow Council members who think they’ve taken Auchindoun will pay for decades of shameless desecration.”

“You’ll help the expedition and the draenei deal with the remaining orcs?” Jace asked.

“Now is not the time for us to beg for peace.” The former Exarch’s spirit said.

Jace felt a wave of relief at that. Sola looked at him and nodded.

They’d been worried that there were still many hardliners in the cult who were utterly loyal to the Burning Legion’s demons. At the critical moment, when they realized something was wrong, they might destroy the portal before the expedition forces and the Kirin Tor mages entering through the channel could link up.

Now, with the souls in Auchindoun helping, those orcs would at least be caught completely off guard from the start. After all, these spirits had long been the raw material for their soul shards. How many would dare stand up and fight back directly?

“I have no other requests.” Jace said.

“Then may everything go smoothly for you from here on. Always remember the Holy Light.”

After speaking the most traditional Eredun blessing, the former Exarch’s spirit bowed deeply. Together with the other spirits, they slowly faded away. The flame in Jace’s hand went out abruptly.
