## Chapter 138: Hillsbrad "Charm"

The next few days on Baradin Bay were as still as a mirror. Whenever things started going smoothly, Jace's guard went up — he couldn't shake the feeling that luck might suddenly haul off and punch him from some bizarre angle.

But on the evening of November 30th, a long thread of lights became visible to the north. That was Southshore — Southshore at night.

Maybe fate had stamina limits too, and couldn't keep screwing with Jace nonstop.

Southshore was the largest town and port in the Hillsbrad region. Jace remembered the last time he'd left, he'd found the place a bit overhyped. Back when he was in the capital, he'd heard Southshore was bustling and prosperous, but when he actually saw it, he'd thought it was just all right.

But after two years in the Kingdom of Stormwind, where he'd seen the towns of the Redridge Mountains and Elwynn Forest up close, coming back to Southshore made him realize this really was a sizable port — especially after another two years of growth since the war. Even at night it blazed with lights, and the streets were packed with people.

He remembered when he'd left, this town had still been under curfew.

Of course, anyone coming from the capital would naturally think the whole world was small towns and countryside. After all, the capital was indisputably the largest city on the continent — even the kingdom's second city, Stratholme, had been considerably more prosperous than pre-war Stormwind.

According to the game's lore, post-Scourge Stormwind had a population of roughly two hundred thousand. Jace certainly hadn't felt those numbers when he was there — the census office had given a figure of somewhere between seventy and one hundred thousand humans.

But considering that in the game, the entire Kingdom of Lordaeron was on the verge of collapse, half its former territory had become plague-ravaged wasteland unfit for normal human life, and thousands of displaced survivors had migrated south into the Kingdom of Stormwind, the population surging to two hundred thousand over a decade or so wasn't out of the question — especially since Stormwind had kept expanding.

And the capital of Lordaeron right now was already about the size that Stormwind would become later — at least, when Jace had been in the capital, his gut feeling had been that it was a lot bigger than Stormwind.

Southshore radiated out from its harbor; every road in town led down to the docks. As they drew near the port, Jace could trace the paths of those streets and take in the whole town at a glance.

The ponytail man had made it to port alive, and had even recovered quite a bit. After a few days' rest, the woman was well enough to come up on deck for fresh air on her own.

The "Night Baron," after all that commotion, whether from a heavier dose of sedatives, being bound tighter than before, or simple resignation, never made another scene — he barely even made a sound.

In short, everything had gone the way the captain wanted. He'd been throwing money around like mad for the past two days — anyone who could help keep things stable on the ship, by any means, got paid, no questions asked. Looked like he'd made a healthy profit hauling the "Night Baron."

Though Jace suspected this job was probably more about the Trade Cartel's reputation in the gladiator-shipping business. The captain's real backers and bosses — the Cartel's owners — wouldn't tolerate any serious slip-up. Captain Nilaz stood at the bow watching the sailors bustle about, preparing to dock, his manner noticeably more relaxed than it had been a few days ago. He probably felt he'd passed the test.

Once the ship was steady, Jace walked out onto the pier and looked at the dense crowds on the road. That old uneasy feeling crept back.

This many people — what if they couldn't find a carriage to Dalaran? And even if they could, it'd probably be crazy expensive.

Grede came up beside him. "Looks like we'll be finding another inn. I've got a feeling empty rooms are going to be hard to come by."

"Yeah. Let's head to the depot first and ask about tomorrow's carriages," Jace said.

"What's the rush?" Grede asked. "We're days ahead of schedule. This ship didn't stop at Menethil Harbor — I honestly didn't see that coming."

Jace pointed at the crowd outside one of the inn entrances. "Take a look at how packed that is. It's giving me the creeps. Even if we're planning to stay a few days, we should lock in a time now — book ahead if we can. Don't forget, back in Stormwind, you forgot to buy a ticket in advance, and we ended up having to take that goblin ship — and nearly got our guts torn out by a Stranglethorn panther."

Grede shot back, "Oh, and you were any better? Blind as a bat, both of you."

Jace sighed. "Which is why I've learned my lesson. Better early than late."

"Then let's go," the dwarf said.

The two of them headed north along Southshore's main road. It was an uphill climb, and the two of them, fresh off the ship, were worn out by the time they reached the depot where the trade caravans gathered.

That was when Jace heard a rushing wind, followed by a string of low, rumbling "koo-koo-goo-goo" sounds, like some kind of engine, coming from the other side of the depot buildings.

Had Lordaeron already built a steam locomotive network?

The moment Grede heard it, his thick ears shot straight up.

"What is that?" Jace asked.

"It's a gryphon!" Grede dragged his bag and bolted around the side of the building. Jace, hearing "gryphon," scrambled after him.

The two of them looked out at the open ground behind the depot. A massive, grey-white lion-bird creature was lifting its wings, shaking its eagle head impatiently.

A good number of people were leaning on the railers or hanging out of windows, watching the beast's agitated display with amused chatter. The gryphon seemed unsettled by all the staring.

This creature was way bigger than Jace had imagined… After all, in the game, even a dwarf riding on a gryphon's back hadn't looked all that much smaller than the bird.

But this gryphon was enormous — its whole body was the size of a tall, powerful horse, and with its thick plumage, its great spread wings, the muscular lines of its four legs, it made the draft horses in the depot look like a pack of timid puppies.

A mage in Kirin Tor purple strode past the stares of the crowd, swung himself onto the gryphon with practiced ease, shouted something, and the beast immediately raised its wings and gave a single, light flap — and its feet left the ground. The wind it kicked up was enough to rival a helicopter.

Spectators' hats went flying. Jace had to grab his own hood. His cloak snapped behind him in the gale. But his eyes never left the gryphon, even with the sand blinding him.

He was nearly rooted to the spot. Goosebumps erupted all over his body.

Under normal circumstances, he might have wondered about the mage's identity — what rank he held in the Kirin Tor, what magic he wielded, how powerful he was, how long it would take to reach his level… The mage had looked fairly young, after all.

But none of that mattered now, because that gryphon was a thousand times more dazzling than anything he could've imagined!
