## Chapter 1383: Soul Divination?

Sabellian's overall strength probably couldn't match Nefarian's. Otherwise, he wouldn't have always stayed by Deathwing's side as an adjutant.

And when Deathwing decided to abandon Draenor entirely and return to Azeroth, he told Sabellian to stay behind and guard the dragon eggs in Draenor. But judging by his subsequent arrangements in Azeroth, it was effectively abandonment too.

That was one of the major reasons he harbored hatred for his father — Deathwing never took him seriously.

And judging by the fact that Deathwing entrusted his most ambitious plan — the creation of a new dragonflight, the Chromatic Dragons — to Nefarian, he clearly saw Nefarian as "the most useful one."

Sabellian had never been willing to admit he was inferior to anyone. Not to the recently vanquished Murmur, not even to Gruul or Magtheridon — even though he couldn't take either of those "Lords of Outland" in a one-on-one fight.

So for him to say "I don't want to admit I'm inferior to Nefarian" was proof enough that he knew perfectly well there was a real gap between himself and his half-brother.

His lie wasn't about that. It was about the "ability to manipulate others' spells." Jace knew the old black dragon was holding out on him.

Even if Sabellian wasn't as good at it as Nefarian, he should at least know something about it, or at least understand the principles behind this strange magic. He just didn't want to teach the human standing before him.

Whether because he knew Jace would go back to Azeroth to fight his brother, and even if he had no love for the other black dragon, he still didn't want to help an outsider fight his own kind. Or because he felt Jace had already learned too much from him and wasn't willing to reveal his deepest tricks without payment.

In any case, he wasn't willing to go any further.

But realizing Sabellian was lying left Jace unable to eat or sleep properly.

To be honest, while Shadow Bolt Volley was a powerful "advanced version" of Shadow Bolt, plenty of people in Azeroth had mastered it too — especially wandering mages who'd studied arcane first and shadow later. And Flame Teleportation was clearly something even some high-level fire-masters in Shadowforge City knew — the audience in the arena hadn't even gasped when they witnessed the announcer's teleportation magic.

But Nefarian's "call" — that was completely unique in the present era.

In fact, before Sabellian had tried to warn him about it, Jace hadn't even considered that he might have a chance to learn that spell.

Of course, before coming to Draenor, he'd never imagined he'd get chummy with one of Deathwing's sons, either.

On the way back to Auchindoun, Jace tried to fish more information about this "call" out of Sabellian indirectly, but Sabellian was clearly regretting having brought it up in the first place.

He refused to discuss anything related to it for the entire journey. He even managed his composure so well that Jace could no longer detect any obvious concealment or even lying — not a flicker of emotional disturbance.

In the end, even Jace started to wonder if the flash of the black dragon prince's concealed emotion he'd caught in that moment had just been his imagination.

But even if the black dragon wouldn't talk, Jace could still piece together a few clues from what little his words had revealed.

In the game, Nefalian's ability didn't have a clearly named casting bar like "Shadow Bolt Volley" or "Banish." He would just pick a target and force their spells to go haywire, or make them use abilities they wouldn't normally use — hence why players jokingly called it "being called out."

Jace had initially thought Nefarian achieved this through powerful mental magic similar to his sister Onyxia's, manipulating the players directly. Making players get owned by their own signature abilities was just his twisted sense of humor.

But aside from making monks roll uncontrollably or forcing rogues to Shadowstep right in front of him... there were some inconsistencies. Like giving certain classes effects their abilities shouldn't have, or making them far more powerful than when the player cast them, or triggering them during cooldowns — none of that could be achieved through mind control alone.

Sabellian's words had hinted at what he used to accomplish this: sufficiently powerful shadow energy.

Jace recalled that death knights had an ability called "Dark Simulacrum," which used shadow energy to mimic an opponent's spells in a certain way.

Were those spells that exceeded the player's normal capabilities actually advanced versions that Nefarian "stole" through a similar method, then unleashed?

Considering that black dragons were masters of necromantic and shadow magic, and later worked closely with Gul'dan's death knights — could it be that the death knights' Dark Simulacrum was originally a "simplified version" learned from the black dragons?

If he went back to Azeroth, he'd probably never get another chance to learn this mysterious ability. It wasn't like he could go ask that brother and sister pair for lessons, could he?

But considering Sabellian's wariness, Jace didn't press further. Maybe after all the Draenor problems were dealt with and the Scepter of Sargeras was destroyed, Sabellian might reveal a bit more out of gratitude.

But even if he was willing to share more, how long would it take to return to Auchindoun from the Throne of Kil'jaeden after successfully completing the mission?

Considering that the expeditionary force would be using these walls to hold off the demon siege the whole time, Jace couldn't possibly slow down the return trip just to give himself time to learn one more spell.

Less than two days... would that be enough to learn this unfathomable ability? He didn't know, but he probably wouldn't get another chance.

Jace watched the orc laborers hauling stone below the terrace, already planning the words he'd use to persuade Sabellian to teach him the spell — as if the Scepter of Sargeras had already been shattered.

He was so focused that he didn't notice an orc warlock approaching until the warlock was right beside him.

"Master Negag."

"Hm?" Jace adjusted his breathing and turned to look, switching the Common he'd been mentally rehearsing to Orcish. "What is it?"

"Important intelligence..." The warlock pulled back his hood. "The Broken diviners have discovered that the expeditionary force is secretly evacuating to Alleria's Fortress. They're being very careful — even the eyes we planted in Terokkar Forest didn't notice."

"Broken diviners?" Jace asked. "Can they be trusted?"

"They were all former members of the Auchenai, Master," the orc warlock said. "The elven rangers are very familiar with our reconnaissance methods, but they didn't count on the restless spirits between Shadowmoon Valley and Terokkar..."

"Don't trust the Broken too much. When Murmur was destroyed, they were the first to collude with their draenei kinsmen outside, trying to escape..." Jace let out a contemptuous snort. "And don't trust the voices of spirits too much either. The spirits of Auchindoun hate the Legion's occupation of this place so much that they might deliberately mislead us."

"The information from Auchindoun's spirits is indeed vague and hard to interpret — very unreliable, I agree." The orc lowered his head. "But the omens those diviners gave through souls aren't that simple. The texts we recovered from the Auchenai temple might contain records of something similar."

"The most important thing is that several Broken soul-diviners all said they saw similar omens. They're the ones in the Shriekhall who were most loyal to Master Wapil and spent a long time helping bind Murmur. Though they're slaves, they do have real ability."

Jace cursed silently. They'd killed Murmur, and now the most capable ones had nothing better to do than cause trouble.

The orc warlock continued, "Not just from Wildhammer Stronghold to here — there are even some coming from the direction of the Dark Portal. A very large number — maybe hundreds or even thousands! Even if it's some kind of illusion, it's worth being cautious about."

"What does Nai'hojar say?" Jace asked. "Hasn't he been in charge of reconnaissance in the area?"

"It was Master Nai'hojar who sent me to find you, Master Negag," the orc said in a hoarse, low voice.

"But we can't notify the Legion or Hellfire Citadel right now," Jace said. "There are black dragons and gryphon riders watching the sky, and Alliance forces blocking the ground. Even if we killed a demon and let its soul carry the news back to the Twisting Nether, it might take months or even years for its spirit to catch the attention of the Legion's lords — more likely it would never be noticed at all!"

The orc warlock bowed his head in thought for a moment, then said cautiously, "So, we're planning to organize a meeting to discuss countermeasures, Master. What if their assembly is meant to stop us from building the portal?"

"I'll be at that meeting," Jace said.
