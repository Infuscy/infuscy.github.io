Chapter 1384: Just Some Warlock's Scheme

By the time Jace arrived at Blackcate's lecture hall, the orc warlocks had already been whispering among themselves for a while. They fell into synchronized silence when they saw him enter.

His gaze swept across the Broken gathered among them. These former Auchenai priests who had followed the Shadow Council had completely lost whatever pride they once had. Even when meeting the eyes of this K'aresh Ambassador, they obediently lowered their heads and looked away. It really reminded one of well-trained hunting dogs waiting for their master to toss them scraps.

These Broken were probably the most outcast among the outcasts — not just among the draenei of Shattrath, but even among other Broken.

"You're here, Negag." Sabellian raised a hand. "I hope they've told you what's happening outside."

"I know," Jace said, looking at the Broken. "But I want more specifics. How many soldiers did you see gathering toward this area from the expeditionary force through this so-called soul divination? What equipment do they have?"

"It's hard to see soul omens that clearly..." a Broken replied in Orcish. "We have to interpret what we can from the visions."

"Then let me understand it this way," Jace said, staring at the Broken priest. "Your so-called omens are essentially the same as the prophetic visions of that draenei seer — the one called Velen. Some are true, some are false, and you have to rely on your own experience and judgment to figure out whether to believe them?"

Some orc warlocks whispered among themselves, some furrowed their brows in thought, others glanced at the Broken. Even the Broken themselves were momentarily speechless, stopped cold by Jace's words.

"Gul'dan's visions led us into Azeroth to find a new homeland, but they also led him to the Tomb of Sargeras to die," Jace said. "Before we decide whether to send word to K'aresh or Hellfire Citadel, let's first figure out how much credibility these so-called soul omens actually have."

He turned to fix his gaze on the eldest-looking Broken priest. "Murmur was just banished. It hasn't even been a month since the Auchindoun catacombs were destroyed. The souls in the entire draenei necropolis are still in the throes of mad chaos. I don't intend to trust any so-called soul divination during this period."

After Jace finished, the orc warlocks gave no response. Sabellian's face was cold too, and Sora beside him watched everyone's reactions with some concern.

"Why not try sending messengers?" an orc warlock said. "Auchindoun is about to be besieged again — that hasn't happened in years, Master Negag. But you seem so certain the Alliance isn't mobilizing, so certain this is just an illusion."

"What makes you so sure, or do you have information we don't?" another orc asked.

"You're truly an exceptional warlock, Master Negag. Perhaps even more powerful than Talon Gorefiend. Among the warlocks I've seen, only Gul'dan himself might match your talent." An older warlock, leaning on his staff behind a table, delivered the praise before continuing slowly. "But talent in demonic magic doesn't mean you're familiar with prophetic magic or divination — and it certainly doesn't mean you understand how the Alliance army thinks."

"I don't understand how the Alliance army thinks? Do you?" Jace fixed his eyes on this warlock. "I'm the one who, together with Master Nai'hojar, Lady Sosara, and our loyal guardian Carosha, crossed the expeditionary rangers' lines and successfully reached Auchindoun — not you. As far as I know, every attempt to break out of Auchindoun so far has failed! The only warlock who managed to bring news of Auchindoun's situation out and inform the Legion and Lord Magtheridon was a messenger from Hellfire Citadel."

"You agree with me too, Nai'hojar!" Jace was practically shouting as he looked at Sabellian.

Sabellian didn't agree immediately. He studied Jace for a moment. When Sora and Needleflame saw that the black dragon prince wasn't answering, they both looked at him suspiciously.

"What you've said is indeed true, but you're still gambling nonetheless." The black dragon prince raised his voice. "You're betting the divination is wrong, betting the Alliance Expedition actually isn't moving, betting the visions are all false... But what if you lose the bet?"

"What if I lose?" Jace chuckled softly, swallowed, then said, "Well then, answer me this. If the Alliance came here to destroy the portal, how did the news leak? In other words, how did the Alliance know we were building a portal in the first place?"

"Escaped draenei must have told them," Needleflame said.

An orc warlock in a blood-red cloak beside her said, "Very possible. We caught many, but plenty more escaped into Bone Wastes. Some went to Shattrath, some probably went to the Alliance, to the one they call 'the Prophet.'"

"So there's the problem." Jace looked at this orc warlock. "Don't you think the response was too fast? Think about it. How many weeks has it been since the Auchenai crypts fell? The draenei cross the Bone Wastes to the Alliance fortress, then convince the Alliance leaders to muster their troops and concentrate their forces to besiege Auchindoun — all of that in just a couple of weeks? Looking at it through the soul divination, it seems like a campaign that would take two or three months of planning."

"You should all know that after Ner'zhul tore Draenor apart and fled K'aresh, communication between Alliance forces was also severely disrupted... They can no longer communicate quickly using arcane magic. They've had to pull back from various positions, including withdrawing from Auchindoun to Alleria's Fortress. Otherwise, Wapil and Blackcate couldn't have seized that brief window of vulnerability to retake this tomb! And now, with this world under the Legion's control, Lord Kil'jaeden and his powerful wards are even tighter than before. And you expect me to believe that Honor Hold, Alleria's Fortress, Wildhammer Stronghold, and Kirin'Var Village — separated by thousands of miles — can coordinate better now than they could then? All based on some vague soul prophecies?"

"It's the black dragons," an orc warlock said quietly. "The black dragons are delivering messages for them. They might even be transporting soldiers, since they've already allied."

Sabellian shot the orc a sideways glance, looking like he wanted to say something but held back. Jace laughed a couple of times, drawing everyone's attention.

"I come from the Dragonmaw clan. I am Chief Zuluhed's most trusted advisor." Jace was practically grinding his teeth. "Chief Zuluhed and Warlord Nekros used a powerful artifact that even Deathwing feared — the Demon Soul — to enslave the red dragon queen herself, and only then barely forced those cowardly red dragons to let them ride on their backs, barely forced them to carry messages for the Horde! And now you're telling me that the Alliance — those hypocritical Light-worshippers just like the draenei — have somehow made Deathwing's own children willing servants? Has Prince Sabellian's neck also been fitted with a chain by Turalyon and Khadgar, hmm?!"

Sabellian's eyes swiveled back, his expression indescribably complicated, but he still managed a couple of strained chuckles.

That shut the orc warlocks up. The timeframe really was suspicious enough to warrant skepticism.

"If you ask me, this is just an Alliance scheme." Jace lowered his voice. "You may have heard that the Alliance has a fairly powerful warlock helping them. He helped them seize the Book of Fel Names. One of the reasons Lord Magtheridon sent us here to summon a new demon lord was to counteract the negative effects of that demon registry... Even Lord Balthas, the guardian of the Book of Fel Names, was defeated by that warlock. He's a fellow who's very skilled at manipulating souls and illusions. Not to mention, as far as I know, that draenei prophet Velen is also on their side now."

"What you're saying is..." Sabellian said, "the Alliance can't muster their forces quickly enough to attack Auchindoun, so they want to fake soul illusions to disrupt our deployment, make us pull people away for scouting and organizing defenses, thereby slowing down the portal construction. They're trying to buy time to find a way to destroy the portal?"

"That's obvious," Jace said firmly.

An orc warlock from farther away said, "That's very possible. Blackcate and Master Wapil — I mean, that traitor Wapil — have also been suspecting that human warlock named Jace Sesso has been trying to infiltrate the Shadow Council."

A scrawny orc warlock beside him said in a raspy voice, "But at the end of the day, Master Negag, that's still speculation."

"Speculation, speculation, speculation..." Jace muttered, raising both hands, then spoke loudly to everyone. "Even in the absolute worst case — even if it's all true, even if the Alliance really is bearing down on us with their full army — what choice do we have left?"

"Even if we notified Lord Magtheridon of everything happening here right this instant, would the Legion's armies still arrive in time to stop the siege of Auchindoun? The souls say their soldiers have already assembled near Alleria's Fortress. In that case, they could be at Auchindoun's broken walls in less than two weeks! Sending messengers now would just be wasting our already depleted manpower!"

"If you ask me, what we should be doing right now is focusing all our efforts on finishing the portal and letting the Legion's armies greet them right here. That's more important than anything! More practical than anything!"

"He's right, Master Negag." An orc warlock sitting nearby nodded. "We can't let these so-called divinations disrupt our plans."

Jace took a deep breath, let it out slowly, and looked at Sabellian. "What do you think now, Master Nai'hojar?"

"I'm convinced," the black dragon prince said. "I think even Lord Magtheridon himself, if he were here, would share your opinion."

The warlocks nodded one after another. The Broken remained silent. Jace licked his teeth and said, "Good. I'm glad none of you are letting this jumbled information derail our plans. Because the Legion's arrival will solve all problems."

"The Legion shall rule," the warlocks and the Cult guards standing at the doorway chanted in unison.
