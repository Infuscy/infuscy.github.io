Chapter 1389: Telogrus

Late at night, the shouts of orc laborers and the rumble of grinding stone could still be heard from outside the hall.

The portal was nearly complete. The finishing point was almost in sight. Jace stared at the circle before him, his hands feeling strangely numb.

"Hope everything goes smoothly," he muttered. Beside him, Morophil couldn't help asking, "I'm curious, master. Every time you mutter a prayer like that to yourself, which god or faith are you praying to? It's not the Holy Light, is it?"

"The sky." Jace pointed upward. "I pray to the sky. You can think of it as the unique faith of this particular warlock."

"So, is that the sky of Azeroth or the sky of Draenor?" Sazeno asked beside him.

"Less chattering." Jace shot it a look, waved his hand to ignite the circle, and began to chant.

But whether his prayer had actually caused something... this communion ritual was even harder than the last.

Jace felt like he couldn't reach the other side anymore. He'd lost his direction, adrift in the Twisting Nether.

He fumbled through the void, searching for the bloody scent of the demonic contract. He burned through two soul shards with no result. It wasn't until the imp scurried over and placed a draenei soul shard into the circle that he finally felt something familiar in the pitch blackness.

"Karelz?"

"Master." The doomlord's confident — even somewhat arrogant — voice came from the other side.

"How is it? Have you reached the Telogrus Rift?" Jace asked.

"I'm already inside this pathetic fragment." Karelz muttered discontentedly. "When I crossed through that portal, I was under the monitoring of countless mages with all sorts of countermagic. Honestly, the greatest humiliation I've suffered since my birth."

"But the ethereals' positioning was accurate enough. And it really is a pitiful, weak, shattered world. Turning it into fel-scorched wasteland would be no problem at all. What me and these mages are more worried about is... whether it can support a portal large enough for thousands of people to pass through."

That was indeed one of Jace's concerns too. Telogrus itself was already a fragment, after all.

"Regardless, those mortal mages have started work. They're fast — much faster than Mo'arg and Gan'arg servants," the demon added.

"What does Master Khadgar say?" Jace asked.

"He believes the Eye of Dalaran's power is enough to sustain this world for several days, until all the soldiers have passed through." Karelz said. "I've seen this so-called Eye of Dalaran. It's a rather enticing object. This human mage's judgment is sound."

"Several days? How many days exactly?" Jace frowned. "Can Khadgar and the mages find a way to hold it open a few more days?"

"To buy you time to find the Scepter of Sargeras," the demon said in a low voice.

"Yes," Jace said. "Prince Sabellian estimates the round trip will take about three or four days..."

"Khadgar says if the mages on both sides participate in stabilizing the portal simultaneously, they might be able to sustain it a while longer." The demon paused, then said, "But then those mages won't be able to participate in the battle against the Legion's forces besieging Auchindoun. Unless we rely on the portal being already linked, and hope even the Legion lords can't detect it."

"That's hard to pull off." The circle Jace was maintaining trembled. Clearly, the communion ritual between Draenor and Telogrus was far less stable than the connection with Azeroth.

He continued, "Once you've seen the kind of passages that connect to this world through the power of the Scepter of Sargeras... the passage in Nagrand was visible from hundreds of miles away. And north of Terokkar Forest, you could even see the void passage near the Throne of Kil'jaeden. Of course, I don't need to tell Khadgar this — he knows better than I do. If a new passage suddenly appears, even if all those eredar mages lose their ability to sense mana anomalies, they'd notice something wrong with their naked eyes."

"Then you'll have to set out as early as possible." Karelz said. "Khadgar wants to know — can the others help activate the portal on your end?"

"No," Jace said. "Auchindoun still has hundreds of soldiers, laborers, and some powerful warlocks waiting for the demon lord they expect to descend. And the souls are waiting for my call to manifest and activate the portal. If I leave early, the situation could spiral completely out of control."

"At the same time, our operation also needs the Burning Legion to notice the chaos in Auchindoun — that will help draw away some of their forces. According to the fel orcs at Hellfire Citadel, the Burning Legion's demons have summoned and gathered massive forces around the Throne of Kil'jaeden and K'aresh, preparing for a decisive battle with the expeditionary force. If they don't move, even the black dragon prince will find it difficult to break through under the watch of Doomguards filling the sky — let alone charge in and complete the objective. As things stand, the only way to make them move early is to let Magtheridon discover that the expeditionary force is about to successfully escape. There's no other way."

After delivering this long message, the imp helped move another soul shard into the circle. The other side was quiet for a while too.

"Khadgar says we'll have to rely on speed, then," Karelz said. "Let's see how fast that black dragon prince can fly."

"There's no other way." Jace swallowed. "How long will it take to build the portal on the Telogrus side?"

"Within five days," Karelz answered confidently. "The stones here are voidstones that have been infused with the Twisting Nether's mana for a long time — excellent material for building Legion portals. With me as the lead and those mortal mages assisting, we can get it built quickly."

"At least that's some good news." Jace breathed a slight sigh of relief. "This side might be a few days slower. Even though we started a few weeks earlier, they're relying on orc laborers to pile it up stone by stone."

"What about synchronizing the Legion portals? How do we determine the exact opening time?" he asked.

"The Telogrus side needs to open first," Karelz said. "When the void energy channel enters Draenor, you'll be at the teleportation coordinates — Auchindoun. You'll feel the approach of powerful void energy... Perhaps, as you said, you'll see a void passage in the sky. That will be the final moment to open the portal on your end. Be careful — if you see this and wait too long, Jace Sesso... the void energy opened from this side will turn into an energy beam that destroys you and everything around you. Possibly even the entire landmass."

"I know." Jace nodded. After all, this portal's energy source — the Eye of Dalaran and the Book of Medivh — were also the accomplices that tore Draenor apart.

"I'll prepare as soon as possible," he said. "When the portal on this side is finished, I'll notify you immediately."

"Khadgar wants me to tell you one more thing," the demon said. "An investigating mage from the Violet Eye found a mysterious bronze cube on one of the fragment's floating islands, sitting in the middle of a completely destroyed altar. Khadgar planned to use the excuse that this object might be related to Dar'khan's research on void energy to get it out of the Kirin Tor's hands and give it to you. But after he expressed this intention, the Silvermoon Council mages used the pretext that this object might advance the investigation of Dar'khan's case and took it away instead."

"Khadgar says that because the Dar'khan case is so far-reaching — from the elven kingdom's runestones to the Kirin Tor, to Stratholme, and because the assassination attempt on you involves the Kingdom of Stormwind... The Silvermoon Council used that as an excuse to take the mysterious cube. The Kirin Tor didn't dare object, and he couldn't find a reason to stop them either... So that thing is probably in Grand Magister Belo'vir's hands by now."

Jace rubbed his brow. He hadn't originally planned to ask Khadgar for that cursed thing anyway. After all, it was the "culprit" that led to those blood elves being consumed by the void and transformed into void elves. Even if he got his hands on it, he wouldn't know what to do with it. He just didn't want the people here to repeat the same mistakes as Magister Ubrich's group and cause problems with the portal.

He just hadn't expected that the excuse he'd made up a few days ago to explain how he knew about it would end up putting it right back into the elves' hands. It felt like some kind of strange timeline convergence.

But even if those elves decided to open it under Silvermoon's watch, even if it did attract a void creature invasion, it wouldn't cause the catastrophic consequences Ubrich's group had suffered.

If Alleria Windrunner hadn't later rescued them — the one who'd already come into contact with the void — they might really have become food for that powerful void lord.

If his existence had changed Alleria's future, then also removing the crisis that Telogrus had brought to those magisters... that counted as a bit of balance, at least.
