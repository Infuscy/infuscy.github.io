Chapter 1390: Gul'dan's Legacy

Since everything was progressing normally on the Telogrus side — and actually seemed to be going more smoothly than Auchindoun — Jace didn't need to worry about the laborers working too diligently and finishing too fast.

The other high-ranking Shadow Council warlocks were far more enthusiastic than him. They often came to the central open plaza to pressure the Cult overseers, driving the laborers to work harder.

After all, for them, the day the demon lord descended would be the day their fortunes soared. Whether the Alliance Expedition's movements outside were real or fake, they posed a massive threat to the portal project in Auchindoun.

Jace arrived at the plaza early that morning. The portal was already mostly complete.

Unlike the Dark Portal — which had two massive sword-wielding statues guarding it — and unlike the Twilight Ridge portal, which grew horrifying demonic horns, this stone gate had almost no decoration beyond the demonic runes carved into it. Standing there under the hazy morning light, it actually looked a bit plain and crude.

Looking up at the gate, his gaze unconsciously drifted to the gray sky behind it.

When the Telogrus portal activated, he should be able to see the spectacular phenomenon of void energy injection in this sky — like the whirlpools he'd seen in Nagrand.

He recalled Karelz's words while observing the broken walls and terraces around the plaza, imagining the scene when the portal opened.

He would come up with a reason to gather all the high-ranking Shadow Council members — including the warlocks and the Cult guard captains — in front of the portal, kneeling to welcome the demon lord's arrival. That way, he could conveniently take them all out with a few rounds of Shadow Bolt Volley.

Once the souls appeared, they could help him deal with whoever was left alive, as well as the soldiers and lackeys who hadn't gathered in front of the portal.

The laborers would scatter in panic at the sight of it, and Jace wouldn't stop them. There was no need to waste mana or consume the souls' energy tangling with them. The priority was protecting and opening the portal so the mages on the other side could come through.

Once Khadgar returned to Draenor and the expeditionary force arrived at Auchindoun... no matter how many hardliners were still hiding here, they wouldn't be a problem anymore.

Jace mulled this over in his mind while keeping his murderous intent as suppressed as possible, not letting the nearby warlocks sense his emotions and grow suspicious or alert.

But just as he was focused on rehearsing that day, hurried footsteps suddenly echoed from a nearby passage. An orc guard rushed toward Jace, drawing the attention of laborers and warlocks alike.

"Anotok has fled! High Warlock Anotok has left Auchindoun, Master Negag!" The guard stopped and announced. "He killed two warlocks before escaping."

The nearby orc warlocks who heard the name hurried over, one of them pressing urgently, "Anotok? Who did he kill?!"

"High Warlocks Kalon and Ormagak, Master." The orc guard answered obediently. "He's probably already fled into the Bone Wastes by now... impossible to track."

Ormagak was the Arakkoa artifact keeper Jace and Sabellian had been watching a few days ago. Jace knew that much now. But Kalon — Jace had hardly ever heard that name. As for this Anotok who'd fled, Jace only knew there was such a person; he could barely match the name to a face among the Council.

These warlocks hadn't been enthusiastic about the portal construction or the gatherings. Could they have been the ones secretly hiding the Shadow Council treasures left by Gul'dan or Ner'zhul?

The warlocks in the plaza exchanged glances, some murmuring among themselves. But one orc warlock was visibly more than just angry.

He walked up and grabbed the guard by the collar, demanding, "What the hell happened?"

"No need to get so worked up, Talamo." Jace looked at the emotionally out-of-control warlock. "Leaving the Shadow Council means they no longer firmly believe in the Legion. And you all know what that means in Draenor."

"I understand, Master Negag..." This warlock seemed to realize he'd lost his composure too. He took a couple of deep breaths, turned, and strode back into Auchindoun, walking faster and faster until he broke into a run, his figure disappearing into the passage.

No doubt about it — this Talamo was also involved in the whole thing. Why was he so angry? Had the fleeing Anotok taken the treasures with him?

Jace could hardly stay calm himself. He didn't know what was happening. Had Sabellian's plan spiraled out of control?

This black dragon had helped his father stir up trouble in the kingdom of Alterac without leaving a trace, had fooled even the Kirin Tor. And he was having this much trouble with a few orc warlocks of the Shadow Council?

"Get back to work! This has nothing to do with any of you. Don't think you can slack off, you worthless lot!"

A Cult overseer barked a few furious roars. After a momentary silence, the clanging of hammers resumed in the plaza.

"Does Nai'hojar know about this?" Jace asked.

"He should be investigating," the guard answered.

"Don't waste manpower searching the Bone Wastes, soldier," Jace said. "That coward won't get far under the black dragons' and Alliance rangers' watch. Take me to where the two warlocks were killed."

"Yes, Master." The guard thumped his chest with a fist.

Following the guard into the Arcane Sanctum, a faint aura of restlessness and panic permeated the entire underground palace.

The place where the two warlocks had been killed was a fairly concealed underground passage. Jace remembered discussing things with Sabellian, Sora, and Needleflame at the end of this passage once before.

The ground was splattered with shadow-tainted black blood. Whoever the blood belonged to, they must have suffered terrible agony from a Curse of Agony before dying.

"And the bodies?" Jace asked.

"Already moved, Master Negag." Sabellian approached from behind, waving for the guard to leave, then said, "Come with me. I'll tell you what I've found."

Jace nodded and followed Sabellian into a branching side passage.

As he passed Needleflame, the young black dragon stared blankly at the black blood on the ground, not blinking. She looked extremely serious on the surface, but a faint, barely concealed smugness was practically bubbling out of her inside.

"Ah... damn it."

Once they entered the corridor, Sabellian sighed. "That little one definitely gave away her emotions to you. Took some of the fun out of my show."

He smiled and handed over a stitched-together bundle of demon hide. Jace took it and said, "You got it."

"No, no, no." Sabellian immediately corrected. "Master Anotok took it with him. This item has absolutely nothing to do with the murder and desertion incident."

Jace chuckled softly and opened the demonhide book. The texture of the leather felt different from any demon he'd touched before. He was very familiar with the Felhound hide and Mo'arg hide commonly made into pages.

"Your guess was right." Sabellian said. "This is indeed Gul'dan's. Most of the contents are related to the souls of Auchindoun. I haven't had time to read it all yet. Take a look first, then figure out how to copy it in a few days."

Jace nodded. The black dragon prince reached over and flipped to a page. "Look here. It records a method for draining the souls of all of Auchindoun to power a portal. Much more efficient than any method I know. Weren't you saying you weren't sure how to manipulate souls to open the portal? This is a good place to start."

"Soul Well?" Jace read the demonic script aloud.

"Yes, Soul Well," Sabellian said.

"This might be what Gul'dan left for Talon Gorlock..." Jace flipped to the second page, which recorded many spells related to the Soul Well.

Some of these spells indicated that the Soul Well could not only drain wandering souls but even drain the souls of the living.

Setting aside its function of converting souls into dark magical power, if this Soul Well were deployed in the center of a village or town, it could slowly torture every living person inside to death. Draining their souls while transforming them — twisted with pain and hatred — into more convenient "fuel" for dark magic. Truly evil to the core.

Jace skimmed through the spellcasting content, flipped further, and came to some messages — or "guidelines" — that Gul'dan had left for the Shadow Council in Demonic. Among them, Gul'dan mentioned that these were spells Kil'jaeden had taught him, which he had passed on to the Shadow Council without reservation. Therefore, the Shadow Council must pledge loyalty to him to receive more opportunities for supreme power, and other such messages.

At this point, Gul'dan probably hadn't yet developed rebellious thoughts against Kil'jaeden. Medivh hadn't yet shown him the power hidden in the Tomb of Sargeras — power far exceeding Kil'jaeden's blessings. So the writing contained a fair amount of useless praise for Kil'jaeden.

But as he read on, a slight worry crept into Jace's mind.

Kil'jaeden clearly knew about Auchindoun's existence. He knew the souls of Auchindoun were enough to create a portal straight into the depths of the Twisting Nether. The power of draenei souls could let a warlock accomplish unimaginable feats.

Had he considered that the expeditionary force might use Auchindoun to escape?

Or had he concluded that the expeditionary force wouldn't be evil enough to plunder Auchindoun's souls, and so hadn't considered it?

And what about himself? What decision would this power-hungry evil warlock make?

Jace closed the book. Though it was just a notebook, not containing any powerful magic, the demonic runes penned by Gul'dan himself still cast a gloomy influence over his emotions.

Regardless of whether Kil'jaeden had realized it or not, it was too late to stop them now. Even if the Burning Legion mobilized right this moment to obstruct the expeditionary force's movements, it would be too late. There were only a few days left.
