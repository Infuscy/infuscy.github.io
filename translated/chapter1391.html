Chapter 1391: Almost There

Every chisel strike from the laborers, every roar from the overseers, was like a countdown... the string in Jace's mind pulled tighter and tighter.

The portal was nearly finished. The orc laborers had dismantled the scaffolding. Some Cult soldiers had already started spontaneously coming to pray in front of the portal yesterday or even the day before.

He'd planned to spend these few days thoroughly digesting Gul'dan's notebook. Besides the Soul Well, there was plenty of other content — not just useless rambling to the Shadow Council. Maybe some of it could come in handy.

But he couldn't focus on reading anymore. Not just because the malicious and corrupting runes made him restless, unable to calm his mind enough to understand the spells. But also because, at this point, he couldn't put his mind to anything beyond the portal.

After Gul'dan's manuscript was discovered, a few more orc warlocks had left Auchindoun. Jace wasn't surprised at all.

They must have mistakenly believed the fleeing orc had taken the manuscript and recklessly left the Shadow Council to chase after it. Jace and Sabellian didn't bother stopping them. Time was running short. No point dwelling on the fate of those lonely exiles.

Most of the expeditionary force troops had assembled at Alleria's Fortress. The draenei refugees — including Velen — had also arrived in Terokkar Forest from Wildhammer Stronghold. Even the ethereals had arrived.

Some of the vanguard was already approaching Auchindoun. Needleflame had relayed the message about the portal's imminent completion and the celestial phenomenon that would appear when it connected to Archmage Vargus.

The mages of Kirin'Var Village — who'd spent years in shattered Draenor and studied void energy in Farahlon for so long — might notice the activity above Auchindoun even faster than Jace.

To the north, Glorion was leading the black dragons toward Honor Hold in Hellfire Peninsula, ready to bring Tol'beorn and the remaining elites back.

That was news Needleflame had brought back yesterday or the day before. By now, the black dragons had probably already departed with the soldiers on board.

Black dragons appearing in groups and landing en masse at Honor Hold meant it was no longer possible to hide the expeditionary force's mass evacuation from Hellfire Citadel's sentries.

Kargath Bladefist might have already notified the Throne of Kil'jaeden about this. The Legion might have already begun mobilizing their army.

But that was just Jace's guess. He had no idea what was happening in Hellfire Peninsula at this moment.

And he was in the dark about everything else right now, not just Honor Hold.

In Shadowmoon Valley, Grede and High Lord Kurdran were preparing to leave Wildhammer Stronghold. Gryphon riders and the red dragonflight would help them evacuate quickly. But according to the message Needleflame had received from Alleria's Fortress, those dwarves probably only set out yesterday or today.

Considering the distance from there to here... Jace figured they probably couldn't reach Auchindoun in time when the portal opened.

I'll see them when I get back from the Throne of Kil'jaeden, he thought, lighting the circle before him.

This time, the communion with the Telogrus Rift wasn't as difficult as the last. The other end quickly returned the doomlord's familiar voice.

"Master. I take it Auchindoun's portal is complete?"

"Essentially finished," Jace said. "How's the other side?"

The demon said quietly, "The portal is complete. But Khadgar wants me to tell you that stabilizing the Eye of Dalaran's power to activate it will take a little time."

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. "How about one day from now? Around this time tomorrow."

"Khadgar agrees," the demon said.

"Any longer would be problematic," Jace said. "The situation in the Shadow Council can't sustain too much delay. Now that the portal's finished, we can't afford to wait any longer — otherwise the orcs will start wondering what we're really up to."

"Khadgar says he understands," the demon replied. "He agrees to this timing."

"Then once I see the transmitted mana, I'll open the portal on this side," Jace said. "By then, I'll try to gather all the Shadow Council members near the portal."

"Listen carefully. When that happens, aside from some laborers with no combat ability, there should be about two hundred orc soldiers gathered. Around thirty of them were once elite warriors guarding Murmur — very strong. There are twenty-six Shadow Council warlocks left, of varying skill levels, but none to be underestimated. The most troublesome are seven Felguard and fourteen Felhounds left behind by Gul'dan. Those creatures' loyalty to the Burning Legion is almost unbelievable — none of them will retreat. So tell the Kirin Tor mages and the Alliance soldiers to be prepared before entering the portal."

"And, you go in first, Lord Karelz."

"That means I'll have to participate in fighting against the Burning Legion, Jace Sesso? Don't use the contract to force me..." the demon said reluctantly.

"What difference does it make? Do you think when the portal's energy breaks through, Kil'jaeden won't know it was you? Unless you're a nobody in the Burning Legion that no one remembers." Jace said coldly. "You've already betrayed Kil'jaeden. If you don't pledge yourself to me, to the Alliance... how are you going to survive in this dark void? I don't want to have to order you to do everything. I'd rather you understand the stakes yourself."

"Of course I'm no nobody." Though Karelz forced himself to say this calmly, the fury inside him was hard to contain. Even the soul shard on Jace's circle flickered briefly, and Morophil allowed herself a slight smile.

After the circle burned out, in the dark chamber, Jace could even hear his own heart pounding. The sound carried a faint, indistinct dark energy, yet simultaneously burned hot — like the dying groan of a red dragon...

Sensing the peculiar aura emanating from his own body, he realized Glorion's description had been more fitting than ever.

"Everything seems ready, master," Morophil said.

"Yes, everything's ready," Jace said. "We'll see what happens tomorrow. Tonight, come with me to the Arcane Sanctum. I'm going to address the entire Cult. You'll stand beside me."

"Aren't you worried they'll recognize me?" Morophil asked.

"There's only one night left, Morophil." Jace reached up and touched the succubus's cheek, feeling the evil life-fire burning beneath her cold skin. "Besides, the portal's already built. Even if they have suspicions, they don't have the time or energy to think it through. Right now, I need your deterrence — let them see what kind of Succubus I can command. Let them genuinely want to follow my orders and gather around the portal to await the 'Legion's' arrival."

Morophil lowered her head and smiled shyly. "When you say 'command' like that, it really makes a girl feel warm all over, darling."

In Blackcate's lecture hall, hundreds of warlocks and officers had assembled on the steps below. Jace observed the demonic servants that had been summoned, and the Broken slaves and priests who didn't dare raise their eyes to look at Morophil.

Morophil looked down at the Legion worshippers below like a true Shivarra matron. Even those senior Shadow Council members who'd usually maintained a hint of arrogance toward Jace and his group had been cowed into submission.

The core members of the Cult had all arrived. Sabellian nodded toward Jace, and Jace walked step by step to the front of the circular platform.

"The portal is complete, everyone. It's been a mere thirty-seven days — but it was bought with decades of torment and pain." He spoke loudly, sweeping his gaze across the countless silent Cult members gathered there.

"I know that many of you, before solidifying your faith in the Legion, asked yourselves this countless times — was my choice the right one? Does this path of shadow, destruction, and flame lead us in the right direction?"

"Having sacrificed everything, abandoned everything — was it only to receive failure, only doubt, only death, abandonment, and despair?"

"When you raised your blade against the mad, the calm, the enemy, the friend, even your own parents, your lovers, your children... was it worth it? What was it all for?"

Jace clenched his jaw as he spoke, suppressing his emotions as he glanced at the cold-faced Morophil beside him.

"Tomorrow, this draenei necropolis finally meets its true destiny. After decades of setbacks and condemnation, the Shadow Council will rise anew in your hands — the greatest warlocks of this shattered land."

"Tomorrow, the master will open a new chapter for Draenor. This once-overlooked world will become a new starting point for the Legion's masters to conquer countless stars. Soon, the shame the Horde suffered in Azeroth will be washed away forever — completely."

"In this great future, under the light of this endless magic and power, our race will ascend. We will prove with blood and strength, wisdom and will, that we are the most unstoppable race in the dark void. The orcs will prove that we can lead Sargeras' countless legions on more campaigns of destruction. Everyone here will witness it!"

"I, Negag, the most loyal envoy of Magtheridon, Lord of Draenor, the student and humble servant of Lord Kil'jaeden, invite every elite of the Shadow Council, every loyal guard who once protected this soul-cave, to approach the portal and embrace the future."

With that, he paused for a moment, then switched to Demonic, imitating the tone of Archimonde the Defiler, and thundered, "Because after tonight passes, fire will rain from the sky, the earth will be stained with blood, and Draenor will be consumed by FLAMES!"

"Legion! Legion! Legion!"

The orcs roared along with him. Jace stood with both arms raised, staring at the tattered purple flag of the Shadow Council hanging overhead.

Behind the tears in the flag was the faded sigil of the Auchenai.
