## Chapter 1392: The Auchindoun Gate

The morning was overcast, with a light drizzle falling and muffled thunder rolling. The already misty skies above Auchindoun had turned a deep gray.

Jace's heart was pounding. He wasn't sure if he'd be able to make out the void energy in the sky in this weather, but then he figured he was overthinking it — what was a little rain against the power of the Eye of Dalaran?

"When?" Sabellian asked beside him.

"I told you, soon," Jace said.

"Soon isn't an answer," the Black Prince said.

"Could be any moment," Jace said.

"Do you absolutely have to open the portal at the last possible second?" Sabellian asked.

"The Telogrus Rift isn't exactly stable, and neither is Auchindoun. So the shorter the portal window, the better," Jace said. "You don't even have to leave. What are you worried about?"

"This is still an operation we've been planning for a long time. Don't you think I want to see it succeed?" Sabellian said.

Needle stood not far away, her eyes fixed on the portal without a word. Her fear and apprehension were completely unconcealed. She probably wasn't ready to cross to the other side yet.

Sora was about the same. As the most easily flustered of the four, her mind was already a tangled mess.

The Black Prince took a deep breath and swept his gaze across the entire Auchindoun plaza. The orcs had gathered there, most half-kneeling or even prostrate, muttering incantations under their breath. In the rain, the whole scene was unbearably grim and oppressive.

These cultist soldiers had been pent up in this place for over a decade. Blaykhart was dead. Warlord was dead. Murmur had disappeared. Even their sworn enemy, Maladaar, had fallen.

One notable warlock after another had died. Some had fled, even defected.

Jace could feel it — many of the orcs were fervent, but just as many were teetering on the edge of breaking point, including some of the warlocks. Every now and then, faint sobbing could be heard across the plaza.

Truth be told, he was the same.

Because this whole plan was so fragile.

Any single link failing could cost the expedition a horrific, unbearable price. But even so, everyone supported him.

From here to the other side. To Prophet Velen and his draenei. To the ethereals of the Consortium. Because everyone knew this was the only solution anyone could think of — even Node Prince Haramad, who'd never admit he was out of ideas.

The other options — fighting to the end, hiding and stalling — were just nice words. As long as the Scepter of Sargeras still existed and the Burning Legion kept pouring into Draenor, those were just desperate ways to die.

Jace's plan was the only one with feasibility, the only one with initiative. Even if it hung by a thread. And the end of that thread was the portal before him.

Another clap of thunder rolled. Sabellian clapped him on the shoulder and pointed at the sky. Jace looked up and noticed that the pale gray sky was actually darkening.

Between the outlines of some nameless white planet, a pale pink passage was spinning, spinning, slowly forming into a spiral.

Sora and Needle both looked over at the same time. Jace said quietly, "It's time."

"Remember to protect the portal, Ney'ho'gal."

"Go in peace," the black dragon said.

Jace walked down the steps, weaving through the kneeling orcs toward the portal. Many of them secretly tracked his footsteps.

He turned back and swept his gaze across all of Auchindoun, then raised his hands high.

Just as he was about to summon the spirits, a commotion broke out on the high walls of Auchindoun — some orc seemed to be shouting something. A few orcs looked up toward it, but most didn't want to disrupt the ritual at a moment like this. They kept their heads down and stayed silent.

Even if Jace couldn't make out the shouts, he knew what they were saying. The orc scouts had probably spotted the approaching expedition.

There might have been a communication problem — maybe a Wildhammer gryphon rider or a Quel'Thalas dragonhawk rider had spotted the void channel and couldn't resist coming to check it out, and the orcs had noticed.

But it didn't matter.

"Spirits of Auchindoun, it's time to answer my call." Jace raised his staff and roared in Demonic: "Now! Fulfill your promise! Open the portal!"

"Promise?"

"What promise?"

Some of the orc warlocks thought the demonic incantation sounded strange, but the howling of spirits that soon filled the entire Auchindoun drowned out their doubts.

Countless towering spectral figures emerged from the damaged walls and broken chambers. They hadn't been drawn by the power of Ulthalesh. Instead, they stood at the edges, at the great gate, between the pillars, chanting necromantic spells steadily, channeling their magic in unison toward the portal.

Jace watched with satisfaction as hundreds and thousands of soul energies poured into the portal behind him. Soon the demonic runes on it began to flash in sequence, and pale green ripples rose from within the frame.

"It's working, it's working..." He looked back at the gate, then at the souls straining to channel energy into the portal, unable to suppress his excitement.

The whole of Auchindoun began to tremble. Dust on the ground started to float. The laborers pressed themselves flat against the ground, too terrified to move. Sabellian stared intently at the spiral in the sky, which was growing clearer by the moment.

Thunder roared. Gale-force winds swept across the plaza. Stone columns and broken walls that couldn't take it any longer began to collapse. The already shattered ground developed cracks.

The spirits steadily channeled their magic. Some of the weaker ones grew faint in the process, but more and more spirits appeared to take their place. Eventually, even the warlocks started to panic.

This wasn't the soul-draining magic they were familiar with. It was completely different from how Gul'dan, Ner'zhul, or even Teron Gorefiend had guided souls!

The runes on the portal flashed brighter and brighter. One by one, they fully ignited. This plain, crude gate grew more sinister and profound by the second.

The sky darkened, then went black. The spiral that covered half of the Bone Wastes had taken on a purple-red color like the blood of shadow. And the center of that spiral was directly above Jace's head — right above him!

At that moment, an older warlock squinted at the void spiral and muttered under his breath, "The Eye of Dalaran?"

Energy suddenly burst from the massive gate, blasting out scorching hot wind. Jace and a few nearby warlocks were shoved backward, stumbling several steps. One warlock even fell off the portal platform.

The orcs began shouting demonic slogans, welcoming the arrival of the demon. But amid those wave-like cries, Jace felt everything around him grow quieter.

With the continuous grinding of iron chains, a massive figure emerged from within.

He was seven or eight meters tall. The enormous wings behind him had already exceeded what the portal could bear. His ugly — almost comical — head was topped with two huge, tawny horns like a buffalo's. His two grotesquely small eyes burned with fel-green light, staring blankly at everything before him.

Sabellian, from a distance, looked at Jace with some doubt, his staff already raised slightly. On the other side, Needle was starting to lose her composure too.

"Bow to Lord Car'lez, insignificant mortals."

The demon grinned wickedly after speaking. His metal armor clanked and clattered with a piercing sound. The last time Jace had heard the guy's voice so clearly felt like a lifetime ago.

The orcs began shouting, chanting titles like "Lord Car'lez" and "Doom Lord." The warlocks were so excited they dropped their staves and knelt on the ground. But the demon didn't respond to their worship. Instead, he looked down at the dark-skinned orc warlock staring back at him.

"So, shall we begin, Jace Sesso?"

The orcs looked up in confusion. The warlocks went wide-eyed with shock. In the distance, a gryphon's shriek cut across the sky.

Figures gradually grew clear within the portal. Pale blue robes. Shields shimmering with arcane light emerged from the ripples.

"You threw off my schedule a little, Car'lez." Jace raised his hand helplessly. The powerful shadow magic gathering around him tore through his disguise bit by bit, revealing his true appearance beneath the dark orc guise. The tall, sturdy young human now stood beneath the doom lord's black hooves.

"But... let's begin."
