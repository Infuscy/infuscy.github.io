## Chapter 140: The Dalaran Mage

Just like the town, the bar was packed to the rafters. Jace and Grede couldn't even find a spot to sit at the counter — they had to grab their glasses, prop themselves against the wall by the window, and drink standing up. Forget about getting a meal. Each of them could only clutch a baguette and gnaw on it. Strictly speaking, this probably counted more as a "Lorder."

"You were saying something about Orgrim Doomhammer earlier?" Grede tore off a piece of bread and chewed. "Orgrim — he's the orc's warchief, right?"

"That's the one," Jace said.

The dwarf smacked his lips. "Orgrim is a wanted man of the Alliance. Whether it's Quel'Thalas, the dwarves, or Stormwind… they all bear a bone-deep hatred for him, Jace. Wherever he's been, he committed grave war crimes. Does the Kingdom of Lordaeron have any idea how important this criminal is? I don't believe he had a chance to break out. It's probably just some random orc chieftain who escaped from an internment camp."

"You know orcish. You know there are plenty of clans — Warsong, Dragonmaw, Frostwolf, and the rest — and every one of them calls its own tribal leader a chieftain, while the leader of the whole Horde is called the warchief. Word gets passed around, and a lot of people probably got it into their heads that the warchief himself escaped from the Lordaeron dungeons. And those dungeons — not even a demon could break out of them. That's not me talking."

"He said 'orc chieftain,' so I took a guess," Jace said. "How am I supposed to know the details? He wasn't about to explain it to me point by point."

"Fair enough." Grede rinsed his mouth with a swig of beer, then glanced around the bar at the people sitting and standing inside. After a moment he said, "Have you noticed anything, Jace?"

"Noticed what?" Jace mumbled, only half paying attention.

"Don't you think there are a lot of mages in here?" the dwarf asked.

Jace had been about to bring up having Grede watch him practice Mage Armor that night, but the question caught the words in his throat. He followed Grede's gaze and took a look.

Now that he was paying attention, there really did seem to be a lot of mages in the room.

At the corner window sat a table of mages. They had warm wool cloaks draped over their shoulders on the outside, but one of them was wearing a Dalaran cloak, and the other two showed flashes of purple robe at their cuffs and collars.

Over by the counter there were two more robed mages. One wore a green robe and cradled a staff in his arms, while the other was actually wearing a Kirin Tor surcoat — the Eye of the Arcane embroidered right over his chest.

Kirin Tor mages were the elite of Dalaran's mages, just like that one who'd ridden the gryphon.

Why were there so many Dalaran mages here?

Southshore was closer to Dalaran, sure, but this was still the territory of the Kingdom of Lordaeron. Dalaran was nothing more than a city-state ruled by mages, its territory limited to the stretch of plains between the southern shore of Lake Lordamere and the foot of the Alterac Mountains, sandwiched between the Kingdom of Lordaeron and the Kingdom of Alterac. It had no real reach into places farther out.

For this many Dalaran mages to show up on Lordaeron's territory, something must have gone wrong.

Had something happened in Southshore?

"Did they come to hunt down that orc chieftain?" Grede asked again.

"Maybe," Jace said. "That orc chieftain might have warlock retainers with him — they'd need mages to deal with that. But we might as well just ask a mage directly."

The two of them carried their glasses through the crowd and approached a mage standing alone by a nearby window, gazing out. Jace spoke first. "Master mage, may I ask why there are so many mages here?"

The mage didn't answer right away. He turned, sized up Jace and the dwarf beside him, then let his eyes settle on the sigil on Jace's finger. "You're from Stormwind?"

Jace felt a flicker of exasperation. In Stormwind, they all thought he was a Lordaeronian. Now that he was in Lordaeron, despite speaking with a pure northern accent, everyone treated him like he'd come from the south.

"Yes. I'm a mage of the Wizard Sanctum."

"Did you hear about Alterac being raided by Horde remnants?" the mage asked. "Not long ago — just a few months back."

"I heard about it."

Jace knew the mage was referring to the incident where Teron Gorefiend had raided Alterac searching for one of the three artifacts, the Book of Medivh, and sought out the imprisoned Perenolde royal family for clues to the artifact's whereabouts.

"That raid ended in a crushing defeat for the Alliance," the mage said. "It proved that although the Horde has been defeated as a whole in the war, the remnants that weren't driven back through the Dark Portal still have the strength to organize counterattacks. So, through the coordination of Archmage Antonidas and King Terenas II, all combat-capable mages are to assist the Kingdom of Lordaeron with defense in the surrounding regions. We've been dispatched to Southshore to investigate orcish activity."

"Is Southshore in danger?" Grede asked.

"Southshore is our staging ground south of Alterac Pass," the mage explained. "For now, the trouble is mainly concentrated north of the Alterac Mountains, on the mountain road between Dalaran and Alterac City. As far as I know, the south side is still quiet for now. At present, we're mainly guarding against surprises."

Jace asked again, "Forgive me for pressing, master mage — but does this have anything to do with the four-day road closure?"

The mage glanced at him. "Judging by your accent, you're from Brill. Are you back from Stormwind to visit family, or does the Wizard Sanctum have business for you?"

Jace caught the mage's tone and understood that the four-day road closure might involve some kind of classified matter, not something to share with non-mages. So he said directly, "I'm headed to Dalaran on behalf of the Wizard Sanctum."

The mage nodded and lowered his voice. "I haven't left Southshore recently myself, but I've heard word that Teron Gorefiend's agents are still trying to carry out sabotage in this area. Over in Ambermill to the west, there appear to have been signs of shadow magic attacks. The kingdom is worried about disrupting the flow of commerce here in Southshore, so they haven't made any detailed public announcements. In any case, if you're planning to take that road to Dalaran, watch your step."

The two of them thanked him and returned to their original spot. Grede said with a worried frown, "If there's trouble on the road between Alteran City and Dalaran… hell, who knows how much more time that'll cost us."

Jace said, "Since Dalaran and Lordaeron are already aware of the danger, they'll have those places heavily guarded. That mountain road might end up being the safest stretch on the whole continent."

Grede stretched and yawned. "Traveling through Lordaeron costs a damn sight more than I expected. I wonder if that gold coin from the ship is going to be enough."

Jace pulled out the Gurubashi gold coin and rubbed the wind serpent sigil stamped on it. "I'm almost reluctant to exchange this one."

Grede shot the coin a look. "What's the big deal? Gurubashi gold coins aren't rare. I've got Amani gold coins back home. I'll get you one to keep — it's a damn sight nicer than that one, at least it's round!"

"No way," Jace gave him a sidelong look.

"Way," Grede said. "Enough about the coin. How about we ask the barkeep if there's any work to be had in Southshore right now."

Jace looked toward the counter. "With this many people coming and going — mercenaries, warrior guilds, they've got to be all over the place. Do you really think there'll be anything left for us?" Even as he hesitated, he walked over. The place was busy, but they had plenty of hands — three barmaids waiting tables. Jace sidled up to the nearest one and asked him to call the barkeep over.

The barkeep was a thin man — which was a bit unusual. When he heard Jace was looking for work, his first question was, "Have you worked in a bar before?"

"No, and that's not what I'm talking about!" Jace stressed. "We're mercenaries. We can handle small troubles, for a fee."

"Ah. Tell me — can you handle murls?" the barkeep said. "A few days ago, some murlocs stole some of our ale. A group of men helped us recover two crates, but there are still a few more crates out on the beach west of the dock. Our inn's guards only watch the property — they won't go looking for our things. If you can get them back, two silver commission. How's that?"

The mention of fighting murlocs made Jace's stomach sink a little.

He'd seen murlocs before, but only technically. Spend enough time in Elwynn Forest and you'd run into murlocs eventually — by the river, the pond, the lake. Sometimes you could spot their figures in the distance.

But Jace had never actually fought one. In Westfall, he'd nearly run into some, but a female naga had thrown all his plans into disarray.

"Only two silver?" Jace said. "And that's for fighting murlocs?"

"The last group didn't run into any murlocs — they just hauled the crates back from the beach. Tell you what: if you do run into murloc trouble, two silver per murloc head. How about that?"

"Really?" Grede cut in.

"Of course it's real. A safer dock means better business for us, dwarf sir," the barkeep said.

Grede said at once, "We'll take it. How many crates total?"

The barkeep held up three fingers. "Three crates intact would be ideal."

"You just wait."

Grede accepted on the spot, then hauled Jace out of the bar by the arm. "Here's our chance! Two silver per murloc head — ten murlocs is twenty silver. We might make the whole trip to Aerie Peak just off this!"

"Are murlocs really that easy to deal with?" Jace said skeptically.

"Of course!" Grede said. "Murlocs are easier to catch than fish — unless your Southshore murlocs got hit by shadow magic and turned into some kind of evil mutant murlocs. After all, fish run from you, and in the water you can't catch them no matter how hard you try. Murlocs come right at you, lad!"
