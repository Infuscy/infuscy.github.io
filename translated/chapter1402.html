Chapter 1402: Still That Same Garden

"Divine Shield?"

"Bubble? Lay on Hands? Light, grant me invincibility, please."

Jace squinted up at the sun overhead, quietly muttering the phrases in the most standard Common he could manage. But no matter how fervently he prayed, there was no response.

After growing accustomed to Draenor's perpetually overcast skies, the crystal-clear blue of Stormwind felt almost unnatural to him now.

Remembering the burning sensation at the top of that black temple... at the time he hadn't felt much torment. It was only afterward, replaying the pain in his mind, that a chill of fear crept in.

At that moment, he'd been at his most chaotic, his most frenzied, his emotions at their most intense. The fight with Sabellian had already left his entire body strengthened by shadow, and when he'd finally faced Sargeras's scepter, he'd mustered every last scrap of dark power still in him to drive his arm forward.

He'd never felt such agonizing burning since surviving Deathwing's fury. Of course, that hadn't been pure flame either.

That radiance had fallen, dispelling the scepter's final layer of protection, driving out all the darkness in the hall... but Jace was part of that darkness too.

Never mind that exorcising light — even the well-meaning purge and healing from that paladin at Dragonmaw Fortress had caused him excruciating pain.

That radiance, combined with the raging void vortex along the way, had nearly killed him.

"Forget it. Better not."

He murmured to himself and continued forward.

He'd grabbed some food from Tharle's, changed into an old coat, bundled his scorched robes and tattered clothes into a cloth sack, and after thanking the bartender, hurried toward the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower.

He'd survived Sabellian's relentless blasts of flame, but his robes hadn't.

The black dragon had slaughtered hundreds of demons outside the temple before appearing before them, already battered and worn, barely maintaining his original grandeur. But even so, he still possessed an overwhelming, near-insurmountable strength.

Yet Jace could also tell — Sabellian hadn't truly intended to kill him. At least not at first.

He, on the other hand, had been different.

For a moment, he'd genuinely wanted to kill the black dragon, to use his soul to complete the final step of the plan. For an instant, he'd truly believed that was the only way out of this situation.

So when Sabellian finally charged at him, Jace had almost felt a sense of release — like he'd been given some kind of permission to fight back without guilt. Everything felt natural, reasonable...

That nearly mind-consuming rage still lingered, serving as proof that none of it had been a hallucination or a dream.

Thinking this, he glanced around at the apprentices and novice mages hurrying past.

After all, the last thing he remembered before closing his eyes was collapsing in Morlofil's arms, unaware of time or whether he'd live or die. Then suddenly he was here — it made him wonder if this was part of some kind of life-flashing-before-his-eyes sequence.

Then he spotted someone standing not far away, staring at him. An old mage with brownish-gray hair.

"Se... you."

The old mage squinted, hesitating. It was Marin.

"Master." Jace rubbed his fingers together hard, suppressing his excitement as he walked over. "Master, I'm... Jace Sesso."

Hearing this, Marin's remaining hair almost seemed to puff up from behind.

The old mage's wrinkled eyes went wide as he looked Jace up and down several times before saying, "It really is you, Mr. Sesso?!"

Jace pushed back his disheveled hair and tugged at his burned, messy beard. "It's really me. But I need to get to Stonewatch quickly, Master. The Expedition needs help — they're still holding out at Auchindoun against the Burning Legion. I have to—"

"Wait." Marin blinked rapidly, glancing around. Only when a passing novice mage curiously peeked at Jace did he turn back.

"The portal is already closed," he said. "Are you really Jace Sesso? How is that possible... what happened? How did you get here?"

Jace paused for a moment, then asked, "What about the Expedition? Did they make it back?"

Marin hesitated, then nodded. But Jace could clearly sense his emotions beginning to churn with confusion, and mixed in with that was something like... hostility.

"Wait, Master. I really am Jace Sesso." Jace raised his hands. "I was teleported. Summoned back, I think."

Truth be told, even now Jace could barely believe whether any of what he'd witnessed at the end was real or not.

The novice mage's insignia. Ursula Dering...

Kil'jaeden's barrier hadn't collapsed when Sargeras's scepter was broken — Jace could feel that much. But Ursula's voice had pierced through the interference and reached his mind. If it was a summoning ritual, though... why hadn't he made it into the ritual circle?

Marin hesitated for a long time without speaking. Jace quickly added, "I'm not some demon in disguise, Master. I'm the real deal."

The old mage's grip on his staff tightened, and even the flame gem at its top began to glow brighter.

The nearby mages passing through the garden clearly didn't notice the brewing crisis. A battle between a warlock who had once defeated Deathwing and the Wizard Sanctum's most powerful fire mage was on the verge of erupting.

Jace licked his lips anxiously, then suddenly raised his hand and conjured a small Light spell. The spell's quality was so mismatched with the Court Mage ring on his finger that Marin stared at the glowing light in shock, blinking dazedly only after it faded.

After acquiring Fang of the Sky, Jace had almost never cast Light again, so the spell had remained at its most unrefined level. But it was precisely this familiar casting style, this familiar feeling, that finally soothed Marin's last shred of doubt.

"Mr. Sesso." Marin loosened his grip on his staff. "It's not that I doubted you, but the summoning ritual failed. Word from Stonewatch said that Khadgar's attempt to summon you never worked."

"Khadgar summoned me?" Jace asked.

"That seems to be the case." Marin explained slowly. "This has been classified as a secret by the Wizard Sanctum and the Kirin Tor. Of course, considering you're a member of the Court Mages, and most importantly, you're the one who was being summoned, I thought it wouldn't hurt to tell you."

"How long ago was this, Master?" Jace asked.

"Two days. Two days and three nights ago, to be precise, when I got the news." Marin took a deep breath to steady himself. "By then, the portal had already been forced shut."

"Now they're planning to open a new passage from Azeroth to Draenor... to rescue you."

Jace looked back toward the Slaughtered Lamb. At least the timeline hadn't been too badly skewed. That was infinitely better than what he'd secretly feared.

"Come with me." Marin gestured. "Now that you're back, there's no need for them to waste any more effort."

Jace nodded and followed Marin onto the garden's cobblestone path leading to the spiral staircase of the mage tower. Then, thinking of something, he said, "Can I go see—"

"Your father and mother." Marin tilted his head. "They're both at Stonewatch."

"What are they doing there?" Jace asked worriedly. "Isn't that too close to the Telogrus Rift?"

"You should be glad they didn't step straight into the Telogrus Rift. The Kirin Tor mages barely managed to convince them to stay at Stonewatch." Marin said. "In fact, King Varian promised Archmage Khadgar that he'd have someone specifically watch them, to keep them from doing anything rash once they learned about you. But I suppose a former Lordaeron guard captain is still hard to contain — they snuck out of Stormwind anyway. Luckily, the guards at Lakeshire noticed them, so we managed to intercept them near Stonewatch."

Jace listened in silence as Marin narrated, thinking to himself that this was probably Henny's idea. He'd never doubted that if Henny hadn't married Vic and had him, she might have become some zealous assassin of the Scarlet Crusade someday.

The spiral staircase of the mage tower was crowded with robed mages bearing the Violet Eye insignia. Jace had never imagined he'd see so many people from Dalaran at the Wizard Sanctum.

They looked curiously at this strange figure — a man in ordinary clothes, draped in a dirty crimson cloak, clutching a tightly-wrapped staff that looked like a withered branch, his face covered in a messy beard and his hair caked with dust.

Usually, someone dressed like this would be a wilderness mage who could barely afford his next meal — exactly the kind of thing the Kirin Tor preferred not to tolerate.

Passing through the Wizard Sanctum's gallery, he saw his portrait again. Jace Sesso, so young that he found it almost unfamiliar.

He stared at his reflection in a blue gem embedded in the hall's door, the curved crystal surface distorting his features. Grimy, with a touch of panic — a stark contrast to the cold, detached figure in the painting.

The great doors slowly swung open, and voices raised in argument spilled out.

"...must be returned to Dalaran, Archmage Khadgar. The Eye of Dalaran and Medivh's book cannot remain in the Kingdom of Stormwind for much longer. If you intend to open the portal again, you can wait until you return to the Northern Lordaeron to reconsider. The Tower of Ilgalar holds no unique advantage in connecting to the Telogrus Rift."

"As a senior member of the Kirin Tor, you should know very well, Archmage Ansirem Runeweaver, that a portal that opens here won't necessarily succeed in a place with stronger ley lines. Unless you can persuade the Sun King to help!"

"Of course I understand that. But this time on Draenor, who would be willing to help us build a portal? Who can still pierce the barrier Kil'jaeden left behind and stay in contact with us? I have no doubt that you and the greatest mages of the Kirin Tor will eventually find a way, but it will require careful planning. In the meantime, we must ensure the safety of these Kirin Tor artifacts."

"I know. I understand. I'm just asking the Council for a few more days. Ah, damn it. Tell Dalaran that I'm withdrawing my earlier promise not to run for the Council of Six. Next year, I'll... no, this year. Before the year is out, I'll challenge Archmage Antonidas himself for—"

"Archmage Khadgar."

"Master Marin." Khadgar responded respectfully, glancing back at the door that had just opened. He was about to draw another breath to continue his argument when he suddenly noticed the person beside Marin.

"Jace... Sesso?"

At the sound of that name, the whole room fell silent. Everyone — whether they'd been busy, frowning, or quietly discussing — turned to look.

"Archmage Khadgar..." Jace sighed. "Sorry, I'm not sure how this happened either."
