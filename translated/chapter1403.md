## Chapter 1403: The Summoner

"I hope you didn't hear too much of that discussion between me and Archmage Ansirem Runeweaver." Khadgar rubbed his brow as he leaned against the wall, glancing up at the spiral staircase above. "From the inside this tower looks pretty spacious, but finding a genuinely private place to talk is surprisingly hard."

With that, Khadgar grabbed Jace's hand and gave it a firm shake. "You're back, Jace. I..."

His expression twisted as he struggled to find the right words, but in the end, not a single one came out. Jace asked, "Does this mean you can finally answer to the Kirin Tor?"

"That's certainly one reason." Khadgar shook his head. "But you surviving is more important to me. I don't like owing people big favors, especially dead people."

Then, as if sensing something, he released his grip and asked, "What happened to your hand? Fel burns?"

"Holy Light," Jace said. "It should be Holy Light."

Khadgar stared at Jace for a moment, then snorted with a laugh. "Are you telling me that the Holy Light decided at the last moment to help Kil'jaeden out, and take down the arrogant fool who destroyed Sargeras's scepter?"

"Maybe." Jace smiled. "You know we destroyed the scepter."

"The first to sense it was Prophet Velen." Khadgar rubbed his brow again. "Ah, I really don't want to remember that moment."

"Velen said that not long after you destroyed the scepter, the vortex over Hellfire Peninsula showed severe signs of instability, confirming our judgment."

"Then the vortices the eredar mages were using to disrupt our portal passage got pulled along too. We thought it was good news at first, but then the Eye of Dalaran also suffered severe interference."

"Without a doubt, the scepter's influence over all of Draenor had deepened over the years. I figure if we could see the anomalies over the Throne of Kil'jaeden, you could probably see what was happening in the skies above Auchindoun too... if you weren't too busy at the time."

"I saw it." Jace nodded. "In fact, Kil'jaeden told me about it too. He said what I'd done was affecting you, and that you wouldn't be able to hold out much longer."

"How considerate of the Deceiver." Khadgar let out a dry laugh, then lowered his voice. "So he really did descend on that mountain peak."

"Don't tell me Prophet Velen sensed that too," Jace said.

"No. None of us had any idea — including the Prophet." Khadgar said. "It was the black dragon who told us."

"Sabellian went to you?" Jace asked.

"He didn't show up at Auchindoun." Khadgar said. "Gorelorio told us. You should know black dragons have some crude form of communication through their blood."

"True," Jace said.

Khadgar nodded. "He said Sabellian witnessed with his own eyes that you died on that mountaintop — killed by Kil'jaeden himself. He said if we wanted to live, we should leave immediately."

"Then why did you still try to find me?" Jace asked.

Khadgar looked at Jace like he was an idiot, then said, "Because Lord Karelz was still helping us maintain the portal, Jace Sesso! Did you really think I'd believe a doom lord would willingly help us escape if nobody was controlling him? He told us you were alive — that the black dragon was lying to us."

He held up two fingers. "An eredar and a black dragon — who's more likely to lie? I chose to believe the eredar."

"So what really happened? Did the black dragon betray you? Was he trying to kill you with his lies? What's your beef with him?"

Jace was silent for a moment, then said, "No. He didn't betray us. He just... didn't think we could win against Kil'jaeden."

Everyone had calculated that there was no way Kil'jaeden could manifest on the shattered Draenor. But when faced with that possibility in reality, even Khadgar couldn't be a hundred percent sure.

No one knew exactly what Kil'jaeden could or couldn't do. He'd reached out from the twisting nether and grabbed Ner'zhul's fleeing soul, then hurled it into the Frozen Throne. He'd set up a terrifying barrier over Draenor across the Twisting Nether, leaving even the Naaru and the ethereals helpless. Even if he'd done it all through the power of Sargeras's scepter, it was still staggering.

Jace knew that Kil'jaeden could only project illusions onto Draenor only because he knew what would happen later in another timeline.

Even after Illidan had betrayed and disappointed him multiple times, in the end he could only threaten Illidan and Kael'thas with illusions in the Black Temple, ordering them to return to Azeroth and destroy the Lich King.

But judging Sabellian by the same standard wasn't fair. After all, Illidan himself had been intimidated by the Deceiver at that time, obediently leaving the Karabor he'd already seized to return to Northrend and fight the death knight Arthas, almost dying in the process.

Not to mention that no one knew what Kil'jaeden looked like in the eyes of a black dragon prince.

"You're saying Kil'jaeden really did manifest there," Khadgar said.

"It was some kind of illusion. Similar to what he showed the orcs," Jace said.

"What did he say?" Khadgar asked.

The stairwell went deathly quiet, leaving only the murmurs from the outer corridor. Jace could hear his own name constantly mixed into the noise.

"Never mind." Khadgar waved his hand. "Don't answer that stupid question. How did you get back to Stormwind?"

Jace said doubtfully, "Master Marin said you led the summoning ritual."

"So you really heard it?" Khadgar straightened up in surprise.

"Yes," Jace said.

"I did take part in the summoning, but I can hardly claim to have led it." The archmage grabbed Jace's shoulder. "It was Lady Ursula Dering! I didn't have the power to lead that ritual. Neither could the other Daggerfall warlocks. Only she could! Only that ring — the one with your name engraved on it!"

So it really was Ursula. None of it had been an illusion.

Khadgar said excitedly, "We tried so many things — so many casters, so many combinations. Only those two together could reach your existence. I don't know how that ring held so much power, how it broke through Kil'jaeden's dreadful magic, but it worked."

"At first I planned to use the Book of Unholy Names, since you said you'd retained some of that grimoire's power... but it didn't work. None of the other belongings of yours they'd collected worked either. Only that ring — the one you gave her before you left. It actually touched your essence. She was sure of it. I was sure of it too."

"But at the time, we thought it was only marginally better than the other items. Just a little bit better. Lady Ursula Dering said you'd responded to her call. We thought she wanted so badly to save you that she was hallucinating. In that situation, we were all a bit prone to hallucinations. Shadows had surrounded all of Auchindoun. The earth had split open, the void was pouring in. Your master, Archmage Eden Marin, had held the portal open too long and was injured, forced to return to Lakeshire early. Even I felt like I couldn't hold out much longer. For that lady's magic to be disrupted was perfectly normal."

"Most importantly, you never appeared in the circle."

"I woke up at the Slaughtered Lamb." Jace pointed southwest. "I don't know why. But I felt my ring. I heard Ursula's voice."

"Anyway. Does this count as me repaying a favor?" Khadgar asked.

"Yes." Jace nodded. "Before I heard the summoning call, I thought I was dead for sure."

"You should donate that ring to Dalaran's vault." Khadgar teased. "They can write the whole story on the display plaque, with my name in it too."

"No," Jace said immediately. "Where's my ring?"

"Still with Lady Ursula Dering." Khadgar smiled. "Speaking of which, I should take you to Stonewatch quickly to see her. Everyone's waiting for news of you there — your parents, the King... How are you feeling? Can you handle another teleport?"

"If possible," Jace touched his chest, "I'd prefer a gryphon."

"Gryphon it is," Khadgar said.

Then the archmage's voice turned cold. "Oh, one more extremely important thing, Jace. Don't tell any Kirin Tor members that you actually saw Kil'jaeden. To be safe, don't tell anyone at all."

With that reminder from Khadgar, Jace snapped back to clarity from the haze of his astral journey.

A warlock who survived a direct encounter with Kil'jaeden himself, who easily and abnormally made it straight home.

Khadgar, the Sons of Lothar, his friends and family — they were willing to believe that he'd been summoned back by the alchemist Ursula Dering, that missing the ritual circle and ending up in Stormwind was just a magical anomaly.

But would others believe that? Would the Kirin Tor? Even Jace himself had his doubts.
