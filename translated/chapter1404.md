## Chapter 1404: Since It's Over, Let It Go

Elwynn Forest was a lush green, the air thick with the fresh scent of spring vegetation. An atmosphere of new hope suffused the entire woodland.

This wasn't just Jace projecting his feelings onto the scenery — it was something he could clearly sense as he sat on the gryphon's back, watching the streams, lakeshores, and oak groves pass below.

He remembered that the Midsummer Fire Festival had just ended when he'd left Stormwind. Now the next summer was already near.

So much had happened in these few months — from Auchindoun to Shadowmoon Valley, Nagrand, Bladespire Mountains, even the edge of Farahlon, then back to Auchindoun, and finally the peak of Hellfire Peninsula... the earth had split, the sky had changed color.

But right now, Mirror Lake shimmered below, flocks of white birds wheeled away from their flight path, and a few hunters looked up at the gryphon passing overhead — as though nothing at all had happened.

The siege of the Dark Portal, the Expedition's great retreat, the defense of Auchindoun, the demons besieging the Black Temple at the Throne of Kil'jaeden, the desperate gamble over Sargeras's scepter — none of it seemed to have made any impression on the endless green.

He couldn't help but think again about what Kil'jaeden had said to him. In Azeroth, people will remember you — that doesn't mean they'll want to remember you.

So, had Kil'jaeden deliberately let him return to Stormwind? Had he even secretly helped?

Unable to convince Jace to embrace the so-called "power of Sargeras," was he planning to let Jace experience this cold reality firsthand before making his decision?

Or maybe — realizing he couldn't stop the summoning ritual — he'd simply thrown a little trick into the works, making it work even better than expected, to deepen the Kirin Tor's suspicions?

Jace realized he was starting to overthink things. Even if this was all Kil'jaeden's scheming, so what? He was back in Azeroth, his feet on solid ground, no longer facing the constant threat of the Burning Legion.

More importantly, Sargeras's scepter had been destroyed. The difficulty for the Burning Legion to invade Azeroth again had just gone up by several orders of magnitude. Kil'jaeden's plan had failed. Even if he had more tricks up his sleeve, they were just stopgap measures to cut his losses.

"What are you planning to do next, Jace?" Khadgar shouted from up ahead, flying in front.

Jace's thoughts hadn't yet recovered from everything that had happened on Draenor. He looked up, sluggish, at the archmage's voice.

"I'm not investigating you for the Kirin Tor, Jace." The mage twisted his head back as far as he could. "I genuinely want to know what you plan to do next. I mean after you reunite with your family and friends at Stonewatch! I was thinking maybe you could come to Dalaran, and I'll treat you to a meal or something."

"Where at? The Violet Inn?" Jace asked.

"You bastard." Khadgar laughed bitterly. "You know I've met Aegwynn. I even asked Aegwynn to help me get into Karazhan. If I threw you into the Violet Prison, you could take me down with you. We're in the same leaky boat now. Harming you does me absolutely no good."

"Besides, do you think I *want* to be the one holding the leverage over Jace Sesso? I wish I hadn't been involved in any of this — like poor Miss Jaina Proudmoore, kept in the dark the whole time, facing Kirin Tor questioning as naively as a sewer rat that doesn't even know it's already in the Twisting Nether!"

"What's this about a sewer rat in the Twisting Nether?" Jace asked.

Khadgar coughed awkwardly. "Don't bring that up with any Kirin Tor members either, Jace."

"Got it," Jace said. "Actually, I think Miss Proudmoore might be thinking the same thing you are."

"Good point." Khadgar laughed loudly. "Don't underestimate those fourteen- or fifteen-year-old kids these days. They're much sharper than us old pumpkins born before the Dark Portal. For a clever girl like that to pretend ignorance, even Archmage Antonidas wouldn't have an easy time. And on top of that, Master Marin's good daughter is willing to take all the blame on herself, which makes things even trickier — since strictly speaking, she's also a hero who helped save the Expedition."

The archmage laughed for a while longer, seeming to enjoy the Kirin Tor's predicament of wanting to pin blame on someone but not knowing who to target.

Jace urged his gryphon to catch up, pulling alongside Khadgar. He took a moment to steel himself before asking the question he'd wanted to ask but hadn't dared.

"How many from the Expedition made it back altogether?"

"Over four thousand," Khadgar answered. "Not counting the ethereals or the draenei."

"That's over a thousand fewer?" Jace asked.

"What?" Khadgar frowned, then realization dawned. "Ah, you got the numbers from Marshal Turalyon? We've never been able to get an accurate count since Draenor was torn apart by Ner'zhul. He and Ranger-Captain Alleria could only make rough estimates, and there may have been some double-counting."

"Final count of those who returned through the portal was over four thousand. About two hundred Expedition soldiers died during the final retreat and the battle at Auchindoun, maybe more. Some of them encountered powerful magic in those conditions — getting an accurate count was nearly impossible."

Jace nodded. Khadgar adjusted his gryphon's reins, then glanced at Jace again. "You're not going to start with that 'they died because of me' nonsense, are you?"

"I told you to report to the Silver Hand as a joke — you're not actually planning to sew your name into their tabard, are you? The Burning Legion's demons committed these crimes, Jace Sesso — not your crazy plan, whatever alchemy-inspired concoction you cooked up. Think about it. Without your help, what would we have been facing across all of Draenor?"

Khadgar couldn't possibly understand what Jace was really worried about. In another timeline, the Expedition on Draenor had never faced this level of difficulty. The variable that caused the difference was undoubtedly him — the anomaly that had barged into the timeline.

Kil'jaeden's eventual appearance proved it. He'd practically been sitting there waiting for Jace to take the bait. The only problem was the result hadn't gone his way.

Killing Teron Gorefiend early had triggered a premature rift between the black dragonflight and the Horde, which ultimately led to Kil'jaeden deciding to intervene early, sparking the battle at the Dark Portal, and even bringing Sargeras's scepter back to Draenor.

So now that the Expedition had been brought back to Azeroth — a cause for celebration, yes — what other changes would it bring?

The advantage his past-life knowledge of the plot had given him was undoubtedly shrinking. It might even start backfiring in the future.

Seeing that Jace still seemed troubled, Khadgar raised a finger. "Tell you what — let's stop at Lakeshire first."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"Won't take long. But I think it's necessary." Khadgar said.

Past Mirror Lake, Lake Everstill wasn't far.

Leaving Elwynn Forest, they flew into the rolling Redridge Mountains, over patches of woodland, until Lakeshire appeared — nestled against its bridge, with its familiar red roofs and white walls, green hills and clear waters.

The town seemed far more crowded than when Jace had left. People filled the streets, the lakeshore, the bridge.

As they descended to the gryphon roost on the opposite shore, Jace noticed that many of the people coming and going wore familiar military shirts and surcoats. They were Expedition members who'd returned from the other side of the portal, temporarily housed in Lakeshire — Stonewatch probably couldn't hold the thousands of men and women who'd suddenly appeared.

Khadgar led Jace onto the Lake Everstill bridge. In the water below, soldiers were splashing around — swimming races, wrestling with the vicious sharp-toothed lake fish, trying every trick to scare off the kids who also wanted to play in the water. Every face was lit with irrepressible joy and satisfaction.

"For Azeroth!"

A soldier in the water shouted, tackling an elf and dragging him under. But the elf countered with arcane-propelled water, launching himself back up and even into the air.

On the shore, wounded soldiers watched their comrades frolicking with envy, some looking like they might tear off their bandages and jump in at any moment. Even the Church priests who'd come from all over stopped to watch, excitedly telling their colleagues the tales from Draenor they'd heard from Expedition wounded.

Khadgar took in the scene, then said quietly, "Look at this, Jace. In all the years I spent on Draenor, I never once saw this kind of ease on any Son of Lothar's face. And that's because of you. Do you think I'm any different? From the moment I closed the Dark Portal, I never thought I'd see this place again. I just came back a few months earlier than everyone else, so I look calmer on the surface."

"Putting it another way," he leaned on the railing and took a deep breath, "the kings of Lordaeron and Stromgarde will definitely be uncomfortable with the Expedition lingering in the Kingdom of Stormwind. Daelin Proudmoore has already expressed his concerns to the Kirin Tor about his daughter's involvement in all this. It's easy to predict that soon Khadgar, Turalyon, and the others will be swept into another vortex. Meanwhile, you — as someone who can't be trusted — can just ignore all those people's nonsense. It's enviable, really. God, saying it out loud makes me want to have you report me to the authorities."

"So," he turned to face Jace, "whatever you plan to do next, I suggest you ask King Varian for some time off. He'll agree. Take my advice. Put Draenor behind you. Put those demon lies behind you. Enjoy all of this with Lady Vereesa Windrunner. You've earned it."

"I will," Jace nodded.
