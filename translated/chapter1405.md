## Chapter 1405: Everyone Kept Their Promises

The mana crystals the Kirin Tor had placed along Stonewatch's walls were visible from a long way off, still floating in place. But even the mages hadn't been arrogant enough to hang the Violet Eye banner on a Kingdom of Stormwind fortress.

Khadgar raised his glowing staff as his gryphon approached the battlements. Stonewatch's sentries called out his name as they gathered around, helping steady the gryphon as he landed.

"Look who I've brought," the archmage said cheerfully, hopping off the gryphon. He glanced back at Jace landing behind him, then said, "Quickly, go to the Tower of Ilgalar and inform King Varian — Jace Sesso has returned."

"Archmage Sesso?"

The nearby soldiers all turned to stare at Jace almost in unison.

Jace could understand why they hadn't recognized him. The last time he'd had a proper meal, he'd still been in his fel orc form. Even after scrounging up something to eat at the Slaughtered Lamb, the ordeal of the astral spiral had completely killed his appetite.

Right now, he looked like a homeless drifter who'd just changed into a fresh set of clothes — a scruffy beard, a mess of hair. Nobody would connect this image with the hero who'd just saved the Sons of Lothar.

But even if they couldn't immediately place the tall, scruffy wizard, Khadgar's word was beyond question.

"Archmage Sesso has returned! Archmage Sesso has returned!"

The shout traveled from one soldier to the next along the wall. Soldiers and mages poured out of the parade ground to look toward the walls.

Many of the soldiers who'd come from Wildhammer Keep recognized Jace without much trouble — he'd been just as unkempt back at Wildhammer, and that tightly-wrapped staff of his was unmistakable. They rushed over, shouting his name, expressing their gratitude, thanking the Holy Light for guiding him safely home.

In the past, hearing that kind of talk might have made Jace mutter to himself. He'd have thought, "What did the Holy Light have to do with it?"

But this time, he really couldn't argue.

As Jace reached the bottom of the stairs, he heard hurried footsteps from the nearby stables.

"Jace! Jace!"

He looked over, and there was the familiar figure.

"Jace!"

Henny practically sprinted to her son and threw herself into his arms, clinging to his waist and refusing to let go.

On Stonewatch's walls, amid the cheers from the parade ground, Jace stood frozen for a long moment before he could react.

It's over. Jace Sesso, this is your mother. You really... you're seeing her.

"Mom..."

"Jace, I knew you'd come back. You're smarter than any mage I've ever known. You know how to protect yourself better than anyone. You've always been like that."

Jace wrapped his arms around his mother's shoulders and breathed in deeply. The guilt washed over him — that he'd thought of Kil'jaeden, of all things, the moment he'd seen his mother.

He hadn't expected that something he knew was just a clownish illusion — a laughably cheap trick — could still hit him so hard, affecting him even now.

Simple, but devastatingly effective.

"You two should have waited for me in Stormwind. This place is dangerous everywhere." Jace pressed his forehead against his mother's. "Thank goodness Khadgar didn't tell me you'd come here. I don't want to worry about whether Dad can handle five gnolls by himself while I'm dealing with demon lords."

"Well, if it came to that, your mom could handle at least two." Henny laughed through her tears. "But the King told us they'd found a way to get to Draenor. Your father and I couldn't wait anymore."

"You've lost weight, Jace."

"I'm taller too." Jace managed a smile. "Can't you tell?"

"I can." Henny wiped the tears from her face, looking back at her husband who'd followed. "But since you've been taller than Vic, I haven't been as sensitive to your height as I was when you were little."

"We didn't know what was really going on until we got here, Jace." Vic hugged Jace. "We thought the mages of Dalaran were planning to go to the other side of the Dark Portal to help you. We thought maybe we could help too. But it turned out to be a dangerously desperate retreat."

"They said you didn't make it back with the main force. But you came with Archmage Khadgar from Stormwind, so..."

"Yes." Jace opened his mouth, then hesitated. "It's hard to explain all at once."

Looking at his father's face, all the words he'd rehearsed died in his throat.

In that black demon hall, standing amid Brultalus's demon blood, he'd thought more than once — if he ever had the chance to return to his parents, he'd pour everything out, no matter the cost.

But now that he was really here, he didn't know where to start. In the end, he could only stand there, silent, and let out a sigh.

Should he really tell them everything that happened on the Throne of Kil'jaeden?

Henny seemed to sense something. "This journey wasn't easy, was it?"

"Harder than I expected," Jace said wearily. "Where are Vereesa and the others?"

His parents exchanged a look. His father said, "They're still in that magical rift — the one leading to the Twisting Nether. The Kirin Tor didn't want us risking going in there."

"The mages are right. That rift is an extremely dangerous place." Jace said. "I need to go find them, Mom, Dad. Otherwise they'll still think I'm trapped in there. When we get back to Stormwind, we can talk properly."

"We know how much they've been hoping to see you safe. Go on, Jace." Henny nodded, then held Vic's hand tightly.

Jace nodded and turned back toward Khadgar. Truth be told, he couldn't say whether he was really eager to find Vereesa and Grede, or just trying to escape his own spiraling emotions.

On that mountaintop, even if he'd had some flicker of will to live, it had been completely extinguished the moment Kil'jaeden appeared in the form of his mother.

"There's someone else at Stonewatch who wants to see you, Jace." Khadgar glanced toward the nearby tower. "Go see her. None of the priests at Stonewatch dare get close to care for her."

"Who?"

"You'd better talk to her yourself," the archmage said. "The Tower of Ilgalar's too crowded. There's nowhere to... put her."

"Put her?"

Jace nodded anyway, pushed open the door, and peered inside. A not-so-pleasant smell wafted out.

The bottom floor of this tower was surprisingly clean. Thick layers of blankets covered the floor. It looked like somewhere for someone to rest, but it was spread too wide. If it was for an animal, why use such nice blankets? Even gryphons didn't get this kind of treatment.

But then he noticed the dark, lumpy mass in the corner. A pair of burning flames lit up from its head.

"Needlefang?"

"No..." The little black dragon was practically sobbing. She whimpered, "Jace... Sesso. Jace Sesso! You really didn't die."

Bandages meant for gryphons were wrapped around Needlefang's legs and belly, which she was trying desperately to hide with her wings. But even without seeing her wounds, Jace could smell the stale herbal medicine radiating from her.

She must have been seriously injured. Even with Solara and the priests tending to her, she couldn't recover in just a few days. She couldn't shift forms either.

"Don't stare at my wounds! You have no idea how many demons I killed!" Needlefang's voice was practically a shriek. The wooden door thumped twice, and Khadgar's voice came through. "Everything all right in there, Jace?"

"Fine." Jace replied, then asked, "So, how many did you kill?"

"One doomguard," Needlefang said hoarsely. "And countless demon guards. Countless!"

"I admit, that's impressive." Jace nodded. "So what did I do to piss you off? It sounds like you weren't hoping I'd make it back alive."

Hearing that, the emotions Needlefang had been struggling to contain collapsed all at once. Her head, which looked oversized compared to her body, thumped onto the bedding. Between sobs, she said, "Tell me what happened on the Throne of Kil'jaeden! Tell me — what was my father doing?!"

Jace finally understood why she was so upset. He hadn't died, which meant Sabellian had been lying. Which meant Sabellian had abandoned him and fled, rather than being forced to retreat after witnessing Jace's death.

"He's not much of a warrior. He's not much of a warrior..." The little black dragon trembled all over. "He shouldn't have left you behind and abandoned that mountain!"

"He's really not much of a warrior, Needlefang." Jace said. "But he has a large family to take care of. You should know what would have happened to your kind on Draenor if he hadn't been there. Don't get me wrong — I'm pretty pissed that he left Morlofil and me behind and ran. But I can't blame him for his choice."

Jace hadn't expected to be the one defending the guy who'd nearly killed him. He'd thought about laying it on thick about how the bastard had tried to pick up Sargeras's scepter, to make Needlefang more committed to staying in Azeroth.

But he couldn't say it. Without Sabellian, the whole plan would never have worked. In the end, Jace just couldn't bring himself to see the guy as an enemy, no matter how things had ended.

Needlefang sobbed for a long moment before she seemed to accept this. She looked like she wasn't going to flare up anymore.

"I'm surprised you were willing to come here with us," Jace said. "I heard from Archmage Khadgar that your father was rallying all the black dragons to flee to Bladespire Mountains."

The snake-like black head drooped as she shuddered a few more times. "Because I had to keep my promise. I promised you first."

Me too, Jace thought.
