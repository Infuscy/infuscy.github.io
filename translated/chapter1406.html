Chapter 1406: Outside the Mage Tower

"I didn't expect you to bring a black dragon back here with you." Khadgar rode nearby. "But are you sure she won't end up like her kin in Azeroth soon enough?"

"I don't know." Jace gave the reins a little shake. "That's my next plan. We need to take her to Grim Batol first, see if she can live with the red dragons."

"And if she doesn't want to — will you keep her in Stormwind?" Khadgar asked. "It was hard enough getting the citizens to accept a good green dragon, Jace. A black dragon might be a stretch too soon."

"Don't worry," Jace said. "I'll try not to cause any trouble."

Khadgar tilted his head, studying Jace, then sighed in resignation.

Back when Jace had first arrived in Stormwind, the stories of the gnoll wars, the Riverpaw tribes roaming Elwynn Forest, had made the forest east of Stonewatch, the Tower of Ilgalar, and even the entire Lake Everstill area seem like forbidden territory.

Hunters from the gnoll tribes had lurked everywhere, rubbing shoulders with orc warlocks and orc stragglers left behind by the war who hadn't made it back to Draenor.

More than that — in the game, this was an incredibly dangerous area for new adventurers too. One wrong move and you'd fall into endless respawn hell, getting swarmed to death.

But right now, the road from Stonewatch to the Tower of Ilgalar had been completely cleared by the Kirin Tor and the Kingdom of Stormwind. Makeshift sentry posts and Kirin Tor mage camps lined the entire route.

Khadgar and Jace rode together, though it felt less like Jace returning home and more like the archmage giving him a tour of Kirin Tor territory.

"When will the Kirin Tor leave?" Jace asked.

"Within a month. It takes time to dismantle all the magical facilities here." Khadgar glanced at Jace. "But I'm not stalling, Jace. You're back. The Sons of Lothar have returned successfully. There's no reason for us to stay at Stonewatch."

"Don't be too hostile toward the Kirin Tor." Khadgar lowered his voice, nudging his horse closer. "I know a lot of them — including Archmage Antonidas — have given you trouble, not to mention Kel'Thuzad. But you have to understand — they've just been through the Dark Portal war. The betrayal of Medivh."

"I know," Jace said. "I just want my mage tower back to normal as soon as possible."

"'Normal'?" Khadgar teased. "You mean back when Chieftain Croc-Tooth's forces fought King Barathen Wrynn's legions over Elwynn and Lake Everstill?"

"Jace Sesso?!"

A shout erupted from the woods ahead.

Seeing the dwarf burst out of the bushes, Jace couldn't wait to dismount and meet him. But the expression on the other's face made him hesitate mid-step.

"You went and played hero without me?!" Grede charged over and threw a punch at Jace's arm. Jace yelped, clutching his arm — that punch felt almost as bad as being hit by a full-body charge from Sabellian.

"Without me, you'd charge into a demon horde?!" The dwarf grabbed Jace by the shirt and shook him. "I killed gods know how many demons at Wildhammer Keep. I flew a gryphon all the way, fighting doomguards and flaming green bats the whole time — I don't even know if I was protecting the gryphon or it was protecting me. Then at Auchindoun I had to fight infernals, and you know those things are taller than houses. I've killed more demons in the past few weeks than I've had drinks!"

"Probably not," Jace said.

"Shut up!" Grede gritted his teeth. "If I went back to Aerie Peak right now, I could out-blow any old dwarf there, even the ones who lived through Grim Batol! And High Thane Kurdran Wildhammer is my witness — he saw the whole thing!"

"But if you'd been left behind on Draenor, what would I have to brag about then, huh? I beat hundreds of demons! I helped Expedition soldiers! I helped countless Wildhammers get back to Azeroth! But I couldn't save my own brother! I couldn't save my best friend! Is that it? I'd get thrown out of Thunderfist Hall like some idiot who drank so much he forgot his own father's name, forgot his own clan name! Jace Sesso, you nearly got me cursed out by all of Aerie Peak. You nearly made me too ashamed to ever go home."

"And most importantly — Mossa would be heartbroken. She'd never forgive me for leaving you behind in another world."

"Anyway, I'm right, aren't I, Thane Thunderfist?" Khadgar dismounted behind them, raising an eyebrow. "I told you you'd see Jace Sesso again. Trust in his ability. No need to lose your temper like that. And no need to fly to the Throne of Kil'jaeden all by yourself. I'm very glad you took my advice—"

"Your advice?!" Grede bellowed, cutting the mage off. "That furry sheep got tied up and hauled off by you against its will, mage! Nobody took your advice! If I'd known you can still move around as a sheep, I'd have headbutted your arrogant ass into the Twisting Nether!"

Khadgar cleared his throat guiltily. Jace quickly said, "Thank you for getting him back, Khadgar."

"Don't mention it," the archmage said with a smile.

"Thanks?!"

Grede jumped up and slapped Jace on the forehead.

"How was I supposed to know it'd be this complicated before we left?" Jace held his head, turning back. "I thought all of Draenor's demons were chasing you! How was I supposed to know they'd kept half on the mountaintop?!"

"That just proves demons aren't stupid either. They know who's the bigger threat!" Grede raised one eyebrow, wagging a finger. "And that old black dragon... gods damn it, I knew it, Jace. I knew it all along!"

"Jace!"

By then, Vereesa had also come running out, with Alleria behind her. To Jace's surprise, Sylvanas was with them too.

Maybe he shouldn't have been surprised. After all, Sylvanas had been involved in this from the start. If not for her and Varian Wrynn's behind-the-scenes support, Jace's group would never have been able to keep their actions secret until Khadgar came back to Azeroth.

Otherwise, the Kirin Tor would have done everything in their power to stop their journey midway, invoking the fear of a second Dark Portal.

Jace pulled Vereesa into a tight embrace. It had only been a few days, but it felt like years.

At the top of the Throne of Kil'jaeden, when he'd realized he might never see her again, the hollow feeling that came from knowing he had to accept it but couldn't bear to — he still didn't dare revisit that sensation.

And from Vereesa's trembling — the shudder of someone who'd survived against all odds — Jace knew she'd felt something similar.

She seemed to have so much to say, but all she could do was sob, unable to form words. At times like this, though, words weren't necessary.

Sylvanas looked like she wanted to come over and say something. Alleria stopped her, unwilling to interrupt the two young lovers' moment. But Sylvanas quietly convinced her sister and approached anyway.

"Jace." She came close, glancing meaningfully at Khadgar behind him. "I don't have anything sentimental to say to you. But don't worry too much about the trouble all this might cause. Sylvanas and Alleria will do everything we can to help you and Vereesa. The Silvermoon Council, the Kirin Tor... none of them will get to use this opportunity to smear what you've done."

"Thank you, Sylvanas. Alleria." Jace said.

"Don't thank us, Jace." Alleria said. "We all know what an incredible task you accomplished at the Throne of Kil'jaeden. Compared to that, this is nothing."

"That's right. And if you ask me, you don't need to worry about anything." Sylvanas said quietly. "Especially since you've got that human on your side."

"That human?" Alleria shot her sister an annoyed look. Sylvanas just stared back defiantly, as if daring her to say something. In the end, the Ranger-Captain and eldest sister could only sigh and let it go.

Around Alleria, the usually composed Sylvanas was acting like a mischievous kid, as if Turalyon, being her brother-in-law, was fair game for teasing.

Holding his quietly sobbing lover in his arms, watching her two sisters laugh behind them — Jace, in that moment, decided not to agonize anymore over what consequences everything he'd done might bring.

"Jace!"

Turalyon had also arrived in the clearing. He pushed through some brush and called out to Khadgar from a distance. "I thought you'd gone back to Stormwind to argue, Khadgar. I didn't expect you to bring Jace back with you! I have to admit, you really are a mage who works miracles again and again."

"This one wasn't much to do with me, paladin." Khadgar raised his hands. "I know plenty of rare and remarkable spells. I can even pull off some magic I'd consider impressive myself. But none of it helped this time."

At this, both Windrunner sisters and Turalyon turned curious looks toward Jace.

"Is Ursula here?" Jace asked.

"Lady Ursula Dering." Turalyon confirmed. "She and the other warlocks are inside the Telogrus Rift. A mage just went in to inform them of your return... did her summoning magic work?"

"Yes," Jace said. "There may have been some issues with the magic that kept me from appearing in the circle, but I'm certain it was her summoning magic that let me escape that place."

"But," Vereesa lifted her head, "that alchemist has been blaming herself all this time for not being able to summon you back. Jace, you need to go tell her the news quickly."

"I'm on my way," Jace said.
