Chapter 1407: The Corruption of Telogrus

Even before he reached the Tower of Ilgalar, the wind through the forest had changed noticeably. This wasn't natural wind — it was the mana-wind surrounding the entire mage tower, where void and arcane energy interwove. The Talar'f'sin crystals spun even faster than when Jace had left.

A void rift was being sustained inside the tower by the Kirin Tor mages. The void light spilling from within was visible from far away.

A group of people was just emerging from the tower. They weren't Kirin Tor or Wizard Sanctum mages, and they still seemed disoriented from crossing the boundary between void and reality.

"King Varian!"

Jace watched the King of Stormwind step out of the Telogrus Rift. Even from a short distance, he could smell the lingering stench of fel on him.

"Jace! I couldn't be more thrilled to hear you're back!" Varian coughed a couple of times, bounding down the steps three at a time to grip Jace's shoulder. "It seems you really did know what you were doing."

"Thanks to you, too." Jace smiled. "When I met the Expedition, if I hadn't dropped your name right away, the Sons of Lothar leaders might not have accepted me so quickly."

"Oh, I wouldn't go that far." Khadgar had caught up, smiling. "At the very least, Ranger-Captain Alleria would have held back from putting an arrow through you, out of respect for Lady Vereesa."

The rangers and Turalyon couldn't help but laugh. Varian smiled too, but then he took a deep breath. "I still have one question, though. About destroying Sargeras's scepter... you did destroy it, right?"

"Yes, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"I heard that scepter was brought into this world by Sargeras himself nearly a thousand years ago. Some senior Kirin Tor mages have confirmed that story for me." Varian said. "The Guardian tried to destroy it, but couldn't, so she sealed it away instead. Eventually, those orc death knights stole it."

"So how did you do it?"

Jace's heart skipped a beat at the question.

He knew Varian was asking out of goodwill. Stormwind was currently facing so many terrible enemies — Nefarian's Blackwing Legion, plus the last powerful orc legion on Azeroth, not to mention the restless Dark Iron dwarves and fire elementals. If Jace possessed the power to destroy Sargeras's scepter, that was practically on par with a Guardian of Tirisfal.

"That's right, Jace." Grede chimed in from behind. "How did you destroy that scepter?"

Khadgar was also watching him, wearing the same curious expression — a clear "since they asked, I don't need to" look. Not to mention the ranger leaders and Turalyon.

"I drank a destruction potion," Jace tried to explain. "And it involved the soul of a powerful demon that was guarding the place."

Varian and the others around him looked completely lost. Jace licked his lips, glanced at Khadgar, then said, "It's hard to explain all at once. I'll tell you in detail when we get back to Stormwind. Right now I need to go find Ursula Dering first. I only made it back because of her summoning ritual."

"So the ritual really worked?" Varian's eyes went wide. He waved his hand. "All right. Go ahead and find that alchemist. I'm not going back in there again. Going through that place even once feels like someone hit you over the head a few times with a club."

Jace breathed a quiet sigh of relief. "Then I'll take my leave, Your Majesty."

The King nodded and let Jace enter the tower.

The moment he stepped inside, his mind started spinning. Other questions people could help him deflect, but how was he going to explain destroying Sargeras's scepter?

After all, even the Guardian Aegwynn hadn't been able to do it.

Maybe he could pin the credit on Sabellian, who would never return to Azeroth. Claim the black dragon prince had used some strange magic. Or blame it on the Book of Unholy Names. Pass the credit to Morlofil.

At least Aegwynn wasn't showing her face anymore. She'd promised to go into hiding somewhere even harder to find. So as long as he came up with a reasonable story, no one would be around to contradict him. It wasn't an impossible problem.

Ursula had saved him again.

The void rift to the portal was in the same spot as the fissure they'd used to enter Auchindoun. That made sense — it was directly beneath the Talar'f'sin crystal, the location of Stonewatch's ley line node. Opening a sustained portal channel here was the most stable option.

"I probably don't need to explain portal safety to you, Archmage Sesso." A Wizard Sanctum mage nearby said.

"No, you don't. Thank you." Jace said.

He looked back at Vereesa and Grede. Grede scratched his head. "I'm not going in either."

"See you in a bit, then."

Jace turned and stepped into the rift. The sensation was like being swallowed by a demon, its fangs and tumors scraping against his body the whole way through.

When he opened his eyes again, the raging void-wind whipped his cloak and coat. Before him lay a wasteland of blood-red earth covered in fel-green scars. This must have been Karelz's handiwork.

Several massive rocks floated in the void. The void-wind carried more debris swirling around these floating islands like a vortex.

On a larger island in the distance, a closed portal stood cold and silent. Quite a few Expedition mages were still nearby. That must have been the portal opened by the Eye of Dalaran.

Karelz was still there. Even from this distance, the doom lord's size was intimidating.

He nodded at Jace. Jace nodded back. The demon's mood held no trace of the surprise or shock the others had shown — because like Jace's other demon servants, he'd known perfectly well that Jace was still alive.

Next, he needed to figure out how to summon Morlofil back as soon as possible. This rift looked like an ideal site for the ritual — powerful void energy saturated the surroundings, and it was a demonic place to begin with.

Before long, Vereesa followed Jace through the portal. But unlike the warlock, who seemed as comfortable as if he were at home, she was not having an easy time.

The ranger gasped sharply as she landed, grimacing as if she was about to be sick. She muttered to herself, "I'm really not suited for this kind of place. But who told me to fall in love with a warlock."

Jace smiled. A few Expedition mages, clutching their cloaks and robes, approached. "Archmage Sesso, it's so good to see you safe."

"The Sons of Lothar were trying to figure out a way to go back and find you."

"Honestly, I don't want to go back to that godforsaken place either. But we couldn't just abandon a comrade who was forced to stay there."

"Thank goodness you made it back safely."

"Thank you," Jace said.

Just then, a particular figure made her way past several mages and soldiers toward him. Even though her silhouette seemed ephemeral in the shadow-light of the Twisting Nether, Jace recognized her immediately.

"Ursula!" He raised his hand and called out.

"Jace!" The alchemist quickened her pace. When she reached him, she clutched her hood, which was flapping in the void-wind, and said excitedly, "You really came back. I'm sorry, Jace."

She pulled the novice mage's ring from inside her robe and held it out to him. "I thought I could do it. You know, I'm not great at hurting people with shadow magic, but I've always had talent — especially with summoning rituals. And this time I had Galders and Khadgar, powerful archmages like that helping me. I thought it should have worked."

"But I followed the manual you left me perfectly. I followed all the ritual procedures from the tomes they'd collected from orcs and Dark Iron dwarves. And I remembered what you said about channeling emotions, doing everything I could think of to help the magic along with my feelings. But in the end... I still fell short. Could you feel me calling to you? I felt like I heard you answer. Please tell me that wasn't my imagination."

"That wasn't your imagination, Ursula." Jace took the ring, looking at his name engraved on it. "Your ritual succeeded."

Ursula stood frozen. She looked at Vereesa beside him, then back at him. "No, but you didn't appear in—"

"Your summoning ritual broke through the Deceiver Kil'jaeden's magical barrier." Jace said. "I don't know how you did it, but—"

"I kept thinking about the basement, Jace. About before you left the Slaughtered Lamb." The alchemist was barely holding herself together. She rubbed her eyes hard before continuing. "You were sitting in that chair. That chair... damn it, I've had nightmares about that chair several times these past two days."

"So that's it." Realization dawned on Jace. "The chair... I appeared in that chair! Ursula! Now I understand why I ended up in the basement."

"What are you saying?" Ursula said. "You were teleported to the basement?"

"Yes, sorry about that." Jace swallowed. "I broke the chair. By sitting in it."

"You're still worried about the chair right now?!" Ursula nearly stumbled from the shock of the void energy. "But that chair wasn't cheap... I heard this rift was full of fel, so I didn't bring it in. I never thought it would end up..." The alchemist struggled to compose herself, muttering, "You owe me big time now, Jace Sesso. Big time."

"Yeah." Jace admitted honestly. "Oh, about those Draenor herb samples I promised you — damn, are they still with the gnome?"

"I have them, Jace." Vereesa said. "They're in my camp at the Wayfarers, north of the mage tower. I didn't know those were for Ursula. I'll take you to find them later."

"Thank you very much, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. I was planning to head back anyway." Ursula glanced at the ranger. "Wait for me while I get my things, Jace."

The alchemist hurried off. Vereesa leaned in close. "I'll help compensate Lady Ursula Dering for her losses, Jace. Don't worry."

"Thank you so much, Vereesa." Jace said earnestly. "I'm lucky to have you."

"Then when we get back, you can tell me the full story about that chair." The ranger flashed a quick smile.

"It's nothing. Really nothing." Jace couldn't help an embarrassed cough. "Damn, the void corrosion here is really bad."
