Chapter 1408: The True Cause of Pain

"There's so many!"

"Senior Alchemist Gribby of Gnomeregan collected these specially. He tried to get samples of every valuable herb on Draenor."

"Senior Gribby? The Gnomeregan alchemist who joined the Expedition? I know him. He's a renowned alchemy master. My father met him many years ago."

"Your father was an alchemist too?"

"Um... he was a botanist. These herbs really... I can feel the extraordinary magic in them, Lady Vereesa. And they seem to be—"

"Contaminated with void energy, Ursula. Draenor is very different from Azeroth. I can tell you about the properties of these herbs. I'm no alchemist or botanist, but I studied the uses of various Draenor plants extensively with my sister and the Farstriders. There was a time when we were fully prepared to fight on Draenor for the long haul."

"What's this one?"

"Fel-fog weed. It grows abundantly along the coasts of Nagrand and the Zangar Sea. It's one of the Draenor native plants with relatively low void energy contamination."

"No wonder. And this one?"

"This is—"

Jace stood a short distance away, listening to the two of them discussing the herbs the gnome alchemist had collected for him, his stomach churning.

Ursula was clearly uncomfortable with Vereesa's enthusiasm. Not just because of what Jace had done in her old house that one impulsive time, but also because Vereesa wasn't just a pure high elf — she was exactly the kind of high elven noble Ursula hated most.

If Vereesa hadn't been Jace's partner, Jace's lover, Ursula probably wouldn't have wanted to exchange more than a few words with her. Just like how she treated the elven nobles of Tirisfal and Eastweald.

Of course, it also helped that Vereesa was far more friendly toward humans than most high elves, making her easier to approach. Even so, her sudden enthusiasm was a bit unusual — especially considering what she'd quietly muttered to Jace back in the Telogrus Rift.

The last time Jace had gotten a good night's sleep had been in Auchindoun. After everything he'd been through, he was exhausted by the time he got here. Even Grede had wondered if he'd taken too much shadow and become gloomier. The truth was simpler — he was just too tired to talk.

But now, watching Vereesa and Ursula talking, his fatigue had completely vanished.

"Jace."

"Hm?"

Jace was so focused on the conversation under the Farstrider tent, pouring every ounce of remaining concentration into reading the emotions of the two people there, that he didn't even hear the approaching footsteps. Someone calling his name startled him into turning around.

"Solara?"

"I heard you were back and heading to the Tower of Ilgalar, so I rushed over from Lakeshire." Solara approached. "Until I saw you standing here, I couldn't believe it was true."

"Did you think I'd die that easily over there?" Jace pointed back at the mage tower. "Besides, you worked so hard to save me from that otherworldly elemental lord, Solara. If nothing else, I owe it to you not to throw my life away."

"Don't make me sound so important, Jace." Solara lowered her head, wiping her eyes. "Before we entered the portal, Vereesa absolutely didn't want to leave Auchindoun. I barely managed to drag her away. But I didn't want to leave either. I didn't want to give up on you."

"Giving up on me was the right call, Solara. We couldn't gamble with thousands of Expedition lives." Jace said. "Vereesa and Grede — they both lose their heads when they're worried. Not to mention, even if they'd gotten past Magtheridon's legion and reached the Throne of Kil'jaeden, there's no way they'd have gotten the rest of us out alive from that demon encirclement. If you need to blame someone, blame the black dragon."

"You shouldn't have trusted Prince Sabellian so much," Solara said quietly.

Despite the anger and contempt in Solara's voice, she still used every honorific she could when referring to Sabellian. And thinking about how Sabellian had mentioned the Dragonqueen with about the same level of sarcasm mixed with forced respect — these dragons were really something.

"Ah, Lady Ursula Dering is over there." Solara glanced toward the Farstrider tent. "I heard from Khadgar that he helped the alchemist complete the summoning ritual, and that's how you got off the Throne of Kil'jaeden. She must have a lot to say to you. I'll just—"

She stopped as she noticed Vereesa walking over with herbs in her hand. She paused, looked back at Jace, then at Vereesa again. The moonlight in her eyes flickered.

"Hey. Don't overthink it." Jace waved a hand in front of her face. "Vereesa is just introducing the alchemist to Draenor's specialties. Nothing else going on."

"All right." Solara nodded meaningfully, then suddenly noticed Jace's hand. She narrowed her eyes. "What's this? Your hand is burned. You were burned by fire? That black dragon actually attacked you?!"

"It wasn't him." Jace said. "But it's complicated. Lucky you're here. At the Throne of Kil'jaeden, I was seriously injured. But I still need to use my blood to summon Morlofil back. She's probably still on that mountain."

"But in my current state, even if I manage to bleed enough, I won't have much mana left. So I'll need your help."

"Of course, Jace." Solara smiled.

"How's Needlefang doing?" Jace said. "It looked like she was badly hurt. I'm not dismissing the priests' efforts, but it seemed like the treatment had limited effect."

Solara sighed. "She was cut down by a very powerful eredar with a battle-axe. He called himself the Prophet of Fury, a commander of one of the demon legions. When Needlefang heard Sabellian was ordering all black dragons to Bladespire, she charged headlong into that group of demon guards. Actually, at that point, Pit Lord Magtheridon had already ordered the demon lord to retreat."

"She was wounded and fell into an encirclement of demon guards. The Expedition soldiers thought the black dragonflight had decided to betray the Alliance. They assumed she was one of them, so they didn't help. It was Vereesa and I who found her and brought her back."

"As Deathwing's offspring, she should have healed quickly. But she couldn't accept that her father had abandoned you and fled. She's been seething with anger and denial. You know, Jace — in that state, she can't heal properly. Especially from demon-inflicted wounds. I couldn't do much either... just barely slow the worsening of her injuries. She is a black dragon, after all."

"But before I came here, I checked on her at Stonewatch. She seemed to be doing a bit better. She said she'd seen you. What did you say?"

"What else could I say?" Jace sighed. "Said some nice things about her dad."

Solara nodded helplessly. "I have no doubt she's a brave and sincere little one — nothing like any black dragon I've known. But she's still a child, like any underage human. Easily impulsive, easily upset... don't be too hard on her."

"I know," Jace said.

"Coming here, I've seen how excited the Expedition soldiers from Lakeshire to Stonewatch are that you made it back safely." Solara looked toward Stonewatch. "And King Varian wants to hold a victory ceremony at the Valley of Heroes to welcome the Expedition back from the Dark Portal. Everyone will want you at the front."

"A victory ceremony?"

Jace paused. He'd never liked these kinds of occasions. Even being a supporting figure at some gathering was bad enough. Being dragged into the spotlight at a ceremony was worse.

Solara noticed his expression and said softly, "That young king knows you won't like it. But he thinks it's necessary."

"It is necessary. It means a lot to the Alliance and Stormwind." Jace said. "Speaking of which — once Morlofil is back, we should find some time. Get Grede, Vereesa, and that little one together at the Pig and Whistle. Have our own celebration. And let her start getting used to our way of life."

"So she's going to become another Grede?" Solara asked.

"I don't think you know them well enough yet." Jace lowered his voice. "You might find she's even more Grede than Grede."

They both chuckled. Then Jace noticed Vereesa and Ursula looking in their direction from the Farstrider tent.

What were they looking at? Jace wondered. Then he noticed Solara turning to look behind them too.

Jace looked in the same direction. Standing in the woods, watching them, was a massive figure in white robes, leaning on a staff of purple crystal.
