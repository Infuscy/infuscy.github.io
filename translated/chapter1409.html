Chapter 1409: Night at the Mage Tower

Prophet Velen looked even more imposing when he was alone — without other draenei nearby. Especially now that he'd left Draenor for Azeroth, he inspired a certain primal fear of large things.

"I'll head back to Stonewatch to tend to the wounded, Jace. See you later." Solara said softly. She could tell the Prophet probably had things to discuss with Jace.

Jace nodded. After Solara nodded a farewell to Velen from a distance and disappeared into the forest, he approached the draenei leader.

"I came specifically to thank you, Jace Sesso." Velen said. "Without you and the Expedition's help, I and those who follow me would never have escaped the demons' final frenzy. And you managed to destroy Sargeras's scepter, as well as the Burning Legion's passage for further invading Azeroth. This is undoubtedly a great victory."

"I should be the one thanking you, Prophet." Jace gave a slight bow. "If you hadn't appeared at Wildhammer Keep that day to guide me, I truly don't know where all of this would have ended up."

"Visions do not always bring good outcomes." Velen's voice grew deeper. "Many times, even I cannot understand what the visions I saw truly meant until the moment everything is revealed. Your decisions were what mattered most."

Jace nodded. "So, what will the draenei do now?"

Velen said, "I am about to depart for Lordaeron to discuss matters with King Terenas Menethil II, leader of the Alliance. I hope to find a dwelling that will not disturb you. For now, we have a preliminary idea: if King Varian Wrynn permits, we would like to settle in a place known as the Black Morass. The draenei will do our best to restore that land, scorched by the Burning Legion, as a small reparation for the calamity the Dark Portal brought to Azeroth."

"I think King Varian will be very happy to hear that," Jace said.

"I hope so," Velen said.

"When I passed through Shattrath City, I saw some Aldor priests and draenei survivors living under their protection." Jace said. "Did they come to Azeroth with you too?"

"On the way to Wildhammer Keep, I visited Shattrath City to meet with them." Velen said. "But the Aldor no longer recognize me as their leader. They believe it was my hesitation and cowardice that incurred the Light's rejection, causing the Naaru to lose faith in us."

"I suppose their judgment is not without basis. But Kil'jaeden's target was not them — it was Azeroth. At the same time, he hoped to find me soon, to find an opportunity to prove how right his choices were. Now that I have left Draenor, it should bring them some relief as well."

At this, Velen paused, then raised his eyes. "You saw Kil'jaeden."

Although Khadgar had told Jace not to tell anyone about seeing Kil'jaeden, facing Velen — who had once been Kil'jaeden's closest friend and was probably the one person in the entire Twisting Nether who understood the Deceiver best — Jace decided not to hide anything.

"Yes." He admitted. "Kil'jaeden projected an illusion onto that mountaintop. I saw him. He wanted me to accept the 'power of Sargeras.' He promised that if I stopped opposing the Burning Legion and left Azeroth with the people I wanted to protect, he and the Legion would leave me alone."

"That sounds like a good deal," Velen said.

Jace looked at the Prophet. "I imagine when Sargeras first appeared before you, the terms he offered weren't about making you leaders of the demon legions either, were they?"

"You know these demons' tricks very well, Jace Sesso." Velen sighed softly. "Long ago, we eredar considered ourselves the greatest and wisest race in all the dark void... but few among us had the will to refuse such a 'gift.'"

"But you were different from them," Jace said.

"No." Velen shook his head. "At that time, I did not see any more clearly than Kil'jaeden or Archimonde."

"I know. Without the vision the Naaru revealed to me, I too would have been overjoyed by the future Sargeras promised — infinite knowledge, the deepest secrets of the void. The terms the Dark Titan offered were exactly what the inhabitants of Argus had spent nearly their eternal lives pursuing. I could not have resisted that temptation either."

Jace understood perfectly. The only reason he'd been able to do all of this — to resist the urge to take up Sargeras's scepter, to refuse Kil'jaeden's offer — was because of what he'd known from his past life. He'd known exactly what lay behind those things.

"You have defeated him this time, but Kil'jaeden does not give up easily." Velen said. "Sooner or later, he will find you again. And that day may come very soon."

Jace looked up into the ancient giant's gaze. He knew a cautious man like Velen wouldn't offer this kind of warning lightly.

Maybe he'd already seen something. But just as he'd said — even he couldn't be sure which visions were true and which were false anymore. So he didn't dare reveal everything and cloud Jace's judgment.

"I know, Prophet." Jace nodded. "Thank you for the warning."

Velen gave a slight bow. Just as he was about to leave, Jace called out, "Oh, Prophet. High Priest Maladaar asked me to pass along a message."

"Maladaar?" Velen turned back in surprise.

"Yes." Jace said. "He wanted me to tell you he feels guilty that he couldn't protect Auchindoun. He asked me to convey his apologies to you and your people."

Velen stared at Jace for a long time. Finally, he nodded his thanks and slowly walked into the forest.

...

That night, Jace lay on the bed at the top of the mage tower, listening to the familiar sound of insects chirping outside the tower walls. These were the sounds of an Azeroth night — the real one.

Vereesa sat by the window, staring into the distance. She didn't move for a long time, like a silver panther gazing at the moon in the night.

Curious, Jace lifted his head to get a better look. The ranger turned toward him. "I thought you were asleep."

"How could I sleep with you here?" Jace propped his cheek on his hand.

Vereesa let out a soft laugh, hopping down from the window and padding over to the bed. "Good timing. There's something I want to ask you."

"Oh?" Jace instinctively shrank back under the blanket. The elf pounced onto the bed.

"I could tell that alchemist has... complicated feelings about you. What's going on between you two? I sensed there was something unusual back in Kalimdor."

"Fine, fine, I'll talk." Jace raised both hands. "We met a long time ago. Around the seventh year after the Dark Portal. Back then, our group of warlocks had no idea how to study spells or delve into shadow magic... I was more of a hunter than a warlock at that point, always going out with Grede. I'd help her find herbs, and she'd brew potions for me in return. We got familiar over time."

"So those herb samples Gribby gave you are just a continuation of that arrangement?" Vereesa asked.

"Pretty much." Jace nodded. "Beating the eredar lord Balthas was thanks to her refined Elixir of the Sages. Think about how expensive that stuff is. I'd say it was a fair deal."

"And your dealings were only limited to herbs, were they?"

Vereesa narrowed her eyes, and Jace was suddenly reminded of a hawk spotting a rabbit in the field...

He instinctively glanced at Fang of the Sky hanging nearby. The ranger threw a dagger, pinning its cord to the wall. Jace looked at the other side, at Usarlis. Another knife pierced the wrappings, passed through a demon's rib, and buried itself deep in the window frame.

"Don't even think about using your little tricks to pull those over, Jace Sesso." Vereesa had another knife in her hand. "Now answer my question!"

"That... can't really be called 'dealings,' can it?" Jace said quietly.

"When." Vereesa leaned closer. "When did you two get to know each other?"

"Before." Jace admitted. "Before we went to Kalimdor."

Vereesa clicked her tongue in annoyance. Jace looked around. "Where are you keeping all those knives?"

"I prepared them all for you. I thought you were too tired and asleep, so I was going to wait until you woke up to deal with you." The ranger said quietly. "If you say the wrong thing, I'll make you suffer."

"Did I say something wrong?" Jace asked.

"Not yet." Vereesa placed the knife on the table.

"You're not upset?" Jace asked.

"Back then, it had nothing to do with me." Vereesa said.

"It was my fault. Don't hold it against her, Vereesa." Jace said. "She's helped us so many times."

"I know." Vereesa lay down beside Jace. "And you're Jace Sesso. If I had opinions about every woman who took a liking to you, think about how many opinions I'd have."

"But I'm just a little... jealous. That she was the one who succeeded in summoning you with magic. That at your most dangerous moment, she was the one whose voice you heard."

Jace pulled out the flower from his robe. "Look."

"What happened to your hand, Jace?" Vereesa reached for his wrist but didn't dare touch the burned skin. "Why did you hide it from me during the day? Does Solara know?"

"I wasn't talking about my hand." Jace shook the little flower in his hand — it was barely more than a few burned remnants. "I meant this."

"What is this?" The ranger took the flower and examined it. First she froze. Then tears suddenly spilled from her eyes.

"I'm still the same Jace Sesso." Jace said. "I've never changed."

"I know." Vereesa hugged him tightly. "I know..."
