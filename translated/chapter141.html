Chapter 141: Above the Forest

Southshore was a lot colder than Stormwind. Jace wasn't sure a couple of weeks of travel was enough to cross that many latitudes and produce such an obvious temperature difference — maybe it was just that December was right around the corner.

Still, it was still green here. Elwynn was already covered in yellow leaves.

Of course, unlike Elwynn Forest's vast stretches of deciduous broadleaf woods, the Hillsbrad Foothills were covered in huge swaths of pines and cypresses. Even in winter the needles would wither and shed some, but that only made the woods a little gloomier — not the full-on autumn cliché.

The next morning, before the sun had even risen, the two of them left Southshore and headed north along the coast. The desolate shore was so cold that even with a mage's tunic on underneath, Jace couldn't help pulling his cloak tighter.

But Grede was plenty excited. Maybe it had been too long since he'd come out to get some practice — he was actually looking forward to his next fight.

Jace kept thinking about Faolble's warnings: that murlocs would charge at you from a mile away in broad daylight and fight you to the death.

He figured that if things really went south this time, he wouldn't hold back on any shadow magic — especially now that Grede didn't seem quite so disgusted by it anymore.

Most importantly, he really didn't want to get his gut stabbed full of holes by a murloc's trident for no damn reason.

"Keep up, Jace!" Grede shouted from ahead, hefting his woodcutter's axe. His voice was plenty high and spirited, but out on the bleak mudflat it rang out thin and flat.

"It's way too cold in the morning here, Grede."

"If you'd told me you were cold, I'd have brought that wolf-pelt coat of yours. Pity it's back at the inn."

"You actually remembered to bring it?"

That wolf-pelt coat didn't look like something you'd wear in autumn. It was the kind of thing you'd only throw on to trudge through a blizzard in deep winter, which is why Jace had taken one look at it earlier and not bothered to grab it.

After all, his crappy little room barely had space for something that big — only Grede's giant woven sack could spare the room for a fur coat like that.

Come to think of it, maybe he should just give the coat to his father.

Before they'd left the capital, his father had already stopped taking outside guard shifts much because of his age.

But the old man still liked to take walks along the walls, and a thick coat would make that a lot more comfortable in winter. Especially if he could brag that his son had killed the wolf himself in the Kingdom of Stormwind — that'd be a hell of a flex.

Thinking about it, Jace couldn't help grinning. He glanced up to see Grede already far ahead, and hurried to catch up.

Rounding a low cliff thick with tree roots, Jace saw Grede standing on a boulder up ahead, squinting at the not-too-distant shore. He came up under the boulder and looked up. "What's going on?"

Grede raised an arm and pointed at the tide. "Take a look over there. See anything?"

"What thing..."

Jace glanced over and saw nothing at all, so he climbed up onto the boulder too, rising on his toes for a closer look. That's when he spotted them — little shacks built from wood and seaweed, withered yellow and brown.

A murloc village. Jace knew the type.

Whether in the game or in this world, murlocs liked living in these little huts. But this didn't really qualify as a village — at best it was a small murloc settlement, probably home to a single clan of maybe ten or twenty of them.

After all, it wasn't far from Southshore at all, only a few miles. From here you could even see the smoke rising from Southshore to the east. Human soldiers could patrol by here at any time.

This must be a settlement that had only recently spread this way after the war, when the humans had been too busy to deal with the murloc infestation.

But there wasn't a single murloc in this little village. It was as if someone had come through and cleared out the mobs not long ago.

Of course, the creatures here didn't respawn out of thin air. Gone was gone — either they'd all hidden, or they'd fled off who-knows-where for who-knows-what reason a long time ago.

Jace guessed Southshore must have gotten back to its pre-war bustle, with plenty of soldiers or hired groups wandering around — travelers and adventurers like him and Grede. The murlocs hadn't wanted to cross them and had no choice but to pack up and move again.

"So where's my money?" Grede scratched his neck. "Where'd my silver coins go?"

"Silver coins with legs tend to run," Jace said helplessly. "You can't exactly expect them to wait around for you to come cut their heads off."

Grede said, "A whole cluster of little huts like this, and they just abandoned it? Those little murlocs have skinny arms and legs — building these places couldn't have been easy."

He hopped off the boulder as he said it, axe in hand, and strode through the sand toward the settlement one step at a time. Jace called after him with some worry, "Watch out for an ambush!"

"Even better!" Grede shouted, running and turning his head as he went. "I'm hoping the silver coins ambush me!"

Jace slid off the boulder too and unslung his staff from his back, ready for anything.

He figured that if a whole pack of murlocs showed up, he'd immediately hit them with a staff-enhanced Fear spell to scare them all off, then grab Grede and look for a chance to flee.

Thinking that, he pulled a mana potion out of his warlock's satchel and tucked it into the binding at his chest.

But before he could cinch the satchel shut again, the imp suddenly popped its head out. "Master... I sense it. A bad presence."

"Get... back in here!" Jace hissed, breaking into a cold sweat. He glanced over at Grede, who was already charging far ahead — he'd passed that raised sand dune and couldn't see what was happening here, which was the only reason the imp had dared show itself.

"What kind of presence?"

The imp stared wide-eyed. "A shadow creature is approaching, Master — and it's brimming with hostility! I can feel it... It's lurking far away right now, but... it's not hiding its shadow aura at all. If anything, it's deliberately radiating its shadow power. Maybe it's casting a spell. Maybe it just wants everyone to see!"

"A shadow creature? What kind of shadow creature — an orc warlock or something? Are you sure?" Jace asked.

"I'm sure! But what creature... I... I can't quite tell." The imp grabbed Jace's button and shook it. "Master, you might not be its target, but be careful no matter what!"

"Got it. Back inside, now." Jace rubbed the imp's head and stuffed it back into the satchel, his hand still stinging from the heat. The imp's head was unquestionably the hottest part — not enough to burn like real fire, but unpleasant enough to touch.

After cinching the satchel tight, he looked out toward the distant shore, then over at the pine woods on land. Nothing out of the ordinary.

Orgrim Doomhammer? Had Orgrim come all the way to Hillsbrad?

If that was the case, the four-day blockade suddenly made a little more sense.

Or naga? Were there naga around here?

He looked out at the sea. Lordaeron's waters were blue-black, a lot deeper and darker than Stormwind's bluish seas. Stare at it long enough and it felt even more bleak and desolate.

But even so, there was no sign of naga. Jace's gaze swept along the coastline in both directions, and he didn't see anything humanoid either.

Lying in wait underwater, maybe?

He touched the pearl chain strapped to his wrist, wondering if this naga ornament would react somehow to a naga's approach — but the pearls were as dull and lifeless as ever, not the slightest reaction.

Whatever the case, with a more troublesome danger in the picture, it wasn't a great idea to keep pursuing a job that paid two silver.

"Grede!"

Jace walked toward Grede, rubbing his still-itching hand. The dwarf had already charged into the murloc village.

Those little huts were ridiculously short — they barely came up to Grede's height. Even a murloc would be cramped and miserable inside. At best they kept the rain off. Hard to say what the murlocs expected these things to do.

"Grede! Watch out — something feels off!"

"It is off."

So saying, Grede swung his woodcutter's axe and hacked hard at one of the huts. The wood-built structure immediately tilted and collapsed.

Jace walked over, confirmed no murlocs were appearing, and asked, "What are you doing..."

"Obviously luring the murlocs over," Grede said as he chopped. "It really is off. They can't just sit by and watch their homes get torn down, can they? Whether these damned little things are hiding in the water or in holes on land, they ought to— ugh!"

Another swing, and a hut came down completely. Shells and dead fish scattered everywhere, scattered further by the tide, giving off a nauseating fishy stench.

Jace held his nose and took a step back, then looked around again, then out at the sea. Definitely nothing there.

"Let's go look for the wine crates instead. Head back toward Southshore a bit and search carefully. Maybe there really aren't any murlocs here, and the boss was right after all."

Grede spat in frustration, jammed his axe into the sand, and gripped the haft as he stared out at the sea.

"I'd had it all figured out — thought we'd make some extra money. Turns out there really aren't any murlocs. Those merchants really won't part with a single drop of blood, will they?"

Just then, Jace caught a shadow flickering across the sky from the corner of his eye. He looked up — a bird, it seemed, flying over the pine woods toward Southshore.

But after watching for a moment, he realized it probably wasn't a bird. It was something considerably bigger — it just looked small because it was flying so high with no reference points around it.

The thing flew further and further, turning slightly, and Jace noticed a long tail trailing behind the shadow.

Another one of those gryphons the mages rode?

"Hey, Grede, is that a gryphon?" Jace shook Grede's shoulder and pointed at the shadow. "Looks dark — a Hinterlands breed, maybe?"

"Huh? There are Hinterlands gryphons here? I don't buy it..."

Grede tilted his head to look, and his expression went from dismissive to frozen, then rapidly shifted to shock.

"That... that..."

"What?!"

"A dragon! That's a dragon!"
