Chapter 1411: The Bargaining Chip

At Stonewatch, in the watchtower the Kirin Tor had rented, Jace arrived to see Khadgar discussing matters with a female Kirin Tor mage.

"Archmage Khadgar."

"Archmage Sesso." The nearby mage nodded. Jace nodded back.

"Jace." Khadgar waved him over. After seeing the look in Jace's eyes, he signaled to the mage. "Let's continue this discussion tomorrow, madam."

"Yes, Archmage Khadgar."

After the mage left, Khadgar narrowed his eyes, like someone who really didn't want to hear what Jace was about to say.

"Where's Amy?" Jace asked.

"She's in Dalaran, under investigation — trying to clear her star student of suspicion. I mentioned it before." Khadgar said. "But she should be on her way here now. She might almost be at Stormwind by this point."

"I can't wait to see her," Jace said.

"You didn't come all the way here just to ask about that, did you?" Khadgar asked suspiciously.

"Well, there's one more thing. The Book of Unholy Names." Jace raised an eyebrow. "Isn't it time to give it back to me?"

Khadgar silently mouthed the word "damn," then said, "The Kirin Tor Council wanted me to tell you that this grimoire is going back to Lake Lordamere with Medivh's book and the Eye of Dalaran. They'll ensure its safety."

"Did you tell them the book's power is linked to my servant?" Jace asked.

"I didn't have to tell them myself." Khadgar said. "The strong connection between this book's power and someone else doesn't take exceptionally talented magic to detect. It's obvious to the Kirin Tor Council of Six. Burning it would mean destroying that portion of power. And the fact that I've been guarding it so carefully — anyone could guess that the other portion of power is with you. Or rather, with your demon servant. The specifics don't matter that much."

"By Kirin Tor standards, merely possessing a grimoire belonging to a Burning Legion leader is a serious enough crime. The only reason I've been able to keep it with a clear conscience is that I argued it might be needed to summon you."

"But I don't want to lie to you. I know you can tell when someone's lying, Jace. Even putting that aside, deceiving those old men in the Kirin Tor who can't even talk straight is enough to weigh on my conscience. Let alone trying to fool you."

"I know you're in a tough spot, Khadgar." Jace said. "But Morlofil is still on Draenor. My mana alone isn't nearly enough to drag her out of Kil'jaeden's barrier. I want to try using this grimoire as a casting reagent, the way you tried to use it to summon me through the portal."

"So that means I'd be handing a Kirin Tor-watched item to a warlock so he can summon a demon killer that even Pit Lord Magtheridon was afraid of, right into Azeroth." Khadgar said.

"A demon that kept Magtheridon from attacking Wildhammer Keep for months." Jace said. "A demon that single-handedly held off the guardian of Sargeras's scepter, letting me focus on destroying it, and saving the entire Sons of Lothar."

"Fair point." Khadgar wrestled with it for a moment. "Can you return it after the summoning?"

"As long as you guarantee you'll keep possession of the book yourself, rather than letting Dalaran hold onto it," Jace said.

"You're worried Dalaran will tear open the book and use it against you while that demon lady is severely weakened." Khadgar shook his head. "As long as I'm in the Kirin Tor, I can guarantee they won't use that book to threaten you, Jace."

"I'm not worried about Dalaran." Jace said. "Your supposedly impenetrable vault couldn't even keep the Eye of Dalaran safe! And now I've made an enemy of Kil'jaeden. Who's to say this book won't be stolen by some other demon, or a traitor like Kel'Thuzad?"

The archmage looked a little offended at first, but in the end, he had nothing to say. He just bowed his head.

"I hate how you keep convincing me like this." He pinched the bridge of his nose, thinking. "But the grimoire is the key to persuading the Kirin Tor to stop interfering with the Telogrus Rift, Jace. To put it bluntly — they think there are already too many potential threats left in your hands."

Seeing Jace's silence, he added, "How about this — I'll do my best to convince the Kirin Tor to let me keep possession of the book. What do you think?"

"You can guarantee that?" Jace said.

"I guarantee it," Khadgar said.

Jace nodded. To get Morlofil back as soon as possible, he'd have to accept this compromise for now.

Leaving Stonewatch and returning to the mage tower, he went to the summoning circle he'd prepared before entering Telogrus. Khadgar followed him in.

"Archmage Khadgar? What are you doing here?" Ursula asked in surprise.

Khadgar smiled, putting on an official tone. "I naturally wanted to thank Lady Morlofil in person, Lady Ursula. For her bravery on Draenor—"

"He's worried I'll sneak the book away," Jace said, arranging the soul shards with the imp.

The archmage sighed helplessly and crossed his arms. "Those two reasons are not mutually exclusive."

Jace exchanged a glance with Ursula, then placed the demon register in position. He stepped up to the circle and began chanting, lighting his own blood on fire.

"Virelinki, Torsentani, Tosesta, Moresfil, Saritor, Ma'arimasta!"

He pulled at the void energy swirling within the Telogrus Rift, connecting to Morlofil who was who-knew-how-far away. This time, his soul felt the connection again, as if finding the soul link.

At the same time, he could sense Morlofil detecting his call. She was grasping at his mana like a woman possessed.

But despite both sides trying, the obstruction in between was too strong. The soul shards before him burned up like paper. The blood-drawn circle let out a sharp, agonized howl. Khadgar stood by, watching silently. Ursula and Sazeno kept adding soul shards to the circle, but it was all futile.

In the end, Jace burned through more than half of his reserves in an instant. He nearly collapsed where he stood, but still couldn't summon Morlofil before him.

"It's still not enough, Jace." Khadgar said. "You're way short on mana."

"But I summoned Sazeno easily enough." Jace looked at the imp. "Not exactly easily, but I didn't need casting materials or that many soul shards—"

"The problem isn't just the barrier, master." Sazeno looked up. "Morlofil's power has reached a new level. Summoning her into this world will inevitably weaken her — like what happened when you summoned Lord Karelz! And you were trying to pull her over completely. With the Deceiver's barrier on top of that, it makes the problem even worse."

"What do we do, Mr. Sazeno?" Ursula asked.

"How should I know?" Sazeno scratched its head.

Actually, there was a very straightforward solution — tearing the Book of Unholy Names apart.

A significant portion of Morlofil's power was sustained by this book. But conversely, the book had become her fatal weakness.

When this register was torn, the possessor of its power would not only lose power but also fall into extreme weakness for a short time. In another timeline, the demon hunter Varedis had died in that weakened state.

This was also why Jace had told Khadgar to bring the register back to Azeroth — to prevent the Burning Legion from stealing it back.

Seeing Jace staring at the book in his hands, Khadgar asked, "What are you planning?"

"Tear it." Jace said. "Weaken Morlofil's power. That'll make summoning her much easier. You take the remnants back to Dalaran. In exchange, the Kirin Tor stops meddling with the Telogrus Rift. Leave it to me."

"Deal," Khadgar said.

Jace tore the grimoire in half. A dull sense of detachment spread through his heart. It took him a long moment to recover his senses.

He'd only held a small portion of the demon register's soul power, and he'd still felt that dizziness. He could only imagine what Morlofil was feeling on the other end.

After resting for a while and downing two mana potions, he stood before the circle again. He didn't have much blood left. If this failed again, he'd have to wait a few more days.

"Virelinki—"

Jace chanted again, reestablishing the connection with the other side of Draenor.

When the incantation was complete, a void rift opened before him. A winged Succubus stepped out of it.

Morlofil.

She looked smaller. Or rather, more "normal." The horns on her head seemed more aggressive. Combined with her half-folded wings behind her, the whole picture felt almost unfamiliar to Jace.

"Something looks different, doesn't it." Khadgar stroked his chin. "But this succubus is still—"

"Your wings are back, Lady Morlofil?" Ursula asked in surprise.

"Lost and found, Ursula." Morlofil said. "But I also lost something else in the process."

She looked at Jace with a wounded expression. "Darling, shouldn't you give me back double what I lost?"
