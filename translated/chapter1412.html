Chapter 1412: Is This Help or Thanks?

Mornings at the Tower of Ilgala felt like being back in Draenor — groggy and disoriented. Jace sat up in bed, hand pressed to his forehead, feeling a dull ache all over his body, like he'd just crawled back from the Throne of Kil'jaeden to Stormwind all over again. Mana drained. Exhausted. Fresh from surviving the flames. "Good morning, master." A clatter of hooves came up the stairs from below. Morofil peeked her head over the railing to check on Jace, then climbed fully up the stairs, leaning against the banister. She gave her wings a small stretch and flicked her long tail. "How are you feeling, Morofil?" Jace asked, still rubbing his forehead. "Shouldn't I be asking you that?" The demon smirked. "You looked pretty worn out yesterday, master. Could it be that the severe weakness from tearing the Book of Fel Names still hasn't faded?" "Definitely," Jace said firmly. "But what about you? Weren't you badly hurt?" Morofil lifted her neck happily. "When I was on Draenor, I felt the Book of Fel Names being torn apart... I was a bit worried that the Burning Legion demons who survived the Throne of Kil'jaeden's collapse would take the chance to jump me while I was weak and rip me apart. I couldn't let them have that opportunity. But fortunately, you summoned me quickly. Before they even realized what was happening, I was already back at your side. As for now — thanks to your 'sacrifice,' my dear — I've recovered from the severe weakness. I'm much stronger now than before I absorbed that grimoire. More importantly, my soul has returned to wholeness. All thanks to Brutaulus's generous donation!" "You don't seem too bothered about losing the soul power," Jace said. "Honestly, I still think it's a bit of a waste. If there were another way..." "Because strictly speaking, it was never yours or mine, my good master." The succubus lowered her voice. "It was only mana we borrowed from the demon roster. Why do you think Lord Batas guarded the demon roster without ever absorbing it? Because he knew he couldn't hold onto that power forever. Likewise, I don't like having something that could be taken away at any moment. It's better to be rid of it." "But when you faced Brutaulus, you seemed pretty happy to have that extra power," Jace teased. "Because Brutaulus didn't have the ability to burn that grimoire!" Morofil laughed and raised a hand. "Besides, that was a stupid and filthy creature. I didn't mind borrowing a bit of power to crush him! I'd been thinking and scheming about how to kill that bastard for who knows how long. Was I supposed to give up that chance out of pride or dignity? In hindsight, he was tougher than I'd expected. Without the grimoire, I really couldn't have beaten him. But seeing that head of his hanging there with that dumb expression — that alone was satisfying enough. I don't really care about the rest." "But as long as I can spread my wings freely before you," she leaned closer, "as long as I can return to your side and gain the rights that even those low, vile Succubi have — that's more important than anything." "I'm glad you came back too," Jace said with a smile. "That's all I ever wanted." With that, the succubus brushed her lips against Jace's. He blinked, a little taken aback. He wasn't used to this — it felt different from before. Maybe it was because her soul had been made whole. The tearing of the grimoire hadn't ripped away the shadow power Jace had drawn from that netherwing, nor had it taken the power Ulthalesh had drained from Momor's soul and fed back to him. He still had a much sharper perception of shadow and emotion than before — especially noticeable when sensing Morofil, a powerful succubus. Or maybe it was just that this heightened perception and Morofil's diminished power created a contrast that made the difference obvious. "I'm glad you're back too, Lady Morofil!" Sazeno chimed in from the side with a flattering smile. "But could you please not hang me from your wings anymore?" "Ah, Master Sazeno! Haven't you noticed? You're much more polite than before!" Morofil's tail swished back and forth as she stared at the imp. "The treatment is working! I'll have to continue." "Damn it!" The imp's head burst into flames from frustration, but he immediately suppressed his outburst. It seemed the succubus's wings really did put the fear in him. Morofil was about to say something else when she suddenly looked out the window with alertness. Jace felt it too and followed her gaze. "It's not Vereesa coming back early, is it?" Jace said, grabbing his cloak. "You look adorable like that." Morofil chuckled as she walked to the window and glanced out. "Unfortunately, it's Khadgar." "Why hasn't he left yet?" Jace said. "I already gave him back the grimoire." Morofil shook her head. Jace sighed, threw on a few pieces of clothing, and jogged down to the base of the tower. Khadgar turned around, hesitating. "I was wondering if I'd come too early. Especially considering you probably had a lot to deal with last night." Jace raised his voice. "You can take that grimoire to the Kirin Tor now and clear things up. Any other problems?" "I came mainly to thank you." Khadgar adjusted his collar. "I'm grateful you understood my predicament, that you were willing to sacrifice so much to help the Expedition return home, and then willing to sacrifice again to help me clear up the misunderstandings with the Kirin Tor." "You don't have to say that." Jace rubbed his eyes and leaned against the doorframe. "If you hadn't spoken up for me with the Kirin Tor, they never would have allowed my demon servants, let alone this floating island." Khadgar stepped closer. "You've hit on something there. To defend all the various things you've hidden away, they've gone over my experiences studying magic at Karazhan again and again. Some people claim I'm definitely about to get possessed by some demon, which is why I'm defending you so vigorously." He chuckled twice. "But that's not a loss for you, Jace. At least it means one less vulnerability for your enemies to exploit." "I understand, Master Khadgar." Jace smiled. "Don't give me that look. I'm not trying to act like I've done you a favor and then ask for more." Khadgar's expression turned serious. "I also need your help with something. Consider it part of my thanks — not on behalf of the Kirin Tor, but my personal gratitude." "You want me to help you, and you're calling it thanks?" Jace frowned. "Let me finish." Khadgar raised a finger. "When I entered Karazhan with Lady Aegwynn before, I found the tower in even more chaos than I'd expected. Without Lady Aegwynn's guidance, I wouldn't have been able to handle the rampaging shadow and void energies inside." "You want me to go to Karazhan with you?" Jace's eyes went wide. "Exactly. But the timing is still sensitive, Jace." Khadgar glanced back at the camp in the forest. "Once all this settles down, once the Kirin Tor isn't so fixated on the south..." "But hasn't the Violet Eye been trying to get deeper into Karazhan all this time?" Jace asked. "Why not work with them?" "The Violet Eye." Khadgar snorted. "Those people are basically just gatekeepers for Karazhan. Cleaning the front hall, scavenging odds and ends — especially things left behind by Medivh's servants and followers. They're good at that. Nothing more. What I'm talking about is actually entering Karazhan. Don't you want to see it? Haven't you heard of the Great Library of Karazhan?" "Isn't that too dangerous?" Jace said. "Yes, it is too dangerous." Khadgar rubbed his nose and raised his eyebrows, leaning in closer. "Which is exactly why I need a trustworthy partner who can control shadow magic to go in with me. Right now, there isn't a third person in this world besides Lady Aegwynn and you." The archmage was practically glowing with enthusiasm. To Jace, he looked exactly like one of those shady older cousins trying to convince a younger kid to join him in some mischief. Jace had been to Karazhan in the game. The ghosts of mana, the enraged statues, the reanimated armor, the undying servants, the chaotic theater... not to mention the arcane golems guarding the magical tomes in the Great Library. From his past experience, this would only be more dangerous here, more deadly. But the man standing before him was Khadgar himself. Back when Jace had entered Karazhan, he'd never had someone like this by his side — only a bunch of uncooperative slackers. "Why do you want to go back into Karazhan?" Jace asked. "To help the Kirin Tor find magical texts inside?" "Don't they already have the Violet Eye for that?" Khadgar smiled. "They have so many people, and they claim to be the 'successors to the Order of Tirisfal.' They shouldn't need an outsider like me to meddle." "That's not my goal. After going in with Lady Aegwynn last time, I discovered that the entire 'security system' Medivh left behind in Karazhan was still active. Specifically, the 'Curator' of the Great Library." "Of course, this Curator isn't a person — it's a powerful arcane golem personally built by Medivh. Which means that even ten years after his death, the Curator can still command all the arcane golems in Karazhan. So I was thinking — maybe I could repurpose the tower's security system, reverse the dark energies it emits, and complete a 'purification.'" Khadgar sighed softly. "Killing Medivh was necessary to stop Sargeras's madness — everyone agreed on that. But the resulting cataclysm also devastated Deadwind Pass and all of southern Elwynn. It destroyed the normal lives of tens of thousands of innocent people from Darkshire to Raven Hill. It shattered the post-death peace of countless war heroes in the great cemetery. I think I have to take responsibility for that, Jace." "I understand." Jace nodded. "There will be something in it for you." Khadgar said. "Once you help me get back into the library, you can read any book there you like. I don't think you'd question the quality of Medivh and Sargeras's research into shadow and void, would you?" Jace had to admit, that was tempting. The Great Library of Karazhan itself... "Unfortunately, the Book of Fel Names has been torn apart," Jace said. "My mana pool has dropped considerably, and my ability to manipulate shadow may have declined with it." "The last person who went into Karazhan with me was Anduin Lothar." Khadgar said quietly. "Trust me, he didn't have even the slightest connection to shadow, but we went in alive, defeated Medivh himself, and came back alive." "But Lord Lothar was a warrior blessed by the Holy Light," Jace said. "No joke, you're not far off." Khadgar smiled. "In fact, I can be honest with you — the moment you decided to destroy that book, I knew for certain I wanted to invite you to go with me." "But you don't have to decide right now. I know you still have a lot to deal with, and I'm not ready yet either. It's just that ever since I came out of Karazhan, this idea has been nagging at me, and I wanted to tell you about it while we're both still at Lake Everstill and can meet easily." "Alright. I'll think about it." Jace said.
