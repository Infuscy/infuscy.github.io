## Chapter 1413: The True Owners of the Rift Fragment

At first, the Dagger Society had sought out the Telogrus Rift to find a hiding place more discreet than the Slaughtered Lamb for secretly studying dark magic.

But by now, damn near the entire Kirin Tor knew about the Telogrus Rift. So much for secrecy.

At least through all the back and forth, Jace had managed to keep hold of the fragment. He didn't like promising things Khadgar couldn't guarantee — which meant the Kirin Tor Council probably really did care about that demon roster, since it was what Kil'jaeden had used to control the demons of Draenor. If handing over the roster meant independence for Telogrus, that wasn't a bad trade.

Still, having the Telogrus Rift connected to his own mage tower bothered him. He stared at the void portal in the distance, thinking: from now on, any member of the Society wanting to come and go would have to pass through his tower?

So if he ever wanted to do anything with Vereesa on the tower roof, he'd have to worry about people running around downstairs the whole time? And if the Society recruited more members later, that would be even worse.

"I'm planning to open another rift to this place elsewhere, Karlez." Jace turned to look at the doomguard. "Can you do that?"

"No problem." Karlez said. "Although this rift is small and well-hidden, there's nothing difficult about teleporting here from Azeroth. As long as I have Master Khadgar's precise coordinates."

"He's already returned the coordinates to me," Jace said.

"Then take me to wherever you want to open the rift." Karlez said. "That way I can open or close the portal whenever you need."

"Take you..." Jace muttered. "I can't exactly bring you into Stormwind City."

"Does the Dagger Society have any safe houses outside Stormwind and Darkshire?" he asked Ursula beside him.

"Not that I know of." Ursula said. "But you'd need to ask Erland Duthmoor about the specifics."

"Jace!"

A muffled shout came from the distance. Jace looked toward the rift and saw a man and woman walking in together — Galders and Jarien Arow.

"You actually got summoned back by that spell!"

Galders, usually so emotionally flat, was clearly excited by Jace's return. Though honestly, Jace felt he was more thrilled that Ursula's summoning ritual had succeeded than that Jace himself had come back.

"I'll admit, when we couldn't summon you at first, I was pretty frustrated. I thought maybe there was some problem with our research into the ritual." Galders said. "I was planning to head to Booty Bay to ask the warlocks there if we'd missed something, or if there were special requirements for the casting materials. I'd even bought the ticket. Then I heard you were back."

"We were looking for Erland," Ursula said. "Didn't he come back with you?"

"I gave him and Sulenna the tickets," Galders said. "They were planning to go to Booty Bay anyway, so it wouldn't be a waste. They registered at the dock just yesterday. Why do you need Erland?"

"To find a new portal route to this rift," Jace said. "So you won't have to pass through my tower every time."

"Are you sure the Kirin Tor is willing to leave the Telogrus Rift to us?" Jarien asked cautiously, looking around the rift. "The Dalaran mages have all left, at least."

"Pretty much confirmed." Jace said. "I agreed to some conditions with Khadgar. He has to clear things up with the Kirin Tor."

"Khadgar..." Jarien raised her eyebrows, looking particularly stunned.

"What?" Jace said.

"Nothing." Jarien said. "Actually, I'm still a bit dazed. Not long ago, when I first joined the Dagger Society, Master Khadgar was still someone I could barely even imagine meeting, let alone knowing he'd joined the Expedition and was presumed dead on Draenor. And now he's speaking up for a bunch of warlocks in the Kirin Tor. Don't you think that's... fantastical?"

"That's because you came later, Jarien." Ursula leaned close. "If you'd come to Stormwind when Gagin did, and seen everything Jace Sesso has gotten up to along the way, you wouldn't be so surprised now."

Jarien shook her head. "I know all about it. I just haven't fully adjusted yet. I mean, this is Master Khadgar we're talking about. But never mind — you probably don't understand how those of us who grew up in magic academies view the Kirin Tor masters."

"That's fair." Galders said. "My doomguard can handle the Telogrus Rift portal channel. Of course, not at the Slaughtered Lamb. We can use that demon to help open a smaller portal here as a backup route."

"Where is this doomguard?" Jace asked.

"In Darkshire." Ursula said. "He's certainly not as powerful as Lord Karlez, but he's still a demon skilled in void magic, Jace. He can help us open the portal."

Galders looked at Jace. "Before Khadgar found us, we'd already thoroughly researched the portal method with the help of your Gaothros. The only thing we were missing was a precise set of coordinates."

Jace nodded. "I can go help that demon set the coordinates and try to establish the connection channel. After I get back from Stormwind."

Galders looked around. "Years ago, when Gagin and I found Tharle Mor and rented that basement from him, I never imagined we'd one day get a real fragment of the Twisting Nether as a sanctuary."

"Here, I have conditions that almost no alchemist in all of Azeroth can match — even Dalaran's alchemists — for making demon and void-related potions." Ursula said.

"You're not the only one with those conditions." Jarien looked into the distance. "Think about the robes and cloaks I can make here. Speaking of which, Jace, you spent so long on Draenor. How's that robe — the Cloak of Morofil — holding up?"

She glanced toward the rift portal to make sure Morofil hadn't followed Jace here, then said, "Can I see it? Let me see what it went through on the other side of the Dark Portal."

"It went through a baptism by the Black Dragon Prince." Jace sighed. "It couldn't take it. Got burned to ashes."

"Prince Sabellian that Khadgar mentioned?" Jarien gasped. "Sorry, Jace. I don't think I can blame my craftsmanship for that."

"Of course not." Jace said. "But I was wondering..."

"A new one." Jarien raised an eyebrow. "No problem, Jace. Let me show you my latest work, especially now that we have this rift."

"I didn't just come back from Draenor with a burned robe, everyone." Jace said. "I also got some things from the Shadow Council. Including the production of soul shards, and some other things..."

"I've been waiting for you to say that, Jace." Galders's voice, though he tried to keep it steady, was already barely containing his excitement.

"That was one of my main goals in going to Draenor." Jace said with some satisfaction.

A few things I'd like to say at this point.

Some of you sharp readers may have already noticed — this novel is entirely pinned to Jace's perspective. The inner thoughts of other characters are all Jace's guesses, and there's never a scene that Jace's eyes don't witness. I've always felt that with a few tweaks, this novel could be written in first person without feeling particularly off.

At the beginning, this wasn't a problem. But as the story went on and more things happened, there are some regrets.

For example: Sora and Grede's journey to Kalimdor; Rhonin rescuing Krasus in Blackrock Spire; the Farstriders' adventure in Bladespire searching for Velen; Khadgar coordinating between the Kirin Tor, the Dagger Society, and Jace to establish the portal in Azeroth; Khadgar seeking out Aegwynn; Vereesa leading the Farstriders to hunt Auchenai priests; Grede and the Wildhammers fighting a fighting retreat against the Burning Legion; Kil'jaeden pondering Jace's actions from behind the scenes and setting him obstacles; the three sisters meeting; Morofil's duel with Brutaulus — all of that and much more is gone, reduced to mere fragments Jace heard about.

Why didn't I write it? On one hand, I wanted to maintain the novel's overall style. On the other hand, I write very detailed. If I forced all that content in, the pacing would become unbearably slow and bloated.

Take this example: Why did Kil'jaeden assign Brutaulus to guard the Scepter of Sargeras? Brutaulus himself understood — he knew why Kil'jaeden sent him specifically. It was to beat Morofil bloody.

But in truth, Kil'jaeden believed Jace would definitely harvest the crystal containing the other half of Morofil's soul to make up for the loss of killing Brutaulus, ensuring he could destroy the Scepter of Sargeras, creating a rift between Jace and Morofil, further splitting the three who had flown to the Throne of Kil'jaeden. But it didn't work. Morofil dared to face Brutaulus alone — and won. So Kil'jaeden wasn't about to admit it. I can only explain it here on his behalf.
