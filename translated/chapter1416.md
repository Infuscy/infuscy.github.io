## Chapter 1416: The Expedition's Return

The church bells rang out in the distance. Jace looked up at the white spire of the Cathedral of Light, and his thoughts drifted back to that morning.

The bells had rung the same way back then. The whole city had been just as noisy. People had gathered at the Valley of Heroes before it was called the Valley of Heroes, crowding the bridge, under the bridge, up the walls, on the gryphon roosts. And now, even the pedestals of the statues were packed with agile young men and women.

They shouted, they laughed, they scattered flower petals from above, calling out the names and titles of the Draenor survivors.

If he and Grede had been squeezing through that crowd back when the statues were already built, they'd have been among those people too.

"Lady Vereesa Windrunner!"

"Long live the friendship between Aerie Peak and Stormwind! Lord Grede Thunderfist!" "Long live the Alliance!"

Grede raised his fist and shouted too. "Long live friendship! Long live dwarves and humans!"

"And Quel'dorei, Grede," Vereesa whispered.

"And elves!" Grede hastily bellowed, which made him cough from the strain. But at least the Farstriders and magisters behind them relaxed their brows, looking satisfied enough.

"Should I not be here, Jace Sesso?"

Needleflame rode beside Jace. She'd already changed back into her girl form, though at least with Sora's help, she'd managed to make her clothes somewhat neat and clean.

She'd never learned to ride, but the warhorse beneath her was the most well-behaved in the entire procession. It knew exactly what was sitting on its back.

"All these humans are waiting for you heroes to return." The little black dragon said quietly. "They don't know me. Even if they did, they wouldn't welcome me."

"If that's the standard you're judging by, then I should be the last person here, Needleflame." Jace looked at her. "Believe me, without you, our operation in Auchindoun would have been infinitely more troublesome. It might not have succeeded at all. For that alone, you should be riding here with us."

"Really?" Needleflame's eyes finally lit up a little. But then she seemed to remember something and lowered her head. "Would my father think so?"

"Honestly, I don't know what he thinks." Jace looked at her. "Do you care about that because your power needs Sabellian's approval? Or because you feel like only if you perform well enough can you become a true Blackwing and earn your father's love?"

"I..." Needleflame hesitated, unsure what to say. Vereesa glanced over at their conversation.

Jace said, "Atop the Throne of Kil'jaeden, I defeated Sabellian. He was exhausted from fighting demons, and my staff had lost almost all its power. I'm not saying it was a fair fight, but I did win."

"I know."

"If the Blackwings worship power, doesn't that mean my approval carries just as much weight as your father's?"

"You're right."

"And as for your father's love... you don't need to be exceptional to earn it." Jace said. "I can feel his feelings for you, little one. Ever since he came out of Dragonmaw Fortress, he's been watching me through various means, trying to determine if he could trust you to come back to Azeroth with me."

"If he didn't care about you, would he take this so seriously? He could have treated you as his private property from the start, refused to let me take you, or just handed you over casually like an object."

Needleflame's lips trembled, and tears streamed down her face.

That caught Jace off guard. He looked at Vereesa, who was smiling at him, and could only sigh helplessly.

Grede, who'd been waving to the cheering crowd, turned around and shouted, "Don't be so gloomy, little one! After we get out of the palace, I'll take you to the Pig and Whistle, let you get a proper taste of Azeroth. Later we'll go to Aerie Peak, show you the real Azeroth. Then you'll forget all about this!"

"We never settled our last match," the little black dragon said grumpily.

"Your face was already black from all that drinking! If you'd kept going, you wouldn't have made it to today!" Grede shot back.

"My face has always been this black!" Needleflame yelled.

Sora, walking beside them, couldn't help but laugh. Even in her green hood, her pure elven form was quite eye-catching in the crowd.

"Did you see that blonde beautiful sorceress? I heard she's a green dragon."

"Is that real or not?"

"Hey! Lady Sora Shadow! Are you really a dragon?"

Sora hadn't expected that a simple laugh would draw so much attention. She looked helplessly toward Jace.

Jace was busy scanning the crowd for his parents. When he saw Sora's look, he subtly made a "shush" gesture — though he knew full well that with hundreds or thousands of eyes on him, no small movement could escape notice.

"Jace Sesso! That's Jace Sesso!" "He's not some old man!"

"Not only is he not an old man, he looks younger than my son."

Behind them, Khadgar rubbed his freshly shaved chin, clearly displeased. He leaned toward Turalyon beside him and muttered, "Should I not have shaved?"

"Why?" Turalyon asked.

Khadgar squinted at Jence ahead. "Look at that. All the attention's on that kid."

"Haven't you had enough attention, Master Khadgar?" Danath Trollbane teased.

"Admit it, Khadgar." Turalyon smiled. "None of us are the young men we were when the Dark Portal first opened. Even without Medivh's curse on you."

"At least I think you look younger than when you left for the Dark Portal, Khadgar." Alleria turned back from her white warhorse.

"Really?" The archmage gave Turalyon a smug look. "I'd love to hear more, Captain Alleria. I trust a ranger's eye is far sharper than a paladin's. Tell me, what makes you say that?"

"Look." Alleria glanced at the archmage statue overhead. "The contrast is striking."

"Damn it. Stop bringing up that statue." Khadgar said through gritted teeth.

"Watch your image, archmage." Kurdran Wildhammer called from behind. "Mind your language!"

"That's not something I'd expect to hear from a Wildhammer." Khadgar grumbled.

"What's wrong with us Wildhammers, huh?" Grede shouted. The few dwarves following on gryphons behind joined in with indignant Dwarvish calls, and the mages and priests around them all burst into laughter.

The Expedition procession made its way from the city gates to the Trade District square. Even the fountain was packed with spectators. Vereesa pointed west. "Look, Jace. See who that is."

She dismounted and slipped through the procession.

Jace followed her gaze and saw a group of mages in purple robes gathered nearby, watching. One of the female mages waved high.

"Amy?"

Jace jumped off his horse too and jogged over. Vereesa hugged Amy, then they both embraced tightly.

Amy wiped her eyes. "I almost thought I'd miss your ceremony, Jace. The portal at Stonewatch was unstable because of the ley line connection to Telogrus, so the Kirin Tor suspended it. I had to ride a gryphon back. But I made it in time to see you."

"You should have come through the city gates with us, Amy," Vereesa said.

Jace wiped her tears. "You gave just as much as any of us. We all know that."

"Don't say that, Jace. I know you almost didn't make it back. Compared to you, I was just vacationing in Redridge." Amy shook her head. "Besides, the one who really helped wasn't me, it was..."

She looked behind her. A blonde girl stepped forward shyly. "It's an honor to meet you again, Master Sesso, Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

"The honor is ours, Miss Proudmoore." Jace nodded. "Thank you for your help..."

"I knew nothing about it, Master." Jaina said quickly. "So I can hardly claim to have helped. You're too kind."

Though this princess of Kul Tiras was only fourteen or fifteen, she already carried herself with the poise of a true noble.

"Of course, of course." Jace smiled, glancing at Amy behind her. Amy returned a mysterious smile.

"The Midsummer Fire Festival is not far off, Master." Jaina said. "My father will come to Stormwind then. He very much wants to meet you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

"Is that so?" Vereesa asked, slightly puzzled. "The Lord Admiral is coming to Stormwind for the Fire Festival?"

"It's Prince Arthas's coming-of-age ceremony, Vereesa." Amy explained. "He'll become a true paladin of the Silver Hand at the Cathedral of Light in Stormwind. Archbishop Alonsus Faol will perform the ceremony himself."

Jaina nodded with a smile. The mages nearby looked at the young mage expectantly — they'd all heard the rumors in Dalaran about the courtship between Kul Tiras's princess and Prince Arthas.

But Jace could clearly sense the turmoil and unease churning within the young woman. She wasn't as excited about this ceremony as she appeared. Maybe from nervousness. Maybe from something else.

"We'll be there." Jace said. "We look forward to meeting Prince Arthas as well."

"That would be wonderful." Jaina nodded. "I'll tell my father the good news."
