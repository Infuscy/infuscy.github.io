Chapter 1417: Standing in the Light

The square before the Cathedral of Light was packed with citizens who had come to see the Expedition's splendor. They waved their hands. Some small children sat on their fathers' shoulders, trying to stretch even higher, raising their arms to wave endlessly, desperate to catch the heroes' attention.

On the way from the Canal District to the Cathedral Square, Jace had started to enjoy this moment of being blessed and thanked by everyone.

The sun was bright. The shadows of buildings, bridges, and walls fell in sharp, clear lines. The breeze was neither too hot nor too cold. Friends laughed and chatted all around. Every third person called out his name. Thanks to the mages, the entire canal-side carried the faint scent of daffodils and roses.

But as they approached the cathedral, everyone dismounted and walked into the square on foot. Jace found himself unable to relax.

Maybe he was just nervous about arriving at the ceremony. Maybe he was impatient. Maybe he was hungry and wanted to get this over with as fast as possible, head to the royal palace for King Varian's post-ceremony banquet, and fill his stomach.

"You're today's hero." Vereesa leaned close. "You don't need to say anything. You don't need to do anything. Don't think about all the etiquette or noble rules. You're not a member of the clergy, and you're not some politician who needs to put on a show. You just need to accept everyone's thanks and respect. You've earned it."

"I've been to occasions like this before, Ver." Jace smiled. "I'll manage. Maybe the stone floor in the square is just a little warm."

"Don't joke about that." Vereesa pursed her lips, glanced cautiously at Turalyon beside them, and whispered, "At least not here."

"Got it."

Jace raised an eyebrow, and the ranger couldn't help but snort with laughter.

A large group of priests had gathered at the bottom of the cathedral steps. Among them were several bishops, some of them familiar faces. Including Bishop Benedictus.

At the center of them was a golden-haired bishop — exceptional in both dress and bearing. Even Benedictus treated him with extraordinary deference, lowering his head to listen to whatever he said.

As the Expedition procession approached the cathedral, surrounded by the crowd, the bishops all turned to look this way. That was when Jace got a clear look at the silver-blue archbishop's crown on the golden-haired bishop.

Was that Alonsus Faol himself?

When they'd set out from Lakeshire to Stormwind, some priests had informed the Expedition members that the master of the Cathedral of Light would welcome them at the square. Back then, Jace had assumed it would be Bishop Benedictus, since he presided over the cathedral most of the time.

Besides, Jace had heard that the Sons of Lothar had other celebrations when they returned to Lordaeron. Stromgarde and Lordaeron each had their own welcoming ceremonies. The ceremony in Lordaeron's capital might be even grander, since it was the heart of the Lordaeron Alliance and the Expedition's starting point.

But Jace hadn't expected that the true master of this cathedral, the leader of the church, Archbishop Faol himself would be here. He'd assumed Faol would stay in Lordaeron to await the Expedition's return.

Come to think of it, despite having lived in Tirisfal for fifteen years before the First and Second Wars, Jace had never once seen Alonsus Faol. His mother sometimes saw him when she visited the cathedral.

Not because the archbishop kept himself aloof from common folk. Jace had heard he traveled frequently, moving between the monasteries and villages of Eastern Weald and Tirisfal, sometimes visiting other kingdoms to teach, spread doctrine, and help the poor.

Also, Jace had never had much interest in priests. He only went when his mother dragged him along, and even then, it wasn't to see the high-ranking clergymen. He was mostly trying to sneak some sweet biscuits and sunfruit, since the church priests didn't seem to mind chasing after the kids running wild through the city.

Maybe thanks to the Holy Light's favor, Faol looked much younger than Jace had expected. His golden hair and beard gleamed healthily in the sunlight. He stood tall and solid, looking barely older than his student Benedictus.

Jace could feel it. Among the envoys from other kingdoms and the nobles of Stormwind, the archbishop stood out like a literal saint.

When his gaze fell on the Expedition's leaders — especially Turalyon — the burning intensity in his eyes could probably only be matched by the King of Stormwind standing nearby.

Then, Archbishop Faol shifted his gaze to Jace. It felt like he'd seen through Jace's use of his emotional perception to scan everyone in the square.

Just as Jace thought that stern look might be some kind of warning — telling him not to use shadow abilities in such a holy place — the archbishop merely gave a slight nod, then raised his head to look at the distant citizens.

"In the name of the Holy Light, we gather here today, heavy-hearted, to welcome our children, our parents, our brothers and sisters home. They have achieved victory. But this victory was hard-won — crossing the barriers of evil and darkness, crossing the distances of dimensions and the void, paying an immeasurable price. So much so that many among us carry complex feelings even on this day of celebration. Before we begin, let us observe a moment of silence for those spirits who fought and gave their lives in that demonic wasteland, in the name of the Holy Light, in the name of this world, in the name of family and friends."

The square fell utterly silent. Jace's mind drifted back to Wildhammer Stronghold through the sound of wind and birdsong. The way those dwarves, humans, and gryphons had looked at him felt like it was right before his eyes.

After the silence, the archbishop briefly recounted everything that had happened on Draenor to the gathered crowd. Every name carved on the statues in the Valley of Heroes was mentioned.

Sylvanas couldn't help but tightly grasp her sisters' hands. Turalyon, Khadgar, and the other Expedition leaders — including Grede and Sora — listened intently.

They weren't the only ones. Everyone present was moved. Jace was no exception.

Unconsciously, he recalled those scenes. Nagrand to Bladespire. Auchindoun. The legions of demons. The blood-red fel orcs covering Hellfire Citadel.

Then the people around him started turning to look his way. He realized he'd become the focus of the square.

Not just the citizens. The priests and bishops. The nobles too.

"...And finally, a great rescue. Court mage Jace Sesso and Khadgar of the Kirin Tor jointly built a portal that allowed our soldiers to break through the demons' blockade and return home."

"Just when the demons thought they still had a chance to invade our world again, to bring destruction and fire to Azeroth, Jace Sesso bravely ventured deep into enemy territory and destroyed the corrupt artifact they used to invade worlds — the Legion's supreme scepter. He permanently thwarted the evil plans of the demon lords."

Gasps and cheers erupted across the square. Though Jace figured most of them didn't really know what the Scepter of Sargeras was, the archbishop had explained it clearly enough in just a few sentences.

"Come, Jace." Turalyon said softly, gesturing.

Jace nodded. He left the Expedition procession, following the priests' guidance toward Alonsus Faol.

But the closer he got to the archbishop, the more he felt a maddening heat throughout his body. It was nowhere near summer yet. The weather wasn't nearly this hot.

He knew exactly why he felt this way, but he could only try his best to stay calm. He could barely even make out Archbishop Faol's quiet prayer. The sweat beading on his forehead and trickling through his hair was painfully clear.

When Jace had come to the cathedral square before and received the priests' blessings, the discomfort had never been this severe.

He understood the cause. The stronger his control over shadow power became, the more clearly he could sense shadow — emotions, dark fluctuations. Especially on Draenor, as he repeatedly used powerful shadow magic, especially spells like Life Tap, absorbing soul energy and terrifying void power — his flesh and blood had inevitably grown more adapted to shadow and increasingly resistant to the Holy Light.

"Sesso? Jace Sesso?"

"Forgive me, Archbishop." Jace cleared his throat.

"Don't worry. The Holy Light understands you." Archbishop Faol gazed at him, speaking softly. His eyes held a faint golden radiance.

"As far as I can see, the Holy Light knows what you've done. I can feel it."

Jace could barely see the archbishop's lips or throat moving, yet the words reached him. He was starting to wonder if the heat was making him hallucinate when Faol fastened a silver shield-and-hammer emblem onto his cloak.

"On behalf of the Church of the Holy Light, I bestow upon you the Sigil of Tyr's Guardian, Jace Sesso. From this day forward, you shall be known as Tyr's Protector. Not only the church, but the entire Lordaeron Alliance thanks you for everything you did beyond the Dark Portal. May the Holy Light bless you."

"And the Sons of Lothar as well, Jace." Turalyon smiled.

The square erupted. Vereesa, crying in her two sisters' arms, had red eyes. Jace turned back to look at her and smiled, then couldn't help reaching out to touch the emblem. He snatched his hand back immediately, as if he'd accidentally touched a freshly boiled kettle.
