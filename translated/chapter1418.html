Chapter 1418: Rain in the Night

The Pig and Whistle was already chaos by night. Dozens of Expedition soldiers had squeezed into Stormwind's cheapest tavern, eating and drinking uproariously.

Jace's group had tried hiding in a corner under their cloaks and hoods, but they kept getting recognized. They eventually retreated to a spot on the second floor to escape the stares from the main hall.

Not that it was much better up here. Laughter, shouting, and the clinking of tankards came in waves. The fire basins lighting the room swayed as tall soldiers passed by.

Even the fire elementals inside hissed in complaint, looking like they'd swallow the whole tavern in an instant if one of them got knocked over.

Grede had barely sat down when he started laughing. Finally, he pointed at Jace. "Hey, hey, did you see him in front of Archbishop Alonsus Faol? Steam was rising right off his head!"

"Grede." Vereesa's face went cold. She glanced at a table nearby and said quietly, "Keep your voice down."

"Everyone in here knows what's what, little elf." Grede coughed from his own spit. "Everyone knows what our little brother does for a living. Even the archbishop himself didn't care."

"To be fair, it wasn't that obvious, Grede." Sora, sitting beside Jace, said. "But I was surprised Archbishop Faol showed no resistance to his... shadow affinity."

"I think the church's leadership is more practical than those fanatical believers down below." Grede said as a barmaid squeezed through the crowd carrying a tray of sliced bread and smoked cod. She set it down on the table, glanced at the group, seemed to recognize something, but wasn't sure.

After she left, Vereesa took a piece of smoked fish and bit into it. "I've heard that the Church of the Holy Light once had a group of shadow priests. Maybe high-ranking clergymen like Alonsus Faol or Benedictus have a less narrow understanding of the Light and Shadow..."

"You say that in front of the king and the archbishop himself, huh!" Grede laughed, taking a stew pot that the barmaid handed him and giving it a sniff. "Finally away from those people. If you ask me, those human noble banquets aren't banquets at all. They're like some other kind of magic ritual. You know what I mean, Jace? Same with you elves — you follow some rule from start to finish. Now you can eat this, then you can eat that. And the carpet pattern on the floor looks like a magic circle too. Everything's in place. If anyone doesn't follow the whole set of rules from beginning to end, the ritual fails."

Vereesa nodded thoughtfully. "That actually makes some sense. Honestly, I didn't want to stay there too long either. Too many unfamiliar human nobles. I don't think King Varian was very comfortable either."

"I was relieved to leave the palace too." Jace sighed. "I'd planned to eat my fill there, but I spent the whole time worrying about how to explain destroying the scepter. I think King Varian and Khadgar were pretty curious about it."

"Is Morofil here?" Grede looked around. "Didn't you say she was coming, Jace? She went with you. Have her help you figure out what to say."

"She's already here." Jace pointed behind him. "She's watching you right now."

Grede looked up at the wall behind Jace. "Well. So having wings isn't all good. She can't just throw on a cloak and pass for human now."

"Even without..." Needleflame slurped from her stew bowl. "Those four horns of hers wouldn't fit under a hood anyway."

"Her horns weren't this long before, little one." Grede scooped a piece of potato from the bowl and chewed. "I'd like to hear Lady Morofil tell us what really happened on that mountain. I thought you went there just to find the scepter and break it. I thought I had it good circling above Terokkar Forest dealing with those doomguards. Now I'm wondering if I killed more demons on this trip than you!"

"Beat me first, Grede." Vereesa crossed her arms. "I didn't see you shoot down many doomguards above Auchindoun. But I personally put arrows through more than thirty elite Mo'arg hearts! At least five doomguards. As for the weaker demons, I won't even bother counting."

"One doomguard is worth at least four felguards!" Grede argued.

"Drinks are here! Drinks are here!" Needleflame raised her hands, babbling happily. The dwarf laughed twice. "All the good drinks here are just about gone. I'll have to settle for Thelsamar Light Beer instead. Close enough!"

Needleflame stared at the bubbles on the glass. "I've never had that. What's Thelsamar?"

"Are you from Kalimdor, little girl?" the barmaid laughed. "You don't know Thelsamar?"

"In a way, she really is from Kalimdor." Jace teased.

The barmaid's smile froze. She put down the glass and left, stunned. Grede couldn't stop laughing.

"So when do we start!" Needleflame licked her teeth.

"How about now!" Grede slapped the table.

"Good!"

Needleflame grabbed a cup, downed it in one go, and picked up another. Strangely, watching this muscular little girl drink like that somehow didn't look out of place. Like she was born to go drink for drink with a Wildhammer dwarf.

Grede watched her drink, and some of the confidence in his eyes faded. Had those two really gone at it tooth and nail back at Wildhammer Stronghold?

"You could tell them the Light helped you, Jace," Vereesa said. "I don't know... if the Holy Light wouldn't help at a decisive moment like destroying the Scepter of Sargeras, when would it ever show up?"

"I thought about that too." Jace said. "But look at it this way..."

"Like you said earlier, plenty of people nearby saw how I react under the Holy Light. How many of them do you think would believe the Light helped me destroy that scepter? And even if they did, how could someone like me summon enough Holy Light power to destroy something like that? And how badly would that much Light power torture me in return?"

"At least Turalyon and that draenei prophet can believe you, Jace." Vereesa said.

"Four cups!" Needleflame snatched Jace's cup and downed it. Grede shouted across, "Fill our table up, Langston!"

"If you ask me, Jace, don't worry about it too much." Sora said. "Master Khadgar believes you. And no one else really understands that scepter. They didn't actually touch the thing like Prince Sabellian did. They don't know how hard it was to destroy."

"You're right, Sora." Jace said.

Rain began pattering on the roof. Vereesa asked, "Where did this sudden rain come from?"

"Alnar's still tied up outside. I have to get her under the awning." Jace grabbed his cloak and staff. "Watch my stuff. If the meat comes, save me some. You lot..."

"No problem!" Vereesa and Sora said in unison.

Jace gave them both a look. He knew perfectly well that not even a kodo could get past those two at mealtime.

Outside the tavern, the temperature had dropped sharply from daytime. Crowds of people going in and out of taverns hurriedly grabbed their cloaks and ran for cover. The rain had come out of nowhere. Plenty were cursing the unannounced bad weather.

His father had lent him Alnar to ride with the Expedition back to Stormwind. He didn't want anything to happen to her, so he quickly untied the reins and started leading her around back.

That was when he felt someone watching him.

He turned around. Amid the dark, shifting figures, a thin, cloaked silhouette stood across the street, watching him.

"You were waiting for me." Jace said. "Ma'am?"

"There's something I'm curious about." The female mage's voice was low, but it cut through the rain like it was entering his mind directly.

"How did you destroy the Scepter of Sargeras?"

"Soul power." Jace answered. "Kil'jaeden's demon roster helped a lot."

The female mage sighed faintly. "In the end... an Eredar creation destroyed an Eredar creation. A demon's soul ended a demon's soul."

"I suppose so." Jace said.

"You accomplished what I could not." She lifted her head, her tone weary. "I am grateful for what you did, Jace Sesso."

As her voice faded, the rain curtain seemed to be yanked away all at once. Jace instinctively looked up at the sky. When he looked back, she was gone.
