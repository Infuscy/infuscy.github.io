Chapter 1419: If You're Going to Find a Teacher, Find the Best

The tavern was still lively, like nothing had happened. To everyone except Jace, it was just a random rainstorm that came and went. People appeared and disappeared — nothing unusual for Old Town on any given night. Jace hurried back to their second-floor seats. His eyes went straight to Ulthalesh, resting beside Vereesa. Good, the tightly-wrapped staff was still there. Ever since he'd struck the Scepter of Sargeras on the summit of Kil'jaeden's mountaintop, triggering the explosion that shook the entire temple and mountain, the staff had been completely lifeless. But Jace could still feel a trace of lingering anger, displeasure. Proof it hadn't been completely annihilated. Grede and Needleflame were still staring each other down over their cups. Sora was tearing into some steaming, juicy wolf meat. Vereesa hadn't started eating yet. She watched Jace walk over, bend down to rummage through his bag, and touch the staff. "I don't want you to think I'm overly nervous just because of that ranger-lord title." Vereesa's voice was wary. "But this rain... did it have anything to do with you, Jace?" "You could say that." Jace said quietly, looking around. "Did anyone come?" At his words, Sora looked up. Grede narrowed his eyes and put down his cup too. "No." Vereesa said. "What about Morofil? Did she sense anything?" "Nobody suspicious tried to approach, master." A bat-like shadow stirred against the wall. "No need to worry." Jace nodded. Vereesa pressed, "So what was that rain about? I felt magical fluctuations, but they weren't obvious. If you hadn't gone outside, I might not have noticed. I can't tell if it came from the Wizard Sanctum or if it was really connected to that rain that came and went so fast." "Your instincts are right, Ver." Jace sat down. "That rain was probably Aegwynn's doing. I just met her." "Aegwynn?" Grede and Vereesa both said the name at the same time. Then they quickly checked the other tables to make sure no one had heard their exclamation. Though Aegwynn was mostly forgotten by ordinary people by now — nowhere near as unnerving as Medivh, who'd helped the orcs open the Dark Portal — the name wasn't unfamiliar among the Expedition soldiers. "What did she say?" Vereesa asked. "About destroying the Scepter of Sargeras." Jace rubbed his eyes. "Khadgar probably didn't tell her much. But after the Expedition returned to Azeroth, and with the archbishop's speech, there was no way to hide it from her." "I said the demon roster's power helped. From her reaction, she might already know I tore up the demon roster." "How would she know?" Vereesa asked. "Khadgar reported it to the Kirin Tor and the Wizard Sanctum right away. Every mage here knows by now." Jace explained. "Maybe that's even part of why Archbishop Alonsus Faol gave me that emblem." He reached out and touched the shield emblem. Still warm, but nowhere near the searing heat from the Cathedral of Light. "I'm amazed you can be so relaxed after meeting that mage." Vereesa shook her head. Jace laughed, nudging his staff. "Don't worry. If she knew everything, do you think I'd still be alive?" "She wouldn't... try something in the middle of Stormwind, would she?" Grede asked. Jace didn't dare predict what Aegwynn would do. But from the fact that she'd waited for him to leave the tavern to talk privately, she wasn't eager to drag innocent people into it. From what Khadgar had said, Aegwynn probably still hadn't succeeded in resurrecting her son. And after all the mana she'd expended on the portal, Telogrus Rift, and Karazhan, that plan of hers — so important to her — would probably have to wait. Sabellian had mentioned earlier that Deathwing had found Aegwynn's research on destroying the Scepter of Sargeras in Dalaran's records concerning the Order of Tirisfal. Even mentioning the possibility of Ulthalesh. Of course, that was also a major reason the Black Dragon Prince had believed Jace could break the scepter. If Jace's actions hadn't touched on something she'd been brooding over for so long, she probably wouldn't have stayed in Stormwind looking for a chance to talk to him. There were too many Kirin Tor people at the Tower of Ilgala — she couldn't slip in without attracting attention. "She doesn't seem hostile." Jace said. "Actually, she thanked me. I think she was thanking us for bringing the Expedition back. After all, the Dark Portal was originally her son's doing." "If she really wants to thank us, she should conjure me ten more tankards of Karanos Thunder Ale right now." Grede raised his arm. "So I can drink this ungrateful little thing under the table... Ugh. I'm sure that's no trouble for a former Guardian of Tirisfal." He went back to drinking without hesitation. "Where's the Karanos... Karanos ale..." Needleflame mumbled, clutching a large half-full wooden cup. "Bring it out already." Jace pried open one of the little dragon's eyelids, watching the yellow-black dragon eye roll around. "Don't let her drink anymore, Grede. Her transformation is barely holding. You don't want the entire Stormwind guard deploying tomorrow morning to hunt down 'Onyxia's remaining spawn,' do you?" "Absolutely not. Are you underestimating me? I'm great at transforming into a human!" Needleflame grabbed Jace's finger. "Look at yourself, warlock. Look at the skin on my face. Look at my human hands. Take a good lesson from me. Your form needs work." Jace tried to shake off her grip, but the little thing was surprisingly strong. She wouldn't let go. "Put her to sleep." Sora nodded reluctantly and blew a breath at her. The little black dragon crashed face-first onto the table with a thump, drool and beer bubbles dribbling from her mouth. "I win." Grede burped, stiffly raising his arms. "I won." "This one's drunk too." Jace sighed. "I was hoping to talk to him about going to Grim Batol." "Grim Batol." Grede's head bobbed like a noddle. "Grim Batol. Kurdran Wildhammer wants to go too. He heard I'd been to Grim Batol, so he wants to go too. He wants to see it too, before going back to Aerie Peak..." "Alright, I got it." Jace said. "I'll talk to Lord Kurdran about it." "Talk." The dwarf barely managed the word before flopping face-first onto the table, mumbling, "Firelord... fight... fire... iron..." "Great." Vereesa crossed her arms. "Now we not only have to carry a black dragon, but a stone dwarf as well." "I can take Needleflame to my place, Vereesa." Sora said. "I noticed some Wildhammer dwarves still on the first floor. We can ask them to help carry Grede back?" Jace looked downstairs. A few barmaids and old Langston were gathered around a cluster of passed-out dwarves, looking troubled. "They're in no better shape than us. We'll have to carry him back ourselves, Ver." "Fine." The elf said reluctantly. After seeing Sora and Needleflame off, the two of them strapped Grede's belongings to him. Vereesa said, "I was learning some nature magic from Sora before, Jace." "But I could tell she never had much confidence in teaching me deeply. She mentioned a few times that after we got back, I should go to the southern Morninglight Woods to find Teralar — the elder green dragon who helped make this bow — and find him as a teacher. I'd already mentally prepared myself to fight on Draenor for years, and then the big retreat happened... so I never gave it serious thought. But now that we're back, while you go to Grim Batol, I plan to go check it out." Jace said, "But Teralar is often away. Remember the first time we tried to find him, to ask about the moonwell and the Temple of Atal'Hakkar? He wasn't there. It was only when the Green Dragon Queen ordered him to wait for us that he showed up." "I know." Vereesa nodded. "But getting stronger always requires sacrifice. Patience is the easiest kind." "If you ask me," Jace said, "just come with us to Grim Batol, Ver. Ask Queen Alexstrasza directly to find Queen Ysera. Wouldn't that be better?" "I hadn't thought of that..." Vereesa thought for a moment, then couldn't help the smug curl of her lips. "I'll ask her, then." Just then, a few mages came in downstairs. Though they weren't wearing anything that marked their affiliation, one of them had a vaguely familiar face. Jace was fairly certain they were Kirin Tor. They'd probably sensed the anomalous magical fluctuations too and came to investigate.
