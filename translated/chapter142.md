## Chapter 142: The Burning Morning

A dragon. A real, genuine dragon?

"How is that possible..."

"I've actually seen a dragon, Jace. That's what they look like from a distance — those wings, that tail! Trust me, I'm not wrong!" Grede finished, then cursed in excitement. "Damn it, why is there a dragon here?! Whatever happens, let's find somewhere to hide. Damn it, that thing didn't see us, did it? If it turns around and breathes on us, the two of us and this little village are all done for!!"

The two of them bolted for the shore and into a stand of trees, finding a slope to crouch in the shadows.

Shifting his position, Jace noticed Grede's hand gripping the axe was shaking nonstop.

"Bloody hell..." the dwarf kept cursing under his breath.

Jace looked in the direction the dragon had disappeared, now blocked by the pine trees. Hiding here didn't feel remotely reliable.

"We should head back into town, Grede."

"Are you crazy? Isn't that where the dragon we just saw was heading?"

"But there are guards there. And mages. Think about it — what if there are other dragons, or Horde soldiers right behind it, maybe even almost here already? Wouldn't the two of us just be waiting to die? During the war, those dragons traveled in packs, didn't they?" Yes, that's right, in packs... Grede took a few breaths. "You're right. We should hurry. If that dragon is a vanguard for orc cavalry, we're screwed."

The two of them got up and headed east, keeping to the treeline.

Whether it was the vanguard of an orc cavalry, or one of a whole flight of dragons, even a single dragon spotting the two of them and banking back this way — they couldn't hide for long no matter where they looked.

Once a fight started, they'd be no match at all. No matter what, they needed to find help first.

Could the threat that Dalaran mage mentioned earlier have been a dragon sighting?

When Teron Gorefiend led the death knights and orc dragon-riders to attack the Alterac Valley, they'd ridden red dragons, not black ones.

And the mage had also mentioned signs of shadow attacks in Ambermill. Could these black dragons still be lingering in the area after the Alterac campaign?

Jace didn't believe that the Dragonmaw clan, hiding deep in the Wetlands and Grim Batol, would have the nerve to circle over the Hillsbrad Foothills on red dragons after the Horde's overall defeat.

What about a red dragon whose mind had been dominated by a Dragonmaw warlock?

"Did you get a good look? What color was the dragon?" Jace asked as they ran. "Red dragon? Black dragon?"

"A red one... I've never seen any other scale color," Grede said. "So many red dragons fought in the Battle of Hillsbrad — there must be a few stragglers. Maybe this is one of them. A shared enemy of the Alliance... A red dragon! An evil red dragon!"

Jace was a little lost, since he'd only seen a shadow too.

The two of them sprinted all the way back to Southshore at full speed, and from far away they could hear the town hall bell clanging nonstop.

"Is that dragon going to attack the town directly?" Grede asked in disbelief.

"Why?"

Jace didn't want to guess either, but the closer they got, the more chaotic the town became — until they reached the docks and saw that the whole place had completely fallen apart.

Every boat was fleeing for the open sea. The ones still at the dock were weighing anchor. Some hadn't even left the harbor yet and were already hoisting sail. Cables flailed in the chaos. Someone had even fallen into the sea and was swimming toward a ship.

Black smoke billowed everywhere across town — definitely not the cooking smoke they'd seen that morning. And yet the dragon wasn't overhead.

Jace and Grede ran onto the street by the docks and saw several buildings already destroyed. Even a house next to their inn was on fire. A mage was chanting incantations no one could understand, calling on ice magic to barely hold back the flames, but it clearly wasn't working well.

Burned victims lay helpless in the street. Wails and screams rose and fell. Jace stared at the horrifying scene. He hadn't expected the dragon to cause this much destruction just by flying over the town.

"Hurry... our luggage is still inside, Jace!" The dwarf grabbed his shoulder. "Come with me!"

The two of them charged up to the second floor of the inn and found their luggage. The second floor was already thick with smoke, and the smell was far worse than ordinary burning.

"What's burning? It reeks!"

Grede covered his nose as he and Jace hauled bags and clothes down to the first floor — and then they heard a child crying upstairs. At the sound, both of them froze and looked at each other.

"You take the stuff out first!" Jace said. "I've got a fire-resistant cloak. I'll go find him."

"I'll be right back to help!" the dwarf roared.

Jace left Grede and charged back upstairs. That mage must have been running on empty — the smoke was a lot thicker now than before, and Jace could even see some actual flames.

"Where are you? Yell something! Anyone!"

He ducked his head and pushed through the smoky air, cloak over his nose and mouth. The kid had probably already been knocked out by the smoke.

The temperature kept rising. Suddenly a beam collapsed somewhere, and the floor tilted under his feet. Jace nearly slipped. The child's voice came again.

"I'm here! Someone's coming!" Jace kicked open a door warped stuck in its frame, and saw a little girl hiding under a bed, sobbing, her face coated in black soot with tear-tracks streaking through it.

Jace scooped her up, tucked her under his cloak, and turned to charge back into the hallway through the smoke.

The fire was spreading too fast. The situation was already orders of magnitude worse than when he'd gone in.

But Jace knew that every second he hesitated brought him closer to death. Since he had the cloak protecting him, he should go all out.

He held his breath and charged straight through the choking smoke without hesitation, until a burning, collapsed wall blocked his way.

He could already see the street outside through the cracks. He kicked hard at the gap but couldn't break through.

Then Grede came charging out of nowhere and swung his axe into the crack, splitting a whole section of wood, then kicked it — the obstacle crashed down in a heap.

He grabbed Jace's collar and roared, "Jump! Better to break a leg than suffocate in here!"

Jace threw himself off the second floor. He couldn't steady his landing, and to protect the kid in his arms he sat hard on the ground.

It hurt like hell, but at least he could still stand. If this had been anywhere near the docks with decent infrastructure instead of dirt roads and weeds, he'd probably have been half-done already.

He turned back to see the inn's second floor already ablaze.

Wails and sobs everywhere outside. The smoke-filled sky turned a clear morning into something out of the apocalypse.

The little girl's parents were nowhere to be seen, looking around in a daze. Jace looked at the limping wounded up and down the street and the soldiers, workers, and farmhands hauling water everywhere. Had Southshore ever suffered a disaster like this?

By now, he wasn't so sure he still remembered any of the history.

A piercing shriek from the distance snapped him from his thoughts. The people who'd just started relaxing all snapped their eyes wide open again.

"It's back! It's back!"
