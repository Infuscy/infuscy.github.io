Chapter 1420: Taking This Little Thing Home

By the time Jace and Vereesa had hauled Grede back to the Dwarf District, it was well past midnight. But Jace didn't go home. Instead, he went straight to the Wizard Sanctum, asked where Khadgar was staying, and knocked on his door.

Khadgar didn't seem surprised to see Jace showing up in the middle of the night. He checked the street outside, made sure no one was nearby, and let him in. The moment they sat down, he said, "Next time, try not to bring that black dragon into such crowded places, Jace."

"The Kirin Tor is following me?" Jace asked.

"They just happened to spot you." Khadgar looked at Jace's skeptical expression. "The Kirin Tor isn't following you — at least, not as far as I know. You're in Stormwind, and you just received a Sigil of Tyr's Guardian that even the Silver Hand's knights don't have. Nobody from the north wants to pick a fight with you on Archbishop Faol's turf."

"But that strange rain tonight was your doing, wasn't it? Master Runevale sensed the source of the magic was the Pig and Whistle."

"That's what I came to tell you." Jace said. "It was Aegwynn. She was waiting for me there."

"Aegwynn... I can't say I'm surprised." Khadgar sighed faintly, rubbing his forehead. "When I heard the archbishop's speech at the Cathedral of Light today, I had a feeling she might come find you. Destroying the Scepter of Sargeras means a lot to her."

"You knew she was hiding in Stormwind?" Jace asked.

"A feeling, Jace." Khadgar said. "Sometimes arcane insight is more mysterious than the Holy Light. It's impossible to explain clearly, especially when dealing with mages like Aegwynn. What did you tell her? You didn't actually say you summoned the Holy Light to destroy the scepter, did you? She'd never believe that. Don't forget — she tried it herself centuries ago."

"I didn't say that." Jace said. "I told her it was the Book of Fel Names. The grimoire's already been torn up, and it's in the Kirin Tor's hands now. I don't think she'll be so wary of me anymore."

Khadgar nodded. "In any case, Lady Aegwynn is stubborn and headstrong, does things her own way... but she doesn't hurt people without reason. As long as you don't go looking for her with any ulterior motives, you'll be fine."

"What about Karazhan, then?" Jace asked. "Would entering Karazhan count as 'reason to hurt us'? She did build that tower."

"No need to worry about that." Khadgar said. "I wouldn't have brought it up if I wasn't sure. She stopped considering that tower her personal property long ago. But now that you mention it — does this mean you're willing to come to Karazhan with me?"

"Yes." Jace said. "Honestly, I'm pretty interested in some of Medivh's research."

"People have a lot of misconceptions about Medivh, Jace." Khadgar said. "When I first arrived at Karazhan, my teacher seemed strange. Moody, difficult, liked testing people. The Kirin Tor's demon scholars thought he might have been tainted by demonic power. But I didn't see many signs."

"It was only afterward that I learned he'd been fighting the Dark Titan's soul inside him the whole time. The person I met was probably him during one of his 'normal' periods. At the same time, he did a lot of research on controlling demonic and shadow power. He ultimately lost that long struggle, but I think that research could help you."

"I'm luckier than Medivh," Jace said.

"We're all luckier than him," Khadgar said.

"By the way, we're heading to Lordaeron in a few days. I remember you're from Brill. Are you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner coming back with us?"

"No." Jace said. "I'm planning to go to Grim Batol with them."

"Ah." Khadgar nodded. "So Lord Kurdran wasn't joking. I wish you a smooth journey. Once I'm back in Dalaran and finish dealing with everything from Draenor, I'll come find you in Stormwind."

"Good." Jace replied.

That morning, the sky was perfectly clear, not a cloud in sight. The weather was already warming up.

"The Midsummer Fire Festival is still over a month away. I wouldn't have dared to schedule a trip to Grim Batol in the middle of that before." Vereesa walked beside Jace. "Not even on a dragonhawk. We'd have to cross the entire Blackrock Mountain. Way too dangerous."

"Don't remind me." Grede said. "Remember how we first went to Grim Batol? On foot. How long did that take? Almost a month round trip."

"And that doesn't include the gryphon ride to Menethil Harbor." Vereesa said.

"True." Grede said.

"We should be thanking Lady Sora for that." Jace said.

Sora lowered her head modestly, brushing back her windblown golden hair with a smile. She'd been keeping this elven form ever since returning from Draenor. After they'd defeated Onyxia, her identity was no longer a secret among Stormwind's high-ranking officials.

More importantly, now that the Expedition's elves were garrisoned in large numbers across Stormwind, Goldshire, and Lakeshire, an elven appearance no longer drew any attention. So she didn't need to hide her preferred form anymore.

"You're actually happy about carrying them, Sora?" Needleflame ran from behind to the front. "Father taught me — never let mortals ride on our backs!"

"That's because your little body isn't much stronger than a gryphon. He was afraid you'd wear yourself out!" Grede shouted.

"Not even when I'm bigger!" Needleflame's eyes went wide as she hopped in front of the dwarf. She was actually a head taller than Grede.

"Then how did I get to the Throne of Kil'jaeden, Needleflame?" Jace asked. "Did I sprout black wings myself?"

"That was emergency cooperation!" Needleflame shot back.

"And what about the trip to Hellfire Citadel? Was that an emergency too?" Jace said. "Prince Sabellian himself said that trip was like a 'pleasant sightseeing tour.'"

"Stop bringing that up! Father told you not to!" The black dragon gritted her teeth.

Sora couldn't help but laugh. She looked at the little black dragon. "Then let me put it this way — I'm very reluctantly taking them to Grim Batol. If it weren't for wanting to visit the Red Dragon Queen, I'd never help them. Does that work?"

Needleflame shot her an unhappy look, then turned away entirely, running over to the gryphons under the bridge near Alleria's statue.

Grede laughed like he was watching a show. Right on cue, the little dragon's hair started smoking from anger. Actual smoke — like the white vapor rising from your head after exercise in deep winter.

Jace was half-afraid she'd get angry and breathe fire down below. She was a genuine young dragon after all, probably not much weaker than the black dragon that had attacked Southshore. One blast and everything would fall apart. With that thought, no one dared crack any more jokes that might provoke her further.

Fortunately, this little black dragon had the same love of gryphons she'd shown at Wildhammer Stronghold. She'd been sneaking off to watch them for the past couple of days. Hopefully the gryphons' cries would hold her attention.

Vereesa's gaze slowly rose from Needleflame to her own sister's statue. She stared at it for a long time before speaking. "This isn't a dream, is it, Jace? We really did it."

"We did it," Jace said.

"Jace! Vereesa!"

Henny hurried over from beside Turalyon's statue. Vereesa quickly wiped the tears from her eyes and went to embrace her. Then Henny hugged her son.

"Good morning, Lady Sesso." Vereesa smiled.

"Good morning, Lady Sesso." Sora bowed her head too.

"Good morning, you three." Henny nodded, then said in Dwarvish, "Good morning, Lord Thunderfist!"

"My honor, my lady!" Grede replied happily in Dwarvish.

"You didn't have to come, Mom." Jace said. "But good morning."

Henny nodded. "Vick said you'd only be gone a few days, no need to make a big deal out of it, like his son hasn't grown up. But I still wanted to see you off."

"We'll be back soon." Jace gestured toward Needleflame. "We're just taking this unruly little thing home. Nothing major."

"That's not my home!" Needleflame bellowed.

The shout startled the young kids who'd been watching the gryphons with her. Even the gryphons below fell silent.

"We need to go." Jace said. "If we wait any longer, she'll tear down the whole city."

"Of course, of course." Henny nodded.

They said their goodbyes to Henny and started calling Needleflame toward the gate. Henny asked, puzzled, "Aren't you taking gryphons?"

"Right! Why aren't you riding gryphons?!" Needleflame grabbed Jace's cloak. "I want to ride a gryphon!"

"Our gryphons are waiting in Goldshire!" Jace glared at Needleflame. "Go back, Mom!"

"Alright, alright." Henny waved. "Safe travels!"

They hadn't taken two steps when Needleflame was still tugging Jace's cloak, muttering in Draconic, "Gryphon... gryphon..."

Noticing that the passersby and guards nearby were starting to pay attention to the strange child's muttering, Sora frowned and grabbed her ear. "Can you behave? That's... enough! Let go!"

The little black dragon, who'd been holding on stubbornly, finally let go when she realized Sora was genuinely upset. Clutching Sora's wrist, she begged, "Sorry, Sister Sora! I'm sorry! I know I was wrong!"
