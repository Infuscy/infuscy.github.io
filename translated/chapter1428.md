## Chapter 1428: The Apothecary's Work

"Ah..."

Vereesa shuddered and gripped Jace's chest, breathing in short, ragged gasps. She wiped the silver hair plastered to her forehead, then bent down to kiss his lips. "I like feeling you without the stench of fel fire."

"Me too." Jace said, kissing the elf's shoulder.

"Me three, you two." Morophil rested her chin on her hand, staring at their slick, pressed-together skin. "The whole room is filled with a mix of slightly acidic bodily fluids. Intoxicating."

"Don't... talk, demon," Vereesa said through gritted teeth, squeezing her eyes shut.

"I don't want to talk either. Who wants to ruin such a beautiful moment?" Morophil smacked her lips. "But don't forget why we came to Darkshire, Master. It's getting a bit late."

"She's right, Vereesa," Jace said. "I need to meet Galders and the others."

Vereesa nodded reluctantly, shifted away, and threw on her cloak. She shot an annoyed glance at the demon sitting by the table, watching them with a smile.

"I gave you time, dear," Morophil murmured, eyes narrowing.

"Don't get too cocky," the elf said, her ice-blue eyes flashing.

Jace looked at them both warily, pulling on his clothes and cloak as fast as he could. "We can continue tonight. I shouldn't be gone long. If their doomguard is strong enough, connecting to the Telogor Rift using the existing coordinates shouldn't be too hard."

"I'll be waiting at the inn," Vereesa said, pulling on her boots.

The streets of Darkshire were full of church priests. Apparently, over the last few days, the dark force blanketing all of Duskwood had inexplicably grown stronger, stirring up new unrest in the wilderness.

Jace guessed it had something to do with Aegwynn using Karazhan's power to locate the Telogor Rift. The Kirin Tor didn't want Khadgar touching the power of that black tower for a reason.

The plan had been to save the expedition and bring them home, and it had ultimately succeeded. But Jace felt a bit guilty about making the people of Darkshire live in fear again as he watched the carriages and pedestrians hurrying back into town around dusk.

He just hoped that the upcoming trip to Karazhan would really do what Khadgar claimed — weaken the dark magic blanketing all of Duskwood.

Though he wasn't very optimistic about that. In the game, Duskwood never improved, even decades later — when even the Plaguelands in the north, ravaged by the Scourge, had seen some recovery.

Morophil kept herself invisible, following Jace across half of Darkshire. Neither the priests from the Cathedral of Light nor the two Kirin Tor mages in purple robes noticed her presence.

With her soul restored, she was nothing like the wingless version of herself. Though she might not be as powerful in raw force as she'd been with the Book of Evil Names' spirit, she was on a whole new level in terms of self-understanding and control. Jace could even feel that ease and confidence through their soul-link.

At the Daggerfall's shelter, Erland was by the roadside fixing a fence. When he spotted Jace, he took a quick look around, then muttered, "Bloody hell... even the Twisting Nether couldn't kill you, Jace Sesso!"

"Erland!"

The two shared a firm embrace. Erland said, "If those vicious idiots in Booty Bay found out a warlock from our circle got titled 'Protector' by Archbishop Faol himself, they'd be so pissed they couldn't even get it up in front of a succubus!"

"That says more about them than their succubi," Jace said.

Erland laughed twice, then his eyes flicked to Morophil, who was sitting on the fence beside him. His face stiffened. "So it's true... Lady Morophil really did get her wings back!"

"That's exactly what I was waiting to hear, Duthmore." Morophil smiled. "All your past offenses against me can be wiped clean."

"Congratulations, my lady. I'm genuinely happy for you." Erland said. "I don't know how you did it... but you could truly be called a perfect banshee now, by any measure."

"If only my master had half your way with words," Morophil said wistfully.

"You could be called a half-perfect banshee now," Jace said.

Morophil shot Jace a sidelong glare. Erland chuckled. "Speaking of which — that soul drain spell you brought back from Draenor is incredibly powerful! I haven't mastered it yet, but Galders has almost got it down. Once I learn it and head back to Booty Bay, I'll probably become the center of attention there... I mean among the warlocks and demons."

"If there's time tonight, I can give you a hand," Jace said.

"Perfect." Erland rubbed his hands together. "But first we need to set up the portal to the Telogor Rift. That's the most important thing — it'll make going in and out much easier. Stonewatch has been crawling with Silver Hand and church people lately, not to mention the Kirin Tor... Galders and the others are eating downstairs. Our Eredun is ready and waiting. We'll start once Ursula gets back."

"Where's Ursula?" Jace asked.

"She's to the south, at the Tranquil Gardens Cemetery." Erland checked his pocket watch. "She should be back soon."

"I heard Duskwood's conditions have been getting worse lately?" Jace asked.

"That's exactly why she's there." Erland explained, "She's trying to use the necrotic energy from the graveyard to grow the herb seeds you brought from Draenor. She found a plant that grows around the necropolis over there that seems to thrive on the necrotic energy here too... Don't worry, her felhunter is with her."

"I'll go check out her herbs," Jace said. "I've already eaten, so I won't join you."

"Alright then." Erland flexed his arm. "I need to go grab some food. See you later."

After Erland left, Morophil leaned in. "I didn't know you'd eaten, Master. Quel'dorei milk?"

"Shut it." Jace glared at her. "Come with me."

The Tranquil Gardens Cemetery, on the high ground south of Darkshire, was much larger than it had appeared in the game. The sun had already set, leaving the entire hillside a murky gray. Tombstones and low fences were scattered everywhere. In the distance, the pale white walls of the abandoned monastery rose on the high ground.

The place was dead silent — at least at first glance, no walking corpses or scurrying ghouls, no sign of the living either. Just like a normal cemetery. But there had to be a reason it had been abandoned since the Karazhan Cataclysm. Much like the grand cemetery in Raven Hill — if you didn't stir something up, you'd never guess how many undead were hiding inside.

So Jace didn't want to shout Ursula's name. Not only might it wake up a bunch of things that shouldn't wake up, it could also attract unnecessary trouble to Ursula if she was nearby.

Instead, he swept the area with a Detect Demon spell and caught a glimpse of a demonic silhouette near the monastery.

Jace walked up the main road toward it. A skeleton with most of its flesh rotted away stirred in the muddy ground, lifting its head to glance at him. Jace could feel the temperature drop with the skeleton's movement — residual cold magic from the dead mages buried here.

But Morophil just smiled at the skeleton's necrotic-glowing eyes and made a "shush" gesture. The skeleton wisely lay back down.

"I thought undead weren't affected by charm," Jace said.

"That wasn't Lady Morophil's charm," the succubus said smugly.

Jace pressed his lips together in resignation. Then he saw a figure waving at him from the monastery entrance — Ursula!

"What are you doing here, Jace?" The alchemist approached and lowered her voice. "It's too dangerous here. I was just about to head back."

Jace looked around. "How dangerous? More dangerous than the Burning Legion's headquarters?"

"Don't be a smartass here, little brother." Ursula frowned, then looked at Morophil, who'd followed him. "Good evening, Lady Morophil. I could see your wings from far away."

"Good evening, dear." Morophil smiled. "Thank you for saying that!"

"I heard from Erland you're trying to grow Draenor plants here?" Jace asked. "The ones from near Auchindoun?"

"A kind of ice-blue flower called the Talador Orchid." Ursula said. "But it's not going well. It doesn't grow nearly as well here as the samples from Draenor. Probably because this magic flower prefers pure soul energy, not necrotic corruption — or even shadow."

"But not everything has gone wrong." The alchemist turned. "I have good news. From what I've heard, you defeated a void-wielding dragon in Draenor using a 'Destruction Potion.' The main ingredient of that potion was..."

"Voidflower," Jace said.

"Correct!" The alchemist snapped her fingers excitedly. "I'm working with several alchemists from Dalaran and the returning expedition — we plan to grow Voidflower in the Telogor Rift. A gnome mage from the expedition suggested it. She said the void storms in the Telogor Rift are similar to a place in Draenor called Farahlon. We might be able to grow Voidflower there. Then perhaps we can reproduce that powerful potion in Azeroth. And we have enough resources now to improve the formula — a few seconds at most lets you cast one or two spells, and while the invention of that formula was an incredible achievement under Draenor's harsh conditions..."

Just then, a white skeleton lifted its head from a roadside flower bed, apparently drawn by the conversation. Before it could make a sound, a felhunter burst from the grass and bit its skull clean off.

"Perfect timing, Jace." Ursula didn't even look at it. She beckoned. "Help me carry some things back."
