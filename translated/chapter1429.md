## Chapter 1429: What Society?

The doomguard Galders summoned wasn't as strong as Karrlez or his lieutenant Dasmora, but it was still one of the more powerful doomguards Jace had seen.

From his estimate, it could probably match those flying demons that had served as vanguards against the gryphon riders during the Battle of the Dark Portal.

While Jace had been in Draenor, the Daggerfall warlocks hadn't been idle. They'd kept trying to control stronger demons while improving their casting ability using Jace's notes on shadow mastery.

But despite its formidable power, the doomguard couldn't tear open a portal to the void rift on its own the way Karrlez could. The warlocks still needed to assist with the spell.

Jace and Galders, as the two warlocks in the Daggerfall most skilled at auxiliary teleportation spellcasting, naturally took on that role.

The casting went smoother than Jace had expected. The void rift the demon tore open couldn't allow hundreds or thousands of troops through like the passage in Ilgara's Tower, but it was more than enough for the Daggerfall's handful of members and demons.

Erland tried entering the passage first. A few moments later, he stepped back out, shaking off the surrounding void energy. He lifted his head. "I met Lord Karrlez — and lived to tell the tale. Congratulate me, everyone!"

Sulenna sat to the side, snorting derisively. "Boring."

They left the conjuration room under the doomguard's watch. The atmosphere was light. Jace looked at the group. "Did you attempt similar rituals before?"

"We couldn't locate the Telogor Rift's coordinates before the Kirin Tor mages found us," Sulenna said, sitting down.

"I should say we never even started," Ursula said.

Galders shook his head. "I tried gathering methods for locating Twisting Nether positions from Ironforge and Shadowforge City. But the more I searched, the more I realized that relying only on the scrolls you brought from the north... it was almost impossible."

"I only managed to make the trip thanks to help from a planar race," Jace said.

Or thanks to Aegwynn and Karazhan. But he certainly wasn't going to say that.

Sulenna nodded, taking a sip of red wine. "Exactly. At the time, we saw no hope of entering Telogor at all. So, under the guidance of that demon — Zakrelok, our honest Eredun gentleman next door — we tried looking for other accessible locations in the Twisting Nether."

"We knew it was dangerous, and Magino Dumas and his dogs had their eyes on us. Mathias wouldn't be willing to vouch for us on this matter either." Galders said. "So after a few attempts with no results, we stopped. But at least we gained some experience opening void portals."

Erland sat to the side, reading Jace's expression. "I know you want us to be careful. But compared to other warlocks, the Daggerfall is practically conservative."

He counted on his fingers. "As far as I know, there's a group of warlocks in Stratholme who tried summoning infernal meteors in Darrowshire. I heard they caused casualties and have been hunted all the way to Khaz Modan by the Kirin Tor and Lordaeron's witch hunters. And they're not even the most extreme case. In Booty Bay, I've heard more than once about warlocks trying to smuggle themselves to Kalimdor to start 'new research.' After the Lighthouse incident, the Kingdom of Stormwind isn't exactly a safe place to hide a dark magic sanctuary anymore. I think you know that better than I do, Jace."

"He's telling the truth. Many orc warlocks have gone to Kalimdor." Sulenna looked at Jace. "The remnants of the Shadow Council. And the two-headed ogre Cho'gall's apprentices."

"It's hard to believe, looking at that ogre, that he was Gul'dan's own prized student." Erland rested his head on his hand. "Now he and his Twilight's Hammer clan have probably almost established a new kingdom in southern Kalimdor. At least, according to the goblins of the Steamwheedle Cartel. Some of them even came back here to recruit new clan members — or I should say, 'believers.' So more and more warlocks will cross the Great Sea to join them on the other side. Even if they don't join that clan, they can conduct any magical research they want over there, even death knight magic."

"Even the warlocks in Stratholme know what those orcs are 'researching.'" Ursula said. "Last time, the rock Galders brought back from Ironforge wasn't enough to wake you up?"

"I've always been on the conservative side, Ursula." Erland said. "And besides, now we have the Telogor Rift. We have Jace Sesso. Our conditions are far better than theirs. I just..."

"You're just warning me," Galders said. "I understand what you're saying, Erland."

"Good." Erland checked his pocket watch. "I'm a bit tired from fixing that fence tonight. Jace, will you have free time in the coming days?"

"I'm free until Midsummer Fire Festival," Jace said.

"Then I'll find you when I get back to Stormwind. I have some questions about making soul shards." Erland stood up. "I still need to pick something up in town tonight. Let's call it here, everyone."

"Ursula." Jace looked at the alchemist. "I need to get some potions from you."

"Come upstairs with me, Jace," Ursula said.

They left the basement and went up to the potion shop. Ursula tidied the shelves as she asked, "What do you need?"

Jace considered his upcoming trip to Karazhan with Khadgar. If Khadgar wanted him along to deal with the twisted dark energy in Karazhan, he'd definitely need protection against a lot of shadow corruption. And probably some residual ghosts too.

"Shadow Protection Potion. Use the best ingredients you can. And can you make Ghost Detection Potion now?"

"It's still the troll formula. I can't make it as well as the Gurubashi or Amani witch doctors. You should ask Erland," Ursula said.

"Then I might as well go to Booty Bay myself." Jace leaned against the wall.

Ursula nodded, organizing the potions. Jace thought about what else in Karazhan might be dangerous.

He'd never actually entered Karazhan, never even gotten close to it. And the libraries in Dalaran and Stormwind didn't have much on what happened inside after the explosion. The Violet Eye's materials were limited too. He'd have to wait for Khadgar to explain the details.

So for now, he had to rely on his game experience.

If Khadgar was bringing him to search the Grand Library for the "Curator," the most troublesome enemies they'd encounter before that — aside from endless ghosts and spirits — would probably be... the "Night Beast."

The Night Beast was originally named Arcanagos, a blue dragon and a friend of Aegwynn. He'd come to Karazhan to warn Medivh against being consumed by dark power, but he hadn't realized that the source of all darkness was the soul of Sargeras inside Medivh. Medivh killed him with fire magic, and he was reduced to a burning skeletal dragon lurking in the valley near Karazhan.

In the game, drawing him into Karazhan for battle required certain conditions. Jace didn't know if he and Khadgar would encounter this skeletal dragon during their infiltration. But to be safe, he should prepare.

Even though he could now withstand a black dragon's flame fairly comfortably, that was still Medivh's — or rather, Sargeras's — fire magic. Jace felt very unsure about that.

"Flame Protection Potion would be good too, or something that further boosts fire resistance. And a Flask of Supreme Wisdom, if you have it."

He put on a smile. Ursula turned around and stared at him with a frown.

"I'll pay. I'm not expecting to take another Flask of Supreme Wisdom for free." Jace said. "I know I've already caused you enough trouble."

"What are you planning to do now?" the alchemist asked. "Didn't you say you were free until Midsummer Fire Festival? That's less than two weeks away. You want to kill another black dragon in that time?"

"Confidential." Jace said. "All I can say is it's at the Kirin Tor's request."

Ursula squinted. "Like when you went to Draenor?"

"It's not that far, and not that... I'm just worried about potential danger."

"Fine, fine. But I don't have any in stock. You'll have to wait a few days." Ursula shrugged.

"No problem." Jace smiled.

"There's one more thing, Jace." Ursula hesitated for a moment. "A royal mage of King Terenas came to me earlier. He wants to recruit me for the... Lordaeron Court Apothecary Society."

Jace blinked. "The what now?"

"It's a society of alchemists serving the royal family. I suppose this would be a chance to become something like a 'court noble.'" Ursula let out a sarcastic laugh.

Well, the name was different. If it had been the "Royal Apothecary Society," that would have been no joke.

Though there might be some inheritance relationship between the two, Jace guessed.

"Honestly, I'm not interested in the title." Ursula sighed. "And I'd have to go to the royal city regularly, live within those damned walls. That's the part I can't accept. But at the same time, it's a good opportunity to connect with more master alchemists, access more formulas and rare magical herbs — especially from Alterac and Northrend... So. I want to hear your opinion."

"Does he need an answer right away?" Jace asked.

"No," Ursula said. "I don't like being pressured into decisions. If he sets a deadline or adds extra conditions, I'll refuse immediately."

"Then wait a year or two," Jace said. "You're working with the Kirin Tor on Draenor herbs right now. Your value to them won't decrease — it'll go up. This also lets you see their sincerity. What if they're only after your Draenor herb research?"

"That's true. I've been wondering about that myself." The alchemist raised an eyebrow. "And it gives me time to really think about whether I want to go back. You're right, Jace."

*And time to see what happens with Lordaeron and Korialstrasz in Northrend,* Jace thought. He didn't want to stop Ursula from climbing higher, but he also didn't want to pretend ignorance and let her walk into a risk he could see coming.
