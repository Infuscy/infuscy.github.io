Chapter 143: Unforgivable

"It's back again!"

Shouts rang out from somewhere, thick with sobs, followed by terrified wailing from another street.

Grede pulled Jace up. "Don't just stand there — run! Find somewhere to hide!"

The two of them grabbed the little girl and ducked into a stone house, into a narrow alley behind it that hadn't caught fire yet. A massive shadow roared overhead, and a few helpless purple arcane missiles trailed after it.

A series of explosions sounded in the distance, then more shouting. Soldiers' furious curses and insults carried clearly even from streets away.

"What the hell does this bastard want? Destroy the whole town?! It's too damn bold!" Grede cursed.

"Stay here, kid. Don't go out! Wait for that monster to leave, then find your parents!"

The girl nodded. Jace finished and followed Grede out of the alley. He watched the giant shape bank in midair and fly to the other side of the town hall. The wind from its wings nearly snuffed out the nearby flames, blowing so hard that Jace and Grede could barely keep their feet.

What kind of dragon was this... an adult? A hatchling?

Jace had no frame of reference for what real dragons looked like in Azeroth. Given that gryphons looked more like mythical creatures than their game counterparts, dragons probably weren't that cartoony either.

Going purely off his vague game impressions, the silhouette he'd seen looked like a hatchling.

But how could a hatchling cause this much destruction? If it was a hatchling... shouldn't it be one of a pack of mobs?

He realized he wasn't making the mistake of applying game logic to the real world for the first time. He had to correct that thinking fast and not jump to comparisons.

The direction the dragon had gone was blocked by buildings. He looked around. A man, his whole body on fire, screamed and leaped into the sea. Another man collapsed at the dockside before he could jump. A few figures rushed to help put out the flames, but they couldn't extinguish them no matter what.

The air was thick with a nauseating burnt stench. Jace felt sicker and sicker. He couldn't tell whether the emotional pressure was causing physical pain or if the smell was genuinely that hard to bear.

He had a growing feeling this was shadow power. And a dragon capable of wielding shadow energy...

The town was in chaos. Most of the soldiers had no ranged weapons. Either they could only stand and stare, or they'd already joined the firefighting and rescue efforts.

The bell at the top of the town hall still clanged away. A Lordaeron knight held his reins, steadying his panicked horse at a street corner, nearly getting thrown off several times.

He shouted, "We need mages! We need every fighter who can fight... archers, hunters, anyone! Reinforcements are coming!"

Several mages ran over from down the street. Their once-pristine robes were covered in dust, their faces blackened with soot.

Jace knew they'd have to hunt that dragon and help the town's victims. They couldn't burn through mana on mobility spells like Blink the way they could in a game — they'd have to chase on foot. In a situation like this, their usefulness was pretty limited.

Then a sharp whistle suddenly cut through the sky.

Hearing that whistle, Grede snapped his head up.

"It's them... it's my brothers!"

Before Jace's brain could even process what Grede's "brothers" meant, an extremely fast shadow burst from the distant pine woods and into the sky above the street!

A rather small gryphon, but incredibly fast. Its rider's form was hard to make out, but the lightning in his hand trailed an incredibly long streak through the smoke-filled air...

A gryphon rider!

Grede roared something in Dwarvish. The rider in the sky seemed to hear him and laughed back in similar words. The voice suddenly grew near, then faded as the gryphon sped away — chasing after the dragon's shadow!

Grede was so excited he practically about to explode. He wanted to find a rocket on the spot and launch himself into the sky to join the fight. But Jace felt his gut tighten.

Could a gryphon rider beat that dragon?

Where were his teammates? Why was there only one rider?

Jace looked in every other direction. Not a single other gryphon appeared.

He didn't have time to think about it. Grede had already taken off after the rider, and he had no choice but to follow.

They rounded a few buildings, and a howling gust suddenly swept overhead. Jace looked up to see the dragon pass directly above — immediately followed by the gryphon's silhouette. The two were locked in a chase over Southshore!

The two of them found an open area and watched the dragon climb straight into the sky, its shadow shrinking rapidly. The gryphon was faster, its shadow more nimble, climbing straight after it.

Grede shouted himself hoarse, as if trying to give the gryphon rider some kind of Battle Shout buff. But both shapes were too high up to make out clearly.

A flash of lightning suddenly crackled — no question, the gryphon rider was attacking!

Quite a few Southshore soldiers looked up to cheer him on. "For the Alliance!" and the like echoed everywhere. Some even climbed onto rooftops to bellow.

A dragon's roar exploded from the sky. Grede laughed. "He can't take it! That evil dragon's trying to flee!"

Hearing Grede say that, Jace's heart, which had been hanging by a thread, eased up a little. A moment later the dragon's shadow reappeared — and rapidly grew larger. Its wings suddenly folded. It was clearly diving toward them.

The townsfolk who'd been cheering instantly went silent. Cheers turned to screams in an instant. Some jumped off buildings, some scattered. The two of them had no choice but to duck and run.

As the dragon neared the ground, Jace finally got a look at its appearance... brown-black scales, wing membranes red enough to border on purple. This wasn't a red dragon. This was a black dragon!

Just as everyone braced for the black dragon's fiery breath, they realized its target wasn't the crowd at all!

It pulled up and banked hard as it neared the ground. The gryphon's silhouette suddenly appeared behind it!

Damn it... the dragon was feinting!

Just as Jace's heart was being wrung out, a blinding bolt of lightning flew from the gryphon's direction, spinning after the climbing black dragon and striking its left wing dead-on. A chain of electrical blasts tore through its wing and chest!

But the black dragon screamed in pain and suddenly spewed a massive wave of blood-red flame!

No...

The gryphon rider's diving speed was too fast... He couldn't stop and flew straight into the fire. Then he burst out the other side in a cloud of black smoke, crashing straight into a row of houses and kicking up a cloud of dust and rubble.

The black dragon screeched, trying to fly higher, but could only wobble northward, getting lower and lower.

Its wings were clearly badly injured. It didn't look like it could fly far.

And the gryphon rider?

Grede cursed and ran toward where the rider had gone down. The two of them cut through several alleys and reached the street.

Across the street, the dark-green gryphon lay in the ruins, its neck bent at a horrible angle. Its guts and innards had spilled out, steaming in the heat. Its feathers still burned with dim flames, giving off a foul stench.

That wasn't the smell of gryphon blood or innards.

Grede paced around the gryphon, calling out something in Dwarvish, but got no response.

Jace noticed a house with its roof caved in by something. Black smoke poured from the hole, and a young girl, clearly terrified, ran out crying.

"This way... Grede, this way!"

He charged inside. A burnt smell mixed with a strange, foul odor hit him in the face. The gryphon rider was right there against the wall.

The rider's body was charred black over a large area, and the heavy impact had bent his whole frame. He lay there like a doll some disobedient kid had destroyed, in an unimaginable position.

He wore gear a lot like Grede's, and had a similar braid on his head. But between all the blood and burn marks, his original skin and hair color were impossible to make out.

Looking at him, Jace even had the illusion that he was looking at Grede.

Did the gryphon rider not know a black dragon might turn around and hit them with fire?

Jace remembered what Grede had said — that if a gryphon believed a sacrifice was worth it, it would never flinch.

Would the gryphon rider rather die than let that black dragon get away?

The Hinterlands were to the northeast. If this black dragon was heading that way, it could get there in a day or two.

Just then Grede ran in too. Seeing the dwarf, his expression nearly froze.

His hands wouldn't stop shaking. Jace could even hear his teeth clacking from rage.

"No... no..."

He threw himself over the dwarf rider, reaching for the body, but Jace held him back.

"Don't touch him — that's black dragon fire, Grede!"

"Damn it!" Grede cursed and stood back up. He looked around, couldn't find what he wanted, and charged back out of the house. He finally found what he wanted behind the building.

A war hammer inscribed with dwarven runes, with a faint shimmer of lightning still dancing over it!

Jace watched the hammer, and when Grede picked it up it was as if it had been reignited — a chain of electrical flashes burst into the air around it.

"I'm going to kill this bastard." Grede stared at the hammer in his hands and roared like a madman. "I'm going to kill this bastard! Right now!"

Jace didn't try to talk him out of it this time. He couldn't say a word.

Southshore had become this. The morning they'd left, everything had been so peaceful and calm. In the blink of an eye, all of it was on fire.

All because of that damn dragon that had shown up out of nowhere.

"Wait for me here, Jace."

So saying, Grede turned to leave.

"I'm coming with you!"
