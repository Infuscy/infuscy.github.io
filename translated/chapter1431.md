## Chapter 1431: The Coming of Age Ceremony (Part 1)

Midsummer Fire Festival in Stormwind was always sweltering.

Though this year the red ribbons on the streets had been replaced with Lordaeron's white shield-and-sword banners — taking some of the festive atmosphere away — the heat and oppressive humidity didn't care about any of that. The nobles, soldiers, and especially those in full armor gathered in Cathedral Square all wore furrowed brows and pinched noses. They looked less like they were participating in a grand ceremony and more like they were enduring torture.

Jace was glad the expedition's victory ceremony two months ago hadn't been this hot. And this time, he wasn't the center of attention, subjected to the scrutiny of the entire crowd. That didn't ease the physical torment much, but at least he could freely make the same pained face as everyone else.

Jace and Vereesa shuffled along with a crowd of Stormwind nobles toward the Cathedral. The square was filled with the buzz of conversation, citizens cheering, and soldiers shouting to keep order — a fair bit of chaos.

"When I was little, I loved occasions like this. Whenever the nobles planned some event, my mother would take me to watch, or I'd go with my friends. I always wanted to climb onto something — a pillar, a brick wall, a friend's back — desperate to squeeze my way right up to the king. Now that I'm standing at the very front, practically right in front of the king... I'm not enjoying it at all."

"Same here." Vereesa wrinkled her brow.

Sweat had dampened her long eyebrows, occasionally sticking to her forehead and cheeks when she shook her head. Jace had to keep brushing them down for her, or she'd start looking disheveled.

"I guess we've both grown up," he said.

"I think it's more about this stupid dress." Vereesa complained. "If I had a light shirt and something practical for shoving through these bloated, clumsy people, instead of this ridiculous blue gown, everything would be much more fun."

The nobles nearby glanced over at "bloated and clumsy." Vereesa stared them down without hesitation.

"You could have gone in through the side entrance with your two sisters." Jace pointed at the side door in the distance. "Instead you insisted on coming this way with me."

"You could have gone through that door with King Varian Wrynn too. Why did you choose this way?" Vereesa asked.

"I thought *you* wanted to go this way, that you didn't want to be with those people," Jace said.

Vereesa stared at Jace and licked her lips. "Well, it's too late to regret it now."

Jace couldn't help but laugh. He looked at the steps ahead. "Whatever. We're almost there anyway. By the way — have Queen Ysera or any of the green dragons contacted you in the last couple of days?"

"Haven't you been with me the whole time?" Vereesa said.

"I thought they might appear in your dreams to talk to you, like Sora does," Jace said.

"Is that possible?" Vereesa raised an eyebrow. "I did have a dream yesterday... but I've forgotten most of it now. Did I miss a message from the green dragon queen?"

"Unlikely." Jace said. "She's the Queen of Dreams. She wouldn't let you miss it that easily. Just wait."

"I hope Queen Ysera teaches me a few powerful spells soon." Vereesa said quietly. "I've learned how to summon roots to entangle enemies now. But it's not very useful far away from forests and wilderness. In a place like Auchindoun, it barely worked at all. When we faced Magtheridon's Legion at the end, most of my nature spells were useless. It's frustrating."

"The green earring doesn't help either?" Jace asked.

"The Dream's power is somewhat different from nature magic, I think." The elf explained. "So imagine Blackrock Mountain. I doubt it's any better than Auchindoun's wasteland."

"Does that mean you've decided to come to Shadowforge City with us?" Jace asked. "You'd turn into a Dark Iron dwarf and go find Lokhtos Darkbargainer?"

"I have to go." Vereesa said seriously. "What if they don't believe in the warhammer? That would mean you, Grede, and Sora have to face the entire Dark Iron army alongside those smiths. Yes, you have Morophil... but she lost the power from that demon register, didn't she?"

"With you there, never mind fighting our way out." Jace lowered his voice. "I'd be confident enough to charge into the Dark Iron Throne and take Dagran Thaurissan alive."

"There you go again." Vereesa murmured.

"Needleflame is planning to come too," Jace said. "That's not nonsense."

"Ah. It seems the warlock's charm has won out over the fear of her uncle after all." Vereesa smiled.

"What's it got to do with me?" Jace glanced toward the Wizard Sanctum's tower spire. "She just wants to prove to all of us that she's special."

"She hates me so much that if I *didn't* go, she'd probably be even more eager to go adventuring with Grede and Sora. Yesterday she even made a condition — she wants Morophil to teach her how to fight."

"She cares very much about what you think of her, Jace... I can't tell if you're being modest or if you genuinely don't understand children's psychology." Vereesa said. "She may be a black dragon, but from what I've observed, she's not much more mature than a human or elven child her age."

The elf shot him another sidelong glance. "Ah, I forgot. You're the Sessos' only son. It's perfectly normal for you not to understand children."

As she said it, the playful expression on her face faded noticeably. Jace gave her a careful look and said nothing more.

The three Windrunner sisters had reunited, but their younger brother remained forever in the flames the Horde had lit. That wasn't a hatred easily forgotten.

Prince Arthas Menethil's coming of age ceremony was indeed a grand affair. One important figure after another entered the Cathedral, some Jace had never even seen before.

A tall noble in a red robe, deep in conversation with Terenas II, bore a resemblance to Danath Trollbane — the same lean face and signature hooked nose. That must be Thoras Trollbane, the king of Stromgarde.

A grand noble in a black suit and orange-red cloak entered the Cathedral accompanied by a retinue, exchanging greetings with the two kings as equals. From his attendants' clothing style, Jace was almost certain — that was Genn Greymane, king of Gilneas.

Jace had been surprised earlier to see Gilneas's black flag at the harbor. He hadn't expected King Genn to actually make it to the Cathedral of Light.

There were more leaders than just these three. Daelin Proudmoore, who was even taller than Trollbane, had thick, wavy dark-brown hair. His hard, coarse features made him look more like an old sea captain than a noble.

Standing beside him was Jaina — in every aspect except facial features, she seemed the complete opposite of her father. Especially in height and build; the comparison made her look even more small and pitiable.

Besides the royalty, the founders of the Order of the Silver Hand were also present. Several paladins in silver heavy armor carrying warhammers stood together — Turalyon, and Saidan Dathrohan of Stratholme among them.

Jace didn't recognize the others, but he knew Arthas's teacher, Uther the Lightbringer, must be among them, along with Tirion Fordring, the lord of Hearthglen.

Seeming to notice Jace's gaze, Turalyon raised a hand and smiled. The other paladins, catching Turalyon's look, glanced over curiously.

Jace had to admit, the combined gaze of so many powerful paladins was quite oppressive. He could even feel the sunlight streaming through the stained-glass windows shifting slightly toward him along with those stares — and that was definitely not an illusion.

Standing across from them, Jaina seemed to have noticed Jace's presence through the paladins' attention. She whispered something to her father. Daelin looked up, spotted Jace, and managed a nod across the intervening priests and guards. Jace and Vereesa nodded back respectfully.

The Admiral seemed friendly enough. Apparently he hadn't held a grudge against Jace for dragging his precious daughter onto the warlock boat.

Varian Wrynn, as the host, was also wearing an unusually formal outfit today. A lion-emblem cloak, a blue tunic with trim, a gold crown set with violet-blue gems... a far cry from his usual attire.

Even so, the twenty-something king still looked a bit awkward among the older monarchs. And it seemed the other leaders still treated him like a child who needed looking after.

In comparison, King Terenas, standing in the middle, looked more like the host. But that was understandable — the Alliance was still, literally, the "Alliance of Lordaeron."

Sylvanas Windrunner, as Silvermoon's representative, stood among the kings. But she kept her gaze fixed on Vereesa and Jace, as if completely uninterested in the other humans coming and going around the Cathedral.

"I heard King Terenas originally wanted Prince Kael'thas to attend this event." Vereesa said quietly. "But something went wrong on Kael'thas's end, so he couldn't make it."

She sighed and added, "There are rumors going around Dalaran about him, Jaina Proudmoore, and the Lordaeron prince. Kael'thas was probably trying to avoid all that. But if you ask me, his absence only fuels the gossip more."

*Maybe it's not just gossip,* Jace thought.

Of course, he didn't dare admit what he was thinking to a Windrunner.

Just as the two of them were quietly discussing the various nobles and kings present, Archbishop Alonsus Faol called for attention, and the Cathedral quickly fell silent.

The priests began to sing hymns. Everyone turned in unison toward the main entrance.

Everything was about to begin. The protagonist of this ceremony was about to make his entrance.
