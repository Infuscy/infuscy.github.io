## Chapter 1432: Coming of Age (Part 2)

Amidst the ethereal, crystalline hymns, the sound of metal boots striking the floor echoed from the corridor beyond the main doors.

Everyone in the cathedral stopped talking and discussing. Through the thin haze of incense, they turned toward the source of the sound. The cheers from outside seeped in faintly.

Then a golden-haired young man in silver armor, carrying a warhammer, appeared in everyone's view.

He was tall — almost comparable to Jace as he was now — and stood out sharply among the nobles packed into the room. Not just tall, either; his build was equally imposing. In this sweltering heat, he wore a full suit of heavy plate armor, a long blue cloak, and carried a weighty golden warhammer engraved with the royal crest of Lordaeron. He walked without any sign of strain.

One way or another, he'd definitely prepared a long time for this day.

Arthas had really grown up. Last time Jace had seen him at the Lordaeron royal palace was three years ago, and the transformation from boy to man in those three years was remarkable. Back then, Jace had struggled to connect that slightly immature, proud child with the impression he'd had from his past life. But this time, it was as if the person before him had stepped straight out of a painting.

Lordaeron's prince kept his lips pressed tight, swept his gaze over everyone in the cathedral, then looked up at the Holy Light sigil at the high altar. His gaze was steady and full of confidence.

Then, guided by several priests, he stepped forward, one pace at a time, toward Archbishop Alonsus Faol.

King Terenas wore an undisguised smile, clearly pleased with his son's bearing.

Daelin Proudmoore smiled too, while keeping an eye on Terenas's expression.

Beside him, Jaina's expression was far more complicated. She watched the Lordaeron prince pass with eyes full of hope, mixed with something between nervousness and worry.

Jace didn't want to probe the shadow magic radiating from the emotions inside the cathedral. Not that the incense and holy radiance that dispelled darkness here gave him much chance to do so anyway. But just watching Jaina's face was enough to read those emotions — he didn't need shadow energy for that.

When Arthas reached King Varian, he smiled and nodded. Varian smiled back like an old friend and even murmured his name as encouragement.

When Stormwind fell, Varian had fled to Lordaeron and lived alongside Arthas for a while. The two had gotten along quite well back then.

In contrast, King Genn and King Thoras showed no particular emotion. They just watched, nodding slightly when their eyes met.

King Terenas was aging. In both spirit and body, it would be difficult for him to rule much longer.

Everyone believed it wouldn't be long before Arthas gradually stepped onto center stage, began ruling in practice, and then took his father's place.

And when he did, he wouldn't just be inheriting the crown of Lordaeron — he'd be inheriting the leadership of the entire Alliance of Lordaeron.

Now that the Orc Wars were over, and even the Expedition had returned, what reason was there for the Alliance of Lordaeron to continue existing? It was a question every noble kept turning over in their minds but found difficult to voice directly.

Archmage Antonidas, the ruler of Dalaran, hadn't come. The Kirin Tor's envoy was Archmage Drenden, a member of the Council of Six.

From the moment he'd entered the cathedral, he'd kept a low profile — just brief greetings with the various kings, with no deeper conversation. Jace only noticed his presence when Arthas walked past him.

Jace didn't know this archmage personally. He only knew of his arrival from what he'd heard, identifying him by the robes and the mantle that marked his rank.

At this sensitive moment, Dalaran seemed reluctant to engage too enthusiastically. Antonidas had been in poor health for a while, conveniently providing an excuse no one could argue with.

Standing among the elven mages and Farstriders, Sylvanas Windrunner watched Arthas ascend the steps with a blank expression. Though she kept her eyes on him, she wasn't really paying the prince much attention. She was probably still nursing her displeasure at having to substitute for Prince Kael'thas at this ceremony.

But when Jace saw these two together, he had a perception that no one else in the room shared.

In another timeline, a few years from now, Death Knight Arthas would lead the undead Scourge to breach Quel'Thalas's defenses. With Frostmourne in hand, he would kill Sylvanas and forcibly raise her as a banshee in service to the Scourge — beginning the Dark Lady's bitter hatred of the undead legion.

But here and now, they were like two parallel lines with no connection. Neither cared about the other. It gave Jace, as an observer, a strange sense of temporal displacement.

Arthas reached Archbishop Faol. He swept back his cloak, knelt before the archbishop and the altar of the Holy Light, and lowered his head in salute, his warhammer resting against the white stone floor.

"Light above, your faithful gather today in this sanctuary to witness a new brother receive your blessing. Bathed in the Holy Light, he shall be renewed."

The archbishop held his crozier and recited the prayer. Jace noticed Arthas's shoulders trembling slightly — whether from intense nervousness or the exhaustion of the long walk in, he couldn't tell.

The prayer ended. The archbishop, the clergy of the church, and the knights of the Silver Hand each presented their gifts — some were holy texts, others were mantles and pendants.

A fair-haired, older paladin, after presenting a shoulder ornament engraved with the Holy Light's sigil, leaned down and embraced the prince. From this distance, Jace could barely make out Faol's prayer, but he could see the glistening tears of emotion in the older man's eyes.

Jace could be almost certain now — this was probably Arthas's mentor, Uther.

Finally, after receiving a sword gifted by Turalyon, the prince rose to his feet. Only now did Jace notice a hint of strain in the young man's posture, though he'd done his best to appear perfect throughout.

"Arthas Menethil, do you swear to uphold the honor and code of the Knights of the Silver Hand?" Archbishop Faol's voice suddenly swelled to a booming resonance. Even Arthas seemed to startle slightly.

The paladins who'd just been looking on with fond expressions now turned serious as well. Arthas lowered his head. "I swear."

The paladin oath was long and solemn. Throughout the entire process, Arthas remained under the gaze of the highest-ranking figures in the Alliance. By this point, Jace almost felt sorry for the guy.

Knowing what he would eventually have to bear, Arthas had to be perfect in everything — starting from this moment, and for a long time beforehand — becoming the flawless Golden Prince in the eyes of every ruler, noble, and commoner across the Alliance of Lordaeron.

Putting himself in that position? Just thinking about it was suffocating.

After the oath, Archbishop Alonsus Faol murmured something to those nearby, then shuffled a few steps aside. The paladins and priests beside the Holy Light altar raised their right hands together, reciting prayers to call upon the Light.

Arthas puffed out his chest and took a few deep breaths. Jace noticed a faint golden glow beginning to emanate from the hands of the Light's faithful.

The cathedral echoed with a sound like delicate wind chimes — the sound of the Holy Light, Jace thought, vaguely familiar. But at the same time, breathing grew difficult for him. The burning sensation returned. His eyes and skin felt hot when he looked toward the altar, forcing him to turn slightly to the side.

Fortunately, everyone was focused on Prince Arthas. No one paid attention to his reaction. Vereesa seemed to sense something was wrong and gripped his hand tightly. Whether it was placebo or not, Jace felt it ease somewhat — bearable, at least.

Before long, a few people in the cathedral began murmuring among themselves. Even expressions among the Silver Hand knights shifted.

It went on for some time. Finally, a faint golden light began to reflect off Arthas's armor and hair.

Only then did everyone burst into applause, shouting "Light preserve you." Terenas hurried over, embracing his son tightly amid the blessings of the archbishop and clergy.

Arthas smiled and said something to his father, then glanced back at Jaina. Jaina was clapping for him with a happy smile. And with that, it seemed, everything had finally come to a perfect conclusion.

But Jace had the vague sense that quite a few people — including Turalyon and Varian — had been glancing in his direction.

Just occasional glances, and Jace wasn't even sure they were really looking at him. But when enough people do it, the feeling becomes unmistakable, hard to dismiss as imagination.

"How are you feeling, Jace?" Vereesa tugged at his hand and whispered. "We can leave early if you want. The ceremony's over."

"I'm fine." Jace straightened his collar. "Was I... being too obvious?"

"No." Vereesa wiped his forehead. "Your hands were just a little shaky, your face was a little red, you were sweating a bit — like you were getting heatstroke. Oh, do you want to come back to the embassy with me and have something to drink? Honey water, maybe?"

"Sure." Jace said. "I just felt like a lot of people were watching me. I thought I'd done something wrong."

Vereesa glanced at the Lordaeron prince, who was now surrounded by a crowd, and said quietly, "I think it's because a lot of the people here attended the Expedition's victory ceremony, Jace."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

The elf moved her lips, looking at Jace as if she was thinking before answering. "Ah, of course you wouldn't know what that was like, because you were right in the middle of it. A lot of people here — including me — witnessed it when you received Archbishop Faol's blessing. The way the Holy Light fell on you... to put it bluntly, it was like it was trying to burn you alive."

"In comparison... Prince Arthas clearly didn't immediately receive the kind of favor he was hoping for — not like other Silver Hand knights when they receive their blessing. But he got it in the end. He completed the ceremony. Thank goodness. I was hoping the ceremony would end quickly, but I definitely wasn't hoping that young man would embarrass himself in front of so many people."

"Yeah." Jace let out a deep breath. "Me neither."
