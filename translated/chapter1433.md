## Chapter 1433: The Banquet

Stormwind Keep's already cramped banquet hall felt even more crowded now. Banners from various kingdoms hung on the gray stone walls — Lordaeron's white shield flag, Stromgarde's red fist flag, Kul Tiras's green anchor flag. Nobles in every color of fine attire packed the room, like an overgrown flower bed on the verge of wilting, turning the hall into an elaborate fashion exhibition.

Compared to that, the musicians and singers Varian had hired sounded almost shabby.

Jace stood in a corner of the hall, watching the King of Stormwind from his high seat. Even from this distance, he could feel the barely concealed tension radiating from him. Despite having been preparing for today's ceremony and banquet ever since the Expedition's victory parade, Varian clearly hadn't done enough mental preparation. The young king had gradually figured out how to govern this shattered nation over the past few years, but in matters of banquets, ceremonies, and formal occasions — he was still clearly out of his depth.

Servants filed in with platters of every kind of dish and pastry. Some still carried a faint metallic tang of arcane energy on their surfaces.

Not because they'd been made with magic, but because some ingredients and desserts that needed to stay fresh had been teleported from the north using arcane magic.

At the head table, King Terenas, dressed in a splendid light golden robe, was grinning from ear to ear at an enormous emperor salmon so large it took four men to carry the platter. More than a few southern nobles followed the fish with their eyes as it slowly moved between the long tables, hoping it would be placed closer to them.

Every table already had plates of smoked seal meat. The stew was made with seal milk too — all northern specialties the Lordaeronians had brought with them. But these cuts of meat and seasonings clearly weren't as good at showcasing Lordaeron's national power as that giant salmon.

Braziers burned. The rich smell of roasted meat and spices filled the hall. But Jace could also smell a thick layer of sweat.

The Holy Light released by the paladins during the ceremony had left him feeling off even now. Overall, he wasn't feeling great, so he'd only eaten a few pieces of fruit since arriving.

When someone came up to him with a glass, wanting to make his acquaintance, he'd drink a little wine to go along.

At the tables behind him, people were discussing the Expedition's battles on Draenor, how the mages had used portals to rescue the Sons of Lothar from besieging demons at the last moment, the roaming orcs in Tirisfal and Hillsbrad, and whether the kingdom of Alterac should be handed to a bunch of shameless traitors' descendants or to a pretty woman driven mad by a black dragon.

At other tables, people traded more gossip — from the princess of Kul Tiras and the prince of Lordaeron, to Kael'thas's meaningful praise of Jaina at several arcane conferences, to Quel'Thalas's rangers and the leaders of the Silver Hand, and even the youngest Windrunner and a warlock who'd used dark magic to secure the position of Stormwind Court Mage.

Of course, few were willing to go very deep into that last topic — both because of the sensitivity of dark magic and because everyone wanted to avoid drawing too strong a connection between this story and Medivh.

What everyone was really interested in was who would be the next hero to marry an elven noble. The most discussed candidate was, naturally, the remaining Sylvanas Windrunner.

It was clear that by now, the rumors about Sylvanas and Ranger Lord Nathanos Marris had spread across the entire Alliance of Lordaeron.

If a king's banquet were really as Grede described — like some kind of complex magical ritual — that would be one thing. But this ritual wasn't just complex; it was filled with every kind of noise, forcing Jace to deliberately tune out all these chaotic topics and glance over toward the elves.

Sylvanas and Vereesa were chatting and laughing over there. Sensing Jace's gaze, Vereesa shot him a shy smile and subtly waved from her waist.

There was no way Sylvanas missed that little gesture. She also glanced over at Jace with an amused look, deliberately putting on a displeased expression of "who's the bastard trying to steal my little sister," while barely suppressing a smile.

"Jace." Turalyon came over with a wine glass. "Feeling any better?"

"I thought it wasn't that obvious," Jace said with a smile.

"Maybe others couldn't tell, but to me... well." Turalyon took a smug sip of wine. "But don't take it the wrong way. I developed that habit back in Draenor, to keep demons or black dragons from sneaking into our fort. Most weak shapeshifters can't endure the Holy Light's radiance."

"And Sabellian?" Jace asked.

"He was too powerful." Turalyon said. "We never expected Deathwing to send one of his own sons to infiltrate us."

"Guess I'm not powerful enough yet." Jace chuckled. "Honestly, I wasn't trying to hide it from you. I just didn't want to interfere with Prince Arthas's ceremony. I'm honored that Archbishop Faol was willing to let a warlock like me into the cathedral for such an important Holy Light ceremony."

"Of course you were invited." Turalyon said. "Archbishop Faol and I were more worried you wouldn't want to come — especially considering what happened at the square during the last ceremony. He could tell how hard that was on you."

"It wasn't much better this time, even though the Holy Light wasn't shining directly on me," Jace said dryly.

Turalyon chuckled along with him, but the easy expression on his face was quickly replaced by something more complicated and serious.

"What's wrong?" Jace asked.

"Prince Arthas's ceremony... it's hard to say it went normally." Turalyon's voice was so low that even Jace, standing right next to him, could barely make it out.

"Even though the other members of the Silver Hand — Uther, Gavinrad — all told me Prince Arthas performed well, and Archbishop Faol believes the Light gave the prince a warm welcome... I don't know how to put it. What do you think?"

"What do I think?" Jace blinked. "Are you asking me about the Holy Light, paladin?"

"Just give me your opinion as a friend." Turalyon smiled.

"Alright." Jace nodded. "Here's what I think... Arthas is a prince. He's going to rule an entire kingdom one day, maybe even the whole Alliance of Lordaeron. There's no way his mind can be as pure and devoted as someone who's served the Light their whole life. So I don't think it's a big deal."

"But he's Uther's most valued and trusted student, Jace." Turalyon said. "Besides, it's not only people who've served the Light their whole lives who receive its favor. You're the best example."

"You might see Archbishop Faol's blessing as some kind of punishment, but every paladin could feel it — the Light affirmed the archbishop's blessing. The Light accepted his blessing, Jace. It doesn't care whether you're a dark mage, whether you study demonic magic, or whether you genuinely respect the Light. If anything, after that ceremony with the Sons of Lothar... the contrast is even stronger. Especially for someone like me, who attended both."

Jace was momentarily speechless. The paladin thought for a moment and added, "But you make a fair point. Maybe I'm taking this too seriously."

"So what does Arthas himself think?" Jace asked.

"He's like he was as a child — a young man who doesn't like expressing his thoughts to outsiders." Turalyon said. "If he were willing to share his confusion and doubts, everything would be much easier. Maybe the Light has different expectations and plans for him. Or maybe this is its own kind of test."

"He's joined the Silver Hand now." Jace took a sip of wine. "Then his path forward isn't just his teacher Lord Uther's responsibility — it's yours too, as one of his predecessors."

"I know," the paladin said.
