Chapter 1435: After the Banquet

After Prince Arthas Menethil's coming-of-age ceremony, Jace could finally relax a bit. From now until Khadgar finished his business up north and came to find him in Stormwind, or until the Thorium Brotherhood finished forging the legendary Sulfuron Hammer... he really didn't have anything urgent to do. A few days after the Midsummer Fire Festival passed, the Kirin Tor left Stonewatch, leaving only two mages behind to catalog the magical traces the Kirin Tor had left in the Redridge Mountains during their stay. But they were stationed in Lakeshire, so it had nothing to do with Stonewatch. Jace closed the energy-draining Telogrus Rift portal. After all, the portal had been built for an army to pass through. Maintaining it had drawn on nearly the entire Ley-Node of Ilgara's Tower and the energy of Talar'thien crystals, and Kallrezz had been needed for constant maintenance. If he kept it running without shutting it down, it would be a massive drain on both the mage tower and the demonic energies. The Daggers had already established their own Telogrus Rift passage, so they didn't need to go through his anymore. From now on, if Jace needed to enter that Void shard, he just had to use a communication array to contact Kallrezz and have him open a small rift portal to Ilgara's Tower. After the rift passage was closed, Ilgara's Tower and the surrounding forest quickly returned to their original appearance. The Kirin Tor mages had cleaned the place thoroughly before leaving — even the traces left by the gnolls had disappeared. Jace felt the place looked even more natural than it had before he'd left for Draenor. "Again, little princess." Morofel folded her wings behind her back and looked at the panting Needlefang. She crooked a finger. "Don't tell me you can't even beat Jace Sesso's pet." "Raaaah!" Needlefang lunged at the demon, raising her blade — a weapon that looked like black scales — with fury. Jace had no doubt the little whelp's strength and speed already rivaled an elite orc warrior of the Shadow Council. But Morofel dodged her attack with ease, caught her by the wrist, and wrestled with her for a moment, laughing. Jace could clearly see that with her wings restored, Morofel's movements had become faster, smoother, more natural. He remembered seeing her fight before, when the broken bones at her back would twitch involuntarily. For succubi, being able to use their wings for combat mobility was second nature. Even though Morofel had lost her wings for who knows how many years... she'd still kept that original instinct. And now, she was finally whole again. More than teaching Needlefang, it seemed like Morofel was enjoying the process of dancing around, savoring how her wings helped her evade every attack and easily provoke her opponent. Not that she couldn't have done it without wings. But doing it with wings was a completely different feeling. "Stab her wings, little black dragon!" Sazeno hopped around, shouting. "Don't let her show off!" Hearing the imp's suggestion, Needlefang immediately slashed viciously at the demon's wings. But Morofel just flicked her wings and sent Needlefang's attack veering wildly off course. Before the little black dragon could reorient herself, the demon had already shifted behind her in a blur of wing-generated illusions. "Think about why Brutallus tore off my wings before he beat me, Sazeno." Morofel locked Needlefang firmly in her arms, holding her still as she looked at the imp. "Countless demons I've fought have tried attacking my wings first. Only one succeeded." "Ha." The imp chuckled. "Just throwing out a suggestion, Lady Morofel." It immediately scurried behind the rock Jace was sitting on. Jace wasn't just sitting there idly watching them spar. The day after the Lordaeron royal party left Stormwind, Jace had gone to Ursula's alchemy lab to ask about the potion brewing. Ursula hadn't finished the Elixir of Supreme Wisdom yet, but the warlocks hadn't been idle either. They'd taken turns drinking Shadow Protection Potions and expensive Multi-Resistance Elixirs, and with the help of their demon servants, they'd spent two or three weeks forcing out a transcribed copy of Gul'dan's manuscript. Then they'd returned the original to Jace. That way, the warlocks could read the manuscript on their own time, when they weren't meeting together. The demonic language Gul'dan had used to write the manuscript wasn't the common language demons used to talk to each other — it was closer to the language of incantations. That was one of the reasons Jace hadn't been able to properly read it back in Auchindoun. Now, with Sazeno's help, he had enough time to slowly work through it. But the first spell recorded in the manuscript — the Soul Well — was a powerful spell Gul'dan had wanted his student Teron Gorefiend and his men to learn quickly so they could drain Auchindoun's soul energy. Even Teron Gorefiend himself hadn't been able to learn it in all that time. Jace was prepared for a long haul. That said, Teron's shadow talent wasn't the most exceptional in the Shadow Council. Jace figured he probably wouldn't learn it slower than him. But he was missing another condition — a test subject. If the Atal'ai were still occupying the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, he wouldn't mind sneaking over there to test out this powerful and wicked forbidden spell. But the temple was now occupied by red and green dragons. Nowhere else was really suitable. Galders had suggested trying the spell somewhere in Duskwood where undead and mad ghosts ran rampant, but he also felt the risk was too great. Better to understand the rest of Gul'dan's manuscript first before deciding whether to learn this one. Jace agreed. Though Jace's resistance to shadow energy was probably already comparable to an ordinary black dragon's, prolonged reading of the original still took its toll. When he got tired of reading, he'd set the booklet aside and watch Morofel and Needlefang spar for a bit to rest his eyes. Those two — one a demon, one a black dragon — really had endless stamina. They went back and forth, one refusing to lose, the other enjoying the game. They'd been fighting since after lunch until the sky was getting dark, with no thought of resting. Leaning back against the rock, Jace relaxed and let his mind drift back to that night at the banquet — the little friction between Jaina and Arthas. Jaina's emotions seemed simpler: some irritation, mixed with a hint of disappointment that wasn't too obvious. For all her brilliance as a young mage, she'd never cared about controlling her emotions. And the banquet hall wasn't the cathedral, so for Jace, reading her emotions wasn't difficult. When he'd first seen her, Jace had honestly thought Arthas was trying to take advantage of the celebratory mood of his coming-of-age and the Fire Festival to get somewhere with her, and it had just ended in some kind of awkwardness. But when Jaina left and Vereesa pointed out Arthas's fleeting shadow, he started to feel something was off. Arthas's inner doubt and anxiety had been pretty obvious — definitely not the kind of frustration or resentment you'd expect from someone who'd tried something and failed. The situation wasn't as simple as Jace had assumed. The problem was, Jace couldn't pin down what exactly Arthas was doubting or hesitating about. Could it really be, like Vereesa had suggested, that he thought the Holy Light favored Jace over him, and that left him feeling unbalanced? But from the end of the ceremony until the banquet began, Arthas hadn't shown any strong emotion when looking at Jace — neither jealousy nor hostility were notable. At most, there'd been some wariness, about the same as most Lordaeronian nobles and Church members. Just the instinctive caution toward a warlock who commanded demons. Nothing worth noting. So Jace hadn't thought much of it during the banquet itself. He hadn't expected that little incident to happen after all. If he didn't know what happened in the other timeline, he might not have given it another thought. But since he did know, he couldn't just pretend nothing happened. He wanted to find a chance to look into what was really going on. Like, find someone up north who was close to both of them, someone trustworthy and reliable? Maybe Amy Marin could help. She was technically Jaina's teacher, and the two of them had a pretty good relationship. But this meant secretly prying into secrets between a princess and a prince... Did he really want to do that? At the thought, Jace couldn't help rubbing his eyes in embarrassment.
