## Chapter 1439: It Really Is a Soul Well, But the Gul'dan Version

Even though entering the Emerald Dream was a cross-dimensional journey, the Dream Gate in the Dawnwood was only separated from Goldshire by the Naveltari River... So Jace had a hard time treating this as a long, distant farewell. It wasn't even as far as when she'd gone back to Quel'Thalas on missions.

And Vereesa's eager anticipation for her future self, as she set out on her journey, also diluted any last shred of sadness.

Jace knew that back on Draenor, even though Vereesa was a Ranger Lord — and the youngest legitimate Ranger Lord apart from the exceptionally promoted Nathanos Marris — her influence among the Expedition forces was nowhere near that of Alleria, who outranked her. That had hit her hard. Aside from Jace, she had no one to talk to about it.

Of course, she fully understood that it was a matter of a century's worth of seniority and experience, and also the identity of being a "Son of Lothar." So she wasn't jealous of her sister.

But in the short term, Vereesa couldn't close that gap. So she'd chosen the fastest, most direct method — training, pushing her own power higher. Like target shooting in the rain, like dueling Morofel in the forest. It was this particular Ranger of Quel'Thalas's habitual way of dealing with stress.

Grede and his kin were probably already planning their attack on the Dark Iron dig site in the Searing Gorge. Vereesa had set out for the Emerald Dream. Now Jace needed to prepare for Karazhan.

According to what Khadgar had said earlier, once the Expedition's return north was mostly wrapped up, he'd come back to Stormwind. That should be in the next few days.

Jace went to the Slaughtered Lamb to check on the potions Ursula had prepared for him. Though the Daggers had set up the Doomguard and Telogrus Rift portals in Darkshire to avoid causing trouble for the Wizard Sanctum and SI:7, the Slaughtered Lamb was still their real headquarters. Alchemy lab, library, everyone's living quarters — it had everything. So for now, they couldn't leave this place.

Down in the basement, Galders and Erland were eating in the main hall. When they saw Jace come in, Galders immediately looked up. "Jace, have you tried any of the soul magic from the manuscript yet?"

"Of course not." Jace sat down on the other side and rubbed his brow. "I've been going crazy the past couple days reading all that praise for the Deceiver Kil'jaeden and the lectures on instructing orcs. I think I'd have an easier time distinguishing the spells from the nonsense if I used your transcribed copy."

"That is a problem." Galders said. "But who's to say those so-called praises and instructions don't affect the spellcasting?"

"Shadow doesn't follow rules like arcane does, as you know well, Jace. Anything can affect the final result... So the best approach is for all of us to slowly study the magic in there together. I mean, using a combination of the original and the copy."

"You're right." Jace nodded, then looked at the two of them. "So if this whole manual is about soul magic, have you found a place to test it yet? You mentioned trying Duskwood before, Galders. But there are still residents around Raven Hill Cemetery and the Tranquil Gardens Cemetery. Maybe we should find some abandoned graveyards where ghosts still roam."

He was about to suggest using the wandering ghosts driven mad by dark energy in the Deadwind Pass as test subjects. But remembering he was about to go to Karazhan, he didn't want Khadgar to suddenly sense some extremely evil, apocalyptic ritual happening in Deadwind Pass while they were walking through the tower... so he shut his mouth for now.

"We don't have to limit ourselves to undead, Jace." Galders traced a rough outline of the Westfall coastline on the table with his finger. "Don't forget — even though the orcs and trolls have mostly left Stormwind's borders, the territory is still full of rampaging monsters burning and looting. If the kobold-occupied mines are too close to Goldshire, then there are the murlocs and gnolls on the Gold Coast... And I've heard rumors of naga appearing there too in recent years."

Erland rested his head on his arm. "During the orc invasion, those cowardly bastards didn't dare show their faces against the orcs who'd crushed their tribes. They only dared to take it out on refugees like us who were hiding. Now that the little king is worried about the Blackrock orcs attacking from the south and can't muster the strength to clean out those bastards, it's up to us — the ones who can't show our faces — to teach those half-human half-monster bastards that times have changed."

Jace smiled. "That's a good idea. But we need to watch out for naga and murloc magic."

"I'm less worried about naga and murloc magic than I am about our own magic." Galders sighed softly. "There's still so much we don't understand about the Soul Well. If we don't actually try it, we'll never figure it out."

"So you still want to start with the most difficult, most complex spell." Jace looked up at Galain Arrow, who had come over after hearing the conversation in the hall. "I saw there are some simpler soul-related spells later in the manuscript. Have you considered working from simple to complex?"

Galders swallowed and glanced at Galain. "Good timing, Galain. Tell Jace what we were thinking."

Galain nodded. "While transcribing the manuscript, I gradually developed a theory, Jace. This manuscript may be entirely centered around one spell — the Soul Well. Yes, there are other spells later — methods for using souls, extracting souls, even some records about soul shards — but they're all based on the Soul Well. Gul'dan planned to use this spell to transform that draenei necropolis into something under his control — an energy source he could draw from freely."

"In simple terms, the Soul Well is a method of harvesting and concentrating souls. The other spells are means of manipulating the Soul Well."

Yes, that was right. Galain's description was getting closer and closer to the Soul Well that the final boss Gul'dan wielded in the Nighthold dungeon from the game.

When Gul'dan summoned that Soul Well, players had to do everything they could to stop it from draining souls, even sacrificing their own souls and bodies... Otherwise, the power it accumulated would be unstoppable.

Before he'd fully understood Gul'dan's manuscript, Jace had only seen the Soul Well as the first spell recorded in it. Combined with its description of converting souls to replenish power, it was easy to mistake it for the warlock's Soul Ritual in the game — basically a healthstone dispenser for the raid.

But Gul'dan's Soul Well was something completely different. It was a dark vortex of maddening souls, a terrible magical array powerful enough to shake all of Suramar and devour countless mighty heroes and demon lords.

If he could successfully summon one at Blackrock Spire... Jace could almost picture the scene: countless black dragons and orcs wailing and fleeing, helplessly having their souls drained, turning into rotting empty husks, as those souls converged into the Soul Well and were devoured by him and Usselus.

As the image unconsciously painted itself in his mind, the staff at his side let out a low hum — something it rarely did these past few months.

It was awake. And it was ravenously hungry, carrying a trace of resentment... Jace knew what it was thinking, what it wanted to say.

If only he'd been ruthless enough in Auchindoun to devour more of those powerful draenei souls, or taken Brutallus's soulstone, ensuring a clean blow to break the Scepter of Sargeras instead of leaving a sliver of life for the Light to salvage...

Then he wouldn't have been forced into a falling out with Sabellian. He wouldn't have fled in such disgrace, nearly dying at the Throne of Kil'jaeden.

You got lucky this time, Sesso. But the Master of the Burning Legion won't let you off. Will you be so lucky next time? Do you think you can escape the Deceiver twice?

Sooner or later, you'll have to face all this again. And without me... there's no way you'll win. You know that better than anyone.

But you treat me like an obstacle. A burden. How disappointing.

"Jace?" Galain asked cautiously. "Do you think my theory is wrong?"

"No, I think your theory is very likely correct." Jace said. "I need to go out for a few days. I won't be long. After that, we'll study Gul'dan's manuscript together and see what progress we can make."

"It's settled, then, Jace." Galders downed his glass of eggnog in one gulp. "We'll step up our reading in the meantime, getting ready for the real attempt."
