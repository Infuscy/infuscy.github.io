Chapter 144: The Scorching Road

A roar came from the north of town, like the distant rumbling of a train — not a sound any normal animal could make.

As the roar gradually lowered, it slowly turned shrill, ending in an off-key shriek that dragged the attention of everyone on the docks and the shore to the north.

"Don't come back..."

The crying was almost more than Jace could bear. He barely kept up with Grede, who was sprinting full-tilt, trying to blot out the horrifying scenes in Southshore and make straight for the woods outside town.

Two weeks ago — no, two hours ago — he would have thought this decision was insane.

But now, an unstoppable force was driving him toward that dragon. The wounded, the burned buildings... the gryphon, the dwarf rider. All of it had to be answered for.

And, more selfishly, he wanted to be part of a fight like this. Even if he only "participated" a little, it wouldn't make this transmigration trip a waste.

Outside town, crowds of civilians were fleeing toward the docks to the south. The summoned soldiers pushed against the tide toward the woods. Some were militia. Some were even carrying farming pitchforks.

But in this situation, a long pitchfork was probably a lot more useful than a sword or blade. Jace mixed in with them, following the direction of the growls. Most of them were covered in black soot like him, some even tear-streaked, but not one of them hesitated. They all charged toward the woods like madmen.

Once they entered the woods, waves of heat rolled over them. They hadn't even seen the dragon's shadow yet and already felt its power.

Jace really couldn't imagine a single hatchling possessing this kind of terrifying force.

Sparks danced between the trees. Quite a few leaves had caught fire. Some soldiers dropped into low-lying areas to dodge the waves of heat, then scrambled up and charged forward again once the blast passed.

He could already see archers taking cover behind trees up ahead. Military orders rang out through the crackle of flames — a good number of Lordaeron soldiers had already pursued this far.

Jace felt uncontrollable tension. He was worried he'd get blown away by a blast of fire before he even caught sight of the dragon.

He looked up. Grede had already charged far ahead.

If he still had any fear, its only source was the worry that the dragon would die before he got there — that he wouldn't get to smash it dead with his own hammer.

Pushing forward through burning debris and dead leaves, Jace saw a middle-aged soldier in chainmail lying in a dirt pit, catching his breath, wiping the sweat from his forehead along with the soot. He wasn't militia — he was a port guard, covered in dust and ash, as if he'd just helped fight the fires in town before answering the call to hunt the fallen black dragon.

Seeing him, Jace thought of his father. He remembered every time his father had come home to visit him and his mother when he was little — he'd looked just like this.

If a black dragon ever made it to Brill, or even the outskirts of the capital... at that point, his father would probably grab a weapon and charge at the beast like this man, age be damned.

Jace glanced ahead at the silhouette of the riverbank watchtower, hidden by the trees. That unplaceable burnt stench hung thick in the air — not the simple scorching smell of an ordinary fire. It was the same smell he'd caught in town.

And here, it was overpowering.

Jace frowned. Strangely, by the time they got here, the smell felt familiar.

He braced against a tree and caught his breath. Sprinting a few miles from the murloc coast had really taken it out of him. No point collapsing from exhaustion right in front of the dragon, unable to lift a hand.

Maybe he'd been pushing too hard, because he could even taste blood in his own mouth. But after stopping to catch his breath, the smell gradually faded without fully disappearing.

Grede was nowhere to be seen. Another wave of heat rolled over him. Jace pressed himself behind the tree to avoid getting hit head-on. This blast was more violent than the last few — like being slammed from behind by a jet engine. Both his ears burned from the heat. In that moment alone, his hair felt like it'd been baking in the sun.

Jace quickly pulled up his hood. The foul stench in the hot wind made him cough, and the burning sensation on the back of his neck suddenly reminded him where he'd smelled this before.

Stonewatch.

The one who'd made this smell wasn't anyone else — it was him!

Shadowflame... no. If this was a black dragon in front of him, then this was the real deal, genuine Shadowflame...

Jace remembered that day atop Stonewatch, when he'd also suspected the fire he'd ignited on his sword was Shadowflame. But he'd figured that was a terrible poison fire only black dragons could unleash, and had nothing to do with a mortal like him.

Only now did he understand: the two probably weren't fundamentally different. Maybe the dragon's was purer, more perfectly fused, while his was weaker, more scattered.

But whether in appearance or in the smell it gave off, they looked like the same thing.

Jace's might even be the more evil of the two. After all, black dragons inherently possessed the power of the earth. Back in ancient times, before they'd been led to fall by the Old Gods, the flames they'd breathed had been molten lava... while the one who'd helped Jace ignite that fire enchantment was a demon, kindled by fel energy.

"Grede!" he shouted. No one answered.

Jace glanced back toward the edge of the woods. Grede had probably already reached the front lines. He had to catch up.

The black dragon's roar was right there. He'd see it as soon as he broke through the trees. He had to be ready.

He flipped open his little spellbook and cast Arcane Intellect on himself with practiced ease, then burned the incantations for Curse of Weakness and Fear into his memory. He touched the mana potion strapped to his robe and clutched his staff tight.

He hoped that with this staff's enhancement, at least one of Curse of Weakness or Fear would take effect.

He remembered in the ship's hold, when he'd used the staff-enhanced Curse of Weakness on the "Baron of Night," and that black panther had still been able to claw him.

So when facing a real dragon like this, he had serious doubts about whether his magic would work at all.

Still, even if it had the slightest effect, it could give the Lordaeron soldiers surrounding the dragon an advantage. Then it wouldn't be a waste of effort.

By now the fire had died down, and the soldiers' shouts were rising again.

Jace stuffed the spellbook into his bag, silently recited the incantations he'd just memorized, and stepped out from behind the tree. He followed two Lordaeron soldiers pushing forward, advancing.

The trees grew sparser. The dragon hunt was close at hand.
