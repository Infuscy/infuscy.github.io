Chapter 1441: Entering the Void Requires a Flying Helper

That day, Jace was at Stonewatch, negotiating with the soldiers about buying some shelf-stable rations — dried goods, cured meat, salted fish — to bring back to Ilgara's Tower.

The quartermaster at Stonewatch flatly refused to take Jace's money, insisting that both the king and the commander of Stonewatch had made it clear that the residents of Ilgara's Tower were to be fed and housed for free. But Jace still felt a bit awkward about it, especially since he'd parked so many demons and even a rift into the Twisting Nether near this fort.

While he was still arguing about it, he heard the sound of gryphon wings beating in the air from afar.

Before long, a small dark-green gryphon flapped down onto the battlements. A mage in a light purple robe jumped off and waved at Jace.

The soldiers called out "Archmage Khadgar," but Jace had to look twice before he could believe it was him. In just the month or two since he'd last seen him, the man's gray-white beard had already covered his mouth again.

"Jace Sesso!"

He trotted down the steps and came over to Jace, completely lacking the gravitas you'd expect from an elder who looked like that.

The two of them left the training ground and entered the keep. In the corridor, noticing Jace's slightly surprised expression, Khadgar raised a hand and stroked his beard. "A little arcane trick, lad. Don't mind it. Didn't I tell you before? To convince those people, I needed my appearance to carry some weight. You know, even though I'm long past being that seventeen-year-old apprentice, everyone still thinks I only look this mature because I'm cursed."

"So did you convince them?" Jace stopped. "What's the Kirin Tor planning to do about me?"

Khadgar glanced at a passing soldier outside the corridor and lowered his voice. "I thought destroying the Tome of Infamy would be enough of a concession to make the Kirin Tor drop their guard against you. But maybe it's the inertia of the last two thousand years from the Order of Tirisfal, maybe it's the Dark Portal giving them some kind of trauma — either way, not every Kirin Tor member chose to trust you because of that."

"You wouldn't believe it — in the end, what really got them to drop the matter was the Church of the Holy Light. Archbishop Alonsus Faol. His admiration for you destroying that book, cutting ties with its evil soul energy, far exceeded anything the Council felt."

"A lot of people heard about what happened at the Cathedral of Light, Jace." The archmage reached out and wiped the emblem on Jace's cloak. "Now from Lordamere all the way to Stratholme... word has spread that Archbishop Faol bestowed this on a shadow-wielding warlock, and that the Holy Light blessed the ceremony. Of course, some think the Light was trying to punish you and failed... but that doesn't matter. You can't convince everyone. It's a good sign overall."

"And will that make the Kirin Tor listen to the Church?" Jace asked.

"The Kirin Tor has many mages who question the Holy Light. That doesn't mean the Kirin Tor can ignore the Church." Khadgar explained. "Even setting faith aside, mages traveling and researching across the Seven Kingdoms still rely on the Church's support and assistance."

"Dalaran isn't floating in the sky, after all, Jace." The archmage smiled and pointed upward.

Jace looked at his finger with a complicated expression. "No, it isn't."

"What about Blackrock Spire? I met Archmage Drenden before. King Varian said he thought Archmage Antonidas would agree to let the Kirin Tor help the allied forces and the Dark Iron dwarves retake Blackrock Spire."

"On the condition that it lets the Dark Iron dwarves allow the Kirin Tor into Shadowforge City, to have proper exchanges with the fire sages, mages, and demonologists there." Khadgar said. "Dalaran is far smaller than it appears on the surface. It's full of all kinds of people. To get the Kirin Tor to support us, you need to win over every faction within it, not just the six seats... Fortunately, those scholars who care about nothing but knowledge hold a good chunk of the influence. Winning their support is simple enough."

"But are you sure Shadowforge City will want to deal with the Kirin Tor?" Jace asked.

"Wouldn't they?" Khadgar's eyes went wide. "I assumed they'd be willing."

"They'll be willing." Jace nodded helplessly. "They'll be willing."

"I knew it." Khadgar muttered. "But being considered reliable isn't always a good thing. The reason I'm a few days late finding you is because of something tricky — and it's related to Karazhan."

"You should know about the Guardian's staff. From the first Guardian — the half-elven archmage Alodi, who first concentrated all the Council's power into one person — there's been a mysterious staff passed down. Aegwynn entrusted it to her son as well. After Karazhan was destroyed, the staff was returned to the Kirin Tor. Now the problem is that two of the staff's keepers have since died for inexplicable reasons, both of them experienced archmages. So the Kirin Tor plans to entrust the staff to me... It's hard to say what their intentions are."

Jace knew what that was — the legendary Atiesh. After leaving Medivh's hands, the staff had killed several of its holders in succession. Finally, Archimonde shattered it when he destroyed Dalaran, putting an end to the so-called curse.

"You're a hero of the Expedition. They wouldn't deliberately harm you." Jace said. "Maybe they think that since you were Medivh's student and mastered the powerful magic you learned from Karazhan, you'd have an easier time controlling the thing."

"I honestly can't think of a reason they'd want to harm me either." Khadgar said.

"So did you accept?" Jace asked.

Khadgar opened his hands to show his silver staff. "Not yet. For one thing, I know very little about that staff. Naturally, I need to think carefully before taking on something so dangerous. For another, I'll be going in and out of Karazhan for a while, like I told you before — trying to use Karazhan's own power to repair its darkness. I'm worried that carrying the Guardian's staff might cause unpredictable consequences. So let's thoroughly survey the tower first, and then I'll go back and give them my answer."

"I'm not commenting on your decision." Jace said. "This is Kirin Tor business, after all."

"It's not exactly a secret anymore." Khadgar sighed. "It wasn't just me and Lothair involved in that operation. There were plenty of Alliance elites. I didn't bring that staff back alone."

"Either way, we need to see what's inside Karazhan first. Maybe we'll find a way to end the staff's curse too. The Kirin Tor once held onto an archmage's artifact — it was originally an arcane focus, but it was tainted with powerful necromantic energy. In the end, we found a surviving descendant of that archmage and used his blood to break the curse... Though I doubt solving Atiesh's problem will be quite so cliched."

"Are you ready, Jace? I can leave anytime. Or we could have lunch first."

Jace looked down the corridor, then asked, "Can I bring my demonic servant to Karazhan?"

Khadgar frowned and thought for a moment. "I don't know how the Curator and those arcane golems would react to a physical demon. In fact, it's precisely because those guardians are sensitive to demons that the void intruders crossing into the tower from other dimensions haven't been able to contaminate the library."

"At least Morofel can turn invisible," Jace said.

"Lady Morofel, yes." Khadgar nodded. "The space inside Karazhan is already extremely distorted. That means magical teleportation could be very chaotic. With demons on top of that... having a winged helper who's good at hunting demons would certainly make it easier to reach the library. What I'm worried about is that Lady Morofel's invisibility can't fool my senses, which means it definitely can't fool those golems' detection either."

Jace didn't say anything. He just glanced to the side.

Khadgar's eyes shifted too. He sighed. "She's nearby right now, isn't she?"

"Yes. She's been following me." Jace said. "Now that her soul is complete, she's very different."

"I knew something felt off." Khadgar rubbed his brow. "Then let's invite the lady to come along and give it a try."
