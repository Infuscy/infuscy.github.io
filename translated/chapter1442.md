## Chapter 1442: The Dark Tower Draws Closer

"Stay away from the window, Morofil." Jace said as he checked his luggage and potion pouch. "Even if the folks in Darkshire have a higher tolerance for monsters and ghouls than anywhere else in the world, a demon lord wreathed in flame spreading his wings by the inn window to say goodnight might be pushing it a bit."

He looked up and smiled. Morofil tilted her head, turned around, pulled the curtain shut, and said grumpily, "I thought after we came back from Draenor, you'd already revealed your identity and everything you have to everyone. As one of your 'possessions,' I'd be able to travel openly with you, dear."

"Still, it's progress compared to when you didn't dare bring me out at all."

The succubus padded over to Jace on nearly silent feet. "Do you know how happy I was when you asked Archmage Khadgar to let me come with you to Karazhan? It felt like after going through countless hardships, I'd finally been acknowledged by the one I admired."

"I just hope all this doesn't vanish like dust blown away by the wind when Vereesa Windrunner returns from the Emerald Dream and defeats me with the magical power she learned from the dragons."

"Don't push it, demon." Jace pointed a finger at her, then picked up the food pack to organize it.

Morofil circled around in front of Jace, folding her wings. "So you're saying I have to defeat her again to truly win your heart."

"You hadn't been dwelling on this for a long time." Jace frowned. "Why'd you start again after Draenor?"

The demon leaned against the table, deliberately arching her crimson rear and fluttering her furry demon wings. "Who knows? Maybe the other half of my soul loves you even more than this half."

"Prove you can avoid the arcane golems of Karazhan first, then we'll talk about other things." Jace tied up his bundle, hands on his hips as he surveyed all the luggage. "Don't forget, when those arcane golems were built, Medivh was possessed by Sargeras."

"A tower that once housed Sargeras's soul. Quite a challenge." Morofil placed her hand on her stomach.

"I need to save up enough mana for it." Jace and Morofil said almost in unison. Jace shot her a look, and Morofil laughed lightly, bending over. Jace wagged his finger. "No, no. We're heading to Deadwind Pass tomorrow. I have to save my mana."

He pushed a mana potion toward the succubus. "Have this to tide you over."

"Ugh." Morofil stared at the mana potion, making a retching face, then grumpily rounded the table to lean against Jace's shoulder. "The archmage said today that we have to walk to Karazhan on foot to avoid any possible magical disruption and conserve mana."

"So that means at least two or three days of walking to reach that tower. You're not telling me you can't recover your mana in that time, are you? Or do you really need full mana to deal with a few lonely ghosts, wolves, and zombies? You're Tyr's Guardian now, my master. That sacred name is etched into your soul. Use your Holy Light to burn my body... ah, just thinking about it makes me want to climax."

"Don't talk that nonsense." Jace let out a deep breath. The demon, as if receiving a signal, spread her wings and wrapped herself around Jace, turning into a burning shadow that pounced onto the bed.

In the chaos, Jace grabbed her shoulders and arms, glancing around nervously. "Don't burn anything, you damned—"

The demon smiled and murmured, "Don't worry. The only things getting burned tonight are the two of us."

"Honestly, Jace." Khadgar held a magic scroll in his hand, staring into the distance. In the direction he was looking, there seemed to be some tall shadows, but Jace couldn't tell whether they were mountains, strange rocks, towering corrupted trees as huge as monsters, or Karazhan itself.

The archmage turned back. "When I first came here over a decade ago, I thought this forest was desolate, silent, dead. I never imagined it could get even more desolate, even more silent, even deader."

"Back when I'd just defeated my teacher and left Karazhan, looking at all this — Deadwind Pass destroyed by the spreading dark clouds, and even Darkshire and places further west and north — there wasn't much stirring in my heart."

"Because I was a hero. Whether by luck or by having comrades and companions worth trusting, I'd accomplished feats greater than any tale in poetry and legend. So I didn't think much about the innocents who'd died horribly in this disaster, or the restless souls reawakened... let alone the battles ahead and the world waiting to be saved. I didn't have the time or energy for more."

"After the Orc Wars ended, after experiencing the collapse of Draenor, standing before the Dark Portal... I slowly started to realize other things. That's why I wanted to come back here and do something. As if, without saving the innocents Karazhan had harmed, everything I'd learned, everything I'd done over these decades, would be missing something."

The archmage turned back. "And you're at the same moment I was then — you've just accomplished feats that bards and singers could scarcely imagine. You defeated the Deceiver, destroyed the Scepter of Sargeras. But the difference is, you didn't immediately put yourself in the role of savior and hero like I did back then. So you're an extraordinary person, Jace. That's what Archbishop Faol thought. He's a wise man — he only needs to look at someone once to understand their character. I trust his judgment."

"I just wanted my parents and the people I love to live, quietly." Jace hugged his staff and glanced at the black forest nearby, the knife-like ravines and streams. "I just never thought I'd go this far for that goal."

"Because living quietly isn't easy in this world." Khadgar said.

"Yeah." Jace nodded.

"You've been here before." Khadgar said as they walked.

Jace's heart jolted. Did the Kirin Tor know he'd hidden an doom lord here before? Or had his trip with Grede to collect necrotic essence here for the goblins been exposed?

Then he remembered. Right — back when he'd gone with his orcish teacher Dushan to Dalaran for the orcish language conference, he'd fabricated that he'd found Dar'Khan Drathir's letters here in Deadwind Pass.

"I've been here. Just never went this deep." Jace said.

"Karazhan's dark energy has revived many corpses — both the recently dead and those from years past." Khadgar said. "The closer you get to Karazhan, the more powerful the reanimated dead might be. Dar'Khan Drathir, the one you defeated, was said to have connections with a group of undead mercenaries here. He was searching for that evil staff — Ulthalesh."

Jace's fingers unconsciously rubbed the tightly wrapped demon bone, nodding. At the same time, he felt through the soul link that the invisible Morofil behind him had tensed up too.

"Recently, the Kirin Tor has detected signs of necrotic plague in Northrend." Khadgar continued. "Master Krasus gathered a group of elite mages in the Council and headed north. You should know Archmage Rhonin's name. He rescued a blue dragon trapped in a cave by nerubians using web-like strands. But the nerubians surrounding that blue dragon had all been transformed into undead by evil magic."

"If Rhonin and the mages following him hadn't bravely broken through and rescued that dying blue dragon from that death-cave, what do you think the consequences would be?"

The archmage stepped over a deep crevice in the road and continued, "Karazhan's death-essence can reanimate a mage as a terrifying skeletal mage. Northrend's necrotic plague can turn an army of nerubians into an undead nerubian army. So a blue dragon could undoubtedly be transformed into an undead dragon with powerful arcane and frost powers."

"When news of this reached Dalaran, Archmage Antonidas had to start taking it seriously. The shadow of Gul'dan's death knights hasn't faded, and dark clouds are already gathering in the north. As far as I know, Master Krasus plans to head north to support the Kirin Tor members there."

"The Deceiver, Kil'jaeden, is a master of necromancy." Jace said.

"Is he?" Khadgar turned back.

"I saw descriptions of the Deceiver in the Shadow Council, written by high-ranking orc warlocks." Jace explained. "And during the Orc Wars, Gul'dan created death knights in a hurry to turn the Horde's fortunes around. Most of his magic was taught by the Deceiver. I mean, it's possible the death knights' necromancy originally came from Kil'jaeden."

"Many Kirin Tor mages think it came from Deathwing." Khadgar said. "But I agree with you more."

"I was thinking." Jace stopped walking. "Is it possible the necromancy in Northrend was originally the work of Kil'jaeden, or other Burning Legion demons — the Nathrezim, for example? Could it be part of their plan to invade Azeroth?"

Khadgar looked at Jace for a moment. "When you put it that way, I have to consider the possibility."

"I'm just guessing." Jace said.

"A reasonable guess." Khadgar said. "When we get back, I'll find the mages who returned from Northrend and organize the evidence."

The two of them talked as they walked. The blurry silhouette ahead was getting closer and closer. It was indeed a tower — a sharp shadow piercing the gloomy dark night.
