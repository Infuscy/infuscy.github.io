## Chapter 1444: The Password

As someone who'd led god-knows-how-many Karazhan raid groups in his past life, Jace knew this place from the game like the back of his hand.

Having these Violet Eye spies force them to take the back door was actually a relief. Most of Karazhan's terrifying spirits lingered near the main entrance — the noble guests, the deadly servants, especially the caretaker, Moroes.

Sure, Jace had dealt with who-knew-how-many spirits of varying strength, but he didn't want to tangle with Karazhan's permanent residents inside this tower.

If they took the back door, they could bypass those guests and head straight for the upper floors. With luck, they'd reach the library directly — the domain of "the Curator." That would be much easier.

Of course, this was all experience from the game. Karazhan looked a bit different here — larger, more complex, full of pillars, high walls, and dilapidated courtyards, with the upper reaches lost in the rainy night.

But the fact that Khadgar agreed so readily to look for a back door suggested he thought this route would be easier too.

The archmage led the two out of the courtyard and around a high wall. Suddenly he stopped dead in his tracks. Jace, following behind, bumped into him. Khadgar let out an "oof" and swayed forward, even letting a wisp of arcane light flicker from his hand.

"Easy..." He held out a hand to stop them, pointing at the ground. Only then did Jace notice the cliff before them — ragged as an open wound.

Below the cliff was a narrow river, churning and splashing in the pouring rain, stretching into the pitch-black depths of the canyon beyond.

Jace thought he remembered this place. To get to Karazhan's back door, you had to cross a river. Though the river looked quite different from the game.

"Are you sure we're going the right way, Khadgar?"

"Should be." Khadgar said doubtfully, glancing up at the tower with lingering unease, then at the cold, dark wall across from them. "But I don't remember a river here..."

"You didn't see this river when you came with Lady Aegwynn?" Jace asked.

"Do you think I had the presence of mind to notice the scenery when I was traveling with one of the most powerful mages in the world?" Khadgar said. "And we didn't come this way. Back then, the Violet Eye had gone to Stonewatch, so there weren't as many mages watching here."

"Then let's just cross." Jace said firmly.

"Aren't we trying to get into the tower?" Khadgar asked. "Why cross the river? I think we should look over there — maybe I got it mixed up."

"You used to live in Karazhan, but you don't remember a river here..." Jace pointed at the ruined town not far away. "You must have been in and out of this place often back then. You'd be familiar with this town. Would you forget an entire river next to it, Khadgar?"

"I shouldn't." Khadgar's expression steadied. "So you think this river is a shadow illusion created by the tower? What do you think, Lady Morofil?"

The succubus stayed silent, just staring at the river below. Khadgar looked back at Jace.

"I don't know." Jace said. "But whatever it is, it's worth checking the other side."

"You're my shadow magic advisor now, Jace Sesso." The mage said quietly. "I'll follow your lead."

"Then Morofil, take us across." Jace grabbed the succubus's hand.

"Oh, so my wings are useful now, Master?" The succubus glanced sideways at the archmage.

"Yes, if you would, Lady Morofil." Khadgar adjusted his hood.

The demon grabbed Khadgar's belt from behind with one hand, and with Jace, leapt off the cliff. She beat her demon wings, arcing over the river through the rain, and landed steadily on the other side, setting them down.

Khadgar held his forehead, barely steadying himself. The two followed the collapsed high wall away from the cliff edge, walking downhill for a while, when suddenly Khadgar flinched — as if startled by something at his feet. He looked up and examined the high wall beside him.

After a moment, he turned back to look at where they'd come from, at the murky shadows above. His brow furrowed slowly.

"Well I'll be damned. This really is... what's happened to this tower since I left? This really is part of the tower. I remember this turret. I used to come in here to clean it! Though I'm still not sure whether that river was an illusion conjured by shadow magic."

Jace certainly didn't know whether the river from the game was some kind of Deadwind Pass illusion. Listening to Khadgar's description, looking at the strange trees and crumbling walls around him, even he felt a chill running down his spine.

"If the tower really created that illusory river to stop us, maybe it doesn't want us to enter." Morofil said softly.

Jace and Khadgar both looked at the demon. Morofil put on an innocent smile. "I'm just guessing what it's thinking."

"But since we've come this far..." Khadgar shrugged. "Follow me. The back door into Karazhan is in this building. We're close."

The front door of this abandoned turret was gone — not removed, but blasted apart by something, with debris still scattered around. Probably Karazhan's explosion.

The warlock followed the mage and demon inside, immediately hearing a sharp whistling wind. They spiraled up the stairs, passing several corridors where rainwater occasionally sprayed through the cracks, biting cold.

The building was full of holes and cracks. Parts of the staircase had collapsed, and what remained seemed ready to give way at any moment.

But despite how precarious it looked, Jace wasn't worried.

This was the Guardian's tower after all. He felt deep down that even if the Hour of Twilight came, Karazhan would still be standing.

Khadgar put on an apologetic face every time he entered a door, as if his teacher might be standing right behind it. It kept Jace on edge too.

When they stepped onto a landing and entered the next section, Jace felt like he could vaguely match the place to his memories from his past life.

After climbing however many floors, Khadgar finally reached the back of a room and pushed open an iron door. Cold wind and rain rushed in.

Jace blinked and looked out. Before them was a narrow stone bridge with no railing — so narrow that two people could barely walk side by side. It looked like something bandits on a mountaintop would use as a defensive pass against attackers.

Below the bridge was pure darkness. Nothing was visible except rain falling into the void. Even though Jace knew that if he fell, the demon and mage beside him would save him, he still felt a knot of worry.

Across the stone bridge was Karazhan's side wall. An iron gate sat in the doorway. And suddenly, memories from god-knows-how-many years ago came flooding back. Yes. This was the place.

Khadgar pointed and walked straight onto the bridge. Jace followed. The demon brought up the rear.

The storm here was ferocious. Walking across, it felt like they'd be blown into the black abyss at any moment.

The three of them moved like tightrope walkers to the other side and grabbed the iron bars. Jace glanced at the succubus, who stood steadily on the bridge with her wings folded, then shouted over the howling wind, "Do you have a key?"

"No!" Khadgar shouted back.

"What the—"

"Watch your language! I'm an archmage of the Kirin Tor. Show me some basic respect, Jace!"

"Then where's the key?!"

"Patience! I can open it!"

"Should I go back and be patient in that room?"

"Trust me!"

"I want to trust you, Archmage Khadgar, but—"

"Trust me!"

*BANG!*

The iron gate slid up. Both of them tumbled through the doorway. The succubus followed.

"What just happened?" Jace was confused.

"The magic password my teacher gave me: 'Trust.'" Khadgar looked at the dim passage ahead. "I didn't think it would still work."

"He used the dwarven meaning of your name as the tower's entry password?" Jace said. "Isn't that a bit casual?"

Khadgar wagged his finger. "Only I can use this password to enter. Other residents or guests of Karazhan have their own passwords, all assigned by Medivh."

"That's too complicated, mage." Morofil examined the raised iron gate suspiciously. "Are you sure it's not that anyone living here just has to shout something and the tower's master hears and opens the door for them?"

"I had the same doubt back then, Lady Morofil." The archmage pointed at the doorway. "Now it's proven to be a real magic password. Though even I can't tell how it works now."

"And if it's the same now as it was then?" Jace asked, pulling back his hood.

Khadgar was about to walk inside. He stopped and stared at Jace for a moment.

"That's not funny, Jace. Not funny at all."
