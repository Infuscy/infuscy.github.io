Chapter 1445: The Way Up

The creaking wooden door swung open. The dark view ahead showed nothing. Ears filled with the whistling sound of wind rising and falling.

A flash of lightning suddenly illuminated an ancient, badly damaged corridor.

Large chunks of wall and brick had peeled away. Cracks and breaks were everywhere. The floor was littered with rubble. After the thunder faded, faint sounds of laughter and chatter echoed through the passage.

The voices were low, barely audible, but the tone was excited — like they were discussing something particularly interesting.

The strange voices came closer, then faded further away, drifting toward the end of the right-hand corridor before finally disappearing.

Jace looked in that direction. He could see a glimmer of light there — clearly lamplight. Occasionally laughter and curses drifted over too. The wind seemed to carry the sound of an organ from somewhere beyond the ceiling over there, though the rhythm and melody were indistinct enough to make you think it was just an auditory hallucination from the howling wind.

Either way, the brightness and liveliness in the distance only made their own surroundings feel more gloomy and silent.

"Interested in attending the ball in the banquet hall?" Khadgar asked quietly.

"I can't dance." Jace said.

"Unexpected answer." Khadgar pointed at the darker passage ahead. "We take the left staircase. That's the way to the library. Hopefully that stairwell survived the explosion."

"There are a lot of ghosts here." Jace listened to the sounds. "Won't they stop us?"

"They're also guests of Karazhan, Jace. Just like us." Khadgar turned back. "As long as we follow the rules and know our place, we'll leave each other alone."

"Of course, the arcane golems are another story. So Lady Morofil, make sure you stay hidden."

"Master already instructed me before we came, Archmage." Morofil said.

"Good." Khadgar swallowed, muttered "good" again under his breath, and headed down that corridor.

The three reached the end of the hallway and climbed a badly damaged staircase, stepping over broken sections and navigating rubble to force their way upward.

"When I drove that sword into his chest, I didn't expect it would lead to this." Khadgar ran his hand along the brickwork of the wall, looking up at the wet window high above. "I know it was to stop Sargeras's plan, to stop the Burning Legion. But in the end, it felt like my good intentions brought about even greater destruction."

"Later, during the collapse of Draenor, standing before the Dark Portal... I was holding the Skull of Gul'dan, realizing the next decision might mean the destruction or rebirth of two worlds. And suddenly I remembered that moment at the top of Karazhan — facing Medivh, holding a sword at his chest."

"But you made the right decision in the end." Jace said.

"It only looks right now because you lot showed up from Auchindoun." Khadgar gave a bitter laugh. "That decision saved Azeroth, yes. But it also doomed thousands of people. Almost every one of them was a hero of the Orc Wars, people worthy of respect."

Jace hadn't expected the archmage to be this sentimental. But he was the leader of the Sons of Lothar, and now the spiritual symbol of the Kirin Tor. He couldn't show this hesitation in front of those people.

If not for coming to Deadwind Pass, to Karazhan — this place where he'd spent years as a teenage apprentice in daily fear and anticipation — he probably wouldn't have shared these old feelings with Jace either.

Actually, Jace understood that feeling. Standing before the portal at Auchindoun, he'd been thinking about the same kinds of questions almost constantly.

And for a transmigrator who'd seen another timeline, the question was even more complex.

He suddenly remembered sitting in Darkshire next to Morgan Ladimore, agonizing over whether to save him from the tragic fate of losing his family... worrying that his every action might drag this world into an unknown direction — especially a darker, more chaotic one.

"What about you? Have you ever had moments like that, Jace Sesso?" Khadgar asked teasingly.

"Probably. But I don't remember them well." Jace said. "I don't like to admit it, but I know I make decisions impulsively. I don't think much about the consequences."

Morofil gave a meaningful smile. Khadgar glanced at the succubus, then took a deep breath. "That's something we have in common. Maybe this Karazhan expedition is the product of that very trait."

With that, the archmage climbed the stairs and pushed open an iron door. The sight before him made him stop short, eyes wide.

Jace stepped in behind him. The path ahead looked like it had been torn apart by some monstrous force. One more step and there was nothing but a bottomless chasm.

And in the white mist above, chaotic magical energy slowly rotated massive chunks of the building — windows, stairs, walls, statues, turrets — all drifting high overhead.

Jace didn't quite remember if the game had looked like this. He recalled that the path from Karazhan's back door to the library had some ghosts and spirits blocking the way, but nothing as dramatic as this.

But before entering the tower, he hadn't seen anything like this from outside. Such a massive magical vortex should have been visible from miles away, especially with the lightning illuminating it.

That meant entering Karazhan was essentially entering another space. Jace was somewhat prepared for that, so he wasn't too surprised.

"Was this... like this before?" he asked.

"How long before?" Khadgar asked. "Do you mean before Aegwynn used the magic here to reach into the Twisting Nether, or when I first came to Karazhan?"

"I..." Jace cleared his throat but didn't elaborate further.

"Can you fly up there?" he asked, looking at Morofil.

"I can try, Master." The succubus said.

"I wouldn't advise flying directly through that chaotic storm." Khadgar said.

"Then what? Is there another way to the library?" Jace asked.

"There is." Khadgar stared into the heights. "But this was definitely the easiest route. The situation isn't as hopeless as it looks, Jace. The arcane energy maintaining this space went haywire when Karazhan's mana collapsed, tearing the tower apart. But I'm confident I can put it back together — at least the staircase, so we can keep going."

"Are you sure arcane magic on this scale won't be disrupted by Karazhan?" Jace asked.

Khadgar pointed upward. "It can hardly get more chaotic up there. Don't worry, I'll do my best to make sure the magic doesn't affect us, or fling us somewhere unknown... Stand back a bit."

"Do your best?" Jace stepped back.

"A hundred percent guarantee!" Khadgar shouted — whether to prove something to Jace or to bolster his own courage.

The archmage carefully climbed onto a broken wall nearby, without using any arcane magic, to get higher. Then he raised his arms high.

He began to chant — it sounded like draconic, or some mix of elvish and Common. Jace listened for a moment before giving up trying to understand it.

Khadgar's chanting was like a sermon or a song. His voice grew rougher, deeper, mingling with the wind, thunder, and rain.

Suddenly, a rumbling like earth and stone splitting apart came from above. Jace blinked against the rain and looked up, seeing the massive rotating fragments start to change direction, gathering toward the same trajectory. Debris, broken planks connected and pieced together, forming an increasingly long zigzagging staircase that slowly reached toward them, trying to connect the upper floors to where they stood.

Jace had seen meteors fall, storms of fire, the earth split open, and dark flames sweep across the land more times than he could count. But this was the first time he'd seen arcane energy channeled like this, chaotic things reshaped into form. He found himself staring, almost dumbstruck.

At the same time, he could clearly sense a note of pride creeping into Khadgar's tone. Jace had no way to judge whether the spell was succeeding, but Khadgar himself surely knew, and it seemed like a sure thing.

But that hint of pride was quickly overshadowed by a thick, dark emotion. Not from Khadgar's heart, but from somewhere higher, further out... the tower itself. Karazhan's dark energy was seeping out of the chaotic arcane vortex, and what Jace felt was the emotional manifestation of that dark invasion.

Khadgar clearly sensed something was wrong too. But the staircase wasn't complete yet. He couldn't stop casting.

Soon, sharp, piercing screams came from high on the walls. Jace had never heard anything so harsh and strange — not on Draenor, not on Azeroth. It made his roots and his mind go weak all at once.

Jace looked up at the source of the sound. A crimson thing was clinging to the wall, mouth full of spike-like teeth, its body a mass that seemed both flesh and tumor. As it crawled downward, its outstretched arms looked like tentacles or an infant's fingers.

The dark, chaotic shadow energy inside it made Jace feel his heart grow cold just from looking. A wave of unwarranted sadness quickly seized his chest.

And there wasn't just one of these strange things on the wall. More and more monsters appeared on the outer walls — even on the staircase Khadgar was assembling. They twitched and convulsed as they swarmed toward them, shrieking even more piercingly.

Flesh.

"Don't worry, Khadgar. Keep going with your ritual." Jace said, raising his hands.

Khadgar didn't respond, continuing his chant. But the disturbed emotions had already settled back down.

"Ucronie, Molgak, Tam!"

Jace shouted the incantation to unravel darkness, drawing on shadow energy to tear apart the nearest screeching, crawling flesh-beast. These parasitic monsters that walked out of the void and nightmares were themselves nothing but clotted shadow-flesh. Against the warlock's shadow-unraveling, they could barely resist. Soon, with one final piercing howl, they burst into foul-smelling pus and blood, scattering into the wind and rain.

The void energy of the unraveled flesh-beast was extracted by Jace and converted back into mana, then used for the next spell. One after another, the flesh-beasts that crawled over were torn apart mid-air by Jace. But the screaming grew louder, and more and more flesh-beasts swarmed from the walls. Even behind them, through the doorway, massive ones were charging in!

These monsters writhed their tentacles and slimy arms, crawling forward in a frenzy, biting and tearing as they tried to stop Jace's spellcasting and Khadgar's ritual. But Morofil stood guard as the last line of defense. No matter how many flesh-beasts approached, they were cut down with ease — the flames of the Red Dragon's fire burning the apocalypse into them, making them curl up and shriek as they burned, falling into the abyss.

"Go!" Khadgar shouted.

Jace looked up. The staircase had taken shape in the white mist.

The archmage jumped onto the steps first. Morofil grabbed Jace and pulled him out of the flesh-beasts' encirclement, onto the stairs. The three stopped fighting the endless horde and ran upward with all their strength.

The flesh-beasts' screeching still echoed in their ears, but the mist grew thicker, obscuring their view of the surroundings.

The staircase was much longer than expected. At first, the three just climbed as high as they could to escape the flesh-beasts. But gradually, even Khadgar started to feel something was wrong.

"Was it really this high when we looked up?" Jace asked.

Khadgar didn't answer. They were like they were on an endless stairway in the sky, surrounded by gray nothingness, unable to see Karazhan's walls behind them or the end above them.

Not long after, even the flesh-beasts' sounds faded. The monsters' assault receded like a tide, leaving an eerie silence.

The three had to stop and assess. Jace even tried to call on fire with elemental speech, but the elements gave no response at all.

Just as he was thinking Karazhan's twisted space had completely cut them off from the elemental powers, a dim, hazy firelight flickered in the white mist.

Wait. That couldn't be...

The fire grew brighter rapidly. A hot wind pushed through the mist. Khadgar and Morofil both noticed the approaching anomaly.

But before anyone could ask "what is that," a sudden roar exploded through the entire space!

"Please don't be that dragon..." The archmage muttered through gritted teeth.

Jace was already reaching for his fire protection potion.
