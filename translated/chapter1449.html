Chapter 1449: On Stage and Off

"A succubus from the Twisting Nether, who has captivated both evil and good with her beauty and elegance, and conquered arrogance and recklessness with her wings and blade."

"An archmage from Dalaran, who has fought for hundreds and thousands of years to protect this world from darkness and corruption — and to this day, has never relented."

"A magic advisor serving His Majesty Llane Wrynn, a member of the royal conjurers — it is because of him that the barbaric otherworldly warlord Blackhand could never win this war!"

"Today, we are honored to welcome three distinguished guests to the theater!"

The blond announcer raised his hand, facing their direction. All the ghostly nobles turned as one to look at them.

The spotlight had reached them. Jace even noticed the light seemed deliberately aimed at the three of them. A ghost standing nearby — the cylindrical beam of light actually had a notch cut out so it wouldn't envelop him.

"Allow me to introduce myself, three honored guests. I am Barnes, the stage manager of the grand theater." The blond man announced loudly.

He looked at the entire audience. "We respect the wishes of everyone chosen. If you are unwilling to participate, by all means, say so."

"What's there to say?" Khadgar swept his gaze quickly across the theater, muttering almost to himself. "Can we even refuse?"

"Probably not." Jace said quietly.

This was all too deliberate. He looked up to scan the second floor of the theater, trying to pick out from among the guests who was orchestrating all this. He was increasingly feeling like this trip to Karazhan was being arranged by someone.

"We're willing!" Morophil raised her hand and shouted.

"Then please come backstage, three honored guests. Our staff will give you plenty of time to prepare..." Barnes strode dramatically to the edge of the stage, pointing at the passage they'd just tried to escape through.

"So that's how we get in." Jace muttered.

"You don't have to tell me!" Khadgar huffed.

Led by a ghostly attendant, the three approached the wooden door that Khadgar hadn't been able to open. The door swung open on its own with a bang.

Jace whispered, "What kind of play do you think they'll have us perform? I know nothing about stage plays or operas. I've never even sung a song."

"With this little time and no rehearsal, they can't have high expectations. Just put on a show and make these ghosts happy." Khadgar said.

While the ghostly attendant wasn't paying attention, he spoke as he gestured subtly toward the inside. Jace understood — he wanted them to scope out the path inside and find a chance to slip into the passage beyond.

But that would have to wait until after the play ended. Canceling on the entire audience would stir up the whole of Karazhan.

Khadgar stepped through the door. The impatient Morophil followed. Jace stepped in after them, but as one foot went through, something felt wrong. When he tried to step back, his foot landed in a pile of soft fallen leaves.

What the hell?

Somehow, he was in a vast, unfamiliar forest.

It was different from Elwynn, Tirisfal Glades, the Gnoll Woods, or any of the other forests he knew well. It also didn't look like southern Elwynn or Deadwind Pass that had been corrupted by Karazhan's dark energy.

This felt more like the north — it reminded him of the forest at the border between Hillsbrad and the Hinterlands that he'd passed through with Grede on the way to Aerie Peak.

Everywhere were giant pine trees. Sunlight struggled through the tangled branches and needles of the canopy, stinging eyes that had grown used to the dark.

Before entering Karazhan, Jace had been prepared for the possibility of crossing between spaces. So suddenly finding himself in a strange place wasn't too shocking.

But where was Morophil? Where was Khadgar?

At least he still had a Soul Link with Morophil. It meant the demon wasn't impossibly far away. He almost called out, but stopped himself.

Facing an unknown, deep forest, it was better to be cautious first.

Through the Soul Link, he could sense Morophil's general direction. He headed that way.

As he walked, he carefully observed everything in the forest. Maybe this was a dimension inside Karazhan, and everything around was real. But it could also be a complete illusion — meaning any accident could happen at any moment.

Or maybe everything before had been the illusion, and this was reality? In the past, Jace could rely on his long experience of tangling with shadow magic to quickly tell real from fake. But this damn place Karazhan was more outlandish than any wizard-made illusion.

One second you'd be on a balcony in a howling storm, the next you'd step into an arcane storm inside the tower that wasn't visible from outside.

Then before you knew it, you'd be through a dragon's destruction and a terrifying explosion, and right next door was a bustling, glorious theater...

Compared to the solid earth beneath his feet, the chaos of the planes inside that tower was hard to call more real.

Especially since the stage manager Barnes's voice had been so clear just moments ago. Beyond the stage, countless ghostly audiences had applauded in anticipation. But stepping through that door had transformed everything into this.

A silent forest. Occasional bird calls. The damp smell of decaying vegetation.

He followed the Soul Link's sense for who knew how long. The sun set. The sky grew dark.

Jace felt like the distance in the Soul Link was getting closer, the sense clearer. But it was still more exhausting than expected.

Was Morophil fleeing from him? Why?

Just when the monotonous dark woodland was starting to fatigue him and he was thinking of resting, a dark gray shadow flickered at the edge of his vision.

But the shadow vanished into the forest's depths after one movement — whether startled or something else.

Jace couldn't make out its shape. Couldn't tell if it was a hostile enemy or a harmless beast. Still, he unstrapped Ulthalesh from his back and held it...

Wait.

He looked at the staff in his hand. No, this wasn't a staff. It was a hunting rifle. Where was Ulthalesh?!

No, this was Ulthalesh. He could feel it. After all, he was the master of this demon staff. The connection in his soul was unmistakable. In other words, it was still hungry.

So he'd been walking this whole time without questioning whether the thing on his back was his staff. Hadn't even noticed it had turned into a hunting rifle...

"Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to tonight's performance..."

Just as he was considering his situation, a sudden announcer's voice rang out from the sky.

Jace looked up, but saw nothing except the shadow of the gloomy canopy faintly outlined by moonlight.

He realized, though. This was part of the performance. He'd never left the grand theater.

"Tonight, nothing is as it seems. Truth and falsehood blur. Tonight, your eyes will deceive your minds!"

More gasps from the ghostly audience followed, along with lively discussion. Jace instinctively looked into the distance, but listening carefully, he realized these sounds all came from somewhere impossibly far away — even from beyond space and dimension itself. There was no way to track their source.

"For example, this grandmother is waiting for her granddaughter. Clearly, a kindly white-haired old woman poses no threat!"

Hearing this, Jace could already tell what script he'd landed in.

"Unfortunately... I cannot say too much, ladies and gentlemen. Because you must see for yourselves the truth hidden beneath the disguise!"

"Then, the show begins!"

Applause spread through the forest, followed by an annoying drumbeat and a slightly melancholy accordion melody. Hearing these in the silent forest felt more like the hallucinations of a split mind.

Jace rubbed his temples and leaned against a tree to calm down. When the miscellaneous noise settled, it was replaced by soft humming drifting from deeper in the woods.

The voice was much thinner, but much clearer. This was definitely not some sound floating in from outside the world. It was real, here.

He immediately crouched down and looked toward the source. A little girl carrying a basket was walking toward him. She wore a simple pale yellow dress like a farm girl from Elwynn Forest, but the red hooded cloak draped over her made her especially conspicuous in the nighttime woods.

Jace lowered the hunting rifle slightly. After all, as the hunter, the little red riding hood in front of him wasn't his target.
