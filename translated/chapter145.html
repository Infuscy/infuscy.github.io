Chapter 145: The Shadow on the Tower

By the riverbank watchtower, crowds of soldiers were hiding behind stone walls or anything that counted as cover. Soldiers who'd just arrived from the woods like Jace charged toward a stretch of collapsed ruins.

Over there, a knight-like officer in full plate was directing soldiers to quickly cross the slope and reach cover.

Jace joined them. As he ran, he kept looking around — but he didn't see the dragon.

It wasn't hard to figure out, though, because soon he noticed the Lordaeron soldiers all staring in one direction — the top of the watchtower.

When Jace realized he could use their gaze to pinpoint the dragon's location, he felt someone grab the back of his cloak and yank hard, nearly pulling him off his feet.

He looked up to see a pitch-black shadow rising over the battlements of the watchtower above.

Even with the sun behind it, he could see the creature's yellow reptilian eyes locked on him — a massive serpentine head, big enough to trigger his megalophobia.

The oppressive presence of something so far beyond normal biology made the hairs on his neck stand on end, even in the sweltering heat of a landscape burning with fire.

"Get out of here, mage!"

Jace rolled with it, sliding back with the force of the yank, scrambling backward on hands and knees — and then it felt like someone had kicked him from behind and sent him flying rear-first into the air.

Before he hit the ground, a wave of flame engulfed him. Red-black fire rolled over his head. The blast of heat sent him tumbling across the ground several times before he stopped.

He staggered up, ran a few steps, and dove behind a wall. He looked back to see a soldier in chainmail, his whole body ablaze, sprinting toward a creek.

"Damn it... damn it... damn it..."

Jace patted out the flames at the tips of his hair, muttering in his native language as he pressed himself against the stone wall. He could feel the wall heating up from the fire on the other side.

Only now did Jace feel a burning pain in his forearm — he hadn't had his cloak over it when he'd fallen.

The cloak had already turned from dark brown to red. If not for that thing protecting him, his whole back would have been done for.

"Use a slowing spell to bind its movement! Mages!" a knight-captain shouted from the other side. "Don't be scared off by its fire!"

Jace had no idea where the mages were. Honestly, he didn't dare stick his head out right now. The archers beside him were pressed against the wall too, not peeking out.

They probably didn't dare go out either, Jace told himself. But then a voice from somewhere — "Archers, ready!" — and these soldiers immediately burst from cover, drawing arrows to their bows.

These Lordaeron soldiers defending Southshore were undoubtedly veterans who'd fought in the Orc Wars a few years back. And Hillsbrad Foothills was the battlefield where red dragons had been most active across the entire continent.

The people here would never question whether orcs had really ridden dragons into battle the way the people of the Kingdom of Stormwind did, because most of the locals had seen red dragons screaming overhead with their own eyes.

To them, a black dragon was just a red dragon with a darker color.

Jace watched his countrymen aim at the sky behind him without a shred of fear, and it gave him the courage to peek out too.

Now he saw the dragon in full.

The black dragon was covered in dark-brown scales. It clung to the tower wall with four sharp claws, scraping off chunks of stone and dust that fell like rain.

One huge wing, dark purple like canvas, fanned outward. The other wrapped around the tower along with its four legs — basically a bus-sized gecko.

An exaggeratedly long tail stood up in the air. Only the spindle-shaped tip, studded with a few spikes, trembled now and then, like a threat display.

Its raised head was aimed at several soldiers fleeing along the river. Black smoke poured from between its scaled lips, and its mouth trembled constantly as transparent drool ran down. The cunning creature was clearly using this watchtower as cover to toy with the soldiers closing in on it.

Twang!

The soldiers loosed their arrows. Bowstrings sang as shafts screamed past Jace and shot straight upward. Jace didn't even have time to see where they landed when the shriek of arcane energy tearing through the air rushed up from behind.

He looked up to see arcane missiles spiraling straight at the black dragon. The dragon keenly sensed the threat from behind. The raised wing shook hard toward the missiles' direction, and every missile suddenly shattered into arcane dust and dissipated midair.

The black dragon turned toward the missiles' source. Its belly swelled red, then the glow traveled up its throat. Jace saw several mages already fleeing in all directions with all kinds of strange mobility spells...

Whoosh!

A mass of scorched-black flame streaked with red lava flew out, exploding the instant it hit the ground. Jace grabbed his cloak to shield himself. The blast of heat slammed into him like a big man shoving him hard into a wall.

Honestly, compared to the raw explosion, the residual red glow radiating purple light on the ground — the shadow corruption — was far more terrifying.

Jace saw a mage near the explosion howling in agony. His burned-through trousers exposed skin that had already turned purple and swollen, about to necrotize from the shadow corruption's damage.

He fumbled in his bag and clutched the last Shadow Protection Potion he had left, tucking it into his robe's binding. He didn't know if this potion could block this black dragon's damage (of course it couldn't), but better some protection than none.

Without realizing it, Jace, huddled under the watchtower, had become the closest person to the black dragon on the whole field.

He didn't dare expose himself, because once he was spotted he'd definitely die fastest.

The archers who'd been hiding with him had either been pulled into the woods with blast injuries or moved to another low wall to look for another opening. The constant clinking and clanking of arrows hitting stone and scales echoed from above.

Suddenly the black dragon roared. Jace glanced over and saw that some lucky archer had driven an arrow right between the scales of the dragon's belly, sinking in deep.

But looking closer, quite a few arrows had already lodged in its skin. It's just that the dragon was so huge that these arrows couldn't deal any lethal damage — only slow it down.

And the mages? What happened to that slowing spell they'd promised?

The black dragon, seeing the attacks growing more intense, groaned in constant pain. It immediately shifted its claws and climbed to the other side of the tower. Stone rained down as it scraped. Jace had no choice but to cover his head and huddle in the corner for a spell.

From the corner of his eye he glanced up at the black dragon and saw that its folded wing membrane had already been badly torn, a chain of wounds running all the way to the ribs connecting to the massive wing. That was the gruesome wound left by that dwarf gryphon rider.

Damn it, where was Grede?
