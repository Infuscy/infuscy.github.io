## Chapter 1450: Act One

Now that he'd confirmed it was the hunter's role in "Little Red Riding Hood," Jace relaxed a little. He just had to follow the story's familiar beats and act it out to the end.

Little Red Riding Hood. The wolf. The cottage. Grandmother.

Since he was the hunter, who was playing Little Red Riding Hood? Was it Morofil?

Had he been following the soul link this whole time because Morofil was playing Red Riding Hood and moving along her designated route — which was why it was so exhausting to keep up?

But Little Red Riding Hood was humming a traditional Hillsbrad nursery rhyme that Jace had heard but wasn't all that familiar with. She hummed it well, her mood light and natural, as if the deep forest was her own backyard.

Did Morofil even know this stuff? Jace doubted she knew the Little Red Riding Hood story at all, let alone this northern nursery rhyme.

Barnes had said they'd have plenty of time to prepare. Jace had assumed they'd go backstage and quickly go over lines or something. But instead, they'd been thrown straight onto the "stage." Morofil, a demon from the Twisting Nether who'd never been to Azeroth before, should know almost nothing about these things. She couldn't have prepared this thoroughly.

From his own situation, it seemed Karazhan didn't forcefully control people to act according to a script's logic. The actors still had to perform based on their own experience. So this probably wasn't Morofil.

More importantly, Jace couldn't detect any demonic shadow through his demonic senses. So if it wasn't her, then... it had to be Khadgar?

That had to be it. Khadgar had lived in Dalaran for a long time. He should be deeply familiar with Hillsbrad culture, and well-acquainted with the Little Red Riding Hood story that was common knowledge across all the human kingdoms of Azeroth.

Thinking about it, Jace found it kind of funny.

After all, not everyone gets to see the leader of the Sons of Lothar, Archmage Khadgar himself, wearing a red cloak, carrying cakes, sausages, and wine for grandmother, tiptoeing along a forest path, pinching his throat and singing.

Little Red Riding Hood walked on. The moonlight between the shadows of the trees seemed to shine right on her. Jace guessed that was the theater's spotlight — so that was center stage.

As a character who hadn't entered yet, he was supposed to stay behind the curtains, observing and waiting for his cue. At least for now, it was too early. The hunter didn't show up until the end of the story.

"The little girl walks alone through the forest. But she is about to realize she isn't truly alone."

Barnes's voice came from deep in the forest. Little Red Riding Hood looked around warily, clutching the basket tightly to her chest. The carefree mood from before vanished instantly.

"Something in the dark is watching her. It thinks it hasn't been noticed. It's even smugly scheming..."

Quiet murmuring from the audience drifted over. Jace pressed his head low against the tree roots. Hearing Barnes's voice, he crouched even lower.

He knew Barnes was introducing the wolf character. But he couldn't shake the feeling that the announcer was actually taking jabs at him, the hunter.

Not just in these few lines. From the moment they'd stepped into the theater, Jace had felt the atmosphere here was strange. So it was only natural that the spotlight had ended up on the three of them.

Just then, a shadow emerged from between the trees ahead of Little Red Riding Hood.

Even though it was already night, Jace could tell this was the same gray shadow he'd been following earlier. Of course, that was also thanks to the "moonlight" shining on Little Red Riding Hood.

Little Red Riding Hood turned sharply, staring at the gray figure. It stepped into the moonlight step by step, and Jace finally got a clear look at the creature.

A werewolf.

Though the beast was hideous and twisted, its eyes glowing with blood-like demonic light, particularly terrifying in the forest. But Jace felt its posture and gait were more like... more like...

"Mr. Wolf." Little Red Riding Hood looked up politely. "Hello!"

"Call me ma'am." The werewolf said, baring a toothy grin.

That was Morofil. Damn it. No wonder Jace felt the wolf's walk was more like a goat's.

"Mrs. Wolf..." Little Red Riding Hood corrected timidly.

Gasps from the audience. Worry for the little girl's fate.

The werewolf looked around the depths of the forest. Jace didn't even know if he should let her notice his presence. He didn't know if Morofil had learned the script through some other channel. If not, they'd be in trouble.

"You have a red cloak too." The werewolf said. "Who are you?"

"Don't you know my name, Mrs. Wolf?" Little Red Riding Hood asked.

The werewolf hesitated. From deep in the forest came Barnes's voice, "It seems even the craftiest wolf can get lost in the deep woods of Hillsbrad."

Laughter followed Barnes's teasing. Jace bit his lip anxiously. The soul link could convey feelings, but not accurate lines. How was he supposed to guide her to continue?

Jace looked at his belongings, then felt his head. He found he was wearing a hunter's cap with a long feather sewn into it.

He focused all his attention on the hunter's cap, trying to send a hint through the soul link to the other end. The werewolf indeed seemed to sense something. She first glanced toward the forest where he was, then, as if realizing something, quickly looked back at the girl in front of her.

"Hood... red..."

"Yes, that's my name, ma'am..." Little Red Riding Hood smiled. "I'm Little Red Riding Hood! We live in the same forest. You must remember me!"

Seeing that it worked, Jace immediately felt around his waist pack. He found a shot pouch.

He used the same trick, focusing his attention on the pouch, hoping Morofil would understand his meaning.

"Little Red Riding Hood, hmm." The werewolf looked the little girl over carefully, focusing on what she held in her hands.

It was working. Jace felt quietly pleased. After all, this demon had been raised on his mana. Their tacit understanding was good.

"What's in that basket, Little Red Riding Hood? Can I have a look?"

Little Red Riding Hood hesitated. "It's for my grandmother, Mrs. Wolf. My grandmother is sick."

"Your grandmother?" The werewolf's eyes darted around. "Where does she live?"

"I... I can't say." Little Red Riding Hood said.

Since when did Little Red Riding Hood get smart? Jace thought. Wasn't she supposed to tell the wolf where her grandmother lived, then the wolf would race ahead while Red Riding Hood dawdled in the forest? Wasn't she going off-script?

Or maybe the Karazhan version of Little Red Riding Hood was different from the Earth version? But even on Earth, there were countless versions of the story.

"Dear Little Red Riding Hood." The werewolf lowered its head, speaking gently. "The forest is so dangerous, and it's already dark. I can scout the way ahead for you, make sure there's no danger. After all, no beast in this forest would dare to challenge me. That way, you'll reach your grandmother's house safely."

"Mrs. Wolf, I'm sorry. Mother told me I can't listen to you." Little Red Riding Hood shook her head. "She said... you're a demon."

"What?" The werewolf froze. Jace was stunned too.

Murmurs of surprise drifted from the depths of the forest. Even Barnes couldn't help saying, "Good heavens."

What now?

Just as Jace was at a loss, the werewolf bared its teeth in something like a smile, trying to ease the awkward atmosphere as much as possible. Though on a beast's face, the expression sent a chill down the spine.

"You see, I'm covered in gray fur, from head to tail." She raised her hands. "My hands and feet are claws, not hooves. And I don't have horns on my head, or spines on my skin."

Little Red Riding Hood uncertainly followed her description, examining her body. She seemed ready to agree, but also unwilling to believe.

"Most importantly, I don't have wings." The werewolf turned around. "Where do you see a demon?"

"True..." Little Red Riding Hood nodded. "Mother was wrong about you."

"It's all right, dear." The werewolf said softly.

Little Red Riding Hood said cheerfully, "My grandmother's house is a quarter of an hour's walk from here. Her cottage is under some big oak trees, next to a fence with a broken lantern... You'll definitely find it. Thank you for scouting the way for me, Mrs. Wolf!"

"You're welcome, little one. Take your time."

The werewolf turned and vanished into the forest. Jace immediately stepped back into the trees, avoiding the moonlight as he followed.

"Morofil! Morofil!"

Once he was far from Little Red Riding Hood, he tried calling out to Morofil ahead, hoping to quickly explain what was supposed to happen next.

But no matter how he called, there was no response.

"Little Red Riding Hood is alone now. And something in the dark is plotting. Hopefully it won't succeed..."

Jace heard Barnes's voice from far away in the forest again. But gone was the mocking tone from before. It sounded more like a warning.

He understood. This tower had no intention of letting them compare notes.

So he just pulled himself together and silently followed the soul link's pull deeper into the woods.
