Chapter 1451: A Turn of Fortune

Following Morofil's direction through the soul link, Jace hadn't been traveling long when he caught sight of a cottage roof through the dense roadside forest.

The house sat beneath several oak trees, next to a fence with a broken lantern — exactly matching the description Khadgar, or rather, Little Red Riding Hood had given.

More importantly, there was a sign out front, written in beautiful Common script: "Grandmother's House." As if they were afraid the actors might not find the place.

Jace held his rifle as he scanned the surroundings. No sign of Morofil or anyone else. He stepped over the fence and approached the house, pressing his ear to the wall.

No voices inside. No footsteps, or the clatter of pots and pans from everyday life. Just a rhythmic "huff huff" of heavy breathing. The breathing was extremely theatrical — neither the snoring of a sleeping person nor the panting of a beast. More like someone struggling to take deep breaths.

The whole way here, Jace had been wondering: if Khadgar was Little Red Riding Hood, then who was playing grandmother?

Strictly speaking, in the whole story of Little Red Riding Hood, the real grandmother didn't have much stage time. It would be normal for the theater to use a ghost actor.

Thinking this, he approached the window and peered inside. In the dim interior, a single weak candle illuminated the figure of someone lying in bed under thick quilts and blankets, but their face was unclear.

Jace used his demonic senses to look more carefully. The shadow under the blankets... that was Morofil. She'd already "eaten" the real grandmother. That part had gone smoothly.

So she knew what came next? Who'd told her?

Jace was about to push the door open and quickly catch Morofil up on the situation. But the moment he reached the door, humming drifted from the path outside. Little Red Riding Hood had arrived.

Earlier, when he'd left Red Riding Hood, he'd sprinted to catch up with Morofil. But Red Riding Hood had arrived at grandmother's house almost at the same time? That was too fast.

The wind pushed aside the canopy. The moonlight grew brighter, shining on Little Red Riding Hood as she approached. Jace had to leave the door and hide in the bushes behind the house.

"Our little girl has safely reached her grandmother's house. There were close calls along the way, but not everything watching from the forest meant harm. The wolf-woman kept her promise!"

Barnes's announcement boomed. Little Red Riding Hood reached the door and knocked.

"Who's there?" A hoarse voice called from inside.

"It's me, Grandmother. It's your granddaughter, Little Red Riding Hood."

"Pull the rope. The latch will open."

Little Red Riding Hood nodded, pulled the rope by the door. The wooden door creaked open. The moonlight dimmed. At the same time, Jace felt the candlelight from the window behind the house grow brighter.

He crept to the window and pressed himself against it. He heard Little Red Riding Hood call out, "Good evening, Grandmother," followed by a sob.

"What's wrong, my child?" The hoarse voice from the bed said.

"I did something wrong, dear Grandmother... I did something wrong. I told a wolf-woman where your house is. I thought she'd come here to hurt you. Seeing you safe and sound is such a relief. My heart was about to jump out of my chest!"

"Oh, my child, you are such a good grandchild to worry about your grandmother. Come to the bedside and let Grandmother see if that beast hurt you."

"It's all right, Grandmother. She was very polite to me, and even promised to drive away the dangers in the forest. That's how I got here safely."

The more Jace listened, the more something felt off. The "grandmother's" words were emotional, but clearly guarded, and a bit theatrical. Not just in tone, but in what Jace could sense through the emotional signature of shadow energy.

But "Little Red Riding Hood" was different. She really sounded like a frightened, innocent child — full of hesitation, doubt, fear. Jace didn't need to peek through the window to clearly feel these complex emotions, even sitting outside the wall.

Had Khadgar learned more than just magic at Karazhan? Had he studied acting too?

"Grandmother, I brought the fragrant pastries Mother made for you, delicious sausages, and red wine... They'll be good for you. Mother and I believe you'll get better soon. I put everything on the table. When you get up, you can try them. They really are delicious."

"Silly child. Just seeing you and your mother healthy and well is enough to make Grandmother better quickly. Come to the bedside and let Grandmother see if you scraped yourself on the way. You're too kind-hearted. The dangers in the forest aren't just wild animals."

"All right, all right... But, um, Grandmother, why are your eyes so bright?"

"All the better to see you with, my dear."

"Then, Grandmother, why are your hands and face so red?"

"Grandmother is sick. My skin has gotten worse. But after eating the good things Little Red Riding Hood brought, I'll get better soon!"

"I'm sure you will. But Grandmother, why are your arms so—"

"Because you've been too disobedient! Wasting time! Come here, you foolish little thing!"

Little Red Riding Hood screamed. Jace watched through the window as the candlelight flickered, and the twisted half-demon, half-wolf monster stretched out branch-like arms, snatched Little Red Riding Hood, and vanished into the shadows. Gasps came from the audience beyond the forest. Someone was even quietly sobbing.

"We all underestimated the malice in the forest — my little girl and I. How terrible. Poor child... And her mother is still waiting far away, unaware of the tragedy unfolding in this cottage. Does she know that an ordinary decision made that morning led her child into this danger? But is this the end? Is this the story's conclusion?"

Barnes's voice rose higher and higher. Jace knew it was his cue.

BANG!

The hunter kicked the door open with his rifle. The wolf-woman on the bed — her body dark red, her eyes blazing with flame — turned her head sharply in startlement.

"You—"

The wolf-woman was clearly surprised. She hadn't expected the hunter's arrival.

The hunter said coldly, "Your evil ends here, beast. Tell me where Little Red Riding Hood and her grandmother are!"

Cheers erupted from outside. Bright moonlight poured through the window, making the hunter look towering and radiant.

But the wolf-woman wasn't intimidated. She twisted her face into a grin. "Cut open my belly and find out!"

Was he really supposed to cut open Morofil's belly?

Jace pulled Azuresong from his waist — no, it was a slightly longer hunting knife.

He walked to the bed, grabbed the wolf-woman by the throat, pinned her down, and raised the knife high.

The wolf-woman stared at him with wide eyes, burning like flame. Jace found it uncomfortable to look at. He squinted involuntarily.

Just then, the whole cabin went dark. The moonlight outside died too.

The next second, the candle re-lit on its own, illuminating everything. Little Red Riding Hood and grandmother had appeared beside the bed.

"Thank you, Hunter!"

Little Red Riding Hood came up to Jace. "I shouldn't have listened to the wolf. I almost didn't make it!"

"Thank you, kind Hunter!" An old woman's voice said.

It was an old woman who looked exactly like Khadgar — beard and all. Jace was startled.

She — no, he — was wearing a rumpled housecoat and a ridiculous giant plaid nightcap that looked absurd on his face. He even walked with a slightly trembling old woman's gait.

"The brave hunter arrived just in time to save the day from tragedy. We can only hope that every misfortune in the world finds such a turn of fortune. But we know that is merely wishful thinking."

The four walls of the cabin fell away with a crash. Jace realized they were back at center stage.

The ghostly band struck up a cheerful tune. The audience erupted in applause and cheers. The nobles hugged each other joyfully, as if celebrating Little Red Riding Hood's rescue.

Morofil and Khadgar had already returned to their original appearances. From both sides, they joined Jace, holding hands as they bowed to the audience. As Jace bowed, he glanced sideways. Barnes was clapping at the edge of the stage, smiling. But... a fourth person?

Backstage, Khadgar immediately waved. "Quick. Follow me."

"So you were the grandmother." Jace said.

"Yes." Khadgar pushed open a door into a dark corridor. "Thank me later. Before the wolf-woman 'ate' me, I managed to tweak the lines a bit to hint at what comes next."

"Being able to understand your hints is proof enough of my exceptional grasp of human stories." Morofil emphasized.

"Undoubtedly." Khadgar nodded.

Jace followed the two into the dark passage. After walking a while, he said, "I have a question, Khadgar. Little Red Riding Hood's mother told her there were demons in the forest?"

"What demons?" Khadgar turned back.

"I mean the script." Jace said. "You're familiar with this theater. Have you seen Little Red Riding Hood before? Earlier, our Little Red Riding Hood said in the forest that she thought Morofil was a demon."

Khadgar stopped, looking at the succubus. Morofil nodded too.

"No." Khadgar shook his head. "I don't remember any discussion about demons."

"Who was playing Little Red Riding Hood?" Jace asked.

"Probably some ghost actor." Khadgar said.

"Are you sure?" Jace asked.

Khadgar took a deep breath, glancing back the way they'd come. "Either way, let's get out of here first, Jace."
