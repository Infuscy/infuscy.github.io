## Chapter 1452: The Demon in the Cottage

Khadgar led Jace and Morofil through a narrow, rough-hewn passage. It didn't feel like a proper corridor so much as a cramped attic crawlspace, or the mezzanine of some ancient castle — dark, damp, and barely lit.

The mage didn't dare summon a light cantrip for fear of drawing the ghosts outside. Though Jace figured after everything that had happened at the theater, whether they stayed hidden or not hardly mattered anymore.

The Grand Theater had already started its next performance. The ghostly actors never tired, and the ghostly audience never grew bored.

But the cheering, the applause, Barnes' announcements grew more and more distant, hollow, until they were nothing but indistinct noise.

"I think it was some kind of warning," Jace said. "Little Red Riding Hood was trying to tell us something about a demon. I could feel it from her emotions — she was asking for help. When she saw Morofil, or rather who she thought was her grandmother, she was so relieved. That emotion was too real to be acting."

Khadgar was quiet for a moment, rubbing his fingers together. "You're saying that what Little Red said when she got to the cottage wasn't acting... she was really trying to warn her grandmother that a demon had gotten into the house? You think the whole play was some kind of allegory, hiding a message?"

Jace thought about it. "I'm not sure."

"Then what does the cottage represent? Karazhan?" Khadgar ventured. "But way before I ever came to Karazhan, Medivh was already summoning things from the Twisting Nether here. Jace, back then I didn't know a thing about it. There are void beings in here, monsters made of shadow and flesh... like the ones we saw outside. And demons too, of course. Remember those succubi trapped in the theater, forever serving the guests?"

"The Kirin Tor aren't a bunch of fools either. They caught wind of this problem a decade ago, but not many people had the ability to enter this tower and confirm the rumors. Either way, Grandma's cottage has been full of demons for a long time now..."

"So maybe this is Medivh's confession," Jace said. "He regrets summoning demons into this tower. Or maybe... the cottage represents Azeroth, and he regrets what happened with the Dark Portal."

"Don't you think you're reading way too much into this whole performance?" Khadgar's voice dropped as he leaned close to Jace. "Maybe the ghosts just thought demons running loose in Karazhan after it fell was more interesting than a plain old Hillsbrad forest wolf as a threat, so they added that element to the script."

"Don't tell me you can't feel it. This tower knows we're here," Jace said. "The master of the tower is very likely steering our path, steering what we encounter."

"Medivh is dead, Jace." Khadgar's voice was low. "I drove that sword into his chest with my own hands. I watched Lothar cut off his head. I witnessed the demon lord's soul banished into the Twisting Nether. I buried his body myself, at the foot of Karazhan."

He closed his eyes, swallowing something, then continued. "There's no spell in this world that brings back the dead. Necromancy at its best can only forge a twisted, corrupted shadow. Once a person dies, they can never truly return. You, me, Medivh, every mortal on Azeroth — same rule. That joke I made at the door was fine for setting the mood — thunder, lightning, pouring rain, crows flying everywhere, all the classic haunted castle atmosphere. But if you're still entertaining that suspicion now, I have to take it as an insult to my teacher."

He took a few steps back, then turned to add, "He was a good man who suffered greatly, Jace Sesso. I believe that, because I truly knew him. He shook off Sargeras' curse. Now you think he'd disguise himself as some ridiculous fairytale character, walk out of the forest, and play guessing games with us? For what? And even if, by some stretch, he still held a grudge — the ones who killed him were me and Anduin Lothar. Lothar's dead. If he wanted revenge, he'd come for me, not you. You've got nothing to worry about."

As he said this, Khadgar's lips trembled slightly, and the hand not holding his staff was clenched into a tight fist.

Jace realized he might have pushed too hard. After all, Khadgar knew nothing about the other timeline where Medivh was resurrected.

"Sorry, Khadgar. I really don't know Medivh."

Khadgar seemed to notice his own emotions slipping. He took a moment to steady his breathing. "Nobody can claim to know him. Not even me. I'm not saying what you mentioned is completely impossible. Maybe some of his power still lingers in this tower."

"I'm the one who should apologize, Jace. You're right. I've been suspecting this whole journey myself — I just didn't want to admit it."

He sighed, leaning against the wall. "All of this is too strange. The staircase ripped apart by that bizarre arcane storm, the sudden appearance of Arcanagos, the theater... then this incomprehensible performance."

"But the arcane storm and the magic that sealed the backstage door don't come from the same person, Jace. Those spells were powerful, sure, but wild, chaotic. If my teacher had cast a ward on that door to keep me out, I wouldn't have been blasted into the wall by such messy energy. I'd just feel like an idiot who forgot his keys at home."

"There's other evidence too. The ghosts here seem to still be living fifteen years ago, which matches what the Violet Eye found. After Medivh died, these spirits kept running purely on the residual magic of the spells he left in the tower... No new master came to take control of everything."

"But what about Little Red Riding Hood? Was she as chaotic as the spells you described?" Jace asked.

Khadgar fell silent. He held his forehead, opening his mouth a few times as if to say something, but never quite committing.

Morofil, who had been listening the whole time, spoke up. "Little Red Riding Hood seems to be part of the space magic itself, master. She's part of that forest, the cottage, our transformation — not just a ghost actor."

"You didn't notice that, Khadgar?" Jace asked.

"I can't say for sure," Khadgar said. "Maybe."

Jace thought about going back to the theater to find Little Red. But he knew that was naive.

If Little Red was really being played by Medivh, or was part of one of his spells, looking for her now — hell, even looking for her the moment she disappeared — would've been a waste of time. In Karazhan, if she didn't want to meet the guests, nobody would find her.

So what was Little Red trying to tell them? What was she afraid of?

Jace knew that in the game, Karazhan had two demon lords. One was a satyr named Terestian Illhoof, the other was the eredar warlock lord Prince Malchezaar.

Illhoof was already dead, killed in the Emerald Nightmare by Grede and Vereesa. In the space of two or three years, there was no way he'd come back.

So was Little Red — or whoever was behind her — trying to warn them that Malchezaar had led the Burning Legion into this tower?

He thought back to Little Red's emotions when she described the demon to her grandmother in that cottage — the terror, the fear. Malchezaar was a powerful eredar warlock, sure, but would his invasion of Karazhan really concern Medivh that much?

Then again, Little Red's emotions might not represent Medivh's.

To Khadgar and Jace, and especially with Morofil around, an eredar lord like Malchezaar was hardly a fearsome opponent. The tower's master didn't need to go through all this trouble just to warn three such guests about danger.

Jace had a nagging feeling that — even though both he and Khadgar sensed something was wrong — what was really going on was more complicated than they'd considered.

Maybe his experiences in Draenor had made him paranoid. Maybe the shadow energy in Karazhan was already affecting his mind. He touched the Chain of Will under his collar. The iron links were warm, but not hot enough to burn.

"The library's close, Jace," Khadgar said. "Once we see the Curator and throw all our questions at him, maybe some of them will get answered."

Jace nodded. Morofil glanced back at him. In the dark, lightless corridor, the flames burning in her eye sockets were unusually bright.

It reminded him of earlier, in that cottage, when she'd stood over him holding a hunting knife, back when she was still in the form of a she-wolf.

Those two burning, blazing eyes had looked familiar, but he hadn't placed them then. Now he remembered.

On that mountain. At the highest point of that demon fortress.

If the demon Little Red mentioned wasn't Malchezaar... was it Kil'jaeden? The thought made his chest tighten.

He didn't want to think that way. It hadn't even been a few months since he'd escaped from Draenor. But Prophet Velen had warned him too — it wasn't over yet.

And once the thought surfaced in his mind, he couldn't shake it.

Ursula had also asked him to find Velen when he had the chance, to ask about any scholars studying void-bloom among the draenei. When he got out of Karazhan, if he had the time, he'd head to Stonard. It wasn't far from here.
