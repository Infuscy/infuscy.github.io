## Chapter 1453: The Foolish Apprentice

Medivh's magic had long since turned Karazhan into a bizarre nexus where countless planes and spaces intersected. His death had only made the chaos harder to untangle.

In the game, the very top of the tower was a passage to the Twisting Nether. That was where the demon lord Malchezaar had invaded Karazhan, trying to seize control of it.

Jace followed Khadgar through the passage, unable to stop himself from glancing upward, as if he could see through this mezzanine all the way to the top of the tower.

He couldn't help worrying about what might be waiting for him and Khadgar up there. Was it the enemy Little Red had warned them about?

Though, he didn't think a demon like Kil'jaeden would bother entering Azeroth through Karazhan.

After all, in the other timeline, aside from a few high-ranking demons who'd made similar attempts, the Burning Legion's main force and its rulers had never treated this as a viable option.

Maybe even for the most powerful demons in the Twisting Nether, the situation inside Karazhan was too uncontrollable. And in trying to take control of Karazhan, Malchezaar himself had become a prisoner of the tower, unable to escape for years, until adventurers finally came and killed him.

So whichever way you looked at it, Little Red's worries had issues that couldn't quite be explained away.

But chalking it up to "just a script adaptation" felt like willfully ignoring the string of "coincidences" they'd been encountering since entering Karazhan.

Jace wanted to ask Khadgar about the connection between the top of Karazhan and the Twisting Nether, but Khadgar had already started wondering whether Jace had been to Karazhan before. He could maybe bluff his way through information about the lower floors, but if he showed knowledge of the upper floors — especially the top — there'd be no explaining that away.

Under the circumstances, Khadgar was the only person Jace could rely on to get out of Karazhan alive. He didn't want to create friction over a series of baseless guesses.

He could feel it — he was hiding a lot from Khadgar, and Khadgar had secrets he wasn't sharing either.

For now, the best choice was to put those thoughts aside and focus on getting to the library. Maybe, as Khadgar said, the Curator could answer some of their questions.

The passage ended. Khadgar grabbed the door handle, glanced back at Morofil and Jace, and said, "Have you noticed? Ever since we got into Karazhan, every time we open a door, something goes wrong."

Jace nodded. "Seems like it."

"If opening this door causes trouble too, I'll believe you. I'll believe this tower really is messing with us on purpose," Khadgar said.

"Deal," Jace said. Morofil behind him also looked expectant.

Khadgar took a deep breath, squared his shoulders, and slowly pulled the bolt. When he pushed the door open, there was a wide spiral staircase inside.

Despite the cobwebs everywhere, the carpets on the steps, the neatly laid stone walls, and the elegant portraits of mages hanging on them all suggested this had once been a very handsome place.

Khadgar slowly let out the breath he'd been holding, looked around to make sure no axe-wielding suit of armor was waiting for them to stick their heads out, then stepped inside and adjusted his cloak. "See? Does that relax you a little?"

"A bit," Jace said, nodding.

"Full of memories, this place." Khadgar walked up the stairs. "Fifteen years ago, I used to come through this stairwell every day. Everything's still the way it was. The patterns on the railings, the arcane lamp fixtures... even these portraits. Looks like they haven't been damaged at all."

Jace followed behind, studying the painted mages one by one. Most were strangers, unlike the portraits in the Wizard Sanctum's tower, where plenty were people he knew, and the rest were at least faces he'd seen in books.

These were mostly human, but there were elves too, and some he couldn't place at a glance. They were all richly dressed, their expressions solemn and brooking no argument. Just the look in their painted eyes was enough to convey immense authority — hard to imagine what it'd feel like standing before them in person.

"Who are all these people?" Jace asked.

"You don't recognize them? They're the past Guardians." Khadgar pointed at one — a young man with black hair and a cold, stern expression. "The first Guardian, Alodi. He created the concept of the Guardian. However problematic that system turned out to be, if he hadn't come up with the idea of pooling all that power into a single person, humanity might never have survived the magical disaster of two thousand years ago, let alone made it to today."

"This is Guardian Scavill, Aegwynn's teacher." Khadgar pointed at a middle-aged mage with half-closed eyes and a red-gold beard. "A long time ago, Deadwind Pass was occupied by an incredibly powerful demon necromancer. That demon used the souls and corpses of countless Gurubashi trolls to weave a dark domain for itself there, trying to corrupt the whole world from that foothold... Until Scavill defeated the demon alone, personally sealing the source of its power — Usaalus, the terrifying demonic weapon that the demon's countless worshippers and followers would later call the Reverse Wind Reaper."

"Aegwynn inherited Scavill's duty. One of the most important reasons Karazhan was built was to seal the nearly incurable pain that soul-devourer had inflicted on this land. She almost succeeded... Only now this tower has itself become another source of darkness."

Jace was already regretting asking. He should've been able to guess who these people were anyway. Truth was, he'd brought it up hoping to ease the tension from their earlier argument.

Morofil behind him was smiling, clearly enjoying watching him shoot himself in the foot.

They hadn't climbed far when a faint tremor came from above, one thud after another.

Jace looked up. A massive shadow was moving up there, and the footsteps seemed to be getting faster.

"The Curator?"

"No, just a guardian construct," Khadgar said.

"Turn invisible, Morofil," Jace said.

"Time to prove whether you're really the most powerful succubus in the Twisting Nether," Khadgar said.

"I'd rather prove it through battle than hiding," Morofil said, the bright flames in her eye sockets flickering unhappily, before she melted into the shadows.

Sure enough, the footsteps stopped. The shadow and arcane glow above stopped shifting too.

Khadgar whispered, "There weren't constructs patrolling here before. Maybe Morofil's demonic aura attracted it."

"What about us? Will it attack us?" Jace asked.

"I don't know," Khadgar said. "The Curator is still functioning, but I can't tell if every arcane construct is under its control. So until we see the Curator, better not take any risks. It should head back soon. Like I said, this isn't its patrol zone."

Before he finished speaking, the shadow above shifted again.

But it didn't seem to be heading back. It took another step down.

One step, two steps. The shadow was getting closer. Khadgar frowned. "Back. Into that door we just came out of."

Jace nodded and turned, only to hear the same footsteps coming from below too.

"Damn it." Khadgar leaned against the wall, glanced at the portrait of the female mage beside him, and muttered under his breath, "You just stand there and watch your good son toy with us..."

Jace gripped his staff and looked at Aegwynn's portrait. "No choice?"

Khadgar silently counted the broken vases on the floor, then looked up the stairs. "Let's risk it. You two follow me. The library door isn't far. We'll charge straight in."

"What if that door's sealed too?" Jace asked.

"Think positive, Jace Sesso!" Khadgar's eyes went wide. "You're a smart guy... Maybe the only reason you haven't earned your official mage's ring from the Wizard Sanctum is that your attitude is too damn pessimistic!"

No need to bring that up, Jace thought.

Khadgar spun and bolted up the stairs. Jace followed on his heels.

They took the steps two or three at a time, keeping one eye on the arcane construct's shadow above, until Khadgar dove into a doorway and pressed himself against the wooden door there.

Jace looked at the door — no handle, no bolt — then glanced back, listening to the approaching footsteps. "Can you open it?"

"Don't rush me," Khadgar muttered.

"These passwords are way too weird," Jace whispered.

"I said don't rush me!" Khadgar shot Jace a glare, swallowed, slowly opened his mouth, and carefully whispered in Thalassian: "The foolish apprentice."

The door swung open with a bang. The mage yanked the warlock inside and slammed the door shut behind them.

In the dark corridor, they both caught their breath. Khadgar pointed at Jace's side. Jace nodded to show Morofil had made it in too.

Once the other side of the door was completely quiet, Jace carefully peered inside. What he saw was a huge, slanted blue wall right in front of them, its top edge pressed against the wall the wooden door was set in, forming a narrow wedge.

He looked left and right. In the distance, more of these bizarre, chaotic walls stretched out — different colors, different patterns. The passage itself was squeezed wide and narrow by them, completely without logic.

"Is this the library?" he asked.

"Yes." The mage raised his hand, pointing at the wall before them. "*A History of High Elf Exile.* An introductory text for Quel'Thalas studies. I must've read it a thousand times when I was a kid."

Jace looked back at the wall. "So you were sixty feet tall as a child."

Khadgar shot him a sidelong glare, his lips twitching, but said nothing.
