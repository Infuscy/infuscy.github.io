Chapter 1454: Everything's Different in Karazhan

Confronted with books and tomes taller than the houses in Goldshire, both of them fell silent.

Jace glanced back at the wooden door they'd come through. "I'm guessing that door's not opening again."

Khadgar reluctantly put his hand on the door and carefully said in Thalassian again, "The foolish apprentice." The door showed no sign of responding.

"You could try it in demon tongue," Jace said.

Khadgar turned and glared at him, eyes narrowing in annoyance. Jace shrugged. "You said it yourself — if opening a door caused trouble, it'd prove the tower was messing with us."

"I meant the previous door, not this one," Khadgar said.

"Let's not split hairs." Jace spread his hands. "What matters now is figuring out what's going on."

Khadgar raised his hands, then let them fall to his sides. "If we were in Dalaran, I could easily tell whether we'd been shrunk or the library had been enlarged. But here, especially after experiencing those weird spells just now, I can't be sure of anything."

Jace remembered that in the game's Return to Karazhan dungeon, there was a shrinking scenario like this. In the library.

A powerful mana wyrm lurked deep in the library, called the Mana Devourer. Once you beat it, the players would revert to their normal size and find the way out.

"This magic has to have a source," Jace said. "If we can find a way to remove the power source, we might go back to normal."

"Every magic has a power source, and a mass-scale spell like this must have a powerful one." Khadgar nodded. "But considering we're in Karazhan, tracing the source here is like looking for where the water in the ocean comes from... Let's head deeper. Maybe we'll notice something."

"What we can confirm is that this is the old book section." He walked past books the size of multi-story buildings, studying their spines. "Tundra Natural History, Songs of the Mountains, Are Frost Dwarves Really Dwarves... We need to get to the magic section. Normally it'd be a few minutes' walk, but now..."

"Not only a longer walk, but we have to climb over these mountains of books and scrolls first," Jace said. "Literally."

"Exactly," Khadgar said.

"Clean out those cockroaches. Quickly. They're getting annoying."

A stranger's voice came from the dark depths of the library. Jace, Khadgar, and Morofil all looked toward the source.

The voice didn't have any arcane resonance mixed in, so it probably wasn't a construct. A human speaker — or at least. A ghost.

Who was it?

Medivh? Or Moroes?

"Who's that? Can you tell?" Jace asked.

"Hard to say. The voice is too muffled," Khadgar said.

"Is it Medivh?" Jace asked.

"Sounds a bit like him, but no," Khadgar said. "Though this is very likely whoever cast the spell."

"What I'm more concerned about is — are 'the cockroaches' us?" the succubus asked.

"Do you even need to ask?" Jace said.

"Then what's coming to 'clean out the cockroaches,' master?" Morofil looked at him.

"Don't worry," Khadgar said. "We're smaller, but our magic power hasn't weakened. Magic in Karazhan can get chaotic, but whatever it sends to clean out cockroaches isn't worth worrying about. Let's keep moving."

Jace and Morofil nodded. Khadgar talked tough, but his pace quickened considerably.

Climbing up onto the books gave them a wider view. Up ahead, the scene was clearer.

The three of them ran across the scattered books toward the corridor ahead. Jace could even see the doorway at the far end of the hall from here, though he knew it'd still take a long time to reach.

They hadn't gone far when a faint "skitter-skitter-skitter" rose from below. Jace glanced down and saw what looked like a dark shadow flicker past — maybe his own shadow.

Khadgar was already far ahead. That mage had ridiculous stamina. Jace had to speed up to keep pace, but before he'd made it more than a few steps, a massive brown-black head suddenly emerged from the gap between books ahead of him, two antennae like tree trunks swaying above it.

"Shit!"

Jace hurled a glob of shadow bolts, smashing the giant insect's head to pulp. But the moment it was hit by the sudden magic, the whole body of the bug leaped up as if shocked, its barbed yellow-brown legs flailing everywhere, rattling pages and shaking books. Jace was thrown off balance and fell into a crevice beside him, barely catching a rope binding some books to stop himself from plunging into the "abyss."

"Cockroach!"

Khadgar's shout came from above. Jace shadow-stepped back, only to emerge from the shadows and get caught midair by a lunging roach. The cockroach's tiny pincer-like mandibles chattered as it tried to crush the little morsel, but couldn't even penetrate Jace's cloak. Morofil sliced the cockroach in half with a single horizontal cut, freeing him.

More and more cockroaches swarmed in. A dozen of the giant insects had crawled up onto the surface now. Khadgar shouted and unleashed a terrifying barrage of arcane missiles, blowing them all to bits, but not before he was splattered with insect juice and chitin shards.

"Go, Jace! Get out of here!" The archmage raised his staff and broke into a run.

The books around them began to tremble uneasily, like lids on boiling pots. Jace chased after him, shouting, "I didn't know you were afraid of cockroaches!"

"I'm not afraid of cockroaches, I'm afraid of cockroaches bigger than warghs!" Khadgar yelled back. "If these disgusting things stay stuck to me and grow with me when I go back to normal size, by the Holy Light, I swear I'll tear Karazhan apart piece by piece!"

More and more cockroaches crawled up onto the book covers, converging on the mage, the warlock, and the demon. These bugs were too stupid to know what they were hunting, only that fresh meat didn't wander in here every day.

Just as Jace started chanting Shadow Bolt Volley to wipe out all the roaches at once, they suddenly scattered in perfect unison. Khadgar noticed something was off and looked back, still running. "What spell did you use?"

"Haven't even cast anything yet!" Jace shouted.

"Clearly, gentlemen, what came to clean out the cockroaches is here," Morofil said.

As she spoke, an enormous, monstrous head emerged from beneath the books.

A hound. Even if it looked like a small dog compared to the books, compared to the three of them it was a horrifying giant.

Jace immediately raised his staff and channeled a volley of shadow bolts toward the hound's head, sending them arcing through the air. But the moment the first shadow bolt touched its skin, the entire hound dissolved into arcane light and vanished before their eyes. The remaining bolts hit nothing — some striking the floor, others smacking into books.

"Phase magic." Khadgar quickly gathered several arcane missiles and hurled them in a seemingly random direction. Arcane light flared there as the hound rematerialized, immediately surrounded by the mage's missiles — but the instant the first missile touched its hide, it dissolved into arcane light again and vanished.

The other missiles didn't fly off wildly or lose their target. Khadgar guided them, chasing the hound as it appeared and disappeared. But every time the missiles caught it, the hound still vanished into light and reappeared somewhere else. The hound blinked closer and closer, completely unharmed. Finally Khadgar gave up guiding the missiles and tried a blast of fire, but the gathered flame elements hit nothing too.

"Not fast enough... Damn it," Khadgar said.

Jace watched the hound leap over piles of books directly at them. "Get us airborne, Morofil!"

Morofil grabbed them both and flew up. Fast as the succubus was, she was still small, which meant their relative speed was slower too. The hound nimbly jumped onto a nearby bookshelf and launched itself into the air — barely missing swallowing all three of them in one bite.

It crashed into another shelf on the way down, knocking over several books, looked back at the three airborne figures, and estimated the height of the shelves.

Deciding it still had a chance, the hound let out a strange howl and pushed its head through a gap in the bookshelf, apparently trying to jump again.

Khadgar seized the moment and chanted an incantation Jace had never heard before. Then he just twitched his finger. The hound instantly transformed into a fat pig, now several sizes bigger, its neck stuck fast in the gap between the shelves. It convulsed and struggled for a bit before going still, soon reverting to hound form and tumbling off the shelf.

"Fast enough this time," Khadgar murmured.

Jace guessed he'd used some powerful equivalent of Presence of Mind to finally outpace the hound's phase-shifting speed.

"Even the dogs in Karazhan are that strong," Jace marveled.

"If you had unlimited mana, you'd be that strong too." Khadgar looked down at the dead hound, studying it for a while. Then something clicked and his voice dropped. "Oh no. I recognize this. This isn't Karazhan's dog. This is Nielas Aran's dog."

"Who?! Who killed my 'Grave Watcher'?!" A hysterical roar came from deep in the library.
