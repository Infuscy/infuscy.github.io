## Chapter 1455: You've Got a Dog, So Have I

"Nielas Aran? Medivh's father?" Morofil asked as they flew. "Didn't he die in Stormwind a long time ago? Why is he here?"

"He did die, which is exactly why he can be here, Lady Morofil." Khadgar dangled in the air as he answered. "This tower's caretaker, Moroes, once told me that when dark energy gradually twists a person's will... for an ordinary person, the most effective resistance is emotion. Feelings for family, friends, the past... those deep connections are what keep a person anchored to themselves."

"He was probably hinting that his master — Medivh — tried the same. He'd tried countless ways to break free from that spiritual enslavement... The most effective was probably the memories of his father, Master Aran."

"But it failed," Jace said.

"It failed, as everyone knows." Khadgar said. "No matter how beautiful the memories, no matter how strong the will, an ordinary person who's never touched shadow magic can't resist the spiritual invasion of a succubus or a nathrezim. Medivh was no ordinary person, of course — but he was up against Sargeras himself. His failure was nearly inevitable."

"Back in Karazhan, there was a legend that Nielas Aran's ghost wandered the library late at night. Many servants thought his soul had come back to take revenge on his son. But Medivh always avoided the subject. I should have realized it wasn't so simple. I should have understood it was a sign that the tower's master was gradually losing ground in his struggle against Sargeras... I think Sargeras, to completely suppress Medivh's will, peeled away that part of his longing for his father, forming this wandering shade in the tower."

"So we're not facing the real Nielas Aran, but the father in Medivh's mind," Jace said.

"I don't want to make any definitive claims about anything in this tower, Jace... If someone told me Medivh had directly summoned his father's soul here, I wouldn't be surprised either." Khadgar tilted his head as he called out. "And it doesn't matter now. What matters is how to counter him, how to get back to our normal size! Who knows how many more magical pets he's sending our way to tear us apart?"

"What are you thinking?" Jace asked. "We can't even find him. How do we counter him?"

"At least we've seen his magic," Khadgar said. "No, this isn't something from those Counter-Spell course scrolls, so it makes sense you don't know. I don't know why he set up such a large-scale transformation spell. But since the spell is being personally channeled rather than stabilized by a magic item... that means I can disrupt the arcane energy he's channeling back into free-floating arcane power. Tear a hole in his magic. Simply put — I'm going to try casting Blink here."

"You said any transformative magic could backfire in Karazhan's spatial turbulence?" Jace said.

"Yes, there's some risk. But no risk, no reward, right?" Khadgar said. "Unless you've got a better way to break this transformation ritual, apprentice mage."

"This apprentice mage can't cast Blink, but I can still manage to break space." Jace looked up at Morofil, who was carrying him. "Could I try summoning a demon here? Even if the summoning goes wrong... it's the summon that gets messed up."

Khadgar was stunned for a moment, then his eyes lit up. "I don't want you to think I'm just shifting the risk, but that's a good idea I didn't think of in time, Jace! But you don't need to risk anything too extravagant. Just need you to shake up this space a little."

"I understand. Set us down on that shelf ahead, Morofil!" Jace shouted.

Landing on the bookshelf, Jace immediately raised his staff and began chanting to summon a servant. But the demon summoning he knew by heart wasn't going smoothly. Karazhan was connected to the Twisting Nether — it should have been easier to summon here.

But right now his connection to the demons in the void was like a small boat battered by storms — barely keeping itself upright, let alone searching for a target to summon.

Then he noticed the shadow energy he was channeling was forming a distorted vortex. Waves of arcane energy were mixing into the vortex like ink of different colors... Archmage Nielas Aran's space magic was tearing at his summoning.

Jace immediately focused, channeling his emotions to strengthen the summoning ritual, pushing back against the arcane energy maintaining the space's integrity. That was when Khadgar came up to him.

"Hold it, Jace! Keep going with the summoning!"

The mage raised both hands, weaving them through the chaotic mixture of arcane and shadow energies. He muttered under his breath...

Jace felt the arcane forces that had been whipping his shadow magic around being guided away by Khadgar, starting to dance to the mage's gestures. Then Khadgar suddenly threw his hands up. The whole darkened library lit up as if struck by lightning. Unbroken waves of arcane distortion began to warp their vision.

"Who?! Who... Do you worthless thieves have any idea what you're doing?! You'll disturb the peace here, and that is an unforgivable crime!"

A string of reprimands came from deep in the library. Khadgar ignored him, gritting his teeth and continuing to channel. Rivers of churning arcane energy flashed between shelves, lifting rugs, blowing books into the air and stirring up dust. Jace's summoning ritual was torn apart in the wild arcane storm — no point continuing.

"Arrogant vandals! You're only making things worse!"

The voice cursed through clenched teeth, sounding like it was being pushed to some limit. But finally it dissolved into coughing and shouting. At the same time, Khadgar clapped his hands together, and the scene before them flickered and blurred in a flash of arcane light...

"Ah... AHHH!"

As the hollow, pale roars echoed through the library, Jace watched his own body swell up, section by section, accompanied by the roar of arcane power and the metallic tang in the air. Soon he banged his head on a shelf and tumbled backward, crashing into a pile of books. Little cockroaches scattered everywhere, fleeing...

Jace climbed up, holding onto a ladder beside him. Not far away, he saw a skeleton lying crookedly on the ground, still wearing a purple robe. Even through the holes chewed by rats and bugs, he could still see the Violet Eye emblem on it.

"Kirin Tor's insolent whelps never learn to restrain their greed or their desires! Strolling into another's home, disturbing another's life. On a normal day, I might show leniency. But not today. Today, I must personally teach you the manners Antonidas should have taught you."

A low, muttering chant filled the library. The strange shrinking spell had been broken, but the arcane flow hadn't stopped. Jace could still feel arcane energy circulating from somewhere deep in the library, channeling the power of all Karazhan.

Khadgar jumped down from a shelf and looked into the distance. "Aran is trying to channel another space spell to trap us in here, Jace. We need to find him before he finishes casting!"

Jace nodded. The three of them ran toward the distant door. A shadowy figure already seemed to be forming in the doorway. Their pace quickened.

But they hadn't gone more than a few steps before the shelves on either side suddenly slid apart simultaneously. New shelves came rushing in horizontally like trains, cutting Jace off from Khadgar.

The entire library erupted in a deafening roar, the ringing of arcane energy mixed in, making their minds go fuzzy. Near and far, wall-like shelves crisscrossed and interleaved, dizzying in their chaos.

"Find Nielas Aran! Find Nielas Aran!"

Khadgar's shouts grew fainter and fainter until nothing. Jace didn't have time to listen further — he heard the thunder of a shelf charging at him from behind.

He spun around just in time to see a shelf about to slam into him and Morofil. He instinctively dove right. The succubus dissolved into shadow and instantly reappeared behind him, dodging the collision. The next few shelves that tried to separate them also failed.

When the shelves stopped and silence returned, the layout of this book section had completely changed. Jace looked in the direction they'd been heading — even the nearby doorway was gone.

"Khadgar!" Jace shouted. No answer.

"These shelf movements aren't as simple as they look, master," Morofil said. "We might have been separated into different planes."

"Learn to respect others, you noisy young people. This is the greatest library in the seven kingdoms, recording endless knowledge from Kalimdor to the Twisting Nether. You struggled through countless hardships to reach this place only to disturb its peace. How disappointing..." The voice came from deeper in the library again, but this time it carried a note of triumphant smugness.

"Find Nielas Aran. Well then, time to go a bit bigger." Jace repeated Khadgar's last words, calmly drank a flask of Purified Intelligence Elixir.

Then he channeled himself into shadowform, steadied his breathing, and began chanting the summoning spell again.

The spatial disturbance wasn't as strong this time. Maybe Aran's current spell wasn't as carefully prepared as his last one, or maybe it was a matter of shifting power dynamics.

Either way, after pushing back against the arcane energy for a while, Jace managed to tear open a demonic rift between the shelves.

He stared at the interdimensional tear slowly splitting open before him, pushing his consciousness into the shadows and pulling. Eventually, he dragged out a sturdy felhound with whip-like tendrils thrashing all over its body.

"Dazharm VI." Jace completed the naming in Annihilan, opened his eyes blazing with purple fel flame, and said in demon tongue, "Lead the way."

Morofil smiled slightly, watching the newborn creature of the void. The felhound let out a strange sound like breaking bones — "clack-clack-crack" — then turned and bolted its bulging crimson body deep between the shelves.
