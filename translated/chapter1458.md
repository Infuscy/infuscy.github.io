## Chapter 1458: Fighting From the Unknown Corner

Torn-up spellbooks and broken pages drifted through the air in the circular hall like confetti, dreamlike against the star-like free-floating arcane energies.

Dazharm VI had fallen in frozen chunks. Morofil retrieved the blade she'd thrown — Apocalypse — and returned to Jace's side, eyes fixed on the archmage's shade still crumpled in the corner, never dropping her guard.

"Master Aran." Jace tried to get his attention. The shade didn't respond.

He just kept his head down, muttering brokenly about "won't forget," "demon," "Burning Legion," "finishing the task."

Jace looked up and decided not to push for more information. The last thing he needed was to say something off-key, set the shade off, and stir up more trouble.

The problem now was — where was Khadgar?

He'd thought when Khadgar told him to find Nielas Aran, they'd take the former master of Karazhan on together. Instead, the guy had wandered off somewhere for the whole fight.

Without Khadgar, Jace didn't think he could leave Karazhan alive, let alone find knowledge about shadow magic in the library.

Especially the things Nielas Aran had said when he thought Jace was Medivh — warnings about the Curator not liking demons in the library, not even ones Medivh brought in.

The whole library stank of arcane metal and shadowy gore, but there was hardly any demon stench. Even though Morofil was very good at masking her scent, she couldn't hide it perfectly here. No wonder those two arcane constructs on the spiral staircase had found her so fast.

The two of them left the circular hall, planning to go deeper into the library and look for where that mage had gone. Morofil walked beside him, still seeming caught up in the recent fight.

"You alright?"

The succubus snapped out of it. The flames in her eyes reignited, and the calm, faintly smiling look returned.

"What could be wrong, master?" she said as they walked. "We won the battle. Even if it was lucky."

"Back there..." Jace said. "I had to use the Fear spell to keep all the apparitions under control and make room for Drain Soul."

"No need to feel guilty, dear." Morofil leaned in and gave Jace a light kiss on the cheek. "Using memories from the soul to provoke emotions, capturing them, then Fear to turn those emotions, then anchoring the Drain Soul on their fear... you're getting more and more like a powerful eredar soul-twister."

"Special praise from a succubus. That can't be good." Jace said. "Actually, I'm curious — what did you see? I mean in the Fear spell..."

Morofil's expression dimmed a little. "What else? What I fear most is... losing my wings again."

"That would be horrible," Jace agreed. The succubus followed behind, watching her master as he walked deeper into the hallway, not looking away for a long time.

Just then, a tabby cat jumped down from a nearby shelf.

"The library was upside down!" it said in Common. Jace wasn't startled, though — this was Karazhan after all.

"Careful. The upside-down hall." It lifted its head and looked at Jace, repeating. "What?" Jace thought he'd misheard.

"Careful." The cat's pupils dilated as it stared at Jace seriously. "The library was flipped."

"I know. It's clearly right-side up now. Thanks for the warning." Jace studied the cat. Something about it felt familiar.

He glanced at Morofil beside him. Master and servant said almost in unison, "Polymorph."

"Library is upside down!" The cat's voice went hoarse with agitation. It circled Jace, repeating over and over, "Upside down... careful, it's upside down!"

Jace pressed his lips together, steeled himself, and booted the cat across the room. It yowled, tumbled onto a shelf, and hurriedly scrambled back, still shouting at them, "Ups... upsidedown!"

"How is this polymorph so hard to break?" Jace asked the succubus. "Is this a real magical cat?"

"Try lighting it on fire, master." Morofil suggested with narrowed eyes. "I've never seen a polymorph that couldn't be burned off..."

"Isn't that a bit much?" Jace summoned a small flame in his hand.

The cat seemed to understand. Its eyes went wide as it backed away, hesitating. "Library... careful..."

"You don't look like you think it's too much," Morofil smiled.

"I really don't." Jace decisively set the cat's tail on fire. With a whoosh, the flames went up. The cat shrieked and tore around the circular hall.

As it ran, it trailed wisps of arcane mist, its body growing larger and larger. Soon it became a white-haired man frantically slapping at the flames on his robe.

"Ow... my god. MY GOD!"

Khadgar put out the flames on his sleeves, caught his breath, and leaned on his knees. "You defeated Nielas Aran! Jace Sesso... and in Karazhan, no less. That's... that's just incredible."

"But I almost died in there!" Jace pointed at the door to the hall. "More importantly, where the hell did you wander off to the whole time? Weren't you going to come in with me to take him on?"

"That was the plan..." Khadgar walked closer, lowering his voice. "When I saw he didn't seem too hostile, I wanted to talk it out, ask him to let us live. But he flung me away without a word, didn't give me a chance to explain. He'd taken control of the entire library — all directions, left and right — Jace, I couldn't fight that. He even counterspelled my Slow Fall, and while I was reeling, he casually turned me into a magical cat!"

"I had to accept the transformation. A magical cat is a strange little beast accustomed to living in wizard towers — it was my only chance to survive. If I'd fought the transformation spell, I'd have died for sure on impact. So I accepted it and waited for an opening. But who knew the polymorph would last that long? And be so hard to break?!"

"Besides, it's not like I contributed nothing to your fight. Master Aran had to split his focus to maintain such a strong polymorph on me — that must have helped you gain an advantage against him... And I did try hard to warn you about the problem in that hall."

"After I'd already beaten the archmage," Jace said.

Khadgar cleared his throat. "Alright, maybe a little late. But regardless — when you two get back, don't mention this to anyone..."

"You think we'll make it back?" Jace asked.

"Trust me." Khadgar said. "We're about to see the Curator. You heard what Master Aran said — it's still running things here. Even he had to show it respect. Because the Curator can restore order to this tower."

"I hope so." Jace didn't sound convinced.

He looked back at the faint shade in the hall. "But are you sure this is really just a fragment of Medivh's longing split off into a shade? I think he felt real."

"If you think so..." Khadgar said. "I truly hope his soul can travel to the Shadowlands instead of being trapped here, suffering. Either way, thank you for showing mercy at the end."

"He reminded me of my own father," Jace said. "I couldn't do it."

Khadgar nodded. They stood in silence for a moment. Then the mage pointed to a corridor in the distance. "That way. From there, we can reach the hall where the Curator waits."

"Master Aran mentioned something," Jace said. "Aegwynn wanted him to help find... a demon's magic. Sounded like some kind of critical clue."

"Any more detail?" Khadgar asked. Jace shook his head. "He's not talking to me anymore."

"They were both masters of demonic research." Khadgar frowned, thinking. "Maybe it's a fragment left in his memory — when he was alive, Aegwynn asked him to help look up some information. Guardians have to know all kinds of demon magic inside out. Hard to give you a specific answer."

"Did you see Master Aran when you came here with Aegwynn last time?" Jace asked.

"Of course not." Khadgar said. "We didn't go nearly this high — we were just tapping into Karazhan's ley line node... and she built this tower, so with her around, there was no trouble at all. But if you're really bothered by this, once the Curator restores order and is willing to talk, I'll ask around and see if I can find some leads for you."

"Thanks. I'd appreciate that." Jace said.

"Don't mention it, Jace." Khadgar's gaze fixed on the door at the end of the corridor.
