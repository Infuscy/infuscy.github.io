## Chapter 1460: Traces of the Search

"No comment on everything, huh." Jace said. "Aren't you Medivh's student? Why don't you have access?"

"What kind of student controls his teacher's library, Jace?" Khadgar turned back to the construct. "Then can you at least tell me where Lady Aegwynn is in the tower right now, Curator? I'd like to meet her. Ask her a few things."

"Record unavailable." The Curator's voice grew flatter. "Lady Aegwynn left the Grand Library at an unknown time. I cannot provide her current location."

"Who played Little Red in the last performance of Little Red Riding Hood and the Big Bad Wolf at the theater?" Jace asked.

"My records do not cover the Grand Theater, Master Jace Sesso. You should consult the stage manager, Mr. Barnes, for such questions," the Curator replied.

"So when he doesn't know, he doesn't know." Jace looked at Khadgar. "And what he does know but can't say — no comment."

"So Aegwynn really did come to the library looking for books." Khadgar rubbed his chin, thinking. "What was she reading, I wonder..."

"Can you provide the Grand Library's borrowing records for the last two months, esteemed Curator?"

"No comment." The Curator said coldly.

"Let's step outside and talk." Jace pointed toward the exhibition hall's exit.

"Fine." Khadgar glanced at the Curator one more time. "We'll take our leave."

"The exhibition hall is always open for your return." With that, the Curator turned and walked deeper into the hall.

Back in the dark corridor between the old book section and the exhibition hall, they waited until the heavy footsteps faded completely. Then Khadgar asked, "What now?"

"If Aegwynn is in the tower, go find her and ask?" Jace said. "I have a feeling... she wants to see us."

"Wants to see us." Khadgar said. "By sending an ancient dragon that magic could barely touch to ambush us? Forcing us to perform on Karazhan's big stage? Trapping us between two arcane constructs? And letting her dear husband deliver the finishing blow?"

"Unless it's some kind of trial." He pinched the bridge of his nose. "She wants to test us."

"Test us for what?" Jace asked.

"For the next Guardian of Tirisfal, obviously." Morofil answered almost immediately.

"Guardian?!" Khadgar stared. "Don't just say whatever comes to mind, demon. We have enough trouble as it is!"

"I was just joking, mage." Morofil said. "Don't get worked up."

"Forget I ever mentioned trials." Khadgar shook his head. "And it's not just obstacles either. What about Little Red? What was that whole bizarre play about being afraid of demons supposed to mean?"

Medivh's heart speaking out, Jace thought silently. Of course, he didn't voice the possibility again.

He knew why Khadgar got upset whenever anyone suggested Medivh might have come back and summoned demons.

Because back in that assault on Karazhan ten-odd years ago, the greatest achievement Khadgar and Lothar had made was purging the evil demon from within Medivh.

And when Medivh did return to this world later, it was solely to unite Azeroth's factions against the coming apocalypse.

But the residual shade of Medivh still in the tower retained its old chaos. In the game, he liked controlling things in the tower to harass intruders, sometimes even showing up in person.

But now, the lingering shade had never appeared to the three of them, and Jace had no direct way to tell Khadgar about that possibility.

More importantly, the Karazhan of right now was different from the game.

Arcanagos hadn't burned out yet. The Curator hadn't lost control. Moroes hadn't stopped the theater's performances. If not for these three intruders stumbling in, the tower seemed to be maintaining pretty "normal" operations.

Jace felt the more he thought, the more confused he got. He increasingly felt he should just discard his memories from his previous life and organize everything using only the evidence at hand.

But doing that felt like letting go of the rope that had kept him from falling into the well — plunging into a bottomless abyss with nothing to hold onto.

He'd just used his knowledge of the twisted relationship between Nielas Aran and his wife to defeat him, after all. He'd keep milking the advantage of having seen this story before.

At the same time, he felt he was beginning to understand Velen's suffering. Foreknowledge wasn't always a blessing. Sometimes it was more like a curse — especially when you had no idea whether it was even true.

"We still need to find a way to question the Curator." Jace said. "See if we can pry more information out of it. Doesn't seem very smart to me."

"The problem isn't whether it's smart." Khadgar said quietly. "The problem is that a lot of the arcane guardians in Karazhan have already gone rogue. How do you know the Curator won't turn on us if you keep pushing its boundaries?"

"Is there a magic artifact that could give us higher authority?" Jace asked. "Something Medivh left behind, maybe. You'd know."

Khadgar thought for a moment, then looked toward the circular hall in the old book section. "No artifact. But there is a person — Nielas Aran himself. Before his death, this tower was actually managed by him. For over a decade, he was effectively the master of this tower."

Jace said, "I just fought him and had to drag his wife into it. I'd be lucky if he doesn't want round two. And you want me to ask him for help? If he was willing to help, he'd have just told us what books Aegwynn asked him to find."

Wait. Why not use sympathetic resonance? If Aegwynn had come back to Karazhan and asked Aran's shade to help her find specific books in the Grand Library, his shade should have left clear enough traces.

"Use sympathetic resonance, Khadgar." Jace said.

"Use sympathetic resonance to track Aegwynn?!" Khadgar inhaled sharply, as if he'd heard something earth-shattering. "The Order of Tirisfal tracked Aegwynn for how many years — how many people died trying — and you only now think of using sympathetic resonance on her?!"

"To track Nielas Aran's traces. Strictly speaking, his shade's traces." Jace said. "We gamble that what he said about helping Aegwynn was about the task she gave him on this visit to Karazhan."

"That is actually a workable idea." The mage narrowed his eyes. "Didn't know you knew about that. Aren't you an apprentice mage? Does the Wizard Sanctum teach this kind of stuff to apprentices?"

"Never mind where I learned it, Khadgar. I already know how to summon all kinds of demons." Jace said.

"Fair enough." Khadgar said.

Jace certainly wasn't going to tell Khadgar that he'd read about it in a novel where Khadgar was the main character.

"Using sympathetic resonance to track a mage is a serious crime in Dalaran." Khadgar looked at the nearby skeleton of a Kirin Tor wizard and lowered his voice. "But I don't think they'll be able to come inside Karazhan to collect evidence of my crime. Follow me."

Back in the circular hall, all three of them stopped at the threshold at the same time.

Aran's shade seemed to have returned to normal. He was now picking up books scattered on the floor, floating up high to put them back, completely ignoring the two people who'd just fought him walking in below.

Khadgar waited for the shade to float higher, then sprinted over, grabbed a book that fell from the shade's arms, and waved frantically, mouthing, "Go, go."

Once they'd slipped out, Khadgar immediately cast a spell on the book and said, "Strong magical residue — he's basically a shadow made of magic himself... Now I just need to track the magical traces."

For someone who claimed sympathetic tracking was forbidden by the Kirin Tor, Khadgar looked awfully excited doing it.

The two of them went back to the Curator, said their greetings, and passed through the exhibition hall into the library proper. This was Karazhan's real Grand Library, not the old book section guarded by Nielas Aran.

Below the steps, the square hall before them was lined with enormous bookcases in neat rows, like colorful wall tiles — they blurred together the longer you looked.

The library wasn't just one hall. There were staircases leading up to other library halls, with no walls separating them. One square hall after another lay within sight, like boxes. Jace couldn't believe such architecture could exist in the real world... but then, Karazhan had long since surpassed what anyone would call reality.

Khadgar didn't seem surprised. As Jace gaped at the halls, the mage quickly ran between these "cubes," gathering books with sympathetic traces. Eventually he found an arcane guardian from another cube to help carry over a huge pile of scrolls and records, all dumped in front of Jace.

Jace looked at the towering stack of books. "How long is this going to take?"

"Don't you see what they have in common?" Khadgar caught his breath, flipping through them one by one. "Look at the subjects. Burning Legion. Dark Titan. Twisting Nether travel. Soul magic. Portals. Eredar warlocks. Portal guardians. Burning Legion conquest records. Resisting demons."

"Master Aran also mentioned something about a demon's magic. Could be related."

Jace flipped through the scrolls as he spoke, noticing many of them were by Aegwynn — on demonic crafting materials, soul energy... It reminded him of what Sabellian had said about Aegwynn.

"The Scepter of Sargeras," he murmured.

"You noticed too." Khadgar tossed a tome on top of the pile. "I think so too. A lot of it is research on how to destroy the Scepter of Sargeras. So maybe we wasted our time. Aran's shade memory might actually be damaged."

Jace didn't answer. Because at the bottom, under a heavy grimoire, was a scroll. Packed with dense, obscure demonic runes — and among them, one name he knew intimately.

Usaalus.
