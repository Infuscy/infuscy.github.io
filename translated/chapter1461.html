Chapter 1461: Aegwynn and the Scepter of Sargeras?

Did she know about this staff?

Khadgar was still flipping through the piles of books and scrolls, looking for other clues that might tie everything together. Jace gazed up at the high steps and the edges of the floors above, as if searching for some figure among those empty railings.

But after watching for a long time, nothing seemed amiss.

These materials were definitely related to destroying the Scepter of Sargeras. Sabellian had said that when Deathwing searched the Order of Tirisfal's library, trying to find the Scepter of Sargeras' weaknesses, he'd come across Aegwynn's research notes — which included using the demonic artifact Usaalus, mentioned by Guardian Scavill.

This scroll was probably a copy of the document Sabellian had mentioned... though considering this was Karazhan, maybe what Deathwing saw was the copy, and this scroll was the original.

Jace picked it up. According to Kirin Tor records, Aegwynn had been obsessed with destroying the Scepter of Sargeras for centuries already. But this scroll showed no signs of aging, even though the date on it read "Year 122."

That was the Guardians' own calendar system — starting from the year each Guardian took office. Aegwynn's Year 122 was around 700 years before the Dark Portal.

A time when she still hadn't completely broken with the Order of Tirisfal and Dalaran, when she was still within the "term limits" most council members had allowed her.

But by that point, she had already defeated Sargeras' avatar, sealing the fel-corrupted body, the Scepter of Sargeras, and other Burning Legion artifacts left over from their invasion of Northrend beneath the Temple of the Moon in Suramar — what would later be called the Tomb of Sargeras.

Even though Jace was now quite familiar with demonic — or rather, eredar — language, and had studied its various dialects, the demonic text on this scroll still gave him a headache. It took considerable effort to work through.

The demonic passage roughly described the origin of Usaalus — a soul-cage forged by the Dark Titan from the soul of a powerful demon lord who had dared to oppose him. It also recorded how its soul-power could tear apart the most powerful soul-forged creations in the Twisting Nether.

Aegwynn had written her own notes in Common below, documenting details her teacher Scavill had told her to corroborate this. She even included a detail Jace had never heard of before.

When Scavill had fought Sataiel, Usaalus' previous owner, the weapon had effortlessly cut through the Guardian's staff — Atiesh. This was probably what inspired Aegwynn to use it to destroy the Scepter of Sargeras.

But it was precisely because Scavill's staff was severed that Sataiel had grown overconfident, exposing too many openings, letting the Guardian seize the opportunity for a desperate counterattack.

However, Sataiel's death caused Usaalus' power to swell to an uncontrollable degree. Scavill had no way to master it, no way to destroy it. He could only send it to a place no one knew.

A travelogue beside it mentioned that Aegwynn had spent decades searching, but had never found where Usaalus was hidden.

She'd even written several lines of furious complaint about it. She noted that Scavill had deliberately erased all information about Usaalus' location, appearance, magical traces — everything that could help find it — so thoroughly that even his successor, who'd inherited his knowledge and power, had nothing to go on.

Aegwynn specifically mentioned that these memories had been magically erased so cleanly that not even the source showed any trace.

Jace couldn't help wondering what this "source" really was. Had she tried raising her teacher from the dead with necromancy?

Considering that before Medivh's birth, Sargeras' soul had been attached to Aegwynn. Even if Sargeras couldn't fully control a powerful Guardian the way he controlled the young Medivh, centuries of subtle influence had gradually made her extreme and twisted. Maybe Scavill had erased everything so thoroughly precisely to prevent future Guardians from becoming a second Sataiel.

Whatever the case, Jace looked at the brief notation and couldn't avoid thinking about worst-case scenarios.

"Damn. I don't even know what to say." Khadgar muttered beside him. "So many of these books and records — any one of them would cause an uproar if it found its way to the Kirin Tor. And here they're as freely available as apples on a Dalaran roadside. I'm almost starting to wonder if the Dark Titan left these here deliberately — fake documents meant to mislead our research, designed to send scavengers like the Violet Eye down the wrong path."

Even as he said it, he was happily flipping through each scroll and manual under the pretense of looking for Aegwynn's clues, completely absorbed. Every now and then he'd glance at the arcane constructs passing in the distance, like a student reading contraband under the teacher's nose during study hall.

He showed no interest whatsoever in Jace's findings about Usaalus. He didn't spare even a glance at the blood-stained, cloth-wrapped staff at Jace's side.

In his eyes, it was just an evil demon-bone staff — perfectly suited for a warlock who liked to summon demons for fun. Not particularly remarkable in a twisted place like Karazhan.

Like Aegwynn, Khadgar had no idea what Usaalus looked like or what magical signature it carried.

If Scavill's secret vault barrier was still intact, untouched by satyrs or Burning Legion demons — if Sargeras-possessed Medivh hadn't sent those black knights out searching... Scavill's security measures could have kept Usaalus buried there forever.

But now, his insurance had become the biggest obstacle for Kirin Tor mages trying to track the staff down — and a kind of protection for its current owner.

His barrier had locked that place away for nearly a thousand years. Even Aegwynn, possessed by Sargeras, had been completely clueless about it. Hard to call Scavill's measures a failure.

Aegwynn's meeting with him on that road in front of the Pig and Whistle proved it too.

If she'd known Usaalus' magical signature, Jace didn't think a few stone walls and a rune-wrapped cloth would have stopped Aegwynn's senses.

If she'd confirmed Usaalus' existence and wanted to take it by force, Jace wouldn't have survived that rainy night.

So why, after that day, did she immediately return to Karazhan and wake her husband's spirit-shade, having him help search through centuries-old records about the Scepter of Sargeras in this library?

The Scepter of Sargeras had already been destroyed. Aegwynn knew that perfectly well — she'd even thanked Jace for completing what she couldn't do. So if this wasn't about searching for evidence, confirming that Jace had used Usaalus to destroy the staff, it was a waste of effort.

But on the other hand, besides Usaalus, there were countless other methods laid out here for destroying the Scepter of Sargeras and the Dark Titan's avatar. If Jace weren't holding it right now, the name Usaalus wouldn't stand out among this mountain of books.

For example, she'd once tried asking the priests of Tyr for help, using powerful Holy Light to destroy the scepter. It had barely worked.

So she thought maybe stronger Holy Light would do the trick — like the primordial Holy Light from the deepest Twisting Nether, better known as the naaru.

There was also the Focusing Iris of the Spellweaver Malygos. She'd considered using her favor — saving the blue dragonflight from Sargeras' avatar — to borrow it from the Spellweaver, but she'd never actually gone through with it for various reasons.

She'd even considered the Defiler Archimonde's Finger of Death, along with some even more extreme methods...

But Aegwynn had never gotten around to trying all these approaches before falling out with the Order of Tirisfal. So those plans were abandoned too.

Until that night, when Jace told her he'd destroyed the Scepter of Sargeras using soul-power. The look on Aegwynn's face when she heard that answer was complicated.

At the time, Jace had assumed she looked tired because she was preparing to resurrect Medivh. Now it seemed more like an expression of — "so that was all it took" — some hard-to-describe feeling.

At the very least, it didn't look like she was certain Jace held Usaalus.

Thinking about it, Jace felt like he was falling into a trap of drawing the target after shooting the arrow.

Maybe all of this was just his paranoia about Aegwynn. Just like Khadgar got flustered around Medivh's whole family. Maybe Archmage Aran's memories really were just garbled, like Khadgar had said.

He let out a long sigh and leaned back in his chair.

The chairs in Karazhan's Grand Library were beautifully designed and comfortable to sit in. They might even have enchantments to spark inspiration or something.

Jace stared at the neatly arranged book spines across from him, and a thought suddenly struck him.

What if someone had specifically told her?

Who?

The people who knew Jace had Usaalus, who were still alive — there weren't many. Grede, Vereesa Saur...

They were completely out of the question. Grede loved to brag, but he'd never mentioned Jace's staff to anyone.

Needlefire? That black dragon still held a grudge against all of humanity. No way he'd share such sensitive information with a completely strange and dangerous woman.

Unless Aegwynn was willing to waste her mana mind-probing them one by one. But considering how careful she'd been at the Pig and Whistle — and how sensitive the Kirin Tor presence in Stormwind had been at the time — she probably wouldn't risk such easily-detected moves.

And if she'd actually extracted solid evidence from their minds, would she have needed to make this whole trip back to Karazhan for more evidence? She didn't seem that certain.

So — Sabellian?

Never mind that he was probably off in Draenor trading fangs with fel orcs. Even if you didn't trust his moral fiber, his precious daughter was still in Jace's hands.

Then Jace suddenly realized he'd missed a name.

In the cottage deep in that forest, the eyes of the demon-turned-she-wolf blazed again. Little Red's tearful confession seemed to echo in his ears.
