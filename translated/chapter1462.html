Chapter 1462: So It Still Had to Be Done That Way

Except for Scavell, none of the members of the Order of Tirisfal knew what Ulthalesh looked like. Many mages had never even heard its name.

But the terrible disaster in Deadwind Pass a thousand years ago — the story of a monstrous demonic warlock wielding a soul-reaping weapon who nearly destroyed the world — had been widely told for a long time. In those days, Scavell had counted the killing of that great demon and the destruction of its weapon as his greatest achievement as Guardian.

Clearly, Aegwynn — his successor — wasn't the only one who knew the staff had never truly been destroyed. The rumor that the Deadwind Reaper still existed had spread for centuries among wild warlocks, necromantic researchers, and even the descendants of Sargeras's followers and apprentices. Many had tried to find it, and it had long been a thorn in the Order of Tirisfal's side.

If Aegwynn had determined that Ulthalesh was in Jace's hands, then as the former Guardian, she would very likely use extreme methods to eliminate this threat.

And if the Deceiver, Kil'jaeden, was the one who'd told Aegwynn about this, then this must be his goal — to use Aegwynn's hand to kill Jace, remove this obstacle, and reclaim the ground lost in Draenor.

But there were still unanswered questions.

One was: how had Kil'jaeden told Aegwynn?

Even the Kirin Tor, who were on Azeroth, couldn't find Aegwynn. How had Kil'jaeden made contact with her?

Through Karazhan? Then the timing and place didn't match up.

When Aegwynn and Khadgar first came to Karazhan, searching for the coordinates of the Telogrus Rift, Kil'jaeden should have still been behind the scenes, calculating and pondering exactly how many souls Jace would need to drain before he could use Ulthalesh to sever the Scepter of Sargeras.

According to his estimation, if Jace was willing to pay any price to gather enough souls, he would very likely end up at odds with the Expeditionary Force, the Black Dragonflight, and even Kil'jaeden's own servants — to the point where he could never reach the Scepter of Sargeras, or would be twisted into a soul-devourer driven by desire.

If Jace proceeded cautiously every step of the way, then he would ultimately be unable to completely destroy the Scepter of Sargeras. Which was what actually happened... and then Jace, or Sabellian, would inevitably have to pick up the scepter themselves to try to salvage everything, falling right into Kil'jaeden's trap.

While scheming all of this, had Kil'jaeden also found a way to wait for Aegwynn in Karazhan and tell her about Ulthalesh?

Judging by the Burning Legion's reaction on Draenor, the Dealer seemed to have only realized the existence of the Telogrus portal in the final days — by which time Khadgar and Aegwynn had already left Karazhan.

Not to mention that Khadgar had been by her side the whole time. If Kil'jaeden had tried to send Aegwynn a message, Khadgar would likely have noticed.

"Khadgar." Jace put down the scroll in his hand and asked, "When you and Lady Aegwynn came last time... did you encounter anyone? Like members of the Violet Eye, or demons invading Karazhan?"

"No." Khadgar looked at the book in his hands and thought for a moment before saying, "Or rather, we did run into a few."

"But Lady Aegwynn took me through hidden magic and her familiarity with the tower to avoid them. No conflict at all. She seemed unwilling to waste even a bit of mana on those demons."

After saying this, he quickly added, "Of course, I'm not saying that by comparison, the two of us are far dumber. I'm very glad I brought you along, Jace. I'm grateful. I don't know what's happened to Karazhan. I expected some trouble, but I didn't expect it to have changed so completely from the last time I was here. If I'd come alone, I would have ended up in a very bad state."

"No need to thank me." Jace nodded.

So it seemed that if Kil'jaeden had made Aegwynn aware of this, it must have happened at some point after the Expedition returned to Azeroth — after Aegwynn had seen Jace but before she returned to Karazhan to search for evidence.

But after returning from Azeroth, even Khadgar hadn't seen Aegwynn. He'd only met her for that one disjointed encounter. Who knew what she'd been doing the rest of the time?

"I need to see Aegwynn again, Khadgar," Jace said.

"Where? You want to find her in this tower? Didn't I just tell you—"

Before Khadgar could finish, Jace added, "Whether she's still in this tower or not, I need to see her. I have things I need to explain."

"About how to destroy the Scepter of Sargeras?" Khadgar asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

"Aegwynn made it very clear to me that she doesn't want anyone guiding anyone else to find her again, Jace. Have you forgotten?" Khadgar sighed. "She said that no matter who came looking for her, she would kill them without asking why, and then blame me for it. And now, after she told me that, the first person to ask me to find her is you? Makes my earlier efforts feel like a complete waste."

"I want to clear up some misunderstandings," Jace said.

"I don't know the specifics of the misunderstanding." Khadgar set the book down gently on a small table beside him and stretched his neck. "But trust me, Jace. Given her personality, her past behavior — if she suspects you of something, or... to put it bluntly, if she plans to kill you, she would never hide behind illusions, traps, or games of cat and mouse. Before, when she wanted to figure out how you destroyed the Scepter of Sargeras, she flew straight to Stormwind and asked you directly. That's the Aegwynn I know. That's the archmage who destroyed the Demon Lord himself."

"Over a decade ago, when she sensed her son seemed to be trying to summon demons to Azeroth, she immediately came to Karazhan and fought a great battle with him. She told me this herself when she and I entered Karazhan. Even though she ultimately lost and was thrown out of this tower... regardless, do you think you're more important to her than her son? Enough for her to go through all this scheming?"

Khadgar was right. That was the second question. Why hadn't Aegwynn simply come and dealt with him?

If Little Red Riding Hood was Aegwynn in disguise, why hadn't she just said everything clearly — like she did outside the Pig and Whistle — instead of putting on this elaborate performance? And why use magic to dress up Morofil's demonic eye to look like Kil'jaeden?

Karazhan was indeed a strange and mysterious place. But was it really this chaotic? After mulling it over for a while, Jace ventured, "Do me a favor, Khadgar."

"Use sympathetic tracking to find Aegwynn." Khadgar lowered his eyes, flipping through a book as he spoke.

"You guessed it," Jace said quietly.

The mage looked up and tossed the book aside.

"Jace Sesso of Brill! The Blood Demon Hunter, the Annihilator of the Pit Lord, the Hero Who Defeated the Black Dragon Princess, the Destroyer of Deathwing, the Benefactor of the Sons of Lothar, the Savior of the Alliance Expedition, the Blade That Shattered the Scepter of Sargeras, the Enemy of Kil'jaeden the Deceiver, the Protector of Tyr, the Lord of Ilgalaratha, the Good Husband of Lady Vereesa Windrunner... future husband!"

He blew out his beard and spat out this entire string of titles in one breath, widening his eyes and puffing out his chest for a moment before pointing at his own heart. "If you want me dead, you don't need to go through all this trouble! Just take your sword and give it to me right here. That'd be a lot cleaner!"

"Do you have any idea how good Aegwynn is at avoiding being tracked? She's probably the best mage in history at it! And I'm not even adding 'one of'!"

"I know," Jace said, lowering his head. "You've said that before."

Looking at the warlock across from him, Khadgar took a few even breaths, calmly picked up an empty glass beside him, and by the time it reached his lips, it was already filled with wine.

"Fortunately," he said, taking a sip, "I happen to be the best tracking mage in the world. Well, one of them. More importantly, you're Jace Sesso. At the end of the day, I owe you my life."

"Thank you, Khadgar. I'll remember this." Jace nodded.

"But only within this tower, Jace." Khadgar raised a finger and drew a circle above his head. "Not just because she's cast many spells in here, making tracking her comparatively easier. It's also because if Aegwynn really is in here, it means she might actually want to see you. So looking for her probably won't get us killed — at least not me. I can always say I was coerced by an evil warlock. Hopefully, for her son's sake... ahem."

"If we manage to leave this tower safely, it means Aegwynn never intended to hurt me in the first place," Jace finished his thought.

"Exactly," Khadgar said.

"Your call," Jace said.

"Then let's start by finding traces Aegwynn left behind." Khadgar brushed the dust off his robes from the books and stood up. "Come with me to find the Curator. She talked with him, and it sounded fairly casual. She might have forgotten to erase her tracks... but don't get your hopes up too high."
