## Chapter 1468: The Ritual in the Observatorium

The magic circle beneath the telescope was incredibly complex. Its runes were a combination of what was called "giant language" — the language left by the Titans — along with ancient Highborne script. With Jace's level of knowledge, deciphering it would be extremely difficult.

Still, Jace could at least tell it wasn't related to void magic. There was no shadow energy, and of course it wasn't a demonic fel circle either. Judging by the linework, it had nothing to do with the Elemental Planes.

Khadgar rubbed his lip and his partially burned beard, staring at the circle for a long time without speaking. That at least gave Jace some comfort — if even the current spiritual leader of the Kirin Tor couldn't figure it out, then it was probably normal that he couldn't either.

Maybe he could take advantage of Khadgar's uncertainty to gently nudge him toward the nature of the ritual.

"Can you tell what kind of circle this is?" Jace ventured. "A necromantic ritual?"

Khadgar looked over, somewhat surprised. "Why would you think it's necromancy?"

"I didn't identify it visually, of course," Jace said. "I just thought... there are ghosts everywhere along the way, and undead skeletal guards. The whole Karazhan and Deadwind Pass is a necropolis full of reanimated corpses. That's what you told me, isn't it?"

"But this isn't necromancy, Jace." Khadgar pointed at the star charts above and the telescope overhead. "Lady Aegwynn chose to perform this magical ritual here for a reason. This is a large-scale divination."

"Is it the location spell for the Telogrus Rift?" Jace said. "You told me that was accomplished through some kind of divination magic too."

"The Telogrus ritual wasn't performed here." Khadgar crouched down, touching the residual lines of the circle. "Still, it's hard to say the two aren't related. I think that magic may have given her some inspiration. She mentioned to me that she hadn't expected a divination ritual using modern-day Karazhan to be so easy. She thought most of the tower's mana had dissipated with Medivh's death."

"So I think this was a genuine divination, Jace. And it's significant." The archmage glanced at Ulthalesh, now soaked in demonic blood, leaning against Jace's hand. "It should be about your staff."

"She saw my future? Or Ulthalesh's future?" Jace thought for a moment, then said, "She saw my soul being completely devoured by this staff, and decided to kill me."

"Are you sure killing you was her final decision, Jace?" Khadgar pointed at the scorched marks left on the floor where the sword-wielding Aegwynn had vanished. "The decision to kill you... was right there."

Jace understood immediately what Khadgar was getting at. He remembered what the warrior figure had said to him earlier — those who escaped, those who died, those who tried to destroy the tower, those who tried to find others... At the time, he'd only been focused on winning and hadn't had time to think it through. But hearing Khadgar say it now...

"The ones we just fought — they weren't Aegwynn's mirror images, or her soul. They were her *decisions*?"

"Remember what I told you before, about Aran's shadow?" Khadgar explained. "The reason I guessed that was because some archmages, driven by strong emotions, can create entities that are neither mirror images nor souls. They dwell in their towers like ghosts with a single, pure obsession. Like these figures — they only wanted to eliminate you, seal your staff, and save the world again."

"So Aegwynn hesitated about whether to kill me," Jace said.

"'Hesitated' is putting it lightly, Jace," Khadgar said.

"Then she must have seen something bad in this divination." Jace said. "Otherwise she wouldn't have considered killing me, right?"

"Maybe it was just the instinct of someone who'd been the Guardian of Tirisfal for centuries — the belief that things like Ulthalesh should not exist in this world." Khadgar said. "Honestly, Jace, if I'd seen this evil staff and recognized it back on Draenor, I might never have trusted you from the start."

"I understand, Khadgar," Jace said.

Khadgar hesitated for a moment, then asked, "Can you tell me how you got this, Jace? Forgive my curiosity, but... this is Ulthalesh. The Deadwind Reaper."

"Guardian Scavell hid it in a furbolg village in Winterspring, trying to conceal its existence through the Dreamscape." Jace said. "If not for that journey when I met Sora... even knowing clues that most people don't, from demons, I probably couldn't have retrieved it."

"The Dreamscape. I see." Khadgar nodded.

"A few decades ago, demons sent by Kil'jaeden to find the staff managed to break its dream barrier. Its mana contaminated the valley, exposing its location." Jace said.

"So this is a classic tale of an adventurer traveling the world in search of treasure, only to be cursed by it." Khadgar's tone was light, but Jace could clearly feel his mind hadn't relaxed at all. Because Ulthalesh was no ordinary "cursed treasure."

"I've known what Ulthalesh was for a long time," Jace admitted.

"What do you mean?" Khadgar said. "You're saying... you knew about the curse, and you still went looking for it?"

"I learned about it from the belongings of a reanimated Kirin Tor mage near Karazhan." Jace explained. "He seemed to have died for that information — about a group of Karazhan treasure hunters searching for the world's ultimate treasures. Ulthalesh was one of them."

"The Black Knights," Khadgar said.

"Yes. That's them." Jace said. "At first, I didn't connect the information to myself. But later, when I took a bounty from the Reliquary and went to Kalimdor hunting the demon lord Dath'mora, I discovered that the mission the Burning Legion had given her... was to search for clues about Ulthalesh. She seemed to have made some progress, but I had no way to decipher those clues at the time, so I didn't learn the details."

"When I came back from Kalimdor, I handed the original clues over to the Reliquary for more pay. That money helped me buy that standalone house by the canal south of Cathedral Square."

"But soon, I discovered that Magister Dalk'Dril of the Dragonflayer had gotten his hands on those scrolls through his connections at the Reliquary, and had even contacted the Black Knights through the Violet Eye to decode them. More importantly, I felt like he'd already set his sights on me."

"You were worried that if Magister Dalk'Dril actually got Ulthalesh, he'd come after you," Khadgar said slowly, nodding. "If it were me, I probably wouldn't have worried at all. After all, so many warlocks and demon worshipers had searched for centuries without finding it... But considering the Black Knights were under Medivh's — or Sargeras's — command, and that demon lord's clues... it's hard to say."

"That was part of it." Jace leaned on his staff. "But another reason might be that after Grim Batol, my mindset had changed. I couldn't let Ulthalesh fall into the hands of the Black Knights or their associates. More importantly, I couldn't let clues I'd found help them get it. And incidentally, I wanted to see whether those demonic clues were real or fake."

"I can understand that feeling." Khadgar said. "Like coming to Karazhan this time. Lothar and I defeated Medivh — a heroic deed — but I could never accept that the people of Elwynn continued to suffer because of it. If one day Magister Dalk'Dril actually got his hands on this staff, and used it to harm Quel'Thalas and beyond — like that demonic necromancer who destroyed this valley — it's hard to imagine how you'd feel."

"From Draenor to Azeroth, I think I can judge what kind of person you are, Jace Sesso." The archmage continued. "You've restrained Ulthalesh's power from start to finish, never succumbing to its temptations. I believe that with Guardian Scavell's barrier already compromised, you are the most suitable person to watch over this staff."

"But I can also understand Lady Aegwynn's hesitation. She herself is a good example." Khadgar looked at the tall windows in the distance and let out a long sigh. "Hundreds of years ago, she faced the master of the Burning Legion head-on in Northrend. If not for that great battle, the world would have been destroyed then and there. And faced with the countless treasures left by the Demon Lord, she destroyed them all without hesitation. What she couldn't destroy, she sealed at the ends of the earth — just as her teacher had sealed Ulthalesh — ensuring the world would be poisoned as little as possible. In those days, who could have imagined that such a great Guardian would also fall to the temptation of power, refusing to give up the power she'd taken from the other members of the Order of Tirisfal?"

"Even if she saw a beautiful future in her divination, she couldn't completely let her guard down against you."

Listening to Khadgar, Jace leaned on his staff and stared at the circle on the ground, knowing the mage was right.

He'd only had this thing for about three years. Maybe he could control its power now. But in another three years? Thirty years? Longer?

Aegwynn had lived for nearly a thousand years. She no longer measured things from a normal person's perspective. To her, perhaps a few decades were but a blink of an eye. A caster like Jace Sesso, growing more powerful by the day, wielding Ulthalesh — that was a threat right at her doorstep.

"What do you think she saw?" Jace asked.

"You want me to replicate her divination of Ulthalesh?" Khadgar gave a bitter laugh. "You're overestimating me. Maybe if I ever get my hands on Atiesh, I could try."

Jace looked at him. The mage's expression stiffened for a moment. "You went and got Ulthalesh. I go get Atiesh. Use Atiesh to divine Ulthalesh. So it's a comparative joke... never mind. I'm actually explaining the punchline. Damn it."

The archmage walked halfway around the circle. "Whatever she saw, no matter how hard that decision was, in the end she chose to leave those decisions behind. That's what made me firmly choose your side, Jace. I can see that these figures weren't her 'true intent.' Maybe, like me, she's willing to believe that you'll eventually steer the story in the right direction."

He stopped at the northernmost point of the circle. "Look at these chaotic magical traces, Jace. She sealed these decisions inside Karazhan herself, using the tower's residual mana to drain their power. And these figures have been trying all sorts of ways to break the seal and create chaos — all to leave this tower and accomplish the single goal they were born with."

"Kill me. Seal Ulthalesh." Jace finished the thought.

"Yes," Khadgar said. "That's what it sounds like. She definitely didn't expect me to come back, let alone bring you and your lady friend."

So Little Red Riding Hood was also one of those figures? And the "demons invading the cottage" she mentioned — were those Aegwynn's information sources?

After Khadgar's analysis of the tower's mana, Jace pieced together a likely scenario in his mind.

At some point after the Expedition returned, Aegwynn learned through unknown channels that he was wielding Ulthalesh. But she didn't believe it at first. She wanted to confirm it with Jace.

Their meeting outside the Pig and Whistle didn't completely ease her mind, so she decided to see the future for herself. She came to Karazhan, hoping to divine Ulthalesh's future through the Observatorium.

And what she saw threw her into conflict.

In the end, she made her decision, but the other choices didn't disappear with it. So she used Karazhan to seal these figures inside the tower, freeing herself from their influence... until Jace, Khadgar, and Morofil walked blindly into this forbidden ground, already in chaos from Aegwynn's spells.

To prove that his reconstruction of events was accurate, Jace would need to either find Aegwynn's information source or figure out what exactly she'd seen in that ritual.

But with the resources Jace had at hand, he couldn't do either.

Still, as Khadgar said, Aegwynn herself had decided not to kill him. She had abandoned these murderous intentions, left Karazhan, and hadn't returned to Stormwind.

He hoped that was the case. He slumped weakly onto a pile of books nearby and let out an exhausted breath. If anything else came knocking right now, he wouldn't have the energy to fight back.

"The source of the chaos is taken care of. Shall we go find the things my teacher left in the tower?" Khadgar coughed into his hand. "Most of the artifacts will go toward restoring the ecosystem in South Elwynn, of course."

"Let's go." Jace gritted his teeth and pushed himself back up.
