Chapter 1469: The Victor's Prize

Leaving the Observatorium, Khadgar led Jace around a corridor and into a somewhat worn-down antechamber.

Inside was a rather beautiful door, half-ajar. No spells, no sealing magic. Khadgar pushed it open with a light touch.

Beyond the door, they stepped onto a landing with stairs descending on both sides. Below was a very impressive hall. Beneath golden chandeliers burning with arcane spectral light lay a chessboard of black and white squares.

The Chess Room. Jace had sensed where they were going, so he wasn't surprised.

But the chess-piece statues arranged on both sides of the board were still very different from what he remembered.

In the game, the pieces on each side represented the Alliance and the Horde — more specifically, the knights and mages led by King Llane of Stormwind, and the warlocks and demons led by Warchief Blackhand of the Horde. Alliance or Horde players would take their respective sides.

Both sides would come here, play the roles of the pieces, and fight with combat skills and spells to decide a winner...

And Medivh's shadow would watch from the sidelines, even going so far as to personally "influence" the outcome.

But the pieces here weren't King Llane and Warchief Blackhand. They weren't even as vivid as the game's characters. They were actual statues.

Both sides were human. The figures were quite vivid, and there were clear differences in their attire — one side more ornate and heavily armored, like the Alliance; the other more wild and primitive, like the Horde. But they were at most symbolic chess-piece representations.

"What kind of chess is this?" Jace asked Khadgar.

"Oh, the youth of today." Khadgar sighed.

"You're barely ten years older than me," Jace said.

"But separated by the Dark Portal into entirely different generations." Khadgar explained. "This is King's Chess, also called Thoradin Chess. Even if you've never played, surely you've heard of it?"

"Ah, right." Jace nodded.

He did know there was a chess game similar to chess in this world, but he'd never really looked into it. It was a game for nobles, after all. He was just a mud-stained laborer from the city walls.

But now, as King Varian's magical advisor, he should probably learn a thing or two about high-class pastimes.

He pointed at the "king" in the center of the right side — crowned, clad in heavy armor — and asked, "That's King Thoradin. And the leader on the other side?"

"Ignatus," Morofil answered. "Chieftain of the Alterici tribe."

"Even a demon from the Twisting Nether knows that." Khadgar shook his head in awe.

"You know that?" Jace asked.

"You mentioned you liked playing board games every once in a while, so I asked around with the warlocks about the different games in Stormwind." Morofil said smugly.

Well, that was a new piece of knowledge. So the game's lore background was Thoradin's conquest of the Alterac Mountains. Made sense.

Jace had always felt something was off. Medivh and King Llane had grown up together. Llane had died by an orc assassin's hand. Why would Medivh allow a chess room in Karazhan that placed his assassinated friend and the orc who killed him as pieces on opposite sides?

Now it made sense. Later, Medivh's already chaotic shadow had replaced the original chess pieces with what he found more "interesting" — purely to toy with and provoke adventurers. The original chess pieces in the Chess Room had always been standard, traditional pieces.

"But Khadgar, weren't we going to find treasures? Why did you bring me to the Chess Room?" Jace asked.

"A lot of the treasures are here," Khadgar said. "Medivh used all kinds of magical items to attract nobles and archmages to Karazhan to play chess with him... these treasures were probably gathered from all over the world by Sargeras's demons, or servants like the Black Knights."

"So all the good stuff was already won away?" Jace asked.

"You think you could beat Medivh at chess?" The mage turned back and gave him a sidelong glance. "Maybe think about how to defeat him with magic instead."

"So it's cheating with the tower's backing?"

"Don't put it so bluntly. We're still in someone else's house." The mage warned.

Khadgar led the warlock and the demon to a nearby passage. At its end was a thick door carved with a golden lion. The door was enchanted — not surprising at all.

"What's the password this time?" Jace asked.

"No password." Khadgar said. "Why would Medivh let his apprentice learn the password to the prize vault?"

He stepped back, raised his staff, tensed his nerves, stared at the center of the door, and chanted a spell under his breath. The magic peeled away, and the door slowly swung open.

"See, Jace Sesso." Khadgar clenched his teeth in excitement. "It opened... see that? The theater was just an accident. This is my true skill. Deathwing's armor, Medivh's vault seal — nothing can stop me!"

"Now that Aegwynn's shadows are all dead," Morofil said with a smile.

"Shut it, demon." Khadgar pointed at the succubus, straightened his collar, and stepped into the vault.

Calling it Medivh's "treasure vault" was hardly an exaggeration. In a modestly sized, elegant hall, all kinds of exhibits were displayed, each with descriptions written in Common and Elvish beneath them.

Right by the door were the Cloak of the King of Arathor, the golden bird statue called "Heart of Aviana," the Scepter of Dominion of the Order of Tirisfal, Nielas Aran's Gem of Calm...

Some of them Jace even remembered, but most he was seeing for the first time. Judging by the descriptions, many of these items were hundreds or even thousands of years old.

No wonder countless nobles and mages had been willing to visit, even knowing the world's most terrible wizard lived here.

Seeing Jace's expression, Khadgar turned back and warned, "Don't think about taking everything, Jace. We're not here as looters. Most of this stuff is useless to us — we'd only sell or give it away. But think about the consequences if some of these items got out into the world. So we'll each take one item — the most important one. Choose carefully. Think of it as payment for cleaning up the tower owners' mess."

"Acceptable." Jace put his arm around Morofil's waist. "But she counts as a person."

Morofil tilted her head — made slightly heavy by her huge demonic horns — happily and pressed against her master.

Khadgar watched these two and sighed. "Fair enough. But your Dazarm the First has already fallen, unfortunately. Don't think about summoning another one and counting it as another head!"

"I know, I know," Jace said.

"Since I invited you to help, you choose first. I'll take what's left." Khadgar said.

"Then I won't hold back." Jace picked up a nearby brick-red engraved curved blade. The description beneath read "Kingsguard."

"You're taking that blade?" Khadgar asked.

"No, no. Just looking."

Jace set it down, noticing Khadgar glancing unintentionally toward the other end of the room.

It looked like a storage area, piled with dust-covered chests. None of the chests had names or descriptions.

Jace immediately ignored everything else and walked into the deeper part of the room. Khadgar's eyelid twitched. Morofil watched the scene with an amused smile.

Jace pulled out a chest and opened it. Inside was a red dagger, seemingly made from demon scales or bone. Nothing identifiable about it.

"Blade of Wickedness." Khadgar introduced it with affected nonchalance. "A perfect dagger. A demon once tried to assassinate Medivh himself with it, and nearly succeeded! Are you picking a third weapon for your lady friend?"

Jace stared into his eyes and shook his head. He pulled out another small box.

Inside the box was a pair of sky-blue leather boots. Their color was so bright and clear they seemed out of place in the vault's dim glow — as if even the drifting dust around them would taint their purity.

"Hm. Fine craftsmanship on those shoes, but that's about it." Khadgar nodded slightly.

Jace looked into his eyes for a moment and said, "I'll take this one."

"Uh." Khadgar coughed. "But they're just... enchanted leather boots."

"But they're not ordinary, are they?" Jace said. "I'm a warlock, Khadgar. I can feel your true opinion from your emotions."

"Wait, Jace." Khadgar reached out. "Fine. These are a former Guardian's personal goatskin soft boots — a treasure for frost-specialized mages. But what does a demon summoner want with them?!"

"Exactly what I thought. Perfect as a gift." Jace set the box aside. "You're so good at Pyroblast — shouldn't you pick a fire item instead? Or find something precious in the transmutation field. I'm sure Karazhan has no shortage of that kind of thing."

"Never mind what I want." Khadgar said. "Who are you giving them to?"

"Amy," Jace said. "Without her, I couldn't have gone to Draenor."

"Ah, Lady Marin. That makes sense." The mage nodded. "Don't forget to mention I had a hand in finding those boots."

"I'll remember." Jace tucked the boots into the box and put them away.

"Look at this, master." Morofil took a crimson grimoire from a small box.

Jace took the grimoire. On the crimson cover, a fanged, horned demonic skull was quite menacing — it didn't hide its evil at all.

He opened the cover. A purple-blue magical light immediately poured out from between the pages. The text inside was completely illegible to Jace — it wasn't demonic, nor was it Shath'yar.

But just holding the book, he felt like he could directly sense the power of the Twisting Nether. When he opened it, Jace even heard Ulthalesh behind him hum softly, over and over.

"I remember this grimoire, Jace." Khadgar introduced it. "The scholars of the Order of Tirisfal called it the Chronicle of Dark Secrets. As the name suggests, it's a historical record. The text is Nathrezim. But because of its author — an ancient Nathrezim's powerful magic — it can help the wielder connect with the power of the void."

The Chronicle of Dark Secrets?

Wasn't this... that chronicle? Why was it in Karazhan?

Jace realized that many of the items stored here seemed to be treasures that, in another timeline, would have been possessed by the demons and undead lords who participated in the Battle of Mount Hyjal.

Had Kel'Thuzad and the Lich's liches later come here to steal them? Or had the Violet Eye smuggled them back to Dalaran in the following years, only for them to be taken during Dalaran's destruction by Archimonde and his followers?

What happened after was impossible to guess. But regardless, it was all in front of him now.

"This one's mine," Jace said.

"No." He immediately handed the book to the smiling demon, correcting himself, "It belongs to Lady Morofil."

Khadgar stood behind them, arms crossed, his expression full of disdain.
