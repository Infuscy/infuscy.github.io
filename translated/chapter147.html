Chapter 147: Like a Dream

Flames filled his vision, thick smoke choking the air. Jace felt like he was going to cough his lungs right out of his chest, his ribs aching as if someone were jabbing them with an awl over and over.

Right now it felt like he'd been slung over a horse. The violent jolting kept him from passing out and made it just as impossible to fully wake up, leaving him swinging back and forth in that half-conscious limbo.

He knew that aside from some burns, he hadn't suffered any other serious external injuries. And those soldiers who thought he'd been hit by shadow damage — that was most likely because he'd drawn in far too much black dragon blood.

That black dragon blood was the source of his current agony.

His chest, his belly, his brain — all of it felt like it had been packed full of scalding lava. No amount of clawing or pounding on his skin could ease the torment inside. He wanted to vomit but nothing came up — after those first two heaves, he hadn't brought anything else up.

Despite the constant nausea and heartburn, his throat and stomach now felt bone-dry, like there wasn't a drop of moisture left in them. He groaned in misery, and a voice comforted him: "Almost there… almost there, mage!"

"How much longer until we reach Southshore? I want water…"

Jace barely managed to force the words out, but the sound that came from his throat was rough as a beast's growl.

A powerful wave of drowsiness surged up all at once. He'd actually been looking forward to a proper sleep — but his body suddenly started fighting it. He remembered those dying patients in hospitals, how the doctors kept telling them don't fall asleep, don't fall asleep, as if drifting off meant never waking up again.

He flailed his arms, trying to grab onto something, which only startled the man in front with a yelp. After one final savage jolt, he finally couldn't hold back the exhaustion that swallowed everything.

No… He snapped his eyes open — and everything around him had suddenly stopped.

No more shaking. No more swaying. No howling wind, no raging fire.

The silence was deeply unsettling. It felt like just seconds ago he'd still been struggling in the dragon's Shadowflame.

He sat up and found himself sinking into a soft, dark-red mattress.

The sheets were so smooth they made him uncomfortable all over. Just touching them told him they were probably outrageously expensive — he couldn't even tell what kind of silk or ludicrously fancy fabric they were woven from. He was almost afraid his sweat and grime would ruin them.

But when he felt himself, he was clean. Really clean.

The skin that had gone half a month without a bath at sea, sticky and salt-crusted, was fresh and comfortable now. The hair that had been plastered to his scalp was fluffy and soft, like it had been carefully washed with shampoo.

His hair in this life was nothing like the wiry black bristles of his last life — it was soft as fleece. Since he rarely got the chance to wash it this thoroughly, the feel of it was still somewhat unfamiliar. Kind of addictive to touch, actually.

The room was spotlessly white, simply and brightly decorated. On the carved brown nightstand sat a cream-colored porcelain vase holding a bunch of little purple flowers he didn't recognize, clusters of blossoms trailing out and giving off a strange, exotic fragrance.

The moment he caught the scent, his whole body felt as if it had been purified — to the point that he started finding his own breath offensive.

What was this place, heaven?

Or… a dream?

If this was a dream caused by shadow damage, it was far too normal. You could even call it a "good dream" — definitely not the kind of nightmare some elder god would cook up.

That was when he noticed the pack sitting under the nightstand — the warlock's bag he'd looted from that orc.

Compared to this beautiful bedroom, the warlock's bag looked even less appealing than usual. With the pattern-obscuring stains all over it, it resembled a trash bag fished out of a mud puddle.

That bag… no one had gone through it, had they?

His heart clenched — but then he noticed the knot tying the warlock's bag shut was one he'd tied himself.

He was sure of it because he was the only one here who used that particular knot, one he'd learned from a cousin in his last life. Neither the people of Stormwind nor the Lordaeronians tied their knots that way.

That meant that wherever he was, whoever had rescued him had never rummaged through the bag's contents.

And if someone had confirmed what was hidden inside, he'd be waking up in a dungeon right now instead.

So what on earth had happened…

Thinking back to before he'd lost consciousness, he'd been through a fight — a fight he'd never forget for the rest of his life.

That dragon had burned down he didn't know how many houses in Southshore, killed god knew how many ordinary folk and soldiers. And that gryphon rider.

The way that gryphon rider had looked lying in that house. The expression on the young woman homeowner's face as she stared at his corpse. Even after passing out and waking up again, it was still carved deep into Jace's mind.

Was that dragon dead?

It should be dead. Jace still remembered that massive head slamming down right at his feet.

That had been a whelp. Its head was snakelike, without the thick whiskers of an old dragon or the sharp, sturdy bull-like horns of an adult.

Jace really shouldn't have underestimated a whelp's power, since even whelps could have fearsome figures like Tremens the Devourer. But at the time, his mind had been too chaotic to recall any of that. Too much confusion, too much pain, too much adrenaline — he'd just wanted to help Southshore, help Grede put an end to that evil dragon's rampage.

Grede… right, where was Grede?

He tried to shift his lower body. Luckily he wasn't paralyzed — his legs and feet were a little numb, but they still worked.

Right now he was wearing a set of loose white clothes that didn't belong to him, a shirt and trousers. His previous outfit, including that cloak, had been cleaned and set on a small dresser-like cabinet by the window.

The cloak had returned to its dark brown color.

Remembering the moment he'd worn that cloak and been singed by the flames, his right palm and his whole right arm throbbed with pain again. His arm was wrapped in bandages now.

Jace remembered that in the final moment he'd cast Life Drain — and he'd cast it while holding that staff.

Logically, the burns he'd gotten at Stonewatch had healed after draining a single sheep. So why hadn't the burns from being grazed by dragonfire recovered after draining the black dragon's life force?

No shadow dreams, no injury recovery — Jace felt like something was off about all of it.

He got out of bed and felt his previously worn clothes, now cleaned and dried.

How long had it been since what happened at that riverside sentry tower? He crouched down and touched the bag, sensing there was still plenty inside — just from that brush of his hand he could feel the booklets and such he'd packed.

Right, where was that staff?

Jace stood up and scanned the whole room, noticing his staff leaning right by the window, still wrapped in the cloth he'd tied on himself.

So aside from the clothes, none of his luggage had been handled.

Then… what about the imp in the bag?

Jace was half-tempted to call him out, but Sazeno's name had barely reached his lips before he swallowed it again.

He looked toward the window — at the very least he needed to see where he was.

Actually, he already had the answer in his heart. He just wanted to confirm it with his own eyes.

Dragging his somewhat heavy steps to the window, Jace looked out. After adjusting to the intense sunlight, a scene straight out of a fairy tale spread before his eyes.

A jumble of little cream-walled buildings with vaguely Byzantine purple domes, packed one against another. Deep-red brick streets ran through them like the strawberry-jam veins slicing through cream.

In the distance, behind a towering, solemn purple-tiled castle, the surface of a boundless lake glittered with light. Flocks of white birds crossed the sky.

Endless green pine forests filled the rest of the view, a perfect natural frame for the picture. Jace pinched his own face. This wasn't a dream. Someone had brought him to Dalaran.
