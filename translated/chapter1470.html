Chapter 1470: Return to the Theater

Coming out of the Chess Room, Jace could already feel that the magic in Karazhan Tower had calmed significantly compared to before they'd entered the Observatorium.

Before returning to the library, Jace wanted to go back to the theater one more time. He still couldn't be one hundred percent certain what Little Red Riding Hood's words meant — who the summoned demon was, and whether it was the one who'd told Aegwynn about Ulthalesh. He was about eighty percent sure, but he wanted confirmation.

Fortunately, Khadgar also wanted to check on the rogue arcane constructs along the way, so they went downstairs together.

Even though they came to the theater for the arcane constructs, the mage kept grumbling about those blue goatskin boots the whole way, even after picking out a very appealing crimson arcane crystal.

One moment he was saying the boots really were a rare find. The next he was noting they looked like women's boots, not men's. Then he reasoned that since they'd belonged to a Guardian, and among all the Guardians over two thousand years, Aegwynn was the only woman, so he had reasonable suspicion that Aegwynn herself might have worn them... completely incomprehensible rambling.

Khadgar clearly wanted those boots. As a mage who could sense people's emotions, Jace could tell easily. But if Khadgar hadn't wanted them so badly, he — someone insensitive to arcane — wouldn't have been able to judge what would make a good gift for Amy.

Once Jace left Karazhan and Khadgar took control of the Curator, he'd effectively become the new master of the tower. All the treasures and valuables inside would basically be his anyway.

What was the harm in taking a pair of pretty boots?

As for the Chronicle of Dark Secrets, Jace had been very interested in it all along.

But since it was a grimoire, he'd need to read it quietly in the library.

And besides, after all these twists and turns, he hadn't forgotten why he'd come to Karazhan in the first place — to find records in the library about the black dragon's "Dark Simulacrum" ability. That was the most important thing.

Jace felt the chronicle might help him master so-called Dark Simulacrum, even if Khadgar had told him the content was mostly historical records, and its inherent magic was the real value.

Still, he couldn't settle down to read in the library without checking in at the theater first.

The theater's activity hadn't quieted down just because Aegwynn's figures had disappeared. Ghosts were constantly coming and going, and skeletal servants stared suspiciously at every dubious character, on guard against spies like Jace and his group sneaking in.

The stage performance was still going on. A tin-man was currently stomping heavily across the stage, bickering with a little girl.

While the audience's attention was fixed on the stage, they went back down to the backstage passages. This time the door wasn't locked — there was a crack left. Khadgar put on a disappointed expression, and Jace waved for them to hurry in.

The backstage ghosts saw two living people and a demon barging in unannounced, but acted as if nothing had happened, continuing to put on makeup in front of their mirrors, adjusting their costumes, chatting and laughing with friends — even though there was nothing reflected in the mirrors.

Not all the ghosts ignored them, though. The stage manager, Barnes — with his beautiful blond hair, blue coat, and crimson cloak — glared at them, trotted over in little steps, pointed at Khadgar and said, "I remember you. I remember you now. You were the master's student... hmm, no wonder I thought you looked familiar last time!"

"Your performance last time was excellent. You and your two friends were outstanding. But I don't know why, after the show, you just disappeared. The audience didn't even get to meet you properly. You must know that the guests here are all wealthy or noble — it was a perfect opportunity to get to know some important people."

He looked at Jace. "Especially you, sir. I could see it in your eyes — that look of someone hoping to climb the social ladder."

"I'm sorry, Mr. Barnes. We just got lost and ended up in the exhibition hall somehow." Jace said. "But you're right — I did want to get to know someone important."

"What a shame. Too much time has passed now. The audience is watching something new; they've probably forgotten all about you." Barnes pursed his lips regretfully, then said, "But who did you want to meet? Maybe I can help with my connections... of course, as a favor to the master's student."

"Much obliged, Mr. Barnes." Khadgar bowed deeply.

"I'd like to meet the actress who played Little Red Riding Hood, Mr. Barnes," Jace said.

"Ah, her! Her!" Barnes seemed quite excited. "She was such a remarkable person!"

"How so?" Jace asked.

Barnes wiggled his finger. "She loved being on stage. Loved playing all kinds of roles, and she played every one of them as if she'd become the character! She often told me it was only on this stage that she could say the things she couldn't say otherwise. She hoped her performances could change people's destinies... even change the whole world."

Hearing this, Khadgar frowned slightly. Jace asked, "Is she still here? Can I meet her?"

"Strangely enough." Barnes said. "After that performance of Little Red Riding Hood, she disappeared. Just as mysteriously as she appeared. You could wait here — maybe she'll be back tomorrow."

Jace exchanged a look with Khadgar, then asked, "Who made the three of us go on stage?"

"You're joking, young man." Barnes smiled. "The spotlight moves completely randomly. No one controls it."

"You're the one joking, Mr. Barnes." Khadgar said. "I distinctly saw you looking our way before the spotlight landed on us."

"Did I?" Barnes glanced at the mage innocently, then suddenly raised his finger as if something had clicked. "Ha! Now I remember. Before the spotlight came on, she whispered behind me... said she wanted the three of you to come on stage and perform Little Red Riding Hood with her. And then the spotlight inexplicably fell on you. Was it a coincidence, or fate?"

"She didn't leave anything behind?" Jace asked. "Costumes, props, personal effects?"

Barnes looked at him for a moment. Though he maintained his smiling expression, somehow the smile in his eyes slowly faded.

"As I said — she vanished mysteriously. Everything vanished."

"Let's go over there, Jace." Khadgar said quietly, pointing at a side door.

"That's the changing room," Barnes said. "Did you leave something in there?"

"Yes, I'm sorry, sir." Jace gave Khadgar a look, and they both quickly made their way to the back, entering the narrow corridor to the changing rooms.

"Right here. I can confirm one of Aegwynn's figures came here. She cast a fairly large-scale spell." Khadgar pointed at a small, open booth.

"Turned the entire stage into a forest. Turned herself into Little Red Riding Hood." Jace said.

"Turned all of us into characters in the play," Morofil added. "Where did she go after that? Just vanished?"

"You said earlier that Aegwynn planned to use the tower's mana to drain her own figures' power." Jace said. "But not long ago, she was here casting such powerful illusions, and then after the performance, she left. She didn't return to the Observatorium. She didn't keep interfering with the tower's magic. Would she really be drained that quickly?"

"I think she'd already accomplished her goal." Khadgar said. "Maybe the decision she represented was to put on that play with us. She fulfilled her purpose, so the energy maintaining her existence disappeared... but even I can't convince myself of that explanation. She could have just told us directly. No need to go through all that trouble."

"Maybe Aegwynn didn't want these 'decisions' to reveal relevant information." Morofil traced a curving path on the wall with her finger. "So this figure chose to accomplish it through a stage play. She found a way to guide us to the theater, told us her concerns through Little Red Riding Hood, then summoned the demon."

"Is that possible?" Jace asked the mage.

"It is one way to get around a prohibition." Khadgar nodded.

"We Sayaad know this game best," the succubus said.

"What are you worried about, Jace?" Khadgar asked.

"I'm worried about how Lady Aegwynn found out about my situation. Whether it's connected to that summoned demon." Jace said.

"There aren't many demons in the world who know our master wields it," Morofil explained. "Unfortunately, Kil'jaeden is one of them."

"Yes. You did cut the Scepter of Sargeras in front of him." Khadgar rubbed his forehead and sat down on a wooden chair nearby. "I should have thought of that earlier. Does this mean Kil'jaeden himself, or his servants, have found a way to contact Lady Aegwynn? But thankfully, the Deceiver didn't manage to manipulate her easily."

"She's better than us at recognizing the Deceiver's schemes, Khadgar." Jace said. "But right now, I want to know how the Deceiver found her."

"If Morofil's guess is right, it seems Aegwynn placed a prohibition on all her figures, forbidding them from revealing this information." Khadgar said. "Not just Little Red Riding Hood — you asked that battle-mage figure too, and she self-destructed before answering."

The mage looked at the demonic skull in Jace's hand and continued, "From this we can infer that even if we find Aegwynn herself, she won't tell us. Even if she doesn't hold this staff against you anymore."

Could it be through the Lich King Ner'zhul? The Lich King could communicate with Kel'Thuzad on another continent from thousands of miles away, guiding the latter on his northern journey. Using the same method to talk to Aegwynn wasn't impossible. But the Lich King hadn't yet demonstrated this kind of ability.

"Maybe it's some agent?" Jace ventured. "Someone lurking on this side."

"Very possible." Khadgar said. "Looks like we can only wait for the Deceiver's next move. We'll be ready. Maybe we can catch him by the wrist directly."

"I don't like this feeling," Jace said.

"Trust me, neither do I," Khadgar said.
