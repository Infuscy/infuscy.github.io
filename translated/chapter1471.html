Chapter 1471: Back on Track

Leaving the theater and returning to the spiral staircase, Jace and Khadgar headed upward while searching for the disabled arcane constructs on each floor.

Unlike before, when they'd actively come to attack them, the constructs outside the library had all stopped moving. Khadgar used his own magical crystals to charge them one by one, activating them. Those that couldn't be activated, he recorded the issues and damage, planning to report to the Curator back in the Grand Library.

The last two constructs were on the top floor of Karazhan. Unlike what Jace remembered — going from the Chess Room directly to the top — they took the central spiral staircase all the way up, the same way they'd gone to the library and the Observatorium. The other door in the Chess Room was sealed.

When Khadgar said they needed to go to the tower top for the constructs, he didn't mention the Chess Room passage. He just assumed they'd take the spiral stairs.

Since this mage had actually lived here for a few years, while Jace had only walked the designer's dungeon route a few times a week, he didn't question it.

Besides, he'd see it for himself anyway.

Following Khadgar to the top of the spiral staircase brought them to a rather worn-down narrow chamber. Still, it was obvious that like everywhere else in Karazhan, it had once been quite ornate.

The floor tiles, walls, stone pillars, and even the stairway decorations still bore remnants of their former glory. The guardian's raven reliefs, the Silver Eye insignia of the Order of Tirisfal — it just looked much more weathered by age than the other areas.

Besides these familiar insignia, there were also some patterns Jace didn't recognize, mainly featuring suns and stone figures.

According to Khadgar, these were the emblems used by Lord Anduin Lothar's ancestors when they ruled Stormwind — the ones who'd led a group of people from the north to settle here and establish the Kingdom of Stormwind.

Jace didn't know about that. From the books he'd read in the Wizard Sanctum's library, he'd seen at least seven or eight members of the Wrynn family who'd ruled Stormwind. He'd always assumed the Wrynns had been ruling here since Stormwind was founded twelve hundred years ago.

It seemed Karazhan had been built much earlier than he'd thought.

Two dormant arcane constructs stood in the chamber. Khadgar approached them with two magic crystals. Jace's attention was drawn to a closed wooden door nearby.

The door vibrated occasionally, thumping. There was quite a wind outside.

Following the game's path, opening this door would lead directly to Karazhan's final boss — the Eredar lord Prince Malchezaar, who'd come through the Twisting Nether.

In the game, the vast area outside that door didn't feel like a tower top at all. More like a fragment connecting Karazhan to the Twisting Nether.

Gray-black cracks, broken edges... almost completely disconnected from Deadwind Pass's valley. Outside was a blurry haze, impossible to see clearly.

A lock.

Jace walked to the door and found the chain wrapped around it was simply looped — no actual lock. So he unwound the chain. Khadgar noticed the sound and looked over. The moment he saw Jace about to open the door, his eyes went wide. But it was too late.

Jace pushed the door open and stepped forward — only to find his foot stepping into empty air as he plunged into a storm. Below his feet was a pitch-black bottomless abyss. The trees of Deadwind Pass below were like a sea of black claws reaching up, eager to tear him from the tower...

He immediately grabbed the door and swung himself back inside. Morofil caught him and pulled him into the tower in time. Khadgar chanted a spell, slamming the door shut. The chamber fell deathly quiet.

"Are you out of your mind?!" the mage demanded.

"You didn't tell me there was nothing out there," Jace said.

"Who knew you'd suddenly go crazy and open that door?!" Khadgar said.

Jace shook the rainwater off his cloak. "I heard Karazhan's tower top could connect to the Twisting Nether."

"Who told you that? Those fellows in the Wizard Sanctum?" Khadgar snapped his fingers, blowing all the water off Jace. Jace's soaking wet hair instantly puffed up like wool.

"Something like that." Jace grabbed at his hair. "But if there's no passage to some strange place here, then what's this door for? There's nothing outside."

"Well, in the past... it seems there was." Khadgar said. "But that was when Medivh was still alive. Besides, even if there was still a passage to the Twisting Nether here, do you think Aegwynn would have just left it alone after coming through?"

Khadgar had a point. Jace thought so too. But he wouldn't have been comfortable without checking the tower top himself.

"Still, that has nothing to do with this door." Khadgar explained. "In the past, this led to the Chess Room. Remember that sealed door in the Chess Room? There was an outer tower that connected up here. But it's the same tower that was destroyed by the arcane storm below — probably wrecked at the same time."

"I see," Jace said.

Morofil pointed outside. "So right now, it looks like that tower was destroyed by the theater figure. She didn't want us to reach the library directly. Her goal was to get us to the theater, so she could tell us about the 'demon summoning.'"

"So I should have just turned around when I was told, instead of stubbornly fighting her magic to rebuild that ladder..." Khadgar said. "My pride nearly got us all killed."

After activating the constructs and returning to the Grand Library, Khadgar went to the exhibition hall to find the Curator and begin further calibration and repair work. Jace stayed in the library.

He found a Nathrezim ancient language dictionary in the library's linguistics section and opened the Chronicle of Dark Secrets alongside it, preparing to decipher its contents. But just getting through the first page took considerable effort.

The first part described the chronicle's author's account of how the Nathrezim had made the Titan god who sought to annihilate darkness understand the world's truth, and guided him onto the right path...

It was full of absurd self-aggrandizement, painting the Nathrezim as the hidden hands manipulating all existence from deep within the Twisting Nether, with even the Pantheon as mere pawns of these ancient demons.

Though Jace knew much of it was fabrication and embellishment, as he stared at these words of immense magical power, he felt himself slowly establishing a spiritual connection with the story.

Even without having seen the Nathrezim dreadlords face to face, he could almost see the author's corpse-like countenance, his blood-red eyes, his pale fangs. In the distance, the Titan — godlike — grew increasingly stern as the extravagant speech unfolded, his brow furrowed, his steel hand gripping a great sword, tightening and loosening in hesitation...

The scene was so vivid that Jace felt he was on the verge of believing it. He immediately flipped the page, and the mass of cold, ancient, unrecognizable characters brought him back to reality. The Chain of Will around his neck was already a little warm.

"Back then, Medivh didn't want me digging into this book either." Khadgar's voice suddenly appeared in front of him.

He studied Jace's expression and said, "I thought as a shadow magic user you'd be able to handle this grimoire's power, but it seems you're struggling too. Are you sure you still want to take it?"

"Definitely. Feels good, actually." Jace said calmly.

"Hmph. I doubt it. But come with me first to test the Curator's operation." Khadgar beckoned. "Ask him for help with some tests, like last time you talked to him."

"You finished it that quickly?" Jace asked.

The mage glanced at his pocket watch. "It's been seven hours, Jace."

"Well, it really is a book that makes you lose track of time..." Jace muttered, stuffing the grimoire back into his bag. "Lead the way, Archmage Khadgar."

In the exhibition hall, the Curator was standing obediently with its back against the high wall. Khadgar walked excitedly to its feet and turned around, like an artist waiting for someone to admire his work.

"Ready, Jace."

Jace acknowledged, looked up, and said, "Esteemed Curator, is there a section in the Grand Library of Karazhan that records the most obscure shadow magic on Azeroth — particularly related to black dragons? I believe Archmage Medivh or Lady Aegwynn must have conducted similar research."

"It exists," the Curator replied.

"Excellent." Jace rubbed his hands together. "Can you take me there?"

The Curator lowered its head and looked at Jace for a moment. "As you command, great Master of Karazhan."

"Great Master of Karazhan?"

Jace froze. Khadgar panicked.

"You're joking, right, Curator?" Jace asked.

"You joked first, Mister Jace Sesso," the Curator said. "This area is not accessible to visitors of any clearance level."

"Just like this tower — old, boring jokes." Jace said. "What if I hadn't asked if you were joking?"

"No comment." The Curator's voice turned cold.

"Wait! Wait, wait, wait. Calm down." Khadgar stepped between the two of them — one big, one small. "There might be a slight issue with the permissions, Jace. Give me some more time to adjust..."
