## Chapter 1473: The Index

"To flip through all these books, why'd you summon *me*? I mean, master! Look at my body, look at my hands, my arms—"

"An imp who can't fight and can't please his master — if he can't even do grunt work, what use is he?"

"In this library, it seems like a Sayad can't be of much use either!"

"If master wants to use me, he can use me anytime."

"Cough." Jace interrupted the two demons' argument, setting aside the book in his hand, *The River of Silithus, Black Wings, and the Vicious Magic of Ancient Kalimdor*.

"Help me find books properly, Sazeno. No complaining. I called you here because Khadgar only allows you into this place."

"Probably because the Grand Library doesn't allow any demons inside," Mor'fel said with a smile.

"Who says, huh?"

Sazeno put on a smug expression, staring at the succubus for a moment before realizing what she meant.

"I'm a *real* demon, you featherless bat!"

"Ah, I never thought a praise from an imp could be so... gratifying."

"What praise?! What praise?!"

"Sazeno." Jace reminded it. "Hurry up and search. If you find clues about that strange magic, I'll reward you with something you actually like doing."

"Huh? What would I like doing, master?" The imp turned around.

"Translating this *Chronicle of Dark Secrets*." Jace said. "And help me compile a dictionary while you're at it. Once I bring you the reference books you need..."

"Ahhh—" Sazeno let out a string of demonic curses.

Jace sighed faintly and picked up another book. The cover read *Accursed Progeny*.

He was just piling every book related to black dragons and the magic they wielded onto this side, flipping through them one by one, trying to find anything related to "imitating others' spells."

Logically, a book about black dragon offspring probably wouldn't have what he was looking for, but he couldn't help opening it anyway.

This book recorded the author's travels through Northrend and Kalimdor, searching for black dragons or people who understood them. It mentioned that Deathwing's current mate was his last surviving one — all his other mates had died during copulation, and their deaths were unspeakably tragic.

The last mate wasn't named in the book. Jace knew her name was Sinthania. Even though she'd survived, she was in an absolutely miserable state.

But the author noted one unusual observation: even knowing their mothers had died horribly at their father's savage power, Deathwing's offspring showed no hatred toward him. This was completely at odds with common mortal values.

The author clearly didn't know why the black dragons had fallen. Like most humans, he just saw them as innately evil magical beasts, only much bigger than stags or chimeras.

But he'd offered one conjecture: that Deathwing might be using some kind of mental enslavement to control all his children. Otherwise, there was no explaining why black dragons, who cared so much about dragon eggs, offspring, siblings, and family, wouldn't care that their father had murdered their mother.

Reading this, Jace suddenly understood Sabellian's indifference to his father's death a little better.

He was Deathwing's eldest son, half-brother to Nefarian and Onyxia — and the mother of those two was the Dragonqueen Sinthania, or "Countess Sinestra." Which meant Sabellian's birth mother was probably one of those earlier female black dragons who'd met a tragic end.

Of course, Sabellian's mother could also have been Queen Alexstrasza, who might have provided her own eggs to other dragonflights before the War of the Ancients. But back then, the dragonflights didn't have the population crisis they'd face later, so they probably mostly used their own eggs.

Jace set the book down, but suddenly recalled the moment on the Seat of Kil'jaeden — Sabellian's reaction when he saw the flame vision. The panic. The trembling.

*What did you see, Sabellian?*

"This... uh..."

A voice came from beside him. Jace turned his head and saw Sazeno hesitating over a book.

"What did you say?" Jace asked.

"Check this out, master." The imp pointed at the page in front of him.

Jace went over and picked up the book. It was called *Seeing Black Dragons at Black Dragon Swamp*. Just from the title, it sounded like a novel or travelogue, but it was actually the story of a dwarven dragon hunter tracking black dragons in Kalimdor.

He looked where the imp was pointing. The passage discussed the different dragonflights' abilities and magic, with the author arguing that a dragon's most powerful weapon was its breath.

He told a story about a black dragon casually destroying a Dark Iron dwarf village with flame as an example, setting the stage for the danger he was about to face.

So Medivh read stuff like this for fun too? And he'd stuffed it into such a secret place?

But the imp's focus wasn't on the passage itself — it was on a handwritten note scrawled on the page after it.

"Mother's close friend, Arcagos, the Aspect of Arcane, once told young me about the various superb shadow magics possessed by black dragons — far more than just spewing lava or releasing black arcane flame."

Was this written by Medivh himself?

Arcagos was the blue dragon he'd personally burned to death with Sargeras' life force. Jace knew that. He and Aegwynn had once been close friends.

Had Medivh known Arcagos for a long time too?

Not that surprising, really. If the blue dragon was Aegwynn's close friend, looking after her unfortunate son — who was burdened with inexplicably powerful magic — would have been natural. It also fit their duty as magical guardians.

And precisely because they might have known each other, Arcagos would have flown to Karazhan without any guard when he sensed the dark magic spreading in Deadwind Pass, intending to warn Medivh of the danger.

Jace continued reading. "Perhaps he thought I would one day follow in my mother's footsteps, protecting Azeroth from all kinds of evil and corruption, so he felt it necessary to tell me about the black dragons' secret tricks — that the most powerful black dragons can take everything you control, even your spells."

From this passage, it really did sound like Arcagos was trying to tell Medivh about the black dragons' ability to copy others' magic. And the part about "everything you control" went beyond what Jace had expected.

Medivh had been Stormwind's most powerful mage since childhood. If Arcagos was telling him this, was it hinting that black dragons could also simulate arcane magic?

He read on. The note had reached the edge of the page, and only one line of small text remained: "Use 'pure sorrow' then 'deep melancholy.'"

What did that mean? Had Medivh already killed Arcagos by the time he wrote this, making it a brief memorial?

But Jace didn't think it was that simple. Those words felt somewhat abrupt, and as a sentence it was grammatically awkward. As a memorial to a former friend, it seemed way too strange.

Was it some kind of cipher, hiding content that could only be unlocked by...

Pure sorrow?

Jace tried to tap into his inner sorrow, focusing on the first part of the phrase.

He gradually emptied himself of all other emotions, letting the shadow energy in his body become more and more pure — until Mor'fel felt the force and looked over. The candle flames nearby trembled with the pulse of that power.

But nothing changed on the page. It still showed the same content.

"Maybe you should take a look, Mor'fel..." Jace looked up toward the succubus, suddenly noticing that the shelves behind her seemed covered in a layer of shadowy veil, and one particular section looked distinctly wrong.

When he released his inner shadow, all the shelves returned to normal. And that one section had completely disappeared.

Jace summoned the shadow again, and that row of strange books reappeared. He walked over and tried to touch them. It felt like stirring duckweed in icy water — the books barely seemed like real objects, and he couldn't make out what was written on them at all.

Deep melancholy... Jace tried to summon his inner melancholy.

But the moment melancholy appeared, the sorrow was no longer pure. That layer of shadowy veil seemed to lift away again with the candlelight. Yet Jace caught the grimoire that had flickered into wrongness within the melancholy at the last moment, yanking it out and holding it in his hand.

All the candles in the storeroom trembled together. The flame color shifted slightly from purple-red toward blue. A significant amount of shadow energy was drained by Jace's move.

But the grimoire in his hand had become much more solid.

So this was Medivh's "index"? Jace caught his breath. What a mana-intensive index.

But when he saw the book's name, his heart went *thump*.

*Dark Simulacrum*.
