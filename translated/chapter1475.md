## Chapter 1475: Attempting to Simulate

For the next while, Khadgar spent most of his time in the Grand Library trying to adjust the Curator's functions. It seemed to be going smoothly — no days where he'd walk out and find arcane golems running amok everywhere.

Aside from being occasionally called out for testing by Khadgar, Jace spent most of his time in the storeroom, carefully studying Medivh's *Dark Simulacrum*. In the past two days, he hadn't left except to go back to the Grand Library to borrow language reference books for the two demons to use in deciphering Nathrezim.

After reading the entire book through once, Jace confirmed a suspicion he'd had all along: this spell, while not directly derived from the Old Gods behind the black dragons, was still intimately connected to those shadow-formed void parasites.

In broad terms, the spell made near-perfect use of shadow's mutable nature. It entrusted part of the caster's ability to sense others' magic to the gathered shadow energy, directing that shadow to use that perception — leveraging its advantage as pure energy to capture everything about the target's spells, movements, and even the way they channeled energy — then automatically "remolding" itself into the shape of the target's magic at incredible speed, independent of the caster.

Medivh also mentioned that sometimes, when facing less skilled shadow casters, the imitator's casting speed might even be faster than the one being imitated — because the imitator was likely closer to shadow's essence.

Jace recalled the battle with Sabellian on the Seat of Kil'jaeden. The speed at which he copied spells was indeed nearly on par with Jace's own casting. After taking the Curse of Agony, Jace had even lost the perception to confirm whether his spell had reached the target.

Which meant that at that moment, his understanding of shadow — or rather, his mastery of shadow magic — was still a distance behind that black dragon prince.

That might not be the case when he left Karazhan, Jace thought.

Medivh had also raised a question in the book: why would black dragons invent such a complex spell?

Jace had the same question. Even if Deathwing — not yet fully transformed by the Old Gods — wasn't as powerful as a Guardian possessed by Sargeras, he was still one of the most powerful living things on this world.

For him to go to the trouble of inventing a spell to imitate others' magic... the motivation was hard to understand.

Or was this not Deathwing's invention at all, but Nefarian's, or some other unknown offspring's?

Medivh offered his own conjecture in the book. During his research on black dragons, he'd also discovered Deathwing's "Chromatic Dragon Project" — the plan to create a new dragon species with the powers of all five dragonflights.

Initially, Deathwing hadn't pinned his hopes on this new species. He'd wanted to find a breakthrough within his own flight. Perhaps Deathwing himself, or his sons, had been driven by that motivation, using shadow energy to invent a spell that could imitate other dragons' magic — which became Dark Simulacrum.

Meaning Dark Simulacrum could imitate not just shadow, but arcane — even life magic, elemental power, and time itself?

Of course, Jace knew that was impossible.

If this spell could really change the black dragons' fate, Deathwing wouldn't have abandoned it. Even when Nefarian used it against the Alliance and Horde armies, it felt more like playing around than seriously pulling out a trump card. Sabellian was the one who seemed to use it as a game-changer.

The spell must have eventually revealed some insurmountable limitation — like it could only imitate, never spontaneously cast — so Deathwing shifted to the Chromatic Dragon Project, and later to the Void Dragon and Twilight Dragon projects.

But for an adventurer like Jace, if he could use shadow energy to imitate an enemy's spell, even faster than them... couldn't he then imitate Counterspell to counter their Counterspell?

Just thinking about it made him impatient.

Jace walked into the shadow-enhanced ritual circle Sazeno had just drawn, cast Arcane Intelligence on himself, and prepared to try Dark Simulacrum for the first time, following the method Medivh had recorded in the book.

This spell had no specific incantation. Jace needed to gather shadow energy his own way. So he closed his eyes and focused his emotions to build up shadow power.

He'd already chugged a mana potion beforehand, so his mana was abundant. Soon, all the candles in the hall burned purple, and the shadow-enhanced circle beneath him glowed with violet light, even sputtering flame and emitting purple-black smoke.

Gathering shadow energy was no trouble for Jace. But merging part of his perception with that shadow energy... was an experience he'd never had before.

Medivh's explanation on this part was vague too. For an astral wizard possessed by a demon lord, kneading part of his consciousness into mana was probably as easy as stuffing meat filling into dough, so Medivh had glossed over the details.

So for Jace, it was a process of slow trial and error.

But sending out consciousness wasn't completely new to him. When he'd set up the communication array with Kaerlezz in Draenor, his consciousness had had to ride shadow energy through the endless Twisting Nether. So this time he tried to find that same feeling, pushing his consciousness toward the shadow...

He fumbled for who knows how long. Mana burned away continuously. Suddenly, the shadow energy inside him seemed to shudder.

He closed his eyes, carefully experiencing that sensation, then tried to merge more perceptual consciousness with the shadow. But after continuing for a while, he felt utterly exhausted.

To avoid being harmed by the shadow energy in return, he had to release the shadow from his body, grab a mana potion, and take a break.

"Master, your eyes." Mor'fel said. "They seem... unusually red."

"Do they?" Jace said. "Is there a mirror in this room?"

Mor'fel's long tail hooked a magic crystal Khadgar had made from her bag and tossed it over. Jace picked it up and looked. Sure enough, he could see his face, somewhat distorted, but clear enough for eye color.

Yes. His eyes did have an eerie purple-red tint, just like when Sabellian had tried to imitate his magic. Jace remembered clearly.

It seemed he was on the right track.

This attempt had gone unexpectedly smoothly, so Jace downed another mana potion, determined to strike while the iron was hot and keep going while the feeling was still fresh.

"Mor'fel, come here." He beckoned. "Stand in front of me and try casting Shadowstep."

"Yes, master." Mor'fel said softly.

"See? A succubus can be useless in the library too," Sazeno said, his hands pressing down on the *Chronicle*. "You could learn a shadow spell and try to be useful, my master. Simulate Shadowstep into a Shadow Bolt and blow this bad woman up."

Mor'fel shot the imp a glare, then looked at Jace. "So, master, shall we begin?"

Jace nodded and gathered shadow energy following the feeling he'd just developed.

At the same time, he focused all his attention on Mor'fel, watching her channel shadow energy, her form gradually fading, while pouring that perception into his shadow mana.

But although the mana reacted faster — even starting after her and only lagging slightly behind her disappearance — it ultimately just dissipated into the hall without bringing Jace into the shadow, let alone moving him any distance.

Mor'fel walked up from behind Jace, looking at the flickering candles nearby. "Don't be discouraged, darling. It was only the first try."

"I still don't understand how shadow perceives and spontaneously imitates the target caster's spell." Jace said. "Maybe there are details in the book I missed. I need to read it again more carefully."

Jace thought he might ask Khadgar for advice. The mage was well-versed in shadow, void, and all kinds of magic.

And Sazeno was almost out of paper for transcription anyway, so he could borrow more from Khadgar. He left the storeroom and headed toward the Curator's location.

The library was very quiet. Especially after the chaos of shadow energy he'd just stirred up, that quietness felt even more pronounced.

But as he walked, he couldn't shake the feeling that someone was watching him from somewhere nearby. Was it Khadgar? Or an arcane golem?

Jace glanced into a side corridor. Pitch black — he couldn't see a thing. No demon detection vison showed anything either.

From far away on the other side, he heard the *thump-thump* of an arcane golem walking. Jace looked in that direction and suddenly saw a silhouette looking in the same direction.

What the...

He tried to follow. Before he'd taken a few steps, the figure was also walking away down the corridor, their pace quickening, as if they realized someone was chasing them.

Jace lunged forward with a Shadowstep, reaching for the person's shoulder. The figure was instantly enveloped in shadow too, as if trying to escape with a spell!

But they weren't fast enough. Jace grabbed them hard by the shoulder and slammed them to the ground...

But at the same time, he felt *someone grab him from behind*!

He was knocked to the ground by that force and immediately pulled out Blade of the Eternals, pointing it behind him. A pair of purple-red demon eyes rushed straight at him from the front, unstoppably plunging into his body...

Jace coughed violently, feeling like he'd been hit square by a Shadow Bolt.

The area went quiet. That strange feeling vanished too.

But Jace had seen the face of that shadow figure clearly. It was himself.

He'd have to be more careful next time he tried Dark Simulacrum. He couldn't just let that "failed shadow" wander off on its own.
