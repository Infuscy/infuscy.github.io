## Chapter 148: The Power of Blood?

Lake Lordamere looked a little unfamiliar seen from the south. Jace had grown up on its shores, but he'd never viewed it from this direction.

The whole of Dalaran wasn't especially large — not compared to a great metropolis like Stormwind, anyway — but the exquisite buildings, the clean brick-paved streets, the soaring towers rising one after another, the gardens everywhere, the flower beds and little fountains at every intersection, all made it easy to believe this was a place of extraordinary wealth and tranquility.

The house Jace was in sat at Dalaran's southern end, in a district that felt relatively close to a normal city — one not especially steeped in magic.

He was on the third floor. Leaning over the metal railing of the windowsill and looking down, the street below was packed with people and bustling with activity. In just one casual sweep of his eyes, he spotted two or three groups of elves walking together.

In the late days of the Arathi Empire, Dalaran had been nothing more than a petty city-state sheltering under the empire's wing.

Back then, most human mages had been concentrated in the imperial capital, Strom. They learned magic from the high elves of Quel'Thalas and received their guidance, aiding the empire in its war against Amani troll incursions.

But as the empire gradually declined and internal troubles and external threats mounted, Strom had also grown increasingly distrustful of this group that revered an alien race.

The trolls found themselves unable to counter the swelling ranks of human mages, and the fighting between the two sides slowly died down. People gradually forgot everything the mages had sacrificed to defend the city.

Later, the mages left Strom, where the anti-magic currents were growing ever stronger, and came to this place in the west — dismissed as a backwater, yet possessed of strange ley-line energies: the tiny city-state of Dalaran.

More and more mages arrived from every corner of the Arathi Empire, and under magic's influence Dalaran grew ever more prosperous and powerful, rising swiftly against the tide of imperial decline to become a brand-new star.

Yet after the founders, who had come only to build a sanctuary for mages, had passed away, the new generation of mages took Dalaran's name as their pride and recklessly abused the power of magic, quickly dragging the city toward the abyss.

The space here grew ever more grotesque and twisted. Terrifying dimensional disasters struck frequently, and even demons manifested from within — until the situation finally drew the attention of Quel'Thalas.

The high elves came to Dalaran to aid the human mages, passing down forbidden knowledge passed down from the War of the Ancients, reestablishing order and restoring the city's peace.

To this day, the city still bore enormous elven influence. Many elves had also settled here under the names of diplomacy and mutual alliance, living alongside humans.

A wave of dizziness swept through Jace's mind. He winced and grabbed the windowsill railing, nearly toppling over.

The pain reminded him of the agony he'd experienced in that groggy half-consciousness before he'd opened his eyes in Dalaran. Now the swelling ache throughout his body had faded, but there was still a constant ringing in his ears, and bouts of pounding head pain would strike without warning.

Back then, even the blood he'd vomited up had looked like coagulated shadow slurry — you could barely make out any blood-color in it at all.

Was he cured now? Had Dalaran's mages helped cure him?

Were there any aftereffects?

Jace went back inside and rifled through the drawers for a while before finding a small mirror.

He examined his face, his eyes, and his teeth, looking for any traces of shadow corrosion — but everything appeared normal.

That was when Jace thought of one possibility that could explain why his arm hadn't healed.

Perhaps when he'd used Life Drain to attack the black dragon, he'd simultaneously drawn in both shadow and life force — after all, the black dragon was a dragon corrupted by shadow, and the shadow energy in its body was immensely powerful.

The dragon's fallen body could reconcile the two forces, but Jace probably couldn't.

The shadow power had entered him and harmed him, but at the same time the dragon's life force had repaired that shadow damage. In the end, though, the shadow power had still been the stronger of the two, which was what had caused his unconsciousness and the shadow-tainted blood he'd vomited. So his arm hadn't recovered either.

But this couldn't explain why there were no shadow-induced dreams.

Before this, even when he hadn't been seriouslyShadow-corroded — even just getting hit by a Fear once would give him a terrifying, tangled dream. It seemed that only at Stonewatch, when he'd been burned by his own shadow fire, had he been spared the torment of nightmares.

A somewhat outlandish possibility that seemed to explain everything floated up into his mind.

Could it be… that the black dragon blood had been absorbed by him?

Because that dragon blood had transformed into his own power, it hadn't produced any healing effect?

Absorbing dragon blood wasn't, overall, some kind of special ability.

Dragons frequently sought out mortals who worshipped them and gave them their own blood, granting them a measure of superhuman power — but the cost was being slowly enslaved and controlled by the dragon.

The end point of that path was the drakonid — a half-human, half-dragon hybrid monster that obeyed the dragonflight's commands without question. Ancient dragons could rely on that covenant-like power to create entire drakonid armies in their service.

Had he stumbled into that situation by accident?

Drakonids did sound pretty strong, but Jace had no interest in turning into an ugly monster with no mind of its own that only knew how to follow orders.

Of course, it had only been that brief moment of Life Drain — it couldn't possibly have consequences that severe, could it?

He even doubted whether a whelp's blood had the same potency as a full dragon's.

Setting those worries aside, Jace put on his own clothes, refolded the white shirt that wasn't his and set it on the table, and stuffed the cloak — not especially suitable for walking around Dalaran — into the warlock's bag.

He looked deep into the warlock's bag and tried saying, "Sazeno, if you can hear me, I want you not to show yourself… find some way to let me know you're there."

But there absolutely no response. Where had that imp gone?

Just as Jace was about to keep searching, a rustling sound suddenly rose behind him.

He turned around to find a magic broom standing in the middle of the hallway leading outside, sweeping on its own. When Jace noticed it, it seemed to sense it was being watched and stopped sweeping, frozen in place.

After roughly three seconds, it suddenly turned and went scritch-scritch-scritch-scritching away.

"Hold on…"

Jace followed the broom downstairs and came upon a man sitting at the downstairs dining table, drinking coffee and reading a book.

"Master Dushan?"

"Uh?" Caro Dushan turned around and saw Jace. "The Light preserve me, you… you're awake! I thought you were going to sleep right through the Orcish language exchange!"

Jace asked, "Miss the exchange… how many days was I out?"

"Four days, Jace Sesso," Dushan said. "It's already the fifth of December now. The Orcish language exchange has been scheduled for the eleventh — less than a week from now… Wait, you're getting out of bed right now? Are you sure you're all right? Those mages said your condition might not be very encouraging. I was just thinking about what other ways to treat you further, and here you standing right there… Is anywhere on your body feeling off?"

"I…" Jace looked himself over. "My arm still hurts. Is it a burn?"

"Yes," Dushan explained. "Your arm was burned by the black dragon's flames, but compared to the main damage you took, that's already nothing."

Jace said, "Hold on, Master. What exactly happened? How did I end up in Dalaran? What about that dragon? What injury did I even suffer?"

Dushan stared at Jace for a moment. "You don't remember anything? What's the most recent thing you remember?"

"I…"

Jace naturally couldn't tell him he'd used Life Drain — that dark magic — to drain that dragon half to death.

"I saw that dragon in the sentry tower looking straight at me, and then I don't remember anything after that."

"That's exactly it, then — you haven't forgotten a thing," Dushan explained. "That dragon attacked you with dark magic. Your injuries were extremely severe. Quite a few of the mages on site thought you were a goner. Southshore definitely couldn't handle a problem like that, so the Lordaeron soldiers urgently called for help from a passing gryphon rider and had you brought to Dalaran, hoping to find higher-level healers or scholars.

So that dwarf rider had really just been a random patrolling passerby. His companions had only rushed over after getting the news — and by then a long time had passed.

The way he'd died in that room — Jace still couldn't wipe it from his mind. It was as vivid as if he'd just seen it.

Dushan continued, "After a series of anti-shadow treatments, your vital signs finally stabilized, but the shadow energy in your body could never be completely purged."

"It's just that in the past few days, the villages and towns attacked by the black dragon weren't only Southshore — Ambermill and Edensblade were hit too. There were quite a few soldiers and mages injured as badly as you who were brought to Dalaran seeking treatment. Dalaran's medical resources are limited as well. Once you were out of mortal danger, they sent you out. I'm the only person in all of Dalaran who knows you, so I took you into my home first."

"I've really put you to too much trouble, Master Dushan."

"Don't be ridiculous, Jace Sesso," Dushan said. "You're my favorite student, and a warrior who just fought a black dragon. How could taking care of you count as trouble? I've just been worried you'd never wake up. And I was also a little afraid your talents wouldn't be there to bring glory to the Wizard Sanctum at the Orcish exchange."

He smiled and said, "Of course, if something had happened to you, Master Andormas might have slashed a good chunk of my funding afterward. That would've been troublesome too…"

Jace smiled sheepishly, then suddenly replayed what Dushan had just said. I'm the only person in all of Dalaran who knows you? Then what about Grede?

"What about that dwarf?" Jace asked.

Dushan suddenly recalled something. "Ah, I nearly forgot about your friend — that Wildhammer warrior named Grede. He's the friend who came with you from Stormwind to Southshore, right? He came by several times yesterday, every time grabbing me and asking if you was all right yet. He's fine, Jace. He's Southshore's dragon-slaying hero, in fact."

"I know you gave him a Shadow Protection Potion. That potion was absolutely critical. If you hadn't poured it down his throat right away, he'd probably have died there — or at the very least suffered severe lifelong aftereffects. But the strange thing is that you don't seem to have any aftereffects yourself. Doesn't that feel off to you? You took the full force of that black dragon's bizarre magic!"

Jace was now basically certain that no one knew he'd used Life Drain on that dragon.

He answered, "I still have a bit of a headache."

Dushan nodded. "Wait here — I'll run to the apothecary and get you something to suppress the shadow energy, at least something for the headache. Try to rest for now. Your friend will probably be here soon."

"It won't be too much expense, will it, Master?"

"The apothecary knows about your situation. The mayor of Southshore has already sorted out the costs of treating your injuries. Don't worry about it."
