## Chapter 1483: Brewfest Means a Lot to Dwarves

Jace reached Stormwind in the afternoon. A light rain was falling, and despite it still being summer, the weather had turned noticeably cool — a sign that autumn was drawing near.

Instead of riding Midnight straight through the main gate at the Valley of Heroes, Jace circled around the city walls to the small gate in the Mage Quarter, where the stable was.

The stable master was an old acquaintance of Jace's, and knew quite a bit about him — even had a fair amount of admiration for the guy. So when he spotted Jace dismounting in the distance, he grinned and waved.

"Master Sesso, good afternoon. Haven't seen you around here in a while. Master Andormas said you'd gone to the other side of the Dark Portal — how was..."

Before he could finish, the stable master caught sight of the horse Jace was leading. His cheerful expression vanished, replaced by wide-eyed terror.

"This... no... wait... this..."

"This horse has a serious illness, sir," Jace said. "Don't tie him up with the other horses. Put him somewhere on his own — he won't mind the conditions."

"This isn't... that man's horse, is it, Master Sesso?" the stable master stammered.

"You recognize this horse?" Jace asked.

The stable master was stunned for a long moment before saying, "Maybe I'm... overthinking things, Master. But he really looks like Midnight — Medivh's horse!"

At the name Medivh, Midnight snorted and turned his head, making the stable master jump back a step.

Jace hadn't expected anyone to recognize Midnight. But considering this was the Wizard Sanctum's stable, and Medivh had once come out of that very institution — if this man's elders or relatives had worked here too, and he'd grown up around the place, he might genuinely have seen Midnight alive back in the day.

Still, Jace couldn't admit it was really Midnight. News reaching the Wizard Sanctum, then Dalaran, would mean selling Khadgar out.

"It's not baseless that he reminds you of Medivh's horse," Jace said. "This is a warhorse recovered from the other side of the Dark Portal. The disease on him might be related to dark magic corrosion. Some priests in Stonard already treated him — he's stabilized now. But I'd still rather not have him with the other horses, just to be safe."

The stable master swallowed hard and tentatively tried to lead Midnight away. When the horse didn't resist, he relaxed a little.

Jace sighed. He still couldn't tell if taking this horse from Khadgar meant getting a helper or a massive headache.

"A warhorse that survived the Dark Portal..." the stable master muttered. "It's not really you, is it, Midnight?"

That question shot Jace's heart straight into his throat. But Midnight showed no reaction at all, woodenly following the stable master toward the stalls — nothing like the way he'd responded when Jace called his name before.

Maybe this trouble wasn't such big trouble after all, Jace thought, slowly letting out a breath.

Then he noticed the pile of books beside him and remembered. "Oh, right, sir — could I borrow a handcart or something?"

"Of course, Master Sesso," the stable master replied.

Jace sent Morofil and the imp ahead to the Slaughtered Lamb, then pushed the cart loaded with books across most of the Mage Quarter to Marin's study.

He knocked, got no response, and was about to assume no one was home when the alchemy lab door beside him opened. Old Marin stepped out and came face to face with him.

"Mr. Sesso?" Marin looked at the books in the cart. "What are all these? Donating old books to the Wizard Sanctum library?"

"No, these are ones I haven't finished yet, Master." Jace said. "Could I temporarily store them here? I'll take them back to Ilgalar's Tower when I go."

Marin lowered his voice. "What kind of books are these? Black magic? I don't want to sound suspicious, but these books — and you — have a... necromantic scent. Don't tell me you've started studying necromancy. That would cross every line anyone has. There are demonologists in Violet Castle, but the necromancy experts are all in Violet Prison."

"I just came back from Duskwood, Master." Jace picked up one of the top books — *The Origins of Ancient Demonic Languages* — and held it up for the mage. "It is related to dark magic, but it's all linguistics. Don't worry, you won't be implicated."

Marin raised an eyebrow at the title and pursed his lips. "Well, come into the study and we'll talk."

They went inside, and Jace asked, "Master, has Amy returned to Dalaran?"

"She has. She went with the Kirin Tor escort carrying those two items." Marin was tidying his bookshelves. "She's already arrived in Dalaran. I got her letter the day before yesterday."

"That fast," Jace said. "She told me she might not leave for a while. I thought if I hurried back to Stormwind I might catch her and give her a gift."

"What gift?" Marin asked.

"A pair of leather boots," Jace said.

"She doesn't need boots." Marin smiled. "Kirin Tor mages all wear similar robes that show their status and rank, and staves are more about function and power — so the one thing they can really dress up, besides their face, is their boots. Heaven knows how many pairs she's bought in Dalaran."

"These boots are different," Jace said. "I'm sure she'll like them."

"You're making me curious." Marin handed Jace a cup of tea, then glanced at the cloth bag in his hand and looked away. "Never mind. I won't be curious about my daughter's business. Speaking of which, Needleflame has been studying Dwarvish very intensely lately — especially the Dark Iron dialect. Is this related to the Alliance's plan to attack Blackrock Spire?"

"It is, Master," Jace said.

Marin clicked his tongue and sat back down behind his desk. "Hard to imagine a dragon her age having to fight enemies like the Dark Iron dwarves."

"She beat two young red dragons black and blue in Grim Batol, Master." Jace smiled. "I don't think we need to worry about her."

Marin raised his eyebrows in mild surprise, then seemed to remember something.

"Oh, speaking of Grim Batol." The archmage set down his teacup, leaned over, and pulled a letter from a drawer, handing it to Jace. "This came from Ironforge, Jace. It's from your good friend Lord Grede Thunderfist. Damn, I nearly forgot about it running into you like this."

A letter from Grede? Jace tore it open immediately. Inside was unmistakably Grede's handwriting.

"About a week ago, I got word from Ironforge, Mr. Sesso," Marin said. "The Wildhammer dwarves, with Bronzebeard support, raided a Dark Iron mine. They even took down a powerful lava giant. But they didn't push deeper, didn't try to fully take that square — they pulled back after they got what they wanted. No serious losses."

Jace finished the letter as Marin spoke. It was exactly what he'd hoped: the last and most important piece of Sulfuron Iron had been found. It would be smelted and sent to Grim Batol.

On September 21, the second day of Brewfest, High Thane Kurdran Wildhammer, Lord Brann Bronzebeard of Ironforge, and Börnheit, the master smith of the Thorium Brotherhood, would gather at Kardros' Anvil in Grim Batol to witness the forging of the Sulfuron Hammer.

And of course, Grede — the dwarven champion chosen by all three clans to wield the hammer — would be there too.

He wanted Jace, Vereesa, and Sora to come to Grim Batol for the ceremony, which was of immense importance to the Three Hammers and to Grede himself.

Jace had absolutely no reason to refuse. After all, he was the one who'd helped bring the hammer together in the first place.

Though he wasn't sure if Vereesa could make it — there was only a little over a month left until September 21.

He left the demon-language reference books and materials in Marin's study. Taking only the *Chronicle of Dark Secrets* and *Dark Simulacrum*, plus the boots and his own luggage, Jace headed straight for Sora's place.

She was the only one who could quickly get a message to the Emerald Dream.

He was hurrying toward the green dragon's residence when he happened to run into Sora and Needleflame coming out of the Blue Heron.

Needleflame's outfit looked a bit more proper than before. She still wore her favorite russet, but at least now she looked like a young mage apprentice who'd just been admitted to the Wizard Sanctum. Her messy black hair remained unchanged, though.

But the moment she saw Jace, the excitement and thrill swelling inside her was — to Jace's eyes — like a fireball exploding at the entrance of the Blue Heron. Outwardly, though, she just blinked, looking completely calm.

"You didn't die out there, Jace Sesso! What a surprise!" She puffed out her cheeks and strode up to him. "You go adventuring for a few weeks and come back looking fatter. Have you been sneaking gnoll barbecue out there?"

"Gnolls eat human flesh, Needleflame," Jace said flatly.

"Eh?" Needleflame blinked. Sora laughed and tugged her ear, then looked at Jace, about to speak — when she suddenly glanced at his midsection.

"You're injured, Jace. I can feel it. A serious wound. Who did this?"

"Someone stabbed me with a sword. It's mostly healed," Jace said.

"Someone dared to stab you with a sword?" Needleflame grabbed Jace's clothes. "You need to be more careful. Take me with you next time, Jace Sesso. I'll show whoever tries to stab you — I'll roast them!"

Jace figured that might be a tall order, but he patted Needleflame's brow and said, "Well, how about coming to Grim Batol with me and your sister Sora afterward?"

"You still want to give me to the red dragons?!" Needleflame demanded.

"Of course not. We're going to watch the Sulfuron Hammer being forged," Jace said.

"Does that mean we're going to Shadowforge City after?!" Needleflame stood on her tiptoes.

"That's right." Jace enunciated each syllable.

"Finally, finally!" Needleflame's eyes showed a glint of something almost like Onyxia's viciousness. Hard to say if the little one wanted to go to Shadowforge City to carry out a mission, or to eat fresh dwarves alive.

"Can you send a message through the Emerald Dream in your sleep, Sora?" Jace handed the letter to the green dragon. "Tell Vereesa about this for me."

"I will." Sora nodded.

"I want to make a small detour to Dalaran on the way to Grim Batol, then head east to meet Grede and the others," Jace said.

Hearing this, Sora couldn't help clapping her hands excitedly. "What?" Jace asked.

"Alterac goats, Jace." Sora leaned in and whispered. "Perfect — take me to find Alterac goats!"

Right. Two dragons. Their priorities were completely different from normal people's.
