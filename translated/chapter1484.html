Chapter 1484: The New Dark Iron

"For the Fire Lord Ragnaros!"

In Sora's room, a dark-haired girl raised a sturdy arm and shouted in the Dark Iron dialect of Dwarvish.

She grabbed Sora's Rod of Domination and swung it a few times. With the fire in her eyes — which, as black dragon fire, was even closer to the earth-fire essence of Blackrock Mountain than a real Dark Iron's — plus the fiery gem on the rod, Needleflame actually looked the part of a fire-wielder.

"What business do you have in Shadowforge City, human?" She planted the rod. "We are at war with the Blackrock orcs and the Black Wing. Our enemies are the Black Wing's prince, Nefarian, a bunch of insidious, scheming reptiles who specialize in infiltration and disguise. So we must be as cautious as possible — stay alert and increase scrutiny of all strangers. Now open your bags and set your cloak aside!"

Dragon linguistic talent was indeed so strong it was hard for mortals to imagine. Two or three months for Jace would at most let him manage basic conversation. But Needleflame — even under crash tutoring from the Wizard Sanctum's Dwarvish master and a polyglot like Sora — had picked up the Dark Iron accent to a frightening degree of accuracy.

The tone, combined with a dragon's natural authority, nearly made Jace instinctively open his bag and submit to inspection by this female fire-wielder — and Needleflame was only three years old.

"That's really impressive, Needleflame. I was actually wondering whether I could take you to Shadowforge City, but now..." Jace nodded, then added, "There are a few things to note, though. For example, Dark Iron dwarves wouldn't call them the 'Black Wing.' That's your polite term. If you call them 'hated reptiles' later but 'the Black Wing race' earlier, isn't that contradictory? A dead giveaway."

"You're right. I overlooked that." The dark-haired girl's expression and tone were a bit fierce, though even Jace couldn't tell if she was staying in character or genuinely annoyed.

"Also, worshippers of the Fire Lord don't call their deity 'Flame King.'" Jace continued. "And most Dark Iron dwarves don't actually know the true name of Blackrock Spire's master. At most, they know he calls himself Victor Nefarius."

"I only said a couple of lines, and there are this many problems?" Needleflame asked. "Did you just make those up?"

"That's just how it is, Needleflame." Sora chimed in. "I've been to Shadowforge City. I noticed these issues too — I just forgot to mention them."

Jace nodded and put on a teacher's serious tone. "Your language is good enough now. So next up: cramming on Shadowforge City culture. Keep at it, Lady Needleflame."

"I don't believe..." The little black dragon poked Jace's shoulder with the end of the rod. "...that the dwarves will be as picky as you!"

"Shadowforge City has a lot of people. Even if only a few are picky, that's trouble enough," Jace said.

"Jace's wound hasn't healed yet, Needleflame." Sora grabbed the rod. "Be careful. Watch your strength!"

"Eh, the spot he got stabbed isn't here." Still, the black dragon obediently returned the rod to Sora.

"Sorry, Jace Sesso."

Seeing her like that, Jace — who hadn't really minded — couldn't resist the urge to ham it up. He covered his wound and put on a pained expression. "It's fine, it's fine."

"This guy..." Needleflame muttered under her breath.

"You went out with Master Khadgar, Jace. You're wounded — what about him?" Sora pressed, still worried. "What kind of enemy did you face that could break through both your defenses and hurt you like this? At the Auchindoun portal, even the Shadow Council's most elite orc warriors couldn't do that."

"Ah, and Lady Morofil too," Needleflame added.

Jace looked at both of them and hesitated. Explaining exactly who had stabbed him with a sword and why was hard to put into words.

The arcane energy damage from this sword wound was very different from a direct blade thrust, making it even harder to find a suitable scapegoat.

Seeing Jace's hesitation, Sora shook her head finally. "I know Master Khadgar doesn't want you to share too much. I'm just saying — if you need my help treating it, don't be reluctant to tell me, Jace."

"You know I'd always come to you, Sora." Jace nodded.

"You won't even tell Sora? You can't say where you went, can't say who you fought..." Needleflame shed her dwarven form in a puff of smoke and leaned into Jace's face. "At least tell me how you beat them. Let me see who managed to do what even my father couldn't."

Looking at her eyes, Jace wondered if she was genuinely concerned about his injury or looking for a way to avenge her father.

"A very powerful mage. I suspect he might be connected to the orcs wandering in Kalimdor — he knew a lot of dark magic and strange arcane spells. That mage used some kind of arcane phantom blade neither Khadgar nor I had ever seen before, caught me off guard with something I wasn't familiar with, and stabbed me. But we came out on top in the end. It was just a little episode on the journey."

"Arcane phantom blade..." Needleflame frowned, lost in thought. Jace quickly tried to redirect her attention. "Tomorrow I'll have Morofil continue your combat training, Needleflame. Take a look at how many troublesome enemies there are in this world."

Needleflame shook off her doubts. "Can't we start tonight?"

"I have other plans with her tonight," Jace said.

"Other plans?" The black dragon narrowed her eyes.

"Don't overthink it. It's a warlock gathering," Jace said.

"A warlock gathering..." Needleflame's eyes narrowed further. Sora couldn't help but laugh. He didn't realize that once you brought other warlocks into it, there was no explaining your way out.

Still, if Jace had gone to Karazhan with Morofil and Khadgar simply to restore order to the tower, he wouldn't have needed to tell his friends everything that happened.

But now that the three of them had encountered Aegwynn's phantom, seen the traces of that powerful magical ritual in the Hall of Watchers, and learned that Aegwynn already knew about Uthalesh... he couldn't keep it from his friends anymore.

So Jace was also considering finding the right opportunity to tell them the whole story — including his concerns about the possibility that Kil'jaeden had already made contact with Aegwynn, Aegwynn's inner conflict, and the things that might appear in prophetic visions.

After all, these people all knew Uthalesh was in Jace's possession. Even if Aegwynn seemed to have decided not to pursue the matter anymore, the friends who knew Uthalesh's secret were all in her field of vision. He couldn't let them remain ignorant of this hidden, terrible threat.
