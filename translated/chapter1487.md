## Chapter 1487: Operation Warlord

The next afternoon, Jace arrived at the SI:7 headquarters. He didn't notice anyone running inside to announce his arrival, but he soon felt waves of barely-suppressed panic spreading from within the villa.

As he stepped through the door, a clatter of footsteps came from the stairs. Matthias Shaw hurried down to the foyer and took a deep breath. "Master Sesso."

"Why am I 'Master Sesso' all of a sudden, Mr. Shaw?" Jace asked.

"I know, I know you're here about the mine incident, Jace." Shaw cleared his throat. "I need to explain. I understand the Dagger Clan meant well, and I've already spoken with 'Blackclaw.' I know you were trying to clear out the gnolls there. But the gnolls and murlocs on the west coast can't be exterminated — it's been that way for centuries. Everyone's used to it. But a dark magic disaster spreading could make things even worse around Moonbrook... After the Orc Wars, some farms there were already abandoned. You don't want the king's hopes for reviving the west coast to die, turning those beautiful golden fields into wilderness, do you?"

"Completely understood, Matthias." Jace said. "But I'm not actually here about the mine today. Well, strictly speaking, it's tangentially related, but not really."

"Ah." Shaw seemed to breathe a slight sigh of relief. "Come upstairs and talk, Jace."

They went upstairs. Shaw poured two cups of coffee and handed one to Jace. "New coffee from Dalaran. The beans are supposedly from Kalimdor. The name sounds as complicated as a spell — I can't remember it — but I know it's popular with mages. They say it replenishes mana."

"Thanks." Jace took a sip, his eyes widening slightly at the acidity. "When I passed through Darkshire, I heard ogres have been active again in the Wogul Mountains south of Raven Hill."

"Yes." Shaw said. "Their leader, a self-styled 'warlord' named Zak'ur, is quite cunning. He's been playing hide-and-seek with us in those mountains for years."

"Back when the Wizard Sanctum and the Farstriders jointly hunted rogue demons and tracked down renegade warlocks around Moonbrook and Darkshire, this Zak'ur took his bandit crew and hid deep in the southern mountains. We thought they'd gone to target the Gurubashi trolls, but it turns out they were just lying low."

"With the king preparing for Blackrock Spire, they've found an opportunity to show themselves again." Shaw looked up. "So you're saying the Dagger Clan wants to move your dark magic experiments from the west coast to the Wogul Mountains?"

"That's the idea," Jace said. "I came to get your thoughts."

"My thought is — how much darker can Duskwood get?" Shaw smiled. "I wanted to send someone to assassinate the warlord. But ogres aren't completely brainless. Not only did we fail, we lost a young agent's life."

"So you know where this Zak'ur is holed up now," Jace said.

"We do." Shaw stood up. "He figures the king doesn't have time to deal with him right now, so he's been brazen lately. He's not bothering to hide his lair very carefully anymore."

He left the room and returned with another agent, carrying a map of the southern Elwynn mountains covered in markings.

"Brubeck, our scout." Shaw said. "He'll fill you in."

The agent named Brubeck spread the map on the table and pointed at an X mark. "There's an old abandoned troll witch doctor's cave here, Master Sesso. Based on intelligence from about six weeks ago, Zak'ur is using it as his command post."

"We can't send scouts in too often. The warlord is cautious — if he senses Stormwind is coming for him again, he'll retreat deeper into the mountains. But he'll also try to retaliate, using his brute force and dark magic to create more chaos. You need to be sure the Dagger Clan can get close."

"Are there many warlocks among those ogres?" Jace asked.

"At least two." Brubeck said. "One is the warlord's advisor. The other we know about from a caravan guard who survived an attack. There might be more with the warlord."

"Then that's where the Dagger Clan has the advantage," Jace said. "Two-headed ogre warlocks are smarter than your average ogre brute, but their desires are also stronger. If they hear about some strange magical treasure they might get their hands on, they won't be able to resist. We can use that — get close under the pretense of trading dark magic knowledge or exchanging treasures, then take them all out."

Brubeck glanced at Shaw, hesitated, then said, "Take them all out? Sorry, Master Sesso, forgive me for saying this, but if you go deep into their territory, you'll be completely surrounded by ogres. The warlord will order his warlocks to do a 'warlock special' on you — take all your stuff and kill you — not the other way around."

"I think, sir, we should contact the Wizard Sanctum and coordinate with our scouts for outside support."

Shaw smiled. "You have no idea who you're talking to, Brubeck. The man in front of you is the one who used magic to destroy Onyxia's arm in her own flame domain. I don't think you need to worry about the 'warlock special' problem."

"So... that flame domain — it really happened?" Brubeck's eyes went wide as he stared at Jace nervously. "Sorry, Master Sesso. I was just worried. I don't want to... I mean, I don't want to underestimate the ogres."

"I completely understand, Mr. Brubeck." Jace said. "We'll be as careful as possible. If dealing with those ogres is straightforward, then there's even less reason to take unnecessary casualties."

"About the support, though, Jace." Shaw said. "Don't bother with the Wizard Sanctum, especially considering the purpose of this trip is magical experimentation. But I do want to come with a few agents, including Brubeck here. On one hand, with more and more demons and strange magic appearing in this world, I want our younger agents to get some real experience before facing the enemies at Blackrock Spire. More importantly, I want to see that warlord die with my own eyes. That bastard has been living under our noses for too long."

"I don't have a problem with that, Matthias." Jace said. "I'll talk to Galders. I'm sure he'll agree."

Shaw nodded. Beside him, Brubeck still seemed to be coming to terms with his shock.
