Chapter 1489: The Splinter Fists of Vul’Gol

At the entrance to Darkshire, several riders gathered by the roadside. Jace sat on Midnight and observed the horses behind him. Every warlock seemed to have chosen a dark brown or black mount.

Clearly, the Dagger Society’s financial situation had improved significantly. These horses were all tall, well-built, and powerful in movement. By contrast, Jace’s Midnight was tall enough but pitifully thin — and worse, wrapped in mageweave bandages in several places, including his eyes, to hide the undead’s stiff skin and slightly festering flesh. The horse looked like nothing more than a pathetic battlefield deserter.

An ordinary person ignorant of magic might think Jace’s horse was sick or just old, but these warlocks could all sense faint traces of demonic heat hidden within the animal.

And because each warlock carried wands and foci infused with shadow energy, the horses nearby couldn’t help but be affected — restless, snorting, pawing the ground. Yet the horse that should have been the most unsettled, Midnight, stood almost perfectly still, not moving a muscle. Except for his black mane stirred by the wind, he was like a silent statue, eerie enough to unsettle even these dark magic practitioners.

So, by unspoken agreement, no one asked where the horse came from.

“Even though the Dagger Society has been around for years, I think this is our first time going out together.” Erlan looked at Ursula. “Getting our alchemist out of her lab, and having Jace and Zardeth both in the Kingdom of Stormwind at the same time — that’s not easy.”

“At least I came with you to the west coast last time, even if it didn’t go well,” Ursula said.

“Erlan’s right. When I think about it, even when Gakin and Mor’zul were around, we never had this kind of opportunity.” Zardeth agreed.

“Speaking of which, where did Mor’zul go?” Jalane Ayrole asked.

“I haven’t heard anything about him since the Lighthouse,” Jace said. “I only know he refused the drakonid’s invitation to join the Lighthouse. Seemed to see through the black dragon’s scheme.”

“He probably went to Kalimdor like the other warlocks, to follow Cho’gall.” Erlan said. “Most of the warlocks who went silent around here are either dead or have been spotted in Kalimdor. I’ve heard of quite a few.”

Mor’zul Bloodbringer — in another timeline, he would later occupy the Altar of Storms abandoned by the Blackrock orcs and attempt to contact the demon realm of Xoroth, ruled by the Nathrezim Hel’nurath, to summon Xorothian warhorses as his mounts.

If he really had fled to Kalimdor because of the Lighthouse incident, Jace wondered who would take over his research into contacting Xoroth. Maybe one of the warlocks he’d met when summoning Kallrez at the Altar of Storms. At least compared to the warlocks who’d fully sided with the black dragons at the Lighthouse, the group at the Altar of Storms were more sensible.

“Mathias Shaw is already waiting for us at Raven Hill. Let’s move.” Zardeth pulled on his reins. “Let’s go.”

The others acknowledged and turned their horses as one toward the dark forest.

Come to think of it, Jace had never been into the mountains south of Raven Hill, except by ship straight to Booty Bay.

In the game, Vul’Gol was relatively simple — just a valley with an ogre cave, close to a few farms. But on the map here, Vul’Gol stretched all the way to the Stranglethorn coast. Looking at the mountain range, even the hill behind Moonbrook where Mor’zul used to live was part of Vul’Gol, just cut off by the Nazferiti River.

In the thousand years before the Gurubashi War and the founding of the Kingdom of Stormwind, this mountain range had kept the jungle trolls from pushing north into Elwynn Forest to raid human farms, while also blocking humans from expanding south into Stranglethorn.

It wasn’t until the Kingdom of Stormwind won the Gnoll War, took control of the west coast and Redridge, and captured several gold mines that the improving economy and growing population pushed farmers and adventurers over Vul’Gol and further south, eventually sparking the fierce conflict between Zul’Gurub and Stormwind. After both sides suffered heavy losses and the war ended, the de facto border settled around Vul’Gol.

That gave some idea of just how rugged and complex this mountain range was — enough to let a group of ogre deserters from Stormwind hide here for over a decade.

In the evening, the group dismounted at an abandoned farm controlled by Stormwind scouts. Led by SI:7 agent Brubeck and his colleagues responsible for Vul’Gol reconnaissance, they headed south into the mountains.

Even though Duskwood’s dark pall had somewhat lifted, deep in the valleys the twisted trees, bare rock outcrops, half-buried troll wall ruins with their snarling beast carvings still made the place feel incredibly sinister.

If not for the SI:7 scouts leading the way, Jace would definitely have been lost by now — once they reached this canyon, the familiar road behind them was swallowed by the forest. His past-life memories were almost useless here.

This place was undoubtedly remote and rarely traveled, but occasionally they’d come across dirt roads, some still paved with broken bricks. These definitely weren’t built by the Kingdom of Stormwind — they must be “relics” of the Gurubashi Empire.

The Gurubashi trolls had once fallen into a bloody civil war over summoning the blood god Hakkar. Back then, the Kingdom of Stormwind didn’t even exist — the rulers of Zul’Gurub still governed a vast territory including Elwynn and Lake Everstill. Hard to say what role Vul’Gol had played in the cult worship of those dark gods. Maybe this place’s gloom wasn’t only due to the dark energy released from Karazhan.

At a steep slope, Brubeck picked two agents to stay behind and keep watch. “Both of them have clashed with the ogres before. We don’t want them recognized. They’ll stand guard here. The rest of us, keep moving.”

He pushed aside the thorny brush and pointed toward a gully of unknown destination deeper in the valley. “There. That’s where the ogres are holed up. About two more hours from here. We’ll likely run into ogre brutes along the way.”

“So we’re your warlocks’ hired muscle.” Mathias walked as he spoke.

“Hired from Moonbrook,” Jace added. “Here to make sure our trade with the ogres goes safely.”

“Great.” Mathias said. “So once the fighting starts, those club-hammers bigger than tree trunks are all going to land on our heads first.”

“Don’t worry, redhead.” Surena said. “Our succubus servants will do their best to make sure those clubs redirect back onto their own heads mid-swing…”

“Let’s hope your demon friends can chant fast enough, lady.” Mathias smiled politely.

They hadn’t walked much further when Jace spotted a charred tree trunk planted on a bare rock outcropping. At the very top hung a huge piece of half-burned clefthoof hide flapping in the wind, marked with blood-red symbols too faded to read clearly. But Jace could still make out the general pattern — ogre totems weren’t exactly complicated.

“Hey, what’s going on? Huh? How…”

A boulder lying low by the roadside suddenly shifted. It was an ogre.

The ogre lifted his head, raised a short spear, and shouted in slurred Common. “Ow! How did humans get here! Straight to here! Our sentries, all killed? Quick! Someone…”

Mid-sentence, he seemed to remember something and switched to even harder-to-understand Orcish, shouting for help. The ogre’s rough bellow echoed through the quiet, dark valley, answered by other ogres in the distance. For a moment, the scene was quite intimidating.

The two younger agents reflexively drew their weapons. Jace immediately raised a hand to signal them to hold, then said in Orcish, “Calm down, ogre brother. I know you’re from the Crushridge clan.”

“Yo, you speak Orcish.” The ogre grunted curiously. “But we’re not Crushridge clan! We got kicked out of Crushridge clan!”

“Seceded from Crushridge clan, you idiot!” Another ogre poked his head out from further away. “We’re the proud Splinter Fist clan! The kind of fists that splinter the Crushridge!”

“The great Splinter Fist clan!” Jace responded immediately. “We’re a group of warlocks from the north. We heard your warlord, the mighty Zak’ur, learned some incredibly profound magic from Cho’gall, so we came all this way to see it for ourselves. In exchange, we’ve also brought some magic and treasures.”

“Warlocks? What do you know! Zak’ur is incomparably mighty! He wouldn’t stoop to learning tricks!” The ogre shouted. “The clever Orguril is truly Cho’gall’s student. He knows plenty of powerful tricks! Even the skinny-arms down south think he’s something! They wanted him to go to Zul’gur…”

The ogre’s tongue seemed to tangle with his teeth. Another ogre roared, “Did you hear that? He said they’ve got treasures to trade! Treasures! Tell King Zak’ur! Tell Orguril! A group of well-behaved humans is here! No smashing, no fighting, they’re bringing good stuff right to us!”

The warlocks and agents watched as Jace talked to the two ogres as if coaxing children, and somehow it seemed to be working.

“So the warlock is named Orguril. We follow them.” Jace said to the group behind him.

“Now I understand how you survived Draenor, a place full of ogres.” Mathias muttered.

“The hard part hasn’t started yet.” Jace said. “Two-headed ones aren’t so easy to fool.”
