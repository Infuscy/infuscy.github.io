## Chapter 149: Dear Mr. Sesso

Dushan grabbed his robe off the rack and bolted out the door. Jace sat on the stairs in a daze for a while, murmuring to himself: "Dragon-slayer…"

Even though Jace knew that was his best brother, it would've been a lie to say he felt no sting of disappointment at all.

But he couldn't tell anyone — not a soul — what he'd actually done to that dragon. Letting everyone believe Grede had dealt the critical blow was, if anything, a way to hide the truth and protect himself.

So long as the dragon was dead, so long as he'd helped the people of Southshore, avenged the injured and the victims, and saved Grede — titles like "dragon-slayer" didn't amount to much anyway.

Then again, thinking about it that way did reek a little of consoling himself.

Jace scratched his head, which only made the headache worse.

He went back upstairs to his room. Recalling the scene of that standoff with the black dragon, he even half-wondered whether it was just because he'd so badly wanted to kill that dragon with his own hands that he'd produced the illusion of casting a spell.

Fooling himself until he actually believed it, in other words.

Meaning he hadn't cast any Life Drain at all, and all of that had just been a nightmare brought on by losing consciousness after being hit by the dragon's dark magic, warped by shadow energy?

How could he confirm what had actually happened back then? If only Sazeno were still around — he definitely would have witnessed the whole thing.

Except now that imp had gone god-knows-where. Maybe he'd been so terrified by the arcane power of this magic city that they'd been brought to that he'd thrown himself off a gryphon to kill himself or something.

"Did you just call for me, master?"

Sazeno suddenly appeared right under his ribs. Jace jumped out of his skin, snatched him up in one motion, bolted up the stairs, and back to his room's doorway.

"Why'd you pop out all of a sudden? Why didn't you come out earlier?"

He glanced downstairs and confirmed that aside from himself and the imp, there was no one else in the house before settling back down.

But this was Dalaran, after all…

Jace peeked out through the window. Dalaran had been an anti-demon headquarters for two thousand years. If Sazeno suddenly showed up on the street and a few arcane turrets materialized in the sky to surgically remove him, Jace wouldn't have been the least bit surprised.

"That's because there was someone in the house just now, master! If there's someone in the house, I absolutely will never show my face — those were your orders!"

"Executed thoroughly. Verbal commendation," Jace said. "Now I've got a question for you."

The imp hopped up and straightened his backbone. "Ask away, master!"

"When we were dealing with that black dragon, did you sense what was happening outside? Let me be direct — did you feel me cast?"

"Of course I felt it," Sazeno said with certainty. "You killed the black dragon with Life Drain. Granted, it was already getting the absolute hell beaten out of it, or you'd be dead for sure… You're not going to get angry at me for telling the truth, are you?"

"No."

Jace wasn't angry in the slightest. If anything, that stubborn little knot of doubt in his chest had finally loosened and fallen away.

Without Grede's hammer blow, the whole thing could've gone any number of ways.

He'd been within the dragon's attack range the entire time. One wrong move and he'd have been roasted to a lump of black charcoal.

Grede was unquestionably the real dragon-slayer. Whereas when he himself had faced that dragon, he'd been flustered and fumble-fingered — not heroic in the slightest.

"We're in Dalaran now. This city is home to the most powerful human mages, and those mages hate demons." Jace looked at Sazeno. "So for the sake of your own hide, you need to hide in that bag for the time being."

"Understood!" The imp said, and hopped back in.

Jace was about to seal the bag shut when he noticed the letter he'd brought along — the one he'd planned to sell to some Orcish-language noble. Teron Gorefiend's secret command.

Thinking of the Alterac Valley raid Teron Gorefiend had launched, and the orc crisis that had afterward swept the entire mountain range… which also included the black dragon rampage. The dragon thing had always struck him as off.

That black dragon had flown to Southshore and didn't seem to be trying to destroy anything in particular. Its targets were completely random — it pretty much just flew wherever and burned whatever it found.

And the black dragons of Azeroth weren't solitary wild beasts whose life's ambitions amounted to eating people, eating animals, looting treasure, and growing as large as possible.

The ancient black dragons of Azeroth — Deathwing and his first brood — ruled the Blackwing Legion. Whether adult dragons, young dragons, whelps, or hatchlings, all of them answered to those ancient leaders' commands.

A whelp who'd dared attack an important stronghold like Southshore at the risk of its life, and even ended up losing that life, absolutely couldn't have been acting randomly or lashing out in a private grudge.

And on top of that, Dushan had just mentioned that Southshore wasn't the only place hit by black dragon attacks.

What on earth were they trying to do?

Why were these whelps running around causing chaos everywhere? Was it to scatter Dalaran's forces — a "lure the tiger from the mountains" play — and then concentrate their strength for a surprise assault on Dalaran?

No. Impossible.

Deathwing really would attack Dalaran later on, but that was to stage an elaborate fake death to pave the way for his next scheme.

And that fake death was a pretty shoddy performance, too — the red dragon mage Krasus had noticed something was off.

If you were going to stage a magnificent fake death, what was the point of all this preliminary fuss, these operations that wasted your own lives? You should wait for Dalaran's defenses to be at their tightest and charge straight in, making everyone believe that the death of the Dragonfather of the black flight was an achievement that the entire magical kingdom had poured its desperate efforts into accomplishing.

Wait. A chill sweat broke out on the back of Jace's neck.

He opened that secret command he'd translated from start to finish and read through it once more, beginning to end.

This time he spotted something he'd completely missed the first time he'd translated it — back when he'd just started learning Orcish runes and his skill hadn't been up to it: a tense issue!

The verb Teron Gorefiend used for "collect the three artifacts" in the command wasn't past tense — it was future tense.

What this letter actually meant to convey was that the three artifacts were about to be gathered. He wanted his subordinate — a suspected death knight named Anggor — to hurry through the Dark Portal to the Horde to welcome the new warchief's ascension.

The key word was "about to" — not "already gathered"!

So which artifact hadn't been obtained yet?

The raid on Alterac had already concluded. This was the exchange Teron Gorefiend had promised to the House of Perenolde in return for Medivh's Book. He must already have Medivh's Book in his grasp by now.

The command stated that Gul'dan was dead and that the Horde had elected a new leader, Ner'zhul.

After the Horde's defeat, Gul'dan had traveled to the fabled Tomb of Sargeras, attempting to devour the evil power left behind on Azeroth by the Dark Titan — only to unleash the countless demons buried within and be consumed by them in return. The Blacktooth Grin orcs who'd followed Gul'dan had fled back across the sea, bringing news of Gul'dan's death and word that the Staff of Sargeras had resurfaced. Only then did Ner'zhul decide to send Teron Gorefiend and the death knights to the Tomb of Sargeras, aiming to seize the staff from the demons.

So the question was — had the Staff of Sargeras been obtained at this point?

Once the tense shifted, everything became uncertain.

As for the third artifact… the Eye of Dalaran, preserved in the Kirin Tor's vault. This letter made no mention of it from beginning to end.

That was the only artifact Teron Gorefiend had seized personally. Sometime between the seventh and eighth years after the Dark Portal, he'd concealed himself and infiltrated Dalaran, relying on his subordinates to divert the mages' attention while he entered Dalaran's vault and made off with the treasure.

The reason Teron had been able to enter and exit Dalaran without obstruction was entirely thanks to the offspring of Deathwing — the black dragons!

Could it be that the black dragons rampaging across Hillsbrad were exactly that group of dragons, doing everything they could to distract the mages so that Teron could enter Dalaran with as little resistance as possible?

Now, this translation error wouldn't have mattered a damn to Jace personally back then. After all, he'd been in Stormwind at the time. Even if he'd translated it accurately, the whole affair wouldn't have had anything to do with him at all.

But things were a bit different now. He was already in Dalaran. If Teron Gorefiend planned to infiltrate this place, wouldn't he be in some danger too? Of course, all of this was just speculation on his part, based on this one command. If he wanted to confirm it, he'd need to know whether the Eye of Dalaran was still in the city.

"Master Caro Dushan! I'm back again, and you know what I've come to ask… Huh? Master? You left without even closing the door? Is anyone in the house?"

Suddenly hearing Grede's voice, Jace's heart clenched. All that tangled frustration over losing the "dragon-slayer" title evaporated in an instant.

He walked out of his room and came face-to-face with the dwarf climbing the stairs.

The moment their eyes met, the dwarf immediately grabbed his crutch and lurched forward, crashing into Jace and pinning him flat to the floor.

"You ass…" He gripped Jace's neck and shook him furiously. "You scared the living hell out of me!"

"Let… go…" Jace grabbed the dwarf's stony wrists and squeezed two words out of his nearly crushed throat. "Let go!"

Jace shoved him away hard. Grede stumbled back a few steps, grabbed the railing, and caught his breath before saying, "You're recovering damn well, Jace. Even stronger than before, I'd say!"

Now that he mentioned it, Jace did feel like something was a little off. But he still said, "You nearly choked me to death, dammit. I really am fighting for my life here."

With that he sat back against the wall and noticed Grede's arm was wrapped in thick bandages.

"What happened to you?"

"What do you think? I cracked a dragon's skull — you didn't think I'd come out of it without losing a single hair, did you?" Grede picked up his crutch and leaned it against the staircase railing. "But these are all minor. They'll heal up soon. I busted my leg when I fell. For us dwarves, this kind of injury heals completely in two weeks with proper rest. It's already gotten a bit better."

"You're sure?" Jace asked. "Two weeks to recover from a broken leg?"

"Of course. Though the Southshore priest already treated me quite a bit, or I'd have ended up crippled." He added, "Crippled further, I mean."

Jace glanced at Grede's eye patch. "As long as it heals completely, that's what matters. I'll take care of you these next few days."

"You take care of yourself first," Grede smiled. "Yesterday I thought you were going to spend the rest of your life lying in that bed. I'd already started thinking about getting your parents' address and sending you back to them — except now I don't know anything except that your father's a Sesso."

"There's only one Sesso family in Lordaeron, though there are a few more in Brill," Jace said. "But thanks for the thought, anyway, Dragon-slayer!"

"If killing a whelp counts as a dragon-slayer," Grede said, "then being a dragon-slayer isn't much to brag about. Three whelps have been killed in the last two days alone — though south of the Alterac Mountains, ours was the only one. All in all, it feels like they've churned out no fewer than a dozen dragon-slayers. You definitely count as one too, Jace. They just happened to be cutting the dragon's head off to celebrate while you was still unconscious. No one's denying your contribution just because you weren't there."

"What contribution did I make?" Jace asked.

Grede glanced downstairs, then fixed Jace with a sidelong stare and lowered his voice. "I know what you did, Jace. I was still awake then — I just couldn't move. But I heard the whole thing. You killed that dragon with dark magic, didn't you?"

"More… or less," Jace nodded.

"What do you mean, 'more or less'?" Grede leaned in close. "I watched that dragon go limp under your magic, its scales losing color all over. You think I've never seen that kind of magic before? Don't forget, I'm a veteran of the Orc Wars. I've heard of those orc warlocks who use a horrifying magic that drains their enemies' lives. I'm guessing I'm not wrong."

"I was just desperate to save you," Jace said. "You didn't go and tell those South Shore soldiers directly, did you?"

"What, do I look stupid?" Grede said. "I told them I saw you use arcane magic to draw that black dragon's attention, which gave the soldiers the chance to spear it to death. As for what kind of arcane magic — well, I'm just a dumb, reckless Wildhammer dwarf, wouldn't know a thing about that, now would I? If you don't want your cover blown, you'd better learn a similar-sounding spell fast. I heard there's some kind of Slow spell or something… Oh, right — just tell them you blinded its eyes with Light."

Jace let out a deep breath. "Thanks, Grede."

"Don't mention it, dear Mr. Sesso," Grede answered in an exaggerated Lordaeron accent.

"Truth be told, just going by the magic you used, you're already one of the most evil warlocks I've ever come across," he added.

Jace glanced at the warlock's bag sitting by his hand. If he opened this bag and showed him what was inside, the "one of" would disappear in a hurry.

Grede slapped the railing with a "hey, hey." "So, evil warlock — how about we go hit a tavern? Dalaran might be prissy as all get-out, but the drinks are top-notch. Those elves with nothing better to do all day really do make something special!"

Jace had been about to say his arm burns hadn't healed yet, but then he thought, ah, screw it.

"Let me leave a note for Master first, then we'll go."
