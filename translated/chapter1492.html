Chapter 1492: Mathias's Suggestion

The ogre-leather banner that had been planted was knocked down, replaced by Stormwind's blue-and-lion flag, fluttering slightly in the morning breeze.

If the sunlight hadn't been shining into the valley just then, Jace wouldn't have realized they'd already put some distance between themselves and Duskwood. Or maybe it had more to do with Karazhan's dark energy gradually weakening.

The soldiers from Raven Hill were counting the dead ogres under the guidance of several SI:7 agents, along with the human bones in the valley and caves.

Many soldiers glanced over as they passed, wanting to see what the caster of that magic looked like.

Most of them had come from Raven Hill. Most had assumed this SI:7 operation was just a probe, a reconnaissance mission — at most a retaliatory warning for Zakh'ur, a forceful response to the recent spate of raids.

No one had expected — maybe not even Mathias himself — that a single spell would wipe out every ogre in the valley.

Of course, a few cowardly survivors, or ones tasked with guarding Fissure-Fist's property, were still hiding in some corners, so shouts and battle cries occasionally rang out here and there, followed by the pained grunts of ogres, or their pleas for mercy in broken Common.

All of the Fissure-Fist warlocks had died in the ritual — these were the ones most interested in the warlocks' Gul'dan magic, so not a single one escaped. Fissure-Fist's advisor, Org'ril, was among them.

The Fissure-Fist chieftain himself had died horribly. When the Soul Well ritual first began, he and a few elite ogre brutes had tried to charge at Jace, and were among the first to be laid low by the Soul Well's power.

Zakh'ur had become a pile of blackened, dried remains, identifiable only by the heavily shadow-corrupted patchwork armor he wore and his rather distinctive orc chieftain's helmet.

Mathias Shaw walked up. He glanced at the two warhorses dragging ogre corpses not far away, then pointed his short sword south beyond the valley. "We have agents who saw about seven or eight ogres fleeing south. I believe those ogres probably don't have the guts to return to Stormwind's borders, but I still need to report this to His Majesty."

Jace was sitting on a rock near the cave, recording his impressions and techniques from the spell he'd just cast. He looked up and followed Mathias's pointing direction. "Looks like there are ogre tribes in Stranglethorn Vale too. They're probably heading to join up with their kind."

"Let's hope so. Even the cunning Bleeding Hollow orcs couldn't survive in Stranglethorn, let alone these ogres." Mathias agreed casually. He paused for a moment, glancing at Jace's notes from the corner of his eye. "You know what I have to tell the king in my report... The ogre remnants won't be the focus. I have to tell His Majesty everything that happened here. I mean, our meeting with the ogres, and more importantly, your ritual and what I witnessed of its power."

He looked up at the slightly pale sky and continued, "At least the spell didn't connect this place to Duskwood's dark energy, so the environmental damage aspect can probably be simplified."

"I know, Shaw," Jace said.

Mathias sheathed his sword, his expression stiff. He tried not to look at Morophil, who was staring at him from not far away. "You're His Majesty's good friend — and I don't mean the kind of friend a king has with an advisor, but a real friend. And he has certain expectations about the dark magic you can summon. I think he'll understand. But I think you and I both want His Majesty to know about every single thing that happens in the kingdom — from the Blackwells' son getting married, to the terrifying magic that devoured the souls of a hundred and twenty-one invaders in the blink of an eye and destroyed an entire ogre tribe."

He handed over a coin purse. "Twenty-eight gold, seven silver, exchanged at the current Lordaeron silver-to-gold rate. Of course, Chieftain Zakh'ur and his two loyal followers, plus Advisor Org'ril and his warlock cronies, are all priced separately. We definitely don't discount on those."

Jace took the purse and rubbed a coin bearing King Trollbane's portrait. "Thank you, Shaw. I'll report to King Varian when I get back to Stormwind too. Though... this isn't technically within the kingdom's borders anymore, is it?"

"The kingdom's borders aren't a ditch you can flip back and forth, but I take your point." Mathias watched the fluttering lion flag. "I won't exaggerate or sensationalize things in my report. Considering this place is far from Raven Hill and any other settlement of the realm, I'll choose my words carefully and avoid descriptions that might work against you."

He thought for another long moment, then said, "Actually, I have another question, Sesso. Was this experiment preparation for Blackrock Spire? Do you plan to use this terrifying ritual against the Blackrock orcs on the Spire?"

Jace replied, "There are plenty of warlocks loyal to Blackhand on Blackrock Spire, and black dragons too. Those dragons and their servants are powerful shadow manipulators. It'd be hard to find the opportunity and space to conduct the ritual there. More importantly, I'm not familiar enough with the ritual itself yet. I don't know if I'll have fully mastered it by then."

"I don't want to sound like an orc sympathizer, especially as the head of SI:7," Mathias said. "But my personal advice is: don't cast this spell when the Alliance army attacks Blackrock Spire."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"Just now, even at the edge of that vortex, I could feel the terrible power at its dark center. Let alone those ogres — I felt like my own soul was being dragged in." Mathias explained. "My young agents witnessed something truly monumental today. I'm starting to worry this might leave them with psychological scars they'll need the Church to sort out."

"We only avoided being dragged in by the magic — and suffering the same fate as those ogres — thanks to the guidance of our partners at the Dagger Society. One of my agents who was a bit slow to retreat was wounded by shadow corrosion and only avoided serious consequences thanks to one of Ms. Delin's potions. And by your own account, this was just a rehearsal, a test."

"When you get to Blackrock Spire, if you plan to cast a larger, full-scale version of this ritual, think about how many Alliance soldiers would be affected. The allied forces include not just Expedition members or the men serving under King Varian, but also people from Arathi Highlands, from your homeland. They'll all see this, experience this. Many recruits these days haven't even seen a death knight on the battlefield, let alone witnessed your magic."

"But what if this magic could overcome obstacles that would otherwise be insurmountable, reducing unnecessary casualties?" Jace asked.

Mathias lowered his voice. "Then let them die honorably, die gloriously, die while charging forward. But don't let them die miserably in your magic, Sesso."

"We have the Sons of Lothar — including Khadgar and the Turalyons, the most powerful warriors and mages in the whole Alliance. Plus you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner, the Wildhammer gryphon riders, and maybe even the Dark Iron dwarves helping. Even if the Emperor of Shadowforge City doesn't join the Alliance as you and Magni hoped, at least they won't stab us in the back during the siege of Blackrock Spire. They're worse off now than when Lothar first attacked Blackrock Mountain. They'll be happy to see the black dragons destroyed. The Alliance is determined to completely uproot the last orc stronghold on this continent."

"So the biggest question mark in this campaign is whether we can kill Victor Nefarian. Focus your energy on hunting those black flying beasts. Leave the Horde scum to the army commanders and the Kirin Tor mages. That cunning female dragon slipped away — we can't let him do the same and rejoin her."

Jace knew Mathias wasn't just looking out for him. King Varian's close cooperation with dark magic was already common knowledge throughout the Alliance of Lordaeron.

It was only because of Stormwind's difficult circumstances over the past decade-plus, and the positive role that warlocks and dark magic had played in rooting out black dragon agents in the various kingdoms' courts and bringing the Expedition back through the Dark Portal — plus Khadgar's staunch defense within the Kirin Tor, and the Archbishop and the Church's endorsement — that public opinion hadn't spiraled out of control.

If the Soul Well caused a serious disaster at Blackrock Spire, rumors would spread like wildfire, and the northern kings would certainly seize the opportunity to stoke public sentiment.

It wouldn't just affect Jace and the Dagger Society — even Varian's rule might come under question.

Jace wasn't indifferent to what had happened in the valley not long ago. The sight of those ogres having their souls painfully drained, their eyes scorched black by soul-fire as they fell, was still vivid in his mind. And the unease he'd felt when the Soul Well's power approached the point of collapse still churned inside him.

The reason he'd immediately started writing down his experience from the spell was partly to capture the first-hand impressions and techniques, but also to calm the emotions that this shadow magic had stirred up.

For a cataclysmic ritual like this, caution was warranted. Even the pure arcane lightning storm that had destroyed the Gurubashi army on Stormwind's walls decades ago had left psychological scars on some older Stormwind residents that persisted to this day.

"I understand, Mathias," Jace said.

"I'm glad you'll seriously consider what I've said, Jace." Mathias's tense expression finally relaxed. He rubbed his hands vigorously. "In any case, I'm honored to have witnessed the destruction of those ogre bastards. The way they died in agony was a pleasure to behold — especially knowing it was by your hand, Jace Sesso."
