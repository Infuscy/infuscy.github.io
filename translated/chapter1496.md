## Chapter 1496: Flying Into the Snowy Mountains

The aerial view of the Stormwind Kingdom all the way to Khaz Modan should have been quite pleasant. But the dream's illusions twisted the visible image, reducing everything to blurred patches of color — white sky, green-gray mountains, and blue sea.

Jace pressed himself flat against Sora's neck, clinging tight. The dream energy that the green dragon channeled to protect her passengers only ended up churning together with the cold outside air, whipping Jace's messy hair wildly and making his tightly bound bundle shudder, as if struggling with all its might for complete freedom.

Even with red dragon blood flowing in his veins and shadow energy supporting him, Jace's stamina and strength far beyond an ordinary person's could barely hold on. Even swallowing was difficult.

Still, Jace wasn't completely unprepared. Compared to Sabellian's mad dash from Auchindoun to the Throne of Kil'jaeden — thousands of miles — this was still manageable.

The Blackwing seemed naturally gifted at high-speed flight. They'd been traveling from outside Stormwind for a while now, and the black silhouette behind was still faintly visible.

It seemed Needleflame hadn't been completely bluffing when she challenged Sora. Staying on the tail of a green dragon pushing dream energy meant she was already flying faster than most whelps.

How was she doing it? Jace remembered the little thing struggling, completely wrapped in spiderweb, stuck to a tree. She'd flown away like a clumsy moth. Granted, she'd been injured and half-starved back then, but the difference between that state and now was enormous.

At the same time, Jace realized it had already been over half a year since they'd met Needleflame. Back then she was about three years old. Three years from hatching to hatchling to whelp, and another half year... was this the growth rate of Deathwing's bloodline?

No wonder Alexstrasza thought she might one day become the Black Dragonqueen. Needleflame might grow into a dragon as powerful as Onyxia.

Even so... Jace could still feel her distance from Sora growing. Once the black dragon's stamina started flagging, she might fall out of sight entirely.

After all, Sora might not be directly descended from an Aspect, but she was a favored whelp blessed by Queen Ysera herself, a potential Emerald Guardian. There was no way a little thing like that could match her, no matter whose offspring she was.

And Sora's competitive drive had been ignited. From the moment she'd said "hold on tight" at the start, she'd been flying in determined silence.

She wasn't about to go easy on the black dragon. Respecting your opponent meant going all out. If she deliberately held back, Needleflame's personality meant she'd be angrier than if she'd been left far behind.

Morophil was right — these two whelps were flying absurdly fast. This wasn't a journey; it was a race.

Northshire Forest blurred past below. The spire of Northshire Abbey flashed once like a sudden glint of reflected light, then vanished.

The mountains of Khaz Modan rushed toward them — peaks, ridges, slopes, pine forests — all churned together like a boiling broth under the kneading of dream energy. Only the distant blue sky and the high sun reminded Jace that he was still flying through the real world.

Then white swallowed everything without warning.

After crossing a high mountain, everything in sight was covered in a layer of white. Jace knew they'd reached Dun Morogh.

The screech of gryphons sounded in the distance, but even those cries were quickly left behind. Sora had no intention of acknowledging any Ironforge patrol. To her, defending the Emerald Guardians' dignity was probably more important than saving the world right now.

Jace made a mental note to write to Ironforge as soon as they reached Dalaran to explain the situation — he didn't want the dwarves thinking that before the assault on Blackrock Spire had even started, the black dragons of Blackrock had already snuck into Coldridge Valley.

The green dragon cut through the snowstorm, looping wide around Kharanos, approaching Ironforge from high altitude. Ahead was their agreed finish line — the abandoned watchtower west of Ironforge, perched on the mountainside overlooking Gnomeregan.

As Sora neared the tower, she circled a few times to make sure no dwarven patrols were nearby before landing steadily.

After Jace and Morophil jumped off her back, she spread her wings and craned her neck, gazing south for a long time, as if proudly proclaiming her victory to her two spectators.

"Don't think I always fall asleep when I fly, Jace." The green dragon lowered her head to where Jace had been sitting. "Though I do really want a good nap right now."

Jace checked his luggage, especially the gift for Amy. "You should sleep more when you fly. If you keep flying this fast, none of us can handle it."

His words sounded like a complaint, but to the green dragon, they were a compliment — she happily curved her neck.

Sora's head, under Ysera's blessing, had already started to shed its serpentine appearance. Her neck wasn't as slender as before, now possessing some of the muscular beauty of an adult dragon.

But the features of a female green dragon were also becoming more pronounced — her warm, full scales, her longer, finer eyes, her sharper snout — all signs of her gradual maturation from another angle.

As a young human whose values might differ from a dragon's, Jace felt that having Sora raise Needleflame alongside her was much better than leaving her to the care of dragons and Aspects who'd lived for millennia.

Those ancient dragons could teach Needleflame incredible power and accelerate her physical growth. But the mark that the black dragonflight's ancestors had left on those elder dragons was too deep. It would inevitably press down on Needleflame in their daily interactions.

Sora's hatred of black dragons mostly came from her elders' descriptions. Her perspective shifted more quickly when faced with an unusual black dragon like Needleflame. And as a comparatively young whelp, she could not only play a maternal role but also be a friend to Needleflame.

After all, Queen Alexstrasza would never have the chance to race Needleflame to see who was faster.

But why hadn't Needleflame arrived yet?

Sora had been resting for quite a while now. Needleflame was definitely slower, but not by hundreds of miles, surely.

"Do you think she got caught by gryphon riders?" Jace asked.

"Only if she decided to fight the dwarven riders," Sora said, also looking at the distant sky.

"Wouldn't that be even more trouble than getting caught? I hope nobody ends up dead. Should we check Ironforge and Kharanos?" Jace stood up and pulled the imp out of his bag. "You can sense black dragons approaching, Sazeno. Do you feel Needleflame nearby?"

"I can sense black dragons because every one of them is a mass of powerful shadow, master," the imp said pitifully. "That little one doesn't have much shadow..."

Sora was about to take off when the sound of breaking branches came from below the slope, accompanied by shouts of "Woo-hoo!"

A dark brownish shape emerged from the trees. It flapped its broad wings, sometimes lifting off, sometimes landing, kicking up clouds of snow, shouting excitedly.

"Jace Sesso! Sora! Is this... snow?! Why did you never tell me that Azeroth had places like this? This is REAL snow!"

Right. In Draenor, the snowy mountains had been the most transformed by Ner'zhul's apocalyptic ritual. After Frostfire Ridge collided with Gorgrond, the endless white snow was never seen again.

Back then, Needleflame had been hiding in the black dragon nest in Gorgrond, never seeing the light of day. She'd never had the chance to see snow.

By the time she came to Azeroth, it was summer, just barely turning to autumn. Even in winter, the Stormwind Kingdom almost never saw snow. Even when they'd gone to Grim Batol, passing through Loch Modan, they'd only glimpsed snowy peaks from afar — never plunged directly into Dun Morogh's great snowy bowl like this.

For Needleflame, this was genuinely the first time she'd ever seen snow in her life.

Jace watched the black dragon roll around in the deep snow like an overturned bat that couldn't right itself, while snowflakes drifted down from the endless sky. For a moment, he didn't know how to respond.

There was so much beauty in this world that was ordinary to him. How could he explain it to an "outsider"?

"You lost, black dragon!" Sora roared, the whelp's cry shaking another layer of snow off the nearby trees.

"Woo! Someday I'm going to live in a place with snow! Snow!"

Needleflame seemed not to hear at all, just kept rolling around, faster and faster, even knocking over a small tree.

The little one... just didn't want to admit defeat.
