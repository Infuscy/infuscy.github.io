## Chapter 1497: The Perfect Gift

At the bridge leading into Dalaran, Jace booked a room at an inn and left the two demons — Morophil and Sazeno — to wait there.

The city's defensive barrier, once sustained by the Eye of Dalaran, had been weakened for several years after Deathwing stole it. Only Archmage Antonidas and the Kirin Tor council's archmages could maintain it. But now Jace could clearly sense that the barrier was far stronger than it had been a few years ago — he could even glimpse arcane motes with the naked eye in broad daylight.

Looking up at the silver spires against the sky, the faint shimmer was even more visible. It was enough to confirm that the Eye of Dalaran had returned from Stormwind to its rightful place. Dalaran was powerful again.

Given that Jace had already stirred up suspicion by revealing too many of his dark secrets when asking the Kirin Tor to help rescue the Expedition, and Khadgar had barely smoothed things over, Jace didn't feel like forcibly dragging two demons — especially Morophil — into the city, challenging Dalaran's barrier and making trouble for Khadgar at this sensitive juncture.

From outside the inn, looking north, he could see straight to the bridge entering Dalaran. Even though he'd had two dragons as company this time, this was still his first time walking through the front gate into the city. For Jace, the experience was novel.

Walking on the bridge, watching the sparkling river on both sides under the sunlight, the clean red brickwork, the glossy ceramic-smooth walls, the jade-white towers, the mysterious purple tiles... It was no wonder Dalaran was the place every mage in the kingdoms most wanted to live. No matter how many times Jace came here, it lifted his spirits.

Sora, combing her golden hair by the riverside, gazed at the mist-shrouded Silverpine Forest on the distant shore. Though her expression was blank, her inner ease was obvious.

Needleflame was different. From the moment they'd landed in the southern forest, she'd been in a bad mood. No — she'd had that look since leaving Dun Morogh, it just wasn't visible in her black whelp form.

"Still upset about losing the race, even here in Dalaran?" Jace asked.

"No. I accept defeat gracefully!" the little black dragon huffed.

"When we leave Dalaran, we can go to that mountain and find some sheep to eat." Jace pointed to the snowy peaks in the east. "See? Over there — Alterac."

"I'm not interested in food!" the little black dragon said coldly.

Sora glanced at her, then looked away, but said nothing.

"Then what ARE you interested in eating?" Jace looked toward the bridge ahead. "Pastries? Ice cream? Or maybe we grab a drink?"

"Dalaran has all these mages — isn't there some arcane arena, a mage free-for-all, something to watch?" Needleflame gripped the bridge railing. "You said you were taking me somewhere fun."

In the in-game version of Dalaran, the sewers did have exactly what Needleflame was looking for — a rogue's den, a black market, an underground arena, everything.

But Jace had no idea whether the Dalaran of this era, before it floated into the sky, had those things. And given the shady nature of the sewers, he couldn't exactly ask for directions in the middle of the street.

"If I were the king of Dalaran," Needleflame said, clenching her fists and knocking them together, "I'd put all the monsters from the Violet Hold in one room and make them fight to the death! Now THAT would be something to watch!"

"Are you SURE there's no shadow corruption in your brain?" Sora looked down at her. "The Violet Hold contains threats captured by the Council of Tirisfal and the Kirin Tor over hundreds of thousands of years. Some of them are the most dangerous things in the world. Making them fight would be insane!"

"So you'd have to charge a lot for tickets," Jace said.

Sora's eyes went wide as she whipped around to stare at Jace. Needleflame burst into laughter, doubling over. "Yeah, yeah! We should mention it to Khadgar! That would be SO much fun!"

"Yeah, yeah, I want to see the monsters in the Violet Hold, Jace Sesso... Come on, didn't the king of Dalaran give you a ring for access to anywhere in Dalaran? Didn't Sora say the Violet Hold held the most terrifying monsters in the world? Are the ones you mentioned that want to harm me in there?"

"Uh." Jace mumbled. "Probably. Not. And that ring was for the library, not the prison, Needleflame."

"I want to see it," Needleflame said.

"No way," Jace said. "I'm not going to the Violet Hold. No matter what you say, it's not happening."

Needleflame snorted. "Coward."

"What does that have to do with being a coward?" Jace glanced guiltily in the direction of the Violet Hold.

"How could that prison just let people in to 'sightsee'?" Sora said.

"Exactly," Jace quickly agreed.

Needleflame walked ahead, looking bored. Jace sighed helplessly. "Take her around Dalaran, keep her out of trouble. I need to go find Amy first."

"Fine, I'll try, Jace." Sora sighed reluctantly as Needleflame had already run across the bridge and into the streets of Dalaran.

Jace arrived at the Dalaran School of Magic. Amy wasn't living in her old place anymore. But after assisting in the Expedition rescue effort from Jace Sesso's tower, Amy Marin had gained some renown in the Kirin Tor. Many of the academy's teachers knew where she was staying.

Following their directions, Jace found a two-story building next to an ivory-colored mage tower. He spotted Amy in the garden below, carefully observing and recording something in a flower bed.

"Amy! So this is where you moved!"

"Jace?" Amy set her tools aside, wiping the sweat from her forehead. "I didn't think you'd actually come to Dalaran to see me. I don't know what to say. Come in, Jace. I still have some meat floss pastries from a banquet I went to a few days ago. I thought you might like them."

Jace nodded and followed the mage inside, watching her bustle around making tea and setting out the paper-wrapped pastries.

"I'm really glad you came to Dalaran," Amy said, sitting down and catching her breath. "Not just because you came to see me, but also because..."

She paused, then continued, "Does this mean things between you and the Kirin Tor aren't as... strained as I thought?"

"I came to Dalaran this time regardless of what the Kirin Tor thinks of me. The main reason is..." Jace set his bundle on the table. "Take a look. The gift I promised."

Amy took the string. She looked at Jace with something like reluctance in her eyes, but finally smiled. "You haven't even asked if I completed your 'task,' and you're already giving me the reward?"

"The gift I'm giving you has nothing to do with that 'task.'" Jace gave the bundle a meaningful glance. "Open it."

Amy hummed softly, slowly opening the bundle and the box inside. A pair of blue sheepskin soft boots emerged.

At first, she put on a surprised expression. But Jace could tell she was happier about the fact that he'd given her a gift than about the gift itself. The surprise didn't last long, though — her expression seemed to freeze on her face, and her inner mood quickly shifted.

The mage frowned, tilted her head uncertainly, looked at the side of the boots, then picked one up to examine the sole. Jace watched her turn the boots over in her hands, confused — they looked fine to him.

"No, no, no..." Amy muttered, almost trembling.

"You don't like them?" Jace asked.

"No." She looked up, asking cautiously, "These boots are beautiful. I really like them... but do you know what they are?"

"Blue sheepskin soft boots." Jace crossed his arms. Seeing Amy still staring at him, he added, "I heard they're favored by frost mages, so I got them for you."

"True, mages would love these boots. But you really don't know what they are." Amy muttered a bit nervously, then said, "If someone else gave me these shoes, I wouldn't think much of it... but you, Jace, you..."

"Did you go to Karazhan?"

"Been to Karazhan?" Jace said. "These boots..."

"You don't know? These are Guardian Larniriel's boots," Amy said. "She was the sixth Guardian before Medivh. Held the position about fifteen hundred years ago. Aegwynn inherited them from her teacher Scavelle and kept them hidden. They haven't been seen in the world since. So many researchers of ancient artifacts in Dalaran believe these boots are still in Karazhan. The Violet Eye has been looking for them too."

"Is it that easy to recognize?" Jace asked.

Amy turned and pulled a box from a cabinet behind her, setting it on the table. Inside was a nearly identical pair of leather boots.

Jace was taken aback. "What's going on? Which ones are real?"

"At least mine are fake. These are replicas!" Amy glanced at the door, then practically hissed, "But yours... I don't know. So, these came from Karazhan?"

Jace licked his lips, thinking for a moment. "Where they came from, I can't say. But when Khadgar first saw these boots, the look on his face was like, like, like..."

"Like a greedy murloc named Gottom who'd found a magic bracelet," Amy murmured, staring at the boots.

"Exactly!" Jace wagged his finger frantically.

"Well, that confirms it." Amy nearly collapsed back into her chair. "Even if Archmage Khadgar isn't a footwear expert, he could tell how powerful the enchantments on these boots are."

"So... do you still want to accept them?" Jace asked.

Amy slammed the lid shut, hugged the box, and said solemnly, "I... will guard them with my life. Thank you, Jace."
