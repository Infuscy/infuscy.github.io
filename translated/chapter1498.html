Chapter 1498: About the "Task"

Jace sat at the table drinking tea for a while. Before long, Amy emerged from her room.

She'd changed into her Kirin Tor robes — white with gold and purple trim. She took a few light steps, the boots barely making a sound on the wooden floor.

The mage turned slightly, lifting the edge of her robe to reveal one blue sheepskin boot. "What do you think, Jace?"

Jace felt like the boots had changed somehow on her feet. He'd been worried that the unknown Guardian Larniriel might have had a very different height from Amy and the boots wouldn't fit. But they seemed to have adapted themselves, snugly conforming to the mage's feet.

They even made her legs, which had been worryingly thin, look a bit healthier, as if the caster might no longer be blown away by an incoming blizzard.

He gave her a final once-over. "They look great, Amy. Really suit you. I can already picture you channeling frost magic in them."

"I'm sure they still looked better on Guardian Larniriel in the paintings." Amy muttered, a hint of disappointment in her voice.

"What? Paintings?" Jace rested his chin on his hand. "Have you been to the Wizard Sanctum to see my portrait? It looks nothing like me. Larniriel was a former Guardian — the highest-ranked mage in Dalaran in her day. Imagine how hard the painter worked to beautify her."

"Don't try to comfort me. That portrait doesn't look anything like you at all," the mage said.

Jace smirked proudly. Amy looked up at him from under her lashes. "Don't get the wrong idea. I mean, the portrait is nowhere near as sinister as you."

"Huh?" Jace froze awkwardly.

The mage gave a mischievous smile and changed the subject. "Weren't you asking me to find out about the Kul Tiran princess?"

"Ah, right." Jace checked the door to make sure it was shut tight. "Did you find anything?"

"Not that I found anything, exactly." Amy picked up her teacup. "The other day, we were talking about arcane addiction and the abuse of magic. She asked me about the differences between shadow and arcane in terms of their dangers."

"Isn't that a dangerous topic?" Jace asked.

"Extremely dangerous. Especially for a young student who hasn't formally joined the Kirin Tor yet." Amy nodded. "And considering she'd just been caught up in our opening of the Draenor portal... But maybe it was precisely because I was the mastermind of the Draenor coordinates case that she dared to ask me such questions. And because I know you, Jace."

"What does that have to do with me?" Jace asked.

"She thinks you're a hero. Even though several Kirin Tor council members and many archmages consider you a dangerous element, a ticking time bomb — that doesn't change her mind." Amy took a sip of tea and let out a slow breath. "She subscribes to Archmage Antonidas's theory about the orcs — that once the demonic power fades, orcs can become a race capable of communication and even cooperation. She thinks the same applies to you — that you can't be defined by the power you use."

"But that night, Prince Arthas seemed to mention you. He thought she was getting too close to you."

"Too close?" Jace frowned. "I've barely even met her. I think the longest conversation I've ever had with her was something like, 'Hello, Lady Jaina Proudmoore, nice to meet you.'"

"I'm sure there was at least one longer sentence than that," Amy shook her head. "But not many. Still, every noble and king in the Alliance knows she played a role in the Expedition's return. Look at the other co-conspirators: me; Lady Vereesa Windrunner, obviously; Lord Grede, your close friend; a mysterious green dragon lady who's close to you; and then Jaina, who almost no one has seen with her two demon friends. We can't really blame the prince for thinking that way."

And indeed, as a de facto princess of a nation, Dalaran's rising star, and the future queen of Lordaeron — being associated with a dark wizard who'd made headlines, even superficially, was hardly a good thing.

"Anyway." Amy continued. "Jaina naturally disagreed with the prejudice against you and argued her case. But Arthas thought she was just defending herself for finding the Draenor coordinates and opening the demon portal — which set her off completely. In the end... well, you saw what happened at the banquet."

"I should probably formally apologize to her sometime," Jace thought for a moment. "But if I went to see her, it would only cause her more trouble, wouldn't it?"

"A hundred percent," the mage said.

"But from the questions she asked you, her opinion of me seems a bit shaken?" Jace asked.

"Well, it was Arthas Menethil," Amy said quietly. "Can you expect a teenage noble girl who grew up on legends and knight stories to stay completely rational when faced with a boy so handsome he practically glows? Especially when that prince had just become a member of the Silver Hand — the embodiment of righteousness! Not to mention they'd grown up together since they were about ten... Honestly, I think the fact that she walked away furious is already pretty remarkable."

"But like you said, Jace, her understanding of you is also limited to legends and stories. She doesn't know you like I do. If too many conflicting opinions enter her world — especially ones that are very persuasive to her — she'll naturally start to doubt. But that doesn't necessarily mean she'll become hostile toward you. I can sense that her doubt includes a certain... frustration that the whole world seems ready to punish you for crimes you haven't committed. Actually, I think if she becomes one of Dalaran's leaders in the future, she might push for a re-examination of void magic and demonic research. After all, it used to be an important subject for the Council of Tirisfal."

"I don't hold it against her. It's normal for her to be suspicious of shadow magic," Jace said. "I'm more worried about what the Lordaeron prince thinks of me. He's going to be the leader of the Alliance one day. King Terenas at least managed to hold back the nobles when they tried to burn me at the stake verbally in court. Now his son can't even keep his opinion to himself while canoodling with his sweetheart."

Amy swirled her teacup. "Then stay in Stormwind."

Amy was right. As long as the Kirin Tor and the red dragons could contain the Lich King's advance, prevent the Cult of the Damned from taking root in Lordaeron, and stop Frostmourne's tragedy from repeating, then even if Arthas hated his guts forever, what could he do about Varian Wrynn?

"Good point." Jace downed his tea in one gulp. "Right now, the land of Lordamere feels hotter to me than the gray stone floor of the Cathedral of Light."

The mage chuckled and refilled Jace's cup. "Recently, King Terenas hasn't been well. Prince Arthas has been taking care of him while handling some of the administrative workload. I think at least for now, he won't have the energy to keep picking fights with you."

"What's wrong with the king?" Jace asked.

"Travel fatigue, supposedly." Amy shook her head. "I don't know the details."

"I thought he took a portal to Stormwind," Jace said.

"You've been through a portal too," Amy said, walking back toward her room. "Given His Majesty's health, going through a portal would probably shake his whole body apart. He wouldn't even have the chance to get sick."

Jace nodded with a smile. Amy changed her shoes in the room and packed the blue leather boots away. "I know these boots. Boots that several Guardians have collected — they probably don't need my care or maintenance to stay in perfect condition. They've lasted fifteen hundred years... The task you gave me was much easier than I expected. I didn't think she'd bring it up on her own. And the gift you gave me is too precious. You should think of something else I can help with, or I'll feel too guilty."

Jace blew on his hot tea. "Actually, there is something I want to ask you."

"What? Go ahead." Amy leaned against the doorway.

"The Violet Hold," Jace said, looking up. "Is it possible for an outsider to... go in and take a look?"
