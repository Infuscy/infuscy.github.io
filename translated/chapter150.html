Chapter 150: The Azure Encounter

Even in Dalaran's outer districts, the cityscape was as meticulously kept as a European noble's estate. You could feel that the planning of Stormwind's Mage Quarter had taken this place as a reference.

Except the Mage Quarter had deliberately set out to avoid that distinctive fusion of elven and human aesthetics in its colors and architecture, with most of its buildings carrying straight on in Stormwind's conservative style.

Mixing that with concepts borrowed from Dalaran's layout had instead produced something that didn't quite belong to either.

Here, the exotic flavor born of racial integration was seamless — as if it had already given birth to a wholly unique style of its own. Nowhere in Quel'Thalas or Tirisfal could you find a place like this.

Walking down the street, Jace couldn't stop craning his neck to gawk at everything like some country boy seeing the big city for the first time. Grede, on a crutch, was still outpacing him.

The two of them walked into a tavern called the Azure Encounter. The main hall was large, decorated in a style much like the room Jace was staying in, just with more landscape paintings hung up.

There was even an oil painting of Elwynn Forest seen from a distance — except, judging by its vantage point, the painter had been positioned impossibly high above the forest, making it very hard to imagine exactly where the artist had sat down to put brush to canvas.

Still, even though there was a good crowd of patrons, Dalaran's taverns were quieter than those in Stormwind or Lordaeron. The occasional burst of laughter stood out like a sore thumb.

So when a Wildhammer dwarf came barreling in, it was practically like a miniature bull charging into a china shop.

Grede led Jace to the bar counter, pointed knowingly at the menu, then turned back and asked, "So when are you planning to head to the capital to see your parents, Jace?"

"Let's wait and see how today goes. I want to observe how my body's holding up," Jace answered. "Master told me the exchange is scheduled for the eleventh. If I'm sure I'm fine and want to leave, I'd better set out early tomorrow morning and be back before the exchange. You're not coming with me — stay in Dalaran and rest up."

Saying that, Jace suddenly thought of the Eye of Dalaran, and a shadow passed through his mind.

"But tell me — can you even travel right now?"

"What do you think?" Grede gave him a sidelong look. "I'm not tagging along on your wanderings. At least let me rest a few days and let my injuries stabilize first. Saying two weeks to heal still means taking proper care of it. I'm not going to Aerie Peak in this condition."

"Then I'll go myself," Jace said. "Be careful here in Dalaran too. Don't go wandering over to the Violet Citadel for no reason. Those mages over there see a Wildhammer dwarf who doesn't care for magic and they'll chase you right back out."

Best to stay as far from Dalaran's vault as possible, too.

"Relax, I'm not looking for trouble." Grede stroked his beard. "Come to think of it, I've been here two days and I've stayed in this area the whole time. Never even went to take a look at those pretty little buildings up north by the lake — the schools, the big fountain, and all that."

"So don't go there yet," Jace said immediately. "Wait until I'm back and the two of us will go check it out together. If you go first, it won't be any fun anymore."

Grede said, "Makes sense. With a mage like you along, one Wildhammer dwarf won't seem too out of place, eh? Ha!"

"I heard from Master Dushan that you only arrived in Dalaran yesterday?" Jace asked.

Grede answered, "Actually, the night before last, in the middle of the night. I came with a Lordaeron cavalry unit through the paths of Alterac Valley. I told them I wanted to get to Dalaran fast to see you, and they just strapped me straight onto a horse's back. I mean, they were being considerate of my injury, but it sounds a bit odd when you put it that way, doesn't it? Though with my condition, I really couldn't sit steady on those highland warhorses built like oxen… Anyway, they saved me a good deal of money and time, and didn't make my leg any worse."

"Didn't you have those gryphon riders give you a lift here?" Jace asked.

He'd just thrown the question out there, but the words were barely out of his mouth before he realized this probably wasn't a topic he should've brought up right now.

Grede's previously easy expression dimmed a little.

Jace knew what the dwarf was thinking of. He was thinking of the same thing.

"Gryphons… forget it," Grede said.

"Hey, I noticed you've been bouncing around this whole time. You sure you're not feeling anything else off? Yesterday you were still unconscious, muttering something the whole while…"

"Muttering what?" Jace asked.

"I couldn't make it out," Grede shook his head. "Even Master Dushan couldn't make it out, and he's studied so many languages. Maybe just delirium from the dragon attack — shadow-induced nonsense."

Jace thought to himself, please don't tell me I was talking in Chinese in my sleep. Though speaking Chinese was still better than babbling in demon-tongue or something.

Just then the bartender brought over two glasses. Jace rarely got to drink out of glass. The liquid inside was a pale pink — it looked for all the world like food coloring. A little suspicious.

"What the hell is this stuff?"

He turned to look. Grede was holding a glass of an even stranger black drink that was fizzing and bubbling away — not the carbonated kind of fizzing, but a lava-like gurgling, and yet it was cold!

"And what the hell is yours…"

"Who cares what it is!" Grede laughed. "Tastes good, that's what matters. Can't remember the elven name anyway. Let's find a seat!"

He led the way, then suddenly stopped mid-stride. Jace nearly plowed right into his back with the glass still in his hand.

Grede turned his head, stepped back, and looked down at someone sitting at the table beside them.

It was an elf man. Golden hair spilled down to his arms, half-obscuring his face. The pointed ears poking through the hair and the long eyebrows gave his race away at a glance.

He was wearing a light-blue silk long-coat of the style commonly seen on elves in the street, leaning lazily in his chair, reading a book with a dark-red cover decorated with green vine patterns. On the table in front of him sat nothing but a cup of plain tea.

Grede plopped himself down right across from him and slammed his glass onto the table with a crack.

Jace stood to one side, his heart flinching. The glass looked thin as anything but was sturdily made — no idea how much it'd cost to break one.

"Well, bugger me, what a coincidence, eh?"

The elf heard the voice and closed his book with one hand in a lazy snap, turning his head to the side to look at Grede.

The moment Jace saw his face clear, he thought, why does this bloke look so familiar…

But he didn't know a single elf. Why would an elf's face feel familiar?

No — strictly speaking, he did "know" one elf.

The elf smiled and asked, "You two are still alive?"

The moment he heard that voice, Jace was certain. This was that guy from Duskwood!
