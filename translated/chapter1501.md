## Chapter 1501: Prisoner of the Magic Prison

This visit to the Violet Hold was partly to cheer Needleflame up, but partly because Jace wanted to see Kel'Thuzad.

He realized he'd been avoiding meeting that man again. But now, with Kil'jaeden's cold chess piece stirring in the north, everything that had happened in Karazhan somehow connected to Kil'jaeden, and Arthas's prejudice against him, Jace felt it was time to see Kel'Thuzad.

In another timeline, Kel'Thuzad's interest in necromancy had caught the Lich King's attention. After he was expelled from Dalaran for his research being exposed, Kel'Thuzad followed the Lich King's voice to Northrend, becoming the herald of the plague. In the end, it was he who guided Arthas down the path of no return, eventually becoming his most powerful lieutenant.

Jace wanted to see whether this Kel'Thuzad had already made contact with the Lich King. Either answer — yes or no — would give him a reference point for judging what might happen next.

Kassan led Jace out of the demon holding area, past the Tal'ral'siv crystal hall into another passage, continuing down a spiral staircase.

Sealing runes were everywhere. More and more battle mages patrolled the corridors, carrying longswords and shields, their robes inscribed with arcane runes for countering magic. Their energy was so potent Jace suspected a standard Arcane Missiles spell wouldn't faze them in the slightest.

Kassan brought Jace to a door, signaled the nearby battle mage to open it, and after a command incantation, the door slowly swung open.

Inside was a fairly simple room, not much different from Amy's old dorm — proper desk, chairs, and a bed. For a prison, it was decent accommodations.

Kel'Thuzad sat in a chair facing the door. He looked much the same as he had during the orcish language meeting — just a bit thinner, his face still clean-shaven as if freshly groomed.

He wasn't wearing Kirin Tor robes. Instead, he had on a simple gray-purple shirt and dark trousers. After all, he'd been expelled from the Kirin Tor.

But there was no barrier between the open door and the seated prisoner — no magical ward, not even iron bars.

Sensing Jace's hesitation, Kassan said, "He's in another dimension and can't access the ley lines. But you can still communicate normally, Mr. Sesso."

Jace nodded and stepped inside. The door closed behind him, dimming the room slightly.

"Sit." Kel'Thuzad glanced at the other chair beside the table. His voice didn't sound like it was coming from another dimension at all — it was as if he were right there.

Jace didn't move. "You sound like you knew I'd be coming today."

"Many criminals enjoy revisiting the scene of the crime afterward," Kel'Thuzad said, leaning back in his chair. "It's a kind of showing off — admiring your own work. The victim is part of the work. The guards, the onlookers, the weeping families of the victims, the panicked, bewildered crowd — they're all part of the work. So I'd say you're actually a bit late."

Jace tried to read something from Kel'Thuzad's demeanor, but the Violet Hold gave him no chance.

As Archmage Kassan had said, Kel'Thuzad was isolated in another dimension. So while he couldn't use magic, Jace also couldn't sense his emotions at all — he couldn't rely on reading shadow energy to determine whether he was lying.

"I'm not here for that reason." Jace asked. "I've always had one question — why? Why me?"

"I thought you'd figured that out by now," Kel'Thuzad said. "From the moment you revealed the existence of the 'Three Artifacts' to all of Dalaran — no, from the moment you decided to use Life Drain to kill that whelp — you caught Deathwing's attention. It's like not needing to see a drop of water fall into a lake to know ripples will spread. He knew your future would be anything but ordinary."

"Did he know he'd die in those ripples?" Jace asked.

"I don't know what he thought, but his death doesn't surprise me." Kel'Thuzad said quietly. "A tyrant with unmatched power but consumed by rage and madness — his failure was inevitable."

"To be honest, I never cared whether he lived or died. My relationship with the black dragons was a transaction. I wasn't their servant, they weren't my masters. I found the Eye of Dalaran for the black dragons, satisfied the orcs' demands, and the black dragons provided what I wanted... I just didn't expect you to escape from him at Grim Batol, let alone that Krasus would make it back to Dalaran alive from that tower. You destroyed all my efforts."

When Deathwing stole the Eye of Dalaran, the Expedition had already crossed the Dark Portal. They hadn't yet defeated the Horde chieftain Ner'zhul, who was still waiting for the Eye of Dalaran, the Book of Medivh, and the Scepter of Sargeras — the three so-called "Artifacts" — to be brought to Draenor so he could open a portal to escape.

By the time Deathwing was heavily wounded by Khadgar's magic in Draenor, returned to Lordaeron to begin his next scheme, and sought out Jace to go to Grim Batol, only another two years had passed.

During that time, Kel'Thuzad had been cooperating with the black dragons, and even tried to escape to Draenor using a portal coordinate given by the orcs, to avoid his necromancy research being discovered by Dalaran. But the portal coordinate later fell into Dar'Khan's hands.

In that same period, Ner'zhul was killed by Kil'jaeden during his escape, frozen into a block of ice, and hurled to Northrend, where he began planning to consume the northern continent starting with the spider kingdom.

But after Grim Batol, Kel'Thuzad was contained. Krasus returned to Dalaran and exposed his betrayal. Before Kel'Thuzad could escape, he was detained in the Violet Hold. When the warlocks caught collaborating with the black dragons at the lighthouse exposed his methods of hiding materials in the Twisting Nether, his crimes were confirmed.

In such a short time, Kel'Thuzad probably hadn't had a chance to establish contact with the Lich King Ner'zhul. And would Ner'zhul bother with this prisoner trapped by Dalaran's wards?

Seeing Jace lost in thought, Kel'Thuzad smiled. "Look at that. You don't even know what you've done."

"Necromancy is a field that the Council of Tirisfal and the Kirin Tor have never taken seriously. It contains a world as mysterious and vast as the real world. In this field, we could achieve so many more possibilities. But fossils like Krasus — rigid, decayed old things — refuse to take a single step forward. The most tragic part is that even young people like you, Khadgar, and Rhonin are stubbornly clinging to the cages they've built for you."

"You must have heard of the Shadowlands since childhood. Believe me, it's not just a simple curse word. It's a gray border you can reach by crossing that veil... Have you never been curious about that world? Gul'dan's death knights were ugly and stupid. Even Teron Gorefiend was the same. But they prove that the door to that world isn't completely sealed."

"The warden can certainly hear everything between us, Kel'Thuzad," Jace said. "Even if they're still considering whether to exile you, these words are enough to keep you locked up here until you die."

"And death is not the end." Kel'Thuzad smiled. "You'll understand the meaning of those words. Goodbye, Jace Sesso."

The door had opened again. A guard stood at the entrance, signaling that Jace's time was up.

Jace looked around the room one more time, then stood and left.

Outside, Kassan was waiting at the end of the corridor.

Death is not the end.

Even without sensing emotions, Jace could see the confidence on Kel'Thuzad's face.

"Kel'Thuzad is no young man, Master Kassan." Jace walked up to him. "I was wondering — if an elderly mage prisoner were to pass away in prison, where would you bury him?"

"After investigating the cause, notify the family, and send him home." Kassan said. "If there's no special cause of death, no known hometown, and no family or friends to contact, he'd generally be sent to the Sepulcher cemetery. However, while Kel'Thuzad has been expelled from the Kirin Tor, he was once a member of the Council of Six and a student and close friend of Archmage Antonidas. I imagine the master of Dalaran would have other arrangements."

"Could I meet with Archmage Antonidas?" Jace asked.

"I am the warden of the Violet Hold." Kassan said. "If you wish to see Archmage Antonidas, you can go directly to the Violet Citadel, Mr. Sesso. You don't need my permission."

"I'll go see him, then." Jace nodded. "Also, will my two friends be kicked out when I leave the Violet Hold?"

"Of course not." Kassan said. "I'll assign a knowledgeable mage to continue guiding them through the demon and void creature exhibits."

"Thank you very much, Master Kassan." Jace nodded, then turned and hurried toward the passage exit.
