## Chapter 1502: Full Supervision?

In another timeline, Kel'Thuzad was the first lich to emerge under the Lich King's command. He'd answered the Lich King's call to Northrend, and after returning he'd led the Cult of the Damned in spreading the plague through Lordaeron. Arthas found and killed him, then cremated his body. Later, his urn of ashes was thrown into the Sunwell by the fallen death knight Arthas, destroying the Sunwell and resurrecting Kel'Thuzad as an incomparably powerful lich.

Jace also remembered another resurrected lich — Ras Frostwhisper — who had slit his own throat in front of the Lich King and been reborn as a new lich.

He couldn't recall many other stories of liches being resurrected. Of course, in this world, with the Kirin Tor and every other mage organization clamping down so tightly on necromantic magic, he'd never had a chance to read records about beings like liches anyway.

But he knew one thing for certain: a lich's creation required its bones, and it needed outside help.

Kel'Thuzad's confident demeanor made Jace suspect that outside the Violet Hold, someone was waiting for him to die inside — someone ready to help him complete an escape that only he could pull off.

Jace was still thinking about this when he walked into the Violet Citadel. A mage who looked like a servant hurried over. "Master Jace Sesso?"

"I'd like to see Archmage Antonidas," Jace said.

"Archmage Antonidas is on the top-floor terrace, master." The servant extended a hand. "May I take your cloak and—"

"No, thank you." Jace nodded to decline. But he immediately noticed the servant's expression shift, and a nearby battle mage's hand went to his sword — the atmosphere in the hall turned taut in an instant.

He glanced down quickly at his own outfit. Sure, he'd dressed a bit nicer than usual for the Grim Batol ceremony, but the red dragonscale cloak, the sword at his hip, the strapped-on staff — it all screamed "ready to throw down."

Considering his recently-thawed relationship with the Kirin Tor, it was only natural that the Violet Citadel's guards would be on edge seeing him like this.

He took a deep breath, handed his sword and cloak to the servant. "Sorry, I was in a hurry."

"Thank you, Master Sesso."

The servant nodded repeatedly, carefully carrying the cloak and sword off to the side. The tension in the room eased. Jace straightened his collar and headed for the stairs.

On the terrace, Antonidas was staring up at the sky. From here you could just glimpse the Council of Six's meeting chamber in the clouds — the place where the Kirin Tor's six leaders convened. Khadgar loathed that place, because "every time I go in there, a bunch of old men and old women rip me to shreds."

"Archmage Antonidas." Jace nodded in greeting.

Antonidas turned around. The old mage's face had grown even more deeply lined; he looked utterly exhausted. The high-altitude wind tugged at his long beard and white hair, and even raising a hand to smooth them seemed to cost him visible effort.

"Mr. Jace Sesso." Antonidas's paper-thin lips worked as he spoke. "Your arrival was very sudden. You didn't notify me, nor the Kirin Tor, nor anyone in Dalaran. Some of my colleagues found it quite alarming. How did you get to Dalaran?"

"My friend brought me," Jace said.

"The green dragon lady who lives in Stormwind." Antonidas nodded. "That explains it. I'd only just received word that you'd left Stormwind, and by then you were already inside the Violet Hold."

Jace wasn't surprised that the Kirin Tor had been monitoring his movements in Stormwind. But he was surprised Antonidas would just admit it.

"I came to see you today, Archmage Antonidas, about the Violet Hold matter too." Jace said. "I met with Kel'Thuzad. I imagine you already know about that."

"Yes." Antonidas said. "Krasus had someone inform me immediately."

"I don't think he's acting alone," Jace said.

"Of course he isn't alone," Antonidas said. "Many of his students and associates were involved in necromantic research. Some even accessed the necromantic knowledge obtained from the black dragons and Gul'dan. The Kirin Tor has conducted an investigation spanning the entire Alliance, with the goal of not missing a single possible collaborator."

"Is the investigation still ongoing?" Jace asked.

"Yes." Antonidas said. "I know you're worried that Kel'Thuzad's remaining followers might retaliate against you, Mr. Sesso. But I hope you can trust the Kirin Tor's firm stance on necromancy. I think you, as an independent researcher of void and demonic knowledge, have felt that stance sharply enough already."

"I certainly trust the Kirin Tor's stance and capabilities," Jace said. "But don't forget, Master — Kel'Thuzad was once a member of the Council of Six, and a specialist in investigating dark magic and evil. I think he knows better than you how the Kirin Tor would investigate him."

Antonidas fell silent. Jace said quietly, "Death is not the end. He wants me to remember those words."

The old mage thought for a moment. "You think that's some kind of threat? That he's mastered some necromancy that would let him resurrect after death?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Master Krasus mentioned that if Kel'Thuzad dies in prison... given his former position in the Kirin Tor, you'd be the one to handle his funeral arrangements."

"He's not wrong," Antonidas said. "But Kel'Thuzad was my student. He's much younger than me. I may not get the chance to handle that."

Jace could clearly feel that Antonidas was hiding something. Even though the old mage seemed to have some special method that made it very hard to read his emotions, Jace was far from an ordinary warlock. Even the faintest trace was enough for him to catch.

"This concerns me directly, Archmage Antonidas." Jace said bluntly. "Not just me — I have family and friends too. So I don't want there to be any possibility of Kel'Thuzad leaving the Violet Hold. By any means. And I don't want anything about this being kept from me."

Antonidas looked as if he'd been expecting Jace to say exactly that. He let out a weary breath, gripped the railing, and lifted his chin. "Fine. On the night those hidden scrolls of his were found, I spoke with him in the Violet Hold. That was my last conversation with him."

"He hoped that if he died in prison, his ashes would be taken to Southshore for burial. I agreed. But the Kirin Tor's standard method for dealing with necromantic researchers among us is cremation, purification by the Church's priests calling upon the Holy Light, and finally sealing the remains in a special urn for burial. Kel'Thuzad knew that very well."

"The Church will be involved too?" Jace asked.

"Yes," Antonidas said. "For a magical kingdom that's existed over two thousand years, this isn't the first time a necromancer has tried to escape prison this way, Mr. Sesso."

"I heard that a mage from the Order of Tirisfal once escaped, breaking through all the prison's magical restraints by controlling the arcane energy of the lift." Jace said. "So even the tightest measures can have loopholes, can't they?"

"That did happen." The master of Dalaran said. "That mage's name was Aegwynn."

Jace was briefly stunned. Antonidas continued, "How about this: if he truly dies in prison — by any means, whether he bypasses the magical bindings to kill himself, or dies by accident, or from illness — the Kirin Tor will notify you as soon as possible, so you can attend the funeral and be present for the entire process."

"Thank you, Master." Jace said. "That would put my mind at ease."

Antonidas smiled slightly. "Due to my health and other reasons, I've never had the chance to formally thank you on behalf of the Kirin Tor and Dalaran for bringing back Archmage Vargus and the many Kirin Tor members who followed the Sons of Lothar through the Dark Portal. I must apologize for that, Mr. Sesso."

"No need to apologize at all, Master." Jace said. "About Archmage Vargus... there's actually something else I'd like to ask his help with. Some demon soul fragments I brought back from Auchindoun."

Antonidas's smile faded. "Trading soul fragments within the kingdoms of Lordaeron and Dalaran is a serious violation of Kirin Tor regulations. Even demon soul fragments."

"For research, Master." Jace said, entirely serious. "Not commercial."

The old mage studied Jace for a long moment before saying, "Then I'll write a letter to Master Vargus."
