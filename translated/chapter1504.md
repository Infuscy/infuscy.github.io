## Chapter 1504: The Dream Journey

Moonlight spilled through the window. Jace's whole body ached.

The frenzy from a little while ago hadn't fully subsided. He rubbed his neck and looked to the side. Vereesa had just steadied herself against the table's edge and taken a sip of wine, her legs still trembling slightly.

This wasn't a dream. She was really back... or rather, the dream had ended — for her, at least.

"So, Vereesa, how did you know I was at the Violet Citadel?"

"And you're only thinking to ask that now?" The elf looked over. "All night long..."

"All night long all that came out of our mouths was meaningless nonsense," Jace said.

"It wasn't entirely meaningless." Vereesa walked back and sat on the edge of the bed, gently kissing the warlock's forehead. "Amy told me. She said you were probably with Archmage Vargus, so I came."

"You went to Amy first?" Jace asked.

"When Amy is in Dalaran, her place is always your first stop, Jace Sesso. Didn't even need to guess." Vereesa pursed her lips and poked Jace's cheek with her finger.

"Of course, I didn't go see her just for you. She was curious about my trip into the Dream and kept asking about it. But... even though I went there and spent so long inside, I can't really describe much about the Dream itself."

"But you at least met a legendary demigod," Jace said.

"I met more than one demigod." Vereesa's eyes narrowed slightly as she looked out the window. "It's just that I can't name some of them. Their souls wandered across endless wilds, like mirages, like wonders that even magic couldn't shape."

"What did they look like?" Jace asked curiously.

"I feel like I should remember clearly, but the only one I can still really picture is Aviana." Vereesa said. "She was a tall goddess, with ivory skin and eyes like pools of silver-blue. Her hands and feet were the talons of a bird of prey, but she could walk as gracefully as any elegant princess."

"I thought she was always flying," Jace said. "Like a harpy."

"Now that you mention it..." Vereesa said. "She really did look a lot like a harpy. Her arms were covered in long feathers, and her silver-white hair was woven with colorful plumage. But somehow, even after spending so much time with her, I never made the connection to harpies! We'd encountered harpies several times in Kalimdor — you'd think it would have been an easy association."

"Ah. Now that I think back on the dream world, it doesn't seem so clear anymore. I can barely tell if I saw those souls shortly after entering the Dream, or just before I left. Dalaran's day and night, its bricks and walls — everything feels too sharp to me now. Every sound, every word, every shadow, even a hare brushing through the grass in a roadside flowerbed — everything's too vivid. I think I need more time to adjust to being awake again."

"Could you feel these things before you entered the dream?" Jace asked.

"My pride wants to say yes, but my gut tells me there's a difference." Vereesa said. "Aviana taught me a lot, but nothing that specific. In the Dream, it felt like she was taking me traveling — showing me the Dream's lands and scenery, introducing me to the beings who lived there..."

"I'd guess that in the Dream, 'feeling' matters more than 'seeing.'" Jace said. "Most harpies are cruel and vicious, but Aviana is a goddess who protects life. So when you were in the Dream, it was hard to connect the two."

"You're right. Feelings, emotions — they matter more than what I saw. Queen Ysera said something similar to me." Vereesa said quietly. "But Aviana isn't a simple benevolent nature spirit. She's beautiful and terrible, protective and piercing, loving and cold. There are struggles in the Emerald Dream too — even hunts. I think that's what they mean by balance. Like the sun and the moon."

"Speaking of the moon — Aviana introduced me to the goddess she reveres: Elune." The elf continued. "I don't usually care about faith — the Holy Light, other priests, whatever — but I know Elune exists. I can even sing that song."

"Listen —"

*Elune, eternal light. She tends to the children of the stars, and we look up to her. Her beauty and her grace, bright as a clear night, clear as a lake.*

Vereesa sang the ancient highborne ballad. Jace's command of Thalassian only let him catch the gist, but not understanding every word only let him sink deeper into the song itself.

Quel'Thalas's haughty bearing made it hard to get close, and Stormwind's gray walls and blue tiles didn't quite fit this ten-thousand-year-old melody. But the moonlit purple eaves of Dalaran outside the window were the perfect backdrop for the mood.

Halfway through, Vereesa ducked her head and laughed sheepishly. "As a Farstrider, singing isn't exactly part of my daily training."

"Not every human gets to hear an elf sing in their lifetime, Vereesa." Jace said. "I don't think I'll forget tonight for the rest of my life."

The elf's lips curled up slightly. She didn't mind being called an elf here — after all, "elf singing" in Common was basically a positive version of a siren's song. A pure compliment.

"Hah. Better forget it for now! Let me go back to Silvermoon and practice my vocals, then I'll sing for you again." Silvermoon. Vocal practice. When Jace put those together, he couldn't help but realize something, and he took a moment longer to think about what to say next.

Vereesa seemed to notice his hesitation. She smiled. "You were thinking about Lirath, weren't you?"

"Yes." Jace nodded.

"I don't know if it has anything to do with those spirits I met in the Emerald Dream." Vereesa said. "But I'm starting to feel like Lirath exists in another world too."

"Look — if demons go to the Twisting Nether when they die, and elementals return to their elemental planes, and wild gods go back to the Emerald Dream... then I think mortals must have a destination too."

"Someone as kind as my little brother, who never hurt anyone, who never even thought of harming the innocent. Lirath never had any decent combat training, but the moment the orcs entered Quel'Thalas, he picked up a weapon to defend his home... If the Shadowlands is where people like him go, then it must be an incredibly beautiful and peaceful place."

"Death is not necessarily the end," Jace murmured.

Vereesa leaned gently against Jace's shoulder. "Yeah. Death is not necessarily the end."

Jace didn't want to bring up that necromancer's threatening words here, but the phrase had slipped out before he could stop it.

"But Quel'Thalas believes in the sun." He rubbed her long, pointed ear with his fingers. "Was Aviana trying to convert you?"

Vereesa explained, "In Quel'Thalas, we see Elune — or what you humans call the White Lady — as one of many symbols in ancient legends. Her status can't compare to the sun's, of course."

"Quel'dorei yearn for the sun. The Sunwell is our source of power. Logically, I shouldn't abandon everything my ancestors and sisters believed in to chase the faith of the Kaldorei."

"But Aviana told me the two aren't contradictory. Elune would never neglect the children who walk under the sun, much less hate them. And by the same token, the sun wouldn't reject the children of the moon and stars. It's not just the sun and moon — this world, and even the Emerald Dream itself, are built on countless such opposites..."

"Balance," Jace said.

"Yes." Vereesa said softly. "Just like you."

"Me?"

"Don't you think so? What she said made me think of you, Jace."

"I... well, I'm not really sure yet."

Vereesa smiled. "I've decided to follow her advice for now — to walk with her and the druids under the Emerald Dream's night sky, to speak with Lady Elune, to try to accept her radiance and the Emerald Dream's life force, to try... to maintain my own balance — spiritually, mentally, and in my power. She said I'm the first Quel'dorei to ever reach this point. She compared me to the ancient Quel'dorei of ten thousand years ago. That took me a bit by surprise."

"What do the other druids think?" Jace asked curiously.

Vereesa said, "They know everything we've done, from Grim Batol to the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, so they respect me. But at the same time, those sleeping old-timers stubbornly believe I won't truly embrace any of this. They didn't say it outright, but I could feel their doubt."

"Either way, I went into the Emerald Dream to learn more powerful nature magic, to find greater strength — that was my original goal. No matter what they think, I'll keep working toward it. Though... I feel like the moment I stepped into that place with Lady Emeriss, I forgot that goal entirely."

"You definitely came back with more power, Vereesa." Jace glanced at the curve of her chest half-hidden by her thin shirt and nodded seriously.

"You mean just now?" Vereesa grinned, quite pleased with herself. "I think that's more a result of exercising during my travels. As for what I actually learned... we'll see when I meet Mor'phil."

Jace slapped his forehead. "You're still thinking about fighting her?"

"Even setting aside the fact that she beat me in that duel in Duskwood, Jace Sesso..." Vereesa nibbled the warlock's ear. "She's had you to herself for months. I can't just let that go without settling the score."

"Again? Are you settling scores with her or with me?" Jace swallowed.

"You're not getting off either." Vereesa's voice tightened, and Jace's heart tightened right along with it.
