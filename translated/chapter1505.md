## Chapter 1505: An Elven Method?

The private room at the deepest end of the Magus Commerce Exchange was filled with a faint, arcane-mimicked fragrance — hard to place which flower it was. A portrait of a high elf magister hung on the wall, and ornately bound grimoires lined the windowsill, giving the room all the ceremonial feel of the magical city.

On the table sat plates of sweet cheese pie, dried snowplums, honeyed venison, and a slender-necked white wine bottle with a tree-ring pattern — probably the honey mead high elves loved best.

Jace was just reaching for the cork to see if his guess was right when Needlefang's excited chatter came drifting in from the hallway.

"You should have seen it, Vereesa Windrunner! The ones called trolls are ugly enough, but half-dead they're even scarier!"

The little black dragon was backing through the door, gesturing wildly to the ranger following her in. She finished by turning to the green dragon beside her. "I'm right, aren't I, Sorah? You saw that undead 'ice troll' too."

"I don't think trolls are ugly," Sorah said. "But that undead creature was indeed fierce-looking. Unpleasant to look at."

"An undead ice troll?" Jace asked. "What's that?"

"You'll regret not sticking around to see it," Needlefang said.

"That mage told us it was an undead creature from a place called Zul'Drak." Sorah said. "Evidence related to a mysterious plague that Kirin Tor scouts brought back from Northrend."

Jace nodded. This was probably a good thing. Zul'Drak was the territory of the ice troll empire; finding ice trolls transformed into undead meant the Kirin Tor should realize the mysterious plague's influence was spreading. The more evidence like this piled up, the sooner the Alliance in Lordaeron would take it seriously.

He knew that the Cult of the Damned, which Kel'Thuzad later founded for the Lich King, had spread so quickly through Lordaeron precisely because it used the promise of immortality to lure countless nobles, wealthy men, and the disaffected. Scattered undead sightings in Northrend could at least make the clearer-headed among them realize what that "immortality" really meant.

"I doubt the Violet Hold has much that would surprise you either, Jace." Sorah sat down across from him.

Jace was still thinking about the ice troll business and nodded absently. Needlefang leaned in and peered at his eyes. "You were so tired last night. Were you mating?"

Vereesa nearly choked on a piece of venison, coughing several times. Needlefang stared at her. "What's the matter? I've never seen my father this tired!"

"I think it's perfectly normal for Jace. At least in the Dream, it was nothing strange for an adult male dragon to be this exhausted after mating." Sorah spoke in a purely educational tone.

"Enough! Stop right there!" Jace clenched his fist. "I didn't call you all here to talk about this. It's about those few weeks I was away."

"I'm curious — you didn't tell Sorah and Needlefang about your mysterious adventure?" Vereesa asked. "Is it that classified?"

"Khadgar didn't want me telling anyone." Jace ran his fingers over the wrapping of Ulthalesh. "But now this involves more than just me... Are you sure this room is safe, Vereesa? No Kirin Tor listening spells?"

"This room is reserved for our kingdom's ambassadors." Vereesa said. "Don't worry about security. And definitely not about eavesdropping."

"What about elves listening in?" Needlefang whispered.

"Yes." Vereesa bent down and stared at her. "Like right now, there's an elf right in front of you, little black dragon... and at this very moment, that elf happens to be the direct superior of Quel'Thalas's intelligence agents in Dalaran!"

"Got it, got it, Lady Vereesa." Needlefang shrank back.

So this little menace only had nerve around him. He really had spoiled her too much.

"So." Jace's gaze swept around the room. "Khadgar and I went to Karazhan."

"Karazhan?" Needlefang looked confused.

"Karazhan!" Vereesa leaned toward him. "This is the chaotic place you mentioned yesterday that's almost as bad as the Emerald Dream... This is even more chaotic than the Dream! And you went on a mission this dangerous without waiting for me and Grede?"

"Khadgar wanted me to keep it quiet. He didn't want too many people involved." Jace picked up a piece of sweet cheese pie. "And I thought since he was Medivh's student, and the tower had gotten a lot quieter after Medivh left... who knew we'd run into so many problems?"

"Did you ever think about why he asked you to go along, Jace?" Sorah asked. "If it hadn't been dangerous, would he have thought of you?"

"I'd just agreed to tear up the Book of Unspeakable Names for him." Jace said. "I thought him letting me into Karazhan's grand library was some kind of repayment. But we made it out alive, barely. Still, there were things we couldn't figure out. For instance, Lady Aegwynn apparently already knows I have Ulthalesh."

"How did she know?" Sorah picked up her wine glass.

"Kil'jaeden." Vereesa exhaled sharply, fire in her voice. "That demon lord must have told her. Who else could it be? Dar'Khan is dead, and his void-and-necromancy cronies don't know where Ulthalesh is, let alone what it looks like. Apart from us, the only ones who know Jace has that staff are the Burning Legion."

The ranger-lord stepped to the window, practically steaming. "But what if Aegwynn does know? Does she plan to listen to a demon's manipulation, hunt down the master of Ulthalesh, and 'redeem' her teacher — even after her target risked his life to clean up the disaster her son caused? Let her come!"

"I'm not afraid either, Jace." Sorah said. "As long as I live, I won't let her hurt you."

"Is there going to be a fight?" Needlefang asked.

"Not so fast." Jace raised a hand quickly. "At least from everything Khadgar and I saw, Aegwynn doesn't seem to want to come after me for now."

Vereesa seemed to calm down a bit. She was probably muttering "at least she knows what's good for her" under her breath. She'd spent that time in the Emerald Dream, maybe gained more power, maybe touched the moon goddess's strength... but no matter what training she'd gone through, it hadn't tempered that fiery temper.

"How can you be sure?" Sorah asked.

"It's complicated..." Jace told the ranger and the two dragons everything he, Khadgar, and Mor'phil had seen in the Observatory. Everyone fell into a thoughtful silence.

"A prophecy ritual." Vereesa mulled it over for a long time. "Even a mage like Khadgar couldn't replicate it in Karazhan? I'm curious about what Aegwynn actually saw."

"I was wondering — could we recreate that ritual at Quel'Thalas's Observatory? The astromancers in the Magisters' Lodge hold ceremonies sometimes. Most of what I know about is checking for potential threats to the Sunwell. I think if it's you, Jace, they'd agree to help."

Jace hadn't known Silvermoon had an Observatory. But in the game, half of Silvermoon had already been destroyed by the Scourge, so its absence there didn't mean much.

Besides, the blood elf elite who later lived in Shattrath and betrayed Kael'thas called themselves the "Astromancers," which suggested Quel'Thalas did have that tradition.

Holding a magical ceremony in Silvermoon with the Sunwell's power to draw on — the energy source was unquestionable both in quality and quantity. But when Jace heard Vereesa say the ritual was used to look for potential threats to the Sunwell, his expectations cooled considerably.

If it was that useful, how come in another timeline the whole of Silvermoth was caught almost completely off guard when the Scourge came sweeping up from the south? Arthas just walked right in and tossed Kel'Thuzad's urn into the Sunwell.

But considering the traitor Dar'Khan was a high-ranking member of the Magisters' Lodge, he could have tampered with things to make everyone let their guard down. It was possible. And it was still a pretty good fallback plan.

"Once Khadgar's out of Karazhan, I'll ask him if it's feasible," Jace said.
