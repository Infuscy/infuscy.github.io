Chapter 1506: The Alterac Gladiator

As a ranger-lord who'd been away for two or three months, Vereesa still had plenty of work to arrange in Dalaran before leaving for Grim Batol and the "Council of Three Hammers." So after meeting with Jace, Sorah, and Needlefang, she quickly returned to the Quel'Thalas embassy district.

Before departure, Amy also needed to rearrange her classes with the other magic instructors at the Dalaran Academy, or finish some tasks ahead of time, to prepare for the trip to Grim Batol.

Jace had a few days to kill, so he figured he might as well fulfill the promise he'd made to Sorah back in Stormwind — head into the Alterac Mountains!

Dalaran and Lake Lordamere sat at the southwestern foot of Alterac. To get to Grim Batol, you'd head southeast — not exactly on the way. So before the Brewfest festivities began, it was best to use this gap in the schedule to take Sorah into the snowy mountains and properly satisfy her cravings.

And whenever there was a trip, there was no leaving behind the little monster who wanted to tag along for everything. Especially since she'd fallen in love with playing in the snow in Dun Morogh — the moment she heard they were going to the towering snowy peaks near Dalaran, she kicked up a fuss about going too... though Jace couldn't for the life of him understand why a black dragon would enjoy snow.

The green dragon stretched her wings, glided past a jagged outcrop of strange rocks, and climbed higher into the falling snow. Needlefang followed behind, curiously eyeing the blurry mountain paths and the abandoned watchtowers and stone huts along the way.

To the west, Dalaran had shrunk into a little purple fairy-tale castle floating on Lake Lordamere, wrapped in a faint arcane aura like a delicate glass snow-globe.

"I don't sense much life here, Jace. Maybe they've sensed my arrival and are deliberately hiding their presence." Sorah banked around a towering snowy cliff and tilted her head to look at Jace with one eye. "Can you feel their fear?"

"Fear?" Jace said. "I think it's more likely they're just not around here."

"Just as the Wildhammers bond with gryphons in the Hinterlands, the Stormpikes see Alterac rams as comrades rather than mounts. In Dun Morogh, the Bronzebeard dwarves have a completely different relationship with their rams. The kingdom of Alterac also has many legends of brave old warriors dueling with rams. Try flying deeper into the northern valleys."

"So they must taste good." Sorah said.

Jace laughed. Sorah folded her wings slightly and dove toward the valley behind the cliff. Needlefang hurried to keep up.

Sure enough, once Lake Lordamere and the city of Dalaran were hidden behind the unbroken snowy peaks, and the valley showed no trace of green from distant Hillsbrad or Silverpine, pale white and yellowish shapes began to appear among the snow-covered pine forests.

"I need you to help flush them out of the forest, Needlefang!" Sorah roared in the dragon tongue, her voice merging with the snow-swept wind as it carried across the sky. Then Needlefang let out a sharper shriek in reply and accelerated into the distance.

Under the two young dragons' coordinated hunt, a small herd of rams was soon separated from the snow forest.

Driven by Needlefang's black shadow, they emerged at the forest's edge — where Sorah was already circling in wait.

The herd had rams of all sizes, huddled together like a family. Jace could clearly feel the intense fear from the smaller ones, but the larger ones were different — their fury was far stronger than their fear.

Though "size" was relative here. Even the smallest of these rams was no smaller than a farm sheep. The largest adult Alterac rams might even be bigger than an Elwynn forest bear. This was Alterac, after all — the pale gryphons of the snowy peaks were beasts that dared to fight young dragons head-on.

"In Draenor, I helped the elders hunt the beasts of Terokkar!" Needlefang circled above the forest edge and roared proudly in the draconic tongue. "I'm great at herding, Somnarius!"

Sorah nodded, extended her long tongue to lick her chin and nose, and narrowed her moonlit serpentine eyes, locking onto the herd before her.

Under the gaze of this nearly-adult green dragon, even the lead rams began to shrink back.

Of course, no matter how brave and strong the local legends made these rams out to be, they were used to facing humans or dwarves who came into the mountains — Alterac snow wolves, even gryphons. Not an actual dragon.

Just as Jace thought the herd was about to accept their fate, an enormous old ram stepped forward, shielding the young and the very young behind him.

He let out a rough bellow — nothing like a sheep or ram. More like a bull.

Then he lowered his head, presenting his thick, coiled horns, and released a wave of fury and pride.

The emotion was so intense that Jace almost felt he was facing a fervent gladiator rather than a beast.

"You don't know your place!" Needlefang roared from the sky. But the old ram didn't retreat an inch — he dropped his head even lower and pawed the snowy mud with his forehooves.

Sorah looked at him and said softly in the dragon tongue, "I accept your challenge, beast."

The old ram seemed to understand the green dragon's meaning instantly. A savage fighting spirit erupted from him, looking to Jace like a burst of flame.

Jace jumped down from Sorah's neck. Sorah lowered her head, and the old ram suddenly launched himself forward with tremendous force, charging straight at the green dragon. Sorah adjusted her head and neck angle and met him head-on.

A dull thump of bone against bone. Jace's eyes twitched as he watched the old ram's body fly through the air, limp and helpless, and crash into the pure white snow. Blood seeped from his mouth and nose.

Sorah walked over, pinned the old ram's body with her foreclaw, bit down and tore easily, taking a few mouthfuls. Then she turned to the rest of the herd, shivering together in the wind-driven snow, and said in the dragon tongue, "You may go."

The herd turned and fled into the forest. Needlefang, circling above, made no move to stop them, simply gliding in silence. The two dragons seemed to share an unspoken understanding — no consultation needed, they'd both decided to let them go.

The old ram was enormous. Even with Sorah's appetite, she couldn't swallow it whole. She had to tear it apart piece by piece, methodically eating until nothing was left. In the end, she even swallowed the cracked skull — horns, fur, and all.

"Delicious, just as I thought, Jace." Sorah's reptilian, scale-covered lips pulled back as she raised her head and looked into the distance. "When I tasted Alterac ram cheese back in Stormwind, I knew the rams here would be good. Didn't need any dwarf to tell me."

Jace nodded. Sorah continued, "But just one isn't nearly enough. We're heading to Grim Batol next, and that journey isn't much shorter than flying here from Stormwind."

"Come find more with me, Needlefang!"

"Coming!"

Needlefang called out and flew off. Jace grabbed the green dragon's foreleg as she launched herself, letting her carry him back into the snowy valley.

They swept over snow-covered pine forests as the two dragons climbed higher into the Alterac range, searching for the strong young rams that dared to fight in the harshest mountain conditions. In the game, Alterac rams seemed to be everywhere in the valleys, but out here in these endless snowy peaks, they weren't so easy to find.

Jace was scanning a distant cliff when a sharp shriek came from Needlefang's direction. Sorah immediately banked and chased after her.

When they caught up to the black dragon, she'd already shifted into human form and was running toward a forest. Sorah landed and transformed too, following the tracks the black dragon had left in the snow.

The wind was picking up now, driving thick, goose-feather-sized snowflakes with a howling sound. Jace noticed Needlefang crouching under a massive pine tree, waving in their direction — there seemed to be something below.

Jace ran a few more steps before he could see what it was.

A person. An orc.
