Chapter 1507: Into the Unknown Future

He was a powerfully built orc — stockier than most orcs Jace had ever seen. Even wrapped in thick furs and padded coats like a bear, with only his eyes showing, you could still tell from the skin around his eyes and nose that it was a dark green.

His black hair was plastered to his clothes and face by melted snow. He didn't look alive at all — like an ancient corpse just dug out of an ice tomb, frozen for a thousand years.

"Is he dead?" Jace asked.

"Not yet." Sorah said. "There's still life flowing through him. And it's not weak."

"How is that possible?" Jace looked around at the pine forest. "Back in the day, walking through Tirisfal Glades at night in winter felt like I was losing half my life. This is the Alterac Mountains. It's not uncommon for whole caravans to freeze to death out here, let alone one—"

"One orc," Needlefang finished. "I thought you knew these guys well enough by now, Jace Sesso. Don't underestimate how tough these bastards can be. What do we do? Leave him here? Or put him out of his misery?"

"The Frostwolf clan in these mountains isn't like other orcs." Jace said. "More importantly, they've helped me before. If it weren't for their chieftain Drek'Thar, I might never have made it to Draenor. Even if we don't take him back to their camp, we should at least see if we can wake him up. Looks like he passed out from the cold."

"Drek'Thar... argh!" The orc coughed twice, muttering in Orcish. "Where... Drek'Thar... I must... I can't die here."

Jace frowned. Both the way Drek'Thar's name was pronounced and the orc's accent were strange — oddly familiar, hard to place, but definitely not Frostwolf orc speech.

The way he said "I must" was very close to the one orc he'd met before — Garrosh Hellscream of Garadar. But the rest didn't sound the same.

Still, that association was enough to make his heart skip a beat. Could this orc be Grommash Hellscream, chieftain of the Warsong clan, the first to drink Mannoroth's blood — Garrosh's father?

Black hair. Dark green skin. Massive build. Couldn't freeze to death in the mountains.

But where was his battle axe? Where was Gorehowl — the legendary axe he'd used to slay the demigod Cenarius and the pit lord Mannoroth?

All Jace could see at his waist was a standard-issue short sword common in the kingdom of Lordaeron. His patched-up rags looked stitched together from human clothes, more appropriate for the local Frostwolf exiles than the Warsong chieftain who'd been skirmishing with the Farstriders in the forests.

Or maybe this was a messenger sent by the Warsong clan to contact the Frostwolves? Got lost in the mountains?

"He might be waking up. Should I turn into an orc and ask him?" Sorah asked.

"The Frostwolves know me. It's fine, Sorah. I'll just ask him directly." Jace said.

The green dragon was about to use nature magic to heal him, but Jace patted her wrist. "Don't waste your mana. I've got healing potions."

Sorah nodded. Jace pulled out a vial of minor healing potion and held it to the orc's lips. The orc seemed to recognize the concoction — he sniffed it, immediately pulled down his heavy scarf and face wrap, opened his mouth, and let Jace pour the potion in.

After drinking it, the orc coughed a few times, recovered for a moment, tried to stand, then slumped back against the tree. He opened his eyes, saw who was in front of him, and jumped up like he'd been shocked.

"Don't think I'll just surrender! Don't think I can't handle you mages!"

The orc pulled his face wrap back up, gripped his sword, and pointed it at Jace, then jabbed it toward Sorah and Needlefang. His eyes were wide, his feet adjusting his stance. He moved like an experienced fighter.

More importantly, he was speaking Common — and with a perfect Lordaeron accent, no hesitation, no orcish inflection. Compared to the Orcish he'd muttered earlier, this sounded much more like his native language. Even with the rough voice, it was like facing a hoarse human brute, not an orc.

"Put down your weapon, orc!" Needlefang snapped. "If we'd wanted to kill you, do you think you'd have had the chance to open your eyes? You even wasted a healing potion on us!"

The orc seemed convinced. He lowered his sword slightly, glancing at Needlefang from the corner of his eye. "Why? Why did you save me?"

"Are you from the Frostwolf clan?" Jace asked in Orcish.

The green-skinned warrior's eyes shifted. He hesitated, then said in Orcish, "Yes. I'm from the Frostwolf clan. Now answer my question, mage. Why did you save me? Where do you come from — Dalaran, Tarren Mill, or Durnholde?"

Claimed to be from the Frostwolf clan, but there was no concealment or deception in his emotions. He genuinely believed he was from the Frostwolf clan.

But at the same time, his accent had absolutely nothing to do with the Frostwolves. And the more he spoke, the worse it got — the last few place names were pure Common pronunciation, with a hint of Lordaeronian noble intonation.

Add to that his unusual sword grip and the practiced footwork of someone looking for angles against multiple opponents, and Jace could almost figure out who this was.

He was Thrall. Or rather, Go'el. Son of Durotan.

Jace hadn't recognized him at all. Last time they'd met, the kid had only been eight years old but was already taller than most humans. Now, six or seven years later, he was built like a small ogre.

He hadn't heard any news recently about the Durnholde arena star going missing. But then again, if that kind of cash cow suddenly vanished, Jace figured he'd keep it quiet too if he ran that castle.

"You ask too many questions, orc." Jace said. "We're from Dalaran. We came here looking for rare herbs in the Alterac Mountains. We have absolutely no interest in whether you escaped an Alliance internment camp or a cage in Durnholde. As for saving you, we're just following Archmage Antonidas's policy of treating orcs as an 'intelligent race.' Also, I wanted to ask you—"

"Did you notice any earthroot around here before you passed out?" Sorah added in Common. Jace nodded.

"Uh... I." The orc clearly wanted to answer in Orcish, but switched to Common in the end. "I didn't see any earthroot, lady."

"Then that healing potion was wasted." Needlefang grumbled.

"Truly a shame." Sorah looked at Jace.

Jace gave a noncommittal "mm" and then asked, "The Frostwolf clan's camp is very far from here. What were you doing in this area? Hunting? There's nothing here — no rams, no wolves, no hares."

"You know where the Frostwolf camp is?" The orc's eyes lit up as he spoke in Common.

"You haven't answered my question." Jace raised his chin slightly.

The orc exhaled a puff of white breath and finally lowered his sword hand entirely. "Fine. I'm lost. I've been wandering these mountains for... I don't know how long. Maybe weeks, maybe months. I don't even remember the last time I saw a living person, mage."

"That's not surprising. The Frostwolves move their camp often." Jace pointed toward the direction the Stonefist ogres had led him and Vereesa before. "But last I heard, they were active in that direction."

The orc narrowed his eyes, squinted at where Jace was pointing, and nodded.

Jace could feel the suspicion radiating off him. He didn't believe Jace's group had come all the way to Alterac just to gather herbs, and he didn't think the reason they saved him was that simple either. But that valley Jace had pointed to might be his only chance to stay alive — he wasn't about to doubt that.

"Oh, and one more thing." Jace said. "If you get lost again, try asking the mountains for help."

"Ask the mountains for help?" The orc's eyes widened slightly as he glanced at the distant Alterac peaks.

"Yes. Ask the mountains for help." Jace repeated in Orcish. "You can go now. Good luck."

The orc nodded once, then backed away step by step until he reached the forest. Only then did he spin around and bolt into the trees.

Sorah watched his retreating back. "That orc was full of lies. He can barely speak Orcish. When he claimed to be from the Frostwolf clan, he didn't even sound confident. He might be a Warsong assassin. Could even be from Blackrock Spire."

"But our dear Jace Sesso let him go." Needlefang snorted. "You're a completely different person from the demon warlock in Auchindoun. I barely recognize you. And honestly, orc meat is pretty good too, Sorah. Aren't you interested? Too bad Jace Sesso wouldn't give you the chance today."

"Then let the chance slip away. I'm not interested at all!" Sorah waved her hand.

Because of Jace and Vereesa's visit to the Frostwolf camp, many Frostwolf orcs already knew the slave gladiator star was probably Durotan's son.

If the Alliance couldn't exterminate every orc scattered across the continent, then they needed a leader — and a Frostwolf orc was the better choice. At least better than Blackhand of Blackrock.

Still, rather than having Thrall lead a bunch of wandering Lordaeron orcs and exiled Gurubashi trolls to found a new Horde, like in another timeline, Jace figured it'd be better to find a way to send them back to Draenor. To their homeland.

Especially now that the Legion was planning to withdraw, the Expeditionary Force had already left that world, and Prince Sabellian — who'd proclaimed himself "Lord of Outland" — had promised not to touch the orcs of Nagrand.

Just as Jace thought the matter was settled, a voice in Orcish suddenly boomed from the forest.

"What's your name, mage?!"

Jace hesitated a moment before answering, "Jace Sesso! From Brill!"

"I've heard that name!" The voice roared from the trees. "I'll remember you, Jace Sesso of Brill!"
