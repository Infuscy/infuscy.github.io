Chapter 1509: Passing Through Southshore

With only a week left until Brewfest, Amy finally managed to free herself from Kirin Tor and Dalaran Academy obligations. Jace's group set off immediately, heading southeast toward Grim Batol.

The journey was a long one, and besides Jace and Mor'phil, Sorah also had to carry Amy and Vereesa. So they needed an early start, stopping in Southshore and Menethil Harbor along the way before finally reaching the Bastion Range.

Aside from resting, leaving time for Sorah to "refuel" en route was essential. They'd left Dalaran early in the morning, and just past noon they arrived at Southshore. To avoid panicking the local farmers, they followed Vereesa's guidance to stay clear of watchtowers and patrol routes, finally landing in a forest northeast of town.

But in the end, a squad of Lordaeron scout cavalry still spotted the two young dragons flying overhead. By the time the riders chased into the forest, Jace's party had already entered town.

Southshore was lively in September, especially with Brewfest approaching — lots of Stormpike dwarves had gathered here, planning to celebrate with their Bronzebeard kin who weren't heading back to Ironforge.

Needlefang was practically drooling at the sight of dwarves in the street drinking from tankards and mugs. She insisted on joining them, and Sorah had to drag her straight into the inn.

Amy watched the little black dragon kicking up a fuss in Sorah's grip and edged closer to Jace. "Has she always been like this, or did she change after meeting you?"

"Always been like this." Jace said. "Ever since we rescued her from a spider the size of a chapel."

"A spider the size of a chapel?" Amy blinked, then smacked her lips. "Draenor really is a strange place."

"You bet. You missed out by not stepping through that door." Jace pointed at a Stormpike dwarf's ale shop. "You go ahead to the inn with Vereesa. I'll head over there."

"So you are going to get that little black dragon some booze." Amy smiled.

"What else am I supposed to do? Listen to her scream the whole way?" Jace said. "I took her to the Violet Hold back in Dalaran and she quieted right down. Works like a charm."

"Fair enough." Amy nodded, but the look she gave her younger friend was thoughtful. Then she turned and went into the inn.

What are you looking at me like that for? It's not like I want to take care of that little squirt. Who'd enjoy looking after a black dragon? Especially in Southshore — everybody here remembers what black dragons did to this town.

Jace grumbled internally as he crossed the street toward the ale shop.

There were plenty of dwarves inside. A grey-haired dwarf who seemed to be the owner was arguing with a group of tall red-haired dwarves painted with blue, red, and yellow tribal markings. Having spent so much time with Grede, Jace recognized them immediately as Wildhammer dwarves from Aerie Peak.

Jace waited, planning to ask once things cooled down a bit. He remembered Grede had promised Needlefang a lot of drinks. He wondered what the Stormpike dwarves of Alterac had that was special — maybe some kind of thunder ale.

Then he heard footsteps at the door and turned to see a mage walk in.

The man wore a purple-striped blue Kirin Tor robe and looked to be in his late forties or early fifties, acting a bit shifty. Jace recognized him — he'd been watching them ever since they'd entered town.

Normally Jace might not have noticed the stares, since he was used to standing out and people noting his unusual appearance. But this time in Southshore, he was hoping to ask around about Kel'Thuzad's unofficial disciple Helcular, so he was extra alert to any attention — especially from a mage.

"Do I know you, master?" he asked.

"You... you're really Jace Sesso. You actually came to Southshore." The unfamiliar mage spoke quietly, studying Jace's rings intently. His nerves were stretched thin as a bowstring about to snap.

"I... am." Jace asked. "Is there something you need from me?"

"Excellent, excellent." The mage pulled a grimoire from his belt pouch, then produced a quill, dipped it in ink he carried with him, and held it out to Jace excitedly. "Could you sign this, master?"

"Ah, right. My name's Lextor Mortem, a Kirin Tor mage. We've met before. I was at Auchindoun helping set up the portal and saw with my own eyes how you used dark magic to clear out those damned Shadow Council orcs. But I had escort duty for the Kirin Tor afterward, so I never got the chance to find you in Stormwind. Now that I've finally run into you, I can't miss this opportunity."

"Uh, well." Jace took the quill, hesitated for a moment, but still wrote his initials on the grimoire — Arcane Hunters Unleashed: A Study of Felhounds in the Twisting Nether.

The mage carefully put the book away. "Thank you so much, Master Sesso."

"Actually, I have something I'd like to ask you, Master Mortem." Jace said.

"Anything. I'm at your disposal, master."

"What do you know about the Kirin Tor's investigation into Kel'Thuzad's students?"

"A fair bit. I was actually part of the investigation in Southshore and Tarren Mill last year."

"So there are students of his in Southshore?"

"Yes. Mr. Shackle. He's already been detained in Dalaran."

"No one else?"

"No."

"This Mr. Shackle you mentioned — was he a Kirin Tor member? Did you investigate anyone connected to Kel'Thuzad outside of Dalaran?" Jace said. "I mean the 'wild mages.' Have you found any secret necromantic rituals nearby, or any forbidden casting materials related to necromancy that someone might be hiding?"

"That's exactly why we're still monitoring Southshore, master." The mage said. "Kel'Thuzad was once a member of the Council of Six. He had many students and complicated relationships — some of them are already high up in the Kirin Tor. Archmage Antonidas also believes there are still undiscovered necromantic researchers who collaborated with him."

"But after he was sent to the Violet Hold... it caused quite a stir and panic across all of Lordaeron. Forget necromantic rituals or forbidden casting materials — now there are even rumors that King Terenas's illness was caused by some student of Kel'Thuzad secretly casting a plague curse in revenge for the investigation and arrests. These wild rumors are leading our investigation astray more and more."

"So what's the real story with the king's illness?" Jace asked. "Did the Kirin Tor examine him?"

"Both the Kirin Tor and the Church sent people to look at him." Mortem said. "From what I know, he was bitten by mosquitoes while resting near Menethil Harbor on his way back from Stormwind and caught a marsh fever. It's not common in Lordaeron, but it's not hard to treat. His condition is under control now."

Jace asked, "What about the others traveling with the king? The archbishop, the prince. Anyone else in the palace come down with something similar?"

"There was a knight-captain and a servant who attended the king. No reports of further infection." Mortem said. "You're worried this might be some necromantic plague related to Kel'Thuzad?"

"I guess Duskwood's problems have me habitually paranoid." Jace said.

"Maybe I shouldn't be so cynical about the intentions of the king across the lake." The mage rubbed his chin thoughtfully. "But I think his conveniently timed illness might be an excuse to decline King Varian Wrynn's call to attack Blackrock Mountain, using the mosquito bite as a pretext."

"He's sick, not his whole army. And what about his son?" Jace asked.

"But if the rumors about a necromantic plague are true?" The mage said quietly. "Wouldn't that be a good enough reason to skip the Blackrock Mountain campaign?"

Jace neither agreed nor disagreed. "Thank you for the information, Mr. Mortem."

"Thank you for the autograph, Master Sesso." The mage said quickly. "I really appreciate it."

Lordaeron. Kel'Thuzad. Thrall. Jaina. Arthas... Karazhan. Aegwynn. Medivh. Kil'jaeden... Northrend. Nerubians. The Lich King.

Jace wished he was just being paranoid, but the footsteps of that age were getting closer and closer.

Back at the inn, Jace pushed through the noisy crowd and set a bag of thunder ale on the table. Needlefang's excited shouts sounded muffled and distant as he couldn't stop thinking about all this mess.

The green dragon beside him shook her head helplessly. Vereesa looked at her. "You seem tired, Sorah?"

"It's nothing." Sorah smiled.

"I can feel the dream magic powering your flight is a bit strained." Vereesa said quietly. "Is it because you're carrying too many people?"

"Uh... is it because of me? I could take a gryphon to Grim Batol." Amy looked guiltily around the table.

"You're much lighter than Grede Thunderfist, Lady Marin." Sorah said.

Jace could tell that carrying this group was even more draining for Sorah than when she'd carried Grede. But it wasn't about weight — rather, both he and Vereesa, as well as the hidden Mor'phil, had grown significantly in magic, so the dream magic was being stretched thin.

"Sorah, let Needlefang carry Mor'phil," he said.

"What?!" Needlefang slammed her hand on the table, gritting her teeth. "Say that again, Jace Sesso."

Even Sorah was startled and waved her hands. "Really, it's fine. Don't make her—"

"Help share Sorah's load." Jace said. "You said before you wouldn't carry mortals. You never said you wouldn't carry demons. Mor'phil's practically your teacher. What's wrong with carrying her?"

"Demons are even worse!" Needlefang turned away angrily.

"Then at least carry the luggage, Miss Needlefang?" Vereesa asked.

"I'm not carrying your stupid bags." Needlefang snorted.

Jace sighed and looked toward the door, wondering if he should rent a gryphon. Then Needlefang stole a glance at Sorah, tilted her head toward Jace, and said, "If you've got the guts not to be scared of falling into the sea, you can get on my back."

"Huh? You'll let me ride you?" Jace was startled by the offer. The others at the table looked equally shocked.

"Less talk." Needlefang took a gulp of ale and said calmly, "I'm asking if you dare."

"Well... what's there to be afraid of?" Jace said. "I've ridden your dad's back before."

"Don't mention that! Don't you dare! Damn warlock, I'm going to regret this!" Needlefang squeezed her eyes shut and growled.

"Sorry, sorry..." Jace hurriedly tried to soothe her.

Amy, Vereesa, and Sorah exchanged glances, suppressing smiles, and said nothing.
