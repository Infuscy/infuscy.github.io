## Chapter 151: Dragons Have Parents Too

"You're not dead either, are you?" Grede jabbed a finger at him. "Tell me — were you trying to lure us toward that southern mill so that warlock could ambush us?!"

"Not a chance." The elf frowned, wearing an expression of faint bewilderment. "I did kill him. Unless he got back up when you arrived? In that case I suppose I don't know enough about orcish anatomy. Maybe their hearts are tucked between their legs."

The elf chuckled under his breath. Grede rapped the table. "Show some respect! That warlock wasn't killed by you at all — it was the warg that killed him. That place was his hideout. If that warg hadn't done the job, who knows how many traps he'd have waiting for us to walk into. Out with it!"

Hearing Grede lay it out like that, Jace had to admit the body hadn't really shown any clear signs of elven handiwork.

But jumping straight to "the elf didn't do it" felt a bit simplistic too…

Still, he couldn't exactly think of anything to say in the elf's defense. All he could do was see if Grede had some other angle he was working.

"That fellow did set up a few things to protect himself." The elf's blue eyes dimmed slightly. He lifted his teacup and took a quiet sip. "But as a veteran of the Farstrider corps, those little toys were no trouble at all. Compared to the forest trolls, his methods were downright crude."

"And yet you still got caught in that bear trap, Farstrider?" Jace asked. The elf nearly choked on his tea.

"That was a special accident under special circumstances."

He turned back to Grede. "First of all, wargs are creatures from the orcs' world, and every warg that came through the Dark Portal was a trained mount of the Horde. There are no wild ones. After losing their masters, those wargs could even fight alongside other orcs as warriors. You can imagine the bond between a warg and an orc — it's probably not so different from the one between you and your gryphons."

"Don't you insult gryphons," the dwarf said coldly.

"Merely an analogy to aid understanding." The elf continued, "Every orc warlock holds a high rank in the Horde — an officer and an advisor. That warlock was no exception. The cut of his robes and the quality of his gear were top-tier. I've fought orcish armies before, and I know a thing or two. Put those two facts together — what are the odds of a warg turning on a master of that stature?"

Grede looked like he wanted to say something, but swallowed it instead.

He folded his arms on the table, drumming his fingers against the wood as if searching for some other angle to save face — but nothing suitable came to mind.

The elf's lips curled upward. "Don't tell me you've been nursing a grudge for two or three months and never once spotted a contradiction this simple?"

"What?" Grede said, annoyed. "I'm allowed to be suspicious, aren't I?"

Before Jace could say a word, the dwarf suddenly turned to him. "Say something, will you! You've always got a dozen big speeches ready, and now you've gone mute? Whose side are you on?"

Jace glanced between the two and quickly said to the elf, "Right, yeah — what he said. We really didn't find any arrows or anything. Just a snarling warg sitting there waiting for us."

The elf said, "Even if that orc was a warlock, his miserable life wasn't worth a single arrow of mine. If it wasn't damaged, why on earth would I leave it behind? As for the warg — everyone gets hungry. A master's corpse is nothing special. Just a pile of fat and meat. No need to stay loyal to a dead man, don't you think?"

That finally took the wind out of Grede's sails. He grabbed his crutch as if preparing to leave.

The elf's arcane-touched eyes swept over the crutch and narrowed. "What happened to you two? Did that warg maim you both? That one's got his arm in a sling, and that one's got his leg wrapped up."

"You do realize it's been two months," Jace said. "These are from injuries somewhere else."

"Hold on." The elf said, "I heard Magtheridon was killed by a one-eyed Wildhammer dwarf and a Stormwind mage not yet twenty years old. That wouldn't happen to be you two, would it?"

"What the hell is a Magtheridon?" Jace asked.

The elf stroked one of his long eyebrows and smiled. "A dragon. My apologies — I was overthinking it. But that is quite the coincidence."

"The black dragon that died at Southshore?" Grede barked. "That bastard who had the nerve to come at me — he even had a goddamn name?!"

Jace looked at him. "Having a name is normal, Grede. That dragon was huge. You think his parents didn't give him one? He's not some freshly hatched whelp spat out of a clutch by the dozen."

"What — dragons have parents who name them? That's even more ridiculous than him having a name!" Grede turned back to Jace.

"I'm guessing too…" Jace said.

He looked back across the table. The elf had gone still.

"So it was you," he murmured. "The Farstriders had been tracking Magtheridon for a long time. We lost his trail in Silverpine Forest, and the next we heard, he'd turned up at Southshore. I wasn't directly involved in the Silverpine operation, but I know that dragon was no small threat. If not for you two, there'd still be Farstrider rangers risking their lives hunting his trail across the Hillsbride region. Allow me to apologize for my earlier rudeness. Your drinks are on me."

"Too late," Grede said. "I already paid! You might as well cover our lunch while you're at it."

"No problem!"

The elf pulled two gold coins straight out of his pack and set them on the table with a crisp clink. Grede's eyes went wide — the sour face of Terenas II stared up at them, clear as day.

"Fine, fine," the dwarf muttered as he sat back down. "I'll let the earlier matter slide, then."

Jace was less interested in the meal than in what these elves knew about the black dragon hunt.

"Since you've been tracking black dragons all this time, do you know why they've been so active lately?"

The elf looked at Jace. "Something's off, isn't it? You feel it too, human. Explaining all this away as a lingering echo of the Alterac campaign just doesn't hold up."

"We all know these whelps take orders from the adult black dragons, and the adult black dragons all serve the oldest among them. Their internal hierarchy isn't exactly complicated, but it's absolutely rigid. There's no way they'd be loitering around this long after a campaign ended, wasting time… If a single stray whelps showed up, you could call it coincidence. But this many? There has to be a significant reason. The Farstriders just haven't managed to pull together every possible lead yet. As for Lordaeron and Dalaran…"

He shook his head. "I hate to admit it, but an air of distrust has already spread throughout the Alliance of Lordaeron. By now, elf and human no longer fit together the way they did during the war. As the leader of the Alliance, the Kingdom of Lordaeron is not inclined to share critical intelligence with the Farstriders. Most of the time, we're on our own. The vast stretch from Hillsbride down to Tirisfal is human territory now. We quel'dorei are outsiders here. Without the help of the local rulers, every move we make is an uphill battle."

"Things didn't exactly fit together during the war either," Grede muttered.

The elf glared at the dwarf. "Of course not. Otherwise the Eversong Forest wouldn't have been burned to a crisp by those red dragons."

"Who was it that did nothing but talk at the start and refused to send troops?" Grede leaned forward. "The Horde's army had already reached Khaz Modan and you were still out sightseeing in the Hinterlands."

"The same goes for you, dwarves," the elf said coldly. "Stormwind didn't fall overnight. Did anyone spot so much as half a dwarf beard in Elwynn Forest? The help you gave King Wrynn was probably worth less than the lava in the Burning Steppes. At least the dark-skinned dwarves down there gave the orcs pause for a bit — kept them from crossing the Redridge Mountains to hit Stormwind Keep straight from the northeast."

"You goddamn deserved it!" the dwarf roared.

Jace wearily tried to intervene. "You two…"

"I'm going to order food!" Grede slammed the table and hauled himself up. "You two have fun talking."

He hobbled off toward the bar, propping himself against the tables as he went. He glanced back at the crutch he'd left behind, then stormed off even more indignantly without it.
