## Chapter 1510: There's Always a First Time

In a clearing among the pine forest east of Southshore, near the Iceflow River, the green dragon was already lying on her side.

Vereesa and Amy secured the luggage, then climbed onto her back along with Mor'phil. Once they were settled, everyone — including Sorah herself — looked over.

The two demons were clearly enjoying the show, ready to laugh. Even from this distance, Jace could see Sazeno's bared teeth, let alone Mor'phil's grin.

Sorah was watching too. Not for the spectacle — she was nervous. Jace knew that if she was willing to offload a passenger onto Needlefang, she must be feeling the strain pretty badly. But at the same time, she was worried about whether Needlefang would manage.

The little black dragon crouched in front of him, tilting her head to eye the warlock with one eye. Her lip twitched. "Are you ready yet?"

"Ready, ready." Jace said quickly. "I'm good whenever."

"Then hurry up." The black dragon's yellow eye flared.

"Could you lie down?" Jace gestured. "You're all upright like this — how am I supposed to climb on?"

"So much trouble, stupid human." The black dragon huffed, blowing two puffs of black smoke from her nostrils. She had no choice but to lower her head and crouch down.

Jace finally walked over, grabbed the black dragon's shoulder, and climbed onto her back. The young dragon let out a series of grumbling sounds of protest.

Just looking at Sorah and Needlefang side by side, Sorah seemed at least twice as big. She was just about to cross the threshold into adulthood, while Needlefang was on the smaller side even among young dragons.

Once he was actually on Needlefang's back, the size difference really hit home. The black dragon's neck and limbs were slender, her shoulders much narrower.

It felt less like riding a dragon and more like riding a gryphon. He had to abandon his usual habit of sitting near the shoulder like he did on Sorah, scooting back instead so he didn't break Needlefang's neck halfway through.

"What are you wiggling around for?" Needlefang twisted her head to look at him. "If you move like that once we're in the air, I'll drop you in the ocean!"

"Don't you dare! My cloak is on Sorah. If I fall in the ocean, I'll actually die!" Jace said.

"So you are afraid of dying, warlock!" The black dragon sneered. "Then hold still!"

"I will. I'll try not to move." Jace lay flat and said.

He had to admit, compared to the smooth scales on Sorah's back, Needlefang's felt terrible. Those spines could slice a gash in him if he wasn't careful.

But he couldn't exactly strap a gryphon saddle onto a young dragon. Even easygoing Sorah would lose her mind over that.

He wasn't the only one suffering. The black dragon clearly felt something was very wrong too.

She kept shrugging her shoulders, shaking her head — like a cat with an elastic band around its neck, trying to get the "foreign object" off.

Jace was already getting a bad feeling. Once they were airborne... she was definitely going to put him through hell.

But even before takeoff, riding her felt awful enough. If she decided to pull some stunt, what then?

Thinking back to Needlefang's smug "you're going to get it" look at the inn yesterday, Jace was pretty sure this wasn't a friendly invitation at all. It was a trap. He'd have been better off renting a gryphon in Southshore.

Though he *had* asked around last night. Renting a gryphon from Southshore to Dalaran was already insanely expensive, let alone crossing the Baradin Bay to Menethil. And renting a gryphon round-trip to the Bastion Range — where there were no gryphon roosts — was way beyond what Jace could afford now.

Given Sorah's strain, he just had to grit his teeth and bear it.

The green dragon stretched, slowly flapped her wings, and lifted off. The whole time, she kept a close eye on Needlefang.

"Needlefang. Be careful."

Needlefang didn't answer, acting like she was still holding a grudge. But she did imitate Sorah, slowly spreading her black wings.

Jace gripped the small horn bud near her shoulder — the kind only young dragons had — and almost closed his eyes.

He'd braced himself for the black dragon to suddenly lunge into the air and throw him into the forest. But instead, Needlefang flapped her wings with surprising care, taking a long time to adjust to a very slow rhythm before tentatively leaving the ground.

Once she reached about the same height as Sorah, the green dragon relaxed and dipped her head. "Let's go."

Needlefang still didn't answer. But when the green dragon flew southeast, she obediently gave a few stronger flaps and followed.

"One last warning, warlock. *You're* the one who suggested I carry someone. If you can't hold on and fall off, don't blame my flying!" she warned in a low voice.

"Got it, got it." Jace said.

Though Jace had never watched Needlefang's flying technique from her back, he could tell those "stronger" flaps she'd just made were only strong relative to her takeoff.

Compared to her usual wild banking and darting through the sky, this was like the difference between a gentle pat and a slap that sent your teeth flying — especially for a young, hot-tempered black dragon.

After a while, Sorah started speeding up. Needlefang increased her pace a bit to match. But Jace could feel she wasn't trying to go fast — she was focused on stability and rhythm. A completely different dragon from the maniac who'd raced from Stormwind to Ironforge.

When Sorah got too fast, Needlefang even growled in complaint, telling the green dragon to slow down so she could keep up.

Though Needlefang didn't say a word for the rest of the trip, Jace could clearly see her sneaking glances at him and the tension she'd been holding the whole way.

But Jace was paying close attention to her emotional state, not just because he was worried she might suddenly dump him into the sea. There was a more important reason.

This was the Great Sea.

Other people might not know, but Jace knew perfectly well — this was the closest they'd come to the malice that could hurt Needlefang the most.

Whether it was luck, or Needlefang being too young and weak, or them not being close enough, or the thing in the deep still sleeping... the entire flight passed without Jace sensing anything wrong. No sudden shadows. No abnormal emotions from the black dragon.

In the end, the black dragon and her passenger made it through most of the day's tension without incident.

No matter how conservatively and steadily the two young dragons flew, they were still young dragons. Their speed was far beyond any gryphon, let alone a ship.

They'd left Southshore at dawn, and by evening they could see the coast of the Wetlands. The green and black dragons swept over marsh creeks, and you could even see the little huts built from waterweed, driftwood, and seaweed. Groups of baby murlocs went "wrlgbrlgl" as they scattered and hid, diving into the water. The war between the Alliance and the Horde ten years ago had clearly left deep psychological scars on the locals here too.

Continuing south, once they spotted the wrecked ships of orcs and humans run aground on the shore, they knew Menethil Harbor wasn't far.

Sorah landed on a relatively dry patch of ground. Needlefang came down beside a large tree. Her movements on landing were just as careful — furling her wings like she was in a china shop, afraid of breaking something with any part of her body.

"Thank you very much, Miss Needlefang." Jace jumped off her back. Only then did Needlefang seem to untie her limbs, shaking herself vigorously before shifting into the young girl's form amid a cloud of dust.

"You'd better be grateful." She turned around, dead serious. "Because you got lucky, Jace Sesso. Thanks to my incredible flying skills, you didn't end up in the ocean, becoming part of a spore whale's dinner."

"Azeroth doesn't have spore whales, Needlefang." Jace said. "We usually say 'shark bait.' Same idea."

"What's a shark?" Needlefang asked.

"One of several reasons not to jump into the ocean." Jace helped straighten the messy strands of hair on her forehead. "Your flying was really good. Especially for your first time carrying someone."

"First time?! There's not going to be a second time!" Needlefang slapped Jace's hand away. "From now on... no, from the moment we get back to Stormwind after this trip to Grim Batol, you're never getting on my back again! I have zero interest in this kind of flying!"

Then she turned and ran toward Sorah, shouting Mor'phil's and Sorah's names. Jace could only let out a long, relieved breath and smile at Sorah, who was watching him with concern from a distance.
