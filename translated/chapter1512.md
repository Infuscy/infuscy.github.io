## Chapter 1512: Brewfest in the Valley

In the forest valley near Grim Batol, the flags of Ironforge's golden ram on a silver-and-red field, Shadowforge City's burning black anvil on a grey field, and Aerie Peak's green hammer-and-gryphon on a green field all fluttered in the breeze.

Though they shared the same valley and the same stretch of woods, the three banners kept their distance from one another — as if even the night wind couldn't make them come anywhere close.

This was Brewfest night, and for a place already packed with dwarves, it was far too quiet. Not that it wasn't lively, but it was a far cry from the atmosphere Jace was used to in dwarven cities.

Jace sat on the Wildhammer side of camp, looking toward the Bronzebeard contingent. A group of bare-chested dwarves drank slowly, keeping one eye on their smaller cousins in cloth underlayers who hadn't even bothered to take off their heavy plate. And of course, they watched the black banners fluttering in the distant northern forest. Even from this far, Jace could catch glimpses of evil red eyes gleaming in the dark.

Don't forget — the Dark Iron dwarves over there were all members of the Thorium Brotherhood, not General Angerforge's Ironforge Brigade, not the slave-driver mercenaries of the Searing Gorge black iron mines, and not the Flamekeepers sworn to the Firelord. Even though the Thorium Brotherhood had openly rebelled against Shadowforge City, and their leaders wanted to reunite the Three Hammers like the other two clans, two centuries of estrangement wasn't easy to bridge.

"Those black-skinned, red-eyed, fire-bearded sewer rats... all crawled back into their holes!" Grede laughed loudly from nearby. "When they saw my lightning-wreathed hammer, my Gorjet that blots out the sky like a thundercloud, they really thought the storm god himself had come to punish them! That's when their Firelord, their Lord of Flame, their Great Inferno — it all became a joke!"

"I saw it clearly!" another gryphon rider said. "We hit that mine without warning. Grede the Thane led the charge, caught a watch tower completely off guard. Those Dark Iron dwarves were still half-asleep, and I could smell the burnt beer in their camp from up in the air. When they opened their eyes, they probably thought Blackrock Mountain had exploded again!"

"Just a bunch of rats. I thought they'd be braver under that fire demon's protection."

"Without that demon, those bastards are nothing but cowards."

Jace lifted his mug to his lips and said quietly behind it, "I hope the dwarves don't suddenly start fighting once we reach the Anvil of Kador. I've got a bad feeling about this. I hope their leaders have more sense than their men."

"If the Three Hammers' leaders had 'sense,' the War of the Three Hammers never would have started in the first place," Vereesa said quietly. "Maybe that's just their nature — fighting from underground to the surface, from mountains to beyond. The peace under Anvilmar was just an off-key note in the dwarves' long history of beating each other up."

"I must take exception to that, esteemed Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner." A rather hoarse dwarven voice came from behind them. It was a Bronzebeard dwarf with a beard golden enough to border on red, dressed in simple brown traveling clothes. He held a wide-brimmed sun hat and bowed to Vereesa, then looked at Jace. "Good evening, esteemed Royal Court Mage of Stormwind Kingdom, Master Jace Sesso."

Even though Jace could barely tell dwarves apart, the distinctive slightly upturned red bulbous nose of this one reminded him of King Magni, whom he'd met at Ironforge.

"Thane Brann Bronzebeard." Vereesa coughed and said, "I didn't know you were over here."

So this was Magni Bronzebeard's younger brother, Brann. Grede had mentioned in a letter that Brann would represent Bronzebeard at the Grim Batol gathering.

"Drink up, drink up!" "To gryphon spit!" "To everything gryphon!"

The sudden shouts from the nearby Wildhammers interrupted the greeting. They were still drinking and cursing the Dark Iron slavers of the Searing Gorge, and some even turned their barbs toward Ironforge.

"Those Bronzebeards would have the whole world believe they're helping the Wildhammer riders and gryphons attack the Searing Gorge, but all they gave us was some rock-hard sour bread that tasted like it'd been soaked in snow!"

"At least they gave us a few watch towers to rest in. That damn place froze my fingers off."

"Your fingers were already bitten off by a gryphon, Handar!"

"Freeze one off in Dun Morogh, burn one off in Shadowforge City — then we'll call you the Three-Hammer Hand!"

"A Three-Hammer Hand with only two fingers left. That's really something to brag about in every tavern, Handar!"

Laughter roared, and no one noticed Brann Bronzebeard and the two Bronzebeard guards following him approaching the camp. Taking advantage of a brief lull in the Wildhammers' shouting, Jace quickly said, "It's good to meet you, Thane Brann Bronzebeard."

"I can't remember the last time someone attached 'Thane' to my name," Brann said, putting on a serious tone. "I've spent most of my life traveling the world, visiting corners no one else has been to, talking to races who've never heard of kings or thanes or dwarves or elves. So I'm more used to being called 'Mud Man,' 'Red Mushroom,' 'Short-Legs,' things like that."

"But since I'm representing Ironforge here at Grim Batol, I suppose I should use my expertise to defend my people a little."

He looked at Vereesa. "At least since two thousand years ago, when the natives of Khaz Modan first met the human adventurers of Arathi, we've had a friendly and stable relationship. When those human adventurers founded Stormwind, we already had close trade with many human cities. And in those two thousand years, right up until the War of the Three Hammers, Ironforge never had any serious conflict with any human city. Internally, Ironforge also maintained relative peace between clans and groups."

"That's true," Jace said.

"I shouldn't have made judgments based on prejudice. My apologies, Thane Brann." Vereesa bowed her head slightly.

Brann shook his head. "It's nothing. The Three Hammers have been at odds for two hundred years. And by dwarven standards, you're practically young. It's natural to have such impressions."

He glanced at the Wildhammer dwarves clinking mugs in the distance. "Besides, the Lord of Ironforge is about to prove in the coming action that his desire for the Three Hammers to reunite isn't just lip service. Magni will personally lead soldiers into the Searing Gorge. And Muradin, my other brother, will fight in person just like he did in the last orc war. This time, he'll make up for what he missed — he'll personally lead soldiers into Blackrock Mountain."

"Muradin Bronzebeard, the Mountain King, is coming too?" Vereesa said. "I heard he's been exploring Northrend."

"Histories, legends, ancient ruins — they're certainly fascinating. But how could they compare to making history yourself?" Brann Bronzebeard said. "Help me pass that message to High Thane Kurdran over there. I think he'll look forward to seeing Muradin again."

"Also... Magni says you should enter Shadowforge City first to spread word that the Sulfuron Hammer has been forged. And while you're at it, tell them that Muradin and the Bronzebeard soldiers are coming to help. Magni thinks that'll be encouraging for those Shadowforge City residents who don't want to be slaves."

"We certainly will," Jace said.

"Now that I've passed on Magni's instructions, there's one more thing of my own." Brann cleared his throat, observed the arguing Wildhammers for a moment, then fixed his gaze on Vereesa and Jace, lowering his voice. "I hear from some Wildhammers that you visited a place called the Spires of Arak on Draenor, right?"

"Yes," Vereesa said. "Jace even went inside."

"Excellent." Brann rubbed his hands together. "Master Jace Sesso, would you have time in the next few days to accept my... interview? The Spires of Arak is the ogres' homeland. I've heard countless ogres describe that place — to them it sounds like a dream. But even many ogres have never set foot inside the Spires, and the two-headed ones who actually lived there aren't exactly easy to get along with."

When Jace first saw Grede's letter, he'd wondered why this explorer's society leader, who loved traveling the world, had suddenly taken such an interest in diplomatic affairs. Turns out the explorer's real goal was this.

"Of course, Thane Brann."

Jace said this, but the Wildhammers in the distance were getting louder, their voices drowning out the conversation again.

He looked toward the camp and noticed that the Wildhammers weren't just arguing among themselves — they were quarreling with another group of Bronzebeards nearby.

Brann looked flustered and hurried over.

"What kind of forest savage would like plain water?" "Still a thousand times better than goat piss!"

"Light beer? Wonder how much piss they mixed in?" "Ha! Haha!"

"What are you laughing so hard about, black-skin? Choked on too much coal dust?"

"None of our business, you little stump! Keep arguing — one likes piss, the other likes plain water..."

"Everyone knows the Darkfire Spirits you brew in Shadowforge City are just slagworm shit!"

"Go to hell, you yellow-haired brat! Your Thunder Malt is just a stolen Dark Iron beer recipe!" "Who hasn't tried your so-called Darkforge Ale... Is that even drinkable? Can't compare to the recipe from Kharanos!"

"Have you ever been to Shadowforge City? Have you ever tasted the authentic recipe there? Didn't the Ironforge Brigade soldiers skin you alive?"

"Authentic, authentic... who doesn't know what your 'authentic' is. We'd probably have to crawl under Dagran Thaurissan's Black Iron Throne and catch the drips from his beard to get a taste of the real thing!"

"Thaurissan? He's nothing! I'll deal with you today—" "Stop! Stop!" Grede shouted. "Today is Brewfest!"

"Brewfest is exactly the right time to deal with you bastards! You red-eyed little— I'll smash you with my hammer!"

"Wait!" Grede ran between the dwarves about to come to blows. Jace got up and followed. In the rage of these three groups, he felt like even Magni's brother would be as helpless as a pitiful lamb caught in the middle, let alone him and Grede.

And Kurdran Wildhammer showed no intention of calming things down. If anything, he was grinning and picking up an empty mug, looking ready to join the fight!

"I have a proposal!" Grede raised Kador's hammer high. "Let's show some respect for our ancestors—"

"Who gives a damn about your ancestors?!" a Thorium Brotherhood member grumbled.

Grede's eyes widened, and a few sparks flew from his hammer. Even the drunk, stuporous dwarves had to sober up a little.

"What, just because you can fight, because you're a Mountain King, you think you can judge whose beer is best among the Three Hammers?!" came a shout from somewhere among the Bronzebeards.

"Even a Mountain King can't judge that!" a Dark Iron roared back. For once, they'd found some strange common ground.

"I'm not judging!" Grede pointed at Jace. "We'll leave it to a fourth party. See this human? Let him judge!"

"Please, no," Jace muttered.

"Everyone knows Jace Sesso is your friend! This human will definitely favor Aerie Peak!" "No way! Change the judge!"

"Right, change the judge!" Jace raised his hand too, shouting in Dwarvish.

Some of the dwarves laughed, and the atmosphere eased from the earlier tension. Grede pointed toward the distant forest. "Then let's call the Red Dragonqueen!"

"That's probably not necessary..." a few Wildhammer dwarves muttered.

The scene went dead silent. No one seemed willing to go to the red dragons' territory and ask the queen to come judge beer. The awkwardness hung thick in the air.

"I have the perfect candidate," Jace said, standing up. "Not a dwarf, absolutely won't favor any of the Three Hammers, can hold her liquor, and has a taste for drink that even dwarves have to respect."

"Who? Who?" the dwarves looked around curiously. Brann shot Jace a skeptical glance.

"Vereesa Windrunner." Jace looked at the elf beside him.

"What? What?" The Ranger-Lord froze on the spot.

"Having an elf judge dwarven beer — that's insane!" a dwarf roared. "She's definitely not biased... she looks down on ALL dwarves equally!"

"Hey, that's true. At least that's not biased." "Huh?"

"Great idea."

"Let her drink! Let the Ranger-Lord drink!" "Ranger-Lord! Ranger-Lord!"

"I never thought I'd live to see the day the Three Hammers shouted my name at the same time. Jace Sesso, this is all your doing..." Vereesa said quietly amid the dwarves' roar. "So what do I do now?"

"Just what everyone expects. Don't favor anyone, go by your own taste," Jace said. "It's Brewfest, Vereesa... It should feel like Brewfest, right? Sincerity is what matters most."

"You said it." Vereesa adjusted her collar slightly.
