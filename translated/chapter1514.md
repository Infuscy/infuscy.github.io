## Chapter 1514: Sulfuron

"How are we supposed to pick that up?" a Wildhammer dwarf muttered under his breath.

As quiet as he'd tried to be, and as much as the constant rumbling of this underground fortress should have drowned him out, in the silence of the moment that abrupt mumble was loud enough for Jace to catch from where he stood.

Right. This Sulfuron warhammer looked like it had just been pulled fresh from the forge and beaten into shape. In that blinding brightness, Jace couldn't even make out its original form.

And unlike ordinary red-hot steel that hadn't been quenched yet, its material was the essence of countless flames and mountains erupting from the Molten Core itself -- the core of a Molten Giant, Sulfuron ingots. Even from this distance, Jace could feel the heat rolling off it in waves. Actually touching it was out of the question.

Its temperature... Jace knew that even with his flame resistance, far beyond what most people could manage, there was no way he was touching that thing.

If his own impression wasn't evidence enough, the look on the little black dragon beside him said it all.

Whenever a wave of heat from that glowing warhammer reached the steps where they stood, Needleflame couldn't help squinting. She'd been born in another "Obsidian Sanctum" that Deathwing had built in Draenor, with the Black Father's blood running through her veins -- and even she dreaded that fire.

The way the Thorium Brotherhood had placed it said everything too. The Sulfuron warhammer wasn't sitting on anything. The Brotherhood's mages had bound it in midair with Dark Iron runes and arcane energy. Easy to understand why -- there wasn't anything in this hall that could hold it for long without burning to ash.

Grand Smith Burnite looked at Grede, his eyes carrying both expectation and barely hidden anxiety. As the hammer's forger, he should know better than anyone just how hard it would be to pick that thing up.

"Trust the lad from Thunderfist Hall." Kurdran Wildhammer stood with his hands on his hips, speaking low.

"Anything to say?" Grede asked quietly.

"I can only say it's not too late to back out now," Jace said.

"You damn brat." Grede looked up, rolling his shoulders. "You say that and expect me to back out?"

"Whether you go or you back out," Jace said with a slight smile, "we'll support you either way."

"I hate to admit it, but... same here, Grede." Vereesa looked toward the center of the hall.

The dwarf fixed his gaze back on that blaze of fire, his smile fading. "Embarrassing myself in front of the leaders of three clans -- at least I could run to the ends of the world and hide, like I did ten years ago. But embarrassing myself in front of you and Jace? Feels like there's nowhere to run. After all, finding a gryphon faster than Solara isn't easy."

"Not 'isn't easy.' It's 'absolutely impossible.'" Solara emphasized, then added, "If you want to step back, there are plenty of other brave souls here to take your place. But I have to say, that hammer suits your temperament better."

"You finally said something nice, Lady Solara Shadowmoon." The dwarf nodded firmly. "That hammer does have an epic quality, just like me."

"No," Solara said. "It's that both have equally bad tempers and equally bumpy faces."

"What?" The dwarf ground his teeth in irritation, then looked at Needleflame beside him, who seemed to want to say something but held back. "I know you're planning to wish me luck, little one."

"If you get burned, Grede, we've got a very capable ice mage here who can help." Needleflame took Amy's hand. "Thanks to Lady Marin, I had ice-cold beer all day yesterday. Felt like I was back in Alterac."

"Pah!" Grede shot a glance at the helpless-looking Amy Marin. "This place is no longer fit for an upright and brave dwarf. I'm going in!"

"Then good luck to you, Thunderfist Lord." Morofel smiled.

"Much obliged." Grede bowed to the succubus.

"Two giant reptiles showing more goodwill than a demon. Guess the evil dragon legends of Khaz Modan aren't entirely made up!"

Muttering to himself, the dwarf walked down the steps, heading toward that blaze of fire under the eyes of two or three hundred dwarves of every clan, plus the red dragons guarding Grim Batol.

He didn't spare a single one of them a glance. Jace knew this wasn't heroic resolve or confidence. It was that Grede simply didn't dare respond to so much attention -- especially from so many leaders and elite warriors. The guy's skin was always this thin with outsiders. Better to just keep his head down and walk.

Down the steps, Grede's face was already flushed red by the flame. From the higher ground, he looked impossibly small and frail against that blazing light. Like another two steps forward and the savage heat would swallow him whole.

Jace felt Vereesa squeeze his hand. Her ice-blue eyes were already half-dyed red by the firelight.

The dwarf pulled off his shirt and tossed it aside, naked from the waist up. He glanced back at Jace and the others, then suddenly his form expanded. Lightning-wreathed tawny skin was replaced by a marble-like surface. Gasps rippled through the dwarves around them -- not many dwarves had seen anyone besides a Bronzebeard awaken the ancient bloodline and become an avatar of the ancestors.

Even Jace, who'd long grown used to his Avatar transformation, had to take a deep breath.

He still remembered the scene at the Dark Portal, when Grede had dropped from the sky and met the Pit Lord Azgoth head-on. Even now, the memory was still stunning enough. He hoped that power, that courage, would help him tame this Molten Hammer.

After transforming, Grede walked step by step up to the flame. The lightning that burst from his body and the fiery radiance of the warhammer contended and intertwined until he was completely swallowed by the blaze.

He reached out both arms and seized the hammer held by the Dark Iron runes. A violent burst of flame erupted, and the entire hall began to tremble. Dust fell from above. The Dark Iron runes flickered.

Some of the dwarves watching looked up; others stared into the firelight, trying to tell where Thunderfist ended and the hammer began -- because by now they were both the same fire. But no one panicked. No one tried to flee, even as the floor and pillars shook as violently as a major earthquake.

But Jace could feel that Grede's power still wasn't enough to make the fire elementals here yield. The blaze was on the verge of going out of control. He could even hear the dwarf's roar through the hall's rumbling.

"Does he need a... Fire Protection Potion, or something like that?" Amy asked hesitantly.

"What he needs is far more than a potion, Amy."

Jace walked down the steps and shouted toward the flames: "You're a student of the sages of Aerie Peak, Grede! You know how to speak to the elements! Remember that day at the top of Aerie Peak! Remember your father's hammer! Remember -- "

Before he could finish, a deafening blast knocked him flat on his ass. The Grand Smith nearby was thrown to the ground too. The dwarves in the front row were all sprawling every which way. Brann Bronzebeard, who'd been closest out of curiosity, went flying with a yelp, landing on top of several other Bronzebeards in a heap.

That sound wasn't the Sulfuron warhammer's fire. It was Grede's roar.

Jace didn't know if he'd heard Jace's words, but the dwarf was growing taller in coils of lightning, now looking a head taller than a sturdy orc.

Of course, his proportions were still those of a dwarf, so he was as solid and stocky as that statue Jace had seen outside the exhibition hall in Karazhan.

But that also meant the Sulfuron warhammer's flames could no longer swallow him.

"If I can't beat you, how am I supposed to deal with the real Ragnaros the Firelord?! You little butt-hammer... I'll fucking destroy you." Grede's elemental speech scraped like rocks tumbling from a height, but his voice grew clearer and stronger with each word.

"Well," Jace coughed in the choking volcanic smoke and murmured, "That's not exactly what I meant by 'talking to the elements'..."

But the flame's momentum was fading. Grede's figure abruptly towered higher. The firelight around them dimmed, no longer red-hot, but intermittently flashing white from strings of lightning.

In the struggle between thunder and fire, the balance had completely reversed. The earthen-body grip on the warhammer's handle gradually cooled, billowing thick smoke that forced the Thorium Brotherhood smiths, who'd barely gotten back to their feet, to stagger backward.

Through the smoke, Jace saw the fire rapidly peel away from the hammer's head. Before long it was gone without a trace. At the same time, the lightning gave one last flash -- nearly blinding Jace -- and vanished. The hall went pitch black.

Some mage, or one of the dragon guardians, cast a spell to light the hall's torches. Warm illumination returned. A figure black as soot walked slowly out of the smoke, holding a dark brown-black hammer.

Grede spat a mouthful of smoke, coughed, and raised the hammer high.

It looked almost exactly like the Sulfuron warhammer Jace knew. A plain, unadorned striking head with a few spike protrusions shaped like spines on a black dragon's back. The whole hammer, handle and all, wasn't large, but for a dwarf it was still better suited for two hands.

And from the look of it, considering the material, this weapon was probably heavy enough to be unbearable for most people. Jace certainly didn't have the confidence to lift it one-handed as easily as Grede did.

The dwarves were all stunned speechless by the elemental chaos. Seeing Grede successfully lift the hammer, they couldn't even form words. Then, from somewhere on the other side, a dwarf began singing an unfamiliar song. Jace's Dwarvish wasn't good enough to make out the lyrics clearly.

But soon, more and more Bronzebeards, Dark Irons, and Wildhammers joined the chorus. Even Brann and the Grand Smith sang along in rhythm. The older dwarves knew it well; the younger ones weren't as familiar and could mostly only follow the melody. But even an outsider who couldn't understand the words could feel the atmosphere.

Whatever the song was called, it was one all Three Hammers could sing.
