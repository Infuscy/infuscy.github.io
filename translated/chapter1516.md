## Chapter 1516: Hide the Lava Inside the Lava

In the middle of the night, the dwarves' ruckus was still going. Dwarves from every clan had mixed together, forgetting the differences in height, skin color, hair color, and eye color. They drank their own strong liquor, reminisced about their respective homelands, badmouthed each other's beer, mocked each other's homes, and laughed their heads off together.

Kurdran Wildhammer and Brann Bronzebeard talked about Draenor-related matters while keeping an eye on the soldiers they'd each brought, just in case things suddenly turned into a brawl. But given the current atmosphere, that probably wasn't likely anymore.

Jace's group sat in a circle around the Sulfuron warhammer, studying it. No one spoke. Then Grede suddenly looked at Vereesa and asked, "How'd it go?"

"How'd what go?"

"The dart competition."

"You're still worried about the dart competition? Your poor kinsmen, of course, were beaten one after another until they cried. What other outcome could there be?"

"That's because Kurdran Wildhammer and Muradin Bronzebeard didn't participate."

"Let's focus on how to get this hammer into Shadowforge City, Grede." Jace tentatively touched one of the "spines" on the thick hammer head. It felt cold, like ordinary metal, completely different from the blazing heat it had radiated near the Cadros Anvil.

"A few of my ideas were shot down by all of you." Grede said. "I don't have any good ideas now either."

"Just wrapping it up definitely won't work. The guards at the gate will inspect it." Jace said. "Don't forget, the Grand Smith said this warhammer is something of a household name in Shadowforge City. Since it's modeled after the Firelord's own hammer, naturally it looks very similar to what the Firelord holds in the reliefs around Shadowforge City. If someone loyal to Thaurissan sees it, we're in trouble."

"You mentioned magical concealment before, and that's also very risky, Grede." Amy said. "The elemental power in this hammer is too violent. Concealing its appearance would cost a huge amount of mana. And the Ironrage soldiers patrol with bloodhounds that are quite sensitive to magic. There are also warlocks with Felhounds in Shadowforge City..."

"But Felhounds can't detect your draconic shape-shifting." Jace looked at Solara. "Can you transform while carrying the Sulfuron warhammer and turn it into an ornament on your body? Like how you shrunk those huge ornaments from your horns into the pendants on your forehead and ears now."

At this idea, Grede and Vereesa, who'd been stuck, immediately brightened up.

"These ornaments all have similar natural power to me." Solara hesitated. "It's worth a try."

She walked up to the hammer and carefully touched the handle, just like Jace had. She was a bit worried about getting burned too. But once she confirmed it wasn't as hot as before, she picked the hammer up.

Solara looked delicate, but in reality she was strong enough to make Grede whistle through his beard in surprise. She held the hammer relatively easily, which gave the onlookers some confidence.

Then, in a swirl of pale pink illusion, Solara transformed into a Dark Iron dwarf. When the illusion fully faded, the hammer had disappeared. But the robe she was wearing was thick and heavy -- completely inappropriate for the season, like a padded coat, and covered in seemingly meaningless heavy metal ornaments.

Solara's expression wasn't great either. Her black face was all scrunched up. Before Jace could ask how she felt, her clothes inexplicably caught fire, bursting into flames and black smoke. A few nearby Bronzebeard dwarves, drunk and bleary-eyed, looked up with "Oi!" and "Whoa!" sounds. She had to immediately revert to her original form, drop the Sulfuron warhammer, and gasp for breath.

Amy's Arcane had already condensed a ball of frost energy. But as Solara returned to her elven form, the flames died out on their own.

More importantly, the source of the fire had left her body. The hammer's head had developed a faint glow, no longer looking like plain metal, but like a huge, dim reddish-brown amber.

"I... can't suppress its power." Solara watched the Sulfuron warhammer gradually return to its dark brown-black iron appearance. "I wonder if Lord Vaelastrasz has any better ideas?"

"He might have a better method, but he can't leave Blackrock Mountain. We'd have to take the hammer to him." Jace said. "On one hand, there's the risk of exposure. On the other, if he can't do it either, we'd have wasted the trip."

Then he turned to look at Morofel, leaning against a tree trunk behind them.

"You can't be serious, master." Morofel narrowed her eyes. "Even ignoring the difference between dark power and pure elemental heat, the size difference between the Book of Evil Names and the Sulfuron warhammer is way too big!"

It sounded like she was denouncing how absurd Jace's suggestion was, but the succubus's tone carried a strange excitement.

"Of course, I'm not refusing. Considering our goal is to get this hammer safely into the Dark Irons' lair, it's unlikely to succeed. But if you're trying to create some novel pleasure in our increasingly dull master-servant relationship -- I mean, with that hint of burnt-flesh madness and pain..."

"Alright, alright." Jace waved his hand. "That's enough."

"What the what?!" Both of Vereesa's arcane eyes were practically floating out of her head. "I'm right here, demon!"

"Mmm." Morofel bit her lip. "I couldn't help myself. No disrespect intended, Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

"I was planning to wait until after Brewfest to find a good opportunity to settle things between us, dear Lady Morofel." The ranger said. "But it seems you can't wait."

"That's correct. I've always accepted any martial challenge, darling." Morofel spread her wings and curled her lips. "Whether friendly or hostile..."

"Let's solve the immediate problem first." Jace grabbed Vereesa's wrist. "Then we can talk about winning and losing."

"Fine then. I obey my master." Morofel tilted her head and sat back down.

"Damn it." Vereesa gritted her teeth and swallowed her anger.

Amy had already wisely retreated several steps. Grede and Solara sat to the side, staring at the dirt and weeds under their feet, not daring to speak. The imp peeked out from behind Grede's ass, looked around blankly, then hid back. Everyone looked like the world had just skirted the edge of doomsday.

"Could Archmage Vargoth help us?" Vereesa cleared her throat, trying to get her tone back to normal discussion-mode. "His expertise in illusion is something even Prince Kael'thas admires. Didn't you meet him at Violet Citadel a few days ago?"

"But that meeting was to ask for his help, and it's a rather sensitive matter." Jace sighed softly. "Master Vargoth is an upright mage. I believe he'd do everything he could to study this hammer for the fight against the Firelord. But he's also at a critical juncture right now... If worst comes to worst, after we get back to Stormwind, the mage who sent him the things I asked for should have arrived. I'll commission that mage to send a message. Hopefully it won't be too difficult for him, or take up too much of his time and energy."

Just then, Needleflame, who'd been silent, just staring at the Sulfuron warhammer, spoke up. "Why don't I give it a try?"

"Little one? Are you sure?" Sazeno said. "Even the green dragon lady, who's so much bigger than you, couldn't do it!"

"Shut up! You're the little one!" Needleflame snapped.

"Ouch! I don't know how much older I am than you, you ungrateful little..." The imp squeaked, but Jace grabbed it and stuffed it back into his bag.

Unlike Sazeno, no one else present immediately objected -- including Solara. In fact, Jace thought Needleflame might actually be better suited than Solara for hiding the Sulfuron warhammer through transformation. After all, this hammer was born from the heart of a mountain and the core of molten lava -- power almost entirely fused with the black dragon's domain.

It was just that since Needleflame hadn't volunteered, nobody had wanted to burden the newcomer with such a troublesome task.

Until now, Jace had always felt that bringing Needleflame along to Shadowforge City was just to satisfy her desire to go out and fight with them.

"Give it a try, Needleflame." Vereesa said.

"If anything feels wrong, let go in time. Don't push it." Jace said.

"The idiot warlock who got himself burned to a crisp on Kil'jaeden's throne probably shouldn't be lecturing anyone about not pushing it."

Needleflame muttered as she walked up to the Sulfuron warhammer. That shut Jace up good.

She lifted the hammer with both hands, looking a bit strained. Grede unconsciously rolled his tongue in his mouth, seeming a bit nervous.

The little black dragon swept her gaze across everyone. Before she'd even lifted the hammer off the ground, she was enveloped in a puff of dust and smoke. When it settled, she'd become a Dark Iron dwarf. The hammer had vanished. The dark brown robe she usually wore had turned into a roughly matching set of armor in a similar color, also adorned with some strange obsidian-like ornaments.

Jace watched her nervously, making sure she wasn't burning up like Solara had. Then he asked, "How do you feel?"

"A bit tired." The Dark Iron girl caught her breath. "A bit heavy, but..."

"Does this count as me beating you, Sister Solara?" She flashed a row of white teeth at the green dragon beside her.

"You win, Needleflame." Solara smiled.
