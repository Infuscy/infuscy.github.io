Chapter 152: Distracted

The elf watched the dwarf's back as he stomped off, then turned around and let out a deep sigh. "My apologies. I've made a fool of myself in front of you, human. By the way — what's your name? We seem to have grown quite familiar without ever introducing ourselves."

"Jace Sesso."

"Ah, Sesso. That sounds like a surname drawn from the old Tirisfal tongue. So you're Lordaeronian. What brought you all the way to Stormwind to study magic? Trying to put as much distance between yourself and Quel'Thalas as possible, like that dwarf?" the elf asked with a smile.

"Nothing in particular," Jace said offhandedly. "Though if Quel'Thalas let outsiders in, I'd study there in a heartbeat."

"You think there are no outsiders there right now?" the elf said meaningfully.

Judging by the elf's none-too-pleased expression, Jace could guess who he meant.

The first human ranger — the one who would later become the "Withering Blight," loyal Dark Lady's Forsaken Ranger-General — was probably training in Quel'Thalas right now. And the organization training him was the Farstriders. This human's presence had stirred up discontent across all of Quel'Thalas, from the prince down to the lowest ranger.

Of course, right now he was still Nathanos Marris — a progressive young man bent on mastering the elves' arts so he could return to Lordaeron, train more fine warriors, serve the crown, and protect the people. Still a long way from becoming that undead minion who'd stop at nothing for Sylvanas's victory. The elf waved a hand. "Never mind all that. I am Kael'fal Moonwing. As I said before, I'm a member of the Farstriders. What happened that day in Duskwood — I'm truly sorry. I was acting on the Ranger-General's orders, tracking orcish movements in the area. I was also doing a friend a favor and gathering some special herbs. It never should have turned into a conflict."

"Moonwing? What's your relation to Halduron Moonwing?" Jace asked, curious.

Kael'fal said, "All I can confirm is that he's a relative of mine. As for the exact nature of our blood tie, I neither know nor care to find out. You seem to know quite a bit about the Farstriders, though. Is Captain Halduron famous in your world?"

"I'm not sure," Jace said. "I've just heard the name mentioned. He's quite the ranger, after all."

Kael'fal nodded. "That much is beyond question."

Jace had brought up the black dragon business hoping to fish for information about the Eye of Dalaran. Somehow the conversation had veered off toward the Farstriders instead.

He tried to steer it back. "Speaking of the black dragons… I wanted to ask — has Dalaran been attacked recently?"

"You mean by black dragons?" Kael'fal asked.

"Anyone," Jace said.

"Protected by the Kirin Tor's magic, this city hasn't suffered any real harm in a long time. Even during the Orc Wars, when the Horde tried to attack here, it all came to nothing." Kael'fal shook his head with a smile. "Unless someone can muster another army on the scale of the one the Horde sent against Quel'Thalas, nobody would dare make a move on this place."

Jace rested his chin on his hand as Kael'fal described Dalaran's formidable — if still slightly inferior to Quel'Thalas's — magical defenses. He understood what this meant: Dalaran hadn't yet suffered Teron Gorefiend's raid. The Eye of Dalaran should still be sitting safely in the Kirin Tor's vault.

The Eye of Dalaran was the critical foundation of the city's entire magical defense at this point. After it was stolen, the burden of sustaining the city's protective systems would fall on the ruler himself — the leader of the Kirin Tor, Archmage Antonidas.

But clearly, the strength of Antonidas, old as he was, couldn't come close to matching the Eye of Dalaran, that dedicated arcane-focus artifact. From that point on, the holy city of magic would be hanging by a thread.

Kael'fal went into considerable detail, and Jace listened just as closely — but at no point did Kael'fal so much as mention the Eye of Dalaran.

Which meant that while this elven ranger had a general grasp of how Dalaran's outer defenses worked, he had no idea what their actual source of power was.

That gave Jace a headache. Should a mage apprentice all the way out in Stormwind, a thousand miles away, even know the Eye of Dalaran existed?

That letter had mentioned so-called "three relics," but hadn't specified what they were. He couldn't very well march up to some Kirin Tor mage waving that letter and saying, "One of these three holy relics is the Eye of Dalaran," could he? Teron Gorefiend only knew where these relics were because he'd been Gul'dan's most trusted lieutenant.

Originally, Gul'dan had wanted him to track down those three relics — among many other treasures — to help the Horde seize Quel'Thalas, a place guarded by layer upon layer of magical wards. But Teron hadn't made much progress before Gul'dan fell from power, conceded the Horde's defeat, and turned to hunting down what he prized most of all — the Tomb of Sargeras.

And the leads on all these treasures had come from Medivh — or rather, Sargeras, who had possessed Medivh — the whole point being to throw all of Azeroth into chaos.

How on earth would Jace be privy to a secret known only to Medivh, Sargeras, and the upper echelons of the Kirin Tor?

The moment word got out, wouldn't he be dragged off and interrogated as a demon sympathizer?

The Book of Medivh must have been taken already. At this point in time, the death knights should have set out for the Scepter of Sargeras — they might have already wrested it from the demons and been on their way back.

Once Teron Gorefiend personally stole the Eye of Dalaran, he'd use the black dragons' aid to race south and link up with the death knight company carrying the Scepter of Sargeras, then cross back through the Dark Portal into Draenor together with his followers scattered across the world.

So how was he supposed to let Dalaran know about this?

Jace couldn't think of a suitable approach right off. But whatever he did, at the very least he needed to avoid running into Teron Gorefiend before he made it back to Stormwind — the man's staff was still in his possession, after all.

Right in the middle of worrying about the staff, a strange, unfamiliar presence suddenly drew near. It was nothing like the cloying sweetness of ale in the tavern or the rich dairy smell of bread and pastries — this was lighter than perfume.

Lost in thought, Jace didn't notice the elven woman until she leaned down right in front of his nose.

She whispered something to Kael'fal. Jace sat up and made room, taking the chance to size her up.

The woman wore a silver cloak lined in green, its hood completely hiding her profile. Her footsteps made not the slightest sound on the tile floor — Jace hadn't sensed anyone approaching at all.

Kael'fal replied in Thalassian. The woman said something else in a low voice in Thalassian, apparently only then noticing there was someone else sitting there.

She looked at Jace, lifted the edge of her hood, and said in stiff, heavily accented Common, "Forgive the interruption to your conversation, human."

"No problem, go ahead."

Jace got a proper look at her face.

Compared to the striking, almost aggressive exotic beauty of most quel'dorei women, the curves of her cheeks were softer — because she was clearly young. Even Jace, a human, could tell that much.

She was thin. The hood and cloak hung off her like they'd been draped over a narrow coat rack. Pale strands of hair slipped out from the faintly flushed nape of her neck. If it were long, it would have been tied up in a bun. Jace realized he had never been this close to an elf before.

Even now, he still wasn't quite used to the slightly different placement of features on an elf's face compared to a human's. But if he forced himself to set aside that sense of the alien and judged her by elven standards, she could only be called pretty.

Kael'fal offered Jace an apologetic smile, then said something to her in Thalassian. She shot back a few irritated words, gave Jace a polite nod, and turned to hurry away.

Jace watched her back until she vanished through the door, then realized he'd been staring a beat too long and quickly looked away.

That indescribable, hard-to-place scent still hung in the air, fading slowly as the woman departed.

"What were you two talking about?" he couldn't help but ask.

"Ah, I nearly forgot you don't speak Thalassian."

When Kael'fal switched back to Common, his accent was suddenly much thicker than before.

"We were originally supposed to set out for Silverpine Forest this evening on a mission. I was planning to move it to the afternoon. She's something of a student of mine these days, and she wasn't happy with my decision. She thinks the orcs hiding there only move around at night — that going in the afternoon would be a wasted trip. And of course, she's right."

"The black dragon we were tracking is already dead. You and the dwarf know that better than anyone… so for now there's nothing but the odd unimportant patrol. It's just that a few instructors and I are responsible for leading this batch of recruits, so there's a small evaluative element to it. She wants results. But given the current climate, there's no need to be overly active on your soil. I'd rather not antagonize both the Sunstrider and Menethil courts just for a few filthy orc heads."

"So the Alliance of Lordaeron really isn't sustainable?"

Jace asked the question casually, because frankly it wasn't something he cared about. The elven-human alliance had never been all that serious to begin with. Sustainable or not, it was all the same. The threat of Teron Gorefiend was far more immediate than that.

"Unless there's another orc invasion — or something else, some catastrophic event none of us would ever want to see."

As he spoke, Kael'fal raised his eyebrows meaningfully and glanced over for a moment, as if he'd noticed that Jace wasn't really paying attention.

"You can tell there's something special about her too, can't you, Jace Sesso?"

"What's special…" Jace snapped back to attention, realizing Kael'fal meant the woman from before.

"Ah, so who is she?"

"You certainly wouldn't know this recruit's name." Kael'fal raised his eyebrows, looking as if he couldn't resist showing off.

"She's only a recruit in the sense that she still has to learn to become a proper Farstrider. But if it came to single combat, she'd have me beat hands down. She's the most outstanding and driven of the last batch of recruits — young and deadly, the full inheritance of her bloodline on display. In fact, one of her older sisters is my direct superior — the Ranger-General of Silvermoon I mentioned earlier."

"Her eldest sister's name — you're certain to know it." Kael'fal lowered his voice. "Alleria Windrunner."
