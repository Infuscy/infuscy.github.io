Chapter 1525: When Paths Cross, Might Makes Right

The terrain north of Blackrock Mountain in the Searing Gorge was rugged, dotted with standing blackened rock spires and isolated hills.

This had also dampened the shockwave from Ragnaros's great explosion, preventing it from spreading unimpeded across the flat Plains like it had in the Burning Steppes, all the way to the northern slopes of Lakeshire. From the Searing Gorge, stretching north from the Dark Iron Excavation Site to the valley below Dun Morogh, tall forests still remained — though the trees' living conditions were dire.

From a high vantage point here, looking north, the silhouettes of several iron towers at the southernmost end of the Dark Iron Excavation Site were visible. Looking south, the northern gate of Blackrock Mountain showed glimpses of stone pillars and faint outlines of long stairways, though the gate itself was likely hidden by part of the cliff.

But they hadn't planned on going through the front gate and dealing with orcs anyway. The two SI:7 agents led Jace's group through a charcoal-filled gully, avoiding orc patrols, with the route map in hand. Before long, the shouts, footsteps, and warg barks of Blackrock soldiers had faded entirely.

Before entering the gully, both agents drank frost potions to combat the heat — standard practice for SI:7 scouts operating in the Blackrock Mountain area.

Jace's body still carried the blessing of the Red Dragonqueen. The environment around Blackrock Mountain certainly wasn't nothing to him, but it wasn't unbearably hot either. Dwarves were naturally thick-skinned, and Grede had the blessing of his ancestral bloodline, so he managed. And Sora, protected by her dragon scales... she didn't even need mentioning.

But what rather surprised Jace was that Vereesa hadn't shown any signs of suffering during the journey either, nor uttered a single complaint.

Vereesa was the most efficient killer in the group, especially with Eranikus's Bow. As a combat-specialized ranger, her physical flexibility and strength were outstanding. But she still relied purely on her excellent physical condition to keep up with Jace's motley crew of monsters and oddballs on their adventures.

This ranger wasn't skilled at using magic to protect herself, nor had she received any external blessings. The jade earring Alexstrasza had given her was useless by the volcanic crater.

Back in Grim Batol, when they'd climbed the slopes away from the rifts Deathwing had torn open, the scorching air had been about the same as it was at the foot of Blackrock Mountain now. Back then, Vereesa had been focused entirely on dealing with the demonic form of Dragonmaw Warlord Nekros, but it was clear she'd been pushed to her physical limits by the heat.

Now, by contrast, only the hair plastered to her forehead beneath her hood seemed slightly damp. The flush on her face was so faint it was hard to tell if it was from the heat or just the naturally pinkish complexion of high elves.

Morophil, following behind Vereesa, observed the rolling black smoke atop Blackrock Mountain with complete indifference. She couldn't even be bothered to watch her footing like the others. Her black hooves had trampled countless fel-fire-scorched lands in the Twisting Nether. Blackrock Mountain was far from the worst.

As for Needleflame... compared to the lightly-loaded Sora, the little one was practically an Ironfury heavy infantryman.

She had a spiked heavy helm clamped over her head, thick chainmail over her body, a vicious-looking two-handed axe in her hand, and barbed-chain-wrapped combat boots dragging on her feet. Even Dark Iron dwarves seeing her in this getup near Blackrock Mountain would probably feel hot for her. But Needleflame walked without a word, not complaining, not even willing to let out a labored breath — determined to prove that carrying the Sulfuron Hammer into Shadowforge City was no big deal.

According to SI:7's intelligence, they were close to the Dark Iron dwarves' secret passage now and could run into a squad sent from the Dark Iron Excavation Site at any moment. It'd be best to hide the Sulfuron Hammer in advance, just in case.

They followed the two agents deeper into a ravine. The sky visible through the cracks above was constantly lit by the molten glow of Blackrock Mountain, making it impossible to tell the time.

Looking at his pocket watch, it was around two or three in the morning. The lead agent crouched by a broken boulder and pointed down. "Look there."

Jace followed his gaze. At the red edge of flowing golden lava, a black boulder stood. There was no visible cave entrance or secret passage.

After about two minutes, a faint shadow seemed to move beside the black rock.

It wasn't an orc — it was a topless Dark Iron dwarf holding a short broadsword.

"That one doesn't look like he wandered here by accident," Vereesa whispered. "Is he the passage guard?"

"Yes, Ranger-Lord," the agent said.

"Only one guard?" Grede scanned the area and the cliffs above. "Why not just take him out?"

"According to our intel from the Excavation Site, the Dark Iron dwarves have been trying to dig tunnels to connect with Shadowforge City around here in recent years. But once they suspect an orc or black dragon has noticed, they seal them up immediately," the agent said. "If this guard dies mysteriously, the passage will definitely be blocked. To give you all an extra exit if something goes wrong — so you don't have to deal with both orc troops and dwarven pursuers at the same time — we should try methods other than killing."

"Sora, can you—"

Before Jace could finish, the dwarf suddenly slumped backward against the rock, his head drooping to one side as if he'd dropped dead on the spot. But his round, gray beer belly was still rising and falling.

"Done." The magical light faded from Sora's eyes. "Let's go down!"

Needleflame's voice came muffled through her helm.

"We won't be entering Shadowforge City with you, Master Sesso," the other agent said. "But we'll stay nearby and keep an eye on things."

"Thank you both very much." Jace nodded, then followed Grede and Vereesa over the rocks and down into the low ground toward the guard.

The Dark Iron dwarf leaning against the black boulder had an oversized tankard that had tipped over beside him, its spilled contents and remaining liquid mostly evaporated.

"A secret passage guard drinks on duty?" Jace remarked. "Now even if he tells his buddies he saw Dark Iron dwarves magically hypnotize themselves, none of them would believe him."

"This is why I never drink before a mission," Vereesa murmured.

"Frail elves and humans are like that," Grede said. "But we're different. Strong drink sharpens a dwarf's senses, makes them more alert to danger. Maybe without that drink, Sora's spell would've knocked him out cold. But with the alcohol protecting him, he's just asleep."

"There is not a shred of logic in anything you just said," Vereesa said quietly.

"Well, dwarves are clearly different from us, Vereesa. Wildhammer dwarves especially so." Jace smiled and headed into the crack in the black rock ahead.

Grede nodded in agreement. Needleflame let out a smothered laugh-like sound before Grede realized the tone of what was said. He lifted his head like he wanted to argue but couldn't figure out how, so he just followed Vereesa and Jace.

Deeper into the crevice, they began to see chisel marks and the tunnel widened a bit. But if a Dark Iron squad came out from the other direction, there'd be nowhere to hide. At this point, Jace, Vereesa, and Grede still didn't look like Dark Iron dwarves — that would be trouble if they ran into anyone.

According to SI:7's intel, the late-night hours saw fewer people passing through, and the tunnel wasn't long. If they picked up the pace, they'd be deep inside Blackrock Mountain soon, where they could avoid Dark Iron patrols and look for Vaelastrasz.

They quickened their pace. But after a short sprint, they heard the unmistakable sound of iron boots scraping on stone — coming right toward them and growing louder.

What now? Were they really that unlucky?

Vereesa stopped in front and looked back at Jace. Before he could say anything, a voice in Dwarvish echoed from deeper in the tunnel.

"Hey! Someone up there? Move aside! This is important magical supplies for the Excavation Site reconstruction. Overseer Thorn Darkheart demands we deliver it day and night to the Great Forge on time! By the Firelord's name, if we're a minute late, I'll put the blame on you!"

So SI:7's intel wasn't wrong — it was just that the Dark Iron Excavation Site had been raided by Wildhammer dwarves and was being rebuilt with support from Shadowforge City. Which meant more supply traffic than usual.

"Can the jade pendant be used?" Jace asked quietly.

Vereesa looked at the dimly glowing pendant. "There's not even a blade of grass here, Jace, let alone a forest. It has no power to draw from."

"Then..." Jace frowned slightly. "Morophil, hide. Let them pass."

"Just let them pass?" Grede asked.

"Right now, the three of us are their slaves." Jace lowered his voice. "Sora, you do the talking."

"Me?" Sora cleared her throat. "Alright, I understand..."

The iron boots were getting closer. As Sora finished speaking, several Dark Iron dwarf soldiers in gray armor — carrying round shields, warhammers, or broadswords — emerged from the tunnel's depths. Behind them, two porters each carried two stacked iron crates.

"By the will of the Molten Flame, sister."

The leader, wearing a round nasal helm, greeted Sora — then looked up, noticed Jace and the others behind her, and flinched so hard his helmet went crooked.

"You let your slaves carry weapons?!" He straightened his helm.

"If orcs attack suddenly, I'd prefer my property to be able to defend itself rather than stand around like sheep waiting for the slaughter," Sora said calmly.

"You've got your reasons, but that's ill-considered, madam." The Dark Iron leader noticed the ranger at the back. "Oho, you've even captured an elven noble?"

"Get lost, you meddlesome fool!" Needleflame grabbed the leader by his helmet and slammed him against the wall. Dust and gravel rained down with a loud bang.

The leader stared at the eyes visible through Needleflame's helm slit, swallowed hard, and didn't dare talk back.

He glanced at Sora, who was watching him in silence, then quickly waved for his companions to keep moving and shuffled away.

The guy was clearly intimidated by the black dragon's strength. A slaver speaking with an ancient Ironforge accent, accompanied by a heavily-armored female axeman with terrifying brute strength as a personal guard — who'd want to stick their nose into that business?
