Chapter 1527: The Dark Iron Gate

From the exit of the secret passage to Shadowforge City itself, they still had a considerable distance to cover. Along the way, they could encounter all sorts of problems — cave-ins, tunneling worm attacks... The Burning Legion's years of warfare, the dwarves' and orcs' struggle over Blackrock Spire, and the back-and-forth over the Tomb of Sargeras have all impacted the roads around Shadowforge City. According to the city's residents, things were far less stable than before the Dark Portal.

Last time, Jace had been led by a Dark Iron patrol. This time, as a group of mysterious treasure merchants escorted by Dark Iron mercenaries, they obviously couldn't not know how to get to Shadowforge City.

Of course, for Jace, the route was indeed somewhat unfamiliar. The Dark Iron dwarves kept blocking one tunnel and digging another... they wandered around for ages feeling like they'd returned to the same place. But when they quietly followed patrols or miners, those groups unhesitatingly chose their paths at forks and pressed on, so Jace's group kept following.

"What a goddamn shithole," Grede muttered.

"Even in the Dockside district, I've never heard anyone pack two-thirds of every sentence with curses, Grede," Vereesa said.

"Hah." Grede laughed. "Hearing that complaint in your Dark Iron scumbag disguise is especially hilarious!"

"Shh." Jace warned. "Don't let those miners ahead hear a bunch of Dark Iron dwarves speaking Common."

Grede proudly craned his neck and leaned toward Vereesa to say in Dwarvish: "Bet you never thought you'd have to speak Dwarvish, little elf. Hope you practiced before we came."

"I don't need your reminders. I've been preparing." Vereesa answered in Dwarvish with a slight Shadowforge City accent. Grede nodded exaggeratedly, like a teacher checking homework.

"You're not much better, Grede." Jace turned his head. "Your Dwarvish has no Dark Iron flavor at all. Once we're inside, you should talk to strangers as little as us two, and let Sora handle it."

"What?" Grede's face went cold. He leaned in. "I know how Dark Iron dwarves talk — 'For the will of the Firelord! Charge those Wildhammer dwarves!' Listen — how's that?"

"Like a Wildhammer trying hard to imitate a Dark Iron," Sora said, merciless in her judgment.

"Uh. Well." Thunderfist looked back and forth hesitantly, then sighed. "Who bothers learning how cave-dwelling black rats a thousand miles away talk? Fine, fine. I'll just speak less Dwarvish."

"Stupid fake dwarf," Needleflame muttered under her breath.

"This is... completely backwards!" Grede whirled to glare at the black dragon. Just then, a Dark Iron patrol ahead called out "Firelord protect" as a greeting, and Grede had to swallow his anger and turn back to join the others in returning the false greeting.

Though the roads to Shadowforge City were more complicated than during Jace's last visit, at least they didn't encounter any cave beast attacks this time. And perhaps because so many new tunnels had been dug, there were far more people coming and going than before, giving them plenty of cover.

After traveling through these complex mine tunnels until dawn, the five little dwarves and two demons rounded a corner and finally saw the gate of Shadowforge City at the end of the wide road.

"By my ancestors..." Grede's jaw hung half-open.

The massive gate before them stood half-open, flames and smoke pulsing outward as if the city were burning fiercely.

"You've never been to Shadowforge City?" Jace asked.

"How could I have been?" Grede said.

"But you've been tossing out Shadowforge City jokes like you know the place inside out," Jace said.

"What, can't make fun of a place without having been there? Is that a law the Firelord passed?" Grede muttered under his breath. "But... damn. I didn't think Dark Iron dwarves could build a city this big this far underground. The outer gate of Blackrock Mountain was impressive enough."

"I'm not surprised at all. The greatest architects in dwarven history have been from the Dark Iron clan," Vereesa said as they walked.

"That's true." Grede quickened his step to match Jace's. "But shouldn't I be the one giving you that lecture, little elf?"

The inside of Shadowforge City glowed with a constant bloody firelight. Jace knew it came from Bael'Gar the molten giant, who guarded the Dark Iron thoroughfare.

The dwarves passing through the massive gate-crack moved like puffs of air Bael'Gar breathed in and out — agitated and angry, pushing and shoving, even cursing.

Everything blurred together — shouting, screaming, metal clashing, clanging footsteps mixing with the city's fiery steelworking din in a chaotic "performance."

Looking at the crowded mass and the yelling gate guards, it seemed impossible to ever squeeze through.

"Stick close to me." Jace looked back at his companions. Grede still seemed stunned by the sight of Shadowforge City's dark iron walls and massive gate. The reminder made him blink and nod.

Needleflame and Sora pushed through a few Dark Iron citizens to reach Vereesa and Jace. Thankfully, among the mages, flamewardens, warlocks, demons, and crowds of ragged miners and Ironfury soldiers, their group of variously-dressed mercenaries didn't stand out too much. Squeezing in together wasn't incongruous.

Their luck didn't last long. Just as they were about to maneuver through the gate-crack, another squad of Ironfury soldiers cut right in front of Jace's group, shoving them aside.

Another bunch of Dark Iron dwarves took advantage of the soldiers' path-clearing to push through, knocking Jace's group back several steps and undoing all their progress.

Needleflame yelled curses at every dwarf that rushed past. Grede muttered under his breath as he instinctively reached for his warhammer. Just then, a helmeted head popped out of a fiery window nearby and shouted down: "General Angerforge has ordered the gate temporarily closed!"

As the words landed, the massive stone door began to rumble shut. Dwarves cried out in surprise as they were forced back. Within moments, the backward pressure of the crowd reached Jace's group.

"What's going on?" Grede stumbled. "You think... it's got something to do with us?"

Jace and Vereesa could only shake their heads. Even standing steady was a struggle.

"When will it open again?" a nearby Dark Iron shrieked.

"I'm here on Lord Lathoric's orders! I have to see the Puppetmaster today!"

"Let's all charge in together!" some dwarf yelled from somewhere, and the crowd grew even more frenzied.

But no matter how frenzied the people trying to enter became, no one could fight the slowly closing door. One dwarf unlucky enough to be pushed to the edge of the road fell with a scream into the abyss.

Jace glanced back and noticed Ironfury soldiers arriving to restore order outside. But there were still lucky ones squeezing through the shrinking gap ahead.

"Morophil."

The succubus, who'd been keeping low in the shadows, nodded lightly. She easily pushed aside a few dwarves, slipped past others, and reached the front. The firelight in her eyes grew brighter, the demonic runes on her body flashing. Many Dark Iron dwarves noticed this obviously-troublesome sayad and made way on their own.

Shadowforge City allowed warlocks and demons to operate openly, and warlocks even held a certain status... But that didn't mean Dark Iron dwarves didn't know how dangerous demons were, or how evil and ruthless warlocks could be. Nobody wanted to pick a fight with a demon like that, let alone its master.

Even the mercenaries blocking the way scrambled aside when they smelled demonic aura behind them, abandoning their desperate attempts to push through the gate. It was the same for the common citizens. Just like that, Morophil cut through the dark, cold metal like a red-hot blade, opening a path for Jace's group.

Taking the opportunity, they charged forward, eyes locked on the shrinking gap, shoving desperately until they tumbled through alongside a few dwarven miners. Around them, some tried to pull friends or family in, others screamed to go back, terrified of being crushed. The bolder ones behind cursed and even started throwing punches...

Jace took stock of his group, confirming everyone had made it through. He'd barely exhaled in relief when Vereesa tapped his shoulder and pointed. Several Ironfury soldiers and a red-robed mage were approaching with two snarling, bloated bloodhounds.

"Halt, demon lady! Who is your master?"
