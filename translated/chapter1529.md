## Chapter 1529: The "Missionaries" of Shadowforge City

After entering Shadowforge City from the Dark Iron thoroughfare, navigating became much easier with road directions, buildings for reference, and the memory of his previous visit.

At this point, Jace realized that his past life's impressions of Shadowforge City and the whole of Blackrock Depths were almost entirely useless. This underground capital of the Dark Iron kingdom might be the place that differed most drastically from its game counterpart.

Jace led the group through a passage beside a great bridge, squeezing up stairways with crowds of Dark Iron dwarves to the upper levels.

Grede barely dared to speak the whole way, nervously watching every Dark Iron citizen who approached them. It seemed Jace's comment before entering — that his Dwarvish sounded least like a Dark Iron's — had genuinely wounded him.

Jace was rather glad he'd given that warning in the tunnel. Otherwise, with Grede's personality, dressed in dark skin and inside Shadowforge City, he'd have wanted to clash with every single Ironfury soldier.

"After seeing so many Thorium Brotherhood members in Grim Batol, I thought I was used to being surrounded by Dark Irons." At an unnoticed corner on the stairway landing, Grede finally seized a chance to speak, crowding close to Jace and Vereesa. "But now that I'm here, I realize I was way too young!"

"Remember, before the Dark Portal orcs came to the Hinterlands, every Wildhammer knew the Dark Irons were the most evil in the world... and Shadowforge City was a demon's nest. Which it basically is — though I'll admit it's a pretty impressive and majestic demon's nest."

"This is the downside of always sending Jace on disguise and infiltration missions," Sora said quietly. "He's been spending all his time in dangerous orc territory. In my book, compared to orcs, vicious satyrs, and the Burning Legion's endless demons, Dark Iron dwarves are practically the most normal mortals you'll meet."

"Alright, alright, I admit it." Grede scratched his smooth black forehead.

"Ha, finally someone understands my pain," Jace murmured. "When you see orcs, you just pull out your weapons and start hacking. But me—"

Vereesa eyed the stairs, bit her lip, and gently bumped him with her shoulder. "I always see you having plenty of fun with Morophil!"

"Who says so, Windrunner?" the succubus said with a helpless mutter. "It's hard to call it that enjoyable."

"What more do you want to enjoy, demon?"

Vereesa shot her a glare, then her eyes snapped back. Heavy footsteps sounded from the stairs above. The fake Dark Iron dwarves all fell silent. The noise on the stairwell dimmed a bit too.

"What is that? Are Dark Iron golems patrolling Shadowforge City now?" Jace asked.

"What's a Dark Iron golem?" Needleflame whispered.

"Something like an infernal," Jace said. "But driven by arcane or elemental power... Dark Iron dwarves are pretty good at making constructs. I don't think those golems can see through Vaelastrasz's disguise, though. No need to worry."

"No." Vereesa murmured. "That's definitely not a golem's footsteps."

"Have you seen Dark Iron golems outside?" Grede asked.

"I haven't seen Dark Iron golems, but I've seen tauren, Grede." Vereesa stared at Grede with her dark red eyes.

"You're saying that's a tauren? A tauren in Shadowforge City? There aren't even that many in Booty Bay!" Grede tried to keep his voice down, but his shock nearly shredded his throat.

"How could there be a tauren here?" Sora was startled too.

Only Jace showed no surprise on his face. It wasn't that he doubted Vereesa's judgment — it was that this city wasn't one hundred percent incapable of having tauren from Kalimdor. His past life memories weren't entirely useless after all.

"Have you lost what little perception you had left, little elf?" Grede seemed to want to lighten the mood, but the smile quickly faded from his face.

A tall, broad shadow stretched across the rough gray stone floor from a blazing brazier above. The lively, swaying movements of its body, and more importantly, the pair of spectacular horns on that shadow, proved Vereesa was right.

Now it wasn't just the fake Dark Irons who were stunned. The real Dark Iron dwarves on the stairs had stopped too. Amid increasingly heavy footsteps, a dun-colored tauren descended around the stairway corner.

His expression was blank — even by tauren standards, it was blank. The dwarves on the stairs parted for him, unable to stop staring. Clearly, this creature was a novelty even for Shadowforge City's residents. Notably, though, none of the Dark Iron dwarves shot him hostile looks.

Considering that the last time Jace had entered Shadowforge City as a human, many dwarven citizens had shown displeasure at his being an outsider or speaking Common — though most held their tongues because he was a warlock — the current residents regarded this tauren with more than just strange curiosity. There was actually... respect?

The only things that earned respect from Dark Iron dwarves here were Dark Iron nobles like Thaurissan, and the arena champions of Shadowforge City. This tauren clearly wasn't Thaurissan, nor could he be an arena champion, because non-Dark Iron pit fighters were captured slaves. Even if they won their freedom, they were expelled from Shadowforge City.

So, was he a flamewarden?

There was indeed a tauren flamewarden in the game, even a minor boss in the Temple of Thaurissan. But this tauren wasn't wearing the signature fiery robe of a flamewarden, had no fire elemental traits, and carried no elemental-communing staff or similar implement. Instead, he had a battle-axe slung over each shoulder and at his hip, like a foreign mercenary.

Another organization's name immediately surfaced in Jace's mind — one that, in the game, had deeply infiltrated Shadowforge City. But he couldn't make a snap judgment. This tauren wasn't wearing their signature purple robes. Was he just a mercenary, and Jace was overthinking?

"Twilight's Hammer," Vereesa breathed beside him, giving voice to the name that had been circling in his head, unable to land.

"How do you know?" Jace asked.

"Look behind his shoulder, Jace." Vereesa pointed at the tauren, who was just turning to descend the stairs. "See that eight-pointed star tattoo? If you look closely, you can see the hammer symbol in the central ring. It's a totem of the Twilight's Hammer clan. I learned about this during my Farstrider training. The Twilight's Hammer that conquered Stormwind were especially vicious, and this symbol was so different from other orcish totems that it left a deep impression."

Jace nodded. At this time, the Twilight's Hammer had just settled in Kalimdor and hadn't yet formed the more organized cult structure they'd have later — unified purple armor and robes, body-covering spikes, and so on. It made perfect sense they still retained many orc clan traditions.

"But that's an orc clan, Vereesa," Grede said.

"Clearly, they've started recruiting tauren now," Vereesa said. "Many humans from Alterac fought under the Blackrock clan's banner too. There are tauren who are naturally vicious and brutal, and would happily follow the bizarre and insane Cho'gall. Think about how Mosa's family died."

"Alright, I'm not trying to challenge you, but I remember their banner was a shining warhammer..." Grede glanced toward the stairs, but the tauren had already descended, his footsteps fading.

"I fought in the Orc Wars too. I've actually seen Twilight's Hammer orcs!"

"Yes, you're right," Vereesa said. "But that's the clan's war banner. The Twilight's Hammer 'missionaries' tattoo that strange, dark eight-pointed star on prominent parts of their bodies."

"Missionaries? Do you hear yourself?" Grede was even more shocked now. "This is Firelord territory. Everyone here fanatically worships the Firelord as the true god. How could they let an orc 'church' preach here? Look at how those Dark Irons looked at that tauren — they actually welcomed him!"

"I don't know," Vereesa said, shaking her head. "But that's what I was taught, Thunderfist. Unless you want to question the Farstriders' intelligence-gathering on the entire Horde..."

"Unless the Twilight's Hammer and the Firelord's followers worship the same god." Jace finished quietly. Everyone fell silent for a moment.

"You mean that story the Red Dragonqueen told you when she gave you gifts — about the iron chain around your neck?" Vereesa asked. "About the enslavement of the elements?"

"That story," Jace said. "A friend from the Dagger Society told me the Twilight's Hammer clan has been developing on another continent and recently showed signs of spreading here. It seems Shadowforge City is one of their targets."

Sora's brow was furrowed. Not just hers — everyone in the group had heard, to varying degrees, about the unspeakable entities behind the elemental lords, and about Cho'gall's bizarre faith that even his teacher Gul'dan found disturbing.

Needleflame's expression stiffened too. She sensed the conversation sliding toward the darkest malice buried in Azeroth — the thing Jace had mentioned before.

"Will this affect our plan?" Grede asked quietly.

"I don't know," Jace said. "But we'd better figure out what the Twilight's Hammer's situation is here first."

But thinking back to how the Dark Iron dwarves had looked at that tauren, Jace couldn't shake a sense of unease.
