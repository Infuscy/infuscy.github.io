## Chapter 153: Almost Forgot the Point of the Conversation

There was a smugness that practically radiated off Kael'fal as he talked about his student being one of the Windrunner family.

So that woman just now was Alleria and Sylvanas's younger sister — Windrunner?

Jace had to admit she did carry a certain raised-in-a-noble-house air about her. But if that was really Windrunner… something still felt missing.

But what exactly? Heroic presence?

Or a golden dragon portrait frame with a skull and a question-mark level tag?

He couldn't quite put his finger on it.

Still, going by what Kael'fal said, Windrunner right now was just a rookie itching to prove herself. Even within the Farstriders, she was nothing more than a rank-and-file member.

Even though everyone knew she carried the Windrunner family's aura, everyone knew she might accomplish great things someday — right now she was still just a girl everyone looked after.

Jace had always daydreamed about getting to know an elven woman or two, but now that he'd actually met one, that detached look in her eyes and the cool edge in her voice left him feeling… off.

He didn't know if it was just how she treated humans, or if she was like that with everyone.

Thinking about those debauched elven women Grede had mentioned, he couldn't help but raise his eyebrows. He was starting to suspect he was a bit of a Lord of the Dragons after all.

What he was more curious about right now was Kael'fal Moonwing himself. He seemed like a serious player — so why had this NPC never shown up in the game?

One of the future three leaders of Silvermoon, the future Ranger-General Halduron Moonwing, was his cousin. And here he was, tasked with training Windrunner. In ten-odd years' time, he'd very likely grow into a major figure in Silvermoon — or at least within the Farstriders.

So why was there absolutely no trace of him in the game?

Right — ten-odd years' time.

In ten-odd years, the Scourge would invade Quel'Thalas and destroy everything in its path. The quel'dorei would come within a hair's breadth of being wiped out. The only elves who survived would be those who hadn't been in Quel'Thalas, plus the tiniest fraction of the luckiest ones.

Even the Ranger-General of that era, the second of the three sisters, Sylvanas Windrunner, had been cut down and killed at the gates of Silvermoon — let alone her subordinates, elites like Kael'fal.

And all the others he'd come to know who never showed up in the game — Caro Dushan, even Grede…

"There's still a trace of Shadowflame's fel taint on you, Jace Sesso," Kael'fal said suddenly. "I'm not looking down on you — I know you were attacked by the black dragon. I'm just worried you might need further treatment."

"Really?" Jace sniffed himself. At that moment, Grede table-hopped his way over and sat down. "The ale here's not bad, but the meat portions are pathetic. What is it with this mage and his sweet tooth? Half the menu is pastries."

"There's none on him," Kael'fal said, looking at the dwarf. "Which is what puzzles me. I heard it was a Wildhammer dwarf who killed that black dragon — yet this human is covered in marks from the dragon's attacks, and you look as if nothing happened at all."

"The only reason I'm still sitting here being roasted every which way by you is thanks to this human kid," Grede plopped down and said irritably. "If he hadn't force-fed me a Shadow Protection Potion, I'd be having a nice quiet afternoon right now instead of listening to your nonsense."

The elf smiled. "So, dwarf — what's your name? I'm Kael'fal Moonwing. The two of us have already introduced ourselves."

"Moonwing?" Grede said. "The name's Grede. Grede Thunderfist. Well? Not exactly a step down from yours, is it?"

"Thunderfist." Kael'fal extended his hand. "A kinsman of mine was wounded in a battle against evil trolls and was saved by a Thunderfist gryphon rider. That is by no means a shoddy clan."

"Hah, an elven warrior brave enough to face down evil trolls is no coward either." Grede extended his hand too.

Two scions of their respective worlds' great houses shook hands, each taking the chance to show off their lineage, leaving only the son of a common family sitting to the side watching them both put on airs.

Dalaran had plenty of northern specialties — king salmon, frenzy fish, venison. These were all rare and pricey in the Kingdom of Stormwind.

Especially with the number of Quel'Thalas-style dishes on offer — local ingredients prepared in elven fashion, like the Lake Lordamere mussel stew simmered with a spice unique to the Eversong Forest, and even some dishes cooked with arcane-precise fire-element manipulation. All in all, it was steeped in the character of this city of magic.

Clearly because the elf was paying, Grede ordered a mountain of different dishes. By Jace's reckoning, there was absolutely no way they'd finish it all.

But seeing the dwarf already charging in at full speed, Jace couldn't very well let a free meal go to waste. Only the elf sat there slowly nibbling on a few pieces of what looked like the cheapest fruit crumb cookies, watching them wolf their food down.

"Jace Sesso." Kael'fal held up half a sweet biscuit and looked at Jace, who was clutching a venison haunch. "Do you know what happened to the soldiers who were burned by the black dragon at Alterac?"

Jace set down his venison. "What became of them? I heard a lot of them ended up disabled."

"Disabled is the good outcome," Kael'fal said. "Some of the humans had their minds damaged by the Shadow power — especially the archers defending the walls, who took the brunt of the black dragon's frontal assault. Many of the ones who didn't get away in time went mad, and there's still no cure. The worst cases are the ones who look fine on the surface — neglecting them only let their condition deteriorate past the point of no return. I'd suggest you get a proper examination before you leave Dalaran. Don't take it lightly."

"Why the sudden concern for him?" Grede asked, squinting. "What were you two talking about while I was gone?"

Kael'fal replied, "I don't want a hero who defeated a black dragon to be left with a lifelong regret. A human life may only be a few decades, but it's no less precious for that."

"That sounds terrifying," Jace said. "If anything feels off, I'll go get checked out."

Kael'fal nodded. Jace glanced at Grede and suddenly remembered one of the key reasons he'd come to Dalaran — finding a way to cure Grede's disability!

Wasn't there a perfect candidate sitting right here? An elf, no less — and a Farstrider ranger with extensive experience fighting trolls!

"I wanted to ask you something, Kael'fal Moonwing." Jace set down his fork. "Do you elves have cases where someone was poisoned by a troll bat and didn't get treatment in time, leaving lasting aftereffects?"
