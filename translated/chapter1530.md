## Chapter 1530: The Old Blacksmith's Dream

At the top of the stairs, on Shadowforge City's upper level, they weren't far from the Black Forge. Though every corner and thoroughfare in Shadowforge City was oppressive and gloomy — most citizens and soldiers either didn't speak at all or spoke only in mockery and insults — the familiar sounds of dwarven community life — lively shouting and the clink of mugs — returned as they approached the famous Black Forge.

Grede, who'd been struggling to adapt to Shadowforge City's atmosphere, finally cracked a smile. The tauren on the stairs and what he represented — a new variable in this journey — had made it hard for anyone to relax.

"Even up in Aerie Peak, I've heard a lot about the Black Forge. Now I finally get to see the real thing!" Grede said. "Hey, warlock, you've been to this tavern before. Is it true that they use succubi as barmaids, like the rumors say?"

"You'll see for yourself when you go in," Jace said. "But don't forget why we're here. Don't cause any trouble inside..."

"I have my limits, you bastard." Grede coughed and quickened his pace toward the tavern.

The Black Forge looked exactly as Jace remembered. A sour stench mixed with alcohol filled the dim hall, and filthy curses rang out endlessly.

"You say I've never done anything noteworthy in my life? Well, last time I had your grandmother on Sador's bridge — that counts as helping that crazy woman fulfill her dream of uniting two continents!"

Someone nearby rattled off an incomprehensible string of words that made even Grede exclaim: "By my ancestors, I need to remember that one."

Jace's attention wasn't on the Dark Iron dwarves, though. He'd been scanning for signs of the Twilight's Hammer the whole way. But since that tauren, there'd been no other members — not on the stairs, not on the way to the Black Forge, not even on the intermediate platforms.

"Welcome, honored guests!" A voice like a bird's chirped from behind the crowd. It was so distinctive that Jace recognized it immediately.

Miss Nammara pushed through several barely-standing drunk patrons with her signature demonic smile — a smile that vanished the moment she saw Morophil.

Her eyes swept across Jace's group of Dark Iron dwarves, then she studied Morophil's broad horns and wings. Frowning, she asked in Demonic: "Have we... met, lady?"

"If you say so, dear." Morophil reached out and brushed her eyebrow. "Now, aren't you going to seat us?"

"Yes, yes, madam." Nammara nodded quickly. The confident succubus of before was gone, replaced by what looked like a lowly imp serving an eredar master.

"Sulfuron Stout, miss! A whole round, right the hell now!"

Grede slapped the table excitedly. He had to admit, when the enthusiasm hit, his manner of speech actually sounded like a Dark Iron.

"Sulfuron Stout, coming right up, sir," Nammara said.

"Just a regular ale for me. Something to quench my thirst," Jace said.

"Same for me," Vereesa said.

"That'll be thirty-three silver, twenty copper, then," Nammara said.

"Thirty-three silver is enough for the warlock here to buy the materials to summon ten succubi!" Grede grumbled indignantly.

Morophil's eyes turned cold, boring into the dwarf. Grede caught her gaze from his peripheral vision and felt like he'd been stabbed — his neck froze solid.

"It's not... that simple," Jace said.

"Cough, right." Grede nodded. "Just one is enough. I mean... you can summon one, doesn't mean you have to."

Nammara nervously touched her own neck and muttered, "I know the price of Sulfuron Stout has been unreasonable lately, sir..."

"Ah, forget it!" Grede tossed a gold coin to Nammara. "Keep the change, demon lady!"

"Thank you kindly, handsome sir! But it might be a moment — we've a lot of patrons right now."

The demon's face flashed a brief smile before she cast a timid last glance at Morophil and hurried away, her hooves going *click-clack-click-clack* across the floor.

"You've ruined my fun of watching succubi, demon." Grede shrugged at Morophil in complaint, then said, "Well, the rest of this place is exactly how I imagined it!"

"First, tell us what Sulfuron Stout is," Vereesa said. "Are you sure you want to drink something that sounds that suspicious in a place this dangerous?"

"If I come here all this way and don't taste the legendary Sulfuron Stout, it wouldn't be a proper visit!" Grede looked at the little black dragon in dwarven disguise. "Aren't you curious to taste a drink named after a god's power, little one? Or are you scared?"

"Scared of what?" Needleflame licked her lips. "Even the name sounds strong!"

Vereesa sighed heavily and exchanged a pained look with Sora.

Jace waited until a mumbling, half-conscious patron staggered past behind them, then gave the two a meaningful glance and murmured, "Vereesa, you and Morophil stay with Sora and wait for the drinks. I'm taking Grede and Needleflame to the back."

"To see that...?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

The three Dark Iron dwarves slipped off their seats, checked where Nammara and the other goblin and dwarven servers were, and snuck toward the back storage room while the tavern was in chaos.

"Hard to believe the greatest blacksmith from Ironforge is holed up living in a place like this..." Grede muttered.

"Shh." Jace raised a finger and motioned for them to follow, leading them down the dark passage to the black iron door.

*Clang, clang.* He tapped the bottom of the doorframe with the demon-bone handle of his staff. "Are you awake, Master? We're here to talk business..."

"I don't recall anyone telling me about a visit today." The familiar hoarse voice came from inside. After a moment, the door creaked open. Old blacksmith Roktus propped himself in the doorway, studying the three dwarves, his gaze finally settling on Needleflame's helmet.

"Do I know you?"

"Yes, Master." Jace showed him the ring. "I'm Jace Sesso. This appearance is magical transformation."

"Jace Sesso." Roktus seemed to snap fully awake. He looked at the other two Dark Iron dwarves. "They're magically transformed too?"

"Yes." Jace pointed at the bearded one. "This is Grede Thunderfist."

"Lord Thunderfist." Roktus nodded respectfully.

"I'm curious how you know my name..." Grede said.

"There aren't many dwarves who haven't heard your name these days." Roktus turned to the female dwarf. "If you're here, it must be about the Sulfuron Hammer. And this is?"

"This is the one who helped us bring the Sulfuron Hammer here," Jace said.

"What do you mean — you've already brought it? Where?" Roktus looked around. "I thought something had gone wrong."

Needleflame transformed in a puff of dust and smoke. The old blacksmith glimpsed the coiled scales and horns in the haze, his voice dropping. "She's... a black dragon?"

"I trust you feel now how I felt when I first learned there were good Dark Iron dwarves," Grede said. "But without her help, we'd never have gotten that thing safely into Shadowforge City."

As a man over two hundred years old, Roktus accepted the idea of a black dragon helping them quickly enough, nodding solemnly.

As the smoke cleared, Needleflame's armor and helmet vanished, and in her hand appeared a heavy warhammer — so heavy she had to rest its head on the ground.

Inside Shadowforge City, the whole thing glowed with a dim firelight. The elements had stained the metal surface a brownish red.

"It was..." Roktus saw the hammer, and his black lips began to tremble. Then his legs gave out, and he collapsed against the doorframe. Jace caught him before the old dwarf could hit the ground.

"The Caldros Anvil actually succeeded... It's even more beautiful, more perfect than I imagined. Bohnett truly carried forward the forging methods the Thorium Brotherhood had guarded for a hundred years. This Sulfuron power..."

The old dwarf grabbed Jace's shoulders. "I don't know how to thank you. When the Thorium Brotherhood's warriors were cruelly executed by Isendios's dogs and their blood splattered on the Dark Iron Anvil, I thought it was all over."

He stared at the hammer's surface, his voice low. "But just having the hammer isn't enough. Even if we brought it to the Dark Iron thoroughfare, even to the Flame Temple to show the whole city, Dagran Thaurissan and those Firelord servants would find a way to smear it as a laughable, powerless imitation."

"Master Blacksmith Bohnett thought the same, Master." Grede said. "He thought I'd have to use it to prove its power to everyone — by smashing the head of one of the Firelord's faithful servants right in front of them. Bohnett called those monsters... flamewalkers."

"That's an excellent idea. Flamewalkers... they represent the Firelord's authority." Roktus wiped his eyes with his arm, a hint of ferocity creeping into his voice. Now that he saw a chance for vengeance against the false god, the old man finally revealed a trace of Dark Iron nature.
