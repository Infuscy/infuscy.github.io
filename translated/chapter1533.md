## Chapter 1533: The Wizard of Shadowforge City

Following the address Lokthos had given him, Jace crossed several large bridges and arrived at the Hall of Magic deep within Shadowforge City.

The Hall of Magic was a fairly massive area, equivalent to Stormwind's Mage Quarter. But unlike Dalaran or the Wizard Sanctum, the Hall of Magic wasn't home only to arcane-using mages. In the Dark Iron dwarves' view, arcane magic, shadow magic, fel magic, natural forces, and elemental energies besides fire were all part of "magic." And the wizards of Shadowforge City weren't simply arcanists like those in the Kirin Tor — they were casters who commanded multiple schools of spellcraft.

The Dark Iron warlock who had previously taught Jace how to summon his own servants was also considered a so-called Dark Iron wizard. But his status clearly couldn't compare with the one Jace was visiting today — Thaurissan's court mage, Kremhoud.

The address Lokthos had given pointed to a wizard's tower connecting the ground to the top of the vast cavern. Someone inside seemed to sense Jace's approach. The door at the bottom opened, and a dwarf walked out.

"Hey. Who are you, and what do you want?"

"Are you Master Kremhoud?" Jace asked in Dwarvish.

"I'm the steward of this tower." The dwarf walked closer. "Answer the question, boy."

"I've returned from outside Shadowforge City and have something to report to Master Kremhoud in person." Jace said. "It's important. He'll take it very seriously."

The dwarf squinted, sizing Jace up, then spat to the side. "I'm sure it's not as important as getting into a drunken bar brawl. Go back to the pub, you stinking piece of filth."

Jace didn't want to casually show Lokthos's Dark Iron ring to just anyone. So he gave Molotov a nudge through the soul link.

The dwarf servant had just turned to climb the steps when he suddenly found a Succubus demon with terrifying horns standing right in front of him. He froze in place.

"I have nothing to do with that miser Kremhoud, warlock. He just hired me to clean up. I get a pitiful fixed salary every week, and it's nowhere near enough to fight a demon queen like this... If you have some blood feud with old Kremhoud, don't count me in!"

He muttered this out in a trembling voice. Sazeno, perched on Jace's shoulder, yelled, "You stupid lump of rock, go back through that door and tell your boss an important guest has arrived! Is that so hard?!"

"Not hard, not hard, little lord. I'll go right now."

The dwarf scurried past the demon and ran up the stairs. Molotov glanced at him sideways, then smiled. "I'm already in love with this city, my master."

"Your love is as indiscriminate as ever." Jace said, walking up.

"Your tone when you say that is getting weaker and weaker, though." Molotov leaned closer.

Jace cleared his throat and didn't respond, walking straight through the door. The succubus followed, smiling.

The interior of the Shadowforge City wizard's tower didn't match the stereotypical dwarven architecture. It was quite spacious and bright. Jace felt that even at his human height, walking in here didn't feel constricting at all.

A hurried footstep sounded from above. A black-bearded dwarf in a black-grey robe ran down the stairs, pressing a sapphire circlet onto his shiny bald head. Jace noticed a ring on his hand engraved with the Violet Eye insignia.

The dwarf observed Jace and Molotov in the hall, then said, "Who are you, to barge into my tower like this?"

"Are you Kremhoud?"

"Yes."

"Good afternoon, Master Kremhoud." Jace held up the Dark Iron ring. "Lokthos Darkbargain sent me."

"Lokthos Darkbargain? I thought that old bastard had died in the Dark Iron Bar." The mage walked down the steps and looked at the ring. "That would explain why that place reeks so badly."

"But since he's still alive, he must have something quite unusual to tell me. Listen—"

"You can call me Slovakia." Jace said.

"Slovakia." The dwarf said. "That's not your real name."

"Kremhoud isn't your real name either." Jace said.

Kremhoud glanced at Molotov, snorted, and said, "Aegwynn, Antonidas, Krasus... Isn't a mysterious single name a kind of fashion for a wizard?"

"You're Kirin Tor?" Jace asked.

"Was." The dwarf mage pointed at a nearby chair and sat down across from him. "Does that have anything to do with what Darkbargain wants to tell me?"

"I'm not sure yet." Jace sat down. "Master Lokthos wants to know which nobles can participate at the very core of the ceremony being held at the bottom of Shadowforge City in forty days."

The mage rested his elbow on the table, propping his rough, black-bearded cheek in his fist, and said quietly, "No matter which nobles can go, it has nothing to do with him or me."

"The Sulfuron War Hammer is complete, wizard." Jace said.

"Ah, that's good news." Kremhoud laughed. "Did Ethrendios forge it on the Dark Iron Anvil for the Thorium Brotherhood?"

"No. On the Cardross Anvil." Jace said. "Have you heard what happened at Grim Batol? The Cardross Anvil, touched by Deathwing's flames, successfully forged the Sulfuron Ingot. Then the Lava Core and the Mountain's Heart poured energy into it, to the point that even the one who forged it could hardly get close afterward. The last person capable of picking it up will tame its heat, cool its fury, and complete the final step of the entire war hammer."

Kremhoud listened to Jace finish all of this, his eyes growing wider and wider. Even after the last words had died away, he didn't utter a single syllable for a long time.

After quite a while, when the room had become so quiet that the surging lava sounds from the deepest Hall of Magic could be heard, the dwarf mage finally spoke. "You're a warlock. But you know more about the Sulfuron War Hammer than most smiths."

"Because I witnessed its birth with my own eyes, and brought it into Shadowforge City with the one who wields it." Jace said. "It's in that filthy, stinking bar right now, Master Kremhoud. That's why Lokthos Darkbargain decided to send me to you."

"So it really has been forged. After two hundred years of bad luck, Lokthos Darkbargain has finally caught a break..." Kremhoud swallowed hard, drummed his fingers on the table for a moment, then stopped suddenly.

"So, the Thorium Brotherhood plans to move against Executus during the ceremony?" he said. "You want the list of nobles at the ceremony's core so you can find people to help you get close to the manager."

"Looks like you've already thought the whole thing through." Jace said.

"The legend of the Sulfuron War Hammer has been around for over a hundred years." Kremhoud said, displeased. "You're Dark Iron too, Slovakia. Blood-red eyes, coal-grey skin — Ragnaros's curse runs in your blood. So don't use that kind of distant tone with me. You know exactly what happens after this hammer is forged."

The mage stood up. "I do have a list here. It's three years old, but it won't have changed much from this year. The ones who were going to die already died in the war between Blackrock Spire and Shadowforge City. The ones left are holed up in the city — even a famine won't starve them out."

He went upstairs, and after a moment came back down with a bundle of scrolls, which he handed to Jace. "Here."

Jace looked over the names, then asked, "Any recommendations? Someone who likes to attend ceremonies with a retinue of servants?"

"Attend with servants?" Kremhoud laughed loudly. "Do you think those fire elementals would let some lowly slaves ascend to the manager's throne? The Dark Iron dwarves allowed near the Flamewalkers are all Shadowforge lords with real power, or the Seven Sages' students, the highest-ranked Firehandlers in Thaurissan's Temple... Even we're not trusted enough to go up there!"

Jace frowned. Did they really have to impersonate the nobles themselves at the ceremony? But as Vereesa said, that was far too risky.

Kremhoud noticed Jace's expression and asked, "So who's the champion who wields the Sulfuron War Hammer? Do I know them? Are they on this list? The most suitable Dark Iron warriors all died at Ethrendios's hands. I thought there was no one left in Shadowforge City capable of the task. I'm impressed you really forged that hammer. Genuinely impressed."

"The one who wields the hammer isn't from Shadowforge City." Jace said.

"Not from Shadowforge City?" Kremhoud's face turned cold. "Then who? The Mountain King Muradin Bronzebeard, or his brother King Magni? Or the wild chief of Aerie Peak?! How could that old fool Lokthos allow this? You should know — we'd rather let those worthless Anvilmar heirs retake the throne, start everything over, and turn two hundred years of the War of the Three Hammers into a complete joke, than let the shameless descendants of Cardross and Madoran step on our heads!"

"The one who wields the hammer isn't a shameless descendant of Cardross or Madoran, Master Kremhoud." Jace said. "It's the gryphon knight who took part in killing Deathwing and fought the black dragon princess Onyxia in single combat."

"You mean Grede of the Thunderfist clan." Kremhoud said blankly.

"Correct." Jace said.

"I heard that one is a rogue Wildhammer who left his clan. Why would he come to Shadowforge City?" the mage asked.

"Probably wanted to test the quality of the legendary hammer." Jace said. "Anyway, he's here."

Kremhoud glanced at the list in Jace's hand again. "So what are you going to do? Replace a few of these through some special warlock means?"

"We're still working out the plan." Jace shook his head. "How to infiltrate the ceremony, how to bring down Executus — nothing's decided yet. I don't even know if the Sulfuron War Hammer can do the job."

"What did you say?!" Kremhoud's beard bristled. He snatched the scroll from Jace's hand. "I don't understand. If the Thorium Brotherhood sent a warlock like you to witness the Sulfuron War Hammer's birth, to bring that hammer into this city, they must trust you implicitly. They've put their lives in your hands, you demon-worshipper!"

"And what do I see? I see nothing but doubt and uncertainty in your eyes, over and over again. What's wrong with you? You don't believe this hammer can challenge the Firelord? Then why did you come this far? Or are you just in it for the money, or to trade for some depraved secret that old three-hundred-year coot is hoarding?"

"The Thorium Brotherhood, Lokthos, even you — all of you believe the Sulfuron War Hammer can change everything." Jace said. "But honestly, after seeing the Ironwarden soldiers' fanatical devotion to the Firelord in Shadowforge City, I still have my doubts."

"Fine, I understand." Kremhoud said. "You look maybe thirty or forty, at most still a clueless brat, led around by the nose by shadow and fel like those wretches below the Hall of Magic, your brain sucked dry by a succubus... Of course you wouldn't believe what we elders believe."

"Isn't that a valid concern?" Jace said. "There are plenty like me in Shadowforge City. Dark Irons like Lokthos Darkbargain who've lived since the War of the Three Hammers can be counted on one hand. Even if that hammer can kill Executus, the Ironwarden troops, the golem legions... are you sure they'll wake up and turn from 'darkness to light' once the Firelord's deputy falls?"

"Even if we set aside my kind and their thinking for a moment," Kremhoud said, "the important thing is what Dagran Thaurissan himself thinks."
