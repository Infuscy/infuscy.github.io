## Chapter 1535: The Robe, Once More

For the Dark Iron dwarves living in sunless Shadowforge City, "night" didn't have much to do with darkness. But it was still the end of a busy day's work — patrolling, mining, tending mushrooms, or outsmarting tunnel worms was enough to wear anyone out. Rest and entertainment at this hour were precious, so the bar was still much livelier than during "daytime."

Jace glanced toward the staircase in the guest quarters, where the sound of stomping, shouting, and laughter drifted over. Molotov's calm mood, relayed through the soul link, told him no Dark Iron dwarves were skulking nearby. Nobody had gotten curious about their little gathering yet.

Once the demon had entered the room, Jace closed the door. He turned to face the group of Dark Iron dwarves in the room. "I talked to that wizard. I think posing as Twilight's Hammer members to enter the ceremony is a solid option, everyone."

"Disguise ourselves as... Twilight's Hammer?" Sora raised an eyebrow skeptically.

"The elementals are far less wary of the foreign Twilight's Hammer than they are of the local dwarves," Jace said. "That'll let us get into the ceremony more smoothly, even get closer to Executus. More importantly, we won't have to maintain a Dark Iron disguise the whole time. Everyone can show up looking the way they're most comfortable with."

"Can I be an orc?" Needle-Flame asked hopefully.

"No," Jace said flatly.

"Fine." Needle-Flame tilted her head in disappointment.

Vereesa let out a small sigh. "Even though I've crammed some Dark Iron dwarf language and culture, I still feel I'm far from a real Dark Iron. Thank goodness this city has no shortage of eccentric lunatics — at least my awkward disguise doesn't stand out too much. But it's hard to say whether playing a Dark Iron dwarf or a crazed dark-god worshipping cultist is harder."

"Just think back to when you were away from Quel'Thalas for weeks with no magic source, little elf," Grede said. "Even the most fanatical cultist would have a hard time matching that weird starving look you had."

"At least it's not as bad as some battlefield butcher who got burned senseless by Sulfuron's power and started talking nonsense." Vereesa said flatly.

Grede coughed violently. Sora asked, "So, do we need Lord Vaelastrasz's help this time?"

"No." Jace said. "According to that Dark Iron wizard, the Twilight's Hammer members in Shadowforge City already cover many races. Humans, gnomes, dwarves, tauren, even quel'dorei. You and Needle-Flame can just shapeshift into something suitable. Grede, me, and Vereesa just need to change clothes. Maybe Vereesa will need a bit of makeup to avoid being recognized by any kinsmen from Quel'Thalas — your face and your sisters' are far too recognizable."

"I'll keep that in mind," Vereesa said. "I have some confidence in how to not look like them."

"So your plan is, we first pose as foreign cultists and blend into the Twilight's Hammer already in the city?" Grede scratched his forehead. "I don't think it'll be that simple, Jace. Cults like them must have some kind of secret code, or maybe some really evil way of verifying each other. Like the Amani trolls — they identify suspicious members by chopping off their limbs. If an elf disguised by magic took that test, there's no way they'd regrow the chopped-off arm in time."

Sora nodded. "Even shapeshifting as powerful as Prince Kael'thas's couldn't cover up an eye damaged by Deathwing."

Vereesa frowned and glanced at Grede, as if already imagining what it felt like to have her hands chopped off by trolls.

Grede's concern made sense. Ragnaros was willing to trust Twilight's Hammer this much. It couldn't just be because of their blood-red eight-pointed stars. It had to be because they truly represented the will of the two-headed ogre Cho'gall, and even the Old Gods.

Going through the Ironwarden Legion's "security check" to enter Shadowforge City had been nerve-wracking enough. It was hard to imagine how chaotic it would be pretending to be Twilight's Hammer and submitting to the cultists' scrutiny.

"The Twilight's Hammer leader in Shadowforge City is a witch named Gestane," Jace said. "I plan to start with her directly. If we can take care of her, joining the Twilight's Hammer would be a lot easier, wouldn't it?"

"And how do you plan to 'take care of her'?" Grede tilted his head.

Sora and Needle-Flame also slanted their eyes over. The atmosphere suddenly turned a bit odd. Vereesa seemed to catch on too, pursing her lips as if she wanted to say something but wasn't sure she should.

"Where are your minds going?" Jace said. "You don't think I'm going to 'take care of her' that way, do you?"

"I'm not deliberately going there, Jace Sesso." Grede raised both hands in surrender. "But a wizard who managed to win over a big-shot Quel'Tharas noble? I don't think there's any doubt about your special 'appeal.'"

"Don't forget there's a succubus too, fatty!" Sazeno cackled twice, then looked at the indifferent-looking demon beside him.

"Ha, now you've walked right into that succubus's trap, little demon!" Grede pointed at the imp on the table. "Even Jace Sesso knows better than you what those emotion-playing demons are really like!"

"Is that so, Thunderfist? Is that so?" The little imp hopped around, staring at the succubus and muttering shrilly. "As if I don't know, huh?"

Vereesa glanced at the imp a couple of times, then looked at Jace questioningly. "Then... what exactly are you planning?"

"Using Molotov, of course." Jace looked at everyone around the table. "You don't think a female cult leader would choose a sudden romance over her idol, do you?"

"Gestane is a lesbian?" Grede sized up Molotov. "She likes women? Or can you turn into a male incubus?"

"I'm not a shapeshifter. I can't turn into a man, Thunderfist lord." Molotov licked her finger. "But that doesn't mean my charm magic doesn't work on women."

With that, she deliberately shifted her gaze toward Vereesa beside her. The dark-iron-disguised elf noticed the seductive look and slowly furrowed her thick black brows.

"Damn it, demon... All these years, our bond was just the result of your charm magic? I thought you really were a comrade worth trusting with my life!"

"So you admit you have feelings for me, Ranger-Lord?" Molotov extended her thin tongue slightly. "And, as a child of the Sunwell, you know this has nothing to do with any kind of magic."

"Thinner than spider silk, thinner than mist and dust — a thread of expendable sentiment, Lady Molotov." Vereesa narrowed her eyes slightly. "And even that little bit has now vanished completely, thanks to your insufferable arrogance!"

"You mean the kind of spider silk that can bind a black dragon so tight it can't break free, Vereesa Windrunner?" Molotov leaned close to the ranger.

Hearing that, Needle-Flame, who had been licking her lower lip and grinning as she watched the show, had her smile vanish instantly.

"Hey, weren't we talking about Twilight's Hammer?!"

"Yes — Twilight's Hammer." Jace tapped the table quickly with his index and middle fingers. "I'm saying I can find an opportunity to arrange a meeting with the Twilight's Hammer leader, scope out their situation, and see if Molotov can work her charm magic. We can still use our traveling merchant cover — say we're looking for a buyer for the robe."

"But your robe is a powerful demonic robe, Jace." Sora reminded him. "These followers of the deep don't like fel energy. They especially reject items touched by demon souls. I think Twilight's Hammer would be the same. She definitely won't want to buy it."

"As a Dark Iron dwarf, I wouldn't know that," Jace said. "Twilight's Hammer followers consider themselves servants and priests of the Dark Lord. They'd probably rather call themselves Void's servants, shadow priests... But Dagran Thaurissan's magical advisor called her a 'warlock' — a term usually used for Shadow Council members. That means the dwarves in Shadowforge City don't actually know about their rejection of demonic power. See? My robe is a shadow magic treasure. She's a shadow-manipulating caster. From a Dark Iron merchant's perspective, that's a perfect match."

"Makes sense, Jace." Sora smiled slightly.

"More importantly — who says she has to buy it?" Jace said. "This is my treasure. Letting her get a look at it will be enough."
