Chapter 1536: Gestane's Secret Means

With the next steps decided, Jace contacted the Dark Iron wizard Kremhoud again and asked him to introduce a magic-item merchant — him — to the Twilight Lord Gestane. He would say he had a special robe that greatly enhanced shadow magic and was looking for a knowledgeable buyer.

Things went smoother than Jace expected. Soon, Kremhoud's servant brought word to the Dark Iron Bar: Gestane agreed to meet.

Two days later, Jace brought Molotov to the agreed location, near the prison not far from the Ring of Law.

According to Kremhoud, this wasn't the Twilight's Hammer's base in Shadowforge City, but Gestane's "office." Here, she helped the Ironwarden Legion and the Firehandlers interrogate prisoners captured from Blackrock Spire and the Alliance.

Jace figured it was good that this wasn't the Twilight's Hammer base. Gestane would have less protection, making it easier for Molotov to take control.

Arriving at the spot Kremhoud had written down, Jace saw a large gate with a purple animal-hide banner hanging above it. On it was sewn a hammer symbol reflected in dark red light — crude and crude, like a fist in a pool of blood.

Under the banner stood a human guard. He wore a yellowed white sailor's vest and a bizarre spiked iron mask showing only his eyes. His arms were covered in horrific scars. On his shoulder was a blood-red spiked eight-pointed star. It was hard to imagine what this guy had been doing before joining Twilight's Hammer.

"You're the robe seller, Slovakia." The human doorman asked in broken Dwarvish, his voice hoarse.

"Yes, sir." Jace stood on his toes and patted the box Molotov was carrying. "I have an appointment with Lady Gestane today to show her the goods."

The doorman glanced at Molotov. "The demon can't enter."

"Think of her as a well-behaved, beautiful maid, sir. Just a bit red-skinned." Jace said. "And she helps me carry the merchandise..."

"The demon can't enter," the doorman repeated.

Jace gave him a cold look, then muttered in Demonic, "Go back for now."

"Yes, master." Molotov, disappointed, pushed the box into Jace's arms, turned, and walked away step by step.

"Can I go in now?" Jace asked.

The doorman didn't answer. He turned and walked through the gate. Jace quickly followed. Meanwhile, the distant Molotov had turned invisible and crept back, stealthily catching up with him.

This narrow, sinister corridor was entirely Dark Iron-made — dark brown brick, smooth walls and floor, square passages and doorways... But with braziers burning shadowfire all around, it always reminded Jace of Auchindoun.

From time to time, hoarse, beast-like howls echoed through the passage. A thick smell of blood and orc sweat quickly replaced the smoky fire smell Jace had almost gotten used to.

The doorman led him around a corner, past a few similarly bizarrely dressed Twilight thugs, to a small door. He tilted his head to indicate Jace should enter. Jace nodded and stepped into the cramped room.

Pale purple shadowfire with a hint of blue lit half the dark hall, casting half the face of a thin woman in the corner into pale light.

She was about forty years old. Her gauntness made her cheekbones prominent, giving her a somewhat harsh appearance.

She wore a pink-purple off-shoulder gown with a gold-and-black striped shoulder ornament. The shirt beneath her robe was covered in silver chains and gemstone decorations — completely mismatched with this pitch-black "dungeon" setting. She wouldn't have looked out of place appearing like this among the great archmages of Violet Citadel.

It wasn't just the clothes. Jace could tell from her every move that she'd likely been well-off since childhood. Before joining Twilight's Hammer, she was probably a noble, maybe a student at some mage academy.

"Lord Gestane." The doorman said in Common. "He's that Slovakia. The robe peddler Master Kremhoud introduced."

"Does he speak Common?" the woman asked.

"Of course." Jace said immediately. "I certainly speak Common, madam. Given our reputation around the world these days, I try to learn as many languages as possible, just in case someone takes my life before I can explain myself."

"How fortunate." The woman smiled slightly and sat down on an ornate carved chair nearby. She crossed one leg in soft leather boots over the other, adjusted her dress a little, and asked, "Where did you learn Common, Mr. Slovakia?"

"Hillsbrad, Tirisfal, Stratholme — many places. Learned by speaking." Jace smiled, placing the box on the table in front of her.

"You speak it very well. I can barely detect a dwarven accent." Gestane said.

"I was doing business in Lordaeron before the Dark Portal opened." Jace put his hand on the box lid. "I've probably spent more time north of the Thandol Span than in Shadowforge City. It's not strange that my Common is good. But spare me your compliments, and save your praise for the treasure I'm about to show you — because you'll soon find you've run out of good Common words!"

"Then open it and let me see." Gestane said.

Jace nodded, unlocked the box, and flipped open the lid. The shadowy light of the entire dark hall was instantly swallowed by a green-tinged demon-soul arcane glow. Gestane's expression shifted. The doorman beside her staggered back a step, leaning against the doorframe. The part of his face visible above the iron mask showed no eyelids — just eyeballs.

Jace had expected Gestane to angrily throw him out, or ask why he was selling a robe with such powerful demonic energy to a Twilight's Hammer member. But the witch said nothing. Instead, she leaned forward slightly to examine the item in the box more closely.

"Take it out, Mr. Slovakia." Gestane said quietly.

Jace nodded, lifted the robe from the box, and shook it open. The demonic face on the robe immediately let out a pained, deep, ethereal roar, rattling the chains attached to it with a jingling clatter. One of the two shadowfire braziers at the door went out.

Fortunately, it was a short robe. As a Dark Iron dwarf, Jace could hold it up high with both hands without it dragging on the floor.

"Remarkable!" Gestane drew her hands to her chest and clapped softly. She stood up and examined the robe from top to bottom. "Unparalleled... It's a work of art! Does it have a name, Mr. Slovakia?"

"Not yet." Jace smiled. "I was waiting for its first owner to name it personally."

"This robe was clearly made for a human or elf build." The witch asked, "And you brought it to Shadowforge City to find a buyer?"

"I imagine a wizard skilled in cross-school magic wouldn't find a robe's size to be a problem at all, madam! At least a few wizards in Shadowforge City have expressed interest in buying it..." Jace replied quickly. "But it was originally made for a human. I just purchased it."

Gestane stared at Jace for a long, silent moment, then said, "Pity. It's a robe made from a demon's soul. But without a demon soul's involvement, how could it have been shaped like this? Imagine — if I wore this treasure, made from the trampled corpses of countless demons, while listening to my master's voice and receiving instructions... Ah... Mm."

She took a few deep breaths, as if already lost in her own fantasy.

Jace could tell — this witch was in love with the robe's appearance. She and Erland Durmoor would have a lot of aesthetic common ground.

If it weren't for the last thing Gestane blurted out about what she liked, Jace would have thought Molotov had already charmed her.

Molotov was lingering at the door. Her invisibility hadn't been detected by the Twilight's Hammer members. But Jace could feel she hadn't used any mental magic.

And through the soul link, she seemed to be hinting at some reluctance. She didn't want to use her charm magic. But Jace couldn't get the specific reason from the vague impressions through their link.

"One hundred and eighty gold coins, Mr. Slovakia. What do you think?" Gestane asked.

"One hundred and eighty gold coins?!" Jace slammed the box shut with a bang. "You have no idea how this robe was made... witch!"

"One hundred and eighty gold wouldn't even cover the fabric, let alone the enchantments, special materials, and countless demon soul fragments! I don't want to say anything too harsh, but this is an insult to my magical knowledge and the hardships of my journey!"

"Then what's your price?" Gestane asked.

"Twelve hundred gold coins," Jace said.

"Are you sure?" Gestane asked.

"In Booty Bay, an elven wizard once offered eight hundred gold for it, Lady Gestane!" Jace exhaled deeply. "And I didn't sell it to him. I didn't even hesitate! Since then, from the cape of Stranglethorn to here, through the Kingdom of Stormwind and into Shadowforge City — I've spent who knows how many gold coins along the way!"

"You might have made that story up." The witch gave a cold, joking laugh. "Don't tell me these sacrificed demons were summoned by Jace Sesso, making the robe that valuable."

"I wouldn't know, madam. Maybe Medivh summoned them." Jace matched her sarcastic tone. "But anything below nine hundred gold is absolutely impossible. Since you don't recognize its value, forget it. I told you — you're not the only one in Shadowforge City who wants it."

With that, Jace turned to leave. The witch's cold voice came from behind.

"Nine hundred and fifty gold coins. I admit it's a rare and powerful wizard's treasure. But that's the highest I can go, Mr. Slovakia."

Jace turned back. "I can't decide on that price. I'll need to discuss it with my backer, gracious lady."

"Then I'll wait for your news, Mr. Slovakia." Gestane said.

Leaving the prison area and walking back toward the Dark Iron Bar, Jace found a less crowded passage and asked Molotov, "What happened in there? What was wrong?"

"She doesn't look like a formidable shadow manipulator, master." Molotov said quietly. "But I couldn't exert any influence on her at all. Her mind felt like it was protected by something unreachable. So I didn't act rashly."

Jace frowned and looked back toward the Ring of Law.
