## Chapter 1539: The Twilight's Hammer Embassy

Jace could tell that Molurus Blackparch wanted to use him to deal with Twilight's Hammer, to ease the pressure on the warlocks in Shadowforge City. But if the Dark Iron dwarf didn't have that little scheme in mind, he probably wouldn't have so readily given away the results of the local warlocks' hard investigation to an outside demon summoner.

Still, this warlock didn't know that while the Twilight's Hammer members looked crazy and chaotic — most of them not too bright either — and there were only a few dozen of them in Shadowforge City, their numbers were large in Kalimdor and growing. Before long, this continent too would gradually fall under their claws, just like Shadowforge City was now.

Even if they killed Gestane now, Cho'gall would just send another Twilight Lord to cooperate with Ragnaros. In other words, as long as they didn't remove the fire elementals' rule over this city, as long as they didn't drive Ragnaros back to his home, nothing would fundamentally change.

But fortunately, the Sulfuron War Hammer existed now. That made dealing with Gestane just one step in defeating the fire elementals.

Jace didn't plan to tell Molurus Blackparch about the Sulfuron's power. After all, he was a warlock too. Jace knew exactly what warlocks were like. As long as the warlocks of Shadowforge City could become part of the force that toppled Ragnaros's faith when the Sulfuron War Hammer killed Executus, that was enough.

After leaving Blackparch's place, Jace followed the spiral staircase up to the upper level of the Hall of Magic. He found Kremhoud's wizard tower at the far end of the street and waited there until evening, when the wizard finally showed up.

"Mr. Slovakia." Kremhoud called out from a distance, then climbed the steps and asked in Common with a thick accent, "How did your talk with Lord Gestane go?"

"Do you know about Gestane's magic gem scepter?" Jace asked.

"Huh?" Kremhoud's brows knitted together. "Weren't you supposed to be selling her something? How did it turn into her trying to sell you a treasure? Or did I write the letter of introduction backwards?"

"No. I heard Gestane might have a scepter with mental protection magic." Jace said.

"Ah, I remember now. That rumor does exist among the wizards." Kremhoud closed the door tight, sealed it with a spell, sat down, and sighed. "But there are a lot of rumors about Twilight's Hammer. This one isn't especially notable. A traveling witch having a small self-protection trinket isn't strange. Even if you're not familiar with witches, you must have heard old legends about witch hunters venturing into sorceresses' lairs. Though I've heard it's a magical yellow cat's eye gem that can block mental disturbance, not a scepter. Where did you hear this news?"

Jace nodded. So Molurus wasn't just making things up.

"Specifically, a scepter set with a magic gem. You're probably right — on a staff, the gem is what actually does the work."

The Dark Iron wizard wagged his finger with a smile. "Don't be so quick to judge. On the Guardian's staff Ateish, those sapphires are just decorative compared to the raven statue."

Jace smiled and stared at him for a moment. Kremhoud coughed into his fist. "Enough digressions. Is there anything I can help with regarding this item?"

"Where is the Twilight's Hammer embassy?" Jace asked.

Kremhoud gestured vaguely northward. "Keep going north. Leave the Hall of Magic and head to the higher area. You'll see a small abandoned temple carved out of the wall. It's got a Twilight's Hammer purple banner on it now."

"Is it far from here?" Jace asked.

"Not far." Kremhoud said. "You want to take a look? What are you planning?"

"I'll decide after I see it." Jace said. "Would you mind guiding me, Master Kremhoud?"

"Nothing else to do tonight anyway." The wizard stood and muttered to himself, "Just had dinner. A little walk is good for you."

They left the wizard tower and followed the increasingly narrow streets northward. As the various spell material shops, rune ink stores, and potion shops on both sides thinned out, a raised platform came into view ahead.

Kremhoud didn't lead Jace straight up the stairs to the platform. Instead, he went into a tailor shop next door.

The wizard greeted the owner, then led Jace up the shop's stairs to the fourth floor and out onto a terrace. From here, they had a clear view of the small temple carved into the stone wall on the raised platform. It looked a bit like the Thunderfist Hall in Aerie Peak, including the stern dwarf-head reliefs on both sides. But overall, it was much darker and more sinister.

A purple banner hung on the temple's stone wall — Jace had seen one like it by the prison. At the dark doorway of the small temple, he could see bean-sized dwarf soldiers standing in groups of three or four near the reliefs and stone pillars. There seemed to be Twilight's Hammer members among them too.

At this distance, Jace couldn't make out their clothes. But in one group of Dark Irons stood a hunched-over troll. Not far away, another group had a figure about the size of a human — could be human, could be elf.

In front of the temple door, two burning fire elemental guards stood. At this distance, if you weren't paying attention, you might mistake them for large braziers. Especially in Shadowforge City, a place full of braziers.

Unlike the dwarf patrols that occasionally moved around or even walked into nearby towers or buildings, those two fire elementals stood completely still.

"So you're planning to steal that thing from inside." Kremhoud leaned against the thick stone railing. "When I was very young, I took part in a mission to recover a primordial black diamond, Slovakia. Your eyes right now are exactly the same as our leader's when he stared at the Ogaz' hideout from a distance. I'm not trying to stop you from making money, or opposing you causing trouble for those Twilight's Hammer types. But aren't you worried this could interfere with your plan? Or is this part of the plan?"

"This is part of the plan," Jace said.

"How did it become part of the plan?" the wizard asked.

"Warlock tricks, Master Kremhoud." Jace said.

"Mental magic... Fine." The wizard glanced at the succubus behind him, apparently understanding.

Jace asked, "Do you think you can get past those guards and fire elementals into that temple, Molotov?"

Molotov looked up slightly, gazing at the small temple in the distance. Before she could say anything, the wizard rushed in. "Don't even bother. Those flame guards are there specifically to keep Shadowforge City's demons from sneaking in. More precisely, they're there for these succubi and the harder-to-spot imps. Of course they can detect invisible demons!"

"Not only that. That temple has other arrays inside. Multiple demon-binding arrays, shadow-disturbance arrays, and planar-lock runes — all designed to capture and restrain demons of various kinds. I don't doubt your servant is more powerful than most demons in Shadowforge City. But don't risk charging into that fully prepared demon trap! If they trace it back to you through your demon servant, I'll be finished too."

"How do you know the interior so well?" Jace asked.

"I am His Majesty Thaurissan's magical advisor, Mr. Slovakia." The wizard pulled his dark grey hood tighter. "I was one of the people in charge of that place's renovation. But after Twilight's Hammer moved in, I wasn't allowed inside anymore."

"And don't underestimate those Black Iron soldiers." He leaned on the railing, looking toward the temple. "They aren't Ironwarden soldiers. They're the elite guards under His Majesty Thaurissan's personal command. Many of them come from wizard families. One of them is my cousin. They have methods for detecting various types of invisibility magic too."

"From the temple's reconstruction to the elite security — His Majesty Thaurissan has been quite thorough in protecting Twilight's Hammer." Jace said quietly.

"The emperor wants to show loyalty to the Firelord and the Twilight's Hammer envoys, to reduce their suspicions." The wizard muttered. "I don't expect you to understand that. Stormwind isn't built on top of Molten Core."

"So, can His Majesty help us get these guards temporarily removed?" Jace asked.

"Ah?" Kremhoud stared at Jace with wide blood-red eyes. "I don't know how you got along with Varian Wrynn in Stormwind, but don't think because Dagran Thaurissan inherited his throne the same way, that he's some wet-behind-the-ears kid a magical advisor can push around!"

After his angry outburst, he was silent for a while, then muttered quietly, "Alright... I... I think I can find a reason to pull them away for a bit."

"How will you do it?" Jace asked.

"You're asking too many questions. I can only give you one chance." The Dark Iron wizard said. "When do you plan to move?"

"One chance?" Jace thought for a moment. "I plan to invite Gestane out to discuss business first, and make our move here at the same time. Once the gem is destroyed and she loses her protection, she won't be a problem anymore. So the exact timing depends on the Twilight Lord's availability."

"Fine. I understand. Keep me informed, then." The wizard said. "And those two fire elemental guards — I can't do anything about them. You'll have to handle it yourselves."

"Thank you very much." Jace said.

"What if you fail?" The wizard asked again. "I don't doubt you'll find a way to escape pursuit and get out. Even Deathwing couldn't kill you. But if Gestane figures out the whole thing, I won't be able to talk my way out of it!"

"Blame it on the warlocks." Jace said. "Or leave with us and go to the Kirin Tor."

"Damn it." The wizard muttered.

"Do you know Archmage Khadgar?" Jace said. "He's a friend of mine. He can look after you."

"Ah, what?" The wizard's red eyes suddenly cleared up considerably. "Are you serious... Ses... no, Master Slovakia. I'll leave it to you, then."
