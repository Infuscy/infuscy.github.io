Chapter 154: The Botanist

The moment Jace asked the question, Grede's chewing ground to a sudden halt.

"Yes," Kael'fal said. "And not a few. In the forests the Amani control, if you get poisoned by accident, how could treatment always be administered that promptly? Those aftereffects take a severe toll on combat ability. They impair vision — can even cause blindness. They affect the mind's ability to control the limbs. In short, without treatment, there's virtually no way to continue serving as a Farstrider."

"Is there a cure?" Jace asked. Grede looked up too, eyes fixed on Kael'fal.

Kael'fal said, "Since the days of the Kaldorei Empire, our ancestors fought the trolls. And even then the troll empire had already bred those venomous bats and serpents. In those days, the druids who followed the teachings of nature could channel the power of Nature and Life to heal what seemed like permanent damage, restoring the body to its original state."

"Druids?" Jace asked. "Quel'Thalas has druids?"

"No," Kael'fal said with absolute certainty.

"Then that's the same as there being no cure," Grede said impatiently.

"Why are you the one in such a hurry?" Kael'fal asked.

His gaze lingered on Grede a moment longer when he looked at him.

He glanced at the eye patch, then at Grede's hand. "Jace Sesso wouldn't happen to be talking about you, would he? Is your missing eye the result of bat venom?"

"What else?" Grede said. "You think one of you elves poked it out with those pointy ears of yours?"

"Stop obsessing over pointy ears, dwarf," Kael'fal said irritably. "We've never thought there was anything wrong with the shape of our ears. What kind of insult is that? It's like me calling you 'big beard' — would you feel insulted?"

That left Grede absolutely fuming. Finally he said, "I'll call them whatever I want. So what?"

"Suit yourself," Kael'fal said. "So — do you want your disability cured or not?"

"You just said you can't," Grede said.

"You said there were no druids who could help," Jace said.

"There are no druids," Kael'fal replied. "But the quel'dorei arcanists can mimic druidic magic and manipulate the power of Life. Since we command the Arc, we simply ask the Arc to solve everything. Do you know why the Eversong Forest and the Eastweald are separated by nothing more than a line of hills, yet feel like two entirely different worlds? The Sunwell isn't the only reason. Our arcanists and botanists have a far greater power to reshape nature than those old diehards. Curing your problem is simpler than resurrecting a dead tree, Master Dwarf."

"If we couldn't treat these aftereffects, a good third of the veteran rangers in the Farstriders would be combat-ineffective cripples. After all, we're not dwarves — wounded like that and still able to bludgeon a dragon whelp to death."

Right — botanists. How had Jace forgotten? Those blood elf botanists living in the Tempest Keep in Outland knew all sorts of Nature magic, practically indistinguishable from druidism.

If Kael'fal hadn't brought it up, Jace wouldn't have made the connection for quite some time.

Hearing him talk like that, Grede perked up immediately. Naturally, he didn't want to get this excited over news delivered by an elf, but the expression on his face was getting harder and harder to suppress.

He glanced at Jace, as if to say, "See? It's true! The elves really can do it!" — only to find Jace still staring at Kael'fal without any particular look of excitement.

"To see a botanist, you'd probably have to get into Silvermoon, wouldn't you?" Jace asked.

"Yes." Kael'fal shot the dwarf a look, then turned back to Jace. "That's the biggest problem. Even though these scholars are keenly interested in studying plants from outside Quel'Thalas, they have no interest in setting foot beyond the kingdom's borders. Getting them to leave the protection of the Ban'dinoriel and venture deep into the wilds outside is no easy matter."

"I'd go into your lands — I don't give a damn about smelling like elf!" Grede said at once.

Kael'fal looked at him and smiled. "Dream on."

"Why does everything have to be this hard," Grede said dejectedly.

"There must be scholars with eccentric personalities, surely?" Jace asked. "I mean, relative to the other ones who don't like to travel."

"There probably are," Kael'fal said. "But the botanists in Silvermoon are nobility among nobility. Even I have few chances to cross paths with them. And the other Farstriders wouldn't have much contact with them either, unless they were injured themselves. Take the friend who asked me to fetch herbs in Duskwood, for example — he's the servant of a botanist. You see? I'm still doing jobs for their servants."

Jace felt a flicker of helplessness. Setting aside how hard it would be to even reach an elven noble that a Farstrider instructor couldn't get near — even if they finally found a way to make contact, these elves who lived deep within Silvermoon were mostly traditional, conservative, and fiercely xenophobic. How on earth would you get one of them to so much as meet a dwarf, let alone help cure his disability?

But besides the elven route, nothing else seemed viable either. If there'd been another way, Grede would probably have known about it long ago.

Besides the elves, the ones who clashed with trolls most often were the Wildhammer dwarves — and dwarves had always fought poison by toughing it out. If they didn't have a good solution either, no one else would.

"That's enough for today, you two." Kael'fal stood and straightened his coat. "We should be on our way. I'll settle the bill. Take your time with the food. Until we meet again."

"Thank you, Master Kael'fal," Jace said.

"I haven't done anything."

Kael'fal gave a slight bow and walked briskly out of the tavern, leaving the two of them staring at each other in silence.

"Cruel bastard," Grede muttered. "Dangles hope right in front of you, then snatches it away with his own hands."

Jace picked up one of the biscuits the elf had been eating the whole time and popped it in his mouth. A faint sweetness, almost like a sugar substitute, with a whisper of fruit. "Take it slow, Grede. Don't rush."

He handed one to the dwarf. "These are actually pretty good."

"How am I supposed to not rush?" The dwarf took it, chewed, and let out a deep sigh. "We need to be back in Stormwind before January. That's a month."

Jace said, "A month… even if Quel'Thalas threw its gates wide open, the journey there would take ages. I think we can forget about it this trip."

Grede took another biscuit. "You're right. No chance at all. Let's talk about something else."

"Actually, I've got a pretty good topic." Jace licked his lips and gathered his thoughts. "Say there's a rich man who owns an extremely precious treasure in his house. He's never told anyone about it, and he can't allow anyone to know — because this treasure concerns his whole family's lives, their future, everything. Now there's a highly skilled thief who plans to break in and steal the treasure for himself. As an outsider who knows about the thief's plan in advance, how would you warn the rich man?"
