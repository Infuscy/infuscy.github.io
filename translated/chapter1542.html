Chapter 1542: Nest of Corruption

While the Flameguard was walking to the other side, Jace climbed straight up onto the platform and rushed through the temple door. The sight inside reminded him of the prison cell in the dream Deathwing had woven for him, where he'd watched Krasus being tortured... Blackrock Spire's architecture, the ancient building style of the Dark Iron dwarves.

He followed the trace of that wrong-feeling shadow fluctuation through the corridor with his mind, though it was hard to put into words what chaotic emotion the shadow was radiating.

Aside from the obvious anti-demon wards the Dark Iron dwarves had set up, strange, unfamiliar runes were everywhere.

Jace had never seen this kind of script before. If these were shadow-related runes, they didn't even have any real connection to the "Abyssal tongue" that the ethereals wrote in, let alone Nathrezim or demonic languages.

"Hey, dwarf, what are you doing here?"

A question in Dwarvish came from a side passage. Jace had barely looked that way when a faint dark green light silently drifted past his ear from behind, striking the speaker squarely in the neck. The man went silent immediately and crumpled at the base of the wall.

Vereesa caught up from behind and exchanged a glance with him. Another figure passed the entrance up ahead and was taken down by an arrow before they even saw them.

"Have you thought about how you're going to explain this mess in the embassy later, Jace?"

"We need to find Needleflame first." Jace looked at her.

Vereesa nodded and said nothing more. Jace stepped over the body of a Twilight's Hammer cultist and followed the abnormally fluctuating shadow to a descending staircase.

Down the stairs, he followed that perception into an even narrower corridor. A piercing screech rang in his ears — he didn't know if Vereesa could hear it too, but the noise was growing more and more maddening.

His pace quickened. The footsteps and shouts behind him grew fainter, until he rushed past cell-like dark rooms and, on instinct, pushed open a half-closed iron door.

Violet light suddenly filled his vision. A small figure was curled up before a black stone table, trembling. A yellow round gem was held up before her on a withered branch-like pedestal, trembling, trembling...

No... wait. That wasn't a cat's eye gem. It was a yellow eyeball.

Jace silently incanted the spell for Dark Ward, simultaneously drawing Ursales and tearing off its wrappings to reveal the cruel blood-red scythe. Ursales' roar thundered through his mind as he strode forward, raising the scythe high. But the eyeball suddenly twisted toward him, staring straight into his eyes!

"Jace—"

He ignored every sound around him, every emotion, every word, and brought the scythe down with all his might. But he struck nothing, stumbling forward from the excess force and tumbling into a patch of sand.

Jace scrambled up, searching for the eye, only to find himself no longer in Shadowforge City.

Before him was nothing but sand and bizarre rock formations. The shadows of high cliffs and peaks rose like spikes along some monster's spine, blades of—

You made a mistake.

Your goodwill harmed the one who trusted you most.

Jace grabbed a thorny, alien bush and pulled himself up, then ran into the maze of rocks following that shadow. The stone walls on either side narrowed like two massive mechanical walls closing in, making it harder and harder to breathe for whoever passed between them.

Fool.

Jace leaned against a wall, gasping for breath, then pressed on toward the deepest part at full speed.

Twisted shrubs and withered black ivy covered the stone walls, like the claws of some evil thing slowly blotting out the blood-red sky.

Wind and sand howled through the canyon, mixed with an even more unsettling, eerie shriek.

You let her down.

You delivered her to me with your own hands.

Black vines and thorns writhed, as if blown by the wind, yet somehow independent of it.

The wind grew stronger. Sand and dust blotted out everything, and those strange plants became mere shadows. Jace lowered his head and pressed forward recklessly, until he felt his foot sink into something like a muddy bog.

He pulled his leg up with effort, hearing a sound like stirred muck, followed by a putrid stench thick with the smell of blood. A black monstrosity rose from beneath his feet, letting out a hoarse, baby-like shriek.

It was a black whelpling covered in pustules, writhing in bloody water, not even fully formed.

Jace furrowed his brow and kept going, occasionally stepping on something soft, sometimes something sharp — a sharp pain shot up from under his feet...

You are weak.

Huge, bulging shapes had surrounded him without him noticing. Jace knew what they were. He'd seen something similar deep in Grim Batol, beside the Dragonqueen.

Dragon eggs. Black dragon eggs.

A colossal shadow swept across the sky, blotting out everything, followed by a scorched, fel-like stench. The smell was so sharp that for a moment Jace felt like he'd plunged straight into the Shadowmoon Valley.

"What have you done?" A tall figure beside him looked up, following the great shadow with his eyes, and spoke in a low voice. "I trusted you so much."

"She survived my father. She survived the corruption of the Skull of Gul'dan. And you brought her back here. All that effort, wasted."

The figure slowly lowered his head, turning his gaze to Jace. A pair of yellow eyes were perfectly clear through the sandstorm and blood-colored fog.

And not just this one pair. More and more eyes appeared up high, swaying, watching, their bloodshot veins clearly visible.

But Jace just stared ahead, weaving around broken and intact eggs, shaking off dark purple tendrils that tried to entangle him, enduring the pain under his feet as he followed that last thread of sensation that was almost being swallowed by the entire world.

He came to a cracked egg. The heartbeat inside pounded against his mind. Jace punched the crack in the shell, then punched again, and again, ignoring the jagged hardness of the black dragon egg, until he'd made a larger crack, letting out foul-smelling fluid.

He tore the shell open with all his strength. Dark yellow corrupted egg fluid flowed out and washed over him. Jace plunged straight in, grabbing the small, weak body inside — the girl, the child covered in blood, crying with her eyes closed.

"I listened to you, Jace Sesso."

"I... I didn't touch it. I was going to take it out and give it to you."

"Jace Sesso..."

"I'm here, Needleflame." Jace held her tight. "I'm right here."

"Jace Sesso... is it you? I can feel you, I can feel you, but I can't see you..."

"My neck is burning. It burns... it hurts so much. Did I do something wrong? Is this a punishment?"

"You didn't do anything wrong, Needleflame. Neither of us did anything wrong. So no one gets to punish us."

Jace turned his head toward the eyeball peering into the shell and said in a low voice, "Now it wants to come closer, wants to hurt us. And I'm going to, one by one..."

He picked up a shard of eggshell and drove it into the eye without hesitation. The eyeball trembled and sank back, its stalk thrashing wildly as it retreated outside the shell.

More tentacles grabbed at the shell, widening the crack. More and more eyes emerged from the blood-colored fog, pressing in, staring down at the two clinging together inside.

Blood rain fell from the sky. Dark yellow egg fluid rose like a flood, gradually drowning all the eggs. Jace held Needleflame with one hand and raised a sharp piece of black dragon shell with the other, fighting madly against the encirclement of eyes. One, two, three... None of them would get close.

He didn't know how long he fought. The blood from the cuts on his arm soaked his hand, the shell breaking more and more...

Is it guilt that keeps you going?

Suddenly, a yellow-headed bird flew down from somewhere and landed on the edge of the shell. It looked around, then tilted its head, peering down at the warlock and the girl inside, chirping a few times.

"..."

Jace barely got a syllable out before the foul yellow liquid washed over his head. He lifted Needleflame above the corrupted fluid with all his strength, but drawing a breath of fresh air was getting impossibly hard.

The quick bird shot away from the shell's edge. More eyes gathered, more tentacles burrowed into the fluid...

But before long, another gray bird landed. It flew straight onto an eye and pecked at it viciously. The eye trembled and withdrew. A barbed tentacle reached up to catch the bird, but a brown turtledove pounced, grabbing the tentacle with its claws and yanking it aside, letting the gray one escape.

Soon, more and more birds swooped in from every direction. They bravely pecked and stabbed at the eyes and tentacles, and for a moment the whole canyon was filled with sharp bird calls. But it didn't change the fact that the corrupted fluid was rising higher and higher, soon covering the egg. Jace had fewer and fewer chances to breathe.

He forced his eyes open. His vision was completely yellow. Everywhere he looked were floating dead eyes and broken tentacle parts...

Then a pale white hand, trailing a faint rainbow light, reached down from above. Jace grabbed it without hesitation. A strange, brute force yanked him up hard. The foul yellow liquid churned, the shadow's shrieks deafening, until his head smacked into something with a loud thump — and all the strangeness faded.

Everything was silent, save for the distant rumble of lava deep underground.

He was back in the room at the Twilight's Hammer embassy. Needleflame was curled up in his arms, sleeping peacefully. Her body was no longer trembling, and the Chain of Will around her neck had cooled.

Vereesa was sitting nearby, her eyes fixed on Jace's rising and falling chest, as if she was trying to steady her own breathing.

She had lost her Dark Iron disguise and returned to her original appearance. Jace knew he had too.

The eyeball protruding from the box on the table had withered. The stalk had dried, gone gray, and broken. There was no trace of shadow energy anymore.

"Thank you, Vereesa." Jace let out a breath.

"Say 'thank you' again, and I'll take a few swings at your eyeball too." Vereesa said, annoyed.

Jace cracked a smile. "How long was I out? We need to get out of here fast."

Before he'd finished speaking, hurried footsteps sounded at the door. Vereesa had barely raised her bow when the faint glow of dream magic outlined a familiar woman's figure — it was Gestahn herself.

"The mistress sent me to check on the situation here. She said matters must be extremely urgent..." The Twilight Lord glanced at the fallen Twilight's Hammer cultists on the floor. "Don't worry about this place, you two. We'll quickly take in some new members to cover these losses within a reasonable range."

Jace finally relaxed, slumping slowly against the table. "The Firelord and his servants don't need to know what happened here."

"Of course, master." Gestahn bowed her head respectfully.
