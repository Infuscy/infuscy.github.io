Chapter 1544: Still No Good

By evening, Jace, Sora, Vereesa, and Needleflame were sitting in the tavern. Jace and Vereesa had used Varlastrasz's gray gem to transform back into Dark Iron dwarves, and Needleflame had too.

In front of them sat a plate of red bomb-shaped candies. And they really looked like bombs — red cubes with a black "fuse" on top like a firework's powder trail.

The barkeep said they were called Abyss Chili Gold, a very delicious and very stimulating sweet. Jace had no idea if they naturally grew this way or if the Dark Iron dwarves had shaped them like this out of sheer twisted humor.

Delicious? Jace had no clue. All he knew was that after taking one tiny bite, he'd wanted to stab his own throat open to end the suffering. He was still crying, swallowing mouthfuls of saliva.

Vereesa took one bite and stopped, switching to drinking heavily. Sora watched the two of them in silence and didn't dare touch them. Meanwhile, across the table, Needleflame was already on her third piece.

The idea had been to buy something exciting and new to help shake off the afternoon's terror. But in the end, the one who most needed to escape the pain seemed completely unaffected, while those who weren't so troubled were now genuinely suffering.

The Dark Iron Tavern was as lively as ever in the evening. At the next table, a group of dwarves who looked like they'd just crawled in through a secret passageway were hunters. They were excitedly discussing how the Fireguard had just recaptured a small fortress on the Burning Steppes that the Blackrock orcs had held for three years, though none of them thought the Fireguard troops could hold it for long under the black dragonflight's air raids.

Behind them, a loud voice was boasting about making money by hunting giant bugs in the tunnels for meat. When Jace had gone to the counter earlier, he'd caught snippets of other conversations, but none of them had anything to do with the Twilight's Hammer embassy.

The only thing even remotely related to Twilight's Hammer was a few drinkers at the counter mentioning that some cult members hadn't been careful enough entering the secret passage and had been discovered by orcs, exposing the passage's entrance. Several Dark Iron sentries guarding it were killed, and the passage was collapsed.

Either way, that nightmare on the edge of Shadowforge City might as well have never happened, as far as the people here were concerned.

Jace's rational mind knew that was fine. But he'd just barely clawed his way out of that "world" that afternoon, poured every ounce of his strength into it, and now everyone around him was acting like nothing had happened. Which was a good thing — it meant their operation hadn't been exposed — but the stark contrast was deeply unsettling.

Morophil and Grede appeared through the Dark Iron Tavern's front door. Grede spotted the group at the table and pushed past a few drunk Dark Iron patrons, hugging Needleflame excitedly.

"You're awake, little one. Damn it all..."

"You knew I was unconscious?" Needleflame asked.

Grede was about to answer when his face suddenly changed. He coughed violently several times, then pinched his nose and asked, "What in the hell did you eat?"

"Abyss Chili Gold." Needleflame picked one up with a completely casual look. "See, this."

"Get it away from me!" Grede waved his arm. "Don't you know? This stuff kills more Dark Irons every year than lava worms eat!"

"So I really don't understand where you got the courage to challenge a black dragon to a drinking contest with Sulfuron Firewater." Vereesa said quietly.

"I thought Sulfuron fire and the molten lava those oversized black lizards spit were different things." Grede mumbled as he sat down, glancing at Needleflame. "That Twilight woman told me the human girl had been sacrificed to their great master... scared the hell out of me. If we didn't still need her to get us into the Molten Core ritual, I'd have punched her mean face in."

"I don't understand. Why does she still call that dark thing her 'great master'?" Vereesa looked at the demon, asking quietly. "Hasn't she been..."

"Charm magic isn't total mind control." Morophil said. "She sees me as the supreme being in her life, the master she must obey in all things. But that doesn't mean she abandons her entire former life and beliefs and turns into a rigid puppet. That would be too boring, wouldn't it?"

"This way she can keep running the whole Twilight's Hammer organization in Shadowforge City too." Jace said. "But can you guarantee she'll obey your orders from now until the ritual?"

"In the embassy, it would have been hard to guarantee. So I'll try to keep her at the prison." Morophil said. "When I sent her to rescue you earlier in the embassy, the emergency meant I overlooked the various anti-demon wards and runes the Dark Iron dwarves had set up there. The magical interference almost made me lose control of her, which is why I had to chase after her. Thankfully we didn't fail at the last hurdle."

"You made the right call." Vereesa said quietly. "If Gestahn hadn't shown up to take control of the situation so quickly, and those Twilight's Hammer members had surrounded us and told the Flameguard and dwarven soldiers who were coming back, we'd have been finished."

"That was the effect I was going for." Morophil smiled slightly. "Don't worry. I may not be as good at charming and enslaving as the Banshee Queens, but in countless years of experience torturing and breaking the spirits of countless mortals and demons, I've learned how to use someone's boundless adoration for me to achieve my goals. Lady Gestahn is far from the most powerful target I've ever manipulated. She wouldn't even rank in the top fifty."

"That vain, pleasure-seeking little wretch is much more fragile than she looks. Maybe that's why the Twilight's Hammer leader gave her an Old God's stalk-eye to help her establish her rule here... That ogre knows perfectly well that without that ancient god's help, his so-called 'lords' couldn't last three rounds against these second-rate warlocks in Shadowforge City."

"Sounds like you've learned quite a bit from Gestahn." Jace said.

"Naturally, dear." Morophil tilted her head. "Of course, Ranger, we have you to thank for breaking that dream, tearing apart the stalk-eye's power, and stripping Lady Gestahn of her protection, making all this possible."

"Without that last part, I'd have thought you really believed you did all this on your own, demon." Vereesa snorted and took a drink.

"Teamwork, dear." Morophil raised a hand. "Every participant deserves praise."

Grede grabbed the ale in front of Jace and downed half of it. "Honestly, I didn't do much, demon."

"I didn't do much either." Sora said.

"Well, I think I made a big contribution, Lady Sora." The imp sat in front of a plate, saying, "I think that woman is already fascinated by my deep understanding of demons and demonic souls..."

Needleflame ate her candy piece by piece, sneaking glances at the group, especially Jace and Vereesa. She hadn't said a word.

"Our operation isn't completely over yet." Jace said quietly. "We need to thoroughly investigate what Twilight's Hammer has stored in that embassy, see if there's anything useful — whether about the ancient gods or the Firelord."

"If you ask me, let's wait till tomorrow." Grede glanced at the young black dragon. "Everyone's tired today. And at least this counts as a milestone victory. We should have a good drink, eat some... normal stuff to celebrate."

"I agree." Vereesa nodded. "I'd agree more if it weren't for him suggesting it."

"Either you agree or you don't. What's this 'agree more' business?" Grede stared at the ranger, grumbling.

"Hey, did you know the Dark Iron Tavern has competitions on weekends?"

"What?" Vereesa asked.

"Target shooting! With guns!" Grede mimed holding a rifle. "Never tried it, have you, you arrogant ranger? So we're starting on equal footing! You got the guts to go up against me?"

"Why wouldn't I? You think I'm afraid to compete with a dwarf in marksmanship?" Vereesa sneered.

"What are you laughing at? You're a dwarf too! Ha!" Grede laughed loudly. The ranger's face went cold, unsure how to argue with that.

"Mm, I want to play too." Sora said.

"Everyone's coming. You too, warlock. And you two demons!" Grede pounded the table excitedly. "And you, little one!"

This wasn't laziness or goofing off. After the spiritual torment of that terrifying shadow, relaxation and joy were the best medicine. In some ways, their effects couldn't be replaced by the Holy Light or shadow-purifying potions.

Grede had seen the Old Gods' creations before. He'd seen what those creations did to dwarves. In Winterspring, he'd helped Sora shake off her shadows alongside Jace and Vereesa. So he had plenty of experience in this area too.

Jace knew Grede was trying to help Needleflame move past the trauma without directly bringing up her memories of the nightmare. The dwarf seemed loud and clumsy, but his thoughts were often more delicate than many elves'.

Before long, the tavern got rowdy. A crowd was gathering — it seemed like the shooting contest Grede had mentioned was starting.

Grede and Vereesa jostled each other as they pushed through the crowd. Sora and the two demons followed.

Needleflame held her cup, looked at them, then at Jace as he picked up his cloak. She didn't get up.

"Not going to join?" Jace asked.

"I didn't do anything. I just held you back, almost got you all killed, didn't I?" Needleflame asked quietly.

"What are you talking about?" Jace said. "If it weren't for you, do you think we'd have found that room with the eye so fast?"

"That bastard wizard Cremhold told me the embassy was tiny and easy to search. I forgot the guy started out as a thief. And a Dark Iron dwarf at that. What those people think of as 'tiny' and 'simple layout' is completely different from what ordinary people like us who haven't done this kind of thing before would think. Vereesa and I only found you that fast because we were following the magic that had trapped you, Needleflame."

Needleflame's mouth twitched, but she quickly suppressed it. She looked up and asked, "So... was I important this time?"

"It wouldn't have worked without you." Jace said seriously.

Finally, a hint of smugness crept onto Needleflame's face. "Mm. For me to make such a big contribution, I have you and Lady Vereesa to thank."

"Then you can carry me back to Stormwind on your back as thanks." Jace teased.

"No. Way." Needleflame muttered, taking off the Chain of Will and putting it over Jace's head, then turned and vanished into the crowd.
