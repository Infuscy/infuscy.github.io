## Chapter 1545: It's All for Morophil

The next morning, Jace planned to head back to the prison near the Ring of Law to meet with Gestahn.

To make Gestahn easier to control, Morophil hadn't let her return to the embassy surrounded by anti-demon wards. Being closer to the Dark Iron Tavern from the prison also made it easier for Jace's group to contact her.

Jace confirmed in the mirror that he still looked like a Dark Iron dwarf. Vereesa, sitting by the window, watched him for a long moment before speaking. "Your score was pretty high in last night's shooting contest. Even higher than the dwarf. I mean the real dwarf."

"Is being a better shot than a Wildhammer something to be proud of?" Jace said as he pulled on his shirt. "He's not a Bronzebeard or Dark Iron."

"But you've clearly had training." Vereesa said suspiciously. "Picking up that rifle, shouldering it, aiming... No matter how you look at it, that wasn't your first time holding one of those."

In the relaxed, cheerful atmosphere of last night, Jace hadn't thought much about it.

He hadn't expected that something as basic as picking up a gun, aiming through the sights, and shooting — something even a kid from his past life would know — would look so strange coming from someone who'd grown up under the walls of Lordaeron.

He took a bite of bread and said, "Did I never mention it? I used to hunt with my father back in Tirisfal. I handled hunting rifles too. Lordaeron never had a shortage of dwarves who used rifles."

"Were Lordaeronians and dwarves really that close before the Dark Portal?" Vereesa looked him up and down like a big cat stalking prey. "You've got too many secrets. I'm an open book to you, but you're always hiding something."

"We're going to be together for a long, long time." Jace said. "Wouldn't it be boring if you didn't have new things to discover about me?"

Vereesa snorted. Watching him put on his cloak, she asked, "You're going to see Gestahn alone?"

"Yeah." Jace said. "Stay here with Sora and Grede and look after Needleflame. You saw what happened in there."

"I did see what happened in there. And actually, I'm more worried about you than Needleflame." Vereesa said. "Don't overthink things."

"I know, Vereesa." Jace said.

The ranger leaned in and gently kissed Jace's lips. Jace lightly touched her pointed ear, then headed out.

By the stairs, Morophil sidled up to him. "It's such a shame I can't come with you to see dear Gestahn, master."

"A demon always being seen with Twilight's Hammer would raise too much suspicion." Jace said. "I'll go alone this time. She's completely under your control anyway."

"Completely? Not quite that complete." Morophil flashed a sly smile.

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

"You'll find out when you get there." The succubus said. "I should warn you, dear. Like I said yesterday, the one caught in my charm spell isn't a pure puppet."

"So, anything I should watch out for?" Jace asked.

Sazeno, perched on the stair railing, was about to speak when Morophil wrapped her tail tightly around its neck, choking the words back into its throat with a *gurk*.

"Don't cause me any problems." Jace said.

"Don't worry, master." Morophil said.

Leaving the Dark Iron Tavern, Jace crossed the entire Ring of Law and arrived at the purple banner. The guard at the door had changed from the human before to a rather sturdy Jungle Troll.

Inside was the same chamber as before, the same eerie atmosphere with shadow braziers. The noblewoman sitting by the table turned toward the door and nodded. "Welcome, Mr. Slovakia."

"Thank you very for your help yesterday, Lady Gestahn." Jace said. "If you hadn't arrived at the embassy in time to defuse the situation for me and my friends, everything would have fallen apart."

"You and your so-called friends deceived me with a treasure, then broke into my territory, destroyed my treasure, and cut off my connection to the gods." Gestahn said coldly. "And now you come here to offer hypocritical thanks?"

Gestahn's hostile attitude caught Jace off guard. Sure, most people Morophil had charmed in the past had been generally unfriendly — like when Nek'rosh had handed over the Demon Soul, it had clearly looked like a man forced into a decision he didn't want to make. Jace had never had normal conversations with those charmed subjects before, but Gestahn's current state was a first.

Good thing Morophil had given him a heads-up, or he might have thought the charm had worn off.

"You are her master, so you are my master. And she has instructed me to answer all your questions truthfully, and has explained to me that you had a critical reason for destroying Twilight's Hammer's sacred treasure." Gestahn said, her tone full of anger. "But don't think I can just pretend none of this happened."

"That's enough, Lord Gestahn." Jace said.

"I'm glad we've come to an understanding so quickly. There are no outsiders here now — ask what you want." Gestahn said.

"You said earlier that we cut your connection to the gods." Jace said. "Does that mean that stalk-eye was the medium for communication between you and your gods?"

Gestahn said, "The leader entrusted it to me, told me to bring it to Shadowforge City and protect it. I failed his expectations."

"It let me see many things. From the gray past to the magnificent future. That's the direction we're working toward, Slovakia. If you could see what I saw, you'd long for the path the gods have shown us too. Unfortunately, you've lost your chance. And you destroyed it with your own hands. I regret that."

Though the woman was rambling on and on, she hadn't really said anything. Jace could tell she wasn't even sure whether the stalk-eye could actually communicate with the Old God. Or maybe, to her, the visions of the past and future that the stalk-eye revealed through hallucinations counted as a form of communication.

Jace organized his thoughts and tried a different angle. "Now that your treasure is gone... will your superior — the leader you mentioned — notice?"

"Our leader knows all, sees all." Gestahn paused. "But I must write to inform him of this tragic accident. You might say it's meaningless, but it's a necessary procedure. If dear Lady Morophil hadn't asked me not to tell anyone unrelated about the treasure, I'd have sent that letter already!"

"I'll describe in detail how you blind heretics brutally destroyed Twilight's Hammer's great plan, how you offended the body of a god, how you ignored and even profaned sacred omens. That human girl's fate was punishment, Mr. Slovakia. I hope the disaster her ignorance brought will serve as a grave warning to you!"

"Will he send someone to check on things? Or find a new Twilight Lord to replace you?" Jace asked.

"Do you hope I'll be replaced?" Gestahn asked. "I don't understand. Is Lady Morophil dissatisfied with me in some way? What have you heard from her?"

"Answer the question, Lady Gestahn." Jace leaned back against the chair's armrest.

Gestahn eyed Jace suspiciously for a moment before saying, "I am Twilight's Hammer's highest leader here. Do you have any idea what trials it takes to earn the leader's trust and carry a part of the gods' body into this world! Morophil will understand my value. I know she is the most beautiful and powerful demon queen in the Twisting Nether, but I am also worthy of serving her, of serving her with everything I have."

"So only another Twilight Lord can investigate me or replace my position. And there are very few such high-ranking members in Twilight's Hammer. Besides me, only the main headquarters in Silithus has them. If our leader senses what's happening here, it means he'll have to send a new emissary from Silithus to Gadgetzan, then cross the Great Sea all the way to Redridge."

"About a two-month journey." Jace said.

"Your estimate is correct." Gestahn said. "You and Lady Sothara are indeed very experienced traveling merchants."

"Executus's ritual is less than thirty days away. That means even if someone new does come to Shadowforge City to investigate, it'll be after the ritual." Jace said.

"Yes. I don't understand why you're always hoping the leader's people will hurry up. Don't you have faith in me?" Gestahn said. "This is already the second protection rune strengthening ritual I've led for Twilight's Hammer."

"The stability of the runes means Firelord Ragnaros's domain will gradually swallow all of Blackrock Mountain. You might think the orcs are besieging Shadowforge City, but they've already lost the ability to stop it. By then, the ruler of Blackrock Spire will be forced to face the shaper of Blackrock Mountain. That epic battle will unleash a storm of destruction across this continent, and that's just the prelude to that magnificently radiant future."

"I will prove that even without that treasure, I can complete the mission the leader gave me. This second rune ritual is the perfect opportunity to do that. I hope Morophil will witness my ability through this ritual. I need to show her my value!"

"But the Molten Core doesn't allow demons." Jace said.

"Yes. That's how it is." Gestahn's emotions calmed a little. She said, "Because demons are not to be trusted. They are not worthy of being accepted by the gods. The connection between you and me and those great gods is much closer than you think, Slovakia. As for demons... they are just mad puppets burned by uncontrollable flames deep in the Twisting Nether. They have no real souls, no real flesh, no real hearts... But Morophil is different from other demons. I believe the Sayaad are not ordinary demons. Their manipulation of shadow is just as praiseworthy and worthy of reverence as the servants of the gods."

"But rules are rules. Even if I were willing to make an exception for the beautiful and kind Morophil, Majordomo Executus and the Firelord would never accept her ascending to the seat above the throne. I have to say, that's a real shame. Your concern is valid — she can't witness the ritual's success firsthand. But the success of the ritual itself represents the ability of Twilight's Hammer. It represents my ability. She will hear about it from others. The flame rune strengthened once more, the Firelands drawing closer and closer to Azeroth... She will feel that grandeur. She will feel my participation."

"I can relay it for you." Jace said.

"You would help me?" Gestahn asked.

"Yes." Jace said. "I'm rather... curious about what Twilight's Hammer believes in, Lord Gestahn. Lady Sothara and the guard you saw at the tavern that day as well. We all want to witness it that day."

"But you're not Twilight's Hammer. You haven't officially joined the church." Gestahn said.

"What does that matter?" Jace said. "What's more important than proving your value to Morophil? I am her master, madam. She values my words greatly. And I don't want to force her to value your efforts through commands. A bond built on compulsory orders is thin and fragile. When the contract ends and the master-servant relationship is broken, it will scatter like dust in the wind."

"Exactly, exactly right, Mr. Slovakia!" Gestahn trembled with excitement. "I will bring you to the ritual, to Majordomo Executus's throne, to witness the rekindling of the protection runes! You'll see my important role in the ritual. Please watch closely, relay every detail to her. I can hardly wait for that day."

Jace had figured out the key to communicating with Gestahn in her current state: just join her in enthusiastically praising Morophil.

Realizing this, he suddenly recalled the smile Morophil had given him when he left that morning — that pleased, satisfied, wicked little smile...

That damned demon. She'd definitely already pictured him coming here to negotiate with Gestahn about joining the ritual, and known he'd end up having to sing her praises alongside this Twilight Lord to solve the problem.

Morophil was probably in the tavern right now, imagining him and Gestahn competing to say nice things about her. The worst part was that what was happening here wasn't far off from what she'd imagined.

*What's more important than proving your value to Morophil?*

Jace couldn't help rubbing his face in discomfort. He'd never imagined he'd say something so absurd.

"It will be our honor to participate in this ritual, Lady Gestahn." He said. "But there's no need for Executus to know about the special relationship between you and us. We can accompany you as ordinary Twilight's Hammer members."

"I understand. These fire elementals are irritable, violent, and utterly unreasonable." Gestahn muttered quietly. "I was the same way before. Lady Morophil's arrival saved me and changed me. I'd say it was practically a rebirth — no less important than that meeting with our leader Cho'gall in Booty Bay."

"I apologize for my earlier rudeness, Mr. Slovakia. I used to think all warlocks were cunning, base, cowardly bullies. I never believed there could be someone as warm-hearted as you among them."

"Then to participate in the ritual, we'll need Twilight's Hammer church vestments. One set for a human, one for an elf, and one for a dwarf."

"I'll have them prepared for you." Gestahn said. "In addition to church vestments, you'll need Twilight's Hammer pendants — every member must have one — and your own insignias, representing your rank within the church."

"I hope it can be done quickly." Jace said. "There's a lot more I want to know about the Twilight's Hammer church. I saw many documents in your embassy earlier. I wonder if you'd allow us to go through them? I know it's a lot to ask, but as you know, the beautiful Lady Morophil is eager to understand what gods you and your Twilight's Hammer serve and what powers you wield. However, that embassy — as you know, it still has many wards laid by the Dark Iron wizards, which makes it very inconvenient for her to enter in person. So we'll have to do it on her behalf."

"Is that so?" Gestahn said. "I thought... I thought she wasn't that interested in our cause. Since you say so, I'll arrange your credentials as quickly as possible so you can enter the embassy freely and review the materials."

"Thank you very much, Lord Gestahn." Jace said. "I'm also glad we've been able to clear up some of our misunderstandings."

After leaving Gestahn's office and walking a short distance toward the Dark Iron Tavern, Jace heard the sound of rolling rubble growing closer from behind.

It had to be Lord Roccor. This elemental lord usually patrolled here, keeping watch on the Fireguard Legion and the area around the Ring of Law.

He turned around. Sure enough, dust and smoke accompanied by firelight appeared in the passage. Roccor had shown up, and he was coming straight for Jace.

The molten elemental's burning little eyes ignored all the Fireguard soldiers and bloodhound handlers around him, fixed only on Jace, this one Dark Iron dwarf. As he passed by, he slowed slightly, then turned into a narrow black passage.

Jace understood. He had something to say. He was hinting for Jace to follow him to a quieter spot.

They entered a quiet tunnel piled with rubble. When it was just the two of them, Roccor turned and spoke in Common. "I know what you're planning, Jace Sesso. You plan to use Executus's life to demonstrate the power of the Sulfuron Hammer to every Dark Iron dwarf and elemental in Shadowforge City."

"Twilight's Hammer has fallen into your hands. I don't doubt you can successfully attend the ritual, ascend to Executus's throne. Nor do I doubt that Grede Thunderfist's strength can wield the Sulfuron Hammer and strike down that Sulfuron monstrosity that enslaves flame and lava in Ragnaros's name."

"But have you truly believed the Dark Iron dwarves' arrogant legends? Do you really believe a hammer forged in imitation of the Firelord's power can challenge Ragnaros himself? Killing Executus means completely enraging the Molten Core. Then Ragnaros will return to this world, unleashing his fury on every living creature and elemental here."

"I hope you're sufficiently prepared for this calamity. Otherwise, every Dark Iron dwarf here will follow their ancestors into death, and you and I will be reduced to ashes along with them!"

"Roktus Darkwhisper told me that Executus plans to strengthen seven runes during the ritual. Those seven runes are the 'chains' stabilizing the connection between the Firelands and Azeroth." Jace said.

"Yes." Roccor said. "If the power of these runes is exhausted, the link between the two worlds will gradually collapse. If the runes are not re-energized in time, Ragnaros will lose his chance to rule this world again. What are you thinking? Do you think destroying one ritual and killing Executus means no one can power them anymore? It would take seven winters for all the flame runes to lose their effect, Jace Sesso! Are you planning to have the dwarves of Shadowforge City follow you into a protracted war of attrition against the flame and lava of the Molten Core for seven years?"

The lava elemental's emotions were spiraling out of control. The stones of his body were growing brighter and brighter, until Jace pulled a small crystal vial from his chest and showed it to him. Lord Roccor suddenly dimmed as if doused in seawater, his little eyes nearly going out in fright, and the stones beneath him rolled backward a few steps.

"Essence of Water... How do you have this?"

"Fire elementals aren't short on enemies. I'm not short on friends." Jace said. "The question now is, do you know how to use it?"
