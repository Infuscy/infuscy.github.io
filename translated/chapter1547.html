Chapter 1547: Half a Prisoner

"So if I want to know how to use the Essence of Water, I need to fight my way into the Molten Core, kill those two fire elemental lords, and seize the elemental cages?" Jace asked.

"You don't need both, Jace Sesso." Roccor said. "You only need one of them to be able to speak with that wind prince. He is one of the most powerful wind elementals beneath Al'Akir the Windlord. Even though the Firelord drained most of his essence and split him in two, each half still retains its own consciousness."

Jace was willing to believe that. In the game, the quest needed both "Bindings," but you only needed one side to trigger the quest and hear the Windseeker's tale.

But what difference did killing one make from killing two? Both Garr and Geddon were deep in the Molten Core. Fighting either one would have unpredictable consequences — it might even affect the progress of the rune-strengthening ritual.

What about after the ritual? Kill Executus first, then, while the elementals in the Molten Core were leaderless, hunt down the two element lords in the chaos?

Killing Executus would mean enraging Ragnaros. He'd do everything in his power to crawl out of the lava and destroy every enemy before the flame protection runes lost their power, preserving the connection between the Firelands and Azeroth — just as Roccor had said.

And then going after the elemental lords... if something went wrong — if he couldn't find them, or if killing them didn't yield Thunderaan the Windseeker's Bindings, etc.

The worst-case scenario would be finding them, awakening Thunderaan's power, and then discovering he didn't know how to use the Essence of Water either — that it was all just Roccor's speculation.

Roccor spoke with conviction that a prince like the Windseeker, an ancient elemental who'd participated in countless elemental wars, would definitely know how to use the Essence of Water. But once they made a move on Executus, there was no turning back.

Jace didn't want to be frantically testing possibilities in the middle of a crisis. Since there was less than a month before the ritual, why not use this time to make everything more certain?

"I know it's unlikely." Jace leaned against a broken stone pillar. "But is there a way to get the wind elemental's cage out of the Molten Core without a fight? Something along those lines?"

Jace had expected the lava elemental to mock him for being naive. To his surprise, Roccor was silent for a moment, then said, "It's possible."

"How?" Jace asked.

"Back when the rumor of the Sulfuron Hammer first spread through Shadowforge City." Roccor said. "To suppress those rumors — and to suppress the Dark Iron dwarves' doubts about the Firelord's absolute power — the Bindings of the Windseeker were once brought out by the Seven Sages' students and displayed to all of Shadowforge City. Those Flamekeepers wanted to show that anyone who tried to challenge the Firelord would end up the same way. Even a wind prince who'd rampaged across this world for tens of thousands of years couldn't escape it, let alone a bunch of mortals."

"And that itself was the reason the Firelord brought the two subordinates guarding the cage into the Molten Core — the remaining will of the mighty wind prince was a trophy to showcase his absolute power."

"So why did he split the cage in two in the first place?" Jace asked.

"To guard against his own power-hungry servants secretly siphoning energy." Roccor answered. "That remnant elemental power means nothing to Ragnaros — he'd already drained most of it. But to his subordinates, it's a rare magical treasure. As long as the two cages aren't rejoined and the power isn't complete, his subordinates can't touch it."

Jace said, "Ragnaros didn't give these so-called Bindings to the Flamekeepers, but to a fire elemental and a lava elemental like you. I'm guessing he wanted to keep them away from the Flamekeepers."

"You guess correctly." Roccor answered. "The one who most wants to get his hands on those two Bindings is the Arcanist Shazzrah. In the Molten Core, he's been constantly stirring up conflict between the two — my former master Garr and Baron Geddon — hoping to take advantage of the chaos and steal both Bindings. In fact, my own banishment is related to these behind-the-scenes conflicts."

Jace leaned against the stone pillar and thought for a moment, sifting through all his memories about Thunderaan and the Firelord. Then an idea occurred to him.

In the game, to get the Blessing of the Windseeker's sword, you first needed both Bindings, plus the essence Ragnaros had drained. Then you reassembled them to summon Thunderaan, defeated him, seized the "Wind-Kissed Blade" from his hand, commissioned blacksmiths to reforge it, and finally completed a blade fit for a mortal.

And the quest NPC who reassembled Thunderaan was a high-ranking Twilight's Hammer member who worshipped wind elementals — High Lord Demitrian.

That meant Twilight's Hammer definitely knew about Thunderaan the Windseeker. They knew he'd been defeated by Ragnaros, split in two, and imprisoned in head-shaped cages. Maybe they'd learned it from elementals summoned in Silithus, or maybe from right here in Shadowforge City.

Thinking this over, Jace looked up and asked, "Since the elementals trusted the Flamekeepers enough to let them bring the two Bindings into Shadowforge City for display — could the leader of Twilight's Hammer possibly get one of them?"

"Gestahn came here in the name of an Old God." Roccor said. "If she had a compelling reason to ask to see one of the Bindings, I think those two elementals wouldn't refuse."

"Provided there's a compelling reason." Jace said.

"Indeed." Roccor said.

By the time Jace got back to the Dark Iron Tavern, it was already past noon. He'd expected to catch lunch with everyone, but when he got to their lodgings, he found they'd already eaten and drunk their fill. Grede was already snoring.

A piece of cold cooked tunnel worm sat on the table. Jace picked it up and took a bite. "Pretty crunchy."

"I was waiting for you to come back and drink with me, Jace Sesso." Needleflame said. "You said you'd help me relax, make me feel better. So where is it?"

"You can't be happy without me?" Jace asked.

"It's not like I can't do without you!" Needleflame shot back.

Sora patted her head and asked, "What took you so long, Jace?"

"Ran into Lord Roccor and chatted for a bit." Jace sat down beside Vereesa. "We talked about the Essence of Water."

"You mean that little bottle you brought." Grede's eyes went wide. "Find out how to use it?"

Jace shook his head. "It's... a bit complicated. The situation is, right now, the only one here who probably knows how to use the Essence of Water is a prisoner Ragnaros is holding. To get that prisoner's help, we might need Twilight Lord Gestahn to help us get one piece of the prisoner out."

"One piece of a prisoner." Grede repeated the phrase. "This guy's choice of words always makes me question my Common."

"Some mages, to keep summoned elemental beings stable, will cut the cage sealing the elemental into several pieces and store them separately." Vereesa said. "I'm guessing it's that kind of situation."

"More or less." Jace said. "Fortunately, Roccor says that prisoner is powerful enough that we only need one piece to ask for its help. But since both parts are guarded by elemental lords, even getting one won't be easy. I'm working on a reason that'll let our friend Gestahn request one of the cages from those elemental lords."

Jace briefly summarized his conversation with Roccor and the information about the Windseeker. Vereesa said, "Isn't Twilight's Hammer an organization that worships and studies elementals? In the Twilight's Hammer embassy, I saw quite a few runestones related to elemental summoning, including wind elementals. We could try having that woman request it under the pretext of researching the wind elemental lord."

Jace thought about it. "That's not impossible, but I feel like it's..."

"Not urgent or compelling enough." Vereesa shook her head too.

"Considering that prisoner is important — a wind prince himself." Sora also said. "The Firelord's subordinates probably wouldn't hand it over for such a flimsy reason. Elemental beings probably aren't interested in research knowledge. Fire elementals especially — they'd have trouble understanding that kind of motivation."

"Makes sense." Jace added, "Roccor mentioned that last time it was brought out to intimidate the rebels in Shadowforge City. I'm guessing those two elemental lords won't easily display the cage unless something threatens the Molten Core's status and security."

"You said earlier that Lord Roccor didn't believe you could destroy the flame runes at first, but when he saw the Essence of Water you produced, he was scared." Morophil, standing nearby, asked. "What do you think that means, 'master'?"

"It means that no matter how it's ultimately used, this stuff really does have the power to shake the Molten Core." Jace said.

"Exactly." The succubus narrowed her eyes. "So would you say that counts as threatening the Molten Core's security?"

"At least Roccor thinks so." Jace said.

"Then that's enough." The succubus said.

"And this lava elemental knows the wind prince can use the Essence of Water. Other fire elementals might know too." Vereesa looked up as well. "So could Gestahn use that as a reason to 'interrogate' the elemental's will in the cage?"

"Looks like we finally agree on something, Windrunner." Morophil said.

Vereesa nodded to her, offering the demon a rare smile.

"Shadowforge City prison interrogators, working for the safety of the Molten Core and the rule of the Firelord, interrogating an imprisoned wind elemental." Jace said quietly. "I don't know if the fire elementals will fall for it. But it's worth a try."
