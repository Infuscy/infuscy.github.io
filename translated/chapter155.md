## Chapter 155: Picked the Wrong Opponent

"If he's never told anyone, how do you, an outsider, know about it?" Grede countered.

Jace said, "Just think of it as the rich man's brother traveling back through time to warn him — but without letting him find out he has an identical brother."

"Impressive." Grede said. "If he's got time-travel magic that terrifying, he could just kill the thief any which way. Go back to when the kid was little and kick him to death. Why bother going to all the trouble of warning some rich man?"

"The thief has friends helping him. Powerful ones. The time traveler can't kill him." Jace patched the scenario again.

"If he's got friends that powerful who can go up against a time traveler, why bother stealing a treasure guarded by someone with no ability to fight back?" Grede countered.

"You—"

Jace was about to open his mouth and patch the scenario once more to deal with this pedant, but Grede's last question suddenly struck at something he hadn't thought through carefully before.

—Why were the black dragons helping Teron Gorefiend?

In Jace's memory, Gorefiend hadn't faced much real fighting during the theft of the Eye of Dalaran. Teron Gorefiend was certainly a powerful death knight, but he wasn't so powerful that he could come and go from the Kirin Tor's vault as he pleased.

The reason he'd ultimately managed it was that behind him weren't just the whelps of the Blackwing flight — there was Deathwing himself.

Deathwing hadn't just drawn away Dalaran's defensive strength by sending his offspring to set fires all across Lordaeron. At the final stage, he'd sent the backbone of the Blackwing Legion to assault Dalaran head-on, diverting attention.

He'd personally seen through the vault's ward magic and secretly guided Gorefiend inside, enabling him to steal the Eye of Dalaran without a hitch.

Did Deathwing need the Eye of Dalaran? No. With the unrivaled power he'd inherited from the Pantheon and the Old Gods, he wouldn't think much of the magic in these relics.

His reason for helping Gorefiend pull off this whole elaborate operation was simply to have the death knight legion help him smuggle as many black dragons as possible to Draenor — without drawing the attention of anyone, the other dragonflights in particular.

Further still, he hoped Gorefiend would lead the Horde in helping him build a greater, newer Blackwing Lair in Draenor.

By now, the Alliance host was probably mustering at Nethergarde Keep. The assault on the Dark Portal was about to begin — the fighting might have already started. Time was running out for Deathwing.

So Deathwing was helping Gorefiend carry out these raids on every front with such urgency that he was willing to let orcs and those death knights ride his noble offspring. He was even willing to risk exposing his movements to the other Dragon Aspects by personally moving to suppress Dalaran's defenders. Jace realized he was now plotting to stand in the way of one of the most terrifying beings on Azeroth — and one who had already invested far too much in his plan. They were at the final stroke.

If he came up with some way to get the Kirin Tor to hunker down and fortify the vault, blocking Gorefiend, what new stunt would Deathwing pull in his desperation to see it through?

Would he just tear the place apart with his own hands?

The more he thought about it, the more frightening it got. Helping take down a single dragon whelp had really gone to his head — here he was, scheming in some tavern to outmaneuver the Father of Black Dragons.

"Where'd that question even come from?" Grede asked.

"I was just reading a novel," Jace deflected. "Curious how the thief ends up pulling off the heist."

"What a waste of free time," Grede said. "Anyway, after talking to that Moonwing, I'm not in the mood for any of that anymore."

After the meal, Jace parted ways with Grede and returned to the rented room. Dushan still hadn't finished his lunch.

He looked up as Jace walked in. "I saw your message. With a friend going out with you, I've nothing to worry about. It's just that you haven't fully recovered yet — do take care of yourself when you're out. I left the medicine I bought for you in your room. You'll see it when you go up."

"Understood, Master," Jace nodded.

He was about to open his mouth and tell Dushan about the relics mentioned in that command letter — then thought better of it and swallowed the words. Never mind that Jace couldn't bring up the Eye of Dalaran directly. Even if he told Master Caro Dushan about all this, what more could he do than Jace himself?

If anything, Dushan certainly didn't know the full picture as well as Jace did. Telling him and having him rashly run to Dalaran to expose it might well end up getting him killed.

If he was going to do anything about this, he'd do it himself.

"Is something the matter, Jace Sesso? You look unwell," Dushan asked.

"No," Jace said. "I was just thinking about going home, Master."

"Where's home?"

"Right in the royal city."

"That's close, then. Find a boat at the dock and you can be there in a day. Plenty of ships run between Dalaran and the Lordaeron capital every day."

"I'm planning to go tomorrow."

"Isn't that a bit hasty?" Dushan said. "The mages who treated you told me to come find them if your condition changed at all. I haven't had the chance yet — but your condition right now definitely counts as a major change."

"I'd like to spend a few more days with my parents. It might even help with the recovery."

"That actually makes sense," Dushan said. "In any case, don't go out into the wilds. Take a boat straight from here to the Lordaeron port. There are plenty of people on the way. I'm sure that if anything does go wrong in the royal city, someone will help you at once. Go on up and take your medicine first — ease the headache."

Jace was about to head up the stairs when he said, "Master, I have a letter written in Orcish, but my vocabulary isn't strong enough to translate it in full. When it comes time for the Orcish-language exchange, would you mind presenting it and getting the language learners from all over to translate it together?"

Dushan said, "You take it and present it yourself. I'll speak with the people running the exchange in the next few days and get you slotted into the schedule. I'm not about to steal your thunder on this one. A letter is important language material — there's bound to be some interesting content, possibly even something to do with the movements of the Horde army at the time. You could even share the story of how you came by this letter. I'd think that makes for an interesting tale too?"

"It does," Jace said with a strained smile. "Then I'll go rest, Master."

"Go ahead."

Dushan stuffed a piece of bread into his mouth and went back to his book. Jace watched him a moment longer, then climbed the stairs step by step.

This was the most natural way he could think of to alert the entire Kirin Tor.

He'd come to Dalaran on the pretext of an Orcish-language exchange. At the Orcish exchange, he'd simply make the contents of that command letter public, polish up the story of how he'd obtained it, and add a few more veiled hints on top.

It would be perfectly natural and logical — it wouldn't raise suspicion from any quarter, from any angle.

He just hoped that after he made the letter public, the Kirin Tor mages in the know would be able to connect the disappearance of the Book of Medivh to the safety of another important relic — their Eye of Dalaran.

If they still couldn't figure it out after that, and weren't going to raise their guard and strengthen protections, then fine — let the chips fall where they may. It wasn't as if Jace could do anything about it anyway.

Sitting on the bed, he looked at the medicine Dushan had left — a small vial of pale-purple liquid, and a bottle as clear as water.

He didn't recognize either of them, but he drank them down one after the other. Both tasted decent — a good twenty times better than the Shadow Protection Potion he brewed himself.

After drinking them, he stared out the sky outside and thought of what Master Marin had said.

Don't keep taking responsibility for everything onto yourself. Improving yourself comes first.

Right. Going up against Deathwing was a job for the Kirin Tor. It had nothing to do with a little apprentice from the Wizard Sanctum for the time being.

He lay in bed, gazing at the ceiling. A wave of drowsiness rolled in. This time he didn't fight it. He closed his eyes and surrendered.
