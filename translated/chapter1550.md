## Chapter 1550: Baron Geddon

By the time the firelord Baron Geddon charged before them, even the black rocks seemed to have "caught fire." Thick black smoke and the blinding flames that burst from within blocked the view, barring their way.

He was enormous. From a distance, when he was rushing toward them, you couldn't really grasp it. But up close... there was no doubt he could instantly envelop those mountain-sized molten giants and burn them to cinders.

Standing on the cliff's edge, watching the elemental lord draw all the heat from the lava river and the flame energy from the rock fissures into himself, even Gestahn was blasted backward several steps by the swelling heat. She had to raise her fire protection barrier again to avoid being cooked alive by the sudden temperature spike.

Behind the Twilight Lord, Jace felt this was pressing against the limit of his natural fire resistance. He had to step back several paces to escape the most intense range of the flames.

Thank goodness the Twilight's Hammer vestments were enchanted with flame resistance. If he'd been wearing ordinary clothes, they'd probably have caught fire by now.

Taking advantage of the moment, Vereesa downed another flask of the fire protection potion Gestahn had given her — Twilight's Hammer's own brew. Given how poorly the Dark Iron dwarves outside thought of fire protection potions, Jace had expected Baron Geddon to be angered by Vereesa's action. But instead, the firelord raised his chest and let out a series of rumbling, crackling sounds, like a wind of flame sweeping through a forest — something like laughter. He didn't scold Vereesa at all.

Maybe to Baron Geddon, watching these three pitiful mortals pushed to their limits by his power was entertainment.

Geddon puffed out his chest — if you could call it a chest. Apart from the two ruby-studded black elemental bindings securing the thick, whip-like arms on either side of his body, his entire form had no true substance.

He was a mass of pure raging flame. The bright fire spraying from his top formed a fiery mane. The swirling, rolling smoke beneath formed his feet.

The flames, constantly shifting around his center, sometimes collided at the top, sometimes twined and writhed in the middle, forming shapes that resembled muscles or the skull of a humanoid creature, releasing light that seemed like eyes and a massive jaw.

But setting aside preconceptions about elemental beings, it was entirely possible to attribute all these shapes and lights to Jace's own imagination reading into things.

"A threat critical to the Molten Core, Gestahn? Do you know what you're saying?" The baron's voice merged with the roar of the flames — or rather, the stirring of fire and air was itself part of his voice.

Gestahn squinted painfully, looking up, and mustered all her strength to shout in elemental at the terrifying fire tornado before her. "If I did not have certain information, I would not come to the Molten Core to speak with you, great Baron, child of Ragnaros!"

"Spare me the nonsense!" Geddon's sudden bellow shattered several protruding rocks on the cliff.

The Twilight Lord's composure wavered from the blast. But soon, an inner energy of shadow magic helped her gather herself, find her courage again, and calmly face the flame.

Jace knew that power was related to Morophil. But it was hard to say if it was Morophil's magic at work, or just the Twilight Lord's burning infatuation with Morophil.

"Essence of Water, Baron Geddon." Gestahn said directly. "We have discovered a vial of Essence of Water. We do not know its source, nor who brought it into Shadowforge City."

"Hydraxis." Geddon rumbled. "I will burn that contemptible creature into a cloud of miserable steam sooner or later."

"There is no need to make a fuss, Twilight's Hammer. For the past hundred years, that ignorant fountain has continuously sent mortals or Dark Iron spies to infiltrate here, trying to destroy things with his Essence of Water. But they have all failed, every one. Executus and the incompetent Thaurissan line cannot guard the gates of Blackrock Mountain, but that does not mean the Sons of Flame are equally blind."

"This Essence of Water is not Hydraxis's elemental essence, great Baron." Jace shouted in elemental. "It seems to come directly from Neptulon the Tidehunter. According to records we have brought from Silithus... its characteristics match the description."

"Neptulon the Tidehunter?"

At the mere mention of that name, the baron's entire brightness seemed to dim. The writhing flames grew chaotic, making it hard to make out the elemental monster's "features."

Puffs of thick smoke billowed from beneath Geddon's body. He muttered, head low. "Impossible. Has he appeared in this world again? Or..."

"Bring that thing to me, Twilight's Hammer!" The elemental lord suddenly barked.

"Bring the Tidehunter's essence into the Molten Core?" Gestahn asked hesitantly.

"No, no, no. That cannot be done." The elemental lord's energy fluctuated in turmoil for a moment. "I must not take that risk. I cannot..."

Jace could guess what Geddon was afraid of. Many rumors said Geddon was the former manager of the Firelands — the position Executus now held. He must have made some mistake to be replaced by a Flamewaker.

Making the unauthorized decision to bring an elemental essence that could potentially destroy the Firelands' planar connection into the Molten Core was undoubtedly another risky move. It probably reminded him of the mistakes he'd made before.

Gestahn glanced at Jace beside her, swallowed, and said, "I know you, great Baron, hold part of the cage imprisoning Thunderaan the Windseeker. That ancient elemental, though defeated, still retains some remnant consciousness. He may know something."

"Indeed."

Geddon rumbled in response. His bound, whip-like arms reached into his chest and drew out a swirling mass of air. Within the mass, faintly visible, was a face wrapped in layers of burning chains of air.

Jace looked up at it. That was the Binding of the Windseeker.

"Even after being sealed for so long, you still haven't accepted your fate, Prince Thunderaan." Geddon's fire-eyes fixed on the cage. "You still want to disrupt the Firelord's plans? Still want to escape this place? Even though Neptulon the Tidehunter has given up the fight, even though the Hydraxian water elementals can only hide in the corners of the world cursing their own failure? Who have you pinned your hopes on this time? Do you have any connection to that inexplicably appearing vial of eternal essence?"

Jace could clearly feel an ancient elemental's will "gasping" within the cage. He heard Geddon's voice. The emotions swirling with the air around the cage rippled slightly. It seemed to be observing everything around it through its remaining shred of perception — both the firelord and the three mortals before him.

But whether it was perceiving something or thinking something over, it gave no response at all. It remained silent.

"As always, Prince Thunderaan is tight-lipped." The Baron let out a triumphant laugh. "I do not doubt that the wind prince may have conspired with the Tidehunter in this stillborn plot. But expecting him to confess their shameful defeat to the flames is a fool's fantasy."

"Twilight's Hammer has ways to make this wind elemental confess everything it knows, great and ancient Baron!" Jace shouted in elemental. "In Silithus, countless wind elementals have answered our summons and serve the Old Gods. I believe our rituals will help the wind prince better understand our intentions."

"This is the second time you have spoken. Your voice and courage impressed me earlier, strange mortal."

Jace felt Geddon's attention settle on him. An invisible pressure and temperatures hot enough to turn a mortal to ash gathered above his head. Even his flame-resistant robe was beginning to curl at the edges, sparks appearing, wisps of black smoke rising.

"What is his name, Gestahn?"

"Slovakia." Gestahn answered quietly. "He is the most talented elementalist among us, great Baron."

"Talent? Yes. He sounds quite knowledgeable about wind elementals. But at the same time, I can sense a strong connection to fire within him. Hmm..."

When Geddon had appeared, Jace had suppressed any connection to the elements as much as he could. He hadn't expected the firelord to still sense the red dragonfire within him.

But that wasn't surprising. Geddon was one of the oldest fire elementals in the world. Trying to hide an affinity for flame from him was a joke.

"I hail from Lordaeron, great Baron." Jace lowered his head and spoke in elemental. "I once followed the Dragonmaw orcs in search of greater power, learning the ways of dancing with flame. After the orcs were defeated, I joined Twilight's Hammer and traveled to Kalimdor. In Silithus, I found where true power lies. I found what I have been seeking all my life..."

"The firelight showed me your zeal and passion, and the shadows told me of your humility and sincerity, Slovakia." The Baron's voice shook the entire cliff face. "Take Thunderaan's cage to your ritual ground, Gestahn. Let me see what you and this elementalist are capable of, to make him speak."

"I cannot leave the Molten Core, cannot abandon my post. But I will not exclude myself from this. Incendius will represent me, bringing this cage into Shadowforge City to assist in your ritual."

"Twilight will forever sing of your wisdom and greatness, Baron. Until the twilight hour, when all things end." Gestahn proclaimed loudly. Jace and Vereesa, who had been observing the depths of the Molten Core, quickly bowed as well.
