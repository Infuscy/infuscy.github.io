Chapter 1553: In the Name of the Old Gods

Gestahn led a group of Twilight's Hammer members into a wide chamber in the embassy's basement to prepare for the ceremony. Jace noticed a black runestone about the size of an upper arm set up in the room — a wind stone brought from Silithus, a medium influenced by the Old Gods used to communicate with corrupted wind elementals.

Galders had once brought something like this back from Ironforge. Leaving it in his home for too long had slowly warped his personality and perceptions, until the thing was destroyed.

This wind stone was much bigger than Galders's had been. Fortunately, Jace and the others didn't have to live here permanently.

Needleflame, Sora, and Vereesa had already followed Gestahn into the room. As Grede was about to go in too, Jace called him over to a corner of the hallway.

"What's up?" Grede asked.

"When the ceremony starts, it's going to fail at first," Jace said, keeping an eye on the corridor for any fire elementals that might appear. "The Windseeker won't talk to us because he doesn't trust us. But you're going to say you heard the Wind Prince's voice."

"Me?" Grede asked. "Why not you? You understand Elemental too."

"Because you're a Wildhammer dwarf, a gryphon rider, Grede," Jace said. "The Wind Prince would rather talk to you than us. You're a student of the Aerie Peak sages! You've been talking to the storm and sky since you were a kid!"

Grede mulled it over for a moment, then nodded. "So... what did I hear?"

"The Wind Prince wants all the fire elementals out of the embassy," Jace said. "Because their power is interfering with the Water Essence's magic. He's so weak in his cage he can barely sense anything from outside. With all that flame present, he can't tell if our Water Essence is really Neptulon's soul or not."

"Water Essence, Neptulon, soul..." Grede mumbled through it all a few times, then said, "Damn. I don't know if I can fool that fire elemental... what if he can tell I'm lying?"

"How'd you fool Mossa into thinking you only had one ex-girlfriend? Channel that confidence." Jace glared at him.

"I only had one ex-girlfriend!" Grede emphasized, keeping his voice low.

Jace looked at him for a moment. "Yeah, that won't work. Damn it. I should've just lied to you directly — told you the Wind Prince actually said these things. That would've worked better."

"But if that fire elemental starts pressing me, I might accidentally let it slip that you told me," Grede said.

"True." Jace put his hands on his hips and thought for a moment. "Then don't lie."

"What do you mean?" the dwarf asked.

"Just say what I told you, but don't say who said it," Jace said. "Was any of that untrue? The fire elementals really do interfere with the Water Essence's magical radiation. The Wind Prince really is at his absolute weakest, barely able to sense anything outside. And he really won't talk while that fire elemental is present. Every word is the honest truth."

"That's true," Grede said. "But will it work?"

"Don't say anything extra and it'll work. Trust me." Jace lowered his voice further. "If things get out of hand, don't talk. Let me handle it."

Grede hesitated a moment, then said, "I'll try. But what if it goes completely sideways?"

"Then smash him and all his attendants back to the Elemental Plane with your warhammer, damn it all," Jace said.

"Well, that makes things simpler." Grede nodded, looking noticeably more relaxed.

"The ceremony preparations are complete, Lord Incendius!"

Soon enough, Gestahn's voice came from the corridor. The firelight at the end of the hall grew brighter. Grede took a deep breath, cleared his throat, and walked into the basement. Jace followed behind him. Then Incendius and his fire elementals appeared one by one.

"Place the Wind Prince's cage on the altar beneath the wind stone, my lord," the Twilight Lord said.

The Firelord said nothing. He walked forward, wrapped his flaming tendrils around the Bindings, set it on the black stone table, then slowly withdrew. Jace could clearly feel the complex emotions churning inside as the elemental stared at the cage.

"You may begin, Master Slovak," the Twilight Lord said.

"Thank you, Lord Gestahn." Jace nodded, walked to the wind stone altar, and knelt slowly. "Hear my call, great Wind Prince, ancient one who stood before gods and Titans. Your humble servant beseeches your reply."

He recited a chant he'd just made up on the spot in Elemental. Before him, the Bindings stirred with faint currents of air, but were otherwise as still as frozen stone. Even the will inside seemed to be growing weaker.

Jace picked up the Water Essence. "Behold this rare treasure, Windseeker. Your servant needs you to unlock the secrets it hides and resolve our doubts."

"What kind of interrogation is this? Sounds more like some sort of worship," Incendius said disdainfully. "A shattered remnant and you show it such respect? Pathetic mortal."

"You must leave this place, Incendius," Grede suddenly said.

"What?" The fire elemental expanded instantly, its flames racing along the walls to surround the dwarf. Vereesa and Needleflame both instinctively reached for their weapons, but Jace shot them a glare and they stopped.

Grede remained completely unfazed by the heat surrounding him. He craned his neck to stare at the two flickering "eyes" of the fire elemental and roared back in Elemental, "Your flames are blocking the Water Essence's soul! The Wind Prince can't sense anything! You and your attendants have to leave the embassy!"

As the Wildhammer dwarf chanted in Elemental, little sparks of lightning crackled from his hair and hands. Incendius hesitated, not daring to press further.

"He spoke? I didn't hear anything," the fire elemental said in a low voice.

"Because I'm the apprentice of Aerie Peak's greatest sage." Grede took two steps forward. "Only I can hear the roar of the wind, the thunder's cry!"

"That's why the ceremony requires his presence, Lord Incendius," Jace said. "Please leave here for now. Once we've finished questioning him, we'll tell you everything the Windseeker tells us about the Water Essence."

The flames on Incendius settled back to normal. He hesitated a moment, then picked up the cage from the altar, staring at it. "What game are you playing, Thunderaan? You think these foolish Twilight's Hammer fools can help you escape my grasp?"

"If that's the case, the ceremony is over." The flame's voice dropped. "I'll return to the Molten Core and report everything to the Firelord."

"Wait," Jace said.

"You have something to say?" the flame asked.

"I brought the Water Essence into Shadowforge City, Incendius," Jace said coldly.

"What did you say?!" Incendius nearly exploded with rage. The whole room turned into an oven in an instant. Gestahn yelped in fright. Sora and Vereesa looked equally stunned.

"Burn me, Incendius, it changes nothing!" Jace stood in the flames, glaring at the fire elemental. "My goal was to find the imprisoned Windseeker Thunderaan, to ask him if this truly is the Tidehunter's essence."

"If it is, I need to know how to use it against the Tidehunter, how to control the water elementals. Because he's the last elemental lord I know of who holds the secret of the Water Essence."

"I've summoned powerful sea elementals on countless shores, but nothing came of it. I traveled to the Thousand Needles to seek out the steam elementals there, but none could give me answers. I journeyed to Alterac Valley to speak with the Frost Lord, and he gave me this name — Thunderaan the Windseeker. Only he knows the secret of the Water Essence."

"This is the mission the Old Gods gave me, Incendius. This is the sole goal that brought me from Silithus all the way here. And now, the only thing standing between me and that goal is you."

At the mention of the Old Gods, Incendius's heat pulsed. The flames around Jace receded.

"This wisp of air is toying with you, Twilight's Hammer."

"I don't care." This time it was Jace who pressed forward.

"You want the Windseeker's Bindings too." He stared into the Firelord's eyes. "You've always wanted to replace Geddon as the new Firelord. And the best way to achieve that is to extract the power from these Bindings. The Old Gods whisper to me that you've been planning this for a long time."

After hearing this, Incendius was clearly shaken. Jace knew his judgment was right — this treacherous fire elemental had been plotting over Thunderaan's remaining power for who knows how long.

He clutched the Usarlus in his hand and gave a cold laugh. "I don't want a fight to the death, Incendius. I have no designs on Thunderaan the Windseeker's residual power because my goal is far higher than that! The true master of Ahn'Qiraj will know how to use the Water Essence to reclaim control over the depths of the Great Sea. Don't interfere with his plans, or you'll soon learn how the Firelord deals with a shameful traitor!"

"All of you, get out!" Incendius dispelled all his fire guards with an explosive burst of Elemental. He turned back, lowering his voice. "This is for the Old Gods' great plan, you fool. I hope you achieve your goal. When you realize just how foolish you've been, remember to return the Windseeker's Bindings. I'll be at the Dark Iron Anvil, waiting to mock your failure."

"But if you dare sneak out of Shadowforge City..."

The Firelord let that half-threat hang in the air as he drifted out of sight down the corridor. The entire basement went cold and still in an instant.

When they turned around, the others were all stunned. Gestahn was ashen-faced. Grede stood frozen, mouth half-open.

"Pay attention, Thunderfist," Jace said.
