## Chapter 1554: Wind Element

After the fire elementals left the room, the hall felt empty.

Needleflame and Sola stared intently at Jace standing in the middle, looking a bit dazed by that whole speech.

Vereesa, at least, knew Jace was dead set on luring Incendius out of the embassy, but she was still visibly surprised by what he'd explained to the fire elementals.

It wasn't just Jace's companions — even the genuine Twilight's Hammer cultists hadn't caught up yet.

"I don't understand, Slovakia," Gerstahn said, glancing at Grede first. "I never heard the Prince of Air's voice, and I certainly didn't know the Old Gods had such designs on the Eternal Quintessence."

"So you've been to Silithus too, Slovakia? Are you one of those warlocks who escaped after the Lighthouse incident? I thought Cho'gall had strictly forbidden you from summoning demons and conversing with them. Of course, Lady Morophil is extraordinary, but..."

"You guess correctly, I have been to the Lighthouse, Gerstahn," Jace said. "But I also truly never actually joined the Twilight's Hammer."

Gerstahn shook her head. "Yet you know everything about Silithus."

"I thought you'd made it all up to deceive Incendius, but those stories about Kalimdor, the Frost Lord of Alterac, the abyss of the Great Sea — you know the will of the Old Gods, and you know the inner secrets that only the Twilight Lord knows."

"Who are you, Slovakia? I've never heard that name before. It doesn't even sound like a human name."

"The Twilight's Hammer has only been in this world for thirteen or fourteen years," Jace said. "And you decided to flee to Kalimdor, to hear the Old Gods' voices... less than ten years ago."

"But those ancient ones have existed in this timeline for tens of thousands, even hundreds of thousands of years. Seeking their voices, learning their knowledge, serving their power — that's not a privilege exclusive to the Twilight's Hammer in Silithus. I admit, I did tell some lies. But it was for the Old Gods."

Gerstahn wanted to say something more, but Jace cut her off. "Thank you for helping preside over that ceremony, Lord Gerstahn. We can handle the rest ourselves."

"But the secret of the Aqual Quintessence..." the Twilight Lord hesitated.

"I'll tell you later. You can't help here right now," Jace said. "Also, this wind stone is useless for prying open the Prince of Air's secrets. You can have someone move it out."

"I... I..."

The Twilight Lord lowered her head, her eyes blinking unhealthily. Morophil's charm spell had weakened a bit.

Maybe Incendius's flames had burned away some of the magic — after all, in the game, damage also interrupted charm effects. Though Morophil's charm seemed far more powerful than its in-game counterpart, under the fire elemental's power it wasn't invulnerable either.

"Go find Morophil at the Grim Guzzler, Gerstahn," Jace said.

"She told me I'm not allowed to visit her at the tavern on my own..." Gerstahn's tone actually carried a hint of innocence.

"Tell her I sent you," Jace said. "She'll be willing to see you."

"Wonderful, wonderful. I'll go now." Gerstahn's demeanor instantly returned to normal as she walked out into the corridor.

Several Twilight's Hammer members entered at her command to move the wind stone out. Once everyone was gone, Jace said quietly, "Needleflame, Vereesa — you two follow her back to the tavern. If the charm isn't as strong anymore, put her to sleep."

"Put her to sleep." Needleflame cracked her knuckles. "That I'm good at."

"No." Jace looked at Vereesa. "Like last time at the embassy, when you used those sleep darts on people, Vereesa."

"You mean Wyvern Sting? No, they just died quietly," Vereesa said. "But I can make her sleep too, if necessary."

These two barbarians... Jace grumbled internally, but nodded anyway. The ranger led the black dragon out of the room and chased after the Twilight Lord.

Sola's eyes followed the two until they disappeared through the door. She pursed her lips. "I don't want to hide my doubts, Jace, but the way you looked just now genuinely worried me."

Grede peeked up and down the corridor, making sure no one was there, then chuckled softly. "What are you worried about, Sola? He's a warlock — a mage who stops at nothing to get what he wants! This guy habitually mixes Fear into his words when he's trying to scare people. I'm used to it by now. As long as the one running away scared is our target and not us, that's what matters."

"I'm not talking about Incendius," Sola said. "I originally thought the things you know about the Old Gods came from the Twilight's Hammer. But clearly, even Gerstahn doesn't know what you just said... Where did you learn it?"

"He's been to the Great Library of Karazhan — Medivh's treasure trove of knowledge, it's got everything." Grede joked, but his meaningful gaze still drifted toward Jace.

"I did read quite a bit in Karazhan, but that didn't include the Aqual Quintessence or Ahn'Qiraj." Jace looked at the green dragon. "It's not that hard to understand, really. You know, a very long time ago, before the dragons appeared, the Old Gods fought a war against the elements. They won and enslaved the elements, driving them to battle their enemies."

"Yes," Sola said. "Until they were sealed by the Creators."

"Right," Jace said. "Now it's clear the Old Gods are making a comeback. On one hand, they're using satyr demons to corrupt the Emerald Dream. At the same time, they've sent the Twilight's Hammer to re-establish contact with the Firelord. In Silithus, the Twilight's Hammer is reconnecting with the wind elementals under its will. It's searching for its 'old legions.' Water elementals are among them."

"Lord Roccor once told me that during the elemental war, other elementals used the Tidehunter's Aqual Quintessence to wound his soul. So since then, the Tidehunter refused to teach the new generation how to use the Aqual Quintessence."

"When I heard that, I considered that the Old Gods might also be interested in using that thing to regain control of the Tidehunter. Clearly, Incendius and Gerstahn both find that possibility convincing."

"Your reasoning makes sense." Sola finally nodded. "Sorry... maybe the nightmare experience made me overly sensitive to these matters. I shouldn't have doubted you."

"I went through that terrible nightmare too, along with Needleflame," Jace said. "I understand your concern."

Grede also relaxed a little. Jace knew that speech had worried the Wildhammer too.

"So, the fire elemental problem is settled for now." The dwarf turned to look at the cage on the altar. "What about the wind elemental problem?"

Ever since Incendius left, the Prince of Air's cage had been completely still.

Jace could still feel the will within it — a will that seemed to be observing them the whole time but had never responded to anything happening outside.

He approached the so-called cage, studying the face inside carefully.

It looked like a cloud, or layers of waves. The left half was slightly clearer, the right half relatively more blurred. Jace guessed this was what players called the "left face."

When the two halves came together, they would form a complete, clear Prince's Cranial Prison.

"Prince Thunderaan the Windseeker," Jace said in Elemental. "We are not the Twilight's Hammer, nor the Dark Iron dwarves, nor servants of Ragnaros the Firelord."

"You can probably sense that I'm a demon summoner, carrying a demon's scent. I'm not a righteous person, and I didn't come to Molten Core to rescue you from the fire elementals. But right now, we share the same goal."

Jace's Elemental wasn't as fluent as his Demonic, but he still tried his best to enunciate each word carefully. He also picked up the Aqual Quintessence and showed it to the half-face. Yet despite all this, the cage still showed no reaction, remaining silent.

"Maybe you should try, Grede? Might be he trusts an apprentice of the Aerie Peak sages more."

"Alright."

Grede tried repeating what Jace had said. Clearly, the Elemental he'd learned from the Wildhammer sage was closer to the wind elemental's accent than what Jace had learned from the Dark Irons. But the cage still didn't budge.

Could Roccor be wrong? Did the two halves of the Prince of Air's cage need to be reunited before they could speak? Roccor had once served under the molten elemental Garr, and Garr was the guardian of the right half of the cage. He'd been so certain the half-face could converse — there shouldn't be a mistake.

"Windseeker Thunderaan." Sola spoke in Draconic. "A thousand years ago, dragons and the Kaldorei fought together against the qiraji invasion in Silithus, sealing the Gates of Ahn'Qiraj."

"That great war benefited you too. You know what lies buried behind the Scarab Wall, deep within the city of Ahn'Qiraj. You know who the common enemy of dragons, mortals, and wind elementals was in that war."

"Merithra, daughter of Queen Ysera, remains imprisoned behind the Scarab Wall with no word. Countless of our kin fell there, buried by the endless sea of sand. Does the price the Dreamer's guardians paid still not convince you of our stance?"

As Sola spoke, her eyes had fully taken on the moonlight color of the Green Dragonflight, and faint green scales appeared on her head and neck.

The hall fell silent for a moment. Then Jace heard a whistling wind, indistinct and shifting, followed by an elemental whisper that seemed to come from nowhere.

"Then what exactly do you want to do?"
