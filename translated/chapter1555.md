## Chapter 1555: Freedom Regained?

The Wind Prince's voice echoed through the room, yanking Jace and Grede's attention from Sora back to the altar, now cold from the icy wind.

If the voice weren't so bitterly cold — completely different from the fire elementals' restless, chaotic tones — Jace wouldn't have believed it was coming from the altar.

"Simple," Jace said. "Destroy Molten Core, Prince Thunderaan."

"I thought you were just like those beasts in the Firelands,一心 focused on devouring this last remnant of me." The voice from the cage spoke low. "I didn't expect you to be even more insane than I thought."

"You see this bottle of Water Essence," Jace said. "Duke Hydraxis of the Abyssal Council believes it can sever the connection between the Firelands and Blackrock Mountain. We brought you out of Baron Geddon's grasp to figure out how to use it."

The voice from the cage said, "You brought out a part of me."

"You don't sound very satisfied, Wind Prince!" Grede said. "Do you know what kind of risk we took to get you out of Molten Core, just to earn this chance to talk to you?"

"Is that how you speak to your god?" the cage voice said. "When you and your ancestors and your people looked to the sky from the high peaks for protection, don't forget who was listening to your prayers."

"Well, well." Grede looked at Jace. "I can't believe the great sages prayed so long and got this thing on the other end."

"'This thing' is your only hope of destroying Molten Core." The Cranial Prison let out a faint whistling sound. "Bring that Water Essence closer. My perception has weakened beyond imagination."

Jace brought the crystal bottle next to the cage. The face within the cloud seemed to twist slightly.

"Neptulon's Eternal Essence. I haven't seen a single bottle of this in I don't know how many millennia. That Duke Hydraxis still keeps these essences — I'm not surprised. A water elemental clinging to ancient order is more impressive than an ice-cold molten elemental."

"What makes him trust you so much? Every bit of Eternal Essence lost is gone forever. The Tidehunter can no longer spare even a fraction of his elemental soul."

"Is it possible, great Wind God," Grede said sarcastically, "that he trusts us because he knows we defeated Deathwing?"

Jace shot him a look. Sora guiltily glanced at the dwarf too. But neither of them corrected anything.

Prince Thunderaan had existed in this world for who knows how many tens of thousands of years — perhaps even before the Old Gods came to Azeroth. He had every right to be arrogant. Jace understood his arrogance. But he also needed some fresh knowledge to slightly improve their willingness to cooperate.

Jace clenched his fist, letting the hot blood burn in his chest no longer restrained. He picked up the cage and asked, "Can you feel the Dragon Blood Blessing within me, Wind Prince?"

"Alexstrasza." The cage voice spat out the name, its tone suddenly much lower. "That explains why he naively thought you could wield the Eternal Essence."

"So you... truly intend to eradicate Ragnaros's mark on Redridge Mountain. Even the dragons are involved."

"Absolutely," Sora said. "The Old Gods are planning to make a comeback using every possible force. I believe Blackrock Mountain is one of them."

"You speak true, green dragon," the cage voice said.

"So this really is the Eternal Essence," Jace said. "How do we use it to destroy Molten Core's rune?"

"Neptulon's incantation," the Cranial Prison said. "I still remember that spell. In the age when water and wind united against Sulfuron, Neptulon's Eternal Essence was the thing the Fire Lords feared most."

"Just a spell?" Jace asked. "Can you teach us?"

The Cranial Prison let out an intermittent whistling sound. Though its power was very weak, Jace could still sense it — it was like laughing.

"I suggest you don't waste time learning this Elemental incantation," the voice from within said. "The Tidehunter never intended for mortals to wield the Eternal Essence. Or rather, in that age, there were no 'mortals.'"

"Only elementals can use this thing to cast spells?" Jace asked. "Can I have a mage summon a water elemental in Shadowforge City and have him learn the Tidehunter's incantation?"

Grede snorted. "Summon a water elemental in Shadowforge City. That sounds like a short Bronzebeard joke."

Jace gave him a sideways look. The voice from the Cranial Prison said, "The dwarf's concern isn't unfounded. Even if I taught a water elemental here how to manipulate the Water Essence, that poor little thing could never get inside Molten Core. That's why Neptulon sought the wind elementals' help to enter Ragnaros's domain — because wind is everywhere."

"But," Jace said, "I heard Hydraxis once sent many mortals to try entering Shadowforge City to destroy the runes. They used ordinary Water Essence, but —"

"So none succeeded. They didn't even manage to destroy a single one of the seven runes." The Cranial Prison's voice said. "I don't doubt that some powerful mortal elementals or priests could, through diligent study, prolonged meditation, and attunement, eventually merge with the elements' power and drive the Eternal Essence. But can you, human?"

Jace didn't answer immediately. The fire elementals had agreed to help because of the Red Dragonqueen's Blood Blessing. But the other elements — earth, wind, water — aside from the water elementals cooperating with him because he was going after Ragnaros, none had given any response.

In other words, he really didn't have much talent for becoming a shaman, for communing with the elements — let alone merging with them.

Jace guessed that what Thunderaan described was the shamanic "Ascendance."

That was the ultimate pursuit of many fanatical shaman. Aside from players who could learn skills with a click, it was a mastery only the most gifted — indeed, the most insane — shamans could achieve. A half-baked dabbler like him probably had no chance.

Even if he could achieve it through some external means, it definitely wouldn't happen in the few weeks before the rune ritual.

So how did players use the Eternal Essence to destroy the flame runes?

Right — players included shamans, and they just clicked a button. Damn it... Jace had almost forgotten. Players' summoned water elementals could even cast frost magic right next to Ragnaros himself!

"What do we do, then?" Sora asked.

"Can't you help, Prince Thunderaan?" Jace asked.

"As I said, human." The cage voice said. "You only brought out a part of me — a remnant part. This remnant has no foundation for manipulating elemental power. Everything you see here — the currents, the cold — are just faint traces of energy leaking because I can't hold onto it. Unless..."

"Unless we find you the other half too," Grede said. "I could've guessed."

"Yes, dwarf," the cage voice said. "When the two halves Ragnaros himself shattered are reunited, I can once again grasp my power, even within this sealed cage. Then I can drive the Water Essence."

"I can't make a decision here directly — our people haven't all arrived yet," Jace said. "Why don't you teach us the spell first, and let us try."

"Sounds like you're not giving up yet. I can teach you," the cage voice said. "But whatever you decide, I hope you'll seize my other half from the Molten Lord Garr and set me free."

"They say most of your power was drained by Ragnaros," Grede said. "Without that power, can you leave this cage?"

"Take both halves of my cage to a temple of wind buried in the southernmost desert of Kalimdor. There lies a treasure that can rejuvenate elementals — the key to breaking my seal," the cage voice said. "As for my power, I can return to my domain and wait for recovery. I'm not in a hurry. Just let me return to this world, and I will repay you however I can."

Grede was already licking his lips. And Jace, who knew what kind of reward might be coming, could hardly keep his own heart from racing.
