## Chapter 1556: The Remaining Part

Jace had no idea how Isendius and Baron Geddon managed to carry that wind cage around without leaking its power. But none of them could exactly stash it away neatly in a bag and take it out.

The moment he put it into any container, the container's surface would quickly turn cold. Whether a bag or a box, in Shadowforge City's hot air you could even faintly see vapor rising from it. If he tried to walk out of the Twilight's Hammer embassy with it so openly, the flame guards outside would spot it immediately and report back to Isendius.

Isendius was willing to temporarily drop the matter of the cage because he still believed, for now, that their communication with it served the Old Gods and considered Molten Core's safety.

The moment Jace showed any intention of taking the Cranial Prison out of the embassy's altar, that fire elemental would absolutely not keep trusting this bunch of people.

So there was no choice. Jace had to record the Neptulon incantation the Windseeker taught him right there in the embassy, then return to the Black Forge Tavern with his companions to figure out the next move.

Though Jace and the others didn't dare leave something as important as the Water Essence with the Twilight's Hammer members, leaving the Cranial Prison in their care was fine for now.

After all, whether it was the flame guards outside, the dwarves of Shadowforge City, or the Twilight's Hammer — they were all still loyal to the fire elementals. The Cranial Prison was safest in the embassy for the time being.

By the time they got back to their quarters at the Black Forge Tavern, Gestae had already been re-charmed by Morofel and returned to the Dark Iron prison cells.

The group had a quick meal, went to the sleeping quarters, and closed the door. Morofel, as usual, stood watch in the corridor against approaching dwarves, making sure no one eavesdropped.

Grede was now flipping through the Elemental rune scroll Jace had transcribed from the Wind Prince's dictation, as if he seriously intended to learn this tens-of-thousands-of-years-old Elemental incantation through the elemental communication skills he'd picked up at the Eagle's Nest.

Jace had felt hopeless recording it already. If so-called Elemental incantations were really about using the elementals' language to communicate with them for their aid, then what the Wind Prince dictated was closer to actual spellcasting.

When he tried reciting it, he could sense some connection between the incantation and the Water Essence in his hand. But he couldn't find that smooth, flowing feeling he got when using a soul shard as a material to summon a demon.

"Is this incantation fake?" Grede looked up and asked.

"Put another way — 'I can't learn it'?" Vereesa asked.

"Don't put it so harshly, little elf," Grede said. "If I can't learn it, that's nothing. You weren't there talking to that weird face. He said this thing was never meant for mortals to learn."

"Can dragons learn it?" Vereesa pressed.

"As far as Thunderaan is concerned, dragons are also counted among 'mortals,'" Sora shook her head.

"Now you know how I feel, Sora." Vereesa said with an involuntary smile. Sora coughed awkwardly.

Jace understood. Vereesa had always been annoyed by how casually Sora lumped high elves in with other short-lived races as "mortals." Now that Wind Prince had finally put her in her place.

"What I'm trying to say is," Grede looked at Jace, "this Wind Prince probably just wants us to find the other half for him, take it to southern Kalimdor to 'resurrect' him, and doesn't want us using the Water Essence without him at all."

"That's not necessarily a bad thing." Jace took the scroll from the dwarf's hand. "At least it shows Thunderaan is willing to cooperate. After all, if we can't use the Water Essence, there's no way we can repay him by going to Kalimdor. If he genuinely wants to use us for his resurrection, it means he has hope for destroying Molten Core and getting out of this place."

"Then let's resurrect him!" Needle Flame said.

"You just want an excuse to visit Kalimdor," Sora said.

"Is that not allowed?" Needle Flame asked.

"I'm actually curious too," the dwarf asked. "What do you think he'll give us in return?"

"You actually expect him to give us anything?" Jace stared at the runes on the scroll, speaking low. "Don't forget, Al'Akir the Windlord was once the Old Gods' lapdog too. Even many of the elemental lords' own offspring don't get along with them. This Wind Prince only seems cooperative right now because of their shared hatred of the Fire Lord. Anyway, if he doesn't suddenly draw his sword and chop us all down when the time comes, I'll count that as him remembering the favor."

"The Wind Prince would use a weapon — a sword?" Grede asked.

Damn. Zoned out and let it slip.

Jace glanced up furtively at everyone, then asked Grede, "You've never heard any stories about a Wind God's sword or something?"

"Never. In Eagle's Nest legends, the sky gods all use hammers," Grede said.

"The legends say Al'Akir wielded a blade that released thunder like a tornado when he fought against the giants," Sora said. "So I'd say the sword story is more reliable than a hammer."

"Is that so." Grede nodded.

"We still don't know how to use this incantation. Isn't it too early to discuss rewards?" Vereesa said. "If we end up relying on the Wind Prince to cast the spell himself, do we really need to trick out the half held by the Molten Lord?"

"I don't think that's likely." Jace shook his head. "Gestae said that Molten Lord is much harder to persuade than Geddon. Plus, by now he definitely knows that the half of the Cranial Prison Geddon held is in the Twilight's Hammer's hands. If this half falls into their hands too, that means the Twilight's Hammer has the foundation to extract its power."

"Neither Geddon, Garr, Isendius, nor Shazzrah — that fire lizard lord who's been draining the Wind Prince's power — would ever allow that. The only reason we can hold this half of the Cranial Prison without stirring up discontent inside Molten Core is that just one half can't cause much trouble."

"Then we'll have to do it the hard way," Grede said.

"That's what I'm thinking too," Jace said. "Either way, when we strike down Majordomo Executus during the rune ritual, we're fully tipping our hand against all the fire elementals. Vereesa has already seen where Garr is positioned. When the time comes, we can go straight for the flame rune he's guarding, defeat him in force, seize the Cranial Prison, and let the Wind Prince regain the power to drive the Water Essence as quickly as possible. Now that we've confirmed from the Wind Prince how to use the Water Essence and that it's enough to shake Molten Core to its foundations, it'll be a race against time with Ragnaros."

"And... what about Geddon?" Sora asked.

"You didn't think I was actually going to return that Cranial Prison half to Isendius, did you?" Jace asked with a smile.

"There are still three weeks, Jace Sesso. You think that fire elemental will let you drag this out that long?" the little black dragon said. "In that embassy, he looked like he was about to punch you in the face!"

"Punching me wouldn't solve the root problem. Want him to come try exploring the secret of this Elemental incantation?" Jace shook the rune scroll in his hand. "I definitely can't learn this in three weeks. Whether he believes it or not, wrangling with the Wind Prince is a long-term process. At least we've got him talking now. But trying to pry every secret out of this ancient monster who hasn't opened his mouth in who knows how many years — is three weeks enough? Even elemental fire... can't be that impatient, right?"

"Ha. Then let Isendius go chat with the wind elemental in the embassy's altar himself if he wants. We're not stopping him," Grede said with a laugh.

"Good point." Sora nodded too.

"What if defeating Executus with the Sulfuron Hammer doesn't make the Dark Iron tribe doubt Molten Core — question Ragnaros's authority?" Vereesa said. "If the Dark Iron dwarves don't turn against him, there's no way the few of us can stand against the fire elementals of Molten Core. And the reinforcements outside can't get in."

"That's the most critical part," Jace said. "So before the rune ritual, I think we need to find a reason to meet Dagran Thaurissan in person and get a read on his attitude."

Everyone in the room looked over. Jace continued, "Now that Twilight Lord Gestae is on our side, arranging a meeting with him won't be difficult."

"For now, we basically control the Twilight's Hammer. And as for the mages in all of Shadowforge City — whether arcanists or warlocks — even if they're not willing to side with us, they won't help the fire elementals and the Flamekeepers when Molten Core is shaken. So the biggest problems are the Iron Guard regiments and the Dark Iron golems."

"As long as Thaurissan really does have the intention to throw off Ragnaros's control, like Crumblehide said, then we've got a fighting chance."
