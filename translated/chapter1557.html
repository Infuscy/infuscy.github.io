Chapter 1557: The Dark Iron Emperor’s Loyalty to the Elements

“How do you plan to meet with that emperor?” Grede asked.

Jace answered, “Like I said — going in with Gerstahn as a Twilight’s Hammer member. Regular Dark Iron merchants can’t just waltz in and see the emperor.”

“You’re not going to use Thaurissan’s magic advisor again?” Grede pressed.

“No,” Jace said. “He already took a huge risk helping us get into the Twilight’s Hammer embassy. If we ask him to smuggle us in again to discuss overthrowing the Firelord…”

The dwarf let out an annoyed grunt. “You seem pretty willing to consider that Dark Iron’s feelings. That guy’s participated in countless attacks on Dun Morogh and Loch Modan. He’s a war criminal — a ringleader!”

“Jace’s reasoning makes sense.” Vereesa looked at him. “When we finally face Executus, we’ll still need him to influence the arcanists in Shadowforge City, Grede. No matter what he’s done to the Bronzebeards or your Wildhammers in the past, he’s on our side now. We can’t risk losing him.”

“I can’t tell whether you’re actually supporting Jace’s idea or just looking for an excuse to needle me, the Wildhammer,” Grede said.

“There are so many ways to needle you, Grede, I don’t need to find an excuse specially,” Vereesa said flatly.

“Going in as a Twilight’s Hammer member — how are you going to talk to the Dark Iron emperor about defying the Firelord, Jace?” Sola asked. “I’ve heard some dwarves in the tavern say Thaurissan’s palace is full of pyromancers. Those priests of the Firelord are his assistants and servants, but also his palace’s overseers. Do you think we’ll find a chance to speak with Thaurissan privately?”

“You’re right to be concerned, Sola.” Jace narrowed his eyes. “I’m considering whether I can gauge his feelings about the Firelord without ever speaking to Thaurissan.”

“You mean using our warlock ability to sense shadow,” Needleflame said.

“Exactly, little one,” Jace said. “Gerstahn and I can design an agenda — something related to the Firelord or the fire elemental lords. We’ll enter the palace as Gerstahn’s advisors, attendants, or even guards. When Thaurissan discusses the topic with Gerstahn, I’ll sense his emotions through what the emperor expresses and judge his attitude toward the Firelord’s rule.”

“That sounds a bit…” The dwarf scratched his beard while looking at Needleflame.

“The topic has to be very sensitive,” Sola said.

“Any ideas, Sola?” Jace asked.

“I… don’t know.” Sola shook her head.

“How about starting with the fact that the Twilight’s Hammer doesn’t recruit Dark Iron dwarves much?” Vereesa raised a finger. “Look — the Twilight’s Hammer recruits tauren and trolls. Logically, Dark Iron dwarves who worship the elements just as much shouldn’t be shut out.”

“I hadn’t really thought about why before. But when Jace mentioned that Thaurissan isn’t exactly happy with the Firelord’s rule, it makes sense. If the Twilight’s Hammer is here to help the elements tighten their control over Shadowforge City, of course the city’s emperor wouldn’t want his citizens running off to join the Twilight’s Hammer!”

“Ah, I get it,” Grede said. “Get Gerstahn to use his loyalty to the Firelord to put the screws to the emperor. That’ll show whether he’s genuinely against the elementals!”

“That’s exactly what I mean,” Vereesa said.

“Looks like even though you rarely visit Silvermoon, the endless scheming of that beautiful city has rubbed off on you,” Grede said.

Vereesa shot the dwarf a disgusted look, then turned to Jace. “What do you think of the idea?”

“It’s a good angle. I think it has promise,” Jace said. “Having a Twilight Lord question the Dark Iron emperor about his attitude toward the elements — there’s a lot you can read from that emotionally.”

“But what if the Dark Iron emperor is also good at manipulating shadow, like you?” Grede asked. “He’s the descendant of Thaurissan the Sorcerer and Modgud the Black Witch.”

“As far as I know, he’s not a warlock,” Sola said. “Many Dark Iron dwarves say Dagran Thaurissan is the pyromancers’ leader, a fire mage blessed by Ragnaros the Firelord.”

“Many dwarves also say he’s a fearsome warrior who wields a legendary warhammer and won the Ring of Law championship,” Needleflame added. “I’ve asked about his fights. Nobody’s ever seen him use shadow magic.”

“Alright.” Grede nodded. “Hard to believe the dark lord living in the Dark Iron City doesn’t know black magic, but whatever the citizens say goes.”

“Then I’ll go discuss it with Gerstahn,” Jace said.

Leaving the Grim Guzzler and heading to the prison cells near the Ring of Law, Jace found Gerstahn had completely shed the panic and hesitation she’d shown after meeting Incendius. She was now utterly steadfast — steadfastly committed to doing everything possible to please Morophil.

Jace had figured out by now why she’d been so hostile to him when Morophil first charmed her. Obviously, Morophil was her master, and he was Morophil’s master — so she should have treated him with utmost respect.

But apparently the noble lady had taken him as a romantic rival.

It was almost funny. On one hand she treated Morophil as the most powerful and beautiful demon queen of the Twisting Nether, and on the other she seemed to suspect the demon queen had feelings for this human — this minor mortal…

Jace didn’t know if Gerstahn was inherently contradictory, or if Morophil’s magic had scrambled her brain.

“Has the Windseeker Prince’s Cranial Prison revealed anything, Master Slovakia?” Gerstahn couldn’t help asking as soon as Jace entered.

“Nothing particularly important yet. It’s fine. My friends and I will get him to talk about the Aqual Quintessence,” Jace said.

“Please, sit, Master.” Gerstahn smiled. “I never imagined that with your help, the Twilight’s Hammer would come to witness two elemental treasures — the Eternal Quintessence and the Windseeker’s Cranial Prison.”

“Though you are not a true member of the Twilight’s Hammer, you carry the same great aspiration as we do. I sincerely invite you to travel to Silithus after this ritual to meet our leader Cho’gall. I believe he will thank you for your contributions to the Twilight’s Hammer and invite you to join our church… Perhaps one day you could become a new Twilight Lord and inherit this world alongside the gods at the Hour of Twilight.”

Silithus… only an idiot would want to go to that godforsaken place. But Jace still managed a polite smile as he sat across from her. “Why not? I’d certainly be happy to visit Silithus and meet your leader. But as for joining the Twilight’s Hammer, I’ll need to discuss it thoroughly with Lord Cho’gall first.”

“Of course.” Gerstahn added, “And when the time comes, don’t forget to remind him that I was the one who recommended you to Silithus.”

Jace nodded. He glanced at a Twilight guard by the door and lowered his voice. “I didn’t come here to discuss the Windseeker, Gerstahn. I want to meet with His Majesty Dagran Thaurissan.”

“Unlikely.” Gerstahn shook her head. “Dagran Thaurissan has explicitly banned any demon summoners and demons from entering the palace. He knows they can steal secrets from shadow energy, so he’s extremely vigilant against all warlocks and demons… I can’t very well demand that he meet with you in the Twilight’s Hammer’s name, either.”

Jace leaned against the chair’s armrest, thinking. As the leader of Shadowforge City and a descendant of Modgud the Black Witch, Dagran’s understanding of shadow certainly ran deeper than the rulers of human kingdoms.

But his wariness of warlocks and demons also confirmed what Sola and Needleflame had heard — that he genuinely lacked the ability to conceal his own emotions. That’s why he relied on physical separation to keep Dark Iron warlocks from reading his attitude in his words. This meant their plan had a better chance of succeeding.

Seeing Jace lost in thought, the Twilight Lord added, “It’s not that I don’t want to help. I wouldn’t want Lady Morophil to doubt my influence over Emperor Thaurissan either. But while he respects the Twilight’s Hammer and respects me, that doesn’t mean he’ll do whatever I say. It’s not just me — even Executus the Majordomo can’t fully control this Dark Iron leader.”

“I understand. I don’t need to speak with him as a warlock. I just need to see him once,” Jace said. “As a Twilight’s Hammer cultist, of course, accompanying you to a meeting with His Majesty Dagran Thaurissan. Can that work?”

“But I have no meeting planned with His Majesty in the near term,” Gerstahn said.

“We’ve already thought of an agenda for you to discuss.” Jace straightened up. “The possibility of Dark Iron dwarves broadly joining the Twilight’s Hammer.”

“We discussed this privately a few months ago, Master Slovakia.” Gerstahn said. “The Dark Iron emperor’s stance was still ambiguous then. Though he agreed in principle to eventually allow Dark Iron dwarves into the Twilight’s Hammer, I could tell he was trying to stall. But that doesn’t benefit Shadowforge City — because the end is near.”

“Not just your end is near, Gerstahn.” Jace said. “There are only three weeks until the rune ritual now. You need to take this opportunity to press him again on the matter of Dark Iron followers. What I’m really interested in is His Majesty Thaurissan’s attitude toward the elements and the Twilight’s Hammer — something I can’t hear firsthand outside the palace.”

“Then I’ll try to arrange it, Master Slovakia.” Gerstahn nodded. “But on the matter of Dark Iron followers, Thaurissan refuses to discuss it openly in the throne room. He always talks about it privately in his reception chamber. In that place, he only allows each guest to bring one personal attendant — a guard or an interpreter, for example.”

“Only one person?” Jace thought for a moment. “Then I’ll go with you myself.”
