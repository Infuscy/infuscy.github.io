Chapter 1559: Twilight's Hammer? Or Dragon?

Before Gestae could respond, the emperor continued, "It's not that I'm unwilling to respect your gods and the Fire Lord's will. But honestly, there are some things I'd rather not say so bluntly."

"The bottom line is, I still don't know enough about the Twilight's Hammer as a whole. Don't forget, the Horde took our Blackrock Spire only ten years ago, and the Twilight's Hammer clan participated in that attack. So you expect me to accept you into Shadowforge City in such a short time?"

"I know the Twilight's Hammer was an orcish clan back then. But now you're a church led by an ogre, serving the elements and the Old Gods. This drastic change doesn't bother me — I'm a loyal servant of Ragnaros myself — but I also have to consider the common citizens' feelings."

"You can't expect a Dark Iron farmer who once freely moved in and out of Shadowforge City to suddenly make friends with a race that took our lands and towers not long ago, is currently besieging our city, and is trying to starve everyone to death. The gods may not understand, but we mortals are stubborn and rigid like this. Any change in perspective takes time."

Dagran Thaurissan's voice echoed through the stuffy room like dwarven drums. Jace carefully sensed the emotions within those words — a hint of discontent, a hint of pretense.

Discontent with the Fire Lord? Possibly discontent with the Twilight's Hammer's aggressive attitude over the past two years. Or discontent with Gestae always pushing this issue. Not necessarily directed at the Fire Lord.

Pretense... Jace knew Emperor Thaurissan never considered himself a mortal. That small bit of pretense was probably him playing the part of a mere mortal complaining to refuse the Twilight's Hammer.

Either way, Thaurissan hadn't revealed too many strong emotions. Jace would have to be patient and look for more signs to draw conclusions.

"But from what I can tell, Your Majesty, the citizens of Shadowforge City have largely accepted our presence and even respect us greatly," Gestae said flatly. "In recent months, there have even been those who came to our embassy or our Ring of Law outpost asking to join the Twilight's Hammer. This shows Shadowforge City needs the Twilight's Hammer."

"Even a rude Wildhammer shows some hospitality to visitors from afar — let alone a Dark Iron," Thaurissan said. "In this time of crisis, the Twilight's Hammer is willing to extend a helping hand to Shadowforge City, bringing news of the gods and greater elementals. This is enormously uplifting for our people. It's only natural for them to show welcome and courtesy. That doesn't mean anything."

"Shadowforge City has tens of thousands of residents. It wouldn't be strange if some are drawn to your faith and your ways. Occasionally someone willingly joins — not unusual."

"But since you've lived in Shadowforge City for nearly two years, you should know that most of the people here lean conservative. Accepting friendly outsiders is one thing. Changing their way of life to match the outsiders' style — or even touching their interests — is another. The two must be considered separately."

After hearing Thaurissan out, Gestae's expression darkened.

She said quietly, "Shadowforge City is on the brink of disaster. The Great Cataclysm is coming. The Hour of Twilight draws nearer by the day. I am discussing how to save all the citizens of Shadowforge City from eternal annihilation, and you, Your Majesty, are discussing how to maintain your 'way of life'?"

"The Hour of Twilight..." Thaurissan rubbed his thumb over a sapphire ring and a purple band on his other hand, then suddenly raised his eyes to stare at Gestae. "I serve the Fire Lord with all my heart. I serve the Old Gods with all my heart. That needs no questioning. But first, we need to survive. The orcs are besieging my fortress, blasting the tunnels connecting my city to the outside world, and picking off my outposts and mines one by one across the Redridge range. I must maintain the people's confidence in this war — not pin my hopes on some vague prophecy!"

"The hardships of war will be easily resolved once the Dark Iron dwarves embrace the Old Gods," Gestae said. "I don't wish to criticize the Majordomo, but Executus's lenience and laxness has held you back long enough. Now it's time for the Twilight's Hammer to spread the elements' voices, to spread the Old Gods' true words, so true believers can find their true gods. And in the eyes of the Old Gods — in our leader's eyes — all your worries are nothing but trivial, mundane concerns."

"And what if they aren't resolved?" the Dark Iron emperor interrupted. "What if I accept your proposal, let the Twilight's Hammer reshape the Dark Iron tribe, and it causes irreparable chaos? Tens of thousands of warriors who were once loyal to Ragnaros and the Old Gods are destroyed by a single decision you and I make in this room... Maybe my faith isn't strong enough. Maybe my vision isn't as broad as yours. But I can't see how far away the Hour of Twilight you promise might be!"

"Or give me a definite answer." Thaurissan glanced at Gestae, then at Jace. "When will the Hour of Twilight descend? When will our master emerge from the darkness? When will the elemental cataclysm sweep Blackrock Mountain, kill the dragon perched atop the spire, and take back my Blackrock Spire?"

"Faith means many things, Your Majesty, but it is not a transaction," Gestae said.

"I spoke out of turn, my lady." Thaurissan bowed his head slightly, then said, "But this city has been ruled by me until now, and it will follow my methods."

"One day the Fire Lord will break free from his shackles and truly manifest from the Firelands into this world. In half a month, the rune-empowering ritual will take place — one step closer to that day. By then, I believe the Old Gods will be pleased with Shadowforge City's efforts, and the Hour of Twilight will draw nearer because of the Dark Iron tribe's victory. I am extremely confident of this."

No. Thaurissan had absolutely no confidence in this.

Though his tone was firm, his voice hammering solidly into every heart in the room, the shadow energy bred from intense doubt and a sliver of fear grew stronger with every word he spoke. To Jace's perception, his words sounded like an incantation summoning shadow energy.

Either he didn't believe the Fire Lord would help the Dark Irons defeat the orcs, or he suspected the Dark Irons might not survive this war — destroyed by the black dragon, by Ragnaros's wrath, and now a Twilight's Hammer appeared to threaten his rule.

The mage Crumblehide was right. Dagran Thaurissan had long known he'd be pushed to this point someday. That's why he'd laid down all kinds of fallback plans everywhere. He just hadn't expected the Dark Portal orc invasion to make it come so soon.

"One day you will regret this hesitation and realize how many Dark Iron subjects you've doomed," Gestae said. "I will write to our leader Gul'dan about our conversation here, Your Majesty. Perhaps next time he will need to visit Shadowforge City and meet with you in person."

"Then I welcome his arrival." Thaurissan tapped the sapphire on his ring and glanced at Jace. "Last time you wrote to Lord Gul'dan, what response did you get? He sent this advisor?"

"No, Your Majesty." Jace bowed slightly. "My primary task is to bring new ritual materials and casting methods from Silithus — to better hear the voices of the elements and the Old Gods and receive their guidance."

"How promising." Thaurissan narrowed his eyes. "So, does the Twilight's Hammer recruit black dragons now too?"

"What?" Jace froze.

"Black dragon?" Gestae was also stunned.

"You seem to know nothing about this, my esteemed lady." Thaurissan's expression shifted. He suddenly stood and pointed at Jace. "This fellow is one of Victor Nefarian's accursed whelps in disguise. Arrest him!"

"This is a misunderstanding, Your Majesty." Jace raised his hands. Outside, guards had already rushed in, pressing battle-axes and swords against his neck and back.

"You thought the dragonflight's transformation trick was flawless, but you couldn't escape my eyes." The Dark Iron emperor laughed coldly. "Take him to the dungeon. We've seen plenty of big fire-breathing reptiles in the Ring of Law — lava wyrms and core hounds and such. But a real black dragon? That's a first!"

"Please trust me — I'll clear up this misunderstanding, my lady." Jace looked at Gestae. "No need to rush."
