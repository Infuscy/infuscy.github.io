## Chapter 156: Phoenix Feather

By the time Jace woke up, it was already dark outside.

He glanced at his pocket watch — barely past seven in the evening. The north really did get dark earlier than the south.

Seeing the crack across the watch face reminded him: he'd promised Amy Marin he'd bring her back some kind of souvenir. He might as well head out and see what was available. The trip was short on time as it was, and he had better things to do than lie around the house.

Even if he didn't end up buying anything, at least he'd get a sense of what his options were.

After getting up, Jace noticed his head felt a lot clearer than it had at noon. He felt better all over — even the burn on his arm didn't ache as much. He peeled back the bandage to take a look. Well. Still not great.

He headed downstairs. Dushan was eating dinner. A faint smell of moldy cheese hung in the air. Jace's nose twitched, and he traced the source straight to the food in Dushan's hand.

He had the distinct impression the man had been sitting there eating all afternoon.

Hearing the creak of the stairs, Dushan looked up and saw Jace. "Any better? Did the medicine help?"

Jace rubbed his temples. The headache was gone. "Seems to be working, actually."

"Wonderful." Dushan held up a chunk of green cheese. "Care to join me?"

Jace eyed the thing. He had a feeling eating it would unleash some unknowable catastrophe — it was more terrifying than Stormwind's blue cheese.

"Still pretty full, Master. Thank you though. I thought I'd get some fresh air, walk around a bit."

"Where are you headed?" Dushan said. "You barely get to Dalaran — you ought to see the place properly. There's a lively commercial street to the east. Winter Veil's coming up, and they've already got quite a lot going on. Don't you want to check it out?"

"Dalaran has Winter Veil celebrations too?"

Jace was genuinely surprised. The people of the Stormwind Kingdom had picked up Winter Veil early on, what with their frequent contact with Khaz Modan and the large dwarven population already living in the city. But on the Lordaeron mainland — at least beyond Aerie Peak — the concept hadn't fully taken hold yet. The Wildhammer were a lot more insular than the Bronzebeard; they weren't keen on sharing their traditions with the world beyond the Hinterlands.

Of course, the cooperation between dwarves and humans during the Orc Wars had rapidly deepened that cultural exchange. But in the Lordaeron capital, at least, they still didn't have celebrations on Stormwind's scale.

"Some gnomes and goblins are setting up over there — you should take a look," Dushan said.

That tracked. Dalaran had plenty of gnomes, and the gnomes were heavily influenced by dwarven culture. And goblins could always think of some scheme to make a buck off a trend. Par for the course.

Out on the street, Jace headed east as Dushan had suggested. Sure enough, plenty of houses and shops were already decked out with ribbons, snow decorations, and the like.

The streets were crowded. Dalaran kids in mage robes — ten to a dozen years old — ran around chasing each other with candy canes of every color. Every time a little blonde girl darted past, Jace couldn't help but watch her for a while.

Not because he was some kind of creep. He was just trying to spot Jaina Proudmoore in the crowd.

She should be about ten or eleven right now, roughly the same age as these kids tearing around.

Jaina's father, the leader of Kul Tiras — Admiral Daelin Proudmoore — had sent her to Dalaran to study magic when she was twelve. After that, she'd rarely left the city until she finished her training.

Of course, he had absolutely no idea what this world's Jaina looked like, let alone what she'd looked like as a child. He was just killing time with a silly bit of window-shopping as he wandered the streets.

Or maybe she hadn't arrived yet, and Jace was a touch too early.

Trailing one group of children after another, Jace found himself at an ice cream cart.

The kids were excitedly handing over their coppers for cone after cone. He circled the cart for a while, trying to figure out whether there was a frost mage hiding underneath it or something.

After crouching there long enough to look suspicious, he gave up and bought himself one.

A silver for a single cone. All he could do was marvel at the disparity. Dalaran kids really were loaded.

This cart was making a killing. Jace couldn't be bothered to figure out how they made their ice, either. You could probably just hire two mages — one to make ice and one to teleport it into the cart — and it'd work just as well.

The weather was on the cool side, not exactly ideal for frozen treats. But the cold also meant the cone melted slowly. Jace licked at his ice cream as he walked, glancing at the little shops along the street.

There were plenty of places selling trinkets that could double as gifts, including toy shops that didn't even exist in Stormwind — the whole street had a sort of DisneyLand vibe.

But Amy was pushing thirty. If he showed up with a children's toy, wasn't that just mocking her intelligence?

So what would be a good gift, then?

Books?

Unless he could get his hands on something from the Dalaran library, a book probably wouldn't impress an apprentice mage like Amy. She'd likely already read most of what was out there.

A wand or a staff?

The Marin family seemed pretty well-off. Any wand Jace could afford, Amy would probably turn her nose up at.

In the end, it always came back to money. When Amy had casually mentioned bringing back a souvenir, it had sounded simple enough. But now that he was actually here in Dalaran, looking left and right, he hadn't the faintest idea what to get.

Just as he was standing there in a daze, he spotted a stationery and inscription shop on the corner up ahead.

Maybe a quill would do? Something like a griffin quill — a rarity in the Stormwind Kingdom — wouldn't be too expensive here, and it'd make a decent gift.

With that in mind, Jace walked straight in. The smell of ink and paper enveloped him at once.

"Hello, mage of Stormwind, welcome. We don't get many visitors from the Wizard Sanctum."

The shop was empty. The shopkeeper was a gnome — a proper gnome, not a human with some kind of condition.

With that rapid-fire way of talking, Jace couldn't quite tell if the gnome was being sarcastic or genuinely welcoming.

The gnome was sitting in a chair that was taller than he was, facing the counter. Jace looked at the tall shelves lined up behind the entire wall and wondered how the fellow planned to reach anything.

"What can I get you, sir?"

"How much is your standard scribing paper?"

"Twenty-four silver and forty-four copper, sir."

"How many sheets?"

"A pack of twelve."

"Why so expensive?"

"Sir, our paper is all imported from Stormwind — there's a chance some of it came in on the very carriage you rode here on!"

Jace stared at the gnome for two seconds. "Right. I'd like to see your quills, please."

"We've got your standard turkey-feather quills, of course. Also duck, eagle, iron owl feather, vulture, tallstrider, griffin, clefthoof, wind serpent…"

Jace listened as the gnome rattled them off like a restaurant menu. He hadn't realized there were so many kinds of feathers you could make a quill from.

If these people could somehow get across the Great Sea to Kalimdor to source materials, they'd probably turn harpy feathers into quills too.

"And finally — phoenix feather. Which would you like to see?"

Wait. Phoenix feather?
