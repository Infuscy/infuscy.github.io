Chapter 1560: What Kind of Dragon?

Jace had been to the Ring of Law before — chatting comfortably with orc criminals, wardens, even interrogators. He'd never imagined he'd end up inside one day.

Not the cells along the main Ring of Law avenue used for prisoners about to enter the arena, but the dungeon beneath the arena.

Whether out of fear of a black dragon or respect for a powerful enemy, Thaurissan had given him a cell far better than the other prisoners'. Sure, there were spiders in the corner and scratching sounds from behind the wall of some unidentified creature. But the cell wasn't much worse than a room at the Black Forge Tavern — though in Jace's experience, the Black Forge Tavern's rooms weren't much better than a stereotypical prison cell anyway.

What Jace couldn't figure out was how Dagran Thaurissan had detected something wrong with him — and why he'd assumed Jace was a black dragon.

To avoid being recognized by Thaurissan or other Dark Iron nobles, he'd left his rings, staff, and sword with Sora and Needle Flame when he changed into Twilight's Hammer robes.

Plus his increased height, and the beard he hadn't had a chance to shave, his appearance was completely different from when he'd last come to Shadowforge City. And he'd never even crossed paths with this emperor before... Could the mage Crumblehide have told the emperor about him?

But Crumblehide didn't know he planned to meet the emperor. Jace hadn't told him — worried that the mage was too close to Thaurissan and might either leak the plan or get dragged into it.

Besides, if Crumblehide truly wanted to use his connection with Khadgar to rejoin the Kirin Tor and climb even higher, telling Thaurissan about their plans without consulting Jace would be practically betrayal — and would mean abandoning this side entirely.

If his past cooperation with Lokhtos and other Dark Iron resisters against the Fire Lord were exposed, and he'd also burned his bridge with Dalaran... wasn't that self-destruction?

Or had the emperor found out about him helping divert Thaurissan's guards away from the embassy, and interrogated him?

Actually, when Jace first entered that room, Thaurissan hadn't immediately noticed anything off about this advisor beside Gestae. He'd just given this unfamiliar Twilight's Hammer member a brief once-over before turning to talk to Gestae, though his glances grew more frequent as they spoke.

Could it be that his shadow-sensing had been detected by the emperor, making him think only a black dragon would manipulate shadow like that?

Or had he spent so much time around Needle Flame that he'd picked up a black dragon's scent without realizing it?

Speaking of which — this cell prepared for a "black dragon" wasn't haphazard. The whole room smelled faintly of metal, and the iron door, ankle chains, and wrist shackles were all enchanted with visible arcane energy.

Though he couldn't identify the specific magic, Jace could feel his soul-link with Morofel being tightly pinched by some invisible, powerful force.

Maybe the Dark Iron dwarves knew that black dragons shared some vague blood connection with their kin — especially elders — and this block was meant to sever that connection?

So Thaurissan genuinely thought he was a black dragon?

All this speculation wasn't solving anything. There'd be no progress until the Dark Iron dwarves came to interrogate him.

Then he felt the grip on his soul-link weaken slightly. Was the magic in this cell fading?

No — Morofel was getting closer.

A dark light flickered in the corridor outside. The succubus emerged from the shadows, checked both ways, then approached the door and whispered through the small window, "How do you feel, Master?"

"Never better," Jace said quietly.

"So Master enjoys this kind of confinement play too," Morofel murmured. "That little black dragon is going crazy. She heard you were arrested as a black dragon and wanted to storm the throne room and tell that dwarven emperor he caught the wrong person."

"Tell me she didn't actually do that," Jace said.

"Of course she didn't. But have you thought about how to get out?" Morofel asked. "Or do you really plan to fight your way through the entire Ring of Law without me, without Ussaleth, and without Eversong?"

"That depends on what these dwarves plan to do with me," Jace said. "Have you heard anything about the mage Crumblehide? Did he tell Thaurissan about me?"

"When he heard a black dragon disguised in the Twilight's Hammer was arrested, he came straight to the Black Forge Tavern to ask about it. As far as I can tell, he doesn't know anything either."

"That's strange." Jace said quietly. "Could he really be some hidden master of shadow manipulation, and even my sensing of his emotions tipped him off? Tell the others not to act rashly, not to blow their cover, and definitely don't let the elementals suspect Gestae. Don't let my mistake ruin our plan."

"You've already hinted that to me, haven't you?" Morofel said. "Or do you no longer trust our soul-link?"

"I don't trust this cell's magic enough." Jace sighed. "By the way, can you break this iron door? Use Doomhowl. Whatever happens, I need to be ready in case they decide to execute me."

"So you can't get out on your own?" Morofel grinned wickedly. "Breaking this Dark Iron cage isn't that hard, my dear. But I have one condition..."

Jace stared into the demon's eyes through the window. "Negotiating with me at a time like this? What is our pact, exactly? Spit it out."

"Mm... however you made Vereesa Windrunner happy, you'll do the same for me."

"Screw you. Can you guarantee it or not? Give me a straight answer!"

"Or just give me an order, Master?"

Then voices came from the other end of the corridor. Jace was about to warn Morofel to hide, but the small window was already empty.

This cell's suppression of telepathic connection was strong enough that even Jace, used to living with a soul-link, couldn't keep up with Morofel's movements anymore.

The voices stopped. Footsteps echoed through the corridor, growing closer. After a moment, a shadow blocked the small window, and a red eye peered in.

"He's right here, Your Majesty," someone outside said.

"Open the door."

"Yes."

The iron door creaked open. Dagran Thaurissan walked in, wearing a black-and-red robe, with a claw-like dark red scepter at his hip engraved with flame runes.

This scepter, along with the powerful enchantments from his ornate bracers, rings, and necklace — and most importantly, the fire burning within him — made him feel like pure flame in Jace's magical perception. Without even radiating heat recklessly, the fire in his chest made him blaze brighter than Isendius.

Jace was about to ask why the emperor dared walk into a cell and speak face-to-face with a "black dragon," then realized the concern was completely unnecessary.

Dagran Thaurissan was already the most powerful warrior and priest in Shadowforge City. In the game, blessed by Ragnaros, he could even summon Avatar of the Mountain and fight like a true Mountain King. An average whelp's skull would probably not be much tougher than an eggshell against his "Foe Reaper."

Though Thaurissan hadn't yet taken "Foe Reaper" from Marshal Windsor here...

Jace wasn't sure if he had comparable power. But seeing his exaggerated physique compared to normal dwarves, the surging power within him, and his dismissive attitude toward facing a black dragon — even if there were differences, they probably weren't huge.

"What trouble brings a thief from Blackrock Spire?" Thaurissan sneered. "Didn't Victor Nefarian mock us as a bunch of cunning, treacherous schemers who love dirty tricks? What, now the black dragonflight — who call themselves the world's noblest race — stoops to assassination? If you ask me, when survival's at stake, such methods aren't shameful at all."

"What makes you so sure I'm a black dragon, Your Majesty?" Jace asked.

"So you're going to keep denying it." Dagran Thaurissan extended his right hand, showing two rings among the many on his thick fingers — a sapphire ring and a gold-and-amethyst band.

Jace recalled that during the conversation in the room, Thaurissan had indeed paid particular attention to these two rings. He just hadn't connected that to his emotional shifts at the time. Though both rings were enchanted magical items, they carried no shadow power.

"These two rings — one is called 'Edict,' the other 'Oath,'" Thaurissan said. "They are relics left by Empress Zoe. Zoe Thaurissan was undoubtedly a foolish, incompetent ruler. She sent countless Dark Iron hunters and explorers to ancient ruins searching for whelps and dragon eggs, trying to fulfill her dream of taming dragons. Countless fine warriors' lives were wasted, and in the end, she paid for it."

At the mention of whelps and dragon eggs, plus the rings' names, Jace seemed to recall some details from the game.

Wasn't that the "Edict of Empress Zoe" from dwarven archaeology? A gray-quality ring that sold for a few hundred gold... Hadn't that ring already lost its magic from being damaged?

Clearly, this ring hadn't broken yet.

And what about the other — the "Oath" ring? Jace honestly couldn't remember the details.

Thaurissan said smugly, "But who'd have thought her deadly hobby would become my means of preventing black dragon infiltration nearly a century later? These two rings were specially made for her, for hunters sent deep into Grim Batol to track dragon blood."

"Grim Batol?" Jace said. "Is it possible that your empress's rings weren't made to track black dragons — but red dragons?"

"Not just red dragons, Slovac. Black dragon blood can't escape their perception either." Thaurissan said, but he already seemed less confident than before.

"Are you a red dragon?"

"I'm not a red dragon, Your Majesty. I'm a human with red dragon blood flowing through my veins." Jace said.

Dagran Thaurissan's expression changed slightly. He switched to Common and asked quietly, "Are you saying you're Jace Sesso?"
