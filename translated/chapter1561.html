Chapter 1561: Shadowforge City's Ancient Tradition

"You've heard of me?" Jace asked.

"The human warlock Jace Sesso defeated Deathwing... and received the blessing of the Red Dragonqueen Alexstrasza — at least, that's what Magni Bronzebeard believes."

The Dark Iron emperor paced around Jace in his purple robe as he spoke, full of suspicion, as if he couldn't believe the person before him was Jace Sesso.

Thaurissan stepped closer and looked up. "How do you prove you're Jace Sesso — and not some shapeshifter who just drank red dragon blood? Though I've no idea how you'd survive the terrible burning of dragon blood."

"I have a ring that can prove my identity. It's not with me now, but you can send someone to fetch it," Jace said.

"You stole it," Thaurissan said.

"Do you think a warlock like that would be careless enough to lose his treasure?" Jace asked.

"Even Medivh couldn't guard his treasures — stolen by the Wizard Sanctum and the Kirin Tor." Thaurissan paced further away. "And how do I know yours isn't a forgery? Human ring-making is laughably crude."

"Even if you are Jace Sesso." He turned back, staring into Jace's eyes. "Why did you come to Shadowforge City? Why sneak in disguised as the Twilight's Hammer and infiltrate my palace? What do you want? I know you're friendly with a Wildhammer savage who was in the Grim Batol fight too. But I don't think those stubborn Wildhammers would hire a warlock to assassinate Shadowforge City's ruler, avenging Magdred's dark magic on Grim Batol."

"Did Magni Bronzebeard send you, hm? Does he think a human mage can assassinate the Dark Iron emperor? Then he's even more spineless than his daughter!"

"The Wildhammers and Bronzebeards are indeed involved, but they didn't ask me to harm you," Jace said. "The Alliance plans to attack Blackrock Spire — to pull out the orcs' last nail in this world, and to once again cripple the black dragonflight's threat to the Alliance."

"They want confirmation that the Dark Irons will help the Alliance in this war."

Thaurissan let out two cold laughs and shook his head. "Then they're both naïve fools. Would the Wildhammers and Bronzebeards trust Dark Irons at their backs?"

"The Alliance can trust the Dark Irons," Jace said. "As long as —"

The Dark Iron emperor's face went cold. Quietly, he said, "I warn you, human — shut your mouth!"

"I can easily break these shackles, Your Majesty." Jace rattled the enchained ankle, producing a metallic clatter. "But can you and your tribe?"

"Who gave you the audacity to challenge the authority of the Fire Lord's most loyal servant?!" Dagran Thaurissan's eyes nearly blazed with fire. He grabbed Jace by the collar and shook him hard, his form growing taller, soon reaching Jace's neck level.

His skin turned black as metal, molten light flowing through bulging veins. Jace could feel this Dark Iron fighter was nowhere near his limit. When he fully awakened the Fire Lord's blessing, his frame would likely burst through this cell's ceiling, and with one squeeze he could tear the poor warlock in his grasp in half.

"I haven't said which shackles, Your Majesty." Jace stared into Thaurissan's eyes.

The fire in the emperor's eyes visibly wavered. He clenched his jaw, his hands trembling for a moment before releasing Jace's collar. His form suddenly shrank again.

"Nice trick, black dragon. You almost had me." Thaurissan clasped his hands behind his back and paced the cell twice. "Disguising yourself as your father's killer to stir civil war in Shadowforge City... quite the lengths you'll go. Wouldn't Victor Nefarian grind his teeth to dust — teeth more precious than gold — seeing you like this? Does he see in his dragon-dreams what his father beheld before falling at Grim Batol?"

Jace said nothing. He just watched.

Dagran Thaurissan appeared calm on the surface, like someone who'd seen through an enemy's trick. But inside, he was in turmoil.

He could no longer calmly control his emotions — and he knew that if this man truly was the warlock Jace Sesso, he'd see that emotional chaos too.

"Won't admit it? Fine." Thaurissan nodded. "You'll talk. My Flamekeepers have ways to make you talk — some that would surprise you. Look forward to it."

Having delivered his threat, the Dark Iron emperor turned and walked out. The cell door slammed shut. Rapid footsteps faded into the distance, and silence returned.

Morofel's soul-link grew fainter and fainter, soon pinched tight by that sealing sensation again. She'd probably followed him out.

He hoped the others would listen to Morofel and lie low as Dark Iron dwarves or Twilight's Hammer members. There were still over two weeks until the rune ritual. Jace could already feel the Dark Iron emperor's resolve wavering inside him. He just couldn't make up his mind.

Without a watch, Jace couldn't tell time in the cell. No windows — and even if there were windows, they'd be useless, buried deep beneath Blackrock Mountain.

He slept. He woke to footsteps outside — or maybe he hadn't really been asleep at all.

"Your Majesty." "Your Majesty."

The guards' greetings came from outside. Thaurissan's "methods" had arrived faster than expected. Or maybe he'd already been asleep for days under the cell's magic, completely unaware of the passage of time.

The door opened. Thaurissan had changed back into his gray-silver robe. The two rings were gone from his fingers. In his hand was another ring — one not suited for a dwarf.

"The Ring of the Stormwind Court Mage, Jace Sesso." The Dark Iron emperor held up the ring. "I obtained this from Lady Gestae. You did bring Jace Sesso's ring. Your disguise was thorough indeed."

"Didn't you say your Flamekeepers had special methods to deal with me?" Jace said. "I know you have a runestone that breaks all black dragon magic. Use it on me — then you'll see clearly whether I'm a black dragon in disguise."

"The things you know never cease to amaze me." The Dark Iron emperor raised his eyebrows in mock surprise. "But that thing's power is far beyond your imagination, and you're at most a pawn sent to die. Using it on you would be a waste... and what if you're a red dragon?"

"So I have a better idea." Thaurissan paced, turning in a circle. "They say Jace Sesso is an immensely powerful warlock — he not only defeated Deathwing, but also triumphed in battle against the black dragon princess Onyxia. He even fought multiple opponents and killed Dar'Khan Drathir, the high magister of Quel'Thalas. That mage was a shadow magic user famous across the continent. He once visited my ancestors and studied dark magic with Magdred, and later visited Shadowforge City many times — an old friend of the Thaurissan family."

"My apologies," Jace said.

"Life and death — even an elf can't escape that fate." Dagran Thaurissan said. "Anyway, if you truly are Jace Sesso, then this ancient tradition of Shadowforge City should be nothing to you."

Jace frowned. "I don't understand. What are you planning to make me do?"

"Prove yourself," Thaurissan said.

"Then bring it on," Jace said.

"You thought I was going to duel you?" Thaurissan wagged his finger. "No, no — I'm hardly an ancient tradition."

He turned and waved. "He agreed. Take him."

A group of Thaurissan's guards bound Jace, threw a bag over his head, and dragged him out of the cell. After an indeterminate time, the bag was removed. Before him was a hall lit by the reddish glow of lava.

The hall had two levels. The walls were covered in ancient Dark Iron symbols. Seven massive black coffins were arranged in the corners — four below, three above.

Seeing these seven black coffins, Jace immediately knew where he was.

"Welcome to the Tomb of the Summoners," Dagran Thaurissan said, standing behind him with a contingent of his guards.

"Go in. In here, all truths will be proven."
