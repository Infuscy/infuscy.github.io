Chapter 1564: Who Needs Fair Play?

Watching the three sages slowly walk toward the center, Jace touched his arm. Though he had restored some of his flesh by devouring the stripped shadow energy, it was hardly a natural recovery. The spots where the female sage Hightrail had burned him with the Holy Light still itched like crazy and ached dully. And he suspected that female sage had actually landed a hit with her axe — it was just that, being a spectral weapon, the damage wasn't as obvious as a real one. Anyway, right now his body felt pretty damn weak, but he had to face three people at once. When he said "body felt weak," it was because Jace was in the best mental state he'd been in all day. Or rather... the most extreme state. He'd just used a Life Tap to interrupt two priests' Shadow- Light conversion, and for him, Life Tap was meant to sacrifice life in exchange for massive energy and shadow intensity. Which meant that, at least in terms of casting capacity, he was in better shape now than at any point since entering the Summoners' Hall. "Now then, outsider, you will face the three of us. Sithrell, Thaurrel, and Angreil." The central sage with the shield pointed at the two sages beside him, introducing himself. But before he could finish, his soul-shadow suddenly reflected the dark arcane glow flaring up from the warlock's direction! Jace raised his hand without warning and chanted a spell at lightning speed. The three souls were caught completely off guard. A darkness so thick it swallowed all light and shadow exploded in their midst, accompanied by a wave of black mist and a horrific shriek that spread across the entire hall! The seven black coffins at varying distances rumbled and shook. The other four sages' souls recoiled from the shockwave, retreating in all directions. Even the Dark Iron dwarves watching at the far entrance screamed — some were knocked flat, others sent flying. Shadowfury. There wasn't a single ally of Jace's anywhere in the hall, so he just let loose without any hesitation... The three souls at the center of the hall: the shield-bearer barely realized something was wrong before his soul went fuzzy from the blast; the dual-blade wielder reacted fast enough but couldn't get clear in time and took the hit full-on; the mage with the crystal staff, being closest, was blasted into formless chaos — he didn't vanish on the spot, but he was knocked unconscious where he stood. "Ah! Sages! We haven't finished speaking, why did you start fighting?!" "This is an affront to tradition!" "Not a shred of dueling spirit!" Dueling spirit? You bastards are coming at me three-on-one in a damn gauntlet, and you want to talk about dueling spirit? Who needs fair play? Of course, Jace wasn't about to waste any energy or time arguing with these Dark Iron dwarves. While this Shadowfury had stunned every living and dead person in the hall, and while the mana and dark energy from his just-cast Life Tap were still at their peak, he immediately chanted the next spell while using his awareness to read the emotions of the three sages caught in the hall. Reading a soul's emotions wasn't easy, of course. The first warrior had been easy for Jace to lock onto because he empowered himself through rage — his emotions were simple and pure. But these three different sage souls were another matter. However, they had just taken Jace's Shadowfury, and the shadows hadn't yet dispersed from them. All he had to do was use those lingering shadows as... Shadow energy roared as it gathered into the warlock's chest. The shimmering dark violet between his tightly clasped hands grew blacker and blacker, even his eyes glowing purple. He chanted the incantation at maximum speed, and with the emotions he'd been suppressing until this moment, the rampaging shadows — bought at the cost of his life energy — erupted like a spinning storm! Three Shadow Bolts shot toward the three sages like demon heads trailing dark tendrils. The first bolt struck the warrior-sage's shield as he charged forward, having just recovered from the Shadowfury. Though the warrior-soul was knocked back several steps, stumbling against a pillar, and his shield's shadow dimmed considerably, his soul-form remained intact. This volley of Shadow Bolts came almost immediately after the Shadowfury. The mage-sage Sithrell still managed to turn himself into a flash-frozen ice sculpture before the shadowy orb hit him. The Shadow Bolt blasted layers of ice off the sculpture, but ultimately couldn't touch his soul. A Dark Iron frost mage. Jace wasn't exactly surprised. After all, two hundred years ago, before they reached the Redridge Mountains, the Dark Iron dwarves had lived deep in the snowy peaks of Dun Morogh inside Ironforge. The third sage — the assassin Thaurrel — erupted in shadow. Jace recognized the move: Cloak of Shadows. He'd seen Teron Gorefiend and Verlesh Darkshadow use it to deflect his magic before. But having been burned twice by Cloak of Shadows, even after losing the soul-power the Book of Evil Names had given him upon returning from Draenor, his perception of shadow magic had already risen to a new level. The assassin might have used Cloak of Shadows to absorb his Shadow Bolt and then vanished into the shadows entirely. If his opponent had been an arcane mage or a shaman, he might have gotten away with it. But his opponent was also a powerful shadow-wielder. Jace's perception keenly caught the shadow skulking in the darkness. He grabbed the ambient shadow energy that had spread across the hall from the Shadowfury and Shadow Bolt volley, condensed it into several more small Shadow Bolts, and blasted them in a concentrated barrage. Some flew wide, some exploded against walls, but most chased the fleeing shadow as Jace directed them. The assassin realized he couldn't escape. He emerged from the shadows, raised his blades to block — but it was a desperate resistance. He managed to parry one small Shadow Bolt with his spectral weapon, but the rest bypassed his guard and struck his body. Soon he was blasted into oblivion, and Thaurrel's black coffin glowed with blue light — the assassin had been defeated before he even got close to Jace. Jace didn't let up. He'd only succeeded through surprise. Taking down one didn't mean the remaining two would be easy. His peripheral vision caught the mage breaking out of his ice block. Jace immediately began chanting an Arcane Intellect spell at the iron railing in front of him, but he only got two syllables out before the mage's Counterspell killed it. The arcane energy rebounded, leaving him dizzy and his vision blurry — but this was exactly what Jace had anticipated. The mage realized he hadn't managed to cut off the warlock's shadow-casting ability. Shock and regret flooded through him, and Jace seized on that emotion. Even with the tinnitus and blurred vision from the arcane concussion dulling most of his senses, Jace still managed to use that scrap of emotion to successfully cast Fear again. The mage reacted quickly — sensing Jace casting again, he immediately wasted enormous energy on a second Counterspell. But after his shock passed, he realized his casting was a beat slower than Jace's, who'd already been prepared to cast. A terrifying shadow-scream rang out, and the mage's soul let out an ethereal howl before collapsing to his knees on the black stone floor. Perfect chance to finish the mage. Two Counterspells in quick succession meant he had no energy left for an Ice Barrier to block a killing blow. Jace seized the moment and gathered shadow energy again — but unexpectedly felt a surge of furious intent charging straight at him... Shit. Jace's heart sank. It felt like he'd been hit by a steam locomotive. His mind went blank as he tumbled over the lid of a black coffin and crashed into the iron railing. Angreil had clearly shaken off that Fear scream. And Jace, fooled by the thrill of having suppressed the mage with Counterspell, had let his guard down — forgetting a warrior's strong resistance to Fear spells. Hot lava roasted him from beyond the railing. That shield bash from Angreil had been so powerful it even scattered the shadow control he'd bought with Life Tap. Jace spat blood, grabbed the coffin beside him to stand up, and tried to get away from the lava that was about to set his clothes on fire. But Angreil didn't give him a moment's respite. He charged again with his shield raised. Jace gritted his teeth and triggered Shadowstep to retreat a short distance. Angreil's shield slammed into the iron railing, the spiritual impact bending the metal bars. Jace had no idea if he could still get up after taking a hit like that clean. Seeing Jace back on his feet and ready to chant, Angreil also raised his shield, back against the railing wall, watching him while shouting the mage-sage Sithrell's name, trying to wake him from the crippling Fear. Jace was running on fumes at this point. His first Shadow Bolt volley had only knocked the warrior back; it hadn't even broken through his shield to damage his soul. Even if he gathered another volley, the odds weren't good. Then he noticed the lava flowing like a waterfall behind Angreil. At the border of Ragnaros's domain, it was hard for him to efficiently summon free fire elementals, and even harder to cast Shadowflame. But wasn't the fire already here? Jace drove his shadow energy with his will. Angreil taunted by banging his soul-warhammer against his shield. His goal wasn't to overwhelm Jace in one go — he wanted to stall the warlock so the mage could snap out of it and fight together. What Angreil didn't expect was that Jace's magic wasn't aimed at him at all — it was aimed at the lava behind him. The slow-crawling, boiling waterfall visibly darkened as purple shadow energy infused it. By the time Angreil realized something was wrong, it was too late. The blackened, corrupted lava suddenly exploded into a cloud of blood-red mist. With a piercing roar, it blasted his entire soul backward. Shadowflame set even his soul ablaze. He burned in midair for a moment, trailing a bloody streak, before dispersing entirely. The warrior's black coffin glowed blue. Seeing this, the mage stopped resisting. He raised his right fist in a Dark Iron salute to Jace, then walked back to his coffin. The assassin and warrior's souls reappeared before their coffins one after the other, both saluting Jace. The dwarven nobles at the entrance were frozen. Whether they hadn't recovered from the Shadowfury or couldn't believe this human had actually won this round...
