Chapter 1566: The Abandoned Hall

After the seven sages' souls vanished, two stone doors slowly creaked open at the far high end of the Summoners' Hall. Thaurissan walked through, then turned and beckoned to Jace, who'd been lingering at the entrance. Jace followed. It was a deep passage lit only by braziers, with no guards or anyone else around. The nobles and mages hadn't followed, so it was just the emperor and Jace climbing higher. The adrenaline from the continuous battles was only now beginning to cool. The wounds from shadow corrosion, Holy Light burns, and spectral lacerations pulsed with every heartbeat. Jace had thought this path would lead to the Lecture Hall like in the game, but after countless steps — and a stream of discomfort threatening to exhaust his patience — they suddenly arrived at a strange, empty hall. This definitely wasn't the Lecture Hall. It was far less grand, slightly dilapidated, as though no one had been here in ages. At the far end of the hall was a terrace. Walking to its edge, you could see the magnificent black abyss outside and the lava flowing at the very bottom. "Where is this place?" Jace turned and asked. "A long time ago, Dark Iron heroes who'd passed all the trials would come here to receive the Firelord's blessing." Dagran Thaurissan pointed at the lava river in the depths, speaking in Common. "They could see Ragnaros's flames here. When those flames wrapped around their bodies, they became blessed warriors..." "Did many people pass all four rounds of trial?" Jace asked. "A few." Thaurissan said. "But their trials were different from yours. They only had to pick three of the six sages besides Master Dumrel." "Then why did I have to face all of them?" Jace asked. "Because others only wanted to prove themselves worthy of being chosen by the flames." Thaurissan said in a low voice. "What did you want to prove?" Jace didn't answer. He just nodded. He looked at the lava river below the terrace and asked, "If this is where the Firelord blessed Dark Iron warriors, do you think it's a good place to talk about this?" Thaurissan said, "It's been a long time since this promotion ceremony was held. This hall has been abandoned. With the seal of the sages' souls, the flames have long since left this place." "Why?" Jace asked. Thaurissan looked up. "When you send your most trusted warriors to the Firelord, watch them swear to the Elemental Lords and receive the 'blessing,' it feels like betrayal. I don't like it. My ancestors must not have liked it either. So the ceremony was held less and less often. The last one was decades ago." "Majordomo Executus didn't raise any objections. I suspect his trust in us has been waning too. Why else would the Twilight's Hammer suddenly come to Shadowforge City? He'd rather trust a two-headed ogre who appeared out of nowhere in Blackrock Spire than continue trusting us — the believers who've served the Firelord for two hundred years. That ugly bastard." "You received this blessing too." Jace said. "So I know better than anyone what this power brings." Thaurissan said. "Even the seven sages' souls haven't been able to shake it off after all these years. Even death couldn't free them. In these two hundred years, the sages' souls have come to feel that what they've done has nearly destroyed the Dark Iron's future. But a few of them still want to believe this destruction is actually rebirth, that this punishment is also part of the blessing." "You're telling me all this because it means you're willing to believe I'm not a black dragon." Jace said. "You were right about one thing before. The couple's rings left by Empress Zoe can only track red dragon blood. I can't be sure they can track black dragons." Thaurissan admitted. "That's why I sent people everywhere looking for ways to see through a black dragon's disguise." "Shadowforge City hasn't provoked the red dragons in years. In fact, we share a powerful common enemy right now — Victor Nefarian. I couldn't understand why a red dragon would disguise themselves as Twilight's Hammer to infiltrate my palace. So I suspected you might actually be a black dragon, and maybe the rings could identify other dragon blood too." "But if you really are that young mage, then everything makes sense. Add in the demonic spells you used fighting the sages... the rumors say you and Thunderfist defeated Nefarian's sister Onyxia face to face. Now we believe it." The Dark Iron emperor leaned against the wall, staring at the lava below in silence for a while. When a deep rumble came from beneath the earth, he narrowed his eyes and looked up. "What do you plan to do?" "The Sulfuron Hammer is in Shadowforge City." Jace said. "No, no, no." Thaurissan's dark brows knitted together. "Never mind how you made it — there's no way you can get it past all those flames and soldiers. Unless I talk to Forgewright about it." "I am His Majesty King Varian's Black Magic Advisor." Jace said. "I have my ways of smuggling things in. We plan to let that hammer unleash its true power during the rune-reinforcement ceremony." Thaurissan paused. "Executus?" "Yes." Jace said. Thaurissan shook his head. "You're not qualified to attend that ceremony, Jace Sesso. And no one can force their way up that mountain while Executus is showing himself in front of everyone. Not even you." "About that, we've already found a solution, Your Majesty." Jace said. "I just hope that when the Sulfuron Hammer truly reveals its power, those ancient Dark Iron legends come true. Without your help, the Bronzebeard and Wildhammer reinforcements can't get through, and we won't be able to use magic to destroy the runes and stop Ragnaros's fury from destroying everything." Another rumble came from deep underground. Dagran Thaurissan stared at Jace for a long moment. "What if we help and you still fail? You can blink out of a ring of fire with teleportation or a demonic ritual in a second, but what about me and my country? My people? Is that what the Wildhammers and Bronzebeards want to see?" He lowered his voice. "You forged the Sulfuron Hammer. You even found a way to destroy Executus's runes. I admire your courage and ability. When the moment comes, I will act to assist you. I can even help you deal with those mad Flamewielders, deal with that ancient temple named after my family." "Once victory is achieved, I will help the Alliance attack the black dragons' Blackrock Spire on King Varian Wrynn's terms. If Magni Bronzebeard is willing to express his gratitude to me in person, I can even withdraw my forces from Khaz Modan for the sake of this friendship and stop raiding the trade routes from Menethil Harbor, the Thandol Span, to Thelsamar." "But all of this is built on one foundation..." He paused, then continued, "If you fail to kill Executus, or if you fail to stop Ragnaros from returning to Blackrock Mountain, I will have no choice but to reaffirm my faith and pledge my loyalty to the supreme Firelord. But I will pray for you. I'll pray that you can run fast enough and save as many lives as possible." "Pray to the Firelord, Your Majesty?" Jace asked. "Very funny, Jace Sesso." Thaurissan's face was stone, but one corner of his mouth twitched. "Acceptable." Jace said. "I didn't come here to turn this whole city into a hellscape. Though there was one Dark Iron who once pointed a shotgun at my back and threatened me to hand over everything valuable I had." "Then he should be glad he didn't pull the trigger." The Dark Iron emperor said. "Before you leave, I have one more thing to give you." "What?" Jace asked. "Strictly speaking, it's not from me." He pulled a golden badge from his pocket and held it out. "You're not a Dark Iron, and you're not a Shadowforge hero who swore to the Firelord. But you've passed the seven sages' trial regardless. You deserve this recognition." Jace held the round golden badge. It bore the symbol of a crown set with a ruby. "This is the Hand of Justice badge. It was once the recognition of the High King of Ironforge for the mightiest warriors." Dagran Thaurissan said. "The Bronzebeards and Wildhammers have forgotten all this. But the Thaurissan family — the truest heir to Ironforge — hasn't forgotten what it represents."
