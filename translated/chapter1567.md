## Chapter 1567: Bluffing Through

The Hand of Justice. Jace obviously knew this famous trinket from the game. Back in early vanilla, a lot of melee DPS classes really needed it.

In the game, the story behind it wasn't as detailed as what Thaurissan had just told him. Jace only knew it occasionally allowed the wearer's attack to strike twice — a pretty unique effect for its time.

But the "real" Hand of Justice in his hands, aside from the ruby containing a trace of magical energy, didn't have any special enchantment.

It was a damn fine-looking gold medal, though. Well-crafted. He could probably sell it for a decent sum if he just wanted cash.

Then again, looking at it from another angle...

This Hand of Justice didn't mean much to him — a human who rarely entered dwarven cities. But for dwarves, especially those familiar with Ironforge's history, it could carry great significance.

If a Dark Iron warrior got this Hand of Justice and wore it into battle, they'd probably swing a lot harder — say, two extra hammer strikes per second — just to live up to the badge's reputation.

Honestly, having used every ounce of strength, racked his brain for tactics, and nearly been flattened several times just to earn this badge, Jace sure as hell wasn't going to sell it or give it to some other dwarf.

He didn't have a great impression of this Dark Iron emperor, but this was still an important treasure of Shadowforge City.

He'd just hold onto it for now. He did occasionally have to pull out Arcane Reaper to fight enemies, so he was basically half a melee DPS.

Not to mention he'd once cut down Nefarian's lieutenant, the drakonid guard Lashlayer, with a single swing of Ulthalesh.

Leaving the forgotten "promotion" hall, the emperor of Shadowforge City gave Jace a wolfskin cloak taken from orc spoils to cover his battle-damaged clothes. Then he sent two guards to escort Jace down a winding,complex secret passage, eventually emerging from a small door onto a main road where Dark Iron citizens were coming and going.

Jace remembered this place. He and Solara had followed a crowd of citizens going to a Flamewielder lecture northward to the Lecture Hall. Which meant heading south from here would take him to the Black Forge.

Once he got his bearings, the feeling through his soul-link became clearer. Before long, Morophil appeared beside him, walking like a real Succubus on her hooves. She gave his cloak a once-over. "How'd it go, darling? Got yourself some imperial new clothes."

"At least nothing went wrong." Jace looked at the fire elemental guards slowly crossing the Dark Iron bridge in the distant abyss. "Where's Sazeno?"

"That imp is with Needleflame. That way, if something urgent comes up, they can let you know inside." The demon said.

"Mm." Jace nodded and glanced at her. In that instant, that festering demon corpse from the dark hall suddenly crashed back into his mind.

The demon with its heart torn out, demon blood everywhere, demonic fire meteors falling outside the dark hall, the blood-red hall...

These images were so vivid that Shadowforge City felt like a dream by comparison.

Was that Morophil?

Jace didn't know why he was afraid of this demon dying. After Brutallus killed her, she could completely shed her flesh and return to the Twisting Nether.

But the Kil'jaeden Throne at that time seemed to have been transformed by the Burning Legion's portal into a fel-touched land — a juncture between the Twisting Nether and reality. Demons who died here would have their power heavily diminished even if they could be reborn, just like when Morophil lost most of her power the last time she died.

That must be what Jace was worried about. It had to be.

"Master?"

The Sayad followed behind him, her voice soft. "Why did I read... fear in your emotions when you looked at me?"

"Just got hit by a Fear spell." Jace said.

"You got hit by Fear? Sounds like something interesting happened in there." Morophil said in demonic. "But you still won. I picked up some bits from that emperor and a noble's conversation. Not being able to see your dark magic run wild in that tomb is causing me genuine pain."

Jace smiled faintly, but his mind was still full of images from his duel with Dumrel. Brief, but left a deep impression.

Dumrel was an incredibly powerful warlock, even as just a soul. Though his position among the Dark Iron tribe meant his combat experience couldn't match Jace's — who'd scrapped his way through all kinds of demons and orcs for years — his raw magic and the oppressive force of his shadow energy still left Jace shaken. The effects from that initial Fear exchange were still lingering even now.

From the Summoners' Hall to this very moment, those desires inside him — devouring souls, draining power — after defeating the shield-warrior, the assassin, and the mage, he'd even looked forward to fighting that last sage-leader, rather than the impatience he'd felt after beating the first warrior, desperate to just get through this forced trial of Thaurissan's.

It wasn't just the urge to fight and destroy. There was also worry and unease, and even the occasional sudden terror...

Jace had a nagging feeling that not all of this was Dumrel's doing.

Was it the shadow damage left by that nightmare he'd been dragged into at the Twilight's Hammer embassy?

Or was it the Life Tap?

"You know a lot about mental effects, Morophil. Look closely at my eyes. What do you see?" Jace pointed at his pupils.

The demon extended her slightly-too-long-for-a-humanoid neck right up to his face, tilting her head slightly, her two fiery red demon eyes darting.

"Don't get too tense. You're just hurt. Not lightly, but nothing to worry about. The Holy Light healed your flesh, but it hasn't replenished your spirit. To recover as quickly as possible, no matter how that ranger pester you tonight, don't give in..."

"Huh?" Jace shot her a sideways glare. "So you're going on the offensive now?"

"So are you finally willing to admit I'm a contender, master?" Morophil shot back.

Jace was caught flat-footed. He was about to retort when he suddenly realized — why was he arguing with a succubus about feelings? What the hell was he, asking for trouble?

But Morophil's eyes suddenly flicked into the distance. "Small problem coming. I need to hide for a bit."

With that, the demon vanished into the shadows. Jace could feel her soul-link zip away from the road like a rocket, disappearing into some unknown corner.

In Shadowforge City, there was only one thing that could make Morophil this nervous even while invisible.

A blazing, swelling mass of fire emerged from beside a Dark Iron warrior statue on the distant bridge. It didn't travel far before noticing Jace.

The moment it registered the human's presence, it abandoned its original route and began accelerating toward him.

"Lord Isendrius." Jace called out.

Had this fire elemental sensed what happened with the seven sages?

Even though Thaurissan hadn't brought any fire elementals or Flamewielders into the Summoners' Hall, it was hard to say the fire elementals wouldn't feel anything with all that fighting happening in Shadowforge City. The thought made Jace's stomach tighten.

As Isendrius drew closer, Jace stared at him, trying to read some emotion from the fire elemental's heart. But the other was perfectly calm — calm for a normal burning blaze, anyway.

"Mr. Slovakia, this way." The pair of flickering flame-like eyes at Isendrius's top tilted sideways, and he turned toward a doorway leading to a dark passage.

Once inside the passage, he turned back and asked in Common, "Ten days have passed. What did the Windseeker say?"

"He confirmed it's genuine Water Essence." Jace said. "But beyond that, he won't say anything unless we get him the other half of his cage."

"That's completely impossible!" Isendrius exploded into a burst of flames, the rolling inferno nearly singeing Jace's eyebrows and hair off. He switched to Elemental, roaring in a low voice, "That fool has no idea what situation he's in... he still can't see the reality!"

"I'm telling you, mortal, I know some of you Twilight's Hammer types think that whether it's wind or water, they're like fire — forces that will inevitably swear allegiance to the ancient gods again one day. But right now, the only one who truly wants to help the ancient gods be reborn from the darkness is the Firelord! And that Wind Prince is a treacherous, shameless degenerate just like his 'father.' He's never once thought of truly serving those ancient rulers."

"Don't think about worshiping him or expecting any rewards from him. You don't need to risk offending me or Molten Core to curry his favor... My patience has limits. I want to see progress, and soon!"

"Understood, Lord Isendrius." Jace said calmly.

Isendrius's blazing flames dimmed slightly, and his voice calmed a few degrees.

"I heard Emperor Dagran Thaurissan caught a black dragon scion who'd infiltrated the Twilight's Hammer cult. He's locked in the Ring of Law dungeon now. Do you know about this?"

"Just a dragon-worshipper who drank dragon blood." Jace said. "The seven human kingdoms are full of fools who think throwing themselves at black dragon enemies will earn a black dragon's favor."

"That's Ghestan's territory. Have your superior interrogate him properly. Don't underestimate the tricks Nefarian that madman can come up with."

With that, Isendrius slowly moved out of the passage. Jace watched his flames gradually disappear beyond the doorway and breathed a slight sigh of relief.

At the very least, this meant the fire elementals knew nothing about the seven sages' trial.

Dagran Thaurissan, as emperor of Shadowforge City, had managed to raise this many dissenters right under Majordomo Executus's nose. He definitely had his own tricks.
