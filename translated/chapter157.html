Chapter 157: Trade Name

Jace thought, You've got to be kidding me. Phoenix feather? Since when does World of Warcraft have a feather-pluckable phoenix?

"You carry phoenix feather quills?"

"That's right — phoenix feather." The gnome repeated it. "One of a kind. Very expensive. Would you like to see one?"

"I'd very much like to see one," Jace nodded.

The gnome gave his wand a little wave without even turning around to look at the shelves. A small wooden box whooshed through the air and stopped right in front of Jace.

This was getting more and more like Harry Potter by the minute. Jace couldn't resist sneaking a glance out the door. He confirmed the bricks were still red and the walls still cream-colored — otherwise he'd think he'd transmigrated straight into Diagon Alley.

The gnome flipped open the lid with a smug, disdainful grin. Jace was increasingly convinced that those grand opening lines had been a dig at the Wizard Sanctum.

Fine. Today he was going to find out exactly what this so-called phoenix feather was.

"Forty-five silver a piece, sir. This quill is expensive, I won't deny it. But it'll last you a lifetime — short of deliberate destruction, it's very hard to wear out."

Jace didn't care about the price at all, since he had no intention of buying it.

Though the price was genuinely staggering. A turkey-feather quill in Stormwind cost a few coppers at most — though it'd be lucky to last two weeks. If this thing truly lasted forever, it was hard to say whether it was expensive or not.

The moment the quill was revealed, he knew for certain this was not a phoenix feather. Though honestly, he'd known that before the box even opened.

A phoenix was one of the most powerful elemental beings in the Firelands. Every single feather burned hotter than a charcoal flame. If there'd been a real phoenix feather in here, this flimsy box would've gone up in flames — and the shelves behind it along with it, never mind all the paper and ink in the shop.

But the feather was genuinely beautiful. And very long.

Purple shot through with crimson, with a golden eyespot at the tip — like a miniature peacock tail feather. And Jace could sense a faint trace of residual magic on it.

Given its size, you could probably pass it off as a legendary phoenix plume and plenty of people would buy it.

Jace narrowed his eyes at the gnome. The gnome looked thoroughly pleased with himself.

"So where'd this phoenix fly in from?"

"I knew you didn't know anything." The gnome said, "One of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six — Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider of Quel'Thalas — keeps a true phoenix as his companion. Haven't you ever seen the Quel'dorei banner?"

"That's a fire phoenix, and this feather clearly isn't golden," Jace said.

"The banner is stylized! A real phoenix feather looks exactly like this!" the gnome insisted.

Jace laughed. "Go on, admit it, or I'll report you to the high elves for trading on Prince Kael'thas's good name! A phoenix is an elemental being — you think you can just pluck a feather off an elemental?"

"Elemental being…" The gnome muttered the words to himself, leaned back in his chair, and said, "The lad from the Wizard Sanctum knows a thing or two, doesn't he?"

He glanced toward the door, then lowered his voice. "Fine. Call it a trade name."

"A trade name…" Jace gave him a sidelong look. "A minute ago you were swearing up and down about Quel'dorei phoenixes and Kael'thas Sunstrider, and now it's a trade name?"

"Are you here to pick a fight? You just won't let it go, will you?" The gnome rapped his hand on the counter. "Fine! You say this isn't a phoenix feather — then tell me what bird it does come from. Guess the creature this feather belongs to, and I'll give it to you right now."

"You're serious?"

"Without forty-odd silver I'm not going to go out of business, lad. I don't believe for a second you can do it." The gnome muttered, "Coming into my shop and causing a scene…"

Jace reached out to touch the feather, and the gnome immediately smacked his hand away with his wand.

"Don't touch! If you haven't got the skill then get out — don't break my merchandise. I'll give you one chance. Guess."

Jace pulled his hand back and studied the feather.

This feather was remarkably different from a normal bird's to begin with. The length alone — several dozen centimeters — ruled out a good number of small and medium-sized birds right off the bat.

And looking at it more closely, it was less like a feather and more like one of the antennae off a moth's head — that was the most unusual thing about it. Its barbs were far looser than a typical bird feather.

What made it even more moth-like was that while it was long, more than half of it was soft. Only the very tip — that stiff, hard, bone-like quill — was something you could actually grip to write with. The rest of it flopped about uselessly, more like a decorative flourish that would rest against the back of your hand while you wrote.

Jace had the feeling using this quill to write would be more about looking impressive than actual practicality. If you were writing anything longer than a few lines, the soft part was bound to drag through the wet ink.

It'd probably look pretty grand for some king or general to sign their name with, though.

That ruled out birds, or harpy-like bird-creatures. A griffin or a wind serpent, maybe — and probably not the flight feathers.

Could it be a hippogryph?

No — the color was wrong. Hippogryphs were blue or purple, occasionally grayish-white. None of them had feathers this vivid.

And then there was the gnome's claim that this quill would last forever… What kind of animal grew a feather that was this soft and yet could never, ever break?

The gnome's little catalog had basically covered every feathered creature Jace could think of in the Eastern Kingdoms — he'd even included wildkin.

This was harder than he'd expected.

Watching Jace sink into thought, the gnome sneered. "Stop pretending. If you can't afford it, hurry up and go. You're costing me business."

Did chimera have feathers? Jace was trying to remember whether the chimera of Kalimdor had feathers at all.

Even if they did, they wouldn't be this vividly colored. There were the blue-and-white ice chimera that lived on snowy mountains, and the ones on Stonetalon that were roughly the color of wild boar — and they were mostly covered in down, anyway.

By now Jace had mentally run through every flying creature in Azeroth, and every flightless creature with feathers for that matter, and nothing fit the bill.

He was starting to consider dragonkin.

Wait… dragonkin?

Jace thought of a creature that normally flew completely under the radar — but whose likeness the game's operators had once used for a transmog set. And that set's hat had two floppy feathers sticking out of it!

And this little critter looked an awful lot like a moth!

"Faerie dragon antennae," Jace blurted out.

"Get out!"

The gnome swore loudly, waving his wand. The box slammed shut and rocketed back into the shelf, making the whole rack rattle and clatter.

"Did someone hire you to come here and scam me, huh?!"

Jace couldn't help laughing at the sight. He walked to the door. "Wait for the Sunstride royal guard to come knocking on your shop door."

"Hey! Hold on."

The moment he stepped over the threshold, the gnome called out behind him. Jace turned. The box came flying into his hands.

"Take it and get out! Don't come back!"
