## Chapter 1571: The Warlocks' Choice

That day, Jace, disguised as a Dark Iron dwarf, brought Morophil and Sazeno to the lowest level of the Hall of Magic. After passing through several corridors, he arrived at the stone door of Molus Blackburn's residence and knocked with his staff.

Before facing Executus — before showing his cards to the elementals — Jace still needed to talk to him. He didn't need to knock hard. The person inside could feel the powerful shadow energy in his staff, so before long, Molus chanted and opened the door, knowing exactly who it was.

"Master Slovakia, Miss Millie..." The Dark Iron warlock's fiery red mustache twitched from behind the crack in the door. "I thought you'd already left Shadowforge City. It's been so long since you last visited."

"There's been a lot going on." Jace said.

"Please, come in." Molus rubbed his hands together, turning back into the house. "Master Slovakia is here, dear! What was that snack you made last time? The one — anyway."

The dwarf sat facing Jace with a full smile. "So, Master Slovakia, what brings you here this time? I'm sure it's not because you're missing that barrel of orc ale from last time, right?"

"I came to thank you." Jace said. "About the Twilight's Hammer secret you told me before — I've verified it."

Hearing this, the smile on Molus's face froze for a moment. Then he asked, "You mean the mysterious magical stone scepter that Lord Ghestan keeps in the embassy."

Jace nodded. "That scepter does exist. And it's in the embassy."

The dwarf glanced at Morophil, who was sitting to the side with a pleasant smile, and asked tentatively, "So you got your hands on it?"

"We obtained some new spells from another continent, Blackburn." Jace said. "After a while, once I've recorded and organized them, I'll share them with you, as repayment for the scepter intelligence."

"As expected of Master Slovakia!" Molus's blood-red eyes went wide. "What kind of spells? Summoning? Curses? Or powerful destruction magic? The Twilight's Hammer claims demons are all uncontrollable mad things, which is foolish and insane enough in itself. Do they really have no demon summoning spells at all?"

"Don't get too excited." Jace said. "I told you, I still need to record and organize."

"I'll wait patiently." The Dark Iron dwarf straightened his red robes. He took a plate of meat pies from his domesticated succubus and carefully placed it on the table. "Please, try these. They're dust spider meat. We haven't been able to hunt real dust spiders since we lost Blackrock Spire, Master Slovakia. The dust spiders living deep in Blackrock Mountain — their meat has this sweet, fresh flavor. Paired with a bit of Shadowforge City's unique spicy seasoning, it's truly an unbeatable combination."

"Let me try."

Jace picked one up and took a bite. It was indeed sweet — almost like honey. Though he wasn't used to eating nearly purely sweet meat with no salt seasoning, and insect meat at that, he had to admit it tasted pretty good. But before he could fully enjoy the sweet taste, the familiar spiciness ambushed him. Jace coughed and quickly recognized the "Shadowforge City's signature spicy seasoning" as that spicy candy he'd tried at the Black Forge before.

Blackburn's succubus timely offered a glass of chilled light ale. Jace took the glass and downed a few gulps. The cold sensation instantly drowned most of the spicy candy's kick, bringing a perfectly pleasant refreshment.

Good thing it wasn't pure spicy candy — just a bit of seasoning. Any spicier and it would be like that time at the Black Forge, with no refreshment at all.

"Delicious." Jace nodded at the succubus, who smiled and bowed her head in return.

"It's my honor, Master." Blackburn said, glancing at Morophil beside him. His eyes narrowed. "You still have something to say, don't you, Master Slovakia?"

"I won't press you for details about how you got the chance to learn spells from Lord Ghestan. It couldn't have been easy on the surface. But there's no way you'd just hand me these magic secrets for nothing. What are your other conditions?"

That unexpected spiciness had made him lose control of his emotions for a moment, and the dwarf had caught it.

Not that Jace minded. He hadn't planned to hide it anyway — he just hadn't found the right opening.

"I've been thinking, Blackburn." He said. "If the warlocks of Shadowforge City had to choose between the Twilight's Hammer and His Majesty Dagran Thaurissan, which side would you pick?"

The Dark Iron dwarf's red pupils shifted back and forth. "I don't understand. As far as I know, His Majesty has great trust in the Twilight's Hammer. He's even been seriously considering expelling the warlocks. He's never shown us demon-summoners any favor..."

"Hypothetically." Jace said.

"Hypothetically." The warlock nodded. "Then I would stand with His Majesty."

"Ghestan wants to wipe us out. If she didn't have to maintain the Dark Iron public's impression of them, I'm sure Ghestan and the elementals would have acted already. Shadowforge City has quite a few warlocks — a lot more than the Twilight's Hammer, anyway. But if the fire elementals decided to help, we wouldn't stand a chance. Our best weapon against elementals is Banish..." He let out a cold laugh and continued, "But it would just banish those fire elementals back to the deepest part of Molten Core — basically sending them home for a rest."

"One time, a female warlock had a personal grudge against the leader of the mining field's flame guards — 'Conqueror' Pylon, one of Isendrius's servants. She tried to banish Pylon on the eve of the fire rune's weakest moment — about this time of year. She succeeded and was happy for less than a week before Pylon crawled back out of Molten Core and killed her. That's the power of the seven fire runes. They've basically woven a real fire elemental plane under Shadowforge City... though we don't know how the Firelord managed it."

"Remember your choice, Mr. Blackburn." Jace said. "I hope the other warlocks of Shadowforge City feel the same way."

"Is something going to happen?" Molus asked quietly. "Is His Majesty in conflict with the Twilight's Hammer? What about the fire elementals?"

"Probably not the way you're imagining." Jace said. "I just wanted to see where you stand, and maybe find a way out for Shadowforge City's warlocks. It looks like the Dark Iron tribe is about to lose Blackrock Mountain entirely, squeezed between the flames and the black dragons."

"That's the truth." Blackburn said. "The Twilight's Hammer came to Shadowforge City not just to drive out warlocks. They'll eventually get together with the Flamewielders, and their goal is to eliminate every Dark Iron. Those Flamewielders... they're not even Dark Iron dwarves anymore. They're madmen whose minds have been broken by the elementals. If Dagran Thaurissan had any sense, he wouldn't keep giving ground."

"Either way, don't leak anything about our conversation today." Jace said. "If any word gets out, any rumor starts spreading in Shadowforge City, the Twilight's Hammer can use the warlocks as an excuse for 'sowing discord' and push His Majesty Thaurissan to act against us without hesitation."

"Of course I'd never leak a word of this!" Blackburn pointed wildly at the ceiling. "Why else would I have picked a house with walls this thick as my refuge?"

"Good." Jace stood up. "The pastries were delicious. The ale was good too. I have nothing else to say, Mr. Molus Blackburn. Just remember — when the opportunity comes, seize it firmly."

"I don't know if I fully understand what you're trying to say, Master Slovakia." Blackburn said. "But I'll remember your words. Whether it's for the stronger spells and power you can give me, or simply to survive, or... for this city."

Leaving the warlock's dwelling and walking outside, Morophil asked quietly in the passage, "Aren't you planning to destroy all those ancient god spells, master? Then why promise to teach them to these dwarves?"

"Of course I'm going to destroy them. By then, whatever I say goes." Jace smiled slightly. "I'll pick a few that aren't too closely tied to the ancient gods, add some of the Auchenai priests' shadow spells, and some soul spells from the Shadow Council."

Walking out of the passage onto the bridge outside, Jace looked at the dome of the Hall of Magic. In the distance, the mage tower between firelight and shadows shimmered with a faint blue arcane glow.

Though Shadowforge City was only two hundred years old — one of the youngest cities on this continent — the flames at the bottom of Blackrock Mountain made it look so ancient and haggard, as if it had suffered through everything.

In less than two weeks, Jace and countless dwarves would decide Shadowforge City's fate. Everything in sight might be changed.

Looking at the dwarven citizens coming and going — the mages and apprentices, the squads of Iron Legion soldiers — it weighed on his chest.
