## Chapter 1573: The Majordomo's Throne

Today was the fire rune ceremony. News of the earlier date had spread throughout Shadowforge City over the past few days. The Lecture Hall was even busier than during the sermons — the grand hall was packed, crowded with Dark Iron citizens in their finest attire.

The dwarves willing to enter Molten Core and watch the reinforcement ceremony were probably the most fervent believers in Ragnaros's power among the Dark Iron.

For these people, seeing a dwarf wield the Sulfuron's might to crush the ruler of Shadowforge City — Majordomo Executus, a demigod worshipped by the Dark Iron and immortalized in countless reliefs and murals — would be the most powerful blow against that faith.

Jace's group gathered in a corner of the Lecture Hall. Ghestan was nearby, chatting and laughing with a few Flamewielders.

Compared to the dwarves, the Twilight's Hammer members here were dressed relatively simply — even shabbily — but they drew a lot of attention.

Maybe because besides normal humanoid races like humans, dwarves, elves, and gnomes, there were even two tauren and a few trolls and orcs.

To the Dark Iron, if tauren were just strange beasts from some unknown corner of the world, trolls and orcs were outright enemies.

If these few orcs and trolls weren't wearing Twilight's Hammer robes andidentity pendants, the nearby Iron Legion and Forgewright soldiers would probably have charged them with warhammers and short swords already.

But Jace's group was happy to have these tauren and others nearby. So far, few dwarves had paid attention to Jace and his companions — by comparison, they looked perfectly normal.

Besides the "freak show" appeal, another reason the dwarves kept casting odd glances their way was that this was only the second year the Twilight's Hammer had participated in the ceremony as special guests. Many Dark Iron nobles hadn't gotten used to this sudden privilege.

More and more Shadowforge citizens gathered. Jace could even feel jealousy and envy permeating the air.

The stronger these emotions were, the better. If Executus hadn't planned to replace Dark Iron dwarves with the Twilight's Hammer, Jace's group might not have made it this far so smoothly.

The pocket watch hit 11 AM. The door at the far end of the Lecture Hall rumbled, and then two massive stone doors ground open against the floor.

A Flamewielder raised his burning staff and called out in Dwarven, "The entrance to Molten Core is open! Citizens of Shadowforge City, the ceremony is about to begin! Follow me!"

When Ghestan had taken Jace into Molten Core before, they'd entered through a cave near the lava river at the bottom, similar to the game's entrance.

Shadowforge City's main entrance to Molten Core connected to the Lecture Hall. This entrance didn't exist in the game, and Jace only found out about it from Ghestan on this trip. This was the first time he'd seen it open.

Actually, Jace had always thought Molten Core, as Shadowforge City's sacred ground, seemed a bit too plain with its unguarded entrance that looked like a sewer.

Now that question was answered. That was probably just one of several passages to Molten Core. The real entrance was right here.

Following the Dark Iron dwarves through the grand doors, towering Dark Iron warrior statues stood on both sides — not just huge, but fierce-faced. Jace felt their features strayed somewhat from the Dark Iron dwarven standard, closer to the appearance of Flamewalkers.

This might also represent the wish of those who erected the statues — that one day, Ragnaros's blessing would allow Dark Iron dwarves to undergo an upgrade like the dragonkin and drakonids, becoming half-elemental beings like the fire demons. This was probably what the Wind Prince had referred to as the ultimate form of shamanism — elemental ascension.

And the wide walkway from the Lecture Hall into Molten Core itself felt like a journey from accepting the flames, to worshiping the flames, to becoming the flames themselves.

He had to admit, Dark Iron dwarves were indeed the best of the Three Hammers when it came to architecture and carving. Walking into this place, looking up at the magnificent statues beneath the towering black walls, listening to the echoes from deep within the hall — whether it was the low roar of elementals or the rumble of lava crashing against the mountain's innards —

Even the group planning to assassinate the ruler of Molten Core couldn't help feeling a sense of awe, let alone the citizens who lived here long-term.

The Twilight's Hammer members followed the Dark Iron citizens deeper. The sounds of footsteps, conversations, and spontaneous hymns praising the elementals' greatness were gradually drowned out by the intensifying rumbling.

Of course, it was probably also because the relentless waves of heat were becoming unbearable even for Dark Iron dwarves. The surrounding citizens — from robed mages and armored soldiers to blacksmiths who clearly spent their days at the anvil — all looked pained.

Only the Flamewielders at the front kept marching forward expressionlessly, chanting prayers. The people behind grew more and more silent.

Vereesa had been into Molten Core with Jace and Ghestan before, so she was mentally prepared this time. Grede's brow furrowed tighter and tighter. Behind him, Solara — even though she'd drunk a strong Fire Protection Potion like Vereesa — still looked pained, probably not expecting it to be this hot.

Needleflame, though, was walking steadily in her thick armor, her expression not much different from when she carried the Sulfuron Hammer outside. So she hadn't been completely bluffing — Molten Core's heat was nothing much to this black dragon.

Good thing the Twilight's Hammer members had special privileges and could openly drink Fire Protection Potions in Molten Core to protect themselves. After all, none of them were Dark Iron dwarves, and Executus didn't want to see these outsiders roasted medium-well before reaching his throne.

Thinking about it, disguising themselves as Dark Iron dwarves would have been far riskier than dressing as Twilight's Hammer.

As the crowd passed the last statue and went through another doorway, the traces ofman-made construction dwindled, approaching the natural cave state of Molten Core's depths.

The view ahead suddenly opened up. Even without much smoke, the cave ceiling was so high it couldn't be seen clearly in the firelight.

A heavy, rhythmic footfall approached, growing clearer. Jace looked up and peered into the distance. A giant shadow, radiating fire and smoke, was drawing near.

It was a monster wearing black iron chains and covered in solid lava boils on its skin. It had two heads, and lava-like drool dripped from its fang-filled jaws.

As it passed by, one head stared straight ahead while the other turned to observe the ceremony procession.

The dwarves beside him lowered their heads, praying in Elemental. It didn't sound like they were praising the elementals' power — more like begging for forgiveness in terror, pleading with the elementals to let them survive this ceremony.

Passing the patrolling Core Hound, the Flamewielders led the dwarven citizens to the shore of a lava river. This river led directly to a distant lava lake. This place was far more expansive than the entrance Jace had entered with Ghestan and Vereesa before — it was like another world at the bottom of Blackrock Mountain.

Against this magnificent underground landscape, the thousand-strong procession of Dark Iron citizens looked like pitiful ants crawling across a dusty floor. Especially when passing by the colossal, dull-eyed Molten Giants — they seemed even more pathetic and insignificant.

Grede walked while sizing up the Molten Giants' size, as if estimating how hard he'd need to hit to blow them up in one go. Vereesa had been observing her surroundings since entering Molten Core — she hadn't made it this deep before, and to find the fire runes, she needed to familiarize herself with the terrain.

Jace was looking around too. Though he didn't expect to remember every route, he hoped some detail or road layout would remind him of something from his past life, helping him determine where they'd entered from.

Before long, he noticed a figure in the distant lava lake.

It was a tall fire demon on an island in the lake. Its shadow was blue-purple — the color came from the powerful arcane energy it commanded. Even from hundreds of meters away, just looking in its direction, you could feel it.

That must be Shazzrah — the arcane-wielding fire demon. Through him, Jace could roughly tell what part of Molten Core they were in.

He turned to look at the opposite shore of the lava lake. That towering, deep, smoke-shrouded giant shadow should be the "mountain" Ruk'tus mentioned — Executus's throne. The Flamewielders at the front were leading the Dark Iron dwarves along the lava lake's shore in that direction.
