## Chapter 158: Smack the Orc in the Face

After leaving the gnome's paper-and-ink shop, Jace fled out into the street. He'd just scored a gorgeous quill for free — if he brought this back for Amy, wouldn't she grab him and plant a big kiss on him?

But when he opened the box and looked, he was honestly a little reluctant to part with it. A faerie dragon feather quill… Then again, why was this thing only selling for forty silver?

If it really came from a mysterious faerie dragon, Jace figured the price should have been in gold.

Forty or fifty silver wasn't cheap by any means. But faerie dragons lived deep in the primordial forests of Kalimdor, in places saturated with magic. Just getting the merchandise over here would cost a fortune in transport.

And aside from goblins, Jace couldn't think of anyone else willing to hunt down these gentle, peaceable magical creatures.

Maybe someone around here needed the rest of the faerie dragon's body for something else, and the head antennae just happened to get turned into quills on the side. That would explain the relatively low price.

Lost in thought, Jace wandered into a street-corner square. The Winter Veil atmosphere here was even livelier than outside Dushan's front door. A white-bearded dwarf in a red robe was standing on a platform by the fountain, hands on his hips, turning somersaults.

That couldn't actually be Great-father Winter, could it? The man had way too much energy.

A big crowd had gathered in the square. The various food carts were all decked out in holiday themes. Across the fountain, a cacophony of clanking metal mixed with some strange music. Jace walked over and found goblins running amusement devices for passing travelers.

Jace took a quick look around. Nearby there was a menacing-looking automatic suit-up/suit-down machine. He lingered for a while, but not a single woman tourist gave it a try, so he moved on.

Next to it was a multi-function fully automated hairdressing device, a self-propelled motorcycle, and more. They all looked pretty beefy. Plenty of bold kids and adults were willing to give them a go. Fortunately, no blood had been spilled yet.

There was one piece of equipment that nobody was touching, too — a human cannon pointed at Lake Lordamere.

Two silver a pop, the sign said. It'd launch you to the Dawn Isle at the lake's center. There was just one goblin salesman standing beside it, shouting about it. He claimed it came with a parachute to guarantee a safe landing.

But plenty of people only glanced over from a distance to watch the spectacle. Nobody dared step up and try it in person.

For one thing, they'd only guaranteed landing safety — they said nothing about launch safety. And more importantly, who the hell wanted to visit the lake's central islands in the middle of the night, the ones half-occupied by murlocs?!

Unless there was a return human cannon on Fenris Isle, two gold a ride, and if you didn't take it, you just waited around for the murlocs to finish you off.

Jace was reminded of that shady couple at the Twin Colossi in Feralas — one teleported you up the mountain, the other sold overpriced parachutes.

Either way, he was not getting swindled this time. What Jace wanted was something like a power meter — one of those carnival games where they give you a punching bag, you hit it, and it gives you a score.

Ever since he'd shoved Grede away that morning, he'd felt like his strength was different somehow. He'd been looking for a chance to test whether drawing on the black dragon's blood had actually made him stronger.

After passing a few more bizarre attractions whose purpose you couldn't guess without reading the instructions, he finally found something close to what he had in mind, tucked in a corner.

A string of big letters across the top read: SMACK THE ORC IN THE FACE.

The so-called orc looked like a humanoid mannequin with a green cloth bag tied on for a head, with some straw stuck in for orc hair. From a distance, it actually looked the part.

The sign beside it, written in crooked Common, said ten copper a punch. Score over five hundred and you'd get a piece of candy.

So it was basically ten copper for a piece of candy. That candy had better be the size of a meat pie.

But what kind of score was five hundred, exactly?

Jace sat down on the edge of a flowerbed's stone ledge and watched to see if anyone would step up.

Before long, a pretty solid-looking man walked over, handed over a fistful of coppers, and drove a punch straight into the orc's head.

The guy's form said he'd been in a fight or two. The orc's head jolted, and a scoring board behind it clattered wildly before settling on a score in the seven hundreds.

The goblin happily handed over a little green cube of candy. The man sat down contentedly to eat it, all smiles. Everyone was satisfied.

Then a boy a couple of years younger walked up, took a running start, and threw a punch that scored in the four hundreds. Unsatisfied, he tried again — four hundreds once more. He'd spent twenty coppers, and stalked off grumbling unhappily.

Though Jace had never played one of these goblin carnival games in his life, he was fairly sure he wouldn't have been as strong as that big man. The guy was tall and brawny, with a scar across his ribs — looked like a veteran.

If he walked up there now and scored higher than that guy, then something had definitely changed.

But when he braced his hands on his knees to stand, the burn on his right arm flared with a fiery pain. He'd been having so much fun he'd completely forgotten about his injury.

Forget it. Wasting money and wrecking his body over some vague, half-baked test — was it really worth it?

Just then, the boy walked up again, handed the goblin ten coppers, shook out his arms, shadowboxed the air a few times, and bounced on his feet to warm up.

Jace turned his head, curious to see what he'd score this time.

The goblins started clapping and cheering him on. The boy, who'd been frustrated moments ago, was lively again.

"Hah!"

With a loud shout, he swung and smashed his fist into the orc's head. But this time the scoring board didn't move at all. Had he broken the machine?

A mechanical arm rose up from behind the scoring board. The sandbag orc's body spun around without warning, and its arm suddenly whipped out and slammed into the chest of the boy, who'd just turned to check his score!

The boy let out a choked grunt and hit the ground. He clutched his chest, coughed a few times, and started wailing.

"Good heavens!" The nearest goblin put down his coin box and rushed over. He leaped onto the orc's back trying to work the controls, only for the mechanical arm to grab him and fling him across the street.

"Unknown aggression detected. Trade Robot 800-A initiating self-defense protocols. Protecting Trade Cartel property!"

With that announcement — clearly stitched together from a goblin's recorded voice — the fake orc stood up and took two steps. The base wrapped in rags turned out to be a pair of iron feet!

It looked like something had been stripped off it — panels were missing, its inner workings fully visible, and every movement belched a puff of smoke.

"Cease operation! Cease operation! End process! Cancel command!" A goblin beside it stamped his feet and shouted nonstop. But the fake orc paid him no mind, striding straight toward the boy lying on the ground.

Both arms broke free of whatever had been holding them with a couple of snapping sounds. The robot shook off all its restraints and charged straight at the boy at a run.

"Stop him!" one goblin yelled at the man who'd scored over seven hundred. "Black, stop him!"

"You didn't pay me to fight a robot!" Black shouted back at the other goblins. "You lot built this thing — you deal with it!"

Plenty of people in the square were looking over now. Even Great-father Winter stopped dancing.

The boy scrambled backward helplessly, trying to get up and run, only to slip on the bricks again. This time, Jace couldn't tell if it was from fear or from the pain of the fall, but he let out a wailing cry.

One of the robot's spring-loaded arms whipped toward the boy — but a scoring board came flying in and struck it off-course. The fist smashed into the paving stones instead.

"800-A detecting higher-threat target."

The robot turned its head. The ill-fitting orc head-sack had slipped sideways, revealing the cold metal face beneath and a blinking red light.

Jace was standing right in front of him. "Give me something I can hit with," he said to the goblin beside him.

The goblin handed him something. Jace looked down. It was a big wrench.

"Uh…"

The goblin had already fled.

The robot suddenly belched a cloud of black smoke from its back. Its other spring arm whipped around so fast it was nearly impossible to track. But Jace swung the wrench and deflected it cleanly, stepped forward, batted away the other arm with another wrench stroke, and drove the wrench hard into the robot's chest, stripped of its protective casing!

A deafening clang rang out. The robot staggered back two steps.

"800-A core damaged… activating backup…"

Jace smashed the wrench into the robot's shoulder without hesitation, shutting it up with one blow. The whole robot clattered apart into a heap of parts.

After wobbling for a moment, a spinning transmission device from the lower half crawled out on its own, dragging itself a few meters before finally collapsing in a puff of smoke.

Jace looked at his hands. He could feel his blood vessels throbbing with every heartbeat. This was not his normal reaction speed or strength — every aspect of him had stepped up a full tier.

The robot, for all its stiff movements and questionable accuracy, was fast — genuinely fast — and it hit hard enough that trading blows with it wasn't easy.

These goblins had clearly just taken a decommissioned trade robot with combat capability, given it a quick retrofit, and stuck it out here as a punching bag to make money.

This time Jace was certain: drawing on that black dragon's blood had brought real changes.

But was it the black dragon's vitality merging with his own? Its shadow fusing with his shadow power? Or both, lumped together under the same heading?

Murmurs rippled through the crowd around him. The goblins pointed fingers at each other as they ran over to haul away the completely wrecked robot's parts.

Jace held his aching arm, helped the terrified boy to his feet, grabbed his things, and fled the scene.

Dalaran guards would probably be here soon enough. Who knew how much trouble the shadow energy radiating from his body would cause him.

On the way back to Dushan's place, it hit him — he could use the black dragon's attack on him as a reason to explain the shadow energy clinging to him!

When he went to the Hinterlands in a while, he could even tell the Wildhammer dwarves that he'd fought a black dragon, been injured, and that was why he was steeped in dark energy.

That… was a near-perfect excuse.

Though that excuse probably had a shelf life of a few months at most. After all, if you were still radiating shadow energy that long later with no obvious side effects, people would slowly start to get suspicious.

Still, it'd be enough to cover any surprises that might come up on the Aerie Peak trip.
