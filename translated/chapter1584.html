Chapter 1584: Ice and Fire

With the food and water the wizard had left, everyone perked up considerably.

Even for two dragons like Sora and Needlefang, drinking ice-cold water and eating some magical food in this scorching place was enough to relieve the pressure of constant fighting and the oppressive heat.

Shortly after the wizard left, Jace felt Morophil getting closer.

The heat in Molten Core was nothing to a demon who'd spent years in the Fel Fire lands. And with Molten Core in chaos — flamewakers busy choosing leaders to follow, elementals in complete disarray — no one cared about a stealthy demon skulking through the corners. Morophil had no trouble.

Still, the Wind Prince's cage had quite a presence. After spending time in Garr's cave, the temperature here was noticeably lower than outside, and cool breezes often passed through...

But if Geddon and Itherion had charged into Shadowforge City, how had Morophil evaded their search?

"Teacher Morophil!" Needlefang shouted excitedly, looking toward a patch of shadow.

"Hello, little one. Good to see you all still in one piece." Morophil emerged from the shadows and tossed Sazeno over. "Though you look like you just escaped from some demon lord's dungeon after who knows how many years of slavery."

"Enough chatter, demon," Vereesa said. "Where's the other half of the sealed skull?"

"I was waiting for you to sing my praises properly." Morophil spread her hands. "I risked everything to sneak into Molten Core, gave up the joy of battle, made such a huge sacrifice, and all I get—"

Jace noticed Morophil wasn't holding the sealed skull, and there was no chill around her — quite the contrast with Grede, who carried the right half.

The sealed skull in Grede's hand had even opened its once-closed eye. An icy blue light pulsed in its socket, as if craving something. This was a state Jace hadn't even seen when they'd communed with Thunderaan in the Twilight's Hammer embassy.

"You don't want to know how she brought that half-skull in here..." Sazeno whispered.

"Shh." Morophil pressed a finger to her lips, then suddenly began to heave as if vomiting. Her throat and belly writhed with each spasm, and air rushed from her mouth — some of it crackling with electricity, answering the right half in Grede's hand.

"Damn it, I knew it!" Grede's eyelid twitched.

Morophil spat the left half of the cage into her hand and held it out to Vereesa. "Here it is."

"What — what —" Vereesa stared wide-eyed at her hand for a long moment. When Morophil had spat out the Tome of Blaspheming Names in Nagrand, Vereesa hadn't been there. Clearly, this scene was a bit much for her.

Grede quickly shoved his half of the bindings into Jace's hands. "This was your idea. You got the Eternal Quintessence. Jace Sesso. So you get to finish it."

Jace took a deep breath and steadied himself.

To everyone else here, this was just piecing together the shattered soul of a fading ancient elemental. But to Jace, it meant something different.

Of course, given everything he'd been through in this life — life and death situations — assembling a pair of rare items in an online game was hardly an achievement.

But his memories of his past life had a natural filter, and that part of his experience was so deeply tied to everything in this life that he couldn't separate it objectively.

After all, these were the Bindings of the Windseeker.

As he watched the two elemental faces slowly open their eyes, he could almost hallucinate an orange border around them.

Jace took the sealed skull from Morophil and placed the two halves on a stone in front of him. The airflow swirling around them grew faster and faster, causing his robe and cloak to flap wildly, forcing him back a couple of steps.

The lava lord's cave grew colder. The lingering flames and scorch marks on the fallen molten elemental remains nearby extinguished one by one. The chamber's firelight dimmed, but was soon replaced by the flash of thunder and lightning before them.

"I have waited... too long for this day..."

The roar in the elemental language of air drowned out the distant core giant's bellowing, echoing between the stone walls of the great hall.

"That doesn't sound like a friend speaking," Vereesa said.

"It really doesn't. Sounds more like some evil..." Grede muttered.

Jace stared at the two skull halves being drawn together by the currents, his expression serious.

Grede's concern wasn't unfounded. Wind Prince Thunderaan was an absolutely terrifying menace to all of Azeroth.

Even if he disagreed with his father Al'Akir and refused to bow to the Old Gods, even if he was Ragnaros's mortal enemy — that didn't mean he had any good intentions toward mortals.

But no matter how cold and ruthless his nature, he was in Molten Core now, a prisoner of the fire elementals. If he wanted to be reborn, gain freedom, and escape Ragnaros's control, he had to help Jace destroy the fire runes. Lying, cheating, or playing mind games would do him no good.

This was also why Jace was willing to trust him and rely on him to use the Eternal Quintessence.

Wrapped in cold wind, the blurred left half and the solid right half drew together, blurring into a mass in the swirling ice wind. The cold light in both eyes grew brighter and brighter. The cave, once filled with lava and fire elementals, was actually turning cold.

"I never thought the ones who would reunite my shattered soul would be mortals."

Thunderaan's face had merged back into one. Beneath the swirling ice wind, his face shifted between a cold mask and a lion formed from storm and air.

"I take back my contempt for Hydraxis the Tide Duke. He believed mortals had come to dominate this world, and that one day the elemental lords would have to face that reality seriously. That was why he placed his hope of destroying Molten Core in mortals. I always thought he was just making excuses for his cowardice."

"Put away your pointless superiority, Wind Prince. Whether mortal or immortal hardly matters right now." Jace took the water elemental's crystal bottle from Morophil. "The fire runes are right ahead, and Ragnaros's servants are doing everything they can to awaken him. If you've recovered enough power to drive the Eternal Quintessence, now is the time."

Thunderaan laughed a few times. "Take the Eternal Quintessence before Ragnaros's fire runes, demon summoner. Open its seal and chant the elemental tongue incantation: Zaran, Dino, Kan, Kuso, Tadilom."

Jace nodded and walked toward the fire rune. The rune's ring of fire seemed to sense something, blazing more violently, as if trying to push Jace and his Quintessence away. Advancing against the fiery shockwaves grew harder and harder.

When Jace felt he could go no further, he gripped the bottle's seal and chanted the incantation Thunderaan had taught him.

After he finished, he opened the bottle. The ring of fire was blasted backward a huge distance, as if struck by something. Jace felt his hand go numb from the sudden cold, losing all sensation...

Behind him, the Windseeker's skull began chanting in a language Jace couldn't understand — some ancient elemental tongue, impossible to trace. The sound was so deep it wasn't like speech at all, more like waves in a storm, thunder in a downpour... Even Grede, who knew wind elements best, stood stunned. The others were even more affected.

A pale blue light streamed from the bottle's mouth. The Wind Prince's voice continued. The ring of fire was disrupted, trembling like a candle in a draft. The earth began to tremble. Cracks appeared in the ground beneath the fire rune and the wall behind it.

The whole of Molten Core was shaking. It reminded Jace of when Executus had been forced to interrupt his empowerment ritual. The power of this fire rune was weakening!

The mad roar of fire elementals rang in his ears. Jace could directly feel that explosive rage bearing down on him from beneath Blackrock Mountain. But he held on to the crystal bottle, until the Wind Prince's chanting suddenly stopped. The fire rune before him blazed one last time, then dimmed completely, going dark — dead.

"Feel this helplessness, dear Ragnaros."

The Wind Prince laughed. In that slightly crazed laugh, the earth trembled more and more violently. Walls crumbled. Stone pillars fell from the ceiling. Vereesa looked up. "We need to get out of this cave, fast!"

"Go!" Jace shouted.

Five more, he thought to himself.
