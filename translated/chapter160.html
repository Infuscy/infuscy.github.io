Chapter 160: The Old Craftsmen's Quarter

Jace followed his father into the city. Along the way, his dad talked nonstop about everything that had happened over the past two years — from the orc stragglers in Silverpine Forest and Tirisfal Glades, all the way to the Horde's escaped warchief from the underground city.

That confirmed it for Jace: the one who'd escaped was indeed Orgrim Doomhammer.

But he wasn't all that concerned about it right now.

His father talked about how his mother went to the chapel every single day, how she'd corner anyone coming back from Stormwind to ask about conditions over there — whether the orcs had returned, and the like. When she heard there were gnolls in the Redridge Mountains, she'd gone straight to the priest in tears.

His dad mocked the prison guards and called his mother a coward, all at the same time — but his hand stayed wrapped tight around Jace's and wouldn't let go.

At first Jace felt a little awkward. But as he listened, slowly, he didn't want to let go either.

The Old Craftsmen's Quarter was one of the capital's oldest districts — and the most run-down. The ground was nothing but mud, rain or not, as if the lake beneath the walls had seeped up through the whole neighborhood.

Sesso led his son along the base of the wall, dodging the carriages barreling through. Jace hopped along behind him, glancing across the street toward the city center.

Before his eyes rose the familiar rows of brown-brick towers — the blue and white domes, the colonnades and the spires.

Servants and attendants pacing between windows, glancing down from time to time. He remembered used to press his face against the window and stare right back at them as a kid.

Those people had been wondering whether the little idiot had something wrong with his head. Jace had been testing whether he had some kind of mind-control cheat or other.

"Hey! Hennie! Come see who's back!"

Jace's hand, wrapped around the dried meat, was sweating. He watched the old house door ahead bang open, and a thin woman poke her head out.

That was his mother, Hennie Sesso — the daughter of a small Brill farmsteader.

Since she was little, Hennie had known that with her older brothers and younger brothers around, the farm would never have anything to do with her.

Her father hadn't liked her. And the reason her stepmother had taken such pains with her was that she thought the girl was pretty — she had to learn how to dress herself up and snag some noble from the capital, or at least a big family like the Ardomanns.

From the time she could remember, she'd fought back fiercely against her parents, all the way up until she ran away from home and secretly married a capital guardsman — the tall, strapping man in front of her now, Vick Sesso.

Every time she thought about her childhood, it was hard to imagine that barely a decade and a half later she'd become such a crier.

In truth, she was only in her thirties now.

His mother lifted her skirts and ran over in one breath, throwing her arms around Jace. It took some effort on his part to catch her. In Jace's memory, Hennie had always been a woman who looked small but was surprisingly strong.

She stood there in the mud and held him for a long while, saying nothing. Until his father stood to the side and murmured, "Alright, alright. It's cold out here, Hennie. Let Jace inside."

"Don't you talk!" she snapped. Then, lowering her voice: "You haven't written me in months, Jace Sesso."

"Paper's too expensive, Mom." Jace said, helpless. "If I wrote you every time nothing was wrong, I couldn't even make rent."

"You could've had someone pass along a word."

Hennie grabbed her son's hand and, wiping at her tears, started pulling him back inside.

They hadn't gone more than a few steps when she suddenly turned back. "What happened to your hand?"

Back in Dalaran, Jace had planned to regale his parents with tales of how he'd fought valiantly in Southshore, how he'd joined a Wildhammer dwarf and a host of brave Lordaeron warriors to slay a terrifying young dragon.

But seeing the worry in his mother's eyes, not a single word of the grand story he'd prepared came out.

"A lamp fell and caught fire on the boat. Burned my arm," Jace said.

"How unlucky," Hennie said. "Let me take a good look when we get home. I know how to treat burns."

"The Southshore priests already treated it, Mom," Jace said quickly. His mother gave him a look, and only just barely nodded.

Once they were inside the house, Jace caught the familiar faint smell of sausages and felt as if he'd never left.

Stormwind, Elwynn Forest, the Wizard Sanctum, the Redridge Mountains, elves, dwarves — it all felt like an illusion, shut outside the door with a click along with the cold wind.

His mother brought over a plate of sliced bread spread with savory sauce, and set down a plate of sausages beside it.

Jace stared at the table. His stomach was already growling.

As a kid, he'd always felt his mother's air-dried sausages carried a faint whiff of old socks beneath the meaty aroma. They tasted great, but the hanging-to-dry process had always made him reluctant to stay in the house.

But now he understood. That was the smell of home.

"You're wearing a mage's robes." His mother pointed at the potion bandolier across his chest. "Did you learn magic in Stormwind?"

"Yeah. I originally just went to make money off the mages, but I didn't make much — they had me learn magic instead."

"I always knew you had a gift for magic when you were little." His mother sat across the table. "I've seen children who can do magic, Jace. You were so quiet as a child — more sensible than the others. You didn't make a fuss, didn't cry. Just like those children who are born knowing how to call on the arcane, only they haven't found the trick of it yet."

"It's really nothing special," Jace said. "They just thought I was working hard and were willing to teach me a few things."

The reason he hadn't cried was that by then he'd already recovered the memories of over twenty years from his past life. He'd suddenly realized he'd transmigrated into an ancient world and was still in a daze, naturally. No expression whatsoever.

Especially those first few years, when he'd realized he didn't have a single cheat to his name — every path he tried hit a wall. He'd seriously considered just lying down and giving up entirely.

Now, Jace actually felt like his psychological age was younger than it had been back then. Having finally caught a glimpse of room to grow, he even still had a little fire to keep pushing forward.

"Since the mages taught you magic, show your old man a trick," his father said, leaning against the doorframe and watching.

Jace could only pull out the one arcane spell he had that was even remotely visual — Light.

He murmured an incantation and conjured an orb of arcane light at his fingertip. Its brightness instantly overpowered the lamps in the room, bleaching the walls white.

His mother clapped delightedly. Jace felt himself blush — he couldn't concentrate at all this time, with both his parents watching.

If he'd pulled out an orb like this at the Wizard Sanctum, Master Marin would've mocked him for sure.

But every truly dangerous spell he knew, he couldn't cast a single one of them in the house. Any one of them and the place would be done for.

His father set three bowls of vegetable soup on the table. The family finally sat down together. Jace chatted about some of the things that had happened to him in Stormwind — mostly trivialities, of course. The truly life-and-death stuff, he didn't dare mention a word of.

If his parents knew he'd been fighting for his life against gnolls atop Stonewatch, staring down a pack of a dozen wolves in Elwynn Forest, or trading blows with an orc shaman on the shores of Lake Everstill, they'd never let him set foot outside the city walls again.

And he absolutely could not stay here. Because his ultimate goal wasn't just to leave on his own — it was to get his parents out too.
