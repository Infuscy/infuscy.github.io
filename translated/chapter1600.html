Chapter 1600: The High Places of Blackrock Mountain?

Thaurissan's palace hadn't been badly damaged in the tremors of Blackrock Mountain, but it was crawling with Thaurissan's guards and spies everywhere. Living here meant better care, sure, but it was incredibly uncomfortable too.

After the battle in the Molten Core, telling day from night in Shadowforge City was no longer an impossible task. In the areas where the glow of molten rock was less bright, you could look up and see the cracks high above, even the light filtering through the thick black clouds.

That evening, Jace and the others left Grede -- who spent ninety percent of the day unconscious -- in the palace in Vaelastrzasz's care and headed to the Black Iron Tavern for a good drink, to wash away all the pain and exhaustion accumulated in the Molten Core.

And, of course, to see what exactly they'd pulled out of Shazzrah's hoard.

The idea of going back to the Black Iron Tavern for drinks -- and checking the loot together -- had Needleflame practically bouncing off the walls. She hopped and skipped the whole way, peppering Vereesa and Sora with questions about what had happened in Magmadar's cave.

Vereesa, finally having an audience for her story of "The Last Rune," certainly wasn't going to pass up the opportunity.

"Lucifron. That's definitely the fire elemental I saw when I first went into the Molten Core with that Twilight's Hammer leader Gestayn and Jace," Vereesa said. "Fire elemental. It really is a fitting name. Much better than 'Flamewalker,' the name they give themselves. Suits those ugly monsters."

"Because black dragons are the true Flamewalkers." Needleflame's tone carried a villainous confidence.

"Flamewalker seems a bit too light for a black dragon," Vereesa said.

"Evil, arrogant, and mad Flamewalker," Jace muttered under his breath.

"Is that so." Needleflame grabbed Jace's belt and yanked hard. "The evil, arrogant, and mad black dragon saved your life the day before yesterday. Sure, Grede is strong, but without me... you think those legs of his could have carried him from Ragnaros's flank all the way to the hammer?"

Jace had to admit she had a point.

He could even picture it -- Grede sitting up from under Ragnaros's last shockwave, shouting for Needleflame to take him in for another charge.

And he could hardly describe how exhilarating it felt when, at the moment he was certain he couldn't withstand the final hammer blow, he suddenly heard Needleflame's screech -- which in hindsight was probably the sound of Grede's boot connecting with her face.

"Thank you, Needleflame."

The moment she heard that, Needleflame -- who had been all swagger a second ago -- pursed her lips awkwardly.

"I thought you were going to make fun of me for eating dirt from his kick! You left all your sense of humor in the Molten Core, Jace Sesso!"

Needleflame rattled off in Common with a hint of draconic accent, then turned her face away, let go of Jace's belt, and hurried ahead.

Vereesa glanced sideways at him, as if blaming him for having teased Needleflame too much in the past. Jace could only shrug helplessly.

Sora watched the black dragon's retreating back and smiled.

"I'm not saying Vaelastrzasz is the only one who finds this strange... I find it strange too. I think after that Old God eyeball incident, she's really changed a lot."

"Really? I haven't noticed at all," Jace said.

"Then no wonder she complains about your attitude," Sora said.

She looked at Morophil, who was silently following behind the three of them.

"Speaking of which, where did that Twilight's Hammer leader Gestayn go?"

"My charm magic was basically completely broken when Executus's ritual was disrupted," Morophil replied. "Now I can't sense her at all. Maybe she's dead. Maybe she managed to escape the Molten Core."

"Could she have escaped into Blackrock Spire?" Vereesa asked.

Morophil shook her head silently. If even the demon who had controlled Gestayn for a month couldn't answer that, Jace and Sora certainly couldn't.

"Speaking of Blackrock Spire..." Sazeno wobbled around in Jace's bag, looking up at the heights. "Don't you think the whole chaos in Blackrock Mountain is missing a key group?"

"The black dragons and the orcs," Vereesa said.

"They both stayed put," Jace said.

"Not exactly staying put." Sora's voice was thoughtful. "Vaelastrzasz told me the upper parts of Blackrock Mountain also suffered serious tremors. The lava lake in the Chamber of the Forge of Souls is directly connected to the Molten Core. There were extremely violent tremors there too. Many orcs died in the fire. Blackrock Spire was the same -- a lot of it collapsed. Those orcs probably don't have the spare energy to launch an attack on Shadowforge City right now."

"No wonder," Vereesa said. "But this is Deathwing's son's last chance to gain an advantage. Once the Dark Irons recover from the chaos, plus the Wildhammers and Bronzebeards, and then the Alliance reinforcements -- especially the Sons of Lothar and the Kirin Tor... I know Alleria will follow Turalyon and Khadgar to Blackrock Mountain too. There's no doubt about it. The orcs in Blackrock Spire can't hold it anymore. The black dragons' defeat is right in front of them."

"If I were him," she continued, "I would fly away from Blackrock Mountain right now. Go to another continent and join my sister, Princess Onyxia. That way they might still have a place to raise their offspring. If he's as smart as Sabellian described him, he should know that's the wisest choice."

"So why hasn't he done that?" Jace asked.

Vereesa frowned slightly, unable to answer right away.

"Maybe, like his brother who insisted on staying in Draenor, he chooses to stay in Blackrock Spire and share the fate of the lair." Sora walked onto an iron bridge as she spoke. "Black dragons' attachment to their lairs and eggs might be the strongest among all dragonflights. Shadow's corruption didn't make them abandon that instinct -- it twisted it instead."

"But that's just my guess. Maybe he simply thinks the orc army can still hold the Alliance back with Blackrock Spire's defenses. Maybe he isn't as cunning as his brother thinks. Maybe he's just plain crazy."

"Could be any of those," Vereesa said. "Maybe he's already made some elaborate preparation, waiting for us to enter Blackrock Spire and give us a surprise."

"Can't rule that out," Jace said.

"Don't forget about 'the other prince,' Jace," Sora said. "We promised Thunderaan the Wind Prince we'd take him to Kalimdor."

"That'll have to wait until Blackrock Spire is dealt with," Jace said. "Do I really have the time to take him across the ocean right now? Unless some mage is willing to help teleport him."

Vereesa said: "Even with just a sliver of his soul left, that guy can still channel the power of the Eternal Essence. No mage could teleport him across the Great Sea."

"Then that's his own fault for being too powerful," Jace said. "No rush, Sora. We'll go back to Kalimdor eventually."

"It's not that I'm in a rush." Sora lowered her voice. "Be careful with Dagran Thaurissan. That emperor doesn't just want Grede's Sulfuras. He's got his eye on the Bound Soul of the Windseeker too."

What, does the guy want a second legendary too?

But considering what Jace had said to Thaurissan before, and the attitude he'd shown, it seemed likely Thaurissan would rather let Grede give up the Mountain King position and get Jace's group out of Shadowforge City's power center -- to stop interfering with his rule -- than stir up more trouble over a sealed wind elemental.

But he was a dwarf. For these guys, getting completely carried away over treasure and terrifying magical items wasn't some far-fetched fantasy. That went for kings and emperors too. So Sora's concern wasn't unreasonable. They needed to stay on guard with these Dark Irons.

Chatting, the group walked into the tavern. The Black Iron Tavern seemed even livelier than before the Molten Core battle. But there were more bandaged wounded. The air was thick with the smell of blood and the stench of shadow-corrupted wounds.

Before Jace and the others arrived, Shadowforge City hadn't been entirely without outsiders. But having this many "tall ones" show up at once was bound to draw attention.

A drinker near the door squinted, recognized Jace and the others, and whispered something to the dwarf beside him, pointing in their direction. More whispers followed from further away.

Word spread. One told two, two told four. Before the new arrivals could even pick a table, the whole tavern had gone quiet. Only a few weak coughs broke the silence.

Staring into all those pairs of blood-red eyes, Jace had to admit he was a little creeped out. Especially while he was still recovering from his injuries.

Then a Dark Iron dwarf raised a foaming mug.

"To you. Outsiders."

"And to you, outsiders. That was one hell of a damn good fight."

"I don't know if I'll ever see another scene like that in my life."

"Tomorrow you'll be dead from pissing yourself, you leaky bastard, so of course you won't!"

"Pah!"

"To you, outsiders. Jace Sesso! I think I pronounced that right!"

"Can't find a mage this strong inside Shadowforge City either. Worthy of us taking the time to figure out how to say his name right, even in that sissy Common!"

"That's right!"

"Damn straight!"

The Black Iron Tavern erupted into noise again.
