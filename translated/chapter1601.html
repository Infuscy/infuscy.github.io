Chapter 1601: Outside the Tavern Door

The Dark Iron dwarves' enthusiasm exceeded Jace's expectations. Many soldiers who had taken part in the Molten Core battle stepped forward to share with everyone how these outsiders had appeared out of nowhere and saved them all while they were fighting those mad fire elementals.

Chasing the fleeing Executus. Descending from above near the ridge runes. Crushing a large force of fire elementals guarding the flame runes. Attacking Golemagg's lair alongside hundreds of elite Bronzebeard and Dark Iron warriors. The chaos-shadow of Magmadar's cave.

Listening to various Dark Iron soldiers vividly describe what had happened -- how desperate the situation had been, how heroic Jace and the others looked -- far exceeded Jace's own memories of events. Some embellishment over drinks in a tavern was inevitable. As long as it wasn't on the level of "a mighty green dragon wiped out the entire core hound pack with a single breath of poison mist," Jace and his group couldn't be bothered to correct them.

When the conversation turned to the fight against the Firelord who had tried to exterminate the Dark Iron dwarves -- that final battle -- many Dark Iron dwarves eagerly recounted what they had seen and done.

The heroic feats of the two Mountain Kings. The way Dagran Thaurissan himself had struggled free from Ragnaros's grip and fought back. The demons' twin blades that the fire elementals couldn't stand against. The Windrunner's magic arrows that could shatter runes. The green and red dragons' blessings, covering hundreds of soldiers at once. The young black dragon who had shown no fear in the face of an evil god. And a human whose center of gravity didn't look all that stable, somehow tanking two hits from Ragnaros...

Even the normally sharp-tongued Dark Irons were unstinting with their praise. Between the compliments, they also made sure to "casually" mention the brave deeds they themselves had performed alongside these heroes. Some even couldn't resist coming up to get a closer look at Vereesa's Belanor, or Jace's terrifying staff -- so dark it could eclipse even Ragnaros's flames.

But faced with all this praise, Jace couldn't quite feel happy.

Not because of the risk of exposing Ulthalesh. But because he couldn't stop thinking about that period after Ragnaros fell, when they fled from the collapsing, exploding heart of the Molten Core.

If one of them had actually died...

Grede not being here at the tavern made the lingering fear worse. This was exactly where he should have been. The more Jace thought about it, the more he had the illusion that Vaelastrzasz saving Grede had been some kind of dream -- that in reality, that dwarf was already gone.

"Jace." A soft call pulled him out of that half-dream, half-reality daze.

Vereesa's arcane eye glanced lightly toward his hand. Jace noticed that blood was seeping from the wrappings on Ulthalesh. The Dark Iron dwarves around them, caught up in drinking, singing, and excited boasting, hadn't noticed anything unusual yet.

She nodded toward the tavern door. Jace nodded back.

While the dwarves were exclaiming over Needleflame actually being a black dragon returned from Draenor, clamoring for her stories about that world, Jace and Vereesa quietly slipped out.

The noise of the Black Iron Tavern was suddenly shut behind the iron door. The familiar sounds of metal striking metal and rumbling ground from Shadowforge City rose again. Vereesa watched the dwarven teams hauling stone across the balcony ahead and smiled.

"This is my first time in Shadowforge City. It's overturned a lot of stereotypes I had about its people. Based on what I used to think... I would have said the Dark Iron dwarves here would never acknowledge that we outsiders saved them from Ragnaros."

"Well, at least one stereotype is correct: these Dark Iron dwarves really do worship power." Jace slumped onto the warm stone steps. "That's what made them worship Ragnaros. And it's what makes them admire warriors like you, Vereesa. Elemental or Quel'dorei, it doesn't matter. I think that's the key reason Dagran Thaurissan was willing to trust us. And the main reason the Dark Irons see us as heroes."

"So they'll see Grede as the greatest hero too." Vereesa's smile slowly cooled. She looked at Jace. "You don't need to worry about him anymore, Jace."

"There isn't a better healer than Vaelastrzasz anywhere in all of Blackrock Mountain. I wouldn't even describe him as 'highly skilled' or 'medically excellent.' That red dragon is just plain powerful. He can not only save that dwarf, but even if there are Dark Irons who don't want to thank Grede, or remnants still worshipping Ragnaros who mean him harm... that red dragon could crush them into paste without breaking a sweat. Even Dagran Thaurissan himself."

"I don't doubt that at all, Vereesa." Jace paused. "Actually, Vaelastrzasz thinks I... might be losing control of my shadow power."

"He has to consider what you were facing, Jace." Vereesa's voice was soft. "Fighting an enemy like Ragnaros -- each of us had to throw off all restraints and give everything. Otherwise, the difference between one thought and another could mean the difference between life and death."

"When I drew my bow to aim at Ragnaros's second-to-last rune, he had already noticed my movement. He was ready to consume me and all the dwarves around me in flame. I didn't know if this one magic arrow of mine could actually shatter that rune. I wasn't sure if I'd still have room to dodge the Firelord's counterattack if I insisted on firing that arrow. At that moment, I had to make a choice."

"I released the arrow. The rune shattered. That one shot blocked his attempt to attack you -- even though I was hit by his shockwave immediately after, barely surviving because I ran a second too late. But at least I held on for those two extra seconds. These past two days, though, I keep dreaming about that moment. Afraid that I might not have persisted. Afraid my hesitation could have killed you and everyone."

"If you don't fully use your power, if you don't give everything you've got... then everyone dies. And then there's no chance to worry about controlling shadow power."

"Every fall starts with an unsolvable problem," Jace said, almost to himself.

Vereesa blinked.

"If I ever really become a fallen warlock..." Jace looked up. "Don't hold back."

"Fallen." Vereesa repeated the word. "Does that mean you and your shadow have become something that frightens everyone? Or does it mean you've finally set out on that path of pursuing power at any cost, even if it destroys what you hold dear?"

"The latter. Maybe both," Jace said.

"Then the world must have gone wrong," Vereesa said, almost without hesitation.

Jace smiled. "You sound more fallen than I do, Vereesa."

"Do you remember," the ranger asked, "why you first touched shadow?"

"Like when I first left Quel'Thalas -- and ever since -- it's been about crushing any ambition and attempt of outside enemies to harm the Quel'dorei, to invade Quel'Thalas's borders. Specifically, orcs and trolls. Draenor. Blackrock Spire. All part of my goal."

Jace looked down, thinking.

"More or less to fight back against a warlock who tried to make me wet myself in the street with Fear."

Then he shook his head.

"No. It was to earn a few silver coins for next month's rent. So I took a Curse of Agony instead. In short, for money. To... get my parents settled in Stormwind sooner. So they could live peacefully in Elwynn."

Vereesa pressed on: "Why did you help Varian Wrynn attack Blackrock Spire?"

"If we let the black dragons and orcs keep building up in the Burning Steppes, one day they'd bring the war to Northshire, Redridge, even Stormwind," Jace replied.

"In that case." Vereesa's eyes met his. "What kind of world? What kind of people would force you -- Jace Sesso -- to abandon everything you've ever stood for and 'fall'? Why wouldn't they just let you and Mr. Vik Sesso and Mrs. Hennie Sesso live quietly within Stormwind's walls?"

"If that's your reason for fighting back against this world, then I will stand by your side without hesitation, Jace."

"Same here, Vereesa." Jace's voice was quiet. "Maybe I'm just feeling a bit uncertain about the upcoming attack on Blackrock Spire. Especially after hearing Vaelastrzasz's warning."

"You're not the only one who needs rest, Jace." Vereesa's voice was firm. "Never mind the red dragons -- the Alliance has been preparing for this. From Silvermoon to Dalaran to Stromgarde to Stormwind..."

"From what I know, not only is Alleria coming -- do you remember Nathanos Marris? He's coming too. I think Sylvanas hopes he can distinguish himself in the final offensive of the Orc Wars and shut up all those gossips in the kingdom. Just like you did."

"Maybe the first time they heard I was running after an unknown warlock to hunt Teron Gorefiend, some of them doubted my choice. But now? How many Quel'dorei inside Bantinorel and out still dare say the person I love is 'some common mortal of unknown origin'?"

Jace smiled sheepishly. Vereesa went on.

"I know the Menethil royal family has been dragging their feet this time -- finding every excuse to shirk responsibility, even using 'mosquito bites in the Wetlands' as a pretext. But in the end, they'll send someone too. This Alliance is still called the Alliance of Lordaeren, after all. Not to mention the mages of the Kirin Tor and the Silver Hand. Really, we don't need to list everyone else. You should ask yourself: how confident is Nefarian, in single combat, against Archmage Khadgar -- the man who crippled his father with a single spell?"

"We've already made our greatest contribution to the assault on Blackrock Spire -- securing Thaurissan's support. If you ask me, instead of moping around here, you should be thinking about the wedding."

At the end, Vereesa glanced sideways at Jace. He snapped his head up as if someone had grabbed him by the hair.

"Wedding? Did we set a date?"

"Nathanos Marris..." the elf said quietly. "Why do you think Sylvanas is so eager to send him here? She can't wait any longer!"

"Oh, damn," Jace said. "I'm suddenly more nervous than when I heard Ragnaros was coming. What do I do?"

"Like hell you are." Vereesa jabbed him in the ribs with her elbow.

"Ow. I haven't recovered yet, you know." Jace winced. "I have one more question."

"Ask." Vereesa was twirling a small dagger, flashing it in a blur.

"If Quel'Thalas and the Kingdom of Stormwind ever went to war, which side would you pick?" Jace said. "A few hundred years is hard to predict, you know."

A glint of cold steel, and the dagger was pressed against Jace's chest.

"Neck or heart, take your pick, Jace Sesso." The ranger stared at him. "Unless your Shadow Shield incantation has four words. Let's see if your mouth is faster than my hand."

"C-can I choose neither?" the warlock glanced at the blade's tip.

"Of course you can." The ranger gave a satisfied smile.
