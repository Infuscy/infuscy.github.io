Chapter 1603: Before the Celebration

Today was the day Dagran Thaurissan had set for the victory celebration. Since the Hall of Learning had been badly damaged in the battle -- and that place had represented the faith of Ragnaros for hundreds of years -- Thaurissan had moved the venue to the still-intact Black Anvil Bridge.

It had barely been a week since Ragnaros's defeat. Many of the seriously wounded soldiers still couldn't stand on their own. Even Grede needed a crutch and assistance to get out of bed. But the citizens of Shadowforge City didn't seem to mind. Many had been unable to wait for this celebration as early as a few days ago.

Last night, Grede had Jace help him all the way back to the Black Iron Tavern. Today, he took off the clothes the Dark Iron dwarves had given him over the past couple of days and put on his own Wildhammer gear.

If he was going to attend the Dark Iron dwarves' celebration, he insisted on making his origins as clear as possible.

Word had it that Ironforge had sent other important emissaries -- besides Muradin Bronzebeard -- to Shadowforge City to express goodwill toward Dagran Thaurissan. So for the Three Hammers, this was a pretty big occasion.

Last time, when Grede picked up Sulfuras in front of the elite warriors of all clans at Thaurissan's Temple, Jace had felt like a half-cooked duck leg.

So this time, he needed to clean himself up properly too. Wear something decent. As one of the few humans in Blackrock Mountain, he also represented the image of Stormwind -- even Lordaeron.

"What do you think, Vereesa?"

Jace turned to look at Vereesa. The ranger-lord was wearing a typical Quel'Thalas high noble's favorite -- a blue short robe threaded with gold. Combined with her silver hair -- singed a bit here and there, but tied up in a way that made it look even neater and sharper -- she made Jace look like a lumbering idiot by comparison.

Still, Vereesa squinted and smiled.

"Very handsome, Jace. If you showed up at a Silvermoon ball looking like this... I might not feel too confident myself."

"Really?" Jace scratched his chin. "Even with this messy patchwork of singed beard?"

"What messy beard?" Vereesa deliberately looked him over. "All I see is the glorious battle scars from your death match with Ragnaros the Firelord."

"Easy for you to say." Jace muttered. "I'd rather the Firelord had burned me cleaner. Finding a razor in Shadowforge City right now is harder than finding a Wildhammer."

"What kind of brain comes up with such a stupid joke?" Grede frowned in their direction.

He had lost most of his beard in the Molten Core too. When he was completely charred black earlier, it hadn't seemed that off. But now that he was washed clean, his remaining beard trimmed decently, and dressed in his familiar old gear again, he just looked wrong.

"Don't look at me with that punchable expression, kid." The dwarf grumbled, his voice still a little hoarse. "Unless you want me to use this crutch to poke a third eye in your forehead."

"Much obliged." Jace straightened his collar. "You know, in an old legend, the third eye on the forehead belongs to gods who can see through illusions. For example, a war god widely known in the heavenly court."

"Court of heaven?" Vereesa asked. "You mean Skywall? Is he a son of Al'Akir the Windlord too?"

"No," Jace said. "More like a Pandaren god."

"Don't act like I'm ignorant. I've heard goblins talk about those guys who drink from double-headed hammer-shaped wooden pots." Grede adjusted his shoulder. "But if I were that Pandaren war god, I'd rather have an extra arm than an eye. That way, besides Sulfuras and the Sky-Shatterer, I could carry a third weapon too. Like that magic sword--"

"What sword?" Jace squinted at him.

"That lava sword! The three-pronged one! What did you think?" Grede turned his head. "That dagger the fire elemental tried to kill me with. I told you to grab it. You didn't lose it, did you?"

"Of course not," Jace said.

"Good!" Grede headed for the stairs, raising a hand to the two dragons down there. "Morning, Sora. Little Black Dragon. I need to get out of here as fast as possible. That warlock's dark energy is bad for my health."

Sora and Needleflame guiltily followed Grede's descent with their eyes, then looked at Jace. Jace glanced at Vereesa beside him.

"So you didn't tell him."

"None of my business." Vereesa kept her voice low. "I didn't get anything. Why should I have to say it?"

"Damn, that's actually a good point." Jace grumbled. "He's still not doing great. We'll tell him when he's recovered a bit more. It's for his own good."

Vereesa nodded slowly, but her face was full of the look of someone who just wants to watch the world burn. Jace shot her a glance, picked up his staff, and headed for the stairs. Sora and Needleflame up ahead both wore expressions of "nothing to do with me" as they tugged each other along, snickering, and fled down the stairs.

"Jace." Vereesa caught up. Her gaze followed the two young dragons until they disappeared into the tavern entrance, then she spoke quietly. "How are you feeling?"

"A lot better, Vereesa." Jace's voice was low. "Don't listen to that dwarf's nonsense about dark energy. I'm glad you were there to listen to my downer talk that day."

"Don't say that. I know what it's like to have no one to talk to, Jace." Vereesa looked at the steps beneath her feet. "Even if I can't fully understand the pressure you're carrying. Or some of the emotions I can't quite describe."

She glanced at the succubus peeking into the tavern from the doorway, and lowered her voice further.

"If there are things you find hard to say even to me, tell Sora. Or even Morophil. She might understand your situation better than I can. Either way... at least don't bottle it all up inside."

Jace paused for a moment.

"You're willing to trust that demon now."

"She is a demon. But when facing Kil'jaeden, she chose to be loyal to you. When facing the Firelord, she protected you." Vereesa's voice was firm. "Dar'Khan Drathir is my kin. He's unquestionably a Quel'dorei noble. But he chose to strike you down when you were at your most isolated and helpless."

"Maybe I still carry deep-rooted Quel'dorei supremacist ideas. And prejudice against demons. But I'd rather trust what I've seen with my own eyes, what I've experienced myself."

Clanging sounds came from the warehouse inside the tavern. Jace looked in that direction and realized he hadn't been to see Lokhtos Darkbargainer since leaving the Molten Core.

But the Firelord was defeated. The Thorium Brotherhood would be moving back to Shadowforge City from Thorium Point soon. Why was Lokhtos -- the spiritual leader of the Brotherhood -- still hiding in this tavern?

"Should we go, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

"You go ahead with them. I'll catch up," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded and hurried after the others. Jace turned and walked into the warehouse.

He weaved through the crates, reached the door, and knocked on the iron. When Lokhtos opened the door and looked up, his blood-red eyes flickered.

"You're here, Jace Sesso."

"Master Lokhtos." Jace inclined his head. "Sorry I haven't come to see you these past few days. Things have been complicated. And I thought... you'd already left."

"The Dark Iron dwarves have won. The Thorium Brotherhood has earned the right to return to Shadowforge City. I don't understand why..."

"But I'm still a traitor." Lokhtos smiled, sitting down in the iron chair beside him. "Sit. Speak, Mr. Sesso."

"Dagran Thaurissan came to see me a few days ago. He asked me not to go outside anymore. Actually, he didn't need to tell me. That's what I want too. To most people in Shadowforge City, I'm already a figure in the history books. And not one of the better-regarded ones. Not to mention I've gotten used to this life now. I've forgotten how to be a leader. Forgotten how to..."

He looked at his hands.

"Look at me. I'm practically fused with this blackrock and iron ore."

In Jace's heart, the spiritual leader of the Brotherhood -- who had silently supported the rebellion against the Firelord all along -- didn't deserve to be forgotten like this.

But looking at Lokhtos's smile now, completely free of all his burdens... a Dark Iron who had lived nearly three hundred years, witnessed the Three Hammers' division and reunion, the destruction of Redridge, the rise of Blackrock Mountain, the Firelord's curse, the fall of Thaurissan's city, countless deaths and exiles of the Thorium Brotherhood, and now this... who could say this simple life wasn't what he truly wanted?

"But." Lokhtos's voice was rough and deep. "Sitting here doesn't mean I don't know what happened in the Molten Core, Jace Sesso. I can hear the fire elementals' roar. The Firelord's bellow."

"Grede Thunderfist defeated Ragnaros with the Sulfuron Hammer, and seized the flame power of both him and his warhammer," Jace added. "The thing in his hands now is the true power of the Molten Core. The true Sulfuras, Master Lokhtos."

"You're right. But what truly made all this happen was you." Lokhtos's voice was beginning to tremble. "I believe Lord Grede Thunderfist and everyone in the Thorium Brotherhood -- even Dagran himself -- knows this very well."

"I still remember... the day you came to my door, looking for a way to break Onyxia's illusion."

"I don't know why. When I saw that human mage standing before me -- young enough to be a child -- I felt that things... were about to change. Maybe when a dwarf lives to my age, you get some premonitions from unknown sources."

"Truth is, I was a little afraid you would come find me, Jace Sesso." The dwarf laughed. "Because this poor, old man has nothing left now but muddled ramblings. Nothing valuable left to give you as thanks."

"Keep that Dark Iron ring. It's rough and heavy, I know. But at least it's not worthless."
