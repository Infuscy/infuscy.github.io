Chapter 1608: Demon? Or Black Dragon?

"Are you sure this is some kind of plague?" Jace asked. "No, it's just a guess," Vaelastrasz said. "But we can't ignore the possibility. Corpses are the source of corruption and necromancy, Jace Sesso. With Ragnaros around, it wasn't easy to gather strong necromantic energy in Blackrock Mountain. Now that the Molten Core is destroyed, Nefarian might be using these corpses to collect necromantic power as an energy source for some vile plague." "Now that I think about it, I did notice some Blackrock soldiers collecting recently killed Dark Iron dwarf corpses on the Burning Steppes. But at the time I didn't connect it — I just thought the orcs wanted the dwarves' weapons, armor, supplies, or maybe food stores." Collecting corpses, spreading plague among the besieging army as an obstacle... the more Jace thought about it, the more it felt like the Scourge's playbook. Especially reminiscent of the Lich King Arthas's tactics in another timeline, when the Alliance and Horde armies reached the foot of Icecrown Citadel. "Do you know about the necromantic plague in Northrend, Vaelastrasz?" Jace asked. "Of course," the red dragon said. "Prince Korialstrasz went to Northrend specifically to investigate that plague. And wasn't it you and Somnily who encountered problems there and notified the Dragonqueen? Why? Do you think this necromantic plague is connected to the one in Northrend? Are you suggesting Nefarian's already made a move in Northrend, or that whoever's behind that plague has allied with the Black Dragonflight?" "I don't think someone holed up in Blackrock Spire as their last stand could launch a war against an entire spider kingdom," Jace said. "During your time in Blackrock Spire, did you notice Nefarian — or the Blackrock clan's chieftain — meeting with any suspicious outsiders?" "Like I said, Nefarian spends most of his time at the very top of the Spire. He rarely comes down." Vaelastrasz said. "Rend Blackhand, ever since his brother died, has been eating out of Nefarian's hand, obsessed with breeding powerful new dragon mounts. He can't even be bothered with orcs anymore. He's basically handed Blackrock Spire over to the dragonkin generals Drakkisath and Vim'salaar. I'm more familiar with those two servants. As for these three, I can say for certain: no mysterious high-ranking dark magic teachers have visited them in recent years." The Lich King's necromancy came from the Helm of Domination and Kil'jaeden, and the Nathrezim dreadlords that Kil'jaeden had sent to Azeroth to assist — or rather, to monitor — the Lich King. Thinking this over, Jace tilted his head. "Please go down and check those orc corpses for traces of demonic magic. Eredar, Nathrezim." "Yes, master." Morophil flapped her wings and leapt gracefully into the crevice. "You think this is demon work?" Vaelastrasz asked. "I discussed this with Archmage Khadgar of the Kirin Tor before," Jace said. "Considering the large-scale use of corpse-manipulating death knights during the Horde's invasion of Hillsbrad... and those death knights were creations of Gul'dan and his teacher Kil'jaeden. It's possible the necromancy in Northrend is also connected to the Burning Legion." Vaelastrasz crossed his arms and pondered for a moment. Below, Morophil had already looked up. "It's hard for me to say if this is related to the Eredar or Nathrezim. They might have just had their residual souls and necromantic energy drained by some powerful dark magic, leaving a trace of dark arcane residue. It could be orc warlocks, or it could be demons..." "Or it could be Nefarian himself," Vaelastrasz said. "Nefarian and his father are both powerful necromancers, Sesso. And I think with his arrogance... it's unlikely he'd go to demons or orcs to learn necromancy." Vaelastrasz had a point. In the game, Nefarian could fight players while using terrifying shadow magic to resurrect all the dead drakonid corpses into skeletal soldiers to continue fighting the invaders. Jace thought he might be too fixated on Prophet Velen's earlier warning, and on the destruction the Lich King Ner'zhul had caused in another timeline. Every time he encountered traces of necromancy, he automatically connected it to Kil'jaeden and the Burning Legion. Noticing Jace's pensive expression, Vaelastrasz said, "Whether this is a demonic attempt to spread plague among the Alliance, or Nefarian's final defense, it's all just necromancy in the end. The Silver Hand and the Church of the Holy Light have experience dealing with death knights. We children of Queen Alexstrasza have some methods against necromancy too. The next step is to prepare ourselves to counter their dirty tricks." Beyond the red dragonflight and the Holy Light's methods — if this really was some kind of plague — Jace remembered the purification potions they'd gotten from the furbolgs during their second trip to Kalimdor, specifically designed to cleanse the necrotic plague of the Frostsaber Crags. But those purification potions... they were essentially made to purify the dark energy that Ulthalesh had seeped from the Emerald Dream. He wasn't sure if they'd work on Black Dragon magic. At the time, Jace had also considered that those potions might come in handy during a Scourge invasion. But back then, he hadn't really been thinking about going to Draenor. Onyxia hadn't come to Stormwind yet. Who would have thought that a few years would pass in the blink of an eye, and the threat of plague would be looming right overhead? "Collect some organs from those orcs, Morophil," Jace said. "I still have some of that enchanted cloth Shazzrah used to wrap magical items. We'll bring this stuff to SI:7 and see if we can find a way to deal with it." "Leave a copy for me too," Vaelastrasz said. "Of course," Jace said. "Don't worry too much, Sesso." The red dragon looked down at the lava flowing in the deep crevice below. "Once we conquer Blackrock Spire and destroy Nefarian's wicked schemes, I'll head straight to Northrend to help Prince Korialstrasz. Whatever entity is behind that necromantic plague — whether it's part of Kil'jaeden's plan to invade Azeroth, or whether it's Sintharia, Nefarian's mother, causing trouble — we and the Kirin Tor will get to the bottom of it." "Much appreciated, Vael," Jace said. "Nothing to appreciate." Vaelastrasz said. "Northrend is a nice place. The air is cool. Endless snowfields stretching beyond sight, unbroken mountain ranges and forests. More importantly... lend me your Azure Song when you get the chance, Sesso." "Why?" Jace asked. Vaelastrasz raised a bushy red eyebrow. "Maybe Azure Song's blessing will introduce me to a few beautiful, elegant Azure ladies. I'll say your sword was corrupted by Prince Nefarian's dark magic and needs to be purified at the Nexus..." Jace took the wrapped orc limbs from Morophil. "Maybe it'll be Kalecgos who handles it instead?" "That whelp? Forget it." Vaelastrasz waved dismissively. Leaving the abandoned Dark Iron mines and heading toward the Chamber of the Ironforge, the stench of orcs grew stronger. Compared to it, even the smell of volcanic brimstone wasn't so noticeable — though that was mainly because Jace had just crawled out of the Molten Core and Shadowforge City. He had to admit, right now he almost found the Chamber of the Ironforge refreshingly cool. But this coolness wasn't just relative. With the flame protection rune broken and the Molten Core collapsed, Blackrock Mountain — and the Burning Steppes and Searing Gorge beyond it — was genuinely cooler. Not to mention it was almost Winter Veil, the coldest time of the year on the continent. The cold winds from the Dun Morogh peaks were pressing south, accelerating the cooling. After Vaelastrasz used magic to disguise Jace as a Blackrock orc and changed his own form to match, they emerged from a rocky crevice into the Chamber of the Ironforge. Looking from here toward the high bridge leading to Blackrock Spire, the damage here seemed even worse than in Shadowforge City, which was closer to the Molten Core. Two of the thick chains had snapped, one crashing down to smash a massive dwarven statue on the cliff face. The Chamber of the Ironforge itself, supported by stone pillars and chains, had tilted, looking like it could tumble into the lava lake at any moment. The high bridge was badly damaged too. Few orcs remained on the parts that still held together. Most of the Blackrock soldiers who'd previously been camped or stationed on the bridge had withdrawn to Blackrock Spire. When the smoke thinned, you could make out the figures patrolling the corridors on the Spire's cliff face, their torchlight flickering through the windows. Though Blackrock Spire and the bridges within Blackrock Mountain were also Franclorn Forgewright's work, no architect's creation could withstand centuries of repeated conflict. Still, this worked in their favor. The disaster in Blackrock Mountain had forced the orcs to abandon this area — which could collapse further at any moment — and retreat back into Blackrock Spire. This gave them plenty of space to leave Shadowforge City and exit Blackrock Mountain, without relying solely on the narrow secret tunnels. Groups of orc laborers were hauling stone from outside Blackrock Mountain into Blackrock Spire. Jace and Vaelastrasz mingled easily with these laborers and the passing Ragefire soldiers, carrying the orc limbs tainted with necromantic magic out through the gates of Blackrock Mountain and onto the bridge leading to the Burning Steppes. Looking out into the distance from here, though the ground was pitch black and smoke billowed everywhere, the sky was no longer a oppressive black mass of clouds blocking everything. Instead, it was hazy gray. It immediately reminded Jace of the classic old movies or cartoons about heroes hunting evil dragons that he'd seen in his last life. Like when the Demon Lord was defeated, the black castle would transform into gleaming white marble in a golden light, sunlight would break through the dark clouds to shine on the earth, and the world blighted by the Demon Lord would seem to come back to life. He knew full well there was still a "Demon Lord" perched in the tower beside him. But that didn't stop him from feeling a surge of relief. After all, he could finally see the sky again.
