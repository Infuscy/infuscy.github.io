## Chapter 1609: Morgan's Watch

Vaelastrasz was a fully grown adult dragon. There was no way he could fly over the Burning Steppes without catching the attention of the Black Dragonflight.

To keep Nefarian from knowing that a red dragon had slipped into Blackrock Mountain right under his nose, Vaelastrasz had to leave the Blackrock orcs' control range with Jace and the SI:7 agents before mounting a gryphon to fly to Morgan's Watch.

A red dragon riding a gryphon might have seemed a bit comical, but considering the red dragonflight included an elder brother whose combat power in human form was far greater than in his original form, it wasn't that strange.

Morgan's Watch had originally been just a stronghold for the Morgan family's guerrilla forces skirmishing with the Dark Iron dwarves. It was completely destroyed during the orc invasion. Marshal Lothar's army had rebuilt it when they marched south to counterattack Blackrock Spire, but it was quickly abandoned.

Now, with the Alliance gathering again, Stormwind had clearly reinforced and expanded it. Numerous supply convoys were heading toward that mountain hollow. From a distance, you could already see the vivid blue lion banner of the Kingdom of Stormwind fluttering among the black boulders.

Morgan's Watch had been equipped with a gryphon roost. As the gryphon landed on the hillside, Jace spotted Varian in a blue cloak jogging up the mountain path toward them.

"Jace! I've already heard the reports from SI:7!"

As soon as Jace jumped off the gryphon, Varian grabbed him by the shoulders and shook him twice. "It seems that even the mad Dark Iron dwarves have no choice but to bow their heads and cooperate with the Alliance when faced with you."

"I think they were more concerned about the Black Dragon and orc threats, Your Majesty," Jace said.

"The Black Dragons and orcs have been entrenched there for over a decade," Varian said. "But the Dark Iron dwarves still had plenty of time and energy to raid us and King Magni's lands. Don't downplay your own achievements, Jace."

"I absolutely agree with Your Majesty. Making the Dark Iron dwarves bow their heads can't have been solely because of a common enemy."

Vaelastrasz had also dismounted from the gryphon, speaking as he approached: "The Bronzebeard reports should have mentioned the war in the Molten Core. About that name — Ragnaros. Ah, I haven't introduced myself. I'm Vieran, a student and servant of Archmage Krasus. An honor to meet you, Your Majesty."

"Master Vieran." Varian bowed solemnly. "I must extend my thanks to Archmage Krasus for his help during the Black Dragon Princess's disruption of Stormwind. Without his timely intervention, the entire Stormwind Keep might have been brought down by that evil dragon."

"As for the letters from Ironforge... they did mention a great deal about Ragnaros, but I didn't fully understand. What is it? The Fire Lord... I've heard the legend. They say he was the true ruler of Blackrock Mountain, behind the Dark Iron dwarves and Thaurissan."

"Strictly speaking, it's not a 'person,'" the red dragon said. "It was the ruler of the Fire Elemental Plane. Long before the creators descended to this world and gave rise to countless living things, Ragnaros ruled Azeroth."

He reached out, grabbed a handful of flame from a torch held by a nearby soldier, and held it in his palm. "If he had succeeded in fully entering this world, even this flame could have become a mad creature completely loyal to him, mindlessly attacking everyone present. Every candle and every oil lamp in Stormwind City..."

"I... can't imagine that." Varian frowned. "So it's real? An actual god?"

"A god who failed to manifest," Vaelastrasz explained. "But genuine nonetheless. The champions of the Bronzebeards, Wildhammers, and Dark Irons, along with their gryphon riders, contained the Fire Lord's ambition in the deepest reaches of the Molten Core. Jace Sesso and Vereesa Windrunner, those two Mountain Kings — especially Grede Thunderfist — shattered the Fire Lord's final spell at the moment of despair, saving Khaz Modan and your Elwynn."

"I witnessed everything alongside the dwarves who fought in the Flameheart battle. Soon all the Eastern Kingdoms will know of this, King Varian."

Standing nearby, Jace listened to the red dragon's account and shifted his weight uncomfortably. Generally speaking, their group — Vereesa didn't care about human opinions, Grede cared more about loot and money, Sorah agonized daily about whether to go home, Morophil and Sazeno were always scheming about how to get more mana sources, and Needleflame just wanted a fight.

This red dragon was the first one who'd bothered to carefully explain to others exactly what kind of enemy they'd defeated and what hardships they'd overcome.

After hearing the red dragon's account, the knights present, the gryphon master, and the soldiers nearby all seemed stunned, as if struggling to digest the information.

Ragnaros — even for the Dark Iron dwarves themselves — was a thing unknown. The vast majority of Dark Iron dwarves, even Locus the Darkbargainer and many Flamekeepers, had only ever seen him in relief carvings. Let alone the humans of the Kingdom of Stormwind.

How could anyone accept that someone standing right in front of them had just participated in defeating a god?

"The Fire Lord." Varian hesitated for a long moment before sizing up Jace. "But you look alright, Jace. Are you injured? What about Lady Vereesa Windrunner? Is everyone alright?"

"We're all fine, Your Majesty," Jace said. "Grede's in less optimistic shape, but he's been well cared for, thanks to Master Vieran here."

"When you first said you wanted to go to Blackrock Mountain to negotiate with the Dark Iron dwarves, I never imagined you'd have to deal with a... god," Varian said. "Ah, there's some food here, and some wine. Jace, Master Vieran. I'd love to hear about what really happened beneath Shadowforge City."

Jace had originally planned to hurry to the Tower of Ilgalar, go to the Telogrus Rift to discuss the summoning ritual with Kazzak, and not linger at Morgan's Watch. But when Varian mentioned food, his gut — which had been tortured for over a month in Shadowforge City — couldn't resist the temptation.

"Any meat?"

"Are you kidding? Of course there is. You know how many wild piglets have sprung up around the woods and the Stonewatch Falls since the orcs pulled back from Lake Everstill?" Varian said with a grin. "I might not be able to get you Lordaeron salmon, but wild boar liver is in endless supply."

"Then let's go." Jace waved his hand.

The warlock and the red dragon followed the king into the tent. Jace sat down and started eating, essentially leaving it to the red dragon to slowly explain the situation to Varian.

When Varian heard that Jace had taken Ragnaros's warhammer head-on, he couldn't help turning to look at the warlock licking grease off his fingers. He seemed unable to connect the story the red dragon was telling with the guy stuffing his face in front of him.

Back when he played the game, Jace had always thought that recovering health by sitting and eating was a bit of a stretch. Even if food could replenish nutrients, it shouldn't work that fast. Only a healing potion should have that kind of effect.

But now, eating Redridge-style stewed meat and bloodfang boar liver, tasting the familiar spices and grease again — it felt like all the pain he'd endured in the Molten Core was being driven away, bite by bite, by the fragrance and the pleasure of swallowing. He could finally understand why game adventurers recovered so fast.

At this point, he couldn't be bothered with formalities, even in front of the king.

"Honestly, Varian, this is better than what they make at the Pig and Whistle." Jace looked at the bare gryphon leg bone in his hand. "Can you pack a few servings for me? I'll bring some to my parents when I get back to Stormwind."

"Take what you need, Jace," Varian said. "And give my regards to Mr. and Mrs. Sesso."

Just then, a horn sounded from outside. Varian and Jace left the tent. In the distance, a cluster of dark red banners fluttered — several crimson-clad knights of Stromgarde were riding toward them.

"The knights of Arathi are eager," Jace said.

"It concerns the peace of the Thandol Span," Varian said. "King Thoras Trollbane has always placed great importance on the stability of Blackrock Spire and all of Khaz Modan. This time, he's not only sent General Danath Trollbane to Blackrock Spire but also his son, Prince Galen. This is the final battle of the Orc Wars. He probably doesn't want his heir to miss it entirely."

Galen Trollbane. The same Trollbane who'd gotten into a massive brawl with Grede at the Pig and Whistle Inn. Jace was already looking forward to seeing those two meet again — and Galen's reaction.

"What about Lordaeron?" Jace asked.

"Lordaeron has shown more sincerity than I expected," Varian said. "King Terenas Menethil II didn't send his prince, and his own health won't allow him to enter the Burning Steppes. But at least they've dispatched a leader of the Silver Hand — one of its founders."

"Turalyon?" Jace said. "Turalyon was planning to come anyway. It's not really King Terenas who sent him — he wants to avenge Marshal Lothar."

"No, it's Lord Tirion Fordring," Varian said. "He's been mired in that orc-sympathizer scandal recently. King Terenas is having him lead soldiers in the assault on the Blackrock orcs — probably trying to help him rehabilitate his reputation."
