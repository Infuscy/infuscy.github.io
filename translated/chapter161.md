## Chapter 161: Old Vic

Jace pulled the wolf-pelt coat out of his luggage. "Dad, try this on."

"You brought a gift back?"

His father slipped the coat on for a size. It fit — luckily, Grede had ordered it a bit large on account of thinking Jace was still a kid who might shoot up another inch, otherwise Vic might not have made it work at all.

"Is this from the forest wolves south of Redridge?" his father asked. "Must've cost a fair bit. It's warm, though — winter's coming, so the timing's perfect."

"Didn't cost much. I killed the wolf myself," Jace answered offhandedly.

His mother cut in at once. "The wolf didn't bite you, did it?"

"We've got wolves here too," his father said. "A bite's nothing to fret over. I taught Jace how to handle wolves when he was small."

Jace scrambled to recover. "I got it hunting—"

He spun a yarn about going out hunting with a few dwarves and militiamen. The story was full of holes and contradicted itself left and right, but his mother listened with rapt attention all the same.

They talked late into the night, and finally Jace steeled himself to lay his cards on the table.

"Mom, Dad — I didn't just come back to visit. There's something on my mind I wanted to ask you about."

"Met a girl you want to marry?" his dad asked.

"No," Jace said. "I'm thinking of buying a house in Stormwind, or maybe Goldshire, and having you two move up there eventually."

"That's not a decision you make in a few nights, Jace," his mother said.

Jace said, "Take your time. I haven't earned enough anyway — it's just a thought for now."

His father chuckled. "You've barely scraped together two years' worth of coppers, and you're already dreaming of buying a house."

"Why would you say that?" his mother said. "If the boy wants to go out and earn his keep, let him. What's the point of saying things like that, just to beat his motivation down?"

"I know Dad's just worried about me," Jace said.

"If it's too hard out there, the city's full of good, pretty girls — ones like your mother," his father said. "Besides, Tirisfal isn't so bad. Or you could go to the Eastweald, or Stratholme."

Hearing the name Stratholme, Jace grew even more resolved about getting them out to the Kingdom of Stormwind.

His mother hesitated a moment, then said, "I actually agree with that."

Jace could feel it — his parents wanted him to go out and see the world and broaden his horizons, but at the same time they were torn about letting their child go too far. He was their only son. The two of them couldn't bear the cost of losing him.

Late at night, as his mother mended a tear in his shirtsleeve, Jace sat on the front steps and stared blankly at the people passing in the street.

A lilting melody drifted from somewhere far off — he couldn't tell if it was court musicians playing in the palace — mingling with the clatter and rumble of wagon wheels up close.

Then his father came and sat down beside him.

"Jace, do you think I'm just some old fart who doesn't understand anything?"

"Why would you ask that?"

Jace glanced at his father guiltily. The man wasn't old, not really — just over forty, though that made him one of the older ones among the guard. But after wandering the whole world, it was hard not to come home and feel that the parents who'd raised him hadn't seen what he'd seen, that they'd drifted apart a little. He must've let something slip in his tone, and it had gotten his dad's hackles up.

"The burn on your arm — that was dragonfire, wasn't it?" his father asked quietly.

Jace frowned. "How did you know?"

"There's a very particular smell on you, Jace. I've only ever smelled it on one kind of person — people attacked by black dragons." His father said, "The capital took in a batch of wounded soldiers rescued from Alterac not long ago. I helped the priests dress their burns. That smell, I'll never forget it for the rest of my life."

The Shadowflame that black dragons spewed — even people who understood Shadow magic had a hard time stomaching that bizarre, foul reek, let alone soldiers and townsfolk. "I heard Southshore and Ambermill both got hit by black dragons. Did you pass through either of those places?" he asked again.

Jace nodded reluctantly. "Southshore."

"I figured. And that string of pearls on your wrist." He glanced down at it. "Care to tell me where that came from? And don't you dare tell me some girl gave them to you."

Jace had originally planned to give them to his mother as a gift, but then he'd figured naga trinkets might carry some kind of evil taint, and if they harmed his mother's health, that'd be the last thing he wanted. So he'd never brought them out.

But he just kept wearing them, never taking them off.

"So I'm not allowed to have grown up these past two years? But you don't actually know what that is, do you?"

"Naga jewelry," his father said.

"That's impressive, seriously. Where have you even seen naga? Around here?" Jace's hackles rose.

"Your old man grew up in Tarlenmil. You don't become a captain of the capital guard without some real skill." His father looked at him. "I only wanted to settle down after I married Henny. And the reason I could attract a girl as beautiful and well-off as your mother wasn't just this face of mine."

He smiled. "When you were little, the two of us knew you weren't any ordinary child. The Kingdom of Lordaeron is vast, but it'd never be enough to hold you back. That's why, two years ago, when you wanted to go all that way by yourself — even though I hated the idea, I persuaded Henny to let you go."

"So don't you worry about us. Your old man spent years as a scout cavalryman in Durnholde — I've seen things. We shouldn't be something you need to fret over. Your mother needs me to protect her, and someday you'll have someone who needs protecting too. Don't let the two of us hold you back. I'll find a way to put your mother's mind at ease."

Jace felt his nose sting. He drew a deep breath. "Dad, I really, truly need you both to come to Stormwind. It's not about holding me back or not — but the specific reasons..."

He trailed off. His mind went blank and he couldn't think of a single reason on the spot. He could only avoid his father's gaze and stare out at the street, his thoughts churning into a complete mess.

"We'll go," his father said, and clapped him on the neck. "Give me a few years to sort things out here, and we'll move to the Kingdom of Stormwind. And if you can't earn enough money, Jace, we'll sell this house. Honestly, I don't think Henny wants to tangle with her family anymore either, and neither do I."

"I'll absolutely earn enough, don't you worry." Jace pulled out the Gurubashi gold coin. "Take a look at this."

"Troll trinkets."

"The point is that it's a gold coin! But the hell... how do you know everything?!"

"You went to Stormwind and all you learned was Light and how to swear?"

Jace cleared his throat. "Sorry, Dad, I didn't mean it... but I definitely didn't just learn those two things. And I'll tell you — the things I can do would probably scare you."

"Then show me. Let's see if you can scare me."

"Better not..."

"Hmph. Not only do you brag, you look down on your own old man. Sit here by yourself, then." Old Vic stood up, straightened the wolf-pelt coat, and added, "This thing's really comfortable. Feels like it was made by a dwarf tailor, though."

"And you know it's made by a dwarf now..."

"Isn't it?"
