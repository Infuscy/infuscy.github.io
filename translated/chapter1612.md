## Chapter 1612: Strictly Speaking, Not Killed

Jace couldn't be one hundred percent certain those were void blossoms. He hadn't seen many living specimens of that flower before. But at least from this distance, the flowers looked very similar to the void blossoms in the game.

Combined with the fact that Ursula Delin had been dead set on using the Telogrus Rift's shadow energy to grow void blossoms, and these similarly-looking plants had appeared on this barren floating island in the wasteland, it basically ruled out other possibilities.

"Morophil, fly me over to that floating island," Jace said.

The succubus nodded, lifted Jace into the air, and carefully landed among the strange long-stemmed flowers. The moment he set foot, Jace felt surrounded by an overwhelming sense of despair and sorrow.

This was what the void blossoms emitted. Their shadow energy was made of incredibly complex negative emotions — fear, panic, confusion — making him recall all the horrors of Draenor.

The leaders of Garadar discussing the ogre, demon, and fel orc sieges in hushed tones amidst a lively banquet, the gradual shattering of Nagrand. Sabellian speaking confidently of the Black Dragonflight's power while inwardly unable to stop worrying about the future of the Blade's Edge Mountains' black dragons. Alliance soldiers in the Wildhammer stronghold crouching on the walls, awaiting the demonic army's arrival. The Broken and Lost Ones in Shattrath City's slums gazing up at the Aldor priests' dais. Looking up to see Kil'jaeden's seal falling, unable to find a way out no matter what...

That was it. This was exactly what the draenei herbalist had described. If the void blossoms of Farahlon were plants born from the despair of Draenor's countless disasters, then simulating a similar environment could likely succeed in cultivating similar void blossoms on a second landmass.

It looked like Ursula had succeeded. Jace could even see that some void blossoms had already been harvested. He wondered how the destruction potion brewing was coming.

But how had she done it?

Ursula wasn't in the Telogrus Rift right now. Instead, the void lord Gaoseros was channeling mana in a circle some distance away. Jace noticed him as soon as he landed on the floating island.

"Gaoseros, what are you doing?"

"Master." Gaoseros stopped chanting and lowered his spectral arms. The runic circle slowly dimmed along with them.

"You once said that I should assist Lady Ursula Delin and the Dagger's Edge warlocks in maintaining Telogrus's energy stability. She wished to practice alchemy here, and I knew this was also your wish. So I naturally began assisting her. According to the shadow oil she brewed, I adjusted the flowing void energy in this area to reach the state she desired. And these splendid shadow condensates are the result — what you call 'void blossoms.'"

"So that's how it was," Jace said to himself. She'd gotten a void lord to help adjust the shadow energy... no wonder it went so fast.

"Where is Ursula?"

"The warlocks of the Dagger's Edge have been cooperating with SI:7 recently, hunting down and eliminating the ogres still lurking in this kingdom. They want to use ogre soul shards to practice Gul'dan's soul magic, master. So I believe Ursula, as a member of the Dagger's Edge, is likely with them."

"Ah." Jace nodded. "So she should be in Stormwind. Thank you, Lord Gaoseros."

"You're welcome, master. Though I feel your understanding of my words might be slightly off." Gaoseros said. "Additionally, I overheard your conversation with Lord Kalrez. You and the mortals are attempting to destroy Blackrock Spire, occupied by a powerful shadow lord and his followers of flesh. Though I am not a mortal, but a pure shadow being, that doesn't mean I will side with the shadows in this war. I will fight for you, Jace Sesso, my master — as long as you give the order."

That was the issue. Jace had actually considered bringing Gaoseros to Blackrock Spire. But Black Dragons were all immensely powerful shadow creatures. Nefarian himself was a shadow manipulator with few equals — whether it was mind control or destructive shadowflame, he was far more pure than his sister.

Against an enemy like that, Kalrez — who commanded fel energy and had spent countless ages fighting void beings — was definitely a more specialized choice than a void lord.

"If Blackrock Spire needs you, I'll summon you with a spell," Jace said. "Until then, it's best you stay in the Telogrus Rift and maintain its stability. Including cultivating these void blossoms. Ursula Delin's alchemy is also an important pillar for our victory and survival. Your role here is no less significant than going to the front lines of Blackrock Spire."

"As you command," Gaoseros said.

Leaving the Telogrus Rift and returning to the Tower of Ilgalar, Jace blindfolded Midnight, mounted him, and set off for Stormwind with Morophil and Sazeno.

He'd originally planned to rest at Lakeshire or near the Eastvale Logging Camp's garrison. Then he realized he couldn't judge Midnight's travel speed by normal horse standards. This utter monster passed Lakeshire without expression, circled Lake Everstill in half a day, and charged straight to the Crossroads. Before the sky had fully darkened, it was like he'd already entered Elwynn Forest.

Though the night he'd spent resting with Varian Wrynn had been exhausting thanks to Morophil, he had at least gotten some proper sleep. He wasn't tired now, and he couldn't wait to get back to Stormwind. Midnight didn't need rest, so neither would he.

He arrived at the Trade District just as evening fell the next day. Many people in the square shot curious or fearful looks at the rider in the dark red cloak and his strange black horse.

Jace glanced toward the spire of the Cathedral of Light by the fountain. He'd planned to go straight to his parents' place with the food Varian had given him. But as he was about to flick the reins, he noticed his own clothes — burned ragged and tattered. Even the Stormwind citizens looked nervous seeing him. His own parents would be even worse.

Not to mention that the demon bag behind him contained a terrifying dark robe woven from over a hundred demon souls, and even an orc finger bone. Even though it was properly wrapped in enchanted cloth to prevent any leakage, bringing something tainted with necromancy into his parents' home felt wrong. Better clean up and go tomorrow.

Jace changed direction toward the Mage Quarter. He arrived at the Slaughtered Lamb, greeted Tharle, and went down to the basement. As expected, the faint smell of brewing herbs hung in the air. Ursula, as he'd guessed, definitely wouldn't be running around extracting soul shards with the rest of the Dagger's Edge.

"Jace, is that you?" A voice came from the alchemy lab.

"It's me," Jace replied.

"Good heavens... you're finally back."

Ursula walked out of the hallway, grabbed Jace's wrist and looked him over. "I heard from SI:7 that you ran into trouble at Blackrock Mountain. Even the Mountain King from Ironforge had to come support you. We could feel the ground shaking all the way in Stormwind during that time. What the hell happened in Shadowforge City?"

"Short version: we killed a god," Jace said lightly, sitting down in a nearby chair.

Ursula's face had been full of worry. Now she frowned and squinted at him suspiciously. "A god? You killed a god?"

"The Fire Lord Ragnaros. We took him down. Heard of him?"

"Then that fire protection potion I gave you really came in handy!" Ursula snorted, hands on her hips. "So are the Dark Iron dwarves incredibly grateful that you saved them from the Fire Lord's terrifying yoke?"

"Of course!" Sazeno exclaimed. "Those Dark Iron dwarves were incredibly grateful to us. You should have seen the scene, alchemist! You wouldn't have regretted it!"

"He's not wrong," Jace said, crossing his legs. Morophil stood nearby, flashing a meaningful smile.

"So did they give you anything as thanks?" Ursula glanced at the imp. "Like Blackrock Depths spicy candy, or that special Dark Iron gunpowder? You didn't remember to bring any for me, did you?"

"You never reminded me," Jace said.

"I thought that after serving as my hired herbalist for so long, you should have developed some basic sensitivity about these things!" Ursula said.

"My mistake. Though I don't have those, I did bring something pretty special." Jace pulled out the wrapped orc finger bone. "This came from an orc corpse. Be careful — it might be carrying necrotic plague. Remember that Winterspring furbolg purification tonic?"

"I know it." The alchemist nodded. "Thomas Waller has already brewed a medicinal soup based on your recipe, used to treat patients suffering from necrotic poison in Duskwood. Not as fast or effective as the Holy Light, but it works."

"That's the one," Jace said. "I want to see if that tonic can cure the necrotic infection on this finger."

"No problem." Ursula took the wrapped package and asked, "Is there any difference between this and the fingers of those ghouls in Duskwood?"

"The difference is that this corpse was killed by a Black Dragon's necromancy," Jace said.

"A Black Dragon's necromancy." Ursula froze for a moment. "I understand."

"What about the void blossoms..." Jace asked tentatively.

"Ah, yes, the void blossoms!" Ursula said. "I was so worried about you in Shadowforge City that I almost forgot to tell you the good news. Damn it... I'm currently contacting the Kirin Tor, contacting Master Vargos, learning about the destruction potion brewing from him. I'm confident we'll have results soon, Jace."

"Excellent," Jace said.

"I need your help with something related to the void blossoms' power. Come with me." Ursula beckoned, then stopped and looked back at Morophil. She frowned and turned back to Jace. "That god you mentioned, the Fire Lord Ragnaros... you didn't really, literally, kill him, did you?"

"Strictly speaking, no," Jace said.

"Ah, I knew it." Ursula let out an awkward laugh. "You don't brag much, kid, but when you do, it's terrifying."

"We just destroyed Ragnaros's physical existence in this world," Jace cleared his throat. "Banished him back to his own plane."

Ursula's smile froze on her face. She swallowed hard.
