Chapter 1614: Along the Canal

In the early morning, Jace came out of the Mage Quarter and crossed the canal bridge. Passing the old rental house he'd lived in a few years back, he followed his habit of stopping by the bank to check his reflection on the water's surface. Satisfied that the wild mess of his beard was mostly trimmed, and that he looked like a normal Stormwind citizen rather than someone who'd just barely escaped being hunted for hundreds of miles by orcs, he relaxed and continued toward the Cathedral of Light. He'd left everything demonic with Ursula — including Morophil and Sazeno — since he was going to visit his parents. But the thought still nagged at him. His parents knew their son had picked up some dark magic and might have ties to demons. And they were among the most open-minded pre-Dark Portal generation — not just in Lordaeron, but even in Stormwind, a city that had plenty of contact with dark magic. Still, Jace didn't want to dig up everything hidden in the shadows and flaunt it carelessly in front of them, expecting them to just accept it. Let alone the citizens of the other six kingdoms. Hell, even in his previous life, in an age of information overload, changing the deep-rooted preconceptions of ordinary people was nearly impossible. He climbed the steps to the door and looked at the large nameplate reading "Sesso" beside it. Jace let out a long breath. No matter what kind of magic he'd used, in the end he'd managed to settle his parents here, gotten them a house that only nobles like Lescovar could have afforded back in the day. Why worry about anything else? Jace knocked. The rapid clatter of footsteps came from inside. The door swung open, and Hennie first shot a worried glance at Jace's chest, then snapped her eyes up, and finally lunged into his arms, squeezing his waist tight. "Jace... finally here in person. That earthquake in Stormwind — so many people said it came from the north, and then you mentioned the king wanted you out of the city. I was so afraid it had something to do with your trip, that you might be hurt!" "You might want to let go first, Mom." Jace held his mother's shoulders and said quietly. "Pretty soon your grip strength is going to cause more damage than that earthquake did." Hennie let go and punched him in the chest. "I've been worried sick. Do you think I'm joking, Jace Sesso?" "Jace is back?" A questioning voice came from inside the room. Hennie shouted back, "Yes, and this time it's not the king's men, Vick!" A much heavier set of footsteps came from the staircase. Hennie smiled and waved in that direction, then turned back, her expression growing serious. "So, are you saying the earthquake affected you too, or was it actually connected to you?" "Somewhat connected," Jace said, nodding. "Let your son in first, Hennie. It's cold out there." Vick called from behind. "Go cut some of that dried sausage he likes. I'll grab the white wine little Denman gave me from the cellar. We'll drink and talk." "How's Arna doing, Dad?" Jace asked with a smile. "She's fine." Vick waved from the stairwell. "She doesn't have to go to the Burning Steppes and kick Dark Iron helmets off with her hind legs, Jace!" Jace licked his lips with a wry smile. He knew that even if he hadn't told his parents exactly what he'd been doing, the rumors around the city had given them enough to guess. "Didn't expect you'd know the Denmans, Dad. Aren't they jewelers?" Jace asked. "Their youngest, little Terrence, likes taking part in Fighter's Guild operations too." Hennie was slicing sausage in the next room, calling out, "Your father met him during that operation searching for naga traces by Lake Everstill. And what's so strange about that? Your father's surname is Sesso, and with that classic old gangster accent of his from Stratholme — anyone who spends a little time with him can easily figure out whose father he is. So who should be more honored to know whom, Jace?" "Ha." Jace let out a proud laugh. "Then doesn't that make him pissed? Back in Lordaeron, people looked out for me because of his name. Now it's completely the opposite." Hennie wiped the table, set down a plate of sausage, and sat. "He's your own father. He couldn't be happier." But then she lowered her head slightly and said quietly, "Though, I still feel a little..." Jace picked up a slice of sausage and popped it in his mouth. "What?" Hennie's lips moved as if she was thinking how to put it. Then she said, "A lot of people think you're by the king's side, his advisor, no different from the Privy Council — even more trusted than them, even higher in status. And because of that, they see us as that kind of noble too." "But I've never experienced the life of a noble. I don't know how a noble is supposed to dress or act. I feel more at ease being a soldier's wife, a troublemaker's mother, or a runaway's daughter." She flashed a quick smile and looked up at the ceiling. "Of course, this house is comfortable enough. It's not like I can't adapt to the nice things nobles enjoy." "Sorry, Mom," Jace said quietly. "What's there to apologize for?" Hennie took his hand, but Jace could only feel the worry and unease she was desperately trying to hide underneath. "So, are the rumors true, Jace?" Vick set a keg of wine on the table, twisted open the tap, poured a cup, and pushed it toward Jace. "Burning Steppes, Dark Iron dwarves, Shadowforge City. I've heard too many rumors lately. A lot of people say Blackrock Mountain collapsed entirely, and that's why there was such a big earthquake." "They're not rumors," Jace said. "We went to the Dark Iron dwarves and convinced them to stop being our enemies. The 'convincing' process was... a bit intense, but in the end, it worked out." Hennie stared into Jace's eyes and took a deep breath. "It was far more dangerous than that, wasn't it, Jace?" "Mom, I'm right here. Not missing any limbs, all parts accounted for." Jace said. "And as the king's advisor, my job in Shadowforge City wasn't to pick fights. The one actually throwing punches was Grede Thunderfist." "Then what about the burn on your hand?" Hennie asked. "You can't go to Blackrock Mountain without getting a few scrapes." Jace smiled and took a sip of wine. "This is good, Dad. No bitterness at all." "So what about Blackrock Spire?" Hennie asked. Jace swallowed. Vick said, "We all know. You went to the Dark Irons' territory to clear the last obstacle for King Varian's assault on Blackrock Spire. And when the assault comes, you'll be part of it." "Yes," Jace admitted. "Many Alliance heroes have gone, Jace." Hennie said. "That day I saw the Prince of Stromgarde. I hear Lord Tirion Fordring of Hearthglen is coming too. Lord Fordring is a leader of the Silver Hand paladins. Those orcs and Black Dragons can't stand against him." Jace took her hand. "Your not-so-bright son isn't new to dealing with Black Dragons. Grim Batol, Stormwind, even beyond the Dark Portal — I might know Black Dragons better than any of them. That's why I have to go." "I'm not stopping you." Hennie glanced at her silent husband, hesitated, but couldn't finish the sentence. "I understand what you mean," Jace said. "The king's advisor, Mathias Shaw, gave me similar advice. Vereesa said the same thing. They think that with so many Alliance soldiers present, it might not be the best time for me to use the kind of magic I specialize in. In short, I'm not the main character in this assault on Blackrock Spire. But I'll be fully prepared, just in case something unexpected happens." "That makes me feel a lot better, Jace." Hennie said quietly. "I don't know why, but I've had this knot in my stomach lately. Even seeing you doesn't fully settle it. That tower is the lair of Black Dragons and orcs. Even Marshal Lothar..." Hennie couldn't finish. Jace felt his own heart sink. An uncomfortable silence settled over the table for a moment. Then Vick said softly, "Let's talk about something nice. I don't remember you being this worried when I used to go hunt naga along the coast." "You're really going to compete with your son for attention, you old bastard?" Hennie glared. "Fair enough. Let's switch to a more positive topic." Vick downed his wine in one gulp. "Last time at Stonewatch we didn't get the chance. When can you take us to see the Tower of Ilgalar?" Jace nearly choked on his wine. "Let's stick to Blackrock Mountain, Dad."
