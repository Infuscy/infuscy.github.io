## Chapter 1616: A Simple Wish

Shamefully, in the nearly ten years he'd been in Stormwind, Jace had never observed the Rack of the Holy Light at the deepest end of the Cathedral's great hall this closely before.

Accompanied by a soft chant in ancient Tirisfalian coming from somewhere, and the scent of incense mixed with parchment and scrolls, Jace gazed at the symbol. It somewhat resembled a cross, but had plenty of differences.

Some people said it was a sword of justice freshly drawn from its sheath. Others said it was two hands holding up a circular mark in the center, representing light and hope. Still others said it represented the radiance believers saw when the Holy Light manifested. Many interpretations.

To Jace, it was quite simple — almost a stylization of the naaru's image. Though he had no idea how humans had depicted the naaru. Maybe the third explanation had some basis to it.

But in the atmosphere of this place and time, countless friars and citizens stood with heads bowed before it. Almost no one looked directly at its form the way Jace did — not even his mother. The ambiance infected him, and he found himself lowering his head slightly, his gaze falling on the steps in front of him.

"Merciful Light, under your watch I met Vick. You witnessed the birth of our handsome son, Jace. Though like every mischievous child, he was unwilling to follow your guidance, that doesn't mean his heart isn't good. That doesn't mean he can't save the innocent and protect the weak like a clergyman would. He's been proving that since the moment he was born, under your gaze, beneath your radiance — and you know all things."

Hennie was a few people away from Jace. Head bowed, hands clasped tightly to her chest, she murmured, "Light, I pray to you that one day Jace can stop frowning. Stop worrying about hardships and pain. Stop suffering from troubles he can't escape. That he can... take care of himself. Live for himself."

Her voice was low. She thought Jace couldn't hear her at this distance. But he heard every word clearly.

Listening to his mother's prayer, Jace took a deep breath and squeezed his eyes shut.

"Jace." His mother came to his side. "Your turn."

"I have to pray too?" Jace asked.

"It's not like this is your first time praying." Hennie took his arm. "Even if you don't truly believe in the Holy Light, say a few words. Who knows if it might help?"

It really wasn't Jace's first time praying. Not even his fifth. Jace recalled how, whenever he found himself in the worst situations, he instinctively called out to the Holy Light for help. Most of the time, the Light didn't respond in any obvious way. But he still called out the next time anyway.

Thinking about it, he figured he really was becoming a genuine Azerothian.

"I won't listen." His mother smiled and stepped aside. Jace nodded.

He raised his head again to look at the symbol and said softly, "Holy Light, I know you don't really listen to me that carefully. But... today I pray to you: let Mom, Dad, and my friends always be happy. Or at least as happy as they can be. That's all I wanted to say. Oh, and Morophil and Sazeno count as friends too, if you have jurisdiction over demons."

That evening back at his parents' house, Vick had somehow produced a fairly large catfish. "Let's make a thick spined catfish chowder for the boy, Hennie. Remember that recipe I mentioned last time? Whip up some mayonnaise, mushroom slices, green onions, some sweet spices, and..."

"First tell me, where'd you get the catfish?" Hennie asked, hands on her hips. "Don't tell me you fished it out of the canal. Is the fish in that river even edible?"

Jace, who'd been sneakily grabbing a biscuit from the table, froze for a moment and looked between the two of them.

"Fished it from the Valley of Heroes," Vick said.

"I think you fished it from the market," Hennie said, taking the fish.

"The market's fish are also from the Valley of Heroes." Vick crossed his arms with considerable confidence. "You don't think they dare fish from Crystal Lake, do you?"

"Oh, right." Jace pulled a wrapped package of meat from his bag that he'd brought from the Burning Steppes. "Redridge stewed meat, and Bloodfang boar liver. Really good, Mom. Let's have it all together tonight."

Hennie took it, unwrapped the paper, sniffed it, and her eyes lit up. "My goodness, where did you get this?"

"King Varian Wrynn's personal chef made it. Quality guaranteed," Jace said.

"Really?" Suddenly Vick was interested too. "Don't put it in the soup. Just roast it a bit over the fire and let's taste it!"

"Whatever you say," Hennie called back from the other room.

That dinner had meat, fish, soup, and goat's milk. The whole house was filled with a milky, meaty aroma. Jace could barely remember the last time he'd had such a hearty meal with his parents.

"If only Vereesa were here," Hennie said. "I feel like I haven't seen her in a long time."

"She's still up north," Jace said. "Next time, Mom. Next time I'll bring her here, and we'll eat together again."

As he said that, he paused thoughtfully and muttered quietly, "And who knows, maybe we'll be eating at a wedding banquet soon enough."

"What did you say?" Hennie nearly jumped out of her chair. Vick tightened his grip on his spoon.

"We're planning to get married, Dad, Mom." Jace scooped up a piece of the soft catfish meat. "A wedding."

"Really?!" Hennie grabbed Jace's wrist, causing the meat he'd scooped to tremble back into the bowl. "She accepted your proposal?!"

"Strictly speaking, ahem." Jace used his other hand to straighten his collar proudly. "I accepted Lady Vereesa Windrunner's proposal."

"Just look at you!" Hennie burst into laughter. Vick quickly reached out to steady her and asked, "The wedding — when?"

"Maybe right after Blackrock Spire," Jace said. "Her second sister plans to..."

"Wait." Vick said quietly. "Don't tell me it's really that Marris boy?"

"You mean Nathanos?" Hennie looked at him.

"Him," Jace said. "Nathanos Marris is coming to Blackrock Spire too. Sylvanas intends to use this final campaign to earn him the trust of the other elves."

"So he actually pulled it off." Vick crossed his arms, looking annoyed.

"You sound jealous," Hennie said.

"These buzzard legs are really good, Jace. And the boar liver too. Almost as good as the bear steak from Caer Darrow."

"I think you're just jealous, Vick Sesso!"

"Jealous of what?" Vick said. "I bet someone that kid would never marry an elf, and now I've lost. Can't I complain a bit? But that means... all three Windrunner sisters are marrying Lordaeron men?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Marshal Turalyon and Ranger-Captain Alleria Windrunner's first wedding was too rushed. Quel'Thalas hadn't fully emerged from the war back then... This time, she probably wants to have a grand ceremony together with her two sisters. You'll both have to come. You could visit Quel'Thalas, maybe even take a portal."

"Honestly, I've never been through a portal," Vick said.

"I can't wait, Jace!" Hennie said excitedly.

But the excitement on her face cooled quickly, because all three of them knew Jace still had to go to Blackrock Spire.

"Stay safe, son." Vick patted Jace's shoulder. "That Black Dragon is on his last legs. Everyone in Stormwind knows the Alliance will win this time. Marshal Lothar's vengeance will soon be achieved."

"I know, Dad," Jace replied.

Leaving his parents' house that night, the streets of Stormwind were nearly empty, very quiet. Walking along, Jace couldn't help thinking: once Varian Wrynn won at Blackrock Spire, the area around Stormwind would finally be stabilized. He really wouldn't have to run around so much anymore.

Then, as agreed, he'd go to Kalimdor to release the Windlord. As for whether the reward was really that legendary Sword of Wind... he turned to glance back. Through the doorway, beside the canal, his parents were still watching him.

Frankly, he didn't care all that much anymore.

He rounded a corner into an alley, and Morophil emerged from the shadows, carrying his demon bag.

"Master's emotions are quite complicated tonight." She chuckled, spreading her wings and blocking the light from the canal as she walked past Jace from one side to the other. "What are you worried about?"

"If one day I stop hunting demons, will you two still follow me?" Jace asked.

"At the very least, I'd drain every last drop of your mana first." Morophil said. "You don't think warlocks like you are easy to find in the Twisting Nether, do you, darling? You need to have some confidence in yourself."

"She's right, master," Sazeno said. "Besides, staying here is better than running back to the Twisting Nether. When you finally get killed and the contract gets broken — maybe I'll still be alive by then."

"Then I'll kill you before I die," Jace said.

"Hey, don't, great master." The imp laughed awkwardly. "Besides, if you're about to get killed, when would you have the time to deal with me?"

"Don't worry about such small things, lesser demon." Morophil said. "I'll remember to send you off first."

"You... Hey? I... Hah!"
