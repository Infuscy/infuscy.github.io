Chapter 1617: Premium Elemental Earth Supplier

Back at the Slaughtered Lamb late at night, Ursula's alchemy lab was at its most intensely fired. Even in the Winter Veil season, the heat forced Jace to take off his coat and roll up his sleeves as he came down the passage. "You stay up this late every night and can't wake up in the morning." Jace looked at the alchemist's thin back. "Ever considered sleeping earlier?" "I stay up partly out of habit, but mostly..." Ursula looked up. "Many potions — like the Nightmare Potion I'm brewing now — work significantly better when made at night than during the day. It must be the influence of Talendra roses. The shift between day and night isn't just about brightness, as many people think. It affects many things most people don't notice. You've been to Kalimdor. You should know the ancient forest elves call themselves 'Kaldorei' — children of the stars. Legend says they are blessed by the night and are stronger after dark than during the day." She examined her own palm. "Maybe the elf blood in my veins comes from those ancient forest elves. It gives me sharper focus at night, which lets me brew higher-quality potions." "Whatever Kaldorei blood you have, it's probably been bleached dry by ten thousand years under the Sunwell." Jace asked, "So are there potions that brew better during the day?" "Of course there are." Ursula offered a faint smile. "But it's night now. Actually, I was about to pack up and sleep. I thought you'd stay at the Sessos' place and come back tomorrow morning." "I thought so too." Jace looked at the cauldron emitting a faint red steam. "But then I decided against it. Deep in the Molten Core, my control over shadow... nearly crossed a line. Not just the people around me sensed it; I could feel it myself. With Morophil watching, I might be able to sleep safely. But there's no way I'm letting a demon into their house." "Why?" Ursula crossed her arms and leaned against a cabinet. "I mean, what caused the loss of control? Jace, I don't think I've ever heard of you having this kind of problem. Is it because of your injuries?" Jace thought for a moment. "Partially. But it might also be related to a relic I encountered from the Twilight's Hammer. Its power could drag people into nightmares." "Like Nightmare Potion?" Ursula pointed at a pale green bottle nearby. "No." Jace said. "The nightmare that relic creates traps everyone affected together in the same space. More like... a nightmare realm. I nearly lost my life in there." "I can't imagine what you experienced in that nightmare." Ursula said quietly. "So it sounds like severe shadow corrosion caused you to lose control over your command of shadow energy? The Twilight's Hammer... is this connected to those wind stones Galders mentioned before?" "Yes," Jace said. "Damn it," Ursula whispered. "There's another major reason." Jace said slowly. "A good friend of mine nearly died in that battle. Grede Thunderfist. You know him. At the time, I thought he was really going to die." "I know he came to Stormwind with you," Ursula said. "More than that." Jace pressed his thumb against his temple. "I met him in Southshore. At first I thought he was just a drunk dwarf who'd fallen into the sea. Then, when he heard I was coming to Stormwind, he suddenly decided to blow every copper in his pocket on a ship ticket with me. Truth is, without him, I wouldn't be alive today. I might not even have lived long enough to meet any of you." "I see that's the real reason, Jace." Ursula said. "How is he?" "Half dead. But at least alive." Jace said. "A friend of mine — a very skilled physician — said he just can't participate in the upcoming Blackrock Spire assault. I think that's a good thing for him, even if he isn't too happy about it." "Sounds like a decent outcome to me." The alchemist tilted her head. "I think so too," Jace nodded. "But you're still trapped inside it." Ursula took out a notebook. "Let me see how to help you get out." "Did you minor in psychology when you studied alchemy?" Jace asked with a smile. "What psychology?" Ursula looked up. "As a shadow alchemist, I've never liked the Church friars' approach. I handle things simply and roughly — I target the shadow in your mind directly." She held a quill and flipped through yellowed pages, muttering, "First, a strong sleep potion using Dreamleaf and Golden Sansam as the main ingredients. That potion induces completely dreamless sleep and will help me use other potions to adjust you further. Then a little Arathi old moss eater spider venom powder and Northrend Icethorn. That gives us... hmm, this should be enough to restore a certain level of calm." "Just having you listen to me talk about this already makes me feel halfway better, Ursula. Today I didn't even know how to tell Mom and Dad. Say their son is essentially a black ticking time bomb? And just this morning I was telling Bishop Benedictus not to let Stormwind turn into a den of dark magic. And here I am..." "According to the clergy, confession is part of the Holy Light's healing process." The alchemist tapped her quill toward Jace. "But I charge for it, kid. You know the materials for these potions add up to almost more than Black Lotus." "How much?" Jace asked. "We can bundle it with the Restorative Potions." Ursula said. "Whatever the bear mongooses cure for the plague of undeath turns out to be, based on your description, it sounds more like a post-onset treatment — probably better for prevention and controlling the plague's spread. But it's limited against other necromantic and shadow magic. For the vanguard fighting on the front lines, the Restorative Potion is more suitable. Remember how Restorative Potions helped you fend off Teron Gorefiend's Death Shadow curse for almost a month?" "If the Black Dragon really is in league with the remnants of the death knights, or if the orcs have cooked up some new death knights, then the Restorative Potion is our most powerful weapon against them." "But as always, the already expensive Elemental Earth is nowhere near sufficient supply. The apothecaries and doctors in Northrend desperately need the same material for plague prevention potions, Restorative Potions, and cold-resistant enchanted clothing. I only have two bottles left. Unless you have a way to get Elemental Earth." "See? I'm not price gouging. Miss Arkhana's Arcane Star Alchemy Lab also needs to sustain itself. You can't expect me to bankrupt my family business healing you, only to end up having to call in a favor with Erland Dudemoor and join the Stonemasons building watchtowers in the Burning Steppes." "I know, I know, I know..." Jace sighed. "Suddenly I feel like my mood isn't so bad anymore." "The Restorative Potion is still necessary." Ursula said. "Don't forget your opponents are powerful orc priests, forest troll hunters, and ogre magi. Not to mention the Black Dragons who excel at shadow and necromantic magic. The Restorative Potion will do more for you in that situation than you can imagine." "If the Alliance had been able to get enough Elemental Earth from Ironforge during the Battle of Hillsbrad Foothills, the Silver Hand's fight against the death knights might not have been so brutal. Of course, the main problem in Hillsbrad was those red dragons. But that's a different story." "Elemental Earth from Ironforge?" Jace asked. "If it weren't for the Dark Iron dwarves causing trouble, Elemental Earth wouldn't have been so hard to gather, from the southern desert of Loch Modan all the way to the Burning Steppes," Ursula said. "Ah. I just thought of a premium Elemental Earth supplier." Jace held out his hand. "I need to write a letter. The Alliance forces are still gathering. I think there's still time." "A premium Elemental Earth supplier? Who? Someone even I don't know?" Ursula handed him paper and a quill. The moment Jace wrote down the full name of the recipient, Ursula gasped sharply. "Dagran Thaurissan? Wait... Jace Sesso, are you serious? No, even if you're really writing to the Emperor of Shadowforge City, can you at least use some honorifics?" "Almost forgot." Jace added a line at the end, muttering, "His Imperial Majesty... hello. There. Done." The alchemist forcefully closed her gaping mouth, grabbed a cup of tea, and downed it in one gulp.
