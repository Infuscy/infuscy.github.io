## Chapter 1619: "Dark Meteor", The Next Step (Part 1)

The area around Crystal Lake, northeast of Goldshire, had beautiful scenery, but no one had ever tried to settle near it. As his dad said, even fishermen and hunters didn't want to go near the place.

A long time ago — years before the gnolls started fighting humans for Elwynn Forest — the murlocs had already settled around Crystal Lake.

These monsters had even once occupied the riverbanks all the way from here to the Neltharion River, blocking Stormwind's residents from traveling to Lakeshire or heading east to Grand Town. Back then, not many farmers dared to clear land and farm in this area.

As the Kingdom of Stormwind grew and developed, humans eventually pushed the murlocs back to Crystal Lake's shores. From that point on, the road between Goldshire and Eastvale Logging Camp was only occasionally threatened by a stray murloc attacking lone travelers — stable enough for regular passage.

But the victory over the murlocs also marked the accelerating expansion of the human kingdom, which eventually led to the protracted Gnoll War and the later Gurubashi Troll War.

Since then, the murlocs had never left Crystal Lake. They settled there, harboring resentment and fear toward humans and gnolls, but not daring to start conflicts with their neighbors. That repressed tension was also why murlocs — whose intelligence couldn't even match gnolls — could wield shadow magic...

But this also made Crystal Lake's shores a decent place for shadow magic practice. At the very least, there was absolutely no way to practice Hand of Gul'dan in the Slaughtered Lamb's practice room.

As evening fell and the sun hadn't fully set, Jace and Ursula rode to the area and followed the lakeside for a good distance. Despite the legend of murlocs controlling Crystal Lake, they didn't see a single murloc along the way. Jace couldn't even sense any emotion — neither hatred, nor hostility, nor fear of outsiders.

But looking at it from another angle: after seeing Midnight — an evil horse burned into a ghost by demons — and smelling the mad aura from Karazhan's Black Tower, any murloc who still planned to stay and fight would be wasting the intelligence they'd used to compete with gnolls and humans for territory all these years.

Jace jumped off Midnight's back, patted its rump to signal it to move away, and looked at the alchemist who'd stopped not far off. "I thought you wouldn't want to come out and try new spells."

"I never said I don't like learning." Ursula tied her horse to a nearby tree. "What I don't like is going out on mercenary work with Erland and the others — tiring and troublesome. Of course, I'm not saying Crystal Lake is much better. Nobody wants to come near this place unless they're traveling with a mage like you. Can you smell that rotting stench in the air? That's definitely the smell of human flesh. Some unlucky bastard must have delivered takeout to the murlocs."

"I feel like murloc dwellings generally smell like this." Jace stretched. "How was the trip? Did you get familiar with the casting guide I gave you?"

"You wrote it clearly enough." Ursula glanced at the scroll in her hand again. "But there are parts scratched out and revised, plus some beginner tips. Did you figure these out yourself during your own learning, or... I'm curious who you taught this to before."

"Demisette Klaus," Jace said. "Do you know her?"

"Ah, I know her. Of course I know her." Ursula's eyes flickered. "She was a sorceress with remarkable shadow talent, very skilled at summoning demons. Word was she'd been in close contact with some wandering Shadow Council members, so she had many obscure abilities. A few years back, she was quite famous among the warlocks in the south. Lady Klaus... Galders wanted to invite her to join the Slaughtered Lamb. She seemed interested too — probably because of you. But it's a shame."

"A shame?" Jace asked.

"She's dead, Jace," Ursula said.

Jace froze. "Why? Who did it?"

Ursula shook her head. "A group of warlocks killed her. Some say it was the assassins from Eastern Weald. Others say she made enemies with shadow mages from Gilneas. Some speculate she was hiding immensely powerful orc spells and refused to share them... In any case, a warlock dying by another warlock's hand is as common as a dwarf dying in a tavern. Nobody really cares. By the time the news reached me, it was already vague."

She smirked. "Why did you teach her that spell? Did she use that approach that works so well on other warlocks?"

Jace managed a weak smile. "She did try that approach on me. But at the time, I was more lacking in tangible goods than physical comfort. I took a few gold coins from her. And I didn't guarantee I could teach her."

In truth, what ultimately made Jace decide to teach her Hand of Gul'dan was helping her infiltrate the lighthouse. And he had a faint suspicion that her death was closely tied to her betrayal of those warlocks at the lighthouse.

But as Ursula said, a warlock dying in the pursuit of dark arts was all too common. The real reason might not match Jace's guess anyway.

"Good thing you said that." Ursula raised her eyebrows, silently recited the Hand of Gul'dan incantation once, and said, "Now let me try."

"I can demonstrate first?" Jace offered.

"You're not recovered enough for that, Jace." The alchemist said. "Let me try first. You guide me from the side. Remember how we first learned new spells? Just a single incantation, nothing else. And we still managed to build a thick spellbook. And my shadow control is much better now than it was a few years ago."

Jace nodded and raised his hands slightly. Ursula walked to the open space ahead.

The blood-red sunset soaked half the forest, shining on the dark lake water. The alchemist drank a bottle of shadow oil she'd brewed, steadied her thin frame on the shore, and cast down two soul shards. As she chanted the incantation, her robes began to drift gently. Jace could almost smell the shadow shifting in the air.

This shadow was nothing the murlocs could muster. A fierce cold gripped Jace's heart, as if nothing familiar remained within his sight — or even for hundreds of miles around.

Loneliness. Ursula was refining the first emotion according to the casting method Jace had summarized for Hand of Gul'dan.

Ever since she'd snatched Jace back to Azeroth from Kil'jaeden's grasp, Jace had never doubted Ursula's shadow talent — even if she still relied on potions to aid her casting.

In theory, someone like her — half-elf with a trace of elven blood — was actually the most suitable race for becoming a warlock. Most half-elves could never escape loneliness, a miserable chaotic childhood, and the difficult process of growing up in the cracks. As a result, most half-elves harbored strong hatred for both humans and elves.

It was happening now. Jace could even feel the emotion radiating from Ursula as she raised her hands — an emotion that rejected everything, even stirring a sliver of panic in his own heart. Hatred. The second emotion for Hand of Gul'dan.

But just as the emotion peaked, Jace sensed that the dense shadow energy lacked a grip. The soul shards had been consumed, but the energy was spinning aimlessly in the air, dissipating far too quickly.

The difficulty of the casting process worsened the alchemist's mana consumption. And her body didn't have a mana pool as deep as Jace's. She went weak at the knees, lowered her arms, and started panting heavily.

"What's the difference... between this Dark Meteor and Shadow Bolt, Jace?" Ursula asked.

"Shadow Bolt doesn't need soul shards," Jace said. "So this spell's energy gathering involves the Twisting Nether. That's why it needs a much larger casting space than Shadow Bolt. You can't gather a powerful enough Dark Meteor just by relying on your own power and the shadow energy floating in the surrounding space, the way you can with Shadow Bolt."

"Soul shards. The Twisting Nether." Ursula gazed at the lake, lost in thought for a moment. "I understand. Let me try again."
