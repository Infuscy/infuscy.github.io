## Chapter 162: The Royal Cathedral

"Jace! Jace Sesso!"

"Coming."

Jace sat up and glanced around, only to realize suddenly that he was at home, not his rented room in Stormwind.

"Get changed, Jace." His mother leaned against the doorframe. "I'm taking you to see a priest today."

"What do I need a priest for?" Jace asked.

"To look at that burn on your arm, obviously!" Henny said. "It's a week's walk from Southshore to here! Even if you took a boat across Lake Lordamere, it'd still be four or five days total, and I know you didn't pay that burn a lick of attention the whole time. It must be a mess under those bandages by now."

"I'm fine, really, Mom. Don't make me go see a priest..."

Jace actually found it strange himself — given that he had the Black Dragon's Blood, the burn's healing rate hadn't sped up at all. Black dragons were supposed to be practically immune to fire, after all. He should've just found some random animal in the wild and drained it to heal his wounds before coming home, to avoid worrying his mother like this. But Dushan had warned him not to go out into the wild, and he understood the danger himself. He still got dizzy and headaches now and then — not as bad as when he'd first woken up in Dalaran, but if something went wrong and he collapsed outside the city walls, that'd be the end of him.

"You're just as stubborn as when you were little." His mother frowned. "The Holy Light isn't going to hurt you!"

Yeah, when I was little it wouldn't have. But now? Not so sure!

Jace screamed that internally, but he couldn't say it out loud. He only scratched his chin and asked, "So, are we going to the chapel? I'm a little scared of that old priest..."

"We're going to the cathedral," his mother said. "I've prayed for you every single day these past two years, Jace. You're going to tell me that in those two years in Stormwind, the Holy Light never once looked out for you? I think the fact that you're standing here whole and healthy in front of me again — besides your own effort and luck — the Holy Light must've had a small hand in it, don't you think?"

Jace figured he really couldn't argue with that. The Holy Light had helped him out a few times, after all. Whether it was the result of his mother's prayers, though, he couldn't say for sure.

But did the effect of her prayers even matter?

He sat up and hugged his mother, who was thin as a child. "Fine, Mom. I'll go with you."

Henny broke into a grin at once, and at that moment Jace felt like she didn't resemble his mother so much as a little sister.

She dolled herself up a bit, put on a pretty yellow dress and a white scarf. Jace pulled on the shirt she'd mended the night before, and the two of them headed out.

The terrain of the Lordaeron capital wasn't as rugged and complicated as Stormwind's, with its highs and lows. It sat on a flat plateau, so getting around was a lot easier for someone used to life in Stormwind.

Henny led Jace through gate after gate with practiced ease. The closer they got to the cathedral, the smoother the roads became.

Red-brown high walls, bright green eaves, domes — gradually the distant palace came into view.

The Lordaeron capital was like a larger, faded, deliberately weathered version of Dalaran with the fairytale colors bleached out. Austere, all sharp edges, unapproachable.

When he was a kid, Jace had always felt like this ancient city with a thousand-year history was a magnificent, unrivaled, outrageously lavish high-security prison. After two years wandering the outside world, that feeling had only grown stronger.

"How long are you planning to stay home, Jace?" Henny asked as they walked.

"I'm heading back to Dalaran the day after tomorrow," Jace replied.

"Leaving so soon?" Henny's voice carried a hint of disappointment.

"I'll come back to visit as soon as I can. I won't wait another two years," Jace said. "I'm a little more comfortable financially now. If I've got the time, I'll come back."

"No need to worry about us so much. Vic and I are still young — don't treat us like a couple of old folks who can barely move." Henny said.

"You must have something important keeping you busy in Dalaran?"

"I do," Jace said. "There's a linguistics event. Your excellent son is representing Stormwind, no less."

"Representing Stormwind?" Henny asked, a teasing note creeping in. "Does King Varian know you're representing Stormwind? Actually, King Varian came through here last year. I saw him — he was riding a horse like a real knight, tall and strapping, almost as imposing as Vic was when he was young."

Jace thought her infatuation with his old man really knew no bounds.

Then again, he'd never seen Vic Sesso riding a horse in his youth — maybe he really had looked like a palace knight.

"Of course Varian knows," Jace said. "You remember that mage's outfit I wore here? He's the one who paid for it. I actually know him personally."

"Really?" Henny exclaimed. "How did you get to know him?"

And that's when Jace realized he'd shown off to the wrong person. How was he supposed to tell his mother about Stonewatch?

"It was just... excellent grades at the magic academy, and then I got an audience with the king."

Henny frowned. "Your ability to lie without blushing is completely useless on your mother."

Jace smiled. "Anyway, just don't push it. As long as you know I'm not lying."

"Fine, let's say it's true. Who else in this world but me would know you're lying and still be willing to believe you?" Henny said helplessly. "But King Varian's a good man — it really is a blessing to know him. The Kingdom of Stormwind's going to recover and get better and better."

"How can you tell he's a good man?" Jace asked, curious.

Henny said, "When he entered the city, he wasn't wearing any of the fine clothes a king ought to wear. His hair was a mess, like he'd just been attacked by the Horde before riding in. I could tell he was eating himself alive inside over his father's murder and his homeland being ravaged by orcs. That's a proud young man."

Jace said, "Maybe those politicians were just putting on a show for you all — playing the victim, fishing for sympathy so the people of Lordaeron would donate a little more to Stormwind's reconstruction."

"A little more would be good too — wouldn't that end up in my son's pockets?" Henny laughed.

Jace was about to laugh along, but then he thought about how he'd come to Stormwind to work as a laborer and his entire gold coin's worth of compensation had been whittled down to a few dozen silvers by god-knows-who — and just like that, he couldn't laugh anymore.

Those bastards, skimming off the workers' pay. Sooner or later the Defias Brotherhood's going to scatter your ashes to the wind.

As they chatted, Henny led Jace up to a main road — only to find it blocked off by guards ahead.

"What's going on up there?" Jace asked.

"Our little prince must've gone to the cathedral for services again," Henny whispered. "He's been coming quite often lately."
