Chapter 1621: From Gul'dan to Ursula Delin

"Sorry, master. I'm late." Morophil folded her wings and landed beside him. Sazeno poked his head out of the bag beneath a nearby tree, staring blankly at the scene. "What happened? I zoned out for a second and this is what I come back to? Weren't you two practicing magic? Why does it look like the Burning Legion invaded?" "You're not late. Everything happened too fast. We weren't prepared either." Jace patted the succubus's partially spread wing membrane. "I'm in bad shape, but I can still handle a single Doomguard. And Midnight helped." "I knew the demonic beast we tamed couldn't be as docile as it looks..." Morophil muttered. "But that was a Doomguard, Jace." Ursula still seemed shaken. "Before this, the Dagger Society needed at least three people for a Doomguard summoning ritual. The incantation was extremely complex, the ritual was strictly regulated, and every single demon minion we had — from felhounds to succubi — had to be on standby. Otherwise we might not have been able to handle what we summoned. But just now... it just manifested into the world through that meteor." "I swear I didn't chant any other incantations, or follow any other casting methods from your manual. You have to believe me. And you only used two soul shards — why did three demons appear?" "It might be because of my staff," Jace said. "My staff can store a bit of soul energy for emergencies." Ursula seemed to relax a little at that. She glanced at Jace's staff, then quickly looked away, staring again at the corpses. "I'll need to call Erland Dudemoor to dispose of these bodies. I don't expect to get much money for them this time. I just hope Maginor Dumas and Mathias Shaw's people don't find out too soon and give us trouble." "Nobody usually comes to Crystal Lake, Ursula. I'll explain it to them." Jace said. "Right now, the important thing is — I don't think my staff was the main reason that Doomguard was summoned. Ursula, we only provided the mana. You were the one directing the ritual. You know that. The key is, what emotion were you channeling at that moment?" "This was my first time trying such a terrifying spell. I was nervous just maintaining the required emotions during the incantation. Paying attention to anything else was beyond my ability." Ursula's voice strained to stay calm. "Can't you sense it, Jace? I know you're a powerful shadow manipulator. A warlock at your level can detect the slightest hesitation in someone's tone, the smallest concealment in their heart through shadow perception." "I'm not used to sensing the emotions of people close to me." Jace shook his head. "And just now, time was too tight. I was too focused on maintaining shadow stability to notice." Ursula stared at him for a moment, then blinked. "I think it might have been just some nervousness, or—" "Then let's try again?" Jace asked. "Morophil can help sense emotional shifts too. She helped me figure this out when I first tried this spell. She wasn't here just now." "I'd be happy to help, Lady Delin." Morophil gave a slight bow. "You want me to deliberately use this meteor to summon a bunch of powerful demons?" The alchemist quickly waved her hands. "I can't do that, Jace. On one hand, the shadow oil's effect has worn off. On the other, my energy is mostly spent—" "Morophil is here this time. And you've seen Midnight — it's not an ordinary horse. You don't need to worry about the demon danger." Jace added after a pause, "And if you're worried about something like what happened to Jagin happening again, you really don't need to be that tense." "Don't push me, Jace!" The sudden outburst startled Jace, but Ursula immediately felt her own loss of control. The tension that had been straining her voice collapsed. She hugged her head and crouched down, shoulders trembling. The sight of Ursula reminded Jace of the moment in the Molten Core when he'd been at Grede's side. Only now, he was standing where Vastraz had stood. The alchemist's whole body was enveloped in subtle but wildly chaotic shadow energy. If she'd been weak right after completing the spell, she was now on the verge of losing control entirely. "Sorry," Jace said. "I might have been too eager to figure this out. Legend has it that the creator of this spell, the orc warlock Gul'dan — or even Kil'jaeden himself — could use this Dark Meteor to summon powerful demons from the Twisting Nether. Like infernals." "You're saying I channeled an advanced version — one even you never achieved?" Ursula asked quietly. "Without a doubt," Jace said. "I've cast this spell more times than I can count. Not once did I come close to what you just did." Ursula lowered her head and thought for a moment. "Maybe it was impatience. Wanting to learn the spell quickly." For Jace, who was eager to understand the spell's mechanics, gathering shadow energy using impatience was simple. But the moment he summoned the energy, he immediately realized it was not only different from what he'd sensed in Ursula's ritual — it was the complete opposite in terms of how active it felt. "Let's call it a day." Jace let out a long breath. "I think you're exhausted too, Ursula. And we've already made good progress. Your emotional control — you've seen it yourself. You can reach a very high level, even with such difficult energy. Want to go to the Lion's Pride Inn for a drink?" "Inferiority," Ursula said, holding her forehead. "I think that was it." So that was it. Jace felt as if a fog had lifted from before his eyes. Indeed, that chaotic, spiraling energy he couldn't quite put his finger on — it was a powerful sense of inferiority. Only, after encountering stronger and stronger opponents on his journeys, it had been a long time since he'd felt that emotion himself. When he'd touched it with his consciousness just now, he hadn't even recognized it at first. So this is how you unlocked the spell's maximum power, Gul'dan? Suddenly it all made sense. But... "Did you deliberately put that emotion into the ritual?" Jace asked carefully. "Or... did you take the wrong potion?" Ursula angrily threw a stone at him. Jace caught it instinctively. She shouted in frustration, "Casting shadow magic in front of your damn little prodigy — wouldn't it be weirder if I didn't feel inferior!? Why do you have to ask such stupid redundant questions! I've already told you the answer. Go test it yourself! Leave me alone!" "Sorry, sorry!" Jace dropped the stone and raised his hands. But Ursula's mood already seemed less tangled and low than before. Getting what was weighing on your heart out into the open was always good, whether or not the people around you could understand. Still, why would an emotion like inferiority summon a Doomguard? That didn't match anything Jace knew about the Ered'ruin race. "Demons like the Ered'ruin feed on inferiority and cowardice, master." Morophil, as if sensing Jace's confusion through his soul, leaned close and said, "The spell you wield isn't really a proper summoning ritual. I don't think that Ered'ruin wanted to be summoned into this world. If I'm not mistaken, he was probably chasing prey and fell into a 'trap' — forced to fight you." "That fits your theory," Jace said. "Let me try it myself this time. I'll control the spell's scale and protect both me and Ursula, Morophil." "Of course, dear," the succubus smiled. Jace took a strong mana potion from Ursula. The alchemist's expression shifted. "Hey, you're not really going to try again, are you?" "I can still have that drink after one more try." Jace walked to the open space, raised Ulthalesh, and focused. He chanted the incantation, this time infusing his gathered dark energy with a feeling of inferiority. Honestly, inferiority wasn't easy for him to muster these days. But with patience, searching every detail in his memory, he could still scrape some together. After a few attempts, he finally found the feeling close to the version he'd channeled with Ursula, and continued the incantation. Sure enough, as the chant reached the middle, the planar resonance of the Twisting Nether intensified — much stronger than any previous Hand of Gul'dan he'd cast. The difference this time was that he was directing the ritual. He could clearly sense something pursuing his consciousness. Then, as countless black serpents twisted together and the Dark Meteor gathered, his demonic vision clearly caught the demon souls mingled within... Jace suddenly raised his right hand, clenched his fist, and cut off the incantation. The Dark Meteor was forcibly detonated! The purple-red demonic mist exploded in the air not far away. The shockwave hammered the lake shore, stirring waves. Countless fragments of demonic armor and flesh scattered. The half-remaining skull of an Ered'ruin rolled to his feet. The demon skull stared at Jace, its lips moving once before going still — not even a sound. Its demonic heart had certainly been shattered even more thoroughly than its head. "He... he hadn't even landed and you killed him, Jace?" Ursula stared wide-eyed. "No point giving him a chance to fight back. He was going to come after us anyway." Jace said. "The question now is how to make the demons this spell summons obey my commands completely. Just like Gul'dan could."
