## Chapter 1622: A Winter Friend

By the time Jace and Ursula made it back to Goldshire, it was pouring rain.

The main street through town was empty in the downpour, but the Lion's Pride Inn's windows were still brightly lit.

They tied their horses across the street and jogged into the inn. The main hall was fairly quiet. Jace thought it might be empty, but when they stepped inside, there were barely any empty tables. Most people were drinking and eating quietly. A few glanced toward the door but didn't seem particularly interested in the cloaked pair.

After getting used to the Black Forge, even the once-cozy Lion's Pride Inn felt somewhat deserted now. Honestly, ever since coming back from the Molten Core, everything felt cold. The weather was cold. The inn was cold. Even the people felt colder. Jace was having trouble adjusting.

He led Ursula to a windowless corner. A barmaid came over quickly. "Good evening, you two. Anything to eat or drink?"

"You first," Jace said to the alchemist.

"Since it's Winter Veil, I'll have a glass of eggnog," Ursula said.

"Same for me." Jace said. "And what food do you have lately? Pastries or something?"

"We've got a popular dessert in Goldshire lately. It came from the north." The barmaid said. "It's called the 'Prince's Favorite' — it's that prince's most beloved food. Would you two like to try it?"

"Prince's Favorite?" Jace looked up. "The Prince of Stromgarde, or the Prince of Lordaeron?"

"The golden prince of Lordaeren, of course." The barmaid smiled, as if comparing the two was ridiculous. "And the pastry itself is as golden as his hair, as elegantly curled. Honestly, it's not just Goldshire — every big town in the Alliance, from Stratholme to Darrowshire, including Stormwind itself, is serving it."

"What's in it exactly?" Jace asked.

The barmaid gestured. "Minced chicken and mushrooms cooked into a sauce, layered in baked pastry... like a small fruit pie. Very fragrant. A very refined pastry."

"Sounds pretty good. Not the weirdest combination I've ever eaten," Jace said.

"It's not just tasty." The barmaid said. "A lot of people say eating a 'Prince's Favorite' is like praying to the Holy Light — praying that Lordaeron's king will soon recover from his illness. It's also supposed to keep you healthy. Good omen, right?"

"Lucky for us, neither of us is exactly healthy today." Ursula flashed a quick smile. "I'll have one."

"Me too. Might as well bask in Prince Arthas's glory." Jace nodded.

"Coming right up, you two." The barmaid left cheerfully. Ursula cleared her throat and said quietly, "What's going on with Terenas II, really? I feel like he's been bedridden for months now."

"Word is it's from mosquito bites in the Wetlands." Jace said. "Kirin Tor mages told me Lordaeron might be using this as an excuse to dodge responsibility for attacking Blackrock Spire. I think that guess has some merit."

Ursula nodded. "He's milked enough sympathy from this. Sending the Hearthglen lord makes it so Varian can't argue. Maybe once Blackrock Spire falls, this guy will jump right out of bed and deliver a victory speech on behalf of the whole Lordaeron Alliance. Or have his good son do it. 'Golden Prince.' Such a Lordaeronian of Lordaeronians. What a pain."

She caught herself and looked up. "Sorry, Jace. I didn't mean to badmouth Lordaeron."

"I'm practically a Stormwind native by now." Jace smiled.

"Fair enough," Ursula smiled too.

"Speaking of which, it's not easy to get a meal and a drink with you," Jace said.

"That should be my line." Ursula said. "At least Sulenna, Erland, Galain and I still come here occasionally. Though the last time was about a year and a half ago."

The barmaid set down two glasses of eggnog. The alchemist took a big gulp and was silent for a while. The rain outside grew louder.

"This might sound stupid," she said quietly, "but I want to apologize for my attitude just now. Letting my pride affect the spell's emotional component — that's unimaginably dangerous for a warlock exploring new incantations."

"I was a bit surprised you'd feel inferior about shadow magic compared to me." Jace thought for a moment. "Let me put it this way: I've been traveling and fighting all these years, with plenty of chances to practice shadow magic. You've mostly been in your lab, brewing potions and researching recipes, occasionally going to Lordaeron or Dalaran. I should be impressed that you channeled such a powerful Dark Meteor on your first try."

Ursula didn't respond. She just lowered her head and took another sip.

Jace didn't try to sense what she was thinking. He just let his mind go blank and savored the sweet, creamy taste on his tongue. "Let's rest for a couple of days. No rush on the Destruction Potion. I don't think that shadow energy's effect on you can be shaken off that easily."

"Simple," Ursula said flatly. "Mountainsnout plus a little Grave Moss, mixed with a shadow oil tuned to the spell's required emotion. I'll be as good as new by tomorrow, Jace Sesso. I'm not like you, toughing everything out on raw meat and bone. I have a full alchemy lab."

"Then I won't tell you how to do your job," Jace said.

The barmaid brought two small plates and set them in front of each of them. One piece per person, each the size and shape of an egg tart.

Jace picked his up and took a bite. Yep. Tasted exactly like a chicken-and-mushroom egg tart.

"Not bad." Ursula chewed and nodded, then said casually, "I'm suddenly curious, Jace. In the Molten Core, when you said you almost thought Grede Thunderfist was going to die... that emotional loss of control nearly made the shadow consume you. It scared Vereesa and the others."

"Yeah," Jace nodded.

"What if I almost died?" Ursula asked with a provocative smile. "Or someone else in the Dagger Society, like—"

Jace shook his head. "I don't want to imagine those things, Ursula."

"Grede — that guy's usually so timid, but when push comes to shove, he fights like a reckless madman. So I used to imagine him dying. Over time, I thought I'd come to terms with the possibility that one day he might just die from something. But when I actually faced that moment... I realized I was nowhere near ready. As for you... I don't know. I can't even bring myself to think about it."

"So that's another benefit of studying shadow magic — you don't die as easily in this messed-up world. So your friends and family don't have to grieve."

As they ate and talked, Jace looked up. Ursula was already wiping her eyes.

"Is this advice coming from the world's most evil shadow manipulator?" she asked quietly.

"That's right." Jace smiled. Ursula laughed too.

"What's wrong?" Jace asked.

"Just... it's rare to meet a friend like you. Half-elves aren't easy to like, no matter how diluted their blood. Why are you friends with me? Because my potions are the cheapest?"

"Your potions are absolutely not the cheapest, Ursula Delin."

"Fine. You caught me."

"I honestly don't know why, Ursula. No reason. Does it need one?"

"It really doesn't. Cheers."

"Cheers."

They downed their eggnog in one gulp and set the empty glasses on the table. Ursula took a long, deep breath, then said quietly, "So, how are you planning to figure out controlling those demons?"

"I'm going to ask Karrels," Jace said. "He should know something about it. I'll head back to the Tower of Ilgalar tomorrow. With Midnight, it won't take long."

"I'll keep trying to feed the cauldron shadowfire. After tonight, it should go more smoothly," Ursula said.

"It will." Jace licked his fingers and stood up. The barmaid rushed over. "That'll be seven silver coins, you two."

The male warlock looked at the female warlock. The female warlock looked at the male warlock, shrugged, and raised an eyebrow slightly. The male warlock sighed and pulled out his coin purse.
