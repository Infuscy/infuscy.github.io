Chapter 1623: The Northern Paladin (Revised)

After picking up a set of purification potions specially formulated by Ursula to alleviate wounds and Shadow corruption, Jace set out from Stormwind and headed back to Irgalar's Tower, intent on studying the "advanced version" of Gul'dan's Hand that the two of them had accidentally stumbled upon. Whether it was a player using Gul'dan's Hand to summon demons, or Gul'dan himself using the spell to summon them, every demon that came through obeyed the caster's commands. If the Gul'dan who had emerged from the Warlord of Draenor's peaceful timeline was loyal to the Burning Legion, and so the demons he summoned naturally served him, then the imps summoned by players made no sense under that framework. There might of course be gameplay reasons involved. Jace knew full well that things probably wouldn't map one-to-one here, but if he could use Gul'dan's Hand to summon one or even several Doomguard-level demons to fight for his side, then when they assaulted Blackrock Spire, those swarms of orc soldiers would meet another group of allies. He really wanted to make headway on this spell before the Alliance army marched on Blackrock Spire. Midnight galloped like a black shadow through the sparse trees of Elwynn Forest. To avoid running into Stormwind's night patrols, Jace deliberately kept south of the main road. At least this horse wouldn't stumble or trip over rough terrain like an ordinary warhorse—it could only go slightly slower. Once he passed Eastvale Logging Camp and entered the Redridge valley, the roads would open up and he wouldn't have to worry so much. Traveling through the dense woods of Elwynn now, Jace no longer needed to be as cautious as he'd been back when he first came here hunting with Grede, when even a surprise wolf pack or wild bear could have given them a rough time. Midnight's demonic aura handled all the unnecessary trouble for him, far more effective than the old black dragonscale cloak. But just as he was nearing the Redridge mountain pass, Jace felt something... off. He couldn't help glancing left and right into the forest. "You feel it too, master." Morophil spoke from inside his cloak, turning her head to look back the way they'd come. "What?" Sazeno grabbed Jace's collar. "I don't feel anything." "It's probably not arcane magic," Jace said. In fact, it wasn't arcane magic at all, nor the scent of beasts, nor undead creeping over from Duskwood across the River Neltharion. It was something else... The rustling of leaves overhead was drowned out by the sudden roar of wings. Midnight snorted sharply beneath him. "A gryphon?" Jace jerked his head up. A golden blaze of light—as intense as a helicopter searchlight—suddenly lit up the entire forest and stabbed into his eyes. Jace felt a scorching heat wash over his whole body. Midnight let out a pained whinny. Morophil grunted and rolled off the horse. Dirt and broken branches flew everywhere under hooves and demon wings; many dead leaves were incinerated the moment they entered the light. "Die, demon! Your escape is over! Face the judgment of the Holy Light!" A loud shout exploded from above. Against the light that was dimming but converging to a single point, Jace glimpsed a young paladin in golden armor riding a gryphon, holding high the source of that radiance—a long-handled warhammer. He plummeted straight down like a holy fire meteor tearing through the night. Jace had deliberately avoided the main road to prevent exactly this kind of situation. And yet here he was—not only facing a paladin, but a paladin on a gryphon? "I'm Jace Sesso! Advisor to King Varian Wrynn—" Before Jace could even finish, the warhammer was already swinging at his face. Midnight screeched and pulled up short trying to dodge the gryphon's dive but didn't make it in time. Ulthalesh dragged a blood-red arc of magic in the opposite direction to meet the strike. The holy fire that illuminated everything and the bloodlight that devoured all in the shadow of night clashed head-on, producing a piercing shriek that stole the breath from every living thing. The gryphon in white-and-gold barding screeched and tumbled backward; the Lordaeron-crested white tabard around its neck had been torn apart by the collision. "Taelan!" Another voice, much older, came from behind. A second paladin on a gryphon swooped in from an angle. Jace gripped Ulthalesh and turned. The blow from meeting the first paladin head-on had left his arms throbbing and numb—after all, his arms were still flesh and blood, not forged by the Titans themselves like Ulthalesh. "Father, beware his scythe!" the young paladin shouted. "Lord Tirion Fordring! Keep your son in check!" Jace pulled back his hood and called out to the older voice. "I'm not a death knight! Look—I'm alive!" At those words, the paladin behind him finally halted. Morophil was already planted between him and Jace, her wings fully spread, looking even larger than the Hinterlands gryphon before her. The blazing flames on the twin blades in her hands now outshone the radiance of both paladins. But then several more gryphon riders descended from above. Now even the Apocalypse in Morophil's hand seemed dim in comparison. Among the newcomers were several priests with staves and another paladin with a spear. All of them, like the first two riders, sat on armored gryphons draped in Lordaeron-crested tabards, wearing the silver war-plate of the Silver Hand. Jace stroked Midnight's black mane, a chill of afterthought running through him. If this time Arna had been with him and been mistaken for a death knight right alongside him... that single clash had nearly killed the Hinterlands gryphon. Not even an Arathi warhorse could have survived that. The young paladin in front of him had his long-handled warhammer completely destroyed. The arm that had held it was covered in blood. He was breathing heavily, glaring at Jace, his dark-gold hair and stubbly face making him look about the same age as Jace and Varian. This was Taelan Fordring. Tirion Fordring's son. Jace had heard the others call his name clearly just now. Between their tabards and crests, the news he'd heard from Lordaeron before, their appearances, and the way they'd reacted when Jace named Tirion Fordring—he was one hundred percent certain now. In another timeline, after Lordaeron was destroyed by the Scourge, Taelan Fordring of the Silver Hand had joined the Scarlet Crusade and become one of its supreme leaders. But unlike the other Crusade members who grew increasingly fanatical through hatred and demonic manipulation, Taelan Fordring remembered his father's words and held onto his last shred of reason. By then, however, the Scarlet Crusade—secretly led by the dreadlord Balnazzar—could no longer tolerate any doubt or hesitation. He hadn't met a good end. If this young man was Taelan Fordring, then the sturdy middle-aged man with light-golden shoulder-length hair behind him was the elder Fordring—the Lord of Hearthglen. Tirion Fordring wore ornate dark-golden heavy plate armor beneath a thick blue cloak, carrying a long-handled gold-veined silver hammer. The charge of being an "orc sympathizer" had not diminished his authority as a leader of the Silver Hand. He was also a formidable paladin—Jace could feel the horse beneath him, Midnight, unusually alert, muscles tensed. After all, this horse was the real deal as far as undead went. But Stormwind had no news of the Fordrings having arrived at the harbor. They just suddenly appeared— —Arna had been with him, mistaken for a death knight right alongside him. Even if it was a dreadlord in disguise, they'd have to at least put on a better act, go through the proper protocols for a northern lord. "If I'm not lost, this is Elwynn, not Hearthglen, Lord Tirion Fordring." He pointed his blood-stained staff at the wincing Taelan Fordring behind him. "So I think we'd all better calm down and figure out what the hell is going on before this gets out of hand." "The Silver Hand is hunting death knights along the River Neltharion in the name of King Varian Wrynn," the young knight called out. "If you are truly Jace Sesso, then as His Majesty's advisor, you would know nothing of this?" "In King Varian's name? Is this related to the necromantic plague at Blackrock Spire?" Jace asked. The paladins didn't answer. Jace wasn't surprised—they weren't sure what to make of this strange solitary rider and weren't about to share sensitive intelligence. "Stand down, Taelan." Tirion Fordring approached. "I apologize for his impulsiveness, Master Jace Sesso. But according to what King Varian has informed us, a fallen knight wielding dark magic has indeed appeared in this area. We had to be prepared." "We met briefly at the Cathedral of Light before. Though your appearance is not far from what I remember, I cannot be one hundred percent certain. What I can be certain of is this—we all witnessed Archbishop Alonsus Faol personally bestow Tyr's Steadfast Seal upon you in the cathedral square. Please show it to us." Jace nodded and reached over his shoulder to grab the Seal pinned to his cloak. He grabbed nothing. Damn it. He'd deliberately worn an old, dark-gray linen cloak to avoid looking too flashy—not his ornate red dragonscale cloak with the Seal on it. Since he was planning to return to Stormwind anyway, the Seal was in a package with his other valuables, left behind in Ursula's alchemy lab. Show off when you shouldn't, and your mother scolds you. Then when you actually need the thing, you don't have it on you. Jace frowned. Was his relationship with the Holy Light always this tangled? "That Seal is a critical item for me. I don't carry it while traveling." He took off the Lordaeron Knight's Ring from his finger. "But this ring—bestowed by King Terenas II himself and placed in my hand by Prince Arthas—should be proof enough of who I am, shouldn't it?" Jace had expected this to dispel the Lordaeronians' hostility. Instead, the Light-worshippers' expressions grew even more serious. "What's wrong with the ring?" "Forgive our discourtesy, Master Jace Sesso." The paladin lord spoke. "Please come with us to Lakeshire. There is someone there who can confirm your identity. If this truly is a misunderstanding, I will offer my sincere apologies on behalf of the Silver Hand." Jace let out a long sigh. "Lead the way, then."
