Chapter 1624: Shadow of the Knight (Revised)

"Keep an eye on your magical beast. The griffons here are wary of it. We're not far from Lakeshire, but there's still a stretch to go, and it'd be best if we and our mounts all get along during this trip," Tirion said. "Then keep those griffons' beaks and your warhammers away from me and my companion, my lord," Jace said. "Otherwise it'll be hard to explain to King Varian what really happened." Taelan Fordring was clearly still bristling, glancing at Jace, but he lowered his head again from the searing pain in his arm. A priest had already bandaged him up. "Fair enough." The Paladin Lord nodded in Jace's direction. "Please don't converse further with this Death Knight, Lord Tirion Fordring," one knight warned. "Don't forget what trouble that orc brought you." Tirion swayed gently on his walking griffon's back, saying nothing in reply. On the road, a group of griffons were proceeding on foot — a rare sight. Two of them had been injured in the earlier fight and couldn't fly. Combined with Jace's horse, the paladins had decided to walk back to town. "King Varian said these Death Knights were spreading plague under the Black Dragon's orders," a priest said. "Should we really be bringing him back to Lakeshire like this?" "Archmage Khadgar should have arrived at the Lake Everstill camp by now," the Paladin Lord said, urging his griffon forward with a dwarven call. "He'll sort everything out." Jace called ahead. "The commander of the Silver Hand came south. Why not visit the Cathedral of Light to meet the bishops and Stormwind's citizens?" Tirion turned back. "I came to the Kingdom of Stormwind to root out evil. I'd rather start hunting for traces of Death Knights immediately than waste time on ceremonies and formalities. Besides, I'm still a man under sentence. I don't deserve the welcome of a people who've suffered so much from orc invasion." As a follower of the Holy Light carrying the sin of being an orc sympathizer, Tirion Fordring's choice wasn't surprising. But what intrigued Jace more was why a knight from Lordaeron was so eager to hunt Death Knights. Combined with the necrotic plague inside Blackrock Spire, the more he thought about it, the more wrong it felt. They hadn't traveled far when the sky began to lighten. In the distance, the hazy outline of the Stonewatch waterfall came into view. The lead paladin blew his horn, and from the camp at the far end of the bridge, a horn answered. Before long, Jace spotted a blue figure at the stockade gate. It was Khadgar. That meant rescue. Just as old Fordring had said — Khadgar would surely have a way to verify Jace's identity. What Jace hadn't expected, though, was that Khadgar didn't seem especially thrilled to see him either. Particularly when his gaze landed on Midnight beneath Jace. "Jace Sesso. It's you." The archmage strode over quickly. "I finally found you. Where the hell have you been? I searched from Ilgalar's Tower to your parents' house. I even made a trip to Shadowforge City. Wasted no small amount of mana!" "Something about the plague, Khadgar," Jace said. "I've been running back and forth the past few days." "Necrotic plague... Necrotic plague. Damn it." Khadgar rubbed his forehead, muttering. "That horse needs to be taken aside first, Sesso." "Fine," Jace said. "Help Master Sesso take that horse to the stable. Don't tie it with the other horses. Find something to separate it. Don't let the other soldiers see it." The archmage pointed anxiously at a nearby apprentice. "So he really is Jace Sesso," Taelan Fordring said, caught off guard. "The genuine article!" Khadgar started to reach out and slap Midnight's departing rump, then pulled his hand back. "I gave him that horse. Even without using any arcane magic to verify his identity, the fact that the horse recognizes him is proof enough!" "My apologies, Master Sesso." Taelan Fordring cradled his injured arm, his voice low. "I shouldn't have attacked without thinking." "I am his father." Tirion Fordring gave the Silver Hand salute, fist to chest, then bowed his head slightly. "I should have fulfilled my duty as a father and a commanding officer, Master Jace Sesso. Failing to judge the situation beforehand was my negligence." The other clergy and paladins added their apologies as well. Finally Tirion said, "I will report this immediately to King Varian and the Silver Hand, along with a formal apology. To have injured the hero who saved the Sons of Lothar on the eve of our joint assault on Blackrock Spire... this truly was a grave misunderstanding." Khadgar looked at them. "I thought you were escorting Jace Sesso here. Turns out you dragged him here as a Death Knight? What a mess." "We can discuss apologies later, everyone," Jace said. "So what intel do I not know about regarding the Death Knights and the necrotic plague, Khadgar? And what does Midnight have to do with it?" The archmage glanced around, then pointed to one of the tents in the camp. "Let's talk inside." Jace, Khadgar, and the Fordrings entered the tent. Khadgar immediately cast a spell to seal the entrance, looked at the three people before him as if he wanted to say something but hesitated, then finally said, "You should tell him, Lord Fordring." The Paladin Lord nodded. "A few days ago, when we first arrived at the harbor, the priests of the Cathedral of Light informed me that Death Knights might be active inside Blackrock Spire. More importantly, those Death Knights were spreading plague, just like the legends from Tirisfal and the Eastern Weald. So we immediately flew to Morgan's Watch on griffons to meet King Varian." "The Silver Hand didn't believe the rumors at first," Taelan Fordring said. "Since the Orc Wars, those kinds of stories have never stopped, and there was never solid evidence of plague spreading." "The Kirin Tor had the same attitude," Khadgar added. "But since there's a clear similarity between what's happening at Blackrock Spire and what's happening in the north, we have to consider a connection." "Yes," the younger paladin said. "Could you tell me more about these rumors?" Jace asked. Taelan nodded. "In recent months, the north has seen legends of Black Knights wielding blood-red blades, riding black horses with evil green eyes. Like Teron Gorefiend and those fallen knights who attacked Hillsbrad Foothills ten years ago, Master Sesso. Many commoners and even priests believe the king's illness is linked to these Black Knights." Black Knights wielding blood-red scythes, riding black horses? Jace felt his heart pound at the description. He glanced at the staff resting nearby, still seeping blood. Was the implication not obvious enough? "The king's illness is groundless speculation." Khadgar had finally looked away from Ulthalesh. "His Majesty hasn't spread the illness to anyone caring for him since returning to the capital. It was clearly just a fever from mosquito bites in the swamps." "The church couldn't cure him? After months, he's still not better?" Jace asked. "King Terenas has actually improved a lot. The problem is that he's old and frail. A serious illness like that isn't something you recover from easily with just potions or the Holy Light." Khadgar finished and glanced at the two paladins. "My apologies, gentlemen." A sleepless night, unhealed injuries, the suspicion of the journey, and this sudden ominous news — Jace felt exhaustion creeping through his entire body. "What do those Black Knights look like?" he couldn't help asking. "I mean, their faces." "So far, no one has seen their true faces," Tirion answered. "But someone witnessed a knight passing nearby wearing a silver Lordaeron Knight's Ring on his hand." Now Jace understood why showing his own ring had only made the Silver Hand more suspicious. "Is this why you've been looking for me these past few days, Khadgar?" "More or less," Khadgar admitted. Jace could guess the unspoken half of that sentence. By now, plenty of people in Lordaeron had probably begun conflating the legendary Black Knights with Jace Sesso. At that thought, he couldn't help pressing hard on his brow.
