Chapter 1625: Step by Step

An image of a Black Knight wielding a blood-red scythe — clearly aimed at him. Jace knew it. Ulthalesh... except for the previous night when Taelan Fordring's sudden arrival had left him no choice but to pull it out and show the scythe's true form, and Khadgar who'd seen it before, Jace couldn't think of anyone else who'd witnessed its appearance. And the ones who knew about Ulthalesh now — aside from his companions in Shadowforge City and Khadgar — were only Aegwynn and Kil'jaeden. From the way those paladins and Khadgar had reacted to Midnight, that Black Knight probably also rode a horse similar to his. If this was Kil'jaeden's work, how did he know about Midnight? Aegwynn had gone to great trouble to leave Karazhan back then, even leaving behind a significant portion of her own mana. If she'd wanted him dead, Karazhan would have been the perfect place. Even afterward, she could have come back to ambush him while he was weakened. No need to go through all this trouble and circle around. Was it Khadgar? Jace looked up at the archmage, who was still discussing Death Knight sightings with the two paladins. Their voices suddenly felt distant and strange. But he hadn't been riding Midnight for just a couple of days. News that Jace Sesso possessed a tall black strange horse had probably already reached the Wizard Sanctum and the Kirin Tor. That alone proved nothing. He realized his thoughts were spiraling out of control and gave his head a slight shake. Tirion Fordring stopped mid-sentence, apparently sensing something off about Jace. He looked over. "Master Sesso, I apologize for all the trouble. Perhaps you should rest for a while to recover from last night's ordeal. I'll find an excellent priest to treat your wounds." Jace said, "I appreciate the offer, but I've already brought the right potions. I just need to take them on schedule. Also, I have some things I'd like to discuss with Archmage Khadgar, so..." Tirion nodded. He looked at his son, signaling it was time to leave. Taelan Fordring seemed to want to say something, but held back and followed his father out of the tent. Only Jace and Khadgar remained. Once the footsteps outside had faded enough, Jace asked, "What do you make of this?" "You mean someone disguised as a Death Knight to stir up trouble in your name?" Khadgar said. "I'm not even sure Black Knights actually exist. So far it's just unfounded rumors. But rumors themselves are worth watching — they mean someone wants to ruin your reputation." "Going to all that trouble to ruin my reputation? I'm flattered," Jace said. "Explosions are explosions, but there's a difference between a gnome bomb and a goblin bomb," Khadgar said. "If the necrotic plague at Blackrock Spire really is connected to the orcs' Death Knights, once that news reaches the north, it'll cause even greater panic. By the way, I've been meaning to ask — the Death Knight that Vereesa and the Farstriders hunted down in the Badlands, the first one you found. His name was..." "Angel Deathwish," Jace said. "Right." Khadgar paused. "Was he really the last orc Death Knight?" Jace thought for a moment before answering. "All I can say for sure is that he was the only subordinate Teron Gorefiend was planning to turn to after his failure in Dalaran. When Gorefiend realized Angel wasn't that loyal, he chose to flee to the ruins of Thaurissan's city to drain soul energy and search for Marshal Lothar's remains to resurrect himself, rather than look for other surviving Death Knights." "Honestly, you don't seem right." Khadgar studied the warlock's eyes. "When you came back, there was still a hint of bloody shadow in them. It reminded me of when you learned that dark spell in Karazhan that could mimic others' magic. You used Ulthalesh, didn't you?" "Tirion Fordring's fine son nearly put a warhammer through my skull," Jace said. "What did you expect me to do?" Khadgar raised both hands. "I'm not saying you shouldn't have fought back. I'm just giving you friendly advice, Jace. That lad should count himself lucky he ran into the least murderous warlock he'll ever meet." "Stop joking around," Jace said quietly. "No more jokes." The archmage turned serious. "Don't go back to Lordaeron. No matter what family, friends, or important business you have there — don't go back to Lordaeron, don't go back north, don't cross the Thandol Span or Baradin Bay. That's what I've been trying to tell you these past two days." "Joining the assault on Blackrock Spire is a good thing for you. A very good thing. Not just because Stormwind needs to be freed from Blackrock Mountain's threat, but because it keeps you under the eyes of countless soldiers throughout the campaign. Everyone can alibi you. Combined with the Dark Iron dwarves and SI:7, that shows you had nothing to do with those baseless rumors up north." "If you wanted an even better outcome, you'd have to lock yourself in the Violet Hold next to Kel'Thuzad. I actually considered that." "You just want to get me into the Violet Hold," Jace said. "I admit, that would be quite an achievement," Khadgar said, raising an eyebrow. Jace chuckled softly. "So as long as the Alliance takes down Blackrock Spire, defeats Nefarian and Rend Blackhand, and figures out what's really going on with the orcs' necrotic plague..." "Yes. Then we'd have some initiative in countering these rumors," the archmage said. "We can figure out how to deal with the northern problem after that." "Will the Kirin Tor be on my side?" Jace asked. "I'll definitely be on your side," Khadgar said. "As for the Kirin Tor — trust me. Those old men might always look at you with suspicion out of past prejudice, but they won't back shadowy rumor-mongers based on a pile of unfounded village tales. They at least have to maintain the appearance of wisdom and rationality." "Thanks," Jace said. "Don't thank me yet. There's another reason I came to find you." Khadgar leaned in and lowered his voice. "So, the Fire Lord really appeared? The Molten Core? When I first heard it from Varian, I couldn't believe it. Those elementals actually summoned Ragnaros again?" "True," Jace said. "Then you have to tell me all about it." The archmage raised a finger. "Considering how actively I've been helping you out." "It's not that I don't want to brag to a fellow mage, but I'm really not in the mood right now, Khadgar," Jace said. "Lord Tirion Fordring was right — I really should get some sleep. After that I need to head back to Ilgalar's Tower and make preparations for Blackrock Spire." "I'll go with you to Ilgalar's Tower," Khadgar said. "What?" "Forget what I just told you? If trouble comes later, are you planning to have Lady Morophil testify about what you were doing at Ilgalar's Tower? That would require the Silver Hand or the church to actually believe her..." He paused. "And tell me about the Molten Core on the way." "Fine." "Your tent is the one north of here, Jace. Easy to spot — it's the one with Wrynn's lion banner." "Thanks, Khadgar. See you later." Leaving the tent, Jace looked back and spotted a patch of blue among a sea of white banners. He was about to head over when he saw Taelan Fordring hurrying toward him, his paladin armor only half-removed, as if he'd been waiting for Jace to come out. "Master Sesso." "Knight-Lieutenant Taelan Fordring." Jace asked, "How's your arm?" "It's nothing, Master." Taelan stood straight, took a deep breath, and said, "I know it might be late for this, but..." He glanced at the church priests discussing something in the distance, then continued, "I still want to explain about last night's misunderstanding." "Last night we were patrolling the Death Knight sightings King Varian had reported. We sensed necrotic and demonic aura, but after chasing for a while, my father felt our target was different from those orc warlocks possessing knights' bodies, and he told me his doubts." "But it never occurred to us that it might be... I don't know how much you've heard about Hearthglen." "I know roughly what happened," Jace said. "Your father blocked Alliance soldiers to protect an orc." "Yes." The paladin paused awkwardly. "Because of that, he's been under investigation by the Silver Hand and the church. This campaign to support Blackrock Spire is part of that investigation process. So when it comes to orc dark magic, he had to take a firm stance. He couldn't let the Silver Hand and the church think his attitude toward that orc wanderer was anything like the Perenolde royal family's betrayal." "So when I saw him hesitate back there... I didn't want our companions to think he'd run into a situation like the Eastern Weald again. Plus we were nearly at Lakeshire, and time was tight. I made an impulsive decision." A squad of soldiers passed by, led by a shield-bearing church priest. Taelan paused for a moment, then continued, "I hope you won't blame him for this, Master Sesso. It was my fault." Jace said, "I understand your father's predicament. I've faced similar suspicions myself. But when I reach Morgan's Watch, I'll still report the whole thing to King Varian." "I have no objection to that. I'm sure my father feels the same." Taelan gave the Alliance military salute. "That's all I have to say, Master." "May the Light be with you, Knight-Lieutenant," Jace said. "I need to rest for a while." The paladin paused, then quickly replied, "May the Light be with you too, Master."
