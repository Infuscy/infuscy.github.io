## Chapter 1626: Journey to Stonewatch

Two riders, one behind the other, made their way up the slope toward Stonewatch along the shore of Lake Everstill.

"What a battle worthy of being carved into song—especially considering its significance to Khaz Modan and the entire Alliance." Khadgar finished listening to everything that had happened in the Molten Core and drew in a sharp breath. "I'm willing to bet the Dark Iron bards in Shadowforge City, suppressed under the fire elementals' rule for who knows how many years, have already begun putting your names to verse."

"That said, this helps me understand how you feel even more. I appreciate you not pressing charges against that Knight-Lieutenant for the sake of Blackrock Spire later. He was surely trying to avoid having his 'orc sympathizer' father turn into a 'demon sympathizer' as well—especially where a succubus was concerned."

Khadgar kept glancing back at Morophil seated behind Jace. The demon was trying her best to fold her wings but still cut a figure like a broad black-red cloak, and she was hard to miss.

"But come to think of it..." Khadgar said. "If I were a member of the Silver Hand and I spotted her from a distance, I'd be shouting 'Holy Light preserve us' too."

"That sounds like an acknowledgment of my power," Morophil said, tilting her head.

"You can think of it that way, Lady Morophil!" Khadgar called out.

"I don't blame them for being sensitive," Jace said. "Those death knight rumors that have been cropping up around Darkshire and Goldshire lately are probably connected to me. Besides, I was the one who reported the necromantic plague at Blackrock Spire to King Varian and the Cathedral of Light."

"Don't tell me you've been riding this horse all over the place since I gave him to you." Khadgar said. "I recall telling you not to make a spectacle of it."

"I've tried to keep a low profile." Jace shook his head. "I'm sure not many people have seen me riding Midnight. I avoid crowded areas as much as I can, and I put blinders on him to cover the fel energy. I didn't cut corners on those blinders, either. Before, even when I rode him into town, as long as he wasn't running like the wind, it was hard for anyone to tell him apart from an ordinary black horse."

"You think there really are surviving death knights lurking around Elwynn?" Khadgar asked.

"No solid evidence. Master Maginor Dumas has no news, and the Dagger Syndicate hasn't spotted any traces either." Jace said. "Not like before, when people were actually wounded by death knights between Raven Hill and Darkshire, or when Medivh's treasure hunters stole something from someone—those stories all had real victims."

"Or should we count the necromantic plague at Blackrock Spire in there? Vaelastrasz said those necromantic traces were more likely the Black Dragonflight's handiwork, but he couldn't rule out death knight involvement... Speaking of which, do you think it could be the Black Knights of Karazhan? Could the legends stretching from Lordaeron to Duskwood be connected to your teacher's mercenaries?"

"I won't say it's completely unrelated." Khadgar said. "But I've been working with the Curator to clean up the uncontrolled undead in Karazhan as thoroughly as possible—especially the ones tied to harmful curses Medivh left behind, like the ones trapping ghostly servants or the guests... we haven't found any traces of those Black Knights."

"Does the Curator know where they all went?" Jace asked.

"The Curator said Medivh's treasure hunters never returned after heading to Winterspring to search for clues," Khadgar said. "Medivh's commands seem to have lost their effect."

"Ulthalesh," Morophil said softly. "They went to claim Ulthalesh."

"Ah, the incident you mentioned before, Jace." The archmage said. "Looks like we don't have to worry about these fellows who can't even save themselves."

He looked at Jace and continued. "So, do you think the people spreading rumors in Lordaeron... are also spreading rumors in Stormwind?"

Jace listened to the mage's words, remembering the hunters he'd encountered in the valley at the mouth of the Deadwind Pass who'd fled at the sight of him, and the jumpy patrolmen in Stonard who'd heard rumors of a death knight reappearing.

"I'll need to ask Mathias Shaw of SI:7 about that," he said.

"SI:7." The archmage gave Jace a meaningful look. "You do remember I'm a member of the Kirin Tor, right?"

"Don't play dumb like you don't know SI:7 exists," Jace said.

"I can claim to know now." Khadgar smiled. "From what I hear, they've got operations in the Eastern Weald and Tirisfal as well. They really are better suited than you to investigate this in depth."

"They'll need the Kirin Tor's cooperation too," Jace said.

"At least on this matter, I'll find a way to help them," Khadgar said.

In the distance, Lakeshire looked gray and overcast. The sky was heavy. The two horses' hooves fell on dirt paths and gravel, a somewhat clattering, uneven rhythm.

"What are you thinking? You're not actually planning to go back to Lordaeron, are you?" Khadgar asked.

Jace looked up at him. "I think Ranger-General Sylvanas Windrunner might be planning a wedding after Blackrock Spire is dealt with. Alleria and Vereesa will definitely want to make up for what the orc wars cost them."

"Quel'Thalas can transport you from Stormwind straight to Silvermoon City," Khadgar said lightly. "With dozens or even a hundred elven mages witnessing the ceremony, I don't think that's a problem. Besides, as you said, it's after Blackrock Spire. Maybe once the Alliance and Shadowforge City defeat Nefarian's forces, we'll figure out what's really going on with that necromantic magic."

"Maybe the death knight legends will turn out to be nothing after all—just the citizens' memories of Gul'dan's death knights from before carving too deep a fear into them, or some hero buried in a Lordaeron cemetery who happened to hold a Knight's Ring getting raised by a warlock's dark magic, something like that cemetery at Raven Hill. It sounds overly optimistic, I know, but unnecessary worry does happen all the time."

"You really need that optimism right now, Jace. I'm not trying to turn you into a fool who ignores every possible danger. Don't think I'm nagging—what I'm saying is, no matter what, don't become the danger itself."

"I understand, Khadgar." Jace said.

He knew exactly what Khadgar was talking about. When Tirion Fordring and his son had descended from the sky, Jace had genuinely wondered if both paladins and the priests around them could be black dragons, or even dreadlords in disguise—even though the radiance in their hands had nearly outshone the blaze from Morophil's twin blades in her fury.

And the archmage in front of him—Jace couldn't help suspecting that this guy was also some demon in disguise, or that he'd secretly defected to Kil'jaeden back in Karazhan, gifting him Midnight just to trap him in the whole Black Knight legend.

Even though he knew full well that even without Midnight, he'd been mistaken for Gul'dan's lackey more than once or twice.

The afternoon sun hadn't yet set. The two didn't stop at Stonewatch to trouble the soldiers preparing for the Blackrock Spire campaign. Instead, they cut straight through the forest and followed Stonewatch River east toward Irgalar's Tower.

While hitching the horses, Khadgar glanced up at the spinning Talar'felsin Crystal above and said, "Breaking through Kil'jaeden's blockade here with you, Miss Amy Marin, and Lord Kallrez last year—those were unforgettable days. Even though I was basically bending the Kirin Tor's prohibitions on dark magic every single day... but that's part of the charm of exploring magic. So, what's your secret weapon this time?"

"I just said I need to make some preparations. When did I say I had a secret weapon?" Jace asked.

"All the way from the bridge you've been scribbling in that notebook of yours, muttering incantations." The archmage pointed at the notebook on Jace's belt. "Don't think I can't guess what kind of 'preparations' you mean."

"Fine. Since I brought you along, I wasn't planning to hide it." Jace said. "You'll know once we get to the Telogrus Rift."

"What, you plan to experiment with new magic in the Telogrus Rift?" The mage followed Jace up the steps. "The forest isn't big enough for your spells anymore?"

"Kallrez!" Jace chanted the incantation to awaken the blood circle on the ground, speaking in Demonic: "Open the Telogrus Rift."

As the rift in space before him was pried open by black claws, a sinister dark wind quickened the rotation of the Talar'felsin Crystal. Startled calls rose from the surrounding forest.

Khadgar clutched his robe and raised his staff to deflect the airflow slightly, shouting, "I suddenly feel like keeping an eye on you is still very necessary!"
