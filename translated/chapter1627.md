## Chapter 1627: What an Eredar Does Best

The magical disturbance radiating from the Talar'felsin Crystal and Irgalar's Tower, stirring up magical winds through the gnoll forest, was nothing compared to the chaotic storm swirling around the Telogrus Rift.

Khadgar gripped his staff tightly as he stepped onto the wasteland scorched by fel and shadow, surveying the entire expanse of void fragments before finally fixing his gaze on the small floating island glowing with purple light in the distance.

"Void blossoms," Khadgar said. "Lady Ursula Drenn really did it."

"The Destruction Potion might prove useful in the coming battle at Blackrock Spire, Archmage." Morophil said. "The alchemist and my master have basically worked through all the difficulties in the formula. All that's left is time."

"Your master and I are among the few spellcasters who've drunk the Destruction Potion and understand what kind of power it holds." Khadgar said. "I used a single bottle to withstand the combined force of the Scepter of Sargeras and the Twisting Nether, holding an entire floating island together—even I couldn't imagine I could do that. He used a bottle to devour the energy of a void-corrupted black dragon and destroy the Scepter of Sargeras itself."

"Memorable moments." Morophil glanced at her master beside her.

"I know what you mean, Khadgar." Jace said. "The Destruction Potion definitely can't be sold openly like Mana Potions."

"I'll take that as confirmation that you've already reminded Lady Drenn of that," the mage said.

"But it's not just Ursula driving the development of the Destruction Potion. The Kirin Tor has a big share in it too." Jace said. "When the time comes, the Destruction Potion will bring huge benefits to the Kirin Tor's research on magic intensity. But most of the work—from cultivating the raw materials, to the facility, to the final brewing—has been on us. We can't be volunteers just because the work is dangerous."

"Is that so?" Khadgar said. "I've been in Karazhan all this time. I haven't been out long enough to catch up with Master Vargus or the alchemists of Dalaran about this."

"If the Kirin Tor plans to control the sale of the Destruction Potion and the spread of its formula, they'd better establish a long-term partnership with the Dagger Syndicate," Jace said. "Once the product is ready, I believe Ursula will be able to calculate the associated costs and the expenses needed for further research and improvement."

"Hm. You should really talk to Master Vargus about this." Khadgar pointed vaguely northward—or what he thought was north. "He's basically a lock for a seat on the Council of Six this time. Back on Draenor, he was the one leading the Destruction Potion project..."

"I can't go to Lordaeron," Jace said. "You're the one who reminded me of that."

"True enough." The mage rubbed his forehead and let out a long sigh. "Fine, fine. I'll help negotiate with Lady Ursula Drenn."

"Much appreciated." Jace smiled, then raised his voice to a roar. "Lord Kallrez!"

The doom lord spread his wings and descended from the high floating island to land before them. He studied Khadgar beside him and rumbled in Demonic, "No wonder the strain on my portal felt many times heavier than last time... so it's you, the intruder, sneaking through with Jace Sesso. If the Kirin Tor intends to gift us this place, they should have the decency to stay far away."

"An intruder? I thought we were old acquaintances by now, Lord Kallrez." Khadgar replied politely in Demonic.

"Relax. Khadgar's not here to spy on me. He's just curious about my magic," Jace said. "When I was experimenting with Dark Meteor recently, I accidentally discovered a way to summon demons while gathering power from the Twisting Nether through the meteor's main body. Have you heard of this summoning method?"

"Infernal Meteor, or the Eredar's Conflagration." Kallrez said. "They call that spell—the kind capable of destroying an entire mortal town—Armageddon."

"Not fel constructs like Infernals. Real demons—Felhunters, imps, even Eredar." Jace said. "But it's not a pact summoning ritual. The demons brought through by this spell are very likely from the Burning Legion."

"Hmm. So you want my help finding a way to control these summoned demons and make them serve you," Kallrez said.

"Exactly," Jace said.

"Then show me first," the doom lord said.

Khadgar's gaze shifted between the two as they spoke. He seemed to want to say something but held back, and eventually even looked at Morophil, as if hoping the succubus would urge her master and his hulking colleague to calm down.

But Morophil just shot him a "what are you looking at me for" look of distaste, leaving Khadgar to exhale, furrow his brow, and step back a few paces.

Under the watchful eyes of the two demons and Khadgar, Jace walked to an open space and unwrapped the bindings from Ulthalesh.

Even before he began channeling the spell, the soul-force of the demon bones was already stirring the raging shadows of the entire Telogrus Rift.

He began murmuring the incantation, summoning the three emotions one after another. When the final wave of intense self-contempt settled into place, Khadgar—who had been standing to the side with his arms crossed, watching the darkness spiral overhead—found himself cupping his own chin.

Kallrez's small, fel-green eyes suddenly shifted, scanning different directions within the Twisting Nether. As strange dark mana-serpents converged from all around, several of them looked like the same "channels" the Scepter of Sargeras had torn open across the high skies of Draenor, streams that eventually merged into those same void spirals.

Perhaps it was because the shadow energy of the Telogrus Rift was lending tremendous power to the spell, or because Jace was better prepared this time—his body in better condition thanks to Ursula's restorative potions compared to that day by the Crystal Lake. Either way, Jace's perception of the demon souls being drawn into that dark star was far more tangible and vivid than before.

By the latter half of the incantation, the dark star spinning in midair was even releasing strange, iridescent light, standing out all the more against the backdrop of the black-purple Twisting Nether.

Jace shouted the final word of the spell. The dark star obeyed his command and plummeted from above, striking the ground with a piercing shriek and a trail of violet-red energy.

The explosion sent tremors through the entire floating island, cracking it open and sending fragments scattering to scrape against the drifting debris overhead. In the dissipating flames, several figures of varying heights emerged.

Amid shouts in Demonic, several Felhunters burst out of the rolling smoke. Behind them, two Mo'arg demons cracked whips, driving the hounds forward to scout the path. At the rear, a gaunt Eredar clutching a staff cursed the other demons in Demonic, complaining about why they'd been brought to this strange place.

But as the smoke fully cleared and the ground's trembling subsided, Morophil spread her wings slightly—and especially when Kallrez's immense shadow fell calmly over the demons before them—every snarl and growl abruptly died.

"Kallrez? How..." The doomguard warlock spoke in Demonic. "I am certainly no emissary of Lord Kil'jaeden. My arrival here was purely accidental. I never imagined I would encounter you here, esteemed Lord Kallrez."

The Felhunters and the two Mo'arg beast-handlers—shorter than the doomguard—were already frozen in terror before the banshee and the doom lord. Behind them, Khadgar seemed unsure whether these demons answered to the Burning Legion or to Kallrez. He just gripped his staff tightly, hesitating whether to attack.

"Impressive." Kallrez's tone carried genuine surprise. "Where did you acquire this spell? At first I thought it was merely a powerful shadow bolt of sorts, like Shadow Bolt, only more deeply connected to the Twisting Nether for a more satisfying, more thorough destruction. I never imagined you could deepen that connection further and use it to capture these lesser demons..."

Jace wanted to say that higher-tier ones weren't out of the question either. After all, the powerful servants Gul'dan had summoned with Gul'dan's Hand were well-known demon commanders of the Burning Legion, their status no lower than Kallrez's. But since he was here asking Kallrez for help, he didn't plan to deliberately provoke the doom lord.

Khadgar asked, "How exactly are you enslaving these demons, Kallrez? A spell, or..."

"You scheming mortals have no idea how many victories obedience and order have won for the Legion within the Twisting Nether," Kallrez's voice rumbled.

"In plain terms, they're just cowards, Archmage," Morophil said, tilting her head with a smile.

"Cowards, yes. But I have a way to ensure the demons you summon with this method obey your master absolutely, banshee." The doom lord laughed a throaty laugh. "I've already figured out how this strange spell communicates with the Twisting Nether. But these rabble here are useless now."

At those words, the fear inside the doomguard warlock surged out like a tidal wave. After all, dying in the Twisting Nether meant utter annihilation.

Just as everyone was talking, the Eredar moved with astonishing speed, using the shadow energy of the Telogrus Rift to tear open a small void portal in an attempt to flee. Jace didn't even have time to stop his spellcasting with a Suffocation spell.

He was, after all, from the demon race that built portals for the Burning Legion. But there was another Eredar here... Kallrez simply raised a finger and ripped the portal apart, then slammed the demon warlock to the ground with a Cripple spell.

"Lord Kallrez, no—wait—"

Before he could finish, the souls of several demons were entirely devoured by Kallrez, their bodies turning into blackened, empty husks. Jace felt Ulthalesh emit a faint, hollow wailing in his mind, as if the souls being taken by this greater demon beside him were a sheer waste.
