Chapter 1628: The Doom Lord's Spell

"You've grown faster than I expected, Lord Kallrez." Khadgar eyed the lifeless demon husks sidelong. "It hasn't even been a year since we opened the portal to Draenor together." "This isn't growth—it's recovery, mage." The demon lord laughed coldly, clenching his fist blazing with green fel-flame, savoring the soul-energy he'd just extracted. "As for why I was crippled to this extent, you can ask Jace Sesso and those lying warlocks... and that impertinent, foolish imp who devoted all his scheming to defiling my power." "I didn't summon you with my own mana! I used my master's blood—that's hardly defilement, great Lord Kallrez!" Sazeno, hearing the doom lord bring him up, immediately shouted with all his might. "And I used every last one of my master's most precious soul shards!" "Besides, it was to save my master's life. By now you should understand how important my decision was!" "Now I have finally returned to the Twisting Nether." Kallrez spoke as if the imp's torrent of nonsense hadn't reached his ears at all, continuing on his own. "Soon I will descend upon Blackrock Spire and make the mortals of Azeroth see my existence clearly. I will make you understand what it means to be insignificant." Khadgar crossed his arms and frowned. "If I didn't know we'd be heading to the Burning Steppes together next, I'd think you were about to slip through that rift and launch an assault on Stormwind right now. That doesn't exactly sound like an ally talking." "An ally?" Kallrez extended a claw the size of a whelp's horn toward the mage. "Having a shared goal does not make us—" "Stop!" Jace raised both hands high. "I thought you two finished all your arguing last year." "Now you've seen my spell, Kallrez. My mana is limited. Trying unfamiliar variations of the incantation only drains more. I don't have many chances to demonstrate it over and over. So if you say you can help me control the demons this spell summons, you'd better mean it." "I have no reason to deceive you." Kallrez switched back to the Demonic tongue, which came more naturally to him. "Keeping you alive and making you stronger does me no harm. We already have a pact between us." "You can't even fully control the demons you summon yourself," Khadgar said. "The demons Sesso summoned earlier obeyed you out of fear, but that doomguard warlock still nearly escaped. That proves they aren't absolutely loyal. Find an opportunity to escape, and they'll find an opportunity to backstab their commander." "This isn't arguing," the mage said, looking over. "This is legitimate doubt." "He's right," Jace nodded. Kallrez narrowed his already near-squinted, fel-fire eyes—looking none too pleased that these two humans were questioning his ability to dominate lesser demons. "The creator of this spell received help from high-ranking Legion commanders. So the demons forcibly dragged here are all Legion foot soldiers. If you want to forcibly control these Legion soldiers with enslavement magic, Jace Sesso, you'll have to put in several times the effort. They carry their masters' brands—they may come from Kil'jaeden, or from the Defiler Archimonde, the Destroyer Mannoroth, Tichondrius... The presence of these brands helps them resist suppression from high-ranking lords like myself. It helps the Legion's soldiers conquer powerful demon warlords and resist the mental corrosion of the Shadow realm." "And you?" Khadgar asked. "As a former high-ranking member of the Burning Legion, what brand did you carry?" "The Great One's own, of course." Kallrez's tone was proud. "The supreme brand of Sargeras himself..." When the brand of Sargeras was mentioned, Jace glanced toward Morophil. The succubus caught his look and muttered with displeasure, "Why are you looking at me? I don't have any brand of Sargeras... at least not a complete one." "As things stand now, even the supreme demon brand didn't guarantee you couldn't be controlled by other warlocks, Kallrez," Khadgar said. "Because the power of the pact has overcome the binding of the brand, mage. Besides, all magic has an upper limit to its power. Even the mightiest Titan cannot guarantee that a fully-powered seal will last forever..." Kallrez leaned his heavy head toward the mage and said in a low voice, "And frankly—do you expect Jace Sesso to establish pacts with every demon this spell summons, whether high-ranking soldiers or wretched imps, seizing their true names and ensuring their absolute obedience?" "I certainly don't have the time or the mana to waste on that," Jace said. If that were the case, Kallrez's speculation also matched what Jace had been thinking. It was precisely because Kil'jaeden was backing Gul'dan from behind that Gul'dan could summon demons directly loyal to him, without wasting mana to control them. Gul'dan only needed to pay the mana cost of summoning those powerful demons into the world. But in another timeline, Gul'dan, empowered by the Nightwell and various artifacts, commanded power on an entirely different level from Jace. He could directly use Gul'dan's Hand to summon demon lords on the level of Kallrez and have them stand on his side without breaking a sweat. That difference in mana cost and time investment—added on one side and subtracted on the other—turned Gul'dan into an absurdly powerful opponent. The power of the Gul'dan's Hand he cast became nearly impossible to surpass. "So what's your solution?" Jace asked. "Summon wild demons, Jace Sesso." Kallrez said. "As a Legion commander who has crossed countless worlds, I have enslaved who knows how many wild demons, bringing them under mine and Kil'jaeden's command. The Telogrus Rift has no wild demons roaming it—no room for me to work. But I do know how to 'attract' wild demons." Kallrez crouched down and carved demonic runes into the ground with his sharp claws, murmuring as he carved, "Valtargalga, Payelr'aa." "Add this incantation to your spell. I believe you should use it to replace the 'Anteya, Mil' section. I must warn you—I have no interest in the phrases mortals use to communicate with the Twisting Nether. I can only do my best to help you based on what I've heard in the past, Jace Sesso. It might come out very differently when you cast it." "Perhaps we should find a senior Eredar expert to help," Khadgar said. "If the brief lives of mortals bore you so much, it might be worth a try," Kallrez snorted. "If you can be enslaved, then an Eredar can too. I don't think that would be much trouble for Jace Sesso." Khadgar said. "Unless you admit the Eredar are inferior." "Silence... mage!" the demon snarled. While those two bickered, Jace copied the doom lord's incantation runes into his notebook, recombining them with Gul'dan's Hand long incantation as the demon had instructed, and transcribed the whole thing again. Then, without channeling any emotions, he calmly recited it several times. He could indeed feel the shadow energy responding to the summons in a slightly different way—but it also became more chaotic. "I think it's close enough. Even if there are differences, it shouldn't need too many revisions or attempts, Khadgar. At least he's not blowing smoke." Jace looked at the string of demonic runes he'd just written. "Wild demons are easier to control than Legion lapdogs. I believe with your power, Kallrez, the wandering demons summoned here will follow your orders. But there's no way you can follow me around like Morophil or Sazeno with that size of yours. I need a way to command them without you present." At this, Kallrez burst into laughter as if he'd heard the most ridiculous thing imaginable. The very ground trembled with the demon's laughter. "You are not the same person you once were! The Jace Sesso who first summoned me to the Storm Altar and the one standing here now are night and day! Apart from those mad Eredar and the wild legends from the Void's corners, you are the most powerful mortal warlock I have ever seen—even setting Ulthalesh aside. And you have likely never tried to unleash the full power of that ancient artifact, which is precisely your problem." "Do you want to become a master of shadow, a lord of demons, or don't you? It's hard to tell whether the energy you spend suppressing your own ambition or your actual magical power is greater. I can hardly tell. Or perhaps that suppression of yours is itself part of the shadow power you can draw upon? After all, the bond between mortals and shadow has always been difficult to pin down, thanks to the Curse of Flesh."
