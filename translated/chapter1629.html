Chapter 1629: Dark Aura

Khadgar listened to the demon's words. He seemed to want to say something but held back, instead turning his head to look at the warlock beside him. "I don't know what you're trying to say, Kallrez." Jace said. "I thought we were discussing the matter of controlling wild demons." "Unleash your heart, Jace Sesso." Kallrez's voice was almost a growl. "Then those demons will fear you as they fear me! When that happens, even Legion members may bow to the terrifying power of you and Ulthalesh, let alone wanderers. Kil'jaeden's brand will have difficulty suppressing their fear and worship of you! The Legion already knows you wield Ulthalesh, and that you and this staff are both in Azeroth. What excuse do you have left to hide?" "He's right, master," Morophil said. "A waste of time. The two of you are a complete waste of time..." Sazeno sat on the succubus's shoulder. "I've been saying that since the first day I met the master. But what good does it do?" "Hey. I'm still standing here, you know." Khadgar raised a hand. "Want me to step out for a bit?" "Foolish mage." The demon lord suddenly swiveled his head toward the mage. "If Jace Sesso had not seized the Book of Dark Names, drunk the Destruction Potion, devoured the power of the Netherwing, and extracted the soul-force of the alien elemental lord, he would never have been able to defeat the Black Prince alone, let alone cut through the Scepter of Sargeras atop Kil'jaeden's Throne! You and the so-called Children of Lothar would have been trapped on Draenor forever, until that pitiful, fragile world collapsed entirely!" Khadgar took a deep breath. For once, he remained silent, offering no rebuttal. "Who is the master, Kallrez?" Jace's voice turned cold. "You are the master, Jace Sesso." The demon lord tamped down his emotions and replied. "Who is the master?" Jace's voice rose. "Who is the lord of the Telogrus Rift?" "You are my master," Morophil replied softly. Sazeno nodded too. "You're the master..." On another floating island in the distance, Gaoth'seros lowered his two "eyes" like void stars. Two Felhunters peered timidly from the edge of another island in their direction, letting out pale howls. "Yes. I am the master." Jace's voice was calm. "And you are all my assistants, my servants, my shadow magic advisors. So don't speak to me in that tone. If I truly am the most powerful mortal warlock you've ever seen—more powerful than even many Eredar—then doesn't that mean my choices are the right ones? At the very least, the most correct ones so far?" "Ah, damn it. Why am I even wasting breath on this nonsense?" He pressed a hand to his forehead and continued. "So. Is there a more direct way for me to control the wild demons I summon with this spell, without you present? If there is, say so. If there isn't, let's wrap this meeting up now and I'll find someone else to work with. No more of the extra talk." As his words fell, even the shadow storm swirling around the broken floating islands seemed to grow calmer. Khadgar stood nearby, watching his friend. His expression was complicated, but he said nothing. "There is indeed a way," Kallrez said. "Good." Jace snapped his fingers. "What is it? A spell? Or..." "Lesser demons fear the pressure of greater demons. This pressure is not an abstraction—which is why the Legion's brands can partially counter it," Kallrez said. "In the Kirin Tor, we call this power a demon's 'dark aura,' or 'dominance aura.' Some Eredar possess it too," Khadgar said. "Many Eredar can only mimic it through fel-tattoos, using them to more easily enslave demons to their service," Kallrez said. "Lucky for me, my master is very good at that." Morophil turned around, showing off the tattoos on her body. "See? He inked these himself. They're perfect. And they're even stronger now than when they were fresh on me." "Nice craftsmanship, Jace." Khadgar lowered his voice. "Planning to tattoo a set of demonic runes on yourself too?" "I don't think my skin is as thick as a demon's," Jace said. He did have reservations about demon tattoos. This kind of marking was a form of demonic corruption. Once you bore fel-tattoos, you inevitably inherited some demonic traits—like the demon hunters. "No other way?" "You either paint them on yourself, or you paint them somewhere else." Kallrez raised a fist, the clanking of metal plates and chains accompanying his proud declaration. "Spells used by mortals detached from the Twisting Nether struggle to maintain this aura's existence. It is innate to the noblest and greatest of races." "Somewhere else, like... the ground? That would mean the aura is limited to a fixed area and only lasts a fixed duration," Jace said. "That is the compromise." Kallrez said. "However, I will permit you to use my demon blood to inscribe these runes. That way, you can project my dominance aura—or, as the Kirin Tor arcanists like to call it, 'Kallrez's Dark Aura.' When the time comes, the demons you summon will not only submit to my power but will fight all the more fanatically for my master—that is, for you." Blackrock Spire was not a mountain range, nor a city. For attacking that location, a fixed-range, fixed-duration demon-enslavement spell seemed perfectly adequate to Jace. Most importantly, he wouldn't have to pay the price of demonic corruption. Besides, who knew how long these summoned demons would last against Nefarian's Blackwing Legion and the remaining orc holdouts? They were meant to be expendable anyway—emergency cannon fodder to shield the Alliance soldiers and his own small group, reducing casualties. "Sounds good. Teach me how to draw it, then," Jace said. The doom lord bent down, cut a gash in his palm with his claw, and smeared burning fel-blood across the ground, deftly carving out several runes that hissed with green flame. Jace had already grown accustomed to the pressure of standing before a demon as massive as Kallrez. But when those runes were complete, he recalled the night he'd first summoned this demon to the Storm Altar. The pressure of a high-ranking demon was real. When the demon finished, Jace dipped Ulthalesh in the fel-blood and quickly copied Kallrez's runes on the ground. With this additional layer of runework, even Morophil, standing not far away, showed an uncomfortable look on her face. "Close enough, Jace Sesso." Kallrez tilted his head. "You learn fast." Jace conjured a handful of Shadowflame and burned the fel-blood off Ulthalesh. "How else would I become your master?" After transcribing the rune patterns onto a scroll of parchment, Jace and Khadgar left the Telogrus Rift. To test whether his dark aura runes would actually work, he needed to get far away from Kallrez's plane of existence. Preferably leaving Morophil behind too—a banshee was hardly an ordinary demon, and Jace had seen more than enough of her "pressure" on those other succubi. He and Khadgar pushed deeper into the primeval forest to the east until they came across an abandoned camp that had once belonged to a gnoll tribe. Jace picked out a dilapidated hut that might have once housed a gnoll chieftain, or perhaps an orc, and decided to use it as the target for Gul'dan's Hand. He copied the runes from the scroll using Kallrez's blood from a vial, inscribing the dark aura rune. It was almost like being back in the Telogrus Rift. Standing there without the doom lord present and yet still feeling the demon's oppressive presence was a strange experience. He recited the recombined Gul'dan's Hand incantation. It felt somewhat off, but he managed to gather streams of strangely shimmering mana-serpents through his familiarity with and control over shadow energy, coalescing them into a black meteor strong enough. The meteor fell, instantly obliterating the largest ruined hut. It sent a spray of wreckage flying. A low figure rose from the burning blaze. As the smoke cleared, a one-armed Mo'arg demon was revealed. He looked like an unarmored demonic guard, but his body bore no fel-tattoos linked to the Burning Legion. He carried a crude long-handled war-axe and stared this way with a twisted snarl. "A wild demon. Kallrez's incantation worked," Khadgar said. "It's still a bit off. Only summoned one weakened demon, gravely injured. The incantation or the mana channeling needs adjustment," Jace said. "Agreed." Khadgar nodded. "Obey... command." The Mo'arg coughed up a mouthful of blood. "Kill. I need the commander's orders!" Khadgar glanced at the blood-rune on the ground. "That works too, but being able to suppress a demon this weak doesn't prove much. I can help you refine the incantation. I have a few suggestions." "You're willing to help me revise a demonic spell, Khadgar?" Jace asked. "Why not?" Khadgar cleared his throat behind his hand. "As long as you don't name the spell 'Khadgar's Destruction' or 'The Sesso Meteor, courtesy of the Kirin Tor.'"
