Chapter 163: Gold

Jace looked toward the guards in the distance and asked, "Why? Prince Arthas started celebrating dwarven holidays too?"

Henny said, "That's because the princess of Kul Tiras is heading home for some traditional year-end ceremony and she's been staying in Lordaeron for a while. The prince is finding excuses to see her."

"He can see whoever he wants. Why does he need an excuse?" Jace asked, squinting.

"Well, doesn't that mean Arthas Menethil and Jaina Proudmoore are both in there right now?"

"Her name's Jaina?" Henny said. "I didn't know that, but yes, that's the princess of Kul Tiras — Vic told me. I heard she's already a little apprentice mage. She came through a few months ago, heading to Dalaran to study magic, but the girl can't be more than twelve..."

Jace crept forward, and could even hear people chatting about how Kul Tiras and Lordaeron were going to team up against Gilneas, and how this "golden couple" inside the church was proof of the two nations' ironclad alliance...

Whatever. Alliance proof or not, he just wanted to see what these two future star-crossed lovers actually looked like. Next time he came back to Lordaeron, he definitely wouldn't catch a scene like this again.

Jace stood on tiptoe and peered in. Of course, the crowd of onlookers — all taller than him — were packed in even tighter and craning harder, wave after wave of people trying to squeeze past the guards to get a look at the princess. But even if you could see past them, the view was completely blocked by Lordaeron's banners. You couldn't even make out the full shape of the cathedral, let alone what was inside.

Jace suddenly remembered — when he was a kid, there was a broken window in the back where you could sneak past the guards and peek in from behind.

"Mom," he turned and asked, "that spot in the back..."

"Bricked up ages ago," Henny said, giving him a sideways look.

Jace blinked. "Aw, come on, Mom, I want to see."

Watching her son's frantic expression, Henny sighed. "Fine. They smashed in a new one, though. Still want to?"

"Yes, Mom," Jace said. "How could I miss this?"

"Come with me." Henny beckoned and pointed to a side alley. "That way."

Jace was about to move when Henny's hand shot out and blocked him.

"Promise me you'll put safety first. Don't get so caught up in looking that you forget you've got a life."

"I promise."

Jace followed his mother through the alley and into a narrow gap between two buildings — so tight Jace felt like if he ate two more bowls of Redridge stew he wouldn't fit through.

On the other side of the gap was an overgrown patch of weeds nobody tended, and then the towering city wall. Jace could even hear faint murmurs of conversation from the guards up on the wall. A strong smell of urine hung over the whole patch of grass. Jace covered his nose, not daring to take another step for fear of stepping on a landmine.

Henny edged along the wall, looked back at him, and asked, "Don't you want to get to the cathedral or not?"

"Why does it stink so much?!" Jace asked.

Henny waved him over. "Walk fast and you'll find out why any second now."

Jace glanced up and spotted the guards keeping watch from above. It clicked immediately. He cursed under his breath and hurried after his mother, running toward the cathedral that loomed over the area, casting its shadow across the ground.

Back on proper paved road again, Henny straightened her clothes and brushed the wall dust off her skirt, then walked forward. Around a bend, and Jace saw it — at the end of the alley, a guard leaning against the wall with a halberd.

"Crap, is he on patrol or on duty?"

"On patrol. Wait for him to pass, Jace, and then you run. Once you're over there you'll know how to get up and look! I'm wearing a skirt, I can't run — I'll wait here."

Jace was still processing his mother's instructions when the guard indeed got up and walked away.

Urged on by his anxious mother, he had no choice but to sprint across.

Through a narrow path he reached the back of the cathedral. He looked up — here the cathedral's wall and the city wall had a gap between them, very close together. You could brace against both sides and climb up, all the way to a windowsill about four meters up, then jump over to a broken window beside it and peek in.

So that's how. Man, this spot would be perfect for an assassin's ambush.

Jace scrambled up to the windowsill with a few kicks — this kind of thing was child's play for him now. If not for his injured arm, he'd have climbed even faster.

If he ever got sent back to the 21st century, maybe he could work as a stuntman.

He jumped from this windowsill to the broken one, steadied himself, and heard a faint sound of chanting from within.

He tilted his head and stole a glance inside — and from the side caught a view of Lordaeron's archbishop and a gathering of nobles.

From this angle, the most eye-catching figure was a red-haired dwarf in a light-blue coat, his braided beard, bound with leather straps, hanging all the way down to his knees.

Beside him was a blond boy, surrounded by the crowd, with the dwarf standing right behind him at the closest position.

The boy was looking up at the archbishop. The archbishop had his back to the group, reciting something. The cathedral's vast space threw the sound back and forth so much that you couldn't make out the actual words.

Of course, Jace didn't have much interest in Holy Light prayers and the like. That blond boy was unquestionably Arthas Menethil.

From this distance his face looked more or less like any ordinary little boy. He was indeed a bit taller than other kids his age, but the most obvious thing about him was his outfit — so expensive it was terrifying, as if he'd gone out of his way to use every single spot on his body to hang something on, piling on half a gold shop's worth of jewelry.

But even all that ornamentation couldn't dull those gorgeous golden curls.

No wonder even the nobles of the Kingdom of Stormwind called him the "Golden Prince" the whole Alliance had their eyes on — and it was literal.

If that was Arthas, then that dwarf had to be Lord Muradin, the king of Ironforge's younger brother — he was practically Arthas's mentor and guide in every aspect of his youth.

But the most important question... where was Jaina?

Jace hunched down even further, his sightline dodging past a crossbeam not far ahead, and finally caught sight of some of the nobles behind Arthas.

Two blond girls were standing not far behind the dwarf, one tall and one short. The short one was gripping the tall one's hand and whispering something, while the tall one turned her head slightly to listen, occasionally covering her mouth to stifle a laugh.

On the other side stood King Terenas II, his pale-gold hair cascading down, staring fixedly at the two of them — he wasn't even looking at his own son.

Jace tried to squat lower to get a better look, because both girls had their backs to him. He could only catch glimpses of their profiles from the way they leaned their heads together. But it seemed like Terenas II, who was facing this way, had suddenly sensed something and looked straight toward him. He yanked his head back and ducked behind the wall without hesitation, then leaped straight back to the windowsill he'd come from, jumped down, bounced off a cushioning wall, landed steady as anything, and bolted back into the alley. He hopes he hadn't been seen... he thought silently to himself.
