Chapter 1630: Thaurissan's Reply

Asking a Doom Lord about summoning demons or the Twisting Nether was definitely a good call. Though Jace had to admit, part of the reason it went so smoothly was because he already knew the conclusions from the game and worked backward to figure out how to get there.

Of course, so far all they had was a method for summoning wild demons. The spell still needed optimization.

Jace had some talent for learning languages, but he was no linguist, especially when it came to spellcraft. Luckily, Khadgar was willing to help. The guy kept insisting over and over that he knew absolutely nothing about shadow or demonic lore, but whenever an interesting dark magic topic came up, he couldn't help but stick his nose in. Classic Karazhan University graduate.

As for that dark aura—or Domination Aura—Jace immediately thought of all those toggle-able aura skills in the game. Especially the hero auras in Warcraft, some buffing allies, others debuffing enemies.

But turning it into a rune inscribed on the ground made Jace think of something else from the game: the sigils Demon Hunters placed on the ground. Different sigils used fel or shadow energy to create various magical effects, and that power also came from demon runes similar to these magic patterns.

So this temporary "Domination Aura" he'd learned from Karrlez, if it were in the game, would probably be some kind of "Domination Sigil"—something to boost control over demonic minions while weakening enemies' willpower.

Thinking about it, Jace felt a little disappointed. When he'd first thought of inscribing runes on the ground, for a moment he'd imagined this new approach to auras might amount to creating a whole new spell—one that could help other warlocks achieve something like a powerful demon lord's presence without having to accept demonic tattoos or risk corruption. But when he calmed down and thought about it, the Eredar had probably already milked this concept dry long ago. Otherwise Karrlez wouldn't have been so unimpressed.

After leaving the Tower of Ilgalar, Jace and Khadgar made their way down the mountain toward the northwestern pass. Their next destination was Morgan's Watch—Jace needed to report to Varian on what had happened recently, especially his unexpected encounter with Lord Tirion Fordring.

Karrlez had no clue how to piece together mortal spells, and Jace didn't need the doom lord around to attempt summoning wandering demons on his own. So they'd meet again at the Altar of Storms.

The Alliance army had already cleared the road from Lakeshire to Morgan's Watch. In the past, traveling through here meant watching out for gnoll bandits, roaming dire wargs, the occasional orc straggler squad, or even infiltration teams from Blackrock Spire—not to mention the chaotic battlefields littered with wrecked siege engines.

But now, Alliance blue banners lined the road, and supply caravans and soldiers from various kingdoms passed back and forth constantly. With the Molten Core destroyed, the area wasn't nearly as hot as when Jace had first come here years ago looking for Dark Iron gunpowder. You could even see blue in the sky, let alone sunlight.

"After Winter Veil passes, a lot of new sprouts will grow here, Jace." Khadgar set down the spell tome he'd been reading and looked up. "Keep Midnight away from them, and in a few months this canyon might be a pleasant stretch of green."

"That dramatic a change?" Jace asked.

"I might not be a top-tier master of divination," Khadgar tilted his head back, shading his eyes as he looked at the dead branches on the high cliffs, "but I wouldn't lie about something this trivial. I felt the change as soon as I arrived in the Redridge Mountains. I figured you'd found a way to destroy the Molten Core's protective runes—I just didn't expect it'd require actually defeating Ragnaros himself."

"You might not understand how someone like me thinks, Jace. Sometimes... these things. The grass, the trees, even the wolves, the giant spiders that could bite through my skull with one fang, even the gnolls—I'd give everything I have to protect them, to fight the invaders from other worlds to the end."

"For a normal world," Jace said.

Khadgar chuckled. "Seems you understand the feeling too, Jace. That's right—for a normal world. Or at least one that looks normal, like your Tower of Ilgalar."

Jace laughed at the jab. His Tower of Ilgalar was indeed a perfect example of something that looked normal on the outside but was absolutely chaotic inside.

Hearing Khadgar talk like that, Jace suddenly remembered the trip he'd taken with Vereesa to hunt down the death knight Angor Deathwish, and the night they'd spent at Eastvale Logging Camp.

A few years had passed since then. The memory of that quiet night in Elwynn Forest—the wind, the melodious leaf whistling—had grown hazy. But he understood what Khadgar meant.

The orc-built wooden gates had been completely demolished. At the pass leading into the Burning Steppes, a group of Stormwind guards stood watch. From here, Jace could see Morgan's Watch with its camp tents and the silver, red, and blue banners fluttering everywhere.

Looking northwest, his gaze traveled across the entire Burning Steppes to the peak of the volcano. Though he couldn't make out Blackrock Spire at all, he could still feel that cold, brooding presence like a black cloud.

The Alliance offensive was nocked and ready on the bowstring, and the dark throne at the highest peak seemed prepared as well.

As Jace crested the slope leading into Morgan's Watch, he noticed a group of Ironfury soldiers resting in a corner. The moment he spotted them, the Dark Irons clearly noticed the warlock riding Midnight through the gate. One officer immediately got up and ran toward him.

"Master Jace Sesso!"

The Dark Iron dwarf ran up, saluted Jace with a Shadowforge City military salute as Jace dismounted, and shouted in stiff Common: "By order of His Imperial Majesty, we've delivered the goods you requested. They're over there."

"Our mission is complete. The cargo suffered no damage at all, and neither orcs nor black dragons spotted us en route!"

"That fast?" Jace asked in surprise.

The Ironfury officer said excitedly: "The Alliance army is pinning down the orc forces on the Thaurissan Plain. The Ironfury has already retaken the Tomb of Franklorn and secured the northern gate into the Searing Gorge! The orcs and black dragons are falling back to the Blackrock Spire gate and the southern passages, but those treacherous oath-breakers know their situation."

"Soon the Ironfury will retake the southern Blackrock Mountain gate, cutting the route from the Altar of Storms to Blackrock Mountain. This is a victory unlike any since the black dragons descended upon Blackrock Spire. Nefarian must be cowering among his sister's eggs!"

"His Imperial Majesty asked me to convey his gratitude to you, Master... Ah, here is His Majesty's reply." The officer could barely contain his excitement. His hands trembled as he reached into his pocket—whether from the thrill of retaking the Tomb of Franklorn or from meeting Jace like a starstruck fan, it was hard to tell.

Jace took the letter, broke the seal, and skimmed through it. Beyond Dagran Thaurissan's standard-format self-praise and high-flown praise for the battle in the Molten Core, the most valuable information was his apparent hint about Grede's decision.

That is, Grede indeed had no intention of taking Anvilmar's place as the new Lord of Ironforge and ruler of the Three Hammers. He'd be content with the title of High Thane—becoming the greatest hero of the Three Hammers in two hundred years, a Mountain King recognized by all, and most importantly, getting his hands on Sulfuras.

Jace was glad Grede had chosen this path. The Three Hammers had been separated for two hundred years—their languages were still mutually intelligible, but the rift between them was severe. Never mind the Dark Irons—even the Wildhammer and Bronzebeard had been on terrible terms before the Dark Portal forced them to stand together. Keeping the peace and restoring communication through this incident was already a major achievement. None of the Three Hammers' leaders wanted a new king above them.

Even if there could be a great dwarf king who could reunite the Three Hammers, it probably wouldn't be Grede. The guy... Jace wasn't trying to put down his best friend, but the two of them really weren't cut out for the ruler business.

Between the lines, Jace could tell Dagran Thaurissan had no problem with Grede being honored as the Master of Sulfuras—as long as the Dark Iron nation was left to him. Most citizens of Shadowforge City agreed.

That was enough. Now Grede wouldn't have to steal Sulfuras from Khaz Modan and flee to Kalimdor as they'd both initially feared. He could take up the warhammer and go find Moza with his head held high.

Jace's lips curled into a faint smile at the thought. He was genuinely happy that Grede might finally get what he'd been after.

"Please convey my thanks to Emperor Dagran Thaurissan for the gift." Jace put the letter away. "And I appreciate you delivering the goods despite the danger."

"It is my honor, Master Jace Sesso." The Ironfury officer said. "May the Flame bless you, and guide our coming assault!"

"Flame bless you," Jace replied.

After the officer left, Khadgar, who had just finished feeding the horses, walked over and asked, "What cargo?"

"Elemental Earth," Jace said. "For making Restorative Potions."

"Preparing for necromancy? But Elemental Earth isn't—" Khadgar paused, then slapped his forehead. "I forgot, they have the Molten Core. Damn, I'm too used to treating Shadowforge City as an enemy to the death."
