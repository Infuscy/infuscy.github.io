Chapter 1633: "Destruction" Rises Again?

From Lordaeron to Stormwind City, all the various rumors that had appeared over the past few months genuinely worried Jace. But more than the smears against his own reputation, he cared about whether these things would affect his parents and friends.

Especially Vereesa. He really didn't want to drag all this messy trouble into the wedding hall.

As for whether this was Kil'jaeden's plan—the Lich King, the Plague of Undeath, dreadlords, death knights, the Burning Legion... it all seemed like one familiar plot point after another playing out in sequence. But strangely, he wasn't all that panicked.

After all, strictly speaking, he'd been preparing for this for over twenty years.

After leaving SI:7, Jace brought a crate of Elemental Earth down to the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb. The moment he stepped through the iron door, still far from the alchemy lab, he caught the fishy stench of shadow fire—even stronger than the last time he'd been here.

When he reached the lab doorway, Ursula was pale-faced, directing the blaze beneath her cauldron, completely unaware of Jace's arrival.

Only when that round of channeling finished did she suddenly notice someone standing at the door. She shuddered, a violet-red glow still swimming in her eyes.

A wave of intense fear rolled out of her body before the alchemist's face slowly regained some color.

"I don't know if the Destruction Potion was a success, but your shadow manipulation has improved a lot," Jace said.

"Making the Destruction Potion really does demand a lot of shadow control." Ursula wiped the sweat from her neck with a towel. "Thanks for the guidance and demonstration earlier, Jace. Without watching a high-ranking warlock like you cast spells firsthand, I feel like it would've taken me years to grasp the technique."

"You've got more to thank me for than just that today." Jace placed the crate on the table. "Open it and have a look."

Ursula examined the crate, took the key from the warlock, unlocked it, and lifted the lid. She pulled out a small Dark Iron box engraved with the crest of a black mountain, opened the clasp, peeked inside, then quickly closed it again. She swept her eyes over the rest of the crate and drew in a sharp breath.

"All Elemental Earth?"

"All of it," Jace said. "And this is just a small portion. Of course, I can't give you everything Dagran Thaurissan sent me—a fair amount has to go to the Wizard Sanctum's alchemy lab."

"My god." Ursula's lips trembled. "I've never seen this much Elemental Earth in my entire life, and of such high quality. I thought it was a bunch of Shadowforge City gunpowder at first. The Dark Iron emperor really just agreed to that?"

"What else would he do?" Jace leaned on the crate's edge. "Are you underestimating your little brother here?"

"Who would underestimate you?" Ursula could barely suppress her grin. She hugged one of the small Dark Iron boxes to her chest and asked quietly, "How many bottles of Restorative Potion are you expecting?"

"At least enough for a month." Jace held up his fingers. "For five people."

"And the rest of the Elemental Earth is mine?" The alchemist's eyes were practically glowing.

"That's right," Jace said. "Consider it compensation for coming back empty-handed last time."

"No problem, no problem!" Ursula gave a gleeful laugh. A surge of energy seemed to come from nowhere as she hoisted the crate—which Jace had struggled to carry—and stuffed it under a cabinet.

"By the way, tell me you've been taking your medicine on time, Jace. Lady Morophil, I specifically asked you to make sure he takes it."

"I reminded him whenever I could, but if he doesn't want to, there's not much I can do, Delin..." Morophil leaned against the doorframe, putting on a pitiful expression.

"Don't slander me, demon," Jace said. "I've been taking it on schedule, rain or shine. Don't you think I've recovered a lot?"

The alchemist got up, dusted off her hands, and gave Jace a quick once-over out of the corner of her eye. "You have—you look healthier. So my treatment works."

"My god." Jace plopped down in a nearby chair. "So you weren't even sure the treatment would work until you saw I was recovering?"

"For a freak like you who's drunk who-knows-how-much demon blood, who knows if the same therapy that works on normal people would have the same effect?" Ursula planted her hands on her hips. "Even the same medicine works differently on humans and elves."

Jace laughed and subconsciously mulled over what Ursula had just said.

"During the Destruction Potion brewing process, judging from what you said, does shadow manipulation affect more than just shadow fire?"

"Of course it does." Ursula said. "But I'm not surprised. A lot of potions and elixirs place demands on the alchemist's own magical skill."

"You know Master Vargus's apprentice? The one called Nabrius." Jace asked.

"Of course I do." Ursula said. "He was a very important member of our Destruction Potion collaboration. I've kept records of the brewing process—many of the steps were recorded and revised by him."

"Do you see any problems with the steps and procedures he wrote?" Jace asked.

"Nothing I can see. They match the draenei records pretty closely." Ursula said. "I can tell his understanding of shadow power is far behind yours. The reason he could successfully brew the Destruction Potion is partly his alchemy skill, and partly—as you guessed earlier—because Kirin'Var Village was surrounded by the Void, which provided a much better environment than my lab."

She spread her hands. "But even though we have Telogrus and we're not far behind them, I'd still prefer alchemy to be more controllable. Especially on the first try, with costs this high."

Jace nodded. "So, did you get results?"

"You finally asked." Ursula grinned smugly, like a teenage apprentice who'd just learned the archmage was about to quiz her on her favorite spell. She scurried back to the rear storeroom and retrieved a delicate enchanted vial.

"Take a look. What's the difference between this and the Destruction Potion you saw in Draenor?"

Jace took the vial and examined it closely. The same terrifying shadow magic fluctuation, the glowing liquid. Ursula and those Dalaran pharmacists had indeed recreated the potion's original appearance.

"I'll taste a bit."

"Taste it? Here?" The alchemist's face changed. "In the basement?"

"Relax, I won't use any destructive spells," Jace said.

The alchemist let out a half-doubtful sigh. "Still, be careful."

The two of them moved to the practice room. Jace pulled out the stopper, and a fishy odor wafted out.

The alchemist warned, "Just a small sip, Jace. Don't drink the whole thing. It's way too expensive, and I need to check if the duration meets requirements."

Jace nodded. To be honest, he didn't have a clear memory of the Destruction Potion's taste. Both times he'd drunk it before, he hadn't exactly been in a state to savor the flavor. Now, tasting it quietly... well, it tasted as awful and strange as he'd imagined. A sickly sweet aftertaste lingered in his mouth, making it even more nauseating.

But once it went down, his hands trembled, his heart rate spiked, everything around him seemed to slow down. All kinds of magical energy surged from his chest. They were all back—Dragonmaw Fortress, the Throne of Kil'jaeden. Memories of the past vivid as a projection.

The effect came fast and faded fast, probably because the first batch wasn't refined enough. Seizing the moment, Jace tried to draw Karrlez's demon blood to inscribe the "Domination Sigil" Karrlez had just taught him. But his thoughts raced at an insane speed, and in an instant, he "slammed" a blood sigil onto the ground, erupting in a burst of wild, raging fel-fire patterns in the dark basement.

From the outer hall came the howling of felhounds and imps. Ursula stumbled back a few steps and pressed against the wall, shouting the felhound's name twice. But the felhound paid her no attention at all. Aside from the weird "whoosh" of burning fel-fire, the only sounds were Ursula's imp assistant shrieking...

"My great master's... new great master!"

The effect dissipated rapidly. Jace felt the world suddenly go quiet, as if sealed under a muffled glass bell. The Domination Sigil burned out, leaving only a shapeless patch of black ash.

Under the effects of the Destruction Potion, a sigil he'd barely learned and would normally require intense concentration to inscribe had nearly become "instant"?

Jace even thought: if the cost of the Destruction Potion ever got low enough, he could use this stuff to develop the "highest-tier forms" of his spells, then work toward that level of effect under normal conditions... of course, that assumed the Destruction Potion had no undiscovered negative side effects.

"What kind of magic was that, Jace?" Ursula asked.

"A sigil that can overawe nearby demons. I'll teach it to you right away," Jace said.

"Overawe?" Ursula glanced toward the doorway. "I might need to get a new imp."
