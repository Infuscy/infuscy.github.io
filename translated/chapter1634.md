## Chapter 1634: Hunter and Pretender

Now that the Elemental Earth was in hand, it still took time to brew — Ursula Delin was preparing Restorative Potions for several people all by herself.

As troops from the north gradually arrived in Stormwind and the Burning Steppes, the dwarves around Blackrock Spire had already begun their slow counter-offensive, picking away at the Blackrock orcs' control bit by bit. The Alliance army's final assault wasn't far off.

Everyone was gearing up for war. Vereesa and her companions had likely already joined the Three Hammers' soldiers in skirmishes over caves and ruins inside Blackrock Mountain. Jace, back in Stormwind, couldn't just sit around in the Slaughtered Lamb's basement waiting for Ursula to finish her potions.

He left Sazeno with Ursula as a temporary assistant for brewing the Destruction Potions and Restorative Potions. In the morning, he headed out to the Mage Quarter with Morophil, bringing the remaining Elemental Earth to the alchemist at the Wizard Sanctum — the Alliance elite needed Restorative Potions to counter necromantic magic.

"Morophil." Jace stood in the shade, watching apprentices and mages rush through the garden in the Mage Quarter, speaking quietly to the succubus hidden behind him. "My life could be on the line this time. If you don't want me to die for nothing or get locked in the Violet Hold forever separated from you, then come clean about your abilities — can you see through a Nathrezim's disguise or not?"

"If it's 'emissaries' or spies sent by the rulers of Nathreza to various worlds, their disguises are worthless before me, dear." Morophil answered confidently. "Rest assured — none of the mages over there are Nathrezim."

"I know that," Jace said. "So what about the rulers themselves? For instance, Tichondrius the Darkener."

"I've never fought the Nathrezim king, master," Morophil said. "But I once tracked his lieutenant, Detheroc. Before Kil'jaeden's armies attacked Brutallus's fortress, Detheroc disguised himself as one of Brutallus's Fel Lords and spread fear of the Burning Legion throughout the stronghold."

"Brutallus might be hot-tempered and reckless, but he'd ruled that fortress for hundreds or thousands of years. He knew other demons' methods and abilities well. It didn't take him long to realize a Dreadlord might have infiltrated his ranks. He ordered me to lead my hunter succubi to find the traitor. In the end, I exposed Detheroc's disguise — but it didn't change much."

"Do you think Kil'jaeden would send Tichondrius after you, master?"

"To reclaim Ulthalesh and avenge the humiliation of Draenor?" Jace said. "Besides, Kil'jaeden and Sargeras have spent tens of thousands of years preparing to invade this world. Do you think I'm not worth calling on Tichondrius's help?"

Morophil didn't answer right away. She seemed to be thinking something over. Jace, for his part, was surprised at her lack of confidence in spotting a disguised Dreadlord. In the game, even elite Demon Hunters with a few years of training could see through a Dreadlord's disguise. Could this demon killer with who-knows-how-many years of experience really be worse than Illidan Stormrage's students?

"You're not confident?"

"It's not that I'm not confident." Morophil tilted her head. "The more confident I am... the more useless I'm liable to be, dear."

"Why?"

"Why do you think Kil'jaeden assigned Brutallus to guard the Scepter of Sargeras? You must understand clearly by now." Morophil's voice went cold. "Kil'jaeden wanted you to use Ulthalesh to take the remaining half of my soul. He didn't believe you'd spare me for my sake, to recover what was missing. He thought you'd seize every bargaining chip you could to destroy the Scepter of Sargeras — and that would drive us apart."

"Actually, I did almost fail to destroy that Scepter in the end." Jace glanced sideways at her. "If I'd taken that half of your soul, it really would've gone more smoothly."

"Hard words." Morophil let out a snicker. "You're clearly madly in love with me. I knew it all that day."

"Love my ass," Jace muttered. "Get to the point."

"The problem is, this means Kil'jaeden already knows I'm loyal to you now." Morophil said. "Maybe Lord Batas's soul told him, at the same time it reported the Tome of Infamy and Ulthalesh to the Legion... That's how he came up with the plan: send Brutallus through a portal to the Throne of Kil'jaeden, holding the last line of defense."

"Which means Kil'jaeden knows I have a hunter succubus by my side who can expose a Nathrezim disguise," Jace said.

"Exactly," Morophil said.

"Is that why Khadgar wouldn't let me go to Lordaeron?" Jace asked. "I really don't want to suspect him."

"He's definitely not a demon, my dear master — that much I can guarantee." Morophil said. "Even a dragon's form shift would show cracks in a heated battle. A transformed Nathrezim could never escape my eyes while casting spells. Maybe even your demon detection would catch something off. Looking on the bright side — maybe he's already communed with the Twisting Nether in Karazhan and pledged himself to Kil'jaeden."

"You sure know how to comfort a guy." Jace crossed his arms.

"Well, of course. That's why you mortals call us succubi." Morophil laughed even more brightly.

Jace gave her a helpless look. "What if it wasn't his idea to rush to Stormwind and warn me not to go to Lordaeron? What if someone else tipped him off?"

"Oh?" Morophil's smile froze for a moment.

Jace continued, "He didn't think it through carefully. He figured that under the circumstances, staying out of Lordaeron was a clean way to distance himself from the Black Knight appearing in the north, so he came straight to me. But maybe he was being used. When I get back to Lakeshire and bring the Destruction Potion to him, I'll ask if someone gave him that advice. That might bring us closer to our real enemy. And given that SI:7 has already been sniffing around the Kirin Tor... doesn't that make it more likely?"

"I can't go to Lordaeron, but you can, Morophil. After Blackrock Spire is over, head straight to Lordaeron. I'll draw up a list of suspects, and you can check who's actually tangled up with demons."

"Then I won't be able to protect you at all times, dear. How heartbreaking." Morophil said.

"Then I'll just summon another succubus," Jace said casually.

In an instant, Morophil's expression twisted violently. Even the shadows couldn't smother the blazing infernal runes across her body. The demonic claws at her fingertips sharpened, and her wings suddenly unfurled halfway as if facing an enemy.

But then she caught herself — she was hidden in plain sight among a crowd of passing mortal mages. She forced down the rage that had nearly erupted, her chest, glowing with demonic fire, slowly softening again.

Jace smiled. "I'm sitting in Ilgalar's Tower — which Nathrezim would dare try me there? Do you think even Tichondrius could guarantee a win against Kael'rez in a fair fight?"

"And what if it's not Tichondrius who comes for you, but Vereesa Windrunner?" Morophil asked.

"Oh, now you're on her side," Jace said.

"Facing Kil'jaeden, even a paladin would ally with a Black Dragon." Morophil said in demonic tongue.

"Logically sound." Jace said. "No more joking. Keep yourself hidden and come with me to the mage tower ahead. SI:7 says the Wizard Sanctum has no traitors, but that doesn't reassure me. I need you to look it over. And — what if those Dreadlords really aren't worried about you?"

"Ah, so last night, you were so forward, so vigorous and focused, just to have me help you scout for Nathrezim today?" Morophil said. "Oh, that feeling won't fade for years. I'm still savoring it."

"And what did you think it was? Love?" Jace asked.

"If not love, then at least pity, or a 'reward.' I thought I ranked slightly higher than a pure tool..." Morophil said plaintively.

"Alright, alright, alright..." Jace scratched his neck uncomfortably and scurried off toward the mage tower.
