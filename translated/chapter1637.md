## Chapter 1637: Reunited at the Storm Altar

Pale, sickly sunlight fell across the rough, dark gray stone. The Storm Altar at the foot of the cliff looked so desolate it reminded Jace of Auchindoun.

The towering black-faced swordsman colossus, the round platform carved with summoning circles, the magma pools that once churned with lava and black smoke — all had completely solidified. The dull rumble from deep underground was gone. The distant collision of storm clouds, the roar of the volcano — all silent. The place was so still it made Jace, who had visited many times, deeply unsettled.

The collapse of the Molten Core had affected the Burning Steppes more profoundly than he'd imagined. The familiar landscape "north of Lakeshire" was going to look completely different. Even the Storm Altar, so close to Blackrock Spire, had transformed entirely in just a few dozen days.

According to the Dark Iron officer who had delivered the Elemental Earth, the dwarves had used the earthquake from the Molten Core battle to launch a surprise assault on the Blackrock orcs occupying Blackrock Mountain. They had cut off the orcs' connection to the various strongholds in the Burning Steppes, and in doing so, retook the road from Lakeshire to the Storm Altar.

The mages from the Wizard Sanctum had arrived as soon as they heard the news to survey the altar's arcane energy firsthand. Maginor Dumas was directing a group of young apprentices as they copied the pattern from the magic circle scroll Jace had brought onto the altar's round platform. Jace stood at a higher vantage point, watching the accuracy of their lines and runes, ready to correct any mistakes.

He had originally expected to sneak out here and summon Kael'rez in secret. But with the Wizard Sanctum's help, everything was much smoother and more efficient — he even got to stand around supervising.

Not that it came entirely free. The pigment the apprentices used to draw the magic circle was mixed with his blood. Without using a covenant master's blood, Jace could never summon a demon lord at full power on the Storm Altar by himself.

"I looked at the Destruction Potions you brought, Jace." Khadgar came up behind him. "I honestly didn't think Ursula Delin could replicate such a perfect finished product in under a year. I figured if she could cultivate a single Voidbloom in five years, that'd be decent. Well, you helping her out certainly didn't hurt."

"The day I tried the first batch of Destruction Potion," Jace said, "I found out the spell enhancement it gives is even more intense than I expected. Remember that demonic sigil we tested at the foot of Ilgalar's Tower? With the Destruction Potion's boost, I could inscribe the sigil in an instant — it felt like I'd already mastered it through years of practice."

"I need to write that down." Khadgar pulled out a notebook, muttering. Jace noticed a wound on his hand — some kind of beast claw mark, barely healed, with faint traces of Holy Light treatment still visible.

"What's that from?" Jace pointed at the scar.

"Nothing." The mage brushed it off.

Jace shot him a look. "When you used Destruction Potions back in Draenor, did you not have a similar experience?"

"Honestly, I don't remember clearly. The situation was too critical back then — one moment of distraction could've cost dozens of lives." Khadgar shook his head. "But it's worth further investigation. Whether that's an inherent property of the Destruction Potion, or something Lady Delin brewed differently."

He glanced at the Wizard Sanctum mages nearby and lowered his voice. "Any chance she'd be interested in going to Dalaran?"

"The Lordaeron royal alchemists invited her too. She turned them down," Jace said.

"Dalaran is different." Khadgar said. "And I hear she has some issues with people from her homeland. Dalaran welcomes anyone with talent — especially magical talent — whether elf or human."

"You've investigated her," Jace said.

"After Draenor, the Kirin Tor has basically turned the Dagger Society inside out," Khadgar said.

"What was Galders doing before?" Jace asked.

"I didn't expect you to be more curious about 'Black Claw.' Don't you want to know who Lady Delin's elven ancestors are?" Khadgar asked.

"Not really," Jace said.

"Even if you did, I couldn't tell you. It's not like I'm on the Council of Six." Khadgar flipped his notebook to an earlier page and pulled out a few sheets. "Here. My revisions to your spell."

Jace took the notebook and looked over the incantation. It was not only longer than the version he and the two demons had settled on for the Hand of Gul'dan, it was even longer than the version with Kael'rez's added passage added.

"What did you put in?" Jace asked.

"Added some... well." Khadgar said. "Remember the first demon you barely managed to summon? Forcing two incantations together like that should never have worked. But shadow energy favors you so much that even a disjointed spell like that got forced into shape by your power."

"But it's still inefficient — more effort for less result. So I added some shadow-linking passages and made a few tweaks. You can trust my ability. I may not rank in the Order of Tirisfal's history books for original spellcraft, but the fundamentals of modifying and optimizing spells are solid. I have my good teacher to thank for that."

"Do you have to mention your teacher every time we meet, Archmage?" Jace said. "But are you sure this works? I can't test this new incantation in front of all these people. Did you try it yourself?"

Khadgar used his sleeve to cover the scar on his hand. "How could I possibly try such a corrupt and degenerate spell? Haven't you heard about the Dalaran demon summoning disaster? Even the Wizard Sanctum must teach that tragic history in their apprenticeship program."

"A Felhound?" Jace asked.

"Cough." Khadgar lowered his voice. "Don't worry about anyone stealing your signature trick, Jace Sesso. I'm never trying that damn spell again in my life. Hey, look down there — Master Maginor Dumas is waving at us."

Jace looked down. The magic circle was finished.

"It's not really my signature trick anymore, Khadgar. Don't worry — even if you did steal it, I wouldn't care."

He ran down the steps. Khadgar called after him, "I'm not interested!"

---

Standing at the center of the Storm Altar, the carefully crafted summoning circle complete, Jace could already feel the energy vibrations from the deepest part of this desolate gorge.

The collapse of the Molten Core didn't mean the ley lines had collapsed along with it. The Storm Altar was still the most powerful ley line node in the entire Redridge mountain range. Gul'dan had chosen well.

He poured a handful of Soul Shards from his pouch onto a circular pattern. These Soul Shards weren't the energy source for summoning Kael'rez — they were the fuel to link the Storm Altar with the Twisting Nether.

This time, Jace was summoning Kael'rez completely from the Telogrus Rift into the world — which meant he alone would be driving more power than the seven or eight summoners at Mothul Bloodcaller's ritual had channeled together.

But with Ulthalesh in hand, Jace had plenty of confidence.

He raised the staff — one that no one present except Khadgar recognized. The apprentices had long since retreated from the round platform, watching from above as the warlock in the center grew stained with blood-red light.

The ground began to tremble. Magical winds howled through the gorge. Maginor Dumas's expression grew increasingly tense. As the mage responsible for investigating dark magic crimes in the kingdom of Stormwind, facing a scene like this must have stirred complicated feelings.

The incantation gathered arcane power, and the arcane power amplified the summoning chant. By the halfway point of the spell, the gorge — which had been brightening under the sun — darkened again. The Wizard Sanctum mages and Stormwind soldiers, despite having already moved well back, took a few more steps away, unwilling to be caught by the wind and the source of the unidentified roar.

This summoning was nothing like the one in Deadwind Pass. Back then, Kael'rez had been forcibly dragged into the elemental plane, had fought Onyxia, and had been thrown back into the Twisting Nether when the plane shattered. He was at his weakest.

Now he had gathered immense power from Karazhan and the Telogrus Rift. Through the incantation, Jace locked onto Telogrus's location. When he heard Kael'rez's voice echo back, he felt his body become a chain binding the Storm Altar's wild arcane power to the infinite energy of the Twisting Nether — both sides threatening to tear him apart at any moment.

But the demon lord wasn't the only one who had grown stronger. Jace had mostly recovered from the Molten Core battle and faced this tearing with far more confidence.

Behind him, Morophil watched, unable to help herself — she licked her lips, shifted her stance, and wagged her tail furiously behind her.

Soon, a void rift was violently ripped open by black demonic claws. The starlight of the Twisting Nether spilling out made even the entire Storm Altar seem to dim.

The swordsman statue above — its black hood released a purplish-red fel glow. The lines and runes of the magic circle burst into purple flame. A pair of demonic horns emerged from the rift first, followed by plates of fel iron armor, a blood-red maw dripping with fel saliva, and then suddenly unfurled deep-brown demon wings — so powerful that one motion shattered a decorative piece off the statue.

"Back in this old place again."

Kael'rez's black hooves struck the ground, shaking the entire round platform.

"Does the destruction begin now, Jace Sesso?"

The senior mages who understood demonic language turned pale with terror. Khadgar couldn't help coughing, as if reminding Jace to manage the situation. The apprentices, on the other hand, had been told in advance what Jace Sesso would be summoning here — they had no idea what was really going on.

Jace adjusted his breathing and lowered his staff. "Not here, Kael'rez!"

"You never fail to disappoint." Kael'rez laughed, then turned to look at Blackrock Mountain in the distance, shrouded by dust and haze.
