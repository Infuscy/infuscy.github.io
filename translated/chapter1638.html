Chapter 1638: Before the Assembly

"Before the assault truly begins, I need one more favor from you, Karrlez."

Jace shouted in Demonic, turning to look at Magno Dumas, who stared back with obvious tension. Every mage from the Wizard Sanctum to the Kirin Tor who had participated in the final battle of Draenor knew that this great demon was the one who had helped recover the Sons of Lothar.

But this was still a Doom Lord—an Eredar several times taller than any of his kind, a high-ranking demon lord rarely seen in Azeroth for who knows how many years.

Only a handful of those present had seen him in person at the Telogrus Rift and Auchindoun. The last time most had seen a demon of this scale was before the Dark Portal, watching Azgath crash into the walls of Nethergarde Keep.

After all, this wasn't far from Stormwind City. A demon like Karrlez could spread his wings and fly south at full speed, crossing the mountains and reaching the Cathedral of Light in less than a morning. Jace understood their concerns.

Added to that, this time Jace had used the full incantation and spell components, and had summoned Karrlez with the full power of the Altar of Storms. The oppressive force radiating from him was nothing like that first summoning, when he'd deliberately used a closed portal to badly wound him.

Even the mages weren't the only ones on edge. Khadgar himself looked unsettled, and even Jace—the demon's master—felt his chest tighten under the terrifying fel energy pressure.

Jace continued, "I need you to work with the mages of the Wizard Sanctum here to erect a portal using the Altar of Storms' power. As a reward—once you do it perfectly—you can follow me to Blackrock Spire and do what you want."

"A reward?" Karrlez said in a mocking tone. "You were going to need me at Blackrock Spire to deal with the black dragon either way, you glib little thing. Did you think I couldn't see through you?"

"Fine, you saw through me. Now follow orders," Jace said.

"Orders obeyed." Karrlez let out a hissing breath, straightened up, and looked around at the three sword-bearing statues surrounding the altar.

He was probably used to maintaining portals for the Legion. On the surface he was angry, but deep down he didn't really mind. Taking orders from Kil'jaeden the Deceiver or taking orders from Jace Sesso—it was all the same.

Jace was already familiar with the Altar of Storms' power, but compared to a Doom Lord, he was still far out of his league. After all, this altar had been built by the Horde using Burning Legion magic, originally constructed specifically to seek help from demons.

Karrlez raised his hands, and Jace immediately felt the shadow mana he'd been resonating with get yanked away. He relaxed, releasing his grip on the shadows. In moments, the altar was wreathed in a rapidly intensifying vortex of dark magic. The hoods of the three sword-bearing statues blazed with fierce shadow fire, casting a purple glow across the desolate ravine.

"Our portal mages tried for days to harness this altar's power," Magno Dumas marveled from not far behind. "This relic Gul'dan left behind is more fearsome than we imagined."

"I told you Jace Sesso would have a better idea than any of us," Khadgar said. "Though I didn't know his bright idea would be to recruit a Burning Legion servant for help."

Magno Dumas didn't know what to say to that. He just nodded. Jace looked at Khadgar, thinking, as if you didn't know.

A high-ranking Eredar like this could fully rely on the Altar of Storms to project a rift into the Mage Quarter, provided there was a suitable anchor point. But the Mage Quarter wasn't the Telogrus Rift or the already-destroyed Auchindoun. No one would risk letting a great demon casually tear a hole into the Twisting Nether in a densely populated residential district.

So to create a safe two-way passage, they still needed the mages at the Wizard Sanctum to cooperate—using the portal in the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower as the "target." Similar to how they'd projected a passage from the Telogrus Rift into the heart of Auchindoun using the Scepter of Sargeras, onto the portal Jace and the orcs of the Shadow Council had built. Of course, this time the scale was much smaller, and the portal, casters, and mana were all already in place.

A gryphon rider left the Altar of Storms with a letter from Magno Dumas setting the portal activation time, flying south over the cliffs. Within a day or two, he'd return with a reply from the Wizard Sanctum.

With the portal passage nearly established, the mages and Kirin Tor advisors here were excited, having completely forgotten about the Doom Lord standing in the middle of the Altar—a creature large enough to rival a monastery.

Jace had no intention of just sitting around during this time. The Alliance soldiers were gathering on the southeastern edge of the Burning Steppes. Once the portal passage was complete, he would head to the Dracodar Plain to join them before marching on Blackrock Spire.

Which meant the time he had left to memorize and familiarize himself with Khadgar's modified version of the Gul'dan's Hand incantation was just these last few days. The chance to find a quiet place for magical experiments was even rarer.

That evening, he took Morophil and Khadgar to a canyon where he and Mosul's group used to practice magic back when they were at the Altar of Storms.

"This has got to be the longest incantation I've ever seen," Jace said, looking at the notes in his hand.

Khadgar chuckled. "Longer incantations usually require more complex power to channel, so they tend to mean more powerful magic—that's just how it is. But that Dark Meteor spell you gave me could still be trimmed down a bit. It has room for optimization."

"I'll need to keep picking your brain on this," Jace said.

"Picking my brain is a bit much." Khadgar said. "After Blackrock Spire, you can come to Karazhan whenever you want. Midnight can get you in safely—few ghosts there dare mess with that horse."

"Then it's settled."

Jace set his notes aside, stepped forward, and tossed two soul shards on the ground, preparing to cast according to Khadgar's incantation.

The moment he began chanting, he realized the spell flowed much more smoothly than the last time he'd cast it outside the Tower of Ilgalar. The gathering of shadow mana was stronger, and his connection to the Twisting Nether was clearer.

If he were more familiar with the incantation and his gestures were more precise, even a spell this lengthy could be chanted at an acceptable speed without being easily interrupted in combat.

Compared to last time, he'd swapped the order of Gul'dan's Hand and the Domination Sigil. This time, as the Dark Meteor took shape in midair and no longer required him to keep channeling shadow energy, he hurled the meteor toward the target and immediately began inscribing the sigil on the ground with Karrlez's blood.

This time, he had plenty of Karrlez's blood, no need to conserve it like in that basement experiment. Jace moved quickly.

But the meteor exploded in front of him, and several felhounds plus a bare-chested Mo'arg gladiator wielding a spear charged out of the flames, roaring toward him. By the time Khadgar had set down his notebook and grabbed his staff, considering whether to intervene, Jace finally completed the Domination Sigil.

The sigil released a golden-green magical light with a thunderous roar. The demons froze instantly, as if hit by a Fear spell. One felhound was even knocked over by its companion and rolled several times. The Mo'arg gladiator bent low and swore allegiance in Demonic. The felhounds lowered their heads too, rumbling low in submission.

Seeing the Domination Sigil take effect, Jace breathed a sigh of relief. "Without the boost from the Destruction Potion, I'm not used to my own casting speed anymore."

Khadgar put his staff back down. "How fast could you cast with the Destruction Potion?"

"I didn't get to try it with Dark Meteor—I only drank a little of that first batch, and with limited time I only attempted the sigil." Jace recalled the feeling. "I don't have a clear memory of exactly how fast, but Morophil and Ursula Delin saw it."

"Master completed it almost instantly," Morophil said. "He threw down a complete demon sigil like it was nothing... graceful and effortless."

"That's an enormous difference." Khadgar murmured in amazement, then quickly added, "Not that I'm criticizing your casting speed. But the same person improving that much proves just how terrifyingly effective the Destruction Potion is."

"I also noticed a side effect, besides the cost." He raised his quill. "Have you noticed that our memories of the period under the potion's effect are a bit fuzzy? Like the taste, emotions..."

"Yeah, I noticed that too," Jace said.

"Well, for a potion primarily powered by shadow energy, that's not unusual. Orc void power potions have similar side effects." Khadgar held up a vial of Destruction Potion. "When you drank that bottle at the Throne of Kil'jaeden, did you feel it affected your judgment?"

Jace knew what the mage was worried about—that the Destruction Potion might cause his shadow powers to go out of control.

He shared the same concern, of course. But at least at the top of the Black Citadel, when he'd drunk the Destruction Potion, he hadn't "lost control" at all. If anything, with his power replenished and his hope of destroying the Scepter of Sargeras growing stronger, he'd become more determined and more clear-headed.

"No," Jace said.

"Either way, we both need to be very careful with it, Jace," Khadgar said. "The both of us."
