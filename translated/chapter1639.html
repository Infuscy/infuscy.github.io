Chapter 1639: A Hero's Resting Place

After contacting the Wizard Sanctum, both sides agreed to open a teleportation portal from the Storm Altar to the Mage Quarter at noon that day. Around the appointed time, gryphon scouts brought back word that the surroundings were clear, and the ritual began. On this side, Khadgar, with Kallez's help, had already set up a portal-maintaining array together with Stormwind's mages. Once the Doom Lord opened the passageway, the spellcasters from the Mage Quarter would take over the portal from him. Kallez had taken full control of the Storm Altar, so the passageway was established without a hitch. He simply raised a hand and tore open a portal rift, and through it Jace could already see the floor of the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower on the other side, along with faint reflections through its green-tinted glass windows. Having grown used to the portal's other end being either a treacherous swamp, a fragment of the Twisting Nether, or Auchindoun's crypts, seeing a normal scene through the portal felt strangely jarring. The first mage to come through was Master Andormas himself, along with several of his assistants. Andormas touched down, greeted Magino Dumas and Khadgar, then turned to thank Kallez -- whether the demon was interested in conversation or not. "Jace." Andormas walked over. "As expected, it takes a demon to be familiar with the ley nexus the orcs built. Now even the most conservative faction at the Wizard Sanctum, the ones who opposed accepting dark magic's help, will have to face reality sooner rather than later. After all, their knowledge didn't help us put this altar back to use -- and more importantly, turn its power against Blackrock Spire, the last orc stronghold in this world." "He's not hinting at you, is he, Master Dumas?" Khadgar laughed to Magino. "I'm not included in what he's referring to, Master Khadgar. I made my position clear after the lighthouse incident." Magino replied cordially. "Dark magic, like arcane, is ultimately just a tool for a spellcaster. But we mustn't forget Dalaran's bitter lesson from over a thousand years ago." Khadgar's smile turned a bit awkward. He quickly said, "Both fel and shadow can lend us tremendous power, but they also invite catastrophe all the more easily." "Even setting aside the calamity from a thousand years ago, Master Medivh's mistake is sobering enough." Jace nodded. The Wizard Sanctum mages who had been lightly ribbing Dalaran suddenly looked much more serious. After all, Medivh was a son of Stormwind. But Khadgar didn't gloat about the Kirin Tor having scored a point in this little sparring match of barbs, given that he was Medivh's own student. "I haven't heard the words 'Medivh' and 'Master' put together in over a decade, Jace." He finally muttered under his breath. Before them, mages emerged from the portal rift one after another, and everyone exchanged greetings and celebrated the ritual's success right in front of a Doom Lord easily three ogres stacked tall. A few days had already gotten the Alliance members working near the Storm Altar accustomed to the Doom Lord's presence. Jace wondered -- if someday Warcraft III actually came out in this timeline, would the Human faction get the "Storm Altar" as a hero-summoning building? Would there be a demon summoner unit? Could he himself be one of the heroes? Though, even if a strategy game did come out along this timeline, the changes to every faction would probably be far more extensive than just the humans. Once the portal passage was complete, Jace and Khadgar immediately set off south toward Drak'Thadar and Bloodaxe Keep -- the location the gryphon scouts had reported as the Alliance vanguard's encampment. Drak'Thadar was a stretch of sloping ground at the southern foot of Blackrock Mountain. Over the past twenty years, Dark Iron dwarves, orcs, Alliance soldiers, and black dragons had fought over it countless times. The Dark Iron dwarves had built sturdy fortresses and outposts there; the Bloodaxe orcs had seized it and converted it into a horde fortress bristling with spikes and crude fortifications. Now, blue Alliance banners flew from those ominous black watchtowers and walls that blended orcish and dwarven styles. Jace and Khadgar arrived at the main gate, scarred with craters and scorched black by dragonfire. In stark contrast to the burned wreckage, the soldiers coming and going wore bright, clean armor, capes, and mages' robes. Inside, there was even a Draenei arcanist in a pale violet robe helping Alliance soldiers conjure drinking water. His build, easily as tall as a wooden wall, stood out sharply against the surrounding humans and Bronzebeard dwarves. But his height wasn't the only thing remarkable about him -- being able to conjure enough drinking water for the camp's soldiers in this godforsaken place called the Burning Steppes... these people really lived up to being kin to the Eredar warlocks. Horn calls rang out from the walls. A herald shouted Jace and Khadgar's names. As they dismounted by the stables, a figure in green eagerly burst out of a tent. At first glance, Jace almost thought it was Vereesa. But it was Alleria Windrunner. Walking out beside her was the paladin Turalyon, dressed in a light gray shirt. Alleria hurried over happily and pulled Jace into a tight hug. After the embrace, the Ranger-Captain held his shoulders and said, "I'm so glad to see you again, Jace." "Same here, Jace." Turalyon walked over and clapped Jace on the shoulder. Glancing at the Sigil of Resolve hanging at his chest, he said, "That badge suits you well." "It's only recently that I realized how important it is to keep it with me at all times." Jace smiled. "I heard about what happened." Turalyon said. "As a member of the Silver Hand, I'm sorry, Jace. Were you hurt?" "No." Jace said. "That misunderstanding didn't go too far. I'm already in a state where I can enter combat at any time." Alleria lifted Jace's hand and examined the faded burn marks on it. "Hearing you say that puts Turalyon and me at ease." "I feel like I'm no longer part of the Sons of Lothar, the three of you." Khadgar cleared his throat from the side. "You always find a way out of trouble, so I'm not too worried about your safety -- unless you go to Karazhan again." The Ranger-Captain smiled. "But regardless, it's still good to see you in one piece, Khadgar." "Khadgar." Turalyon said in a low voice. "Remember the news Jace and Vereesa got back when they were hunting Teron Gorefiend's soul in the Thaurissan ruins up north? About my mentor, Marshal Anduin Lothar's remains." "Of course I remember." Khadgar said. "Back then Draenor hadn't even exploded yet." "Teron Gorefiend's spirit claimed the Marshal's remains were to the west. From the map, I'd guess near Scarshield Fortress." Jace said. "The intelligence the Farstriders got from the death knights in the Badlands says the same thing." Turalyon said. "And we've searched through several other orc camps and fortresses with no results." "The burial pits here at Bloodaxe have been cleared out too. No telling if it was the Dark Iron dwarves or the orcs." Alleria said. Turalyon continued, "Lord Tirion Fordring is advancing toward Scarshield Fortress with the Silver Hand and Stormwind soldiers to find the Marshal's remains. He shouldn't face much resistance -- the orcs are pulling back to Blackrock Spire." "But?" Khadgar asked. "There's a 'but' coming?" "But... the recent undead problems and the rumors of the Black Knight have me unsettled." Turalyon said. Jace inhaled sharply. He'd had a vague worry nagging at the back of his mind, but listening closely to their conversation, he hadn't connected the dots. Turalyon had just dragged his concern right out into the open. "You suspect the black dragons are trying to imitate Gul'dan and create another batch of death knights?" he asked. "Damn it." Khadgar cursed under his breath. "It's hard to say what that mad black dragon might do. He's no Sabellian." The paladin said. "Right now all we can do is hope Lord Tirion Fordring brings good news." The Ranger-Captain pinched the bridge of her nose. "If they really plan to do this, I won't leave a single living soul in that tower. Whether black dragon... or orc." "This is no time for mercy." The mage said. "Mercy to evil is cruelty to family and friends. I understand that perfectly." Turalyon said without hesitation. "I know what you're hinting at, Khadgar. If the time comes and I have to face him, I won't hesitate. Just like I dealt with every death knight before him. No difference." "Turalyon..." Alleria said softly. "Marshal Anduin Lothar's heroic spirit has already returned to the Holy Light's embrace." Jace said. "Well said, Jace." Turalyon said. "We'll bring his remains back to his home, Stormwind City, and give him a proper burial."
