## Chapter 164: In a Flash

Jace hid in the narrow path and waited a while. No alarms, no shouted orders, no hurried footsteps. Before long, the guard from earlier seemed like he was about to come back, so he slipped out of the path and went to find his mother.

Terenas II had grown milder in his old age, his temper softer. He rarely lost his temper over trivial matters anymore. Even if he had actually seen him, he probably wouldn't have pursued it.

Now, replaying the image of those two girls in his mind, he could more or less guess who they were.

The taller one had to be Arthas's older sister, Calia Menethil. The shorter one wearing the dark-green cloak should be Jaina — Jace remembered the two of them had been fairly close as children.

Calia was probably already fourteen or fifteen and had started to fill out. Even from the side profile you could tell she was a remarkably pretty, classically beautiful girl in the making. And Jaina... was genuinely just a little kid. From that distance he couldn't make out a thing.

"Did you see them?" Henny asked eagerly.

"I saw them, I saw them," Jace said. "I even saw the king."

"With the king there, let's go check out the chapel on the east side of the city instead. That old man does everything at a snail's pace," Henny said.

Jace nodded, his mind still full of Arthas gazing up at the archbishop. It was hard to imagine that in a little over a decade that child would personally destroy his entire kingdom, and half a continent along with it — turning all of Tirisfal and the Eastern Plaguelands from a thriving northern paradise of farmland and forest into a haunted deadland crawling with zombies, ghosts, and rotting beasts.

Back on the street, he walked and said, "Mom, you have to come to Stormwind with me someday."

"I will, sweetheart. If your father refuses, I'll yell at him until he agrees," Henny said, looping her arm through his.

Jace looked at her and thought... that didn't quite match what his dad had told him.

"Dad will definitely listen to you. As long as you're willing, that's what matters."

Henny smiled and said nothing.

When they reached the small chapel on the east side of the city, there were hardly any people around — probably all off watching the spectacle at the cathedral.

The priest sat by the chapel entrance and spotted Henny from a distance, calling out, "You're late today, Mrs. Sesso. Did you go over to the cathedral too?"

"The cathedral's been taken over by the king," Henny said. "So I came here instead."

"Coming to pray again today..."

The old priest smiled and nodded, then noticed the lanky figure beside Henny.

"This is... Mr. Jace Sesso?"

"It's me, priest. Long time no see," Jace said, a little guiltily.

"Blessings of the Light," the priest said with a smile, walking over and patting Jace's face. "You little rascal — did you get caught stealing snacks by Archbishop Alonsus Faol?"

Jace stared wide-eyed. "I wouldn't dare steal anything from the Cathedral of Light."

"So you're just bullying this old man who can barely see?"

"No... I'm not a kid anymore."

The priest took Jace's hand and looked into his eyes. "Two or three years and it's like you're a whole different person."

"Teenage boys grow fast," Henny said from the seat beside them. "Priest, Jace's arm got burned. Could you take a look at it for us?"

"Oh. I smelled something unsettling — young Sesso, you..."

Jace glanced at the priest. The priest gave a knowing nod. "For a burn, let me change your bandage first."

The priest unwrapped the bandage from Jace's arm. Although there was still some dried blood and fluid on the bandage, the skin underneath didn't look too bad — just a bit rough and flaky.

Henny had clearly caught the unpleasant smell wafting off it. She frowned. "Priest, it looks like it's almost healed. Why does it smell like it's something serious?"

"It's the bandage," the priest said. "I'll apply some medicine and change it, and it'll be fine."

"Is that true?" Jace pressed. "It's really... no problem?"

The old priest looked at Jace seriously and emphasized, "It's really no problem." With that, the priest went into the chapel to fetch some medicine, saying that there'd been quite a few burn patients lately so there was plenty left over — just enough for Jace. As he applied it, he went over a few things to watch out for.

Jace had been worried the priest might hit him with a Holy Light spell or something, so that put his mind at ease quite a bit.

But while the priest was muttering prayers and rubbing the medicine on, Jace genuinely feared his hand might just burst into flames, and then the other priests in the chapel would swarm him and pin him to the ground as some hidden demon. But in the end, nothing happened.

Considering that one of the Four Horsemen of Naxxramas had gone from paladin to death knight and could still call upon the Holy Light, perhaps the Light wasn't as fundamentally incompatible with the dark as most people believed.

On the walk home at dusk, Henny seemed to be carrying something on her mind the whole time. Jace could tell, so he came right out and asked, "Mom, what are you worried about? My injury's been treated."

"Have you met any girls you fancy in Stormwind?" Henny suddenly asked.

"I'm still a month shy of eighteen. Why are you two in such a hurry?"

"I already had you by eighteen."

"Well, Dad didn't meet you until he was in his twenties."

"Your father was in the army when he was a teenager. I just hope there's someone to take care of you for me."

"Lots of people take care of me, Mom. Don't worry about that." Jace glanced at his mother and said, "Besides, you've set the bar impossibly high for me. If I search and search and the woman I find isn't even as pretty as you, isn't that just moving backwards? I need to take my time and be careful. Can't rush it."

Henny smiled. "Looks aren't everything."

"I know, I know," Jace said. "I wonder who it was that saw a tall fellow on horseback galloping down the Brill main street and became completely obsessed without even knowing his name."

The two of them joked and laughed their way home. The family had dinner, and that night Jace slept more soundly than he could remember.

It felt like it had been ages since he'd fallen asleep feeling that at ease.

But the time off always flew by. Days spent standing guard with his father, fishing at the busy docks, eating fruit from passing peddlers, evenings spent strolling with his mother and talking as a family about the future — two days gone in the blink of an eye.

On the morning he left, Jace boarded the boat and watched his parents standing on the dock, smiling at him. He waved as hard as he could.

Their figures looked even thinner than they had two years ago.

The farther the boat pulled away, the more his father pulled his mother into his arms. From a distance, Jace saw her shoulders shaking without stop.

For the first time, he wished his eyes weren't so sharp. He sat down and stared at the ripples on the water instead.

Heading back to Dalaran now.

Orcs, death knights, black dragons — those things were like storm clouds on the horizon, looming closer by the day, and the boat was sailing straight toward them.
