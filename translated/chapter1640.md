## Chapter 1640: Farstrider Intelligence

In the afternoon, Jace, Turalyon, Alleria, and Khadgar shared a meal together in the camp tent. Even Morofil the demon had been invited.

By now, everyone in the Sons of Lothar knew what had happened at Kil'jaeden's Throne. Brutallus -- the most bloodthirsty and battle-hungry demon gladiator under Kil'jaeden's command -- had fallen to her.

The Sons of Lothar might owe their return to Azeroth to Jace's planning and personal effort, but if the final link hadn't been a banshee who understood Brutallus better than anyone in the Twisting Nether, the Scepter of Sargeras's power might truly have torn Auchindoun's portal apart under the Eredar's magical manipulation, cutting off the escape route for everyone left behind.

Even setting that aside, Morofil had fought alongside Khadgar in Karazhan -- the three of them had faced the scorched blue dragon Arcanagos, the chaotic opera hall, and Aegwynn's three "Mana Embers" together. She had long been considered one of their own by the archmage.

As Khadgar muttered over his drink, the fact that a succubus could be accepted by a member of the Kirin Tor like him probably meant -- as Magino Dumas had subtly jabbed -- that Dalaran had long forgotten the demonic catastrophe of nearly two thousand years ago.

So for now, everyone chatted about lighthearted recent events. Jace had just finished opening the portal passage at the Storm Altar. Turalyon and Alleria had just arrived at Drak'Thadar after a long ride. They'd finally found a moment to eat together like family, and no one wanted to bring up serious topics. But precisely because of the timing and the setting, serious topics inevitably crept in anyway.

Turalyon and Alleria talked about how the people had seen them off when they left Lordaeron in the north. Although the royal family and nobility hadn't shown as much enthusiasm for this assault on Blackrock Spire as they had for the defense of Hillsbrad Foothills back home, the common citizens were brimming with passion to wipe out the last evil in the world.

After all, even ten years after the orc wars ended, rogue orc raiders still roamed the forests of the Lordaeron continent like wild beasts, never fully eliminated. Many believed that once Blackrock Spire fell, these scattered remnants would lose heart and give up their resistance.

With the people so passionate, the royal family couldn't ignore it. King Terenas had given some support to the Blackrock Expedition. Beyond sending the Lord of Hearthglen to atone for his past, he had personally received Turalyon and Alleria at the palace before their departure, holding a formal investiture ceremony. For a monarch who had been bedridden for months, this level of support was more than enough for the two Sons of Lothar.

Hearing that Turalyon had recently met the King of Lordaeron, Jace asked, "How did he seem? The King, I mean."

"At least he looked fine." Turalyon said. "But this trip to Stormwind really aged him another twenty years in the blink of an eye. Or maybe my impression of him was still frozen over a decade ago, so the contrast felt sharper."

"Human aging comes so suddenly." Alleria said quietly. "And with him coming south specifically for the Prince's coming-of-age ceremony, it's hard not to feel something about it."

Even a paladin like Turalyon couldn't detect anything unusual. That put Jace somewhat at ease. Maybe his vigilance about the Scourge apocalypse from his past life was just making him overly cautious.

"And the Prince? How is he?"

"The rumors from the north reached your ears too, didn't they." Turalyon said. "Let me answer the question you're most concerned about outright. Prince Arthas... at least officially, he has denied everything related to you. He's separated the King's illness from the so-called necromantic magic and the plague rumors about the Black Knight."

"Officially?" Jace said.

"Privately, he asked me some questions." Turalyon admitted. "He really was worried those things involved you. Of course, I put his doubts to rest. But I'm not trying to defend Prince Arthas. If you stood in his shoes, you'd naturally suspect every possibility. After all, that was his own father."

"I know, Turalyon." Jace said.

"Don't tell Vereesa about this, Jace." Alleria said softly. "If she knew about Prince Arthas's attitude, she'd probably ride a dragonhawk straight into the Lordaeron royal palace after the campaign. She's the Ranger-Lord now -- technically my superior. There's no way I could stop that little girl when she loses her temper."

"I'll try to stop her." Jace said with a smile.

"That's what worries me most." The Ranger-Captain looked at the paladin.

Turalyon couldn't help but laugh too. Khadgar set down his cup and joined in.

As the sun set that evening, Turalyon donned his armor for his usual nightly patrol, and the small gathering had to end early.

Khadgar went off to the mages to help them conjure drinking water. Alleria came over to Jace and said quietly, "I don't want to interfere with whatever is going on between you and my sister, Jace. But I think at the wedding, it'd be best not to have a demon lady make a public appearance. Setting aside Dalaran or Stormwind, even for Silvermoon City, that would be a bit too progressive."

"Wedding?"

Jace drew in a breath. Alleria leaned closer and whispered, "Don't play dumb with me, Jace. Even if I knew nothing about shadow energy, it wouldn't stop me from seeing right through you. I'm sure Vereesa has already blurted out that Sylvanas wants all three of us sisters to hold a proper joint wedding."

"Ha. You really do know her better than she knows herself." Jace asked awkwardly, "What do you think?"

"What else is there for me to think?" Alleria let out a soft sigh. "If back in Terokkar Forest I still had some... I don't know how to describe it, reservations about her loving a demon summoner. I know it was you who helped her move past the shadow of Lirath's tragic death and find her fire again. I know it was you who let me see her one more time. But... regardless, after everything that happened on Draenor, Turalyon and I have truly come to know the kind of person you are. And I genuinely want to entrust Vereesa to you."

"Thank you, Alleria." Jace said.

"I should be the one thanking you, in every respect." Alleria said. "So what about the wedding?" Jace asked.

"About that." The Ranger-Captain smiled. "What more could I ask for? I love them both more than anything in this world -- Turalyon and you included. Sylvanas's wish is naturally my wish too. I'm looking forward to that day. If you're worried about a Quel'Thalas wedding, all I can say is that being invited to hold a wedding in the kingdom is an honor for Quel'Thalas, not something you need to worry about."

"If there's anything about this wedding that does worry me, it's Captain Nathanos. The rumors about the two of them being together have spread everywhere from Quel'Thalas to the Kingdom of Lordaeron -- but those rumors started back during the orc wars. I've been back from Draenor for a while now and I still haven't heard Nathanos admit it himself. I really have no idea what those two are thinking."

"Sylvanas might feel that Nathanos hasn't earned the Quel'dorei's recognition yet?" Jace asked.

"So she sent him to Blackrock Spire?" Alleria sighed. "That's exactly what makes me most uncomfortable. If Sylvanas doesn't think highly of him, why did she meet with him in the first place? Fine, maybe I shouldn't be judging their relationship from the sidelines. I just hope Sylvanas truly respects that Lordaeron man, instead of treating him like a puppet to push and pull according to her own whims --"

Before the ranger could finish, a commotion rose from the camp gate. A group of Farstriders riding hawkstriders had returned, led by none other than Nathanos himself.

The two of them exchanged a glance. Speak of the devil.

Nathanos Marris landed lightly, draped in a brown cloak. He was tall and lean, his face so sharp it looked like it had been chiseled. Despite his typical Lordaeron features, under the cloak's shadow he hardly seemed different from the elven rangers accompanying him.

"Captain Alleria." Nathanos strode over quickly. When he saw Jace, he politely gave a Farstrider salute. "Master Sesso."

"Captain Nathanos." Jace nodded. "Speak freely." Alleria said.

Nathanos nodded. "The Farstriders, along with Prince Galen and Stromgarde's knights, chased down a group of Spirestone ogres that didn't make it back to Blackrock Spire in time. We interrogated the survivors. One of the prisoners let slip the location of a hidden burial ground that hasn't been pulled back to the Spire yet. Apparently the orcs have preserved the bodies of several Lordaeron knights there. But the Farstriders couldn't get the exact location out of him."

"I've already sent a Farstrider to Scarshield Fortress to inform Lord Tirion Fordring. I think we should move ahead and search the area before the orcs can transport the bodies back to Blackrock Spire. Even if Marshal Lothar's remains aren't among them, there may be other war heroes."

"You're right." Alleria said. "But Turalyon just left. I'll go tell Khadgar --"

"I'll join the search too." Jace looked at the demon not far behind him. "Set up a circle for me to contact Kallez, Morofil."
