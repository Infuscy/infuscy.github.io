## Chapter 1641: Death Canyon

Though the Burning Steppes were far clearer than they used to be, the smoke rising from flowing lava still obscured the view.

A squad of Farstriders led the way, with Khadgar and the others riding gryphons ahead, followed by a massive demon in flight. Every beat of the Doom Lord's enormous wings kicked up a chain of vortices through the smoke that blanketed the plains.

Jace and Morofil rode on the Doom Lord's shoulders, surveying the gray-black landscape below. They were less than a morning's journey from Blackrock Spire now. From here, they could even clearly make out the fortress's tall, smooth walls on the mountainside in the distance.

At this range, the roads that had once been crowded with patrolling orc wolf riders and Scarshield soldiers were now empty and desolate. Even the orc-skin banners and the severed heads mounted on spear tips had been pulled down, replaced in many places by the deep red banners of Arathi or Stormwind's worn blue flags.

With the remaining Horde forces and black dragons cleared from the three key strongholds -- the Storm Altar, Drak'Thadar, and Scarshield Fortress -- combined with the Dark Iron excavation sites in the Searing Gorge and Shadowforge City's cooperation, the encirclement of Blackrock Mountain would be complete.

The orcs had realized this long ago, which was why every stronghold was busy retreating. The fact that they'd left behind a burial pit from Gul'dan's era, one used to manufacture death knights, was genuinely surprising.

Jace thought to himself -- if there were any warlocks still around who had preserved Gul'dan's old techniques, maybe they could figure out what the black dragons and the Horde were up to inside Blackrock Spire. At the very least, they could get some of Gul'dan's preserved necromantic knowledge, which could help the Alliance in the coming assault.

"You're going the wrong direction." Kallez roared in Common. "If you're taking Jace Sesso to find a group of necromancers, it should be over there!"

He pointed northwest with his dark brown finger. "I can sense the necromantic and shadow energy in those canyons. The further you fly this way, the farther you get from it."

The Farstriders looked back. Khadgar shouted, "Listen to the demon, Farstriders. He spent his time in the Burning Legion conquering shadow creatures. Don't feel bad that a demon outclasses you in tracking this kind of evil!"

"Yes, Master Khadgar." One of the elven rangers replied.

"Under your guidance, everyone in this Alliance that prides itself on light and justice grows more corrupt by the day, darling." Morofil murmured.

"You don't like it?" Jace asked.

"Does my preference matter?" Morofil asked.

"It has reference value." Jace patted Kallez's pauldron. "Head that way, Lord Kallez."

The demon spread his wings, bringing a gust of foul fel energy, followed by a sudden jolt of turbulence. Jace had to admit he'd never ridden anything more stable than Sora in all his life -- from the black dragon prince to the demon lord, not even anything close.

But the Doom Lord wasn't slow either. His sheer size meant even a slight flap of his wings carried them a huge distance.

The mountain face that had been at the edge of their vision, looking like cracked, black-and-blood-red skin, was now rushing toward them at speed. The remnants of Dark Iron high walls buried within it emerged, and they could even make out the spikes the orcs had added.

Figures were moving near the walls -- clusters of orcs heading toward Blackrock Spire, pulling what looked like wolf-drawn "wolf carts."

"Corpse wagons?" Jace asked.

"No doubt about it. Summoning Lord Kallez was the right call, Jace!" Khadgar shouted.

"Should we call for reinforcements, Masters?" A ranger asked.

Khadgar called back, "You return to Drak'Thadar. Notify Turalyon and the others. The rest of you, follow me!"

The archmage spurred his gryphon forward as he spoke, the tip of his staff already glowing.

Almost simultaneously, the figures on the wall spotted the approaching enemies. It wasn't so much that they'd seen the light on Khadgar's staff as they'd spotted the impossibly enormous demon looming in the distance. Soon, the dull sound of bone war-horns echoed from the seemingly abandoned old walls.

Before Jace could make out who was in the gaps of the broken walls, Kallez folded his wings and stomped down hard. His black hooves crushed the ancient stonework like a warhammer in a single blow. He spread his wings again and slammed a ruined tower into rubble.

"Someone's coming! Someone's coming!"

"Kill the demon! He's not on our side!"

Mad orcish shouts came from the tower, but each cry was followed by a volley of shadow bolts slamming into it, then silence.

Jace had barely jumped down from Kallez's back when the successive waves of searing shadow and fel energy nearly forced his eyes shut. The demon lord had already charged silently into the Horde outpost, slaughtering every living thing that still moved. Even Morofil squinted uncomfortably at the shadow of his massive, beating wings.

"Leave one alive, Kallez!" Jace shouted. But the demon was already striding away.

"You've been keeping him pent up too long, Master. I think we'd better keep our distance." Morofil said. "His shadow bolts don't discriminate."

Jace started to chase after him, but a skeleton's head, caked with black rotten flesh, emerged from the rubble of the wall Kallez had trampled. Its hollow eye sockets burned with bloody lights, useless decayed eyeballs still dangling inside.

"Watch out!"

Behind him, a Farstrider's Arcane Shot was quick -- it shattered the skull first. But the skeleton still wobbled, refusing to fall, taking a few more steps before collapsing.

A skeleton soldier?

Jace walked over and examined the fallen remains. The bones had been charred black by shadowflame.

A hoarse roar echoed from the canyon. Jace and Morofil abandoned the corpse, joined the rangers in cutting down a few shambling dead, and chased after Kallez. The path was already littered with orc corpses rotting from shadow corruption. Ahead, the enormous demon's winged shadow flickered among the jagged rocks.

Another winged shadow appeared from above, diving straight at the Doom Lord. The pale gray sunlight fell on the creature's body, revealing rows of bare bones.

A reanimated undead whelp? That roar earlier had been its call.

Even undead, it was just a whelp. Between the cliffs, Kallez laughed wildly and hurled a barrage of shadow bolts into the black flames spewing from the whelp's mouth. The whelp took several hits, losing its balance, and the demon seized the opportunity to grab one of its wings and rip it clean off.

The dragon showed no sign of pain or struggle -- it just fought back with everything it had, sinking its teeth into the demon's side armor. But the bite didn't hurt Kallez at all. He swung his fist down and smashed the whelp's skull open. Dark, rotting fluids splattered everywhere. In an instant, the entire canyon was thick with the sickening stench of decay.

The demon tossed the whelp's mangled corpse aside. Khadgar and the remaining Farstriders, who had been chasing the orc wagon convoy, caught up.

Seeing the sight before them -- the chaos of shadow magic, the shattered dragon corpse -- even the archmage couldn't help but click his tongue. The rangers were even more shaken.

Beneath the demon lay a burial pit, piled with stinking corpses corroded by shadow energy -- mostly orcs and Dark Iron dwarves. The Farstriders immediately fanned out and entered the pit to search for clues.

"Over here, Masters!" A ranger called from across the pit. Beside him, a dwarven knight who had been dead for who knows how long and a human knight wearing Stormwind armor were hung on crooked racks made of black iron, as if being prepared for some purpose. What purpose -- it wasn't hard to guess.

Khadgar went over and examined the human knight specifically. He shook his head. "He's not Anduin Lothar. The hair color alone is wrong. But we should take him back for burial too... but why would there be a reanimated whelp here? And some resurrected dead."

"The aura on them is very similar to those corpses we saw in Blackrock Mountain before." Morofil said.

"Infusing these corpses with shadowflame to reanimate them. A very heavy power." Kallez said in a low voice.

"If you hadn't killed all the orc warlocks here so thoroughly, we could at least have caught one to ask what's going on, Lord Kallez." Morofil said.

"There's still time, banshee hunter. Don't forget who I am." Kallez raised one hand and clenched it suddenly. An ethereal scream came from his palm. Out of nowhere, he had captured the ghost of an orc warlock, holding it fast so it couldn't escape.

"Who reanimated the dead here? Be honest with me, and I'll send you where you belong." The demon asked.

"The great power of Victor Nefarius. He commands life and death over this land from atop Blackrock Spire."

The orc ghost answered with a shriek. But mid-sentence, the soul began trembling and convulsing without warning... then the spirit turned its ghostly eyes toward Jace below, and spoke in orcish in a low voice: "You thought you could escape paying the price forever, warlock? You thought you were about to win?"

The soul exploded instantly in Kallez's hand. Even the demon lord hadn't seen it coming.

More and more gryphon riders gathered overhead -- mages and paladins among them. Alleria's voice called down from above. They had likely received word and rushed over to help.

The soul dissipated completely, but Jace felt as though the orc warlock's whispers still echoed around him, drowning out even Alleria calling his name -- until Khadgar suddenly gripped his shoulder.

"We'll talk back at camp, Jace."
