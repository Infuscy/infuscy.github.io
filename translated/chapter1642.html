Chapter 1642: Casting Aside the Last Hesitation

Flames crackled and burned. Choking, acrid black smoke billowed and surged, blocking the view. Dark figures stood motionless in the haze. The priests' prayers continued without pause, punctuated now and then by a shadow's shriek. Holiness and evil churned together, echoing everywhere, making the gray-black valley feel all the more eerie. The soldiers who had arrived were working under Prince Galen Trollbane's command, tossing the dead orc necromancers one by one into the burial pit to be burned with the rest of the corpses. Everyone believed that the pit held more than just orcs, trolls, Dark Iron dwarves, and ogres -- it also held Alliance heroes who had followed Marshal Anduin Lothar south on campaign. But the necromantic energy permeating the place was too dense for any living person to enter and sift through the shattered limbs and bones, the rotting cloth and rusted armor. At most they could leave out the human and dwarven knights' bodies that the orc warlocks had placed on the surface. Plague and blight still threatened to spread. To keep the disease from being carried back to the various armies besieging Blackrock Spire by the soldiers who had just arrived, everything had to be done simply and quickly. "Imagine if these corpses were taken back to Blackrock Spire -- how much necromantic power they'd give those black dragons." Alleria said. "For every burial pit like this we find and destroy before we breach the Spire, the necromantic curses our soldiers face will be that much weaker, and the paladins and priests will have an easier time of it." Khadgar said, "Hard to say whether these bodies were meant to produce a necromantic plague or to create powerful death knights. I'm starting to worry about what dark plan the black dragon is brewing, Alleria. If you'd seen that reanimated whelp, you'd know what I mean." "I saw that enormous corpse, Khadgar. Thank the Light it was a darker denizen of the Twisting Nether that faced it, not our soldiers." Alleria said. "It reminds me of the rumors Sylvanas told me about from Northrend. The magisters found that some corrupted insectoid necromancers were trying to reanimate the ancient bones buried there -- magnataur, even dragons... They probably wanted to use those undead colossi to assault the Vrykul and troll fortresses." "What do you think, Jace?" The ranger asked. "Very possible." Jace nodded. "Captain Alleria! Come look at this body -- it might be a Quel'Thalas magister or Farstrider. This piece looks like your armor." Galen Trollbane called out. "I'll be right there, Prince Galen!" After the ranger left, Khadgar looked over at Jace. "Do you really think the situation here is similar to Northrend's?" "I honestly suspect so." Jace said. "After returning from Draenor, I looked at some undead samples brought back from Northrend in Dalaran." Khadgar said. "The undead there are much purer -- quite different from here. You should know it's not just necromantic energy that can raise the dead. Even arcane power can achieve a similar effect in another form. Shadow is even closer to it." Jace understood that perfectly well. Fel and arcane could both "resurrect" the dead -- it was hardly different from stitching body parts together and pumping energy in to make them move. Like the arcane golems that walked the streets of Silvermoon City, or the Infernals that fell from the sky. But the orc soul... "You're still hung up on what that soul said." Khadgar said. Jace didn't answer, but he didn't deny it either. "Think about who you are." Khadgar said in a low voice. "Then look at that orc. His soul was nearly corrupted by shadow and necromantic energy, so of course he was jealous of you. He thought you'd paid no price because he failed, and you haven't. Every witness who's walked this road with you knows how much effort you've put in." "I feel like what that soul said might not have been its own meaning." Jace said. "You think someone twisted the soul's will, trying to send you a message." Khadgar said. "Kil'jaeden? Or some necromancer serving him or the black dragon?" Kil'jaeden... Neither Morofil nor Kallez had connected that soul's voice to any Burning Legion commander. If Kil'jaeden really was plotting some secret trap around him, he wouldn't expose himself like this now. He surely knew Jace was watching for his next move. If not him... then someone who could change a dead soul's will so easily. An orc tongue full of jealousy, hatred, and pain. Everything pointed to the possibility Jace least wanted to consider. The Lich King. Ner'zhul. "But I don't think that's the point." Khadgar said. "Orcs, Horde, black dragons, demons -- they've been in league for a long time. The important thing is their goal. They want to unsettle you." "...Doubt. Self-doubt is a seed. It can grow into anything -- good or bad. That soul, or whatever was behind it, wasn't thinking of anything good. It wanted to use that seed to slowly devour your strong will as its nourishment, until it finally grew into a tree of chaos. Jace Sesso, don't let it win." "Maybe you've understood this all along. Maybe you've already reached the same conclusion I have, but you just can't commit to it yet... So I hope my words can help solidify your thoughts from the outside." "I'm not thinking about anything else now, Khadgar." Jace said. "Have a little faith in me." "I've always had faith in you." Khadgar glanced ahead at Alleria, lowering his voice almost to a whisper. "Otherwise, would I have taken you to Karazhan?" A sudden scream came from the burning pyre pit, followed by a hysterical roar. Amid the chaos, a frenzied figure leaped from the flames, attacking every living thing in sight. Wielding a sword grabbed from somewhere, it swung wildly. The soldiers who had been carrying and burning corpses scrambled to dodge or draw their weapons to defend themselves. One was struck in the shoulder and fell. "He's alive! He's alive!" "Kill him! He's no Alliance hero anymore -- he's a fallen undead, no different from a death knight or an orc warlock!" "No... Don't!" "Holy Light..." "Help!" A paladin pushed through the panicked crowd, raised his sword, and murmured a prayer for the Holy Light's blessing. Golden holy fire wrapped around his blade, pushing the smoke from the pyre aside as it struck the maddened figure square on. A grating screech of metal. The figure fell and rolled into the burning pit. As it came to a stop nearby, Jace saw the blue-and-gold lion emblem on its shoulder -- nearly scorched black -- glaring harshly in the firelight. "The corruption spreads faster than we thought, Your Highness." The paladin sheathed his sword and reported to Trollbane. "We should leave this place quickly. It's best not to transport the Alliance knights' bodies back to Stormwind or anywhere else. This place should be burned clean -- no trace left behind." "I agree with his assessment, Prince Galen." Khadgar said. "The assault on Blackrock Spire hasn't even begun yet. The Alliance doesn't have the manpower or the time to properly handle these remains. I don't think these spirits would want to see the people of the seven kingdoms risk necromantic infection for the sake of their honor. Burn this place." Galen Trollbane stared at the burning pit, his expression heavy. He was clearly hesitating. "If a decision needs to be made, now is the time, Your Highness." Jace said. Galen looked once more at the Alliance hero's body slowly burning in the pyre. He drew a sharp breath. "Burn it. Burn it all!" "Yes, Your Highness." The knights and soldiers immediately began tossing every last corpse into the pyre. Some were even quietly sobbing as they carried the bodies. Marshal Lothar's fall. The Battle of Blackrock Spire. For most of the people here, it was an epic campaign. They didn't want the participants in that battle burned alongside the orcs. "Look at what the orcs have done to our heroes!" Prince Galen roared, his voice hoarse against the crackling of the flames. "The good news is that the time for revenge has come. Soon the Alliance's banner will fly over every part of that black tower!" "For the Alliance!" "For the Alliance!" Energized shouts rose from around the pyre.
