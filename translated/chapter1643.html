Chapter 1643: At the Foot of Blackrock Mountain

With the relentless harassment and coordination from the Dark Iron dwarves and Jace's allies on the inside, the Alliance offensive outside was making rapid progress. After the Blackhand clan's last stronghold in the Burning Steppes -- an outpost held by Smolderthorn trolls -- was easily taken by Stromgarde's soldiers, the allied army's advance had reached the foot of Blackrock Spire. But over the next few days, Turalyon and Alleria led their respective forces in searching every captured location. Not just Horde outposts, but also abandoned Dark Iron dwarf mines and bunkers. None of them yielded any trace of Anduin Lothar's body. This meant either the orcs had destroyed Lothar's remains long ago, or the Alliance soldiers had burned them alongside the orc corpses, or they'd simply been discarded in some unremarkable corner of the Burning Steppes and never found, or they'd been taken back inside Blackrock Spire. Even if the Blackhand brothers had abandoned Gul'dan's methods for creating death knights long ago, they wouldn't have tossed aside the Alliance commander's corpse like useless trash or piled it with common orc and human soldier remains. Teron Gorefiend had been certain Anduin Lothar's body was still in the Burning Steppes. He must have known the Horde's forces were still preserving it properly, otherwise he wouldn't have risked wandering here alone. And from the look of that burial pit they'd just found, Rend Blackhand had apparently stopped caring about black magic or the ancestors. Either the black dragons had forced them to use every means necessary to hold off the Alliance and Dark Iron coalition, or they had completely abandoned their old principles under the black dragon's rule. The elite knights' bodies that had been picked out also suggested, from the side, that the remnants of the Shadow Council really intended to carry on Gul'dan's legacy. So Anduin Lothar's remains would be even less likely to still be outside waiting for the Alliance to recover this late in the game. In the distance, Danath Trollbane -- the leader of Stromgarde's red-cloaked knights, clad in silver armor with a longsword in hand -- stared solemnly at the black tower flickering with distant sparks of fire. Not far away, Turalyon sat astride a sturdy white horse, wearing the same expression. Every member of the Sons of Lothar knew that once they entered Blackrock Spire, orc battle axes, troll poison spears, ogre warhammers, and even the black dragon's fel flames would not be the most terrifying thing they faced. They would eventually have to confront their resurrected countrymen. And the name none of them wanted to mention right now. Was this the scheme of that malicious prince, Nefarian? To pit the heroes of the Second War against the fallen of the First War? Marshal Lothar's significance to the Sons of Lothar and the entire Alliance was extraordinary. Having to fight a fallen version of him would be a nightmare for every soldier marching toward Blackrock Spire. For Jace, though, it wasn't quite so severe. He was more concerned about how Blackrock Spire connected to the Scourge, or the Burning Legion. The necromantic plague, the Nathrezim, Ner'zhul, the Kirin Tor... He had too many names and questions buried in his mind that he couldn't share with anyone. "Don't brood too much, Master. Your inner turmoil is making your mana taste bitter." Morofil descended from the sky, hovering before Midnight with her wings beating. Her size was far too large for a succubus, and each wingbeat kicked up clouds of dust, startling nearby warhorses into panicked neighing. The cavalry behind her had to steer around her. But she seemed completely unconcerned as she brought her face close to Jace's. "Whether this is the Nathrezim's handiwork or Kil'jaeden's scheme, you still have to deal with that black dragon. Keep your goal in mind... just like on Draenor." "You sound like a paladin." Jace said. "The way you insult people is just awful..." The demon put on a hurt expression. "Find a chance to get inside Blackrock Spire. Help me locate Vaelastrasz. He'll be waiting for us and Vereesa somewhere not too deep inside." Jace said. "Use your soul and wait for my good news." Morofil took off abruptly. Jace's gaze followed her as she rose. Dark clouds rolled in the sky, lightning flashing within them and casting twisted shadows. It had been a long time since the clouds had been this thick, not since the Molten Core was destroyed. But these black clouds brought no cold wind or freezing rain. The air remained scorching hot. On the slope behind him, the gryphons shifted their claws restlessly, as if the ground would burn them if they stayed too long. The destruction of the protection runes had severed the connection between the Firelands and Azeroth, but it hadn't fully quenched Blackrock Mountain's fury. This terrifying volcano still belched black smoke continuously, poisoning everything at its feet. Quel'Thalas's Farstriders and hawkstrider cavalry stood out in this gray, chaotic landscape like a string of bright crimson and emerald pearls -- pristine and beautiful. Stromgarde's forces, led by the two Trollbane royals, had dyed another mountainside red. Their banner was like another flame competing with Blackrock Mountain's evil fel fire. At their head, the golden shield Prince Galen Trollbane carried bore a fist emblem visible from a great distance. Lord Tirion Fordring, passing before Jace with the Hearthglen and Silver Hand forces, lowered his head in greeting. Jace smiled and returned the gesture. The Silver Hand's troops advanced behind him, like a gleaming silver lance thrusting straight into the chaos ahead. At that moment, a warhorse draped in blue barding approached from behind. It was Varian Wrynn. "Jace." As they rode side by side, Varian's horse still showed some wariness of Midnight beneath Jace. The King tugged hard at the reins, gently stroking his mount's neck. "The assault on Blackrock Spire's walls is about to begin. But we haven't seen any sign of the black dragons. Do you think this is normal?" He looked up at the highest point of Blackrock Spire -- the area shrouded in smoke and dust. "If I were that black dragon, I'd have my brood swoop down from above when the Alliance army is attacking the walls, at the hardest moment of the siege, and deliver a crushing blow from the heights. We might have gryphons and powerful mages to help, so those black dragons might not gain much of an advantage. But once we break inside the Spire, their aerial advantage disappears." "But the black dragons don't see the orcs as troops worth preserving." Jace said. "To them, the orcs are just a bunch of expendable slaves gathered around their lair. They'll do everything they can to make the Blackhand's forces absorb the worst losses. Then, when both sides are exhausted, they'll either step in to finish things or just cut their losses and flee the tower." "And apparently the interior of Blackrock Spire is enormous -- even the dragons can fly inside it. At least, that's what the Dark Iron dwarves claim." "That's the kind of explanation a qualified court mage would give, Jace." Varian said. "Now, as a shadow magic advisor?" "As a shadow magic advisor, I'd say maybe the black dragon prince doesn't think this is the best moment to strike us hardest." Jace said. "I feel the same way." Varian Wrynn said. "Are you prepared for whatever schemes come your way, Jace?" "More or less." Jace looked at him. "Just like you're prepared to face the most powerful black dragon in the world." Varian let out a laugh. He nodded. "That's about as prepared as one can be." The King pulled a flask of Restorative Potion from his pocket. "Drink this too. They say it slows down most forms of terrible necrotic decay and shadow curses." "This was made from the Elemental Earth I brought back to Stormwind." Jace said. "I already have some -- already drank it." "Ah, right. You know a very talented alchemist." Varian nodded and took a sip, swallowing with some difficulty. "Don't worry, Varian." Jace said. "I'm here. Turalyon and the others are here too." "You're practically mocking me." Varian snorted. "I don't fear strong enemies. And I don't fear death." "We'll bring Marshal Lothar's remains back to Stormwind City to rest." Jace said. Varian nodded, his gaze growing a few degrees more resolute. Blue and white banners fluttered in the howling, scorching wind as they passed before the skeleton of a dragon by the lava river. In the distance, the Alliance army advanced like closely arranged red, white, and pale blue pieces on a chessboard, placed across the cracked black-gray wasteland. At the foot of Blackrock Mountain, the orcs had already destroyed the bridge leading to the mountain's gate. They intended to fight to the death inside the tower against the Alliance forces. But the Alliance was far from helpless. Khadgar had already led the Kirin Tor and Wizard Sanctum mages to the very front of the formation. They were ready to use arcane magic to swiftly rebuild the bridge so the besieging army could cross. If Khadgar could build a staircase in the turbulent magical storm of Karazhan, maintain a floating island and its watchtower against the tearing force of the Scepter of Sargeras, then rebuilding a Dark Iron bridge with a team of mages was well within his capabilities. The entire allied force had full confidence in the Kirin Tor. But the orcs weren't about to sit idly by while the mages rebuilt the bridge. Above the gate, the black tower's windows -- lit as if on fire -- were crowded with figures. They were ready to do everything in their power to disrupt the bridge builders. Even at this distance, Jace could feel the fury and bloodlust radiating from inside. And Jace, along with the Wizard Sanctum mages and countless gryphon riders from Ironforge, Stormwind, and Aerie Peak, would use every means available to prevent the orcs from interfering with the bridge's restoration. Until the allied soldiers breached the gates of Blackrock Mountain and charged into Blackrock Spire together with the Shadowforge City soldiers of the Ironcast Crypt. Just then, the orcs' war horns drifted down from the tower, like the mournful howl of some evil beast from the abyss. "Sounds like the Horde can't wait any longer." Varian drew his longsword. "Then let's not keep them waiting any longer."
