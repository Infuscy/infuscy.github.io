## Chapter 1644: The Bridge

Above the commanders' roaring shouts and beneath the fluttering banners of every kingdom, armor that reflected Blackrock Mountain's fires glinted like silver dragon scales, swaying with each step. Alliance soldiers cried out for the Holy Light's blessing as they converged on the collapsed bridge from every direction.

Kirin Tor mages had already taken up positions on the nearby high ground. Khadgar stood at the highest point in his blue robes, staring up at the black cliff face.

This was the terrible fortress the Dark Iron dwarves had built two hundred years ago with the help of Ragnaros the Firelord. It had been designed not only to withstand the united assault of the Wildhammer and Bronzebeard clans but also to repel invasion from other elemental armies. The structure was infuriatingly sturdy, layered with fearsome magical wards, and built to shrug off elemental bombardment. Even though the collapse of the Molten Core's protection runes had lowered its defenses by a notch, it was still far from something that ordinary magic could reshape or destroy.

Orcs, trolls, and ogres hid behind walls carved from the mountain itself, barely mounting any attack. No arrows, no magic. It was as if Lothar's army could not even scratch this fortress — the same fortress the Dark Iron dwarves had never managed to breach. The Alliance army would only be dashing itself against a rock.

"Have the Dark Iron forces already broken into Blackrock Spire?" Galen Trollbane galloped over to the Farstriders, shouting. "The orcs have been pulled to the other side to fight off the troops pressing from Shadowforge City and the Burning Steppes. They have no strength left to resist us."

"It's not that simple, Your Highness!" Nathanos called back, wrestling with his irritable hawkstrider. "They're waiting for our weakest moment."

"Weakest moment?" Sazeno muttered quietly.

"The moment the mages hold the bridge and the soldiers cross," Jace said in a low voice. "Kirin Tor!"

A shout from the front line drowned out Jace's words. Amid the growing rumbles from Blackrock Mountain, he could not tell whether it was Khadgar's command.

But then a sharp arcane ring that could pierce the air for miles gave the whole army a clear signal — the siege had begun.

On the high slope, Khadgar raised his staff above his head with both hands, conjuring a dazzling starlight. An arcane circle blazed beneath his feet, visible from hundreds of meters away. As arcane power gathered, dust and ash sprayed up from the canyon floor.

More and more mages gathered around Khadgar, lifting their staffs to join the grand spellcasting ritual.

Before long, boulders of all sizes began to rise from the cliff base and the surrounding slopes and hilltops, wrapped in arcane energy. They stacked onto the broken foundation of the bridge one after another, as if gripped by invisible hands, extending the deck longer and longer. The seams between each stone fit so tightly they were nearly seamless.

The crash of stone against stone drowned out the volcano's eruptions, drowned out the wind's howl, drowned out the cries of the Horde defenders inside Blackrock Spire. But it did not drown out the excited cheers of the Alliance soldiers.

Knights of the Silver Hand gathered at the bridgehead, raising the silver Tyr's Hand and Lordaeron's white banners. Close behind, Stromgarde soldiers lifted their round shields toward the dark tower above.

A piercing arcane ring exploded again. Varian Wrynn, at the front, wheeled his horse, turning among the blue shields and white armor of Stormwind and Lordaeron, raising his sword high.

"The time to end the Orc War is here! Heroes of every kingdom — every one of you has seen with your own eyes what the orcs and the black dragon have done to our homes and families. Every one of you has lived through it. Words are not enough! Now is the reckoning. Our blade of vengeance and our roar of fury will be etched into history! Follow me into Blackrock Spire! For the Alliance!"

Galen Trollbane raised his finely crafted Arathi sword and shouted until his voice almost broke. "For the Alliance!"

"For the Alliance!"

The roar echoed far across the gray wasteland. Jace spotted a blinding golden light suddenly flare at the front of the Silver Hand knights. That was Turalyon, no question — the man stood out even among a crowd of valiant paladins.

Turalyon charged onto the bridge with his warhammer held level. Behind him, Tirion Fordring and the other Silver Hand paladins followed not so much like they were charging with him but like they were racing him, just a hair slower off the mark.

More and more soldiers stepped onto the bridge. The arcane light supporting the structure grew brighter, and the Kirin Tor mages' chanting seemed to grow clearer, too.

"Such mighty arcane power... endless, Master," the imp muttered from Jace's shoulder. "Holding up that bridge is real work!"

"Prepare yourselves, Farstriders!"

Alleria gave the command in Thalassian. Unlike the soldiers streaming toward the bridge, her eyes were fixed on the heights of Blackrock Spire.

"Take me over there, Midnight," Jace said quietly.

Midnight gave no response but had already started moving. The black infernal steed cut around the Farstriders' flank, weaving back and forth through the advancing soldiers, heading straight for the Kirin Tor mages' formation. Jace had not even given a clear direction — he just looked that way, and the horse felt its rider's intent.

Another horn blast from Blackrock Spire cut through all the shouting. The heavy doors before the Silver Hand knights groaned and shifted. Not closed tighter — open a little wider.

"Hahaha, haha!"

With a few chillingly dull laughs, several spiked ogres in heavy armor squeezed through the gap in the doorway. These brutes were armed to the teeth with crude metal plates, carrying all sorts of warhammers, spears, and battle-axes. A couple even charged out holding chains of unknown purpose.

The Silver Hand knights showed no fear. The lead ogre took a glowing warhammer to the face — helmet, head, and all — and crumpled, falling off the bridge. The ones that followed looked big and fierce, but they did not fare much better against the Light-blessed weapons of the paladin leaders.

But things did not stay as smooth as they looked at first contact. Danath Trollbane cut through one ogre warrior's neck with his sword. The ogre's head and weapon tumbled into the gorge, but its body toppled forward, nearly pinning Trollbane beneath it. It landed solidly on the bridge, making the arcane-supported stonework tremble.

More and more ogres threw themselves into the crowd despite the slashing and bashing. Most were stabbed to death immediately by spears and swords thrusting from every direction, but their corpses piled up among the Alliance soldiers.

The situation worsened. The pressure on the arcane energy mounted, and even though the Blackrock Mountain bridge was wide as a street — enough for dozens to walk side by side — the piled ogre corpses blocked the following troops. Soldiers had to fight off the ogre charge while shoving the bodies over the edge.

But then, what Alleria and Nathanos had been dreading finally happened.

The firelight in Blackrock Spire's windows began to flicker. Soon, dense shadows appeared behind the smoke, looking exactly like the demonic bats the Nathrezim had turned into when they fled.

But that was just Jace's association — he had had the Nathrezim on his mind. These shadows were no bats. They were whelps!

The whelps' hoarse cries filled the canyon and the broken bridge in an instant. Some dove straight onto the bridge to tangle with the front-line soldiers, while others flew directly at the Kirin Tor mages on the slope.

The Silver Hand knights and soldiers could not break through the ogres' blockade, and the flying whelps intercepted them from above. Meanwhile, the Kirin Tor mages were fully occupied channeling the spell to hold the bridge and could hardly spare a hand to deal with these frenzied little monsters. Chaos erupted in every direction.

Fortunately, the Farstriders had prepared for this. Volleys of arcane arrows flew upward like inverted raindrops. On the Silver Hand's side, the Wizard Sanctum's mages launched strings of Arcane Missiles to help the soldiers on the bridge shake off the whelps' harassment. With the timely arrival of gryphon riders sweeping across the bridge, lightning tearing through the whelps' encirclement, the formation was finally stabilized.

Jace urged Midnight forward. A blue whelp with an arcane arrow through its chest fell at his feet. He glanced down and noticed — this one was blue.

Not just this one. Looking up, red ones made up most of the swarm, but blue and green ones were visible too, and even a few bronze ones now and then. They darted back and forth, screeching and spewing acid, frost, and flame. Individually they were not that strong, but in numbers they were deadly enough to disrupt an army assaulting the gates.

But look as he might, Jace could not find a single black one among all those whelps. He had a pretty good guess why. These were whelps the black dragons had snatched from all over the world for their chromatic dragon experiments, now thrown out as cannon fodder to fight the Alliance.

Most Alliance soldiers did not think dragons of other colors were much better than black ones, but the Farstrider elves knew better. They had noticed the problem too, and kept shooting while their expressions soured. But the whelps were already under the black dragons' control, leaving them no choice.

Another muffled horn sounded from inside Blackrock Mountain. But this time it was not the orcs — it came from Shadowforge City. Jace was very familiar with that sound.

"The Dark Iron dwarves kept their promise!" a gryphon rider shouted as he flew past overhead. "Hold the line, brothers! We're pinching these bastards!"

"He's right! Hold fast, Quel'dorei! Their counterattack won't last long!"

Alleria shouted in Thalassian as she drew her bow to full, firing a volley of arcane-glowing arrows that dropped several whelps into the gorge.

But the Blackrock Spire defenders had more than one trick. Jace had not even reached the Kirin Tor mages when he felt deep shadow energy gathering from somewhere in the tower, beyond what the windows showed.

Shapeless, writhing shadows oozed from cracked walls and windows, accompanied by increasingly clear scraping sounds and low, guttural growls.

"Watch the canyon!" "Demons incoming!"

The Farstriders were the first to spot these ambushers emerging from the dark. Right — the whelps had drawn the attention of Stormwind's mages and the Farstriders. Now it was time for the Horde's hammer to swing: waves of felhounds were circling toward the Kirin Tor mages' formation.

Blackhand had finally abandoned his prejudice against the Shadow Council, letting the orc summoners cut loose and call forth helpers from the nether.

Lucky for them, the Alliance was doing the same.

Jace raised Ulthalesh and muttered in Demonic: "Your turn, Lord Kar'lez."
