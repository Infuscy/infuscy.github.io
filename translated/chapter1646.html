Chapter 1646: She's On Our Side!

Whelps the size of calves fell from the sky, breaking their necks, tearing their wings, crying out in pain. On the ground, felhounds whose hearts had not been pierced still dragged their mangled bodies forward, until knights with lances came to deliver the final blow. Outside Blackrock Mountain's gate, the Horde had lost the ability to hold back the Alliance army. The orcs' and ogres' roars had already been drowned out by demonic howls. Under Kar'lez's oppressive presence, more and more of the surviving felhounds turned traitor, crawling back through cracks and windows to hunt their summoners instead. Jace downed a potion of Strong Mana. That last Hand of Gul'dan had consumed nearly half his energy, but judging by the results, it was well worth the cost. Back when he had tested Khadgar's revised Hand of Gul'dan in the canyon outside the Storm Altar, Jace had not used Ulthalesh — just two small soul shards. The effect had met expectations, but nothing surprising. This time, with endless demon souls trying to flee into the Twisting Nether at his disposal and Ulthalesh's amplification, the resulting power was incomparable to that test cast. It was not even in the same league as the Hands of Gul'dan Jace had summoned before. Not to mention the void demons that had leaped from the meteor's explosion. Those few Eredar, and the imps, Mo'arg servants, and felhounds under their command — Jace did not care whether they were genuinely loyal to him or Kar'lez's orders, whether driven by fear or greed for souls. Before them were countless mortals terrified by the shadow bombardment. Why not feast while the feasting was good? Fighting the high-ranking Eredar outside held no advantage. If they were killed, their souls might be eaten as well. Jace knew Khadgar was a rare magical genius, both in arcane arts and void magic. The fact that the Kirin Tor had sent a seventeen-year-old to Karazhan, where he had quickly earned Medivh's favor, was proof enough. No further demonstration needed. But after witnessing this spell, that talent felt far more tangible to Jace. "Move up, Jace!" Khadgar shouted, holding down his wildly flapping collar and cloak against the magic wind. "The bridge still needs a little more time to stabilize. Go help Turalyon and the others!" Jace nodded and looked back at the demon lord, who was driving felhounds and felguards up the high walls. "Destroy that section of wall, Kar'lez. Don't let the demons get another chance to harass the mages." "As you command, Master," Kar'lez replied in Demonic. Khadgar looked genuinely surprised to hear Kar'lez respond that way. He even cast another glance at Jace. He was not the only one. Jace himself found it unusual — the demon rarely acknowledged his master so openly in front of so many mortals and lesser demons. Before, even when he had obediently said "master," there had always been a hint of defiance and sarcasm in it. Seems a proper display of power was necessary even with demon allies bound by contract. Jace followed Varian's squad onto the bridge now held by arcane energy. Soldiers were moving the wounded and the dead off the bridge, and shoving piles of ogre and orc corpses and severed limbs over the edge into the deep gorge. By now, the stench of shadow energy from the Hand of Gul'dan's explosion, thick blood, and burnt meat filled the air, mixing into a foul cocktail that made everyone's skin crawl. Jace did not mind it much. Anyone who had been through the Temple of Atal'Hakkar had seen worse. But quite a few soldiers were already vomiting. He knew it was not just the disgusting smells that were getting to these seasoned troops — it was also the necrotic aura from the mounting pile of corpses. Jace had half-expected Blackrock Spire to gather the corpses and lob them at the Alliance army during the assault, triggering Corpse Explosion with necrotic energy to spread plague and chaos. That was the death knights' signature move in the Lordaeron campaigns. But it did not happen. Though restorative potions and the paladins' Holy Light had fended off much of the warlocks' and impish curses and corrosion, the black dragons had not used any notable necromancy since the assault began. Jace did not have time to think it through. A thicker wave of blood stench, sulfur smell, and the harsh clang of weapons hit him as he pressed forward. The ogre legion inside the gate had not given up. Their maddened war cries thundered deafeningly through the passage. Though they were being pushed back step by step and could not hold, they lost nothing in ferocity. In the distance, the massive chains securing the Ironcast Crypt in the center of the lava lake were covered with Shadowforge City soldiers. Clad in dark silver armor, carrying huge round shields and warhammers, these soldiers fought the orc troops diving from above along the heavy, swaying chains. The fighting had reached the point where neither side could swing their weapons or raise their shields properly. They rammed with helmets, shoved with their bodies, using every method to throw the enemy off balance and send them falling from the chains. Dark silhouettes dropped constantly. The lava lake below, glowing bright red, splashed and bubbled with each impact. The Alliance broke through another gate. They could almost see the entrance to Blackrock Spire ahead. A paladin suddenly fell, a javelin piercing his back. Nearby soldiers raised their shields toward the heights behind. Jace looked up and saw a group of forest trolls perched atop dwarf statues. Not just those. Higher up, on more statues and in the windows of abandoned Dark Iron buildings, Smolderthorn tribe javelin throwers were waiting everywhere, poised to strike from above once the Alliance had pushed this far. When they saw the Farstriders' arrows, the trolls laughed and ducked behind the statues. As soon as the rangers had to turn their attention back to the front line, they popped out again. Some Farstriders even climbed the statues to fight the trolls in close quarters. The demons Kar'lez had sent in through the outer walls had already taken some of those windows from the inside, driving the trolls away. But the javelin throwers were too scattered and mostly cut off from each other. Clearing them all out with demons alone was impractical. Most of the mages were still outside finishing the bridge repairs and could not get inside to help any time soon. Jace studied the high windows and statues. He worried that if he cast another Hand of Gul'dan here, the collapsing walls and statues might hit the sloping paths and bridges below. If they fell into the lava lake, the black dragons would not care about the orcs and ogres, but almost none of the Alliance soldiers besides the strongest paladins and mages would survive. As he was thinking this, a sharp dragon roar cut through all the shouting of ogres, orcs, and dwarves from deep within. A swift black shape streaked past above the Ironcast Crypt. Then a black dragon burst out of the lava lake, spewing a stream of burning magma at the windows where the trolls were perched. Black smoke blanketed that whole stretch of dwarven windows. Magma poured straight into the rooms with the force of the blast. The trolls' screams and wails told the story well enough. Alliance and Horde soldiers alike flinched as the black dragon swept overhead, craning their necks to look. Farstriders notched arrows and took aim. Troll javelin throwers at other windows raised their weapons. For a moment, this sudden arrival was everyone's target. "The black dragon is on our side!" Jace shouted at once. "She's not an enemy!" Hearing Jace, the Farstriders lowered their bows. Alleria looked over. "Is she the one..." "Are you serious, Jace?!" Varian yelled from up ahead, craning his neck over the sea of bobbing heads. The black dragon had already flown higher, diving toward another cluster of trolls at their windows. Amid the chaotic, vision-blurring light and shadow above the lava lake, Jace could hardly make out her features. But the dazzling golden ornament hanging from her horn was enough to confirm who she was. "Needleflame!" Jace shouted with all his might. The black dragon, who had just spat out a mouthful of magma, whipped her head around to look at him. Her amber eyes went wide. She rasped back, her voice hoarse, "Jace Sesso! I see you!" "The black dragon is one of us!" Turalyon shouted, having heard Needleflame's voice. "She came back from Draenor! Mark her face! Keep pushing! She'll help us deal with the trolls!" Realizing this black dragon was on their side, the Alliance soldiers' morale surged, their cheers growing louder. But Jace's heart tightened. Because he knew — if Grede had been willing to give that necklace to Needleflame, it meant the dwarf had accepted that his injuries could not carry him through the battle for Blackrock Spire.
