## Chapter 1647: Rendezvous at the Lava Lake

The black dragon circling above the lava lake, blasting molten fire at the glowing dwarven windows, reminded Jace of that one patrol unit flying outside Blackrock Spire in the game. Except this black dragon was on his side.

With only this one heavy aerial "unit" in play, the troll hunters who had been strutting about on the high ground, using the statues, walls, and chains for cover, suddenly lost their attitude. They tried several times to hurl javelins back at the young dragon. But Needleflame, beast that she was — even young, even without much combat experience — had scales reinforced by Neltharion's necklace. No forest troll javelin was punching through that.

With her clearing the way, the trolls could not keep up their resistance. Freed from the high-ground suppression, Nathanos Marris and his Farstriders quickly climbed the dwarf statues, took out the troll hunters hiding in the shadows from the dragon's bombardment, then scaled the cliff walls and iron chains to reach higher positions. They seized several vantage points, clearing the overhead threat for the paladins who were fighting the Horde vanguard head-on down on the bridge and slopes.

The momentum shifted in an instant. The ogres and orcs ahead could no longer hold back the Alliance's charge.

One after another, heavily armored Horde bloodguards wielding massive axes were hammered off the bridge. The biggest ogre champion, carrying a long-handled warhammer made from a whelp's skull, had half his face smashed in by Tirion Fordring's jumping horizontal strike. He tumbled screaming between stone pillars and cliff gaps before rolling into the lava lake.

The strongest fighters fell one by one. The Horde line collapsed like paper, retreating toward Blackrock Spire.

"Taste this!" An orc warrior howled as he dove from above, bringing his axe down on a paladin's shield with all his weight behind it. The shield cracked on impact. But before the orc could swing again, Jace hit him in the hip and leg with a Shadow Bolt. His lower limbs rotted so fast the bones could not hold him, and he soon tumbled over the edge.

Even with defeat clear, the Blackrock orcs fought on like madmen. It reminded Jace of the Shadow Council members stationed in Auchindoun.

But those cultists worshipped the Burning Legion, believing that one day the demon lords would descend on Auchindoun to lead their flesh and souls into the Twisting Nether for an eternal crusade. What about the orcs here?

They had abandoned Gul'dan. They had abandoned the Burning Legion. Grim Batol had fallen, and neither the Dragonmaw nor the Shattered Hand clans had shown any intention of joining Blackrock Spire. The Dark Portal was closed, meaning Ner'zhul and Kargath Bladefist had abandoned them too.

Could Rend Blackhand truly make these orcs feel they belonged to the true Horde, the way his father Blackhand the Warchief had? Or had they simply accepted being the black dragons' servants, worshipping Nefarian as a god?

A stream of multicolored magical arrows arced up like a rainbow from below, striking the high wall, dropping an orc warlock who was casting shadow spells at the Farstriders, and nailing several troll hunters as well.

Jace looked over. Vereesa was following Needleflame's flight with her gaze. The Dark Iron dwarves beside her had already seized the iron chain and were cutting down from above. The ogre and orc forces ahead were caught in a pincer, and without their aerial support, there was no way they could turn the battle around.

Another horn sounded from deep within Blackrock Spire. An orcish horn. At this signal, the orcs and ogres finally decided to withdraw into the tower. So these fighters were not entirely mindless berserkers after all.

The Horde retreated at last. Alliance soldiers raised their swords and shields and cheered. Even many Dark Iron dwarves were hopping and jumping in their heavy armor and shields with joy.

"Turalyon! And Danath, my old friend!"

Kurdran Wildhammer slid down from above, high-fived Turalyon, then gripped Danath Trollbane's arm tightly. He looked around. "Where's the conjurer?!"

"The conjurer just clocked out, High Thane Kurdran!"

Khadgar caught up from behind. Kurdran pointed at the mountain king behind him, Muradin Bronzebeard. "Ask him. I'm not bragging — back in Molten Core, I thought I'd never see you lot again. All thanks to this dark wizard beside you!"

"He's not bragging, Master Khadgar," Muradin said, his golden beard swaying. "That shadow magic meteor of his stopped Ragnaros the Firelord's hammer dead in its tracks. No joke."

"That shadow magic meteor of his was scarier than the Firelord's hammer as far as I'm concerned, everyone," Danath Trollbane said. "If you'd seen it with your own eyes, you'd know what I mean."

"Not a word of exaggeration," Khadgar murmured.

"Jace!" Vereesa leaped down from a stone pillar, landing perfectly on a dwarf statue's boot, then jumped again and threw her arms around the warlock's neck.

"I smelled a fierce shadowfire. I knew it was you — you only use magic of that level in the most dangerous situations. I was worried. Seeing you safe..."

"I'm fine, just spent. A few mana potions and I'll be right as rain," Jace said. Vereesa relaxed, though she still frowned as if shaken. Jace asked, "Where's Grede?"

"He's still in the back. He only has one job now — hold onto that warhammer." Vereesa finally smiled. "For him, even carrying that hammer is a serious task right now. Don't expect him to move fast."

"As long as he's okay." Jace felt a little relieved.

"Master Vieran said he might need a few months to recover. That's fine — he'll definitely be back in shape by the time we head to Kalimdor..."

"My little moon!" Alleria jumped down from above. Vereesa spun around at the shout, beaming, and the two sisters embraced and spun around several times.

"You've grown taller. The power of love truly is more mysterious than the deepest alchemy."

"Don't start with that, sister!"

"Alleria!" "Kurdran!"

"Well, we're all together again! And right beneath Blackrock Spire."

"That's right. Beneath Blackrock Spire."

The Sons of Lothar gathered. Up above, the black dragon swirled into a coil of black smoke and landed nearby. Needleflame burst out of it and sprinted to Jace, throwing herself into his arms.

"You said you'd be back soon."

"Is a few weeks slow for a dragon?" Jace asked.

"Tell me that when you've lived a thousand years, Sesso!" Needleflame grumbled quietly.

"I've lived a thousand years and I still don't think it's fast, Jace." Sora walked over as well. "I'm really glad to see you safe. For some reason, I couldn't shake the worry while you were away. Even though I knew you were just going back to Stormwind, not some dangerous place..."

It had actually been pretty rough, Jace thought. But he just nodded. The things he had seen and felt on that journey were not easily summed up.

"King Varian!" Khadgar turned to the approaching king. "The bridge has been fully rebuilt. Supplies will be following shortly. I've arranged for my Kirin Tor colleagues to guard the bridge in case the Horde tries to cut off our retreat."

"Thank the Dalaran for your help, Master Khadgar. You've worked hard," Varian said.

"It is our duty, Your Majesty," said Khadgar.

"King Varian Wrynn." Kurdran Wildhammer stepped forward and saluted. "You look more and more like your father, King Barathen!"

"That was my grandfather, High Thane Kurdran. But I appreciate the kind words." Varian sheathed his sword and shook the dwarf's hand with a smile.

Kurdran chuckled awkwardly. Muradin also came over to greet Varian. But soon, everyone's attention turned to the last figure approaching — a Dark Iron dwarf.

"Varian Wrynn." Dagran Thaurissan walked over, his heavy staff stained with orc blood. "I believe this is our first meeting."

"It is indeed, Your Majesty Dagran." Varian said. "Though it's our first, we've proven that the Alliance and Shadowforge City can coexist and cooperate."

"I never thought I'd fight alongside Dark Iron dwarves. I imagine my cousin and father would feel the same," Galen Trollbane said. "But I'll remember this day, Thaurissan."

"Put it aside for now, human. Live long enough, and you'll see everything." Dagran Thaurissan's gaze swept over the Sons of Lothar and the Alliance leaders, finally landing on Jace. "Don't forget that the reason we stand here today is also thanks to a demon summoner and shadow mage's efforts."

"That's true," Turalyon nodded.

"The black dragon is still hiding in that spire." The Dark Iron emperor raised his scepter toward the heights above. "So now is not the time to turn on each other."
