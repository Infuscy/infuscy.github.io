Chapter 1648: Volcanic Fissure

With a series of heavy clangs, Blackrock Spire's iron gates slammed down one after another, mercilessly trapping dozens of ogres and orc soldiers who had not made it back inside.

They scrambled to climb the cracked walls around the windows, but it was nothing more than desperate struggle.

The Farstriders' arrows picked them off one by one. But even without the Alliance's interference, Jace doubted those heavy brutes could scale the smooth black stone very high.

Though called a spire, Blackrock Spire was far more than a watchtower. It was essentially a fortress carved from Blackrock Mountain itself to protect Shadowforge City below.

It was a full military citadel. Otherwise, Blackhand could never have held thousands of orcs and ogres here to face off against the Dark Iron clans of Shadowforge City for so long.

Outside, Kar'lez led the demons in pounding Blackrock Spire's outer walls. The sounds that reached the inside were reduced to dull rumbles and demonic shrieks that seemed to come from miles away.

The Alliance commanders knew the demon could not breach the outer walls quickly on his own. Nor could he and his demon pack establish a channel big enough for a thousand elite vanguard to charge through and open the battle.

Dragging things out here too long meant more than just shadow corrosion from dark magic. The scorching heat inside the volcano was a severe trial even for ordinary soldiers — even for the Silver Hand paladins.

"The Kirin Tor can destroy the gates, King Varian," Khadgar said. "The material strength is not an issue for us. The enchantment runes are just thick mana with simple protective incantations. All we need is enough mana and time."

"No need to waste the Kirin Tor's time or mana." Dagran Thaurissan raised his hand. "If it's a Blackrock Spire problem, let the spire's true master handle it."

"You know how to break the enchantment, Your Majesty?" Khadgar asked.

"The orc warlocks rewrote it a decade ago, mage," Thaurissan said. "But we have a simpler and more professional way to dismantle Blackrock Spire's defenses. And we've been preparing for a while."

On the slope leading to the Burning Steppes, Iron Fury soldiers squeezed aside to make way. More and more soldiers began chanting a dwarven battle cry in unison.

Jace's dwarven was good enough to catch that it had something to do with dragons.

"Behold, the Dragon Breath Cannon!" Thaurissan announced. "Siege variant!"

Soon, a massive red dragon's head emerged from among the dwarves, pushed forward by soldiers chanting in coordinated, powerful rhythm as it crept toward Blackrock Spire's entrance.

From a distance, the cannon looked exactly like the front half of an adult red dragon. Thaurissan proudly ordered the Farstriders and mages to watch the statues and windows nearby, ensuring no stray orc warlock or troll headhunter could ambush and damage his cannon.

It was a bit theatrical — the Farstriders on the high ground had pretty much lowered their weapons and were just watching the show. The remaining troops had fled back inside the Spire. What danger was left?

"Aim!"

The Iron Fury general shouted as the cannon's barrel slowly adjusted.

"May we fire, Your Majesty?" "Fire!"

The Dark Iron emperor bellowed. Jace did not see any dwarf running up with a torch. He was wondering how the cannon was supposed to fire when a fire elemental came wobbling up from behind at full speed and dove straight into the red dragon's rear!

The whole "red dragon" body flared bright red. Then its massive mouth let out a strange roar and belched a blinding fireball. The fireball slammed into Blackrock Spire's gate with a blast that shook the whole mountain. The Farstriders grabbed onto chains and boulders for balance. The Spire's iron door buckled, and the magic runes on it flickered weakly.

Curses in orcish and ogre shouts began echoing from inside. Dagran Thaurissan yelled excitedly, "Again! Again! We've got plenty of fire elementals!"

Jace and his companions did not have time to watch the Dragon Breath Cannon peel away Blackrock Spire's front defenses layer by layer. He had no intention of joining the frontal assault — the terrain inside was too tight.

In open ground, a well-placed Hand of Gul'dan that caught the Horde and their demons could still give the front-line soldiers a slightly guilty boost. Trying the same trick again here was harder to predict — the positive and negative effects were anyone's guess.

Even without the news from Lordaeron, he planned to heed Mathias Shaw's advice: avoid making a flashy display in front of the entire Alliance army. Especially not now.

While the Alliance was breaking into Blackrock Mountain and securing the Ironcast Crypt, Morofil seemed to have contacted Vaelastrasz. Jace could feel the succubus's response through the soul link. He wondered if Vaelastrasz had a more hidden way into Blackrock Spire.

As a green dragon, Sora would be more useful supporting the main battlefield than following Jace's group through the side passages. So Jace took Vereesa and Needleflame — both better at infiltration and ambush — and followed Morofil's soul trace to meet Vaelastrasz.

Passing a balcony corridor, he glanced back through a window at the gathering Alliance and Shadowforge City forces below. It looked familiar.

He looked at the passage to his left, which led into Blackrock Spire but was blocked by a rune-sealed iron door. Then he remembered where this was.

This seemed to be the entrance to Blackrock Spire from the game. And just like in the game, without the right lockpicking method, that narrow passage was impassable.

The Farstriders had checked other routes too. Either blocked by collapsed stone or sealed by early-dropped magic doors.

For some reason, Blackrock Spire was heavily fortified even against Shadowforge City inside the mountain. Combined with Thaurissan's claim that their Dragon Breath Cannon was "professional" at breaching the Spire's gates, it was easy to guess that the Dark Iron dwarves and the Spire had fought more than a few civil wars before.

But if Morofil could find Vaelastrasz, that meant the defenses were not airtight.

Kar'lez's felhounds led the way ahead, guiding them past several passages the demons had already destroyed. After rounding a corner, they came to a sudden chasm.

The corridor had been completely torn apart by seismic activity. Below was a river of lava, nothing but a golden-red thread in the black abyss. This break was likely caused by the collapse of Molten Core — just like the cracks visible on the Spire's exterior.

The other side of the corridor was too far even for Jace to jump across at full strength.

He turned around, weighing whether to risk the jump or have Needleflame carry them, when a shout came from above.

"You three! Look up!"

Jace looked up. Several levels above, at another broken corridor section, a red-robed orc was waving down at them.

"That passage down there won't get you into Blackrock Spire. The Shadow Council warlocks have abandoned the outer area and sealed it from the inside. I don't doubt you could break through by force, but it'd cost you a lot of time!"

"And this is the shortcut you found for the Alliance?" Jace pointed at the lava below.

"There's no way the whole Alliance can come through here. But a few Alliance members? Not that hard." The red dragon called down. "Can you get up here? I can't spread my wings in this crevice!"

Jace turned to the young black dragon. "If you turn into a dragon, fly us across, and turn back — can you still keep going after two transformations, Needleflame?"

"Barely. I just turned back." Needleflame sighed. "Unless I take you up there and you carry me the rest of the way."

"So I'd have to carry you while I fight your uncle?"

"I can put in a good word for you then." Needleflame smiled. "If he doesn't see you, maybe he'll go easy."

Before Jace could finish that thought, he felt something grab him from behind. Needleflame yelped too. A violent whirlwind swept the black smoke from the crevice. Jace felt a rare chill in the oppressive heat of Blackrock Mountain. When he opened his eyes, he was already beside the orc-formed red dragon.

"You've met the Moon Goddess's messenger, Vereesa Windrunner." Vaelastrasz's face showed a hint of surprise. "That is Aviana's magic."

A flurry of magical feathers scattered from behind Vereesa and faded away. The ranger nodded. "She is something of a teacher to me."

"Perfect." Vaelastrasz rubbed his hands together. "Follow me. The rest of us will be enough. And you, Needleflame — we need to free the whelps and young dragons the black dragons have taken from all over the world. Liberate them from Nefarian's control, and remove a troublesome enemy from your assault."
