Chapter 165: Our Parents

A light rain was falling that evening, the kind that came down in a steady drizzle. The occasional passerby hurried along with their coat pulled tight and their hat braced against the weather.

Jace stood outside Dushan's door, unable to let go of the memories of the past few days with his parents.

Maybe he was far from independent — maybe he was still the kid his parents protected — and yet here he was, constantly talking big about wanting to protect them.

The door suddenly opened. Dushan stood in the doorway and saw Jace. "When did you get back? Why are you standing out in the rain? Your meeting's tomorrow afternoon — you don't want to get sick right now, of all times, do you?"

Jace nodded and went inside, peeled off his wet clothes, and changed into a dry shirt, but his mind was still elsewhere.

"I've got something urgent to discuss with the Kirin Tor masters about tomorrow's arrangements, Jace." Dushan shook out his large hat. "By the way — how's the family?"

"They're well, master. Thanks for asking."

"These days, the steadiest place is where you are. The walls of the Lordaeron capital are even thicker than our castle. Nothing to worry about there. How's your recovery?"

"Basically fine now, master," Jace said.

"Youth is a fine thing," Dushan said with a shake of his head. "If I'd been scorched by a black dragon's fire, all I could hope for would be a quick death. Well — see you this evening."

Not long after Dushan left, the door started pounding again.

Jace assumed his teacher had forgotten something, but when he opened it, there stood a short figure in a dark-gray cloak.

The stranger brushed the rainwater off his black beard and looked up at him.

"I figured you'd be back tonight, Jace." Grede flipped back his hood, beaded with rain. "If I still couldn't find you tonight, I was going to organize a team to search those murloc huts on the islands and see if we could find your arms and legs or something."

"And you'd find me sitting on the island, surrounded by murloc arms and legs," Jace said.

"Fair point. You are my apprentice, after all — you'd have a healthy appetite." Grede stepped inside, lifted off his cloak, and hung it on Dushan's coat rack.

He hopped up onto a table and sat. "Got any plans for tonight? Are you going to prep for tomorrow's meeting, or are you coming out to have a drink with me?"

"Anything new on the menu?" Jace asked.

"Plenty. We only tried two kinds the other day," Grede said. "And then that elf had to go and ruin the mood. No fun at all. Let's go again tonight... Actually, is your meeting in the morning or the afternoon?"

"Afternoon," Jace said.

"Perfect. We'll find something with a real kick, drink enough to be wasted till noon, sober up, and head straight to the meeting. How's that sound?"

"Sure enough." The two of them braved the rain and headed to the Azure Encounter. Despite the bad weather and the fact that it was already past nine, there were still a few people in the tavern.

Grede ordered two cups of lemon wine, and they found a corner to sit in.

Jace drank and tried to think of something to start the conversation with, but in his heart he felt like no matter what topic he picked, Grede definitely wouldn't be interested, and he himself didn't have much enthusiasm for talking about anything either.

Even that black dragon in Southshore — the one named Ma-something-la — Grede had probably already blown it up into a tall tale for those Southshore soldiers a dozen times over. What would be left to say if he talked about it with Jace again?

"Jace." Grede spoke first. "I found some short-term work over where I'm staying, helping out another dwarf. He runs a Dun Morogh wine shop here. Winter Veil's coming and it gets too crowded, so he needs extra hands watching the place. Seventh to the thirteenth, ten silvers. I figured it's a good deal."

"You're working for a Bronzebeard?"

"He's a Wildhammer dwarf."

"Ah. So... your legs are doing okay?"

"I reckon they're about fine. That job just has you standing around during the afternoon and evening rush, making sure drunk idiots don't wreck his stuff. You barely have to move. Besides, you can't have me sitting idle the whole time, can you?"

"But shouldn't you be working right now, then?"

"I've been standing there all afternoon already. Taking a little time off's not too much to ask, is it? I'm not the only one watching the place."

"Ah, and after the thirteenth?"

"Once the dwarves all head back to their hometowns it won't be so hectic. Come to think of it, I never expected Dun Brahder to have so many dwarves living there — I always thought it was just a small village..."

"Maybe it's because of the soldiers massing here during the war," Jace replied absently.

Grede looked Jace in the eye, took a swig of wine, and swished it around his mouth. "I'm guessing your parents treated you pretty well."

"Why bring that up all of a sudden?" Jace raised his eyes.

"If they'd been terrible people, you wouldn't be like this. You'd be buzzing with the thrill of someone who just broke out of prison." Grede laughed another bout, then seeing the complicated look spreading across Jace's face, asked, "Or are you the type who thinks 'no matter how my parents treat me, they're the greatest people in the world'?"

"No, you guessed right on the money," Jace said.

"Having parents who treat you well ought to be something to be happy about. But you fools who don't know how good you've got it mope around like poets, listless and gloomy." Grede rested his chin on his hand. "What, have you been learning too much Shadow magic?"

"Watch your mouth — this is Dalaran!" Jace hissed.

"I know, I know..." The dwarf clinked his cup against Jace's. Jace went to drink, glanced into his cup, and found it nearly empty already.

"We should order another round of lemon wine. It's really good," Jace said.

"I'll drink to that." Grede looked up and bellowed, "Two more of the same! Don't make me come up there and order myself, you conjurers... arrogant as the day is long."

His voice trailed off by the end like a plane going into a nosedive. Jace felt like after just that one large cup, Grede was already starting to show signs of being drunk.

Though he wasn't much better himself. He glanced at the bar and found himself genuinely wishing that half-poured cup of lemon wine would just fly straight into his face, the way a gnome would control that box with the faerie dragon feather inside.

No matter how he tried to snap out of it, he couldn't shake the bizarre thought from his head.

Grede said, "Don't go quiet on me. Cheer up!"

"I'm just unhappy about leaving my parents, so what? You can laugh at me for not being grown up yet," Jace said.

"I'm not saying you're not grown up, Jace." Grede took the cup the bartender brought over and downed a huge gulp. "If I had a family that cared about me, I wouldn't want to leave Aerie Peak either. What's so great about running around out there? The world beyond is an ugly, messy place."

"Weren't your parents good to you?" Jace asked.

"You sure you want to steer the conversation here, Jace?" Grede gave him a grin like he was settling in to watch a show. "You're not in a great mood either. You trying to make things even more awkward?"

"You're the one who brought it up in the first place. And I rarely hear you mention your parents." Jace said. "Your dad was a raptor hunter, I know that. That's about it."

"Because they're all dead. There's no new story to tell," Grede said.
