## Chapter 1650: Through Hordemar

Vaelastrasz led Jace, Vereesa, and Needleflame to the end of the passage. It was a tower that had collapsed who knew how many years ago — or rather, a stairwell. It might have already been like this before the Dark Portal even opened.

Morofil was waiting by a broken window nearby. When Vereesa saw her, she said, "Glad to see you're all right, Morofil."

The succubus offered a polite smile. Even without deliberately sensing Vereesa's emotions, Jace could tell how hollow that greeting was.

"Fill Khadgar in on our situation, Morofil," Jace said. "You need to be our liaison with the Alliance attacking the main gate. Tell them our plan — the whelps and young dragons, the shadow egg. And most importantly, find Grede among the Dark Iron dwarves. Have him bring the dragon blood pendant to Varian."

"I am certainly capable of that task. But I feel I could do far more than play messenger, Master." Morofil glanced at the ranger. "Or do you two need some private time after your reunion?"

"Honestly, I don't think your presence would affect their alone time at all, Sayaad!" Sazeno jeered.

"Then why not send him?" Morofil pointed at the imp.

"Simple — I can't fly!" The imp said smugly. "Look at all those chasms between here and there. One look down and I'd feel my homeland beckoning!"

"That's true." Vereesa put on a sweet smile. "You're the only one who can do this, Morofil. Invisibility, stealth, and best of all, you come with your own wings."

"With everyone showering me with such unexpected praise, I can hardly refuse..." Morofil said. "As you wish, darling."

The demon blew Jace a kiss, then dissolved into a mist and vanished behind them. Vereesa rolled her eyes and quickened her pace.

Vaelastrasz cleared his throat and muttered to Jace, "This sort of thing isn't easy even for Queen Alexstrasza, especially when you know that Sayaad demons and Quel'dorei are both more prone to jealousy than we are."

Jace studied the aged stonework of the spiral stairs and muttered back, "Maybe things would be easier if I were six hundred feet long."

"Trust me, it's not that easy." Vaelastrasz lowered his voice further. "And don't repeat that conversation to the Queen."

"I know." Jace replied in barely a breath.

"And don't tell Prince Korialstrasz either," the red dragon added.

"I know," Needleflame whispered from behind.

"When did you sneak up?!" The red dragon jumped.

Another heavy boom — the Dragon Breath Cannon again, probably. The tremor traveled all the way up to them. The whole Spire wouldn't collapse from the continuous pounding, but no one knew how long this little tower inside would hold. Everyone grabbed the wall and waited.

Once the orcish cursing outside had died down, Vereesa moved to the bottom and peered through the dark doorway. She did not step out immediately.

"What's ahead?"

Jace and Needleflame came down the stairs to join her. The ranger stopped them both and said to Vaelastrasz, who had followed, "There are trolls ahead. We need to be careful."

"Over there." Vaelastrasz pointed at a passage across the street. "We can go through that building to the other side and bypass the Smolderthorn tribe's camp."

Vereesa nodded and slipped across first. The others hurried after her, ducking through the doorway. Inside was what seemed to be a Dark Iron dwarven warehouse that the orcs and trolls had not gotten around to using yet. Broken and half-open crates were scattered everywhere. The Dark Iron dwarves had clearly abandoned the tower in a hurry.

Everyone knew trolls were natural hunters, and forest trolls were hunters among hunters. Even their witch doctors and mystics had hunting instincts far stronger than the average human tracker. So they moved carefully across the warehouse, avoiding any debris, making for the door on the other side. They were here for a precision strike on that mysterious shadow egg, not to pick a fight with the whole of Hordemar.

But even with all this caution, when the ranger reached the door and was about to go out, she frowned. She turned back and signaled for silence, then listened intently. Jace could faintly hear something overhead, but could not tell what it was.

Vereesa looked back and mouthed, "They've noticed us. Stay close." Everyone nodded.

"Invisibility potion?" Jace mouthed.

Vereesa wagged her finger. She pulled a small bottle from her pack, uncorked it, and sprinkled a little pale yellow powder on Jace, Vaelastrasz, and Needleflame, then some on herself.

The powder had a faint, unpleasant smell. Jace found it familiar but could not place it.

She put the bottle away, held her breath, kept her eyes on the ceiling, nodded through a slow count, then suddenly dashed out the door. Jace and the two dragons followed immediately.

They kept their steps as quiet as possible, crossing a dark tunnel to reach a pile of rubble. The ranger stopped short and held out her arm. The three of them froze in the shadow of a high wall like nails driven into the ground.

Soon, Jace noticed where they had come from. Several troll hunters hurried across the rooftops, scanning the streets below as they ran, and soon disappeared.

Once they were gone, Vereesa let out a breath. Vaelastrasz pointed in Needleflame's direction, and they all moved off that way quickly.

"Troll halitosis," the red dragon said as they walked. "That's what gave us away, Lady Vereesa."

"That's right." Vereesa nodded with some pride. Jace tugged at his collar and said nothing, just kept walking.

Needleflame, at the front, had frozen. She was staring at something in the dark ahead, mixing draconic and Common in a way that made neither language coherent.

Jace followed her gaze. Between the dark walls and ruined rubble, there were white, web-like things everywhere. The indistinct orc and ogre shouts from further away seemed to mix with some scratching sound that made the skin crawl.

Spiderwebs. Those were spiderwebs. No wonder Needleflame was terrified.

Back in Shadowforge City, Jace had heard Dark Iron dwarves lament more than once that when the orcs and ogres had taken Blackrock Spire, they had also taken their most precious food source — the main source of spider eggs. This must be the place they were talking about. The Smolderweb spiders' lair.

"Do we have to go through here?" Needleflame asked.

"It's the most direct route to where Vim'salak guards the shadow egg," Vaelastrasz said. "Neither orcs nor trolls want to set foot here. There's no safer or easier passage."

"But there must be a reason the orcs and trolls don't come here, right?" Vereesa asked.

"Aren't you supposed to be good at talking to beasts, Vereesa?" Jace asked.

"Spiders?" Vereesa said quietly. "Maybe. I'm not sure. Frogs were hard enough for me, Jace. Otherwise we wouldn't have had so much trouble finding the silithid hive in Kalimdor."

"Don't worry. I have a decent relationship with the spiders here." Vaelastrasz stepped forward. "They've helped me out during my long stay. Follow me."

Jace was a little uneasy, but the red dragon was a master of life. No reason to doubt his confidence.

And so the red dragon led them over the rubble into a dark space formed by a collapsed wall propped up against the mountainside. Everywhere was the stench of rot. Fresh webs, old webs, dusty webs — layer upon layer, thicker and thicker. The scratching of insect legs on stone grew more frequent from all directions.

Everyone knew that countless hunters could be lurking in the darkness, ready to strike. But they could not see a single trace of them. The masters of this place had chosen to avoid the intruders. They were giving Vaelastrasz face.

Still, it was enough to make Needleflame's eyes dart around wildly. Even her little black face had gone pale.

"So even a Blackwing dragon is afraid of something?" The red dragon chuckled. His attitude was not at all befitting someone addressing a junior a thousand years his junior. They really seized every chance to mock black dragons, even the little ones.

"Well, back in Draenor..."

Before Jace could finish, Needleflame suddenly jumped up, clamped her hand over his mouth, and bared her teeth. "Don't you dare! Don't you dare!"

"Mmph mmph... fine, fine..."
