## Chapter 1653: Taking the Lower Spire

If they had not destroyed that evil broodmother's egg, Jace would never have guessed the Horde had hidden so many whelps in Blackrock Spire's lower levels.

When they emerged from Vim'salak's chamber, enraged whelps were flying everywhere. These little creatures posed no real threat to a young dragon like Needleflame, but they were not something an ordinary Blackrock orc could handle easily. The dim tower was filled with sharp whelp screeches, orcish counter-roars, and panicked shouts.

Some whelps desperately bit at the mortals who tried to enslave them. Others seized any chance to escape, flying around looking for cracks or windows to flee Blackrock Mountain.

Even so, these whelps would attack anyone trying to stop them. The Bloodaxe orcs rushing to the southern high wall found themselves caught between demons seeping in from every side and whelps erupting from behind. Orcs kept tumbling from the southeastern black cliffs, smashed to pulp on impact.

Just as Vaelastrasz had said, even these freshly hatched whelps, knowing nothing, did not need to actively help the Alliance attack the Horde. As long as they refused to fight the Alliance and caused enough chaos inside the Spire, they had already done plenty.

Jace stood on a black tower, looking down at the building across the way where the ogre "rulers" were hiding. He chanted in Elemental, calling on the fire for help.

With the protection runes destroyed, the flames within Blackrock Mountain were no longer suppressed by Ragnaros. They eagerly answered their "savior's" call, turning the ogre-occupied building into a sea of fire in an instant.

The ogres patrolling the nearby bridge had no idea what was happening before they were surrounded by flames. Some ran in every direction, trailing black smoke and open flame. Others jumped straight into the abyss below. Ogre columns still marching toward Blackrock Spire's gate on orders from their warlord turned back at their comrades' agonized screams.

Jace was not done. He infused the firestorm with Shadowflame. The flames shifted toward violet-red, the burnt smell mingling with increasing stench. The shouts were now mixed with cries and confused questions.

Jace knew why. Many ogres, even forest trolls, must have mistaken this fire for a black dragon's breath from somewhere above. Being slaughtered by their own masters who dwelled on high was far more damaging to morale. And that was exactly what Jace had intended.

Before long, an ogre in black chainmail armor, carrying a black spiked mace, stumbled out of the tower in panic. He looked around frantically, shouting in orcish, apparently calling for his subordinates and allies to help. But the shadowfire had cornered him. No one could cross Jace's flames to help him.

"That's King Omokk," Vaelastrasz pointed at the ogre.

"I see him." Vereesa nodded from above and drew her bow to full. A faint streak of colored light disappeared into the flickering violet-red glow. The ogre grunted, clutched his chest, leaned against the wall for a breath, then his legs gave out, and he tumbled off the stone platform into the abyss.

"King Omokk is dead! King Omokk is dead!"

Cries came from the bridge across the way. An ogre was hanging from a windowsill with both hands, having witnessed the ogre king's fall. Thick black smoke from the shadowfire billowed out of the window behind him. He could probably hold on for a while, even climb back, but climbing back would only mean death.

Vereesa raised her bow to end his suffering. Jace raised his hand. "Wait."

"King Omokk is dead! Oh my god! King Omokk is dead!"

The ogre kept screaming, surrounded by purple-tinged smoke. He had no attention to spare for the figures on the roof.

"What?! King Omokk is dead?!"

Some ogre in the distance shouted. Word spread quickly. Throughout the tower, the streets, everywhere, ogres were yelling "King Omokk is dead!"

For ogres, a king's death was a major event. It meant either finding the powerful warrior who killed the king and making him the new king, or holding a tournament to elect a new king and decide the clan's next move.

But neither option was viable now. Better to just run.

The ogres who had been laughing and charging to the front line to meet the Alliance soldiers turned tail the moment they heard the king was dead. Suddenly, the roads and bridges below were full of white, fat bodies scrambling away.

Seeing the ogres flee, the forest trolls hesitated. The Bloodaxe and Scarshield legions were falling back step by step. The Alliance had breached the tower. The black dragons in the upper Spire ignored what was happening below. The dragonkin who ruled Hordemar had gone silent. The whelps had gone mad. And now the Spirestone ogres — the backbone of the front line — had stopped fighting. What was there left to hold?

These followers of Zul'jin, left behind in the tower, had little loyalty to the "new" Horde of Blackrock Spire anyway. With things this bad, there was no reason to die defending Rend Blackhand's legacy.

At the farthest slope, golden-glowing paladins led a charge of blue-cloaked Stormwind soldiers into the fortress. Trolls and ogres fled like a tide toward the deepest parts of the Spire and the broken walls. Only a few scattered orc war parties tried to hold, but they were quickly crushed under the Alliance's advance.

The ogre hanging from the windowsill finally lost his grip and fell from the tower. Vereesa slowly lowered her bow.

"So what was the source of the shadow egg's power?" she asked.

"Is it... my uncle?" Needleflame asked from behind, crouching.

"I don't think so," Vaelastrasz said. "Jace must know how unique the power inside that shadow egg was. That could not have come from Nefarian. And if he truly commanded shadow power that strong, he would have taken direct control of all the whelps' wills himself, rather than using his mother's egg and having dragonkin mages channel his shadow into it to affect the whelps. Don't you think that second method is a bit too roundabout?"

Jace looked at the orc and ogre corpses strewn across the streets, slopes, and collapsed walls. "It means he can't fully control the source of that shadow power."

"Possibly," Vaelastrasz said. "He may have found something... here in Blackrock Spire."

Jace's mind churned. He searched his memory of Blackrock Spire, Blackwing Lair, even Onyxia and Deathwing's loot tables for some special source of shadow. A weapon? An artifact? Nothing seemed to fit. Either the timeline was wrong, or the power level did not match.

For example, the trinket worshipped by every magic-user — Neltharion's Tear. Jace had no doubt it would be a terrifying magical artifact here too.

But if that item could exert this much influence, and Nefarian controlled it in the game's timeline too, why had it never produced similar effects?

Far off, the ogre tower was burning itself out. Paladins walked through the black smoke, searching for surviving Horde soldiers who had not managed to flee.

"And Princess Onyxia?" Needleflame asked quietly. "She and Prince Nefarian always got along well, didn't they? Why did they suddenly become enemies?"

"Honestly, I'm surprised too." Vaelastrasz said. "Those two have always been partners in crime, serving Deathwing. It's true there hasn't been word of Onyxia in Blackrock Spire for a long time. But I never heard Nefarian order his dragonkin to attack Onyxia and her servants, or even her blood-related sisters. This might even mean their sister, Princess Nyxiandra, has been shut out of Blackrock Spire as well."

"Whatever the reason," Vereesa said, "it at least means our real enemy is down to just one. Victor Nefarius. His mother and his sisters have all abandoned him."

"Not a bad point," the red dragon said. "He's all alone now."

Gryphon riders were already sweeping into the skies above Hordemar. The Alliance soldiers' cheers drew closer and closer. Lower Blackrock Spire had fallen. The remaining orcs would not hold out much longer.
