Chapter 1654: The Spire's Ace

Though Hordemar was the lower section of Blackrock Spire, it was already a small town that had collapsed countless times and been remodeled by Dark Iron dwarves, orcs, dragonkin, and trolls in turn.

The Horde defenders had collapsed like a landslide, but who knew how many malicious stragglers still lurked in every nook and cranny. To clear the way for the assault on the upper and top levels, paladins and rangers had to search every room, crevice, and cellar, hunting down hidden enemies while also seeking clues and advantages for the coming offensive.

For fleeing Horde members — whelps, goblins, even gnolls and kobolds who had followed the orcs and settled into the Spire — the Alliance let them go. They would not waste energy on deserters.

As for the rest, the Alliance was killing every last one.

It had been ten years since the Orc War ended. Most orcs who doubted the Blackhand's new Horde had left the Spire for the north long ago — like the orc warlock Jace had met at the Storm Altar, the shaman at Lake Everstill, and the orc warrior Tirion Fordring had encountered outside Hearthglen.

Some had joined Warsong Chieftain Grommash Hellscream. Others had gone to Alterac Valley. Still others wandered alone, like the remnants of the Bleeding Hollow and Shattered Hand, mostly just circling internment camps trying to free orcs they knew.

After the fever of demonic power had broken and they realized the orcs had only been deceived and used as cannon fodder on Azeroth, the only ones still insisting on rebuilding the Horde to conquer the Seven Kingdoms and Quel'Thalas were Blackhand's fanatical followers or those who had chosen to worship the black dragons.

As for the ogres — needless to say. Apart from a few two-headed ogres who had taken their dim-witted friends and servants to Booty Bay to mingle with goblins, most ogres who left the Horde still hid nearby, forming gangs to harass villagers and raid settlements.

And the forest trolls — the Farstriders showed them no mercy. But these cunning Amani hunters were masters of sneak attacks and even better at running away. When things turned sour, they collectively slipped off. The rangers pursued hard but could not catch many.

Jace, Vereesa, and Needleflame returned to the main gate with Vaelastrasz. Everywhere were piled corpses of wargs, orcs, and ogres. The stench of blood and the charred smell of burning flesh was thick wherever they went.

"Get all the bodies out of here, soldiers!" Khadgar pointed outside. "This damned tower is full of troll witch-doctor totems and wandering shadow energy. And don't forget what we saw on the Burning Steppes — undead. Trust me, dying from necrotic plague is far more painful than taking a sword through the heart!"

"You're in a mood for jokes, Khadgar," Jace said as he walked over.

"This place doesn't need my grief and mourning, Jace Sesso." Khadgar turned. "You're back. Lady Morofil told me what you found. I never imagined there were so many whelps in this tower."

"Who knows how many casualties a blind charge would have cost. We've already used a massive amount of arrows. Even with the Storm Altar's supply line, things are getting tight. I hope we can hold out for the assault to come — shooting down an adult dragon is orders of magnitude harder than picking off a whelp."

"The Farstriders can summon arcane aid," Vereesa said.

"Mana is also limited, Ranger-General. And harder to replenish than arrows," Khadgar said.

"Where's Morofil?" Jace asked.

"Lady Morofil went to find Lord Grede Thunderfist. He should be here soon." The archmage looked at Vaelastrasz. "By the way, Master Vieran, Lady Morofil mentioned the whelps were controlled by a shadow artifact? A corrupted dragon egg?"

"Yes," Vaelastrasz said. "But the problem is, the energy in that egg wasn't its own. It was channeled from somewhere unknown. Considering Molten Core has collapsed, the Firelord has left this world, Shadowforge City has allied with you, and the Storm Altar is under Alliance control — there are no strong energy sources for dozens of miles. So..."

"So the most likely explanation is that the power comes from the very top of the Spire." Khadgar looked up at the oppressive black ceiling. "So that strong dark suppression force I've been feeling coming down from above — it's not an illusion or some negative emotional magic projection. It's real."

"I felt it too." Vereesa said. "That shadow energy. When Sora and I attacked alongside the Dark Iron forces, I sensed it. But at the time, I thought it was your power, Jace."

"His dark power did mask it to some extent when he was channeling that meteor into the Spire's outer wall." Khadgar said. "That misjudgment isn't your fault, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. I was fooled by Jace's magic too, to a degree. It wasn't until you mentioned the energy source that I could be sure there was another, stronger... I don't know how to describe it... gentler shadow at work."

"I don't understand. What shadow energy could be stronger than Jace's power, yet gentler?" Vereesa asked.

Vaelastrasz and Khadgar both looked at the ranger-general but said nothing. Needleflame stared at them silently, a faint fear creeping into her heart. Jace recognized that emotion. She was remembering the eye stalk she had encountered in Shadowforge City.

An Old God. Black dragons. A stronger, darker shadow power. The archmage's silence. Now even Jace had to wonder — what kind of help had Nefarian gotten?

Khadgar stared at the red dragon. "Is it possible...?"

"You're thinking of the artifact called Xal'atath, aren't you, Khadgar?" Vaelastrasz asked.

"What is Xal'atath?" Vereesa asked.

Xal'atath. Jace was thinking the same thing.

That thing was a dark artifact created during the ancient empires' era, hundreds of thousands of years ago. Some even whispered that it was the remnant limb of an Old God who had died long ago, its soul hiding within the blade.

And in truth, that was close to what it was.

"It's an ill-omened object passed down from ancient times, Ranger-General." Khadgar explained. "Master Vieran, being a close friend of Master Krasus, knows all about it. I don't think the Kirin Tor needs to keep secrets from you anymore. Rumors say that trolls, dwarves, and Gul'dan's servants all possessed it at various times during the Orc War. If Nefarian has found it too, everything adds up."

"But Nefarian knows as well as you do what wielding it means." Vaelastrasz said. "I don't think he'd rely on that artifact to complete his plans. That blade would eventually destroy Blackrock Spire and undo all the work he and his father have done."

"What if he's arrogant enough to believe he won't share the same miserable fate as the blade's previous owners?" Khadgar asked.

Vaelastrasz did not answer. But Jace had his doubts.

After being lost during the Orc War, that blade had been found by a human high priestess. She had been a bishop of Stormwind. And Jace had never seen her since coming to Stormwind. Which meant she had likely taken the blade and gone into hiding, just like in the other timeline — out of sight for a very long time. Had Nefarian found her?

He focused on the powerful dark force enveloping Blackrock Spire. His doubts deepened.

The soul hiding in that blade was an ancient, cunning thing. For tens of thousands of years, it had passed from hand to hand among mortals, always keeping itself hidden, watching for its chance. Even the Kirin Tor could not track it.

So why would it suddenly let Nefarian wield it, using its power against the Alliance? Had it decided now was a good time to reappear?

Shadow.

Then another possibility struck Jace.

"You said my shadow magic easily blends with the energy in this tower, right, Khadgar?"

"Indeed," Khadgar said. "What are you thinking?"

"It might be wrong, but I need to at least rule out one possibility first," Jace said. "Is there a Draenei mage with us here at Blackrock Spire? I met one before, at Drak'Thadar."

"Master Semid." Khadgar said. "I'll have him brought here."
