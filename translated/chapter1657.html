Chapter 1657: Breaking Into the Upper Spire

The defenders of the upper Spire did not stay flustered for long after the seal was broken by a young black dragon they had never seen. Once the few large dragonkin blocking the door were taken down by the Alliance vanguard, arrows and bolts came flying from every direction.

Galen Trollbane knocked an orc to the ground, only to take an arrow to the shoulder as he rose. Another arrow pierced his leg. He seemed not to feel it — he swung his sword and cleanly severed a Blackhand soldier's arm, then clashed with a dragonkin wielding a short spear before finally seeming to realize his arm and leg had lost their strength. He staggered back a few steps and leaned against a black stone pillar.

Several Stormwind soldiers drove the dragonkin back with spears. Two red-cloaked soldiers ran over with shields to support Prince Galen. Danath Trollbane heard them calling the prince's name and looked back, but quickly had to raise his sword to meet three Blackhand warriors charging at him.

A priest sprinted over and slid to his knees beside Prince Galen, trying to call upon the Holy Light to staunch the bleeding and heal his wounds. But even after closing his eyes and praying intently, only a faint glow flickered from his palms. The priest himself looked momentarily startled.

It was not just the priests. Jace noticed that the holy light sigils on Tirion Fordring's armor, who was fighting several orcs up ahead, had gone completely dark. The shadow energy had grown heavier since they had broken through the seal into this hall.

The Kirin Tor mages raised arcane shields to block the bolts for the Alliance. The upper Spire's chambers were narrow, with low ceilings. The mages could not unleash powerful destructive spells, so they focused on support. But Khadgar still had tricks. He murmured a spell, and in an instant, several orc crossbowmen on the balconies were teleported to who-knew-where, leaving only a faint shimmer of starlight.

Vereesa's arrows twisted like guided magical serpents, nimbly weaving around the arcane shields to strike several dragonkin mages and orc warlocks channeling spells on the high ground. The explosive impact of the magic arrows also forced many orc crossbowmen to take cover behind pillars.

With the crossbowmen's fire temporarily suppressed, the other Farstriders scaled the walls with speed that would put trolls to shame, seized the crossbowmen's perches, killed the gunners, and turned their bows on the dragonkin holding the line in the hall below.

The Farstriders had dealt with the crossbowmen. But the shadow still hung over every member of the Alliance vanguard.

Prince Galen, who had charged in alongside Danath and Varian, was now breathing heavily, his brow deeply furrowed and his lips pressed tight. He seemed to be in immense pain. For a warrior of the Trollbane bloodline, two arrow wounds should not have affected him this badly.

He was not alone. Every soldier wounded in the first wave of the breakthrough was suffering far worse than their physical injuries. Mental torment. The cold shadow was seeping into their hearts, twisting their emotions. Despair. Regret.

Perhaps Blackrock Spire could never be taken. Perhaps assembling the Alliance in this hellish, doom-filled world had been a terrible mistake. This war would cripple Stromgarde and Stormwind, while Gilneas, Kul Tiras, and Lordaeron sat back and reaped the benefits.

So many young, brave soldiers had died fighting their way here from the Burning Steppes. You orchestrated all of this. From start to finish, if you had not insisted on attacking Blackrock Spire, if you had not picked a fight with Deathwing's son, they would all still be alive. You should regret this...

Jace let out a slow breath. As he thought these things, his mind merged with the shadow filling the hall. Now all he had to do was...

He raised a hand and whispered the incantation for Shadow Bolt. He fixed his gaze on every twisted, savage orc, and turned all that cold despair and regret into something easier to control — the urge to destroy, the overwhelming desire to level the entire tower.

The darkness in his palm grew denser. But at the same time, the holy light in the nearby priests' hands blazed up again.

The swords and warhammers of the paladins locked in melee with the orc warriors also flared with brighter radiance.

The shadow energy permeating the hall was all drawn toward Jace's emotions, and the darkness suppressing the holy light weakened with it. Feeling their confidence return, the paladins called upon the Light's blessing. The Alliance soldiers rallied and shouted their assault. Several heavily armored dragonkin who had held the gate since it opened fell in quick succession. The remaining dragonkin warriors and orcs retreated step by step, leaving only furious, helpless roars.

"You fools! Lord Nefarius's magic is no excuse to slack off! Retake the gate! Prove your loyalty to Warchief Blackhand and Lord Nefarius! For the Horde!"

A brutal roar came from somewhere. The orc warriors seemed reinvigorated and howled back. Ahead, black and silver helmets and armor clashed, battle-axes and blades swung back and forth. The line actually inched forward again.

Members of the Scarshield, Bloodaxe, and Firebrand legions were not allowed into the upper Spire. So only the most trusted elite soldiers of the Blackhand Legion and the Frenzyclaw dragonkin under General Drakkisath were stationed in this critical military zone.

Though the Blackhand Legion did not often appear outside the Spire to fight the Dark Iron dwarves, the veterans here were almost all the Horde's core warriors — those who had followed Rend's father, Warchief Blackhand, across the Dark Portal.

Even if they had not expected the Alliance to open the gate so quickly and easily, the orcs who hastily gathered to counterattack this antechamber were decisive enough. They wore uniform black-gray armor and helmets, most carrying sturdy shields and battle-axes. Their combat prowess and equipment were far beyond the orc legions in the lower Spire and outside.

Even facing the paladins' head-on assault, even with the Farstriders raining arrows from the high ground, they did not flinch.

But courage and high morale could not bridge the gap in combat effectiveness. Especially not when the Iron Fury soldiers, maddened to retake Blackrock Spire, and the Alliance soldiers, burning for revenge for two Orc Wars, matched the Horde holdouts in spirit and will.

Whether he was eager to prove he was no orc sympathizer, or because he had heard too many terrifying stories about Blackrock Spire from his orc friend Eitrigg... Tirion Fordring swung his silver warhammer like a merciless judge at the front, smashing every enemy who came before him. Almost no orc survived a second blow from this paladin lord's hammer. Every swing was accompanied by the crunch of breaking bones.

And Turalyon, standing beside him, went without saying. The young paladin leader had killed so many orcs in Draenor that even Tirion Fordring would gasp. As he crushed a bloodguard, a Frenzyclaw dragonkin tried to ambush him from behind. Turalyon spun and snapped the dragonkin's polearm with his hammer, then grabbed the creature by the throat. The Holy Light burned through the dragonkin's eyes and throat on the spot. He pushed it to the ground like a deer with a broken neck.

Muradin Bronzebeard, who had climbed onto the balcony with the Farstriders, simply jumped back down into the orc formation. Though no one could see exactly what he was doing in there, after a few dwarven battle cries, several orcs went flying through the air in various directions. The mountain king, who had withstood the Hammer of Ragnaros, did not need anyone to worry about him getting hurt by orcs or dragonkin.

The veteran Danath Trollbane cut down orcs row by row with his longsword like a precise killing machine. The man acknowledged as the greatest warrior across the Seven Kingdoms was not living on reputation alone. He even killed dragonkin with a single thrust through the chest. After fighting countless demons in Draenor, he was long used to fighting aberrations — no matter how strong, they were nothing compared to packs of doomguards.

Varian Wrynn had always looked up to Danath Trollbane. But for now, the young king still lagged behind the Son of Lothar's leader. In the end, only Grede stood at the back, fuming. The dwarf could barely manage a brisk walk, let alone fight alongside the orcs. Sora had to call on nature's power to bolster the soldiers' spirits while holding the dwarf back from doing something reckless like Muradin and jumping into the orc pack. Grede was about ready to curse every green dragon he knew.

"Hang in there, Jace!" Turalyon smashed an orc axe-wielder's head in and shouted. "These Horde bastards can't hold much longer! Don't let the shadow magic protecting them push back!"

Jace did not answer. He was focused on controlling the shadow. Even a slight shift in his emotions could put more pressure on the Alliance soldiers. The best way was to release the energy he had gathered.

Orcs were still pouring in from the corridors. He spotted a group of a dozen or so emerging from a high passage, weapons raised, charging toward the elven rangers on the balcony. He hurled his Shadow Bolt at them.

The Shadow Bolt did not destroy any railings, pillars, or brick walls. It was like a thick black mist that swallowed every living thing standing there. When the mist cleared, the orc soldiers who had been charging the Farstriders with axes and blades were nothing but a few blackened, corroded lumps of rotting flesh.

"Help me find the passage to the outer wall, Captain Alleria!" Jace called to the ranger. "My demons can climb that high and support from outside. The seal is broken — they should be able to enter freely now!"

"I'm on it, Jace!" Alleria shouted to the other side. "Captain Nathanos! The passage to the outer wall is on your side! Open the windows and the doors to the outer balconies for the demons!"

Nathanos acknowledged from across the way and led a few rangers into a passage. Jace nodded at Alleria. Then a muffled horn sounded from deep within Blackrock Spire, followed by a frantic orcish shout.

"The Warchief Blackhand commands! The seal cannot be restored! Abandon the gate! Fall back to the barracks!"

"They're running!" Dagran Thaurissan raised his staff and waved it, shouting — in Common, no less. "After them! Kill as many as you can! Warriors of the Dark Iron! Don't let these humans and elves think they're the only ones who can take our home!"
