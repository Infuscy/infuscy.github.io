Chapter 1658: Another Charnel House

"Jace, can you feel it?" Khadgar stared into the dark passage where the orcs had retreated. "The master of this tower is channeling magic. All that dark power behind the seal came from this source."

"I can feel it. So can the paladins and priests." Jace said. "This dark power is slowing us down."

"I think that's exactly his intention," Khadgar said. "So you need to stay near the front, helping the Alliance soldiers fighting the orcs by redirecting the shadow energy. The Kirin Tor mages and I can't twist the curse enveloping this tower as easily as you can."

"I can try to weaken the shadow in a certain area, but I can't reverse the energy of half the tower." Jace said. "As we climb higher and our supply line stretches longer, I'll either have to focus on the front-line fighters and neglect the wounded, priests, and supply carriers behind, or the opposite."

"Focus on the front." Khadgar pointed at the paladins ahead. "Don't put too much pressure on yourself, Jace. The Kirin Tor and the Wizard Sanctum have sent plenty of black dragon researchers. They may not manipulate shadow as freely as you, but they can alleviate the problem. For instance, your teacher Master Marin — in these cramped passages, even his best Fireball is hard to cast. But on the front line, the soldiers will struggle far more without your help."

As the archmage spoke, a large force of Dark Iron dwarves, urged on by Dagran Thaurissan, climbed the steps with swords drawn and charged into the passages, completely ignoring the shadow energy echoing through every corridor and chamber. Khadgar coughed upon hearing their war cries. "Well, except for the Dark Iron dwarves. Let's follow."

With that, Khadgar Blinked to the other end of the hall and disappeared into the passage behind the dwarves and Stormwind's blue-clad soldiers.

According to the map of the Spire's interior that Shadowforge City had provided the Alliance, the barracks the orc commander had mentioned were on the next level. The shadow corruption throughout the Spire was fairly uniform. Once Jace had mastered these emotions, he could easily wrest the shadow away from Nefarian's magic, clearing the way for the advancing priests and paladins to call upon the Holy Light.

But it required intense concentration. He had to replenish his mana frequently through various means. Fortunately, with mages from Dalaran and Stormwind around, neither mana potions nor mana crystals were in short supply.

"Watch out, Master!"

Sazeno yelled from the pouch at Jace's belt. A Blackhand assassin, bare-chested, leaped out of a crack in the stone wall. He had clearly picked his target — he lunged at Jace with a black dagger.

A "whoosh" sound passed Jace's ear. An arrow flew straight into the orc's mouth. The assassin burst into fel fire, screaming as he tumbled over the railing and fell into the lower Spire amidst the imp's laughter.

Jace turned. Grede was standing there with a Dark Iron crossbow aimed his way.

"What are you looking at, lad? I'm the son of Grim Batol's greatest hunter. Can't swing Sulfuras right now, but you think I can't handle this?" He wagged the crossbow. "I've still got the Hand of Justice on me. Did you expect me to sleep through the whole battle in Shadowforge City and wait for news of victory? No chance!"

Jace smiled. Grede was wearing the carapace cuirass the Kaldorei had given him — the kind only worn by heroes of the War of the Shifting Sands. Though years of intense combat had left it a bit battered, the Kaldorei craftsmanship was still striking.

On top of that, he had a Wildhammer noble's cloak and numerous magical charms, including Onyxia's Blood and Leshrael's Life Gem. And yet, he was holding a standard-issue dwarven crossbow, looking entirely unapologetic about it.

The mismatched ensemble reminded Jace of those times in his past life when, facing a boss that melee fighters could not reach, even a fully epic-geared tank had to stand at range with the casters, pulling out a ranged weapon they had not used in forever and plinking away at the boss for DPS.

"Whatever you say. Just be careful," Jace said.

"Why are you fussing like Sora?" Grede waved his arm. "Go on. I'll watch your backs, you tunnel-vision idiots."

"Watch our backs?"

Jace chuckled and moved forward. But he did keep a bit more caution. Plenty of orcs were still hiding in the corners, ready to cause trouble from behind.

The Farstriders on the other side had noticed too. Alleria called out, "There are still Blackhand assassins lurking in the shadows! Watch the inconspicuous rooms, the cracked gaps, the passages! We need to root them out, Farstriders!"

Around the same time, demon roars echoed from deep within the branching passages. Kar'lez's felhounds had already burst onto the balconies and corridors below. These creatures might be even better than the Farstriders at sniffing out warlocks and assassins hiding in shadow.

But the demon roars also made some paladins tense up. A particularly burly felhound suddenly appeared ahead. Turalyon had just felled a dragonkin trying to cover the retreat of other Frenzyclaw soldiers. Sensing a demon nearby, he raised a hand and blasted it with holy light, burning it to ash. The burst of holy radiance was so intense that even Jace, a dozen meters away, felt like his skin and face were being scorched by the sun.

"Turalyon." Jace called out. "I can guarantee that any demons appearing nearby are on our side. Whether summoned by me or by the orcs."

Turalyon raised his hand, about to apologize, when a demon lord's roar erupted from several passages away.

"What are you doing, you fool?! Focus and see which side I and my servants are fighting for! I must be insane to help you ants fight! But are your eyeballs blinded by holy light too?!"

Turalyon looked toward the source of the voice, unsure how to respond. Jace sighed. "Drinks on me later, Turalyon."

"I'll pay," the paladin lord said with a brief smile, then raised his warhammer and charged ahead.

The orcs and dragonkin had fully abandoned this level, planning to make their last stand at the barracks. The Alliance soldiers pursued quickly and soon took the entire hall and all surrounding corridors. They also captured a good amount of Dark Iron-forged weapons and equipment the orcs had left behind.

The Blackhand Legion had blocked the door to the upper level. But with Needleflame as their "key," even if Nefarian raised another seal in a hurry, it would be meaningless. They did not even need the mages — the Dark Iron dwarves' bombs would breach the heavy stone door in no time.

Amid the explosions, Jace could feel uncertain emotions, even a hint of panic, seeping through the cracked stone of the level above. The Blackhand orcs were mortals after all. They knew their place in Blackrock Spire.

But just as Jace was waiting for the Iron Fury soldiers to blast open the door, Needleflame hurried over to him.

"Sesso, come quick... Vaelastrasz and Sister Sora are there too." She kept her head low.

Jace glanced at the dwarves working on the door, nodded, and followed Needleflame immediately. They passed a long corridor littered with dead dragonkin and buzzing flies, and came to a low door that had been blasted open with magic.

Even approaching the door, he was hit by an overwhelming stench of rot.

Several paladins and priests were standing before the door. Vaelastrasz and Sora were there too. The expressions on both dragons' and the clergy's faces were complicated. Needleflame beside him looked ashen, staring at the door, unwilling to approach and unable to speak.

Jace had to cover his nose and mouth as he neared the door and looked inside.

Countless whelp and young dragon carcasses were piled into a small mountain inside this hall. Scattered around were broken dragon eggshells everywhere. The necrotic poison spilling out from within almost choked Jace, forcing him to step back.

The corpses inside were mostly mangled. Many had swollen from the dense shadow corrosion and improper storage. The buzzing of carrion flies gnawed at everyone's ears and minds.

And these dragons... were all black dragons.
