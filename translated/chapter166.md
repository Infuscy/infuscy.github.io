## Chapter 166: The Benefits of Alcohol

The moment Grede said that, Jace regretted it.

It had just been the conversation running away with itself — once it veered toward the topic of parents, Jace had felt like Grede ought to share something on the subject too, so he'd asked offhand.

Maybe it was the alcohol, or maybe he hadn't really been paying attention to the conversation in the first place — either way, he should have seen this coming.

Grede took a long pull of his drink and said, "My da fell to his death. Stepped right off a cliff one night on his way home, couldn't see where he was going. My ma was a warrior. When I was real small, the trolls took her — never found out what happened. Though I reckon she wouldn't have wanted to live in Zinsara to this day anyway. Truth is, my memories of her are getting blurry now. Her face… I can't picture it no more. Might be the bat venom, too. Come to think of it, I'm starting to forget my da's face as well. Maybe I've been drinking too much? He was a distinctive-looking bastard, too."

"Sorry," Jace said.

"Nothing to be sorry about, lad," the dwarf said. "My da said he was a hunter his whole life, barely rode a griffin more than a few times, so he envied them warriors who could ride griffins into battle against the trolls. He said as long as I became a griffin rider, he'd die with no regrets. And I did join the order, and he went around bragging about it to everyone, drinking himself stupid every night. Then one morning this real ugly woman with hair like a griffin's nest knocked on my door and told me he was down at the cliff bottom, said I ought to come take a look. I figured he'd gotten drunk and gotten into a fight again, so I went. And then I saw him, all… the branches and everything had torn his face all to hell." By this point Grede's eyes had gone completely glassy, staring at the cup in Jace's hand while he mumbled on mechanically.

"I didn't even feel that sad at the time, because I knew he didn't have no regrets left. His body was getting old, sick with all kinds of ailments, going on about the old days at Grim Batol every single day. If he'd kept on living, the suffering would've just gotten worse and worse, and he would've ended up dying without any dignity at all. Dying up on the mountain like that, probably half-drunk, without any pain — if anything that was a blessing. I truly believe that. Me and Gweta — that was the griffin I was bonded to — I carried his body back to Khaz Modan, to the mountain, to say a final goodbye. I believe he left this world smiling. That's all there is to it."

Jace stared at him for a moment. "Sounds like your griffin Gweta flew you both there on her own, rather than you riding her…"

Grede's eyes snapped back to his face. He froze for a second, then burst out laughing, and it took him half a minute to recover, coughing so hard he nearly choked on his own spit.

"That's — that's exactly what happened! If the mood hadn't been so wrong for it, that big girl would've taken a nasty bite out of me. Over in Kushehaven she slept three or four days straight, she was that worn out!"

Jace started laughing too, and Grede couldn't help but launch into the story of how he'd met Gweta, how they'd bonded, how they'd trained and fought together.

Then word came about the Dark Portal, and Gweta — along with the other fiercest griffins they'd picked out, and a company of the most elite griffin riders — was sent straight to Ironforge to reinforce the war effort in Khaz Modan, and the two of them never saw each other again. By the time he got to this part, Grede sounded like he was about to run out of air.

Jace could practically picture what had happened after that.

A griffin rider facing an unprecedented alliance of orcs and Amani trolls — a rider who could have charged into battle alongside the partner he'd lived and died with for decades, only to be suddenly paired with a complete stranger right before the fight.

What kind of bullshit was that?

Maybe the Wildhammer dwarves never imagined the war would ever reach the Hinterlands.

"I'm grateful you're willing to listen to all this useless rambling, Jace," Grede sighed. "Wonder if he'd see me now and call me useless."

"Your dad?"

"Of course my da. You think I care what that flying madwoman thinks?!"

"Ah, madwoman?"

Jace felt like his brain was struggling to keep up. He scratched his face, which itched from the alcohol, and said, "If you think your father's still around and he'd call you useless — does that mean he'd think you were useless to the whole clan, or that you're useless because you can't take care of yourself?"

Grede grabbed his cup and stared at Jace for a good while, his lips parting a few times like he wanted to speak, but closing again each time.

In the end he never answered the question. Maybe his brain had already turned to mush.

The dwarf took another huge gulp of ale and said, "Goddamn it, I'm gonna find a way to get to Quel'Thalas."

Hearing that topic, Jace perked up a little.

He gathered his thoughts and said, "Maybe we should scout the outskirts first. Going to Quel'Thalas is something we need to think through carefully. When we head to Aerie Peak, we can stop by the elven settlement in the Hinterlands and take a look — there might be their botanists doing research or something. Though don't get your hopes up."

"I wasn't holding out any hope to begin with," Grede said. "I don't have a great feeling about how that elven outpost's gonna treat us, but you're right — we can take a look."

"In that case," Jace said, "just got our itinerary even tighter. Though I figured this trip might have some surprises, I didn't expect it to turn into this much of a goddamn mess."

"Well, we're already in Dalaran." Grede took a massive swig of his drink and squinted against the sourness.

"And you got to see your parents too. I'd say that's no different from the original plan at all."

"No difference," Jace said. "Except somebody went and smashed an extra dragon to death in Southshore."

"You're the one who whipped it to death, don't try pinning that on me. That's got nothing to do with me." The dwarf pointed at Jace's nose to correct him.

Jace swatted his finger away. "Bullshit. Me and all the soldiers saw it — you smashed it to death with your own hands."

"I don't care, you whipped it to death."

"Smashed!"

"Whipped…"

"Smashed…"

"Whipped…"

…Maybe the lemon wine was just too strong. The two of them couldn't manage any more coherent conversation, just kept talking nonsense nonstop.

Still, when they parted ways, the light rain on Jace's face sobered him up a little.

"Grede!" he called after the dwarf's back. "Where are you staying?"

"Hornwood Lane!" the dwarf shouted with both hands cupped around his mouth, his voice cracking. "Remember to come find me after that orc get-together!"

Jace watched his back and muttered to himself, "So where the hell is Hornwood Lane…"
