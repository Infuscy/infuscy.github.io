## Chapter 1660: The Blackwing Prince's Preliminary Results

Felhounds had a natural, powerful resistance to magic. But even they were not completely immune to the black dragonkin's breath inside Blackrock Spire.

As the Alliance soldiers charged up through the passage, the ground was littered with demon corpses burned to death. But between the orc warlocks, Kar'lez, and Jace's Hand of Gul'dan, enough felhounds had been summoned to absorb the losses.

As long as a few felhounds reached the dragonkin and triggered the magic-devouring ability that made even Kirin Tor wizards shudder — draining their magic, forcing them to stop breathing fire and deal with the hounds instead — it would weaken the dragonkin's ability to scorch the passage, giving the mages room to counter the flames with frost magic and the paladins a chance to lead the charge.

Jace had not even reached the top of the upward stairs when the roars of orcs, dragonkin, and humans echoed from above.

Tirion Fordring and his elite guards and priests from Hearthglen had already chased the felhounds into the dragonkin. Metal clashed and weapons scraped.

Blasts of holy light and dark fire tangled with each other, as if competing to see which could light up the black corridor more brightly. Behind them, the mages' Arcane Missiles whistled overhead, adding flashing white arcs of energy that nearly blinded anyone looking.

The Horde had seized the opportunity, using the narrow entrance to defend against the Alliance's assault, and they had prepared well — shielded infantry and dragonkin guards at the front to block the doorway, with dragonkin mages, orc warlocks, and crossbowmen providing ranged fire from behind, and the dragonkin's breath weapon used to create obstacles in the passage ahead of time.

But even that could not hold back the Silver Hand leaders and their elite paladins.

The lord of Hearthglen and his men had barely engaged the dragonkin guards when Turalyon caught up.

As a paladin who had led the charge against Ner'zhul's Black Temple, the Blackhand Horde and dragonkin before him were nothing but a ragtag collection of defeated soldiers — a weak obstacle, as far as he and the Expedition were concerned.

With dozens or hundreds of dwarves and human soldiers between them, Jace could still see Turalyon advancing quickly, surrounded by a golden, almost reddish radiance.

It reminded Jace of Morgan Ladimore's desperate charge at the weakened Darkhan Drakthul in Duskwood. This must be Turalyon's Divine Shield — and it was far more brilliant than Ladimore's holy protection. Fire, arrows, and slashing blades were either deflected, bent, or shattered on contact.

His holy radiance flickered so brightly that even the paladins nearby — old Fordring, young Fordring, the Stromgarde and Stormwind soldiers — had their spear tips, blades, and shields gleaming with silver light, as if blessed by some holy prayer.

When Turalyon crashed into the front line, the dragonkin who had barely been holding against Fordring collapsed like sandcastles washed away by a wave. The youngest founding member of the Silver Hand did not recite any prayers or make any appeals to a higher power. The blazing holy light simply enveloped him like guardian "angels," striking with every swing of his hammer, pouring down golden judgment like lightning.

Jace had half-expected Turalyon's repeated tolerance of his demon summoning, shadow manipulation, and dark magic usage to make the Holy Light question his will and faith — or at least give him a little warning punishment. But instead, this most accepting of paladins was now the most dazzling among the shining Silver Hand.

The orcs who had tried to block the Alliance army in this narrow corridor were now facing the paladins who had broken through with the Light's blessing. Close behind, Khadgar's Kirin Tor battle-mages used Blink to bravely plunge into the barracks camp.

The flashes of Blink spells disrupted the orcs' and dragonkin guards' vision. Mana Explosions followed, breaking their formations. Counterspells disrupted the casting of dragonkin mages and orc warlocks further back. Even without large-scale Mass Counterspell, the continuous thunder of arcane silencing was enough to rattle every spellcaster present and make it hard to focus.

Jace, who was focusing on controlling the shadow energy throughout the hall, felt his concentration waver. If he did not know these were friendly mages at work, he might not have been able to calmly maintain his spell.

When the Blackhand soldiers at the front realized that several purple-robed mages had slipped behind them, their will to hold against the paladins began to crack. And the felhounds that had not been burned to death were still running around everywhere, biting every orc warlock and dragonkin who carried even a trace of magical energy. When no magic was left, they bit the orcs anyway...

Within less than thirty minutes of contact, the defensive line at the barracks' antechamber had essentially collapsed. The Horde had been forced from a stubborn defense into a chaotic melee.

As the first defensive line crumbled, shouted orcish commands echoed from deeper and higher in the Spire. Between the surrounding roars, screams, and his own intense concentration on the shadow magic, Jace could not make out the commands clearly. But he could guess — abandon the barracks, fall back to the commander's hall. Once the Alliance reached General Drakkisath and Warchief Rend Blackhand, the top of the tower — the Blackwing Lair itself — would be just one step away.

But before the orcish commands had even ended, Jace felt something fine brushing against his face. The Dark Iron-crafted hall, lit by braziers and hanging lamps that had long since been destroyed by various spells, now had only flickering holy light, black dragon fire, Arcane Missiles, and a few mages holding Light spells aloft for visibility. The field of vision was already limited.

With whatever was brushing against his face, it grew even darker. Soon, Jace felt like sand had gotten into his eyes. Because it was sand. Real sand. A sandstorm was kicking up inside the tower?

Many soldiers covered their faces or rubbed their eyes as the sand grew thicker. Jace turned to look at the young black dragon who had just hopped off a dragonkin corpse. "Needleflame! That necklace Grede gave you — can it dispel this sand? It's a treasure of earth power!"

"But this isn't elemental earth power, Jace!" Grede had already taken a high perch on a pile of rubble, crossbow in hand, shouting. "Can you hear the elementals?"

"No," Jace said.

Right. There was no elemental roar. In Alterac Valley and in Molten Core, even when the fire and frost elementals refused to communicate, Jace could still feel their anger when they attacked. But this sand was completely silent. Not a single elemental whisper.

Which meant it really was not earth elementals. Was it... Nozdormu's children finally deciding to move against him?

The strange thought passed in an instant. If it really were bronze dragons, they probably would not use such a crude method to cause him trouble. As he thought more, Jace quickly realized who might be creating this sand.

Shadows of dragonkin emerged from the sand. They were shorter than the dragonkin guards, stocky and sturdy, about the size of the red-robed black dragonkin mages.

But as their silhouettes grew clearer in the arcane light, they showed unusual features. Their scales shimmered with an oily, multicolored purple light.

Chromatic dragonkin? Nefarian's chromatic dragon descendants?

The battle-mages at the rear were the first to face these chromatic dragonkin. Their arcane magic had little effect. Spells that would shatter an orc's limbs only made these dragonkin stumble!

A battle-mage, not realizing his spells were so weak, took a chromatic dragonkin's spear through the abdomen. Fortunately, a paladin charged in from the side and drove the dragonkin back with a hammer blow, saving the mage's life.

Chromatic dragonkin had arcane magic resistance rivaling blue dragons. Of course they did not fear arcane power!

But luckily, there were not as many chromatic dragonkin as black dragonkin. They could suppress the mages for a while, but in the end, they fell to the vanguard's blades and the Farstriders' arrows.

Jace cast a quick Curse of Agony on the chromatic dragonkin who had been knocked back. He could feel part of his shadow power being resisted, but the remainder was still enough to inflict deadly pain — especially with the shadow energy blanketing the entire floor.

But when Jace tried to refocus on manipulating the shadow, it was not so easy anymore.

A wave of intense drowsiness hit him. His thoughts slowed. He was not alone — the soldiers and mages around him were the same. A ranger trying to climb a dwarf statue for a better vantage point fell halfway. For an elven ranger, that was normally impossible.

"Soporific mist, Jace!"

Sora lowered her staff, leaning on it to keep herself upright. "These strange dragonkin can cast dream mist. Even I can't resist it. I don't have time to dispel it..."

"Sleep magic?!" Khadgar immediately covered his mouth and nose, but it was clearly too late. Even the paladins up front were moving sluggishly and drowsily. The strange, sweet scent of the soporific was growing thicker in the air.

Grede had already collapsed and was fast asleep. Needleflame was holding her breath against the wall, trying to distinguish the chromatic dragonkin through the sand and mist.

Vereesa seemed to be holding up better. But the field of vision ahead had become very unclear. Everywhere were orcs running back and forth and Alliance soldiers staggering. She raised her bow but could not bring herself to loose. Every so often, she had to cast dispel magic again to fight off the drowsiness.

"Why aren't the orcs getting sleepy?" she asked suddenly.

Right. Reinforced by the sleep mist, the orcs at the front were rapidly counterattacking. The tide seemed to be turning. Orc and dragonkin roars grew louder.

Jace watched an orc kick a paladin to the ground. His eyes were bloodshot, his teeth clenched and chattering, and his normally dark skin was flushed red.

Not just him — every orc was the same.

The orcish chanting and drumming from deep within Blackrock Spire grew clearer. Was this the Blackrock clan's priests invoking Bloodlust? Using the chromatic dragonkin's magic to try to push the Alliance back in one surge.

"Curse of Recklessness, Morofil." Jace forced himself through the drowsiness and called out in Demonic. "Curse of Recklessness!"

"Give it to you, Master?" Morofil turned back.

"Give it to Sora!"
