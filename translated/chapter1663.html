Chapter 1663: Human and Orc

The moment the King of Stormwind's boots struck the floor with a resonant clang, Jace felt as if his own heart had been struck the same way.

Varian Wrynn was without question the most capable fighter among the younger generation of the Seven Kingdoms' royalty — and even among nobles of all ranks. Compared to young Fordring, Mograine, Menethil, the older Trollbane, the two Proudmoores — dead or alive — and the Perenoldes "wandering" abroad.

But among the knights present, especially compared to Danath and Turalyon — both fresh from Draenor, having carved their way through orc and demon blood — the gap was clear.

Not to mention Alleria, a hunter who had matched wits with the Amani trolls for centuries, and her sister, whose true strength was hard to estimate from age and appearance alone.

As for Rend Blackhand — perhaps he was not as notorious as his brutal father. But considering how he had taken command of the Blackhand Legion after Orgrim Doomhammer's failure at Blackrock Spire and been acknowledged as the new Blackrock Chieftain and Warchief of the Horde, and considering that even Nefarian had not simply killed him upon arriving but allowed him to continue ruling the orcs, trolls, and ogres — in the Old Horde, an organization that worshipped strength, the man had proven his martial prowess through these achievements.

More importantly, the long-handled war blade Rend Blackhand held, carved with orcish runes, looked very much like a Blademaster's weapon.

The orcs' Blademasters were legendary warriors originating from the Burning Blade clan. Their skill and strength were unparalleled among orcs. Even Warsong Chieftain Grommash Hellscream was said to have learned fighting techniques from a Burning Blade Blademaster.

Their signature weapon was this long-handled runed war blade. Legend said the runes helped them commune with the elements, allowing them to fight guided by the elements even if they lost their eyes.

If Blackhand was wielding one, it might mean he had once studied under a Blademaster, or had received training from the Burning Blade clan through his father's connections.

Jace's heart sank further as he watched Varian raise his lion's-head sword.

In his past life playing the game, Jace would not have worried at all. A minor boss in UBRS could never defeat Varian Wrynn — the man who had beheaded Onyxia.

But now, he slowly realized his feelings for the Varian before him had completely separated from the High King of the Alliance he had known in the game. This was his friend, his king — a young warrior facing the Horde Warchief's challenge.

He was not the only one. The other leaders looked grim too. But since Varian had accepted the challenge, forcibly stopping him or interfering would severely damage the Alliance's morale and Varian's own reputation. It would also give the remaining elite Horde warriors a reason to fight to the death.

Jace glanced at Khadgar beside him. Khadgar was looking back at him. Both of their expressions seemed to say, "Why are you looking at me? Why didn't you stop him?"

It seemed there was nothing to do but hope Varian would win.

Thump. Thump. Drums sounded from somewhere. As Jace turned to look for the source, the fight in the arena began without warning.

Blackhand, though much older and even more powerfully built, launched forward as if shot from a catapult. He wielded his long-handled blade like a dagger, spinning it in a blur of wind as he went for the young king. From above, it was hard to tell whether the strike was a thrust or a slash.

But Wrynn did not fall behind. His sword, which had been pointing at the orc, suddenly shifted through a series of motions. A few clear clangs, and Blackhand's ambush was deflected.

They had exchanged positions. The king's expression was dead serious. He loosened and tightened his grip on his sword. Across from him, the chieftain was also catching his breath, his hand slowly sliding down the blade's hilt. Jace suddenly recalled sparring with Varian in Stormwind Keep. Back then, he had been able to parry a couple of exchanges. Watching now, he realized the king had just been playing with his friend.

"Huh! Huh!"

The Blackhand Legion soldiers beat their shields and roared, making the scene even more savage and dark. Several groups of dwarves had just arrived through different passages, confused about why the orcs and Alliance were all staring down at the arena. When they saw the King of Stormwind and the Blackhand Chieftain squaring off, they fell silent too.

"Gouge out that other eye, Varian Wrynn!" A Dark Iron dwarf's shriek cut through the orcish roars. Soon, large numbers of Dark Iron dwarves joined in with creative cursing. If one understood Dwarven, it would have been high-grade psychic damage.

Khadgar sighed, watching the dwarves. Suddenly, boots stomped below. Wrynn leaped at Blackhand. The orc twisted with agility unbelievable for his size, dodging the attack, and whipped his war blade back. But the king's sword caught it with a harsh metallic screech.

They clashed back and forth across the bloodstained arena, lit by braziers — like an old, thick black panther and a young, lean golden lion. Swords and blades collided. Firelight flickered across their edges. The shouting and jeers fell completely silent.

With a sudden shout from Wrynn, the Blademaster's war blade and the Stormwind royal sword crashed together with a thunderous bang. Both warriors gritted their teeth, locked in a contest of strength. The orc was clearly gaining the upper hand, his form rising higher while the king was being pushed lower. Jace clenched his fists, tense to the breaking point. The nearby Alliance soldiers and commanders leaned forward, brows furrowed, as if trying to will their eyes into the arena.

A longer weapon meant greater advantage. Rend Blackhand was already bigger as an orc, and his weapon was longer too. Wrynn was at a disadvantage in every aspect, now pinned down and barely able to move.

Jace instinctively reached out with his awareness, feeling for the shadow energy around him, debating whether to use it to help Varian. He noticed Khadgar was repeatedly tapping his lips with his fingers, his eyes darting around. He was probably wrestling with the same dilemma — whether to subtly screw the orc over. They were thinking the same thing. After all, honor was not worth dying for.

But then, a surge of rage erupted from below. The king's face contorted as he glared at the Blackhand Chieftain, his whole body trembling. The orc, too, seemed to be tiring from the struggle, his footing slipping slightly.

Jace felt that burning emotion. He could almost sense what Varian was thinking. Blackhand. The Horde. Stormwind had been burned to ashes by this so-called Warchief's father. Rend had spoken the truth — today, Varian had finally come here. He had not even come for the black dragon. He had come to kill the last Blackhand, destroy the Blackhand Legion and Blackrock Spire, and put an end to all the rage and hatred consuming Stormwind. And he would not fall here.

Varian let out a mighty roar and pushed the orc back, then lunged with his sword. But the thrust missed. Everyone present — even the orcs — gasped in surprise. Rend Blackhand, who had seemed to be losing in the contest of strength and stumbling, now dodged the attack as if using a mage's Blink!

But Rend's ability did not end there. Jace watched as his movements did not stop after evading Varian's thrust. Instead, they grew even more indistinct.

The orc circled around the king with impossible speed. Each step left a trail of shadow in the wind. These shadows seemed like separate entities, or maybe just afterimages from Rend's incredible speed. But they all raised their shadowy war blades simultaneously and stabbed at Varian. The threat was real enough.

Even Turalyon and Danath's eyes went wide as they gripped the railing. Muradin Bronzebeard could not help shouting Varian's name.

But Varian spun and cut his sword through the air beside the last afterimage. The blade sank deep into the orc's shoulder, all the way into his chest.

The Blackrock Chieftain grunted and dropped to his knees. The long-handled war blade fell from his left hand. He swallowed hard a few times. The afterimages had dissipated sometime during the exchange.

Was this the Blademaster's Mirror Image technique?

Jace had never seen such a skill in this life. Dazzling.

But at least they had won. It felt like the air and time had started flowing again.

He was not the only one. After a long silence in the arena, the Alliance soldiers erupted in cheers. Rend Blackhand coughed heavily. "How did you see through it, human?"

"Those afterimages lacked your killing intent, Warchief." Varian said quietly. "My adviser once told me that even a disguised black dragon cannot hide their emotions."

Rend chuckled twice and nodded. Khadgar leaned in and whispered to Jace, "When did you teach him shadow magic, Jace?"

"Don't talk nonsense," Jace said immediately.

"Now answer my question." Varian said. "Where is Marshal Anduin Lothar?"

"A word of advice. Don't bother trying to recover Lothar's body." Rend said. "He's the black dragon's bait."

"The Alliance would have come to Blackrock Spire to destroy your legion regardless, orc." Varian said coldly. "You went through all that trouble for nothing."

"You'll understand, little king. Remember — don't try to recover him." Rend's last words were cut short as he coughed up blood and his head drooped.
