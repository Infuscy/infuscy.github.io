## Chapter 1668: Where the Souls of Blackrock Go

Nefarian's laughter still echoed from the depths of the hall. The wild fury had been suppressed considerably compared to earlier. Jace felt that if he tried to channel the shadow energy now, it would be several times easier. He even had enough focus to gather a volley of Shadow Bolts into the air, helping the mages and rangers kill the whelps circling among the high rafters.

But that was only possible because Sora was pouring everything into her Tranquility. Whether it was the difference between green dragons and druids, or between the game and reality, Sora's Tranquility lasted much longer than it did for players in the game. Jace had experienced it before -- especially at Wildhammer Keep, when Kil'jaeden's magical blockade had descended. Sora had grown a lot after going to Kalimdor and meeting Ysera.

But Sora's Tranquility wouldn't last long enough for Jace and Mor'fel to leisurely investigate. So despite his professed confidence in the succubus's abilities, he kept urging her to hurry.

Mor'fel circled for a while by the old dwarf windows on the high wall. A whelp flew up from below, craning its neck to snap at them, but Needleflame dove down, grabbed it by the neck with sheer mass and momentum, and spun them both around until they crashed together into a group of dragonkin.

Jace glanced down, worried that Needleflame would be surrounded by the closing dragonkin. But Dagran Thaurissan was already leading a group of Dark Iron warriors to charge in and scatter the dragonkin trying to encircle her. Mor'fel grabbed his hand and dove sharply through a window, yanking his attention back.

"Demon!"

"The master said to kill any demons on sight!"

Jace had barely landed when orc shouts erupted from the corner of the room. Two soldiers in heavy armor, wielding broad axes that looked like demon talons, rose and charged at him. One of them bellowed, "For the Blackwing Clan!"

Their orcish accent was the Dragonmaw dialect Jace knew all too well. But hearing "Blackwing Clan" in orcish -- not draconic -- was bizarre. Other orcs, even the Dragonmaw who had once served Deathwing and Sabellian, only ever said "black dragon." These refugees from Grim Batol had completely degenerated into black dragon slaves.

Despite being black dragon slaves, the armor these two wore was made from whelp and dragonkin scales, shimmering with a pale blue mixed with dark purple glow. Far superior to the gear of the Blackrock orcs below, even Rend Blackhand's personal guard.

But no armor, no matter how good, could withstand Mor'fel's assault. One orc took a hoof to the chest the moment she landed, leaving a crater in his armor. He coughed up blood and collapsed. The next orc lost his head to a clean blade stroke, his axe waving once before his body went limp and fell. The whole thing was over before Jace could even open his mouth to cast a spell.

Mor'fel raised a hand lightly toward the orc she'd kicked, who had been on the verge of death. He immediately forced himself up against the wall.

"My master, my most beloved master, I can't imagine how I could have been so disrespectful to you just now. I... I'm sorry I can only speak to you in this filthy tongue, because my command of demonic is still poor. From today, I will dedicate myself to learning this beautiful language, just to--"

"Who is controlling the dragonkin commander below? The mad one who charged into the allied ranks?" Mor'fel cut him off coldly.

"You mean Captain Razorgore." The orc said.

"Razorgore?" Mor'fel turned to look at Jace. Sazeno piped up, "He mentioned that name, master! He said it was a mad dragonkin general who told him about the shadow dragon egg! You must remember, master!"

"That must be the name then." Jace nodded.

"Correct. Who's using magic to control him?" Mor'fel asked.

"It's Master Gritok. Lord Victor Nefarius appointed him as the orb's controller..." The orc answered obediently.

"Lead the way. Take us to him." Mor'fel said.

"How convenient. It's very close from here, noble mistress." The orc nodded like a chicken pecking rice. "I was actually in charge of guarding the controller, Master Gritok, so we'll be there soon..."

"Of course it's close, you idiot. I was chasing that magic to kill you all, and you should feel honored." Mor'fel laughed coldly. "Stop blabbering and lead the way!"

"To be slain by a noble master is indeed a reward. I'll go at once!" The orc turned and ducked into a small doorway behind them.

Mor'fel followed, looking back with a smug expression. Jace muttered, "Hurry up. I'm about to throw up."

"Oh? Vomiting? That's a new game..." The succubus covered her mouth with a soft gasp.

This demon didn't care at all about the allied soldiers below, but Jace wasn't in the mood to banter with her. Fortunately, the orc moved fast enough. His chest injury from the kick looked serious -- he limped with every step -- but whether from the overwhelming "love" for Mor'fel, he still forced himself forward at a brisk pace. It was almost touching.

"What's going on down there with those two? You, Rudta, Bigg -- take some men down to check! Lord Nefarius ordered that the orb must not falter before Captain Razorgore dies! Do you want them to throw me to Chromaggus as a snack?!"

A shout in orcish came from above, followed by the clatter of boots. From the sound of it, maybe a dozen or more.

This place might have once housed the Dark Iron nobility's guards and servants. Though slightly wider than the collapsed and crushed corridors outside caused by the Molten Core's destruction, fighting seven or eight -- or even a dozen -- orcs here would still waste enough time. And if Nefarian gave an order like that, and these dozen reinforcement soldiers didn't report back, more Dragonmaw orcs would come swarming.

Jace pulled out a bottle of Invisibility Potion. "Invisibility, Mor'fel."

"Yes, master."

The demon slipped into shadow. Jace drank the potion and felt like he'd been submerged in water, everything around him covered by a thin layer... It was a familiar feeling. But this time it was different. He could dimly see the shadows on the walls, even the flow of magical energy in the hall outside. As Mor'fel dragged him along behind the orc, he could clearly sense the demonic energy and the shadow magic flowing through the orc ahead of him.

Ursula was right. The improved Invisibility Potion was a huge upgrade in vision over the old Lesser Invisibility Potion. He just hoped the stealth level had improved too, since the Dragonmaw elites the black dragons had taken an interest in had to be keener than a bunch of wandering ghosts from Thaurissan's ruins.

"What happened back there?!"

"A demon invasion, but I repelled them, Captain Bigg. Badad is dead, though. A demon cut his head off!"

"What a waste!"

"Many demons are burrowing everywhere under some Pit Lord's orders. Those damned Kirin Tor wizards somehow got a Burning Legion commander to work for them. Even the Blackrock Clan is finished. Chief Rend has either fled, betrayed us, or died. Regardless, we Blackwing Clan must defend Lord Victor Nefarius's property! No time to rest. Tell Master Gritok what's happening!"

"Zog-zog."

The orc Mor'fel had enslaved was pretty clever. Hard to say if what he was saying was influenced by her Charm spell, but the dozen orcs quickly headed toward the room they'd come from, while Jace and Mor'fel continued deeper.

Jace took a glance back at the hall through the special vision of the Invisibility Potion. Sora's Tranquility was flagging, the heavy shadow energy pressing back like a descending night...

But beyond these interweaving, struggling chaotic energies, there were more shadows. Souls. The ghost-shapes visible through the potion's vision.

So many had died in this battle within Blackrock Spire. Orcs, trolls, ogres, dragonkin, dragons, allied soldiers. Aside from those the warlocks had harvested on the spot for soul shards to fuel their spells, and those Jace had snatched with Ulthalesh to burn for mana, countless more still wandered inside and outside the tower. Too far away, blocked by walls, unable to be devoured by Ulthalesh in time. Trapped by the shadow energy saturating the place, unable to escape easily -- like the guests and residents who had died in Karazhan's cataclysm.

But now, these souls didn't seem to be wandering aimlessly. They were all slowly drifting deeper into the tower, higher up. Drawn toward something. Guided by some force.

Wait. Was the magic Nefarian was channeling -- the shadow enveloping all of Blackrock Spire -- not a dark ritual to slow the allied army? Was it the precursor to an actual spell, gathering power?
