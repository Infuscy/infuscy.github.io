## Chapter 167: Under the Pine

The next day, December 11th — finally, the day of the Orcish symposium.

Jace woke up past ten. Thinking about the meeting that afternoon, he figured he ought to use the little time he had before heading out to polish up his remarks.

He took out the draft he'd written earlier — the paper covered in such tiny, dense characters that it was bumpy all over, like it had grown a crop of little warts — and turned over in his mind how to help the Dalaran mages realize that the relic known as the Eye of Dalaran might be under threat, without revealing the existence of the three relics mentioned in the command.

The three relics referenced in the command were already blindingly obvious, and there weren't that many places in this world known specifically for storing relics. Karazhan was one, Dalaran another, Quel'Thalas another, the capital of Lordaeron another — and Stormwind used to count too, until it got looted and couldn't anymore.

Karazhan could be ruled out, because its outer grounds had been essentially under orc control during the war, never mind that its master was the very architect of the Dark Portal incident. Any mage with a shred of judgment about the war situation would assume that if there'd been anything in Karazhan that could help the orcs press their invasion of Azeroth, Medivh would have already handed it over to Gul'dan.

Stormwind had once been the hiding place of the Book of Medivh, but after it was stolen, it only became clear after the fall of Alterac that the book had been taken by agents of the Alterac royal house, the Perenolde family — and by the time anyone realized that, Teron Gorefiend was already at the gates. Far too late.

Quel'Thalas had been hit hard in the Second War, but even under that terrible assault, the main city had stood firm without serious damage. And the elves guarded their own territory, their walls, and their treasures with a conservatism that put the rest of the continent at ease — no one much worried about some foreign thief sneaking into Silvermoon to steal anything.

So steering everyone toward watching out for Dalaran and Lordaeron was practically the natural conclusion.

And as everyone knew, though the Lordaeron capital was wealthy and prosperous, it didn't hold nearly as many magic-related holy relics as Dalaran did.

If he just argued hard enough on this point, he could definitely make everyone believe that Dalaran was in danger.

Of course, he also cut out a few particularly aggressive — or particularly self-righteous — sentences, so that everyone would believe he was speaking out of genuine, well-reasoned concern for Dalaran's safety, rather than sitting on some secret knowledge he wouldn't share, trying to manipulate the old fellows of the Kirin Tor into doing something.

And he reminded himself: the moment any mage in that room sensed something off, he'd shut this dangerous manipulation down immediately.

He cared about the Eye of Dalaran, sure — but he cared about his little life more. He absolutely could not expose himself as a transmigrator who could see the future.

At half past three in the afternoon, Jace arrived early at the meeting spot. The building Dushan had told him about didn't have many people outside yet. They were close to the Violet Citadel now; the pedestrians on the street were almost all mages, every last one in purple or blue robes.

Last night's rain had been on and off right up to now, and everyone was wearing broad mage hats and heavy cloaks. Look as he might, Jace was the only idiot out there going bareheaded.

Dalaran's main city was less a city and more a giant school.

Jace had heard that once the new semester started after the new year, this whole area near the Violet Citadel would be closed off to anyone who wasn't a mage — stricter than a lot of schools he could think of.

To make sure he wouldn't get kicked out on the spot, he'd dressed quite neatly: a shirt his mother had sewn on the inside, the Wizard's Sanctum's mage robe over it, new trousers his mother had bought him. He looked, more or less, the same as the mage assistants on the street.

He gave the documents and materials he'd brought one final check. Most important was the command letter, plus his own transcribed copy — confirmed he hadn't brought the wrong thing, nothing missing, nothing damaged.

And of course the Wizard's Sanctum's apprentice sigil — that represented his identity. Dushan had reminded him more than once to remember to wear that ring to the meeting.

The meeting started admitting people at four. When it would actually begin, Dushan hadn't said for certain, but Jace had a feeling it would run well into the evening, so he'd grabbed a short nap after lunch and felt reasonably alert.

He didn't plan to go in early and wait for everyone to trickle in. Instead he holed up in the little park outside the domed building, pacing circles around a giant pine tree for shelter, reciting the key points of his prepared speech while keeping an eye out for Dushan.

Just then Jace spotted a group of elves approaching quickly. The red-haired elf in the lead was walking fast, talking instructions to his companions as he headed through the door.

This must be the elven delegation coming to the Orcish symposium. Jace had considered that there might be elves at the meeting, but he genuinely hadn't expected this many.

Someone had mentioned before that the elven prince, Kael'thas Sunstrider, held a very poor opinion of orcs. He approved of the internment camps — but he approved because he wanted them to efficiently round up all the orcish prisoners and then deal with them in one go, to avoid future trouble.

The appearance of this elven group was proof enough that Quel'Thalas had people who disagreed with Kael'thas. After all, if you dealt with all the orcs, there wouldn't be much point in learning Orcish.

After the lead elves went inside, a group of elves dressed more simply stayed outside. Judging by their scattered positions, they were probably the guards assigned to protect the delegation.

Some of the elven guards had taken up positions this way, and Jace spotted a familiar face among them.

It was Kael'fal Brightwing.

When Jace saw Kael'fal, the elf noticed him too. He gave a smile in Jace's direction and walked over.

"Jace Sesso. What, you're here for the Orcish symposium too? I always took you for a fairly open-minded sort — the type who'd go learn the language of those beasts."

"I'm not learning Orcish to be open-minded," Jace said. "What about you — you're not interested in Orcish too, are you?"

"Interested, but I haven't learned it yet." Kael'fal hitched up his blue mageweave belt threaded with gold thread. "I'm just here providing security for the Sunreavers."

"Sunreavers?"

"Aethas Sunreaver," Kael'fal said. "Never heard that name? He's Quel'Thalas's ambassador in Dalaran. He's very interested in learning Orcish, and he supports continuing to study the orcish culture beyond the Dark Portal — that 'Zul'ano' thing."

"I didn't expect you elves had an official that curious about the outside world. I remember Prince Kael'thas isn't fond of orcs," Jace said.

Kael'fal said, "Aethas has a relative in the Reliquary. The Reliquary has never cared one bit about outsiders or insiders. Any artifact or holy object that holds power, they're interested in it. They've been digging up this land for hundreds of years and were just starting to worry there was nothing left to research, nothing left to write about — and then a bunch of lunatics from another world show up, speaking a strange language, wielding unique blades, using bizarre magic. You think they'd pass up that opportunity? Those researchers backed by the Sun King couldn't care less about offending an unpredictable prince." He widened his eyes and pursed his lips in an exaggerated show. "Spoke out of turn there."

Jace laughed. "Do the Farstriders handle guard duty too?"

"Not normally." Kael'fal said. "But thanks to you and those brave dragon-slayers, Jace, black dragons have barely shown themselves the past couple of days. And General Sylvanas hasn't issued any new orders yet, and the Dalaran embassy's guards have all been assigned to protect the prince, so the Sunreavers' security falls to us rangers with nothing better to do."

"Black dragons haven't been showing up the last couple days? Why?" Jace asked.

Kael'fal explained, "It's been a few days now. The last sighting was on the seventh — someone saw a black drake flying over Strahnbrad. The nearby towns, both the Lordaeron and Dalaran sides, have deployed enough mages and archers to defend against them. With defenses that thorough, I'd think the black dragons, after running their heads into the wall a few times, wouldn't want to waste effort on pointless attacks."

Jace felt his throat go dry and swallowed involuntarily.

Maybe it was nerves about the meeting being about to start. Maybe it was that something felt off.

If the surrounding towns' defenses were already in place, didn't that mean Dalaran's defenses were at their weakest right now?

He glanced over at the magnificent Violet Citadel — soaring so high it didn't look like something built by human hands, especially that floating structure at the very top half-veiled by clouds, nothing short of a magical wonder. Maybe the people living at the very top had never imagined a place like this could ever be violated.

"Well, being a guard isn't so bad," Jace said. "At least in a safe place like Dalaran you can loaf around a bit — not like those uninhabited forests where you've got to be on edge every second, worried there's an orc trap somewhere, or an enemy ambush."

"Safe equals boring." Kael'fal wiped away a raindrop that had fallen from a pine needle onto his forehead. "Otherwise I wouldn't have come over here to find you to kill time. I'd rather pick another fight with you and that dwarf. Then it's another few hours of standing at post — just thinking about it is pure agony, especially knowing someone on the same squad doesn't have to be here. Makes it even worse."

"Who?"

"Who else? Lady Vereesa, obviously." Kael'fal smiled.

"Why isn't she here? Got another mission?" Jace asked.

Kael'fal narrowed his eyes. "Are you serious?"

"Is that strange?"

"You want the younger sister of Silvermoon's Ranger-General providing security for this Sunreaver?"

"Ah. Yeah, that does seem off." Jace gave an awkward laugh, just in time to spot Dushan approaching from a distance.

"It's time, I'm heading in. Hope you find some other entertainment here."

"Al diel shala," Kael'fal sighed, leaning casually against the wet tree trunk and tossing out the Elvish farewell.

Jace glanced back at him, because he'd understood that sentence. Al diel shala — "safe travels."
