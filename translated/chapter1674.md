## Chapter 1674: The Tower of Flame and Shadow

With King Varian Wrynn's command, the Alliance soldiers who had been caught off guard by the time acceleration magic were reinvigorated, charging at the now one-headed Chromaggus.

After losing that head, Chromaggus not only lost the ability to invoke bronze dragon magic again, but the shifting speed of his colorful scales slowed dramatically. Against Arcane Missiles and arcane-enchanted arrows especially, some scales were shattered and pierced before the beast finally reacted to change color.

Facing over a hundred elite Alliance soldiers launching a swift counterattack, the chromatic drake finally couldn't hold on. His low whines sounded like a lone beast gradually realizing the danger closing in and beginning to panic. Some of his scales even shifted colors chaotically as his composure crumbled, until dwarven axes and handheld cannons shattered them, sending them clattering down in showers of foul blood.

By now, the only chromatic dragon power he could still rely on was the red dragon's formidable vitality. He really was tough, stubbornly holding on for his master, as if one more second might change something.

But something did change. Jace could clearly feel the oppressive fury that had been pressing on his spirit and searing his consciousness rapidly cooling. Last time it had been when Razorgore destroyed himself to wipe out the dragonkin legion. This time, the once-ferocious Chromaggus was finally defeated — nothing unusual about that.

But soon the shadow-fury kept diminishing, dropping to a weakness Jace hadn't felt since entering Blackrock Spire and first sensing that shadowy pressure. It was as if something was draining all the shadow away. Jace no longer needed to suppress the power — instead he instinctively tried to hold onto some of it before it was all taken, but he couldn't keep much.

Khadgar immediately sensed something was wrong too. He knew that even Jace, skilled as he was with shadow magic, couldn't just dispel the darkest oppression in the entire Cylinder Hall on his own — especially when Jace himself looked bewildered.

The Kirin Tor wizards raised their heads one after another, followed by Vereesa, Alleria, and the other rangers. Elves had always been extremely sensitive to magical anomalies. Soon even the paladins felt it.

Chromaggus's pained groans, choked by dragon blood flooding his throat, gradually twisted into smug whispers. He muttered something repeatedly — a complete sentence in the Dragon Tongue.

"Father's... request is done. Father wanted me—"

"Yes, my most loyal child, Chromaggus. This proves you're not entirely useless to my cause. You're not a complete failure. Heartening..."

With the shadow power drained away, the hall the Dark Iron dwarves had abandoned fell deathly quiet, making Nefarian's low voice ring out as clear as a bell.

"The door to a new world has finally opened. A brand-new future lies ahead. Blackwing will be free at last. All the foolish, shortsighted, and cowardly will be left far behind — just like you, my child... just like Onyxia, who cruelly betrayed me, and countless other kin lingering in the old world. No, they are no longer my kin. At least from this moment on..."

A black-red bolt of lightning, accompanied by a sudden piercing shriek, struck down through the hall, hitting a long-dead whelp. The flesh on its corpse instantly erupted like mad fire, the blood flowing down igniting like oil.

The flame was ferocious. Flesh, skin, scales, eyes — everything that still held a hint of living substance was visibly blackened, carbonized, then blown away by the conjured magical wind. Only the fire-blackened, red-cracked bones, horns, and claws remained. Then this skeletal frame, wreathed in fierce flames, spread its wings like a burning ship in a black storm.

The undead monster immediately declared its "rebirth" to everyone in the hall with a hoarse, shrill, and utterly deranged cry. At the same time, more "black lightning" struck every fallen corpse in succession — whelps, dwarves, humans, even the severely wounded. Each target met the same fate: burn, then rise again.

Soon the hall filled with more and more struggling undead rising to their feet. The Alliance soldiers who had been charging at Chromaggus were now locked in melee with them... Even Chromaggus's dead head was struck by a bolt of lightning. The other head was burned away by the blazing Shadowflame. Right before everyone's eyes, the aberrant monster went from a living freak to a skeletal puppet wreathed in black-red flames. The soldiers who had been hacking at him moments before were blasted back by the spewing evil flames. Some couldn't dodge in time and caught fire themselves, their screams filling the air.

"For Father Nefarian!"

A burning skeletal whelp shrieked in the Dragon Tongue, and all the reanimated skeletons in the hall — including the humanoid skeletons that had just risen — roared back in the same tongue. Amid Nefarian's utterly unhinged laughter, more and more black lightning began flashing continuously further out, some breaking through cracks in the walls, others slamming into corridors.

"This dark magic is resurrecting every corpse in Blackrock Spire — including the dragon skeletons! We must stop him!"

Vaelastrasz's dragon roar echoed up from below. Jace finally understood how Nefarian planned to break free of the Old Gods.

In the center of this terrifying dark vortex formed by the storm of shadow-lightning, even the Holy Light summoned by the Knights of the Silver Hand was pitifully dim. All the Alliance soldiers were forced to fight enemies they'd just felled — even their former comrades. And Chromaggus, granted new unlife, had lost all his chromatic dragon versatility but become even more ferocious with the empowerment of Shadowflame.

Though Vereesa destroyed two of his heads in rapid succession with precise arrows and shattered his spine, it no longer mattered. Nefarian's new legion was already taking shape. Chromaggus and all the chromatic dragons were meaningless in this new era.

"It IS you, Vaelastrasz. Ever since that Kirin Tor mage successfully rescued Prince Korialstrasz from my surveillance, I knew someone was meddling. And of all Alexstrasza's children, you're the best at these sneaky little tricks. But that doesn't mean I don't appreciate you. I'm truly sorry you couldn't join my legion from the start. But I knew your noble sense of duty would keep you in my tower until you had no choice but to serve me. And you will become a perfect example — showing Queen Alexstrasza, Lord Malygos, and all other great beings and mortals a new way. A way to solve problems even the gods couldn't. To end the lingering poison of the Black Empire, which has persisted for hundreds of thousands of years. To end the hidden catastrophe stretching from the Pantheon to the most humble bear den and rat hole in the Dragonblight — a catastrophe you and all the living have deliberately ignored."

"Nefarian, you utterly depraved madman. You've destroyed the great Black Dragonflight with your own hands — completely, permanently! Don't you realize that yet?"

The black dragon gave no answer. The only sounds remaining in the tower were the shrieks of the dead, the shouts of battle, the screams of terror, the roar of magical winds rushing through corridors and grand halls, the crackling of flames, and bursts of cold laughter.

"Jace!" Khadgar's blue robes whipped violently in the wind kicked up by the dark magic. Clutching his staff tightly, standing among a group of mages fighting off surrounding undead, he shouted toward Jace: "His mass resurrection spell relies on those souls! Drink the Destruction Potion now and try to take those souls — otherwise there's no point drinking it later. Unless you want to shout 'Long live Nefarian'!"

Jace didn't need Khadgar's reminder; he'd already realized it. He downed the Destruction Potion and tossed the bottle into the deep pit, raising Ursalius to begin the soul-drain incantation, ignoring all the flaming skeletons closing in around him. Grede grunted as he hefted a war hammer — someone's discarded weapon — and smashed one of the skeletons to pieces, coughing as he shouted, "Do your ritual, Jace! I'm right beside you. I might not be able to kill a two-headed giant crocodile right now, but helping these lads break free of the black dragon's magic control is no problem!"

I know, I know, Jace thought to himself.

In the sky, bone dragons trailing smoke and ash circled around Sora and Needleflame, belching black fire continuously. Sora could still rely on natural wind to shield herself, blowing away the flames while countering with toxic acid. But Needleflame's lava breath, against these undead monsters resurrected by Shadowflame, was nearly useless beyond the sheer impact of the molten rock.

Arcs of Farstrider arrows and mage Arcane Missiles traced paths between the dragons locked in combat, with black lightning occasionally mixed in, carrying skin-crawling shadow screams.

As Jace chanted his incantation, the demonic eye atop Ursalius's head was wreathed in bloody red and the ghostly blue light of soul-draining. A soul-drain channel extended upward from his staff head, cutting through the tangled magic and monsters, straight up to the chains binding Lord Lothar at the very top.

Jace focused, ignoring all the chaos around him. But even with the Destruction Potion's enhancement, even with Ursalius, he felt something fiercely resisting his attempt to seize those souls. That resistance was even clearer than the last time. Jace stared at the center around which the souls gathered — Lord Lothar. For a moment, Jace even felt as if Lothar had opened his eyes.

I... must protect the innocent.

It was him. It was the will of Marshal Anduin Lothar. The Atama Crystal had amplified his will and spirit, and Nefarian was using him to repel all soul-drain magic!

As if sensing Jace Sesso's renewed failure, Nefarian's laughter grew even more unbridled, as if defeating this warlock alone meant claiming ultimate victory.

The soul-drain channel was torn apart by more and more clawing, snarling ghosts gathering around. Ursalius grew restless and dissatisfied, howling and cursing into its master's consciousness.

Jace looked up at the highest point of the circular hall, the shadow magic circle beneath his feet dissipating quickly. "Get everyone out of here, Khadgar! Now!"

"What?" Khadgar asked. "Go where? You want us to retreat NOW?!"

"Auchindoun, Khadgar! Haven't you heard of that magic?!" Jace shouted.

Khadgar's face changed instantly. He looked up at the highest point, then shouted toward Turalyon and Varian: "Everyone, come to me! While there's still a trace of my Destruction Potion's effect left! Now! Immediately! Right now! Don't hesitate!"

Hearing the archmage's voice, Turalyon, the dwarven leaders, and Alleria all looked over. Without a single question, they called out orders for their subordinates and companions to gather toward the Kirin Tor. They knew that when Khadgar gave an order this serious, it needed to be carried out without doubt — and Varian knew it too.

"What are you going to do, Jace?!" Grede roared. "Are you getting that stupid 'lone hero sacrificing himself to save the world' idea again?!"

"This isn't a damn self-sacrifice-to-save-the-world play, Grede!" Jace roared back at him. "But if you don't get to Khadgar right now, you'll be the only idiot who sacrificed himself and saved NOTHING!"

"Damn, damn, damn," Grede said. "I'll trust you one more time."

"Get moving now! Needleflame! Sora! Leave him — this guy has parents and a chest of gold at home. No way he's throwing that away to save the world! Go!"

"Let's go, sister. Trust Jace. On my command — we're leaving! Farstriders!"

"Knights of the Silver Hand! Lord Tirion Fordring — we're retreating!" "Kirin Tor! To me! Cast the mass teleportation ritual!"

The blue light flashing below was no longer worth watching. At the very top of the tower, the surrounding souls grew ever more tantalizing, ever more alluring. The last blue light vanished. The entire hall was silent except for the howls of undead and the roar of dark magic... But you wasted too much time. Lost too many souls. That pathetic prince was right — you pathetic, spineless worm.

I almost hope he defeats you in the end, Jace Sesso. In all the tens of thousands of years of my journey, I've never been so bereft of patience.

"Ayazi, Val'tavar, Sheluti..."

Jace ignored every sound, closed his eyes, and chanted. "Kaokayon tuat, Tayani..."

As the incantation deepened, every soul echoing through the hall — even the hatred and frenzy of the undead creatures charging at him with weapons raised — became achingly clear. Each syllable of the ancient demonic tongue seemed to strike these emotions, resonating through every spiritual shadow.

Soon, the deep pit at the center of the circular hall darkened, as if filled with black water. More and more undead creatures were sucked into it, vanishing with a single wail, becoming part of that "water."

The reliefs, statues, and runes on the walls began flaking away under the erosion of the thick darkness. The black lightning stabbing into Blackrock Spire began to twist, more and more of it curving into arcs that plunged into the "water." The souls circling above grew panicked, unsettled, then afraid. The first soul screamed as it fell into the surface, then the second, the third...

"GUL'DAN!"

A furious roar shook the hall. Anduin Lothar's eyes had opened — when? — and blazing void-violet fire shot from them. He thrashed against his chains, as if trying to tear them apart and leap down to rip his enemy to shreds. But the more he struggled, the more souls "fell."

Nefarian's laughter stopped. Every sound was drowned out by the corpse's shouts and moans, the clanging of chains against the walls. Jace kept his eyes shut, continuing the incantation.

"Kayazi, Sheluti, Paravat, Yetien, Paikaya'an."

The incantation complete, Jace clenched his right hand. The dark well before him suddenly released a clear violet radiance. All the free-floating souls in the hall began to sway, tremble, then slowly drift with the shadowy ripples in the well, flowing and circling around the hall's center.

The black lightning grew weaker, fewer. Blackrock Spire, which had looked as though it were burning with black Shadowflame moments before, now seemed from the outside to be pulled into the Twisting Nether by another dark magic. The narrow windows of the upper and topmost floors emitted violet light in succession, even coloring the nearby black mountains and dwarven ruins purple.

Beneath Hordemar City, on the bridge outside the tower walls, in the canyons, hills, and plains — every soldier still transporting wounded, purifying corpses, or fighting the burning undead that kept rising again couldn't help but look up.

Halfway up the mountain, Karlrez, pressing a hand against a thick wall on Blackrock Spire's upper level, laughed wildly under the shadow-light's glow, driving more and more Felhounds through the cracks he'd torn open with his own hands to devour the last lingering soul-energy of the collapsing weak undead.

The red dragon stared at the shadow-light flickering through the cracks in the ceiling and said quietly, "Nefarian's magic is weakening, Lady Morophil."

"No one can toy with souls in front of Jace Sesso, red dragon..." the demon said.

With the soul-powered Shadowflame's burn extinguished, the newly risen undead fell again. The dragon skeletons that had been shaking as they assembled into skeletal constructs disintegrated once more. The burning whelp corpses flying above crashed down, shattering into piles of bone.

The paladins, rangers, and Alliance vanguard who had been teleported by the mages to various spots inside and outside the base of the tower turned back to see the black dragon's magic contained. They cheered and celebrated with each other. The Alliance soldiers who had thought they'd be surrounded and killed by awakened enemies and former comrades in this hell-tower also felt saved. But the dark magical light rumbling more terrifyingly from above made everyone unable to fully release their joy and shout victory.

Khadgar had cast Time Acceleration and then a mass teleportation; by now he was so exhausted he could barely stand straight, and even the arcane light on his staff had gone out.

But he still looked around worriedly, then raised his head to gaze upward.

Outside Blackrock Spire, atop an orcish black tower in the Burning Steppes, Mathias Shaw couldn't help but narrow his eyes. He tightened his grip on the slightly bent iron railing and murmured to himself, "He didn't listen to me. Damn it."

"Trembling! Terrifying! My master!"

Sazeno clung to Jace's shoulder, shrieking. Ursalius simply let its master press its head down firmly, silently, wantonly drinking from the well's depths, until all the souls were devoured. The spikes and claws on this demon bone seemed to grow even sharper.

Marshal Anduin Lothar's corpse had dimmed. He no longer struggled or shouted, just hung there. The top of Blackrock Spire had never been so deathly silent.

"My father and my sister both made a fatal mistake — they thought you were still worth winning over."

The shadows parted. On the high steps, a dark figure had appeared — when? — his voice sounding as if he were right beside them.

"Even my brother... even Kil'jaeden. I should have realized long ago. If he couldn't finish you off on the other side of the Dark Portal, in a world he controlled, then coming back here made it even less likely."

The shadow lowered its head. For a moment, Jace almost thought it was Sabellian who had come. But Jace knew exactly who it was.

And when he saw that face, he knew he didn't even need to take out the tablet.

Because the other was already dead. On that face, beautiful beyond what seemed possible for a human, the death-energy in the eyes had replaced the black dragon's characteristic gloomy amber, shot through with shadow and molten fire.

Between his black robes, on his exposed chest, a violet light blazed brighter than the Twisting Nether itself... the Atama Crystal... Soul Song.
