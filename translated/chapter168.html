Chapter 168: A Big Shot

"You're about to see a big shot, Jace Sesso," Dushan said as he led Jace toward the door. "The host of this meeting is sure to be a pleasant surprise — anyone who's studied orcish culture or learned the orcish language ought to have heard his name."

"Who?"

Jace thought, I'm pretty sure I haven't.

"Well then it wouldn't be a surprise if I told you," Dushan said.

Still, if they were talking about a senior mage who studied orcish culture and learned the orcish language, there probably weren't that many candidates.

It wouldn't happen to be the Lord of Dalaran himself, Archmage Antonidas, personally hosting this meeting, would it?

Antonidas had always held a position on the orc question similar to that of King Terenas II. He'd had more than one public dispute with Kael'thas Sunreaver over the matter — some of them coming awfully close to outright arguments.

Fortunately, though Kael'thas was terrifyingly powerful, stubborn, and over two thousand years old, his cherishing of Dalaran was no less than that of its ruler, Antonidas himself.

So the arguments within the scope of the Council of Six never escalated to anything too serious. The prince of Silvermoon was, more often than not, the one who chose to back down after letting off steam. In the corridor outside the meeting hall's entrance, the rain outside made the interior quite dim.

Jace saw language researchers who'd come from all over the world. Some of them weren't even mages.

The most eye-catching were a man and woman in long dark-gray coats, wearing red and dark-brown scarves, carrying wide-brimmed hats — classic Gilnean dress.

Quite a few people were already chatting away, as if they'd known each other for ages.

Among all these people, besides Dushan, Jace only knew one: the red-haired elf across the way, quietly chatting with the mage beside him — the very elf Kae'fal had mentioned, the elven mage Aethas Sunreaver.

After the Scourge invaded Quel'Thalas and the Sunwell was destroyed, most of the high elves, answering Kael'thas's call, renamed themselves Sin'dorei and took vengeance as their new mission.

To that end they even used the fel crystals the prince had found in Outland as a new source of magical power, replacing the crippled Sunwell as the race's foundation.

The eyes of the elves who called themselves Sin'dorei were stained fel-green from then on, and this already paranoid and arrogant people quickly became even more hysterically extreme and crazed.

The race's hunger for fel energy alarmed many of their kin. They left Quel'Thalas for Dalaran, joining the survivors who'd been living outside to take part in the rebuilding of this magical city. They still called themselves Quel'dorei and established a new organization independent of Quel'Thalas — the Silver Covenant.

At that time, though Aethas Sunreaver had sworn to become Sin'dorei and acknowledged Kael'thas's rule, his former identity as Quel'Thalas's ambassador to Dalaran was carried forward, and he earned the Silver Covenant's respect as well.

Because the Silver Covenant chose to stand with Dalaran, they naturally drew close to the Alliance and stood opposed to the Sin'dorei who would later join the Horde.

Against that backdrop, Aethas naturally became the mediator between the two fractured peoples — and indeed between the entire Alliance and Horde — within the city of Dalaran.

His position at that time was particularly unique, far more prominent than the awkward ambassadorial role he held here in Dalaran under Kael'thas at present.

As Jace watched those elves, two Bronzebeard dwarves walked in clutching several large tomes. He noticed that everyone here was carrying one or two thick booklets stitched together from stacks of paper, or an outright bound volume.

Judging by the book held by the Stromgarde man nearby, these were all the various nations' research notes on the Orcish language.

So — what level was these people's Orcish, exactly?

Jace had only been learning Orcish for a month or two at best, and he hadn't practiced much during the recent stretch of traveling. Would he embarrass himself walking in there?

But the only ones who'd come from the Kingdom of Stormwind were the two of them, which meant Dushan believed no one else in the whole kingdom could do this better than him. At least Jace had to be a cut better than whoever was supposed to have shown up here in the original timeline, right?

Just then, the door that had been tightly shut opened, and a young mage in Dalaran robes walked out, the ring on his hand clearly the sigil of a full mage.

He bowed slightly. "My thanks to all of you for coming from so far away. Please, come in."

Jace followed the other mages in leaving their hats and staffs outside, and entered the hall together with the scholars. The scent of aged wood hit him immediately.

The hall's structure was a bit like the chamber of the United States Capitol, only much smaller. The central podium sat at the lowest point, with the audience in seats set slightly higher all around.

That podium would've been fine for displaying a flag, but for showing something as small as a letter, everyone would probably have to crowd up to the front to see — after all, there were no projectors or anything like that here.

The venue quickly grew noisy. Dushan and Jace found seats off to the side nearby.

Amid the hubbub, a tall mage jogged up the steps beside the podium in quick, two-at-a-time strides and strode to the center.

As he took his position, the conversations around the room slowly died down.

The mage wore Kirin Tor robes — the purple stole was extraordinarily fine. He wasn't just a member of the Kirin Tor; he had to be someone of very high standing. This must be the surprise Dushan had mentioned. Jace certainly didn't recognize him.

His hair was already gray-white, possibly a bit older than Marin, but far more vigorous — he looked much more alive.

This wasn't Antonidas. He was much younger — but the biggest difference was that his face was clean-shaven, so his real age was probably considerably older than he looked. After all, Jace was already used to seeing bearded mages.

In magical academia, age tended to mean authority, especially among elven mages who lived for hundreds or thousands of years. Many older mages liked to use their beards to overawe them in presence.

Antonidas, for instance, was probably around eighty, but facing Kael'thas — over two thousand years old yet looking about thirty by human standards — without that magnificent long white beard to anchor him, he'd look awfully lacking in presence just standing there.

Even Dushan beside him had a mouthful of stubble.

So someone clean-shaven like the man up there was practically a breed apart.

The mage raised his head and looked out over the thirty or forty attendees. "My sincere apologies, scholars and mages who've come here. A small matter delayed me for a moment. Let us begin now."

"I am Kel'Thuzad. I will be hosting today. Some of you may have heard of me; some may not have. A brief introduction, if I may. I am one of the members of the Council of Six, an archmage, and a professor of the academy — but in the study of the Orcish language, I am merely a beginner, a true student. Everyone here could be my teacher. So for the time ahead, I hope we can set aside distinctions of identity and rank, and focus ourselves on knowledge…"

The long string of words that followed Jace could barely take in. The host's name kept circling round and round in his mind.
