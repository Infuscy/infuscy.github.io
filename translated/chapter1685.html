Chapter 1685: The Plague and You

In the center of Holdmar City, Sora had returned to her dragon form and climbed to the top of the highest tower, previously occupied by ogres.

"Are you ready, Somniliar!" Vaelastrasz called out in the dragon tongue.

"Ready, Lord Vaelastrasz," Sora replied.

The red dragon mage raised his dragon-head scepter high, spinning it to whip up a whirlwind of fire around himself. He chanted a few words in the dragon tongue. Then a pillar of flame struck down like lightning onto Sora's head. With a burst like a firework cracking open, the whole of Holdmar City dimmed, and everyone watching the center had to shield their eyes from the blinding light.

When vision returned, Sora's scales were glowing with colorful dreamlight. Even Bellanur's Bow on Vereesa beside her bloomed in response.

Jace could feel this was the same kind of power as the blessing Queen Alexstrasza had given him — the essence of a red dragon.

Except, unlike a blood blessing that could last for centuries or more, Vaelastrasz's blessing could only last a short time, but it exploded with far more power in that brief window — enough for Sora to harness the strength to awaken life itself.

In the game, Vaelastrasz's Red Dragon Essence gave players near-infinite energy for a short time. With that boost, Sora seemed to gain confidence.

She spread her wings. The brilliant gold of her belly and wing membranes shimmered brightly under the red dragon's flame. As she murmured in the dragon tongue, paladins, priests, and even the infected being carried to quarantine rooms in every level of the tower couldn't help looking up. It was as if the fatigue and worry of the past few moments had lifted, and even the advance of the necrotic plague's corruption had slowed.

"Quite... magnificent," Vaelastrasz said, looking up at her. "I can see an unwavering conviction in her eyes. She possesses the pride and confidence that green dragons have gained from guarding the Emerald Dream for ten thousand years. At the same time, she carries a humility rare among her kin."

"That makes her more suited than many of her stronger kin to guard the Emerald Dream, to become an Emerald Guardian of Queen Ysera, Jace Sesso. I don't understand why she turned down Queen Ysera's invitation. That's a position every green dragon dreams of — even for adults."

"I thought you, being a dragon yourself, would understand better than me," Jace said.

"Hard to say." Vaelastrasz said. "I've heard of dragons who traveled with mortals. Blue dragons do it most. Bronze dragons, I've heard of some cases. Almost none of the others."

"Why? Does it have to do with color?" Jace asked.

"It actually has to do with the Aspects," the red dragon explained. "Lord Malygos stopped focusing on his duties as Guardian of the Leylines ten thousand years ago. He stopped caring about his flight — even his own son, Arygos, meant nothing to him. So many blue dragons left the Nexus to fight their own battles. As for the bronze dragons... I've only met King Nozdormu twice at the Dragonblight. I'm sure most bronze dragons have seen him even fewer times than I have."

"Actually, I'd love to go on adventures with you to unknown places."

"You're welcome, Vaelastrasz." Jace said. "That way I can fill in the 'missing' stories of other-colored dragons traveling with mortals."

Vaelastrasz laughed twice. "Unfortunately, the Queen would never permit it. And my responsibilities wouldn't allow it either. So, regrettably, no."

"Truly a shame." Jace sighed. "I was hoping we'd have a bigger dragon to ride on our trip to Kalimdor."

Vaelastrasz's face stiffened. Jace quickly added, "No — I didn't mean it like that."

Sora's shriek shattered the silence of Blackrock Spire. A fierce cold wind blasted through the corridors and chambers, making Jace shield his eyes. The whole interior of Holdmar City howled with sharp gusts.

The wind was like a signal — a whistle. As it calmed slightly, more and more flies and beetles emerged from every direction, as if answering the wind's call, answering Sora's call.

Jace could even feel the demonic energy still lingering on these insects, though much of it had been purified by that gust. The swarm grew thicker, gradually gathering into a black cloud in the air. Now every Alliance soldier watching panicked — the priests had just warned that the main vector spreading the necrotic plague was the flies in Blackrock Spire. Many dwarves yelped, slapped their heads, and ducked into doorways to hide.

"Didn't Sora say she was going to deal with the bugs? Now I feel like she's planning to solve us with the bugs instead!" Grede shouted.

"Aren't dwarves immune to bug bites?" Jace asked.

"Damn, that's too many bugs!" Grede yelled.

The dwarf's shouts were nearly drowned out by the insect's buzzing. They gathered around the green dragon, their mass darkening even the dreamlight on her scales.

Just as the onlookers' hearts began to pound, not knowing what would happen next, the oppressive swarm suddenly became chaotic. The buzzing lost its rhythm. The flying insects began to fly in disarray.

But in the process, many bugs died and fell. If you looked closely, you could see they were tearing at each other, chasing and biting. Big ones ate small ones. Small ones ganged up on big ones. Similar-sized ones shredded each other.

Before long, the swarm thinned. The stone floor was carpeted with a gray layer, like dark cotton — wings and insect corpses.

The soldiers stared dumbfounded as it all happened. Even the dwarves who'd hidden came out cheering. There were barely any bugs left in the air now. Sora shot out her long tongue and swallowed the last survivors. Her body was radiant once more. Blackrock Spire felt significantly quieter than before Sora had climbed up — so quiet it was almost unsettling to those inside.

The priests of the Silver Hand and the Church banged their staves and swords against their shields excitedly. Varian breathed a huge sigh of relief.

Alleria and Vereesa, who had been clutching each other's hands in tension, finally relaxed. As long as the demon's insect swarm in the tower was eliminated, winning against the necrotic plague was half done.

"Sister Sora!" Needlefang shouted excitedly. "Well done, Sora!"

Jace raised his fist in excitement too. Sora twisted her head to look over, squinting proudly. She was still a young dragon, after all.

"Too bad I couldn't catch the demon, master," Morofil said, sounding guilty.

"We'll find them," Jace said quietly.

"That was... astonishing!" Kurdran shouted.

"The one who should thank Sora most is the master of this tower — Dagran Thaurissan, that is." Muradin said disapprovingly. "Where is he?"

"He was worried the Blackrock Spire treasury would be emptied by fleeing orcs and dragonkin, so he took a personal guard of Dark Irons to secure the gate — under the pillared hall Prince Nefarian and Master Sesso wrecked, Thane!" a Wildhammer dwarf shouted.

"Why didn't anyone tell me? I've got to see that too!" Kurdran said. "Grede! Want to come?"

"Of course, High Thane!" Grede shouted. "Let's go! We've got to claim Sora's payment for purifying this tower!"

Jace was about to follow — he couldn't help much with plague treatment anyway — when a priest came over and stopped him and Vereesa.

"Master Sesso, Ranger-Lord Vereesa. We've just given some of the infected soldiers that furbolg purification potion."

"I hope it works," Vereesa said.

"One officer — his infection was relatively mild — responded very quickly to the potion," the priest said.

"That effective?" Vereesa raised an eyebrow. "Even for the furbolgs, it takes days to work..."

"Maybe the restorative potion provided a foundation. He was in the vanguard attacking the upper spire, so his restorative potion hadn't fully worn off," the priest said. "I suspect the necrotic plague the insects here gave the soldiers might be the same type — or at least very similar — to what the Winterfall furbolgs contracted. I had some questions about this kind of necrotic plague that might help with further treatment."

Vereesa glanced at Jace. "Hold on. We have a few things to discuss first. I'll find you later, brother."

"Much appreciated, Ranger-Lord." The priest pointed to a door around the corner. "I'll be in that room."

After the priest left, Vereesa checked that no one was nearby. Then she lowered her voice. "How do we explain this to the priests, Jace? About your staff?"

Before Jace could speak, Needlefang grabbed Vereesa's belt. "What does it mean, the plague in Kalimdor is similar to the plague here? Is the demon looking for your staff also a Nathrezim?"

Jace shook Usar'ru gently. "No. This staff originally belonged to an ancient Nathrezim king. But the demon looking for it... is an Ered'ruin. Named Dasmola."

"Wait, Jace." Vereesa lowered her voice. "Did you forget? The plague in Winterfall was caused by this staff's power seeping out of the dream. Dasmola might have helped the satyrs break the dream barrier that the guardian Scavel had set, but the necrotic plague plaguing the Frostwhisper furbolgs wasn't her doing."

"I haven't forgotten," Jace said.

Then he realized a problem.

If this plague spread, would someone connect the necrotic plague with Usar'ru and the wandering Black Knight?

Thinking about it, a chill ran down the back of his neck. He couldn't help swallowing.

Beside him, the ranger's face had gone pale too. Jace knew she was thinking the same thing.
