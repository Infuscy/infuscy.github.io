Chapter 1686: Haunting Presence

The dwarves had all run off after Nefarian's treasure and gold. Undeniably, most of it was probably Dagran Thaurissan's stash — the orcs and black dragons occupying this place for over a decade probably hadn't spent their time scouring the world for treasure. But it looked like the Wildhammer and Bronzebeard dwarves were going to forcefully deny that one.

Vaelastrasz joined the clergy in sweeping the tower for remaining undead and helping the infected. Varian and several Alliance commanders also joined in. The Trollbane cousins were both wounded. The Farstriders were still searching every corner of the tower for lingering enemies. The most elite gryphon riders had already rushed to Northshire to guard Nefarian's corpse. Blackrock Spire was sorely lacking hands for the cleanup.

But the demon's insect swarm had been cleared — meaning the root of the disease's spread was nearly eliminated. The furbolg recipe was effective too. Containment efforts would now be twice as effective with half the effort. The Church of the Holy Light and the Kirin Tor were optimistic.

They each said their goodbyes and left. Now, on this platform, only their group remained, plus a pile of bugs on the ground that had lost the ability to fly — cleansed of all demonic power by Sora's magic and left twitching, waiting to die.

Vereesa and Jace were both silent for a while. Needlefang looked at them and didn't dare say anything. Sora came over. She'd been quite pleased with herself for handling the plague bugs in the tower — but seeing the grim looks on her three companions' faces, her excitement faded.

"What's wrong?" Sora asked.

"Hopefully we're just overthinking it," Vereesa said quietly. "But... the potion we brought back from Kalimdor to suppress the necrotic plague — it works very well on the plague in this tower."

"Isn't that a good thing?" Sora asked.

"Good for the mortals here. But good for my master?" Sazeno jumped from Jace's chest onto the green dragon's shoulder and whispered in her ear. "Don't forget — the furbolgs spent decades refining that recipe to fight the necrotic power leaking from Usar'ru. Why would it suddenly be so effective here? Those damn Nathrezim!"

"Everyone knows the demons did it, Jace," Vereesa said. "The plague here is very similar to the dreadlord magic recorded in Dalaran. Even the Kirin Tor mages say so. The Sons of Lothar know too."

"But nobody actually saw a demon cast that spell," Jace said.

"I saw it. Vaelastrasz knows exactly who was behind it. So do you..." The ranger paused. "Even if it was in a dream."

"But nobody knows what your staff really is either," Sora said. "Why would they connect the demon's chaos here to you?"

"Kil'jaeden knows, green lady." Morofil said. "The Nathrezim he sent to this world know too. Even Nefarian knew."

"Yes. Nefarian said before he died that he'd be waiting for me in Usar'ru. He knew I'd use the staff to devour his soul. He knew what the outcome of losing to me would be. There's no way the orcs told him that," Jace said.

"He said the demons tricked him," Needlefang said.

"So he finally realized the demons never actually intended to help him build an undead legion to break free from the Old Gods?" Sora asked.

"So these Nathrezim came for you?" Vereesa said. "Nefarian was just a tool — to gather the Alliance's elite in Blackrock Spire... then pin the blame for spreading the plague and causing a necrotic disaster on you?"

"My apologies, master." Morofil bowed her head, folding her wings tight. "I did underestimate that Nathrezim's cunning. I didn't think he could pull off such a large-scale plague ritual so quickly... If I could have rooted him out, everything would be much clearer."

"You're saying the hunter was toyed with by the prey, Sayad?" Sazeno said. "Or do you think Kil'jaeden would send someone like Tichondrius or Varimathras — someone even you would struggle with — to deal with my master?"

"I'm not making excuses for my failure, Sazeno." Morofil stared at the imp. "But do you think the holder of Usar'ru isn't worth the combined efforts of the current Nathrezim king and his most powerful lieutenants? And this Nathrezim clearly knew my capabilities, and knew from the start how to deal with me. Like Lady Vereesa Windrunner said — he was just waiting for me to chase him."

"Damn it." Sazeno dropped his head. "You've got a point. My master is also Usar'ru's master. The ones who want this soul-scythe back most — besides Kil'jaeden — are those devils of Nathreza!"

"So did the demon king miscalculate?" Vereesa looked at the soul shard in Jace's hand. "The necrotic plague is contained within Blackrock Spire. Instead, he lost an Atama Crystal that was already in the Burning Legion's hands, and one Nathrezim died at Jace's hands."

"But he did show the entire Alliance elite Usar'ru's power." Jace said quietly. "I had to use it to raise the Soul Well, to seize the souls Nefarian was using to resurrect the entire tower's corpses... Many of Quel'Thalas and Dalaran's magisters and archmages know that Dar'khan Drathir tried to kill me at Raven Hill before because he thought I had leads on Usar'ru. I'm actually, step by step, proving Dar'khan's suspicions right."

He looked up. "The souls protected by the Soul Song — by Marshal Anduin Lothar's lingering will — even Kadzan couldn't touch. Even Usar'ru's soul drain couldn't overpower them. Only by raising the Soul Well — such a powerful spell — could it tear through that will. And in doing so, it showed the whole Alliance what I really am... Maybe that was part of the plan from the start."

"Wait, Jace." Vereesa raised a hand. "How did Kil'jaeden know you mastered that spell? Grede and I both saw it for the first time. Even Master Kadzan was shocked. Sora and I had only heard you briefly mention it before."

"You're not the first to witness it," Jace said. "A group from SI:7 and the Dagger Clan already saw it in Duskwood. King Varian knows about it too. I used that spell to drive off the ogres in that valley."

"You're saying one of those people was a demon in disguise?" Vereesa asked.

"Then just dig out his soul, and you'll know whether he's a demon or a mortal. Simple enough..."

"No, Vereesa." Jace shook his head. "What are you saying? How could I do that?"

"What? Do what?" The ranger blinked.

Jace looked up and glanced at the demon skeleton lying nearby. Then, a figure emerged from the stairwell below.

Vereesa turned to look. Her expression became incredibly complicated.

"Mathias Shaw."

"You're all here." Mathias Shaw waved. "Glad to see you're alright, Master Sesso. I thought—"

"Thought everyone would think I'm a demon, after seeing that spell?" Jace asked.

"Uh..." Mathias's face turned pale instantly. He stepped back, stumbled on a piece of rubble, and his hand instinctively went to the sword at his hip, then jerked away as if shocked.

"That spell was certainly terrifying. But it saved everyone, Master Sesso. Don't get me wrong. My earlier warning not to use it was because I was worried about you casting it against the orc army — not in a crisis like this. If that black dragon king had succeeded in casting his necromantic spell, the entire Burning Steppes would have become a fortress for the undead army. Even the corpses at my camp would have risen! And I was at least four or five miles from Blackrock Spire's gate!"

He took a deep breath to calm himself. He looked around to make sure no one else was nearby. "I'm trying to say... your reputation was at a very delicate point. I was worried that any behavior the tradition wouldn't accept could break that fragile balance. Even if it was well-intentioned... Not only would you suffer, but your friends, King Varian — even the whole kingdom of Stormwind — would fall into trouble. That's what I was worried about. Not any doubt about your intentions!"

"Even if you were right about that, Sesso—"

Needlefang grabbed Jace's cloak. She barely breathed the words: "A lot of Shadow Council members eventually found out about us taking the journal. The warlocks who escaped Auchindoun were loyal to the Burning Legion. They definitely told Kil'jaeden about Gul'dan's journal being stolen!"

Jace said nothing. He looked back at Morofil. The succubus shook her head. Jace knew what she meant. She was sure there were no Nathrezim in disguise among that group in the valley — not even among the ogres.

Right. If those Nathrezim knew Morofil existed, how could they expose themselves so easily in front of this hunter?

The shadows seemed to lift. The sounds around him became clearer. Jace looked at Needlefang's bright yellow eyes and took a deep breath. The little black dragon's grip on his hand was trembling from tension.

"Relax, Mathias. I'm not blaming you. Just... joking."

"Don't joke at a time like this..." Shaw steadied himself. "Damn, I could barely breathe there. My heart almost jumped out. If I hadn't already taken a restorative potion, I'd think I'd caught the necrotic plague too."

"Sorry," Jace said. "What did you come to the tower for?"

"Ah, I almost forgot." Mathias tugged at his collar. "I was looking for the king. To discuss bringing Marshal Lothar's body back to Stormwind."

"The king is one floor up, with the church priests." Jace pointed above.

"Much appreciated, Jace." Mathias nodded. "And... thank you for saving this entire campaign. I didn't personally witness your magic. But if the black dragon king had successfully reanimated every orc and troll corpse at my camp, my agents and I would definitely be one of them by now."
