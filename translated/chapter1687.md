## Chapter 1687: Tear of Neltharion

On a terrace outside Blackrock Spire's upper levels — far from the half-collapsed pillared hall, far from the lower spire thick with lingering necrotic and shadow energy — Jace let the scorching wind blowing in from the Burning Steppes wash over him. It helped him relax a little.

Earlier, Sora had told him that maybe spending too much time in Blackrock Spire had made his mind run wild. He'd been doubtful at first. Now it seemed green dragons really knew best about self-care. He did feel lighter, not seeing everyone as a potential dreadlord anymore.

The sky was darkening. The occasional sounds drifting from the tower — priests chanting, dwarves laughing, humans and elves chatting — felt out of place. These weren't the sounds that should be coming from inside Blackrock Spire.

Dagran Thaurissan had been eager to get all the Alliance soldiers out of the tower as soon as possible. He was just afraid the Wildhammer and Bronzebeard clans would stay too long and get confused about who Blackrock Spire actually belonged to. But after hearing about the necrotic plague ravaging the place — that it couldn't be cleared in a day or two — and worrying the Dark Irons couldn't handle it alone and might bring the plague into Shadowforge City, he stopped rushing them out.

No new infected had appeared in the last half-day. The Silver Hand and the Church had relaxed a bit. The Alliance soldiers' mood was starting to swing back toward the celebratory state they'd been in right after defeating Nefarian.

"Jace."

A familiar voice came from the doorway behind him. Jace turned. It was Kadzan.

"I didn't see a gryphon fly in, Kadzan."

"Now's still an acceptable time to use teleportation openly." Kadzan bowed politely, then approached. "Lady Vereesa Windrunner said you were here. I didn't quite believe it. I didn't expect you to actually be here."

"What's so unbelievable about it?" Jace asked.

Kadzan squinted, clasped his hands behind his back, and gazed at the black, scorched earth of the Burning Steppes. "A long time ago, I read a book. About a Bronzebeard champion who defeated a legendary evil dragon on a ridge in eastern Loch Modan. After his victory, he sat on a cliff, staring at the shimmering surface of the Lochnal, and stared for a very long time."

"What was he thinking about?" Jace asked.

"I don't know either." Kadzan said. "But it definitely wasn't about a nest of Nathrezim hiding in Ironforge, planning to sway minds with rumors and paint him as some new evil dragon."

Jace chuckled. "They told you?"

"That little black dragon's mouth runs like a Gnomeregan machine gun, non-stop. Hard not to hear it." The mage said. "But honestly, I'm not surprised either."

"When I learned Nefarian had gotten an Atama Crystal from the Burning Legion, I already suspected dreadlords might be involved. We'd heard in Draenor that Kil'jaeden had sent Nathrezim to search for the lost Atama Crystals. Combined with the rumors of scythe-wielding Black Knights and plagues spreading in Lordaeron — which we've discussed before..."

"Dreadlords aren't particularly powerful in raw magic or strength. But that makes them harder to guard against. They don't need elaborate summoning rituals to manifest on Azeroth."

"Thinking about that valley, it was quite a close call. Without a green dragon's help, the consequences would have been unthinkable. My apologies, Jace. I thought you might not be able to catch Nefarian, so I returned to Stormwind early to organize defenses and notify people to evacuate — including your parents. The result was that I ended up completely stuck inside the city walls, accomplishing nothing."

"Returning to Stormwind early let us focus on dealing with that dragon, Kadzan," Jace said.

"It was you, Jace." Kadzan corrected. "Drop the plural."

"Everyone saw how you fought Nefarian in that tower. Afterwards, the black dragon prince was so weakened he couldn't even stabilize an elemental channel. I just used a simple anti-magic pulse to shut down his dimensional portal, forcing him to fly away on his wings... And that, right there, is the key to winning this coming chaos."

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

"Become that hero." Kadzan said. "Become the hero who ended the Orc Wars. The dragon-slaying hero who defeated the black dragon prince Nefarian atop Blackrock Spire. Become the hero who won the third Windrunner sister — beloved and admired by both Quel'Thalas and the human kingdoms. And this time, even the newly crowned Mountain King of Molten Core, or the beautiful and deadly Windrunner princess herself, can't steal your glory."

"The Sons of Lothar, returned from Draenor. Hundreds of soldiers from every kingdom who fought in this Blackrock campaign. Prince of Stromgarde, for one. And King Varian Wrynn. They're all your allies. They can all testify to what you did here. No matter how the kings, nobles, and high mages who've doubted you in the past see you — they can't just blow away the people's flowers and applause with a puff of air."

"Then let's see how Kil'jaeden plans to smear a hero like that. See how much effort and how many methods they're willing to expend. Every attempt increases their risk of exposure. This time, when he tried to control Vaelastrasz's mind, one of his most capable lieutenants, vying for the Blackwing Lair, ended up in your hands. By the way — let me see that soul shard?"

Jace handed over the soul shard. Kadzan examined it from all sides and continued. "Like I said — summoning a Nathrezim into this world doesn't happen without a trace. You know there's a very powerful Nathrezim imprisoned in the Violet Hold in Dalaran — Kasanathir. Let's see how many more mistakes they make. One day, the demon's scheme will be uncovered. Two thousand years ago, Guardian Alodi and his friends won that victory. Now it's the last Guardian's apprentice's turn — me, Kadzan, and my friends — to carry on the spirit of the Order of Tirisfal."

"As for Usar'ru — remember this one thing. No matter how the rumors develop, don't admit it's in your hands." The archmage lowered his voice. "No one has seen it with their own eyes except you and your group. So no one can convict you. The Nathrezim are in the shadows. Don't forget — your staff is in the shadows too. Sometimes I wish I didn't know it really was Usar'ru. What rotten luck."

"I know," Jace said. "But what if I get 'uncovered' before the demon's scheme is?"

"Is being a warlock a prerequisite for assuming the worst in everything?" Kadzan smirked. "Worst case — locked up in the Violet Hold. I assume you've been mentally prepared for that."

"That's true." Jace chuckled too. "Thanks for being willing to help me out like this, Kadzan."

"Don't mention it. Personally and professionally, I have to do everything I can to help you." Kadzan turned around, leaning on the stone railing. "Turalyon and Alleria even more so. You'll be family soon. Don't feel like you owe us anything."

Jace nodded. "About the Nathrezim — do you know Onyxia is still alive? I'm planning to go to Kalimdor to find her and ask about Nefarian."

"Vaelastrasz mentioned it." Kadzan said. "Are you sure she'll be willing to talk?"

"As long as she wants to take down the demons who destroyed the Blackwing Lair's nest," Jace said.

"Logical enough. But it still sounds insane." The archmage let out a long sigh, then pulled a box from his robes. "Take this. To prove Nefarian is dead. Carrying this to Kalimdor is easier than hauling his head — which is bigger than two carriages."

"What is it?" Jace opened the box. Inside was something like a piece of obsidian shard, with silvery liquid flowing inside its black, translucent shell.

"The Kirin Tor found it on Nefarian's horn." Kadzan said. "Back when Lord Daval Prestor visited Lordaeron's capital, his nephew wore this treasure around his neck. Many mages and nobles had very vivid memories of this strange jewel... Thinking about it now, that young Prestor was probably Nefarian himself."

No doubt about it. This was the Tear of Neltharion.
