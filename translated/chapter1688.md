## Chapter 1688: About the Funeral

In the early morning, sunlight streamed through the cracks and broken windows of the shattered pillar hall, passing through the volcanic ash drifting down from Blackrock Mountain, forming pale grey beams of light.

The shadow scars left by the Hand of Gul'dan and the black lightning had mostly faded naturally or been purified by the Church's priests, save for a few scorched black patches that still remained.

But the broken steps, the collapsed statues and pillars, the vast stretches of collapsed high walls -- they still seemed to tell the story of the decisive battle from a few days ago that had made the entire Burning Steppes tremble.

Even Jace himself hadn't realized, until after the battle ended, just how far the maddening shadow storm the two of them had stirred up had actually reached. While in the thick of it, especially under the influence of the Destruction Potion, all his attention had been on the humanoid dragon, leaving no room for anything else.

Today, the Sons of Lothar planned to retrieve Marshal Anduin Lothar's body from the chains. Though they hadn't announced it to the entire Blackrock Spire, word had already spread.

Before dawn, aside from the sick soldiers, the wounded, and the medics tending them, most of the allied soldiers had gathered here of their own accord.

Dark Iron dwarf craftsmen had climbed to the top early, ready to dismantle the chains. Under the gaze of a thousand pairs of eyes, the Kirin Tor mages used Levitation to rise high and support the Marshal's fragile remains. Then Dagran Thaurissan shouted the order, and the craftsmen tore down the chains, the iron links swinging down and striking the walls like funeral bells, while the mages carefully lowered Anduin Lothar and brought him slowly to the ground.

The entire process was utterly silent, with barely even the sound of wind. Aside from the hum of arcane magic, there was only the faint rumble from deep within Blackrock Mountain.

But whether it was because the Molten Core had collapsed, or because even the elements of earth and fire held respect for this human hero who had fought against the Orcs and the Burning Legion, even the mountain's rumbling sounded subdued and solemn -- not like the restless stirring of an abyss, but more like a silent prayer.

This was the first time Jace had seen Anduin Lothar this close. Long before the Orc Wars, Lothar had already been a hero renowned across the Seven Kingdoms during the Gurubashi Troll Wars.

He had grown up in Lordaeron, but had heard tales of this great warrior countless times since childhood. Over time, the memories of Lothar he'd brought from his previous life had gradually merged with the information from this world, and his view of the man was no longer limited to that of a story character or fictional figure.

So even if he felt things a bit differently, he could still understand what every Alliance member in this great hall felt in their hearts.

In the legends, Lothar was a knight with white hair and beard, clad in the golden lion armor of the Kingdom of Stormwind, wrapped in the crimson cloak representing Arathi royal blood -- a godlike figure, as though the Holy Light itself had descended. But now, all that remained was a hunched, severely damaged, collapsed and twisted suit of armor, with scraps of cloth clinging to a gaunt skeleton.

Rend Blackhand and Nefarian had never intended to discard his remains. Lothar's body hadn't severely decayed in the ten-plus years since his death, and Jace could still feel the power contained within those remains -- the will amplified by the Soul's Song, strong enough to resist Ursales. That was probably why Teron Gorefiend had been so determined to seize it from the Blackrock orcs and be reborn as the most powerful death knight in history.

The mages laid Marshal Lothar on a blue blanket embroidered with the Stormwind lion crest. After they stepped back, the leaders of the Sons of Lothar couldn't help but approach.

Khadgar knelt beside the blanket and murmured, "Even now, I still find it hard to accept that you're truly gone, my lord."

Turalyon stood beside Khadgar and took a deep breath. Alleria looked at him for a long time without a word.

The paladin looked around at the Sons of Lothar leaders -- Danath and Kurdran -- then spoke softly: "We've retaken Blackrock Spire, teacher."

"We've defeated Ner'zhul and his Horde, closed the Dark Portal, and forever shut their corruption out from this world. And just as the Sons of Lothar promised when we were founded, we've not only avenged the countless soldiers, civilians, and innocent lives lost in the Orc Wars -- we've also destroyed their demonic fortress and dark magic, ensuring they can never open another Dark Portal to plague other innocent worlds."

"For over a decade, this world has seen more and more heroes emerge. More young people have grown up, as brave and steadfast as your generation, as faithful to the Holy Light, to goodness and justice. And this world has been saved by their strength, time and again. I think... teacher, you can rest easy now."

Whether it was an illusion or not, Jace felt that after hearing Turalyon's words, Anduin Lothar's body seemed to relax a little, even if it wasn't outwardly obvious.

The pale grey sunlight seemed to shift, drawing closer to the Sons of Lothar and the Alliance Marshal they surrounded. If a wizard like him wasn't particularly sensitive to omens or the "revelations" of the Holy Light, many priests in the hall couldn't help but look up, gazing at the light streaming through the cracks above. They must have felt it too.

For a moment, Jace felt as though he wasn't at the peak of Blackrock Spire at all, but back in that little church in the corner of Lordaeron's capital city -- small, dim, but warm.

"If I die someday, you'd better stand in front of my corpse and say something like that, too -- move all the Alliance kings and nobles to tears. Jace Sesso."

Grede stared at the Sons of Lothar ahead as he spoke, then couldn't help but let out a long sigh. "Recite all my heroic deeds, the powerful enemies I've defeated, all those... profoundly philosophical and inspiring things I've said -- the ones I'm always telling you -- like you're reciting poetry."

"The deep blue skies of Kirthaven, the magnificent scenery of Thundermar Peak, set against this solemn scene, ah." Saying this, the dwarf even couldn't help but squint his eyes.

"No," Jace said.

"Why not?" Grede asked.

"Way too much content," Jace said. "Find a bishop or one of your dwarven sages to recite it slowly. If I had to ramble on that long, I'd rather die first."

Grede cursed in Dwarven. "Can't even agree to such a simple request. What can I even count on you for?"

"More importantly." Jace watched Khadgar helping to arrange Lothar's clothing. "When that time comes, if I'm not dead yet, I'll probably be the most infamous warlock in the Seven Kingdoms -- not much better than Medivh. You think I'd even be allowed to attend your funeral?"

"Then just resurrect me on the spot," Grede said.

Jace stared at him in shock. The dwarf chuckled and waved a hand. "Just kidding. Don't look so tense. But you really are worried about those disguised demons getting you thrown into the Violet Hold, aren't you?"

He leaned in close to Jace's ear. "If that day really comes, I'll take Sulfuras, smash open the Violet Hold's gates, shatter those magical seals, and break you out. Trust me... it'll be a piece of cake! Then we'll get those two dragons to carry us to Kalimdor. That girl Sora's already been completely corrupted by you, and Needleflame's always been a little menace anyway. No one's gonna say no."

Hearing this, Jace couldn't help but glance at the little black dragon perched up high. She was still solemnly imitating the paladins, paying her respects to Lothar's wrapped body.

"I'm the thief who stole the Dwarven artifact Sulfuras. You're a Dalaran fugitive being fiercely protected by a crazy elven murder-"

"Wait, what? Crazy elven murderer?"

"Jace Sesso gets thrown in Dalaran's prison -- just imagine what happens to those mages... So the three of us will be a perfect match with that evil daughter of Deathwing, plus a green dragon who has no idea what's going on." The dwarf muttered quietly. "Anyway, the Order of Tirisfal's already been wiped out by Medivh. Nothing to be afraid of. Maybe we'll end up as neighbors with Lady Aegwynn at this rate."

Hearing the idea of being neighbors with Aegwynn, Jace choked and coughed several times, then noticed that over by the Farstriders, Vereesa was glancing in his direction. When she caught his eye, she smiled. She probably had no idea what the tall human and short dwarf beside her were whispering about.
