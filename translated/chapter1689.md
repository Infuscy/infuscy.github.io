## Chapter 1689: Dinner in the Tower

The hall at the upper entrance of Blackrock Spire had been temporarily converted into a "dining hall" by the Allied forces. Compared to the filthy and chaotic city of Hordemar -- contaminated by troll witchcraft and ogre habitation -- at least the quarters of the Horde's elite troops still retained some semblance of order that Alliance members could recognize.

Marshal Anduin Lothar's body had been properly preserved, set to be sent to Stormwind City for burial once the remaining sick and wounded had recovered enough to travel. The soldiers, who had been quite lively for the past couple of days, were now unusually quiet.

The night before, when Jace and the others had eaten here, they'd still heard plenty of dwarves outside excitedly talking about the battle in the Foundry Forge.

The Dark Iron Ragefire warriors and the Horde's Splinteraxe Skirmishers fighting at great heights on chains had left a deep impression even on the Alliance soldiers. Then there was the drakonid battle after the mysterious black dragon girl opened the gates of Blackrock Spire, and Varian Wrynn's duel with Rend Blackhand, which made many Dark Irons look at the young king with new respect...

After that came the drakonid commander Razorgore, who blew himself up to collapse half of the Blackwing Lair, and the twisted beast Chromaggus, who could control the flow of time. Many people felt that the legendary battles they'd experienced in this Blackrock Spire campaign were more spectacular than all the tavern poets' songs they'd heard in their entire lives put together.

But in the eyes of the veterans who'd come back from Draenor, it was thrilling enough but not that exaggerated. The siege of Hellfire Citadel, the assault on Karabor, the great escape from Auchindoun, the tens of thousands of Burning Legion demons and fel-crazed orcs who'd drunk the blood of Magtheridon, the final collapse of Draenor...

Now, maybe because Lothar's body had finally been found, lifting a weight that had hung over every Alliance soldier's heart since the last Battle of Blackrock Spire, the tension of all these years had finally relaxed. Weariness had somewhat dulled the joy of victory.

Sora and Needleflame were wolfing down coarse bread and wolf meat smoked sausages. The two of them hadn't been able to get enough to eat these past couple of days -- they'd been fighting constantly, dealing with the plague, and they were exhausted. Their appalling table manners were almost indistinguishable from scavengers eating carrion in a swamp.

Vereesa was carefully picking at a piece of bread. Grede had been watching her for a while as he ate, and finally couldn't help asking, "What are you doing?"

"These dwarven bakers don't check the ingredients before baking," Vereesa said.

"You think you're still living in the royal palace of Silvermoon City!" Grede snorted. "They're full of bugs, Thunderfist. A lot of dead bugs!" Vereesa said.

Grede paused, then coughed heavily, spitting the unchewed bread into his hand. He looked for the bugs for a moment, couldn't find any, then turned to Jace. "I won't catch anything from eating them, will I?"

"You're a dwarf, what are you afraid of?" Jace said.

"Uh, fair enough." Grede shoved the bread back into his mouth.

"If you don't like it, give me the rest of your bread." Sora watched the dwarf chewing. "You don't believe I can purify all the flying insects in this tower, do you?"

"It's not that I don't believe you." Grede lowered his voice. "Alright, maybe a little." Sora muttered a few curses in Draconic and kept eating. The dwarf whispered, "Didn't catch that."

"Earlier Khadgar mentioned he wanted to give you something that could help in Kalimdor. What is it, Jace?" Vereesa asked.

Jace placed the box containing the Tear of Neltharion on the stone table. "This."

In the game, the Tear of Neltharion greatly boosted spell power and spell hit chance, but here, Jace had no idea what effect it would actually have. He hadn't yet fully adjusted to Nefarian's demonic soul or Ursales' suddenly strengthened power over the past couple of days, and he wasn't keen on testing the device's energy by casting spells.

But Needleflame, who'd been eating bread, noticed the thing and immediately tilted her eyes toward it, her constantly moving mouth slowing down.

"Hard to say if showing this to Onyxia will make her willing to talk or fly into a rage on the spot," Jace said. "But at the very least, it proves we actually killed her brother."

"Uh-huh. Killed her brother, and then she has to sit down for a nice chat." Grede muttered. "But we've already seen how special the Black Dragonflight's family values are back in Draenor. I wouldn't be surprised even if she genuinely didn't care."

"Speaking for myself, at least I'd feel more at ease." Vereesa said quietly. "If Sylvanas ever turned into an undead and kept trying to turn me into one too, all while telling me how great being dead would be... just thinking about it gives me chills."

The others just raised their eyebrows or sighed. Jace stared at Vereesa for a moment. He didn't say anything, but his heart was pounding furiously. He really didn't like this strange coincidence.

"So, what's the plan?" Grede lightly tapped both hands on the table. "Deliver the Wind Prince's head to the desert he mentioned, visit our little Needleflame's aunt, and... I want to meet Mosa Timber."

"You don't have to say that last one so quietly, Grede Thunderfist." Jace tore off a piece of bread and tossed it into his mouth. "Mosa Timber is more important than the first two combined."

Grede gave an embarrassed, sheepish laugh. Vereesa smiled too, seeming to breathe a sigh of relief. She exchanged a glance with Sora beside her, and Sora looked at Jace, as if also a little reassured.

"The Wind Prince's head carries too much magical influence, so unfortunately, we can't travel by ship this time." Jace looked at Needleflame and Sora. "But we can take a ship on the way back. Let you two experience the mode of transportation us pitiful mortals use."

Sora chuckled. "I'm actually looking forward to it."

"Just so you know, I'm not carrying anyone's luggage. Doesn't matter where we're going." Needleflame muttered.

"Don't worry, we won't make you carry luggage. You can carry whatever you want, little one!" Grede said.

"I'm not carrying anything!" Needleflame immediately shoved a sausage into her mouth. Everyone at the table couldn't help but chuckle quietly.

"I'm thinking we head to the southernmost desert in Kalimdor first." Jace continued. "We can rest at Gadgetzan, resupply a bit, then go to the place the Wind Prince Thunderaan mentioned. Then head north to the tauren's plains to find Mosa Timber. Finally, head to the swamps to find those black dragons. If Mosa's in Ashenvale with the Kaldorei, we'll go find Onyxia first."

"Sounds good. South to north." Sora said. "Just hope the wind elemental's temple isn't too hard to find."

"He can talk, can't he? We'll just let him guide us." Vereesa said.

"On the way, I want to stop by Mulgore." Grede said. "I hear that's where the tauren lived for generations, before the damn centaurs took it from them. I've gotta see what our new home looks like, right? What do you all think?"

"Looking at the map, it's not too far off our route. As long as Sora's willing, we can do it." Jace said.

Sora nodded. Needleflame looked up again. "You've met Princess Onyxia, Jace. What... what kind of black dragon is she? You all seem really afraid of her."

"She is terrifying." Jace said. "But I don't think she'll necessarily have any ill intent toward you. Even Nefarian didn't try to hurt you."

"I remember how he looked when he saw you at the end, Needleflame." Vereesa added. "Maybe your kind... even Nefarian... aren't as completely insane as we thought."

Needleflame didn't say anything, just pondered for a while. But nobody could give her a definite answer now anyway.

Late at night, Jace and Vereesa found an empty corridor. The ranger looked around, then grabbed Jace's hand. "Morofel isn't here, is she?"

"She's not in the mood to cause trouble right now," Jace said.

Vereesa curled one corner of her mouth, wrapped her arms around Jace's neck, and pressed him to the ground, eagerly pulling off his shirt. But when she saw the sword wound on his abdomen, her expression tightened.

"This is... Nefarian's work?"

"Could've been mine, too." Jace gave a helpless smile. "Hard to explain."

"What happened..." The elf looked again at the ghastly wound, half-healed by shadow flesh, and frowned. "I've got patience. I'm listening."

"I... accidentally mimicked Nefarian's mind control spell using Dark Simulacrum," Jace said.

"So you controlled him?" Vereesa wagged her finger back and forth. "Or did he control you?"

"Both, I guess." Jace said. "We both controlled each other, but each retained a sliver of consciousness... Anyway, it's complicated. It didn't even last a few seconds, but it was long enough for him to control my hand and stab me with a sword."

"Does it hurt?" Vereesa asked.

"It hurts." Jace grinned roguishly. "But it won't get in the way of what we're about to do."

"You..." Vereesa sighed. "I still don't get it. You already knew black dragon mind control magic?"

"I didn't." Jace said. "But that's the power of Dark Simulacrum. It wasn't me mimicking the spell -- the shadow itself did the mimicking. I still have no idea how to control someone's mind like a black dragon."

"Incredible." The ranger said softly.

"I think so too." Jace glanced at his wound. "Do you find it horrifying?"

"You mean the scar the son of a black dragon left on my lover's body with Krol Blade?" The ranger slowly lowered her head. "I think it's damn sexy."
