## Chapter 169: Ulterior Motives

The man before his eyes — this was Kel'Thuzad?

Kel'Thuzad was the first lich, the most powerful mage seduced and corrupted by the original Lich King, Ner'zhul.

In the years ahead, he would not only orchestrate the necromantic plague that devastated the entire Kingdom of Lordaeron and found the cult devoted to the Lich King, the Cult of the Damned — he would also assist the Lich King in guiding Prince Arthas's fall.

And of course, his greatest "feat": using the Book of Medivh to summon Archimonde, bringing that terrifying demon lord back to Azeroth after ten thousand years.

If the night elves hadn't sacrificed the ancient power sleeping within Mount Hyjal to destroy his physical form and blast his demonic soul back into the Twisting Nether, the whole world would have been destroyed before World of Warcraft even began.

Why was *he* the one hosting?

Dushan leaned over and whispered, "Heard of this archmage, Jace? Surprised?"

Jace nodded. "I've heard of him before."

Not much of the "surprised" part, but the "shocked" part was more than enough.

Kel'Thuzad wasn't like those people who started out relatively normal and only caused trouble later. Mountains of evidence showed he'd been fascinated by the dark arts even before the Orc Wars — in particular, the necromantic magic related to immortality. When he took part in the Battle of Hillsbrad and witnessed those death knights created by Gul'dan, it only hardened his resolve to pursue his research. The orcish necromantic arts showed him another path to realizing his ideal of eternal life, besides becoming a Guardian of Tirisfal.

A decade and more later, when his activities were exposed and Antonidas intended to expel him from Dalaran, he'd argued his case fiercely, trying to make Antonidas recognize the future of necromantic magic — because by then his craving for eternal existence and returning from death had reached the point of utter mania.

So the reason Kel'Thuzad wanted to gather Orcish-language researchers here in Dalaran wasn't hard to guess.

At this moment, the most powerful wielders of necromantic force on Azeroth — besides Deathwing, who delighted in studying bizarre dark magic, and his kindred-spirit eldest son Nefarian — were still the orcs left behind on this continent.

If Kel'Thuzad wanted to push his research further, he needed to learn Orcish as a tool, collect all sorts of death-knight-related materials gathered from across the world, and extract information useful to his research from them.

If that was the case, the command letter in Jace's hands might well give Kel'Thuzad the inspiration to head to the Kingdom of Stormwind in search of that lost servant of Gorefiend — or at least the magical traces he'd left behind.

But if he didn't hand over this letter, how was he supposed to warn the Kirin Tor about the Eye of Dalaran?

Wait — this letter didn't mention the recipient's specific location, because it had been delivered directly by an orc warlock. Only that orc warlock and Gorefiend himself knew where the recipient "Angor" was.

Jace could simply make up another place and claim it was where he'd found the letter, to mislead Kel'Thuzad, without changing the content of the letter at all — while still getting across all the information he wanted everyone to know.

So where should he pick?

Since he lived in Stormwind, the place couldn't be too far from Stormwind. And it had to be somewhere remote, somewhere few humans would dare to go, so that an orc corpse could lie there undiscovered long enough for him to stumble across it.

The map in his mind expanded outward in circles, and finally settled on one location: Deadwind Pass, where Karazhan stood.

He'd just say he'd gotten lost on the way to Duskwood and ended up wandering into Deadwind Pass. And as for why he'd gotten lost… well, anything could happen around Karazhan.

Just to observe the strange phenomena around Karazhan, the Kirin Tor had established an organization called the Violet Eye to slowly probe its way in.

He didn't believe Kel'Thuzad would head to Karazhan alone on the strength of some vague transfer order, running the risk of being discovered to study some necromantic force.

And even if he dared go, it wouldn't do him any good — after all, Jace was making the whole thing up. He wouldn't find anything.

While Kel'Thuzad delivered his speech up on the podium, Jace quickly fabricated a new adventure story about how he'd come across the letter, jotting down the key points in the margins and blank spaces on the sides and back of the speech he'd already written. By now the whole page was covered tight.

Dushan couldn't help but ask, "He's only giving his opening remarks. What's there to write down so much?"

"That's Kel'Thuzad, Master Dushan," Jace whispered. "Every word he says I need to carefully write down. A celebrity — his words always have something unique about them."

Dushan said, "Not necessarily. Sometimes the person standing in the middle of the stage is just some lucky fool."

Jace completely agreed — but Kel'Thuzad absolutely did not fall into that category.

The archmage on the stage was still speaking at full volume.

"Whether or not you accept coexisting with orcs, you have to admit that Orcish is a new type of culture we must engage with. Those kings, ministers, and nobles outside — many of them haven't realized this yet."

"And I believe everyone sitting here has come to understand the diversity of cultures and of perspectives through the process of engaging with different languages, and has been able to quickly realize how important that is to us. Because language is the key that opens door after door to civilization, and in the process of exploring them we learn to view new things objectively and rationally — we learn to cast off the biases we've cultivated for decades from within our own cultural cages, and to consider from a practical, neutral standpoint what new things will bring to our own society and people — in terms of both the good and the bad."

New things — did necromantic magic count as a new thing?

Listening to Kel'Thuzad's long speech, Jace couldn't shake the feeling that the man had ulterior motives. After finishing that passage, Kel'Thuzad paused, then added, "Perhaps many young people may disagree with what I've said. That's all right — these are merely my own views."

"Next, allow me to first present to everyone a very special carrier of Orcish runes. My student, Mr. Bromiam, will recite the Orcish runes inscribed upon it. He is a researcher gifted in both the Orcish language and the study of incantations — even my own command of Orcish recitation falls far short of his. He is already able to hold nearly barrier-free conversation with the orcs in the internment camps."

The name Bromiam sounded vaguely familiar to Jace.

Just then, several assistants helped carry a piece of red-tinged metal armor — as if it had been soaked in blood — up onto the stage for everyone to see. Everyone in the room craned their necks and half-rose from their seats, trying to get a clearer look.

This was a rune-etched suit of armor, and the runes on the armor had been written in blood.
