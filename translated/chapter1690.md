## Chapter 1690: Return to Redridge

Keeping several thousand elite soldiers fed in Blackrock Spire was an enormous burden on Stormwind City. Even with the prestige of conquering the Horde's last stronghold in the name of the Alliance of Lordaeron, and with material and financial support from the other five kingdoms, the pressure of staying there was becoming increasingly unsustainable.

The pressure from Shadowforge City didn't help either. At first, the Dark Iron emperor was grateful to the Alliance for helping him retake Blackrock Spire and defeat the black dragons. He didn't say much, even offering Shadowforge's supplies to help treat the soldiers' plague. But after a few weeks, even he was getting impatient.

Still, Varian did his best to mediate between Thaurissan and the envoys from the various kingdoms, holding out until the very last sick soldier had recovered inside Blackrock Spire. Only after the Church and the Knights of the Silver Hand inspected everyone and found no new suspected cases did he give the order to march home.

The veterans of the Battle of Hillsbrad Foothills knew well that if even one soldier hadn't recovered from the orc warlocks' vile plague, bringing him back to a town or city could mean dozens or hundreds of new infections. Orgrim Doomhammer and Gul'dan's servants had wanted to use exactly that to break the Alliance's camps. So unless the last man was clear, everything was in vain.

The Draenei, who had arrived at Blackrock Spire not long after its liberation, also made an enormous contribution to helping the Alliance escape the plague's infestation. Though few in number, they were even more skilled than the priests of the Silver Hand and the Church at curing demonic curses, undead plagues, and soothing ghostly possessions.

Jace had heard a few days earlier that Prophet Velen had personally arrived at Morgan's Post, inspecting soldiers and supply caravans alongside the bishops there to ensure no one brought the plague back to Lakeshire or even Stormwind City. He could guess that aside from willingly helping the Alliance with the aftermath, Velen was definitely also here to see the fragment of the Ata'mal Crystal -- the Soul's Song.

The Winter Veil festival had passed. Spring had arrived. Without the Molten Core's blistering heat, Blackrock Mountain's temperature had dropped by half. After a few days of travel away from Blackrock, nearing the valley of Morgan's Post, the air even felt cold.

Jace Sesso had faced the Lich King in Blackrock Spire at the moment when a terrible necromantic ritual was about to raise all the dead into ghouls. He had used an even more terrifying dark magic to strip the Lich King of the power to enact his ritual, preventing an apocalypse of undeath. This crazy -- but not that crazy -- story had already spread across the Burning Steppes. Clearly it had reached Stormwind City, and even farther, long before.

The priests and Draenei could stop the plague from spreading, but they couldn't stop the story from spreading by word of mouth. Fortunately, not many people had mentioned Ursales yet.

When Nefarian had finally blurted out those words about Ursales, Jace had worried they might make the Kirin Tor members and high elves in that valley who knew the scythe's name start to suspect him.

But as it turned out, the situation had been chaotic, Nefarian's voice had been muffled, and even Vereesa -- who'd rushed over at the first sign of Draconic speech -- hadn't clearly heard what the black dragon prince said. The only ones who'd actually caught his final murmurs were probably him and Needleflame.

"Still worried about the plague?"

Sora, riding beside him, was looking at him. Jace realized he hadn't even noticed her approach, and could only nod.

The green dragon pointed toward the dark mountain outline in the distance. "Look over there. What do you see?"

"Redridge?" Jace said. "Past it should be the Tower of Ilgalar."

"You want to cross over, but I can see what's trying to cross over here." Sora smiled. "New sprouts, new trees. They look like they've discovered a new world, singing about crossing that ridge to explore this side -- a place they never imagined they could reach. I can see their excitement, hear their joy."

Jace looked back toward where Sora was looking. Now that she mentioned it, the barren, dead slopes and withered forests really did seem to be dotted with faint patches of budding green.

"The mad fire elementals don't belong here. The black dragons and the orcs have no right to invade this land." The green dragon said softly. "Our struggle is restoring this land to what it should be."

"Yes," Jace replied.

"Many of my kind think I should return to the Emerald Dream and accept the duty Queen Ysera has given me..." Sora gripped her reins tightly. "But by your side, I've proven time and again that even without being in the Dream, I can still fulfill the responsibility of a guardian of nature."

"Don't act like I've been that much help to you, Sora." Jace said. "Without your magic, a lot of people here might be lying in mass graves. You looked like a goddess of the forest when you cast that spell."

"Thank you." Sora lowered her head. "But... that's not what I wanted to say, Jace."

"Those hateful fiends -- Old Gods like Ragnaros the Firelord, or fallen wizards like Nefarian, even Medivh -- they always turn the places they inhabit into something terrible and hostile. They make their followers more ruthless and insane. In the end, they just wait for silent destruction, or a violent end."

The green dragon's moonlit eyes fixed on the warlock beside her. "But you... even though you wield shadow and walk in darkness, everyone close to you, every place connected to you, is either saved... or healed. Like the Redridge Mountains. Like the Temple of Atal'Hakkar."

"Because I'm not a cruel fiend or a fallen wizard," Jace said.

"You're not." The green dragon said. "And I won't let any demon or jealous person twist you into one, Jace."

*I'll never become one of those fiends you hate either, Sora.*

Jace thought this in his heart but didn't say it out loud. Behind them, the sturdy Khaz Modan ram carrying the snoring Grede and his thick enchanted cloth-wrapped Sulfuras looked like it was struggling with every step.

Vereesa had been excitedly whispering with Alleria the whole time, occasionally glancing this way with her sister, then laughing along with the other Farstriders.

Up ahead, Needleflame stealthily glanced sideways. When she saw Jace looking at her, she immediately turned her head away.

Jace wrapped the cloth binding around Ursales a few more times until it couldn't be tightened any further. After leaving Blackrock Spire, this demonic skeleton -- which had devoured who knows how many demon and orc souls -- had quieted down. He had to keep it that way.

The column rounded a low hill. From the front came murmurs and occasional cheers.

When they came around the other side, Needleflame up ahead stared blankly into the distance, making the people behind her quite curious. Sora caught up and looked in the same direction, eyes wide.

Soon, Jace saw what they were looking at too.

It was Nefarian's head, lying sideways on a wide cart in front of the gate of Morgan's Post camp, surrounded by a group of soldiers and priests.

The black dragon prince's head was now just a heavy, oversized skull, completely scorched black by fire. Jace knew it was Nefarian's head, but looking at it now, he didn't have the same feeling he'd had when watching his body slowly die in the valley. It felt like a stranger, something he'd never encountered before.

After all, the Alliance planned to hang Nefarian's head on the walls of Stormwind City to proclaim the victory at Blackrock Spire to the world. This skull had already been purified by the Church's priests. After clashing with Nefarian using dark magic, Jace had become intimately familiar with this black dragon's corrupted mana. His understanding of him was now more grounded in the realm of power. Seeing this withered shell, completely devoid of soul and magic, naturally didn't leave much of an impression.

Still, it at least meant the Church's priests had done thorough work. They had done their best to purify the remains of this black dragon leader, so the citizens of Stormwind could safely share in the Alliance's joy without being harmed by the dark mana and death plague the black dragon left behind.

Jace noticed two Draenei priests standing beside the skull, speaking with the cavalry captain of the transport team. They might have participated in the purification process too.

Not far from the priests, near the camp gate, stood Prophet Velen. Draped in a silver-white robe, the pure arcane glow from the violet crystal staff in his hand stood out starkly against the scorched earth of the Burning Steppes.

Jace knew. He was waiting for him.
