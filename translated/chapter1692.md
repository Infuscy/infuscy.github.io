## Chapter 1692: Becoming a Hero

The Soul's Song.

Until the moment he took it from Varasztus and held it in his hands, Jace had never imagined he'd actually possess it one day.

The Ata'mal Crystal... Even on Argus, only the highest-ranking Eredar rulers had ever seen such a thing with their own eyes. And today, Velen was willing to entrust it to him?

Why?

Jace knew perfectly well this wasn't some reward for defeating an orc chieftain or a black dragon prince. It didn't have much to do with rescuing the Draenei from Draenor either. Velen must have had a special reason for giving it to him.

Was it connected to the future omen he'd seen through the Soul's Song's power? Prison. Freedom.

Jace couldn't figure it out. Because if he was ever going to be thrown into the Violet Hold, the Soul's Song would be confiscated along with Ursales and locked away in the Kirin Tor's vault.

Even if the Kirin Tor couldn't figure out what Ursales was, the Soul's Song alone would be valuable enough for the Kirin Tor Council to pile up enough charges to keep him locked away until he died, and keep the Soul's Song for themselves.

Velen wouldn't want this thing to end up in Dalaran's vault, would he? And about this prophecy... should he tell Khadgar?

"When were you planning to tell me that Prophet Velen gave you the Soul's Song, Jace Sesso?" The middle-aged mage with the white beard gripped his reins and stared at him.

"Now," Jace said.

"Well, at least that saves us some breath." Khadgar gritted his teeth and muttered. "But this is a Naaru relic. Didn't the Eredar prophet tell you why?"

"It might have something to do with Kil'jaeden," Jace said.

That wasn't just a brush-off. It almost certainly had to do with Kil'jaeden or the Burning Legion. What else could it be?

At this, the archmage's expression grew dim. "I was a bit envious just now. But now that you mention it, something's dawning on me... Could it be that Prophet Velen wants to use you as bait too, to draw out Kil'jaeden's schemes?"

"'Too'?" Jace asked. "So you finally admit you've been using me as bait?"

"It was for your own good," Khadgar said.

"Does the Kirin Tor want this?" Jace asked.

"Do you even need to ask?" Khadgar said. "And the Kirin Tor isn't afraid of going up against the Burning Legion. Its master's had his eye on us for two thousand years anyway. But you make it sound like the Kirin Tor wants to take it from you by force... That won't happen. At least not right now."

"Whatever the case, I can't shake the feeling that my connection to the Violet Hold is getting stronger, Khadgar." Jace gripped the Soul's Song in his hand. "I hope you'll do your best."

"We've discussed this before. Why bring it up again? I've already told you I'll do everything I can to help you avoid that miserable ending." Khadgar raised one hand, crossed his fingers in a simple gesture. "If you're still not convinced, I swear by the Holy Light. If I fail, lock me up in there with you."

"You don't need to put that in the oath. If I end up in there, you're definitely coming with me." Jace said. "Even if you don't make it in on your own, I'll help you get in from the inside."

"Listen to this guy." Khadgar muttered. "We should make this conversation public. Let everyone see what ingratitude looks like."

"Just cooperating with an investigation, Archmage." Jace said casually. "As an important member and de facto leader of the Kirin Tor, you wouldn't want the worst criminals in the Violet Citadel to be keeping secrets from you, would you?"

"Damned warlock." Khadgar chuckled. "When do you plan to leave for Kalimdor?"

"After Marshal Anduin Lothar's funeral," Jace replied.

"Listen." Khadgar nodded. "Let me give you my opinion: if Onyxia refuses to negotiate for peace, kill her. Bring her head back to Stormwind City and hang it next to her brother's skull. It'll do your reputation some good. The Kirin Tor has an office set up in Gadgetzan. If you run into trouble, you can contact Dalaran through them."

"You think I'd hesitate against that female dragon?" Jace said. "If she's still nursing any thoughts about Stormwind Keep, or if she means to avenge her brother or her father, there's no way I'd risk letting her live. Stormwind City and these towns can't afford that risk. Neither can my parents and I."

"I'm not worried about you. I'm worried about that one. The black dragon." Khadgar looked toward Needleflame ahead and lowered his voice. "Princess Onyxia is her aunt. With dragons, blood ties are hard to predict."

"I actually think she might be a key factor in getting Onyxia to agree to a truce." Jace said. "Before Nefarian died... you didn't get to see it. That mad black dragon prince, when he saw Needleflame at the end, he just seemed to deflate. He completely stopped resisting."

"Well, if I were Nefarian, I'd go mad too." Khadgar said. "Spend your whole life trying to achieve something, and someone else is just born with it. Ah, but when you think about it, it's not that hopeless. My existence didn't drive the Kirin Tor Council insane. Lady Jaina Proudmoore's teleportation magic didn't make me have a mental breakdown on the spot when I first saw it. Speaking of which, that damned little girl really is... damned."

Hearing Khadgar admit Jaina was more gifted than him was a unique experience. Though Jace wasn't sure if this was genuine feeling or self-deprecating modesty.

In another timeline, Proudmoore -- with Aegwynn as her secret consultant -- was young but not far behind Khadgar, who had trained in Karazhan. But would Jaina in this timeline still meet Aegwynn?

Instead of worrying about the Princess of Kul Tiras, Jace should probably worry more about their trip to Dustwallow Marsh.

"Speaking of which, Khadgar. Is there any chance we'll run into Lady Aegwynn again?"

"What are you thinking?" Khadgar laughed as if he'd heard a joke. "After everything that's happened, Jace -- you know what I'm talking about. You think she'd stay in the same place? She's completely erased that house of hers by the coast."

"You checked afterward?" Jace asked.

Khadgar suddenly coughed a few times. "Anyway, rest assured. Go to Kalimdor. There is absolutely no way you'll run into Lady Aegwynn. Even if you turn that savage continent upside down."

Jace was wondering just how much of Khadgar's words he could trust when murmurs and excited voices came from the front of the column.

He looked forward. Unnoticed, the victorious Allied army had already arrived at Lakeshire.

After crossing a small hill, he could see the road stretching down to the wide Lake Everstill bridge. Blue lake water among the oak groves, red-roofed houses by the shore, and crowds gathered along both sides of the road.

They waved their caps, walking sticks, coats -- anything bright and colorful -- at the approaching Allied soldiers.

People shouted all kinds of celebrations. Children and young people pushed forward to the front of the line to get a look at the black devil's head.

When Nefarian's skull finally emerged around a bend in the rocks, revealing itself to all the onlookers, gasps and excited shouts rose wave after wave. Some children born near the end of the Orc Wars were even scared into crying.

The knights escorting the dragon head cart all puffed out their chests as they passed through the crowd.

The crowd's excitement was so overwhelming it made Jace feel dizzy. Not emotionally -- it was a real, physical sensation. In his mind, everyone's joy and excitement rippled through the air. Compared to that, the lake waves slapping against the shore and bridge pillars under the wind felt gentle, even weak.

Was this the Soul's Song's influence?

No. Jace was fairly certain this was far more likely the power Nefarian and the countless drakonids, whelps, and orcs in Blackrock Spire had fed into Ursales after being devoured, now flowing back into him.

What the Soul's Song could actually do for him, he still had no practical experience with. And it wasn't surprising that the residents were this excited. Lakeshire was the closest Stormwind town to Blackrock Mountain. If the black dragons and orcs ever decided to attack humanity, Stonewatch and Lakeshire would be the first to suffer. What had happened in another timeline proved that. So of course, the residents of Lake Everstill were the happiest to see Blackrock Spire fall.

"Master Sesso, is it true you killed that dragon?!"

A childish shout came from somewhere. Jace turned toward the crowd by the roadside, but couldn't tell which child had asked. It seemed like everyone was staring at him, waiting for an answer.

"Master Sesso is right there, the one in the red cloak on the black horse..."

"He's just a bit behind His Majesty the King!"

"That horse he's riding is so tall!"

Jace glanced at Khadgar beside him. The archmage just tilted his head slightly and gave him a look.

"It was me," Jace said, looking toward the crowd. "The black dragon prince was a formidable opponent. He commanded incredibly powerful dark magic."

"But he still wasn't a match for you, Master Sesso!" a boy shouted.

"Hard to deny that, young sir." Jace smiled at him, rubbed his chin, and called out. "After all, my head's still on my shoulders."

The children cheered excitedly, pushing each other as they shouted "Long live the King, long live the Alliance," and "Great Jace Sesso." Up ahead, Varian and the Sons of Lothar heard Jace and the children's shouts and burst into laughter.

"You're better at playing the dragon-slaying hero than I expected, Jace." Khadgar muttered.
