Chapter 1694: Rain in the Valley of Heroes

The rain continued on and off, and hadn't stopped even when the Allied army arrived at the gates of Stormwind City two days later.

Even though the weather wasn't cooperating, countless citizens still gathered by the bridge and on the walls, wrapped in raincloaks, to watch as Nefarian's head was hung from the archway at the entrance to the Valley of Heroes. Even the gryphon roost was packed with people. Murmurs, cheers, curses aimed at the black dragons and orcs, the rain, the grating of sturdy Dark Iron chains against grey-white stone...

The gryphons were all too terrified by the enormous magical beast's skull to make a sound. Even the war gryphons that the Wildhammer elite knights had brought to the front lines of Blackrock Spire had trembled at the sight of dragons flying through the sky. These younger, less experienced gryphons had no chance. No matter how brave and fearless a beast, faced with a true adult black dragon, it was nothing but weak and pale. Let alone facing Nefarian himself.

But even Nefarian's head, surrounded by the massive statues in the Valley of Heroes, didn't look as "spectacular" as it had when he fell in that valley.

Varian stood beside Turalyon's statue and announced the victory at Blackrock Spire to all the citizens of Stormwind City, while also mourning the brave warriors who had fallen in the battle. The curtain of rain slightly cooled the enthusiasm of the Allied soldiers and the citizens of Stormwind, and lent a heavier air to the ceremony.

Blackrock Spire -- a red-hot black blade that had hung over the Kingdom of Stormwind for over a century, nearly falling in the last decade or so -- had finally been removed. Jace could feel the joy and relief spreading through the crowd of citizens who had gathered in the Valley of Heroes. But at the same time, a sense of irrepressible sadness drifted through the cracks between the more positive and cheerful emotions.

Stormwind City had been directly conquered and occupied by the Horde before. Kilrogg, Blackhand, Orgrim, Cho'gall -- all had used the city as their clan's base at one time or another. Most of the Stormwind citizens still standing here had lost family and friends in those years of darkness and chaos.

When Nefarian's head -- symbolizing the Horde's complete defeat -- was hung upon the walls of Stormwind City, the sweetness of vengeance naturally arrived first, spinning like a red storm. But then came the longing for the departed. Grey, bitter, astringent.

These emotions grew clearer and more concrete in Jace's perception. They almost overwhelmed him, overpowering his observations of reality -- or the surface of things. The soul power he'd taken from Nefarian was far too strong. This growth in power and perception was more concrete and real than what he'd drawn from the Tome of Noxious Names.

After all, the power from the Tome of Noxious Names had been based on that grimoire and its runes. But this time was completely different. Ursales and its master had nearly fused together -- soul, flesh, and bone. Even if Jace deliberately suppressed its power, he couldn't change the fact that his strength had surged.

The devastating clash with Sargeras's scepter had destroyed most of its magic. But it was still the void artifact that even Kil'jaeden and Sargeras feared.

At this moment, Jace felt he could easily manipulate these shadows. A desire welled up inside him, urging him to reach out and stir these emotions like ink in a pool of water.

And he knew these complex, chaotic emotions wouldn't really melt into the air like uncontrollable ink. They would change according to his will, becoming part of his power -- just like when he used the ritual of the Soul's Song against Nefarian in Blackrock Spire.

Those feelings of longing could easily be dyed black, turned into increasingly scorching pain. Continue pouring mana into them, and the pain could take physical form -- a terrible curse gnawing at organs and corroding bone and muscle. The Curse of Agony. Far more powerful than the Curse of Agony ordinary warlocks could drive with demonic incantations.

Those feelings of vengeance could easily be ignited, turned into blazing shadowflames. These flames could expand rapidly until they went out of control, with just a little catalysis from Jace -- just as the Curse of Recklessness described.

And there were so many more emotions to exploit. The fear in the gryphon roost... If he just sowed them throughout the Valley of Heroes, he could instantly twist all the dark, grey energy into deeper fear. This rainy celebration would change color in an instant. The ensuing chaos and madness would be easy to foresee.

For even a moment, he realized he didn't need to manipulate the shadows at all. He was the shadow itself. The incantations calling upon dark power weren't words for him to speak, but "prayers" for others to offer in his name. The entire city seemed to be laid out before his eyes, in a form both unfamiliar and familiar. But...

Jace looked up at the sky. The thick grey clouds were like sick fish skin covered in a layer of fuzz. The spreading rain reminded him of spirit souls chasing him from dark well water, from the Twisting Nether, slapping against his face in waves, sending chills through him one after another.

At this moment, this overwhelming sense of control stirred a hint of unease in his heart. He couldn't help touching the Chain of Will around his neck. This piece of truesilver was now colder than the rain itself. That meant no external force was manipulating his consciousness or spirit. It also meant there was no convenient scapegoat to blame.

If he already had this kind of perception and power now, what would happen with the Soul's Song's blessing?

He'd thought about this more than once over the past two days. Now, looking at the purple Ata'mal Crystal fragment in his hand, he couldn't help letting his mind wander even more.

Prophet Velen and the other Draenei hadn't given him any hints about how to use the Soul's Song. In the previous battle at Blackrock Spire, he hadn't learned anything related to the Soul's Song from Nefarian either.

It just lay quietly in his hand. If he looked at it as a purple gemstone, it might even seem a bit cheap and ordinary at this point -- like a piece of dark glass. Especially with the Tear of Neltharion sitting beside it, the Soul's Song looked even more unimpressive in comparison.

But Jace just couldn't tear his eyes away. On one hand, it truly contained extraordinary power. On the other, Velen's prophecy and the meaning of this gift were things he couldn't quite wrap his head around.

But in the end, he did nothing. He neither manipulated the shadows lingering around him nor stirred them in the slightest.

He didn't try to explore the depths of the Soul's Song's power either. He just held it in his hand and looked at it.

You must control the shadow, Jace Sesso. Not let the shadow control you.

Think about your defeated opponent, Prince Nefarian. What did he pay in his attempt to master the shadow and break free from enslavement? His head hangs right there, pale and lifeless.

He didn't know how long passed. The howling of the shadows gradually faded, like a storm that had just raged through, leaving only damp, cold air behind. Lord Bolvar Fordragon's voice became clear again. Then the Prince of Stromgarde, the Knights of the Silver Hand, the bishops of the Cathedral of Light...

Jace adjusted his cloak and looked up at Varian speaking below the statue. A wet, ice-cold hand gripped his tightly.

He looked at Vereesa beside him. She was looking at him too.

"Hold on to me, Jace."

"Mm."
