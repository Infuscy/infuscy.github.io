## Chapter 1695: Father and Son

The banquet for the Sons of Lothar and the Allied soldiers wasn't over yet, but it was already late. Jace was still hoping to get back to his parents' house before it was fully dark, so he'd been distracted throughout the feast.

He planned to take Vereesa and leave the throne room, first saying goodbye to his companions. As he was thinking this and heading out to the circular corridor, he found Grede was already there.

The dwarf was leaning against the wall, looking out over the Stormwind City district. It was still drizzling, but that couldn't dampen the citizens' enthusiasm for celebrating the fall of Blackrock Spire. Firecrackers were going off everywhere in the city, and every now and then a firework would soar into the sky and burst into a brilliant blossom of color. From the harbor to the Cathedral of Light, to the Trade District, the city tonight looked even livelier than during the Winter Veil festival.

"Another statue of you is going up there someday, Jace Sesso." Grede pointed toward the middle of the Trade District.

"What about yours?" Jace asked.

"In Shadowforge City." Grede replied. "A godforsaken place where no one who knows me can see it, and the people who can see it don't know me. And Dagran Thaurissan is probably looking for an excuse to tear it down every day. How could a Wildhammer statue be allowed in the Dark Iron Empire?"

Jace chuckled. "How's your wound?"

"Good enough to punch a thunder lizard to death." Grede said. "Don't worry about me. I'm fully prepared for the upcoming trip to Kalimdor. In fact... maybe a little too prepared."

"Oh?" Jace asked. "What do you mean?"

"Tell me." The dwarf asked. "Is there any point in me coming back here?"

Jace didn't know what to say for a moment. He just leaned against the wall, stunned. Finally, he coughed into his hand and said, "Weren't you just saying you'd break into Dalaran's prison to rescue me, and then we'd escape together?"

"Haha." Grede laughed loudly. "That's not mutually exclusive. And besides, you don't exactly look like you're about to get thrown into prison right now. The people of Stormwind like you, Jace. They stopped caring long ago whether you're a Lordaeronian or a warlock. And the Kirin Tor wizards, the Silver Hand, most of the Alliance, the Three Hammers... they're all willing to stand by you. And I think this look suits you."

"Does it?" Jace asked.

"You're a true savior." Grede's usually high-arching beard drooped. "From pulling me out of the water at Southshore, to the elves, the green dragon... and that damn black dragon! Now it's King Varian. And Stormwind City. And the whole Alliance."

"But I'm not like you. I don't really enjoy this feeling. To be more accurate, I can't handle it. All this stuff has put way too much pressure on me. Far harder to bear than the real Sulfuras smash of Ragnaros the Firelord. Not that I'm saying my Sulfuras is fake, but you know what I mean."

"I understand that part. But I don't really get the part about pressure." Jace crossed his arms.

"I don't expect you to truly understand." Grede wiggled his fingers. "Put simply, I don't want to be some lord or High King. I just want to travel with guys like you, kill a few gnolls, earn some silver coins, or find some rare treasure. Stuff like Sulfuras is already way beyond that scope... Anyway. Travel, adventure, and forget about Aerie Peak and everything about dwarves in the north, except my father's grave. And maybe a few other places in Kirthaven."

"I'm not good at dealing with the stuff that's in front of me now. Or all the stuff that's coming. Girls like Vereesa, daughters of great nobles, were born to handle this kind of thing. Thunderfist is a big name in Aerie Peak, sure, but I personally have nothing to do with the title of nobility. Those two dragons couldn't care less. And those two demons even less."

"What about Grim Batol?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "Grim Batol is already on the right track. You and I brought High Thane Kurdran back. We met the Dragonqueen. Now even the Anvil of Kardros is back in operation. And don't make it sound like I'm walking off to the edge of the world never to return. If you get into trouble over here, you can send word through the wind or fire elementals, and I'll come right back. Don't underestimate me -- I can hear the elementals too. And they'd be happy to do you that favor."

"You've really thought this through." Jace said. "So you're sure you want to stay in Kalimdor?"

Grede hemmed and hawed for a while, then flailed his arms. "Don't say 'sure' or 'not sure,' dammit."

"Are you drunk?" Jace frowned.

"Pretty sure I'm not," Grede said.

"So you're sure about that?" Jace asked.

"Don't start that!" The dwarf bumped Jace in the chest with his shoulder. "If I were drunk, maybe I wouldn't be thinking about this stuff."

"I feel like I've lost the ability to get drunk anymore. Do you know what that's like? If I lose to a little brat like Needleflame, at worst I feel like a useless dwarf. But now I feel like a freak. If a dwarf can't get drunk, is he still a dwarf?"

"You lost to Needleflame again?" Jace asked.

"Hmph, you son of a..." The dwarf muttered. "I'm not drunk. Just a bit confused. My brain's having trouble turning. Maybe this means this bizarre, long journey is finally coming to an end. Subtitle -- 'The Legendary Adventures of Jace Sesso the Warlock and Former Gryphon Rider Grede Thunderfist.'"

He stared blankly at a painting on the wall for a moment. "I'm getting sleepy. I'll just crash here tonight. Tell me a story."

"What story?" Jace asked.

"A bedtime story. Like the one I just described." Grede said. "My dad used to tell me stories like that. Amazing ones. Like the green dragon deep in the Hinterlands forest... Dammit, I should ask Sora if she knows that dragon. I can't believe I never thought to ask all these years. Hurry up and tell me something interesting."

Jace thought for a moment, then sat down beside him. "Once upon a time, there was a druid."

"Mm-hmm."

"He crawled into a great den, trying to enter the Emerald Dream. But instead, he ended up in a strange world."

"What kind of world?"

"A very strange world. There were buildings a thousand feet high, roads a hundred feet wide. A single city could hold millions of dwarves."

"Whoa. That means millions of pounds of crap every day. A world made of crap!"

"So he really didn't like that stinky place."

"Purify it with nature magic!"

"He lost all his magical power in that world. Maybe that world doesn't have any magic at all."

"A pitiful world with no elements, no life force, no leylines -- and yet so many people living there? Are you making this up? This sounds boring, Dad. Tell me another one."

"Uh?"

"Tell me an adventure story, Dad. Keep telling that one... about a black dragon who snuck onto a Blackwater pirate ship and drifted all the way to Tanaris. Mm, I'm starting to forget what happened earlier."

"Dad, I miss you so much. Where are you?"

This guy was crying. Crying like a gnoll who'd come back from a hunt to find his camp set on fire by Stormwind patrolmen.

Jace just let the dwarf's enormous head rest on his shoulder, until he stopped hiccuping and babbling in barely intelligible Dwarven. Until his snores drowned out the noise from the banquet hall behind them.

*Sleep well, my good son,* Jace thought to himself.

"By the eternal sun." Vereesa appeared behind him at some point. She glanced at the dwarf in Jace's arms, utterly shocked. "What happened?"

"Got drunk." Jace tilted his head at the ranger. "Lost to the black dragon at drinking again. Mental breakdown."

"I should have guessed. I just came from the Farstriders and wondered why Needleflame looked so happy." Vereesa chuckled softly. She raised her eyes. "What about you? I felt like you had something on your mind today, Jace. I barely saw you at the banquet."

"I'm fine, Vereesa. I just want to see my parents as soon as possible." Jace said.

Vereesa looked at him doubtfully for a moment. "I'll come with you."

"Of course. But first, let's find a room for this guy." Jace said.

The elf nodded. The two of them tried lifting the dwarf -- one grabbing an arm, one grabbing a leg -- but couldn't budge him. Even dragging him by one leg didn't work. Finally, they both collapsed from exhaustion. Jace couldn't help muttering, "How is he so heavy?"

"You're surprised?" Vereesa sighed. "You're only realizing this now? I thought you'd have figured it out back when you saw this guy take a direct hit from a pit lord without even flinching."

Jace couldn't help a bitter laugh. "Don't just stand there feeling sorry for yourself. Give me a hand."

"I'm here, Master!" Sazeno popped out. "What can I do to help?"

"Sorry, I meant someone else," Jace said.

Morofel emerged from the shadows, kicked the imp aside, folded her wings, and said coldly, "Come now, Master. This is precisely what a Succubus should do -- help carry a drunkard, and ask for nothing in return."

"Losing track of a demon isn't a big deal, Morofel." Vereesa lifted one of Grede's legs. "Even the most skilled hunter misses sometimes. We Farstriders know that best."

The succubus grabbed Grede's other leg and flashed a smile with no warmth in it. "Save the act, Windrunner."

Vereesa just snorted softly, not seeming particularly bothered.
