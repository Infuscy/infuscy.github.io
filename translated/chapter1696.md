## Chapter 1696: "The Poison-Spitting Dragon in the Room"

After leaving Grede in the care of the palace attendants and Sora, Jace and Vereesa hurried out of Stormwind Keep and headed along the canal toward the Cathedral of Light.

Though it was already late, the sky was fully dark, and rain was still drizzling down, the streets were filled with excited shouts. Firecrackers and fireworks went off in the distance from time to time, making the children watching from afar wave their hands and laugh.

The war was over. Yes. Finally over.

When they arrived at his parents' doorstep, Jace knocked. The door swung open with a bang, and for a moment Jace couldn't tell if he'd knocked first or the door had opened first.

Before he could even take off his raincloak, Henny had already thrown her arms around her towering son, burying her face in his shoulder and sobbing. Jace hugged his mother back, silently grateful that he'd listened to Vereesa and changed into a clean robe before going to the palace. If he'd worn the clothes he'd had on inside Blackrock Spire, all the weird dark energy clinging to them would be soaking into his mother right now.

"Mrs. Sesso! Mr. Sesso!"

"Welcome, Vereesa!"

Facing his parents, Vereesa seemed incredibly nervous. Jace wasn't trying to pry into her feelings, but what she was feeling right now was just so obvious -- so different from usual. Even during her Ranger-Lord promotion ceremony, facing the Sun King and Prince Kael'thas, Jace had never seen her this scared. Though her face was perfectly calm...

After all, this Quel'Thalas ranger-lord was about to become a member of this family. Of course, both Henny and Vic had long considered her part of the family. But it was still different from being a married couple who'd actually had a wedding ceremony.

"Don't think you've ruined the evening. You coming back -- everyone should be happy." Henny wiped her tears, somewhat frantically helping Jace take off his raincloak, then glanced toward the kitchen. "Uh, right... have you two eaten?"

Father chuckled. "Would King Varian let his returning heroes go hungry, Henny?"

"I've never quite gotten used to Stormwind's cooking." Jace cleared his throat. "I still prefer the flavors from back home."

"That's what I said." Henny's tears broke into a smile. She hurried back into the kitchen, saying, "I knew I should have prepared something for you tonight. And Vereesa, I know elven warriors like you have endless energy and can never get enough to eat. Just sit and wait a moment..."

"Thank you so much, Mrs. Sesso!"

Vereesa clasped her hands together and slowly sat down on a chair nearby. Jace collapsed onto another one beside her. He noticed Vic putting away his still-damp coat and asked, "You just got back too?"

"Yeah." Vic said. "I've been spending a lot of time on the coast north of the harbor lately. I figured tonight you'd be at King Varian's banquet with the Alliance soldiers, so you wouldn't be coming by the canal. But your mother insisted I come home early and make sure everything was ready for you."

"Don't think she's overreacting, Jace." Father placed the sword King Terenas had given him by the door. "We heard a lot about what happened at Blackrock Spire. Orcs, black dragons, Dark Irons, black magic. For a while there, walking through the streets and overhearing the guards talking to people -- it felt like going back to the days when the Horde conquered Stormwind. Rumors everywhere, impossible to feel at ease."

"Not to mention when I went with Henny to the Cathedral of Light for prayers, a bishop there told me you were fighting the black dragon prince at the top of Blackrock Spire with magic..."

"Something like that." Jace leaned slightly against the edge of the table with a hint of pride, pouring himself some coffee. "The news travels fast in Stormwind."

Vic looked back and forth between the warlock and the ranger, frowning. "The son of the Dragon King? I know you've mastered a lot of spells, Jace. You're the king's advisor. You've fought demons in Draenor. But..."

"To be honest, I'm curious how he beat that black dragon too, Mr. Sesso." Vereesa admitted. "I wasn't there at the time. He basically single-handedly defeated Nefarian in single combat."

"Single-handedly?" Vic gaped in surprise at first, then slowly nodded. "Nefarian. Sounds like an evil and ominous name. Or maybe it's because the human ancestors who created Common already linked black dragon names with evil and omens. Those black dragons are ancient monsters after all."

"No wonder so many people compare you to that mage, Jace." Vic smiled. "But I don't think so. At least you haven't blown me up with arcane magic."

Jace choked on his coffee at that. Even Vereesa couldn't help sucking in a sharp breath.

"What kind of stupid thing are you saying, Vic Sesso?" Henny grumbled unhappily. She carried over several bowls of pumpkin soup rich with the aroma of milk and set them on the table, hands on her hips as she pointed toward the harbor outside the window. "Even if a siren dragged you out to sea one day, Jace would never be associated with that wizard in even the slightest way. If you ask me, those worthless scumbags are good for nothing but gossiping behind people's backs. Maybe Medivh hid himself in that magic tower because they drove him to it!"

A flash of white light streaked past the window, making his mother jump. Thunder rolled soon after. The rain seemed to be getting heavier.

"Light curse that thunder!" Henny cursed and ducked back into the kitchen.

"Ahem." Vic picked up a piece of pumpkin from his soup and chewed as he said, "I think it's better to avoid mentioning that name."

"He's dead, Mr. Sesso. Finished by Archmage Khadgar's own hand." Vereesa said. "Nothing to worry about."

"Ever since the Dark Portal opened and the orcs came, dead things come back, living things die -- nothing's certain." Vic said quietly.

Vereesa could only raise her eyebrows. The former guardsman wasn't entirely wrong. In the past, Jace might have thought his dad was oddly on point. But now, nothing was certain anymore.

After Aegwynn helped the Sons of Lothar return to Draenor and cast that prophecy spell at the top of Karazhan -- one even Khadgar dared not replicate -- she'd clearly exhausted a significant amount of her power. If she was still planning to resurrect Medivh, this whole ordeal would have at least delayed her plans by a significant amount. Whether she could even succeed anymore was debatable.

A mage like Aegwynn couldn't recover her power just by sleeping more, eating sweets, and downing alchemy potions like regular mages. Khadgar thought she'd gone somewhere no one could find her -- probably because all the Karazhan and Draenor business had left her weak, and she needed to lie low for a while.

Henny brought over a dish of baked cheesy potatoes and bacon, placing it in the center of the table. She changed out of her outer clothes, sat down, and asked, "Speaking of those undead monsters, a lot of people have been saying the orcs spread a death plague inside Blackrock Spire. Worse than the ghoul disease south of the Neltharion River. Is that true?"

Jace and Vereesa exchanged glances. Jace nodded. "It's true."

"I haven't lived as long as an elf, but I've seen a thing or two." Father sighed. "My experience is... no matter how tightly you handle things, there's always some fish that slips through the net in unexpected ways."

"Counting the transport teams between Lakeshire and Morgan's Post, the logistics... over ten thousand people were involved in the attack on Blackrock Spire. How did you make sure that terrifying plague didn't find its way into Stormwind City? Things have been pretty tense around here, honestly."

"The plague didn't infect everyone in the tower, Dad." Jace took a sip of soup. "And the best clergy in the Alliance, the elite of the Church, including Lord Tirion Fordring and Turalyon, were all there."

"That explains it." Henny said, relieved.

"Seems like a lot of the stories we heard were embellished." Vic also said. "Some people said most of the tower was infected, that Shadowforge City had turned into a plague town. A lot of folks in the Warrior's Guild were even gleeful about it for a while. Honestly, even if the Dark Irons had suffered through a disaster like that, I wouldn't have shed any tears for them."

"More importantly, Sora was there too." Jace said. "She used her magic to purify the rats and insects spreading the disease in the tower, protecting everyone."

"Ah, I almost forgot about her." Henny nodded. "That flower she gave us -- Tenax -- it's really something special."

"When the weather turned cold and Vic came back from working by the coast, he seemed to have caught a chill. But after one night sleeping in that room, he was completely fine -- faster than going to the Cathedral of Light for a priest to heal him. I'm not trying to question the Holy Light or anything, but a sorceress that kind and with such powerful magic -- I don't doubt for a second she could handle a plague. You should bring her to our place for a meal sometime, along with Vereesa. I want to thank her properly."

"Quite a few people in Stormwind already know she's one of the green dragons now, Mrs. Sesso." Vereesa held her spoon. "So it's best she stays off the streets and doesn't attract attention. Or she needs to learn another shapeshifting spell to disguise herself quickly. But with her standards, she'll probably spend a long time being picky..."

Henny and Vic both froze. Jace stared wide-eyed at Vereesa too.

"What..." Vereesa stopped chewing, looking at the three stunned people in front of her. "Did I say something wrong, Mr. Sesso?"

"Wait... what did you say, Vereesa?" Vic asked.

"Uh, I mean, a lot of adult dragons know shapeshifting spells, Mr. Sesso." Vereesa said. "Even though Sora technically isn't an adult yet, she's been well taken care of..."

"A green dragon? She's a dragon in disguise?" Henny cut off Vereesa's words, stammering. "There was a dragon in this house?"
