Chapter 1697: Rumors and Rumors

"You didn't know?" Vereesa asked, surprised.

"Seems like not as many people know about her as I thought, Vereesa." Jace said quietly.

"We heard there was a green dragon in Stormwind City. But who would have guessed it was that blonde girl who likes wearing green?" Henny said. "I couldn't tell at all... She looks way too thin. Nothing about her suggests she's one of those terrifying magical reptiles."

"If either of you had figured it out, she'd be furious and hide in her room refusing to come out." Jace chuckled. "Oh, and don't use the word 'reptile' in front of her, Mother."

Henny quickly nodded, then sighed. "So that many of the rumors were true. At my age, I'd stopped easily believing all those stories floating around out there. Now I feel like I've never really lived."

"So that flower was green dragon magic too?" Vic asked.

"Of course." Vereesa said. "The green dragons guard the Dream. That flower is likely something from the Dream itself."

"Do you hear that, Vic?" Henny said quietly. "Our house has been enchanted with green dragon magic. But at least it's not like those stories about the Hinterlands -- the ones about green dragons spitting poison everywhere."

It's exactly that kind of green dragon. The words were almost out of Jace's mouth, but he swallowed them back. Better let Mom and Dad sleep soundly tonight.

"Unbelievable. I thought it was a hypnotic charm, since I fall asleep so easily in that room." Vic said.

"It almost certainly does have hypnotic effects," Jace said.

"I meant the other kind of 'hypnotic.'" Vic said. "The stuff they sell in Old Town. Fast-acting hypnotic potions, but they're actually a paralytic poison. A small bottle goes for ten silver. This flower's magic is clearly more on the positive side, right?"

Jace squinted. "I think your knowledge of this city is getting deeper than mine, Dad, and I've been here years longer."

"That's because the enemies we face now are all kinds of different. Not just murlocs, kobolds, and gnolls anymore." Vic walked deeper into the house and brought out a small keg of ale, setting it on the table. "You wouldn't believe the things that have been crawling up onto the shore these past few weeks."

"Is that the thing you mentioned about dealing with something north of the harbor?" Jace asked.

"Yeah." Vic said. "Have you heard of 'lobstermen'?"

"What, lobstermen?" Jace asked. "Don't those monsters usually live on another continent?"

"That's what the scholars at the Wizard Sanctum thought too, a month ago." Vic poured two glasses of wine, handing one to Jace and one to Vereesa. "But apparently we're not the only ones who know how to move. Those guys have also figured out how to cross the Great Sea to make a living over here. Maybe too many orcs or ogres went to the other side and forced them to flee here. Now that we've just ended the Orc Wars, there's room for them. Either way, nobody likes those aggressive, oversized creatures getting too close to the harbor. So the Warrior's Guild has some new work."

"Those lobstermen are tough. Way tougher than kobolds or murlocs. A group of them took over a gnoll cave, killed and ate all the gnolls. Not a bone left -- just some bone fragments and damaged Riverpaw armbands, and that's how we figured out what they'd eaten. So the harbor folks are paying well. Good money."

Henny glared at him sideways. "You sound pretty happy about it. Why don't you tell your son about how Thomas Cleverow's arm got chopped off by a lobsterman claw? How did you put it -- 'looked as easy as snapping a grasshopper leg'?"

Vereesa looked at Jace. "Maybe we should go check it out too."

"That cave's already been cleared out." Vic said. "She's just trying to scare you. Lobstermen are slow and clumsy."

Listening to the three of them discuss hunting the lobstermen, Jace looked out the window. The rain was heavier than before. It was late. The streets had quieted down.

Even though he couldn't quite put his finger on it, something about the appearance of these lobstermen didn't feel like a good sign to him.

"Any naga sightings?" Jace asked.

"No." Vic took a sip of wine. "I've only heard of naga showing up near Southshore recently. Don't worry, Jace. Even if there are naga, it's not like it's my first time dealing with them."

"Better worry about the sea witches than about the Black Knights," Henny said.

The mention of "Black Knights" made Jace's heart jump.

"Aren't Black Knights only supposed to be in Tirisfal?" Vic said. "Anyway, we've already moved. All we can do now is pray for the farm back home."

"But they're here too. And the rumors about Black Knights on this side came much earlier than in the north. They just died down for a few years in between." Henny said.

"By 'Black Knights' you mean the death knights created by orc warlocks, Mrs. Sesso." Vereesa looked at Jace, then said calmly. "The last death knight was defeated by Jace and me together, just east of Blackrock Spire. Though Nefarian tried to use the bodies of many knights and nobles, including Marshal Anduin Lothar, to create more death knights this time, the Alliance stopped him before he succeeded. Marshal Lothar's body has been safely returned to Stormwind City. So there won't be any more Black Knights roaming around."

"Did you run into any death knights inside Blackrock Spire?" Vic asked.

"Well... no." Vereesa said.

"Doesn't that mean that if there were death knights wandering around before, they never returned to the Horde's stronghold, and they were never defeated?" Henny said.

"Because the whole thing was fabricated to spread panic, Mom." Jace drew a circle on the table and pointed to the center. "To create a death knight, you need the soul of a powerful orc warlock, a place with concentrated necromantic energy -- like a necropolis or an orc burial ground -- then guide the gathered souls through a necromantic ritual to complete a soul infusion. The process is extremely complex... It can't be done in secret without anyone knowing. Before, by the Neltharion River, a death knight tried to secretly swap bodies, and it left a trail of magical residue all along the way. That's how Teron Gorefiend exposed himself and was eliminated. Vereesa and I tracked the whole process. We know exactly what's involved."

"That's right." Vereesa said. "If any more death knights truly appeared, we would tell you both immediately."

"Rumors -- some true, some false. We need to get better at telling them apart." Vic said. "We've already got a dark magic advisor to the king in this family. No need to listen to the nonsense outside, Henny."

"That's right. We'll listen to you, Jace." Henny smiled. "I feel much better now that you've explained it."

"Enough of this disgusting talk." Vic said. "Let's eat."

"We might be heading to Kalimdor in a couple of days." Jace said. "Won't be gone long."

"Oh, is this what people call a honeymoon trip?" Henny asked with a grin.

"Something like that," the ranger said softly, looking embarrassed.

The atmosphere in the room suddenly became much warmer. After all, for a family gathering, talking about nothing but fighting, black magic, and undead... that really was depressing.

Late at night, Jace went outside to check on the horses -- to see if Arna was getting rained on. When he reached the fence, he glanced toward a nearby shadow. Two dark red glints appeared in the darkness.

Arna wasn't startled by Morofel's presence. The mare had spent enough time with this demon.

"Don't follow us to Kalimdor, Morofel." Jace said quietly in Demonic. "Stay here by this house. Protect my mother and father. Don't give those nathrezim any chance to scheme against them. For this task... you're the only one I trust."

"It is my honor, Master," the demon replied.
