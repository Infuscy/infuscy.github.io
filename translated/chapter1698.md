## Chapter 1698: Simultaneous Operations

"At last, the victor of Blackrock Spire returns."

Drudgemoor's voice echoed from the shadowy hall. The central hearth-fire burned low, and the whole room smelled of roasted meat. The members of the Dagger Society and their demon servants were all there, including Zardeth and Surena Caledon.

"Welcome back, Jace," Zardeth said.

"It's a bit unusual seeing everyone at once without a scheduled meeting time," Jace said as he descended the steps.

"Exactly right." Erlan smiled. "Because we did schedule it — we've been waiting here for you."

"We've all heard about the spell you used to defeat that black dragon prince, Jace," Surena said.

Jace glanced at Ursula, wondering if she'd told the whole Dagger Society about Dark Simulacrum. He wasn't really ready to teach that spell to anyone yet.

Even if Medivh hadn't thought much of the spell's practical use, the fact that he'd hidden it away in such a secret place said enough about how dangerous he considered it. And when Jace had tried to replicate it, the blurred shadow he'd summoned only made him more aware that using this spell improperly could lead to all kinds of unforeseen consequences.

"A Soul Well beyond anything I could imagine..." Surena stood up excitedly. "I don't think even Gul'dan himself ever had the chance to cast soul magic on that scale. Don't you have any experience with it you'd like to share with us?"

"I wouldn't be surprised if he doesn't know how to teach others the technique, Sul." Erlan chuckled. "Just from the rumors coming out of Blackrock Spire, I'm starting to wonder if Jace Sesso was even still human by the time he reached the Slaughtered Lamb's door."

Erlan smiled, but when he saw the stiff look on Jace's face, he quickly dropped it.

In the old days, a joke like that from Erlan would have had Jace laughing along. But this time around — or rather, ever since coming out of Molten Core — Jace had found it hard to casually joke about shadow magic anymore, even with his Dagger Society companions.

"At least you look like you're in good shape to me, Jace," Ursula said. "A lot of mages at the Wizard's Sanctum say the shadow magic you've been casting is too powerful and might end up hurting you, so I've been worried."

"Your Destruction Potion helped a lot." Jace sat down, picked up a clay cup, and poured himself some wine. "As for the Soul Well... I'm not sure I could replicate what happened at Blackrock Spire without the Destruction Potion."

"Maybe casting it in the Telogrus Rift, using the power of the Twisting Nether, could replace some of the Destruction Potion's effects?" Zardeth asked.

"Those two conditions are hard to compare, Zardeth," Ursula said.

"I'll write down everything I experienced at the top of Blackrock Spire when I cast that spell — the emotions, the process of manipulating souls — as detailed as I can," Jace said.

"That would be best." Zardeth held a small cookie in his hand, glancing between Surena and Erlan. "I heard from Mathias Shaw that you're heading to Kalimdor next to look for Onyxia. That gives us time to correct our own practice based on what you write down."

"Don't think I'm being overly cautious." Jace set down his cup. "After Blackrock Spire, I don't have more confidence in the Soul Well — I'm more worried about it. The scale and power of that spell feel... limitless. If I'm going to cast it at the Telogrus Rift, I'll need to be even more careful about losing control."

"It might have no upper limit for you, but for someone without any special talent like me, it's different." Erlan let out a self-deprecating laugh. "At least let me practice. No matter how badly I screw it up, the worst that happens is I burn myself to a pile of charred bones. It won't cause any real damage."

"Don't sell yourself short, dear," Surena murmured. "You might not even manage to burn yourself to death."

Laughter rippled around the hearth. The chaotic mood of this group of warlocks even tinged the flames with a hint of black.

"Jace." Jalane Ayrole said. "I hope you remember to bring that Demonic Soul Robe we painstakingly crafted together. I didn't charge a single copper for labor or materials — all I asked for was a detailed customer review."

"Of course I brought it. You almost made me forget." Jace handed over a bundle. "All wrapped up for you."

Jalane's excitement was barely contained as she untied the bundle. "By the way, you never gave it a name, did you?"

"A name..." Jace scratched his head. "Look at everything I've been through lately. When would I have had time to think of a name?"

"Actually, it already has one." Ursula leaned back in her chair. "Even someone like me who doesn't follow the tailoring scene has heard tailors in the Mage Quarter talking about that robe Jalane made for you. Do you really think a terrifying garment with demonic souls swimming across its surface would go unnoticed? I'm sure every time that red cloak of yours billows up in the arcane wind, whatever shows up underneath scares the hell out of Alliance soldiers."

"Ursula's right." Jalane nodded. "A lot of people call it 'Sesso's Shroud' now. Mention that name around any wizard or tailor who cares about robes, and they'll know exactly what you're talking about. I doubt any official name you come up with now would be more popular than that one."

"Works for me," Jace said.

"You wore this shroud to repel the Firelord and defeat a black dragon prince... without a scratch." Jalane carefully lifted the robe out of the bundle, examining it as she spoke. "Maybe I should be paying you. What other tailor's work has such a legendary history?"

"I wouldn't say 'without a scratch,'" Jace said, a little sheepishly.

The tailor held the robe up and immediately noticed the cut on the side. Her face went cold. "What could pierce this robe?"

"Azuresong," Jace said.

"Isn't that sword yours?" Ursula asked. "And your wound?"

"The wound doesn't hurt much anymore. Don't forget, the Spire is practically occupied by the Silver Hand and the Church now, Ursula." Jace patted his side. "The black dragon prince caught me with a mind-control spell for a moment — long enough to try to make me hurt myself."

"Did you hear the incantation for that spell, Jace?" Erlan suddenly sat up and leaned forward. "None of us have ever faced an adult black dragon's mental magic!"

"What do you want with mental magic?" Surena squinted at him. "Is there a female goblin in Booty Bay you haven't bedded yet?"

"This is about magical advancement, the same principle the Kirin Tor archmages follow, Surena Caledon. You can't call yourself a high-ranking warlock without mastering a broad enough range of spells!" Erlan declared righteously.

"Unfortunately, I didn't hear any incantation," Jace said. "That black dragon seemed to be born with that kind of power."

"According to the warlocks in Shadowforge City, black dragons really do seem to be born with the ability to control minds," Zardeth said, nodding. "If it were a Nathrezim-type demon's mental magic, we might actually be able to get our hands on the incantation."

"I doubt it," Jalane said. "When I was in Dalaran, I read some records about the Nathrezim. Their mind-control tricks are probably even more varied than the black dragons'."

"There's something I've been meaning to tell you all," Jace said. "About the Nathrezim."

"Go on, Jace." Zardeth took a sip of wine. "I'm always interested in demon matters."

"During the Blackrock Spire campaign, the Alliance discovered that the black dragon prince had allied with the Nathrezim," Jace said. "This isn't just speculation. We found evidence left behind by Nathrezim demons — high-level necromancy, mind-control magic... not all of it related to black dragons. You all know the Nathrezim are high-ranking demons. Summoning them to Azeroth requires rituals that can't be hidden. King Varian, the Kirin Tor, the Church, and I all want to find more traces of these rituals."

"You suspect a warlock is helping the Nathrezim contact the black dragons," Zardeth said.

"Yes." Jace said. "Not all the warlocks fled to Kalimdor after the Lighthouse incident. Demons can't just teleport across the Twisting Nether out of nowhere."

"We'll ask around for you, Jace." Erlan said. "I've heard recently that a group of warlocks in Booty Bay is trying to summon stronger demons. The goblins there have an observer helping them. Who knows what kind of nightmare they'll summon."

"Nothing out of Booty Bay would surprise me," Zardeth said. "But I don't think it's likely to be Nathrezim. Still... better to check thoroughly. You and Surena head south. Jalane and I will ask around in various places up north."

"At your command, Mr. Black Claw," Surena said.

When it came to investigating demon summoning rituals, the Dagger Society's contacts and leads were probably richer than the Kirin Tor's — and they attracted less attention. Jace couldn't just rely on Khadgar's mages to lure the enemy out. He had to be proactive too. While he went to investigate Onyxia, he needed to use every resource at his disposal.
