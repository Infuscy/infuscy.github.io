Chapter 170: Setting the Stage

Kel'Thuzad had already transcribed the crooked runes from this suit of armor, annotated them, and sent a copy to every kingdom represented.

Jace looked carefully at the lines and felt that with his Orcish level he could more or less grasp what they were trying to say.

He'd assumed the lines might be some kind of enchantment incantation, written in blood by warlocks using the power of blood to inscribe even more sinister runes onto this already extremely evil-looking armor. But these passages all described scenery — green grasslands, towering mountains, a great sea dividing the two…

The more Jace read, the more he felt this was an orc's praise for this new world, Azeroth, written after fel energy had corrupted his own planet. But at the same time he found it strange — why would an orcs write these things in blood?

It was only when he reached the last two runes that he understood.

"Can't go back."

This was actually about Draenor?

Connecting it to the game's content — the green grasslands were Nagrand, or maybe Terokkar? The towering mountains were Frostfire Ridge, or Gorgrond… and the sea, was that the Zangar Sea?

Jace could hardly understand why this orc had written these things before dying. Was he homesick for his homeland? Did Azeroth remind him of home? Or was it the pain of defeat, of being unable to return through the Dark Portal? There was no way to know.

Kel'Thuzad explained the content of the runes to everyone. He intended to use this suit of armor, inscribed with such peculiar content, as the anchor for his opening remarks — to let everyone see a different side of orcish relics.

"Glimpsing another face of orcish civilization."

That line could be called quite politically incorrect, especially because this metal armor suggested its owner had likely been a very powerful warrior of high rank — since most orc foot soldiers wore light armor, or even fought bare-chested.

The Warsong clan, for instance, went into battle with their chests bared.

Orc clans that relied on heavy armor for protection — especially armor in these black and red tones — were most likely the most ferocious clans in the Horde, such as the Blackrock or the Dragonmaw.

Which meant the owner of this armor might have killed any number of humans, only to find himself thinking of home at the very end before death.

The Orcish-language scholars were, in theory, already the segment of the populace most tolerant of orcs. Yet hearing Kel'Thuzad's interpretation, some of them still wore complicated expressions.

Jace tried to guess his motive. Whether as one of the leaders of the Kirin Tor, as the host of the Orcish-language meeting, or as a secret practitioner of necromantic magic — in any of these roles, Kel'Thuzad would certainly want the people who came to speak without reservation, to say whatever was on their minds.

So he might be planning to use the most boundary-pushing possible opening to set the floor, to put everyone at ease and keep outside public-opinion factors from interfering with the sharing of knowledge.

That way, he could also extract the maximum amount of information from the Orcish-language materials sent from every direction.

Next, the scholars from the Kingdom of Kul Tiras presented a letter and the untranslated runes on a war sword.

The letter roughly described the orcish war fleet setting out from Stormwind's harbor to attack Kul Tiras. Of course, with the war over, the letter no longer held any military value.

Even during the war, the Horde's warships hadn't been particularly outstanding in combat compared to Kul Tiras's warships. What had been truly terrifying were the red dragons that appeared on the seas alongside the orcish vessels.

The runes on the weapon, translated jointly by everyone, yielded a name and a surname with practical meaning. They guessed the weapon's owner had probably been a Blood Guard of the Blacktooth Grin clan.

Jace noticed that Kel'Thuzad wasn't particularly interested in these two items, but still said some polite pleasantries, praising the materials the Kingdom of Kul Tiras had brought, and made a show of discussing knowledge related to the weapon and orcish warships.

Perhaps it was because these contents had nothing to do with necromantic magic.

Jace glanced at his pocket watch. It was already past five. If they let every visiting scholar present their materials one by one, the time might push toward nine or ten o'clock. Kel'Thuzad, the orcish materials, the real purpose of the meeting…

Thinking all this, Jace had begun to feel uneasy — especially adding in what Kae'fal had told him earlier, about the black dragons rarely showing up anymore.

After tonight passed and tomorrow morning came, he planned to talk to Grede about leaving Dalaran for the Alterac Mountains. In any case, this place was no longer suitable for staying.

The other scholars and students, however, showed no sign of weariness.

Sometime around seven, after the Gilneans' summary and discussion of Orcish pronunciation concluded, Kel'Thuzad looked over in their direction.

"The next portion of time is reserved for Master Caro Dushan of the Kingdom of Stormwind and his student." The Kirin Tor archmage glanced at the materials in his hand. "Master Dushan has brought materials concerning the orcs' interactions with other Azerothian races, as well as records of the orcs' attempts to learn Common in Stormwind."

The Kingdom of Stormwind was the place that had suffered the most from the orcish invasion. As Dushan stepped forward to report on the linguistic traces the orcs had left in the Kingdom of Stormwind that he and his students had spent the past year investigating and uncovering, the scholars from the other countries listened with particular attentiveness.

Jace wasn't in the mood to listen to his own teacher's presentation, because his turn was coming up right after.

Facing Kel'Thuzad, he felt there were some things from before he could no longer say carelessly. After all, Kel'Thuzad was an extremely sharp mage. The hints and warnings about the holy relic had to be handled even more carefully.

But if he was too careful to the point where they couldn't understand him… The script still wasn't finished being revised when Jace suddenly heard Kel'Thuzad call his name.

"Next is Mister Jace Sesso. You have an untranslated command from a high-ranking orc officer, correct? You may bring it up. If possible, I can ask Mister Bromiam to help you recite it in Orcish, and we can translate it together."

Kel'Thuzad's tone was no longer as warm as before — after all, there had already been several so-called battle plans, troop-transfer orders, and the like, all outdated materials from several years ago.

"Thank you, Archmage Kel'Thuzad." Jace stood up. "This command letter, I believe, is of great importance. I speculate that its author was a student of Gul'dan — Teron Gorefiend himself."

Hearing that name, the crowd broke into discussion. Dushan's eyes widened too, and in Kel'Thuzad's composed gaze flickered a barely perceptible shift in emotion. He was interested?
