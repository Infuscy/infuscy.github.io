## Chapter 1700: Rest

Despite days of drizzling rain, Anduin Lothar's funeral proceeded as scheduled.

Before the gates of Stormwind Keep, the funeral procession had already gathered. The Marshal's casket rested on a beautiful, ornate carriage, its exterior decorated with gilded motifs of lions, suns, and floral crowns. The casket was draped in deep blue and bright Arathi-red velvet, upon which lay Lothar's golden-lion-crested right pauldron and a runic Grand Marshal greatsword set with a single massive, unadorned fire opal.

Six handsome Arathi horses pulled the carriage. On both sides rode Stormwind's knights in gleaming silver armor, blue-tasseled silver nasal helms, and flowing blue-and-gold lion cloaks. Turalyon himself led them, dressed identically. He could have waited at the Cathedral of Light with the other nobles and clergy, but he insisted on riding with the knights to escort Marshal Lothar on his final journey.

The procession passed along the canal, through the gates of the Dwarf District, and headed toward the Cathedral of Light. Blue banners and golden tassels stretched far into the distance.

The streets were lined with citizens who had come of their own accord to see off Marshal Lothar. There was barely enough room on the roads. People filled every rooftop and every distant bridge, some nearly pushed into the canal. Even so, more and more citizens kept pouring in from the Trade District and Old Town.

The guards lining the roads had to nervously urge the crowd to make way, but no one felt like shouting at them. Everyone knew what Marshal Lothar meant to this kingdom. Without him, this city — this kingdom — might not exist at all. His body had lain on the Burning Steppes since the war's end, never returned. Every person saved by him and his armies had carried that guilt all these years.

Before the steps of the Cathedral of Light, Mathias Shaw and Stormwind's other nobles and military leaders had already assembled. On both sides of the high stairs, sword-bearers in heavy blue ceremonial robes formed two long rows stretching all the way to the square's flowerbeds and statue fountain, clearing a path for the carriage.

According to a court mage, this funeral was entirely at the level of a king — even grander than King Llane Wrynn's had been.

Of course, part of that was because Llane's funeral had been held while Stormwind was still in crisis, so everything was kept simple. But it still spoke volumes about Anduin Lothar's standing in Stormwind and the Alliance.

Jace, Vereesa, and Grede stood alongside the leaders of the Sons of Lothar. Like the other court mages, Jace was properly dressed in a blue-and-purple robe, but he wore his Olastrasz Hide cloak over it.

Varian believed the cloak had helped Jace withstand orc and dragon sorcery and flame, saved the entire campaign, and rescued the Marshal's trapped body. It was one of the most important symbols of victory from the Blackrock Spire campaign and deserved to appear at this funeral, even if its dragonhide exterior was a little intimidating to common folk. It wouldn't bring any negative influence, so Jace wore it.

Varian and his queen Tiffin stood at the foot of the steps, watching the direction the procession was coming from. If the funeral was at royal scale, then this was the position of a prince and heir.

In truth, Marshal Lothar had been almost like a father to him. After Llane Wrynn was assassinated, Lothar had raised him, taught him, and helped him reclaim the throne of Stormwind. It was only fitting that Varian stood here today.

When the carriage reached the steps, the citizens followed the procession into the square. The silence was deafening. In the drizzling rain, even the faint patter of droplets on the marble steps was clearly audible.

Alleria, Turalyon, Danath, and Khadgar — the four leaders of the Sons of Lothar — stepped forward, two on each side, and lifted the Marshal's casket from the carriage. Kurdran Wildhammer stood at the back, raising a hand to steady it.

As the cathedral bells tolled, the five slowly climbed the white steps. Varian nodded to Jace and the others nearby, and everyone followed the king behind the casket into the cathedral.

Even the sound of rain fell silent. The scent of incense quickly masked the damp earthy smell of rain. The usually dim and somber cathedral hall was now ablaze with light, somehow brighter inside than out.

Nobles and lords from every kingdom who had come for the funeral were already waiting. Among them were even high-ranking members of the Magisterium from the Sunwell Plateau, along with representatives of Bronzebeard, Wildhammer, and even Dark Iron.

Sora and Needleflame were also there. Sora wore an elaborate dark green robe that contrasted sharply with her usual style, and beside her, Needleflame was dressed in a black-and-red longcoat, looking every bit a true princess.

Vaelastrasz was also present, still wearing his usual simple dark red robe, separated from Sora by only two gnomes from Gnomeregan — standing not far from a few elven magisters.

To someone who didn't know them, this group of unfamiliar-looking nobles seemed to represent no particular nation or faction. But those in the know understood they represented the dragonflights' gratitude to Marshal Lothar.

When the Dark Portal first opened, with the calamity of Deathwing's rampage and the Demon Soul's emergence, the already-weakened dragonflights had been dealt a devastating blow, unable to stop the demon and orc invasions. It was the heroes led by Lothar who had stepped forward, preventing an even greater catastrophe.

The cathedral filled with hymn-singing, praising the Holy Light's grace and blessings. The melody was moving without being intrusive, stirring the soul and making one think of where this warrior's spirit had gone — as if the afterlife were not so frightening or mysterious after all.

The Sons of Lothar placed the casket on the dais, the hymn reached its climax, and then slowly faded.

Archbishop Benedictus recounted Marshal Lothar's life: from the Gurubashi War to the fall of Stormwind... to the moment when the seven kingdoms were crumbling, and it was he who stood up and called upon all the living to fight for this world.

He had reminded every king and leader in Lordaeren of how, during the two-thousand-four-hundred-year empire, the seven kingdoms had stood together against the Amani Empire; he had appealed to Anasterian Sunstrider about the ancient pact between Strom and Quel'Thalas that the king himself had signed, calling him once more to join the war that would decide the fate of the continent; he had led the Alliance to crush the forest trolls allied with the Horde, and alongside the Wildhammer, broken the siege of Ironforge, earning the trust and friendship of King Magni Bronzebeard. From that, he and his followers built an unprecedented grand alliance that shattered the barriers between dwarves, elves, and humans — the Lordaeron Alliance. Then they marched south, retook Stormwind, dismantled the once-mighty Horde that had seemed unstoppable just two years prior, and drove the remnants back through the Dark Portal.

Now he lay in the casket before them, leaving the joy of victory and the promise of peace to the successors and the peoples of the nations he had protected. May the Holy Light guide his spirit peacefully to the other shore.
