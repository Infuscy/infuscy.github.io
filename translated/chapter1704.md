## Chapter 1704: Gray Beach

Night had fallen. The raptor, neither particularly large nor small, was more than half-eaten. From a distance it looked like a decomposed baby plesiosaur carcass washed ashore.

Needleflame and Grede had long since passed out. The little black dragon had flown all that way and then sparred with Vereesa on the beach all afternoon — it was surprising she'd lasted this long. Grede, even blessed by his ancestors and strong enough to stand before Ragnaros without flinching, hadn't improved much in the napping department. Add in the fact that this island had no real dangers, and a good meal was all he needed to conk out.

Not just them — Vereesa too. She hadn't slept since coming back from Blackrock Spire. Now she was sitting under a nearby tree, eyes half-closed. Jace, though, wasn't tired yet. He listened to the waves crashing against the gray beach and rocks, staring at the moonlit sea and cliffs, waiting for drowsiness to creep up on him.

Sora sat motionless beside Needleflame, who was curled up like a little snake. If Jace hadn't caught a glint of moonlight in her eyes, he'd have thought she was asleep too.

"Aren't you tired?" Jace asked.

"I've been sleeping the whole way here, Jace." Sora replied. "You should get some rest. Vereesa is tired too. Morophil didn't come with us, so I can keep watch tonight."

"Alright," Jace said. But he could feel the green dragon's melancholy.

She hadn't eaten much tonight — or at least, not much compared to her usual feasting in Elwynn Forest. Normally both dragons had no qualms about raw meat. Needleflame had even gnawed at the raptor carcass when she wasn't satisfied with the cooked stuff. So Sora, being older and larger, should have had no hesitation either.

But she sat there like she had no appetite. Clearly she was still thinking about what Grede had said earlier, even though he'd dropped the subject once everyone started eating.

It reminded Jace of that time in Sun Rock Tauren, when he, Vereesa, and Grede had first come to Kalimdor. Vereesa had somehow gotten onto the same topic with him there too — about lifespan. And now Grede had just mentioned Sun Rock. What a coincidence.

In Azeroth, most humans — mages aside — didn't live past fifty or sixty. They died to accidents, war, or disease.

That's what Jace had expected too. When he hit his twenties, he figured he had maybe another twenty or thirty years left. Then he became a mercenary who ran all over the place. He'd need insane luck just to reach that age.

So when Grede said he had maybe a hundred or two hundred years left, and Jace might live longer, and Vereesa could live one or two thousand years... Jace felt like he was losing all sense of those numbers.

It had only been about ten years since he'd settled in Stormwind. So much had happened in those ten years, especially since he became Arcanist Malin's assistant. Who knew what the next ten years would bring, let alone a hundred or two hundred?

Grede's tone was entirely joking — he was just trying to give Sora some confidence, hoping she'd become the Green Dragon Queen someday. Jace figured it was the same as friends teasing each other, like "when you're king, make me a duke."

But they'd completely overlooked Sora's feelings. She was already a thousand years old. The War of the Shifting Sands, which happened around the time she was born, was practically a mythical tale that almost no one alive today had even heard of. Even for the long-lived elves, it was a distant legend from so long ago they could barely remember hearing about it.

But for her... it felt like she'd only blinked a few times. Sora herself had said more than once that the past few years of adventuring with them had been more eventful than all her centuries in the Emerald Dream.

Time in the Dream could be slow or fast. A Kaldorei druid might walk into a cave while humans were still fighting the tribal wars and wake up to find the Arathi Empire had been founded, flourished, and fractured. Dalaran had gone from a lakeside village to a free city famous across the kingdoms, then to the capital of magic.

It was entirely possible that if Sora accepted Ysera's appointment and returned to the Emerald Dream as a dream guardian, she'd wake up to find the world had changed beyond recognition — everyone she knew in the grave, maybe not even their graves left to find.

Jace couldn't help thinking about what things would be like three thousand years from now. Sora wouldn't even be considered old among dragons then. And Needleflame even less so — she was Deathwing's direct bloodline, destined to live far longer than dragons like Sora.

By then, would they even remember this fleeting adventure? If Needleflame ever became the new Black Dragon Queen, would she still remember her "childhood playmate"?

"You're not asleep yet, Jace?" Sora asked.

"Not yet." Jace shrugged. "Probably Needleflame's rough flying. My brain's still rattling around in my skull. Can't sleep."

Sora laughed softly. Then she looked into the distance, thinking for a while, before saying, "There's a question I've been wanting to ask you. But it might make you unhappy, Jace."

"Go ahead," Jace said. "Just don't ask how much savings I have."

"We don't have a habit of hoarding gold and jewels." Sora raised a golden eyebrow. After a pause, she continued, "If Queen Alexstrasza hadn't given you the blessing of Life Fire, do you think you'd still be with Vereesa Windrunner?"

"Huh? That's your question?" Jace chuckled. "You weren't at Grim Batol, Sora."

He told her. "I used a trick to destroy the Demon Soul. The power of four Dragon Aspects escaped and was about to disperse back to the Aspects who owned it — including Queen Alexstrasza, who was fighting Deathwing right in front of us."

"Deathwing had two choices. One: retreat and face the four Aspects who'd regained their original power, while he was still badly wounded from Draenor. Even fighting the newly empowered Life-Binder alone would have been a stretch, let alone with the other three. If he gave up, the black dragonflight might never recover."

"So he chose to gamble everything — he swallowed all the power and took the second path. He might have become the most terrifying thing to ever walk Azeroth. With one breath, he completely erased Thanystrasz's life. Then came Queen Alexstrasza, and then..."

"And then you," Sora said.

"Yes." Jace said. "Facing Deathwing, every second felt like it could be my last. So I seized the moment and told her. Who knew back then if something like the Shadowlands even existed?"

Sora just watched him, listening to him describe that scene. Hard to tell what she was thinking.

"And that's what I want to say to you," Jace said. "Maybe in Draenor, in Molten Core... we could have lost our lives. A hundred years or a thousand years wouldn't have mattered then. Don't dwell too much on what's coming. Live in the moment. Treasure the now. Look at the Burning Legion breathing down our necks — Azeroth might not even last another thousand years."

Sora finally laughed out loud. She took a breath, gazed at the White Lady on the sea, and said softly, "Yeah."

Even Jace felt a little comforted by his own words.

He thought back to when he'd sailed to Menethil Harbor. Back then, he really had put all thoughts of what might happen next out of his mind.

What was using dark magic compared to the threat of Deathwing? What was the Kirin Tor's lockdown, the Dalaran prison, compared to having two friends willing to follow him into death?

But still... "Sometimes," Sora said quietly, "sometimes I still wish you were all my kind. I wish you were a green dragon too, Jace. I know it's selfish..."

"If I were a green dragon, I'd definitely marry Queen Ysera and become the Dream Lord," Jace joked.

"Only the strongest and most excellent male dragons can become Queen Ysera's mate, Jace." Sora explained anyway. But then she looked at him and added, "Although I don't doubt that if you were one of us, you'd earn the Queen's favor."

"In that case, I'd be your dad, wouldn't I?" Jace laughed.

"Of course," Sora said seriously.

That shut Jace up. When you try to mess with someone and they don't even get mad, what's the fun in that?
