Chapter 1705: One Kind of Green, Another Kind of Green

The next morning, everyone took off again and continued westward.

Honestly, Jace was a little surprised they hadn't run into some secret ancient troll tribe ambush on this island. By past precedent, whenever it seemed like they might be in for trouble, they usually were — especially back in Feralas.

Probably because Sora's intel from the mountain forests was far more reliable than Vereesa's scouting back then.

Come to think of it, since setting out again, Sora seemed much more relaxed. From a distance he could hear her bickering with Grede, and she occasionally traded banter with Needleflame when they flew close. The anxiety and sadness from the night before had mostly cleared.

This wasn't something he guessed from her appearance — he could feel it through their connection. He didn't like probing his companions' emotions, but sometimes the change was too strong not to notice.

By the afternoon of the fourth day, the familiar golden sands of the Tanaris desert appeared at the edge of the blue horizon. Grede let out an excited roar. Even though the sight of the vast blue sea had been thrilling at the start of the journey, a week-plus of that had turned it into an endless blue-black hell.

"The desert! So this is a desert? I thought Azeroth's deserts were all like Gorgrond — I didn't expect this! All gold!" Needleflame whooped below, the vibration in her chest making Jace's whole body tingle. "I bet the bronze dragons rule this place!"

Jace blinked. "How do you know that? Did Sora tell you?"

The Caverns of Time, where the bronze dragons lived, was indeed located deep in Tanaris. Occasionally there were rumors that ancient time guardians lived in the depths of this desert, but no one knew exactly where, and no mortal had ever explored deep enough to find the entrance to that temporal vortex and successfully bring back concrete information to Zul'Farrak or Gadgetzan.

Even in Dalaran, only the oldest and most senior Council members — or high-ranking archmages like Khadgar — connected the time guardians with the bronze dragonflight.

"The chromatic dragonkin in Blackrock Spire cast bronze dragon magic that involved sand." Needleflame said smugly, like a detective who'd found the missing piece of evidence. "So it makes sense the bronze dragons live in the desert, doesn't it?"

You got lucky with that guess, Jace thought.

"The bronze dragons do live in the desert in southern Kalimdor, but I'm not sure if it's specifically Tanaris, where the goblins live." Sora flew closer.

"Then maybe this time we'll get to see real bronze dragons. Would they disguise themselves as goblins and live in goblin cities, sister Sora?" Needleflame asked. "If I meet a real bronze dragon, that just leaves the blue dragons that I haven't seen."

"You've actually seen them all, Needleflame," Sora said.

"When?" Needleflame twisted her neck toward the green dragon. "I didn't know that."

"They came to Stormwind just to see you, but they didn't tell you," Sora said.

If Sora hadn't said that, Jace might not have thought anything of it. But now he suddenly remembered the two gnome mages at Marshal Lothar's funeral, standing near Vaelastrasz, watching the casket slowly approach the dais.

One wore a black-and-gray robe, the other a milky white tunic. They were probably a blue dragon and a bronze dragon.

Come to think of it, that was a rare moment when all five dragonflights stood together. Ever since the black dragons betrayed the others, aside from the Wyrmrest Temple gatherings where everyone still refrained from attacking the black dragon emissary out of respect for ancient tradition, nowhere else had seen such harmony.

Not long after, they spotted the palm groves and the Steamwheedle Port nestled among them. The two dragons gave the port a wide berth, circling south over the sea before landing and shifting into human form.

A small path led toward the port. Looked like the goblins of Gadgetzan traveled south now and then. Jace's group followed the path north toward Gadgetzan. They hadn't gone far when a bunch of goblins suddenly popped out from a slope and the roadside bushes, shouting and blocking their way.

"Stop!"

Jace initially thought it was the water thieves that had been terrorizing Gadgetzan. But looking closer, each goblin carried a metal round shield with the Gadgetzan Water Company emblem. Probably just Steamwheedle Port security.

"Identify yourselves! You weird-looking lot don't know whose turf this is?!" The lead goblin guard brandished a "warhammer" whose gear-head started spinning wildly with a deafening little engine roar.

Needleflame stepped forward and grabbed the spinning gear, stalling the tiny motor until it smoked. The goblin guard leader yanked frantically at his hammer but couldn't pull it free — there was no way a professional security guard in that tiny body could match a real young dragon's strength.

"Let go, you freaky strong brat!" the goblin shrieked. The other goblin guards rattled their scimitars as if to close in, but the sight of this unreasonably strong human girl made them hesitate. Finally Jace had to say, "They're not enemies, Karosha."

"I'm from another continent, sir." Jace showed the goblin his Stormwind court mage ring. "I'm here on a mission from King Varian Wrynn of Stormwind to investigate the black dragon's trail. We got blown off course by the recent storms and only just found the road to Gadgetzan."

"Storms?" The goblin yanked his "warhammer" free from Needleflame's cold-eyed grip and glanced at the sea. "What storms? How long have you been traveling to get here?"

Jace exchanged looks with Sora, Grede, and Vereesa. Apparently that mysterious storm hadn't reached Tanaris.

"It's not that I don't believe you, mage." The goblin said. "I know the kingdom of Stormwind. I know the new king, Varian Wrynn. But I don't recognize this ring..."

"Mr. Noggenfogger of Gadgetzan knows us," Vereesa said. "Don't waste time, guardsman. Take us to him."

"Ah, Noggenfogger the moneybags. Well, if you'd said that sooner..." The goblin turned around. "Boys! Rackaski and Grizzle, stay here. The rest of you, come with me — let's escort these distinguished guests from the other continent to the port for a rest! This way, please..."

The guards trotted ahead on their stubby legs, wearing oversized boots. Jace's group followed. Needleflame kept looking at the little goblins with barely concealed disgust, as if she wanted to punch each one.

"Next time, don't throw the first punch so fast, Needleflame," Jace murmured.

The little black dragon whispered back, "Look at them. Sneaky little bastards. Green skin, yellow skin, black skin, pointy ears. Shifty and wicked. They look like a bunch of malnourished orcs!"

That shut Jace up, because he happened to think the same thing...

"Ha." Grede chuckled. "By that logic, green dragons are just severely mutated flying orcs?"

Sora's face went cold. "Watch your mouth, dwarf. You better not fall asleep tonight, or I'll give you a nightmare you'll remember for the rest of your life!"

"Oh, now I'm even more looking forward to it, Sora!" Grede laughed. "What are you going to throw at the High Thane in my dreams? Let me guess — twenty black dragons? Don't worry, I'll show you what kind of power I have in dreams..."

Needleflame turned and muttered, "Why would..."

"Much scarier than black dragons!" Sora shot back.

"What is it? What could it be?" Grede cupped his ears with an obnoxiously smug look.

But Sora couldn't actually come up with anything. Jace knew she was good at putting people's minds into dreams, but she had no experience filling them with scary things — even after her own nightmare corruption.

"I wonder when you last saw your own chin, Grede." Vereesa smirked. "Help him revisit that memory tonight, Sora."

"WHAT?!" Grede's eyes went wide. "Have you lost your mind, elf?!"

"You can call me 'pointy ears' again, you boring lump of dirt." Vereesa said sweetly.

"Agh! Bloody hell! Jace Sesso — did you put her up to this?"

"That's got nothing to do with me..."
