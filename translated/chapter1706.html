Chapter 1706: Meeting the Master of Gadgetzan Again

"Welcome, welcome! Grand Master Jace Sesso, magical advisor to the Stormwind King! It's been a few years, hasn't it?"

Marlin Noggenfogger rose from his white lion-hide throne, spread his arms, and grinned so wide his mouth nearly reached his ears. His voice was raspier than the average goblin's as he bellowed happily.

His voice was deeper probably because he'd put on a lot of weight since last time. He used to be a core member of the Steamwheedle Cartel; now many recognized him as "the moneybags of Gadgetzan." Jace figured he'd effectively become the ruler of this desert city.

But Noggenfogger hadn't gone in for the gold and jewels like Booty Bay's fat cats. He still wore that simple white shirt, though the buttons were strained a bit tighter.

Jace could also tell that while his smile had a business component, it was genuine happiness.

"For us short-lived folk, the esteemed Ranger-General Vereesa Windrunner..." Noggenfogger personally ran over to a deerskin sofa, dusted it off, and tidied the blanket. "A life doesn't have that many 'few years,' so I truly treasure every chance to see you all."

"Sorry about wrecking your zeppelin last time, goblin," Grede said in a tone that was trying to be polite.

"Ha! That zeppelin!" Noggenfogger wagged a finger. "I didn't want to pay the insurance premiums anyway. You wouldn't believe how much the Bilgewater Cartel's new bosses would have squeezed out of me. Not that I had many options. Letting that zeppelin get shot down by a black dragon was the perfect ending. Even their professional adjusters couldn't find a good reason to deny the claim..."

"And the black dragons have been laying low lately. Even the ogres who were running wild in that swamp under their protection have calmed down. Gazlowe and I are thinking about further developing that ugly-looking but untapped goldmine. Well, 'untapped' isn't quite right — the black dragons have been exploiting it for a thousand years. But as long as we clear out all the obstacles, I can just 'rediscover' the place, can't I?"

"It's an honor that you'd share your plans with us, Mr. Noggenfogger." Jace said. "Though it doesn't really have much to do with us."

"How not?" The goblin tycoon agilely turned his slightly paunchy body. "My employees said you're here to investigate the black dragons. And right now, the black dragons are standing right between us and prime development real estate..."

"Looks like the Steamwheedle Cartel's been doing well. You're planning to declare war on the black dragons?" Vereesa asked.

"It's not like I've made a few gold and gotten too big for my britches, Ranger-General." The goblin grinned. "Deathwing is dead. You were there — you saw it. And the Alliance is currently besieging Blackrock Spire. From my intelligence, even if that orc-ruling black dragon has any tricks left, he's dead meat. Especially now that the Alliance Expeditionary Force sent to Draenor has returned — it's going to be a brutally one-sided massacre."

"I'd bet the black dragons wandering that swamp have been lying low because they keep getting slaughtered on the other continent, and there's nothing they can do about it except watch things get worse and worse."

"A nest that loses its leader is doomed to collapse. The whelps inside only have starvation ahead. And the black dragons aren't even that scary — I've tested some black dragon scales. They're no tougher than mithril. The Alliance sent you here to investigate the black dragons, which means they want to eliminate the threat for good. The Steamwheedle Cartel's interests align perfectly with yours. It's a win-win, isn't it?"

Jace could feel Needleflame's anger burning beneath her calm exterior.

Onyxia and her offspring were not on Needleflame's side, but they were still blood kin. And even putting Needleflame aside — as her friend, hearing Noggenfogger describe black dragons like wild animals made Jace uncomfortable too.

"Is the cooling in this room broken, damn it?" Even Noggenfogger frowned. Jace glanced at Needleflame and narrowed his eyes slightly, signaling her to calm down.

Jace said, "You'd need a proper army to assault a black dragon's lair, not a small mercenary team. We're only here for preliminary reconnaissance — preparing for a possible future Alliance expedition. We're not planning to take any real risks and tip them off."

"All I'm asking isn't for you distinguished guests to fight personally." Noggenfogger quickly said. "Truth is, we have the same problem. My previous scouts and the northern expedition teams couldn't pinpoint the black dragon lair's exact location. Aerial reconnaissance by zeppelin would be suicide. The cartels' insurance companies aren't stupid — they won't insure a zeppelin on black dragon reconnaissance duty, no matter the premium."

"All I'm asking for is the exact location of the black dragon lair. That's not too much, is it, Master Sesso?"

"It's not," Jace said.

"Excellent." Noggenfogger clapped. "I know that engaging a Stormwind royal advisor, a Quel'Thalas Ranger-General, and Aerie Peak's most elite gryphon rider comes at a considerable price..."

"High Thane," Jace corrected. "The last one isn't just a gryphon rider. He's a High Thane."

"High Thane!" Noggenfogger slapped his belly and laughed for a good while. Then he looked at the five expressionless faces before him and slowly sobered up.

"Uh, if I remember correctly, 'High Thane' and 'Mountain King' are two different words in dwarven, Master Sesso. I think my Common is passable — they're two different words in Common too. The former is practically archaic. Nobody uses it anymore."

"Your memory is fine. And so is mine." Jace said. "The dwarf in front of you is the shared sovereign of the Three Hammers — the acknowledged leader of Ironforge, Grim Batol, and Shadowforge City. The new High Thane."

The goblin froze. His gaze jumped across each face, then landed back on Jace. He swallowed. "So you're serious."

"I've said it clearly enough," Jace said. Grede cleared his throat and straightened his back.

"Wait. No. The Three Hammers? The Three Hammers War is over after two hundred years?" Noggenfogger almost shrieked. "And why would an Anvilmar be sitting in my office? It's not that I don't believe you all, but what the hell is going on?!"

"The Anvilmar era ended two hundred years ago. The new High Thane is a Thunderfist." Jace leaned back and propped his head on one hand. "I think your intelligence agents might have had a shipwreck in that storm, or you'd have heard about this a month ago."

"I did hear the Wildhammer were planning to help the Bronzebeard retake Shadowforge City from the Dark Iron. Ah, damn. That should have been the prelude to the Alliance's attack on Blackrock Spire. I'll have to confirm this." Noggenfogger bowed deeply to Grede. "My sincerest apologies, Your Highness. I mean — it's just so far from the other continent. All intelligence is delayed by months, even years. Please forgive me."

"That's all secondary, Mr. Noggenfogger." Grede smiled. "The point is — what kind of sincerity can the Steamwheedle Cartel offer to send the new High Thane on a reconnaissance mission?"

"Utmost sincerity!" Noggenfogger nearly shouted.

Jace wanted to point out that the ever-silent candidate dream guardian beside them was hardly less significant than the sovereign of the Three Hammers. And the cold-eyed young woman behind them, occasionally grinding her teeth — Neltharion's own blood, Princess Onyxia's little sister and niece — was even more heavyweight. The three mortals in front really didn't compare.

"Everything the Steamwheedle Cartel and I can provide in Gadgetzan is on the table as compensation, ladies and gentlemen!" The goblin spread his arms wide, showing off his generosity. "Whatever you need, just ask!"

"But we're not heading north to the swamp immediately, Mr. Noggenfogger." Jace said. "We have some business in the south first. Once that's handled, we'll come back to you."

"What business? Maybe Gadgetzan can help." Noggenfogger said. "And it won't count toward the 'compensation.'"

"We're looking for a desert altar. Might be related to wind elemental worship," Jace said.

"Ha! You mean the Temple of Thunderaan's Wrath?" The goblin said. "There've been quite a few people from the other continent looking for that place lately."

Jace had never heard of the place — not even in his past life — but he could clearly feel the chill seeping from Prince Thunderaan's head in the Dalaran box intensify. And from the temple's name...

"Looks like my engineers have been efficient. The cooling system's fixed." Noggenfogger planted his hands on his hips and looked at the ceiling. He coughed. "Maybe a bit too efficient."
