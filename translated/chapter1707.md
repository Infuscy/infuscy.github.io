## Chapter 1707: The Lost Ruins

"I don't like that little orc."

In a street-side restaurant, Needleflame leaned by the window, watching a group of goblins pass by outside. "It's not that I want to side with my aunt, but that little thing's attitude really pissed me off. I wanted to tear him apart right then and show him how much stronger my claws are than mithril."

"If you actually plan to do that, count me in." Grede grabbed a glass of sparkling wine and took a gulp. "I was kind of hoping Onyxia would turn on Jace, so I'd have an excuse to chop her head off with the axe the Earthen gave me and take it on tour around Stormwind. But now that I'm actually supposed to kill that princess... I don't want to anymore. Don't call me childish or contrary or whatever. That's just how I am. Or put it another way — that guy pissed me off."

"He's basically the emperor of Gadgetzan," Vereesa said. "And he's a goblin. You know what goblins are like."

"What about the Dark Iron dwarves? Even the emperor of Shadowforge City has to bow to me," Grede said.

"Because that Dark Iron emperor saw you fight Ragnaros the Fire Lord with his own eyes. This goblin..." Vereesa snorted. "He has no idea what he and his little city are actually worth. Honestly, even if Onyxia lost a wing to Nefarian, she could still destroy this ridiculous toy city of a desert in one breath."

"So I didn't agree to anything," Jace said. "But he's still useful. Last time he got us a zeppelin to Winterspring. There were some... complications, and we crashed north of the swamp, but—"

"And we met Lady Aegwynn," Vereesa added.

"Right." Jace sighed. "Anyway, this time he told us where the altar is. The Temple of Thunderaan's Wrath. Does anyone else hear anything odd about that name?"

"Thunderaan's Fury," Grede said. "In the language of the sky, it means 'Thunderaan's Wrath.'"

Thunderfury, Jace thought. This temple must have been built specifically to worship Prince Thunderaan. And it hadn't been around for just a day or two. But why was there no information about this temple in the game?

He'd assumed the temple might be in the ancient desert of Uldum, and he'd mentioned it to the Gadgetzan leader in advance just to keep him from suspecting the Alliance group's motives for venturing into the Tanaris desert. He hadn't expected Marlin Noggenfogger to blurt out the temple's name right away.

Because as far as Jace knew, before Deathwing's Cataclysm in another timeline, even the goblins who'd lived in Tanaris for ages didn't know what Uldum's depths looked like.

He pulled out the map Noggenfogger had marked for him. Aside from the words "Unknown Creators' Ruins" in dwarven script on a mountain range at the very bottom, there was nothing about Uldum. And the so-called Temple of Thunderaan's Wrath was marked northeast of those unknown ruins, in an area labeled "Ruins."

He couldn't exactly ask Prince Thunderaan about it — he wasn't supposed to know what Uldum looked like.

But if Noggenfogger thought this was where the temple was, and Thunderaan had hinted the same, then they'd go check it out first.

"When do we leave? Do we need a goblin guide?" Grede asked.

"I'm not letting any stranger on my back, Grede Thunderfist," Sora said.

"Ah... damn. Almost forgot." Grede covered his mouth.

Sora huffed. "I think you really are treating me like a zeppelin."

"That's not what I meant... I mean, you could carry them by claw. Like you did with that raptor," the dwarf said.

"Can we eat them too?" Needleflame asked.

"If you'd asked this morning, I'd have said better not. But now I'd say go for it." Grede grinned.

"We don't need a goblin guide. Don't let the people in Gadgetzan know who they really are or what we're carrying. Less trouble that way." Jace lowered his voice. "If we fly close and still can't pinpoint the location, this head will help us. It's not like he can't sense his own temple, right?"

"Fair point." Vereesa said. "Let's get out of the city first."

They finished the remaining pastries on the table, packed up, and walked out of Gadgetzan. The moment they stepped through the gate, they were met by an endless sea of sand. No roads, no clear landmarks — just a long-abandoned sand troll watchtower standing in the distance, looking like it wouldn't hold up much longer.

This was the first time Jace's group had ventured deep into the desert. The previous two times they'd gone north from here, either into the Shimmering Flats toward the Thousand Needles, or straight over the quillboar-infested Thorn Ridge to take a zeppelin to Dustwallow Marsh. According to Noggenfogger, even among locals, only elite explorers dared head south from Gadgetzan into the desert.

So who were those people Noggenfogger mentioned who'd asked about the temple before them? It wasn't hard to guess — either goblin treasure hunters, Explorers' League members, or the Twilight's Hammer.

In the game, the Tanaris desert was huge, but it didn't take long to cross on a flying mount. Out here, though, once they took to the sky and Gadgetzan disappeared behind them, it felt like they'd left the deep blue sea only to enter a golden one.

Fortunately, Sora — nearly an adult green dragon — didn't rely purely on eyesight for navigation. She didn't get lost in deserts or over the sea, and Needleflame could follow her confidently.

But this golden sea wasn't as monotonous as the deep blue one. It wasn't all just uniform water and waves. Sometimes they saw enormous bones spanning across hills, sometimes ancient sand troll ruins jutting from the earth.

As Needleflame flew higher, the outlines of those bones became clearer.

She kept pace with Sora ahead while occasionally looking down. Finally, at the third set of bones, she couldn't help asking, "Are those bronze dragon bones?"

"Remember the War of the Shifting Sands Sora mentioned?" Jace said. "If I'm not mistaken, the bronze dragonflight were the first to fight the qiraji. These remains might be left over from a thousand years ago."

"So the bronze dragons beat the qiraji?" Needleflame asked.

"They won. Together with reinforcements from the other dragonflights and the Kaldorei, they sealed the qiraji behind the Scarab Wall," Jace said.

But a thousand years later — which was now — the qiraji had started digging tunnels from underground, releasing silithid swarms to invade all of southern Kalimdor. Not just Tanaris — the giant insects' feelers had even reached Feralas to the north. The first time Jace's group came to Kalimdor, they'd gotten Ursula's desired silithid samples from a hive in Feralas.

Thinking about it, Jace suddenly realized something. He pulled out the map and looked again at the location of the Temple of Thunderaen's Wrath. He understood in an instant why this temple had never seemed to exist in the game.

Because if his memory was right, this area in the game had been overrun by the silithid hives of Tanaris. The sand was pockmarked with huge burrows dug by enormous insect limbs. Everything that had once been there — oases, forests, cities, villages, ruins — was either occupied by the swarms or had collapsed into the caves with them. Nothing could have survived in its original form.

So what about now? Had the qiraji silithid hives spread this far?

A sandstorm hit. Sora and Needleflame flew higher to avoid the dust, and the desert ahead grew increasingly blurred.

The sky darkened. The sandstorm passed. Endless dark yellow mountain ranges appeared at the edge of the horizon.

"I see it! A ruined town!" Vereesa rose up and shouted back toward Needleflame and Jace. "Do you see it? The walls, the low buildings..."

It was asking too much for Jace to make out at this distance what Vereesa could barely see herself. But before long, he spotted white specks in the sand.

They were architectural styles he'd never seen before. Nothing like troll, human, dwarf, or elf construction.

After flying past a tall dune, they saw a large statue half-buried in the sand behind the hill. It was an eagle-headed monster with shoulders more like a humanoid's.

"This should be it. The ancient city marked on the map." Jace muttered, looking at the map in his hand. "At least the position is about right. There don't seem to be any other ruins nearby."

And it hasn't been infested by the swarms yet, he thought.

"But it's dead here. Like there's nothing at all, Sesso." Needleflame said. "Nothing alive or dead. I can't even smell any magic. Do you think we'll find what the Windseeker wants?"

"I don't know. We'll just have to take our chances," Jace said.
