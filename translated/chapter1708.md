## Chapter 1708: Wind and Sand

This desert ruin looked to be about the same size as Gadgetzan. Maybe centuries of wind and sand had buried most of the structures — all that was visible now were the taller tower tops and statues.

But unlike the game model, this wasn't just a few mud-wall huts clustered together. It was a full mechanical siege-city at the heart of the desert, with crisscrossing streets, tall buildings, narrow alleys, and factories. The ruins were the same way — far larger than the scattered sand-ruins that would appear in the game. Searching the whole place wouldn't be easy.

"We're looking for your temple, Windseeker."

Jace steadied himself on Needleflame's back against the wind and sand, clutching the box in his hands as he looked at the Wind Prince's imprisoned skull inside.

The wind swirling around him didn't seem as fierce as it had on the other continent. Shouldn't he be getting closer to his homeland by now?

After all, if they crossed these mountains, found the path into the Uldum ruins, and reached the southernmost edge of that ancient desert, they should see the throne of the Wind Lord — his father's temple.

"To find your things, you'll have to lead us. Don't make us circle around here on our own." After Jace spoke in elemental, Thunderaan didn't answer right away. Jace could even feel his power weakening. He couldn't sense any emotion from the Wind Prince, but just by feeling the fading elemental energy, he knew it wasn't a good sign.

After a long while, when Solara led Needleflame to the edge of the ruins and found nothing, about to turn back and search again, the elemental lord finally spoke.

"The wind resists me, Jace Sesso. I cannot give you the answer."

"The wind resists you?" Jace snorted. "Sounds like your arms and legs not listening to your brain."

Thunderaan replied, "Even a mortal's limbs can refuse their master's will in sickness or paralysis. All the more so for the elements."

"And I am not the mind of the wind elementals. At the highest peak of Skywall sits my father, who commands all. I imagine he is very dissatisfied with my performance. The winds and sands of Tanaris no longer obey my command."

"Dissatisfied? You didn't finish what he wanted you to do?" Jace asked.

Thunderaan didn't answer. Needleflame tilted her head and asked, "What's he saying to you, Sesso?"

"Doesn't matter. We can't count on him. Land and search." Jace said. "He doesn't even know where his own temple is?"

Needleflame grumbled something unhappy but finally called out ahead in draconic. Solara heard the sound cutting through the sand and dust, lowered her altitude, and the two dragons coordinated to find a clearing to land.

They touched down on a dune dotted with a few scattered low buildings of unfamiliar style — no eaves, no spires. Hard to tell if they'd always been this short or if most of them were still buried in sand.

"I don't get it. A Wind Prince can't get information from the wind anymore?" Grede trudged over, his tightly wrapped Sulfuras hammer on his back, sinking halfway up his leg with every step.

"No idea. Sounds like he's got some family drama with his dad." Jace said.

"At least he's got a dad." Grede coughed and spat out some sand.

"Elemental father-son relationships are probably very different from yours," Solara said. "And probably completely different from ours, too."

"Even if I don't know where that temple might be," Vereesa said, "by the usual layout of mortal cities, a temple should at least stand in the most important place. Let's start by searching the center. There might be details we couldn't see from the air."

"What if this city had multiple faiths?" Grede said. "Some people worshipped wind elementals, some worshipped the Holy Light, some worshipped... I dunno, weirder stuff. Like that toppled bird-headed monster outside the city."

"I saw that statue too," Vereesa said. "Anyone know what that is?"

Needleflame and Solara both shook their heads, but Jace actually had some memory of that thing.

During the War of the Shifting Sands, the qiraji had been retreating against the dragons' onslaught when an Anubisath giant stepped forward. He hurled an obsidian sword that severed the massive wing of a dragonflight leader, sending him crashing into the insect swarm to be killed. That had turned the tide, opening the qiraji counterattack against the dragons.

The Anubisath were jackal-headed colossi, originally placed by the Titans as wardens around the Temple of Ahn'Qiraj — the prison of the Old God C'Thun. But they had later fallen to corruption and become followers of the Old God's servants, the qiraji, fighting against the troll and Kaldorei empires and later the dragonflights.

The giant who had won that first victory was named Ossirian. Though grievously wounded by the dragon he'd crippled, he was rewarded: a new eagle-headed mask to protect his damaged skull, and his life essence bound to an ancient qiraji crystal, granting him rebirth. Though he could never leave Ahn'Qiraj again, becoming its eternal guardian with an invulnerable body, his followers called him "the Unscarred."

That toppled statue looked just like Ossirian the Unscarred.

Had the residents of this desert city once worshipped both the Wind Prince and Ossirian the Unscarred?

If they worshipped both, that almost certainly meant these people had actually worshipped the Old Gods. Which would also mean the hatred between Thunderaan the Windseeker and Ragnaros was probably just an internal power struggle among the servants of the old Black Empire.

But when they'd talked to Thunderaan back in Shadowforge City, Jace hadn't sensed any reverence for C'Thun or the other Old Gods in his words. Though that wouldn't surprise him either. Hundreds of thousands of years ago, the elemental lords had been defeated and enslaved by the Old Gods. Even after the Old Gods were sealed by the Titans, Al'Akir had never abandoned his loyalty to the old masters — just like Ragnaros.

Thunderaan was probably just pretending to have no connection to the Old Gods and Ragnaros. He knew Jace's group would never compromise with servants of the Old Gods. After using the "Rebirth Vial" to be reborn, he'd either immediately turn on them or abandon them and flee.

But Jace was already prepared to have it out with this guy. He was sure Grede and the others wouldn't object either.

Actually, this might be a good thing. During their duel, Ragnaros had devoured most of Thunderaan's power, sealing the remaining elemental spirit fragments into the Brass Prison.

In the game, for players to fully resurrect Thunderaan, they had to take the Essence of the Fire Lord from Ragnaros and combine it with the Brass Prison and the Rebirth Vial. They hadn't gotten that essence from the Fire Lord.

But Thunderaan had also mentioned that although the Rebirth Vial couldn't fully restore the power Ragnaros had devoured in a short time, as long as he spent time reabsorbing elemental wind energy, he could eventually recover his original state.

That meant once Thunderaan was reborn, they'd be facing an extremely weakened son of the Wind Lord. If it turned out he was still loyal to the Old Gods and had only pretended to be friendly to use them for his resurrection, then they'd strike while he was weak, take his Wind-Kissed Blade, and see if they could forge Thunderfury, Blessed Blade of the Windseeker.

Jace had never had moral qualms about dealing with servants of the Old Gods.

The group walked among the sand-buried buildings. Dust flew, wind strength fluctuating, making the shadows of structures ahead appear and disappear. Things that were hard to see from the air were clearer on the ground, including inscriptions carved into some buildings. But none of them could read the script — it wasn't elemental, not troll, and not any other language they'd seen.

As they walked, Jace felt the arcane box in his hands grow cold. Thunderaan seemed to want to say something.

"Hold on." Jace called out to Vereesa and Grede ahead of him, opened the box, and asked, "Do you recognize the writing here?"

Thunderaan spat out a single clear word in Common: "They're coming."

"Who?" Jace asked.

The wind and sand grew stronger. Sand and gravel stung their faces. Even Grede nearly got blown over and had to grab a leaning stone column. The others fared no better.

Thunderaan was still trying to say something, but Jace couldn't hear anymore. The shrieking wind swallowed his words.

Within the sandstorm, several shifting shadows gradually became solid. They weren't distant buildings — they were humanoid shapes, drawing closer. Sometimes they merged with the sandstorm, sometimes they separated again.

Higher up, dark clouds were gathering. The sky darkened. Rumbling thunder drowned out every other sound. Soon Jace realized this wasn't ordinary wind — it was the roar of wind elementals.

"You still dare to return here, Thunderaan... Come back to Skywall with me and witness his thunderous wrath."
