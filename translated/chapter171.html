Chapter 171: The Three Relics (Part One)

"Teron Gorefiend?"

"Could it be correspondence between Gorefiend and another Horde clan?"

"Connected to the attack on Alterac?"

"Are you certain? How do you know Teron Gorefiend's name appears in there? How do you even read Orcish?"

"Master Dushan, did you help this young student with the translation?"

"I know just as little about the contents of this order as you do, Lady Mavis," Dushan replied wearily.

The moment Jace said that, a murmur rippled through the entire conference hall. Even Aethas Sunreaver, who had kept silent ever since entering the room, unconsciously turned to speak with the Stromgarde scholar beside him.

Because Teron Gorefiend and his forces were, aside from the Blackrock orcs occupying Blackrock Spire, the only confirmed Horde army still remaining on Azeroth.

Whether it was the Frostwolf clan, the Warsong clan, or the Dragonmaw clan — all of those were still only rumors.

Granted, the Wizard Sanctum had already confirmed that the Dragonmaw clan was running amok in the Wetlands, though it hadn't yet gained widespread recognition from the Lordaeron Alliance. Precisely for that reason, Andormas intended to send Morgas — who understood both orcs and shadow magic — to take part in the further exploration of the Wetlands. In any case, the threat posed by Gorefiend's order was far more immediate than any other outdated material.

Amid the clamor of discussion, Jace walked up to the podium, gave Kel'Thuzad a slight bow, and said, "Master, before coming here, I translated as much of the contents as I could. This is an order commanding subordinates to return to the Dark Portal and pledge loyalty to the Horde's new warchief. The tone is of an extremely high rank — I believe it was issued by a high-ranking leader whose status falls only below Gul'dan and Orgrim."

"So it's merely a guess, then," Kel'Thuzad said, his brows relaxing slightly.

"That's right," Jace answered. "Which is said I'm only suspicious."

The discussion in the hall grew even more chaotic at that. Jace even noticed one of the high elves let out a contemptuous laugh.

Jace knew he had a decent dislike for being looked down upon, yet faced with that high elf's dismissive attitude, he felt no ripple of emotion in his heart.

He wasn't suppressing his anger — he genuinely didn't care.

Because he was getting closer and closer to the advancing edge of Deathwing's plan, and compared to that destructive force, one that could bring about a true apocalypse, these people's attitudes — whether praise or disdain — didn't matter one bit.

Jace placed his materials on the table. Kel'Thuzad took the letter he had copied out in his own hand and passed it to his student — the one who had been handling the reading of materials all along, Bromiam. Bromiam was quite young, probably only twenty-five or twenty-six, but the ring he wore was already the sigil of a Dalaran mage.

In other words, he was already a full-fledged mage. Given his age and talent, admission into the Kirin Tor was probably only a few years away. No wonder Kel'Thuzad trusted him so much.

After taking Jace's copied order, he immediately began reading it aloud, his pronunciation and intonation eerily close to a real orc's — he even imitated the uniquely bestial throat-trill of the orcs remarkably well.

Jace had been busy revising his speech notes earlier and hadn't even stopped to properly gauge this fellow's skill.

After Bromiam finished reading, the transcript of the letter was passed around for inspection, and by now many people had managed to piece together the general picture of the letter.

Jace could already hear discussions above about the "three relics" and "the death of Gul'dan," and even Bromiam, whose Orcish was extremely proficient, wore a grave expression.

There might have been a few unfamiliar terms in there, especially ones related to names, but it was obvious he had understood most of it.

"This is a very recent letter… Master," he said, looking toward Kel'Thuzad. "Do you remember the death knight prisoner who revealed the news of Gul'dan's death? That was only a few months ago. Which means this letter was likely written within the last three months."

Kel'Thuzad nodded to his student, then walked to the front and said, "This passage is not difficult to understand, everyone." Of course it wasn't a hard thing to translate — when Jace had first gotten the letter, he'd been able to translate about eighty percent of it in the library just by flipping through books, though he'd gotten one tense wrong. With this many Orcish masters here, they could hash out the complete meaning after a little discussion.

"Could you tell us about the origins of this letter, Mr. Jace Sesso?" Kel'Thuzad asked.

"I found it near Karazhan," Jace said. "Of course, that's something I only realized afterward."

Hearing the name Karazhan, everyone — Bromiam and the other scholars alike — perked up their ears. Kel'Thuzad also stroked his bare chin with interest.

"There are many rare herbs in southern Elwynn, especially after that explosion. I'd originally planned to rest in Glantown, then go looking for ingredients to make a mana potion, but on the way to Glantown I was overcome by inexplicable drowsiness. After a brief rest and setting off again, I found I could no longer find the road to Glantown."

"There are many rare herbs in southern Elwynn, but not many suitable for making mana potions," a Gilneas scholar said.

"I had only just entered the Wizard Sanctum at that time," Jace replied. "I was completely clueless about what I was doing, just blindly searching for ways to improve my mana capacity."

"I walked into a blighted forest full of indescribable monstrous trees, and lurking between the trees seemed to be some unknown creatures harboring unclear malice. I was terrified and just wanted to return the way I came, so I tried heading north, only to find a dead orc by a lifeless little river. I guessed he'd run into a similar situation as me."

"How dreadful," one scholar said.

Jace glanced at him and continued, "That orc's corpse was swollen and reeking, and there was a constant strange crackling sound coming from inside its body, but I had to go through his belongings because I was starving to the point of collapse. In the end I found some food that was about to go moldy, and… this letter."

"After I got this letter, I kept feeling like something was watching me from the shadows, until I saw a grotesque tower swaying unsteadily in the distance on the other side of the river. I was certain it must be Karazhan — it was like a watchful sentinel staring me down, filling me with more and more terror. So I immediately fled in the opposite direction, and I don't know how long I ran before I finally made it back to a forest that still had a shred of life left in it. But in the end I never found the road to Glantown and could only return to Goldshire."

"Karazhan should be to the southeast of Elwynn," a dwarf said. "If you got lost and walked north the whole time, why would you end up near that tower?"

Jace hesitated a moment and said, "I'm sorry, sir, I can't explain it either… I did my best to follow the sun to get my bearings."

"It'd be strange if he could explain it," an elf said. "I've never seen anyone who got close to Karazhan after the explosion and still kept a clear mind, let alone an apprentice."

Jace glanced at the elf. That was a pretty professional setup — feeding him the perfect straight line.

By now nobody was questioning his story anymore. Kel'Thuzad pondered for a moment and said, "The arcane phenomena around Karazhan are currently completely unpredictable. It seems even an orc messenger whose veins flow with evil blood could not escape them. You are very fortunate, Mr. Jace Sesso."

Jace had never gone anywhere near Deadwind Pass. It seemed Hennie was right — in this whole world, she was the only one who could tell when her own son was lying. Not even Kel'Thuzad could.

At that point, a Lordaeron scholar said, "The importance of this letter speaks for itself. Judging by the tone of the writer, if it was written by a living person, then aside from Teron Gorefiend, there really aren't many other candidates."

Bromiam, from behind, asked, "What do these three relics refer to? If the one issuing the order is Teron Gorefiend, could the legendary relic stolen from Alterac be one of them?"

"Those aren't Alterac's relics. They're the Book of Medivh, stolen from Stormwind by the treacherous Perenolde. It's looking more and more like he meant to hand that treasure over to the Horde from the very beginning!" a Kul Tiras scholar angrily declared.

These scholars had immediately linked the three relics to the Book of Medivh, which caught Jace off guard.

For all he knew, he might not even need to drop any hints — the Kirin Tor would figure out the Eye of the Arcane on their own, especially smart ones like Kel'Thuzad.
