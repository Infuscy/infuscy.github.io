Chapter 1710: Making Good Use of the Earth's Affinity

"Oof!" Grede finally pulled Needleflame out of the constantly sinking hot sand, set her aside, and laughed. "Earth Warder, huh?"

"I'm NOT the Earth Warder!" Needleflame spat out a mouthful of sand, then spit a couple more times. "They came so fast, I couldn't react!"

"You can't blame Needleflame. The wind elementals control all power here, Grede." Solara said. "They say the entrance to Skywall — the realm of the wind lords — lies deep in the southernmost desert of Kalimdor. Even the servants of the Earth Mother, Therazane, are enslaved here, forced to build their magnificent Wind Temple."

"Sister Solara is the oldest and most knowledgeable," Needleflame said, brushing sand off herself. "Unlike some people who just like making digs at others, Thunderfist. Think about how long I've been flying Jace Sesso around! All the shape-shifting just to travel with you lot! It's impressive I've still got any energy at all."

"Your hard work on the journey is definitely appreciated." Vereesa said with a smile.

"But right now I don't see any Skywall or any Thunderfury Temple." Grede said, hands on his hips. "Speaking of which, why was that wind elemental lord waiting for us here? Did that prince in the Brass Prison secretly tip off his dad?"

"That elemental came for him specifically. At first he didn't know who we were — thought we were a bunch of stowaway warlocks coming to join Twilight's Hammer. We weren't his target." Jace said.

"Then how did he know Thunderaan the Windseeker was in these ruins? Could that Noggenfogger guy have tipped off the elemental lord?" Vereesa asked.

"He needs machines to cool things down in the desert, Vereesa." Solara said. "Hard to imagine a faithful servant of a wind elemental lord would be that hard up."

"The goblin's clean." Jace said. "Maybe this Sarsalun was here for the same thing we were — looking for the Rebirth Vial — and just happened to run into us."

"Well, now this city's a complete mess." Vereesa spread her hands and looked into the distance. "How do we search now? That temple might be completely buried in sand now..."

"Let Needleflame look." Grede said, trying not to laugh. "This little one's best at digging through sand."

"You bastard dwarf." The black dragon kicked Grede hard in the side, and soon the two of them were tangled up wrestling again.

Jace sighed and looked around. If the city had been searchable street by street before the elemental attack, now he had no idea where to even start.

But just as he was feeling stuck, he spotted something in a pile of broken rubble — an unusual three-pronged stone sculpture caught his eye.

The carving was about the size of a horse, shaped like a trident head or the ornament on a crown. There was a groove in the middle that looked like it was meant to hold an elemental stone or some other gem.

Jace stared at it and felt a flicker of recognition. In his past life, he'd never gotten Thunderfury, Blessed Blade of the Windseeker in the vanilla game, but he'd helped plenty of guildmates get theirs. He'd fought Thunderaan more times than he could count. And this symbol looked exactly like the ornament on Thunderaan the Windseeker's helmet. He was sure of it.

Still, he wondered if he was just so obsessed with the Wind Prince that he was seeing things — seeing wind elemental lord armor everywhere, connecting every sharp-edged object to Thunderfury.

But even if Thunderaan couldn't observe anything outside his prison, and this stone carving had no magical power for him to sense, Thunderaan's remnant soul was sealed in the form of a head inside the cage. Could he see something similar on that head? Jace quickly opened the box. An unhappy voice came from inside.

"What do you want to ask now, Jace Sesso?"

As Thunderaan muttered, Jace saw inside the box: the unclear, two-part head wrapped in a cyclone, and the three-pronged blade ornament on its forehead — perfectly clear! That stone carving was a heavily weathered three-pronged ornament, and the groove in the middle should have held a blue magic stone of unknown purpose — because Thunderaan's soul-head had exactly that!

"Found it." Jace pointed. "Over there!"

Grede and Needleflame stopped their scuffle and looked over. Vereesa, who had climbed to a high point, turned back too. Jace showed them the Thunderaan symbol from the Brass Prison, and everyone got excited.

But when they all ran into the pit and reached the carving, they realized the pillar the carving sat on extended deep into the sand. Had the temple really been buried under the desert?

"What now?" Grede said. "Go borrow some shovels from Gadgetzan and dig it out?"

"You might as well say there's a gold mine in there." Jace said. "I guarantee those goblins would have an excavation site set up within days."

"And then they'd get their hands on that bottle we're looking for?" Grede said. "If they find it first, we can't exactly rob them, can we?"

"Fair point." Jace scratched his chin, looked over the group, and settled his gaze on Needleflame and Solara.

"Why don't we do it ourselves? We've got two dragons."

"Ha! I can help break up the rocks, make your digging easier!" Grede said.

"I can keep watch." Vereesa said. "If there's any movement in the desert, or more wind elementals threatening this place, I'll come warn you."

"Good thinking." Grede nodded, then pointed at Jace. "So what's this warlock good for?"

"Just because I study magic doesn't mean I don't have muscles." Jace said.

"Oh, go sit this one out!" Grede laughed, tossing two flasks of Strong Mana Potion to Needleflame and Solara. "Let's work overtime and dig down to see if this is really the place. Before that wind elemental lord calls for more help, let's find that thing!"

Vereesa hopped up onto a tall statue in a couple of strides, standing on its raised scepter to watch the distance. Solara and Needleflame shifted back to dragon form and started digging down from the three-pronged carving, following the pillar.

At first Grede and Jace helped too — breaking up boulders for the dragons, moving debris so they could dig easier. But eventually Needleflame got so into it, her thrashing tail and kicking wings flinging sand everywhere, that she nearly buried Jace and Grede alive twice.

"You sit this out too, dwarf!" the black dragon roared. Grede surrendered and climbed out. Eventually even Solara could barely get a claw in and just stood there watching Needleflame go at it alone.

Got to admit, black dragons had a special talent for digging.

Needleflame kept tunneling down along the base of the pillar for a while. As dusk approached, she hit something hard.

Before anyone above could see what it was, she rammed her head into it without a second thought. Jace heard a thud from below and peered closer — it was a stone slab, already cracked from the impact.

"Wait, Needleflame—"

Before he could finish, the black dragon slammed her head into it again. The slab broke open with a crash, revealing a dark opening. Needleflame yelped, and before she could lift her wings, she was half-sunk into it. She scrambled to climb out, but the collapsing sand pushed her back down.

Solara immediately dove down to grab her, but the whole sandpit was caving in. Solara slid a bit, managed to take off, but couldn't catch Needleflame. She could only watch as the black dragon fell into the pit.

"We've got to go in too! Vereesa! There's a hole down here!" Grede shouted toward the outside.

Jace jumped into the pit without hesitation, following Solara down into the collapse. Grede came right behind. The group pushed through the shifting sand as they fell, then felt their feet drop into open air — darkness. Jace immediately called on the Hide of Orastraz's power, hovering for a moment in the void before landing steadily. Grede crashed heavily onto Needleflame, who had just crawled out of the sand pile. She let out a wretched yelp that felt like it might have cracked her spine.

The yelp echoed mournfully through the dark, unfamiliar, cold chamber. Vereesa was the last one down. The sand slowly stopped falling, and the whole space went completely quiet.

"Is this the temple we're looking for?" Grede asked, his voice jarring in the silence.

Jace raised the Song of Clear Skies, casting light, and immediately noticed spiral patterns on the nearby pillars — like airflow or cyclones.

A short distance behind them was an altar. On the wall behind it, a faded painting lingered, depicting what seemed to be a powerful wind elemental lord. Jace guessed it was either Al'Akir or Thunderaan the Windseeker himself.

"This is it. We found the right place."
