## Chapter 1711: One Step Too Late

The hall was lit a pale blue by the Song of Clear Skies. Aside from the two dragons and the others picking themselves up, brushing off sand, and muttering complaints, there was nothing else.

After a moment, everyone fell silent. Jace stared into the depths of the hall, straining to hear any sign of life — traps, lingering magic, elemental guards being roused. Nothing. The quiet was unsettling.

"What part of the temple do you think this is?" Grede whispered.

Vereesa looked at the elemental lord's painting, now lit blue, and said, "Looks like some kind of chapel. So should we be looking for a treasure vault now? Doesn't the Wind Prince know where the vault is? Have him guide us."

"You're the master of this temple, Windseeker. It's your turn to help." Jace said, holding up the Brass Prison in its box.

"I am the object of worship in this temple, not its owner or architect, Jace Sesso." Thunderaan replied.

Jace sighed. "This guy knows nothing except that there's some treasure here that can resurrect him. We're on our own."

"Ah, bloody hell. This is the sky god my venerable ancestors used to worship every day?" Grede complained.

"There's a passage over here, Jace." Vereesa's voice came from the shadows about ten meters behind them. All they could see from here was the arcane light in her eyes. "But... there are a lot of footprints."

Jace and Grede hurried over and followed Vereesa down a set of steps. Around the corner of the stairs, they could see a narrow passage like the entrance to a chapel crypt. The ground at its entrance was covered in scattered sand and dust, some of which clearly showed traces of boot scuffs.

"How long have those been here?" Grede looked at them. "Or are they still here?"

"Hard to say." Vereesa said. "There's no wind in here, and no one else has come in... Hard to judge how fresh they are from how clear the prints are."

Grede pointed up at where they'd fallen in. "But how did they get in? We had to smash through a ceiling to get down here."

"The temple's worshippers wouldn't come in through the ceiling. There has to be a main entrance or side doors somewhere." Vereesa said. "Maybe that wind elemental lord who stopped us stirred up the sand and buried the entrance, which is why we had so much trouble getting in."

"But he also blew away a lot of sand, letting us see that strange carving Jace thought was related to the wind element." Grede said. "Didn't that Noggenfogger money-bags say lots of people coming to Gadgetzan have been asking about this temple? Looks like we're too late."

"Let's take a look inside." Jace said.

"We'd better be careful." Vereesa said.

"Solara, you and Needleflame stay here and rest. If someone comes in from outside, give us a shout." Jace called up the stairs.

"Got it, Jace." the green dragon replied softly.

"I wanna go too." Needleflame grumbled.

"If you still have the energy to shapeshift, then come."

At that, only a sigh came from above, followed by the smell of something burning.

The passage was tall but narrow on both sides, giving a claustrophobic feeling like walking through a drainage ditch beside a highway. It made you wonder if this was some kind of sewer, but the beautifully carved stone tiles on the floor, though cracked, suggested it was meant for people. Either way, walking through it was extremely unpleasant.

Grede led the way, carrying the enchanted small shield Turalyon had given him, a stone axe in hand, and Sulfuras wrapped on his back. Honestly, Jace thought this so-called High Thane's body was probably tougher than that shield by now. But as an old warrior and gryphon rider, carrying a shield was better than nothing — it gave him some basic sense of security.

As for Vereesa following behind, she might not be the most seasoned Farstrider, but in pure combat ability, she far exceeded what her title of "Ranger-Lord" suggested. Even Alleria admitted that.

So Jace thought Vereesa was being overly cautious. If whoever left those footprints was planning to ambush them, they'd probably be dead before they could even show their faces, and Jace behind them wouldn't even have time to chant or channel shadow energy.

Jace was still thinking about the skirmish up on the surface. That wind elemental lord couldn't even withstand a simple thrown strike from Sulfuras. That was the first time the hammer — forged by the Thorium Brotherhood and blessed by the Fire Lord — had seen real combat, and its effect had far exceeded Jace's expectations. Its suppression of lower elemental lords was terrifying.

After a turn in the passage, the path began sloping downward. They reached the bottom without encountering any enemies or hearing any strange sounds.

Vereesa might not be the best at judging potential threats compared to other more experienced Farstriders, but her basic senses — both hearing and sight — were exceptional. She wouldn't miss any nearby movement, and could even detect tremors in arcane energy with a Quel'dorei's sensitivity.

Even if Vereesa wasn't entirely reliable, Jace's own shadow perception was equally empty. There was no emotion whatsoever in this underground space. The only excess "rage" floating around was from his earlier contest with the wind elemental lord, still lingering above the temple on the surface.

Maybe they really were overthinking things. The temple was too sealed off — no wind could get in, nothing could disturb the footprints. Those prints could be years or even decades old, for all they knew.

The dwarf at the front paused and gestured ahead with his finger. Vereesa tossed out a light spell, illuminating a broken stone door.

Grede peeked in with his axe raised, then suddenly widened his eyes and waved everyone over. Jace rushed up and looked inside — and saw bodies lying everywhere. In a single glance, he counted over a dozen: humans, Dark Iron dwarves, gnomes, most wearing black-gray or purple clothing, most holding weapons.

"Twilight's Hammer."

Jace picked up a purple pendant from one of the corpses. It was exactly the same as the Twilight cultist identity badges they'd gotten from Gorthann in Shadowforge City.

The bodies were badly desiccated, but still dressed in relatively intact clothing, so in poor light they looked like they'd died recently.

But the Twilight's Hammer clan had only been under Cho'gall's control and migrated to Kalimdor about seven or eight years ago. And Noggenfogger in Gadgetzan had mentioned people only started asking about these ruins recently. So they'd probably only been dead a few months.

The room wasn't very large. Bloodstained kodo leather lay tattered on the floor. In the center sat a cat-shaped figure, holding a tray above its head with both paws like it was presenting something — but the tray was empty.

"Dead end. So there's good news and bad news." Grede spat. "Good news is, Twilight's Hammer already disabled every lethal trap in this temple for us. Bad news is..."

"They already took the thing." Jace said.

"We came all this way for nothing? So what now? Do we find Twilight's Hammer's hideout and take it from them?" Grede asked.

Too late after all? Jace couldn't help wondering. Had that Twilight Bishop, the one so desperate to summon Thunderaan, already taken the "Rebirth Vial" buried here?

Grede cursed in Dwarven and pointed at the Kirin Tor magic box Jace was carrying. "I'd really like to know what treasure this Wind Prince is hiding that's worth all this trouble!"

"Don't jump to conclusions, Grede." Just then, Vereesa picked something small from a dead Dark Iron dwarf's body. "Look at this."

"What?" Grede asked impatiently.

"You don't recognize it? It's a bullet." the elf said. "Do you think this forgotten ancient temple would have your guns installed as traps? Looks more like the scene of an internal conflict to me... It's not like Twilight's Hammer is a particularly harmonious bunch."

Grede took the bullet from Vereesa and examined it. "That's not an Ironforge gun bullet. The caliber's too small, Vereesa."

"Then maybe Shadowforge City?" the ranger gestured at the Dark Iron dwarf bodies on the ground.

"You kidding me? No way a Dark Iron dwarf would make bullets smaller than a Bronzebeard's. They use hand cannons!" Grede said.

"There are a lot of gun shops in Gadgetzan. Could it be goblin make?" Jace asked.

"Ask Noggenfogger." All three of them said in unison.
