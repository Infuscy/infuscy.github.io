Chapter 1713: Master of the Shimmering Flats

"Don't tell me that guy installed the Rebirth Vial into his race car!"

Outside Buckshot's gun shop, Grede peeked back at the two goblin shadows through the door curtain, making sure they weren't eavesdropping. Then he huddled closer to the other four companions. "So what do we do? What if that goblin already used up all the elemental power?"

"I don't think so." Vereesa said. "He spent so much money and effort to get that thing — no way he'd just pour it all out in one go. And if it's powerful enough to resurrect a wind elemental lord's remnant soul, he must know something about magic. I think he'll find someone to make proper use of it."

"So here's the problem." Jace said. "He spent all that money, hired all those people, went through all that trouble. How do we get him to hand it over?"

"Smash his car." Needleflame said. "Just steal it. Does he sleep in the car every night? Where's this Shimmering Flats? Leave it to me."

"Isn't that going a bit far, Needleflame?" Solara said quietly.

"The fact that she's thinking of stealing instead of just eating the guy is way more than I expected." Grede said. "Don't forget, she's Deathwing's child, Morofil's student, the Burning Legion's pawn, and an ally of ancient evil gods — all working together in her blood."

"Shut up, dwarf!" Needleflame hissed.

"We still need to talk to Noggenfogger." Jace said. "But we absolutely can't let him know what this thing really is."

"Does this Puzik know?" Grede asked. "Hopefully they didn't learn from Twilight's Hammer what this thing is actually for, or they'll jack up the price sky-high."

"By the way," Solara said, "can the Wind Prince hear us from inside that box?"

"Seems like..." Jace looked at the others. "Well, it's not our fault."

"Call Noggenfogger out."

Grede waved toward the door. Noggenfogger came out and asked, "Have you decided?"

"Take us to meet this Mr. Puzik, Mr. Noggenfogger." Vereesa said.

"So you still want that thing." The goblin mayor rubbed the bit of flesh behind his pointed chin. "It's not that I don't want to help. Truth is, you're all heroes from the other continent. After the orc war, the Steamwheedle Cartel had some friction with the Alliance. Maybe Rivendare and Monte Gazlowe aren't too fond of the Alliance, but at least I've always advocated rebuilding relations. After all, we'll all be traveling across the Great Sea and doing business together in the future."

"But Mr. Puzik — I should warn you — he values his racing career more than life itself, and more than gold. He even gave up his senior engineer position in the Bilgewater Cartel to come to Gadgetzan and build the 'Scenery' racetrack on the Shimmering Flats. Frankly, if that cooling system is really that amazing, it's not something money can solve."

"I think we still need to go to his racetrack and talk to him in person." Jace said.

"Of course, that's your choice. I just wanted to give you a heads-up about what to expect." The goblin said. "Since that's the case, follow me."

Noggenfogger arranged for several desert striders to carry them out of Gadgetzan and up the mountain path to the Shimmering Flats.

It wasn't as comfortable as the airship, nor as fast as riding Solara or Needleflame. Sand flew everywhere, and vultures circled overhead, cawing annoyingly. But at least Needleflame was having a great time.

She was practically best friends with the strider she was riding by the end. This little one's criteria for choosing friends was always unpredictable.

Returning to the Shimmering Flats, Jace felt like a lifetime had passed.

The last time he'd set foot on this burning white salt flat, even the basilisks hiding in pits had made him wake up screaming at night. Grede had charged at centaur hunters as if he was ready to die.

Now Grede had enough power to save the tauren race. The trials on the other continent and on Draenor had bound him ever more closely to the power of his earthen ancestors. With the Sulfuras hammer in hand, Jace didn't think those centaur tribes strutting around Mulgore could stop him.

Jace had bought a house and settled in Stormwind, he and Vereesa were about to make things official. Aside from the Quel'Thalas wedding, Grede didn't have anything left to worry about on the other continent.

Looking at the dwarf still bickering with Solara up ahead, thinking about the words they'd exchanged in the Stormwind palace corridors that day — this might be their last big trip to Kalimdor together. The sky in the distance was purple, the sunset beautiful, and the wind carried a chill through his heart.

"Don't let such slow things enter my racetrack, Noggenfogger!"

A hoarse shout came from the distance.

"Then cut off your own legs first, Puzik!"

Noggenfogger laughed, jumped off his strider, and trotted over. The two shook hands and chattered in their rapid-fire goblin way. Noggenfogger turned and pointed back at the group. "Look, Puzik. Distinguished guests from the other side. They have something very important to discuss with you."

"Something very important? Did Fizzle-Coppershimmer send some news? I thought that guy died at the hands of the orcs." A pale green-skinned goblin in a leather jacket came striding over, reeking of tar. "I'm Puzik. Pleased to meet you. Elf, dwarf, human — no gnome, but that's fine. I like making friends."

"We're sorry, we don't bring news from Gnomeregan, Mr. Puzik." Jace stepped forward. "We're here to talk about that cooling system. My name is—"

"Bloody hell." Puzik's face changed and he turned to run. But somehow Needleflame was already blocking his path.

"Dammit, Noggenfogger! Did you hire someone to kill me?! Or have you gone mad too?!" He jumped up and screamed. "Security! Security! This is when I need you! Don't think the silver I pay you every month is for you to stand around eating and watching the show!"

A series of clicking sounds came from nearby — guns being loaded. In moments, green heads popped up from walls, towers, and behind abandoned race cars. Jace's group was surrounded by who knew how many gun barrels.

These gunners were highly professional. In Jace's eyes, they showed no fear at facing obvious threats — if anything, they seemed enthusiastic. Puzik must be paying them well.

"I didn't expect you to be in bed with those cultists too." Puzik sneered. "Looks like the tables have turned. The 'Scenery' team has the upper hand now. Didn't expect me to overtake on the outside of such a sharp curve, did you?"

"This is all a mess!" Noggenfogger shouted in panic. "It's just a cooling system! How did it come to this?"

"Relax, Noggenfogger! Those Twilight's Hammer bastards have scrambled your brains, but I'll set you straight!" Puzik yelled.

"We're not Twilight's Hammer cultists, Mr. Puzik." Jace said. "I'm Jace Sesso. If you know much about the other side, you should know who I am. A lot of warlocks only came to Kalimdor to join Twilight's Hammer because of me."

"You're Jace Sesso?" Puzik frowned. "Then I'm Orgrim Doomhammer! Jace Sesso has a blue dragon's sword. Show me!"

"I'll have to draw my sword. Tell your men not to shoot." Jace said.

"Go ahead! Let's see what you pull out!" Puzik shouted behind him. "Everyone, let him draw first!"

Jace carefully drew the Song of Clear Skies. The blue light it released shone brightly in the Shimmering Flats' twilight.

"That's really the blue dragon sword..." Puzik froze. "You really are Jace Sesso. Then this elf lady is Vereesa Windrunner? The youngest Windrunner? The original owner of the Song of Clear Skies?"

"I really don't understand how the title 'High Thane' can't compare to 'the youngest Windrunner.'" Grede muttered unhappily.

"That's mainly because news travel slower than I fly, dwarf." Solara said.

"Bloody hell. This is really—" Puzik seemed to relax with relief, waving for the gunners to lower their weapons. "My apologies, my lords, lady, Master Wizard Sesso. It's just that in the past few months, countless Twilight bastards have been trying to get me — sneaky, open, every kind. They've forced me to keep upgrading my defenses here... But why do you want to see my cooling system?"
