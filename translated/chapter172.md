## Chapter 172: The Three Relics (Part Two)

"The Book of Medivh is very likely one of them," Kel'Thuzad said. "But as far as I know, that book contains too many spells and pieces of knowledge that are difficult to translate. Relying on this one clue alone, we cannot determine the purpose of whoever issued this order."

"All right, let's count the Book of Medivh in for now. Then what about the remaining two relics? Master Kel'Thuzad is right — without the other two, we have no idea what the Book of Medivh could even be used for." Aethas Sunreaver's particularly distinctive Common accent made his question stand out all the more.

"Perhaps they're in Quel'Thalas," a Gilneas scholar said, looking at Aethas.

"Then Teron Gorefiend might as well give up on the idea," Aethas said. "Silvermoon's magic cannot be breached unless it's taken by force — infiltration is out of the question."

"I think this matter should be discussed at a political or military conference, not a language symposium," Kel'Thuzad said. "Let us keep our focus on the language of the letter itself. As for the contents of this letter, I will soon bring it before the Kirin Tor council for the other masters to discuss, including Master Antonidas…"

"But the very reason we study the Orcish language is for the Alliance's benefit, Master Kel'Thuzad," Dushan said.

The Kul Tiras scholar said, "I agree with Master Caro Dushan's view. Given that the contents of this order are extremely recent and concern the safety of treasures that may be held by every kingdom, we Orcish-language researchers should make full use of our knowledge and abilities to interpret everything this order conveys. You saw the disaster at Alterac — those wounded soldiers and burned walls. The combined forces of Lordaeron and Stromgarde actually suffered a crushing defeat at the hands of a remnant Horde force! By the Holy Light, if we had obtained a similar order sooner, perhaps we could have prevented that tragedy."

Kel'Thuzad looked at him and said, "The failure at Alterac was months ago, Master Radimo. To this day, Teron Gorefiend has yet to show himself — even the Holy Light doesn't know where he is."

He looked back at the others and said, "Perhaps he has already headed to Stranglethorn or the Hinterlands. The other two relics could very well be preserved in the cities of the Gurubashi and Amani trolls. These two empires existed on this world far earlier than recorded history, and the ancient artifacts they keep in Zul'Gurub and Zul'Aman exceed ordinary imagination."

"What Master Kel'Thuzad says… is indeed possible."

"Could it be one representative treasure from each of the three races — human, elf, and troll? The Book of Medivh is unquestionably the representative arcane artifact of the human kingdoms. Let's hope the expeditionary force at Nethergarde Keep can stop that thief and prevent it from flowing to the other side of the Dark Portal?"

"What about the dwarves? Perhaps it's that evil artifact beneath Grim Batol. Gorefiend's men may have already found that place and begun excavating."

"Let's keep the topic in the real world, Mr. Coroso, and not bring up distant legends."

"Grim Batol is real!" a dwarf shouted.

And just like that, the discussion went completely off the rails. Jace could hardly believe it — Kel'Thuzad had actually steered the focus in such an absurd direction.

He'd been counting on this mage to connect the dots to the Eye of the Arcane soon enough, especially since everyone had already made the clues so obvious.

Jace took a deep breath and said, "Master, esteemed teachers — even if Gorefiend may have gone elsewhere, I believe Dalaran should strengthen its defenses. Master Kel'Thuzad, after all, Dalaran and the Kirin Tor also safeguard many of the most powerful artifacts known to mankind…"

"This young apprentice speaks the truth. Dalaran, where we stand, is the place that needs to be most on its guard," Aethas agreed.

"The vault's defenses are already perfect, gentlemen — in no way inferior to Quel'Thalas," Kel'Thuzad replied calmly. "However, I will raise this suggestion at the next Council of Six meeting."

"The orcish order you brought is extremely valuable, Mr. Sesso. Please keep it safe. I believe quite a number of people will want to lay eyes on its original form again. After all, going by what you've said, this was very likely written by Teron Gorefiend himself… Now then, let the scholars from the Kingdom of Lordaeron present their research findings."

After Kel'Thuzad finished speaking, his gaze never left Jace for a moment. His expression was cold as ice, yet it seemed to conceal some kind of fierce warning — a naked threat.

Was this an illusion? Was it because of his own prejudice against Kel'Thuzad that he felt this way? Wait…

Why had Kel'Thuzad been trying to skip past the topic from the very beginning, and then later steered the focus of the discussion toward other irrelevant places?

A possibility Jace didn't want to consider bubbled up in his mind. Could it be that Kel'Thuzad had also been drawn into the scheme of the black dragons and Teron Gorefiend?

Or worse — what if Kel'Thuzad was an inside man, and there was some secret deal between the two?

He helped Gorefiend break into the vault and steal the Eye of the Arcane, in exchange for guidance from the death knights — or even the black dragons themselves — in his research into the powers of necromancy?

Thinking of that look, Jace's thoughts churned into a complete mess.

He glanced back at this distinguished member of the Council of Six and deeply felt there was nothing more he could do here to further the matter of the Eye of the Arcane.

Because if his guess was right, the contents of this letter might not even make it out of this room!

And how many of these scholars would have the motivation to keep investigating the identities of the relics after the conference ended?

The best they could do would be to warn their respective kings and lords after returning home. How could they possibly concern themselves with Dalaran's problems on top of that?

"Thank you. I will be careful, Master Kel'Thuzad."

With that, Jace turned and walked up the steps. He felt a little guilty for not fighting his case further, but what else could he do now?

After clearly seeing Kel'Thuzad's problem, was he supposed to openly confront him?

Jace's goal was to survive the crisis — not to gloriously become an inconspicuous piece on a flesh abomination in Kel'Thuzad's basement.

But before he'd taken a few steps, the entire conference hall suddenly shook, and dust drifted down from above.

Kel'Thuzad frowned and looked up at the high skylight. The scholars present burst into exclamations.

"Does Alterac have earthquakes too?" the Stromgarde woman scholar asked in a panic.

The Gilneas scholar gripped the armrest of his chair and said, "Strictly speaking, this isn't Alterac. But Dalaran sits at the border between the Gilnean peninsula and the Alterac Mountains, madam — even if there were an earthquake, it wouldn't be anything unusual."

Jace had just steadied himself when a stumbling apprentice came running up the steps from behind the podium. Upon seeing Kel'Thuzad, he immediately shouted in a trembling voice: "Master — Dalaran is under attack!"

At that, everyone up high was startled.

"Speak clearly — attacked by whom? King Terenas II?!" Aethas Sunreaver stood up and demanded angrily.

"Black dragons, Master — a whole swarm of black dragons!"
