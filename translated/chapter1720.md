## Chapter 1720: Pack It All Up

"You're not going to grind us arrogant mortal worms into dust for disrespecting the Elemental Lord or anything?" Grede called out.

"Though the power in this Vial of Rebirth was great, it's already quite an achievement that it let me reform a body from this remnant soul. I don't have much power left to waste," Thunderaan said. His voice wasn't particularly frightening, but it was clear through the raging storm of wind, rain, and lightning. "I still need to rest, gather my strength, and wait for the day I fully recover."

"You said you're breaking with the Skywall. Why?" Jace asked.

"Salsalan's appearance confirmed what I've long feared — that the master of the Skywall, my father... has accepted the worship of the Twilight's Hammer cult," Thunderaan said. "This sword was forged for me by him. I don't want his power to keep influencing my will. Shattering this blade is proof of my separation from the Throne of the Four Winds."

"In the future, I'll leave Uldum — leave all of Kalimdor, even — and find a place where I can gather strength without interference from other elementals or wind elemental hunters. I hope Al'Akir will believe I've cowardly gone into hiding. The truth is not far off. But I won't hide forever. One day I'll make him see reason. I'll make him understand that the wind cannot forever follow others — the wind must guide its own power. I hope his elemental core hasn't been completely corrupted by Cthun, that there's still a chance for him to see clearly."

"But if things have already reached a hopeless state, I won't go easy on him just because he's my creator. I'll build my own legion, establish my own strength, and sooner or later I'll ascend the Throne of the Four Winds."

"Wouldn't leaving the elemental plane weaken your power, Wind Prince?" Vereesa asked.

"The wind is everywhere, elf." Thunderaan clenched a fist, and his power sent lightning crackling not far above their heads. "As long as there is thunder and lightning, as long as the wind sweeps across the grasslands, there will be my voice — and my followers'."

"I think you should seek out Duke Hydraxis of the Abyssal Council," Jace said. "From what I know, he's stationed off the coast of Azshara. And we defeated the Fire Lord Ragnaros together — he'll certainly look favorably on you. You might even become allies."

"I'll consider it." The Wind Prince tossed a spark toward the electrified blade embedded in the ground. "Take this thing back with you. Have it forged into a weapon suited to your size. If you ever find yourselves in danger, call my name to this weapon, and I'll find a way to come and help. It's been my honor to share the epic battle of Molten Core with you mortals, to personally destroy the hateful runes Ragnaros used to burn this world. And in the end, I even gained rebirth."

"Let's hope we never have to call you with this blade, Prince Thunderaan," Jace said.

"Let's hope so. But the affairs of the world are as unpredictable as the storm." The Wind Prince looked down at Needleflame, his voice low. "Good luck on your journey ahead. Though the Black Dragon Princess Onyxia is weak and badly wounded, cornered black dragons are often more dangerous and unhinged. You've seen what the followers of the Black Empire are like atop Blackrock Spire."

Needleflame said nothing. She just stared up at the abstract elemental lord's face, her expression still tense. But Jace felt she was already prepared to meet Onyxia.

"We'll remember that, Wind Prince." Jace put an arm around Needleflame's hood and called up. "I hope you become the master of the Throne of the Four Winds one day. I think you'd suit that seat better than Al'Akir."

"Though you've never been there and can't truly judge, I appreciate the sentiment, Jace Sesso."

The clear voice grew fuzzier and fuzzier, soon swallowed by the gale. The storm passed faster than it had arrived, so unnaturally quick it was almost unsettling.

Before the group had fully recovered from the shock of the Wind Prince's sudden appearance and power, Thunderaan had already departed with his newly gathered "followers."

Even the winds that had been swirling around Freewind Post had died down by more than half. The night sky was clear and still — so calm it was hard to believe they were sitting hundreds, maybe even a thousand meters above the ground. Only the puddles of rainwater everywhere and the creaking of the half-ruined huts nearby served as evidence that the Wind Prince had ever visited.

Well, that and the electrified blade still sticking out of the muddy ground.

This blade was barely more than a massive, strangely-shaped piece of metal of unknown material. Not a weapon yet.

Given that it was a foreign object from the Skywall, and that in another timeline the elemental metal needed to forge Thunderfury, Blessed Blade of the Windseeker was Arcanite Ingots, it was reasonable to guess this thing was also some special sky-metal from the Skywall.

Grede walked over and reached out to touch it. A loud crack and he yelped, "Bloody hell!" as he jerked his hand back.

He stared at his blackened fingertips in shock. "If I was twenty years younger, that probably would've left me crippled for life. Thank the ancestors for watching over me."

"How do we carry it out of here?" Vereesa asked.

Nobody had a great idea. The ice-blue blade was still crackling with electricity, lighting up the entire top of Freewind Post. Looking at it too long made their eyes ache.

If Jace hadn't just recently defeated Nefarian single-handedly, he'd be wrapping this thing up as tightly as Uthalesh right now, to keep the wyverns flying through Thousand Needles or the Grimtotem on other stone pillars from noticing them.

When they took it with them, they'd need a similar approach. Otherwise, they'd have no way to hide their trail, and they'd have to explain the thing's origin to everyone they met — though admittedly, this was far less incriminating than Uthalesh.

"How about we ask the Wind Prince again?" Jace suggested.

"He's gone," Needleflame said, pointing at the sky.

"Didn't he say to call him if we run into trouble? Does this count?" Jace said.

Vereesa folded her arms and glanced over at him, unable to suppress a laugh. "Looks like another tough fight is unavoidable."

"Fight or no fight, I'm not getting drenched again," Grede said, scratching his chin as he paced around the blade twice. He stopped, puffed out his chest, and took a few deep breaths.

Then, in a fairly serious tone, he addressed it in elemental language, mimicking the sages: "Calm your fury, thunder force. Uh... we're friendly... good people. Restrain your power?"

Even though Grede wasn't sure what to say, he muttered a few phrases and the blade rapidly dimmed, as if it had understood him.

"Solved," Grede said, looking at the others. "Who wants to touch it and see?"

"Whoever touches gets to keep it?" Vereesa asked.

"Huh?" Grede's eyes lit up. He immediately reached out and touched it — sure enough, no shock this time.

But the elemental power inside hadn't weakened. Which meant this weapon was something that could communicate, like a living thing? An elemental creature, like... Uthalesh.

"So it's mine now?" Grede grinned.

"I just asked. Nobody's decided yet," Vereesa said.

"Huh?" The dwarf pointed at the ranger. "What do you mean by that, pointy-ears? And it was my elemental language that made it stop being dangerous."

Sora looked interested. "I can speak some elemental language too, Grede."

Sora hadn't really claimed any significant loot since joining the team. When she spoke up, even Grede couldn't bring himself to argue.

"Let's just take it with us and figure it out after we get back to the other side," Jace said. "We don't know what the Thorium Brotherhood can forge it into yet. If it ends up as a short sword, would you still want it?"

"Don't forget who taught you swordsmanship, Jace Sesso." Grede dusted off his hands. "But fair point. What if they turn it into a spearhead or something..."

"We'll wrap it in the tauren hides here," Vereesa said. "Let's rest tonight. Tomorrow night we head north toward the grasslands."

"You seem even more eager to see Mossa than I am," the dwarf said.

"Of course," the elf said. "She might even miss me more than she misses you."
