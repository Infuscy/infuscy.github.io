Chapter 1721: Mulgore

Jace and his group had no idea where Mossa was right now — whether she was in Ashenvale or with other tauren. To find Mossa, they'd need to find tauren first. Mossa was the first of a new generation of tauren druids to train in the Emerald Dream, and she was already well-known among her people. Her tribesmen should be able to point them to her easily.

The biggest problem was finding a group of tauren.

Before, they'd run into a band of tauren hunters along the banks of the Southfury River who'd been tracking them — including the son of Chief Cairne Bloodhoof himself. That was how they'd learned Mossa Woodbough had gone to Ashenvale looking for "reinforcements" against the centaur.

But those hunters were a roving patrol, always on the move, hunting prey and watching for centaur and any other potential threats. They had no fixed camp.

And back then, it was only because one of the tauren had been tracking them and accidentally got caught that he'd led them to the hunters' temporary camp. Now, trying to actively search along the Southfury River — which ran across half of Kalimdor — for any sign of their movements would be a massive waste of time.

But as Grede had been hoping all along, they could go see Mulgore — the homeland the tauren had been driven from by the centaur tribes.

Jace figured that since the tauren had always wanted to reclaim their territory, they'd surely set up outposts along Mulgore's borders to keep an eye on centaur movements. Those places might be easier for the group, flying high above, to spot.

Sora carried Vereesa, Grede, and most of the luggage — the heaviest of which was the Sulfuras war hammer. That left Needleflame with the lighter load, so naturally she got the job of carrying the Wind-Kissed Blade.

The wind blade was much lighter than it looked. The little black dragon whelp carried it through the air, letting it sway back and forth with each beat of her wings, without seeming to struggle much.

They left Freewind Post and flew west from the heights of Thousand Needles. After Thunderaan departed, the place had become so clear and sunny it was hard to believe this was the same land with all those wind-related names. They'd passed "Windbreak Canyon" earlier, and now they were approaching "Blackcloud Peak." These names didn't exactly evoke a calm, quiet place — yet the sunlit, clearly-defined valleys, cloudless skies, and dry canyons seemed to describe an entirely different region. Last night's torrential storm felt like a dream with no warning signs.

The goblin-made Southern Kalimdor map Jace got from Gadgetzan was fuzzy on the marshlands and the western Kalimdor coast, but at least it was fairly detailed on most of Thousand Needles and the northern grasslands and the Southfury River basin, with many places marked.

At the very least, using this map alongside his memories from his previous life, he could roughly determine Mulgore's location to the northwest.

After following the direction of Thousand Needles' massive rift valley for most of a day, huge, spectacular thornbushes began appearing on the high mountains.

"Remember these thorn-covered mountains?" Vereesa shouted back to Needleflame and Jace. "Mossa led us through these thorns before, dodging the quilboar elites, to get into the grasslands. I think we're almost there!"

"If that's the case, the map says we need to turn northwest!" Jace shouted.

"You sure this legendary Mulgore is to the northwest, Jace?" Grede yelled. "Doesn't look like there's any beautiful scenery over there — just yellow hills, no end in sight!"

"At least that's what the tauren say!" Jace shouted back.

"Fine, we'll trust him," Grede said. "It's not like a detour's gonna make a tauren die of old age."

They flew out of Thousand Needles and into the Agamaggan Highlands. The thorn-covered mountains grew more frequent below. In the valleys and ravines between these ridges, simple huts and hide tents became more and more densely packed — some small enough to be outhouses, others large enough to be palaces, barely inferior to the great den the orcs had built at Stonard.

This was the quilboar settlement beyond Razorfen Downs — the place the game called Razorfen Kraul.

Sora flew ahead, Needleflame behind. As the two dragons passed over these structures, they could clearly hear piercing pig-like squeals from below, along with the low sound of war horns, as if the whole city had stirred to alert.

Jace spotted a dozen quilboar climbing the thorns and staring upward, screeching threats in what might or might not have been actual language. If the distance wasn't so great, he wouldn't have been surprised if they'd even launched a few crossbow bolts.

Though they couldn't hurt the two whelps, Needleflame was seriously pissed. She already had a mouthful of flame ready, just waiting for the green light to go down and shut them up.

Jace found it strange too. This place wasn't that far from the black dragon's lair in the marshes — basically a day's march across the thorny ridges.

If the goblins knew there were black dragons living there, the quilboar who'd lived here for thousands of years should know too. At the very least, they must have some awareness. Normally, a race like this would avoid a terrifying higher existence like a black dragon at all costs. How dare they come out so brazenly and provoke?

Remembering how even a wealthy figure like Noggenfogger was talking about organizing an army to attack Dustwallow Marsh and develop new industries, Jace wondered if all the monsters in Southern Kalimdor had realized the black dragons in the marsh were on their last legs. Onyxia's injuries were probably more severe than they'd estimated.

Thinking about it, Jace even felt a little worried. The creatures who knew the most about the Nathrezim's movements, aside from the already-dead Nefarian, was Onyxia — who'd split from him. If Onyxia died from her wounds in her lair, that lead would be a dead end, and this whole trip to Kalimdor would become nothing more than a sightseeing tour.

Given the goblins' ambitions and the quilboar's brazen attitude toward a black whelp, it wasn't impossible.

As the massive thornbushes scattered across the slopes grew sparser, it meant they were leaving quilboar territory. Legend said these thorns grew from the blood of the quilboar demigod Agamaggan, whose corpse had protected his descendants for ten thousand years.

They crossed a tall ridge, and suddenly Grede let out a cheer. The view before them was suddenly filled with vibrant life, as if the barren wasteland and thorns had been swept away — along with the monotony of endless yellow earth.

This was the Mulgore basin. The vast, tender grasslands rose and fell like an ocean. Slopes were dotted with tall pines. Beneath the setting sun, golden streams wound through the lowlands. In the far distance, stone pillars rose from the plains like the peaks of Thousand Needles — but they stood alone or in clusters of two or three, without the cramped arrangement of the stone forest. These pillars looked more like gentle, magnificent giants wandering across the prairie.

All of it together felt like a fairy-tale ending captured in an oil painting. Just one glance left you wanting more.

"I take back every stupid thing I said!" Grede spread his arms, facing the setting sun, and shouted in excitement. "No wonder the tauren were so determined to come back here... Now I get it!"

"Somehow, the real Mulgore is even more beautiful than in the Dream," Sora said, her voice full of wonder.

"This reminds me of Nagrand," Vereesa said.

"It reminds me of Nagrand too, but without all those floating rocks in the sky or a giant void vortex staring at you. I like this better than Nagrand." Needleflame turned her head and asked, "What do you think, Jace Sesso?"

"I feel the same," Jace said.

Actually, it should be the other way around, he thought.

In another timeline, people saw Mulgore first, then got to enter Nagrand in the expansion. But then again... that didn't really matter anymore.
