Chapter 1723: Who Dares Hurt Sister Sora?!

"I told you we should've ridden — no, let Sora carry us while tracking. Look how pathetic we are now! If I'd trusted you, we wouldn't have been captured by those Kaldorei in the rainforest!" "Shut up and be quiet for a second! Sora flies too high — she can't see clearly at low altitude. The tauren know this forest. They can easily find cover and avoid our line of sight. And the trees here obviously don't want to talk to her... More importantly, I can read these tracks!" "More important than that is that we're not even faster than those tauren now! What good does reading tracks do?" "Relax. They just got a head start. I'll catch up eventually — even trolls can't escape my tracking. My only worry is if we find out they're Grimtotem members." "Stop worrying about what-if scenarios! Mossa's enemies must know better than us where she probably is, so whether they're Grimtotem or not, it's the same. Besides — last time you were here, you kicked a centaur's best warrior to death in front of a whole crowd of them. Now you're scared of a few fleeing tauren?" "I just don't want to save the wrong people. But you're right." "So, uh, still no luck with the trees?" "They've been very quiet, Jace." "If it's really not working, why don't you fly high and coordinate with our ground search?" "I just shifted back to human form..." "Shh." Vereesa stopped abruptly, blocking the others behind her, and raised Belanoor, nocking a shadow-draped elven arrow. Behind her, Jace and Sora fell silent. Grede likewise pinched his throat and whispered, "Are those tauren hiding nearby?" "Not just them," Vereesa said quietly. "Something's off." The forest began to stir. In the darkness, Jace couldn't tell what was approaching, but the rustling leaves, swaying trunks, and snapping branches were coming from everywhere — not just ahead, but from all around. The group warily watched their surroundings. Jace's mind raced, trying to think what kind of strange beast could be causing this commotion in Mulgore. Giant striders coming to feed? A kodo that had wandered into the forest? The next second, a massive tree trunk swayed forward on thick branches and roots, "walking" out of the forest! Treants! Jace had seen treants before — in Maraudon. But these were enormous, like the ancient trees that lived alongside the night elves, not the smaller ones. Probably because they didn't need to squeeze through caves — they'd grown up naturally in Mulgore's open grasslands, so they were much larger. Half a dozen treants of varying heights came "running" out of the deep forest. Life energy blazed like fire through cracks and holes in their trunks, looking uncannily like the eyes and mouths of aged mortals. They surrounded the group in the clearing, whipping their branch-like arms, radiating clear hostility — like they were coming to kill. Fine then. Jace wasn't about to be polite with the locals. "Stop!" He raised his hand, his incantation barely two syllables out, shadow energy still gathering — when Sora's furious shout cut through everything, breaking the rhythm. The green dragon's angry roar echoed through the forest. Every single treant froze in place. Two of them were so frightened their life-force actually "went out," and they looked ready to topple over. "You wanted to protect the locals here by not answering my call. I don't blame you for that." Sora looked around, raising her staff and pointing at them, continuing in the Dragon Tongue: "But now you dare to attack a child of Ysera?!" "I think you've been corrupted by the magic of those ancient Old Gods — how dare you act so recklessly?!" Calling them Old God corrupted was a stretch, honestly. Jace didn't sense any shadow energy corruption on them — just some murderous anger. But the treants, having been shouted down like this, seemed to realize who this green-clad elf really was. Their emotions calmed considerably, but they also looked bewildered. They seemed to have been following someone's orders to attack. "Somnilyr? Is that you?" A familiar voice came from the forest. A massive figure emerged from between the trees. This figure wore heavy brown leather robes and carried a staff made of a tree trunk that Jace had never seen before. On its head was a wide bear-skull half-mask. If you didn't look closely, it really did look like an old, upright brown bear walking out of the forest. But the fur color beneath the mask, the way it walked, and most importantly — the voice — was enough for everyone there to recognize her. "Mossa!" Grede shouted in excitement. "Gre... Grede? You're here too?!" The giant figure pushed up the bear-skull mask, revealing the tauren head beneath. As she stepped forward, a pair of horns emerged from the moonlight. She couldn't wait to leap down from the slope. Her agility and grace, the lightness of her landing despite her bulky frame and cloven hooves, was striking. Grede dropped his axe and rushed to meet her. These two — one tall, one short, both stocky and sturdy — collided in a tight embrace and spun around several times. If Jace didn't know what their relationship was, he'd be absolutely sure they were about to start a world-shaking, god-defying ultra-heavyweight wrestling match. "You've grown taller, Grede." Mossa's voice was almost choked with tears. "Sure have," Grede laughed and shouted. "Still got a way to go to catch up to you, though!" "Now that's a pair of lovebirds," Vereesa whispered, tilting her head. "If I told him he'd grown a bit taller, he'd go home and smash Silvermoon City with that fire hammer of his." "Ver... Jace... you're all here." Mossa wiped her eyes, trotted over, hugged Vereesa and Sora one by one, and finally scooped Jace into her arms. Honestly, it had been a long time since Jace had traveled with Mossa. When he felt the ground shake as she approached, he'd be lying if he said his heart wasn't pounding. "So, what was that all about?" Jace asked. "We were chasing some tauren prisoners, and suddenly we were surrounded by treants you summoned." "I thought you were slaver mercenaries hired by goblins. You looked capable, so I just wanted to protect them and let the treants slow you down." Mossa spoke in fluent Common. "It's getting harder to catch ogres these days, so those greedy, shameless goblins have turned their attention to us. How was I supposed to know it was you guys? The forest wouldn't tell me who you were or where you were going. Otherwise, how could I treat you as enemies? Now I see Somnilyr is with you — no wonder." "These trees really know how to play both sides," Grede said helplessly. Sora sighed quietly. "But why were you chasing them?" Mossa asked. "And what's with that black dragon? Did you come here to hunt it? It seems to have quite a grudge against those Kolkar centaur..." "That black dragon's with us," Grede said. "Wait, what?!" Mossa's small eyes went wide with shock. Grede started to explain, then realized it wasn't that simple. He just pointed at Jace. "Let him explain." "It's a long story." Jace sorted through his thoughts, starting from Draenor. He organized his words, then looked around — and suddenly realized something was off. "She didn't come with us?" "Uh... well, I think she's still back at the centaur camp," Vereesa said. The moment she finished speaking, flames erupted from the direction they'd come from, growing fiercer and brighter by the second. In just a moment, the entire visible area was blazing red. "What mad forest dares to hurt Sister Sora?!" Hearing that dragon roar, Vereesa thought, "Oh no." Sora gasped. Mossa was so spooked she immediately pulled her bear mask back down. The treants didn't wait for a second invitation — they turned their branchy limbs and scattered into the forest. Jace cupped his hands around his mouth and bellowed, "Needleflame, it's a misunderstanding! Needleflame, stop!" But his voice was completely swallowed by the roar of flames crashing into the forest. "Forget 'Needleflame'! Get out of the way first!" Grede grabbed Jace and yanked him aside. Black wings swept overhead, shadowing the canopy. Savage flames swept across half the clearing...
