Chapter 1724: Survivors in the Cracks

Mossa led the outsiders through the forest and up a slope. According to her, the tauren here lived in crevices high on the cliffs. Centaur didn't like climbing slopes or scaling rocks, and many tauren used that fact to keep a safe distance. They survived in the nooks and crannies among Mulgore's outermost mountains. Over time, they'd grown used to this kind of life.

The forest was still thick with smoke from the black dragon's breath. Fortunately, the night was damp and cold enough, and with Jace's help from the elements, the fire hadn't managed to spread through the woods.

"A year ago?" Mossa walked and looked at the little black dragon following behind, eyeing her suspiciously. "So she's been alive for about five years in total?"

"Yes," Jace said. "She's a descendant of Deathwing, Mossa. I mean — not like those black dragons who chant 'Father of the Flight' all day. She's Deathwing's direct bloodline. Like her elders and siblings, she's the kind of dragon who'll grow into a true dragon."

"If I didn't know all this, I'd definitely advise you to kill her right now." Mossa's voice was low. "But now... I don't know."

Needleflame frowned but said nothing. She'd been saying all along that she wanted to see what Kalimdor tauren were like. But meeting Mossa — and finding out Mossa was a close friend of the group — she was still a little wary.

Probably because she'd had too much contact with those strange tauren in the Twilight's Hammer, or because she hadn't fully adjusted to the fact that these "giant monsters," so different in appearance from most mortals, had such gentle personalities.

"The Dark Portal, orcs, Draenor, demon armies, Blackrock Spire, black dragons..." Mossa said. "Ten thousand years ago, demons descended on the palace of the Kaldorei Empire. Many Kaldorei, tauren, and other races of ancient Kalimdor took part in that war. In the end, we won. Humans hadn't even appeared on this continent yet. But listening to your stories, I can feel it — you humans and Quel'dorei have lived through your own 'War of the Ancients.' And you're the main characters in that grand story."

"Well..." Grede jerked a thumb at Jace. "He's the main character. Forget Draenor — I didn't get to see what happened on that mountain. But this guy went one-on-one with the Black Dragon Prince on top of a tower and won. All we could do was watch from below."

"I'm not surprised in the least." Mossa looked at Jace and said in Darnassian.

"So, what brings you to Kalimdor this time? I have to admit, I'm nervous. I can't imagine what kind of terrible disaster would need a group like you to assemble."

"It doesn't need to be that serious," Vereesa said. "Just a dwarf who's been thinking day and night about coming to Kalimdor to meet his sweetheart."

Grede laughed awkwardly. Jace said, "We came to see you, Mossa. You might think that sounds pretentious, but seeing you really is one of the most important parts of this trip."

"And it's not just because of Grede. We've all missed you," Sora added.

"I've missed you all too." Mossa lowered her head. "I still often remember Maraudon — the adventures we had together, when we fought those satyrs. I think if my parents and tribesmen were still alive, I might be traveling the world the same way you do now."

"Speaking of which, we passed through Thousand Needles on the way here, Mossa," Vereesa said. "Do you know about the village on the highest peak?"

"The Grimtotem village?" Mossa asked. "Blackcloud Peak?"

"No," Jace said. "Southeast of what Blackcloud Peak is on our map. A bit further."

"Freewind Post, yes," Mossa said. "Some tribes lived there to escape the centaur threat. Then a band of Grimtotem raiders took it over and drove the original residents away."

"Well, the Grimtotem are gone too now," Grede said. "I'd love to say we ran them off, but no. When we got there, there wasn't a living soul. Like everyone suddenly packed up and left for some reason."

Mossa said, "The Grimtotem near Mulgore — including the ones on the prairie... they've all left too. They seem to have moved to the marsh, according to some stories."

"Why?" Sora asked. "Does it have something to do with the black dragons?"

"Very likely," Mossa said. "But lately there's been a persistent rumor — and not just among us. Some Kolkar, the goblins in Gadgetzan, even the quilboar all know about it. The black dragons hiding in the marshes seem to have lost many of their number."

"We even ran into some Kolkar warriors wearing armor made from black dragon scales. Some Kolkar prisoners confessed they got them from quilboar and travelers coming out of the marsh. Those Grimtotem probably thought they'd seize the opportunity to find some treasure too."

"What's going on with that?" Vereesa looked at Jace. "I mean with the dead black dragons."

"I don't know either," Jace said. "But I'm guessing a lot of black dragons were killed by Nefarian in Blackrock Spire too. The ones here that were willing to follow Nefarian... Onyxia killing them sounds pretty normal, right?"

Grede looked between them. "So right now is when that female dragon is at her most irritable?"

"Doesn't that give you a good reason to cut her head off and take it home?" Vereesa asked.

"Fair point." Grede snapped his fingers.

Sora shivered slightly at the exchange but said nothing.

"We used to think the black dragons in the marsh were just scary stories. Now they're real, and a lot of people have even found black dragon scales." The tauren looked back at Needleflame. "Does bringing this black dragon lady here have something to do with the rumored Black Dragon Princess?"

Jace looked at her, then said, "Sort of. But it's a long story."

"You must be exhausted from your journey. Why don't we head back to our shelter, rest, and eat something? We can talk over dinner. The hideout is just ahead."

Mossa pointed upward. At the edge of the forest, against a cliff wall, a tauren warrior holding a spear seemed to be watching below cautiously. If Mossa hadn't pointed, that warrior with the dark brown mane would have been invisible in the night. Only the spear in his hand faintly reflected the moonlight.

They followed Mossa up the cliff, squeezed through a crack in the rock face, and entered what Mossa called the tauren refuge.

From Mossa's description, Jace had expected a small village that took in tauren whose families had been destroyed by centaur — at least some kind of settlement. Instead, counting Mossa and the guard outside, there were only about eight or nine tauren. A few of them were injured to varying degrees, lying further inside recovering — apparently prisoners who'd just escaped from that centaur camp.

Even so, when the tauren heard that this group of foreign adventurers were friends of the dragon who'd destroyed the Kolkar village and freed the prisoners, they did their best to bring out fruit, grain, and a pot of soup made with their last scraps of meat as hospitality and thanks.

Living in a place like this, having to avoid centaur hunting parties while hiding their tracks and searching for captured kin — this group of tauren couldn't possibly have abundant supplies. Offering what they did must have been a real sacrifice.

During the meal, Mossa made brief introductions to her kin. Jace couldn't understand Tauren, but from the earlier conversation, he could pick out the word for "dragon." He noticed Mossa didn't mention what kind of dragon had rescued them — it was dark out anyway, and easy enough to brush off.

Mossa was a remarkably open-minded druid. She'd gradually accepted a demon friend like Morofel within a few months. A good-natured black dragon she could probably stomach just as quickly. But not every tauren was as open-minded as she was, so Jace understood — this way, she'd avoid a lot of trouble.

The tauren ranged in age, young and old. Regardless of their experience or years, they were all stunned by the stories this group had lived through.

That wasn't surprising. Even Jace, looking back on the road they'd traveled, sometimes didn't know how to describe it.

Everyone gestured and relied on Mossa for translation as they discussed what had happened on the other continent and the recent rumors in Southern Kalimdor. The dinner atmosphere was actually pretty good.

Needleflame slowly opened up too. Faced with a young black dragon whose personality was roughly equivalent to a human kid just past ten, even Mossa, who'd been wary of her at first, let her guard down completely.

But after dinner, the meager meal couldn't fill anyone's stomach. Jace could tell the rest felt the same — especially Sora and Needleflame. For the two dragons, this meal was practically nothing.

"How about we go hunting tomorrow?" Jace suggested.

"Great idea," Vereesa said. "What are we hunting?"

Jace looked at Needleflame. "Thunder lizards."

"I'm in!" Needleflame said excitedly.
