Chapter 1725: Over the Plains

The next morning, before the sun had even risen above the Barrens horizon, the sky was a hazy, dark blue. The green dragon soared at extreme altitude, nothing but a tiny speck to the animals below — hard to distinguish from any other bird of prey.

The group had left the less valuable but heavier luggage at the tauren camp. Sora carried them over the savanna. Even without most of the cargo, carrying Jace and a lioness meant she wasn't flying light.

But she didn't need to go far today, and she didn't need to fly hard. Because the one doing the heavy lifting today was the one far ahead.

Needleflame had already rushed ahead impatiently. She had no idea where to find thunder lizards, but flying in front was good enough. Jace didn't even need to probe her emotions to know the little one wanted to find a legendary thunder lizard and go one-on-one. A true warrior faced their dream rival alone. No backup. No kill-stealing.

"A lot of thunder lizards live on these plains, along with some famous heavy hitters." Mossa held Sora's shoulder with her front claws, struggling to form Common words with her lion mouth. She couldn't shift back to tauren form now — the massive weight she'd carried with nature magic would all press down on Sora's right shoulder.

Fortunately, as a druid shapeshifted into a big cat, her mouth wasn't as clumsy as a real lion's or leopard's.

"When I was young, I might have taken you to find the legendary 'Elder' Skum. For centuries, he's been a legend in Southern Kalimdor. Think about it — centaur, quilboar, tauren have all lived in these parts over the centuries. Everyone's tried to hunt him, but no one's ever actually killed him... That tells you how tough that old guy was."

"What happened to him?" Grede asked.

"He disappeared. Maybe he died of old age. No one knows," Mossa answered. "If he really did die of old age, then in a way, he won the centuries-long contest on these plains."

"What a shame," Vereesa said.

Actually, he escaped, Jace knew.

Skum. He remembered the name. He'd run Wailing Caverns countless times in his previous life — both on his main and leveling alts. Playing Horde, it was unavoidable. Even twenty years later, he still remembered almost every boss's name in there.

Skum was a giant thunder lizard locked in a druid's menagerie in that dungeon. He didn't have any special tricks as a boss, but chain lightning on repeat plus bite damage was painful enough.

But before hearing Mossa's story, he'd had no idea that thing was a centuries-old veteran.

Speaking of which — what was Wailing Caverns like now? That corrupted pit dragged into the Emerald Nightmare by the slumbering night elf druid Naralex...

They'd just come out of Mulgore and were flying east. So Wailing Caverns should be north of here.

The first time they'd come to Kalimdor, they'd stopped at the oasis near the entrance to Wailing Caverns, but in the end, they didn't go in.

Back then, they'd had a mission. No need to risk their lives exploring a cave with uncertain rewards. But now...

Sora didn't seem to have any particular reaction to the scattered oases and ponds on the grassland either. Normally, even the druids of Thunder Bluff could sense the anomaly here. A green dragon should definitely be able to feel it. Could it be that in another timeline, the danger of Wailing Caverns had already been neutralized early — thanks to Alexstrasza helping Ysera purify the Nightmare's corruption?

If so, that was good. If Mossa, Grede, and the other tauren settled here in the future, a pack of fang druids corrupted by the Nightmare hiding nearby would be a destabilizing factor. After dealing with Onyxia, if they went to Ashenvale to meet with the night elf leader, Priestess Tyrande, maybe they could stop by Wailing Caverns on the way and see what the situation was.

With a green dragon by their side, Mossa as a local, several adventurers who'd already taken a trip through the Emerald Dream, and even a black dragon... why worry about the dangers in that cave?

Better to worry about tripping and breaking a leg falling into one of those pits.

Even if they didn't go in, they should at least ask Priestess Tyrande about where Naralex was. If he hadn't fallen into the Nightmare, or if the Nightmare's corruption had been cleansed and he'd been saved, he should still be in contact with other druids. That would confirm the plains were safe for now.

"You never need to worry about running out of powerful enemies around here, Vereesa," Mossa said. "Because not long after Skum vanished, another thunder lizard overlord took his place. I think the common tongue would call it... 'Blue Thunder' — Owathanka."

"Blue Thunder?" Sora said. "I thought thunder lizards were all red."

"Blue is a rare variant, and much stronger than red," Mossa said. "Last time we traveled the plains, we ran into an old one. Remember?"

"Didn't seem that tough," Grede snorted.

"'Didn't seem that tough'? You mean the fight where we barely held it together and nearly capsized?" Jace said. "And that was with Morofel."

"Can't underestimate these beasts. And the crystal-blue Owathanka is the champion among them." Mossa said. "A hunter spotted it near an oasis to the east last time. We should reach it soon. It's said Owathanka was blessed by the Wind Prince himself, with strength and speed no mortal can match. The thunder it unleashes can destroy half an oasis."

"Wait, Wind Prince?" Grede raised a finger. "You mean Thunderaan?"

"No, I think it's a different prince," Mossa said. "Thunderaan the Windseeker was defeated by the Fire Lord long ago. I imagine even Aerie Peak, which worships the sky, would have heard that news through the winds. For years, some Kolkar stormcallers have tried to use rituals to revive him, hoping his terrible power would destroy our tribe, or even our entire race. Most tauren don't really believe the Kolkar can actually earn Thunderfury's blessing, but the wise old priests in the tribe have always been worried."

Grede, Vereesa, and Jace exchanged glances. Grede waved a hand. "Well, you don't have to worry about that anymore."

"What..." Mossa seemed to realize something. "Ah, you mentioned Blackrock Mountain and Shadowforge City last night... The Fire Lord's been driven back to the elemental plane of fire by the Three Hammers. Does that mean Thunderaan's soul has been utterly destroyed, never to return?"

"Quite the opposite." Jace smiled. "He successfully revived — just a few days ago. And we were the ones who brought him back."

"We found the cage that held his soul from the Fire Lord and brought him here to be reborn," Vereesa said.

"What? No, no." Even in her lion form, Mossa's face showed clear horror. "Don't joke like that, Jace. The stories about Thunderaan ravaging the land and showing no regard for life aren't as rare as those about the Windlord hiding behind the scenes."

"He's got no choice but to hide behind the scenes now. He's completely broken with his father." Vereesa said. "No matter how little he cares for mortals, at least with us involved, he won't help the centaur against you. You can rest easy about that."

"How can you be so sure?" Mossa asked.

"His sword is dangling under Sora's belly right now," Grede said, pointing down.

"That's my stomach, you idiot dwarf!" Sora craned her neck and roared.

"Fine, fine, stomach, stomach." Grede muttered, "What's the difference anyway..."

"That thing wrapped in animal hide?"

Mossa lifted the rope up with her claws and opened the wrapping a crack.

Seeing the bright blue, faintly shimmering surface of the strange metal inside, she couldn't help tapping it with a claw — then immediately yanked back.

"Grede's not joking," Jace said. "This sword was Thunderaan's. He left it for us as thanks. He said if we need his help, we can use this blade to summon him. We haven't tried it yet — it's only been a few days."

Mossa couldn't help muttering something in Tauren. Jace could feel her claws and lips trembling, her mane-covered tail swishing constantly.

"Can we check with him?" Jace asked the others.

"Can we really do that?" Mossa immediately perked up.

"After we find that Owathanka and eat our fill, we'll find a spot and try," Grede said. "What's the harm?"
