Chapter 1727: Wind of the Plains

"Ha!" Grede clapped his hands, spun in place, then crouched, kicked, turned, and straightened up, swinging his arms — a picture-perfect dancer in action. The flames flickering under the nearby roasting rack seemed to move in time with his steps.

Mossa watched her love with a gentle smile, clapping along to the beat, unable to resist shifting her hooves and swaying with him.

Watching the dwarf's powerful, rhythmic dance — even without music, it was far from dull — Jace understood more and more why so many girls from every race, north to south, from humans to dwarves to elves, had fallen for this guy. The fact that two-thirds of his face was hidden behind a thick beard and the rest looked like it had been carved from granite wasn't an obstacle at all. In fact, by a lot of people's standards, that might be a selling point.

With a final spin, Grede stopped abruptly, spread his arms, and even threw in a bow. Who knew how many innocent maidens he'd performed this routine for around campfires before?

"Your turn, Jace." Grede caught his breath, then seemed to remember something. "Oh right. You don't know how to dance."

"Good luck fitting into Quel'Thalas high society," he added, jerking a thumb at Vereesa, then plopped down on a stone slab.

"It's fine. I can't dance either," Vereesa said.

"No wonder you two ended up together," Grede said. He looked at Mossa. "What about you? I heard in Booty Bay that tauren love singing and dancing... Or is that just a goblin stereotype?"

"It's a stereotype, but it's also true," the tauren said. "It's just that I grew up training more as a druid and hunter. Not like the girls who grew up normally in the tribe."

"None of us are normal here, Mossa," Jace said. "If you were normal, that'd be the weird thing."

Mossa let out a happy little hum, a sound that really did resemble a calf nuzzling its mother.

She'd taken off her hat and heavy leather cloak, shed the dignity and seriousness of a tauren leader, and for a moment Jace remembered the tauren he'd first met in Thousand Needles years ago.

And it wasn't just her who'd returned to that old self. He had too. Grede and Vereesa as well. Meeting a long-lost companion again felt like traveling through time back to the days before they'd parted. Everything that had happened in the intervening years seemed to vanish like smoke, as if it had never existed.

"Speaking of which, are you and Vereesa getting married, Jace?" Mossa asked.

Before Jace could answer, Vereesa sat bolt upright, snapped her fingers, and said, "Yes, now that you mention it, druid — do you want to come to Quel'Thalas for our wedding?"

Mossa lowered her head, as if unsure how to decide. Vereesa frowned. "You're just one person. I can ask some magisters to teleport you over and then send you back to Kalimdor. It won't take much mana or time. You won't be away from your people long... if that's what you're worried about."

"An arcane teleportation ritual," Mossa said. "Wouldn't that be an ordeal? I don't know."

"So what?" Vereesa snorted. "Plenty of people call me a great noble of Quel'Thalas, a spoiled brat from the Windrunner family. I might as well use my 'noble privilege' for something worthwhile now and then."

"Well, in that case..." Mossa smiled and rubbed her fingers together. "I won't say no. I've always wanted to see Quel'Thalas."

"That's great, Mossa," Jace said. "I honestly didn't expect you to come."

"I just hope the Quel'dorei don't treat me like a freak," Mossa murmured.

"If a dwarf can get in, why not a tauren?!" Grede patted Mossa's shoulder. She naturally leaned closer to the dwarf.

Jace felt Vereesa quietly take his hand in the grass beside him.

They'd been together for years now. But on this quiet night, in the grass by the roasting fire, under the White Lady and the Blue Child in the sky — even Jace, as dense as he was, couldn't help feeling his heart pound.

While the two couples were enjoying their romantic moment, a series of bone-cracking sounds came from Owathanka's massive corpse behind them.

Grede looked up and shouted, "Could you two maybe take a break from eating and leave some for Mossa and her people? The tauren on the plains really need the meat. We've got to take some back!"

Sora craned her neck to take a look, nodded vaguely, and went back to eating. Needleflame didn't even acknowledge him. She just wagged her tail, her head buried deep in Owathanka's belly, body wiggling as she kept munching.

On one hand, young dragons were growing. Needleflame was still small, and Sora was just one step away from adulthood.

And for most eaters, Owathanka's meat was tougher and gamier than the younger thunder lizard's, with a weird sour taste — not exactly good eating. But for Needleflame, the strong enemy she'd killed in single combat tasted hundreds of times better than any ordinary prey.

"Owathanka's bones, spikes, scales, and thick hide can all be used to make weapons and armor," Mossa said, touching the bear-skull on her cloak. "Needleflame is the one who hunted this beast. She has first claim. Besides, Owathanka's body is enormous. It won't take much to fill a lot of tauren bellies. When we go back tomorrow morning, I'll call the tribe to come take the rest."

"Make sure you cook that meat thoroughly," Grede whispered. "Stay away from the parts the black dragon gnawed on. Black dragon saliva is poisonous!"

A squelching sound came from behind. The dwarf warily looked back. Needleflame had pulled her head out of Owathanka's carcass and was staring right at him.

Before Grede could say a word, Needleflame blew a puff of ash right in the dwarf's face, shouting, "Poison you!"

"You little lunatic!" Grede coughed, slapping the ash off himself, nearly stepping into the campfire. Mossa and Vereesa were both doubled over laughing.

"Ah, I'm stuffed." Jace lay back on the grass, holding his stomach. "Should we try contacting Thunderaan through the blade? Ask him about the centaur?"

The dwarf wiped the ash from his hair. "Here?"

Hearing they were going to summon Thunderaan, Mossa's expression turned serious too.

"Let's give it a shot," Jace said. "You're the most familiar with communicating with wind elementals. You do it."

Grede nodded and pulled the blade out of its wrappings. Even as a fragment of shattered Arcanite metal, its edge was still razor sharp. If he wasn't careful — even with Grede's sturdy build — one wrong move and he'd get cut. He moved as carefully as if he were transporting a fragile masterwork painting.

He found an open space, crouched down, and touched the smooth surface of the Wind-Kissed Blade. He muttered something in elemental language. The wind began to pick up across the plains. The campfire swayed. Even the scales on Owathanka's corpse started flickering with electricity, as if it had somehow come back to life. By now, even the two dragons had noticed something strange.

But though portents of wind and lightning came one after another, Jace didn't hear any clear elemental response. Forget a powerful elemental lord descending from the sky.

"Is Thunderaan messing with us?" Vereesa asked.

"I... I don't know," Grede said. "But the sages always commune with the sky from the highest peak of Aerie Peak. Maybe we should try somewhere with strong, concentrated wind? This place is too calm. The wind here is all stirred up by the blade's power — savage and disobedient. Fine for wrecking a few houses, but not much else."

Mossa pointed at a tall mound beside them. "There are altars on top of these hills, left by ancient tauren priests for worshiping the sky and wind gods. Want to climb up and try?"

"Let's go up and try," Jace said.
