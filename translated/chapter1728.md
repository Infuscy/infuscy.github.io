## Chapter 1728: Wind's Aid

The two dragons were resting below, full and content. One had exhausted herself carrying everyone and all their gear; the other had just fought two thunder lizards back to back. None of them had the heart to ask the dragons to carry them up again.

So Mossa simply led Jace, Grede, and Vereesa on foot up the slope along the ridge. It wasn't like this hill was an Alterac mountain peak.

But the hills in the Barrens looked small from a distance — just little mounds. Up close, though, climbing them was quite strenuous. Jace felt that part of the reason Mossa hadn't asked Sora to carry them up was to let her rest. Another reason might be that these hilltops weren't just temples, but also places where the tauren who once lived here bid farewell to their dead.

Along the way, he could even see small stone or wooden totems planted on both sides of the ridge path, identical to ones he'd seen climbing up to that tauren funeral in Sun Rock Retreat.

No wonder Mossa's steps were heavy and her expression solemn as they approached this place.

At the top, looking up at the starry sky felt like being sealed under a dark blue dome. The two moons stared down like a pair of eyes. Standing there gave Jace an incredibly surreal feeling — as if he were the only person left in the world.

On a raised platform ahead stood a tauren shelter. It looked uninhabited, but unlike the houses at Dustwind Mesa, it hadn't suffered much weather damage. It seemed mostly intact — even immediately livable — with some very basic daily tools inside.

Beside the house, at the foot of the steps, sat a stone altar. Jace saw runes he didn't quite recognize. Grede looked at them and said, "They look a lot like the ones on the sages' runestones."

The wind was strong up here. Not like being helplessly tossed in raging waves, as in the Thousand Needles, but more like the wind embraced you, wrapping around this pyramid-like hill. Even before Grede pulled out the Wind-Kissed Blade, Jace felt he could almost hear the wind elementals whispering.

"Can we start now?" Grede asked Mossa.

"Whenever you're ready, Grede." Mossa nodded.

Grede took a deep breath and placed the blade on the stone. Vereesa asked, "What if nothing happens?"

"Then I'll take this blade back, have the Thorium Brotherhood melt it down, and make a little hammer exactly like the Sulfuron Hammer," Grede said grumpily.

"Uh, I'll just assume that's an Aerie Peak style of insult that outsiders can't understand," Vereesa said, hands on hips.

"As long as that Wind Prince understands!"

Grede put his hand on the blade's surface again, closed his eyes, and murmured in elemental. His elemental wasn't complicated for anyone who could understand it — basically "Thunderaan, if you can hear this, speak up, I need to talk to you." But such a simple, direct call was enough to whip up an even fiercer whirlwind on the hilltop.

Soon, sand and stones flew. The old shelter's hide covering shuddered constantly. Jace worried the wind might blow the whole structure away. But the wind grew stronger, and though the hut shook more violently, it stood firm.

This was a wind temple, after all. The ancient tauren priests who prayed to the wind here must have built it to withstand the elements' ravages.

Mossa looked up at the sky, fear evident in her eyes. To the tauren of the plains, Prince Thunderaan was a figure of myth. But in reality, aside from Jace's group and the most powerful beings in existence, few could remain calm in his presence.

Dark clouds rolled across the Barrens' prairie, thunder streaking through them. Grede murmured, "He definitely heard..."

"You'd better not have woken me just to test whether my promise holds!"

A series of thunderclaps came from the sky. The voice was very different from Thunderaan's own, more like the sound from inside the Cranial Containment. Jace guessed this was because his actual body hadn't arrived — he was just transmitting through the storm clouds.

Though his response was full of anger, he was speaking Common. From Mossa's shocked expression, even she hadn't expected that. Who would think a powerful elemental lord summoned by ritual would bother speaking the summoner's language?

"We're not just messing around, Prince Thunderaan," Jace called out. "We wanted to ask — have you heard the prayers of the Kolkar centaurs on these plains?"

"Hmm. Long ago, I knew of their reverence for me." Thunderaan asked. "What do they have to do with you, Jace Sesso? Have those Kolkar priests blocked your path?"

"They haven't blocked our path," Grede said. "But they're about to block the tauren's path to survival! These centaurs have occupied Mulgore. Now they want to drive the tauren off the plains, out of these stone forests entirely. Will they force all tauren to live alongside black dragons? Or flee south to follow your father?"

"I beg you, Wind Prince..."

Mossa started to speak, but Grede shouted and stopped her. "Don't beg him! You said if we ran into trouble, you'd help. Well, my beautiful, strong fiancee has lost her entire family, and her people are about to be wiped out. If that's not trouble, what is?!"

Hearing Grede describe her that way, Mossa actually looked embarrassed.

"Help kill all the Kolkar, Thunderaan!" Grede shouted into the gale. "At least tear down their villages! Drive them back where they came from! Back to whatever... Desolace! And don't forget — that was tauren territory first. And who turned it into what it is now? Your distant cousin!"

"Cousin?" Thunderaan let out a laugh. "I have no relation to Therazane. I certainly don't have such foolish, ugly relatives. Perhaps you're closer to her than I am — you lump of mud filled with rat blood..."

"What did you—" Grede gritted his teeth.

Now they were trading insults. Jace sighed. Vereesa silently covered one eye with her hand.

"So you can't help us, Lord Thunderaan?" Jace asked.

"I cannot unleash my power wantonly on this land, causing mass destruction and slaughter. That might break the balance between elements," Thunderaan explained. "It would create serious conflict between me and other elemental lords who have no quarrel with me. The age of elemental wars is long past. And I am no longer who I once was."

"He has a point, Grede," Jace said. "A single problem might be manageable, but the Kolkar centaur presence covers all of Mulgore and most of the plains, plus the stone forests..."

"Think about it. If we cause an elemental disaster on this scale, the whole prairie's climate could change drastically. Even if the tauren defeat the centaurs and reclaim their homeland, Mulgore wouldn't be what it is now. You mentioned Desolace — don't forget, the tauren now call it the 'Desolace.' That's what happens when you stir up elemental chaos."

That left Grede at a loss for words. Mossa looked disappointed too. Grede had studied with the sages of Aerie Peak — he understood perfectly what Jace meant.

"But since this is your first time seeking my help, I won't stand by entirely, young tauren." Thunderaan's voice continued. "I can command the winds of this land to refuse the Kolkar's summons, and instead respond more readily to the tauren tribes. That way, we don't break the elemental balance of power, but we still help your people reclaim their homeland."

"Can you really do that, Wind Prince?" Mossa said excitedly. "On behalf of the chiefs and priests, I thank you."

It wasn't direct, but if this aid could truly be realized, it would be quite effective. After all, eighty percent of the Kolkar centaurs' magic consisted of wind elemental power cast by various stormcallers. Without wind magic, these nomads' combat effectiveness would be cut in half. With just swords, spears, and bows, how could they match the much stronger tauren?

"Thanks, Thunderaan," Grede said too.

"Don't thank me yet. When I recover my strength, Grede Thunderfist, I'll make you pay for your insolence," Thunderaan said quietly.

"I'll be waiting. Come find me when you're ready." Grede raised his hand, pointing skyward. "Let's see whose 'cousin' that Maraudon princess really is."

"I hope you live long enough to see that day, dwarf."

Thunderaan's voice grew fainter, then vanished with a violent explosion of lightning and thunder. The sky quickly returned to a dark, quiet stillness, though thick clouds remained.

"Chief Cairne Bloodhoof will be thrilled to hear this news." Mossa picked Grede up. "Thank you... Grede. Thank you all. I really don't know how to repay you."

"Repay?" Jace sat on a nearby stone. "You're part of this team. Yet you talk as if you have nothing to do with us. Compared to the costs we've paid and the trouble we've stirred up helping each other before, this is nothing."

"I... I understand." The tauren wiped her tears.
