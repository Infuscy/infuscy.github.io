## Chapter 1729: Rumors of the Grimtotem

The birds carried Mossa's words across the Barrens in all directions, calling the tauren hunters to gather and divide the meat and usable parts of the dead thunder lizard. On the great plains, this was the traditional way for tribal hunters who understood how to communicate with nature.

What dispersed wasn't just the site of Owatanka's death, but Mossa's message as well.

The wind on the prairie was about to turn in the tauren's favor. Ever since hearing Thunderaan's promise, Mossa had become like a different person.

If before she'd only been happy because her friends had come to visit, now she'd finally set down many heavy burdens from her heart. She still had considerable confidence in her people's ability to fight.

In the current situation, Thrall might still have a chance to return to the tribe and succeed old Frostwolf chieftain Drek'Thar. But because of the Scourge and other reasons, being forced to flee to Kalimdor and then running into tauren to help them fight the centaur probably wasn't going to happen anymore.

The tauren were isolated. Even if Mossa grew into an excellent druid, she wouldn't be able to train enough druid apprentices quickly enough to turn the war around. Without help from another side, at best she could only slow the tauren's extinction.

Before Grede and even more Wildhammer dwarves could come crashing in to ally with the tauren tribes, the tilting of the elemental scales — even if it couldn't help the tauren counterattack — could at least stop the decades-long shrinking of tauren territory.

In Jace's view, the Kolkar should withdraw from eastern Kalimdor sooner rather than later, and go back to sorting things out with the other centaur tribes in their homeland of Desolace first.

They reached the foot of the mountain. Needleflame and Sora looked like they'd already eaten and drunk their fill — at least Needleflame had. The little one had changed back to human form, lying spread-eagled like a star by the campfire, bathing in moonlight. Despite looking like a young girl, her snores were thunderous enough that she really did seem like the kind of drinking buddy who could go shot-for-shot with Grede.

Jace sometimes thought she'd fit better as a Dark Iron dwarf woman — even the skin tone was closer. The image of a mischievous little farmer's daughter from Westfall was a bit of a contrast.

Beside her, Sora was watching over the fire and, of course, keeping the camp safe from wandering beasts or more thunder lizards that might emerge from their surroundings. When she saw them approaching, she waved.

"How did it go?"

"Not well," Grede said. "That guy made such a big show, so intimidating, and then he wouldn't help us deal with the centaur. Some excuse about elemental chaos. I think he just doesn't have the capability anymore. Wouldn't even duel me one-on-one — just said something about waiting until his strength returns. And all those sky-worshippers treat him like a champion of the Skywall... Well, at least he's willing to help a little, making it so those centaur can't use wind elemental magic. So it wasn't a total waste!"

Sora shook her head. "For an elemental lord, that's already an enormous concession. Elemental lords value their followers greatly."

"What's there to value about a bunch of dim-witted centaur? The tauren are ancient and wise..." Grede said with utter seriousness, not at all like the old days when he'd called tauren a bunch of super-ogres.

"Are you going back to Ashenvale after this, Mossa?" Jace asked.

"Yes," Mossa replied. "I still need to continue my druidic training."

"Because I have to participate in my people's struggle and can't sleep in the Dream for too long, naturally contacting the Dream's power like other Kaldorei, I need to study even harder. That's necessary to become a qualified druid."

"After we leave the marsh, we plan to go to Astranaar," Jace said. "We heard earlier that High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind wanted to meet with me and Vereesa. It's been a few years and we haven't made it happen. Hopefully she hasn't lost patience."

"Tyrande Whisperwind has lived for over ten thousand years, Jace," Vereesa said. "How could she get angry over such a short time? A few years is probably just a blink of an eye to her."

"Let's hope so," Jace said. "Are you coming with us, Mossa? With all this thunder lizard meat as supplies and the wind element's help, I think you can afford a week or two off. Besides, with Sora and Needleflame carrying us, the trip will be quick."

"I'd love to." The druid smiled, thought for a moment, then asked, "Are you really planning to go looking for the Black Dragon Princess?"

Hearing the words "Black Dragon Princess," Needleflame, who'd been snoring, suddenly went quiet, though she kept her eyes half-closed, maintaining the pose of being fast asleep.

"Of course we are, Mossa," Grede said. "It wasn't my original goal, but the humans think Nefarian has been defeated, and the only remaining threat is his sister. Jace's parents live in Stormwind now. Last time, Onyxia was driven out of Stormwind — beaten badly. She'll definitely want to fly back for revenge one day. We need to put an end to that notion."

"That's right." Mossa hung her head. "But the way you've been talking, it always felt like a joke. What I mean is, if you're planning to go deep into the marsh to find the Black Dragon's lair, could I come too?"

"Last time in Winterspring, you saved me from those Highborne ghosts, but I couldn't follow you to Frostwhisper Gorge to fight those demons and black knights. I've always felt bad about that. This time, facing the Dragon Mother Onyxia, I have to stand by your side, no matter what. And don't think I'll hold you back. I can at least protect myself and do everything I can to help you fight those black dragons and the lair's guardians."

"Holding us back, Mossa?" Vereesa said. "That time in the depths of Eldre'Thalas against the demon Dasmola, if it weren't for you, we'd all have been killed by her magic. Even Morofell the demon was almost helpless."

Jace shot a glance at the elf. Was she actually saying something nice about Morofell? Though it was hard to tell if that counted as a compliment.

"We'd love to have you along, Mossa," Jace said.

Mossa nodded seriously. Although she was the largest of the group (not counting the two dragons in their true forms), you could feel she was still afraid of facing Onyxia. Unlike Jace and his crew, who'd been through countless disasters and faced all kinds of nightmarish opponents.

But then again, facing nightmarish opponents was practically a mandatory initiation into Jace's group. A rite of passage.

"I'm not just saying that I can help," Mossa said. "Since you've been to the goblins before, you should know... finding a black dragon's hidden lair is extremely difficult."

Jace opened the map the goblins had given him, looking at the area north of the Shimmering Flats where the mountains and forests bordering the marsh were practically scribbled in. "Yeah, those goblins haven't found the exact location after all this time, and they're offering a big reward for anyone who does."

"Those goblin zeppelins can't survive more than a few breaths against a black dragon. How can they compare to us?" Grede said.

"But we should still minimize conflict with the black whelps as much as possible," Sora said. "If, as Jace guessed, Lady Onyxia has already killed the rebels in the lair who wanted to pledge loyalty to Prince Nefarian, then the ones left are absolutely loyal to her — her followers and offspring. If we have to harm them, there will be less room for rational conversation with Lady Onyxia. Don't forget, she's an evil leader, but she's also a mother."

"True." Jace looked toward the tauren. "Do you know the exact location of their lair, Mossa?"

"I don't," Mossa said. "But the Grimtotem should."

"There's actually a theory. We haven't confirmed it yet, but it might be true. That the Grimtotem members went to the marsh because they've pinned their hopes of defeating the centaur and ruling all tauren tribes on that Black Dragon Princess."

"Wait, let me get this straight." Grede raised a finger. "The Grimtotem want to kill the black dragons, skin them and use their bones to make weapons and equipment, and then start a war?"

"Sounds like the opposite," Vereesa said. "If I understand correctly, it sounds like the Grimtotem are going to ally with Onyxia."

"We didn't believe it at first either," Mossa said. "As far as I know, while there were always some voices within the Grimtotem worshiping black dragons, it was never mainstream... It's like they suddenly changed direction after getting a response from the black dragons. We have a friend inside the Grimtotem — an, uh..."

"An inside agent," Jace said.

"Yes, an inside agent," Mossa said. "I want to verify this rumor with her. If it's true... she should be able to lead us to where the black dragons are hiding."

"I don't think so," Grede said. "I think they just want to sneak in and pick at the scraps while the dragons are weak, like the centaur. That arrogant, self-important female monster like Onyxia — how could she possibly value them?"

"To protect the dragon eggs," Vereesa said. "Nefarian nearly killed her. She was already injured. The lair is full of traitors who want to serve Nefarian. The black dragons are at their most vulnerable. She needs any ally she can find to help guard the lair, like the orc tribe at Blackrock Spire."

Grede stared at the elf for a moment, then finally nodded. "That makes sense. So does that mean she's at least still alive?"

"In a way, that's good news," Jace said.
