Chapter 173: The Deathless

Teron Gorefiend was here — tonight?

Jace looked toward Kel'Thuzad and could detect a hint of unease on his face, though it was being suppressed by his formidable mental fortitude.

To someone unaware of the full picture, his emotions were too calm. But to someone who knew the whole story, his emotions weren't calm enough.

Jace could only surmise that Kel'Thuzad probably knew this was going to happen, but hadn't been able to pin down the exact timing.

Still, he was in no mood to investigate whether Kel'Thuzad had any connection to Gorefiend — or even to Deathwing.

"Everyone!" Kel'Thuzad's powerful, ringing voice overwhelmed all the arguments in the room. He swept his gaze across the entire hall and said, "Please remain calm here. Dalaran's defenses are impregnable. If the black dragons couldn't break through our defenses during the Orc Wars, then they won't get a second chance after the war has ended! For your own safety, please take shelter inside this building for the time being. Once the situation is under control, I will send someone — or come myself — to escort you out!"

"If Dalaran is under attack, then Quel'Thalas will not stand idly by." Aethas stood up as he said this, then issued a few commands in Thalassian, after which he strode out of the hall with several elves.

Kel'Thuzad's gaze followed them closely as they left, then he turned, walked down from the head platform, and left the hall alongside the apprentice who had run in.

Jace sat beside Dushan, exchanging bewildered looks with all the scholars, but he kept feeling like he'd forgotten something.

Those few minutes of agony felt like years. It was only when he saw the rainwater continuously pouring down from the skylight above that he remembered — Grede was still watching someone's shop!

If a death knight showed up, would Grede charge in and fight him to the death?

For a salary of ten silver a day, that seemed excessive…

But did Grede care about money? Wasn't that guy the type to throw his life on the line the moment he saw someone being bullied?

The more Jace thought about it, the less he knew whether he really understood him or not.

The more he considered it, the more he couldn't sit still in this hall. He had to find Grede before the death knights entered Dalaran, to keep this guy from getting into trouble. If he was going to leave, it had to be now.

"Master Dushan, where's Horn Tree Alley?"

"What do you want to go to Horn Tree Alley for? Stay here and wait for news from the Kirin Tor!" Dushan was trembling as he spoke — clearly already frightened into a panic by the situation.

"Tell me, Master Dushan!" Jace asked urgently once more.

Dushan hesitated a moment, then said, "It's… it's on that road of ours, just a bit further south — that spiraling alley, not more than a few hundred meters away."

"Much obliged."

Jace ran down the steps, charged out the conference hall door under the watchful eyes of several scholars, grabbed his staff, and came outside. The rain was already pouring down hard, torrents flowing through the streets like little streams.

Squads of Kirin Tor spellguard with purple shields were marching down the streets heading south. These were the men who should be going to the Violet Citadel to protect the Eye of the Arcane — not getting drawn to the southeast to fight the black dragons!

But looking toward the southeastern sky, the horizon had already burned red, and distorted, terrifying shrieks rolled in unceasingly.

In this situation, who would think they should funnel the bulk of their forces into the deepest part of Dalaran, the Violet Citadel, instead of sending troops south to reinforce the outer defenses?

"Apprentice, leave the streets and stop loitering — go home or back to your dormitory! Dalaran is under attack right now!"

A robed guard shouted in this direction. Jace listened and moved off to the side a bit, avoiding his gaze.

"Spiraling alley… south…"

He muttered to himself as he ran along the road from the city center, diverging from the direction the spellguard were running. On the road were many mages, running while exclaiming in shock at the blood-red sky to the south.

"Is it the orcs? Have they returned?"

"It must be Orgrim Doomhammer!"

"Will they break into the city?"

"Nonsense — Dalaran cannot fall…"

"Please leave the streets! Return to the buildings!"

Jace passed by these mages and guards. Above the sky, the peak of the Violet Citadel occasionally flashed with lightning-like radiance. After each flash, the light rapidly rolled outward toward the southern city gate. Every time its flash illuminated the sky, Jace could make out vague shadows passing through the clouds.

Was this the power of the Eye of the Arcane?

At the very least, it was still there — the death knights hadn't made their move yet.

As for those shadows… were those black dragons circling overhead?

Dalaran's arcane defenses had already taken effect. Whatever was circling outside couldn't break through into here.

So how would Teron Gorefiend get in?

To cut across faster, Jace dashed into a circular corridor near the central plaza. The sound of wind, the sound of rain, and the whistle-like roars became hazy and indistinct, while his own pattering wet footsteps were the only sound left.

Just as he was about to run out of Dalaran's arcane district ahead, he heard the "clank, clank, clank" of someone else's footsteps coming from a corridor junction up ahead. This person's footsteps weren't urgent at all — it was like they were out for a stroll. This absolutely could not be a mage rushing to reinforce the front lines or fleeing in terror!

Jace immediately turned and ran. The moment his footsteps quickened, that person's footsteps also sped up.

He reached a garden and vaulted over the railing from the open colonnade.

This place, normally a lively spot packed with couples and groups of friends, was completely empty now.

At that moment, his peripheral vision caught sight of a robed guard lying not far off below a railing, face already turning purple-black, eyes glazed.

Whether that man was dead or alive, Jace had no time to care. He plunged through the rain and sprinted across the garden toward the corridor on the opposite side.

Just as he entered another corridor, he turned and glanced back, and saw a tall, cloaked human man walk out of the corridor exit.

He was at least two meters tall and terrifyingly muscular, his body slightly hunched as he walked. When he looked in this direction, a strange light — hard to say whether it was purple or a dark yellow — flickered from within his hood.

That single instant of eye contact made Jace's whole body crawl.

He was holding a black short blade or a long dagger. This probably wasn't Teron Gorefiend himself, but it was unquestionably a death knight.

The death knights crafted by Gul'dan were far from being as skilled at warfare as the later Scourge death knights — they were merely necromancer souls occupying the bodies of human warriors, so they didn't always seek out heavy, powerful weapons.

The death knights must have infiltrated Dalaran before the black dragon assault began. Once the black dragons launched their attack, they appeared inside Dalaran to draw the attention of anyone still in the city and assist him in stealing the Eye of the Arcane…

Jace cursed under his breath. Realizing this now was genuinely too late.

The sound of metal boots striking the flagstones behind him grew more and more urgent. He couldn't hesitate any longer and summoned every ounce of his strength to sprint in the opposite direction.

Suddenly, the footsteps vanished. Jace stopped in the corridor and looked back and forth, confirming that the death knight hadn't used some strange magic to close in on him, and only then relaxed slightly.

But where had the other one gone?

Perhaps he was chasing other mages. After all, the mission of these death knights was probably to cause chaos, not to single-mindedly hunt down any one person.

Having settled on that, Jace continued running south. The exit wasn't far now.

A dark shadow suddenly flashed out, blocking the exit right in front of him. Jace watched as that sickly, blackened, rigid human face slowly curled the corners of its mouth, its eyes glowing with a nauseating necromantic light staring at himself like a cat's eyes in the dark.

"Fulk't!"

Both of them spoke the same incantation at the exact same moment. Jace instantly dropped to one knee, feeling a fierce fear seize his heart. A burst of agony stabbed through his chest, and in that single moment he thought of his teachers, thought of Grede, thought of Hennie and Vik, and even scenes buried deepest in his imagination — visions of them dying in agony — surfaced like river-bottom mud being churned up to the surface…

Jace roared and punched the wall — bang! The sharp pain in his knuckles snapped him back to his senses at once!

All of this was false, nothing more than the negative emotions brought by shadow magic. Shadow could not control him in turn — not his own, and not anyone else's!

The death knight was also being tormented badly by the Fear spell. He leaned against the wall, lowering his head and muttering a curse in Orcish, but soon forced himself to straighten up and raise his head. The long dagger in his hand was already burning with violet flames.

"Surrender your resistance, join us…"

The death knight advanced with large strides, lips constantly moving, the flames on his dagger growing more and more ferocious. He raised his right hand, about to stab down violently. Jace stood up, pointed his staff at the death knight, and loudly spoke the incantation for Curse of Weakness!

Supported by the staff, the spell took effect extremely fast. The death knight's legs gave way beneath him and he dropped to one knee on the ground. The flames on his dagger were extinguished in an instant, and even his rigid face managed to show an expression of shock that a person could comprehend.

"My lord's…"

"Surprised?" Jace said in Orcish. He stepped forward, snatched the dagger, then held the hilt with his left hand and pushed, driving it straight into the death knight's blackened face! The other man bellowed and raised his hand to block the thrust. Flesh was pierced, but the finger bones caught the dagger.

The death knight exerted force — it was plain to see that under the influence of Curse of Weakness, he was summoning every last scrap of strength he could muster, desperately gripping the hand that had been skewered, clutching Jace's dagger.

At that moment, his other hand suddenly grabbed Jace's shoulder, going for his neck!

"Go to hell!"

Jace ignored that hand and swung his right palm down heavily onto the dagger hilt once more. This time he drove the dagger, along with the man's hand, straight into the hood!

The death knight let out a piercing shriek and his whole body began to tremble. Violet flames erupted from beneath his hood and cloak, engulfing the dagger and the pierced hand. A wave of foul stench hit Jace full in the face.

Jace tried to step back, but the death knight's hand had a tight grip on his sleeve. Seeing the flames about to travel up the man's desiccated arm and spread over to this side, he could only wrench himself free of his coat.

He staggered back a few steps, watching helplessly as the corpse collapsed in the flames, turning into a puddle of soft, putrid sludge that crumbled onto the red bricks. The flames, screaming, completely incinerated Jace's robe, and the remaining wisps and flashes scattered in all directions like startled ghosts until they vanished.

Was he finished? At the very least, the robe was finished — twelve silver down the drain.

Before Jace could even register his astonishment at having actually killed a death knight, he felt the hairs on the back of his neck suddenly stand on end.

He felt someone watching him, but when he looked up and down the corridor, there was nothing — no living thing or dead thing in sight.

A faint whisper sounded from behind him — half threat, half sneering laugh. Jace turned around, but there was nothing there either.

Was this some kind of whisper?

Was it Teron Gorefiend's voice?

Jace looked at the long staff in his hand, thinking of how that death knight had muttered "my lord" — had the general heard his call?
