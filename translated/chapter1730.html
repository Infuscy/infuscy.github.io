Chapter 1730: A Special "Guide"

Back at the Mulgore tauren refuge, Needle Flame and Sora helped stock up the stationed tauren with fresh meat. After Mossa briefed them on things to watch for in the coming days, she set out with Jace's group back across the Barrens toward Dustwallow Marsh.

Last time, they'd taken a zeppelin into the marsh. After enduring a violent storm, before they could even rest, the ground below had turned damp and dark, the air filled with a stench.

This time, approaching from the sunny plains — though the boundary between marsh and grassland wasn't as clear as in the game — they could visibly see the trees and grass turning yellow and black. Crisscrossing streams grew more numerous, and the forest coverage expanded.

Before they knew it, they were deep in the marsh. Jace instinctively grew alert. The landscape wasn't the only thing changing — the emotional shifts in his perception were drastic. Compared to the peaceful plains, the entire marsh forest radiated an inexplicable tension and anxiety.

This time, Sora had to carry Mossa and the extra Wind-Kissed Blade — a heavier burden. Even if Mossa turned into a big cat, she wasn't much heavier than a person, but she was still extra weight. So more of the luggage was distributed to Needle Flame.

Needle Flame hadn't refused as adamantly as before, but she was fuming silently the whole way. Grede kept worrying she'd dump everyone's luggage into the marsh out of spite, constantly eyeing the bags hanging from the black dragon's claws.

Jace even noticed Needle Flame flying more steadily now — she'd gotten used to carrying him. He'd half expected her to renege on her promise to carry the extra luggage and give him a hard time. Her being this well-behaved was almost disappointing.

Before entering the marsh, Jace hadn't thought Mossa and the tauren could help much in finding Onyxia's lair. In the game, Onyxia lived in the center of a charred plain, with a mountain fortress-like hill and the entrance to the Dragon Mother's lair shaped like an evil dragon-head gate — extremely conspicuous.

Even without the glaring green glow of the dungeon entrance marking the spot in the game, Jace could have located the terrifying mother beast and her brood through shadow perception.

But actually flying over southern Dustwallow Marsh, he realized the problem. Looking further south, there were "fortress-like" bare rock mountains everywhere. Each one looked like it could house a giant black dragon. Add in the endless dark forests, ponds, and streams, everything was chaotic and looked incredibly dangerous. No wonder even goblin expeditions, despite the bounty, were reluctant to venture deep here.

As for shadow... aside from the chaotic emotions of vigilance, terror, rage, and bloodlust radiating from countless lurking beasts throughout the marsh, Jace couldn't sense much unusual. Mainly because the area was too vast. No matter how keen his perception, he couldn't sense things dozens of kilometers away.

Even Sora said the marsh trees had lost the ability to communicate with her, as if long-term immersion in deep fear had driven them mad. Jace wasn't surprised. Part of why the black dragons hid here was to avoid red and green dragon. How could Onyxia let the trees and beasts become their eyes?

The creatures that survived here were no kinder than the black dragons.

Coming out of Gadgetzan, the goblin map and the narrow corridor of the Thousand Needles had guided them, almost making Jace forget the sheer savagery of this world he'd felt on his first trip to Kalimdor. If before this had been a relaxed journey plus a reunion spanning thousands of miles, now it was time to regain that sense of awe.

The further south they flew, the thicker the fog grew, the visibility worse. The world became eerily quiet. From beneath the faintly visible black treetops, the splash of something falling into water was clearly audible even at this height.

"Should we land here, Mossa?" Vereesa called from Sora's back to the druid beside her. She wasn't shouting, but Jace could hear her clearly up ahead.

He looked at the map again. The two dragons had indeed flown into that scribbled-over mountain pattern on the map. But looking at the map was pointless now.

"Almost," Mossa answered.

Jace patted Needle Flame's shoulder. Needle Flame beat her wings backward, plunging into the yellowish fog below. Her feet landed on exposed rock with a clatter of. Sora landed too, sending more stones sliding down the slope. The noise was so loud Jace felt people dozens of kilometers away could hear them arriving — like they were through this fog.

"It wasn't like this last time," Grede complained, then immediately clamped his hand over his mouth, feeling his voice was too loud. Fortunately, the distant roars of beasts drifting from somewhere soon reassured him.

"Last time we were chased by a bunch of drakonids all the way to the north coast," Vereesa whispered. "This time we've flown this far in without any black dragons bothering us."

"Use this and they'll come." Grede pinched Neltharion's necklace.

"Don't mess around!" Jace stared at him.

"I know, I know..." The dwarf muttered unhappily. "Can't even make a joke! If I hadn't used this last time, we'd all have crashed in the Thousand Needles."

"Carry this first!" Needle Flame shoved a bunch of bags at Jace, then several more at Grede and Vereesa, and stood there with her hands on her hips.

Jace shouldered the bundles and looked toward a patch of yellowish darkness in the distance. "So how do we find that Grimtotem mole, Mossa?"

"Can Grimtotem tauren even survive in a place like this?" Grede muttered. "Wouldn't every step a tauren takes be detected by countless marsh beasts? How would they be hunters? Even evil tauren need to eat, right?"

"Whoo!"

Mossa suddenly let out a strange call — somewhere between an ape and some kind of odd cat.

The sound spread quickly without much echo. They waited in silence with Mossa, who was crouched nearby.

After a while, a faint call came from a distance, similar to Mossa's. Jace couldn't tell how far away it was. Grede whispered impatiently to the druid, "Someone answered you?"

The big cat shook her head, put a furry little paw over the dwarf's lips, lowered it, and stood waiting.

After another moment, a "hissing" sound came from somewhere unknown. Jace felt the hair on the back of his neck stand up. Vereesa and Grede tensed too. Even Sora clenched her fists when she heard it.

"Is that a wind serpent?" Sora whispered. "That sounds like a wind serpent."

"It is a wind serpent," Mossa said. "Why are you so reactive to wind serpents? Kalimdor... has wind serpents in many places. The deepest part of this marsh has them too. My friend is very good at mimicking wind serpent calls. She heard me... Follow me."

Probably because of that time they almost suffocated to death in a sewage pit the hands of a wind serpent god's avatar, Jace thought.

He wanted to explain, but swallowed his words. The other three were much the same — they exchanged glances, none eager to revisit that nauseating memory.

Mossa led them down the slope, through a gully, across a stream, toward the wind serpent sound.

After about ten minutes, Vereesa seemed to sense something. She squinted, peering into the foggy forest ahead. Soon Mossa stopped too. Then, on a rise across a ditch, a small tree moved — it was actually a thin female tauren standing there.

This woman was much thinner than Mossa, but half a head taller. Her fur was jet black, her face and body hair painted with various runes in blood or blood-colored pigment. Her eyes were cold and proud — she looked tough.

More importantly, even this close, Jace could barely sense her emotions.

Which meant either her emotional control was absolute, or she'd studied shadow magic — and deeply, too. Probably at least as skilled as Ursula Delin.

The tauren greeted Mossa in their language, exchanged a few simple remarks, while her eyes swept over Jace's group. She looked more skeptical than hostile.

Mossa responded calmly. But from the limited tauren Jace had learned from Mossa, he could make out this tauren's name: "Nathra Grimtotem."

An actual Grimtotem?

Not just that — she was named Grimtotem, not just a member of the tribe.
