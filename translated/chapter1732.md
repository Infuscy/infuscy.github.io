## Chapter 1732: Village in the Mist

The black female tauren stood silently among the gloomy trees for a long time. The sky was growing darker. In the thick fog, it felt like a shadow pressing down on everyone's hearts, forcing Nathra Grimtotem to make a decision quickly.

"I've also heard about how the black dragons have suffered repeated blows on the other continent, and the legends of you few defeating dragons." Nathra Grimtotem looked toward Jace. "But I know that means my aunt knows the relationship between you and the black dragons even better than I do. There's no way she'd agree to see you."

"We've never met her. We've never even clearly identified ourselves to any tauren other than Mossa and you," Jace said. "So your aunt can't possibly recognize us."

Vereesa added, "At least the goblins mentioned that more mercenaries and wandering warlocks have come to Kalimdor in recent years than in the past few decades combined. We're nothing special."

"Then how do you want me to explain it?" Nathra stared at the human and elf. "Why would I lead a group of outsiders from another continent through the fog and forest to Blackhoof Village — a place with almost no non-tauren faces... even almost no non-Grimtotem people?"

"'Almost,'" Jace said. "Which means your village doesn't completely ban outsiders."

"The Dragon Mother's lair doesn't ban outsiders either, Jace Sesso." Nathra's tone was stern. "But no one who enters, except trusted followers of the Dragon Mother, comes out alive. Even the followers... aren't always safe."

"Then we'll pose as followers and take our chances," Jace said. "Don't worry, Lady Nathra Grimtotem. We won't make your aunt suspicious of you. Here's the story: we're mercenaries working for the black dragons. Messengers, bearing urgent news from the other continent. And I believe no one else is capable of bringing this important news here right now. Your great Dragon Mother — Princess Onyxia — will be very interested."

"What news?" Nathra asked.

"Blackrock Spire has fallen. Nefarian is dead," Jace said. "And we've brought important proof of his death."

"What? So soon?" Nathra asked. "Your army has already retaken Blackrock Spire? Is this true, or something you made up on the spot to see the Dragon Mother?"

"What, you don't believe us?" Needle Flame asked irritably.

Nathra looked at Needle Flame, took a deep breath, and lowered her head, staring at Jace Sesso with wary eyes.

"Don't think I don't know who you are, Jace Sesso. I know even if you tried to hide it, I couldn't detect it. You're a very powerful warlock, yes. But don't think you can easily fool me."

"This Grimtotem has some skill," Grede whispered.

"She's a fairly capable witch," Sora said too.

"How can you tell?" Mossa asked.

"Characteristics of shadow magic, Mossa." Vereesa whispered. "We'll explain later."

"True or false doesn't matter right now." Jace looked at the Grimtotem woman. "What matters is that I can make your aunt believe. As long as she's really met Princess Onyxia and understands the power of the black dragons."

"What about Mossa Woodbough?" Nathra raised her long arm, pointing at the druid behind them. "My aunt doesn't know your faces or clothes. But she knows Mossa Woodbough. She knows Mossa has been in contact with a dwarf thane from another continent."

"If she sees Mossa with you, she'll immediately figure out who the rest of you are, just like I did. Your lie will be exposed instantly, and I'll die along with you. I'm her family, but she won't care about my life in a situation like this."

"Can't she shapeshift?" Grede said. "She can turn into a big cat."

"I can also turn into a vulture or a hawk," Mossa said. "But don't underestimate an elder's knowledge and discernment, Grede. The Grimtotem have many shifters too. I have no confidence in fooling Magatha Grimtotem's eyes."

"That is a problem," Jace said. "What if you stay back when we enter the village, Mossa?"

"Like before?"

Mossa said it dejectedly, but Jace could sense she was already mentally preparing to temporarily leave the group again.

Just then, Grede pulled out a dark grey magic gem from his pouch. "Here, Mossa. I don't know if this thing still works..."

"You haven't returned this to Lord Valthristrasz?" Sora asked.

"What is that?" Nathra narrowed her already-small eyes.

"Never mind!" Grede shoved the magic stone into Mossa's hands. "Try it. There's no Blackrock lava or Shadowforge City heat here... but I haven't used it since we left there."

"I don't understand... what does this thing do?" Mossa asked, holding the gem.

Jace smiled. "You'll know when you use it. What's the incantation again..."

"Val'thalasiel Quin," Vereesa prompted.

Mossa nodded, silently repeated it a few times, then clutched the gem and whispered the words. A rush of red dragon flame enveloped the tauren girl's body, making her gasp in surprise — but that was the last trace of her tauren form.

When the flames faded, a dark iron dwarf woman stood before them.

The "dwarf woman" felt her head, looked at everyone's legs and waists, then jumped in surprise as she found herself at eye level with Grede. She looked up sharply and gasped.

"This shape change feels really wrong..."

"Because even Magatha Grimtotem couldn't see through this transformation," Jace said. "I guarantee it."

"This way, you can enter the village with us as another 'mercenary from the other continent,' Mossa," Sora said.

"Mmm." Mossa nodded happily. Seeing a dark iron dwarf with such a pure smile, Jace felt deeply unsettled.

Mossa was still getting used to her new body. Jace leaned over to the Wildhammer dwarf and whispered, "How does it feel, Grede? Your loved one suddenly turning into your most hated form."

"Very boring." Grede folded his arms.

Vereesa overheard their whispering and shot Grede a meaningful glance. Jace turned to look at her, and their eyes met knowingly.

Who knew what this dwarf went through every night with Mossa to consider dating a dark iron dwarf "boring"?

Across the stream, Nathra hadn't heard their whispers. She let out an unhappy breath. "I hope you know what you're doing. I'm risking my life here, and the risk of being branded a traitor by my tribe."

"Thank you, Nathra." Mossa's voice was now indistinguishable from a dark iron dwarf's. "I know you've done a lot to peace between the tribes."

"You too, Mossa Woodbough." Nathra turned and walked into the forest, her figure quickly growing indistinct. Jace's group immediately followed.

The marsh fog thickened and thinned, but the distance remained unclear no matter what. Even on high ground, it was the same. In his two decades in this world, Jace had rarely felt like he was back in his past life, playing a game with a weak graphics card and limited draw distance.

But Nathra never seemed to doubt her direction. Her aunt knew the way to Onyxia's lair, so she must know how to enter Blackhoof Village too. Jace just hadn't figured out how she navigated.

Vereesa watched the Grimtotem's back, partially obscured by the thin fog, and moved closer to the "dark iron dwarf woman." "I never thought you'd be friends with a real Grimtotem, Mossa. How did you meet?"

"A few years ago, in the ruins south of Eldre'Thalas. I rescued her from a cage guarded by some ogres. When I found out she was Magatha Grimtotem's niece, I admit I regretted it a bit." Mossa said. "But over the following years, she passed me a lot of important information... preventing several conflicts between our tribes and the Grimtotem."

"I haven't forgotten the blood debt. I hope one day those Grimtotem who participated in killing my family die by my hand. But I don't want the centaurs to gain the upper hand because of it."

"These things take time, Mossa." Grede nodded. "Believe me, looking at your black skin and red eyes now, I understand that feeling better than anyone."

"Shh." Jace pressed a finger to his lips, pointing ahead. Nathra Grimtotem had stopped.

Faint points of firelight appeared through the mist ahead.
