Chapter 1733: Matriarch Magatha

The sky over Dustwallow Marsh had gone completely dark. Rain began to fall. The village at the fog's center seemed reduced to only black and white — gloomy and cold.

Several figures of varying heights, wearing soaked dark cloaks, arrived at the wooden wall gate. Nathra approached and exchanged a few words in the tauren language with two guards much larger than her. Both were jet-black tauren in dark green and brown clothing, with blood-red totem tattoos visible on their exposed skin.

After speaking with Nathra, one guard glanced sideways at the group, then turned and entered the village.

Jace and the others waited, getting soaked for a good while. When the guard who'd entered returned, a stir began inside the village. It wasn't obvious externally, but Jace could read the emotions radiating from within — even the cold rain couldn't block his shadow energy perception. Jace guessed Nathra had reported Nefarian's fall at Blackrock Spire from the other continent.

The guard said a few more things to Nathra. She turned back. "You can enter. Matriarch Magatha has agreed to see you."

"Easier than I expected." Grede looked at the dark iron dwarf woman, who was stretching her neck trying to peer through the gate. "From how Mossa described them, I thought Grimtotem were all bloodthirsty monsters — like the tauren I heard about as a kid."

"You've told me way too many versions of the tauren you heard about as a kid. I'm starting to think you just make things up," Vereesa said.

"Wildhammer dwarves are the most honest of all dwarves," Grede said. "We just might have bad memories. Cut me some slack, elf."

Jace smiled and was about to follow Nathra through the gate when a spear suddenly barred his way.

The tall tauren glared at him through his nostrils, then looked at the sword at Jace's waist in its sheath — Azuresong. He spat out two Common words. "Weapons. Leave them."

Vereesa and Grede immediately dropped their easygoing attitudes. Needle Flame frowned, her hand already moving toward the dagger hilt at her lower back.

Jace grabbed the tauren guard's spear shaft, staring into his bovine eyes. The shaft rapidly blackened and rotted. The startled tauren gasped, dropped the spear, and reached for the short axe at his belt — but Jace was already pointing at his forehead.

"If we intended to harm anyone, guard, we wouldn't need weapons." He spoke in Thalassian, which was close to kaldorei.

Nathra stared at the rotted wooden spear shaft, now lying on the ground, dissolving in the rain and merging with the mud.

Mossa too watched intently as shadow energy gathered at Jace's fingertips, as if the tales from the other continent had suddenly found factual basis.

The tauren guard glared at the dark thing floating at Jace's fingertip. Finally, he slowly released his grip and nodded, carefully stepping aside.

If you worked for black dragons, you had to carry yourself with a bit of their style.

This was Blackhoof Village, the one Nathra Grimtotem had mentioned. It was bigger than Dustwind Mesa.

Jace had some memory of this village from the game. It was hard to find, but not this hard. In the game, the Grimtotem here weren't black dragon worshippers — they'd been constantly clashing with Jaina Proudmoore's human port city of Theramore.

He could see the village was expanding. Its original size had been much smaller. Only a few standing remnants of the old wooden wall remained. Beyond them, hide tents were pitched everywhere, with Grimtotem members still felling trees in the rain to expand further. The current scale was already considerable — perhaps housing two to three hundred villagers.

Two to three hundred people didn't sound like much. On a sunny day, standing atop the Wizard Sanctum in Stormwind and looking down at the crowd below, you might see that many heads. But these were tauren — every individual a fighter on par with an ogre. And they were smarter, more adapted to Kalimdor life since they were natives. No wonder Onyxia had sought them out to help guard the lair.

More importantly, they didn't have a strict master — not like the quilboar who worshipped Agamaggan, or the centaurs who worshipped the earth elemental princess Theradras. That made them easier to manipulate.

The reason so many had gathered here was probably that most Grimtotem scattered across southern Kalimdor had answered Magatha Grimtotem's call to serve Onyxia — including those who'd lived at Dustwind Mesa earlier.

Jace seriously doubted whether they'd ever actually seen Onyxia. Her dragon wing had been torn off by Nefarian and grafted onto Chromaggus. One of her arms had been shattered by Jace's Hand of Gul'dan, with the broken bones and sinew either in the Wizard Sanctum tower or — the strongest tendon — on Vereesa's back.

Right now, she was undoubtedly severely crippled. Judging by how indiscriminately Onyxia was recruiting lair guards, she knew her situation was dire.

If the Grimtotem knew their new patron had fallen this far, would they still count on her to help rule Kalimdor?

Walking toward the village center, Jace caught a distinct stench of black dragon blood. It was completely different from Needle Flame's scent — the particular smell of black dragons tainted with corrupted blood.

Was it because the dragon lair was nearby? Or because these tauren had collected too many dead black dragon bodies?

As Jace was identifying the source of the smell and assessing the shadow energy intensity around him, an aged voice came from nearby.

"Welcome, guests from the east."

The Common had a slight accent, like troll language, but was overall quite fluent — at least smoother than Nathra's.

Jace looked toward the source. The speaker was a female tauren wearing a simple leather skirt and a leather headband inlaid with blue stones. Her fur was entirely black, with no totem runes visible on her body at all.

If not for her voice, Jace could barely tell her age. Perhaps the streaks of white hair behind her slightly shorter black horns gave some hint.

"Nathra, thank you for bringing messengers from the other continent to our village. But before bringing them near this wall, you should have found a way to inform me, rather than deciding on your own, my child. That displeases me."

Nathra responded humbly in the tauren language, head bowed to the side. Clearly, this woman was Magatha Grimtotem.

In his past life, Jace hadn't known much about her. But as the leader of a rather radical — even savage — tauren tribe, she looked far too kindly. Both in appearance and dress.

And she stood there alone, without any guards or security measures, like a kindly grandmother watching over the whole village. But Jace knew Magatha was among the most talented shaman in all tauren-kind. The elemental power she could command was not to be underestimated. This was probably her confidence in facing these mercenaries alone.

But Jace was also curious. Azeroth's four elements: water, fire, earth, wind. Fire element's power was in Grede's hands. Wind Prince Thunderaan was on their side. Earth element had a pure-blooded black dragon overseeing it. If it came to a fight, only water element might be under Magatha's command. But considering a significant portion of Kalimdor's water elementals were controlled by the Tide Duke Hydraxis — if he knew his water servants were being used against Jace Sesso...

Setting aside these intangible power dynamics, the fact that Magatha had no idea Thunderaan had just been summoned on the nearby Barrens, despite her connection to the elements, said something.

The elements hadn't told Magatha about Jace's group beforehand. Which meant the local elements, as Jace had suspected, were no longer as inclined to assist this veteran shaman.

"I am the leader of this village, outsider. You should have heard of me from Nathra. I am Magatha. Come, let me have a good look. What kind of brave warriors has the Dragon Mother's offspring sent across a thousand hardships to reach us?"

But as she approached, Jace suddenly caught a strong stench...

The source of that dragon blood smell was her. She must have recently touched that stuff — perhaps even drunk it.

Drinking dragon blood was almost always fatal. Even the greatest heroes and wizards, with the help of potions and power, could hardly avoid harm. And there was almost no benefit.

If a mortal deliberately drank dragon blood, other than being tricked by ancient legends... there was only one possibility — a transformation ritual.

Was Magatha Grimtotem planning to gradually transform into a drakonid under Onyxia's guidance?
