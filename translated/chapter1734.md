## Chapter 1734: A Kind of Greeting

Jace didn't know much about the draconic ascension ritual for dragonkin. He had a vague idea of the concept — back at the lighthouse, he'd heard rumors of warlocks who'd managed to earn the right to ascend, but he'd never watched a mortal complete the full transformation into a dragonkin.

So he had no idea if Magatha Grimtotem standing before him had already drunk the dragon's blood, or if she was just involved in some preparatory rites. If she had drunk it and the ascension had begun, what stage was she at? Had she established a psychic resonance with Onyxia or some other black dragon? Had she fully become a dragon's slave? He was basically flying blind.

But at least externally, she looked like an ordinary black tauren. There was a hint of gray-white pattern on her nose — whether it was natural or age-related, Jace couldn't tell. Either way, he couldn't spot any draconic features. No horns, no cloven hooves, nothing.

He glanced back at Needleflame and Sora behind him. Both of them had their eyes locked on him, clearly sensing something was off too. Vereesa and Grede, who'd dealt with black dragons more times than they could count, were in the same boat. None of them looked particularly relaxed.

But Jace knew they couldn't offer much insight right now either. What Natila Grimtotem had said — that the "cooperation with the black dragons had spiraled beyond control" — was she referring to this aunt of hers, or maybe even more of the tribe, who'd already decided to go from tauren to dragonkin?

Either way, Jace had to be more careful with every word from now on. This meant Magatha might know more about the Black Flight than he'd anticipated.

"What's your name, human?" The Grimtotem matriarch looked down at him.

"Call me Durdekaldon." Jace pushed back his hood, looked up at the rain outside the eaves, and said, "But who I am doesn't matter. What matters is what I've brought."

"My guard Sointay has already told me the news you brought, Mr. Durdekaldon. You say Blackrock Spire has fallen, Prince Nefarian's head hangs from the gates of Stormwind, and the black dragon forces beyond the Great Sea are finished in all but name." Magatha's tone was calm and slow, like she wasn't discussing recent events but some ancient legend from long ago.

"Incredible. As far as I know, even at the height of the War of the Dark Portal, the humans couldn't completely conquer Blackrock Spire or Grim Batol, or wipe out the orc remnants. And now, barely a few months after the Dragonmother met with her brother, things have changed so dramatically."

Jace said, "You should know that Jace Sesso brought the Alliance Expeditionary Force back from the other side of the Dark Portal. Those expeditionary forces defeated the orc clans of Draenor, and after returning, they aimed to finally put an end to the Orc Wars' aftermath."

"Even Prince Nefarian couldn't resist that warlock?" Magatha asked.

"I have no doubt that warlock used some clever tricks. And don't forget, he had help from archmages like Khadgar and a flock of red dragons." Jace paused, then continued calmly, "But that's how it is."

Grede, wearing a heavy coal-gray Shadowforge City ceremonial cloak, couldn't hold back a throat-clearing cough beside him. Jace knew what he wanted to say — probably something about "and the Thunderfist Lord of the Aerie Peaks" too.

But either he knew this wasn't the time to steal the spotlight, or he was well aware that he hadn't actually done much in Blackrock Spire because of his injuries. Either way, he held it in.

The tauren matriarch's expression grew serious. Jace got the feeling she didn't know that Onyxia and Nefarian had completely fallen out. That confirmed his earlier suspicion — Onyxia didn't dare reveal her weakness to these new servants she'd scraped together from all over.

"Who sent you?" Magatha asked.

"Karalan." Jace said, "Report this name to the princess. She'll know."

Karalan Windbrey. If the blue dragon Azuregos hadn't tipped him off about this black dragon agent's name, Jace would have completely forgotten this NPC existed.

This guy had been running secret missions for Nefarian across the Eastern Kingdoms, and hadn't shown up during the battle at Blackrock Spire. No one knew if he was alive or dead — perfect fodder for Jace to use as a cover story.

Before entering Blackhoof Village, Jace had considered using Nyxondra's name instead — Nefarian and Onyxia's sister. But Nyxondra knew them too well, so using her was too risky. Karalan was a minor player with a much lower profile, harder to verify.

Sure enough, Magatha had never heard of this black dragon agent. She thought for a moment, then said, "Sointay also mentioned you brought evidence of Prince Nefarian's death, Mr. Durdekaldon."

"That's the core of this mission, madam."

Jace took out the small box containing Neltharion's Tear and opened it for display.

The moment Magatha Grimtotem caught sight of the black, amber-like object inside, she seemed utterly transfixed. It took her a long time before she remembered to blink.

"I will deliver this to the Nestmother, Mr. Durdekaldon. I believe this will be enough—"

She reached out her thick fingers to take it, but Jace snapped the box shut with a click. "I must hand it to the Dragonmother personally, madam."

At this, the tauren matriarch's heart finally stirred with a ripple of anger, though she didn't show it on the surface. "I have been appointed by the nest's master as the guardian of this lair. I must block any outsider who might encroach upon the nest's territory. You've completed your task. Once I deliver this evidence and this news to the Nestmother, you will receive additional rewards. Wait here quietly."

"My task is to deliver this item into Princess Onyxia's own hands." Jace's voice suddenly turned cold. "Mr. Karalan Windbrey did not tell me I could hand it over to some nest guardian, or a dragonkin, or a princess's messenger. If I want to claim my bounty, I need to ensure the task is completed with absolute, one hundred percent accuracy. There is no room for negotiation."

"Mr. Karalan Windbrey risked his life to seize this treasure from the Alliance. We risked our lives to carry it across the Great Sea, through every hardship, to reach this swamp. Now we're one step away. I will not compromise. I hope you understand."

"I can feel your displeasure, even your anger. I can feel the elements dancing to your fury... But since we took this task, since we dared to risk angering the entire Alliance and the Kirin Tor, it means we have the ability and the confidence to clear any obstacles and complete the mission."

"One piece of advice." He glanced back at his companions. "Don't make yourself and your kin part of those obstacles, Magatha Grimtotem. We all serve the Black Flight — whether temporarily or permanently. There's no need to quarrel over this."

Mossa watched Jace's eyes and uncomfortably adjusted the collar of her robe. Then she noticed it wasn't just Jace — beside him, Vereesa had also shifted, surveying Blackhoof Village like she'd put on a different face entirely. As for the little black dragon, she seemed to already be calculating whether all this beef was better roasted or pan-seared.

The rain masked the matriarch's silence. Not far away, Natila Grimtotem watched quietly, not even noticing that a change in the wind had left her standing in the rain.

"Then I have one last question, mercenary." Magatha said quietly.

"Ask away." Jace said.

"Resul, Horusador, Kurulsus." Magatha suddenly chanted something. It wasn't elemental language, nor demonic tongue, nor any shadow magic incantation.

Based on his past experience, Jace felt it sounded like draconic. But he couldn't understand a word of it.

No surprise there — the draconic he'd learned at the Wizard Sanctum was barely enough for everyday conversation. Draconic had a long history and countless branches of evolution, and Jace had never had a deep opportunity to study it.

Given Magatha's relationship with Onyxia and the dragon blood, it wasn't strange that she'd picked up some mysterious draconic phrases. But this seemed to be a problem — how was he supposed to answer?

In an instant, a flood of guesses and ideas raced through Jace's mind. But they all converged on one thought: *Crap. We're busted.*

"Mishandar, Ilomazus."

Needleflame behind him suddenly muttered this under her breath. Magatha's eyes snapped to the brown-black cloaked girl.

"Tomorrow morning, I will take you to the master's lair. Rest tonight, warriors of the black dragon."

The Grimtotem matriarch turned and walked back into her house. Jace looked back at Needleflame. Even Sora behind her seemed surprised she'd been able to answer that strange question — apparently even the green dragons couldn't understand that draconic phrase.

"What does it mean?" Jace asked quietly.

"I'm not sure how to translate it. It's like a greeting of trust." Needleflame said. "Father sometimes greeted us and other members of the flight that way."

Jace nodded, exchanging glances with the others. Vereesa said, "I'll keep watch tonight. Especially on that matriarch."
