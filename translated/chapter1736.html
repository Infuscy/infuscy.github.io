Chapter 1736: The Dragon's Maw

The rain fell steadily all night long, leaving Blackhoof Village soaking wet. Aside from Grede, none of Jace's group had managed to sleep. They really should have rested well before facing an ancient dragon. But they were in a Grimtotem village — sleep was a luxury. Just sitting quietly for a while was already good enough. Morning came, and the rain hadn't stopped. But the elder Magatha Grimtotem hadn't broken her promise. She stood early at the village's eastern gate, wrapped in a thick, light-brown cloak made from some unknown beast hide. Her niece Natila was behind her. Beside them stood several tall tauren in ceremonial garb, their bodies covered in blood-red tattoos. Jace figured that aside from guards, two or three were probably her nephews or even sons — the decorations on their horns, the headbands, and the finely crafted pauldrons and cloaks set them apart from the ordinary attendants. Following Magatha out of Blackhoof Village into the depths of the white fog, the village's wooden walls, totem poles, and houses were soon swallowed up and disappeared from sight. Magatha's party led the way about a dozen meters ahead. Through the mist, the tauren figures looked gray and hazy. The tauren were quiet throughout the journey. No one made extra noise, no one whispered. Even when Magatha occasionally reminded her followers of something, her voice was too low for those behind to hear. It made Jace and his group feel awkward about talking too. Jace could tell Grede had plenty to say, but under this strange, oppressive silence, he kept swallowing his words. Strictly speaking, there was no real path. They were basically picking their way through the swamp, looking for barely solid ground. After the previous night's rain, the difference between hard ground and mud had become nearly indistinguishable. They could only rely on faint hoofprints in the puddles to follow the route others had taken before. As they went deeper, the swamp occasionally revealed massive skeletons of dragonkin, even black dragons. They were all bigger than Needleflame, about the same size as Sora. Ribs and wing bones jutted haphazardly from the hills and streams. From a distance, in the trees, they were hard to tell apart from the dead branches — especially since the bones were all caked with rotting flesh and mud. When had all these dragons died? In this swamp, Jace had lost his ability to estimate time of death from the corpses. But he knew that in all of southern Kalimdor, no force could come to the Dustwallow Marsh to fight black dragons and leave them looking like this. These dead black dragons had probably been killed by Onyxia herself. Based on the rumors Mossa had told him — after the sibling rift, both sides had executed a purge of "traitors" who wanted to serve the other. But the black dragons killed by Nefarian in Blackrock Spire had at least still had their skin and flesh, just piled up carelessly to rot. Here, the black dragons were nothing but bones. Onyxia must have fled back to this lair after being wounded at Blackrock Spire, then clashed with the black dragons loyal to her brother — and that timeline was even later. So the flesh on these black dragons hadn't rotted away naturally over time. It had been eaten. These scenes made Jace recall what Krasus had told him. Dragons like Onyxia, once seriously wounded, couldn't just be patched up with a few healing potions. They needed a long recovery period, and during that time, both nutrition and treatment had to be kept up. "What did Mossa's Grimtotem friend say to you last night?" Grede finally couldn't hold it in anymore and asked. "She wants me to stop it." Jace looked at the fading white figure of Magatha Grimtotem on the road ahead, keeping his voice as low as possible. "Stop her from becoming a dragonkin." "I don't get it." Grede grumbled. "Don't they want more power? I figure any dragonkin could probably take a tauren in a fight." "But it means becoming our slave." Sora whispered. "Most mortals can't accept that." "She's the leader of the Grimtotem. If even she's accepted it, what good does her niece's opinion do?" Grede practically breathed the words. "I don't think Magatha herself knows the real cost of this power." Jace said. "It's not unusual for black dragons to trick mortals into becoming dragonkin to serve them." Sora said. "Are we going to help her?" Vereesa asked. "The problem is we don't know how." Jace said. Sora shook her head too. She wasn't fully adult yet, and she wasn't a black dragon. Not knowing was normal. "Do you know?" Jace looked at Needleflame. "I don't know. But my father would." Needleflame said quietly. "Though if he knew you kept calling him 'that guy,' he probably wouldn't want to tell you." "That guy gave you to me. What else could he possibly hide?" Jace asked. "He didn't give me to you!" Needleflame snapped. "He told me to leave the lair and gain experience!" "Whatever you say..." Jace was mid-sentence when he noticed Natila ahead, following behind the Grimtotem matriarch, glance back at them. When she caught Jace's eye, she quickly turned away. Now that she'd gradually come to understand what Jace Sesso and his group were capable of, it seemed she was putting all her hopes on them too. But originally, Jace had only demonstrated a bit of magic and spun some tall tales to her and her aunt — just to make them believe Jace's group had a way to deal with the black dragon, so she'd willingly lead them through the fog to meet Onyxia. He'd never actually planned to help the Grimtotem with anything. "What do you think, Mossa?" Jace asked. "Should we help the Grimtotem?" "I don't care about Magatha Grimtotem's fate." Mossa looked at the tauren ahead. "But I think at least Natila shouldn't be turned into a dragonkin. If Magatha insists on turning the whole tribe into those purple-blooded monsters, I'll find a way to get her out. She's harsh. I know. But she's actually a very kind person." "I know, Mossa." Jace said. The forest on both sides of the "road" grew sparser. Gradually, there wasn't a single tree in sight — only broken stumps remaining in the ground. This was like an open marsh, reminiscent of the vast wetlands below Grim Batol. But the air was thick with a burning stench mixed with the heavy swamp odor — the sickly sweet smell of foul water, rotting corpses, and dead water plants. Soon, another stench joined in. Oppressive. Lonely. Even despairing. It drifted without shame through the white mist floating over the streams. The shadow energy grew heavier. The smell of black dragon blood in the air thickened — even stronger than when they'd passed the dragon corpses earlier. Needleflame frowned and pressed her lips together, looking like she might vomit at any moment. The Grimtotem ahead slowed their pace. Jace's group followed suit, watching the swamp depths warily for anything that might emerge from the fog. But what appeared wasn't from the sides — it was from directly ahead. At first a dark mass, then bigger, and bigger, until it covered most of their field of vision. When its shape fully emerged from the fog, Jace's group was genuinely startled. A dragon's skull, massive as a small hill! Grede immediately grabbed the hammer Saffron's handle behind his back, his breath catching in his throat like he was about to roar. But he quickly realized, along with everyone else... this wasn't like a small hill. It was a small hill. Paired with the pitch-black cave beneath it, it really did look like a massive dragon crouched in the marsh's heart, its abyssal maw gaping to swallow every adventurer who arrived here. The ground beneath their feet grew firmer and harder, until the road leading to the cave became gray stone, layered step after step — whether solidified lava or deliberately carved stairs. Magatha Grimtotem stopped before the gray stone path. Straightening herself, she looked up at the entrance high above and called out something in Tauren, finally shouting Onyxia's name. Her voice echoed through the cave and fog for a moment. Then, accompanied by the scraping of armor and scales, a blurry figure appeared at the entrance. At a glance, it looked like a massive whelp. But after it lingered long enough for them to make out its outline, Jace realized this was a dragonkin guard as large as Vemonsathrac. "The Mother permits you to enter." The dragonkin's draconic was clear and sharp.
