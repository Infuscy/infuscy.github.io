## Chapter 1743: A Teacher?

Leaving the wooded hills south of the swamps, the thick fog thinned. That meant they were far enough from Onyxia's lair for Sora and Needleflame to take flight, free from the black dragon's disrupting magic, and properly orient themselves.

Natira and Mossa had barely spoken along the way. Jace could tell they hadn't reached much of an agreement last night.

"I have to go back," Natira said. "I trust you found your way here on your own. You can find your way back too. You don't need my help anymore."

"Thank you, Lady Natira," Vereesa said. "We understand the risk you took bringing us in."

"Just a thank-you?" The Grimtotem looked at the ranger. "I thought you could defeat that black dragon, drive her away, open up these swamps. But it turns out you're really on her side."

"Have you forgotten what you said before?" Jace asked. "You said you didn't oppose Magatha's decision to lead the tribe into cooperating with the black dragon. For the tribe to grow strong, they needed a powerful backer. You were just worried about your aunt losing control... afraid she'd be tricked by the dragon."

"But as it turns out, she knew exactly what she was doing and accepted it. Onyxia didn't plan to turn you all into dragonkin either. Or have you changed your mind? Do you want us to help your aunt see reason, forcibly reverse her decision?"

"That's not what I want. I just..." Natira hesitated, swinging her massive black head. A snort blasted from her nostrils, as if she were deeply conflicted inside.

But in the end, she could only say quietly: "I don't know."

"Come with me, Natira." Mossa, now back in tauren form, tapped the ground with her staff. "If you can't accept becoming the black dragon's servant, come with me to the plains. Chief Cairne Bloodhoof welcomes every helper. Even the Grimtotem are part of the tribe."

"The Grimtotem fight the centaur too, Mossa. The glory of claiming dominance for the tauren... can't be monopolized by Bloodhoof, and it can't be stolen by you and your druid apprentices." Natira emphasized: "I must stay in Blackhoof Village. I must... do everything possible to keep my aunt and her children clear-headed. The black dragon hasn't deceived her yet, but that doesn't mean she won't in the future. I won't abandon my tribe, just like you never gave up avenging your family, Mossa Timbermane."

"Then good luck, Natira." Mossa said. "I hope that one day Magatha Grimtotem and her dead husband's brothers will return to Mulgore, to the high tents we built on the bluffs, to stand trial for the ugly crimes they committed."

"And when that day comes, I will not defend them," Natira said.

Mossa nodded and said something in Taurahe. Jace didn't understand the words, but after hearing them, Natira's brow relaxed slightly.

The two immensely tall, powerful women embraced tightly and patted each other on the shoulder. You'd never guess they were from enemy tribes.

Jace sincerely hoped that the Grimtotem would one day truly rejoin the tauren family. The tribe was cruel and vicious, but that also meant they were not short on fierce warriors and elemental summoners who could command the wind and rain. If someone like Natira -- willing to accept tauren from other tribes -- could become their leader, they might walk a less twisted path.

But for now, she had a long way to go before she could replace Magatha as the new matriarch.

"Let's go," Mossa said.

"Wait a moment." Natira looked at Jace. "Will you come back to the plains in the future, Jace Sesso?"

"What do you care about that for?" Grede said. "That's got nothing to do with you Grimtotem."

Natira said: "The warlock who came from the other continent -- researchers of shadow and demons all know of you. From humans, to Quel'dorei, to orcs and dwarves... even if they can't recall your name exactly, they know what you've done."

"I haven't witnessed your full power, but even a glimpse was enough to impress me. Even the dragon mother whom my aunt fears so much invited you into her lair, despite your history of opposing her family..."

Jace glanced at the little black dragon beside him. That was true in a sense, but it wasn't entirely because of him.

If not for Needleflame -- well, now Miss Phydygonia -- if not for this young dragon appearing near the lair with them, Jace doubted the process of rebuilding trust with Onyxia would have gone this smoothly.

Before coming to Kalimdor, he'd worried that Needleflame's black dragon blood might make it easy for Onyxia to control her, maybe even turn her against them. But he could also deeply sense the confusion in Needleflame's heart since arriving on Azeroth. She needed to truly understand the difference between herself and the clan here, to recognize who she really was. That was why Jace decided to bring her along. And it wasn't entirely because of the thunder lizards.

Of course, Jace's original goal was to help her distinguish herself from Nefarian's black dragon lot. He never expected Onyxia to accept her as a daughter... It was hard to say whether that fulfilled his original intention, or if it was an unexpected gain, or if things had just developed in a more uncertain direction.

But seeing Needleflame seem like a different person now -- so much more spirited and confident -- Jace felt that was good enough.

"While we do occasionally help people solve problems for some extra coin, Lady Natira," Jace said, turning back. "We have urgent business ahead. We can't stay long. If you're looking for mercenaries to help with your aunt, you might try the goblins."

"That's not what I meant, Jace Sesso." Natira took a moment to organize her words before speaking slowly. "I was thinking... if you ever come to the plains again, I want to... learn from you. About mastering shadow magic."

Jace and the others didn't know what to say. Even Mossa's ears flicked in surprise.

Seeing no one respond, Natira added: "Even among tribes like Timbermane or Bloodhoof, personal strength is an important basis for having a say. The Grimtotem are even more so. My aunt respects the strong -- whether a powerful hunter, a skilled elemental priest, or a deadly witch..."

"Alright," Jace said. "Next time I come to the plains, if the timing is right."

"I'm very grateful," Natira said promptly. "Teacher Sesso."

"I haven't agreed to be anyone's teacher yet." Jace waved his hand. "We can exchange knowledge about shadow magic, learn from each other. And my last student... didn't exactly end well."

"I'm prepared for that." Natira seemed to be trying her best to pronounce Common clearly, making her tone as solemn as possible. "As a close relative of Magatha Grimtotem, as a witch determined to learn to master shadow, I've never expected a good 'ending' either."

Jace looked at Mossa, who stood silently nearby. He'd expected Mossa to object to Natira learning shadow magic, to steer her toward the right path like druidism or the elements. But she said nothing. There was even a hint of anticipation in her eyes.

"Alright," Jace nodded.

"Excellent, Teacher." The Grimtotem said. "Many excellent witches come from the other continent. To find a guide here, you'd either have to seek out the legendary ancient demons, or find those called the 'Twilight's Hammer.'"

"You know about the Twilight's Hammer," Jace said.

"Of course I do," Natira said. "Many shadow apprentices around here know of them. I know some tauren have already joined."

"If you want to be my student, I have only one condition," Jace said. "No connection to the Twilight's Hammer at all. And if you can influence the entire Grimtotem tribe, make sure no one else joins them either."

"Why?" Natira asked. "I have heard some rumors..."

"If you're worried your aunt might be controlled by the black dragon," Jace said, "then you should be even more careful about the Twilight cultists' mind magic."

"I understand." Natira answered. "I can promise you that, Teacher Sesso."
