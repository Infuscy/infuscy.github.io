Chapter 1745: Dark Scenes Under Sunlight

As the morning sunlight cut horizontally across the tall grasses of the Barrens, the two dragons took off with their passengers and one big cat, energized and ready.

This journey would span the entire great prairie -- practically from the southern edge to the northernmost point. Even on the game map, that distance wasn't short. Here, it was a real trek.

Halfway through, Jace kept scanning the plains below, looking for prey whose blood he could collect to establish a communication spell with Kalrez.

But two young dragons flying low over the prairie were far too conspicuous. Even though Kalimdor had no gryphons, the prairie's incredibly sharp-eyed beasts had long learned to dodge wyverns or wind serpents flying overhead. There was almost no sign of wildlife below.

They flew for about half the day before reaching the great plains in the central region, nearly devoid of hills. Jace finally spotted a dying kodo that had been left behind, mortally wounded on the ground. It might have been injured fighting a rival, or maybe a predator had injured it and fled when the two approaching dragons scared it off.

But one prairie kodo had enough blood to sustain the ritual. Vereesa gave the kodo a quick death. Jace collected enough blood, then the two of them found a gully to paint the formation. Sora and Needleflame had already started eagerly dividing up the kodo carcass. Grede and Mossa planned to collect some meat for the evening meal too.

"I don't know how you feel, but Grede..." Vereesa watched the warlock drawing runes in blood on the ground. "He really enjoys this kind of life. Roaming the prairie freely with a loved one, hunting prey, making a meal on the spot, eating your fill, lying down to sleep, waking up at dawn and doing it all over again."

"I like this life too." Jace looked up. "Of course, I only started liking it once my magic was strong enough to take down a thunder lizard."

"If I weren't still a ranger-lord of Quel'Thalas, I'd prefer this kind of life too." The elf's gaze went slightly unfocused staring at the blood. "Actually, I envy Grede. Acknowledged by all his people, yet free to do as he pleases... Does saying something like that make me seem irresponsible, Jace?"

"If you didn't have those two sisters..." Jace finished the last circle of the formation and straightened up with a breath. "It would seem a little irresponsible, yeah."

"The only advantage of having two overbearing sisters is that you get to be the child forever." Vereesa clasped her hands behind her back.

Jace smiled, tossing two soul shards into the formation and snapping his fingers. The formation ignited with purple flames, and the high sun dimmed by a notch.

"Evil warlock! You're not going to spoil our meat, are you?" came a complaint from outside the gully.

"Kalrez, are you still at the Stormrage Altar?" Jace asked in Eredun.

"I am here, master."

"How are things on your end? Stormwind? The North? Anything important happen lately? Have you been in contact with Morophil?"

"She's in Stormwind. Unless something serious comes up, she wouldn't contact me, master. As for everything else -- same as when I first got back to the Stormrage Altar from Blackrock Spire. Nothing unusual. Maybe to the mortals here, I'm the most unusual thing happening." The demon said: "Recently, some Kirin Tor mages have come to this valley and set up camp. I think they're here to supervise me rather than monitor the Burning Steppes and Blackrock Spire. But they're clearly not very good at it. These mortals get scared by every little thing I do. Like right now."

"Who sent them?" Jace started to ask, then immediately thought better of it. "Wait, don't answer that first. They can hear your voice, can't they? There are other mages on the Stormrage Altar right now?"

"Yes," Kalrez replied. "But I can't answer your question either, master. They're not warlocks -- they have no interest in chatting with me about such things."

"Find a way back to the Telogrus Rift," Jace said. "Use the power of the Stormrage Altar to tear open a portal. I think that shouldn't be too hard for you."

"You want me -- a former Burning Legion lord -- to openly teleport back to the Twisting Nether right under the noses of these supervisors?" the demon asked.

"Call the administrator here. I'll talk to him. Quickly. I'm using beast blood, not demon blood. It won't burn for long."

"As you command, as you command," the demon said.

After a moment, he said in a low voice: "This Kirin Tor battle mage calls himself Yakkus. He wants me to send his regards. He says he's also from Kirin'Var Village and is honored to speak with you."

In the past, Jace always felt a kinship with mages from the Sons of Lothar. From Auchindoun to Blackrock Spire, it was the mages from Kirin'Var Village who were the most reliable.

But after meeting Onyxia, realizing Vargus might already be a Nathrezim in disguise, the phrase "Kirin'Var Village" made him uneasy, as if every mage associated with that name was a potential demon crony.

"Tell this battle mage that I need you to return to the Telogrus Rift immediately to maintain dimensional stability. Otherwise, the rift could collapse at any moment, and the dimensional coordinates we worked so hard to get would be lost. Not only would the Kirin Tor lose the ability to study the Twisting Nether there, the void blossoms they have a stake in would all be destroyed too. We've run into a small problem on our Kalimdor trip and might be a few days late returning, so I can't personally escort you back to Telogrus. You'll have to teleport yourself."

The demon acknowledged and relayed the message to the mage. Jace checked the beast blood's consumption, tossed in another soul shard, and added: "Have him inform the Kirin Tor about this too, especially Khadgar. He had a hand in opening the Telogrus Rift, so ask him to help me assess the dimensional stability and let me know the result. Khadgar will do me this small favor."

"I'll remember, master," Kalrez replied.

The demon's voice was distorted, thin. Jace hadn't felt much mana drain yet, but the kodo's blood couldn't hold out. The soul shard was burning to ash. The shadow energy he'd gathered had nowhere to anchor and began to disperse. So he could only add "That's it for now" to the demon, then clench his fist and dismiss the formation.

The purple flames blew out instantly, leaving only a patch of dark purple ash scattered by the breeze through the gully.

"Your mana seems much stronger than before, Jace," Vereesa said.

"That's because I'm not crossing through Kil'jaeden's magic from Draenor to talk to Azeroth this time." Jace said. "Still, something feels off. I feel like..."

"What?" Vereesa asked.

"Before we came to Kalimdor, Grede mentioned something to me." Jace looked at the dry grass lit by the shadowfire, reaching out slightly to extinguish it. Then he continued: "Grede said he wanted to stay in Kalimdor. With Mossa. He didn't want to deal with the kingdoms, the Three Hammers, any of that anymore. He just wanted to fight the centaur here, live with the tauren. Maybe work as a mercenary for the goblins now and then."

"Hasn't he expressed similar wishes before?" Vereesa said teasingly. "Maybe he was more serious about it this time? But now all he wants is to get back to the other side, as if he can't stand being here a moment longer. He has to go to Dalaran to deal with those demons. I can't say which version sounds more like him."

Jace looked at her without speaking. Vereesa suddenly realized what Jace was really worried about.

"The demons set a trap for me, waiting for me to jump into it, Ver."

"No, Jace." Vereesa walked over. "If Grede wanted to stay in Kalimdor, it was because he thought you didn't need him looking after you anymore. Now he wants to go back because it's obvious the Kirin Tor can't handle demon lords, can't protect you. So he doesn't want you to go back and face the demons alone! If he still wanted to stay in Kalimdor under these circumstances, that would be the real sign something's wrong with the Wildhammer dwarf!"

Jace took a deep breath and sat down on a rock. "You're right, Ver. I shouldn't think that way."

Not only that, but Grede could control Sulfuras and command the wind elementals to listen to him. There was no way a dreadlord could fake that.

Vereesa was right too. His control over shadow power was much stronger now than before Blackrock Spire.
