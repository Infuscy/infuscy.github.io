## Chapter 1746: Who's the Mount?

After flying across the vast prairie for nearly two days and nights, around noon -- with clear skies holding and still no lush forests of Ashenvale visible on the horizon -- they could already make out a towering blue silhouette in the distance.

Mount Hyjal, home of Nordrassil, the World Tree, and the second Well of Eternity. Though this magical lake, watered from the vials of the original Well of Eternity, could at best be called a pale shadow of the first, it was still the most refined essence of all Azeroth. Even the Sunwell paled in comparison.

Its contained magic was so potent that it would later draw Archimonde the Defiler during the Third War, driving him to attack Mount Hyjal recklessly, intent on devouring the World Tree and all the power of the Well beneath it to claim what he believed would be godlike strength rivaling the Dark Titan himself.

In the end, that ambition would be his undoing.

But at this moment, as Jace gazed toward Mount Hyjal, his mind was on something else entirely. The demon hunter imprisoned beneath that mountain.

If there was anyone in this world besides Morophil who might be able to see through a dreadlord's disguise, it had to be him... Illidan Stormrage.

But Illidan had been imprisoned by his brother, Malfurion Stormrage, for repeated acts of betrayal that had enraged all the Kaldorei. Forget asking him questions or getting his methods for identifying dreadlords -- even getting a single audience was nearly impossible. Just mentioning his name might get you thrown out of Astranaar.

Even if they might give Jace some leeway for his role in defeating Deathwing and not treat him so harshly, bringing up Illidan Stormrage -- that taboo subject -- would surely anger them.

And frankly, Jace couldn't even explain how he knew the legendary "Betrayer" was still alive after ten thousand years.

"Hey, Jace!" From the green dragon flying steadily ahead, Grede twisted his head and shouted: "Are you sure Khadgar will understand what you mean? What if he thinks what you're asking isn't important and can't be bothered?"

"I don't have a great excuse ready yet!" Jace shouted back. "The rest is up to Khadgar. I trust he'll figure it out!"

"We're about to enter Kaldorei territory." Vereesa squinted into the distance and called out: "What now..."

"You remembering your glorious history of being captured by their sentinels twice?" Grede laughed loudly.

Vereesa glared at the dwarf and muttered something, then called out: "I mean, the Kaldorei hate black dragons. If Needleflame just flies in like this, won't there be problems?"

"Since we're here to visit Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind, I think honesty is better, Vereesa." Mossa's voice was too muffled by the wind to hear clearly. "I can accept a kind young black dragon. The ancient Kaldorei, who've lived millennia or even tens of millennia, should be even more open-minded..."

"The fact is, old folks tend to be more conservative," Grede said.

"Don't forget I'm a warlock too, a demon summoner. In that regard, I'm not much better than a black dragon. But Priestess Tyrande has always welcomed us." Jace shouted ahead.

"Ask her opinion?" Vereesa called out.

"What do you think, Needleflame?" Jace asked. "If you fly into the forest like this, they might see you as an enemy. Or you could disguise yourself as a human and come to Ashenvale as our ordinary friend."

"Would explaining why you're traveling with a black dragon be too much trouble for you, Sesso?" Needleflame asked.

"Explaining to the Kaldorei..." Jace said. "It'd be a little trouble. But I wouldn't mind explaining carefully."

"Then I'll fly in like this," Needleflame said.

"Agreed," Grede shouted. "Let's go!"

This was the first time Jace's group had entered Ashenvale from the Barrens side.

The first time they came to Kalimdor, they never made it to the north. The second time, after escaping the quilboar pursuit at the northern end of the Rushing River, they'd fallen into a night elf encirclement.

Back then, entering Ashenvale's dark forest, with starlight-like arcane energy scattered everywhere, had left a deep impression on Jace.

But this time, there was none of that dreamlike feeling of stepping from a barren red desert into a deep shadowy valley. They flew a long time and the distant Mount Hyjal remained faintly visible, as if they were getting no closer.

The terrain grew rougher beneath them, with more trees, but withered grass, bare slopes, and dead trees were still everywhere. It wasn't like in the game where passing an orc watchtower meant plunging into a completely different world.

Probably because in the game, the trees here had all been logged by the orcs to build Orgrimmar and Warsong Hold. The night elves, pushed past endurance, had to defend the forest's edge at the front lumber camp, creating that stark contrast.

In the game, the Kaldorei hadn't joined the fight against the Burning Legion because the Alliance asked for help... By then, the Alliance had already crumbled in name due to the Scourge invasion.

They'd eventually realized the world was in crisis and actively participated in the final battle. But initially, they'd been dragged into the chaos by the orcish migrants invading the forest.

Jace was actually worried that this time, Tyrande might just treat them like honored guests, making polite conversation while giving no real commitment to help.

After all, in the other timeline, the Scourge had already descended, Lordaeron had fallen, and demon-blooded orcs had marched into Ashenvale. But now... comparatively speaking, things were peaceful.

More than peaceful. The black dragon had just been dealt a heavy blow. Humans, dwarves, and Quel'dorei on the other continent were celebrating the war's end and a new era. Everything was looking up.

And in this celebratory atmosphere, a black wizard riding a black dragon shows up with apocalyptic prophecies. Awkward timing.

At least this black wizard wasn't some raven-cloaked old man nobody recognized. That gave Jace a small advantage.

In the distance, the enormous, towering canopy of Nordrassil, the World Tree, was now visible as a silhouette.

When Archimonde the Defiler had tried to devour its power, his body had swelled to nearly half the World Tree's height -- a terrifying size comparable to Deathwing's. You'd see it clearly from this distance. Hard to imagine.

Horns sounded from the misty forest in the distance, startling a huge flock of unknown birds into the air. Several agile dark shapes flew closer from afar, accompanied by a series of low, wild cries -- a greeting or a warning.

It was the call of hippogryphs. Jace knew it'd be easier to let the night elves notice them than to search for the Silverwing Outpost themselves.

"Why do Ysera's daughters fly in the waking world?" the Kaldorei called out.

The green dragon ahead turned her head and replied: "I am Somnalir. Several years ago, Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind requested that the dwarf Grede Thunderfist bring Jace Sesso and Vereesa Windrunner to Ashenvale. And now we have come."

"Sesso..."

The hippogryph riders flew closer. Until one of them suddenly made out the color of the slightly smaller dragon behind. The lead hippogryph let out a shrill screech.

"A black dragon? A black dragon has entered Ashenvale?!"

"Wait, that black dragon is carrying the human wizard Jace Sesso..."

"Don't be alarmed, Kaldorei!" Jace shouted at the riders circling them. "This black dragon means no harm. She's with us. Queen Alexstrasza has accepted her, and I believe Queen Ysera already knows of her existence."

Even though Jace explained immediately, the hippogryph riders kept a wary eye on the black dragon. Still, compared to how other mortal races reacted when seeing Needleflame for the first time, these night elves were relatively calm.

These beings could hardly be called "mortal" anymore. A young dragon wasn't enough to panic them.

"Unbelievable," a night elf rider said in Common. "I never imagined anyone could tame a young black dragon as a mount, Archmage Jace Sesso. Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind has indeed been awaiting you at Astranaar..."

Before he could finish his sentence, Vereesa, Grede, Sora, and Mossa almost simultaneously snapped their heads toward Jace, their expressions filled with horror. Jace felt a cold shiver run down his spine.

Oh no.

Needleflame let out a piercing shriek and started thrashing wildly. Jace barely managed to grab tight onto her neck and hold on -- otherwise he'd have been flung off instantly.

"Who's the mount?! Who's the mount?!" The black dragon screamed in the dragon tongue, eyes squeezed shut. The surrounding hippogryphs flapped wildly in chaos -- some dove, some climbed, some flew away. The air was in complete pandemonium.

"No one is worthy of riding on your back, noble Lady Phydygonia! I only asked because I have no wings and walking takes too long..."
