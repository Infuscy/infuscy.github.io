Chapter 1747: City of the Moon

Ashenvale was as dark and tranquil as Jace remembered -- no matter which memory he drew on.

From the unseen depths came what sounded like a clear, melodious female voice humming ancient Kaldorei verses, though it might have been some kind of hallucination, flickering in and out of earshot.

The group rode massive nightsabers, following the sentinel riders northwest along the main road.

The ancient trees lining the path seemed to bow their branches slightly as Sora passed by, as if pressed down by the wind. But seeing Sora occasionally look up and smile in return made it feel as though the old trees really were responding somehow. Hard for an outsider like Jace to say for sure.

Vines wrapped around lampposts glowing with starlight-like energy. Egg-white giant owls perched in high branches gazed down at the travelers. Spotted deer peeked out from the thickets. The whole forest felt like a living, breathing beast, curiously examining these foreign "invaders."

Needleflame met every curious look with hostility. After landing, the little one provoked the nightsabers to bare their fangs and growl at her in unison. It took the night elves a while to find one willing to carry her.

Now on the road, every sudden movement from the surroundings made her whip her head around like a startled black cat, glaring at whatever suspicious thing had moved.

The sentinels they passed might not have seen through her human disguise, but her jumpy behavior made them alert too. They kept asking the leading sentinel riders who these people were, and every time they got the answer, their faces showed surprise -- or outright shock -- as they glanced back at the group.

Especially the war ancients -- the massive walking trees patrolling the forest paths, just like the one roaming the gates of Eldre'Thalas. Those Ashenvale guardians made the little black dragon especially wary.

And in turn, those ancient trees seemed to sense something off about her too. Whenever they passed one, it would lower its amber, nature-energized magical eyes to study her.

Even Jace Sesso, carrying Ursales, didn't get this level of attention. Though that wasn't surprising. Ursales had been on Azeroth for a thousand years without the night elves noticing, so it must have some property that kept it hidden. Without someone carrying it, it wouldn't even move.

Anyway, Needleflame definitely didn't like this forest full of strange energy. And this forest definitely didn't like her. So the two vastly different entities kept up their tense standoff.

Jace was happy to let her focus her attention elsewhere. At least it helped her forget about that "mount" comment from earlier.

Jace was wearing the Hide of Orathastaz, so he wasn't too worried about being thrown off the black dragon and injured. But coming all this way to visit Astranaar only to end up in an undignified mess would've been pretty embarrassing.

Fortunately, after watching the black dragon throw such a fit over the mount comment, the night elves actually warmed up to her a bit. These ancient forest dwellers were wary of Blackwing dragons, but they had plenty of patience for children.

Jace hadn't wanted to land and switch to riding. It would waste almost another day getting to Astranaar.

If he hadn't already arranged with Lord Kalrez to contact Khadgar, he might have insisted that Sora and Needleflame fly over the entire forest to save time. Even if it might have seemed disrespectful.

But this was fine too. Immersed in the peaceful depths of Ashenvale, he could relax. His mind had been unsettled from the shadow energy -- surging and powerful -- that he was still learning to balance after the demon ritual.

The further northwest they went, the more common the sights became: towering treehouses, Kaldorei towers wrapped in vines and nearly blending into the forest, lakeside pavilions grown from interwoven roots that rose like twisted branches.

Even Vereesa, who had seen countless magical wonders in Quel'Thalas, stared wide-eyed. This was a simple beauty from an entirely different world.

Unlike Feathermoon Stronghold in Feralas, or Silverwing Outpost on Ashenvale's southern edge, this wasn't a sentinel fortress or defensive line. This was where the Kaldorei had truly lived for over ten thousand years. No sharp edges, no thick bark or spikes meant to repel invaders. Everything was harmonious and gentle, blending with nature, the night, and the moonlight.

Astranaar was the heart of Ashenvale. Arguably the center of the entire Kaldorei world, second only to Mount Hyjal itself.

Teldrassil hadn't been planted yet. Darnassus didn't exist. The lands north of Ashenvale hadn't been corrupted by demon blood. Astranaar hadn't been ravaged by demons and orc legions. Everything was as it had been for millennia.

From the way the night elves spoke, Jace could tell. The sentinels often used "Astranaar" as a direct substitute for their superiors. They called Tyrande Whisperwind's orders "Astranaar's orders," and officers sent here were said to be "from Astranaar."

In other words, in the Kaldorei's minds at this time, Astranaar was the true capital. Completely different from sacred sites like Nordrassil and the Well of Eternity, places they didn't often need to visit. Though in the game, after Darnassus was built -- especially in the later expansion -- this town didn't get much attention. Many new players barely knew it existed. Jace was the same way in his past life, even though he'd played both Alliance and Horde.

After rounding a massive tree root that forced the Kaldorei to build a wide-angle curve in the road to go around it, a phantom-like city in the shadows appeared at the edge of the horizon.

It was a city built on an island in the middle of a lake. Though calling it a lake island was a stretch -- the island seemed almost as large as the lake surrounding it, making it hard to tell if it wasn't really just a spot where two wide rivers flowed around it.

Mist drifted across the water, pale blue in the moonlight. Deep purple rooftops, warm yellow window lights clustered beneath vast, dark green canopies that blotted out the sky.

Along the lakeshore near these wooden houses, moss-covered white stone pillars stood or lay fallen, guarding altars that still shimmered with moonlight. Some of the nearby broken elven stone statues looked like relics from the ancient Kaldorei Empire, standing watch over this ancient land for uncounted millennia.

It all suggested this city might have witnessed Queen Azshara's era, survived the Great Sundering, and endured all the way to today...

"Astranaar has arrived, everyone."

The lead sentinel captain called out loudly, then repeated it in Common. It took a moment for the group to snap out of their daze.

Not just Jace, but Vereesa and Needleflame had almost the same reaction -- struck speechless by the Kaldorei city before them.

It was far larger than in the game. Though nowhere near as vast or bustling as Stormwind, Jace estimated from a quick scan that at least several thousand residents lived here. Among the lights between the buildings, he could already see dozens or even hundreds of moving figures crossing back and forth. And that didn't count the ones they couldn't see -- those inside homes, or out on patrol or travel.

"I knew you'd react this way." Grede looked smug. "Sorra, didn't I tell you? Even a noble like Windrunner looks like she's never seen the world when she comes here..."

"I can be awed by anything beautiful," Vereesa said. "That has nothing to do with how much I've seen."

"Does that beautiful anything include a demon worshipper?" Grede whispered.

Vereesa rolled her eyes at him. Sora sighed. "You're hopeless, Thunderfist."

"Hey, everyone..." Mossa called out, pointing toward the front of the road.

Everyone followed her gaze. At the bridge leading into Astranaar city, a full formation of nightsaber riders stood waiting. In front of them, riding an enormous white tiger, a woman in silver robes with long teal-green hair drew particular attention.

Looking at that figure, Vereesa unconsciously swallowed. She didn't need to say a word. Even at this distance, through the dim night and misty forest where details were hard to make out, Jace could immediately guess who it was.

That was Tyrande Whisperwind, High Priestess of Elune. And during the slumber of Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage, the supreme leader of the Kaldorei.
