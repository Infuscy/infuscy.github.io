## Chapter 1749: Dinner in Astranaar

Needleflame seemed completely surprised that Priestess Tyrande would be so open-minded about her. She just stared blankly, letting the nightsaber's gait bob her head back and forth. But Jace wasn't all that surprised.

Ten thousand years ago, during the War of the Ancients, the betrayal of the Black Flight had shocked not only the dragons but also the Night Elves of that era. Neltharion had been the leader of the Dragonflights back then, supposed to be the strongest reinforcement against Archimonde and the thousands of elite demons under his command. In the ten thousand years since, the dragons and these ancient races had almost given up trying to bring Neltharion back to reason, even starting to call him "Deathwing" instead.

Now, suddenly seeing a young one who reminded them of what the black dragons once were — even Queen Alexstrasza would be moved, let alone the Night Elves.

But Tyrande only nodded to the little black dragon and called her name. Jace could feel something ignite inside her, but it cooled just as quickly. He thought he understood why the Priestess had calmed down so fast. The black dragons were what they were now. As far as the Night Elves knew, Neltharion's mate was missing, and Onyxia was still fighting a losing battle. One young whelp, even if she was Neltharion's descendant — what could she change? She would need hundreds, even thousands of years to grow up before she could truly shift anything. The variables in that span were enormous, and Tyrande Whisperwind, who had weathered ten thousand years of upheaval, knew that all too well.

That Tyrande could accept Jace — a demon summoner who used shadow magic — and could accept a black dragon who had broken free from shadow's grip, said enough about her as a pragmatic leader. Of course, Jace suspected they had Illidan Stormrage to thank for that. With a "half-demon" blessed by Sargeras himself as a close friend, Tyrande and her husband Malfurion had always had a fairly high tolerance for allies who tread the edges of dark magic.

Maybe because the arrival really was so sudden, some residents of Astranaar didn't recognize the outsiders who had suddenly walked into town. From the looks in some passersby's eyes, Jace could tell just how long it had been since any race other than Night Elves had set foot here. That didn't make the whole walk feel like a circus parade, though. These ancient residents, hundreds or thousands of years old — even tens of thousands — showed no signs of gawking curiosity. They understood well enough that any outsider Tyrande Whisperwind personally welcomed could not be ordinary.

Still, compared to Jace and Grede, Vereesa drew the most attention. News hadn't spread that fast, and most onlookers didn't yet know why these people had come to Astranaar. Humans and dwarves weren't that interesting, but a silver-haired Quel'dorei was plainly visible, and most of the eyes tracking her were hardly trusting.

In the elven language, "high elf" and "highborne" were the same word — Quel'dorei. Dalaran's scholars had coined two different terms in Common to distinguish them by era. Ten thousand years ago, the highborne nobles of the Kaldorei Empire had abused magic, channeling the power of the Well of Eternity and drawing the Burning Legion's attention, bringing demonic invasion. Though the mortals of Kalimdor had paid a terrible price to win in the end, some surviving highborne nobles still refused to learn their lesson, attempting to use the new Well of Eternity atop Mount Hyjal for their spellcasting. They were eventually exiled from Ashenvale, crossing the ocean to wander to Tirisfal.

Thousands of years later, many Kaldorei who had witnessed that schism were still alive. Remembering those arrogant, headstrong nobles of old, and now seeing these people still calling themselves Quel'dorei, they naturally grew suspicious — especially without knowing what these particular individuals had done.

Jace had no idea how much sway Tyrande held in Kaldorei society. Even if he could convince her, what about the other Night Elves? Could they respect a Quel'dorei hero like Vereesa, and extend that same respect — even their lives — to help other Quel'dorei?

Looking ahead at the grand hall built against a giant oak's trunk, seeing the armored sentinel guards with their warglaives, and the Night Elf cooks and other guests waiting inside, Jace felt no sense of relief at finally being able to eat and rest. Even if Tyrande was willing to host a warlock like him, setting up a demon communication circle in Ashenvale would be too profane toward this land and its people. So Jace couldn't contact the Telogrus Rift during this time either. He'd have to wait until they left and returned to the Eastern Kingdoms to check in.

He hoped this two- or three-day stopover would yield some valuable aid, rather than just a polite meeting with the Night Elf leaders followed by nothing. But walking into Astranaar and feeling the atmosphere here, he realized this was no simple task.

Besides the great barrows where druids slept their deep slumbers, Night Elves generally preferred semi-open buildings like this — a massive roof for shelter from the elements, letting the forest breezes and lake winds drift freely through the wooden pillars into the hall, keeping company with guests and hosts alike. Even in dangerous zones like Feathermoon Stronghold, where they might battle naga and sea giants at any moment, they preferred building this way. Fortunately, Ashenvale wasn't Feralas, nowhere near the naga-occupied coast. And it wasn't like Stormwind, crammed with every kind of citizen, narrow streets noisy and chaotic, sewage and fish stench everywhere. Night Elves could savor the forest's breath without reservation.

The feast featured the Night Elves' traditional light fare, cooked in simple, conservative ways: raw fish and shrimp, shellfish, rice soup and bean soup, vegetable cakes, plus some unidentified roasted or smoked beast meat served in huge chunks. Those stood out awkwardly against the rest of the dishes served on leaves and in wooden bowls — clearly made specially for these "savage" outsiders. But Jace had to admit it hit the spot. At least Grede wasn't pretending anymore, completely forgetting his earlier prejudiced judgments as he dug in.

Jace had been eating "Sulfuras Roast" the whole trip, and finally seeing some real roasted meat made his stomach growl too. But more than filling his belly, the important thing was how to broach the subject of the Nathrezim and the undead plague with Tyrande Whisperwind. Just coming out and saying it felt awkward — like showing up after years of no contact and immediately asking for help.

Vereesa and Sora sat closer to Tyrande. One of them could skillfully avoid sensitive Thalassian vocabulary and speak a Kaldorei-adjacent elvish with her; the other was fluent in Kaldorei, so conversation flowed more easily. They were talking about what happened on Draenor — from the demon lord Kil'jaeden, the Dark Portal, the Alliance Expedition's push from Hellfire Citadel to the Black Temple, the destruction of Draenor, the demonic lockdown, and the Alliance army's reluctant alliance with the black dragons.

When they mentioned that Sabellian — Deathwing's ten-thousand-year loyal dog — had actually accepted an invitation from the very warriors who'd helped kill Deathwing, every Night Elf present looked stunned. Compared to that, a pure black dragon raised on Draenor willingly returning to Azeroth with humans seemed almost unremarkable.

"Master Jace Sesso destroyed the Scepter of Sargeras?"

Tyrande Whisperwind's sudden switch to Common snapped Jace back to attention. When the Scepter of Sargeras came up, even the ancient Priestess looked astonished.

"The Scepter of Sargeras was a key instrument for demonic invasion of Azeroth," Jace said. "Before leaving Draenor, we had to find a way to destroy it, even at great risk. If we hadn't, the Burning Legion could have launched a Third Invasion easily."

"How did you destroy the scepter?" Tyrande asked.

"Arcanite Reaper," Jace said. "I borrowed the power of the Spell-Weaver, Malygos... to finally achieve that goal."

Tyrande studied the sheathed sword hanging by the doorway alongside his robe and cloak. She was clearly not convinced. But Malygos's power was enough to persuade the other Night Elves present. The High Priestess looked around at their reactions and nodded. "Breaking through the demonic legion's protection to reach that dark temple guarding the Scepter of Sargeras, and landing the final blow — both are heroic feats I cannot imagine, Jace Sesso."

"You're not the only one who can't imagine it, High Priestess." Grede didn't even look up from his meat, mumbling, "There's probably only a guy like Jace Sesso in this world who could do something like that. You don't normally find someone whose brain works and doesn't work at the same time..."

The Night Elves who understood Common all laughed. But it wasn't mocking laughter. The older they were, the better they understood what facing Kil'jaeden meant. When the Scepter of Sargeras came up, the banquet's atmosphere had frozen solid. Thank the Light for Grede, who could crack a joke in any situation and thaw things out just a little.

Needleflame quietly gnawed on bones beside them, head down. While everyone talked about the Scepter of Sargeras, she said nothing, as if she didn't want to be noticed at all. She didn't even mention that Sabellian was the one who had brought Jace to Kil'jaeden's throne.

"When I destroyed the Scepter of Sargeras, I thought I had accomplished something even Aegwynn couldn't do," Jace said in the elven tongue as best he could. "That together we had bought Azeroth another few hundred years of peace. But in truth... things didn't go the way we and some Alliance leaders had hoped."
