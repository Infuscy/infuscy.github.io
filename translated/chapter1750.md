## Chapter 1750: That's Right, Him

"Kil'jaeden couldn't use the Scepter of Sargeras to rebuild the Dark Portal and send his armies directly to Azeroth," Jace said. "But that made him even more irritable and impatient. After we returned to Azeroth, he immediately took steps to restart the invasion. The Alliance Expedition's retreat — while it cut off one of his direct invasion routes — actually pushed forward his 'backup plan.'"

"What steps do you mean, Master Sesso?" A rare male Night Elf asked in Common.

"The demons found the black dragons and planned to use them to spread the undead plague," Jace explained, meeting his gaze. "Kil'jaeden wants to poison the entire human world with necromancy, turning every living soul into the demons' undead slaves. In Northrend, they've probably already begun deploying their forces. We have plenty of evidence that the plague spreading across the north, the spider war... all of it may be linked to the demons."

The Night Elf guests began murmuring among themselves. The High Priestess's expression was unreadable as she looked back and forth across the room. Jace couldn't tell what her stance was.

"We found some evidence from the black dragons, clues pointing to Nathrezim activity," Jace continued. "But it's far from enough to find all of them. We know they're plotting something, and destroying the black dragon's nest didn't stop it — in fact, they're getting closer to their goal. But overall, they're still operating in the shadows."

"You want Astranaar's help," Tyrande said.

"We're seeking help from anyone who can give it, High Priestess." Vereesa said. "If the black dragons finally wake from the demons' manipulation and decide to help us fight the demon legions, we wouldn't hesitate to stand with them temporarily — just like on Draenor. Let alone you. You are the first force in this world to fight the Burning Legion, and you achieved a great victory."

"And your ancestors were the ones who summoned the Burning Legion." A purple-haired Sentinel leader who seemed to hold high rank spoke up. "I mean no disrespect to you personally, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. But this sounds more like humans and many 'Quel'dorei' mages reaping what they sowed."

"A morally corrupt mage heeds a demon's whispers, opens the Dark Portal, and summons countless demons and invaders from another world into their lands — bringing death and suffering. That's not an unfamiliar story to us. When humans, descendants of the earthen, and Quel'dorei grew prideful over your many arcane fonts and magical academies, you should have seen this day coming. In fact, you've passed a thousand years in relative stability. To me, fate and time have been generous enough."

"I think many young races need a lesson like this to understand what should and shouldn't be done," another Sentinel officer echoed.

"Haven't we had enough lessons already, brave ladies?" Grede set down his bone. "Even the folks of Aerie Peak, who don't much like magic, know that the reason all those powerful mages on that continent study magic is to fight demons. But the way you tell it, it sounds like they study it for..."

"The convenience magic provides, Honored Thunderfist Lord — or more directly, the pleasure." The purple-haired Sentinel leader finished his sentence.

Tyrande said nothing, simply watching as Grede and the Sentinel leader went back and forth, arguing whether magic was meant to fight evil enemies or to satisfy selfish desires and even attract demons. But Grede was clearly outmatched. The Kaldorei had been wrestling with these questions for thousands of years — there had been plenty of internal debates and arguments. Vereesa stood by, unsure how to interject. She and her people were practically mixed-race children forged by arcane waters. She didn't even have Grede's advantage as a Wildhammer dwarf on the "moral high ground" regarding magic.

"I think..." Jace raised his voice slightly. "Everyone has their own views on what role the arcane plays in this world. Changing long-held opinions over a single dinner isn't realistic."

"But the facts are these: the Well of Eternity and the world-tree Nordrassil stand atop Mount Hyjal, visible for dozens of miles around. The immense arcane power they radiate pulses with all of Azeroth's vitality, shining like a star in the darkness of the Twisting Nether. Every demon that arrives in this world can sense it. I don't think any of you can deny that, respected residents of Astranaar."

"I think you can also imagine this: once the Alliance falls in this struggle, and the other continent falls completely under demonic and undead control, they will cross the Great Sea and attack Mount Hyjal. And their power will be far greater than when they first entered this world..."

"If that day comes, the Kaldorei will defend Nordrassil to the last soldier, Master Jace Sesso," the Sentinel leader said quietly.

"But you would be defending it alone — when perhaps you wouldn't have to fight 'to the last soldier,'" Jace said.

"Master Jace Sesso speaks truly, Lyane Wolfwalker, my dearest friend."

Tyrande spoke in the Kaldorei tongue.

The purple-haired Sentinel leader bowed her head slightly. Tyrande continued to the others nearby. "Just as the safety of the Emerald Dream concerns the purity of the sacred mountain and the world-tree, it also concerns the forests and all living things on the other continent. Once that continent falls to the demons, Ashenvale cannot stand apart. We all know what the Dark Titan's ultimate goal is..."

"But I think the humans and Quel'dorei have at least used their magic and courage to repel demonic invasion time and again, High Priestess," a white-robed Moon Priestess said quietly. "This time, they may not necessarily be pushed to the brink."

Tyrande looked at her, hesitated a moment, then turned back to the group. "When you return to the other continent, you can tell your kings and leaders: when true danger comes, the Kaldorei will not stand idly by — no matter whether our stances have ever aligned, or whether we trust each other. I think every commander and warrior here agrees. If Malfurion were here, he would agree too."

"The Archdruid has always been that way, High Priestess," a male Night Elf agreed. "When it comes to the Burning Legion's invasion, he would surely advocate sending troops."

Those in the hall nodded in agreement or voiced their assent. Jace and the others exchanged glances. Grede and Vereesa both seemed relieved. The High Priestess had made a formal commitment, and while the Kaldorei were stubborn, they weren't likely to break their word.

Jace certainly felt they had gained something. But the problem was... this wasn't yet "true danger." Without a bronze dragon around, only he knew what the arrival of these dreadlords could trigger down the line. Having just the red dragonflight and the Kirin Tor holding back the Lich King in Northrend wasn't enough. If they didn't deal with the dreadlords soon, they might end up waiting for "true danger" to arrive.

"Thank you very much, everyone. The Alliance will be greatly encouraged to hear this." He tried to continue. "But we have solid evidence that several Nathrezim demons have infiltrated multiple kingdoms. They were the ones who brokered the connection between the demons and the black dragons."

"As Vereesa said, you were the first in this world to fight these demons. In the ten thousand years since, you've clashed many times with demons trying to infiltrate — shapeshifting satyrs, rogue demons left over from the War of the Ancients, each of them experts at disguise and concealment. Surely you have enough experience dealing with this kind of hidden demon."

"Even a shapeshifting satyr cannot compare to the cunning Nathrezim, Master Jace Sesso." The Sentinel leader called Lyane Wolfwalker spoke up. "Stopping the summoning ritual from the start is the most effective method. Nathrezim are not ordinary demons — their summoning cannot be conducted without leaving traces..."

"Among us, not everyone is ignorant of how to find a Nathrezim, Lyane," Tyrande said quietly.

Lyane Wolfwalker looked at her commanding officer in confusion, then suddenly widened her eyes as realization struck. Not just her — many Night Elves in the hall seemed caught off guard. No one said anything outright, but everyone knew who Tyrande was referring to.

*Yes, that's right, him. And I didn't bring it up...*

Jace thought this as he secretly breathed a sigh of relief. After all that circling and hedging, they had finally gotten to that person.
