## Chapter 1751: The Person Who Should Never Be Mentioned

Grede sensed the shift in atmosphere but couldn't figure out what the Night Elves were so afraid of. He just exchanged blank stares with Needleflame nearby. Sora, Mossa, and Vereesa clearly knew who Tyrande was referring to. Sora was already glancing nervously toward Jace.

"Who?" Jace asked, completely lost.

"Wait." Lyane Wolfwalker practically thrust out a hand to stop him. "Please think carefully, High Priestess. Contacting that person is even more dangerous than speaking to a real demon lord — even for his guards, let alone outsiders."

"Heroes who have defeated countless black dragons, demons, and invaders from another world, who saved the Emerald Dream, have traveled a great distance asking nothing in return but some advice." Tyrande Whisperwind looked at the tall Sentinel who matched her in stature. "If sending sentinels and druids to the other continent really isn't the right time, then arranging just one 'prison visit' — I don't think that's too heavy a price for Astranaar."

Tyrande's tone had grown much more serious. Jace felt he was starting to grasp her intent. As someone who had witnessed the entire War of the Ancients ten thousand years ago, she surely knew what the initial Nathrezim infiltration meant. Back when Queen Azshara summoned the Burning Legion to the imperial capital of Zin-Azshari, a few demon lords had appeared at court as vanguards, and back then, few Kaldorei from top to bottom thought the situation was out of control.

Even if she wasn't like Jace, who knew the future story of another timeline, she could certainly foresee that if disaster were left unchecked, everyone on both continents would be swept up in it. So in truth, she was on Jace's side, wanting to help as much as she could. She just couldn't control the entirety of the Kaldorei forces alone.

That was why she had first made a vague promise that didn't need immediate fulfillment — to manage the emotions of the more conservative, less trusting subordinates and colleagues around her. Then she could take a step back and propose an easier way to help against the demons, making it hard for the others to refuse.

Of course, it was also possible that Tyrande Whisperwind simply hadn't had a good reason in too long to visit her lover's younger brother — who had once been her dearest friend — and wanted to use this opportunity to see him. Or maybe she wanted to give him a chance to redeem himself, to help these heroes who had aided the Emerald Dream and the druids.

But these were just Jace's idle speculations. The fact was, this retreat-one-step-to-advance tactic worked remarkably well. A figure who was absolutely taboo in Ashenvale was brought up so directly, and the other Night Elves didn't outright object. Even Jace found that surprising.

"That prison has had no outside visitors for a very long time. None of us know his current state, High Priestess," another priestess said.

"At least the Wardens haven't reported anything unusual," Tyrande said. "I will first communicate fully with Warden Maiev Shadowsong. And of course I will also ask the Archdruid for his opinion. Just as you said, Lyane — Nathrezim are extremely dangerous enemies. We should not take this lightly."

Once Tyrande invoked the Archdruid again, the resistance from every Sentinel, priestess, and the few awake druids in the room quietly died. Jace could practically feel the collective defeat radiating through the hall. He guessed it was because once Tyrande went to Malfurion Stormrage, there was no point in objecting. The Archdruid, who had personally blocked Archimedes and destroyed the Well of Eternity's Legion gate ten thousand years ago, had guarded all of Mount Hyjal from any evil for ten thousand years. Though he rarely appeared before others, his prestige was even higher than his partner Tyrande Whisperwind's.

But everyone also knew that when it came to anything that could make his younger brother's image slightly more positive in Kaldorei society... he would absolutely never refuse. So once this matter went to the Great Barrow, it was as good as approved.

"You all seem to be deep in discussion, but we have no idea who this 'person' you keep talking about is," Grede said.

"His name is Illidan Stormrage," the High Priestess said.

When that name finally reached everyone's ears, every Night Elf's face darkened — even the few squad leaders who had initially seemed sympathetic to Jace's side. Even the most devoted priestesses of Elune harbored doubts about the leader's decision. But Tyrande looked resolute, as if she had already decided to let Jace see Illidan from the moment they arrived asking about Nathrezim.

At first, Jace had felt that Tyrande Whisperwind's temperament and style seemed somewhat different from what he'd seen and known in the game. But at this moment, he finally caught a glimpse of the "spirit" that would later let her cut through the Wardens' protection and free Illidan Stormrage from his prison.

"So it is him." Vereesa said softly. "In Quel'Thalas, many thought the Kaldorei had already executed him."

"No, Lady Vereesa Windrunner. He is still alive." Tyrande said. "He has been imprisoned in an underground dungeon on Mount Hyjal, watched over by the Wardens, for several thousand years. The Well of Eternity at the summit of Mount Hyjal today was created from two vials of water from the ancient Well that he stole. Though it nourished the world-tree Nordrassil, it also passed the old Kaldorei Empire's problems down to us."

"He is an unforgivable traitor, a stubborn magic addict. But he also made his contributions in the War of the Ancients. At the same time, he is without question the person in all of Ashenvale who understands demons best. If you want to know how to see through a Nathrezim... he is the one to ask. As long as Malfurion Stormrage and the Warden in charge of him permit it, I will take you to see him."

"Are you sure about this, Jace?" Mossa, sitting nearby, leaned in. "According to many legends... Archdruid Malfurion's twin brother is practically a true demon already."

"By many people's standards, so am I," Jace said.

"Not in my book," Sora whispered.

"Don't worry, you two." Jace said. "No matter how hard he is to talk to, he can't be harder to deal with than Kil'jaeden. For a way to see through demon plots, I think it's worth the risk."

He raised his voice a little, addressing Tyrande and Vereesa. "And... Illidan Stormrage is still locked in his prison, with no risk of escape, correct, High Priestess?"

"The cell holding Illidan Stormrage has never been breached. You can rest assured on that, Master Sesso." Sentinel leader Lyane Wolfwalker sounded a bit offended by Jace's doubt. "But let me tell you in advance, on the High Priestess's behalf — that cell controls his freedom, but it doesn't control his will. Even if both leaders and the Warden agree to let you see him, that doesn't mean he'll cooperate."

"I understand," Jace said. "Just having this opportunity is already more than we could ask for. Thank you, everyone in Astranaar."

The Sentinel leader looked toward the High Priestess. Tyrande nodded slowly in response.

Nearby, Vereesa took a deep breath as she looked at Jace. She was very tense — at least more tense than Thunderfist, who wore an expression that said "Let's see what this is all about." As a former magic apprentice at the Farstrider Academy, she surely knew the history, knew exactly what kind of figure they were going to visit.

Jace wasn't exactly tense, but he couldn't help feeling a little trepidation. After all, this was Illidan Stormrage.
