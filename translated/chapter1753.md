## Chapter 1753: A Place That Should Not Be Known

Flocks of hippogryphs lifted off from Astranaar, rising into the layered deep greens of the high canopy. Even during the day, Ashenvale was dim and dusky — sunlight turned murky as it filtered through, and no matter how far they flew, it felt like they could never escape Nordrassil's shadow.

Hippogryphs were much smaller than gryphons. They had the front half of a great raven and the hindquarters of a horse, with a pair of antlers atop their heads. Many in Kalimdor believed they were hybrid magical beasts born from the blessings of multiple Wild Gods, like the faerie dragons, created to guard the forests. Regardless, their bodies were more fragile than gryphons — even compared to the Hinterlands gryphons. So the purple hippogryph carrying Grede was the strongest the Night Elves could find, and even then it struggled mightily with the dwarf's weight. The Kaldorei hippogryph handlers were curious why the dwarf was so much heavier than he looked. Grede claimed it was from all the rice and noodles he'd eaten the night before, absolutely refusing to show them Sulfuras.

Compared to Grede, everyone else had it easy. Mossa had no trouble shifting into a great cat. Needleflame had somehow gotten along well with the hippogryphs — plenty were willing to carry her to Mount Hyjal. And Sora was a green dragon, so that went without saying.

Jace was genuinely happy to finally ride one of these strange, beautiful beasts properly. Back when he first arrived in Azeroth, one of his earliest dreams was to ride every available mount in Azeroth. Back then, even riding a gryphon had seemed nearly impossible. Now he'd even ridden Sabellian, so standing before a hippogryph didn't get him as excited as when Grede first took him on a wild Hinterlands gryphon. Still — not many non-Kaldorei had ever climbed onto a hippogryph's back. Saying he felt nothing at all would be a lie.

Unlike them, who approached this like a travel adventure, Tyrande Whisperwind kept her eyes fixed on the peaks of Mount Hyjal, not speaking much with the Kaldorei knights escorting them. Even from this distance, Jace could feel the turmoil of complex emotions boiling within her. How long had it been since she last saw Illidan? If these outsiders hadn't come crashing in asking for ways to fight demons, who knew how much longer it would be before she found a reason to visit that underground prison.

The last time they'd approached this area, they were escorted by sentinels on nightsabers, heading toward Winterspring at the foot of Mount Hyjal. They'd even run into a group of furbolgs lying in ambush. This time they were heading straight for Mount Hyjal. As they climbed higher above the foothills, still far from their destination, the magnificent crown of Nordrassil already blotted out the sky like deep green storm clouds.

In another timeline, after Archimonde's death, the Night Elves would plant another world-tree — Teldrassil — off the northwestern coast of Ashenvale. The city of Darnassus would be built upon that tree, and other parts of its canopy would house many Night Elves and beasts. It would be like an entire hidden valley in the sky. But now, looking up at Nordrassil from up close, watching the massive branches thick enough to walk on, the treehouses wedged in the crooks, and the deeper, darker recesses within... Jace had no doubt there was another world inside there too. Maybe there were furbolgs, nightsabers, or druids living there. Maybe even harpies. After all, it had stood here for ten thousand years — anything could have grown inside it.

Jace tried to look down, seeing the trunk like a dark brown wall blocking all view, thin clouds drifting around it, and beneath the clouds the barely visible rivers, valleys, ruins of highborne cities, and tangled roots stretching into unfathomable depths.

Jace at least had some expectations. Grede and Vereesa were simply stunned into silence. Seeing the shadow of this giant tree from the edge of Ashenvale, they might have anticipated it would be huge — but flying closer, they felt not just awe but an almost crushing fear. So now Jace only had one question: how had Archimonde the Defiler managed to grow big enough to hug this tree?

Without coming here yourself, without seeing it with your own eyes, without measuring this tree's size from a mount's back, it was impossible to grasp just how hopeless the Alliance and Horde armies must have felt facing that demon lord.

The hippogryphs didn't continue toward Nordrassil. Instead, they turned downward. The underground prison holding Illidan Stormrage wasn't near the world-tree but on Mount Hyjal's mountainside. Sweeping low over stretches of pine forest, Jace could see pale blue wisps of light drifting through the deep woods, sometimes spinning in flight, sometimes rising and falling. If he wasn't mistaken, those were the mysterious "wisps" — nature spirits that many believed were the incarnations of departed Kaldorei, forever guarding the sacred peak of Mount Hyjal in life and death.

Jace wasn't sure if that was really true, but at least in the game from his past life, when a Night Elf player died and ran back to their corpse, the soul wasn't a humanoid spirit — it was indeed a wisp. And it ran faster. In the Third War, Archdruid Malfurion had summoned all the wisps of Mount Hyjal to converge and detonate their power together, destroying Archimonde. So in the game's timeline, wisps had become rare. But here, they were everywhere — especially as night fell and you looked into the distance. Mount Hyjal's forests and valleys were filled with these glowing lights, mysterious and beautiful.

*If we can successfully stop the demons' plans*, Jace thought, *maybe Nordrassil could keep thriving like this forever. And those living in its canopy could continue in peace and tranquility.*

Without realizing it, the hippogryphs flew lower and lower, landing in a shadowy valley. Strange "hoo-hoo-hoo" sounds echoed from afar, like giant owls calling. The sound grew clearer and more distinct, and Jace realized Tyrande and the sentinels were actually following these strange raptor calls in flight. After a while, even the wisps disappeared. Wildly overgrown trees, bushes, and vines tangled together layer upon layer, turning the area into a maze. Eventually, even the hippogryphs had no room to fly, landing in a meadow covered in colorful flowers.

"Based on my experience, this place has absolutely nothing to do with a 'prison,'" Grede muttered.

The hippogryphs looked uneasy, as if they'd been led into an unsettling unknown territory. It wasn't just them — the sentinels who'd come along also seemed nervous. Tyrande Whisperwind lifted her head, scanning the canopy around her, and took a deep breath. Even she seemed to not recognize the way.

"High Priestess, this way."

A call came from the forest not far off. Jace and the others looked over. Somehow, a tall, dark green owl-like figure had appeared among the treeline shadows. It wasn't an owl. When the figure stepped out, they saw it was actually a warrior in an enormous owl-headed helm — or rather, one that resembled an owl's head — clad in dark bronze heavy armor beneath a long dark green cloak. The warrior held a strange ring-shaped weapon in both hands, spinning blades along its edge leaving no doubt about its lethality.

"Maiev... Warden." Tyrande said. "It's good to see you. You should have received my letter."

*So this is Maiev Shadowsong.*

Actually, seeing the armor, Jace knew this must be one of the legendary Wardens. But there were more than one Warden, so Jace couldn't immediately tell if this was their leader. Still, since the High Priestess herself was visiting, it made sense for Maiev Shadowsong to greet them personally.

"Yes, I saw it." The Warden said. "Are you certain you want these outsiders to meet Illidan Stormrage? You should know how dangerous this decision is."

"These 'outsiders' you speak of are heroes blessed by the Dragon Aspects, Maiev." Tyrande said. "I made that clear in my letter. Even if you cannot trust these guests from across the Great Sea, you should at least trust Lady Somaeliel, Lady Ysondre, and Queen Ysera."

"I do not distrust these guests," the Warden said quietly.

At these words, Tyrande's eyes cooled slightly. "Regardless." She took another deep breath. "Malfurion knows and has agreed. You should have word from him too. So lead the way, Maiev. I've almost forgotten how to enter this place."

"You were never meant to know the exact location of his imprisonment, respected High Priestess." The Warden's heavy helm tilted slightly. "Now follow me."

The companions behind Jace exchanged glances. The same question was written on every face: "What the hell is going on here?"
