## Chapter 1754: The Dungeon

Jace couldn't remember exactly what Illidan's prison at the base of Mount Hyjal looked like. Illidan's escape happened before the game's timeline, and Warcraft III — being an isometric RTS — had depicted the place rather roughly. But if he went by his own stereotypes of "Illidan," "Wardens," and "prison" thrown together, this place should have been like a demon's maw — full of spikes and blade-like decorations, wrought-iron bars, braziers burning with fel fire, and a few faceless jailers standing coldly at the entrance staring at visitors...

In reality, it looked like an ordinary cave. Roots hung from the ceiling, and the damp air carried a faint wild-beast stench. The only difference was that it was much larger than a normal den — at least wide enough for four or five people to walk side by side.

Another Warden in an owl-faced heavy helm stood silently at the cave entrance. Maiev Shadowsong passed her without any acknowledgment, and the other Warden made no move either. Tyrande glanced at her but said nothing. The figure seemed more like a statue marking this cave as no ordinary beast's lair than a living, breathing defender. Only when Vereesa tried to greet her in the Kaldorei tongue did the Warden nod slightly.

"Like I said," Grede muttered. "Nothing about this says 'prison.'"

"It looks like a place where a druid would rest," Mossa said.

"Your instincts are good, tauren." Maiev didn't turn around. "Long ago, this was indeed where the claw druids slept — until furbolg explorers dug deeper from below, breaking into this cave and disturbing their slumber, driving them out. Many years later, even the furbolgs abandoned this den, leaving the tunnels to become a haven for burrowing beasts and underground plants. But whether it was furbolgs or druids — they came and went, and none realized this was actually the traitor's cage."

"Ten thousand years is enough to make many things forgotten," Tyrande said.

"But some things remain fresh even after ten thousand years, High Priestess." The Warden replied in the elven tongue, then turned to look back at the High Priestess behind her. "Seas change into mulberry fields. The Wardens have never forgotten their duty."

"Every Kaldorei remembers your sacrifice, Warden," Tyrande said.

Warden Maiev Shadowsong nodded and said nothing more. She led them deeper and deeper. Just as she'd said, the place felt like a druid's hideaway cave or one of the furbolg burrows Jace had visited before. Nothing like a prison meant to contain someone like Illidan.

From the shadows too dim for torches to illuminate and too dark for the naked eye, occasional hissing sounds emerged — whether from burrowing reptiles or poisonous spiders lurking in the dark was anyone's guess. When another Warden appeared in the torchlight up ahead, Jace first mistook her for a giant vampire bat or some dark beast crouching there.

"We're here." The Warden switched back to Common. "But only one person may enter with us from here."

"One person?" Grede asked. "You mean whoever goes in has to face that monster alone?"

"This has been the rule for thousands of years, dwarf. No matter who comes." The Warden said. "Allowing the High Priestess to enter as well is already a compromise given the current crisis."

"Jace going in is enough, Grede." Sora reminded him quietly. "Or do you think any of us could learn anything valuable from that demon?"

"That's true." Grede crossed his arms, staring at the two Wardens before them. "But I can't remember the last time I let this guy go into a place this dangerous alone."

*Blackrock Spire, a few months ago*, Jace thought to himself.

"Are you certain, Warden?" Vereesa asked in the elven tongue. "I hope this place isn't as..."

"I will ensure safety, Ranger-Lord." The Warden said. "As long as Jace Sesso cooperates and attempts no dangerous deviations."

"Don't worry, Vereesa." Jace looked at the Warden. "I promise to cooperate."

The two Wardens, nearly indistinguishable in size and equipment, exchanged glances. Maiev Shadowsong looked at Tyrande Whisperwind and nodded. From start to finish, the Kaldorei leader voiced no opinion. That alone showed that when it came to Illidan Stormrage's cell, the Wardens held nearly absolute authority — even the High Priestess or Archdruid had to comply.

"Then follow me, you two." The Warden headed deeper. Tyrande followed, her expression noticeably stiff.

Watching her, Jace grew more convinced that this visit wasn't arranged for them — it was arranged for herself.

As they went deeper into the tunnel, things increasingly resembled what Jace had imagined. Walls were dimly lit by a cold blue-green magical glow, hard to tell if they were blue or white. This looked less like a demonic dungeon and more like the architectural style of Eldre'Thalas — just more ruined and gloomy. When Illidan's prison was built, the Kaldorei of that era had probably still largely followed the style of the highborne period.

The deeper they went, the more Jace felt something was off with the atmosphere. He could sense shadow energy permeating the air. But the emotions in this energy were hard to identify — like rage, hatred, and resentment that had once been fierce, then gradually solidified and cooled over an unknown span of time, becoming thick and sluggish. Hard to recognize unless you looked closely. The only thing suggesting they were still "alive" was the rhythmic pulsing like a heartbeat.

But as they walked deeper and even those strange blue-green lights faded, Jace realized the rhythm wasn't a heartbeat — it was being affected by something else. He observed carefully, using every sense to probe the deep darkness ahead. Gradually he realized that what was affecting the shadow's pulse wasn't deeper inside... it was right beside him. It was Tyrande Whisperwind's footsteps.

"Hard to imagine, Maiev. Hard to imagine a tedious woman like you could come up with a trick like this. But considering you've been torturing me for several thousand years — maybe ten thousand — before finally thinking of this way to gain an advantage... I suppose that fits my impression of you."

A hoarse laugh came from the darkness. The man's elvish was so strange Jace could barely make out the meaning.

"Disappoint you, Illidan," Maiev Shadowsong said quietly. "I will remain as 'tedious' as always. And you will continue to endure this monotony until the day you and I and the world end together."

"Hmm. Tedium and monotony. The beloved Warden's ultimate weapon..."

The Warden raised her hand. Several braziers flared to life in front of them. Inside a black rune-covered cage layered with powerful seals, a hunched figure sitting with his back to them slowly turned.

He was very different from the Illidan in Jace's memory. Not as tall. Not as overwhelmingly imposing. No terrifying demon horns. No dark purple demon wings blocking all hope of victory. No twin blades of Azzinoth that had made countless demons tremble.

Quite the opposite. The Night Elf before him was as gaunt as a starving wolf. His upper body was bare. Above his black blindfold, hanging strands of hair and his high ponytail were caked with mud and debris. The contrast between him and the robust, orderly male druids outside was stark. But he was indeed Illidan Stormrage. Even in this dim light, Jace could see the faint fel-fire brands on his back — the marks Sargeras himself had left on him.

Jace tried to cast Detect Demon toward the cage. He didn't see anything clearly — instead, his whole consciousness seized as if something had grabbed it.

"Hm? Who's there?"

The prisoner in the cage tilted his head.

"Illidan." Tyrande's voice was low.

"Tyrande... You really came?"

"It's me," the Priestess said. "I've come to see you."
