## Chapter 1755: Prisoner of Hyjal

"How long has it been?" Illidan asked.

"Ten thousand years," Tyrande replied.

"I figured as much." The prisoner let out a snort of laughter, his bony shoulders twitching. "Ever since you received that blessing, you've grown increasingly... detached from time's passage, haven't you?"

"You are one of us too, Illidan. Nordrassil blessed you as well," Tyrande said.

"Then thank the tree up there for me." Illidan's voice was strained with suppressed anger, his teeth grinding audibly. "Thank it for blessing me to endure these endless years, to fully savor the boundless darkness you left me with."

"Were you waiting for me to lose my mind? Expecting me to become what you decided I would be — a complete madman, a demon?"

His emotions were nearing the breaking point. His voice still sounded calm enough, but the echoes in the dark chamber came out like the growls of a wild beast. Tyrande waited until everything had gone quiet before she spoke. "I only hope you are well, Illidan."

Chains scraped across the stone floor. Illidan turned his face away again.

Maiev Shadowsong stood motionless beside them, maintaining her silence. Jace almost wished he had one of those helmets of hers to hide behind right now.

"You can see what I am now better than I can myself." The prisoner let out two self-deprecating chuckles, then lowered his voice. "But at least my ears still work well enough to hear why you've come to see me."

The High Priestess was silent for a moment, then looked toward Jace. The prisoner in the dark cell impatiently scratched his blade-like jaw with his sharp black nails, shaking his head slightly. "You don't expect me to be naive enough to believe you came all this way just to see if I'm still alive?"

"It's not me," Tyrande said. "It's..."

"He sent you?" Illidan tilted his head. "The esteemed savior needs help from someone like me?"

"I asked the High Priestess to bring me here to meet you," Jace tried, speaking the elven tongue quietly. "Illidan Stormrage."

At first Jace thought Illidan would be angered by an outsider interrupting. But the prisoner only twitched his cheek slightly.

"That was you earlier?"

He suddenly spat out a string of demonic language, his tone full of warning and threat.

"He is using the demonic tongue, Jace Sesso." Maiev Shadowsong warned from the side. "But his malevolent magic cannot break through this rune-cage's restraints."

"I know. Thank you for the reminder, Warden." Jace nodded at Maiev, then switched to the demonic tongue, speaking toward the cage. "It was me. My apologies, Illidan Stormrage. I instinctively used demonic sight. I had heard many things about you before coming."

Illidan laughed. Maiev's helmet dipped slightly, then she looked toward Tyrande. No one could see her expression, but she was clearly surprised.

"Your Eredar is much easier to understand, human." When the prisoner spoke the demonic tongue, the rasp in his voice was thick as a true demon's — completely different from when he spoke elvish, and a stark contrast to his gaunt frame. The faint arcane energy on the cage bars pulsed stronger and weaker with the cadence of his voice, as if his words alone could threaten the stability of the entire prison.

Jace wasn't alarmed by the tension from the demonic speech. Instead, he thought: *If I could pronounce like Illidan, maybe I could manipulate demonic spells as freely as Kael'Thas said I could.*

"Then speak, this... Jace Sesso."

"The Burning Legion is preparing for another invasion," Jace said in the demonic tongue.

"And what do they plan to use as the power source for their portal this time? Dath'Remar's Sunwell?" Illidan crooked his mouth in a grin, speaking the demonic tongue. "I'm sure it won't be Mount Hyjal. Malfurion would never make the same mistake as Azshara... er, he wouldn't, would he?"

Illidan burst out laughing. Maiev asked coldly in the elven tongue, "Do you think he is you, Illidan?"

"He certainly isn't me. If he became like me, or if I somehow suddenly became him, I would burn myself to death without hesitation — and relieve you of your meaningless heavy responsibilities as well, dear Maiev."

The prisoner turned his ear toward them, as if waiting for someone to curse him out. But Maiev Shadowsong only adjusted her cloak and said nothing more.

"The Burning Legion must be looking for a power source." Jace observed the three people before him and continued. "Maybe the Sunwell, maybe the ley line nexus in Tirisfal. We don't know yet. The demons are infiltrating human kingdoms, likely toward that end. I came here to find a way to track down those disguised demons."

"Nathrezim," the prisoner said.

"Yes," Jace said.

"Outdated. But effective." Illidan braced himself on the ground and stood up. He looked like a silhouette compressed in the center of infinite darkness. The fel-green runes floating on his body were clearer than his face. Beneath the black cloth binding his eyes, faint wisps of green flame flickered weakly.

"Then you've come to the right person, Sesso. If you're not blind, you should be able to tell... I am blind. But that's a minor, optional price. Because now, what I can truly perceive far exceeds your imagination... if not for this cage blocking me."

"Let me out of here. Let me come with you to the human kingdoms. Let me see what tricks the Nathrezim are playing. These cowardly deceivers cannot hide from me."

"Don't delude yourself." Maiev said calmly. "To leave here, you would have to step over my corpse."

Jace could tell that remark wasn't aimed only at Illidan, but at Tyrande, and at him too.

"You don't seem to have reached an agreement," Illidan said.

"Since you won't consider letting me out of this stinking, filthy cave... then why should I help you, human?"

He laughed, raising his arms high, switching back to the elven tongue as he declared, "Let the demons come! Let them climb Mount Hyjal! Then my self-righteous kin will realize what a fatal mistake they made locking me in here."

"He saved Malfurion, Illidan. He has done a kindness to Ashenvale." Tyrande said. "When Malfurion and the druids fell into that deathly slumber, he rescued a trapped emerald dragon from the Dream while fighting the satyr demons, and learned how severe the Nightmare's corruption had become. He sought out Queen Alexstrasza for help. That was how the Nightmare crisis was resolved. Do you know who has been trying to corrupt this forest for ten thousand years, seeking revenge against Malfurion, venting his hatred?"

"Xavius."

"Yes. He is dead. If not for Jace Sesso, Queen Alexstrasza would still be captive in the hands of the demon's servants. I and the other sentinels would never have known what was happening in the Dream. The druids would not have been freed. Once the Dream was corrupted, Ashenvale would have faced a crisis unseen in thousands of years. Your brother might have slept forever. You know very well that was what Xavius wanted."

"A pity," Illidan said quietly.

"He is beyond saving, High Priestess." Maiev said. "I know you and the Archdruid have always believed he simply lost reason like those nobles, craving greater magical power. You thought he might still turn back. But in truth, this is his nature. Mad and self-righteous. I have seen it clearly for ten thousand years."

"Mad and self-righteous." Illidan repeated the words in the Warden's flat tone. Then he said, "You want me to help these mortals, but you won't let me out. Who is mad and self-righteous? Or do you think I can twist all disaster from thousands of miles away with a flick of my finger, like that living god with a pair of ridiculous antlers? Then you think far too highly of me. Throw him in this cage he made himself and see how he does."

"You were once the finest mage of the Kaldorei Empire, Illidan. Your arcane talent stood supreme even among the most exceptional Quel'dorei." Tyrande said. "More importantly, you are the one among us who understands demons best. I know you must have a way to counter the Nathrezim's magic."

The defiant smile on Illidan's face quickly faded. He faced the nearby brazier, unsure if he was feeling its magical warmth or simply unwilling to face those outside the cage.

"For Malfurion's sake..." Tyrande tried again.

"Malfurion, Malfurion! I have never owed him anything, just as I have never owed any of you anything!"

The thundering echoes faded. The dark prison fell silent again.

"But you are right." Illidan said. "I do have a way to see through the Nathrezim's disguises."

"I can give this Jace Sesso some advice. But not for his sake. Because you are here, Tyrande."
