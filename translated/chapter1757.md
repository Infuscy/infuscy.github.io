## Chapter 1757: Wishes

A Warden had appeared at the doorway of the cell sometime earlier, pausing for a moment. Maiev Shadowsong looked over. The two exchanged glances. The other Warden then looked toward Priestess Tyrande in the distance. They nodded to each other, and the Warden turned and walked up the steps.

Jace understood now why Maiev Shadowsong hadn't wanted too many people entering the depths of this prison. The Wardens, relying only on information from Astranaar, couldn't know the full background of Jace's group either — after all, who knew how many years it had been since they'd last met any outsiders. The blessings of Mount Hyjal and the world-tree, even with the severe weakening of demonic power, weren't enough to give these Wardens confidence in dealing with a group of outsiders connected to demons. Not to mention that the Wardens had Tyrande — a deeply powerful High Priestess of Elune — on their side. Maiev Shadowsong knew very well the bond between Tyrande, Malfurion, and Illidan. Who knew which side Tyrande Whisperwind would stand on if conflict broke out.

All in all, by this point in the meeting — especially after demon summoning came up — the atmosphere had grown quite tense.

Once the Warden had left, the prisoner in the dark cell spoke again. "You need a sufficiently powerful beholder lord's eye to withstand the Nathrezim's soul-fire, Jace Sesso. Otherwise, before you can crystallize it with arcane energy into a durable gem, it will have already burned up along with the soul."

"If you fail, summoning a demon like that — or finding another Nathrezim foolish enough to let you take its soul — won't be easy."

"Would summoning a high-ranking beholder lord require a specific incantation?" Jace asked.

"I don't like those incantations, and I especially don't like reciting them, Jace Sesso." Illidan said. "Maybe you young mortals don't mind humbly intoning those grating prayers, begging the Twisting Nether for favors. But I have my preferences."

Jace nodded. Standing before a demonic knowledge master like Illidan, asking about how to learn the incantations for summoning low-level demons would indeed be a waste of time. Besides, even the goblins of Booty Bay could summon a tiny beholder to help with ship security. Warlocks like Erland Durnholde, who practically lived in the Cape of Stranglethorn, had probably obtained the relevant knowledge long ago. And that chattering little beholder servant on the docks — Jace could hardly imagine its tiny eye becoming a weapon capable of rooting out a Nathrezim lord like Tichondrius.

"Besides, the incantation is the least important part of summoning a beholder." Illidan said quietly. "If you want to summon a powerful beholder with sufficient insight, luring it with mere soul energy or magical power won't be nearly enough."

"You need something strange that it cannot refuse. An incantation no one knows. A trick even the demons have never heard of. Use these as bait. Bizarre, rare knowledge is the one thing these strange beings roaming the outermost edges of the dark void — and beyond — cannot resist."

The more Illidan talked about an incantation no one knew, a spell the demons had never seen, the more Jace's mind drifted toward Khadgar. It looked like he really was going to have to drag him onto this pirate ship. Not just waving from the dock or saying hello — actually getting on deck, raising the sails, and setting out to sea. Because if Illidan was right about what bait in Azeroth could attract this kind of beholder... it would probably be Medivh's family and their magical library. And Khadgar was practically a walking, breathing, youth-version embodiment of all those elements.

"Thank you, Illidan Stormrage." Jace switched back to the elven tongue. "I think I know what to do."

"Well then..." Maiev Shadowsong was clearly eager to be done.

"'Thank you'?" The Demon Hunter tilted his head slightly. "You asked me many questions, human. A poorly-pronounced 'thank you' is far from enough repayment."

"How about you answer a question for me too?"

"Alright," Jace agreed.

"Why?" Illidan's voice was hoarse and low, like some kind of shadowy conspiracy. "I've never been to the human kingdoms, but I can tell from your description that your people don't like demonic power either. Don't like the Void. In short, they have their own opinions about the benefits and drawbacks of power... and they might even get along well with my brother. Am I right?"

"You could say that," Jace replied.

"Then you and I must have something in common. Except..." Illidan paced back and forth inside his cell. "Except you haven't been locked in a place like this yet."

Jace didn't respond. Illidan smiled, then suddenly took a step toward the bars. "So why? Why do you fight these demons? What drives you? Why go up against the Nathrezim?"

"The hive of these cunning demons — a place called Nathreza — is the treasure trove of dark knowledge and deep secrets in the Twisting Nether. Even Argus cannot compare. I'm sure you've heard of it. Don't you want to go to that mysterious place and have a look, before your short life burns out?"

"That's enough, Illidan!"

Maiev almost roared. The sound of Warden steel boots striking stone tiles echoed from the outer passage. Armor and blades scraped and clanged.

"Illidan..."

Tyrande took a step forward, as if to stop something. But the Warden shouted at her, "Stand back, High Priestess!"

"Why?" Illidan ignored all the chaos and pressed on. "To save the world? Save it. I can sense this world hasn't treated you well. That's not a reason that could convince me. Or is it for someone? Someone you cannot let go of, no matter how many disasters and pains you endure..."

"If you have what you came for, Sesso..." Tyrande looked toward him. "We can leave now. Your friends are waiting outside. I think we've been here long enough. We've already interfered with the Wardens' duties."

"Thank you, High Priestess. But..."

"You don't have to answer his question, human," Maiev warned.

Jace looked at the pale green glow in the Warden's eyes through her great helm. He waited until her tension and vigilance had eased slightly, then turned back toward the cage.

"The reason might be simpler than you think." His tone was calm. "Kil'jaeden didn't give me a choice. He wanted me dead. He wanted... to destroy everything I have, to take my soul. And I — I want to keep living."

The smile on Illidan's face slowly stiffened. "You've met the Deceiver."

"In a manner of speaking," Jace said.

Hearing Jace's words, Maiev Shadowsong slowly lowered her hand. Several Wardens stood at the entrance, having apparently prepared to forcibly remove the two visitors from the dungeon. The Night Elf in the cage was silent for a moment, then said, "If it really was the Deceiver, he would have appreciated your talent and power, human."

"That might have been the case," Jace said. "Until I shattered the Scepter of Sargeras with my own hands and burned that bridge. Now I have no choice but to fight to the end."

Maiev Shadowsong whipped her head around to look at Priestess Tyrande. Tyrande nodded slightly at her. The other Wardens exchanged glances among themselves.

"Is that reason enough for you, Illidan Stormrage?" Jace asked.

"It is sufficient." Illidan spoke in the demonic tongue. "Then I wish you enough luck to keep living, Sesso."

"Thank you." Jace replied in the same tongue. "And I hope I live to see the day you walk out of this prison in the light, with your people's understanding."
