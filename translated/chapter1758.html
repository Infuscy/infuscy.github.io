Chapter 1758: Farewell to Hyjal

Walking through the tunnels of the Wildclaw Den, listening to the distant echoes of beastly growls from gods knew where, Jace replayed Illidan Stormrage's final question in his head.

Even with Malfurion Stormrage's runic cage blocking a clear read of Illidan's thoughts through shadow energy fluctuations, he could still feel it — this demon hunter had always seen Sargeras as the ultimate "prey," from ten thousand years ago right up to this moment.

Yes, he knew what happened in the other timeline. He was supposed to already know exactly what Illidan was really after, skulking through shadows and slipping between worlds and planes.

But once you set foot in reality, walked into that deep prison, and faced a creature so complicated and unpredictable... he couldn't be sure of the bastard's true intentions at first.

Not when everyone called him a traitor, a criminal — even Tyrande and his brother Malfurion, the people he treasured most. And the line between shadow's master and shadow's slave was so damn blurry. Jace understood that better than most people in this world.

Just like Illidan couldn't trust him at first either.

But in the end, especially after talking with him, Jace could still sense the things that were certain.

Once he left this great den, Jace had no idea if he'd ever see Illidan Stormrage again. He hoped not — who knew what kind of catastrophe would force the night elves to finally let him out.

"Thank you, High Priestess," Jace said as they walked. "Thank you and Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage for allowing me to meet with Illidan Stormrage. Facing an invasion of Nathrezim, my kin on the other side and I were truly at our wit's end."

He turned and raised his voice to Maiev Shadowsong, who was following at a distance. "And thank you too, Warden. Thank you for trusting us."

The Warden said nothing, only nodded slightly.

"And I should thank you too, Jace Sesso," Tyrande said.

"This meeting brought back many memories. It reminded me of what he used to be like — before he walked down this path of no return, back when all he wanted was to be a great mage."

"Careful, Priestess," Maiev spoke from behind. "Is this fair to the Kaldorei he hurt or killed? The War of the Ancients is over. The demons were driven back to the Twisting Nether. And he caused that calamity for his own selfish reasons."

"I know, Maiev." Tyrande turned back. "I know he deserves what he's gotten. But you must admit — he never betrayed us."

"I never denied he was once a hero," Maiev said.

Tyrande looked at her for a moment, then said nothing more.

When they reached the entrance of the cell block the Wardens guarded, before Jace could even step into that beast-stinking cavern hall, Vereesa rushed up to him.

"How did it go, Jace?"

She finally let out a small breath when she saw him emerge, glancing between Jace and the two night elf leaders behind him. "You were in there a long time, High Priestess, Warden."

"It dragged on a bit," Jace said. "Getting information out of that legendary criminal about how to deal with the Nathrezim wasn't exactly simple."

"So did you get anywhere?" Grede couldn't wait. "Did that Yudi... whatever... put some dark curse on you?"

"It's Illidan," Sora corrected from behind.

"Whatever," Grede muttered.

"We got somewhere, Grede," Jace said. "But we might need to go back to the other continent before we can start preparing. At least we need to find Khadgar first."

He glanced back at Maiev Shadowsong. "It won't work here."

Vereesa, Sora, and Grede exchanged looks. They were probably thinking about the failed Kalelez communication ritual from before, and could guess what kind of method Jace meant by "won't work here."

"It's not that I doubt Illidan Stormrage's skill at hunting demons," Vereesa said. "But how can we be sure his methods work on Nathrezim? We don't even know where any dreadlords are right now, or who they might be disguised as."

"Don't worry about that," Jace said. "As long as we can get Khadgar's help, there's a Nathrezim named Kasanathir imprisoned in the Violet Hold. If the method works on him, we're golden."

"That fake old man Khadgar sure drew the short straw knowing you, Jace Sesso," Grede said.

"I'll buy him a meal to make up for it," Jace said.

And this is just the beginning, he thought. That mage has way worse times coming.

"So should we leave as soon as possible and head back to the Eastern Kingdoms?" Sora said. "I'm ready whenever. After all this time at Mount Hyjal, it feels like all the weariness from the journey has melted away."

"I can feel that Nordrassil likes you, Sora," Mossa said.

"Why don't I feel anything?" Needleflame squeezed in to ask. "Doesn't this big tree want to bless me?"

"Because when Nordrassil was born, there was no dragon like you in this world yet — a black dragon so rational and kind. It doesn't know you yet," Tyrande said.

One sentence from the High Priestess and the little black dragon could barely hold back her smile. Jace thought, thank god she didn't blurt out "your biological grandfather almost murdered the dragons and people who grew this tree, bless you, dream on" — otherwise they could kiss their return tickets goodbye.

"Really? But that's ten thousand years," Needleflame said. "Ten thousand years... and it never met a black dragon like me?"

Tyrande let out a soft breath. "Ten thousand years pass very quickly, young Princess Fidegonys. You'll understand someday."

The black dragon nodded, half-understanding. She might have learned a lot from Sabellian and Jace's crew, but some things about age and time — those you had to experience for yourself.

"Well, we should prepare to head back," Jace said. "Thank you so much, High Priestess. Please give my regards and apologies to Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage. I know letting an outsider see Illidan Stormrage is extremely risky and taboo. I'm truly grateful."

"The Burning Legion is the enemy of us all," Tyrande said. "And these taboos were created to protect this world in the first place. Though I am surprised you're planning to leave so soon."

"We've been away from the other continent for some time now, High Priestess," Vereesa said. "The demons could act at any moment during that time, and our people over there don't know the new intelligence we've gathered here. So to minimize the danger, every day we save matters."

Tyrande nodded. Grede patted Mossa Mu-ji's robe and said, "Thanks for the hospitality, High Priestess Tyrande. I never thought there'd be elves this friendly to my kind in this world. If I get the chance, I'll visit often — especially while Mossa's here studying."

"Don't expect me to give you rides then," Sora muttered.

"I'll have Gorjet then. Who do you think gets to carry the Mountain King on his mountain patrols?" Grede shot back.

"Then you'd better not taint your grand title by riding me on the way back, Mountain King," Sora said.

Grede immediately changed his tune with a placating grin. "Hey now, come on — the way back's all ocean. I'm not the Ocean King, am I, Lady Somelier?"

"Illidan was a proud, arrogant mage," Tyrande said quietly, looking at Jace. "Since I've known him, I've hardly ever seen him genuinely admire anyone — except his brother. I know he has plenty of complaints about Malfurion, but deep down, I know he loves his twin."

"But I could tell he admired you, Jace Sesso. Maybe because you have something in common with him. More importantly, you've given everything, like he once did, just to keep the Burning Legion out of this world. I'll tell Malfurion everything that happened today — what Illidan and you discussed. I believe he'll be happy to hear it too."

"Hopefully the Archdruid won't decide to ban me from ever setting foot on Mount Hyjal again," Jace joked.

"You don't know him well enough. He would never think that," Tyrande said with a smile.

(End of Volume)
