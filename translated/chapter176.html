Chapter 176: A Deadly Trump Card

Vereesa flipped backward twice in quick succession, putting distance between herself. Teron swung his arm, trying to channel a spell, but Vereesa severed his casting with a torrent of arcane energy.

Teron coughed violently, snarled a curse, and his muscles flushed purple again as shadow power crawled across them. He leaped at Vereesa, hammer swinging — she sidestepped cleanly and loosed an arrow on the retreat. Teron shattered it with his vambrace.

The death knight was faster than before. Vereesa darted back and forth, looking for an opening, but none of her strikes found anything vital. The two of them seemed to have reached a stalemate.

Teron was running on necromantic energy, fighting like one of those tireless corpses — from the very first exchange to now, neither wounds nor spellcasting had slowed him down by a single beat. But could Vereesa keep this up?

Kaelfal had gone quiet a long time ago, badly wounded, and if Jace didn't do something soon, things were going to turn very ugly.

Jace gripped the staff lodged in his left shoulder, sweating from the pain. But pain or not, it had to come out.

"One, two, three — aagh!"

"Nnngh…"

Jace curled up for a moment, stringing together a beautiful chain of Chinese curses. His shoulder burned and throbbed with swelling, but the rest of him was freezing. The puddles on the ground were full of blood, and thin streaks of it still clung to his hands, the staff, and his shoulder, washed pale by the rain.

Thank god the staff was thin and the head had already broken off. If there'd been a gemstone or anything on it, the impact would have shattered his whole arm.

He glanced up and realized the arcane glow in the sky had stopped flickering at some point. Without the shield, the roaring of the black dragonflight was clearer and more terrifying than before.

A thought surfaced in Jace's mind. The Eye of the Arcane was already gone. If Teron Gorefiend was here, then who had stolen it?

Jace clenched his right fist and felt he still had some strength left — but the moment he tried to form another incantation, a wave of agony crashed through his skull. A night of nonstop spellcasting and chaos had burned through every last ounce of his energy.

His warlock's satchel was nowhere near him, and he had no mana potions. Worse, Teron was slowly gaining the upper hand against Vereesa. This Shadow Council leader, heir to Gul'dan's legacy, didn't just wield dark magic — in melee he was terrifying as a berserker.

Vereesa drove her twin blades through Gorefiend's armor and body again and again, but couldn't land a telling blow. As ghost-quick as she was, if Teron caught her even once, she was dead on the spot.

If she went down too, he was finished. Was this the final act? If he didn't play his trump card now, there wouldn't be a next chapter.

Jace spoke an incantation he'd scribbled in the corner of his spellbook — one he'd flipped past a hundred times but never once dared to utter. This was the first time he'd ever spoken the spell out loud, completely and deliberately.

The moment the words left his mouth, shadow energy across the sky surged toward him like a pack of wolves starved beyond reason. Even the loose shadow power that Teron's wild magic had scattered into the air closed in around Jace.

But once they drew near, they didn't do anything. Jace felt like a lamb surrounded by wolves — the shadow pressed in, watching him, but not feeding, as if mocking him.

Had he mispronounced it? Luhelawenketa, Lualajiputa… He muttered it again, this time carefully following the Nathrezim pronunciation Sazeno had taught him.

All at once, the shadow power lunged at his skin, his face, smashing through layer after layer of his body's outer defenses! And the shadow dormant inside him seemed to go mad at the same time, devouring everything it could reach!

Jace straightened up. He couldn't even describe the pain anymore — but soon the escalating agony was drowned out by a surge of killing intent that swelled up from somewhere deep inside him.

The whites of his eyes had turned purple. Dark veins crawled across his skin. Purple-tinged blood trickled from the corners of his mouth and his palms. And he had never felt more alive.

This time Jace stopped resisting. The Shadow Maiden drew him into her embrace, and her wicked dagger pierced his neck, carving a bloody line. And he savored every second of it.

It was like running his fingers through the blood and pus of a wound and bringing them to his mouth, eyes closed, savoring the sickly sweetness. In that pleasure that nearly swallowed his reason, the two elves, Teron Gorefiend, the black dragons circling overhead, the Kirin Tor mages dueling them, the guards running panic-stricken across the ground — all of them were nothing but a swarm of chittering insects, grating on his nerves.

Jace raised Teron's staff. The moment his bloodstained hand closed around it, the weapon eagerly erupted with arcane light and belched shadowfire. Even the rags wrapped around it caught fire.

He fixed his gaze on the fiend hammering away at the elf woman and spoke the Blood Drain incantation once more. This time his voice sounded like a real demon fresh out of the Twisting Nether — even the trills that humans should have found easy came out strained and wrong, twisting the spell into something alien and unrecognizable.

Maybe his hearing was going. No matter — the spell had fired. The corpse-black flesh of Teron Gorefiend's arm suddenly began to swell and blister. He halted his assault in a panic and turned to stare at Jace. The smile was gone from his face now. He frantically spoke another incantation and thrust out his hand — but this time his staff gave him nothing.

"Betrayal—!" Teron roared, but Vereesa gave him no time to think. She drove a vicious stab into his jaw.

Teron gagged and spat a mouthful of black blood. Vereesa pressed for another strike, but Gorefiend snatched her by the throat with one hand and lifted her off the ground! He tried to throttle the slight elf, but with his vitality being drained away, his strength was failing — he could barely hold on!

Vereesa thrashed and stabbed at Gorefiend's arm again and again. Teron cursed wildly in Orcish, still trying to fight, and the two of them grappled for a long, ugly moment — but Teron was clearly running out of steam. Finally he gave up, bellowed, and hurled Vereesa off the roof. His whole body dissolved into a bolt of fel shadow and shot into the sky, vanishing into the night.

What was that? Jace felt the spell he was channeling suddenly lose its anchor, dissipating into the air. Without any support, he sat down hard. This hadn't been magic — it was Teron Gorefiend's soul.

The shadowlight faded. Jace watched the enormous shell rapidly wither until it could no longer stand and toppled off the roof. A lifeless thud echoed from below.

Jace pushed himself up and realized his shoulder hardly hurt anymore. He reached over — a hole, his fingers flinched. It had that strange numbness, like being under anesthesia in surgery.

Had the shadow already devoured his body? Was he going to turn into a shadow creature, just like the imp had warned? Jace didn't want to think about it any further. He made his way to Kaelfal, who was slumped against a wall on another rooftop, coughing in pain, his right hand weakly clutching the bloody wound below his ribs. The grazing blow from Teron's war hammer had nearly killed him.

"Kaelfal, hold on a little longer. I'll get help — at least don't die saving my life."

Kaelfal gurgled something and managed, "You think I wanted to save you? If I'd known I was chasing Teron Gorefiend himself, I—" He stopped mid-sentence, his eyes landing on Jace's shoulder, and swallowed the rest.

Jace was too tired to argue. He'd just gotten to his feet when Kaelfal coughed again and panted, "Where's Vereesa?"

"She fell."

"Go check on her… hurry, or I really am done for."

"Professor Kaelfal—"

Vereesa came running up from halfway across and pulled a bottle from her pouch, pressing it to Kaelfal's lips. He immediately widened his eyes and took a small sip, then let out a huge breath. Color rushed back to his face. So the reason he'd been worried about Vereesa was that she had a healing potion… the bastard…

Jace figured the two of them could manage, so he stood back up. He'd already wasted too much time — he had to find Grede. But he'd barely taken a few steps before his body convulsed and he vomited a spray of greenish filth onto the ground. He stared at the puddle of green slime. This was bad.

He hadn't tried to drain that earlier death knight precisely because Jace knew these half-dead bastards couldn't have anything good inside them. What he'd just thrown up had neither a shred of life force nor much shadow energy — just pure necrotic corruption, actively harmful to a living human being.

Gul'dan's death knights — even Teron Gorefiend — hadn't bonded perfectly with their human hosts. They weren't much different from a bunch of soul-possessed abominations. Draining them was never going to end well… and honestly, Jace had seen it coming. He just hadn't had a choice. Still, at least the agony of being tortured by necrotic filth proved he was still flesh and blood, not a shadow creature.

A bottle appeared at his lips.

"Here," Vereesa said.

Jace glanced down at it, hunched over. The liquid inside seemed transparent, though he couldn't quite pin down the color. Strange. Was this a healing potion?

"You're in bad shape. Drink."

There was a hint of displeasure in the elf's tone — probably at Jace's slightly suspicious look, but he was just exhausted from throwing up.

He grabbed it and knocked back the remaining half in one go. Vereesa's eyes went wide with surprise. She looked like she wanted to say something, but the words never came. In the end she just stiffly took the empty bottle back.

"Thank you, I… I still need to find my friend."

The moment he opened his mouth, it reeked of dead flesh. He didn't want to say another word.

Jace walked to the roof's edge, looking for a way down. When he'd sprinted over here earlier, all his attention had been on Gorefiend and Kaelfal. Now the thought of leaping from rooftop to rooftop was out of the question — he wasn't a goddamn stuntman.

"Hey, warlock! Catch."

Jace heard Vereesa's voice and turned. He caught the saber she tossed him in his right hand — one of her two weapons. A gleaming bright-silver blade with a guard curved like golden-green vines. The weapon was so finely crafted it looked like a work of art rather than a tool of killing. But the way Vereesa had been using it to carve through Teron Gorefiend's armor moments ago proved its destructive power was anything but ordinary.

"Don't die. Remember to give it back."
