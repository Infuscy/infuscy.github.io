## Chapter 1760: Closing In

"The first time I met you, Sora, I thought you were some kind of terrifying forest witch — like the ones my dad used to tell me about, deep in the Hinterlands. Evil troll witch doctors. Hard to say who's worse between those and the Amani headhunters and voodoo priests of Zul'Sin... but anyway, enemies on the battlefield always feel more straightforward. The ones who lurk alone are way creepier."

"When you mention me, could you maybe find some nicer words?"

"It's not that Grede's trying to be mean. My first impression of you was pretty much the same, Sora... standing in that doorway, staring at me and Jace like we were the Silverwing Sentinels we'd just managed to shake off — except you had our kind of face."

"Sora's clearly very cute."

"Her mortal form is pretty, little one. But pretty and dangerous aren't mutually exclusive. Same goes for you."

"That's because Mossa was missing and I was desperate for help. I was just... worried. Besides, you lot looked like a bunch of shady mercenaries. I had to establish some authority..."

"Yeah, understandable. But it turned out you were just a slow-reacting giant green goose. The whiplash was real."

"I swear, Grede, you have a real way with words. Even with Sora's good temper... ah, Jace is back."

"See that, Mossa? Sora's face was exactly like that. And wouldn't you know it, she was standing in the doorway too." Grede patted Mossa's wrist and looked at Jace Sesso standing silhouetted against the light by the entrance. "What's the situation? You look terrible, Jace. Couldn't afford the postage?"

Jace walked over and sat down. "No, it's not that. I heard some nasty rumors at the goblin post office. About me."

"Someone's pinned King Terenas II's illness on me. Linked it to Prince Arthas's coming-of-age ceremony."

"I don't get it," Grede said. "Didn't Khadgar say the King of Lordaeron was doing much better? How'd you get dragged into it?"

"He did improve," Jace said quietly. "But from what he said, King Terenas's health has always been fragile. Even without getting sick, he was never in great shape. He didn't say the king had fully recovered. I'm guessing he doesn't know the full picture either. That goblin was pretty vague — he didn't even know whether I actually attended the ceremony. He couldn't say who told the Steamwheedle Cartel, or how the rumor started."

"None of this was happening before we left for Kalimdor," Vereesa said. "Several Lordaeron nobles attended Lord Lothar's funeral, and Terenas himself sent envoys with letters congratulating us on the Blackrock Spire victory. He didn't seem seriously ill at all."

"What I mean is, he fell sick almost two years ago, right after we got back from Draenor. Why are these rumors suddenly circulating again?"

The ranger's brow was furrowed. Grede didn't look too happy either. The relaxed atmosphere from their casual reminiscing evaporated in an instant, because everyone knew the answer to Vereesa's question.

"It's the demons," Needleflame said softly.

"But is this the same illness as before?" Vereesa asked. "People said the king caught it from mosquito bites in the wetlands around Menethil Harbor... If it was the demons, did they ambush the king at Menethil Harbor and cast a curse on him, or did they just recently exploit his illness to smear Jace?"

"If this was planned from the start," Sora said, "that means Kil'jaeden's demons never really counted on Jace losing to Prince Nefarian at Blackrock Spire?"

Vereesa pressed her hand hard over her eyes and let out a long breath. "Then it means my guess was right all along. Blackrock Spire was nothing but a tool to frame Jace from the very beginning."

"Or maybe they just hedged their bets," Grede said. "One group of demons helping the Blackwing at Blackrock Spire, another group going north to spread rumors about black knights in Dalaran and Lordaeron, trying to climb the Kirin Tor ranks like Onyxia said. If the Blackrock plan failed, they'd go all in on the north... Five demons, plenty to split into two teams."

"I think you're underestimating our enemy, Grede," Vereesa said.

"I'm just guessing a possibility, damn it. I'm not trying to underestimate anyone." Grede pressed his forehead with his palm, voice low. "Then what? You're saying we never escaped the Deceiver's palm even after coming back from Draenor? That we've been played the whole time, little elf? Is that the version you prefer?"

"As you can see, I've basically completed my mission. The ending just wasn't perfect. Either way, what happens next has nothing to do with me. Good luck, Jace Sesso."

A goblin barmaid walked over. Jace clenched Pitaselun's soul shard in his fist. The demon's final words still echoed in his ears.

"Can I get you something to drink, sir?" the barmaid asked with a smile. Something to drink? Blood? Mana?

Are you a Nathrezim too? Did you spread the news to these goblins?

Have you already laid a trap on the other continent, waiting for me to jump right in, and now you can't even wait to disguise yourself as some ridiculous goblin waitress and watch me squirm?

The barmaid pressed her lips together, looking a bit lost. She sneaked a glance at the others at the table. Everyone was staring at Jace.

"Just some hot drink. Whatever's fine," Jace said.

"Right away, sir!" The barmaid nodded and hurried off. The room seemed to resume its flow of time.

"Jace?" Vereesa asked. "You okay?" "I was just thinking about Blackrock Spire," Jace said.

"Everyone," Mossa said. "Since this king you mentioned was already sick before, and nobody blamed Jace, why did that suddenly change... What evidence do these humans have?"

"What evidence do demons need to fabricate lies, Mossa?" Vereesa shook her head. "They've already spread rumors about black horses and scythe-wielding death knights in the north. Fabricating a story about a southern warlock poisoning a northern king to create division in the Alliance isn't exactly hard."

"Haven't you heard the imp's voice, Jace?" Grede said. "If Mount Hyjal could block demonic power, we've left it far enough behind by now. You should be able to hear it."

"Nothing so far," Jace said.

"Is the timing normal? How long's it been since you last contacted your demon servant with that array?" Grede asked.

"A long time." Jace looked toward the kitchen. "I'll perform the communication ritual here in Winterspring."

Finding a live, bleeding beast in Winterspring wasn't exactly easy. Jace just bought some bear blood from the inn's cooks. The shadow of rage buried in those brutal giants' bodies actually made it a better medium for demonic communication.

He went a distance from Everlook to a high hill where the cross-dimensional ritual wouldn't bring shadow pollution to the town. Jace expertly drew the array, set out the soul shards, and chanted the incantation.

This time he wasn't so casual. He controlled the shadow energy inside him as carefully as possible, improving efficiency. Not because he lacked energy — but because it slightly reduced the chance of shadow energy rebounding and disturbing his own mind.

"Kalelez?" "Yes, master."

"From the communication ritual's senses, it seems you've returned to the Twisting Nether." "Yes, master. I've returned to the Telogrus Rift."

"I told you to find a mage to contact Khadgar at the Kirin Tor. Has he come?"

"Neither Khadgar nor the Kirin Tor have responded yet... But two days ago, a human claiming to be your friend — named Mathias Shaw — came to the Rift with the help of a human warlock from the Dagger Syndicate. He asked me to pass along a message: the Kirin Tor has sent envoys to Stormwind, requesting the king allow Violet Eye agents to investigate the Ilgala Tower. The king refused. He hopes you'll return as soon as possible if you receive this news."

Jace watched the bear blood burning away under the torment of the demon lord's voice, and replied in demonic tongue, "I understand, Kalelez."
