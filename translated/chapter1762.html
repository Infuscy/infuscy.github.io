Chapter 1762: Hope to Meet Again

Near Everlook, Jace and the others packed their gear and prepared to set off.

Along the way, Vereesa kept an eye on the mercenaries and travelers passing by — looking for anyone who might have just arrived from the other continent, listening for any mention of Dalaran, the plague, or Jace.

But most of the humans, dwarves, and even elves who made it to Everlook were long-term Kalimdor residents. Their closest connection to the other side was Booty Bay. Forget Dalaran — they didn't even have topics related to the other continent. Not surprising.

"Hearing about the troubles you're facing on the other side, I really wish I could cross the Great Sea with you, Jace." Mossa said as they walked. "If the Wind Prince has promised to stop answering the centaurs' summons, that means the Kolkar and Galak raiders will have a harder time hunting our scouts and wardens with elemental help. So right now, my people need me less than you do, Jace. And if the plague the dreadlords created is similar to what the Winterspring furbolgs caught, I can help too."

"Whether it's a tauren or a lioness, either one would be way too conspicuous in Dalaran, Mossa." Jace said. "Besides, you can still help us from here. Go back to Ashenvale and tell High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind about the situation we might face on the other continent."

"I will." Mossa said.

"And," Jace smiled, "help me find a village suitable for humans to live in, just in case."

"I'd rather you come to our village as a guest or to live because you chose to," Mossa said, "not because the situation on the other side forced you into it."

"Don't feel bad about it, Mossa." Grede shifted Sulfuras on his back. "If I'm with him, you're with him too."

He looked around at the group. "Honestly, everyone, when I came to Kalimdor this time, I wasn't planning on going back."

"But look at the situation now. Demons, plague, a Kirin Tor that's lost its mind. I can't just let it go without teaching them a lesson — showing them who's been looking out for them all these years. Maybe I don't deserve the 'Mountain King' crown. At least not compared to the legacy Anvilmars left behind. But I sure as hell don't want to end up labeled as a demon's accomplice or the source of a plague outbreak."

"At least I agree with Grede on this." Vereesa looked at the tauren. "And I swear this in the name of the Farstrider Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas, and as a member of the Windrunner family — my wedding to Jace Sesso will not be canceled for any reason. When the time comes, I'll ask the magisters to send an invitation to your chieftain."

"And we will certainly attend, Vereesa." Mossa said.

"Will you be able to invite Chieftain Cairne Bloodhoof?" Jace asked.

"I'll mention it to him, Jace." Mossa nodded. "He knows a lot about you — about how you rescued me from the satyr demons in Winterspring. I haven't had the chance to tell him about the Wind Prince's good news in person. If he hears it, I believe he'd be willing to cross the continent for your wedding."

"Why are you so interested in this tauren chieftain?" Grede asked Jace. "You've been quiet all this time, and now that."

"Can't I be curious about a chieftain I've never met?" Jace surveyed the surrounding pines. "I look forward to his arrival, Mossa."

The tauren walked over and pulled Jace into a hug, his voice low. "I've always thought you were a good human, Jace. And I believe your people — even those who refuse to trust you — if they ever got to know you the way I do, they'd see I'm right."

"I may be a young druid — not an archdruid or prophet or priestess who can see past and future — even if many tauren druid apprentices see me as their guide and teacher. But I know, deep down I believe, that you will escape all this eventually. And eventually... you'll get what you want."

"Thank you." Jace patted the tauren's thick arm and looked up. "I'll remember your words."

"Get what he wants? Like a gold vault he can swim in?" Grede muttered.

"Ow!" The dwarf's beard was yanked by Vereesa, his head twisting sideways.

A heavy sound rumbled through the air — the rustle of grass and the tinkle of wind chimes that had no business being in this snowy valley.

If any beasts or even chimeras were watching from the surrounding peaks and forests, they'd probably be terrified. A jade dragon that almost never appeared in mortal sight had just shown up next to a goblin town?

Unless you were standing right there, you couldn't fully appreciate how much Sora had changed over these years.

Compared to when they'd first set out for the southern end of Winterspring looking for Uzallus, she was now close to a full-grown green dragon. Her emerald scales made the pines on the nearby slopes seem to lose their color. The bulb on her tail had grown into proper spines — no longer anything like a corncob.

Of course, her horns weren't as massive as her elders' — especially the ancient green dragons' — reaching up like branches of Nordrassil. Even in her dragon form, she looked more like a young girl in a green gown sitting quietly on the ground than a magical beast.

But compared to the giant green snake-lizard she'd been when they first met, the blessings of Queens Ysera and Alexstrasza had made her practically unrecognizable.

If Sora didn't insist on turning the scepter Jace gave her into a purple crystal pendant on her chest every time she transformed, Jace would've thought Isendrel was visiting Winterspring again.

As for Needleflame — the size gap between her and Sora was no longer as huge as it'd been when she first escaped the spiderweb in Terokkar Forest, like an adult and a child.

The black dragon nestled beside her green dragon sister, staring at Jace Sesso. But when Jace shifted his gaze toward her, she immediately turned her head away, stretching and folding her black-and-red wings, flicking her golden corncob tail tip — like a single look made her uncomfortable all over.

Unlike Sora, Miss Fidegonys showed off a full set of sharp horns when she lifted her head. Even at this age, those horns were already as wide and intricate as a crown. Jace was instantly reminded of her uncle Nefarian — and they looked different from when he'd seen them at Blackrock Spire.

That was probably thanks to the power Deathwing left her. Months could bring visible growth. But Jace also believed Onyxia, as her newly recognized mother, had given Needleflame more than just a name.

On another snowy slope, Vereesa hugged Mossa goodbye, then picked up her longbow Belanur and hurried toward Sora. Grede was still complaining about her rough handling of his beautiful black beard while reluctantly hugging Mossa farewell. Those two probably still had plenty to say to each other.

Watching all this, the warlock felt dazed. What did Jace Sesso really want?

Fame, power, influence, wealth... or... vindication? He wasn't sure.

What he could be certain of right now was that he only wanted these companions in front of him, and the family and friends not here — the people who cared about him, who looked after him — to be happy, to live in peace in this world.

And no one was going to meddle with that. Not orcs, not black dragons, not demons. No one.

"Sesso!" The black dragon snorted two plumes of smoke with glowing embers. The roar shook a thick layer of snow off the nearby pines.

"What are you standing there gawking at, Sesso? Are we going or not?!"

"Going! Going! Right now, sorry, coming!" Jace patted the snow off his hood and jogged over.

"Goodbye, Jace! Goodbye, everyone!"

"Goodbye, Mossa! Don't forget to come to me and Jace Sesso's wedding!"

"I won't forget!"

One large and one small dragon slowly rose, kicking up spiraling clouds of snow. The gusts bent the pines on both sides. Mossa, shrinking below along with the trees, snowbanks, and Everlook town, couldn't help raising his hand to shield his eyes.

At the edge of the horizon, Mount Hyjal and Nordrassil were just faint outlines against the gray sky.

Goodbye, Kalimdor. Hope to meet again.
