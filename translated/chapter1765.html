Chapter 1765: A Change of Image

One green and one black young dragon flew over Baron Silverlaine's castle. The wind from their wings pressed down the treetops. But not a single guard, passing caravan, or patrolling cavalryman looked up. Well, maybe someone did look up. Jace couldn't tell. The Dream Pendant of Queen Ysera made everything outside itsscope hazy — like looking through a water's surface, only able to make out outlines. Sora channeled the pendant's magic from the lead. Needleflame stayed close behind to remain within the pendant's cover and keep her invisibility. The vast Silverpine Forest provided unimaginable power for the pendant. Add in the fact that a real green dragon was activating it — the area it covered and the invisibility effect from the dream energy were even stronger than when they'd used it to avoid black dragon scouts in Dustwallow Marsh. Unless these Gilneans were more sensitive to dragon magic than Onyxia's dragonman guards, they wouldn't see a thing. Jace wondered if Aegwynn happened to be nearby again, would she still be able to strip their illusion at a glance? If this were a game, standing at Shadowfang Keep — or Silverlaine's Castle — they should already be able to see the Greymane Wall to the south. But in reality, it was still a hundred miles away. Even without the mist that blanketed all of Silverpine, it probably wasn't visible. The Greymane Wall had been under construction for ten years now. And from the latest news Jace had heard, King Genn Greymane had no plans to stop. Even with the wall's enormous cost. Even though Blackrock Spire and Grim Batol had fallen to the Alliance. He still insisted on leaving the Alliance and believed this wall would one day shield the entire peninsula from catastrophic disaster. If a few months ago, when Blackrock Spire had just fallen, the Alliance's leaders could still laugh at King Genn's excessive caution and dismiss this great wall as a complete waste of resources... well, now that the rumors of plague and death knights hadn't faded with the defeat at Blackrock Spire and the black dragon, Jace wondered if those kings and nobles were still laughing. At least Jace wasn't. This wasn't Jace's first time in Ambermill, so he wasn't completely unfamiliar with the area. He'd come by carriage before. From above, he could still recognize the landmarks he'd memorized. Seeing large clearings among the pines ahead meant the logging camp wasn't far. Blurred purple and blue figures on the road were becoming more frequent. Ambermill was close. The two dragons landed in a dense pine forest. After making sure no hunters or passing woodcutters were around, they let Jace and the others dismount and dropped the Dream Pendant's veil. Without Morofil around, quietly dealing with an innocent witness would be much more troublesome. "We only have one polymorph stone left." Grede said. "And it doesn't have much power. No lava around either... Not sure why it ran out so fast this time." "Maybe turning a Wildhammer into a Dark Iron dwarf didn't use that much energy," Jace said, "but turning one into a tauren would be different." "No, no, Wildhammer to Dark Iron would take way more energy." Grede said. "There's a fundamental difference between us and those shameless bastards. Maybe they're not even descendants of the mountain and the earth. Maybe Kaz'goroth the Mountain Lord slept with one of Ragnaros's daughters or something..." "All that storytelling won't restore any mana to this stone." Vereesa said. "And were you planning to walk into Ambermill as a Dark Iron dwarf? People's minds have opened up a lot since the Dark Portal, but not that much." "I think if you just trim your beard a bit and change your cloak, you'll be fine." Jace said. "Don't tell me you Wildhammer boys can recognize every face by those tiny eyes of yours." "We actually can." Grede said. "At least half the time." "So that's the same as not recognizing anyone." Vereesa said flatly. "Not everyone has magic eyes like you elves. But point taken, Jace." The dwarf said. "What about you? You're Lordaeronian. What's your plan?" Vereesa asked Jace. "Better worry about yourself, little elf." Grede said. "This guy's so shy he barely goes to any banquets. I bet the common folk of Lordaeron still think he's some red-bearded, black-cloaked, scar-faced half-demon old warlock. Look at him now — a mess of hair, pitiful excuse for a mustache that you can't tell from armpit or chest hair, pushing seven feet tall. They'd mistake him for an ancient Arathi barbarian before anything else. Throw in that fake old man Khadgar and they look like long-lost brothers. Just ditch that fake dragonscale cloak." "Real dragonscale," Jace said. "Try telling that to Vaelastrasz. Don't bother with me, Jace Sesso." Grede shot him a look. "Jace..." The green dragon behind him sighed. "Sorry, Sora." Jace glanced back. "I'm fine." Vereesa expertly tied on a face veil, then pulled up her hood. Many rangers operating around Lake Lordamere dressed like this. They didn't usually like dealing with humans, so a mask wasn't particularly suspicious. "Thank the Light for Lordaeron's cool summer weather," Jace said. "Otherwise your hair would be stuck to your face in no time in that getup." "Thanks for the concern. I don't sweat nearly as much as you do." Vereesa muttered. "Right, what about your silver hair?" Grede pointed at the elf ranger's hood. "Quel'Thalas silver hair is more common than you think." Vereesa said. Sora lowered her head and asked, "Should I take human form or quel'dorei form? I'm quite skilled at human form now. I don't think anyone in Dalaran except Prince Korialstrasz could easily tell... and he should be in Northrend by now." "Don't, Sora." Vereesa said. "Stay as quel'dorei." "Won't quel'dorei stand out too much?" Jace asked. "Sora's human form is plenty eye-catching too, Jace Sesso. You're a human male — you should know that better than I do." The ranger crossed her arms. "Besides, in Lordaeron, two quel'dorei walking around together is much more normal than one. The Farstriders here always patrol in pairs or threes." "Alright then." The green dragon kicked up a cloud of pink mist like moth dust. When it cleared, she was back in elven form. But this time, instead of her green robe, she wore a brown traveling tunic. She really did look more like a young Farstrider apprentice out on patrol. With Vereesa as the mentor. The mist hadn't just taken the green dragon — the black dragon was gone too. Needleflame jumped out from a nearby bush, brushed off her dark gray cloak, and announced, "I'm done transforming too!" "That look won't work." Jace said. "Too many people recognize you. As a black dragon, they'll definitely remember that face. There might be Sons of Lothar or Blackrock Spire veterans in Ambermill." "Then what do I do?" Needleflame asked. "I can't turn into an orc. Well, I'm good at turning into orcs." "Might as well go join Orgrim Doomhammer's rebellion in the forest as an orc, kid." Grede looked her up and down. "Can't you turn a little lighter? You look like a child laborer fresh out of a coal mine." "What's wrong with being dark?! Dark is beautiful!" Needleflame's eyes went wide. "Then why's your human mom so pale?" Grede's eyes went wider. "Uh." Needleflame faltered, looking at Sora. "How do I get lighter, Sora?" "Belly." Sora patted her own belly quietly. "The color's lighter there." Jace, Grede, and Vereesa all froze. What the hell? "Isn't it about transformation? What's the belly got to do with it?" Grede asked. But Sora and Needleflame seemed to have completely ignored the dwarf's question. More importantly, Needleflame didn't find anything strange about it at all. She nodded, and suddenly she was surrounded by sand. When the sand cleared, her skin really had gotten lighter... well, more yellow. Lighter, anyway. At least not as dark as before. Suddenly having lighter skin made it a lot harder to tell if she was the same person. Even for someone who hadn't spent that much time with her, Jace found her a bit unfamiliar. So how exactly did these dragons do their shape-shifting? Weapons turned to decorations, horns to crowns, scales to clothes, wings to cloaks, tails to skirts... and apparently belly color had some strange connection to skin color... The three "mortals" exchanged looks and somehow reached a silent consensus: better not to ask.
