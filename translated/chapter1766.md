## Chapter 1766: Same Recipe

Looking at the familiar gray-white city walls, the familiar silver Lordaeron banners turning into things he needed to watch out for and avoid... the feelings were complicated.

But the flow of people coming and going at Ambermill's gate seemed no different from when Jace had last been here.

As they walked along the muddy road toward the gate, a Kirin Tor mage was complaining about his new robe getting dirty on this lousy road. People on a cart were discussing whether there was any news about orcs lately. It was as if the Lordaeron royal infectious disease Jace had heard about in Everlook, and the whole campaign against the dark warlock Jace Sesso, had never existed.

Or had the news not reached Ambermill yet?

It hadn't even been two weeks since they left Everlook. Sure, maybe some villagers and farmers in the backwaters of some kingdom hadn't heard the rumors yet. But this was Ambermill. Aside from Hillsbrad to the east, this was the closest town to Dalaran.

Grede, walking ahead, kept glancing back at Jace with the same question in his eyes. He'd trimmed his beard a bit. Looked cleaner — like he'd shed twenty or thirty years. Which was honestly pretty funny.

"Hold up, folks." A guard with a spear stopped them at a distance. When he spotted Vereesa and Sora nearby, he swallowed his question. "Have there been orcs around Ambermill, Lady Farstriders?"

"I haven't received any such intelligence. We're just on routine patrol under Captain Denaya Dawnrunner's orders, sir." Vereesa said. "Escorting these travelers to Dalaran."

"Seeing you two makes us all a bit nervous." The guard smiled, exchanged glances with a few colleagues nearby. "Give my regards to the beautiful Captain Denaya if you get the chance, ladies. A few weeks ago, she helped us drive off some orc raiders here. Can't believe that even with Blackrock Spire fallen, these bastards haven't gone into hiding — they're still bold enough to rob nearby farms..."

Vereesa didn't answer. Her attitude was cold enough, but being warm-hearted as a Farstrider here would've been more suspicious.

Grede looked at Jace, then nodded toward the guards. Jace nodded back. Grede cleared his throat. "Hey, I heard back in the Aerie that there's a plague going around Lake Lordamere... is that true? Doesn't feel like anything's wrong."

"Hate to admit it, but the rumors are true, dwarf." The guard said. "They say some mysterious figures loyal to the black dragon and demons of Blackrock Spire have been appearing in the forests and around the graveyards. Riding black horses, carrying giant blood-red scythes. Anywhere their black hooves tread, the plague follows... The soldiers who fought at Blackrock Spire caught it a lot, which proves it."

"But thank the Light, the crisis is over now." He continued. "The Kirin Tor's wizards helped us guards in town identify the suspected infected and quarantine them. And in recent months, those mysterious black-cloaked figures haven't appeared anymore. They say those freaks fled to another continent with the wandering warlocks after the orcs' and black dragon's complete defeat — a place many mages call 'Kalimdor.' Ever heard of that mysterious continent?"

"I've heard of it," Sora said.

Grede glanced at Jace. On the surface, Vereesa was still calm as still water. But the anger roiling inside her was so fierce it was almost burning Jace's consciousness.

These bastards had a whole serialized drama planned out. Every step was wrapped up neatly.

"Anyway, I don't believe a place like that exists." The guard shrugged and continued. "More importantly, those mages and church priests brought back a medicinal broth from the Kingdom of Stormwind that can cure demon plague. Very effective. I guess all that exploring in Stormwind's haunted southern forests finally paid off. Like the old saying goes — 'the antidote is within a hundred steps.'"

"Medicinal broth?" Jace asked tentatively. "Is it the furbolg remedy?"

"Furbolg?" The guard frowned. "What kind of monster is that? You mean worgen or something?"

"Never mind." Jace said. "Anyway, could I take a look at that broth?"

The guard's expression changed. "Where are you folks from? Have you caught something? Feeling unwell anywhere?"

"We don't have any infectious diseases, sir." Jace pointed at his companions. "We just want to buy some medicine. We're worried about running into orc warlocks or something on the road who might hit us with some demon plague out in the middle of nowhere."

"Oh, alright." The guard relaxed and pointed toward an alley entrance in the town. "Go in that way, first right turn. You'll see Arvath Abbey outside. Across the street from the abbey, there's a house with a white Holy Light emblem. You'll need to go up a narrow staircase. Brother Dursten offers free checkups and treatment for anyone worried they might have the plague. He keeps some herbs for brewing. You can talk to him."

"Thank you very much, sir." Jace said.

"Come on in, travelers. And you two, Lady Farstriders."

They thanked him and entered the gate. As soon as they were through, Vereesa couldn't help whispering, "Do those damn priests and mages never mention who went through hell to bring that medicine here?"

"We're not sure it's the same furbolg recipe..." Grede said.

"What else could it be?" The elf stared at the dwarf. "We all saw how well that medicine worked at Blackrock Spire."

"At least they haven't brought up the staff yet." Jace said.

"They're probably working on it." The ranger turned her head. "It's just that the fishmongers and boatmen around Lake Lordamere probably can't grasp concepts like Winterspring, Kalimdor, magic seals, or the ultimate bane of the Burning Legion yet!"

"Then Brother Dursten might know a bit more." Jace said.

The ranger sighed, swept her gaze across the crowd in the nearby square, then nodded.

They went through the alley to the intersection. The house with the Holy Light emblem was just ahead. Climbing the stairs, Jace pushed open the door. Inside, a bald man in gray monastic robes adjusted his spectacles. "Light be with you. Please, come in, sir."

"Light be with you, Brother Dursten."

The moment Jace stepped in, he caught a particular herbal scent. No doubt about it now — it was the furbolg remedy. After spending all that time at Blackrock Spire, he knew the smell better than anyone.

"How can I help you?"

"A guard mentioned you have a medicinal broth here that can treat the demon plague going around..."

"Ah, yes. The Duskwood recipe." Brother Dursten nodded. "Where are you from? Brill?"

"Stratholme." Jace said. "Still have a long way to go. I'm worried about getting sick on the road. I'd like to stock up on some herbs to brew on the way. Nothing too much, Brother."

"No problem, sir. One moment." Dursten got up and went through the back door, gathering supplies as he asked, "How's the situation in Stratholme? Many infected there?"

"I'm not really sure. I left a few months ago." Jace said. "So who came up with this recipe? I'm curious. I know a bit about herbs. Used to be an herbalist myself."

"Hard to tell, sir." Brother Dursten smiled, carrying two small bundles of herbs tied with string as he came back out. "They say it was Waller from the Hamlet. He used to be a Kirin Tor geography and herbology scholar. Did a lot of research on necromantic plagues. If anyone could develop a cure like this, it would have to be him."

"Thomas Waller?" Jace asked. "You know him?" the priest asked.

"We've met. I used to work for him, Brother Dursten." Jace said. "He used to be in Raven Hill, not the Hamlet. That's where the big cemetery is. The worst haunted area."

"That's him." Dursten said. "Small world, meeting someone who knows Mr. Thomas Waller. Though I've also heard some rumors that the broth was actually brought from another continent by Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner of Quel'Thalas. Which would explain why the recipe is so strange."

"Is that so?" Jace frowned.

"But you must know who Lady Vereesa Windrunner's fiancé is, sir." The priest said.

"I do." Jace said.

"Yes. That was my reaction when I first heard it too." The priest sat down nearby, lowering his voice. "But some of my colleagues in the church, and a Kirin Tor friend who came through Ambermill, all say the same thing. Anyway, the broth definitely works — it cured His Majesty Terenas."

"The king was cured by this broth?" Jace asked in surprise.

"This very recipe." Brother Dursten smiled. "Who would've thought? A strange illness that had plagued the King of Lordaeren for nearly two years, just gone like that. Light be praised. I hope these herbs never need to be used — that plague is quite nasty."

"Uh." Jace looked at the medicine bundle in his hand. "Who suggested using this recipe for His Majesty Terenas's illness?"

"Well... I'm not really sure about that, sir." The priest said.
