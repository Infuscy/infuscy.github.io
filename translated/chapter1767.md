## Chapter 1767: Plague and Cure

The streets of Ambermill were calm as ever. Three blue-robed apprentices who looked like Dalaran mages were chatting. Two children chased each other across the wet, muddy road.

In a second-floor inn room, the warlock in his dark hood lowered the window shutter, then snapped his fingers to light a candle on the table.

The ranger slipped in and closed the door, nodding to the others. The hallway outside was quiet. Empty.

"Is there something wrong with these herbs?" Jace asked.

"No." Sora shook her head, then lowered her face to sniff the crushed herb powder spread out on the table. "These are mostly from the original recipe. Slightly different, but only in a few common herbs. Same formula as the medicine King Varian brought to Blackrock Spire. No difference."

"Those changes are normal." Jace said. "Back then, Ursula Delin said the original recipe wasn't really suitable for humans. The dosage was too strong. Probably only orcs could drink it without problems. So I commissioned Mr. Thomas Waller in Duskwood to modify it. From what we saw at Blackrock Spire, his changes were pretty successful."

"But Thomas Waller was planning to learn necromancy from the Black Knight to resurrect his wife, Jace." Vereesa said. "Are you sure he didn't tamper with the recipe?"

"Can you confirm there's definitely no disease or poison in this, Sora?" She looked at the green dragon.

"Unless there's something humans can't tolerate that I don't know about... but I really can't see anything wrong, Vereesa. At least no curses attached." Sora said.

"Haven't you figured it out from what that priest said?" Grede pointed at the window. "Those Kirin Tor mages, the church people — none of them want anyone to know we brought this medicine from Kalimdor. They're keeping all the credit in-house. But there are still a few with consciences left in there, or soldiers who came back from Blackrock Spire who know who really found the recipe and can't stand seeing them steal the credit!"

"Think about it. The plague was spread by black knights with red scythes. The plague was cured by the Kirin Tor... using a remedy that also cures the plague from Blackrock Spire. And the same type of illness the old king caught in Stormwind. The intent is obvious. They want Jace to keep wearing that villain's hat — the one who's been sabotaging everything but never quite succeeding."

"If what you're saying is true, the dreadlords didn't tamper with the medicine we brought back." Vereesa walked to the table in front of the dwarf. "So they're being generous? They spread the plague to frame Jace, then just let the medicine circulate and cure everyone instead of turning the whole Lordaeron continent into a plague nest and making the survivors hate Jace even more?"

She picked up the unopened herb bundle. "There has to be something wrong with this medicine. Either the demons only tampered with part of it, and the batch we got is just one of the normal ones."

"Well, there's no way to prove that." Grede spread his hands. "How about you answer a question for me too, little elf."

"If Jace is already framed for creating the plague and his reputation is ruined in this world, who'd dare drink that medicinal recipe you brought back from Kalimdor, Ranger-Lord? These townsfolk didn't go to school in Dalaran or Farstrider Academy, but they're not brainless idiots! Isn't this contradictory? Everyone knows your relationship with this 'evil warlock'!"

Vereesa lowered her head, thinking. The more she thought, the stiffer her expression became. Finally she managed, "Can you say 'evil warlock' a little less?!"

"Me saying it less won't make a damn difference, Vereesa Windrunner." Grede panted with anger for a few breaths, then crossed his arms and leaned against the window. "Because there are probably thousands, tens of thousands out there saying it. Even the goblins in Everlook know!"

"At least the gate guard didn't mention Jace's name." Sora said.

"Brother Dursten at the clinic didn't say his name either, Sora. Just 'the one connected to Lady Vereesa Windrunner.'" The dwarf looked at the green dragon. "Which is actually worse. Because no one wants to say his name anymore. I wouldn't be surprised if there are already rumors circulating in the Tirisfal countryside that 'saying Jace Sesso's name will make you sick'!"

"Quite the imagination, dwarf." Vereesa said coldly.

"Reality is always more absurd than you imagine!" Grede shot back.

Elf and dwarf went back and forth. Doubts. Anger. Swirling through the room.

One was a hunter forged in the Emerald Dream. One was a Mountain King with ancestral power. A successor of the Dream Wardens. The wild blood of an Aspect's descendant. Every single one of them had emotional fluctuations far stronger than any ordinary person outside.

More importantly, Jace's emotions were far from calm either.

"Calm down, Vereesa, Grede." Sora said. "Maybe things aren't as bad as you think. Maybe the church is actively distributing medicine just to contain the plague quickly. Maybe they didn't publicly credit us for the recipe because — like Grede said — they didn't want Jace's reputation to slow down distribution. After all, most residents here don't trust him. Don't know him."

"I think you're being too charitable, Sora." Vereesa said.

"But at least no matter what the demons are trying to do, their goal is to frame us." Sora said. "Those Nathrezim want to put limits on him. Make him face investigations. Stop him from rallying support and resources to hunt down the disguised demons like he used to."

"What we need to do is..." She hesitated, looking nervously at each person in the room, then at the silent Needleflame beside her. When she was sure everyone had calmed down a little, she continued. "What we need to do is ignore these fragments for now. Focus on rooting out the hidden demons. That's our purpose in going to Dalaran. As for everything else — the guards at the gate, the priest at the clinic — none of them can know the whole picture."

"Sora's right." Jace said. "Right now, the most important thing is to find Khadgar and complete Illidan Stormrage's magical artifact. That's how we get some initiative back. As long as we drag even one demon out in front of everyone, most of the suspicion on me will be cleared. Every Kirin Tor mage knows how dreadlords wreaked havoc on the kingdom of Dalaran two thousand years ago."

"I agree." Vereesa nodded. "And whoever thought of having Terenas Menethil II try that medicine — they're very suspicious. Even if they're not a demon in disguise, they're being manipulated by one."

"Khadgar should know who proposed it." Jace said.

"So what's Khadgar's situation? What did that priest say?" Grede asked.

"I didn't ask him that specifically." Jace said. "For one thing, he didn't seem clear on the details happening in the Lordaeron court or Dalaran. Can't blame him — he's just an Ambermill apothecary. Also, asking too much would raise suspicion. We'll see for ourselves when we get to Dalaran."

"Then let's not waste time in this town. Let's eat something and head for Dalaran before sunset." Vereesa said. "If we push hard on horseback, we can get there by noon tomorrow."

Everyone nodded, packed up, and went downstairs.

As they set out at night, they could see a faint aurora-like arcane magical glow in the sky in Dalaran's direction. Jace knew that was the magical barrier Antonidas had set up using the Eye of Dalaran.

As long as it wasn't under attack, it was nearly invisible during the day. But at night, it gave off this strange light. Hard to see in Dalaran's bright city center, but visible in the outskirts and even further away — like city light pollution from the twenty-first century.

This powerful barrier had once held off the orcish horde's siege, withstood black dragonflight bombardment, frustrated demons trying to infiltrate Dalaran, and given countless Dalaran residents peace of mind. Now it had become the biggest obstacle on Jace's mind.
