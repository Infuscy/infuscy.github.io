Chapter 1768: Phantom Memories

Dalaran, floating on Lake Lordamere, still looked like a fairy-tale castle from here. But like many fairy-tale castles, it also hid devils disguised as good people. Jace handed his horse off to the local stable and looked around at the rows of cramped houses south of the bridge and the bustling crowds. He figured that right now, to most citizens of Dalaran and Lordaeron, he was probably the devil disguised as a good person. "Did you hide everything properly?" Vereesa asked. "Everything related to the demon... the Demon Soul." "I think when you said 'you all,' you could've just used the singular," Grede said, glancing at Jace. "Done," Jace said. "All the soul shards, including the robe, are packed in the magic sealing box the Kirin Tor gave me. If the Eye of Dalaran can still 'see' through that, either the Kirin Tor don't know their own craft, or... the Kirin Tor don't know their own craft." "Still cracking jokes, Jace Sesso." Vereesa sighed softly, then fixed her gaze on the staff behind Jace. "And... that." "Trust it. I've walked in and out of Dalaran with this thing on my back more times than I can count," Jace said. "Besides, technically speaking, it's an ancient Nathrezim — used to be their leader, even. It knows how not to be seen by other Nathrezim." "That's assuming it chooses not to be seen, Jace," Grede said. Ulthalesh let out a low hum in Jace's mind. Whether it was mocking Jace's naivete, sneering at his blind trust, expressing some kind of confidence, or protesting the 'mortals' around it looking down on it — Jace couldn't tell. Aside from the hunger that wanted to devour everything and the impulse to destroy it all, eighty percent of the noises this thing made were equally incomprehensible. As they neared the bridgehead, the Dalaran arcane guards keeping watch at the city entrance noticed the group. One purple-robed guard with a shield raised his hand toward them, signaling for Jace and the others to wait for inspection. "Remember how to ask?" Jace whispered. "I remember," Vereesa said. "Leave it to me." "Apologies, but the inner city is currently closed to unrelated parties." The purple-robed guard strode up and sized up Vereesa and Sora. "Even for members of the Farstriders — I believe the Farstriders' mission in Dalaran concluded quite some time ago, ladies." Vereesa produced the Windrunner family crest and handed it to the guard. "I am Elenar Dawnstrider. This is Samira Shadowglen. We are here in Dalaran on an important mission under orders from Lady Sylvanas Windrunner. These humans and the dwarf are important 'evidence' — they were ambushed by orcs in the Eastern Wildlands. There may still be traces of orc warlock magic on them. I need to bring them to the Quel'Thalas embassy for examination by a Magister before the traces fade. You should be well aware that Quel'Thalas also does not permit 'unrelated parties' entry." Vereesa's tone was as frigid as ever, making even the guard a little uneasy. After checking the Windrunner crest, he took a deep breath and looked at Jace and the others. Finally, he said, "The crest checks out, Lady Dawnstrider. I don't want to delay the Farstriders' pursuit of Horde remnants either. But please remove your mask for identification." Vereesa glanced sideways at Jace. Jace flicked his eyes ever so slightly toward the guard — just enough of a signal for her to make a quick decision. The ranger pulled down her mask. The guard squinted. "You..." Jace seized the moment, steering the guard's emotions in a different direction. Faced with this warlock's forceful intrusion, the anti-dark magic runes on the guard's body were completely useless. "Yes?" Vereesa asked. The guard shook his head. "No, Lady Dawnstrider. I must have been mistaken. Please, go ahead. You know where the Quel'Thalas embassy is." "I do," Vereesa said. "One more question, sir. Are you aware of the ley line fluctuations that came from the south a while ago?" "I've heard of them," the guard said. "Is Quel'Thalas also paying attention to this?" "Yes," Vereesa said. "I heard that Archmage Khadgar is already preparing to investigate." "Indeed, but I'm not well-versed in Archmage Khadgar's plans, Lady Dawnstrider. Your Quel'Thalas Magisters should know more detail," the guard said. "When it comes to the ley lines, the Kirin Tor has never trusted the Silvermoon Council or the Magisters, and there's almost no cooperation. Khadgar is human, after all," Vereesa said. "I don't think the Silvermoon Council and the Magisters are in much of a position to criticize the Kirin Tor on that front," the guard said with a faint smile. Vereesa returned an equally unfriendly smile, then stepped past him onto the bridge. The others quickly followed. They passed through Antonidas's protective barrier without issue. Jace glanced at Ulthalesh on his back. That thing had never let him down — well, at least when it came to hiding itself. The streets of Dalaran's inner city seemed quieter than usual. The group ducked into the nearest tavern on this side of the bridge, called "Phantom Memories," grabbed a few glasses of sparkling wine, and found a corner table. After confirming none of the other patrons were paying them any attention, Vereesa leaned in. "You used your power, didn't you, Jace?" "Used what?" Grede looked at Jace blankly and lowered his voice. "Wait — did you learn to mess with people's minds from that black dragon too?" "'Messing with' is a strong word," Jace said. "But making him question his own thoughts? That wasn't hard." "So have you ever used it on me? Messed with my 'thoughts'?" Grede asked. "Yeah. I made sure you always believed you're a tough-as-nails dwarf," Jace said. "What — hang on —" Grede froze for a second. Jace chuckled. "Forget it. The point is, we're through. Now the problem is — even that guard doesn't know what's going on with Khadgar." "That could mean a lot of things," Vereesa said quietly. "Maybe Khadgar really has been captured, and the Kirin Tor is covering it up. Or else..." "Or else Khadgar's in on it with the Kirin Tor," Needleflame whispered. Grede stared at the little black dragon. "That's basically worthless. He is part of the Kirin Tor..." "So do we really have to suspect even Archmage Khadgar?" Vereesa cut the dwarf off. Jace looked out the window at the distant spires of the Violet Citadel. "I'm not going to suspect him." "If Khadgar wanted to catch me, with how well he knows me, there's no way I'd still be sitting here drinking in peace." "But I can understand why the Kirin Tor wouldn't want to spread the news that they've got Khadgar locked up. He's nothing like me. Even in Lordaeron and Dalaran, he's a hero with tremendous prestige — one of the leaders of the Sons of Lothar, a star graduate of the Kirin Tor, a top-tier archmage. Not some shady no-name outsider like me." "So what do we do? Who can we ask?" Grede downed half his glass in one gulp. "Isn't there any other mage we can trust?" "Lady Amy Marin?" Sora suggested softly. "Wouldn't she be implicated too?" Vereesa asked. Right. Amy Marin. Jace rubbed his temples. If Khadgar was getting dragged into this, how could Amy possibly have escaped? She'd been deeply involved in the Draenor expedition, after all. Though at least she wasn't part of the Karazhan business — her connection to the demon and plague issues probably ran more distant. "Either way, we can't go to Amy," Jace said. "If the Kirin Tor went so far as to investigate Fort Ilgaral, then even if she hasn't been arrested, she's definitely under surveillance. If we go to her, we'd be walking into a trap and dragging her down with us. Even if the Kirin Tor had no reason to suspect her before, they'd have no choice after that." "You're right. Damn it," Grede muttered. "Then who else can we ask? Proudmoore?" Vereesa asked. A burst of laughter came from nearby. The group went quiet for a moment and looked over. A small, unstable arcane construct was slowly ambling its way around tables and chairs, making noises. The things it was 'saying' had the apprentices in stitches. "Shh," Jace warned. "These arcane constructs report everything they hear to their masters. Every single one could be a spy — including the magic brooms and such. We need to watch what we say." Grede and Needleflame nodded. Vereesa cleared her throat. "We should head to the embassy soon. We can't linger here too long. The orc warlock's magical traces could fade any minute." "Yes, ma'am," Grede said obediently. "Those warlocks really scared us half to death..." "Stupid apprentice!" The drifting arcane construct shouted at Vereesa. "Stupid apprentice..." "What?" Vereesa frowned. The young mages across the room were sneaking glances their way, and seeing Vereesa's reaction, they burst out laughing again. "Don't mind it, beautiful Quel'dorei lady. This minor arcane anomaly seems to have a little glitch," one young mage called out. "It's definitely not talking about you!" "It might be talking about you, since —" another young man pointed at the first speaker, and the whole group roared with laughter. "What a ridiculous parlor trick," Grede grumbled. "Stupid ap —" The arcane anomaly mumbled again. Vereesa and Sora clearly had no patience for these Dalaran-specific oddities. But Needleflame noticed that Jace, sitting across the table, had gone still, staring at the construct. "What's wrong, Sesso?" she asked. "Shh." Jace put a finger to his lips. Under the bewildered looks of the others at the table, he glanced at the mage apprentices still joking and laughing in the distance, then leaned quietly toward the arcane anomaly. He tentatively whispered in Thalassian: "Stupid apprentice." The arcane construct suddenly switched to a slurred demonic tongue. "You took your sweet time getting here. Follow me, keep your head down, kid."
