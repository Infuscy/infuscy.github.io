Chapter 1769: Khadgar's Servant

"What's going on?" Vereesa asked the warlock across the table, keeping her voice low. "Do you know why that arcane construct is on the fritz?" "This is, uh..." Jace watched the apprentices laughing and chatting as they filed out of the tavern, then said, "You all stay here and keep drinking. I'm going to follow that thing and check it out." "Is it... that mage?" Grede practically mouthed the words. "Yeah." Jace nodded and pushed himself up from the table. "Should we go with you?" Needleflame asked. "Too many people. I'll go alone. It wants me to be low-key — definitely doesn't want to draw attention," Jace said. "Gentlemen and ladies!" A voice called from the bar. A server tidying up the empty glasses on the counter raised a hand toward them. "Don't talk to that thing. Don't get too close. It's been wandering around here with some kind of malfunction for weeks now. My boss has called several mages, none of them could get it to unsummon." "It hasn't done any real damage yet — just a foul mouth — but it's still concerning. There's just no high-rank mage around here right now who can safely dismantle an unstable arcane anomaly. I'll ask around at the Academy in a couple days." "I can take it to a friend of mine who might know how to fix it," Jace said. "I know a mage who's done serious research in the conjuration and summoning schools." "Is that so?" The server raised an eyebrow. As they exchanged these few words, the arcane construct had already started drifting toward the door on its own. The few remaining customers in the hall all followed it with their eyes as it moved — which didn't quite fit the "low-key" the construct had asked for. The server hesitated. "Are you sure you're taking it, and not the other way around?" "I'm taking it." Jace coughed and made a show of calling out in Thalassian: "Outside." The construct obediently turned at the corner and slowly made its way toward the door. The server's eyes lit up. "Thank you so much, Master. You're the first person in weeks who's been able to control it." "Happy to help. All in the name of maintaining order in Dalaran." Jace waved him off and hurried after it. Before stepping out, he shot his companions a "don't worry" look and slipped through the door. The streets of Dalaran had gotten a bit more crowded. Jace was now wearing an unassuming brown cloak, carrying a bent staff wrapped in magic-dampening cloth on his back. He looked like some backcountry mage who'd traveled here from Arathi. Nobody recognized him. Nobody even noticed him. It reminded him of eight years ago, when he'd first been brought to Dalaran by a gryphon knight after passing out from his fight with that black whelp beneath the watchtower. He'd woken up in this city that felt like something out of a dream, walking its streets knowing nothing about anything. Jace realized he kind of missed that feeling. But now he was an enemy of this magic kingdom. First Aegwynn, then Medivh, now him. Whether he liked it or not, whether he wanted to be humble or not, that was the truth. Behind him, at the distant tavern entrance, Needleflame and Vereesa stood watching. Jace noticed Vereesa point in his direction, and Needleflame stepped down from the doorway to follow. The girl had quietly changed into a light blue robe that looked just like what a group of twelve- or thirteen-year-old female apprentices across the street were wearing. She fit right in — just seemed a little rushed. But when she caught Jace's look, she slowed her pace a bit. There was no way those guys were going to let him follow some random arcane construct alone. No question about it — this arcane anomaly was left by Khadgar himself. "Stupid apprentice" in Thalassian was the exact opening password to the Karazhan library. Nobody else could possibly know that except him — Khadgar, Medivh, Medivh's mother, and Morophil, who'd gone with them. So Khadgar must have used this magic servant to send him a message. How the hell did he figure Jace would sneak back into Dalaran to find him? And that Jace would run into this particular arcane construct? What if Jace had been stubborn and gone straight back to Fort Ilgaral? Actually, thinking about it that way... the end result would probably have been him getting dragged back to Dalaran anyway. Same destination, different route. But where was this arcane anomaly taking him? Through the Dalaran sewers to some breach in the Violet Hold — like that sewer door entrance to Karabor's Black Temple in the game? Or a fully armed and ready mercenary rescue squad waiting for Jace and company to arrive? Or some magical gadget that would let Jace teleport Khadgar right out of the Violet Hold and in front of him? Jace was lost in these random thoughts when a thick metallic smell hit him in the face, along with a wave of heat all over his body... He'd walked straight into the arcane construct! "Patience... Shut up! Keep moving!" The construct suddenly grunted out those two phrases. Jace frowned and looked it over. From behind, this magical anomaly looked like a guy who'd trained his entire upper body like a bodybuilding champion but completely forgot he had legs... except it really didn't have legs. It was like a semi-transparent, pinkish-purple Aladdin's lamp genie. The problem was, it moved way too slowly. It just crawled along the street bit by bit. Jace followed behind awkwardly, but the mages passing by didn't seem to think it was anything unusual. — Dalaran, the city of magic. An arcane jewel of the Lordaeron continent. A voice suddenly rang out in his mind. Jace felt his ear itch and his hair stand on end. He whipped his head around — nobody nearby. But the voice was like someone whispering right in his ear. — Two thousand years ago, demons invaded this city, beginning... wait, was this the arcane construct talking? Jace looked down. Sure enough, the arcane energy inside the thing was pulsing in rhythm with the mental whisper. This triggered some painful old memories. Oh, come on. Khadgar, seriously? Right now? This isn't Shattrath... The level 70 Burning Crusade version — every player arriving for the first time in Shattrath City on Outland had to go through it: follow Khadgar's Servant around the Terrace of Light to get familiar with the city. The most excruciating, agonizing mandatory quest in the game... because you couldn't choose a faction in Shattrath without doing it! And now, apparently, this was a required step here too. No pain, no Khadgar. But this pain was still better than throwing down with the guards at the Violet Hold. Jace let out a long sigh and crossed his arms. But the arcane anomaly ahead of him cut off its own telepathic transmission, turned around, and whispered: "Follow closely. Be patient... Shut up! Keep moving!" Shut up? Jace was getting a little annoyed with this thing, but he followed anyway. No point getting mad at a magic summon. — The Kirin Tor, the soul of Dalaran, ever since... Shut up! Keep moving! Jace stared as this thing calmly narrated Dalaran trivia through telepathy, then abruptly interrupted itself mid-sentence to snap two orders. Couldn't tell if it was talking to him or to itself. But after cutting itself off, it obediently shuffled forward again, and even seemed to pick up a little speed. — From here, follow the main road and you'll see Krasus's Landing, where... Shut up! Keep moving! — To the north of the street is the Ashen Hills Guild, founded seven hundred years ago... Shut up! Keep moving! Slowly, Jace started to get it. This anomaly was probably originally a "tour guide" Khadgar had made for the city of Dalaran. In an emergency, Khadgar had hastily repurposed it into a "communication tool." To keep it from glitching out, he'd kept most of its original logic and just forced commands to interrupt it when it started wasting time on its original function. Of course, Jace didn't really know much about arcane magic. This was just a guess based on logic he could understand. — Look to the west. The monument at this intersection commemorates Ranger Vereesa Windrunner, Kefal Brightwing, and the young mage of Stormwind, Jace... Shut up! Keep moving! Hey, that part you could've left in. Jace thought to himself, glancing across the street at the refurbished little garden square and the nameplate on the small monument in the middle of it. He felt like he'd almost forgotten about that meeting. After gods knew how long, the arcane construct led him through a gap between two buildings, wound through a few alleys, and then suddenly opened up — they were at the shore of Lake Lordamere. There was practically nobody here. Just a solitary figure sitting by the water with his back to them — a fisherman. Jace walked up behind him and noticed the fish net sitting in the water was completely empty. "Good afternoon, sir." "Light above! Could you make some noise when you approach?!" The fisherman lifted his hat and turned around with a start. Was this... was this Khadgar? His beard was shaved clean as a baby's bottom. The fisherman frowned. "Hold on. Are you Sesso?" "What, you've got amnesia? Don't recognize me?" Jace asked. The fisherman squinted. "How is this my fault? Did you get lost in Kalimdor or something? Can't afford a meal? Can't afford a razor? Take a look at yourself in the lake. I barely recognized you." "That's... not important." Jace turned and looked back at the arcane construct. It had vanished without a trace. "I thought you'd been thrown in prison. I sent someone to ask for your help. Why didn't you come? Or did you never get the message?" "Of course I got the 'orders' that lord passed along. But I really couldn't go. I'm not in prison, but I'm under house arrest in the Violet Citadel. For all practical purposes, it's the same..." "How did you get out?" "I didn't." Jace was so caught off guard he coughed a couple times. He glanced at the fish net in the water — somehow, two Lake Lordamere Frenzied Fish had appeared in it. "Then how did you manage to pull two live fish out of thin air?" "Pulled? What do you mean? They were there all along." The fisherman lifted the net, his face completely straight. "Let's talk somewhere else."
