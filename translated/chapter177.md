## Chapter 177: Two Headless Flies

The rain kept falling. Whether it was the downpour or not, the arcane glow in the sky had become completely invisible.

But the dragon roars coming from the south were still terrifying, echoing through the rain-soaked alleys of the night.

Jace walked downstairs, and honestly, he felt like he could barely move another step.

If this place hadn't been close to the spiral alley Caro Dushan had told him about, he probably wouldn't have had the strength to keep going.

Down on the ground, Jace glanced casually toward where Teron Gorefiend's body had fallen — and caught a faint shimmer of shadowlight.

He shuffled over curiously and found a pile of shattered black metal on the ground. In the middle of the debris sat a round, blood-red gem radiating a shadowy glow.

Was this the gem that had burned in the head of Gorefiend's war hammer?

Jace was just about to step closer when a shadow rose up beside Gorefiend's corpse.

It was a ghoul.

Jace hadn't planned on fighting another one — then he remembered he had an elf's blade in his hand, didn't he?

He approached cautiously, blade ready. The ghoul left Gorefiend's corpse and shambled toward him, dragging a broken sword.

It lurched forward unsteadily. Jace swung the blade in a clean chop and severed the arm holding the sword.

The ghoul's arm burst into silver flame, then caught for real. After burning a few moments, the ghoul staggered back several steps. Jace followed up and hacked its head off in one stroke.

Shame he'd have to give this thing back…

Gorefiend's corpse had already gone the same way as that earlier death knight — reduced to a black, shapeless sludge. Only the scorched scrap of his tattered cloak still held its form.

Jace picked up the gem, still glowing, and set it into the staff's gem socket. It fit perfectly.

Then again, should he even be surprised?

Maybe this gem had originally been taken off this very staff.

Jace tried using the blade to cut a strip from the outermost layer of Gorefiend's wrapped burial cloak — to use as wrapping for the staff. But the moment the edge bit into the fabric, the whole cloak burst into flame.

Ah… this blade really didn't discriminate.

Jace took a step back and watched the cloak burn, flames flickering and smoking in the rain, then dying out a moment later.

He tore off a strip of the singed cloth, wrapped it tight around the staff and the gem on top, and made sure nothing showed from the outside. Only then did he sling the staff over his back and walk out of the alley.

Tonight he'd probably pushed his mana past the point of no return, and his body and mind had been wrecked beyond repair. Thinking back to the wild hallucinations when he'd used Life Tap on the rooftop, he could hardly believe that had been him.

Did warlock players in the game feel this kind of rush when they cast that spell?

Hard to even imagine.

Walking alone through Dalaran's streets, now that he'd finally relaxed, he noticed the windows along the road — some of Dalaran's residents were peering out at him.

They still had no idea what had happened to their city. Probably thought it was just some monster incursion, or that orcs had snuck in or something.

By now his left shoulder was throbbing worse, and he was running on fumes.

Was the shadow power he'd bought with his life force starting to fade?

After he'd walked for what felt like ages, Jace suddenly spotted a shop on the street corner. The sign read "The Bronzebeard's Brew."

The streets here really did spiral — so this must be Horn Tree Alley, right?

Jace walked up and pounded on the door.

"Anybody there!"

"Open up!"

"I'm looking for Grede Thunderfist — does he work here?"

Only after he said that did someone finally open the door.

A dwarf poked his head out. "Who're you?"

"A friend of Grede's," Jace said.

The dwarf looked him up and down. "He ran out a long enough while ago!"

Just as Jace had figured.

Jace dropped onto the ground right there, and a wave of pain crashed through him all at once.

Grede had rushed out in such a hurry most likely to look for him. They must've passed each other somewhere along the way.

He crouched down and buried his head in the crook of his right arm for a moment, trying to collect himself.

So he'd still have to walk back.

By now the black dragon roars seemed to have faded. The city was much quieter than before.

Jace forced himself to his feet, about to retrace his steps — when he saw a dwarf standing motionless at the end of the street, staring straight at him. He looked exactly like one of those reanimated guards.

Gazing at that dark shape, a tangle of emotions Jace couldn't name welled up inside him.

Fear, despair, sadness, or…

Facing Teron Gorefiend hadn't scared him this badly.

Jace's lips trembled uncontrollably. He blinked the rain from his eyes and tightened his grip on the blade in his right hand.

Then he saw the dwarf raise the war hammer in his hands — the very war hammer of that dead gryphon rider from Southshore. Both of them broke into a sprint at the same time. Jace clenched his jaw, and the dwarf charged with a mad bellow. Jace couldn't help but roar back, his voice cracking — and the dwarf's roar turned into sobbing halfway through.

Just before they collided, something felt off to Jace.

Did the dead cry?

"Wait — hold on, stop!"

Jace shouted and slammed on the brakes. The dwarf seemed to catch on and halted too. The hammer in his hand sparked with a flicker of lightning, then died.

"Are you dead or alive?!" the dwarf demanded, jabbing a finger at his nose.

"Alive!" Jace threw everything in his hands onto the ground, flopped flat on his back, and bellowed at the sky: "You nearly scared me to death!"

"You think I didn't?!" Grede shouted, and kicked him in the leg.

"Aagh!!!" Jace sat bolt upright from the pain. "Do you have any idea what I've been through, you bastard? And you still dare kick me?!"

"I killed three ghouls I thought were you!" Grede shot back. "Every single one stopped my heart!"

"I nearly killed Teron Gorefiend," Jace said, dazed.

"What?" Grede blinked. "You mean the Teron Gorefiend I'm thinking of?"

"The Teron Gorefiend you're thinking of," Jace looked at him. "I killed a death knight looking for you, then fought Teron Gorefiend himself, and he personally ran me through the shoulder."

"Huh? Your shoulder?"

Grede reached out like he wanted to touch it, then thought better of it.

Jace pointed at his shoulder, and every ounce of pent-up emotion from the night came pouring out.

"Look at my blood, Grede… look at my blood — it's almost black. Just to come find you, because you're the reckless idiot who loses his mind and charges off to get himself killed every time something happens! If you valued your own miserable life even a little, I wouldn't be like this!"

But after the outburst, Jace realized — if Grede had really changed, would the two of them even still be friends?

"My fault, Jace," Grede said quietly. He blinked, then added, "But your blood is red."

"Uh…"

Jace looked at the blood on his hands. It was red.

Was that the potion's doing?

Thinking it over, after drinking Vereesa's potion the pain really had lessened a lot. His shoulder still hurt, but it no longer had that deep, piercing agony of being run through.

His anger faded with it. Just now he'd been convinced he was a goner — even if he survived, he'd have lost half his life.

Sitting on the steps under the eaves, he thought about that strange potion. It didn't look like the Revival Potion Kaelfal had, and it didn't look like some red healing potion either. The liquid was hard to pin down — just… weird.

Recalling it, Kaelfal had only taken a tiny sip and recovered a lot, while Jace had grabbed it and chugged a huge mouthful… and Vereesa had looked a little shocked.

At the time he'd been too busy being tormented by the necrotic energy he'd drained to think about any of it. Only now, with the tension finally unwinding, could he properly piece it together. That potion really didn't seem like anything ordinary.

Was it going to be ruinously expensive?

But did a Windrunner — elven nobility — strike anyone as short on money?

Still, either way it hadn't been right. When he returned the blade, he'd ask her properly.

Speaking of the blade…

Jace looked to his side. The blade was gone.

"My blade."

"Yours?" Grede was already turning the blade over in his hands. "This is elven. Where'd you get it?"

Jace wiped the rain from his face and sat up. "An elf gave it to me, obviously. Last night I was fighting alongside Kaelfal Mingyi and a female ranger against Teron Gorefiend. Afterward I said I needed to find you, and the elf saw I didn't have a weapon, so she lent it to me."

"This is…" Grede tilted the blade to catch the light. "This is a fine piece, and enchanted at that. Why would she lend you something like this? She wasn't afraid you'd run off with it? If this were sold…"

"Which is why nobody lends you anything," Jace said, snatching the blade back.

"I'm just saying," Grede said. "Now come on, tell me how you fought Teron Gorefiend. Missing a battle like that — what a tragedy."

"Find somewhere to shelter from the rain first. There might still be walking corpses out there."

"My place."

Jace told the story of how he'd left the gathering as he followed Grede back to his rented room. The place wasn't much bigger than Jace's room in Stormwind, and the whole house reeked of mushrooms.

"Keep talking, I'll look at your wound."

Grede opened an old cabinet and dragged out his burlap sack, rummaging through it back and forth.

Jace sat on the bed and caught his breath. He had to admit, Vereesa's potion had some kick to it — even the stench of the dead was gone from his mouth now.

He went on and finished the whole story, only downplaying the part at the end where he'd used Life Tap.

Grede came over with a handful of bottles and jars, glanced at Jace's shoulder, pulled out a salve, and said, "Sounds like that elf woman did most of the heavy lifting."

"Pretty much. That was Vereesa Windrunner."

"What?! Alleria Windrunner's youngest sister?" Grede's whole body shuddered.

"The one," Jace said through gritted teeth. "Could you maybe keep still while you're applying that?"

"Right, right. So this is a Windrunner family blade?" Grede glanced at the blade lying on Jace's table. "Didn't you test it on something?"

"I did. Shame you didn't see it." Jace said. "If you turn into a ghoul, I can use it to stab you to death a second time. You can see what it feels like."

"Dying by this blade would be an honor, lad," Grede sighed. Then he added, "Speaking of which, Jace, I can only do a basic patch-up on your shoulder. The black dragons seem to be pulling back already — no idea when the city will settle down. When it does, you need to go see one of Dalaran's healers."

"It's just serious?" Jace asked.

Grede went on, "From what you said, Teron Gorefiend ran you through the shoulder with your staff… the wound should look horrifying. But right now it actually seems to be healing. Are you sure you were run through? How bad did it hurt?"

"Agonizing," Jace said. "I was pinned to the wall. What do you think?"

Grede said, "You were pinned to the wall and didn't pass out from the pain? Why don't you sleep here tonight? Don't go home — it's pouring outside and it might still be dangerous. Tomorrow I'll take you to a healer."

"Fine." Jace pulled off his shirt and slowly lowered himself onto the bed on his side. Every movement was agony.

Once he was fully lying down, his head was already buzzing.

He stared at the wall in front of him, running through everything that had happened tonight. Teron Gorefiend's whispers still coiled through his mind, refusing to let go.

Grede picked up the war hammer. "Get some sleep. I'll go check on the streets for a bit."

The door slammed shut, and Jace remembered the question he'd been too tense to think about earlier.

Why did Teron Gorefiend know his name?

Who had told him?

He thought it over — and before he knew it, he'd drifted off. He was that exhausted.
