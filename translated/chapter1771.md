## Chapter 1771: From Paper to Practice

"I just don't understand, Jace."

Khadgar tapped his fingers on the leather armrest. "If Onyxia was willing to help you because of the little black dragon, why would the demon hunter Illidan Stormrage be willing to help? More importantly, why would the Kaldorei survivors let you meet with that prisoner? He's still a prisoner, isn't he?"

"More like the Kaldorei leadership wanted to see him themselves, and used me wanting to help the human kingdoms hunt demons as an excuse," Jace said.

"I see." Khadgar nodded. "Right. Their Archdruid and that demon hunter are blood brothers. So let me see how much the Illidan Stormrage described in the highborne survivors' *The Fall of the Well of Eternity* differs from the real one. Tell me about this method you've learned for finding Nathrezim."

"The key is an eye tyrant," Jace said.

Khadgar shook his head. "Eye tyrants can't see through Nathrezim disguises. That's pretty well-established among the Kirin Tor's demon scholars..."

"Maybe you haven't seen enough eye tyrants." Jace leaned forward. "Illidan Stormrage said it has to be a very powerful eye tyrant lord — specifically one with close ties to the Burning Legion, preferably one that serves in the Legion itself and is familiar with Nathreza."

Khadgar smiled. "Don't underestimate the Kirin Tor, Jace. The Kirin Tor of the past was nothing like it is now. Back then, the Order of Tirisfal hadn't fallen this far. And don't underestimate the Nathrezim either."

"Don't underestimate Illidan Stormrage either, Khadgar." Jace said. "You were Sargeras's student. So was he. And I think what he got from the Dark Titan is closer to the true nature of the Twisting Nether..."

"First of all." Khadgar coughed guiltily. "I am NOT Sargeras's student. And second, I have no disrespect for that demon scholar..."

"At least let's try," Jace said. "It's not like you have a better plan right now."

"I don't," Khadgar admitted. "Go on."

"Obviously, summoning a Burning Legion lord is completely out of the question for us right now," Jace said. "So I want to summon a powerful eye tyrant lord instead... Do you know the incantation for summoning one?"

"Illidan Stormrage didn't teach you?" Khadgar asked. "I'm Kirin Tor. How would I know how to summon an eye tyrant lord?"

"He didn't teach me," Jace said.

An awkward silence hung in the air. Both of them sat quietly for a moment before Khadgar's head drooped.

"I do know."

"Then we're set," Jace said.

Khadgar lifted his eyes and shot Jace a look full of complaint and helplessness. "You do realize that summoning an eye tyrant — especially a powerful one — isn't like summoning an imp. You seem pretty confident. I assume you've made the necessary preparations?"

"That's your job," Jace said.

Khadgar rubbed his eyes. "So the last 'step' isn't even one you're taking."

"I'll teach the eye tyrant how to recognize Nathrezim," Jace said.

"Teach?" Khadgar nodded slowly. "Ah, I see. Even if an eye tyrant's perception is sharp enough to see through a Nathrezim's disguise, if it doesn't know what to look for in its own vision, it might as well not see anything."

"Exactly," Jace said.

"I'm reminded of something from a long time ago." Khadgar lowered his voice. "About seventy or eighty years before the Dark Portal. A Kirin Tor archmage and a Church of the Holy Light bishop were arguing about the 'power of the Light.' They found two patients born blind — one they treated with arcane magic, the other with the Holy Light. Guess which one had better results?"

"Uh." Jace thought for a moment. "The Holy Light? Can arcane magic actually heal people?"

"No, the arcane worked better." Khadgar said. "Counterintuitive, isn't it?"

"The patient healed by arcane energy regained her sight immediately, just like a normal person. Her family was moved to tears. So was she. But the one healed by the Holy Light... I'm not sure I'd even call it 'healed.' He said he could see *something*, but he had no idea what he was looking at."

"But actually... the archmage had simply replaced the function of her eyes with arcane vision. The bishop was the one who truly healed the damaged eyes. As the mana faded, the arcane-healed patient eventually went blind again. The Holy Light-healed patient, meanwhile, took a long time to match what he saw with things he was already familiar with from memory. But overall, he was gradually improving."

"Because someone born blind never learned as a child what the things they see actually *are*," Jace said. "The arcane-healed patient was essentially using the archmage's 'memory' to see the world."

"Precisely." Khadgar tapped his finger on the table. "For the Holy Light-healed patient, this strange new world was like a foreign book suddenly thrust in front of him — not a language learning primer, but an epic that's impenetrable even to native speakers."

"So, a Nathrezim's disguise is certainly a 'foreign epic' to an eye tyrant that doesn't know their features. And where is your 'language learning primer'? Do we have enough time, Jace?"

Jace pulled out the Nathrezim soul shard. "You forgot about this. The 'archmage's memory' you wanted."

Khadgar stared at the small, dark purple sliver and let out a long sigh. "I didn't forget. I almost forgot that you're a soul-mage trained under Gul'dan and the Auchenai. Can you please finish everything you have to say in one go next time?"

"You're the one who keeps asking questions," Jace said. "I didn't want to interrupt you while you were showing off your vast experience."

Khadgar propped his head up, looked like he wanted to say something, and finally just said, "Go on..."

"Do you have a place in Dalaran where we can summon an eye tyrant?" Jace looked up at the ceiling. "Like some property owned by deceased members of the Order of Tirisfal?"

"Impossible." Khadgar said. "Under the current circumstances, Antonidas's protective barrier would directly cut off any portal to the Twisting Nether. We can't fight the Eye of Dalaran. Even if you used Ulthalesh's power to punch a hole in the barrier, it would shake the entire city and completely cement your reputation as an archfiend — and me as your shameless accomplice. In short, it's not feasible."

"I know. I meant outside the city." Jace said. "Is there any place nearby you trust? Like Ambermill, or Dalaran's outer district, or Hillsbrad..."

"I do know a few places," the archmage said. "But I can't go with you."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"Because... for this projection to remain stable, I can't leave the barrier's vicinity." Khadgar traced a circle around his forehead with his finger. "But do you really need me there to complete the summoning? I can write down the incantation, the materials — everything you need for summoning an eye tyrant. Then you take them out of Dalaran and try the summoning... Wait, how are you going to bring the eye tyrant back inside?"

"After the summoning, we won't have time to earn the demon's trust. So we'll just take its eye." Jace said. "The eye will be separated from the demon's body, but it will still fear the Nathrezim's power... it will react when it sees through their disguise. At least, that's what the Kaldorei prisoner said."

"Interesting." Khadgar said quietly. "And that way, we won't trigger Dalaran's protective barrier."

"Exactly." Jace said. "But he also mentioned that to keep the demon eye stable after infusing it with soul shard energy, we'd need a sufficiently skilled arcane master to crystallize it."

"Great idea. Truly a great idea." The mage's tone was flat. "And thank you for your trust and high praise, Sesso. But I'm not that 'arcane master' you're talking about. The real master is currently sitting in the Violet Citadel having tea, listening to the two of us chat."

"Uh."

"Put simply, this projection can't handle such fine magical control... Well, maybe it could try, but the margin for error is way too high. You only have one dreadlord soul shard. I'd need to do it in person. The whole point of finding the disguised demons was to clear both our names and get them to let me out. But now I have to get out first just to clear my name? This is..."

"Then who in the city can do it?"

"Anyone skilled enough, I can't trust. Anyone I can trust, isn't skilled enough."

The dim room fell silent again. Jace swallowed, raised a finger, and organized his thoughts before speaking again. "Then could we..."

"Use this fake to swap out the real me?" The archmage let out a cold laugh and muttered, "Absolutely insane."
