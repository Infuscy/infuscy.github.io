## Chapter 1772: Lessons from Predecessors

"Alright, so our goal now is to use this fake to swap out the real me... myself... from the Violet Citadel."

Khadgar looked at everyone present, including Jace. "The good news is, the Violet Citadel's security is simpler than the Violet Hold's. No hundreds of trained battlemages and arcane golems specialized in countering all types of mages. No Tal'arfin crystals so difficult even the Quel'dorei struggle to control them. No runic cages personally designed by the first Guardian, Master Alodi. And..."

"Please skip to the bad news, Master," Grede said.

"The bad news is, Lord Thunderfist." Khadgar fixed his gaze on the dwarf. "The Violet Citadel's magical defenses are integrated with the entire city of Dalaran. They all draw power from the Eye of Dalaran — that terrifying artifact that rescued us from Draenor, whose power is enough to shake the Scepter of Sargeras."

"That doesn't sound like much," Needleflame muttered.

"Smashing it directly might not be a problem for you barbaric types, Little Blackwing." The archmage's projection glanced at Ulthalesh slung across Jace's back. "But right now, we're not trying to destroy it. We need to find a way around it to get into the Violet Citadel, to put this projection of mine into that royal suite wrapped in several layers of magical barriers — all without being detected by Master Antonidas himself, who lives next door and personally controls the entire defense system."

Jace and the others exchanged glances. Vereesa asked, "So how do we get around it?"

"This massive protective barrier..." Khadgar said, "was originally designed in part to keep an eye on troublemakers like us. If I knew how to bypass it or trick it, wouldn't I have swapped myself out already?"

"But now I have all of you — probably the people best suited to sneaking and ambushing in all of Azeroth — to help. I need you to pool your ideas. Otherwise, with the means, resources, and time we have right now, there's no way we can secretly fight those Nathrezim."

"Does this magical barrier ever turn off?" Jace asked.

"When the Eye of Dalaran was stolen by Teron Gorefiend and Deathwing, it did go down for a while due to lack of power," Khadgar said. "But Master Antonidas and the Council of Six quickly took over and brought it back online until we recovered the Eye."

"Right now, Dalaran's arcane vault is more secure than it's been in the last hundred years. After the Kel'Thuzad incident and the reforms, even members of the Council of Six have a hard time accessing it. Even if Deathwing were resurrected, with help from a Council member like Kel'Thuzad, he'd struggle to steal the Eye of Dalaran that quickly and quietly again. So you can forget about copying that crime."

"Wait, Archmage Khadgar." Vereesa said. "If I recall correctly, when Teron Gorefiend attacked Dalaran with the black dragons' cooperation... the black dragons had already started bombarding Dalaran's protective barrier when Gorefiend and his death knight army were already inside the inner city."

"That's right," Jace said. "I remember clearly. I felt the whole city start shaking during the orcish language meeting. When I left the hall and got to the street, death knights had already infiltrated."

Vereesa nodded. "So Dalaran's protective barrier didn't detect those death knights entering at all. The Eye of Dalaran hadn't been stolen by Deathwing yet at that point. The death knights were coordinating with Kel'Thuzad and Deathwing to distract the mages and slip into the vault. Was there some way they tricked the barrier?"

"Maybe it was Deathwing's dark magic," Grede said. "We all know those evil black dragons command strange shadow magics even demons don't use. It wouldn't be surprising if his power could nullify Dalaran's barrier."

"I don't doubt that Deathwing could nullify the barrier, Grede." Vereesa said. "But if he could do that, why did he have the black dragons attack the barrier at all? In that battle, Dalaran's mages killed plenty of evil whelps too. Dead black dragon corpses were everywhere outside the city and in Hillsbrad."

"Kel'Thuzad deceived the Council of Six?" Jace said. "The timing was tight, but he probably had enough time to get back to the Violet Citadel and fabricate some lies... lower the high mages' guard."

"That's probably not it." Khadgar turned around. "I wasn't in Dalaran at the time, but I know the barrier was controlled by Antonidas himself. Kel'Thuzad was the head of anti-dark magic operations and knew the barrier's status well, but he couldn't have hidden anything about the barrier from Antonidas... If death knights had forced their way through the barrier, it would have been impossible to fool the Archmage himself."

"Then we have no way of learning how the death knights bypassed the barrier?" Vereesa rubbed her brow anxiously and looked at Jace. "You absorbed part of Teron Gorefiend's soul, didn't you, Jace? Don't you have any related memories?"

"Gorefiend probably didn't consider it significant enough to remember clearly. I... can't recall," Jace said.

"I think I've got it." Khadgar said suddenly.

"What?" Everyone asked in unison.

"Dalaran's protective barrier, while prepared against all evil and dark magic, has its limits," Khadgar said. "A powerful enough mage, like Deathwing himself, could walk into Dalaran freely disguised as Lord Daval Prestor, and the barrier wouldn't react at all. But his children — the two that occasionally accompanied him to Lordaeron — never came to Dalaran with him."

"Right," Vereesa said. "That's true."

"So to defend against particularly stubborn assaults, Master Antonidas would perform a ritual from the top of the Violet Citadel to adjust the barrier's targeting parameters, preventing attackers from breaking through its 'limit,'" Khadgar explained. "For example, when Gul'dan's orc army reached Hillsbrad, he tuned the barrier's resistance to shadow and fel magic. During the period when black whelps were frequently active around Dalaran, the resistance to shadow and fire was maxed out..."

"So?" The ranger prompted.

"After adjustment, the barrier's targeted resistance would be greatly enhanced," Khadgar said. "But during the adjustment itself — the brief period when the ritual was being performed — the entire city's magical defenses would be severely weakened. This must have been why Deathwing ordered the black dragonflight to attack. When Dalaran detected the incoming black dragons, Master Antonidas would have had to urgently switch the barrier's targeting — and that's exactly when..."

"Teron Gorefiend and his death knight servants slipped into the city," Jace said.

"Exactly." Khadgar said. "As one of the barrier's maintainers, Kel'Thuzad knew this perfectly well. He must have been the one who suggested it to Deathwing."

"How does that help our plan?" Grede looked between the two of them. "Go back to Kalimdor and ask Lady Onyxia to help? Have her launch another siege on Dalaran?"

"No need to trouble Princess Onyxia." Khadgar said. "We just need to create the impression that black dragons are targeting Dalaran again. And we have a pure-blooded black dragon right here."

"You want a whelp to alarm the entire city of Dalaran?" Grede asked.

"Why not?" Needleflame raised her hand. "I can do it!"

"It's not that I doubt your abilities." The dwarf looked at the little black dragon. "But..."

"Thanks to green dragons rarely appearing before mortals," Khadgar said, "most of Lordaeron's scouts and young mages can't tell a green dragon from a black dragon under the high sun. And we have a green dragon lady here who flies fast and is big enough — at least enough to pose a credible threat."

"I..." Sora pointed at herself blankly. "But I don't know how to do black dragon breath."

"Leave the scaring to Needleflame," the warlock said. "You just fly and create 'presence.'"

"Exactly." The mage waved his hand. "Make a few passes over towns and villages, Lady Sora. As long as the little Blackwing princess starts a small fire somewhere — no casualties even needed — and the reports of adult black dragons from other outposts will be enough to make Master Antonidas adjust the barrier. Dalaran won't tolerate even a scratch... especially after the death knight breach last time. It severely shook this city's sense of security."

"I understand," the green dragon said.

"So, alright." The dwarf said. "But even if Antonidas really falls for it... how long are we talking?"

"Ten minutes," the mage said.

"That short?!" The dwarf blew out his mustache.

"Ten minutes..." The ranger said. "To swap out a heavily guarded archmage without triggering any alarms or drawing anyone's attention?"

"Because it's short and fast, Master Antonidas won't be especially cautious about switching the barrier's targeting, Lady Windrunner," Khadgar said. "If the switching ritual were a massive magical undertaking that gave us hours to move at our leisure, do you think you could trick him into doing it so easily?"

"Fair point," Vereesa said quietly.

"So finding a way past the barrier is just the first step, Vereesa." Jace said. "The details will need more careful planning."
