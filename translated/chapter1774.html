Chapter 1774: Enough of a Show

"Who else could it be?" Khadgar looked over. "If there's anyone who could quietly cast dark magic inside a weakened barrier without being noticed, it would probably be you, Jace." "You proved it at Blackrock Spire — you showed me you can cast spells directly using emotions. And what the Violet Citadel has no shortage of, besides all sorts of runic barriers and regulations so suffocating they could choke a man to death... is all sorts of people and all sorts of emotions." "Shouldn't be a problem," Jace said. "Before entering Dalaran, I tried manipulating a guard's suspicion without triggering the anti-dark magic runes on his robe. Once Dalaran's magical defenses are weakened, I should be able to manipulate that magic more freely." "The whole city of Dalaran is steeped in uncertainty right now — maybe because no one knows when the plague will truly end, maybe because of the rumors about me. Either way, it plays right into my hands. Combined with Ulthalesh..." "You're going to use that staff inside the Violet Citadel, Jace?" Vereesa asked. "If necessary," Jace said. "Don't worry, Ranger-Lady," Khadgar said. "Even Karazhan couldn't sense this demon staff slipping in, let alone Dalaran. It's better at hiding itself than you think." Vereesa gave a cautious nod, then looked at Jace, and then at the green dragon beside her. "You think we two can work in sync?" Jace asked. "Yes," Sora nodded without hesitation. "It warms my heart to see you two trust each other so much," Khadgar said. "I half-expected you'd think this couldn't work." "I'm wondering — did you figure all this out long ago?" Jace asked. "I've thought about far more than this, but without your involvement, most of it would be completely impossible," Khadgar said. "So, Lord Thunderfist, have you just about got it straight now?" "So it's like..." Grede's index finger pointed back and forth between the green dragon and the warlock. "Sora's in charge of keepin' your decoy alive, and Jace is in charge of makin' your real self dead..." "No, no, no, can't die," the mage quickly waved his hands. "Half-dead," Grede corrected, then continued. "While the two of 'em cast spells on the cell door to fool the prison's defenses, lettin' one of you in and the other out, me and the sprite'll keep watch and distract the patrolmin' magic guards. Needleflame'll fly around outside makin' news. Each of us does our part, and we finish all the steps inside ten minutes..." "That's it," Khadgar said with a relieved smile. "Good," the dwarf clapped his hands and stood up. "Then let's get start—" "—start looking for a way to open the cell door," the mage finished his sentence. "Cell door? Right, the deluxe suite's door! After all that yammerin' I forgot!" Grede flopped back into his chair. "That's way too many layers!" "But compared to Master Frevin's life barrier, a cell door isn't that far outside our expectations," Vereesa said, looking exhausted. "True, relatively speaking it's just an ordinary enough door. But it still needs to be opened normally," Khadgar said, looking at the dwarf. "Tight security has its downsides and its upsides. The upside is that to be safe, Master Drenden generally doesn't leave the castle, let alone Dalaran, so the key and the cell door are in the same place. Very convenient." "Won't using the key trigger the alarm?" Vereesa asked. "Not as long as it's within that ten-minute window," Khadgar said. "At least, by my calculations, it won't." "Ha!" the dwarf across from him let out a laugh. "Don't tell me this Master Drenden is also sittin' with the key in a heavily fortified room, waitin' for us to peel apart box after box." "Exactly right, esteemed Lord Thunderfist, High Thane in name!" Khadgar turned and pointed at Grede. "But we don't need to peel these boxes ourselves. After all, Drenden isn't under house arrest like me! He can leave his room on his own." The nominal Mountain King coughed irritably. "So we gotta wait for him to come out first? Then lift the key?" "That probably won't work," Jace said quietly. "Because we don't know when Antonidas will decide to perform the ritual. Which means the starting signal isn't up to us — it's up to the Violet Citadel. By then, Drenden could be in that magic room Khadgar mentioned, or he could be elsewhere. So the best approach is to find a reason to lure him out after the ritual starts." "Master Drenden is a very senior dwarven researcher and antiquities appraisal expert," Vereesa said. "We could prepare a relic — say, an artifact unearthed from the ancient Kaldorei Empire or a Titan ruin — and ask him to appraise it. He shouldn't refuse." "So do we have something like that?" Jace asked. "The Kaldorei carapace armor?" Vereesa said. "It shouldn't be completely ruined yet, should it?" "A Kaldorei artifact from the War of the Shifting Sands a thousand years ago?" Khadgar said. "I know those Kaldorei elite guard armors. The main problem is they're too distinctive — you don't need an antiquities expert like Drenden to identify them. If you bring that to the Violet Citadel, they won't call Drenden out for you." "Right, and we only have ten minutes from when we start moving," Vereesa shook her head. "I'm afraid those mage apprentices won't even make up their minds about whether to call Master Drenden before time runs out." "How about we just take yer staff instead," Grede said, looking at Jace. "Nobody'll recognize it anyway." "Is sneaking something in the same as taking it out for everyone to see?" Jace glared at him. "Why not use your roast-beast warhammer?" "Roast-beast warhammer?" Khadgar frowned. "Everyone knows what that thing is, Jace..." Vereesa said helplessly. "The Wind-Kissed Blade," Sora raised a finger from behind. "We can use the Wind-Kissed Blade. Few mortals have seen that blade with their own eyes. And since it's forged from Arcanite metal, it must be ancient — so ancient even the dragonflights would find it hard to fathom..." "Great idea, Sora," Jace's eyes lit up. "What in the world is the Wind-Kissed Blade?" Khadgar looked around blankly at the group. "The tip of the greatsword wielded by Prince Thunderaan the Windlord, Khadgar," Sora explained. "We rescued him from Ragnaros the Firelord's prison. He destroyed this cursed sword — a gift from the Windlord himself — to show his resolve, and granted us the blade tip, which holds the most power." "You actually found a way to free that elemental lord?" Khadgar looked back and forth at the group. "The Windlord, a black dragon princess, a demon prison in Ashenvale... I say, Jace Sesso, didn't you say you were going to Kalimdor to relax a bit on the side? This is what you call relaxing?!" "That's not the point, mage," Jace said. "The point is, if even you don't know what this is, the average Kirin Tor mage certainly won't. So they'll definitely have to call Drenden himself out for a look." "Well, can I see this sword?" Khadgar asked. "I left that thing downstairs," Grede said, getting up and heading down the stairs. "Y'know how tirin' it is to carry somethin' that big and heavy through Dalaran's streets while actin' like it's no big deal? Never mind that I'm already luggin' Sulfuras around." "No one finds it strange for a dwarf to carry a shield of any size, so this still had to be your job," Vereesa said as she followed him down the stairs. "Shield?" Khadgar asked, puzzled. "Wasn't it a blade tip?" "You'll see when you get there," Jace said. The group reached the ground floor. Khadgar spotted the object — wrapped in layers of enchanted cloth and leather, shaped like a massive rhomboid shield — and couldn't help raising an eyebrow. As Grede peeled away the wrappings layer by layer, the archmage's features gradually twisted together. An ice-blue arcane edge peeked out from one corner, releasing a bolt of lightning that flashed the whole hall white. The archmage's decoy clenched his jaw hard. "What do you think?" Jace asked. Khadgar stared at the wisp of lightning that had been covered again, murmuring: "No one will recognize it. Clearly not an ordinary magical item. The moment it appears, it'll seize the attention of the entire Violet Citadel — from the highest-ranking mage down to every single magical guard." "As a way to lure Master Drenden out, it might be a bit much. But as a tool to yank everyone's attention away and give us room to move... couldn't ask for anything more perfect." "Something with this much spectacle should be carried into the hall by the person with the most spectacle," Jace said. Everyone's eyes locked onto Grede Thunderfist at the same time. Needleflame said quietly: "You touched this blade first, remember, dwarf? It's yours now." "What if another dwarf recognizes him?" Sora asked. "He is the Mountain King." "No, no, no, Sora, that won't happen," Vereesa said. "For one thing, it's unlikely any dwarves will be in the Violet Citadel. And more importantly, the legendary Mountain King probably wouldn't have such an ugly beard." "What?!" Grede grabbed his tangled beard and snarled through clenched teeth: "This is still from when yer bloody little sweetheart hacked at it with that bloody crappy sword you gave her?!"
