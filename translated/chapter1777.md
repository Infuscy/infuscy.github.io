## Chapter 1777: More Than Just a Show

"One second, two seconds—time truly shows no mercy," Khadgar murmured.

Jace glanced at the deepest steps of the Violet Citadel's main hall, then looked out toward the rain-shrouded streets beyond the gate. "I hope Sora gets here in time."

"The barrier's already dropped. She shouldn't be detected slipping through the dome, but are you sure those earrings of yours can keep her hidden from all of Dalaran?" Khadgar asked quietly.

"No problem," said Jace. "Aside from ghosts skulking around in the Emerald Dream and your teacher's own mother, no one's seen through that pendant yet."

"Her... right."

Khadgar squinted, checked his pocket watch again, and cast an anxious look toward Drenden's group of mages. Then his expression shifted.

"Something's happening, Jace. Drenden is..."

Before Khadgar could finish, Jace's eyes snapped to the scene. Drenden was leading Grede across the central hall toward a corridor further in.

But Drenden's students and assistants were keeping their distance from the Kirin Tor Council of Six member, trailing behind Grede instead. Drenden walked alone at the front, leaving a wide gap between himself and everyone else—a gap that left Vereesa no room to make a move.

As Drenden and Grede drew closer to that corridor, Jace knew that once Drenden got the key back to the reception room and away from the hall crowd, their chance would be gone.

"Wait here, Khadgar." Jace was watching Vereesa as he spoke. The elf, who had been scanning for an opening, caught his gaze.

"What are you going to do?" asked Khadgar.

"Give her a hand."

Jace strode quickly toward the middle of the hall to cut off Drenden's party. Grede noticed his companion suddenly bearing down on them and widened his eyes in surprise. Jace tilted his head toward Drenden as he walked. Grede gave a faint, uncertain nod, then ventured, "Oi... tell me—there aren't any magical traps in this castle of yours, are there? You're not gonna lock me in a room and magic my treasure away, are you? What's-his-name... Grendon... Grimble... Master?"

"You don't trust the Kirin Tor?!" Jace demanded.

Drenden turned around, about to speak, when Jace cut him off. The archmage shot him a helpless look, but before he could offer any polite reassurance, Grede bellowed, "Everyone knows Dalaran wizards love pinching other folks' goodies! Ha—I reckon I've hit a nerve, you little mage!"

The surrounding mages' faces curdled. Jace, seizing the rising indignation, pressed on: "Where are you from, dwarf? Ironforge? You look more like a Wildhammer to me. I hear the sages of Aerie Peak know a thing or two about artifacts of thunder and storm. Yet you bring yours to Dalaran. Makes you wonder if you didn't lift it from Aerie Peak yourself!"

"What?! You've got the gall to call me a thief, you curly-headed little whelp? I'll bloody—"

Grede's tangled beard and mane of black hair bristled out like an enraged miniature bear as he charged straight at Jace. The guards and mages reacted quickly, but how could any of them stop a Mountain King?

The dwarf slammed into them like a bowling ball through a stack of bottles. Even Archmage Drenden was caught off guard, knocked back several meters and sent sprawling. Hard to say if Grede did that on purpose... Shouts, yells, curses—the Violet Citadel's hall was chaos once more. The ornate chandeliers overhead swayed violently; lamps, bookshelves, and ornamental stands along the walls toppled one after another.

Jace stumbled back several steps, instinctively trying to get clear, but the dwarf caught him anyway. As Grede's fist came up, only one thought crossed Jace's mind: *We were just messing around, big guy—why'd you have to make it real?*

Suddenly, bands of bright yellow Arcane energy coiled around Grede's limbs—especially the wrist of his raised fist—holding him fast. Grede let out a roar and snapped the bindings, but more Arcane energies wove in to stop him from bringing that fist down on the helpless apprentice below...

"Enough!" Modera raised her staff and declared, "In the name of the Wildhammer clan and the Kirin Tor fighting side by side through countless battles against the Orcs, and in the name of High Thane Kurdran Wildhammer, leader of the Sons of Lothar—we will not detain you today, dwarf. But you will be expelled from Dalaran. Go cool your head somewhere else and have your treasure appraised there."

"Almost there, almost there..." Jace muttered under his breath.

Grede immediately looked up and shouted, "Sorry—please, no—I was wrong. Don't banish me from Dalaran, Lady Modera. No one at Aerie Peak knows what this thing is. That's why I came here. Your archmages are my last hope—I truly need to know what this is..."

"I gave you your chance, dwarf," Modera said coldly.

By now, many apprentices in the hall were berating the dwarf. Drenden helped Jace up and asked, "Are you alright?"

"I'm fine, Archmage Drenden. Lady Modera saved my life..." Jace glanced at his pocket watch. "Oh no—I was supposed to meet my teacher here to sort through some materials today. I'm going to be late."

"Then go find your teacher. Don't worry about things here." Drenden nodded.

Jace nodded back, rose, and ducked into the crowd. Suddenly a hand grabbed him and pulled him aside. A few curious mages who had been following the unusually tall apprentice lost track of him in an instant.

Elsewhere, Grede had somehow started another argument, and several Kirin Tor guards were struggling—and failing—to drag him out.

As Jace was pulled along backward, he tried subtly nudging the curiosity of those around him away from the nameless apprentice who'd started all this and back toward the troublemaking dwarf... Finally they reached a quiet corridor corner. Khadgar, satisfied nobody was watching, tugged his hood and followed.

"How'd it go?"

"I've got it, don't I?" Vereesa patted her pouch. "Let's get upstairs. Less than eight minutes."

Jace and Khadgar nodded and hurried after the ranger into the corridor. Jace grinned. "I didn't even see you make your move..."

"And if you had, what kind of thief would I be?" Vereesa smirked, tilting her head. "When you visit Quel'Thalas, ask around about who I used to lift things from... Archmage Drenden is sharp, but he's still a step behind the Ranger-General herself. Of course, I owe you for making the opening."

"Don't mention it," said Jace.

The archmage's duplicate let out a relieved breath. But as they rounded a corner and looked up, the smiles vanished.

On the stairs leading to the third floor stood two violet-robed guards. Another guard was ascending at the landing above, and he stopped dead when three figures suddenly materialized below him.

Three pairs of eyes met three pairs of eyes. Air and time alike seemed to freeze.

"Please turn back, three mages," said the foremost Kirin Tor guard, raising his staff. "The upper towers of the Violet Citadel are closed to entry at present. If you have an urgent appointment, please reschedule it for this afternoon."

"The afternoon would be too late, sir," said Jace.

"This is Archmage Antonidas's order. My apologies," said the guard. "This concerns the safety of Dalaran. Everything else must yield."

"...Fine. I'll come back in the afternoon."

Jace, Vereesa, and Khadgar retreated to the second floor. Jace asked the mage's duplicate what was going on—why the lockdown...

"Someone's worried about people slipping into the vault or the Citadel while the barrier's down?" Khadgar ran a hand through his hair. "Damn. In other words, it's meant to stop people exactly like us. Looks like after that Kel'Thuzad business, they upgraded more than just the Citadel's alert protocols."

"Can't we pull some magical trick while we're at it?" Jace whispered. "The barrier's out of commission anyway—it shouldn't be as sensitive to magical fluctuations right now."

"The problem isn't the barrier, Jace—it's me! This duplicate of mine barely scrapes by on Arcane power. Maybe not even as much as you," said Khadgar.

"What... what..." Jace sighed and glanced at his watch. "What do we do?"

"Go through a window." Vereesa pointed at the dim light at the end of the corridor. "The classic solution."

"We've got a little over six minutes. Is that enough to climb?" asked Khadgar. "There are several more tower levels to go."

"Right." The elf took a breath. "I can manage, more or less. But you two..."

"I didn't bring that dragonhide cloak. Can't fly up." Jace shrugged helplessly. "But if I'd worn it, I'd have been caught ages ago. When is Sora going to show up..."

"She's going to the fifth floor invisible, Jace. Don't forget—nobody can see her, and neither can we." Vereesa said. "Don't count on her. We're running out of time. If she makes it into position on her own, that'll be enough."

"The problem now is that we can't get into position either... Don't you have that flight spell?" Khadgar looked at the ranger. "The one you learned from the Emerald Dream—I remember it well. You could take us up."

"That... that's not really a 'flight spell,'" said Vereesa. "I can summon wings to glide a bit or gain some altitude—like to that roof over there with the envoys. But the fifth floor of this tower is way too high. I can't fly that high, let alone carrying the two of you!"

"What if we had a strong enough updraft?" asked Jace.

"Where's the wind supposed to come from?" Khadgar planted his hands on his hips. "That measly breeze outside couldn't even blow my beard up five floors!"

Vereesa frowned at first, then raised her eyebrows as realization struck. "Going back to him so soon—won't he be angry?" she asked.

Khadgar looked blankly at Jace.

"This isn't about whether he'll be angry—it's about whether we'll still be alive!" said Jace. "Wait for me by the window. I'm going back to find that dwarf."

With that, the warlock took off, dashing toward the corridor that led to the second-floor balcony of the hall, leaving the mage's duplicate staring after him in bewilderment.
