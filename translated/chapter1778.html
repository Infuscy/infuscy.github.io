Chapter 1778: One More Favor

Back in the main hall, Grede had already been dragged to the doorway. Jace had no time to hesitate. From the balcony he watched the chaos below, keeping his eyes on the dwarf. Just as Jace was figuring out how to get his attention — to make him look up to the second floor — Grede, mid-struggle and mid-argument, rolled across the floor, spun around, and caught sight of his face.

So the guy hadn't come to the Violet Citadel purely for a brawl after all. At least he still remembered they were here to plan a rescue, and he was still keeping tabs on what Jace and the others were up to.

Jace pointed at the "shield" on the ground, then clapped his hands, repeating both motions over and over — signaling for Grede to touch the Wind-Kissed Blade.

Grede scrunched his nose like he didn't get it, then got tackled by a Violet Guard. Jace smacked the railing in frustration. When the dwarf finally fought his way free and glanced back through a tangle of legs and arms, Jace spun his finger in a cyclone motion, then clapped his hands again. Grede's expression froze. He understood. He definitely understood.

The dwarf yanked the people grabbing him, reclaimed his "shield," tore off the wrapping, and touched the blade. He muttered something under his breath — impossible to hear over the noise of the chaotic hall — but then the sky outside the gates visibly darkened. In an instant, an eerie wind kicked up, howling and shrieking...

"What are you doing here, apprentice?" a Kirin Tor guard asked, walking up beside him.

Jace's hands froze mid-clap. He forced a smile. "The Battle Mages are very brave. That dwarf's a tough one. I was just... clapping for them."

"That piece of metal can awaken the power of wind elementals!" Drenden's voice suddenly rang out from below. "There's a reason it's wrapped in all those layers of rune wrappings! Don't let that dwarf unwrap it any further!"

"You have no idea what kind of terrible artifact you're carrying, Homir Deepstone!"

"The hell are you blathering about?!" Grede roared back. "If I knew what I was carrying, would I have come to Antonidas?! Would I have come to Dalaran?! You just want to steal my stuff, and now you're helping yourselves, aren't ya! Not even pretending anymore! Fine then — come and get it!"

Chaotic as the brawl already was, it couldn't drown out the wind growing louder outside. From the second-floor balcony, Jace could see people on the streets and mages who'd come to watch — their robes flapping wildly, hats flying everywhere, barely able to keep their feet.

The guard beside him saw things going sideways and forgot all about Jace. He ran straight down the stairs to back up Drenden and Modera. At the main entrance, Modera was directing mages and guards to stabilize the situation while constantly glancing up at the highest point of the dome. She must have been terrified that the wind element's tremors would disrupt Antonidas's ritual — or even the entire magical barrier of Dalaran...

Wouldn't that be nice, Jace thought.

He turned and sprinted toward the deepest part of the corridor, glancing at his pocket watch. Four and a half minutes left. Damn it. Grede had caught on fast enough, but that moment of hesitation had still cost half a minute.

Several guards pushed past him, heading down toward the hall. Khadgar and Vereesa were already waiting by the window. The moment Jace was in position, Khadgar shoved the window open. A blast of freezing wind knocked all three of them nearly off their feet.

Outside, the entire city of Dalaran had been thrown into chaos by the sudden storm. From tall towers to mage spires to inns and hotels — nearly every open window had one or more frantic wizards or servants scrambling to shut it.

"Thunderaan!" Jace screamed into the gale, shouting in the Elemental language. "Blow upward! Harder! One more favor — I'm sure for you this is nothing..."

Before he could finish, the wind shifted direction. The sudden change in airflow whipped Jace's robe up and slapped it across his own face.

Khadgar stared in disbelief. "It actually changed... Bloody hell, that Windseeker is actually willing to help you?!"

"Hurry, we're running out of time." Vereesa gripped the windowsill. "Jump!"

Jace didn't hesitate. He vaulted over the windowsill. Khadgar followed. Behind them, Vereesa spread her wind-trembling wings, grabbed one of them with each hand, and leapt into the gale.

The air current kept pushing upward. Jace worried that flying so brazenly high above the Violet Citadel might catch the attention of mages on the streets below.

But the rain hammered their faces like gravel, the black clouds pressed so low over Lake Lordamere it felt like night had already fallen. Debris and hats filled the air everywhere. Who was going to notice a few robe-like shapes drifting through the sky?

And even if someone did notice, by the time the Kirin Tor freed up manpower, found witnesses, gathered evidence, and investigated who was flying around in this terrible elemental storm, who knew how long that would take? By then Lordaeron itself might already be destroyed.

White tiles streaked past their ears too fast to make out any patterns or decorations. From the second floor to the fifth — it was there in the blink of an eye. The archmage's simulacrum chanted a spell. The window before them flew open, then shattered from the force of the gale. The three of them tumbled inside with a shower of broken glass.

Vereesa landed, looked around, and once she was sure no one was there, asked, "What about the window?"

"What about it?" Khadgar pointed to the right. "Let's go. The wind broke it. We didn't."

"...Fair enough." Vereesa tilted her head. "I suppose flying in through a broken window works too."

Before she finished speaking, they spotted a blond elven mage standing ahead, staring at them in bewilderment.

"You've got just over three minutes. What took you so long..."

"You flew too fast, Sora!" Vereesa pulled Sora into a hug.

Khadgar cut in. "This isn't the time for compliments. My cell is to the right. Move it. The next steps are the ones that'll trigger the wards — they have to be done within three minutes. Everyone. Otherwise my simulacrum gets caught by the barrier, and none of the spellwork that follows will be possible."

Thunderaan had clearly eased off, but the wind outside hadn't fully died yet. This was exactly the moment to strike.

The three of them followed the mage's simulacrum quickly down the corridor. At a turn, Khadgar pointed left. They all turned together — then stopped together.

Ahead, a Violet Guard stood with his back to them, walking toward an ornate wooden door at the end of the hall.

The howling wind outside must have drowned out their footsteps. But he was almost at the door. When he turned around to patrol back this way, he'd spot them instantly.

What to do?

Vereesa, Khadgar, and Jace exchanged glances. Sora didn't hesitate — her lips moved silently. The guard's body went limp, about to fall. Jace Shadowstepped to his side and caught him before he could hit the floor.

"You hypnotized him?" Vereesa asked.

"Before he saw us," Sora said. "Three minutes, Jace. When he wakes up, he'll just think he dozed off for no reason. He won't remember anyone was here."

"No other choice. If we do this cleanly, when he opens his eyes everything will look the same. The prisoner inside won't have left yet. It's fine." Jace propped the guard securely against the wall, then looked at Vereesa. "Open the door now."

The ranger nodded, pulled out the Mithril Key, and inserted it into the door. Arcane Runes lit up across its surface — faint, barely visible.

The door creaked open, revealing a pale green barrier behind it, rippling like water. The white-bearded Khadgar was pressed up against the other side of that barrier, staring out at them.

"Mmph. Still, clean-shaven suits you better. Especially from an 'outsider's' perspective." The archmage stared hard at Jace. "Remember — hold back. Say it with me: don't actually kill me."

"Don't worry." A deep purple darkness was already forming around Jace's fingertips. "I know my own strength."
