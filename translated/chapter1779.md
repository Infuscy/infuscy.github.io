## Chapter 1779: Three Minutes, or Ten Seconds?

"In that case, get ready. I'm going in." Khadgar's clone looked back at the three people behind him.

Sora shot a quick glance at the warlock beside her. The sheer nervousness radiating off her was practically strangling.

Jace nodded. Inside the room, the real Khadgar stepped aside, and his clone casually strode through the barrier. Whether it was because the magical barrier was already falling apart or because that was how the "Life Detection Barrier" was originally designed, the clone passed through without meeting any resistance at all.

So now the room held one completely lifeless clone and one fully living Khadgar. Total: one person's life force. The room didn't trigger any visible alarms. The severely weakened barrier paid no attention to the arcane energy construct that had slipped through the door.

"Can we start now?" Sora asked.

"No time left." Jace said. "Do it, Sora. Just like we planned."

"I know." The young green dragon replied curtly, then focused her concentration on channeling nature's power. She wasn't putting her energy into raw spell strength — almost all of it went into precision. She'd even given Vereesa back Ysera's earring beforehand, to avoid the Queen's power boost making her mana run hotter than usual.

A pleasant, neither-hot-nor-cold breeze blew in from outside the corridor, carrying the faint floral scent that permeated all of Dalaran past the barrier and swirling around the clone.

At the same time, Jace had finished gathering his shadow energy, chanting softly: "Eramanth, Siliondor."

Not just Sora's nature magic — almost all magic worked this way. Jacking up a spell's power was usually crude and straightforward, and weakening it was equally direct. But keeping your magic at a precise level you could control smoothly — that burned far more mana than the spell's actual power demanded.

But for Jace now, especially carrying Usathers, this was nothing.

Khadgar's brow suddenly furrowed tight. He coughed painfully and grabbed the coat rack, the color draining from his face. His lips moved but no words came out.

"Increase the healing spell's strength a bit, Lady Sora." The archmage's clone said from the side. "The life energy balance has dropped below what we need to maintain."

Sora nodded and pushed harder. The airflow grew strong enough to flutter sleeves and robe hems. Vereesa clutched Jace's pocket watch, checking it again and again, her expression growing more anxious by the moment.

Suddenly she whipped her head around to look back down the corridor. Jace knew — someone else was coming.

Sure enough, before long, footsteps and voices grew more urgent and louder, approaching their position.

Had the Violet Citadel staff noticed something was wrong? With less than three minutes left, what else could go wrong now?

As the voices became almost distinguishable, Sora's healing energy wavered. Khadgar's clone immediately warned in a low voice: "Don't get distracted, Lady Sora."

Khadgar himself couldn't speak at all anymore. He slumped painfully against the wall, his legs barely holding him up. Jace glanced at the clone, but it just shook its head. "Not enough."

Not enough? If they kept going, Khadgar might actually die here. Now even Jace's will was starting to crack, let alone Sora's.

The clone looked sideways at the archmage's body with an expression that was utterly calm — almost cold, as if this person had nothing to do with it.

What if this clone was actually a Nathrezim? What if it had tricked Jace and the others into getting Antonidas to lower the barrier, just so this demon could sneak its disguised arcane clone past the citadel's and the prison's "detection"? And its goal wasn't to rescue Khadgar at all — it was to assassinate him and pin all the blame on Jace and company. The Kirin Tor guards chasing them down the corridor were just the finishing move this dreadlord had arranged in advance... the sword that had been dangling over their heads the whole time, just waiting to drop.

"Keep going, Jace Sesso! Not enough yet!" The archmage's clone glared. "Sora's life-healing is about to surpass the life force you're drawing out. Can't you feel that? Don't let go now! Don't let the life energy balance slip!"

No. Impossible.

Besides him, Khadgar, and Morophil, no one knew what had happened inside Karazhan. No one could have come up with that code phrase.

Jace pulled the deep shadow that had nearly swallowed him back into his palm, controlling every last thread of life force with everything he had. Just one last shred remained. It was barely anything. Khadgar looked no different from a cold corpse — just this one last breath away, and then even the Dragonqueen herself couldn't save him.

Just as Jace's barely-reinforced will began to waver again, Khadgar's clone suddenly grabbed its original body and hurled it over the barrier. At the same moment, Sora threw her arms wide and launched a burst of life energy after it, completing the body. The watery life-veil settled as quietly as if nothing had happened — even that initial tremor was gone.

Vereesa immediately pinched the Flow Ice Crystal, releasing a flash of golden light accompanied by a clear chime. Khadgar, who had been more dead than alive, forced his eyes open and coughed violently twice. The arcane gold light in his eyes hadn't faded yet. He reached up, grabbed the Major Healing Potion Jace handed him, and chugged it down. Some color returned to his face.

Behind the veil, the clone looked at its seated original and muttered: "Well, whatever extra lifespan I've built up from all those decades of arcane practice is probably all gone now. What can I say, folks? For Azeroth..."

At the same moment, Khadgar scrambled up and chanted a spell at lightning speed. The rune-covered door in front of them snapped shut, sealing the clone inside. Without the veil's glow, the corridor sank back into dimness.

They'd made it. But no one had time to celebrate. As the door clicked shut, Jace gestured for them to leave quickly. The moment he turned around, three figures were already blocking the T-shaped corridor ahead.

These battle mages held enchanted longswords, their eyes fixed on the passage. Jace and Sora stared back. Khadgar wiped black blood from the corner of his mouth and pulled himself up against the wall.

The two groups faced off for several seconds. No one spoke. It felt like hours...

But soon Jace realized their gazes weren't focused on him or his companions — not even on the colleague sleeping standing up against the wall. They were fixed on the door behind them.

"Everything's normal."

"But where did Master Mustar go? Wasn't he on patrol here?" "Maybe he went that way. There's a window over there that got blown open by the elemental storm..."

"Then, Master Rudis, inform Archmage Modera. Archmage Khadgar's residence is short-handed. Request she dispatch reinforcements as soon as possible."

"Master Surias, watch that corridor and that window. I'll cover this direction. Make sure nothing goes wrong until the ritual is complete and Archmage Modera takes over this floor."

"Yes, Master Preston."

The three battle mages split up and headed away. Jace glanced sideways. Vereesa was holding Ysera's pendant, which glowed faintly with the light of dreams. Somehow, indistinct light and shadow had already enveloped this section of the corridor.

"I used to oppose planting so much greenery in Dalaran." Khadgar breathed a sigh of relief. "Excess life energy puts extra pressure on the barrier. But the Archmage believed that guarding against evil shouldn't interfere with citizens' quality of life... right now, I have to thank him for that beautiful insistence."

"Also thank the Farstrider's quick reflexes." The elf tossed the key over. "Here, Archmage Khadgar. Repair the key on the way down. We don't have time."

"Understood." Khadgar pointed at the Kirin Tor guard still sleeping against the wall. "What about him?"

"You just returned from patrol, Master Mustar. You happened to miss the other guards... you didn't see them because the elemental storm interfered with your sense of sound." Sora chanted softly in front of the guard. "Ten seconds from now, you'll wake up. And this ten-second nap has dispelled an entire morning's tension and fatigue."
