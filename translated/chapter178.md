## Chapter 178: Dream of the North

Snow swirled through the air. A lone young man trudged through the drifts with difficulty — every step blocked by thick snow and howling wind, every step looking like it might be the one that sent him sprawling before his foot could find purchase.

Jace thought this dream was absurd. What was he doing out in the snow wearing nothing but a single thin shirt?

Where was this — Northrend?

He'd never been to Northrend. Why would he dream about Northrend of all places?

Not to mention, as far as he could see there was nothing but white on white. Anything farther out was completely swallowed by the blizzard. Was this even what dreaming felt like?

After walking for what felt like forever, Jace suddenly sensed a towering shadow impossibly far in the distance. A tower? Or a cathedral?

A tower in Northrend… was that Wyrmrest Temple?

Wyrmrest Temple was where the dragons dwelt, and the titan colossus stood on the very snowfield where dragons came to die in their old age — countless dragon bones buried there, which was why it was also called the Dragonblight.

From this direction, the colossus was terrifyingly massive. Maybe it wasn't as exaggerated as some twenty-first-century super-skyscraper, but standing all alone on the snowfield, visible from who knew how many miles off, it radiated an oppressive weight.

Wherever this was, at least that shadow was the only "thing" in his entire field of vision. If he kept walking toward it, maybe he'd wake up.

Jace could feel the biting cold, but wearing nothing but a single thin shirt, he couldn't manage to freeze to death or even pass off from exposure. He just shivered his way forward for what felt like an eternity, never collapsing from exhaustion.

After climbing over a snow ridge, a second shadow appeared in his vision — this one much closer, perched on a cliff edge not far ahead.

It was a person.

"Hey! Who are you — can you get me out of here?"

Jace shouted, then stood where he was, waiting for a response.

The man glanced back over his shoulder at Jace. He was a human male, black-haired, around forty, with a neat mustache above his lips.

He wore a plain but heavy brown outer robe — looked like a nobleman, or maybe a soldier. But what really stood out was his face…

He was almost offensively handsome. The kind of good-looking that made Jace slightly uncomfortable.

A bit like a middle-aged Tom Cruise, the vibe of a George Clooney, but compared to both of them, there was something almost supernatural about his features — the kind of face that made it hard to believe it actually existed.

Then again, this was a dream. That tracked.

"Who are you?" Jace asked.

"You come to my dwelling and ask who I am?" the man said, sounding displeased.

Jace came straight out with it: "I'm guessing that once I figure out who you are, I can leave here."

"Then guess. Who am I?" the man asked.

Jace studied his face, then let his gaze drift past him to that impossibly distant tower at the edge of the sky, turning the man's words over in his mind — *my dwelling*.

"You…"

Jace's eyes snapped open. He was drenched in sweat.

He sat up. It was already light out. Grede was sitting in a chair watching him.

"Another nightmare? What'd you see this time?"

"Uh…"

"Can't even tell me that? You've got too many secrets."

"Fine. A black dragon," Jace said.

Grede stroked his beard. "What a coincidence — I dreamed of a black dragon last night too. Truly terrifying… You think it was those black dragons attacking Dalaran that spooked us both? Come to think of it, I've dreamed of black dragons more than once since Southshore. It's awful. Sleep used to be something I enjoyed, and now I'm actually a little afraid to close my eyes these past few nights!"

Jace turned Grede's words over in his mind, but before he could think it through, a fresh wave of searing pain surged from his shoulder, sharper than before.

"Wait… why does it hurt so much! And it's getting worse." He rolled onto his side on the bed, sweating from the pain.

"I saw you keep a straight face yesterday and thought Lordaeronians were born tough. Turns out you were just too exhausted to feel it!" Grede hurriedly went back to rummaging through the cabinet. "Hold on, let me see if there's anything for the pain… You must have sat up too fast. Can't you be more careful with an injury?"

"That's my fault too?!" Jace pressed his head hard against the headboard and endured it for a good while, until Grede ran over, changed his medicine, and wrapped a fresh bandage. He gritted his teeth through it before the pain finally eased a little.

"You really do need to see a healer, Jace," Grede said as he tied off the bandage.

"Definitely… let's go now."

Jace inched himself upright like an inchworm and took a deep breath. His head was spinning from the pain by now.

He tried to recall the man's face from the dream, but it was already hazy.

If Jace's guess was right, that man was very likely the Dragonfather himself — Deathwing — or at least his son, Nefarian.

After all, there weren't many black dragons who'd dare call the Dragonblight their dwelling.

Being tormented by shadow and then having a nightmare didn't surprise Jace. But dreaming of a dragon like that… he hadn't expected.

Maybe when Deathwing had tried to seize control of the situation last night, the terrifying shadow energy he'd released had affected Jace, ultimately producing this dream. And the fact that Grede had dreamed of a black dragon too only confirmed it.

But Jace had never seen Deathwing or his sons, let alone their mortal forms.

Maybe it was the memories carried in the blood of those black dragons that had given him some impression of those ancient wyrms.

"Speaking of black dragons, they didn't break into the city last night," Grede exhaled, sitting on the bed. "The mages here fought like mad — even killed a young dragon. The dragons fled around midnight, probably after failing several times to break through the mages' defenses. The situation inside the city was actually worse, but after the dragons pulled back, the mages restored order quickly once they returned. Though it was already pretty grim."

Grede glanced at Jace's pocket watch and went on, "I already went by the healers' this morning. Packed with people. The ones who got hurt the most last night weren't the mages — it was the ordinary townsfolk. The death knights killed a lot of people."

Jace thought of the ghouls he'd run into last night, the destroyed buildings, the cowering civilians. What Grede was describing wasn't hard to picture.

"Can you walk?" the dwarf asked.

Jace nodded. He picked up Vereesa's blade and tucked the ruined shirt he'd been through hell with under his arm.

The style of this blade was way too different from his and Grede's gear. Carrying it out in the open was just asking for trouble. The last time he'd gotten hauled into Stormwind's jail still gave him flashbacks.

Jace glanced at the staff again. "Leave this here for now. Don't take it out again before we leave Dalaran."

"So you do know how to be afraid. The past two days I've seen you walking around with this thing slung over your back and thought you didn't care at all," Grede said as he tidied up.

Before this, aside from orcs, nobody had really been able to tell what this thing was — even Stormwind's staff experts hadn't recognized it.

But now… the situation was a little different.

Jace was worried the gem from Gorefiend on the staff would bring extra trouble. Better not to bring it into the main city at all.
