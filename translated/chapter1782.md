## Chapter 1782: First, Confirm the Test Subject

The outer districts of Dalaran weren't as refined or beautiful as the inner city, but thanks to all the wealthy wizards in the magical kingdom, they were still among the most bustling and lively of human towns.

Even with a light rain falling that evening, the streets were packed with pedestrians and horse-drawn carriages moving in an endless stream.

A large crowd of travelers and adventurers from all over the world sat outside the Merchant and Mountain Lion inn, heatedly discussing the storm that had swept through Dalaran that day and what might have caused it.

The knowledgeable ones who knew Dalaran and the Kirin Tor held forth confidently, ranging from the archmages' Blizzard magic to the mysterious owlbeasts deep in the Hinterlands that could summon whirlwinds and sandstorms.

The less educated townsfolk and young people who'd come into the city sat on the sidelines, ears pricked with interest.

Jace was one of them. He sat on the step, eating a handful of peanut-sized smoked sausage pieces from his palm. Suddenly a small hand reached out from behind his ear and snatched a few away.

He spun his head around to see the much-paler black-haired girl grinning wickedly at him.

"Did I scare you?"

"I was ready this time." Jace snorted. "How are they?"

"Got a weird sweet taste." Needleflame glanced sideways at the brilliantly lit Dalaran in the distance. "I heard what happened inside this morning... It had to be you guys. If you're still standing here, that means it worked?"

"It worked." Jace said. "In the end, Archmage Antonidas couldn't just ignore the black dragon threat. Even if it was just a pitiful little whelp."

"Hmph. That's no ordinary whelp." Needleflame jabbed her thumb toward the brightly lit inn entrance behind her. "Everyone's inside?"

"Waiting for you." Jace said.

The inn was packed to the brim. With the inner city closed to outsiders, tons of people waiting to get in had taken up residence in the outer districts. On top of that, it was raining, so pretty much everyone had crammed into the inns, taverns, and merchant houses.

The main hall was full of humans, dwarves, gnomes, and even elves. The strange multi-racial table in the corner on the second floor didn't look particularly special by comparison.

"Over here!" Sora raised her hand from behind the railing on the upper floor. Needleflame excitedly wove through the narrow gaps between tables, bounded up the stairs, and threw herself into Sora's arms. Grede and Vereesa, who'd been quietly worrying about the little black dragon who hadn't come back yet, finally relaxed and crowded around to pat her head and shoulders.

Khadgar, who'd already shaved off his beard, smiled from across the table. "Thank you very much, Miss Needleflame."

He leaned in, scanning the other tables where customers were buzzing with their own discussions. Satisfied that no one nearby could hear, he lowered his voice. "Without you, I don't know who else could have drilled a hole through that castle's tight defenses."

"Don't thank me yet, mage." Needleflame straightened her chair and sat up. "I have to announce something. I have a proper name now, and you need to remember it."

"A proper name. Who gave it to you?" Khadgar took a small sip of Dalaran dark wine from his cup, glanced at Jace, and asked: "Him?"

"No, no, no." Jace shook his head quickly.

"Why would it be him?" Needleflame frowned. "What kind of normal name could he come up with? Of course it's my new mother!"

"Princess Onyxia." Khadgar said quietly. "So that's it. That's why you said she was your mother before."

"Go on then. What's your full name?"

"Phaedegonia." The little black dragon announced proudly.

"Hmm." Khadgar's brow furrowed slightly. "Not a bad name, Princess Phaedegonia."

"You know what it means." Jace said.

"Did your 'new mother' not tell you?" Khadgar asked.

Needleflame shook her head. Khadgar looked slightly embarrassed and said quietly: "My knowledge of ancient draconic isn't that deep, especially in front of Lady Sora. But..."

"I don't really know either..." Sora said quietly.

"Alright." Khadgar said. "I remember reading something in the draconic texts back in that tower. When the black dragons' father — Deathwing, or Neltharion — named his daughters, he used the names of the rarest, most precious, most beautiful gems in the black dragon's palace. For example, blazing fire-opal wrapped in flames..."

"Onyx." Sora said.

"Yes." Khadgar said. "And the deep, night-like purple orb, Princess Nyxondria."

He put his cup down, rested his hand on the table, thought for a moment, and said: "Phaedegonia. If I'd heard this ancient draconic word on its own, I might not have placed it. But with these clues... I'd guess it refers to a type of radiant golden amber that grows like blades in the black dragon palace. Incredibly pure and beautiful. Some ancient trolls used to call it 'Phaedegonix,' imitating the black dragons' speech."

"She knows you're a young black dragon untouched by corruption, Phaedegonia." Sora said softly. "She must have chosen this name to tell you... who you really are, and where you should go."

"I think so too, young lady." Khadgar said.

Needleflame nodded. "Thank you for telling me this, mage."

"No need to thank me. I should be thanking you for giving me a chance to show off my obscure knowledge." Khadgar said. "When I was young and reading all those odd books in the corners, I never imagined they'd actually come in handy someday."

Everyone at the table laughed. They chatted about what had happened in Dustwallow Marsh over the past few days — from the Grimtotems to Onyxia's lair, and the great dragon's barely concealed injuries.

When they got to Onyxia's feelings toward Needleflame, Khadgar sighed. "No wonder her brother broke with her. Like we expected — after Deathwing's death, Onyxia's maternal instincts and her will to protect the whelp won out, suppressing the madness she inherited from her father. Nefarian went the opposite way. That's why she's still willing to cooperate with us."

Grede hoisted the wine jug onto the table. "Until we actually root out a demon from the Kirin Tor's six, we can't prove she's genuinely cooperating with us."

"What about the three today?" Vereesa's gaze circled the table.

Khadgar shook his head. Grede shook his head too. Jace said: "Hard to say. Those impostors wouldn't expose themselves over a little chaos. We need to figure out how to smoke them out. First priority is building that thing — that's the one thing we can be sure of."

"We could..." Jace paused, then lightly slapped his forehead. "Damn. I forgot. We were thinking of going to the Violet Hold to find that dreadlord, Kazanathir, and test the Kaldorei artifact on him, but..."

"But I'm not available anymore." Khadgar said. "Right?"

"I'm not blaming you." Jace said.

"Should we go directly to..." Vereesa lowered her voice further. "Archmage Vargos?"

Khadgar looked at Sora. "Didn't Onyxia give you any other clues? About the demon's hosts."

"The other ones." Sora shook her head. "She wasn't sure either. Only the one in the Kirin Tor she was most certain about, because she'd already broken with the demons cooperating with Nefarian and left Blackrock Mountain by then."

"By the way." Jace looked at the mage. "Who was it that recommended the bear-man potion to cure the king's strange illness he caught in the Wetlands?"

"High Priest Fairbanks of the Grand Abbey." Khadgar said.

"Let's start with him." Jace said.
