## Chapter 1784: An Observer's Curiosity

"What do you mean?" Jace asked.

"When the ritual starts and the void channel opens with the blood of these maddened beasts, we'll show the summoned observer lord a glimpse of the rare knowledge or bizarre spell we possess — enough to spark its curiosity and make it step into our world so we can catch it." Khadgar explained. "Then I'll cast that spell. Let it catch a glimpse through the ritual..."

"Wait." Jace said. "Who are you planning to cast it on?"

"There are only two intelligent beings down here, Jace." Khadgar said. "Or were you planning to learn this spell on the spot and cast it on me? Not to toot my own horn, but even I took about two months to master it. You won't beat that."

"No, no." Jace said. "You're saying you want to make me spill all my secrets."

"Just one secret." Khadgar held up a finger. "This spell is still just arcane magic. Efficient but targeted. It can't dig up everything from the depths of someone's mind, especially not someone like you."

Jace thought about it. The Chain of Will was still around his neck, so he wasn't too worried about external mental manipulation. It was just a demonstration, after all. He nodded.

"Do you have any magic items that resist mental control?" Khadgar asked, almost as if he could read minds. "Like the Flow Ice Crystal, which can consume mana to resist mental interference. I know the Kirin Tor gave you that."

"Vereesa has it." Jace said.

"Anything else? You're covered in priceless magical items. Don't miss any." Khadgar said. "The point is, if this spell doesn't affect the target's mind at all — if it gets blocked by something like the Flow Ice Crystal — it won't pique the observer's curiosity. To them, it'd just be a limited little trick."

"Damn." Jace cursed under his breath. "Can't you use a different spell?"

"What dark secrets do you have that you can't tell me?" Khadgar asked in a joking tone.

"Way too many." Jace said.

"Then randomly picking one won't make a difference. If you've got a hundred lice, you stop feeling itchy." Khadgar said. "The real problem is I only spent a few years in Karazhan. I didn't have that many opportunities to learn bizarre spells. This is the most suitable one based on what you mentioned earlier. If you don't want to be the target, I don't have another option."

Jace sighed helplessly and paced a couple of steps around the half-dried beast blood circle.

The secret he couldn't afford to expose — the one he least wanted to bring up with this mage — was his transmigration experience.

But was a time-space anomaly, from past to future, really some incomprehensible deadly secret to Khadgar — a man who'd lived in Karazhan, who knew Medivh the Star Mage personally?

More importantly, Khadgar might have already met a past or future Medivh.

If this ended up exposing his time-travel, Jace figured he could smooth it over even if the spell did force it out. Bronze dragonflight, Emerald Dream, strange visions...

And Khadgar was in the same boat as him now. If the guy tried to use this against him, Jace had plenty of leverage to get him thrown into the Violet Hold. Who was afraid of who?

Only one month left. Was catching the dreadlord more important, or keeping his lies straight?

Worst case scenario — maybe the spell wouldn't work on him at all. Archmage Khadgar's arcane power versus Warlock Jace Sesso's mental fortitude.

Whether he was getting cocky or not... Jace felt he wasn't without a fighting chance.

Jace took off his cloak, robe, and the Chain of Will, setting them aside. He propped Usathers in the corner, straightened his undershirt, and said: "Fine then. Let's do it."

"Excellent." Khadgar said quietly.

"Have you ever tested this spell on anyone before?" Jace asked.

"Of course I have. Why else would I think it works?" Khadgar said.

"Out of all the deep, dark secrets in Karazhan, why did you choose to spend two months learning this particular mental manipulation technique?" Jace asked.

"Spells that can destroy a castle are everywhere in Karazhan. A spell that uses arcane power to manipulate minds is one of a kind." Khadgar said.

"Fair enough." Jace said. "Alright, I'll start the summoning ritual. You get ready."

"You'd better get ready yourself." Khadgar said.

Jace glared at him, annoyed, nodded, and unrolled the scroll, beginning to chant.

The observer summoning incantation was long and tedious. It was the farthest-reaching incantation into the Twisting Nether of all summoning spells, and the target was the hardest to "please."

Satisfying an observer's curiosity was no easy feat — even for the weakest ones. No wonder Illidan couldn't be bothered to chant these spells. He was the one who got pleased and satisfied, never the other way around. Well, except for Tyrande Whisperwind.

As the incantation progressed, the beast blood circle began to emit a deep violet light. Then the red hue gradually faded, the glow growing bluer and darker, as if the circle's power was reaching toward the deepest, darkest edges of the Twisting Nether — or even beyond.

Khadgar's expression grew increasingly serious. He looked up at the ceiling, apparently worried that the ritual's power might shake the whole town of Aiden's Blade and attract those two Dalaran mages.

"If your mana reaches its limit... don't force yourself to keep channeling, Jace."

Jace didn't answer. For someone holding Usathers, even though this unfamiliar ritual was already draining heavily, he was still far from hitting the bottom of his "mana pool."

Seeing his calm demeanor, Khadgar wisely shut up and quietly continued watching.

When Jace chanted the final lines, the magic circle suddenly bloomed with a bright but sinister violet light. It resembled both arcane and shadow, yet wasn't quite like either.

"Right now. An observer has answered our call. Its eye is watching us... waiting for the rare knowledge we promised in the incantation."

"Jace Sesso!" Khadgar spoke the target's name while raising his right arm, aiming at Jace and rattling off an incantation in a language Jace didn't recognize at all. Then Jace felt the entire basement suddenly spin as a torrent of arcane starlight gathered together, rushing into his eyes with an ethereal hum!

The force nearly knocked him back two steps. The shadow energy inside him was alternately pushed open and dragged by the light of these "stars." He'd never felt his emotions struck so heavily and strangely.

But that was only because he hadn't been mentally prepared. Jace quickly adjusted his mind and mana. His shadow energy suddenly tightened, and the arcane starlight that had drilled into his consciousness was rapidly dismantled and scattered by the more chaotic, unruly shadow...

Still, countless arcane energies penetrated into the deepest parts of his mind. Jace couldn't imagine how arcane power managed to do this, but Khadgar's guidance had clearly made it possible.

The archmage's eyes blazed with blinding white light, as if he were channeling some terrifying destructive curse. And in truth, it was terrifying — Jace could clearly feel those arcane star fragments that had escaped through the gaps in his shadow power, now launching a final assault on "Transmigration" — the thing he least wanted to reveal.

But there was no way Jace would let it out. The spell was indeed strange and sharp, but it fell just slightly short of his own power.

His deep, heavy shadow energy was regrouping, firmly pulling back the arcane light heading toward "Transmigration." The brilliant energy specks were failing. They couldn't reach "Transmigration"!

Not so impressive after all... Khadgar, I'm saying Khadgar, you're still just Aegwynn's son's apprentice, not the original inventor. This little trick is interesting but it's all thunder and no lightning. Even without the Chain of Will, you—

"...Booty Bay!"

Jace coughed, spitting out the word along with bursts of light.

"What?" Khadgar asked.

"Troll!"

Jace coughed out another word. He suddenly clapped his hand over his mouth. The last traces of arcane energy sprayed from his lips and dissipated into the air. The basement flickered once from the light, then fell silent.

"Troll? What? Booty Bay... Zul'Gurub?" Khadgar asked. "What kind of dark secret is that?"

"This, uh." Jace cursed under his breath. "Could your spell be broken?"

"Absolutely not." Khadgar's eyes went wide. "But you did resist a significant portion of it. I've never seen anyone do that..."

"Indeed, astounding to the void! Such exquisite and bizarre arcane forms, such deep yet chaotic shadow storms! Composing such a brief yet magnificent... melody."

A whisper in Eredar came from the magic circle. The voice was hoarse and piercing. Soon, amid drifting runic light and shadow energy, a shadow the size of an ogre lord's belly materialized within it.

The magical light faded. A gray floating creature that looked like a stubby-tentacled squid opened its fang-filled, spike-rimmed mouth. "Greetings, mortals of Azeroth. It has been so long, so long, so long... since I last received word from this beautiful place."

Jace and Khadgar exchanged a glance, then both looked at the massive, bright purple eye at its center that had just opened. "Hello."
