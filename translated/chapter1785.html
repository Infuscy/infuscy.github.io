Chapter 1785: A Local's Understanding

"I am 'Far-Seer' Shinoreb. Two young and highly talented believers. It is a pleasure to see that there exist, amidst the endless void, mortals of such extraordinary power who have not yet pledged themselves to the Burning Legion. It is time to follow me in exploring the more distant secrets of that boundless darkness..."

The observer's central huge eye and all its surrounding small eyes blinked in unison, its entire body swaying with the cadence of its speech.

"What do you think?" Khadgar asked.

"It's not that big, but it has very powerful shadow energy, especially that eye," Jace said. "Compared to the observer I saw in Booty Bay, this one's like something from another plane."

"And compared to the one in Auchindoun?" Khadgar asked.

"Hard to say. Xillak already devoured quite a lot of souls in Auchindoun," Jace said. "And what we need isn't combat power — it's the quality of the magic eye. Just based on its perception... it's not inferior at all. I can feel it from the void energy within it."

"Of course," Khadgar said. "After all the effort we put into summoning this, we naturally got an observer lord with strong enough energy. Too bad he's not a member of the Burning Legion."

"Isn't that exactly our goal? If we killed a Burning Legion member, we'd risk detection by Kil'jaeden," Jace said.

"Fair point." Khadgar nodded. "Then this one it is."

The observer's huge eye swiveled back and forth, tracking the two as they traded words, then suddenly shouted in Demonic: "What are you talking about? Don't think I can't understand you. The communication methods you simple races design don't require high learning costs. Even for an eredar."

The strange demon's brow ridges began emitting a faint purple glow. But almost the next second, its rolling giant eye suddenly locked onto the seemingly ordinary wrapped curved staff leaning by the basement entrance.

The observer let out a sharp, painful shriek that nearly pierced the eardrums of the two men before it, simultaneously yelling a string of words Jace couldn't understand at all. Jace was just about to chant a Suffocation spell to lock the thing in place when, to his surprise, the observer abruptly vanished in a flash of light.

"Phase shift!" Jace shouted. "It's trying to escape by switching planes!"

"I know." Khadgar calmly murmured some spell, his form growing increasingly ethereal. Then he raised his arm, and right as his figure was about to disappear completely, he silently shouted an incantation.

Jace felt a massive expanse of frosty afterimages spread outward beneath his feet. Ice and frost swept across the floor and walls like some kind of Draenei technology projecting a holographic image, instantly engulfing the entire basement. Yet at the same time, it had no effect on Jace or anything else in the room — as if they were overlapping with something. The frost grew fainter, and fainter...

Just as Jace felt Khadgar, the frost, and the afterimage of the observer were about to vanish completely, the demon's scream suddenly burst out again from the opposite corridor.

The monster stumbled and crashed into the hall, covered in snowflakes, each shake sending up a puff of snow. Khadgar reappeared from a flash of light, raised his hand toward the observer — which was struggling to float back up and out of the basement — and released a freezing beam like a snow-white ray.

The moment the ray touched it, the observer's scream cut off abruptly. Then it fell like a massive slab of frozen meat, crashing onto the ground. Several frozen-brittle tentacles beneath it snapped under the weight, shattering and skittering across the floor.

Jace gave a thumbs up. Khadgar wore an expression of "No big deal" as he pointed at the enchanted cleaver on the side table.

"Be careful not to damage the eye, and don't damage the fragile brain beneath the demon's hide. Unlike other demons that rely on their demonic heart, or elementals that rely on their bindings, this thing anchors its physical body to reality entirely through the magic power in its brain. If it's destroyed, it dies here, and the eye loses its function. We have to remove the eye while it's still alive."

The observer, now frozen stiff by the frost magic, seemed to understand. The little eyes around its big one all twitched slightly.

Khadgar continued: "More importantly, if its soul returns to the Twisting Nether and the Burning Legion catches it, our plan will be exposed."

"I won't let its soul return to the Twisting Nether," Jace said.

At this, the few parts of the "Far-Seer" that could still move began to tremble.

Thump thump thump.

A faint knocking came from upstairs. The chaotic mix of shouts, screams, and the sounds of ice magic had made both of them completely ignore everything happening outside the room.

Khadgar started to go up, but Jace stopped him. "What are you doing?"

"Your face is more famous than mine. Don't risk being recognized. Stay here and watch — if the demon tries to escape using phase shift again, you have a way to handle it. I don't."

"Then you go talk to them," Khadgar said.

Jace jogged up the stairs, through the front room, and opened the door. The wind rushed in immediately.

Under the dry, cold sunlight, the people standing outside weren't mages from Dalaran. They were a group of five or six men in tattered robes and helmets, carrying rusted longswords and leather-bound round shields.

"Good morning, gentlemen. Can I help you?"

"You're not from the town?" asked the leader, a man wearing a nasal helmet and a bearskin coat.

"No," Jace said. "Is there a problem?"

"We're the constables here, outsider." The man pointed his sword at the neighboring house. "Your neighbor says you're making too much noise. It's disturbing his sleep."

Jace glanced at the house he pointed to. There wasn't a trace of life inside — it was clearly an abandoned old building. In fact, you could tell just from looking at it — holes everywhere, obviously uninhabited for years.

"I think my dear neighbor isn't home right now, sir," Jace said.

The man looked at the broken wall of the house next door, then casually extended a hand. "Regardless, the racket you just made disturbed the town's peace. You should make compensation."

"My apologies." Jace looked at the red, calloused palm, then raised his eyes to meet the man's. "My hunting dog was just being disobedient. I was trying to teach him a lesson. He'll settle down soon enough."

"See?" he said in a low voice. "He's already nice and quiet now."

"Enough talk!" A tall soldier behind him adjusted his cotton cap and shouted. "Pay up, or get out of Aidensblade! The mountains will welcome you!"

The armed "constables" all tightened their grips on their weapons, raised their shields, bared their teeth, or burst out laughing. But the leader standing in front of Jace had gone completely still and silent.

One of the burlier attendants noticed something was wrong. He tilted his head to observe their leader, who had been quiet for a good while, then looked down and noticed the man's cotton trousers were already soaked through.

He called out to the leader in the Alterac dialect. The leader shuddered violently, then nodded like a chicken pecking grain and mumbled, "I understand... this isn't right... what I did was wrong..."

"This gentleman from down the mountain and his hunting dog... um... this gentleman, good neighbor." The leader turned to his bewildered followers. "Let's go back. We've wasted enough time."

Then he muttered a few more words in dialect and stumbled away into the distance.

The other soldiers had no idea what had just happened, but they could only scramble after him, tramping through the snow as they hurried away.

Jace watched their figures disappear around the corner, then closed the door and went back down to the basement. As he descended the stairs, the observer was still frozen stiff. Khadgar was staring at him.

"I smell a hint of shadow magic, Sesso. I hope that when we leave this place, I won't step on a pile of twisted, blackened corpses the moment I walk out the door."

"Don't worry," Jace said. "Just be careful not to step in the piss."

"Fine." Khadgar coughed, waved his hand, and said, "Let's continue."
