Chapter 1787: Next Stop, the Monastery

The Briar fellow had returned to his beloved Brill.

Before an old signpost, Khadgar gazed into the distance toward the direction indicated by the "Brill" marker. Beyond a vast field, past a massive windmill at the far end, the huddled blue and red rooftops came into view.

"Don't be so poetic," Jace said. "Look at me — back home and looking like a mess. Is there any poetry in that?"

"A misunderstood hero returning to his homeland, forced to keep his identity a secret, looking for a chance to clear his name..." Khadgar said as he walked. "What story could be more poetic than that?"

"How about a genius archmage who's driven the demon lord trying to destroy the world back into the Twisting Nether and saved the world multiple times, ending up in prison?" Jace looked at him.

"You mean yourself? You ended up in prison?" Khadgar turned to ask.

"I'm no archmage," Jace said. "You know exactly who I'm talking about."

"Not me, anyway." The archmage walked as he spoke. "I'm no archmage either. Master Drenden even took away my Archmage's Ring. What kind of archmage is that?"

"But you did end up in prison."

"It was a deluxe Violet suite, not a prison."

"You weren't saying that a few days ago."

"I'm saying it now."

The two bickered as they walked. Brill in summer was as warm and peaceful as Jace remembered from his childhood. It wasn't as hot here as in Goldshire. The warm breeze blowing toward them seemed to carry a hint of cool air from the Alterac Mountains and the northern sea, easing some of the tension that had built up from summoning the powerful observer in Aidensblade.

Jace looked toward the west side of the river, to the red-roofed house on the opposite bank. That was his mother's family farm.

He wondered if Henni's father and brothers still lived there, what his mother was thinking right now, if his father was doing okay, and if they knew about demons lurking in the shadows, waiting for a chance to harm him.

As long as Morophil was around, he thought. In Blackrock Spire, those Nathrezim didn't dare confront Morophil directly — they could only work around her, using diversion tactics. That meant they knew exactly where they stood against this banshee huntress.

As long as Morophil was around, his parents could live relatively normal lives while he was out hunting dreadlords.

When he'd left Lordaeron for Stormwind, he never imagined he'd one day entrust Victor and Henni's lives to a demon that made countless Burning Legion soldiers in the Twisting Nether tremble in fear. But he'd actually done it, and he even felt at ease about it.

Maybe the Kirin Tor's investigations and suspicions of him weren't entirely baseless.

The "Journey's End" Inn was on the right side of the street. The familiar smell of fried cheese pies wafted over again. Everything was exactly as it had been in his childhood.

Now, instead of the "End of Fear" Inn from the game, he was more familiar with this "Journey's End" here.

He silently prayed that this inn, this whole town, and the surrounding fields and forests wouldn't end up like they did in that other timeline. Let things stay just as they are.

He walked through the sparsely occupied common room, up the back stairs to the second floor. Vereesa was leaning against the railing, watching them.

"Is everyone here?" Jace asked.

"Of course. We flew, after all." Vereesa led the two to the room at the end of the hallway. Sora, Grede, and Needleflame were already waiting there.

"Couldn't you have used a teleport?" Grede said, eyeing Khadgar. "Last night we were about to head to Aidensblade to see if Dalaran had dragged you off."

"Then you should've checked the Violet Hold first, dwarf," Khadgar said.

"Wouldn't have been any point," Grede coughed twice.

"Is the Kaldorei demon hunter's artifact finished? Let's see it," Needleflame said.

"Of course. You all completed your tasks perfectly, and we completed ours."

Khadgar placed the pale purple magic orb on the table, wrapped in rune cloth. The ranger frowned. "Looking at it reminds me of that nightmare from the Red Dragon."

"Didn't you say you were going to summon an observer lord?" Grede said. "This eye looks smaller than that little observer's eye from Booty Bay."

"It was bigger than your head. This is what it looks like after crystallization," Khadgar said. "Otherwise, what would be the point of crystallizing it? Carrying a magic orb bigger than your head into the royal city or Dalaran — I might as well have just enslaved the observer and let it phase-shift on its own. That'd have a higher success rate."

"Speaking of which, that thing's phase shifting was impressively fast. Faster than most of the 'geniuses' at the academy. No wonder these creatures have taken over the Twisting Nether..."

He looked at Jace. "If we weren't drowning in problems right now, I'd have let you keep it and bring it into the Violet Hold to study. See if there's anything worth learning."

"Did you take care of the demon's body?" Vereesa asked. "I have some people stationed in Strahnbrad. They go up to Aidensblade occasionally to check in with the mages there. I'm worried they'll smell the dead demon."

"Better if they do," Khadgar said. "Might even help draw some attention away. We only need a few more weeks to finish what's next. If it works, none of it matters. If it fails, none of it matters either... Don't worry. Your lover's skills at cleaning up demon corpses are beyond question."

"A few felhounds, Vereesa," Jace said quietly.

"Fine. I understand." Vereesa nodded.

"By the way, Khadgar. We heard something while stopping in Strahnbrad," Grede said. "That Archbishop Farbanks you've been suspicious of — he's already left the royal city and gone back to the monastery. The one in the northeast. I haven't been there, but you probably have?"

"Why?" Khadgar asked.

"How should I know?" Grede said. "We heard it from some priests there."

"When?" Jace asked.

"About a few weeks ago. We overheard people talking outside a small chapel." Vereesa looked at the mage and the warlock. "You two sense something off?"

"If the Nathrezim want to influence the Kirin Tor, they bring a demon to the Council of Six," Jace said. "Logically, if they wanted to influence King Terenas, using an archbishop would make sense too. But if that's the case, why would he leave the royal city where he could keep an eye on the king, and go back to the monastery instead?"

"Is it related to our actions?" Grede asked. "Did news of our return leak?"

"Don't forget, Grede." Vereesa looked at the dwarf. "That archbishop had already returned to the monastery before we came back from Kalimdor."

"Ah, right." The dwarf nodded. "So what's going on?"

"Maybe Archbishop Farbanks really did just happen to discover that furbolg medicine could treat the king's illness..." Sora said quietly.

"I don't believe in coincidences like that," Jace said. "The time between when we finished our battle in Blackrock Spire and when we left for Kalimdor was so short, and yet a Church archbishop was already rushing to use the new medicine we used in Blackrock Spire to treat the king's strange illness he caught in the Wetlands. These people had clearly planned this."

"Maybe Archbishop Farbanks — or rather, the demon behind him — wasn't after controlling the court after all, Jace," Khadgar said. "There's a Nathrezim in the Kirin Tor. So wouldn't it be natural to plant another demon in the Church as well? Especially since the Church — and especially Archbishop Alonsus Faol — is more inclined to trust you than Archmage Antonidas. Maybe the Burning Legion worked even harder to corrupt the Church than to influence the Kirin Tor. It's just that the nature of the Church... the clergy are harder to break through."

The Church. The Kirin Tor. The Kingdom of Lordaeron. As Jace listened to Khadgar, he thought: this time Kil'jaeden was really trying to flip the entire world over and bury him alive.

He couldn't help looking out the window. The clear sky and white clouds were calmer and more beautiful than the shattered sky beyond Alleria's Keep, wrapped in strange magic. But somehow, they felt more suffocating.

"We'll know everything once we get to the monastery," Vereesa said, staring at the strange orb on the table. "I hope Illidan Stormrage is as good at hunting demons as the legends say. I hope his idea works."

"I hope so too," Khadgar said.

"Then let's not waste any time," Grede said. "Let's head out for the great monastery as soon as possible. I say we leave this afternoon. Otherwise, if the life energy in this mage's decoy runs out and I get exposed, Highlord Kurdan will be in trouble too!"

"But even I can feel the dark energy in this orb," Sora said. "Are you sure you want to bring it into that great monastery? Some of the holy scriptures and Light sigils there might react strongly."

"If a dreadlord can stride right into that monastery, why can't this orb?" Khadgar confidently wrapped the orb back up and looked at the group around the table. "Don't underestimate me, Lady Green Dragon. Since I've joined this team, I've got to have some tricks on par with the rest of you."
