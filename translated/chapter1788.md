## Chapter 1788: An Age of Doubt and Anxiety

What the locals called the Great Monastery — the Tirisfal Monastery of the Holy Light — was a very ancient temple, built even before the founding of the royal city. So old that Jace had never heard anyone mention how long it had stood on the northeastern peninsula of this region.

Tirisfal was an important source of human faith. The name itself came from the fallen site of Tir, the Guardian whom humans had first worshipped. This monastery was very likely one of the first Holy Light churches in all the human kingdoms, and without question the largest.

It sat at the northernmost tip of the entire Lordaeron continent. On a map, it looked like a lighthouse on a mountaintop, overlooking the whole human world to the south. Most human cartographers liked to draw it especially large and detailed on their maps, to show proper respect.

By comparison, Quel'Thalas to the east was rendered in the roughest of strokes — mainly because no human mapmaker had ever set foot in elven territory, and few had even managed to get their hands on an elf-made map of Quel'Thalas.

Even though, after the Orc Wars, Stormwind's Cathedral of Light had to some extent replaced the Great Monastery's position in the Church — and even the Menethil royal family had moved the young prince's coming-of-age ceremony south to show respect for Archbishop Alonsus Faol and the will of the Church — that was only a matter of the past decade or so. As the lighthouse of the "Old World," the Monastery of the Holy Light still held an irreplaceable position across all of Lordaeron.

Never mind the Lordaeronians on this continent, the Alterac highlanders, the Arathi, or the Gilneans — even the Kul Tirans, isolated across the sea, would willingly cross the ocean and then climb mountains to make a pilgrimage to that high peak.

For Jace, this place had another layer of extraordinary significance. In the other timeline, it had another name — the Scarlet Monastery.

He'd finally made it here, he thought.

When he was a child, the journey was too far. Even though his mother had occasionally made pilgrimages here before she was pregnant with him, she'd basically given it up after he was born. Later, when he was old enough to travel here, the Orc Wars broke out. Rumor had it that orcs had snuck into the area around Lake Darrowmere and the Alterac Mountains, which made the citizens of the royal city even less willing to head toward the Great Monastery.

And once the Orc Wars ended, Jace set off for Stormwind. Given the magic he was studying, the rather controversial reputation he'd accumulated along the way, and the attitude of Lordaeron's upper and lower classes toward him, Jace had thought he'd never visit this place in his lifetime. But it turned out fate — or something else — was dragging him toward that peninsula after all.

Traveling from Brill to the Great Monastery was a difficult, lengthy journey for pilgrims. Even though they hadn't yet left the Tirisfal region, the Kingdom of Lordaeron was vast — the distance was comparable to going from Goldshire to Moonbrook. And they had to cross Venomspider Gorge along the way. Yes, that's the actual name.

Many people thought Venomspider Gorge got its name only after Lordaeron fell, when the Scourge and the Forsaken corrupted the forest into a land of the dead, and terrifying venom spiders took over the gorge. But in reality, the gorge had been called that for a very, very long time.

After the Forsaken and Scourge remnants took over Lordaeron, the venom spiders in the gorge just became more brazen. This was also the main reason Henni and Victor had never considered taking young Jace on a pilgrimage to the Great Monastery.

Of course, for Jace Sesso and his motley crew, the venom spiders crawling through the gorge were hardly a problem. Sora and Needleflame used Vereesa's earrings to conceal themselves and flew straight over the gorge toward the high mountains. When they passed a clear lake shaped like a silver ring, the distant silhouette of Tirisfal's northern peaks came into view.

The group stopped to rest in a small town at the foot of the mountain. The town was called Barimand. His mother had mentioned that in the ancient Tirisfal tongue, it meant "a step away" — meaning the Great Monastery was just a few steps away. For pilgrims, the goal was right ahead.

The town was very small. Jace didn't remember it existing in the game. It was basically one road, lined with a few relay stations, houses, and two or three shops that earned a few copper coins from passing travelers. Most of the people gathered here were dressed as priests or pilgrims. Probably, after the Scourge invasion, this tiny place that relied on the monastery and pilgrims was quickly razed to the ground, vanishing from the memories of both the living and the dead.

Jace and his group planned to stop here, get a feel for the situation, gather information, and prepare the kinds of things pilgrims usually carried — so they could blend in with the pilgrim crowds when visiting the Great Monastery.

At first they'd worried there might be no elves here, and Vereesa's presence might stand out too much. But as it turned out, even in a small town like Barimand, there were two elven priests from Quel'Thalas chatting in front of a shop.

That set them a bit more at ease. The communication between Lordaeron's Church and Quel'Thalas's Church was more frequent than they'd imagined.

Grede watched the two priests turn and head toward a small inn across the street, then said, "So what do we need to prepare?"

"Holy candles," Khadgar murmured. "Nothing too expensive. Buying honey candles to donate would draw too much attention from the nobility — and attention is the last thing we need right now."

He glanced at the warlock beside him. "Your mother used to go to church regularly, didn't she? I thought you'd know all about this."

"I never paid attention to what she did there. I only cared about whether they were serving gingerbread or sacred bread that day," Jace said.

"Perfect." Khadgar spread his hands. "We're headed to the biggest monastery on this continent, and the most devout follower of the Holy Light in our group is a fugitive mage from the Kirin Tor. Couldn't be better."

Jace shot him a look and walked into the shop. It looked less like a store and more like a small chapel, or the information desk set up for pilgrims next to a cathedral. Various candles and sigils were neatly arranged around the room, a thick incense smell filling the space. The elderly shopkeeper sitting in the corner could have passed for a priest.

"If you're looking for medicine or herbs, go straight to the monastery, sir," the shopkeeper said, licking his finger and turning a page in his book without looking up.

"We'd like to buy some candles, sir," Needleflame said.

"Hm?" The shopkeeper looked up. "You didn't buy any in Brill, miss? The candles here are rather expensive."

"We encountered venom spiders on the way," Jace said. "Lost a few."

"Light protect you." The old shopkeeper pushed himself up and shuffled to the back. "What kind would you like?"

"The kind that sells the most," Jace said.

"This box is one silver, fifteen copper."

"Thank you, sir." Jace counted out the copper coins as he spoke. "Speaking of which — is that medicine really that effective? I heard the king's illness was cured by it."

"I've heard about it too," the shopkeeper said. "Before that medicine recipe came up from the south, almost no travelers dared head toward the monastery. It's only started to recover recently. But I suppose that's when faith becomes truly precious — in times of crisis. Though it's always only a minority who can weather such tests."

"Maybe I shouldn't say this, especially when even the archbishop is promoting this medicine... but I've heard some people say that medicine has something to do with that warlock. Who knows whether this 'miraculous recipe' that appeared out of nowhere is a blessing or a curse."

"I believe rich rewards must be earned through hardship. Things that fall into your lap from the sky are rarely good. Remember my words, young man, especially in this age of doubt and anxiety. The only way to get through all this confusion is to always keep the teachings of the Light in your heart."

"I understand. Thank you, sir. Light bless you."

"Light bless you."

Jace left the money on the counter, picked up the box with Needleflame, and left the shop. The expressions of the elf, dwarf, and mage outside weren't great — they'd clearly heard the conversation between Jace and the shopkeeper.

After they'd put some distance between themselves and the shop, Khadgar said quietly: "Don't listen to the ravings of that doddering old fool. That medicine is our most effective weapon against the demons right now — whether it's the plague spread or the frame-up against you. If the Light alone could save us, it should have sent down two beams of light at the very start, when Medivh opened the Dark Portal, to sever his hands channeling void energy."

"I agree with every word, mage," Grede said.
