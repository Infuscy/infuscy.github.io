## Chapter 1789: The Holy Light and the Mage

The walk from Bal'irman at the mountain's base to the great monastery was still a long haul. By the time they could finally see the monastery gates, the sky was already quite dark.

But even in the twilight, the row of white walls stood like battlements, towering high, with the shadows of churches rising behind them like straight swords. Amid these barren northern mountains, the sense of pressure they gave off was no less than the spire of the Cathedral of Light back in Stormwind, surrounded by its clustered houses.

The old, yellow-brown eaves, the faded gray-white bricks that had been renovated gods knew how many times, the architectural style completely unlike the royal city of Lordaeron, Stratholme, Dalaran, or Stormwind — it felt more like some old painting of Strom. All of it silently spoke of the years this ancient sanctuary of the Holy Light had endured.

"I don't get it," Grede said, panting as he leaned on his knees. "Why would humans build such a big monastery way up here? Don't none of you want to get a little closer to the Holy Light?"

"Where would you build it instead? A giant cavern two or three hundred meters underground where one misstep drops you into molten lava?" Khadgar asked.

"I ain't no Dark Iron dwarf," Grede said.

"Well, Grim Batol was built by your father's generation," Khadgar said. "I'd have thought you'd understand the psychology of 'building miraculous religious architecture in places ordinary folks can barely reach.'"

"We built Grim Batol that way to defend against Dark Iron and Bronzebeard attacks." Grede pointed at the high gate. "What did the Lordaeronians build this monastery up here for? To make their own believers half-dead from the climb so the priests inside can practice their healing spells?"

"That's a rather novel interpretation, Thunderfist." Khadgar murmured. "But as far as I know, the real answer is closer to this: a thousand years ago, or even earlier — probably before Dalaran was founded — a follower of Tyr felt the call of the Holy Light on this mountain. That might've been one of the origins of humanity's faith in the Light. After that, this place became a holy site. To commemorate it, or to follow in his footsteps and receive the Light's guidance like he did, many people came here to live, build homes, churches... and eventually it became the largest monastery in the world."

"Ah, so you actually know a genuinely plausible answer." The dwarf nodded, then after a few pilgrims resting by the roadside passed them, continued, "I thought you were just like that guy back there — spout some nonsense about every new thing you see and hope you guessed right. Though I gotta admit, he usually guesses pretty damn accurately."

"That's because I've actually spent a long time in several enormous libraries, dwarf," Khadgar said quietly.

"Don't drag me into this, you two..." Jace said.

Needleflame, who was at the front of the group, jogged back and stopped the mage and dwarf. "This castle has no guards at all. They just... let anyone in?"

"You sure?" Grede looked up and squinted.

"Just ran up to the gate and checked," Needleflame said.

"Can't blame 'em," Grede chuckled. "Anyone who'd hike all the way up here just to crawl into some boring old place, might as well just let 'em in. What do they need guards for?"

Jace looked up at the heights too. In the game, if you walked this far, you'd probably already have some tough Scarlet Crusade guards spotting you and charging in for a fight. Farther ahead were even more annoying Scarlet wizards, battle mages who were fierce in both melee and casting, and scarlet monks.

By contrast, aside from some resting pilgrims and a few priests guiding them, there wasn't a single armed guard in sight. It didn't match the monastery's stern, almost military-fortress vibe at all — felt more like walking into a cathedral square.

But considering the other timeline, Scarlet Monastery was already one of Lordaeron's last strongholds where the army and church resisted the Scourge and the Forsaken. Being armed to the teeth made sense. Here, the Orc Wars were over, the black dragon crisis was basically resolved, and there was now an effective cure for the plague. The monastery didn't have to constantly fear a Stromgarde cavalry raid or Gilnean sneak attack like Ambermill did. They really didn't need tight security.

So Grede's joke, even if it was a joke, wasn't entirely without merit.

"Isn't that a good thing? No guards checking means we can carry whatever we want in." Vereesa scanned the area, making sure no pilgrims were passing by, then lowered her voice. "Including that orb."

"You mean Khadgar's Eye?" Grede chuckled, then turned to look at Khadgar, only to find the mage staring intently at the monastery gate up ahead, his expression tight.

The dwarf immediately dropped the grin. "I was joking, mage-boy. Don't get sore..."

"I'm not angry, Thunderfist," Khadgar said. "The monastery lacking guards doesn't mean they're defenseless against threats."

"Before we came, I made sure the orb's packaging was well-protected — magical isolation, feature concealment, so those priests who are sensitive to dark magic wouldn't sense anything off. I guarantee you, if I had enough time, I could smuggle this thing into the Violet Hold and nobody'd ever know I had it on me till I got out."

"Is that blowing it a bit too far?" Grede muttered.

"But I overlooked one thing." Khadgar flat-out ignored the dwarf's doubt. "And that's that the Monastery of the Light isn't Dalaran, and those priests don't just rely on their eyes or floating magical auras to detect danger."

"You mean the Holy Light," Jace said.

"Exactly," the mage said. "No sacred texts needed, no prayers or divine magic related to the Light. If you're carrying something wrong, or if you yourself seem suspicious — some priests, some members of the Silver Hand... they just *know* you're up to something."

"Specifically, it's some kind of strange perception unique to Light-worshippers. But according to them, the Light descends to reveal the truth and guide them against 'evil.' Even when Sabellian snuck into Honor Hold, he rarely got close to Silver Hand members or spent much time around Turalyon. Of course, we only realized that after he left."

"Down at the foot of the mountain, you were saying the Holy Light was useless," Grede said. "Now why're you so worried?"

"If the Holy Light were completely useless, the church's clergy and the Kirin Tor's scholars wouldn't have been arguing for a thousand or two thousand years," Khadgar said. "It's precisely because sometimes it works and sometimes it mysteriously abandons its followers... that nobody can count on it working all the time — not even the most devout, most fanatical believers. And now, standing on the opposite side of it, we can't count on it *never* working either."

"I'm lost, mage." Grede waved a hand. "Just tell me — can we go in or not?"

"Let me think. I'll admit I don't have much experience dealing with church personnel." Khadgar took a deep breath, frowned slightly, and stared at the monastery gate.

They lingered there for a moment. Pilgrims occasionally passed by, climbing the steps upward. The sky was darkening, night about to fall — their group was looking more and more suspicious by the minute.

"Let's just go in," Jace said.

"Do you have a good solution?" Khadgar asked. "Or do you just want to gamble on luck like Prince Sabellian?"

"If the Holy Light can let a Nathrezim in, why wouldn't it let us in?" Jace asked.

"I don't know. Maybe it's a Nathrezim that worships the Light." Khadgar snorted. "But since we're here, there's no room for regrets. So let's go. Let's see how much fate you really have with the Holy Light."

I wonder, Khadgar, after this trip, will you finally believe I summoned the Light to deliver the final blow to Sargeras' scepter?

Jace thought this to himself but said nothing, just nodded and headed toward the great monastery.
