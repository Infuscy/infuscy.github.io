## Chapter 179: The One You're Thinking Of

Jace stepped out with Grede. After the chaos of the night before, a strange, acrid stench hung over every street in Dalaran. Jace caught the rotting reek of corpses in it and his stomach lurched.

He doubled over against the wall and dry-heaved for a long while, but all that came up was some sour water — no black, chunky necrotic filth.

Even though the clenching in his stomach hurt, it put his mind a little more at ease.

As they passed the mouth of an alley, Grede muttered, "Does a goblin live here or something…"

Jace followed his gaze and saw several walls already demolished, rooftops and window ledges blown in.

After staring a little longer, he realized — wasn't this the exact spot where Gorefiend had grabbed him last night?

Seeing it in daylight really drove home what he'd actually been through… and just how monstrous Gorefiend's raw strength had been.

If Jace wanted to cause this much destruction himself, he'd need to bring in an excavator.

"Last night… it was right here," he muttered.

"You mean Teron Gorefiend?" Grede asked in a low voice.

"Yeah."

"Did anyone else see? You definitely used shadow magic."

"I don't know," Jace said. "At the time I could barely guarantee I'd stay alive — you want me to scan the surroundings too? Bit much. Besides, those two elves definitely saw."

Grede said, "Would they sell you out?"

"I… don't know," Jace said. "Forget it. We'll cross that bridge when we come to it."

"Beat Teron Gorefiend and can't even tell anyone — doesn't that piss you off? I know it'd piss me off," Grede whispered.

"That wasn't really me who beat him," Jace said. "Think about it — if you and nine mercenaries went and killed a dragon, and you were the only one of the ten who got hurt, would you go around bragging about it?"

Grede shouted, "In that case, that dragon ought to have burned us all to death! Maybe then the bards would exaggerate the story a bit and leave us with a fine reputation."

Jace furrowed his brow. The dwarf was still the same as ever — but it meant the message had landed.

They walked a ways farther. Jace kept glancing down the alleys as he went, wanting to see how things looked around where Gorefiend's body had fallen, but after walking a long way he still couldn't spot any place he recognized.

Probably gone in the wrong direction. Dalaran wasn't big, but it wasn't a small village either, and he'd gotten completely turned around last night.

No telling what the Kirin Tor would dig out of Teron Gorefiend's discarded shell.

Crossing half the city, they reached the apothecary and alchemy quarter. From a long way off Jace could already see the crowds packed outside.

All manner of injured people sat or stood around outside — only those close to death got taken inside. An ominous atmosphere hung in the air, laced with the foul reek of shadow and the charred smell of fire.

Every one of those mages wore a grim expression, and the purple-robed guards on the periphery didn't look much better — not exactly the look of a city that had just won a defensive battle.

Then Jace noticed several elves standing against a wall to one side, deep in conversation. A silver-haired elf in a green robe leaned against the side of the building, eyes fixed unblinkingly on the house where the patients were being treated. That looked like Vereesa Windrunner.

"I see that Windrunner. You see her?" he said to Grede. "Good thing we don't have to go looking for her in the elven quarter."

Grede glanced at the large cluster of elven men and women and frowned. "You go. I'll wait here."

Jace nodded and crossed the street. Vereesa noticed someone approaching from far off and looked up with raised eyebrows.

It was obvious she really hadn't slept a wink. The brisk, sharp-edged look he'd seen in the tavern the night before had completely vanished.

"Miss Vereesa."

The elf asked warily, "Who are you?"

Jace blinked. Had he gotten the wrong person? Now he wasn't so sure. Was this even Vereesa?

He tried, "I'm the one from last night…"

"Ah!" Vereesa's eyes went wide.

"You… uh, I feel like your face looks a little different from last night."

Jace said, half-joking, "You don't tell humans apart by face, do you, miss?"

Vereesa said helplessly, "Well… I'll admit, I'm not very good at it. I rarely leave Quel'Thalas, and this isn't my first time making this mistake. I really am sorry."

Jace confessed, "Honestly, I recognized you by your voice and hair color. I can't tell elves apart either."

Vereesa gave an embarrassed laugh, then asked, "By the way, is your friend all right?"

"He's fine."

"That's good," Vereesa said. "Last night I wanted to thank you, but you left in too much of a hurry… Mr. Jace Sesso, honestly, by the end I'd actually lost confidence that we could beat Teron Gorefiend. I nearly thought we were going to die up there."

"You really don't need to thank me. If it weren't for you two stepping in, I'd have died at least twice over."

With that, Jace pulled out the blade he'd been carrying.

Vereesa noticed the wound on Jace's shoulder and volunteered to help him take off his shirt, taking it off his hands and returning it, then sliding the blade back into the empty sheath at her waist.

"I hope it was of some help."

"It helped a lot," Jace said, watching her with his eyes on the blade. "How is Kaelfal?"

"His injuries are severe. He's still inside being treated."

"Will he be left with any disability?"

"The human priests in there say no."

By now, Vereesa had relaxed a lot compared to when Jace had first walked over.

Resting her hand on the pommel of the blade, she said, "His injuries aren't the worst a Farstrider has ever taken."

"A lot of Farstriders were hurt?" Jace asked.

Remembering what Teron Gorefiend had said last night, he'd seemed to harbor some particular hatred toward elves.

Vereesa said, "The Farstriders were only supposed to be responsible for the embassy district's safety, but those death knights… you know they were the vanguard that invaded Quel'Thalas, and they're the ones who murdered my kin. No child raised on that land could ever let these walking dead run rampant like this."

"So being able to defeat Teron Gorefiend… means a great deal to me, Mr. Sesso. We didn't manage to kill him outright, but we taught him a lesson he won't forget. I believe that one day, he will pay the price for every crime he's committed."

Jace could more or less guess who she meant by "kin." It was her youngest brother, Lelas Windrunner.

When the orcs invaded Quel'Thalas, Lelas — who had only just begun his ranger training — was killed resisting the invaders. That event was what steeled Alleria's resolve to cross the Dark Portal.

And what Vereesa had said also partly confirmed Jace's earlier suspicion.

In other words, Gorefiend had actually been playing the role of drawing the Farstriders' attention last night. Someone else had been the one to steal the Eye of the Arcane.

Vereesa took a deep breath, then smiled. "Also, you don't need to worry too much about Professor Kaelfal. I gave him some of the Well's water — he should recover quickly, and he'll need to get back to tending the other wounded as soon as possible."

"Ah, the Sunwell," Jace said with a nod.

Water from the Sunwell didn't just brim with potent ancient magic — it had terrifying healing properties too. In the game, a vial of Sunwell water was an excellent healing trinket.

Of course, the Sunwell hadn't been destroyed yet at this point, and vials of Sunwell water weren't nearly as priceless as they'd later become. Some nobles in Silvermoon kept vials holding a little Sunwell water in their collections — as keepsakes, as status symbols, and most importantly, to save a life.

Hold on…

"Wait," Jace asked. "What I drank last night…"

Vereesa's long silver eyebrows twitched. "That would be the one."
