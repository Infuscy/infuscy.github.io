Chapter 1790: The Monastery

Just like in the game, entering the great monastery wasn't through the main gate but through a narrow archway.

Jace could see a wider road beside it leading to the higher walls — from the monastery's overall layout it seemed more like the actual main approach — but that road was overgrown with trees and grass, the end of it completely ruined, apparently long abandoned. For some reason, the monastery's main gate had been left to rot. Both pilgrims and priests used this side entrance to go up.

Squeezing through this passage felt less like stepping into a sacred sanctuary and more like a cramped, oppressive tomb. No matter how cleanly the steps and walls were scrubbed, a musty, decaying smell hung in the air that wouldn't disperse. It was like descending into a terrifying dungeon from underground, and the higher they climbed, the stronger the feeling got.

Still, at least compared to the Scarlet Monastery in the game, this place was refreshing and plain. Though Jace couldn't quite articulate where the difference was — maybe it was the absence of those scarlet Lordaeron banners. Just that alone made the whole place feel far more peaceful.

After going up the steps, they entered a dark hall lit only by torches and braziers. It didn't feel at all like a place where a grand monastery would welcome its faithful. More like the basement of some chapel, used for storing holy relics or the bodies of dead clergy.

"No wonder Alonsus Faol wanted to build a brand-new Cathedral of Light in Stormwind," Grede muttered under his breath as they passed an empty stone pillar. "This dump doesn't even look like a place to worship the Light. Tell me a necromancer rules here and I'd believe it!"

"Shh." Vereesa frowned. "Are you asking for trouble?"

Just then, two priests in gray-white robes walked out from behind the pillar. Seeing their group of "pilgrims," they smiled and nodded politely. Grede cleared his throat and shut up.

But neither they, nor the other priests and a paladin of the Silver Hand who'd passed by earlier, noticed the crystallized beholder eye in Khadgar's pack. Still, Jace could tell — Khadgar had never been worried about normal detection methods catching them. What concerned him most was always that inexplicable "guidance of the Light" that outsiders couldn't understand.

Still, as they followed the pilgrims forward, nothing unexpected happened. No battle mages suddenly surrounded them, no Holy Light judgment came crashing down from above. They just walked through the narrow, gloomy corridors, exchanging "Light be with you" with passing priests and travelers now and then. Nothing else happened.

"Maybe the Light really does want to bless you."

Khadgar spoke quietly to the warlock beside him as they approached an archway ahead.

"Maybe it was all just your own worry," Jace said. "No guidance of the Light, no sudden 'revelation' descending."

But the moment he stepped through the archway, something felt wrong. Even though the sky was already dark, his whole skin felt like it was being roasted by the summer sun at a harbor port.

Khadgar had already walked ahead. Jace quickly pulled up his hood — not that he expected the tattered cloak to block the "sunburn," but he didn't want the monastery's clergy to see anything wrong with him. Because he knew exactly what was happening.

The Holy Light was descending. Maybe not to guide the priests into grabbing them as malicious intruders, but it was definitely interfering with Jace, a wielder of shadow, pushing deeper into this sacred ground.

As they went deeper into the passage, his skin didn't just itch — it started to sting and burn. Even the soles of his feet felt hotter and hotter. His eyes were watering uncontrollably, like sand had gotten in them.

He watched a female priest standing by the wall guiding pilgrims, made sure she wasn't looking their way, then subtly tapped Vereesa's shoulder in front of him.

"What?" The ranger turned back, and when she saw Jace's face, she frowned. "Your eyes... what happened?"

"What's wrong?" Jace asked, pulling his hood lower.

The elf lowered her voice. "They're red. Really red. Like — "

"Like a fel beast?" Jace asked.

"No," the elf said. "Like you haven't slept in a very long time."

"Okay." Jace breathed a sigh of relief. "That's fine. Nothing else weird on my face, right?"

"No," Vereesa said. "What's going on?"

"Nothing. Let's keep moving," Jace said.

Vereesa watched him a bit longer, still uneasy, before turning to follow Sora and Needleflame.

Luckily, the pain subsided quickly and didn't last long. But even at Prince Arthas Menethil's coronation, his reaction hadn't been this strong — and back then, the Light was summoned to descend. This time, Jace hadn't even seen any glow.

He quietly pulled out the symbol of Tyr's protection and looked at it. If the Light had really sensed his intrusion just now, he hoped it would help him — find that demon hiding in this monastery — instead of targeting them.

He didn't know if his silent prayer had been heard by the Light. Of course, he'd never really expected any result. But after tucking the symbol of protection back into his chest, at least the anxiety and worry from that sudden pain had eased. Whether it was from the Light or just natural, he couldn't say.

He was getting closer and closer to shadow now. Maybe it was the Book of Unholy Names' influence, or Uzallus, or the immense power from those devoured souls, or the repeated Life Taps. Maybe all of it together.

He hoped these powers would help him destroy these demons — including Tichondrius the dreadlord leader — stop Kil'jaeden's plan, and prevent the Scourge apocalypse.

Jace and his group followed the pilgrims forward, and soon they arrived at a corridor lit by candlelight.

Finally, it wasn't like that underground tomb anymore. Old but patterned flooring, clean and tidy brick walls. Walking here, they could even hear the faint sound of water outside.

At the end of the corridor, they passed through an arch and entered a spacious garden. In front of them, behind a pool, was a spectacular artificial waterfall, gleaming under the moonlight. Just looking at it made you feel relaxed.

This was the church garden. Jace had come here countless times in his past life. Though there were archways and side paths everywhere on both sides of the garden, different from the game, the overall layout was basically the same. From the steps beside the corridors by the pool, they could reach the upper level of the garden. Once up there, they should see the main cathedral doors.

Of course, the other major difference was that there weren't 95 mobs from the entire monastery dungeon lined up on the staircase railings charging down to kill him.

Probably because it was nighttime, the cathedral was closed. The priests were leading the newly arrived pilgrims through the side corridors to the monastery's temporary lodgings, arranging for the faithful to rest and wait for tomorrow's service. After all, they couldn't just leave people out in the wilderness where gnolls and venom spiders roamed.

In the game, High Priest Fairbanks was locked in a secret cell in a corner of the cathedral, tortured. He'd witnessed the terrifying betrayal in Stratholme — the future ruler of the monastery, Renault Mograine, impaling his father with the legendary blade Ashbringer — and the entire process of Ashbringer being corrupted and defiled by the young Mograine's sin and madness.

Of course, High Priest Fairbanks was still an advisor to King Terenas now. The Silver Hand hadn't yet become extreme due to the Scourge invasion and Lordaeron's fall, had not yet splintered into the Scarlet Crusade under demonic influence. Meeting him shouldn't be that hard.

But Jace's group didn't have all night to rest. Khadgar was worried the beholder's eye might give them away at any moment, and Jace growing more uncomfortable staying in the church. They had to move tonight.

"Good evening, brother," Jace said to a priest answering pilgrims' questions. "May I ask if the esteemed High Priest Fairbanks is here?"

"His Excellency has only just returned to the library area, sir," the priest said. "May I ask who you are?"

"We're from the town of Vandermar, brother." Jace said. "There have been rumors of orc warlocks haunting the town. The rangers and the priests and mages in Andorhal couldn't help, so we came to the monastery to seek High Priest Fairbanks' aid. We were worried it might be related to the plague."

"I see. It's a long journey from Vandermar to here, you must be exhausted," the priest said. "I'll go inform High Priest Fairbanks immediately."

"Thank you very much, brother." Jace nodded. When the priest left through an archway, he turned back and exchanged glances with Vereesa and the others.

"Follow him," Khadgar said.
