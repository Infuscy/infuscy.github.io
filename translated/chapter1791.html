Chapter 1791: The Church Library

The interior of this ancient sanctuary of the Light was a maze. The routes Jace was familiar with from his past life were only the main roads and passages through the monastery — the branch passages, corridors, and rooms were entirely unknown to him.

For instance, the hallways where the church had just lodged the pilgrims for the night — in the game, there was no way to even enter those.

It was the same with the path the priest was taking now. He followed the corridor until he ducked through an archway with a few bald priests. That should be a shortcut to the library. But in the game, the library and cathedral zones could only be reached through that underground tomb-like hall at the entrance — they had no internal connection between them.

"The rest area is that way, sir and ladies." A young priest in a simple linen robe pointed in the direction they'd come from.

"We're on our way to see a bishop. Thank you, brother." Jace said.

The young priest nodded, but still seemed a bit doubtful. Jace and the others, however, hurried past a corner following the earlier priest, and the young man couldn't be bothered to intervene.

"Is that library open to pilgrims like us?" Vereesa asked.

"Generally, no," Khadgar said. "But there are also scholars and mages from other kingdoms and cities inside, so non-clergy aren't completely out of place."

"When I was studying in Dalaran, I came here a few times with some of the academy's instructors. The most boring of the boring, the most painful of the painful. Now I regret not paying better attention back then — I wouldn't be walking in here now feeling like I've never been here before, completely lost."

"So if we get stopped, we can claim we're scholars here to look up church records?" Grede asked.

"You just keep your mouth shut when that happens," Khadgar said quietly.

Grede choked on a breath, so irritated he nearly exploded, but he glanced at the nearby clergy and managed to hold it in.

"Then you do the talking," Jace said.

"Understood." Khadgar nodded.

They jogged along the path the priest had taken, descending steps into a dark passageway. It was long, with branching passages on both sides leading to archways.

Khadgar paused, confused. "Damn. I forgot which way we go from the cathedral to the library. Did any of you catch which way the priest went?"

"Right." Vereesa pointed at a doorway and took off at a sprint. The group scrambled to keep up, nearly losing her — this ranger was absurdly fast, especially through these narrow side passages and corridors.

But barely keeping pace, they did spot the priest's back in the next corridor.

Jace had initially sensed the priest's anxious emotions shifting to the right through his perception, but in this monastery, his shadow sense was severely weakened. He could only barely catch traces — nothing as confident as he'd be outside.

No matter how ancient and ominous this place looked, no matter how mad and corrupted it became in other timelines, past, present, and future, this was still the largest bastion of the Holy Light on the entire Lordaeron continent.

Even with his magical power grown to where it was now, Jace couldn't exactly move freely here.

"Can you see through walls now?" Grede panted as he caught up to the ranger. "When we came downstairs, that priest was completely blocked by the wall!"

"This passage smells of old books. Is that unfamiliar to you, dwarf?" Vereesa said softly.

Behind them, Sora couldn't help a quiet snicker. Grede looked helplessly at the people around him and muttered, "So we finally hit a scene where you can really roast me. Can't believe you've still got the energy for jokes when we're about to face some insanely powerful and cunning demon, huh?"

"Cherish the chance to joke while you still can," Khadgar said as he caught up. "If things go wrong tonight, this time tomorrow we might each have our own cell in the Violet Hold."

"The Violet Hold has single cells?" Grede asked in surprise.

"If it didn't have single cells, do you know what kind of explosion you'd get from locking an unstable arcane aberration in with a raging fire elemental?" Khadgar asked.

"Would it be more explosive than locking a male Wildhammer and a female Dark Iron in the same cell?" Grede asked.

"Hey." Vereesa called from ahead. "Khadgar. As agreed. Your turn."

Khadgar was still staring at the dwarf with an incredulous expression. At the ranger's call, he quickly moved to the front. Ahead, the passage opened into a finely decorated foyer. Inside, faintly visible, were paintings on the gray-white walls and, deeper in, bookshelves crammed with books.

Two church staff members carrying long staves stood by the entrance, chatting. The priest who'd gone ahead had spoken briefly to them before entering, so they were probably the library's keepers.

They noticed the elf and the group behind her. Khadgar walked straight up and offered a ring. "I am Alex Himmermill from the royal city."

One of the priests took the silver-white ring and examined it. Jace couldn't read the inscription from behind, but the Menethil royal seal on it was perfectly clear.

"We're here to visit Master Du'an, the arcanist of the Silver Hand," Khadgar added.

"Master Du'an is not in the library," the priest said, handing the ring back. "My apologies, Master Himmermill."

"Ah, that's unfortunate." Khadgar pursed his lips.

"You can wait for his return in the cathedral district. There are empty cots for resting there," the other priest said. "He should be back at the monastery by tomorrow morning."

Khadgar frowned slightly. Jace spoke up: "You mean we should stay with the pilgrims?"

The two priests exchanged glances. Jace looked back at Sora and Vereesa and continued, "These guests from Quel'Thalas have important magical items to demonstrate to Master Du'an. We can't just bunk with people of unknown origin."

Sora and Vereesa already looked impatient. One was a green dragon who'd never cared much about human kingdoms, the other an elven noble raised in Quel'Thalas with an overflowing sense of superiority. It was hard to tell if the disdainful look on their faces that even Jace found uncomfortable was improvised for the occasion or them finally letting their true colors show.

"A book and a few cups of cold tea are enough to pass the night, sir. And this is Tirisfal Monastery's famous grand library," Jace said. "We'd be happy to wait inside for Master Du'an to return."

"If you don't mind, then please, come in." The two priests stepped aside. Jace and the others thanked them and entered.

Once they were away from the foyer, their pace quickened. In that delay, the priest had already disappeared somewhere. Luckily, the library was less chaotic than outside — the long corridor stretched far ahead. They hadn't chased long before they spotted the priest ambling unhurriedly up ahead.

"Thank the Light. I mean it, gotta thank the Light for getting us through." Khadgar muttered. "And now I understand how you and Sabellian sneaked into Hellfire Citadel."

"You should ask those Shadow Council orcs at Auchindoun about that, mage..." Sora said quietly.

"I agree," Needleflame said.

"Don't sell yourselves short." Jace smiled, glancing at the rooms on either side lined with books as they walked. "But where did you get that royal mage insignia? Without that ring, those priests wouldn't have let us in. That Alex whoever... are you sure using his identity won't cause problems?"

"Of course not, because there's no such person. The ring was borrowed temporarily from the Spell-Weaver, and it's already been returned." Khadgar spread his palm smugly. The finger that had just worn the ring was now bare, only a faint trace of arcane starlight remaining.

This guy cast small-scale illusion spells with the practiced ease of a seasoned magician pulling tricks. You couldn't even catch him chanting or channeling mana. But at least this wasn't Violet Citadel — ordinary priests really couldn't see through simple magical constructs.

The library was much quieter than outside. They had to whisper when talking. When two young priests carrying books passed by, they had to fall silent until they were gone.

When the priests had passed, Grede whispered, "Hey, we're in the library now. Time to take out that eyeball?"

"Almost." Khadgar nodded, pulling out the magical device wrapped in enchanted cloth. "Let's hope it does its job before those priests sense it. Just like the Kaldorei prisoner said it would."
