## Chapter 1792: Good at Empathy

Khadgar used his robe to conceal the pale violet beholder's eye as he led Jace and the others quickly after the priest. Nearby, a few church staff glanced at them curiously, but just like the young priests outside, they didn't pay much attention.

After passing through several corridor doors, Jace, who was closest to Khadgar, heard something wrong. It sounded like a demon's whisper or murmur — very faint, hard to pin down.

He looked at Khadgar. Khadgar nodded. It wasn't just Jace's imagination — Khadgar heard it too.

Then behind them, Sora asked softly, "Is it — "

"Yes," Khadgar said. "The beholder's eye is reacting."

"The demon's close?" Grede asked.

"I've never used this thing before. Never hunted a demon with it." Khadgar tightened his robe to hide the eye better. "But right now it's definitely different from when we were at the library entrance."

"Let's get a bit closer and see," Sora said.

They quickened their pace again, following the priest. The distance shrank. In Jace's memory, the library's corridors ended at Arcanist Du'an's study, somewhat like Nielas Aran's phantom library in Karazhan — that circular hall with pillars.

But the priest didn't go all the way to the back of the library. He stopped at a side door in the inner corridor, after another archway, and knocked.

The group stopped at the door as well, not stepping through the archway to reveal themselves.

"High Priest Fairbanks, I'm sorry to disturb you so late."

Jace glanced at the beholder's eye in Khadgar's hands. Its sound had gone from a moan to a chattering, like grinding teeth, mixed with sharp shrieks forced through a throat.

Khadgar was almost worried it was too loud, that it might draw the attention of a few church members reading at the other end of this corridor.

Even suppressed by the Light here, Jace could clearly feel the fear seeping out of that eye. Just like Illidan said, it could definitely sense the presence of a Nathrezim.

"You were right, Jace." The mage spoke quietly. "You were right this time. A demon really has infiltrated this most holy of places."

*Thump.* The door opened. The priest nodded respectfully, and a middle-aged man with golden hair and a gentle face stepped out.

He was dressed plainly — just a warm gray coat, no clerical robes. He looked more like a struggling scholar than a bishop.

"There are some townsfolk from Vandermar who've come seeking your aid, High Priest Fairbanks. I've arranged for them to wait at the church... um, Your Excellency?"

Fairbanks didn't respond to the priest. Didn't even look at him. Because he'd already noticed Jace and the others not far away.

"Why have you followed here?"

The priest looked confused. But Fairbanks' expression was calm as he said, "The Holy Light already told me you would soon be here, Master Khadgar. And the others, I believe, are Jace Sesso, Lady Vereesa Windrunner, High Lord Grede Thunderfist, and the Green Dragon lady said to be living in Stormwind?"

"The Holy Light was a bit late with its warning this time. Probably because it doesn't have much fondness for demons." Khadgar said. "You think you've disguised yourself well, but your mistake was being too eager to pin the blame on Sesso. When did a dreadlord get so impatient, hmm?"

"What... what's going on, a demon?" The messenger priest panicked instantly.

The monastery bells rang out with a *clang clang*, making the priest jump. From outside the corridor came the noise of running feet and shouting — it seemed this group of "pilgrims" who'd snuck in had somehow attracted the attention of the clergy.

Soon, a crowd of staff-wielding monks and sword-carrying battle priests charged through the corridors, blocking Jace's group and Fairbanks in the middle. Vereesa was quick — she drew her blade and pressed it to Fairbanks' neck, while Needleflame grabbed the messenger priest.

Grede pulled out a shortsword he'd kept hidden for a long time. Khadgar drew a wand from his robes and aimed at the church members closing in.

In Jace's peripheral vision, he noticed the beholder's eye in his robe was reacting more violently as the priests pressed closer. Its struggles intensified until it started making sounds like weeping, the murky light inside trembling violently.

"Reacted damn fast." Grede frowned, muttering low. "Did this guy trigger some alarm mechanism ahead of time?"

"Light above, everyone." A high-ranking priest in bright yellow bishop's robes stepped out from the crowd. "I know, Jace Sesso — if you decided to leave right now, all the monastery's defenders combined couldn't stop you and your friends. And Master Khadgar is with you — even the Violet Citadel couldn't hold him."

"But if you do that, you'll never get the chance to speak in your own defense and clear your names."

"Are you telling us to surrender, Voss?" Khadgar said. "The Church knows about the devils in Blackrock Spire. They know the devils plan to smear Jace Sesso and the entire kingdom of Stormwind. Don't fall for their tricks like the Kirin Tor did."

"If Jace Sesso is truly being framed, and there really are hidden devils walking among the kingdoms, I swear by the Light — Archbishop Faol and the entire Church will never side with the demons," another high-ranking priest said quietly.

"I'd say you've come just in time." Vereesa held Fairbanks tight. "Everyone's here. Let's all witness just how foolish you really are."

"Well said, Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

Khadgar pointed his wand, glowing pale blue, at High Priest Fairbanks. Fairbanks himself seemed to have accepted his fate, offering no resistance.

"Wait," Jace said.

"What is it?" Khadgar asked.

The others also looked at Jace in surprise.

Fairbanks wasn't the demon.

This orb made from a beholder's eye couldn't speak. It only made those shrieking noises. But the intensity seemed to vary — maybe indicating distance, or strength.

When they'd gotten closer to Fairbanks, the eye's struggles hadn't escalated. Jace had initially thought this was what Illidan meant by "terror upon seeing a Nathrezim." But after they'd exchanged a few words with Fairbanks, the eye's reaction grew much stronger... which meant that weaker reaction was nowhere near its ceiling.

Which meant the demon was actually hiding among the approaching crowd. Was the priest named Voss the dreadlord in disguise? Or someone else?

Jace couldn't tell. He'd tried using demonic vision, but it was pitch black — nothing. A Nathrezim wasn't afraid of his demonic vision at all.

He tried shadow perception, but the crowd was too dense and noisy. Surprise, fear, timidity, resolve, zeal — all mixed together. There were extremes and there was calm. Even outside the monastery, picking out a specific person from this was hard enough. Here, in this Light-drenched hall, it was nearly impossible.

They'd almost fallen for the Nathrezim's trap.

He could even picture the demon hiding in the crowd, sneering coldly, waiting for him and Khadgar and the others to execute Fairbanks, waiting to see the shock and despair on their faces. It would do everything it could to provoke a bloodbath between them and the Church, to stir everyone's emotions until the entire Tirisfal Monastery was drenched in blood, until Jace was thoroughly confirmed as the monster trying to destroy the human world — merging with the image of the plague-spreading fiend, the Black Knight, the scythe-wielding Reaper.

So when this demon saw him stop Khadgar from attacking Fairbanks, and watched him turn his gaze to the crowd, wouldn't it feel a little frustrated and disappointed?

Nothing. No frustration. No disappointment.

Dreadlords were masters of shadow manipulation. These damned devils could enslave their own emotions too. They could act just like anyone else — surprised, disappointed at the heroes' madness and collective fall from grace, worried about High Priest Fairbanks' dangerous predicament — blending perfectly in with these mortals.

The beholder's eye kept trembling. Even the two bishops had noticed the suspicious thing in Khadgar's hand. The surrounding defenders and priests were growing suspicious too, as if the orb might explode at any moment and help these degenerates escape the monastery.

And a moment of realization? *So this is it — you were using the beholder's eye to track me, I see now.* Nothing like that either.

Jace couldn't help but frown. The more he tried to detect something unusual through shadow and emotion, the more he felt the Holy Light burning and torturing him, and the harder it became to read the crowd.

What now? Surrender? Accept defeat and stand trial? Like Prophet Velen's prophecy — thrown into prison, but eventually freed?

Wait. He sensed something. Something achingly familiar.

Torment.

Yes, torment.

More precisely, *enduring* torment. Enduring the torment of the Holy Light.

Though it was so faint it was almost buried under the storm of emotions in this hallway, Jace could feel it perfectly because he was experiencing the exact same thing. He could *empathize*.

Like a starving wolf suddenly spotting a rabbit dart past, his gaze snapped to someone else — a younger-looking high-ranking priest with red hair.

"What's your name?"

"Isillien, Master Sesso." The high priest replied. "It's an honor to meet you, but — "

"Good. Isillien. Very good." Jace cut him off bluntly, then said coldly, "I was just going to tell you — you shouldn't have sworn by the Light."

"Oh, indeed. I just realized that myself." Isillien smiled coldly, and in a dead, gravelly voice, uttered a single sentence in Demonic: "No wonder Kil'jaeden hates you so much."
