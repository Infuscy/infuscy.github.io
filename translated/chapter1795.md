## Chapter 1795: The Eye-Bearer?

"A Kirin Tor inquiry — exactly." Khadgar said. "Archmage Antonidas mentioned this to me before. He wanted me to persuade you to come to Dalaran and face the Kirin Tor once you returned from Kalimdor. If you agree this time, they'll certainly be happy to get it done quickly."

"We'll have all six of them present at once, and test them all with the Eye in one go. No need to take the risk of meeting each Council member separately, and they'll have no reason to refuse to attend."

"I'm thinking beyond just the six, Khadgar," Jace said. "I mean everyone. Ideally, the leaders of all seven Alliance kingdoms — including the Church and the Silver Hand. Since this plague affects the entire Lordaeron continent, inland and coast alike, we'd better have representatives from Quel'Thalas and the Three Hammers too."

"Ye want to check the whole world in one go?" Grede asked.

"This is the best chance we'll get," Jace said. "What other excuse could you find to bring every potential Nathrezim-controlled target in front of us all at once?"

"And those demons won't find a better chance to publicly and 'fairly' expose yer possession of Urtales either," Grede said quietly. "Ye're right. If I were one of those demons, I wouldn't miss it either."

"What if the remaining demons, after what happened at the abbey, don't dare to show themselves?" Vereesa asked. "They might not know exactly what method you used to find the demon, but they know you did find one. They might be wary of the risk an inquiry poses."

"At the very least, we can rule out a large batch of important figures at once," Khadgar said. "The Lordaeron court, Dalaran, the Arathi — they're all worth scrutinizing. King Terenas II has recovered his health, so he can't use illness as an excuse anymore. Besides, he's been one of the leaders most supportive of investigating Jace. If he suddenly refuses to attend this inquiry, that would almost confirm he's compromised."

"And it gives me a chance to watch their attitudes and reactions," Jace said. "I'm thinking there'll be some tells, something off..."

The mage nodded. "If the Nathrezim isn't among those figures — especially if it's not Terenas or Prince Arthas — things will be a lot easier. Then we only need to focus on one of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six. I'm confident that if you publicly announce your willingness to accept the Kirin Tor's inquiry and investigation, inviting them all won't be a problem. Your influence is more than enough for that now, Jace."

"After all, he's saved this fragile Alliance more than once," Vereesa said. "He deserves that much respect."

"And he's a scary enough potential threat to the Seven Kingdoms, Khaz Modan, and Quel'Thalas all at once," Grede said.

"Those two things sound contradictory, but to the Kirin Tor and the Alliance, they're basically the same thing," Khadgar said.

"Fine," the dwarf grumbled.

"What about the rest of us?" Vereesa looked at Needleflame sitting on the bed, and Sora leaning against the corner of the wall. "We're obviously accomplices in Khadgar's escape from the Violet Hold as far as Dalaran's concerned. I'm not expecting to attend the inquiry, but will we get thrown into the Violet Hold when the Kirin Tor gets here... What do you think they'll say when Dalaran's people arrive at the abbey, Khadgar?"

Khadgar looked at the assembled group. "The Nathrezim threat is now in front of everyone. The armor it left behind is with the Church — that's irrefutable. Unless the remaining three demons have already taken over three seats on the Council of Six, there's no way they can convict us under these circumstances. As for the 'jailbreak' charge... I don't know. Even if Antonidas is only somewhat rational, he should know these issues need to wait until after the demon crisis is dealt with."

"Is he rational?" Vereesa said. "He knew there was a Nathrezim skilled at infiltration at Blackrock Spire, that they'd already started infiltrating the Alliance's ranks, and he still chose to lock you in the Violet Hold..."

"I'm not trying to defend him, but it was a joint decision by the Council of Six. To shut up all those people who were questioning Jace and me..." Khadgar sighed. "I've lost count of how many times I've regretted not joining the Council when I had the chance. Damn it, if I'd taken the seat, Vargos probably wouldn't have been elected."

"Then the Nathrezim might have chosen you instead," Jace said.

"Yeah?" Khadgar perked up and waved dismissively. "Why not say the demons might have chosen you instead? Let them try... Anyway, let's drop this pointless 'what if' talk. And we shouldn't jump to conclusions that Archmage Vargos is the one possessed by the Nathrezim. Don't let preconceptions cloud our judgment."

"But in any case, we won't be able to attend the inquiry — not even you, Khadgar," Vereesa said. "And Jace will be appearing as the one being questioned. Anything he's carrying might be suspected — including his staff. So how is he supposed to bring the Eye in?"

"If this inquiry does happen, where will it be held?" Jace asked.

"I'm not sure," Khadgar said. "But based on my experience, it'll probably be in the Chamber of Air."

"The cloudstone pillars floating above the Violet Hold?" Sora asked.

"Yes, that's the place." Khadgar said. "Getting in requires passing through a heavily guarded portal. Sneaking in or infiltrating with a disguise is virtually impossible."

"Unless you're a Nathrezim," Vereesa said.

"Let's not make jokes at the Kirin Tor's expense about Nathrezim, Ranger-General," Khadgar said. "We've already seen how insidious their mental possession is. Even Jace couldn't sense it. Even an eye-beast — a void creature whose gaze can pierce through dimensions and countless illusions — had to extract a Nathrezim's soul just to sense its presence nearby, without being able to determine exactly who it was possessing."

"The Kirin Tor's defensive measures are still a bit too fragile against high-ranking demons like this. But I'll figure out ways to improve them later — as long as I make it through this crisis without getting kicked out of the Kirin Tor."

"Then who do we find? Is there anyone left we can trust?" Sora asked. "Should we ask High Thane Kurdran for help again?"

"I seriously doubt Kurdran would be the one attending a gathering like this," Khadgar said. "If it's important enough for every kingdom's leader to show up, protocol says it would be High Thane Falstad. Kurdran handed Aerie Peak over to Falstad Wildhammer when he left for Outland, and there hasn't been any formal power transfer back since. Not saying it couldn't be him, but we can't take that risk. What if he just doesn't show? Falstad... we don't know him well. You think you can trust him, Thunderfist?"

"I dunno, I haven't properly been home in a long time, mage," Grede shook his head. "What about the King of Ironforge, Magni Bronzebeard? He's a dwarf too, worth trustin'."

Khadgar pondered for a moment. "King Magni wouldn't use demonic methods to help us 'suspects,' and I can't say for certain otherwise. If it were Muradin Bronzebeard, maybe he'd be willing. But the Mountain King has the same problem as Kurdran — he might not be present at an occasion like this."

"What about King Varian Wrynn?" Vereesa asked. "Would he work? Stormwind is certain to attend. You're his magical advisor — the royal mage — and this whole matter is tied to the Blackrock Spire campaign he initiated. He'll definitely be there in person."

"I agree," Khadgar said. "We can approach King Varian."

"He's not like the Mountain King — and he doesn't know demons the way you or I do, Khadgar." Jace said. "If that Eye makes some sound and tips off the demon, if it bursts out like it did in the library... the demon that infiltrated the Council of Six is bound to be even stronger than the one we just faced. Their position is more critical, they're taking a bigger risk. We don't know what tricks they'll have up their sleeve at the last minute. The risk to Varian is just too great."

"We just don't have many reliable options left — high enough rank, guaranteed to attend, and absolutely trustworthy." The mage leaned in. "Think about it — when King Varian finds out about this, will he agree, or will he refuse out of fear?"

"He'd be more than willing. Probably even thrilled," Jace said helplessly.

"Then let's defer to King Varian's choice," Khadgar said.
