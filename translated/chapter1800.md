## Chapter 1800: The Informant

The hearing was in two days. Jace had heard that Varian Wrynn's party had stopped in Ambermill two days ago to meet with the King of Gilneas, which meant that today — or maybe even right now — he could arrive in Dalaran.

Hopefully Sora could get that eye to Varian smoothly, explain the precautions clearly, and do it without attracting too much attention.

Though that green dragon was probably the kindest and most honest of their group, remembering how she had disguised herself as an orc in Auchindoun to deal with those Shadow Council members, Jace figured he didn't need to worry too much.

Sora had her task these days, and Jace had his own problems to handle. Rather than worrying about her, he should focus on his own situation.

Jace followed two Violet Eye mages through a corridor and into the main hall on the first floor of the Violet Citadel — the same place where Grede had caused a ruckus with the Windreaver's Kiss blade.

Many mages passing through glanced their way, but whenever the warlock's gaze met theirs, each one instinctively looked away, pretending they hadn't been peeking.

Whispers filled the air. He could occasionally make out his name, or something that sounded like it, coming from all directions. The hall was thick with fear and unease — to the point where Jace couldn't quite tell which were the hushed conversations of young Dalaran mages and which was the murmur of Uthalesh.

He hadn't been this far from his staff in a long time. The staff was still in Vereesa's room. Grede and Needleflame were probably there too. But he felt as though the staff were still on him — the tearing of the soul, the resonance of shadow, the low mutterings that occasionally fell in his mind...

Jace. "Jace?"

Jace snapped his head up, realizing that this call hadn't come from inside his mind.

A woman draped in a sky-blue cloak hurried in through the castle's main gate. Against the sunlight, Jace couldn't even make out her face clearly, but just from the silhouette... he already knew who it was.

"Amy?"

The moment he spoke, the female mage wrapped her arms tightly around his waist. Jace held her shoulders and said, "Do you realize you're hugging a suspect of the Kirin Tor right now?"

"You're my brother. My best friend," Amy said softly.

Jace sighed. He hadn't gone looking for Amy precisely because he didn't want to drag her into this mess, didn't want to cause her unnecessary trouble in the Kirin Tor down the road. But now... it didn't matter anymore.

Amy Marin glanced at the two Violet Eye members ahead who were looking their way but didn't dare say anything. "I heard about the hearing."

"Will you come?" Jace asked.

"Unfortunately, I don't have enough standing to attend," Amy said. "But... Jaina promised she'd tell me what happened inside."

"She has higher status in Dalaran than you do?" Jace joked.

"Not at all." Amy couldn't help but smile. "But she does have a more powerful father, doesn't she?"

After saying that, she blinked hard at her slightly moist eyes, then put away her smile and lowered her head. "He believes in you. My father. He believes you're a good person, just like I do. Someone truly worthy of the name 'hero.'"

"I'll prove that to Master Malin," Jace said. "And to you too, Amy."

"You don't need to prove anything, Jace," Amy said.

Jace hesitated for a moment, but finally nodded. "When this hearing is over, we can go try some Dalaran pastries together."

"It's a deal." Amy let go, and the smile returned to her face.

She looked toward the Violet Eye mages ahead. The two of them tilted their heads slightly toward the corridor on the other side. Jace nodded in acknowledgment and followed their steps.

As he walked into the corridor, the noise from the hall swelled behind him. Until the sounds grew hollow and those panicked emotions more and more distant...

The two mages finally stopped before a door carved with an ice-blue magical eye emblem. The glow of the emblem cast a pale light through the dim corridor.

The door swung open, revealing a middle-aged human mage with flowing black hair. He exchanged quiet greetings with the two mages, then turned to look at the warlock.

"Good morning, Master Sesso. Welcome."

"This interrogation room is quite lovely, Archmage..." Jace said.

"Otharos." The mage bowed his head slightly. "I am Archmage Otharos of the Violet Eye. It's an honor to make your acquaintance, Master Sesso."

"This room truly is lovely. Especially for someone like me, who has spent far too long wandering around the Deadwind Pass area, accustomed to its gray towers, ruins, and crumbling walls, rarely returning to Dalaran. But this is absolutely not an interrogation room."

Jace walked in and sat at the table, glancing at the young mage on the other side — sitting upright, a thick record book and several quills laid out before him.

"Not an interrogation room, not an imprisonment, not a prison, not a criminal. I've heard too much of this lately, Archmage."

Archmage Otharos smiled politely, brewed a cup of hot Goldthorn tea, and set it before Jace. "This is merely a conversation... After all, the Violet Eye's ultimate goal is to completely neutralize the dangers of Karazhan Tower, to protect the innocent residents of the Kingdom of Stormwind nearby, and to help the Kirin Tor clear up some unresolved questions left behind by previous Guardians. A fellow mage who has ventured deep into Karazhan and walked out alive could provide us with very valuable information."

"Karazhan Tower? I thought your goal was to investigate the Tower of Il'garok?" Jace took a sip of tea.

"Er." Otharos coughed, glancing at the recorder beside him. "My deepest apologies, Master Sesso."

"At the time, the news from Blackrock Spire had the entire Kirin Tor on edge. The plague. The movements of the Nathrezim... Archbishop Faol and Marshal Turalyon hoped we could quickly investigate the possible means by which Nathrezim demons had been summoned into this world."

"And Karazhan, as a place — you've entered it with Master Khadgar, so you should know that after several horrific magical disasters there, it has become a dimensional nexus where multiple realities intersect. It's the most likely place for demons to slip through. When there were no other reliable clues about summoning rituals, the Kirin Tor also received intelligence that you and Master Khadgar may have entered Karazhan. Coupled with, coupled with..."

"The rumor that I possess Uthalesh," Jace finished.

"Yes, the Windreaver." Otharos lowered his voice. "You may have heard the story of how, centuries ago, Uthalesh corrupted the vast lands from Stranglethorn to the River Neltharion. Thanks to the then-Guardian Skavel..."

"I've heard something about it," Jace said.

At this, the recorder beside them was already trembling so hard his fingers shook. Otharos shot him a glare before his nerves settled slightly.

"Before the Garden Festival," the Archmage continued, "you, Lady Vereesa Windrunner, and High Lord Grede Thunderfist were still hunting the Black Dragon Princess Onyxia in Kalimdor. The situation was extremely tense, and we couldn't reach any of you or know when you might return to Stormwind. So we had no choice but to submit this abrupt request directly to His Majesty King Varian Wrynn."

"So who exactly told the Kirin Tor that Khadgar and I went to Karazhan?" Jace asked.

"I don't have the authority to tell you that, Master Sesso. My apologies again." Otharos lowered his head, flipped through the papers beneath his hand, and spoke with some urgency. "So — did you and Master Khadgar reach the Grand Library? Because Karazhan's arcane guardians seem to have changed their response logic toward us outsiders. We have sufficient evidence to suggest that the Curator may have already..."

"I don't have the authority to tell you that, Master Otharos," Jace said.

The Archmage looked at the recorder, cleared his throat awkwardly, then continued. "The Grand Library of Karazhan holds records of previous Guardians' methods for controlling, sealing, and destroying various demonic artifacts — including the Scepter of Sargeras... and the Windreaver should be one of them. Did you and Master Khadgar enter Karazhan to find a way to control — or of course, destroy — it?"

"If I remember correctly, the hearing about Uthalesh is supposed to be the day after tomorrow, and it's at the Sky Council," Jace said. "Not here."

"Indeed." Otharos scratched something with his quill. "Then, one more question. Was there anyone else in Karazhan at the time?"

"I don't have the authority to answer that question right now, Master Otharos." Jace leaned back in his chair. "Or any of the remaining questions you might have. Not unless you've been given sufficient authority to tell me who informed you about Karazhan."

He raised his hands. "I promise not to do anything adverse to them. After all, they did it for the Alliance. For the sake of keeping this world free from demonic corruption. We're on the same side. And besides, I'm already used to the Kirin Tor making malicious assumptions about everything I do."

"I understand, but this... is also unlikely, Master Sesso." The Archmage's face showed his difficulty.

"Think it over carefully," Jace said. "I do have that important meeting the day after tomorrow to attend, after all."

Seeing the two of them at a loss for words, silence stretched on for a long moment. Jace's gaze flicked between them. "If this isn't an interrogation, but a friendly conversation, am I free to leave whenever I want?"

The warlock stood up. The two mages exchanged helpless glances. Jace politely bid them farewell, straightened his shirt, and walked out of the room, leaving the Violet Eye members at the door and inside the room staring at each other.

The Violet Eye was the Kirin Tor's secret investigative agency, established specifically to study Medivh's legacy. These people would go to any lengths to uncover the secrets of Karazhan.

So the only people who could make them hesitate to reveal the "informant" were basically the six members of the Council.

Which meant — was it one of those Violet Eye mages he and Khadgar had encountered before entering Karazhan? The dreadlord disguised as one of the Six, hiding among them?

No matter. When the hearing came, whether you dared to show your face or not, the truth would come to light.
