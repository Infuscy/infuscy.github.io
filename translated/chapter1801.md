## Chapter 1801: The Sky Council

A team of Dalaran purple-clad guards carrying enchanted shields led the way, while a portal mage guided Jace, who brought up the rear, through the corridors of the Violet Citadel.

As he passed a window, Jace looked out. The streets of Dalaran were packed with people, flags of every nation waving everywhere.

Mages, soldiers, and Dalaran citizens crowded the square below the castle steps. Groups constantly wove through the crowd and entered the castle, stirring up waves of murmuring discussion.

Jace remembered that almost ten years ago, he'd first begun to have thoughts about doing something, changing something. He really hadn't imagined that one day he'd receive so much goodwill, malice, hatred, admiration — that he'd be the focus of attention for the entire world.

In truth, when he and Khadgar were planning this hearing, he hadn't fully considered what a massive shockwave it would send through the entire Alliance once the idea reached Antonidas, the master of Dalaran.

In fact, he hadn't really felt much in the days afterward, holed up in the Violet Citadel, getting most of the news from the servants and the mages who came to question him.

So from here, looking out at the dense crowds and the city's bustling excitement — all of it because of him, because of this warlock who should have been forever lurking in the shadows — it felt strangely out of place.

But if he thought deeper — the second decade after the Dark Portal, the decade before the Burning Legion's invasion, the Third War, the Battle of Mount Hyjal — in that other timeline, it had been a relatively quiet and peaceful decade.

In that decade, humanity rejoiced at having essentially won the Orc Wars, pouring all their energy into healing wounds and rebuilding ruins. The scattered orcs skulked and hid after their great defeat and the closing of the Dark Portal. The Sons of Lothar fought to survive in the dying Draenor.

Meanwhile, the demons waited in the shadows, waiting for their new pawn — the Lich King — to slowly gnaw and grow in the far north where no one was watching, until one day he would gain enough power to shake the world of the living in the south.

But in this decade, he was here. Like a live fish thrown into a stagnant pond, stirring everything into chaos.

This disruptor who had shattered all the stillness, this "interventionist" trying to change every established course — how could he not draw everyone's attention?

This wasn't something he could dodge or hide from.

Throughout the Violet Citadel, the ethereal hum of teleportation circles being cast echoed continuously. Though the constant noise seemed to come from near and far, that faint rusty smell had already spread into this hallway.

It meant the kings and nobles were already arriving. Hopefully Varian could find a way to keep that demon eye from being intercepted by the Kirin Tor's wizards, and bring it smoothly up to the sky, Jace thought to himself.

"This way, Master Sesso."

The mage gestured. Jace followed his direction into a room bleached pale lavender by arcane light.

A floating portal manifested with the sound of wind chimes and arcane thunder. Two mages continuously channeled to maintain its stability. Through the magical rift, Jace could even see vast blue sky and white clouds.

The mage beside him spoke up. "When going through the portal, you may feel strong vibrations. After stepping out, you might experience sudden nausea. But as long as you keep your body from making any large movements during this brief process, there will be no accidents, Master Sesso."

He paused, then added, "Of course, I know that two years ago, you helped the Sons of Lothar pass through a demon gate powered by the Eye of Dalaran and the Book of Medivh. Just imagining that scene... is enough to awe us school-transfer mages. So this shouldn't be a problem for you at all. This is just routine — a reminder given to every non-Kirin Tor member entering the Sky Council."

"I know. Thank you very much." Jace smiled and nodded.

With that, he walked toward the portal. As he tried to step into it, he could feel his inner emotions growing hot. The magical rift before him seemed to struggle to contain the chaotic power inside him, beginning to tremble slightly.

The two mages frowned deeply. It wasn't until the mage who had led him there noticed something wrong and immediately raised his hand to join the sustaining ritual that the portal finally stabilized enough for him to pass through smoothly.

So this was why, every time he asked Khadgar or other mages to teleport him somewhere, they all made faces of difficulty.

Passing through the Kirin Tor's portal was smoother than dipping your face through a film of water — a far cry from what that mage had warned him about.

Leaving the dim room, Jace stepped onto a marble platform. Nearby were battle mages holding crystal staves and wearing enchanted longswords. Before him rose a staircase, completely suspended below, commanding a view that encompassed nearly half of Lake Lordamere and the distant Alterac Mountains.

Wind howled past. High above, amidst several floating colossal stone pillars, sat the Sky Council — an ancient and ornate circular platform.

Kirin Tor purple-clad guards riding gryphons wove through the clouds beyond the stone pillars. Leaders and nobles conversed on snow-white stone seats that reflected the sunlight.

The atmosphere felt less like an impending trial of the world's most terrifying dark wizard, and more like an unprecedented grand gathering of the Lordaeron Alliance — celebrating how Alliance members had repeatedly dealt heavy blows to orc remnants and countless evil forces in recent years, until finally achieving ultimate victory.

The six members of the Council of Six stood on the highest ring. Antonidas. Kael'thas. Modera. Drenden. Krasus. And Vargus.

Everyone was here, Jace thought to himself. In truth, he didn't even know whether he should be surprised or not.

He climbed the steps. With each step higher, the full Sky Council chamber revealed itself around him.

Vereesa, Grede, and many other nobles, lords, high-ranking wizards below the Council of Six, archmages, Farstriders from Quel'Thalas including Sylvanas, and representatives of the Magisterium led by High Magister Belo'vir — all sat on lower floating platforms.

Many Dalaran residents might not have expected Belo'vir to be willing to come here. After all, the Magisterium and the Silvermoon Council had never gotten along well with Dalaran.

But Jace wasn't surprised at all. After all, this involved Uthalesh. Involved Dalk'an Drathir. Without Belo'vir and the Sacred Relics Society of Silvermoon, the dreadlord might not have been able to help the Kirin Tor connect the clues so quickly.

Sora, Needleflame, and Khadgar should be among them too — somewhere among those archmages. But they were too far away. Jace couldn't even make out each person's face from this distance, only distinguishing them by their more detailed clothing and headdresses.

The human and dwarf kings, along with their closest royal members, sat higher up, closer to the Council of Six — and closer to him.

King Terenas Menethil II sat at the center of the kings, chatting with Admiral Daelin Proudmoore of Kul Tiras and his daughter Jaina. He looked well, full of energy, as if he had returned to the days of Prince Arthas's coming-of-age ceremony.

In contrast to his relaxed demeanor, Prince Arthas sat beside him with a grave expression, staring fixedly at the center of the Sky Council — the platform where Jace would soon stand.

Varian Wrynn sat at an angle not far away, one hand on his cheek, his expression stern, not communicating with anyone nearby.

To his left was the aged Thoras Trollbane and his son Galen. After Blackrock Spire, Galen looked much thinner, as if he hadn't fully recovered from the orge onslaught.

To his right was King Genn Greymane of Gilneas, accompanied by Isiden Perenolde, the prince of Alterac.

After Isiden's cousin Aliden had been deemed an accomplice of his father, King Aiden Perenolde, in betraying the Lordaeron Alliance and siding with the orcs, Isiden had naturally become the last legitimate heir to the Kingdom of Alterac.

On the opposite side of the ring from the kings sat the leaders of the Silver Hand and the Church of the Holy Light — including Turalyon and Archbishop Alonsus Faol.

This meant that every leader of the Lordaeron Alliance's member states, except the Sun King of Quel'Thalas, had arrived on site. Even Alterac was present. This was a remarkable scene — one that would be extremely hard to find even in any Warcraft game from his previous life.

Jace walked to the highest point. When he reached the very center of the concentric rings, the entire Council fell silent.

Varian took a deep breath and sat up straight. When he noticed Jace's gaze sweeping past him, he gave a slight nod. At this distance, Jace couldn't read much of Varian's emotion, but at least from his calm demeanor, everything seemed to be going smoothly.

Jace roughly estimated the distance between Varian and all the surrounding leaders, as well as the members of the Council of Six.

Based on the experience at the monastery, Varian was a bit far from the Council of Six — but scanning every king, prince, and princess clearly was no problem. Though if one of the six was a dreadlord, this distance should still be enough for the eye to react.

"Your Majesties, Your Excellencies, and Lords, welcome to the Sky Council." Antonidas announced loudly. "This hearing — the first since the Orc Wars — is a crucial hearing concerning dark magic and the lingering issues left by the Guardians. It undoubtedly concerns the continued peace, tranquility, and stability of the entire world."

"And of course, more importantly, it also concerns the innocence of His Majesty King Varian Wrynn's magical advisor, the Lord of Il'garok Tower — Jace Sesso. All of you seated here know that for years, rumors and slanders about him have circulated continuously without cease. Today, I hope that under the witness of Your Majesties, Your Excellencies, and Lords, we can achieve a clarification and a resolution."

"Thank you very much for participating in this hearing, Lord Jace Sesso. I believe this has been your wish for a long time as well."

"Indeed it has, Master Antonidas," Jace replied.
