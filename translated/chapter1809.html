Chapter 1809: Heavy Rain

Tarren Mill, surrounded by sprawling farmland and fields, was barely visible from a distance — just the stone-carved symbol of the Holy Light atop the monastery's spire. If not for the distant, mist-shrouded Alterac Mountains, you might mistake it for Northshire at first glance.

This little town had suffered more than anywhere else in Lordaeron during the Orc Wars. Darrowshire and Southshore had mostly recovered to their former states, but Tarren Mill still looked battered and broken even today. The ruins of houses at the edge of town lay exactly where they'd fallen, completely ignored. Further out among the fields, unmarked graves dotted the high slopes like a pox on the land.

At least the town center near the monastery had been cleaned up enough to look like a normal town. And today, there seemed to be more people here than usual.

Mages in Dalaran violet robes, alongside Farstriders and Magisters who had traveled from Quel'Thalas, were gathered in front of the inn. Jace, Khadgar, and the Farstriders guiding them dismounted from their dragonhawks and gryphons and walked over. The assembled crowd turned to look.

"Is Lady Caitlyn Runeweaver inside?" Khadgar called out.

"She is, Master Khadgar," a Dalaran mage replied. "On the second floor."

The two nodded, pushed through the crowd into the inn, and climbed the stairs. Vereesa and Sora were already waiting for them.

"Where are Grede and Needleflame?" Jace asked.

"They're watching the luggage back at the Violet Citadel," Sora said. "I flew here with just the eyes."

"But she's not a demon, Jace — eyes can't see through her disguise," Vereesa added.

Jace nodded. If there were only five Nathrezim, each of them would be occupying a critical position. Since one had already influenced Ansirem Runeweaver, possessing his daughter as well would just be a waste of resources.

"How did you find her, Ranger-General? Why isn't she in Dalaran?" Khadgar asked, looking at Vereesa.

Jace actually wanted to ask a different question: why wasn't Caitlyn in Stranglethorn Vale?

In another timeline, after the Battle of Mount Hyjal, Ansirem Runeweaver's daughter had run off to Booty Bay and joined the Blackwater Raiders, earning herself the nickname "Caitlyn of the Blade." Ansirem himself knew about it, but whether out of indulgence for his rebellious daughter or sheer helplessness, he never used his position or influence to force her decision.

But that was three or four years from now. Caitlyn herself was still young, and at this point she was probably still in Dalaran, like every other archmage's child, receiving a proper magical education step by step, destined to become another Amy Marin.

"Any later and she might have gone even further, Khadgar," Vereesa said. "Caitlyn made contact with some rogue gangs in Alterac to help her sneak out of Dalaran. They planned to send her through Stromgarde and across Khaz Modan overland, heading south — that way she could avoid the tight inspections at Southshore's harbor."

"In theory, yes — but she refuses to admit any of it," Vereesa said. "She thinks we're trying to harm her father."

"Truth Potion?" Khadgar asked.

"Didn't work." Vereesa shot him a glance. "The Farstriders weren't about to waste time negotiating with her, especially not now. We fed her two doses the moment we caught her."

"Color me completely unsurprised." Khadgar took a deep breath. "That stuff is overkill for weak wills and useless against real stubborn ones. Let's go see her."

They entered the room. Caitlyn Runeweaver was tied tightly to a chair in the middle of the room. She looked lean and tough — a stark contrast to the soft, spotless appearance of the other young apprentices back in the academy. But she'd clearly been through a lot. Sweat beaded on her forehead, her brown hair was soaked through, and her boots and pant legs were caked with mud. Hard to tell whether two days of running had worn her out, or if the Farstriders and Kirin Tor mages had been taking turns putting her through the wringer.

"You're Jace Sesso!" The girl lifted her head, her features twisted, staring at the warlock who'd just walked in. "You servant of dark magic! How dare you slander my father... helping demons cause chaos? I think YOU'RE the demon's lapdog! You should be locked up in the Violet Hold right next to Kel'Thuzad!"

"If you want to prove your father's innocence — to prove that I'm the demon's lapdog — then you should help us find him. Not buy him time," Jace said.

"Hah! You just want to kill him! You want the demons to kill him!" Caitlyn spat viciously.

Vereesa sighed. "We've told her this many times already, Jace."

Khadgar frowned, then glanced at a Kirin Tor mage beside him. "Does this seem... familiar to you?"

"Some kind of memory tampering spell cast by Master Runeweaver?" The mage nodded. "A Quel'dorei Magister had the same thought earlier, but we found no trace of Arcane residue in her mind, Master Khadgar."

"What if it's Nathrezim shadow magic instead?" Khadgar looked around the room. "What if a dreadlord tampered with her mind using its own power?"

"The dreadlords tampered with YOUR minds! They drove all of YOU crazy!" Caitlyn's voice cracked as she sobbed quietly. "Why won't you believe my father, but you'll believe this warlock? Masters, I don't understand... I don't understand..."

Caitlyn's emotions were a chaotic, frenzied mess. Honestly, in this chaotic shadow energy, Jace couldn't detect any precise details about whatever shadow magic had been used.

"Maybe we can find something in her dreams?" Jace asked. "Like with that alchemist last time."

"Worth a try," Vereesa said.

"What alchemist?" Khadgar asked.

"I'm not sure..." Sora murmured.

Khadgar asked, "So you're planning to enter her dream?"

"Only one person can go in, Master Khadgar," Sora said. "I don't have enough power to send multiple consciousnesses into one person's dream, unless... we had another bottle of that Demolition Potion."

"We don't have time to brew another Demolition Potion now, Lady Sora. So let him go." Khadgar pointed at Jace. "He understands those demons' shadow magic better than I do — at least at a fundamental level."

"Fine." Jace shot the mage a look, coughed, and sat down by the window.

"What potion? What are you talking about? What are you going to do—" The girl stared blankly at the people in the room, but before she could finish, her head lolled to the side and she slumped against her ropes.

Sora raised a hand and looked at Jace. "I'm starting, Jace."

"I'm ready."

Jace straightened his collar. When he looked up again, the room was completely empty.

"Uh... Vereesa? Sora?"

He called out twice before realizing that this room was slightly different from the one he'd just been in. Different decor, different arrangement of objects, and a landscape painting on the wall that hadn't been there before.

He walked to the window and looked outside. The sky was gloomy and overcast, and a heavy rain was pouring down. In the distance, stretches of pitch-black forest were obscured by fog, barely visible.

This wasn't Tarren Mill.

This was Brill.

So this was Caitlyn's dream. Sora's hypnosis had never been particularly gentle with the transition, and by now he was pretty used to it.

Jace left the room and went down the stairs. The air was chilly. The first floor of the inn was completely empty too.

"Anyone here?" He tried calling toward the counter, but there was no answer. Why would it be empty? When had Caitlyn even come to Brill?

He stepped outside. The streets were the same — utterly deserted. For a moment, it felt like being back in Raven Hill. Not the little square in Raven Hill, but the road leading to the big cemetery — those abandoned tall houses that seemed to whisper of Raven Hill's former bustling days, now covered in cobwebs and dust that had claimed every corner of every building.

Wait. Was this really Caitlyn's dream? Or was it...

Jace spun around and stared at the inn's sign. He stared at "Journey's End" for a long moment, making absolutely sure it wasn't "The End of Fear," before calming down slightly.

Even so, that unease in his heart didn't fully disappear. He realized this panic and restlessness weren't entirely his own — they came from this world. From this dream.

Was the emptiness caused by demonic magic?

Maybe the Nathrezim had forcibly erased the most critical parts of Caitlyn Runeweaver's memory — the people she'd seen, the things that had happened between them. But the setting of Brill had survived as a backdrop, eventually forming this dream with its suffocating atmosphere.

So what had happened in Brill?

Jace looked around, and his gaze eventually landed on the stable across the street. For some reason, he felt like there was something in that stable that amplified the unease in his heart.

Was that where the Nathrezim's psychic magic had "landed"?

He stepped into the rain, crossed the street. His heart grew more inexplicably tense with every step.

It had been a while since he'd felt this nervous — even in the Molten Core facing Ragnaros the Firelord, or atop Blackrock Spire facing Nefarian.

Maybe the last time was when Grede had fallen in that sweltering crevice within the mountain...

He reached the stable and peered inside. No stable master, no owner, no horses. Every living thing seemed to have been erased entirely.

That's when he realized the growing tension wasn't entirely coming from this stable. He wiped some rain from his forehead, sniffed his hand, and looked up at the sky.

That was it. There'd been one other time, he thought. The Pig and Whistle. In front of the stable.

Heavy rain.
