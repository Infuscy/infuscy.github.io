## Chapter 181: Two Strange Fellows

Back with Grede, the dwarf hollered from a ways off, "What took you so long? That much to talk about?"

Jace shot back, "Why are you in such a hurry?"

"There's barely anyone here right now — we should get that wound looked at before they break for lunch or whatever." Grede pointed at the door of the building ahead. "What if they decide it's grub time the moment we walk in?"

"It doesn't hurt that much anymore… it's not like I can't wait," Jace said.

"That's because my medicine worked!" Grede said, gesturing at the cluster of elves over there. "Or what, you think chatting up a pretty girl fixed you?"

"The pretty girl who saved my life last night," Jace said. "Not like you, always worried I'm not dying fast enough."

Grede jabbed a finger at Jace's nose, opened his mouth to retort, and promptly forgot his line. Jace said suddenly, "You know what that Windrunner told me?"

"Ah, here come the tall tales," Grede said with a smug grin. "You rescued the damsel, she offered her hand in marriage to repay you, and you turned her down? Kid, I stopped running that routine fifty years ago. Jace Sesso, that's lame."

Jace's mouth hung open. He looked down at the dwarf's insufferably cocky face and honestly didn't even want to say it — he just wanted to kick him.

"She wants to help you with your eye," he said.

"Huh? What?" Grede's expression shifted in a heartbeat. "What do you mean, she can treat something like this?"

"Her older sister is the Ranger-General of Silvermoon," Jace said. "She might have some connections among the Silvermoon nobility — knows a botanist."

"Really?" Grede asked. "Why would she help me? Why would she help you?"

"Obviously I rescued the damsel, she offered her hand in marriage to repay you, and you turned her down, then felt bad about it and decided to make it up to you," Jace said.

"Hahaha, I'm kidding, don't be mad!" Grede clapped Jace on the arm, then asked, "So when are we heading to Quel'Thalas? I don't want to wait a second longer."

"The Hinterlands," Jace said. "She can't get us into Quel'Thalas, but she can get that elven botanist out of Quel'Thalas. At least, that's what she told me."

Grede scratched his beard. "Then we leave tomorrow! Tomorrow morning — first thing! But your shoulder… can you ride a coach with that injury?"

"I need to talk it over with Master Dushan first," Jace said. "As for the wound…"

He thought, I literally drank a vial of Sunwell water. It can't possibly fail at this, right?

"We'll see how it is tomorrow. We leave in the morning. Meet up at the Azure Encounter, and have your luggage packed ahead of time. Oh right, Grede — don't you still have that tavern job?"

"The dwarf running it couldn't keep it together anymore," Grede said. "Last night got too chaotic. He's worried something else'll blow up, so he paid out wages and's heading home early."

"He paid you in full, didn't he?"

"He'd better have," Grede muttered. "Those zombies weren't rioting on my orders. He owed what he owed."

With that, he stretched wide and groaned. "Oh, by the Light… it's finally happening. I never thought I'd see the world through two eyes again. It doesn't even feel real. That elf's something else, actually going out of her way like that!"

"So what exactly did you do last night?" Grede asked. "Did she save you, or did you save her?"

"We saved each other," Jace said under his breath.

Grede squinted at him from over there. Jace glanced back, touched his own lip and nose, and said, "What? Something on my face?"

The dwarf asked out of nowhere, "You haven't fallen for her, have you?"

"What are you reading into? I haven't felt a thing," Jace said.

Grede nodded in that meaningful way of his. "Lemme give you a piece of advice, lad. Those elven women put on this cold, polite, proper act — but that's just for show in human cities. Back in Quel'Thalas, they're wild, every last one of you. Watch yourself, don't get played."

"What would she con me for? I'm no lord or noble."

"Who told you elves care about that? Otherwise where d'you think all those half-elves came from, the ones orphaned and roaming the streets? You think a human noble can't afford one more mouth that eats nothing but biscuits?"

"Ah, that's what you mean," Jace said with a smile. "If she wants to have at it, she can say so outright. Who's playing who, that's the real question."

"You're already walking the same road as those idiots," Grede said. "Think you've got everything under control, just having a bit of fun — then before you know it you're neck-deep in the muck."

"Those idiots?" Jace said. "You mean you?"

"Shut it," Grede said. "That's not the point. Listen."

"Elves who come out into the world get bored with physical pleasures fast enough. There's only so many variations, and they stale quick. Mortal, beast — what's the difference, really? But emotions… that's a whole different beast. Every person, every race, a different environment, a different set of parents — it's a completely different experience. Deeper. Harder to chase. That's the thrill elves are really after. You think an elven woman's fallen for you, that she's got real feelings for you like a human would? No. They're just mining deeper into you, hunting for something stranger, something unfamiliar. One day she finds someone fresher, more interesting, and she'll latch onto them without a second thought — won't give you the time of day. Hell, she'd probably enjoy watching you duel her new lover. That'd thrill her even more."

Jace's eyes went wider and wider. He muttered, "What the hell have you been through? Were you running wild in the Hinterlands or what?"

"Shut up!" Grede pressed on. "Everyone jokes about bringing an elf home, blows hot air at each other — fine. But don't actually get sucked in, Jace! They live two thousand years. You get a few decades. D'you think they'll take you seriously? I don't wanna spend the rest of my days nursing your broken heart."

"This about me and that Windrunner?" Jace asked. "You think there's a chance of that?"

"It goes for every elven woman," Grede said. "I can tell you're already teetering on the edge, so I can't laugh it off anymore. Go back to Stormwind, or head to Lordaeron, find a human girl who actually loves and cares for you — one who only gets sixty years, same as you. You catch my meaning? Stop obsessing over exotic monsters. Tell me — d'you have some kind of weird fetish? Like you need a pair of long ears before you can feel a thing?"

"How would I know? I never grabbed a pair."

"Could always try a troll," Grede said with a dumb grin.

Jace looked over toward the elves again. Vereesa was still standing there, chatting with the elves beside her every now and then.

Thinking back on that conversation — Vereesa had done a lot for him and Grede, sure, but she'd only wanted to repay Jace's help. Maybe it was the difference in race, or something else, but that faint, uncomfortable sense of distance sat between them the whole time.

And really, now that he thought about it, what was so important about a vial of Sunwell water, anyway?

Maybe only a kid who'd grown up on the dirt roads of Lordaeron's capital would make a big deal of it. For someone like a Windrunner, a noble's youngest daughter, it probably wasn't anything special. But to say he hadn't fantasized at all — of course that wasn't true.

He'd even dreamed, from the moment he first came into this world, about picking one from the three Windrunner sisters — or Jaina, or Calia, or maybe the black dragon princess, or the Dragonqueen herself. Why not take the whole set while he was at it?

Of course, that fantasy hadn't lasted long before the cold water of reality, rushed at him the second he transmigrated, had doused it flat.

Grede watched Jace go quiet for a long stretch. "What're you thinking about? Don't tell me you're actually interested in a female troll."

"Might give it a shot if the chance comes up," Jace said.

"Ack… I was just talking. You're serious?"

"You only live once, dwarf. And when it comes to trolls, I've got the height advantage over you."
