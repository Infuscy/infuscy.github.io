## Chapter 1811: Hunting a Guardian?

The Violet Citadel. A light drizzle was falling outside.

Needleflame was still in the Arcane Vault, watching the Kirin Tor mages go through the procedures for opening the vault for inspection. The remaining five people, including Khadgar, gathered in the guest room.

Jace breathed in the faint Arcane aura of the room, listening to the rain pattering against the stone walls outside. It only reinforced his conviction — he knew exactly whose handiwork that rain in Caitlyn Runeweaver's dream had come from.

"Unbelievable," Khadgar said. "The day has come when I, too, am planning to hunt down Aegwynn herself, just like those mages of the Order of Tirisfal."

"At least she passed most of her power to her son, Medivh," Sora said. "Now that Medivh is dead, she's no longer as powerful as the human records claim."

At the mention of Medivh's name, Khadgar couldn't help but glance at the warlock sitting next to Vereesa.

"But if Jace's guesses are all correct," Vereesa said, "that means Aegwynn still has Medivh's Book with her..."

"So to face a mage like that... what should we prepare? Arcane protection potions?" Grede looked at the group. "I've fought orc warlocks, demon witches, and a few second-rate Kirin Tor mages, but I've never gone up against a Guardian of Tirisfal."

"There aren't many people in the world who have that experience, Grede." Vereesa said. "And I think the hard part isn't even how we'd beat her. It's that... we don't even have the right to choose the battlefield, or even whether to fight."

She looked at Jace. "If Aegwynn doesn't want to engage with us, she can simply leave. We have no way to stop her. The whole of Azeroth is like her own bedroom to a Guardian of Tirisfal. Even the blue dragons couldn't stop her from abusing the ley lines' power."

"I've been thinking about that too," Jace said. "But we don't need to defeat her. Forcing her to flee is a victory in itself. If she's been using Medivh's Book to channel a powerful teleportation spell, all we need to do is disrupt her casting. Make her abandon six months of work. That's as good as winning."

"Slow down. Hold on, everyone." Khadgar raised a hand. "I think we shouldn't jump to conclusions. Let's not decide she's the demon summoner and resort to the most extreme solution right away. I'm not defending her, and I'm not denying Jace's reasonable suspicions. But everyone has to admit — there's still a possibility... that this is all a huge misunderstanding."

"I know Aegwynn has hurt and even killed innocents before. Some Kirin Tor and Order of Tirisfal members too. But she's still a former Guardian. She spent centuries protecting this world, even if her methods and philosophy weren't widely accepted. Don't forget — when the Dark Portal opened, she tried to stop her son. Even though those attempts failed."

"What if she accidentally got possessed by a demon?" Grede said. "I'm not doubting Lady Aegwynn's power. But don't forget — her son, who inherited the Guardian's power, was also possessed by a demon."

Khadgar cleared his throat unconvincingly. Jace knew Grede had hit the mark.

Because people who weren't familiar with the details, like Grede, wouldn't know that Medivh hadn't been accidentally possessed in Karazhan. The Dark Titan's soul, which had possessed Aegwynn, had transferred from her body to the infant Medivh — far more vulnerable — when she gave birth.

Of course, even if the Nathrezim were the most adept possessors among demons, their power couldn't compare to the Dark Titan himself. This situation was different from that one. But still — Khadgar probably knew the details better than anyone, so he understood that Aegwynn did have a "track record."

"Do you already have an idea for how to meet Aegwynn?" Jace asked.

"I do." Khadgar said. "Whatever she's thinking now, whatever she's doing, at least one thing is certain: she can't kill you, Jace Sesso."

"At least we know that no matter how much she hates that staff in your hand, she's seen you, she knows where you are, and she hasn't chosen to make a move against you. If your guesses are right, it means she doesn't have the confidence to do a better job than her teacher, Guardian Scavell, at cleaning up the aftermath after killing Ursales' owner. After all, strictly speaking, she no longer has the full Guardian's power."

"Based on Master Scavell's experience, the fate of the last Ursales owner... doing that only makes Ursales stronger. Harder to control."

"Yes," Jace said.

"So I think it's best if just the two of us go see her." Khadgar said. "I'll take you."

"You're even crazier than Aegwynn, Khadgar!" Grede said, leaping to his feet.

"Lady Aegwynn told me that anyone who finds her hiding place, she'll kill without mercy — and then pin the blame on me." The mage took a deep breath, steadying himself, and looked at the dwarf. "So I have to take responsibility. Right now, I'm the only person alive on this continent who knows where she is. I have to be part of this meeting. And Jace... is probably the person she least wants to kill in the entire world, from any angle."

"But everyone else is a different story, High Thane Thunderfist. That includes you. If we all go to that place, she'll sense us easily and get on guard immediately. If things turn out not to be what we suspect, whether she chooses to flee or fight back, the consequences would be dire. I know you've successfully hunted down plenty of opponents before. But this time is different. All of you."

"Yes. Even Queen Ysera's pendant can't fool her. I really can't think of a way to get close without her noticing." Vereesa shook her head.

"So we'll treat this as an ordinary visit. We have no ill associations with any of the strange phenomena in Karazhan." Khadgar said. "The rest of you can wait at the foot of the mountain. Or hide, ready to support as needed..."

"Foot of the mountain?" Grede squinted.

"There are many, many mountains in this world." Khadgar said. "I won't reveal her exact location until we've settled on a proper plan."

"Go on, then." The dwarf gestured with his palm.

"That mountain isn't a strong enough ley line node, and it's not that far from the Kirin Tor's monitoring posts." Khadgar said. "I believe that if someone wanted to summon a demon lord, that wouldn't be a safe, concealed, or efficient place to perform the ritual."

"In other words, if Lady Aegwynn is already conducting a summoning ritual, she would have left that place. If that's the case, the two of us won't face any danger, and it would mean everything is exactly as Jace suspects — that she has indeed..."

"Fallen," Sora said quietly.

Khadgar sighed softly. "If she's still there, then there must be some misunderstanding, and our visit won't seem quite so... aggressive."

"We could say Medivh's Book has been stolen and the world is facing disaster again, and ask for her help," Jace added. "After all, if the summoner really isn't her, then we genuinely don't have many other options."

"That's a good idea." Khadgar said. "But I'm not sure if she'd actually help again."

"Not cooperating with Kil'jaeden would already be the biggest help she could give us," Vereesa said.

"I don't like you saying that, Ranger-General." Khadgar leaned back in his chair. "But it's true."

Grede paced back and forth, gripping his chair, as if he still wasn't satisfied with this plan. But after thinking it over, he couldn't come up with anything better.

"So." He looked at the mage. "Can you tell us now where she actually lives?"

"The source of several tributaries of the Arias River in the northern Tirisfal Mountains. An unnamed peak." Khadgar said. "She's there."

"You mean the top of the mountain behind the town of Deathknell?" Jace asked. "She's hiding within the borders of Lordaeron?"

"When we came back from Northrend, we flew right over that place." Vereesa said. "I never would have imagined Lady Aegwynn living there."

"Maybe that's one of the reasons she chose it," Khadgar said.

"Then there's no time to waste." Jace glanced at the Dalaran cityscape beyond the window. "Let's go as soon as possible."
