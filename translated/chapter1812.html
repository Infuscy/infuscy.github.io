Chapter 1812: Leave It to the Professionals

Deathknell sat in a valley southeast of the Tirisfal Mountains, at a fairly high altitude, surrounded by dense black pine forests. Even though autumn had barely begun and pumpkin harvest season hadn't even started, it was already as cold here as winter in the Kingdom of Stormwind.

The atmosphere was grim and silent. The town itself wasn't small — dozens of houses of varying heights lined the long street along the mountainside, and there was an abbey that rivaled the chapel in Lakeshire in size. But the streets, both inside and outside the town, seemed nearly deserted.

Despite having lived in Lordaeron as a citizen for fifteen years, Jace had never once visited this town he was intimately familiar with from his past life. Partly because it really was out of the way — far from the capital city, deep in the embrace of rolling hills and dense forests. But more importantly, because of its name: Deathknell.

Like the Sepulcher in Silverpine Forest, this was a town famous for its funerary trade. Probably because this place was closer than any other town in the kingdom to the ancient sacred site of Tirisfal — the legendary Tyr's Fall.

Maybe that was also why the Dark Lady Sylvanas would later choose this place as the Forsaken's main "recruiting ground." With cemeteries everywhere and conspicuous tomb chambers carved into the mountainside... if every corpse here were raised, they could probably field an army large enough to fight Lordaeron's military to a standstill.

They passed through the town and continued up the mountain. The road grew worse the further they went, eventually becoming patchy and broken. Hard to tell whether this road had been built by Lordaeron, or by the native Tirisfal tribes who lived here before the capital was founded, or... by the Quel'dorei who had followed Dath'Remar Sunstrider himself and settled here even earlier.

The deepest part of these mountains held a forest that almost no one entered except the most zealous worshippers of Tyr and scholars of ancient giant languages. It was called the Whispering Forest. That name hadn't been given by humans — it came from the Highborne refugees who had crossed the sea seven thousand years ago. They had abandoned this place and continued east to the territory of the Amani Empire, which later became Quel'Thalas, partly because they couldn't bear the "whispers" whose source they could never trace.

Jace knew those whispers were actually the work of a powerful servant of the Old Gods — "the Corruptor" Zakajz. Long before the Sundering, Tyr had killed him here, burying his body deep beneath the earth, where it continued to corrupt this land and forest.

Did Aegwynn choose to hide here because of this history? He hoped not.

But according to the map, the source of the Arias River's tributaries — the high peak before them — was even farther from the Whispering Forest than it was from Brill. Deathknell's proximity was only relative. To actually set foot in the so-called "Tyr's Fall" from here would still require a solid, arduous trek across rivers, valleys, and cliffs — nothing like the wagon-friendly road between Deathknell and Brill.

The Tirisfal Mountains were far larger and more complex than the game or even Jace's imagination had suggested.

"Wait here." Khadgar turned back to look at Grede, Vereesa, and Sola. "If things go smoothly, we'll be back in a few hours."

"And if they don't?" Sola asked. "How will we know you two need help?"

"I reckon there'll be a terrifying Arcane flash or a shadow explosion up on that mountain," Grede said. "Just hold out till she flies us up, lads."

Khadgar shot the dwarf an annoyed look. "Don't worry. It probably won't... end that fast."

"Stay safe, Jace," Vereesa said.

"I know, Vereesa."

The three figures behind them grew smaller and smaller, eventually fading into the thin morning mist.

Their footsteps sounded painfully loud now, whether they were stepping softly into the dark brown pine needles or clacking against the stone path.

"There's something I've been wanting to ask you." Jace followed behind Khadgar. "Why does Aegwynn agree to see you? Why doesn't she just kill you as another agent of the Kirin Tor or the Order of Tirisfal?"

"Probably because she knows I'm different from the rest of the Kirin Tor." Khadgar walked and spoke quietly. "Or maybe — why did you trust me enough to tell me about Ulthalesh? Could be the same reason."

"So if she does decide to kill me, how will you guarantee she won't kill you too?" Jace patted Ulthalesh in his hand. "I'm the evil warlock coming to visit her with this staff. You're the one who betrayed her trust by leaking her hiding spot to an outsider. And you're not me — you're not Ulthalesh's owner. Killing you wouldn't have any messy consequences."

"Ha. You think I'm so naive I came completely unprepared?" Khadgar pulled a spell scroll made from some unknown creature's hide from his pocket. "Greater Rapid Transposition scroll. It'll get me out faster than I can unroll it, Jace Sesso. If things really spiral beyond our control... I'll go back to Dalaran for reinforcements. If I'm lucky enough that you're close by, maybe it'll take you out too."

"Will you unroll it faster than Lady Aegwynn can cast Counterspell?" Jace asked dryly.

"When you swung Ulthalesh at the Scepter of Sargeras, were you really sure you could cut through it?" Khadgar asked.

"No," Jace said.

"When I first entered Dalaran more than twenty years ago, I thought everything in the world ran as precisely and perfectly as Arcane magic." The mage took a deep breath. "But the deeper I walked through that door, the further I went, the more I realized that wasn't true — especially after I entered Karazhan. Sometimes even Arcane itself... can be seen in its mad and violent aspect. Can you imagine?"

"So you prepare as best you can and leave the rest to the Holy Light, fate, or whatever you choose to believe in. Providence, I think I've heard you mention it before."

"Don't make gambling sound so noble," Jace said.

"Then let's gamble that Aegwynn's Counterspell isn't faster than my scroll unrolling." Khadgar shot back immediately.

"And if you lose?" Jace asked.

"If I lose, do I get to live?" Khadgar countered.

"Maybe we could hold out long enough for reinforcements to arrive and force her to retreat." Jace said quietly.

"So what's your proposal?" Khadgar asked.

"Teach me Arcane magic," Jace said. "Take me to Karazhan. Teach me."

"Forget it." Khadgar waved a hand. "I'd rather teach your child with Vereesa Windrunner. That little one hasn't even been born yet, and I guarantee they'd be sharper than you."

"Whether you teach our child or not doesn't affect you teaching me," Jace said.

"That might take an unimaginable amount of time, Sesso." Khadgar's tone was mockingly sarcastic.

"I've got plenty of time," Jace said.

"Alright. That's true." Khadgar said. "But if I win..."

He stopped walking, hesitated for a moment, then turned around.

"Use that endless time of yours to find a way to destroy that staff, Jace."

Are you getting too cocky, mage?

Even with this staff in hand, the Kirin Tor is dead set on throwing me into that eternal prison and taking it from me.

If I ever abandon this power, how would I stand against the arrogance and conceit of you mortals?

Don't even entertain the thought... of that possibility. Do you think you're stronger than Scavell? That you have more means?

Jace breathed in the cold, pine-scented air, clearing the murky voices and chaotic thoughts from his mind.

"I will."

"Then it's settled." Khadgar said.

"Ah, damn it all." He looked up at the mountaintop. "I'm actually starting to hope Lady Aegwynn will unreasonably try to kill us now. That wager is certainly tempting."

The sun hadn't yet reached its zenith, and the morning mist hadn't fully cleared when the two of them neared the summit. At this altitude, there was no trace of a built road anymore — just gaps where beasts or occasional rangers and explorers had passed through, or places where plants had simply failed to grow, leaving "passages" to follow.

Khadgar walked confidently ahead without a moment's hesitation, as if he were completely familiar with this place. Jace kept glancing back. After weaving through several clusters of trees and crossing a few streams, he'd already lost track of the way they'd come.

Eventually, the two of them arrived at a lonely wooden cabin beside a small mountain spring pool.

No magical barrier for concealment. No Arcane ward traps blocking intruders. No lingering traces of a soul ritual for resurrecting someone important. It was as simple and crude as a hunter's overnight shelter you'd occasionally find in the wilderness.

The door was half-open, unlocked. Khadgar stepped inside. The cabin's "decorations" could even be called cozy — a rug, carved wooden chairs, a small bed with decorative runes, thriving potted hanging plants...

Looking at all of this, the mage's face was as stiff as a corpse's.

"She's really not here. You guessed right, Jace Sesso."

"Empathy, Khadgar." Jace said. "Stay calm."

"Yes, empathy. Yes." Khadgar wiped his face vigorously and chanted a spell, then immediately sighed. "No traces at all. I'm not surprised. Why would she leave..."

Jace looked around the cabin. "Do you feel it?"

"What?" Khadgar asked.

"The demonic presence." Jace pushed open the door and stepped outside.

"Is it a Nathrezim?" Khadgar followed, scanning the surrounding forest as he did.

"I can't say for sure. But I know who we should ask." Jace said.

"Who?" the mage asked.

Jace drew Azuresong and gripped the blade with his left hand. "Help me draw a summoning circle."
