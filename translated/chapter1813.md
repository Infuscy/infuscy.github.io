## Chapter 1813: Demon or Demon Hunter

"What a lovely cabin. Master, have you finally settled all your worldly entanglements... and decided to spend the rest of our lives together in this quiet, secluded place?"

The demon stepped into the cabin like a tall model walking a runway, her cloven hooves clicking against the floor. She lifted her head, her fiery red eyes sweeping the room before suddenly turning to look at the archmage.

"But this one is rather in the way."

Khadgar opened his mouth in annoyance, but Jace spoke first. "How are my parents?"

"They were fine a minute ago." Morophil twirled her finger. "But I couldn't tell you about now."

Jace sighed. "If you can't help us find clues, I'll have this mage send you straight back to Stormwind. Trust me, he has enough mana for it."

"Have I been reduced to a little demon servant who comes when called and leaves when dismissed?" Morophil tilted her head in disappointment. "The Kirin Tor have been keeping a secret watch over that house the past few days. Even I can't get too close. No need to worry..."

"If a Nathrezim plans to target the people close to me again, those mages will be outsmarted no matter what," Jace said.

"Fine, fine. It seems even Lady Morophil has to keep proving her worth, over and over."

Morophil placed her crimson finger on the wooden table beside her. Her expression shifted slightly.

"You can feel the demonic presence in this cabin too," Jace said.

"Indeed. Though it's very faint." Morophil squinted and stepped outside. "But once you leave the cabin, you can't sense much anymore. Except for the thick, fragrant scent of your blood, darling."

*Ahem.* Khadgar coughed into his hand. "Can you tell what kind of demon left these traces, Lady Morophil?"

"Not yet." The Sayaad shook her head. "Follow me, you two. The residual clues... are fading by the moment."

Jace and Khadgar nodded and immediately followed the succubus into the northern forest. After a while, even the patchy stone path had disappeared, let alone any trace of fel energy or any magical power at all.

The thick layer of rotting vegetation beneath dappled shadows, the occasional warning calls of wild animals echoing from a distance — this place felt less and less like a secret Guardian hideout or a terrifying demon-corrupted site by the minute.

"Are you sure?" Khadgar panted as he ran. "I'm not questioning the ability of a banshee hunter..."

"Then convince my master to give me more mana, mage." Morophil glanced back with a smile.

"Don't think I can't tell," Jace said. "You've done nothing for months but loaf around Stormwind. What have you been consuming?"

"Simply existing... is a great consumption, darling. Especially for someone like me..."

She stopped mid-sentence. The Sayaad suddenly fell silent, as if something had seized her by the throat. She fixed her gaze on the distance.

"I sense more than one powerful demon. There's a Nathrezim. And... an old face."

"Old face?"

Before Jace could finish the question, Morophil spread her wings and leaped forward. The two mortals behind her had neither wings nor hooves. The distance between them stretched wide in an instant. They scrambled to keep up, clumsy as a pair of bears rolling through the underbrush.

The three of them crashed through a stand of trees and leaped down a rocky ledge. The Sayaad stopped in front a stone wall overgrown with shrubs, scanning it back and forth.

"Here. Strange. An Arcane veil, or... perhaps you should summon another Felhound, Master. Try to uncover what's hidden here..."

"No need." Khadgar caught up, speaking quietly. "I think... I might know what this is about. A Felhound wouldn't be able to find the target anyway."

The archmage closed his eyes, steadying himself as he raised one arm and chanted a spell. A faint breeze stirred through the rocky cliffs as the magic resonated. But when he finished, nothing around them had changed.

Khadgar cleared his throat. "Give me a moment. Let me try another."

"Another? How many are there?" Jace asked.

The mage didn't answer. Instead, he crushed a magic crystal and chanted again with a similar gesture. This time, the ground suddenly trembled beneath them. All three of them turned to look at the stone cliff face before them.

But the stronger tremor came from the slope a dozen yards behind them.

They turned and followed the vibration. Arcane starlight spread across the ground before them, revealing freshly turned soil. The ground shook more violently, collapsing in heaps until a half-open stone door was exposed. Then everything fell silent.

The stench of demon wafted out from within the stone door.

"We should warn Vereesa and the others," Jace said.

Khadgar nodded and fired an Arcane Missile into the sky. The projectile was barely visible against the sun beyond the treetops, but there was no way it would escape the notice of an elven ranger like Vereesa.

After firing the signal, the mage furrowed his brow and waved his hand. "Let's go in."

"Do you know this place?" Jace asked as he descended the steps behind him.

"No." Khadgar lit a mage light, illuminating the dirt-filled passageway.

It was clear that this had once been a corridor with Dalaran-style architecture, but all the carvings had long since faded and fallen off.

"The Order of Tirisfal left too many secret chambers and hiding spots across this kingdom over the past two thousand years. I only know how to open a few of them. The Kirin Tor has to find them and recover the artifacts left behind."

*Yeah, right,* Jace thought. *Then why doesn't the Kirin Tor go recover all those artifacts from the abandoned council members' houses in Dalaran that nobody dares to enter?*

But he couldn't be bothered to dig into Khadgar's ambiguous connections with the Order, Aegwynn, and Medivh anymore. Just like Khadgar tactfully didn't pry into the source of Jace's "divination magic."

"Watch out for lurking Nathrezim, Master." Morophil raised her short blade, which glowed faintly with fiery light — the Duskblade. "Though generally speaking, these self-important deceivers don't expose their presence so readily. But since one has, he won't leave this crypt alive today."

The smell of Arcane metal grew stronger as they walked. Finally, the three of them entered a wide, dark chamber. Broken walls everywhere, collapsed stone pillars. Khadgar tossed the mage light in an arc around the room, and a pile of shattered demon armor in the corner immediately caught everyone's attention.

"Well. An ordinary Nathrezim wouldn't expose themselves so recklessly." Morophil looked at a fel-corrupted sword twisted into a spiral shape on the ground. "Someone got here before us."

The demon's large armor was pure gold, inlaid with ice-blue magical gems that had all burst into fragments. The Sayaad picked up a piece of pauldron. "This is the one who created the plague in Blackrock Spire, Master. I'll never forget the shadow power he left behind. Such a pity."

"Who killed him?" Jace asked.

"Undoubtedly an Arcane mage with incredibly precise and meticulous spellcasting." Khadgar touched the scorch marks on the nearby wall. "Who else could it be?"

"Someone who could simply and cleanly intercept and kill a powerful Nathrezim... they're certainly no weaker than the two of us, Jace. Remember, at the Cathedral of Light, if the Holy Light hadn't helped, we might not have stopped that other dreadlord from escaping."

"I agree." Jace looked at the branching passage leading deeper. "But there's another demonic presence here."

None of them relaxed just because they'd found a dead Nathrezim lord. They proceeded deeper with even more caution. The further they went, the more they could see faint magical light flickering from the end of the path ahead.

They rounded a corner and entered another chamber. Jace immediately spotted a feminine figure sitting on a stone ritual altar in the corner.

Despite spending the entire journey urging Jace not to jump to the conclusion that Aegwynn had allied with the Burning Legion, Khadgar was the first to raise his crystal staff, his other hand fumbling toward the scrolls tucked into his robe. He looked even more nervous than Jace and Morophil, the dark master and servant.

"Lady... Lady Aegwynn?" Khadgar called softly.

The figure lifted her head. The moment those eyes, flickering with an unnatural magical light, met theirs, Jace felt something was deeply wrong.

"Careful, Khadgar. It's a Nathrezim soul... She might be possessed by a dreadlord's soul."

"What—" Khadgar's eyes went wide.

"No, Master." Morophil stared at the shadow. "She's devoured the Nathrezim's soul. Using it as fuel to sustain her existence... She's not a physical body. She's a shade."

"Like the ones on the observatory platform," Khadgar said. "The shades."

"Khadgar. Sesso. Morophilos." The figure murmured, her faint voice seeming to echo from somewhere beyond the room. "You finally found this place... but you're still too late."
