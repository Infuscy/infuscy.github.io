## Chapter 1814: What Is a Lie, What Is Truth

"You know me, Lady Aegwynn?" Morophil asked.

"Wrong question, demon!" The shadow's tone was almost a scolding. "Every thought, every recollection drains a great deal of my spirit. The farther back, the more so. I am already very tired... Do not waste energy and time on irrelevant topics."

"Ursales must be destroyed." Her voice was cold, and the hall echoed with the name "Ursales," "Ursales," like the call of a restless spirit.

"You have done something irreparable, Sesso. What gave you the confidence to think you could control Sargeras' legacy? The Deceiver appeared to me in his form. Utterly foolish. But the things he told me forced me to reconsider. Why could you destroy the Scepter of Sargeras? Why... Until the day I met you."

"I don't know Ursales. I never saw the secret my teacher Scavell kept locked deepest in his mind. But before he died, I saw his pain. I saw the curse he bore..."

"Wait." Khadgar said. "Lady Aegwynn, are you saying that Master Scavell was also once... the owner of Ursales?"

"He spent his entire life running from that reality." The shadow's voice was faint, ethereal. Those eyes, like shadowfire, fixed unwaveringly on the staff in Jace's hand.

"He denied that he had been captured by this demonic artifact, as though he still had a chance to break free from the web it had woven around his soul. But everything he did proved he knew the truth all along. My teacher sealed it, but he could not sever the connection. In the end, he chose to select an heir and pass on the Guardian's power early, to prevent everything of the Order of Tirisfal from being slowly devoured by this thing... He died soon after. But at least he left nothing to it."

"So it's true. Is that why you planned to summon Archimonde?" Khadgar stepped forward, gritting his teeth. "To solve Scavell's problem? To satisfy... your long-held wish? You're going to summon the Defiler? Gamble the entire world?! Even the maddest mages in the Order of Tirisfal over two thousand years never thought to solve their problems with the Finger of Death."

"Because... none of them could master that spell, Khadgar. Not me. Not even he... not even Scavell. Not Scavell's teacher, nor any of the former Guardians. Not even Master Alodi himself!" The shadow's voice trembled. "The Defiler Archimonde's ultimate curse can erase every connection between life and death. From soul to body. From one plane to another. Even the Eredar — even the Deceiver Kil'jaeden himself, his teacher..."

"No one knows where he learned this spell. No one knows how he mastered it to such a degree."

"I know," Khadgar said. "Lady Aegwynn, you don't need to lecture me on this now!"

"But — but isn't this the only way? The books piled up in Karazhan are taller than the Violet Hold. The knowledge they contain is barely a drop in the ocean compared to the secrets of the Twisting Nether. We could find another way eventually. Ursales was created, so it can be destroyed. Destruction is always easier than creation, isn't it, Lady Aegwynn?"

"But the one who created it was the Dark Titan himself, young Khadgar..."

"At least you should have come to us to discuss it before making a decision!" Khadgar interrupted, his voice shaking with emotion. "At least—"

"That decision is right here." The shadow's voice dropped low. "In this very room. Talking to you."

"She... abandoned you?" Khadgar asked.

"She cast me off." The shadow said. "At first, she tried to destroy me completely. But her power was chaotic and frenzied. I nearly faded into nothing. But I survived. I escaped her perception, lingering on with the residual magic of this secret vault, until the demon Mephistroth came here to annihilate me completely. I know she can still sense that I am alive. She doesn't want me left behind... to interfere with her plan."

"But." She let out a couple of dry laughs. "Even this broken, withered shade? Mephistroth — even that cunning lord — could not withstand my power. It was almost an insult... to herself."

"Where did she go?" Jace asked.

"How could she possibly tell me, a 'traitor,' that, Sesso?" The shadow retorted.

"Then... if it were you, where would you go, Lady Aegwynn?" Jace pressed. "Of all the powerful ley line nodes, which one would you choose for the ritual?"

"No ley line node can escape the watch of the Nexus, Dalaran, or the Magister's Platform." Aegwynn's voice was barely a whisper. "She has her own power. And... the Eye of Sargeras."

The Eye of Sargeras. A fel gemstone of immeasurable power. Aegwynn had hidden it in the Sargeras Vault alongside the Dark Titan's avatar. Some believed it was a powerful artifact the avatar wore. Others thought it was literally the avatar's eye. Gul'dan and the orcs who had followed in his footsteps had tried to find it but never succeeded.

Years later in another timeline, Illidan Stormrage, guided by the naga, would enter that ancient temple Gul'dan had raised from the depths, find this artifact, and use it to gather the power of the ley line nodes above the ruins of Dalaran, shattering the barrier around the Frozen Throne on another continent in an attempt to kill the Lich King Ner'zhul directly. But the night elves hunting him would interfere, and the Lich King would survive.

Khadgar and Jace exchanged a glance. Both of them suddenly understood the source of that strange but brief ley line surge.

"The Defiler would never accept the bonds of Ursales. She has decided this is the only way to erase this demonic relic from the world..." The shadow's words grew increasingly unsteady, her form flickering. "I tried to warn you. I tried to warn you both. But every attempt, every choice, was twisted, erased, sealed away by her. She won. She achieved her final victory. She took Aegwynn. She... took me!"

"What happened, Lady Aegwynn?" Khadgar pressed. "How did it come to this? These apparitions, these chaotic 'emotions,' 'decisions'..."

"Because..." The shadow's voice suddenly went calm. "Because of that divination ritual. I used the Observatory, Khadgar. And all the energy of Karazhan. I tried to glimpse the future as far as I could... Because I didn't want to believe the Deceiver's lies. I wanted to believe in your friend. I believed in you, Sesso. I believed you had the strength to guard this artifact. And that my era — the Guardian's legacy, the Order of Tirisfal — should all come to an end."

"But... but you have disappointed me, Sesso. Seeing you again now, I realize that perhaps SHE was right. Perhaps the survivor — the 'decision' that emerged victorious from that bloody arena — was the right one."

"Since we last met, not much time has passed. But you have grown more and more like Scavell. The Eredar's lies are becoming reality, one grain at a time. You will pretend you are not yet affected by it. But is that its voice, or your own thoughts? You know the answer very well. You know you have never heard that demon speak. Everything is your own heart. Your own desires. But you will keep deceiving yourself. Denying it all. Pretending you are still struggling, that you still have the right to refuse. Like my teacher. Like him... just like him. But the truth will pierce through every pretense. No one can escape..."

Khadgar stared at Jace, bewildered. "What voice, Jace? What voice are you hearing? What thoughts? What is going on?"

"What did you see?" Jace didn't answer the mage. Instead, he looked at the shadow. "What did you see in that divination, Lady Aegwynn?"

The dark hall fell completely silent. Morophil stared at the shadow, unmoving. Khadgar turned his head stiffly.

"I saw... black scorched earth. An ice-blue sky. A lonely shadow. Ursales and its fallen master. They had already won everything. They had devoured the last soul on Azeroth... and the first soul."

The mage was stunned, as if he couldn't accept this at all.

"Medivh's madness was still affecting that tower then, Lady Aegwynn... Whatever you are." Khadgar's voice was firm. "That divination ritual was very likely completely twisted by Karazhan! Look at the power you left here. Look at this filthy fel energy. This demonic presence. Isn't that proof enough?"

The shadow smiled, like a kindly mother watching her child babble nonsense.

"You think I will destroy the world one day," Jace said.

"No. The person in the prophecy is not you, Sesso." The shadow said. "Ursales was in MY hands... The person in the prophecy was me."

Her murmuring grew increasingly shrill, impossible to tell whether it was still a human voice or the strange shrieking of fel-shadow energy intertwining. The shade burst into violet-red flames and consumed itself entirely, leaving only a wisp of spinning, dissipating ash.
