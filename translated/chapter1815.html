Chapter 1815: Breaking the Snowball

Among the falling ashes, Khadgar immediately stepped forward, guiding Arcane energy to gather whatever residual traces he could find. Before long, footsteps approached from outside. Grede, Vereesa, and Sora had followed.

"There's a dead Nathrezim outside..." Vereesa began, then she spotted Morophil. She blinked. "What happened here?"

"That dead Nathrezim had nothing to do with me, Lady Vereesa Windrunner." Morophil smiled. "But we did find another 'shade' of your maddened Guardian. That one was my doing."

"Former Guardian," Khadgar said, looking up at Morophil. "Show some respect, demon."

"She was willing to cooperate with the Burning Legion to summon the Defiler, all to destroy Ursales." Morophil's voice was cold. "I see no reason to respect her. Or should I worship her power? In that case, I would have joined the Burning Legion and pledged myself to the Grand Lord long ago."

"That's irrelevant now." Khadgar shot her a glance. "What matters now is finding any residual clues in this vault that can lead us to her real location."

"Would anyone like to explain to me what this 'shade' business is all about?" Grede spread his hands. "So it's the worst-case scenario? The former Guardian really plans to summon this 'Defiler'?"

"It was my misjudgment." Khadgar's voice was low. "Back in Karazhan, I thought Lady Aegwynn had never seen Ursales, so she couldn't confirm that Jace actually had it. I thought there was no need to worry."

"I just didn't expect — she had never seen Ursales. But she had seen Scavell. Her teacher. And Guardian Scavell, after defeating that necromancer, spent the rest of his life struggling against this staff's curse, until he passed on the Guardian's power. So she understood Ursales' curse better than any of us. But Scavell never mentioned it in his records... I can understand that. He wouldn't want to reveal that this thing still existed. Otherwise, countless powerful mages would have hunted for it."

"So that means, the day she met Jace... Lady Aegwynn already knew everything?" Vereesa asked.

"She knew I had lied to her," Jace said.

"It was natural for you to keep it secret, Jace. She must have understood that — her own teacher did the same thing, didn't he?" Khadgar said. "That's why she didn't confront you on the spot. Instead, she returned to Karazhan, used all the void power in that tower, and performed that divination ritual. She wanted to see... the consequences. And it drove both her and the tower into chaos. But at first... she must have wanted to believe in you."

But now?

Jace Sesso. The higher you climb in this world, the more suspicion you'll attract. More envy. More hostility. Endless.

Because this world only knows how to run away. Run away...

Who is the real enemy? The Guardian? The Deceiver? The Defiler? They are all just pawns.

Sargeras.

The source of everything. Only by ending him — only by cutting him down on that cruel throne — will you ever escape these curses. This suspicion. This envy. This hostility.

You know this better than any of the mediocrities crawling across this plane.

Grip what's in your hand tightly. Release its power. It will lead you, step by step, toward that goal.

Hesitation... will only leave you with a prince's pathetic, stupid blade through your chest.

Mercy... will only make you miss the moment of pure, ultimate victory at the most critical juncture.

The Holy Light will not descend from the invisible heights every time to fill in that last step for you.

How deep into the darkness must you go before you realize the light has long since abandoned you? How far down the desolate road must you walk before you understand that you are already completely alone?

"What did she see in that prophecy?" Vereesa asked.

Jace looked up. Khadgar answered beside him. "She saw herself wielding Ursales... killing everyone. Down to the last soul. And the first soul."

"The World-Soul," Morophil said in demonic tongue.

"What does that mean? She wants to take the staff?" Grede asked.

"Take it?" Khadgar said. "You don't 'take' Ursales, High Thane. You just need to eliminate the current owner. The staff absorbs the predecessor's power, and then it's your turn to fall into this endlessly cycling curse. Become its prisoner. It keeps snowballing to a point no one can imagine. According to Burning Legion legends, there's only one being in the Twisting Nether who is exempt from this — the Dark Titan who created it. I suspect that's why Ursales has always wanted to devour the Dark Titan's soul..."

"So there's no difference at all," Grede said. "Then what she saw in that prophecy means that in some incomprehensible future, she killed Jace Sesso. Whatever the reason."

"Not necessarily." Vereesa looked at the dwarf. "Maybe it was many years later. Someone else defeated Jace for some other reason and took the staff. Then Lady Aegwynn found a way to kill that person, but couldn't escape the curse either... But eventually, it all leads to the same result in that prophecy. Maybe."

"Then why not let Jace keep protecting the staff?" Sora stepped forward. "If no one can escape the cycle, then choose the most trustworthy person to guard it. Jace has proven over these years that he's a worthy keeper. This ancient staff has never hurt an innocent in his hands. It has never destroyed anything—"

"On the contrary — the villains, the corrupted artifacts, Prince Nefarian, the Scepter of Sargeras — all have been destroyed by him! That proves he can control its power. That he can suppress this so-called curse."

"Is Lady Aegwynn completely unaware of this?" She continued. "And just because she couldn't do something, does that mean no one else can? She doesn't believe she can resist Ursales' curse, so she thinks Jace can't either?"

"But what if she has a way now?" Khadgar looked back at the green dragon. "What if she now knows a method to break out of this cycle for good? What if she's finally found an opportunity, an ultimate solution that her teacher — the former Guardian — couldn't find for over a hundred years? What if she knows how to prevent that prophecy from coming true?"

"She doesn't know! She hasn't found anything! She just believes she has!" Sora's voice was furious. "Arrogant mortals never understand what centuries-long consequences a single reckless decision can bring!"

Khadgar's face was red. He stared at Sora, opening his mouth several times as if to speak, but in the end, he could only hurl the magic crystal in his hand to the ground.

"AH! DAMN IT ALL!"

The mage said quietly, "You're right, Lady Sora. I must be corrupted by the demonic energy here too. I was starting to make excuses for her mad decision. Whoever plans to summon the Burning Legion... whoever it is... must be stopped. Even if it's the Guardian herself..."

Jace looked at the people before him. Distress. Anger. Resentment. Confusion. Chaotic emotions mingled with the remaining madness of Aegwynn's shade.

He couldn't tell whether the laughter he heard in this moment was a cold laugh, a mocking laugh, a bitter, disappointed laugh... or if it was actually himself.

"What... do you think, Jace?" Grede ventured.

"We need to inform the Kirin Tor and the Magister's Platform as soon as possible," Jace said. "Tell them the summoner has already acquired enough power to open the Legion's portal. They might be preparing the ritual now. Monitoring ley line nodes alone is no longer enough."

"Right. There's no point arguing about this now. I don't know what will become of this staff in the future. But Archimonde is really coming." Khadgar looked around the empty chamber, pinching the bridge of his nose. "The Eye of Sargeras... Maybe she knows some secret summoning method. Maybe she's preparing a huge surprise for everyone. But I can't think of a way to conceal a ritual of this scale. Medivh's Book... We should notify the Kirin Tor. Maybe Master Antonidas or Master Krasus will know something."

"I think we should notify everyone." Vereesa looked at Jace. "If we can't find her location and the Burning Legion's plan succeeds, that means... the entire Alliance must unite again. Everyone must be prepared."

"Yes. I agree," Khadgar said.
