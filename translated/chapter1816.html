Chapter 1816: Who's to Blame?

Medivh's Book had been stolen. That was the first news Jace's group heard when they returned to Dalaran. Needleflame met them before any mage could come to inform them, but no one was surprised.

Even Khadgar admitted he'd never imagined he'd one day hear the news that Medivh's Book had been stolen and feel completely unfazed.

Khadgar didn't waste any time. The moment they landed at Krasus Square, he headed straight for the Violet Citadel to discuss the situation with the Kirin Tor council. Aegwynn intended to summon the Burning Legion, and she'd already obtained the Eye of Sargeras — something even Gul'dan had failed to find. Combined with the portion of Guardian power she still retained, that would be more than enough to activate Medivh's Book and open a second Dark Portal.

Before long, Krasus departed for Northrend, carrying the Council of Six's orders to recall the Kirin Tor expeditionary forces from Northrend back to Dalaran for support. Whether they were facing Aegwynn or the Burning Legion demons that might arrive at any moment, the mages currently in Dalaran alone were not enough to guarantee anything.

Additionally, he sent a red dragon envoy back to Grim Batol to inform Queen Alexstrasza. The first Dark Portal, opened by the last Guardian, had brought the orcish Horde that devastated this continent and cost even the red dragons heavy losses. No one could be certain what even more terrifying thing this second portal might bring.

Vereesa had already rushed to the Quel'Thalas embassy to inform Prince Kael'thas and the Magisters' envoy Rommath. The news would soon be relayed by the Magisters back to Quel'Thalas, all the way to the Sun King's ears. During the First War of the Dark Portal, the elves who had responded passively had suffered terribly at the hands of Gul'dan and Orgrim's red dragon riders. Whether they would learn their lesson this time — abandon their thousand-year prejudice against the Kirin Tor and other outside "mortals," and actively search for Aegwynn's whereabouts while preparing for the coming storm — remained to be seen.

Sora was asleep in a guest room in the Violet Citadel. She wasn't simply resting; she was communicating with Queen Ysera and the night elf druids through the Emerald Dream. She hadn't woken yet, but Jace felt that faced with a potential third demon invasion, the night elves and the green dragons who had awakened from the Nightmare would not turn a blind eye. Especially since High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind had already given her word — if demonic invasion truly came again, the Kaldorei would not stand idly by.

But could they really be counted on? Even if the druids could descend through dream portals from the green dragons into the Hinterlands, most of the Kaldorei army would need to sail from the Black Coast to the Eastern Kingdoms. That could take a month or more...

Jace looked at Ursales, wrapped in freshly purchased mageweave cloth. It was as silent as a long-dead withered branch.

Why would the Guardian Aegwynn plan to summon the Burning Legion's demons?

The Kirin Tor hadn't bothered to spend significant effort investigating this. Khadgar said the Arcane embers connecting Caitlyn Runeweaver's dream to the strange rain in Stormwind, combined with the divination ritual in Karazhan that had further spread the darkness over Elwynn, were evidence enough.

But Jace knew Khadgar didn't fully trust him either. The way he kept testing Jace's attitude toward Ursales, his eyes and the emotions brewing in his heart that day in the abandoned vault on the Tirisfal peak — all of it proved it.

The only reason he hadn't brought up the subject of taking Ursales was that Azeroth now faced a greater threat. The former Guardian. His teacher's mother.

He wanted to destroy Ursales too. No question about it. And deep down, he might even partially agree with using the Finger of Death — just like Scavell and Aegwynn, who had inherited his research. But if he had a choice, he would probably want to learn that Defiler technique himself instead of summoning him directly.

Still, that wasn't the only reason for the look in his eyes that day in the vault.

Jace could guess. He must have been close to losing control again. Just like in the depths of the Molten Core, when he'd seen Grede lying there.

And he could tell from the looks in everyone's eyes lately that this had happened more than once or twice.

Because of this, he'd originally wanted to find a chance to have the Dalaran mages perform a ritual to send him back to Stormwind. To see his parents. To inform Ursula about everything and have them prepare. But now he was hesitating.

A shadow creature that even Khadgar found frightening — how could he show himself before other normal people? He couldn't guarantee he could keep his emotions stable in front of his parents at this point.

He'd just write a letter instead. That was his final decision.

To that end, he washed his hands several times with Purification Potion. Even though Turalyon insisted there was hardly any shadow energy left on his hands, that it wouldn't corrode the quill or the paper, and told him to stop overthinking it, Jace still couldn't bring himself to start.

Was it because of the shadow?

Or because he didn't know what to write?

Was... was all of this wrong?

Maybe he shouldn't have fought Dar'Khan Drathir for Ursales. Maybe he should have just let the necromancer take it, then reported him to the Kirin Tor. Everything would have been much simpler.

Dar'Khan was just a high magister. Even if he'd gotten Ursales, what kind of waves could he have made? This staff's obsession drove every owner to reap souls, seeking one day to take revenge on the Dark Titan. Whether the current owner was strong or weak was just a temporary fluctuation to it.

But then, who would have gone to Draenor to shatter the Scepter of Sargeras — which even Aegwynn couldn't destroy — and save the Sons of Lothar teetering on that broken world?

Teron Gorefiend had died. Deathwing had tried to let his children seize control of the Scepter of Sargeras to dominate Draenor. Kil'jaeden and Magtheridon had increased their military investment in Draenor to fight the black dragon. They could have even launched a Legion invasion through the Dark Portal directly.

If Jace's party hadn't infiltrated Draenor ahead of time, would Khadgar and the others have been able to hold out for over a decade like in that other timeline? Especially with the Naaru no longer descending.

Why had he run into Teron Gorefiend in Dalaran? Why had the death knight wanted to capture him — to use his body, the body of a Stormwind knight, to house his warlock soul?

Because of that disturbance in Southshore. That whelp. Deathwing had seen him through its eyes, or through his blood, and told his allies — Gorefiend and the death knights.

That dream. The snow. The Dragonblight.

"...We should blame you, Grede."

At an unremarkable intersection beside a tavern outside the Dalaran bridge, Jace sat on the steps, watching the people coming and going on the main street. "You should have bought tickets for a different boat. Then we wouldn't have run into that black dragon."

"Then you wouldn't have met the little elf." Beside him, Grede Thunderfist took a wooden cup and drank from it — a Dalaran custom brew of Thundersbrew. "I killed that black dragon. Left those Farstriders with nothing to do but drink bitter coffee in Dalaran to kill time. That's how you got to meet Vereesa Windrunner and fulfill your ultimate fantasy — getting with a white-haired elf."

"Go to hell," Jace muttered.

Grede took another gulp and let out a couple of throaty laughs, then sighed heavily, his beard flying every which way.

"I've been thinking, Jace... Don't get mad."

"I'm not mad. Go on."

"What if Aegwynn really COULD enslave the Defiler? I mean... Khadgar told me the other day that the Defiler Archimonde came to Azeroth once before, a long time ago, and the night elves and tauren beat him. So we shouldn't worry. As long as we work together... he's not that scary. But I'm thinking — a few years ago, before you had this staff, when you were as weak as a goblin, you still found a way to enslave Lord Kazzak. If someone gave a mage like Aegwynn a bottle of Demolition Potion, who's to say she couldn't use some absurd, over-the-top demon enslavement spell nobody in the Twisting Nether knows about to control the Defiler? Use an even bigger binding circle. Stronger weakening curses. Then use that... Finger of Death. Successfully destroy your staff."

After the dwarf finished rambling all that, Jace stared at him expressionlessly.

"Are you thinking I've gone crazy too?" Grede let out a dry laugh.

"No." Jace shook his head, speaking quietly. "If we can stop Lady Aegwynn... my next goal is to find a way to destroy this staff. At least... do what Master Scavell did. Hide this thing somewhere no one will ever find it."

"Good idea, Jace. Show her that not everyone's a neurotic mess. There are still plenty of normal people in this world." Grede breathed an almost imperceptible sigh of relief, as if he didn't think the warlock beside him would notice.

"I'll help you figure it out when the time comes. So will Vereesa and Sora. You know that better than anyone."

Then he pointed at Jace and muttered quietly, "But you can't bring Morophil and Needleflame along. Those two troublemakers will definitely try to dig it up again in secret and claim it for themselves once you're dead and gone. They'll probably outlive all of us... damn it all."

"Very thorough thinking, High Thane," came a soft voice from the shadows behind them.

"Bollocks." The dwarf jumped. "Next time you're joining a drinking party, could you give me some warning?"

(End of Volume)
