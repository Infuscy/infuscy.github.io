Chapter 1818: The Survivor

"Where is this?"

Jace looked at the door before him, adorned with a silver-white Arcane eye sigil.

"The Guardian Sanctum," Khadgar said. "This used to be where the members of the Order of Tirisfal gathered."

"Uh... I'm not a member of the Order. Can I go in?" Jace asked.

"There are hardly any Order members left in the world now, Jace. I'm not one either. Neither are the Six. And one of the few who is, is currently planning to summon the Defiler to destroy the world."

Khadgar waved his hand casually, and the door swung open. He looked at the corridor lit by Arcane lights within. "The Guardian Sanctum had been largely abandoned decades ago, after Medivh succeeded as Guardian. Only a few trusted members remained to guard it. By the time of the Dark Portal... after most of the Order's members were killed, no one maintained the Sanctum's magical energy. The Kirin Tor moved the important artifacts into the Vault to prevent their magical energy from going out of control and affecting all of Dalaran's ley line nodes. For the past decade or so, the Kirin Tor has never given up trying to reactivate it."

"Right now, it's just an old hall that no one pays much attention to. Follow me."

Jace nodded, following Khadgar. "The lady you mentioned... she lives here?"

"Yes." Khadgar said. "Twenty years ago, she survived Medivh's purge. After that, she returned to the Sanctum and never left."

"What's her name?" Jace asked.

"Aeli." Khadgar said. "Actually, that wasn't her original name. But that name... carries too many unbearable memories for her. We just call her 'Aeli.'"

The way Khadgar said it, Jace felt like he vaguely remembered a mage from the game who fit this description. But she didn't seem that important — he only had a faint impression.

"Is she... suffering from some kind of mental trauma?"

"Severe trauma." Khadgar said. "That's why I had to bring you, Jace."

Jace followed the mage around a corner. "I think Sora might... be more useful here than me."

"You want to enter her dreams?" Khadgar turned back. "You want to enter the dreams of a former member of the Order of Tirisfal? Part of the Guardian's power was hers. And she still survived her encounter with Medivh. I'm not surprised, though. Even back in the Arathor Empire era, she was one of the most powerful mages on this continent."

The Arathor Empire era?

That would mean this mage was probably Kael'thas Sunstrider's contemporary. Back when Dalaran hadn't even truly established itself as a magical city-state.

Khadgar led Jace into a side passage. This seemed to be part of the Sanctum's residential quarter. Compared to the aged grandeur of the main corridors, this area was noticeably fresher and cleaner — someone had been maintaining it.

"Don't try to touch or probe her with any form of magic, Jace. Otherwise, the consequences would be unimaginable." Khadgar warned quietly. "I suspect this is also why Master Antonidas doesn't even bother trying to get information from her. As for Masters Drenden, Modera, and Vargos — the younger members of the Six — they might not even know her true identity."

"But that's a good thing. At least for her. Maybe for Dalaran too. Anyway... just talk to her. See if we can learn about any Order of Tirisfal sanctuaries that we don't know about but Aegwynn might. Warlocks are good at talking, aren't they? At least you're much better at it than I am. But be careful — don't mention Aegwynn. Or her son. Preferably don't bring up any names related to the Guardians or the Order of Tirisfal..."

"Including your name?" Jace asked.

"Who's there?" A creaking door opened behind them. A female elf with light blonde hair poked her head out of a room, peeking cautiously. When she spotted the two of them, her suspicious expression immediately crumbled into a brilliant smile.

"Khadgar?"

"Aeli..."

Khadgar smiled too. The mage named Aeli ran over and gave Khadgar a cheek kiss. She wore only a simple, plain old robe with blue trim — the kind commonly worn by Dalaran's mage assistants. She was barefoot, with none of the jewelry typical of Quel'dorei nobility. Her eyes looked nothing like the ancient mage of over two thousand years Khadgar had described. She seemed like an innocent young girl — maybe even more innocent than Needleflame. What was even rarer was that her emotions seemed the same.

"Oh, my little Khadgar. You look so much older. Will you also leave me one day, like all the other friends?"

Aeli touched the wrinkles on Khadgar's forehead, not hiding the loss in her heart at all. Jace was used to seeing elderly elves with expressionless faces that showed nothing but disdain for everything external. This was so jarring it felt completely wrong.

"So I need to find a way to make myself young again, Aeli." Khadgar smiled. "Do you have any ideas?"

"Many ways, Khadgar. So many ways." Aeli seemed to light up with excitement. She raised a finger. "First, tell me — what made you grow old so fast? Even for a human, you've aged far too quickly. Was it the bronze dragons' doing?"

Jace immediately shot Khadgar a look. The mage seemed to snap back to reality. The smile on his face vanished, as if he'd just remembered that this aging was Medivh's handiwork. And Medivh... was a name that must not be mentioned.

"Uh... thank you for your concern, Aeli. But I'm not here today to talk about making me young again." Khadgar said.

"Well, alright then. Come to my room, Khadgar. And you too..."

"Jace Sesso." Jace gave a slight bow. "It's a pleasure to meet you, Lady Aeli."

"Lady?" Aeli was taken aback, then nodded. "Yes. Lady. Lady Aeli."

The elf muttered to herself as she led the two guests to her room. Though the room wasn't large, it was decorated with great care — like a noble's cottage in Quel'Thalas. A magic broom and several enchanted handkerchiefs were busy tidying up for their arrival. These little items were much smarter than the ones seen in magic hotels, their work more meticulous, as if an invisible ghost were gripping them and doing the labor.

Not surprising — this was a former member of the Order of Tirisfal commanding her magical servants. Jace could sense that even after giving up the Guardian's power, even after suffering unimaginable trauma at Medivh's hands, this arcanist might still be one of the most powerful mages he had ever encountered.

"Lovely room, Lady Aeli." Jace looked around.

"Thank you for the compliment, Master Sesso. How kind of you." Aeli hopped onto the bed with its violet cloth cover like an overgrown child, sat down, and swung her legs back and forth, quite proud of herself. "Look at my 'creations.' I've been trying to make them better at keeping this place clean. Maybe one day they'll develop their own aesthetic sense. I believe the 'soul' that Arcane magic has bestowed upon them will eventually grow to achieve this..."

"If anyone else said that, I'd find it ridiculous." Khadgar nodded, sitting on the chair across from the bed. "But coming from you, Aeli, I can only think... maybe."

"Mmm... of course." Aeli seemed even happier, smiling with her eyes narrowed.

Khadgar glanced at Jace, then said, "So... this is something only you can help us with."

"What?" the elf asked.

"Recently, Jace Sesso and I have been playing a... game." Khadgar spoke as if using a prepared script. Aeli's pointed ears twitched with curiosity. "A game?"

If he hadn't known beforehand, going purely by her appearance, mannerisms, and emotions, Jace would never have guessed she was an ancient Quel'dorei.

Khadgar continued. "Simply put, it's a game of hide and seek. Our goal is to hide in the most impossible-to-find place in the world... Not the Twisted Nether, because the magical disturbance from traveling back and forth would definitely attract attention."

Jace more or less understood what Khadgar was trying to do. He was trying to trick Aeli into revealing which places in the world a former Order of Tirisfal member would consider the best to hide. But would she really guess Aegwynn's choice like that?

To his surprise, Aeli froze for a moment, then asked warily, "Who's hunting you?"

Before Khadgar could answer, she suddenly shrank back onto the bed, retreating into the corner, staring at them with ice-blue magical eyes. "Is it HIM?"

"He's back... that crow... is HE back? Stay right here... stay right here. Don't go out. Don't leave Dalaran. I'm begging you, Khadgar!"

"No, no, no, Aeli." Khadgar panicked. "It's just a game. It's not what you think..."

"I think we should be honest with her. She's Aeli, Khadgar." Jace stopped him. "We don't need to hide anything from her..."

Khadgar gave Jace a bewildered look. Aeli asked cautiously, "What do you mean?"

"Actually, we have an extraordinary Arcane creation here, Aeli." Jace said quietly. "It's exquisitely made. Truly unprecedented. Whether there will ever be another like it, I'm not sure. But the problem is — if the Kirin Tor finds out about it, they'll definitely take it away and claim it for themselves."

Aeli seemed to relax slightly. Upon hearing this, she muttered in Thalassian, "Damn the Kirin Tor..."

"That's right. Damn the Kirin Tor." Jace echoed. "But... it's not fully finished yet. It can't be shown to the world. The process of completing it though..."

"Would leak incredibly powerful magic, attracting too much attention, Aeli." Khadgar seemed to suddenly understand what the warlock was driving at.

"Exactly." Jace agreed.

Khadgar nodded, his expression turning serious. "That thing... is so exquisite, I dare say it's no worse than your research and creations. It might even be more... I'm not entirely sure how to judge, Aeli. Because even I can't help but admire it..."

"Uh?" Jace blinked.

How did you manage to remove yourself from that picture?

Khadgar blinked too. He also realized he'd gone a bit overboard with the praise.

It's not yours, is it? Surely it can't be mine? Jace thought to himself.

"It's not your creation, Khadgar?" Aeli was taken aback, her eyes growing increasingly panicked. "If not you, who outside the Kirin Tor could complete it? Could it be..."

"No, no, no." Jace immediately stopped the elf from connecting the dots to any Order of Tirisfal member. "Jaina Proudmoore. Have you heard of her?"

Khadgar shot Jace a look that clearly said, Are you serious?

"I've heard her name, yes." Aeli whispered. "People outside talk about her a lot these days..."

"So it's true. She really is a very talented—"

"Yes, yes." Khadgar's face changed instantly. "I firmly believe that Jaina will soon shake the world with her magical talent. That's why we can't let the Kirin Tor... smother this genius mage girl's great creation. Just because of their... jealousy."

"We actually..." Jace swallowed, organizing his words. "...wanted to find a sanctuary hidden enough to store this great Arcane creation. What Khadgar said earlier... he was trying to protect Lady Proudmoore's privacy. He had to lie."

"But I think we can trust you. So please don't tell anyone else, Lady Aeli."

"I promise."

Aeli pressed her index finger to her lips. Her heart was already fully ignited by curiosity and the thrill of doing something secretly naughty.

She's basically a child.

"I do know of some places. Places even the Kirin Tor Six wouldn't know about. Even Master Krasus! But you have to promise me — when this creation is complete, I get to see it for myself. What kind of thing could make a mage like Khadgar so impressed?"

"Absolutely. Of course. Definitely." Khadgar shot Jace an excited glance.

Sorry, Jaina. Using you as a decoy again. Jace thought to himself with a helpless sigh.
