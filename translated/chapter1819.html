Chapter 1819: Lord, or Marshal?

"Tell me about this creation. Tell me what young Jaina Proudmoore intends to do with it, and I'll see where we can hide it..."

Aeli crossed her arms and settled herself on the bed, looking every bit like a teacher about to help two clueless students solve a difficult math problem.

"In general terms, it's an extremely large-scale transmutation spell device. It might involve incredibly powerful energy flow between Azeroth and the Twisting Nether," Khadgar said.

"A summoning ritual." Aeli's eyes widened slightly. "A summoning portal?"

"A portal doesn't necessarily mean summoning something, Aeli." Khadgar added. "It might have been inspired by a cross-planar transportation operation we conducted involving thousands of people... Proudmoore — possibly the most talented mage Dalaran has seen in decades, a genius in the school of transmutation — wants to explore a more convenient, faster, more mana-efficient method of long-distance transportation. Not just point-to-point within Azeroth, but across planes. Across the boundary between the physical and the void."

"That would be almost impossible to hide." Aeli declared. "You'd need a powerful mana source, like the Sunwell. And when the energy fluctuates, the whole world would feel it. Like seventeen years ago..."

"Hypothetically." Khadgar quickly stopped the elf from drawing further connections to the Dark Portal. He lowered his voice. "Hypothetically, what if we carried our own energy source with us? Something like the Eye of Dalaran."

"Hypothetically." Aeli pondered for a moment, muttering to herself. "Not a ley line node, but still needing a space capable of shielding energy fluctuations... very challenging. Let me think."

Jace relaxed his posture slightly. He glanced at Khadgar sitting nearby and shot him a skeptical look. Khadgar's face didn't radiate confidence either. The fingers of one hand kept tapping on the table beside him.

A mentally troubled elf. Even if she had once been the finest mage, a member of the Order of Tirisfal, even part of the Guardian's power — now she couldn't even bring herself to utter titles like "Guardian" or "Tirisfal" without losing her composure. Could she really think from Aegwynn's perspective?

But on the other hand, who else was there to ask?

Meryl Felstorm. That old mage was certainly an option. Eccentric as the two-thousand-year-old undead might be, as one of the founders of the Order of Tirisfal, his knowledge of Aegwynn, the former Guardians, and the Order's secrets surely surpassed almost anyone still alive. He might even know things that mother and son didn't.

But finding him might be harder than finding Aegwynn, who was probably already preparing her summoning ritual. And even if they found him, explaining why Aegwynn was summoning the Burning Legion would be a monumental challenge in itself.

Just as Jace's mind began to wander, sifting through memories from his past life, Aeli suddenly murmured, "I'll go... ask the Lord of Fenris for help. He'll help us."

She lowered her head, breathing heavily. The color had clearly drained from her face.

"The Lord of Fenris?" Khadgar asked urgently. "Do you mean Fenris Keep, or the Lord of Fenris?"

"Don't ask, Khadgar." Jace said. "She's exhausted."

It was clear that even without mentioning keywords related to the Guardians, Aeli couldn't help recalling associated elements as she searched her memories. And it was putting enormous strain on her.

If this were an ordinary psychiatric ward and Aeli were just an ordinary patient, Jace might have tried to calm her down before pressing further. But this woman's latent power was undoubtedly immense, and with her mental state unstable, it was best not to push.

"I'm sorry." Khadgar sighed. "Aeli, get some rest. I'll come see you again later."

Aeli didn't respond. She just curled up at the head of the bed, like a child who'd just been scolded by an elder and was about to burst into tears.

"Let's go, Jace," Khadgar said quietly.

"What about her?" Jace followed Khadgar outside. "Leaving her alone like this... I think she—"

"Don't worry." Khadgar gently pulled the door shut. "Don't forget who she survived. She'll recover."

"Alright." Jace nodded, following Khadgar's steps.

"The truth is..." The archmage walked and looked at Jace, lowering his voice. "A lot of the time, I forget that you're the dark wizard carrying a doomsday weapon. The monster everyone sees as a plague source, a demon lord."

"Damn it, Jace. Do you actually care whether she's hurting inside? Or have you been playing the role of an innocent — ah, I don't know how to put it — an innocent, kind-hearted young man for all of us this whole time?"

"Have you really had your brain corroded by the demonic power in Karazhan?" Jace frowned and made an exploding gesture with his hand. "What if she suddenly blows up like a magical bomb and kills us both?"

"Alright." Khadgar let out a laugh, as if relieved by something. "You don't need to worry about that, at least."

"Back to the point. Who is the Lord of Fenris?" Jace asked.

"A lord of the kingdom of Gilneas." Khadgar said. "He rules a small island off the southeastern coast of the peninsula. Are you saying there's a secret sanctuary of the Order of Tirisfal there? I really don't know. Did the Order's reach extend that far?"

"What about Fenris Keep, then?" Jace asked.

"The lord of Fenris Keep has been the king of Lordaeron ever since the Lordaeronians took it from the Gilneans. A trebuchet mounted on Fenris's walls could practically hit the palace walls." Khadgar looked over. "Aren't you from Brill? You don't know this?"

"I'm from Brill, so I know it's nowhere near that close. There's half of Lake Lordamere in between," Jace said.

"With Arcane-enchanted ammunition, it might be possible." Khadgar muttered as he climbed the steps. "And with Arcane enchantment, you could probably hit Stromgarde too."

"Should we check Fenris Keep first? Then head to the Gilnean peninsula to meet this Gilnean Lord of Fenris," Jace said.

Khadgar exhaled and glanced back at the deep corridor of the Guardian Sanctum. "Fine. Better than flying around like headless chickens."

"To go to Fenris Keep, do we need to see the king?" Jace asked.

"We're visiting as guests, not conducting a Kirin Tor search." Khadgar said. "Besides, the Kirin Tor has always cooperated with Fenris Keep. We regularly send our finest members to serve as magical advisors to Fenris Keep's marshal. So Fenris Isle has always welcomed Kirin Tor visitors."

"And who is the current marshal of Fenris Keep?" Jace asked.

"Othmar." Khadgar answered. "Marshal Othmar Garithos."

Hearing that name — that surname especially — Jace felt his heart drop.

Because this was the man who, in another timeline, after the Scourge had destroyed most of Lordaeron, became the de facto commander of the remaining Lordaeron Alliance resistance forces.

So he was already the steward of Fenris Keep, guarding the entire Lake Lordamere region. That meant he must be a commander trusted by both Terenas and Antonidas. No wonder he'd risen so quickly to become the Alliance's new leader in that situation.

But he was also the man who dared to throw Kael'thas into the Violet Hold. Who, after the combined counterattack on Lordaeron, ordered the resurrected Sylvanas and her cooperating dreadlord Varimathras to "get the hell out of the capital." And ultimately met his inevitable end at Varimathras's hands.

Thinking about all this, the more Jace turned it over, the more something felt wrong.

"Who's his magical advisor?"

"Archmage Thule." Khadgar said. "He was Kel'Thuzad's student. He was investigated for a while over the Kel'Thuzad affair, but he himself was fine. Why do you ask?"

"I plan to bring Morophil along," Jace said quietly.

Khadgar rubbed his forehead. "Fenris Castle doesn't have a magical barrier sustained by the Eye of Dalaran. It should be fine. I don't think that mage would be able to detect Lady Morophil... But are you suspecting a Nathrezim has possessed Marshal Garithos?"

"Prepare for every possibility." Jace said. "After all, the leader of the most troublesome Nathrezim still hasn't shown himself yet, Khadgar."
