Chapter 182: One Lie and Countless Lies

Even though the crowd had thinned out a bit from before, there were still plenty of people packed in front of the building. Jace was a little worried his shoulder would get jostled around and reopen the wound, so he didn't dare push his way in.

After all, everyone here — except the ones being carried in, or the ones whose injuries looked downright horrifying — had no intention of making way.

The two of them finally found an opening and slipped in behind a stretcher. The corridor inside was, naturally, even more packed than outside.

The air was a three-way brawl between the stench of blood, the foul reek of shadow energy, and the aggressive bite of medicinal herbs. Jace went from waves of nausea to spells of dizziness, and after barely a few minutes he felt like he was about to black out. He could only retreat to the end of the corridor to catch his breath for a moment.

"This one building isn't nearly enough," Grede said. "They've got the students out of class anyway — they should clear out the academy rooms for the patients."

Jace glanced at the dwarf. "The Mage Quarter streets aren't normally open to the public. You really think they'd let the wounded from here into the academy?"

"Those Kirin Tor lot," the dwarf spat, staring out into the corridor.

"Speaking of the Kirin Tor," Jace said, leaning against the wall and watching the flow of people pass, "that elf sold me out. Not completely, but either way the Kirin Tor now know I've crossed paths with Teron Gorefiend."

"Then what do we do?" Grede asked in a low voice. "Are the Kirin Tor going to come arrest you?"

"Probably not to that point yet," Jace said. "Honestly, what I'm more worried about right now is that Orcish-language meeting last night. They might connect that command I mentioned to the whole theft."

"There's a more pressing problem, which is that I told a lie at the meeting… I said I'd found that orc warlock in Deadwind Pass, and that I'd found him on my own. So you've got to say you had nothing to do with any of it. If anyone tracks you down and asks about that command, you have to say you know nothing."

"That's fine," the dwarf said. "But why'd you lie to them?"

Jace was about to explain, but the real reason he couldn't tell the truth was that he didn't want to draw Kel'Thuzad — the one studying necromantic magic — to Elwynn Forest.

Was that something he could just say out loud?

One lie takes countless lies to cover — and that was no empty saying. He was already getting a headache.

"Sorry, Grede. I don't know why either. Maybe I wanted to show off about having been to Karazhan or something. I just lost my head. Didn't want to admit I'd gotten that precious command with someone else's help. I'm sorry."

"Hah, you little bastard," Grede said with a triumphant laugh, pointing at Jace. "I'll keep your secret — but if we sell that letter, you're giving me a bigger cut. And you're buying me a drink."

Jace said, "No idea if we'll even get the chance to sell it. But if we do, forty-six split? I take forty."

"Not bad. But the drink — that's the important part."

"Once we reach Alterac, and my wound's healed up a bit, I'll buy you one right away."

"Then it's a deal."

Not long after, the crowd up ahead thinned again, and Jace seized the chance to shuffle inside.

The moment they walked in, both of them fell quiet on their own. Jace realized that compared to the people lying in here, his shoulder wound was nothing.

Last night's situation had been far worse than he'd imagined. Injured people everywhere. And the dead?

Just last night alone he'd killed two zombies. Not to mention the other zombies he'd seen — at least five or six. And how many had he missed?

The two of them stood against the wall by the window. Grede looked at the soldiers maimed by necromantic magic — some swollen beyond recognition, others missing limbs — and the mages burned by dragonfire, and fell silent too.

Jace watched the herbalists, priests, and mage apprentices hurrying past and didn't dare interrupt them, didn't dare ask them to drop what they were doing to treat him.

Compared to the other wounded, he looked more like a volunteer helper here than an actual patient.

While the two of them were still hesitating, an herbalist walked over and asked Jace directly, "Your shoulder… were you hurt last night?"

"Yes," Jace admitted. "A death knight stabbed me."

"Let me take the bandage off and have a look," the herbalist said. "The fact that you can still stand here is lucky. A good number of the people wounded by death knights last night didn't make it to daybreak."

Hearing that, Jace even less knew what to say.

He sat down to the side and watched the herbalist unwrap his bandage. He almost felt ashamed to show off a wound that had already started healing.

Once it was off, the herbalist looked it over a couple of times and said, "This… looks like a puncture. Did you receive any priestly healing at the time?"

"We used some of our own medicine on him," Grede cut in.

"That explains it," the herbalist said. "Well, I don't know much much about dwarven remedies, but it's healing nicely. I can give you some medicine and hemostatic bandages — you can go home and recover. There are no spare beds here for anyone who can still stand. Do you want to heal fast, or take it slow?"

"Fast," Grede said.

Jace nodded.

"The fast option might cost a bit more. Rest well for about a week and you'll be able to move around again."

When Jace heard the absurd timeline of a week, he immediately suspected the price was going to be brutal.

Sure enough, the two of them paid fifteen silver for a bit of Living Root-based wound powder and a pile of enchanted bandages, and walked back out the front door.

"This time I really bled for it. Living Root's gotten too expensive," Grede muttered. "You need to change out that gold coin. If you don't, we won't have the money to get to Durnholde."

Jace sighed. "Then let's go. There's an old dwarven saying…"

Grede jumped in almost instantly: "A woman is a battle axe — dangerous from the back as from the front."

Jace blinked. "What in the world is that?"

"Ah, that's the one proverb I know best," the dwarf said.

Jace said, "It's supposed to be, 'No one can live their whole life clinging to a single gold coin.'"

"Never heard of it. Sure that's not some Dark Iron nonsense?" Grede said.

He glanced toward the distant snowy mountains and said, "We're right by the Alterac Mountains now, aren't we?"

"We are," Jace said.

"Then once we reach Alterac City, could we spend a few days foraging for herbs in the mountains? Remember what the alchemy shop owner in Stormwind said? Living Root's real common in the Alterac Mountains."

"But we're both banged up right now — how would we even manage that?" Jace said. "And if we set out now, it'd still take at least two days to reach Alterac City. No telling what could go wrong along the way. By the time we got there, Winter Veil season would've already started. Then we still need to get to Durnholde, and from Durnholde to the Hinterlands — that's practically a whole week. And we can't forget we've got to come back…"

"What could possibly go wrong? It's just crossing a snowy mountain!" Grede said, displeased.

"Don't even start," Jace said. "You're going to give me a heart attack."
