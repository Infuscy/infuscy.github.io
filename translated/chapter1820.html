Chapter 1820: The Island Castle

At the gryphon aerie beside Krasus Square, long before dawn, Jace and Khadgar packed their things and met there, ready to head for Fenris Castle on Lake Lordamere.

"So how should we ask?"

In the adjacent corridor, Jace watched the keepers calming the gryphons. "We should figure this out a bit before we go in."

"Just ask if there've been signs of demonic activity across Lordaeron lately, especially around Lake Lordamere," Khadgar said. "Either way, we're mostly killing time. The main thing is to let Lady Morofil get a look at General Garithos and his magical advisor — or whoever else in that castle might be possessed by a dreadlord. Is Lady Morofil here?"

"I'm right here, mages." The demon answered from the shadowy corner behind them.

"Damnit." Khadgar startled and glanced aside at her. "I can never get used to that fear of demonic presence that I can't detect at all. Even if you're on Jace Sesso's side."

"I think you could give me some time and space to use mind magic on that human general. Make him spill every secret he knows directly. That'd save everyone time." Morofil glanced at Jace. "I'm guessing that's the main reason you wanted to bring me along, master?"

Jace didn't deny it.

Khadgar looked between master and servant. "Wait — do you two actually think he might be connected to Lady Aegwynn or the demons? I feel like you have a bias against the Garithos family, Jace. He's a lord from Blackwood in the east, not from Brill. Or do you have some acquaintance up in northern Eastweald that he's been bothering?"

"No." Jace shook his head. "But Lady Alleria did say the secret sanctuary of Tirisfal might be connected to the Lord of Fenris."

"The Lord of Fenris. Could also be the Lord of Fenris from Gilneas." Khadgar glanced toward the southwestern sky. "I'm more suspicious of Gilneas myself. Islands everywhere with no one watching, far from normal settlements. Lady Aegwynn would likely choose an unknown hidden sanctuary there. I think we should be better prepared for the next stop. I even brought that scroll."

"You're that sure there's no Tirisfal sanctuary beneath Fenris Castle?" Jace asked.

"I've read a lot of Tirisfal Council files and records, Jace — more than most people realize. That was actually a major part of my 'spy mission' to Karazhan." Khadgar said. "All those years of reading idle books, and I still remembered what I was actually there to do."

"Fenris Keep is closely tied to Dalaran. The ley lines even intersect there. In the past, the demon lord Kathrana'tir tried to corrupt the waters of Lake Lordamere and create a plague disaster. That island has almost no secrets left from the Kirin Tor these days. After all, the island isn't even as big as the area enclosed by Stormwind's walls — easy enough to search thoroughly."

"You read all those secret files, but you still didn't know Uthalesh still existed." Jace said. "Even the Tirisfal Council's most secret records don't write everything down without concealment."

"How do you know I didn't suspect it?" Khadgar muttered. "When the Dark Titan possessed Medivh's body, he commanded his undead mercenaries to search the world for traces of Uthalesh. His desire was so strong that even after Medivh died and his soul was banished to the Twisting Nether... the command never expired. He always wanted to reclaim Uthalesh for himself. He didn't even trust the Deceiver with it — he used Medivh's body to do it himself."

"But at the time I learned all this, I was only eighteen, not much older than you when you first arrived in Stormwind. I was as innocent as a foal. I had no idea what Uthalesh actually was. My status wasn't high enough yet for the Kirin Tor to reveal Guardian-level secrets to me."

The mage paused for a moment. "But I admit — you're right. I had no idea about the Council's secret vault on the hill behind Death Knell either. Now's not the time to let our guard down."

"Then after this, what about Gilneas?" Jace said. "Do we go see King Genn Greymane first?"

"Of course." Khadgar said. "The Gilnean Peninsula isn't Lake Lordamere. King Greymane has no love for the Kirin Tor either, especially with our identities... but leave that to me, Jace. Don't worry about it."

"Masters, two gryphons are ready to depart whenever you are!" A dwarf called out from under the eaves.

"Much obliged, sir!" Khadgar replied. Morofil had already vanished into the shadows. "Did you tell Grede and the others about our itinerary?" The mage turned back to ask.

"Grede's gone to Blackrock Mountain." Jace said. "We need him to rally Shadowforge City to join the Lordaeron Alliance. But I told Acridfire — she's watching Solara, who's connected to the Emerald Dream. When Solara wakes up again, she can pass on our movements."

Khadgar took the gryphon's reins from the dwarf and nodded, but hesitated to mount. For once, a rare unease stirred in his heart.

After the keeper withdrew, the mage looked over and asked, "Do you think we'll end up having to face the Defiler?"

"Didn't you tell Grede just a few days ago that even Archimonde was nothing to fear?" Jace asked.

"I could see he was nervous, so I figured I'd try to cheer him up a bit." Khadgar said.

"I don't think he's nervous because of any powerful enemy we might have to face." Jace said.

"Then why?" Khadgar asked.

The mage had barely asked the question when he glanced at the staff in Jace's arms and felt it was an unnecessary question.

"A few years ago, before you all crossed the Dark Portal, beside the River Neltharion, Grede Thunderfist saw me use shadow magic for the first time." Jace said slowly. "Just a simple Fear spell — scared off a dozen forest wolves that were trying to surround and eat us. But back then, he hated all magic, especially dark magic, so much that he didn't even really thank me."

"A Wildhammer dwarf. Doesn't surprise me at all." Khadgar chuckled.

Jace smiled too. "Then he said, if I ever fell to dark magic and became a demon — a monster — he would kill me himself. I agreed."

Khadgar stared at him for a long moment. "Just like every Wildhammer dwarf loves their comrades and brothers. The same way they love their gryphons."

"Yeah." Jace said.

"So do you think your skull can withstand a hit from Sulfuras, Jace Sesso?" Khadgar joked as he swung onto the gryphon's saddle.

Jace sighed softly and mounted as well. "I hope I never have to find out."

I hope I never have to find out. For some reason, the words echoed endlessly through his mind.

They left Krasus Square and took to the sky. Flying close behind Khadgar across Lake Lordamere, they plunged into the embrace of the dark pine forest at the horizon's edge. Faces seemed to surge forth from the morning mist, one after another, fragments of voices drifting past his ears...

The Green Dragon Lord howling at his daughter deep within the temple. The Black Dragon Prince glaring at a loser with a whip of flame in hand. The female mage struggling to make a decision atop the black tower...

The Explorer's League dwarf clutching the visage of Yogg-Saron, screaming for the earthen construct to stop — not to destroy the treasure he'd brought back from Northrend...

In the basement of Wildhammer Keep, the young warlock who suddenly realized he could follow another timeline's Illidan Stormrage — use Uthalesh to drain the souls of Auchindoun and solve everything — racing back to the Alliance camp in ecstasy to announce his plan to everyone.

And now that warlock touched the cold Elementium chain around his neck. Are you truly stronger-willed than them, Jace Sesso?

Fenris Castle loomed ahead. White banners of Lordaeron and the Alliance flew in rows atop the walls. The gryphon's speed made the time Jace had once spent sailing back and forth across Lake Lordamere feel like a joke.

Morofil, flying midair, faded from sight. Khadgar raised his staff high from a distance, its tip glowing with pale blue arcane light. Soon, a horn sounded from the watchtower beside the gate. Heads moved back and forth on the gryphon roost at one corner of the wall. Everyone bustled about, preparing to receive the guests from Dalaran across the lake.

A large-statured noble stood on the battlements, waving from above. His black clothes and shoulder-length dark hair and beard contrasted sharply with his white cloak.

That must be Garithos. Jace had never seen the man in person here before, but he was fairly certain.
