## Chapter 1823: Two Paths, Converging at One Point

Jace didn't know the blue dragon envoy's name. Khadgar had never mentioned it either. But clearly, the dragon hadn't gotten lost chasing ley line nodes. He'd tracked the truth to its source before anyone else — and died for being too reckless.

Who killed him?

The blue dragon's robes had several holes burned through by arcane energy, and the skin beneath was scorched. Not many other wounds or damage to the outer garment. The fight must have been brief — over quickly.

A mage who could instantly overpower a blue dragon in arcane magic and kill him in human form... who else could that be?

Given the circumstances, there weren't many possibilities.

Wherever this place was, it had to be connected to Aegwynn. Maybe it was even the site of her demon summoning. But how had Khadgar's teleport brought them here?

Jace replayed the ritual in his mind. Khadgar had expended enormous effort to send both him and Uthalesh away, and the ley lines had howled in protest. Was that the problem?

But he didn't have enough to go on.

Jace looked around the corridor, then headed in the direction the dead blue dragon had been facing when struck.

The corridor was tilted. Every so often, a thunderous roar shook the place, but it was impossible to tell where it came from. Each time, the whole floor trembled.

He hadn't gone far before reaching a broken chamber. Footsteps echoed from a low passage ahead.

A blue-robed mage hurried out, stepping over rubble. "Thank goodness for small mercies — we didn't end up too far apart."

"I don't know what went wrong. The teleport malfunctioned, Sesso... It seems the ley lines have been tampered with. This should have been caught by the Spell-Weaver or the Kirin Tor immediately. I don't know why no one noticed. Either that, or it has to do with this whole bizarre space. Do you think we're still on the Lordaeron continent?"

"Jace?" Khadgar looked up at him. "Stop staring. The priority is finding an exit and warning the outside. Or do you plan to stay trapped here forever?"

"Let's cut the act, Tichondrius." Jace said.

The mage sighed in defeat and dissolved into a swarm of shadow bats.

Just a shadow illusion — not even the dreadlord's real form. In a space this chaotic, it was hard to distinguish any magical energy. But for a warlock like Jace, this level of shadow energy disguise versus an actual living person... the difference was still obvious.

"Kil'jaeden told me you weren't an opponent to take lightly. Especially with Uthalesh's soul power — your strength might have surpassed Gul'dan." A low demonic voice came from beyond the walls. "But from what I've observed, you're nowhere near that orc."

"Given everything I know about Uthalesh's history, you could even call you... disappointing, Jace Sesso. Every master of Uthalesh was a vanguard of the Burning Legion, capable of destroying a world alone. Even its previous owner — the Eredar envoy Satael, sent to cleanse Azeroth a thousand years ago — though she failed the Great One's mission like her predecessors, her power was enough to betray the Legion and even threaten the Great One's dominion."

"Compared to them, the relic of Uthalesh and the Great One's personally forged blade in the hands of a coward like you are as mundane as..."

"Then why haven't you left yet?" Jace cut him off. "Like every Nathrezim who's been exposed. Is this your last hiding place? Do you have to hold this ground, slow the other mage and me down, buy time for Kil'jaeden and Aegwynn to finish their summoning?"

"Don't misunderstand my good intentions. Kil'jaeden still has hope for you." The demon's voice said. "Yes, he hates you. He nurses a burning desire for revenge. But he still must obey the Great One's will — to extend a final invitation to Uthalesh's current master..."

Jace didn't answer. He slit his palm, gathered the flowing blood, and quickly drew a summoning circle on the floor. He chanted as the circle neared completion. When the spell and the circle joined, fel light flared — just as the final line was drawn.

"Because the Great One saw your potential — even without unleashing its full power, Uthalesh in your hands nearly severed the Eredar's pride, their fusion of a thousand powerful souls. Even Aegwynn was astounded. In all her decades of trying to destroy it, she never came that far. Never even came close."

As the demon whispered, Jace finished his incantation. But Morofil didn't appear in the circle before him.

The soul link was still a chaotic mess. The ritual's connection to the Twisting Nether felt like it was being pinched off by something in the middle. His will and spirit ached with suffocating pain. He couldn't help closing his eyes.

The demon chuckled softly. "Long ago, Dalaran's demonologists turned that magic city into a crossroads of Twisting Nether rifts, letting countless powerful demons enter this world. In response, the powerful Order of Tirisfal built a planar fortress in the north to block all external intrusions from the Twisting Nether. From that stronghold, they reclaimed dominance over the ley lines and restored Dalaran's planar order... That is the true Fenris Keep."

"Now, the great hero Jace Sesso, attempting to summon his ally, the witch Morlephidos, to hunt the Nathrezim lord who plagues the world once more, finds himself thwarted by this castle's powerful planar barrier. Forced to face a trial of both flesh and will alone... I can almost taste the irony spanning two millennia. Can you, Sesso?"

"I have one question." Jace caught his breath, easing the dull ache in his skull.

He stared at the bloodstains on the floor, already blackened by shadowfire, and said coldly, "Are you guarding Aegwynn's teleportation ritual?"

"Mortal heroes are always so steadfast, focused entirely on their mission." The demon's voice rumbled from all directions. "And ultimate evil seems to lie just ahead. The one who once protected the world, now turned against it — a savior fallen to corruption and madness. The new hero must end this chaos..."

"But have you ever asked yourself, Jace Sesso... what if you are the dark heart? What if you are the source of the calamity?"

"You saw a future in prophecy and decided to do everything to stop it. Lady Aegwynn did the same... And if I had to choose, I'd be more inclined to believe another person's prophecy. After all... she is Aegwynn."

"So who is the new hero, and who is the fallen, maddened savior?"

"Has the world truly become better because of your attempts to save it? Has Azeroth escaped its destined fate of losing its soul, being utterly corrupted, or shattered to pieces?"

"Take up Uthalesh to fight the Legion. Or use the Legion to destroy Uthalesh. Will you be the one who brings the end, or will she?"

"Which is nobler? Which is right? Which path leads the world to the correct ending?"

"What even is the correct ending? If the visions Lady Aegwynn saw were twisted by the Great One's power — how can you be sure what you saw is real?"

"Answer, Jace Sesso. Or do you simply have no answers to these questions either?"

The warlock was silent for a moment. He checked the pocket watch hidden in his robe, then said, "You also haven't answered my question, demon."

"Is Aegwynn and her summoning ritual under your protection?"

The demon laughed. A cold sound that echoed through the abandoned old castle — but the laughter wasn't so confident anymore.

"Let fate, the Holy Light, the heavens, and time answer those questions one by one — both yours and mine." Jace tucked the watch back into his robe. "We will defeat Aegwynn and stop her summoning. Or she'll complete the ritual and destroy me and my staff. Then all questions will be answered."

"The same goes for you, demon. Once I devour your soul, I'll naturally know whether she's here or not."
