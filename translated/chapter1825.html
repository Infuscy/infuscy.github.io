Chapter 1825: Eye of the Storm

Amid the constant arcane ringing, Jace sprinted down the shaking corridor, driving the surging dark power around him to trigger Shadowstep after Shadowstep — leaping across broken chasms, vaulting over collapsed walls.

With Neltharion's Tear and countless powerful souls — including the black dragon prince — empowering him, he could now freely use most of the spells he knew. Back in Draenor, even a few spells at that scale would have drained him dry. Now, chain-casting Shadowsteps back to back with no rest barely fazed him.

The runes the Order of Tirisfal had carved here two thousand years ago flickered into visibility, some still holding residual mana. How could such a powerful forgotten place exist without Khadgar knowing about it?

Then again... Khadgar was supposed to know nothing about this place.

Why else would Tichondrius be hiding here? Why else would Aegwynn have chosen this location for her ritual, to summon the Burning Legion?

Take up Ulthalesh to fight the Legion, or use the Legion to destroy Ulthalesh? Which was right, which was...

Jace let out a small sigh. He'd heard the Nathrezim boast about corrupting the great Titan Sargeras before, and back then he'd always thought the story made the mighty Titan's sacred mission of purging all demons seem... not terribly resolute.

But now, facing just a few words from Tichondrius, he couldn't shake them.

After all, watching from the outside and being in the thick of it — holding the power that could steer everyone's fate — were completely different feelings.

And besides, at this point, how much help were his memories from his past life really going to be?

So those last words he'd said to Tichondrius... they were meant for the demon, but also for himself.

If your choice and Aegwynn's were destined to be two paths that crossed but could never reconcile — then let fate give the answer.

The flagstones beneath him trembled harder. How big was this fortress? How complex? He had no idea.

The fleeing demon soul was about to slip away. He could almost hear the Nathrezim's laughter.

If Morophil were here, she might be able to catch it. Damn it, Morophil — why were you never around when it actually mattered?

But thinking calmly, it wasn't necessarily a bad thing that Morophil wasn't trapped here.

Khadgar's fate was unknown, and Jace didn't know if he'd escape either. Morophil knew he planned to return to Dalaran and alert the Kirin Tor — and the whole Alliance — to prepare for an assault on Fenris Keep.

She could turn invisible. No one knew she'd been to Fenris Keep. She could leave without bothering anyone. As long as the message got through, his and Khadgar's trouble wouldn't be for nothing.

As for how to notify the Kirin Tor — once Dalaran's barrier sensed her passing through, whether the battle mages treated her politely because of her connection to him, or tried to chase her through the whole city, the message would reach the right ears either way.

With that thought, Jace quickened his pace. Using the near-infinite mana from his Life Tap, he triggered another Shadowstep around a corner.

Flickering arcane light now glowed through the corridor. At first, from the corner of his eye, he assumed they were more residual runes from the Order of Tirisfal — until he noticed the light seemed to be coming through gaps in the cracked walls.

What was happening out there?

The rumbling arcane roar had become like a giant magical beast's endless howl. An ordinary person would have already lost their mind from sheer terror.

Jace felt the floor give way beneath him, like stepping on floating wood and nearly losing his balance. He grabbed a collapsed wall segment, only to feel his whole body being lifted higher along with it...

The gaps in the walls around him widened. The arcane glow beyond grew brighter. But this light wasn't the "clean," "neat" arcane energy he was used to — it was murky and frenzied, emitting a harsh pale purple that somehow seemed twisted into fel-green.

But that wasn't the most urgent thing. The urgent thing was that this corridor was coming apart.

Jace spun and jumped back, triggering another Shadowstep toward the still-intact corridor "exit" he'd come from — only to feel an impossibly powerful force yank him backward the moment his body left the ground.

He suddenly remembered how the ley line nexus had shattered the Spires of Arak. What was behind him — the Twisting Nether?

He glanced back. All he saw was a blinding light. Beyond the crumbling fortress, bricks and stone pillars drifted slowly toward the center of that light, just like his helpless flesh-and-blood body.

Squinting, he could just make out a ring-like silhouette. A... door.

Jace hadn't expected that seeing that vague portal outline would calm him after all the panic.

Wasn't this the very thing everyone had been searching for? Hidden right under the nose of the entire Alliance of Lordaeron — in Lordamere Lake, on Fenris Isle.

He gripped Ulthalesh and silently recited the incantation for the Hand of Gul'dan.

Too bad he hadn't managed to grab a Destruction Potion before coming to Fenris Keep. Plus, trapped inside this dimensional fortress, cut off from the Twisting Nether's dark power... this time the spell might not hit as hard.

But even at reduced power, crushing a portal should be doable, he thought.

Besides, he still had the Ata'mal Crystal fragment Velen had given him — the Spirit's Song. Even if he had no idea how to trigger its power.

Was this the moment? Had Velen seen this scene already? A human warlock drifting toward his fate, about to prove the crystal's power.

Aegwynn, you'd better be ready.

Huh?

Just as Jace braced himself for mutual destruction, he felt himself drifting off course. Another arcane energy wrapped around him like a rope thrown into a raging current.

Squinting through the light, he spotted a figure standing atop a tilted mage statue still attached to the main fortress — shouting something.

But in the deafening magical roar, he couldn't hear a word. As he drifted closer, some sound finally reached him.

"...have you lost your mind?!"

"Why didn't you run? Do you want to end up as bricks for that Legion Gate along with this fortress?! I thought you'd passed out, but here you are staring with those big eyes, damn it..."

"What's wrong with you? Your eyes..."

Khadgar grabbed the warlock's cloak and hauled him onto the statue's head. "What's going on? Don't tell me you've completely lost it and I have to fight you too. Honestly — just one portal and one former Guardian wouldn't be so bad!"

"You won't need to fight me yet." Jace glanced back at the "center." "I just finished dealing with Tichondrius. My mana got a little out of control."

"You 'dealt with' the Nathrezim lord? Tichondrius?" Khadgar bellowed. "Fine, I won't waste time being surprised! That demon lord's soul is probably already 'adding fuel' to that gate, isn't it?"

"You saw it?" Jace asked.

"No!" Khadgar shouted at the top of his lungs. "But inside this dimensional barrier, a demon's soul can't escape back to the Twisting Nether. There's only one direction it can go — in there."

"If this place is completely cut off from the Twisting Nether, how did Aegwynn open a portal for the Legion?" Jace shouted.

"Simple." Khadgar shielded his eyes from the light. "Once she finishes the portal, she just needs to tear through the boundary between this dimension and reality. Using this place's tight connection to the ley lines, she can project the whole thing into the exact same spot in Azeroth."

"Fenris Keep," Jace said.

"Yes. And when that happens, the Fenris Keep outside will be crushed to dust." Khadgar coughed, his voice hoarse. "Lordamere Lake will face whatever she summons — whether it's Archimonde the Defiler or something else."

"We have two choices, Jace." He grabbed Jace's collar, still shouting. "First — stop Aegwynn here, destroy the unfinished portal, and crush Kil'jaeden's plan. Downside is, we'd have to face a former Guardian holding the Book of Medivh in a straight fight."

"Fortunately, her mana's probably already spent. Building this massive portal alone for months must have taken everything she had."

"Second — the scroll's still on me. We gamble on whether it can get us out of here. I didn't make this scroll. Someone else did. Anyway — it should be trustworthy. No more surprises this time. Then we notify the Kirin Tor, gather everyone, and assault Fenris Keep."

"My backup should already be heading back to Dalaran," Jace said. "If time flows the same here as outside, she might have already told the Kirin Tor."

Khadgar stared at Jace for a long moment, then muttered, "I was really hoping for a good reason to run as far from Lady Aegwynn as possible... Well, there goes that. Light curse you, Lady Morophil."

"Looks like there's no choice, Khadgar," Jace said.

"No choice." Khadgar sighed, gazing at the blinding "eye of the storm."
