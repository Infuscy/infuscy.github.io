Chapter 1827: The Portal

Amid the magical currents, Jace let the force carry him toward the portal. The gate's frame was mostly built from the Order of Tirisfal's dimensional barrier, but its surface patterns looked like thousands of tortured demonic faces. Whether it was the demon souls injected into it, or the corruption Aegwynn had brought from Karazhan, or his own warped vision — he had no time to find out. When he reached the top of the gate frame, the magic there was so strong that even Shadow Step couldn't adjust his direction. He finally had to seize the chance to drive Ulthalesh like an ice pick into the seamless runic stonework to hold himself in place. As the soul-scythe struck with a heavy thud, easily piercing the portal's surface, the whole space trembled. The swirling magic wind faltered briefly before returning to its original force. In that momentary weakness, Jace spotted the Eye of Sargeras floating at the gate's center. Behind the dark titan's artifact stood its creator and sustainer — a figure almost invisible against the magical storm. Aegwynn. As Jace watched her, she watched him back through the fel-green demonic gem. One hand raised, channeling the planar mana storm to converge around the Eye of Sargeras. The other hand held a blood-red grimoire. The Book of Medivh. But Jace was certain she'd never call it by that name. "Don't make a foolish decision, Jace." Her voice was eerily calm, almost soothing in this apocalyptic scene. But Jace knew the speaker was hundreds of meters away. If Aegwynn could solve this with power, she wouldn't be wasting words. "I never wanted to hurt you, or take that artifact from you. Because I know you haven't reached that point yet." As if reading his mind, Aegwynn spoke again the moment the thought crossed his mind. "On the contrary — I'm the only one in this world who can help you now. Khadgar can't. You know that." "And I will save you. Not just you — but myself, Khadgar, all of your friends and family... and all of Azeroth." "I will break this shackle. Free us all." In the distance, arcane energy still clashed, erupting in chain explosions. But one side was overwhelming the other. Khadgar was barely holding on. Jace steadied himself, focusing his entire attention on the Eye of Sargeras. "Kil'jaeden is nothing but a pawn," Aegwynn's voice grew colder. "He thinks he can use a Guardian. But even his supreme master couldn't manage that." "Don't listen to those mad whispers. They've been corrupted by Karazhan's insane power. I've already expelled them all. Now I'm in control. Just one last step. Trust me, Jace." "Why won't you trust me, Lady Aegwynn?" Jace raised his hand, aiming at the fel gem. "Right now, you choose to fight against the prophecy you saw. And I choose to fight against the one I saw..." Eramon, Siliondor. Between Jace's lips, the life-draining incantation emerged. Amid the maddening roar of the Legion Gate, his words seemed to merge with the colossal magic and screaming wind — low as a true demon of the Twisting Nether. Aegwynn laughed, though the sound was swallowed by the chanting. The floating Eye of Sargeras let out a bright green thread that Jace gripped tightly in his hand. The entire portal shook. The island floating at the eye of the storm cracked at its edges, crumbling as it was torn apart. The planar storm grew increasingly chaotic. The distant shifting lights and shadows dimmed, shattered, evaporated. Mirror images were torn apart. Chanting voices turned into agonized screams. Khadgar — good luck. Jace had no time or energy left to worry about his life. Because the Eye of Sargeras... had to be devoured. That's what Khadgar wanted, what the Alliance wanted — destroy the summoning ritual, destroy the portal! The green light in Jace's hand twisted through the storm like a mad demon's tail, raging fel energy pouring into him. His whole body felt like it was being torn apart by flames — and compared to that, the searing heat was the lesser pain. At the same time, mad thoughts crashed into his mind one after another. The desire to tear everything apart, like a starving tiger in a cage, roared against iron bars. Hooked claws scraped at his fragile last line of defense. The silvery metal of Neltharion's Tear took on a fel-green stain. The belt at his waist — Angelista's Grasp — caught green fire. Under his cloak, the countless demon souls imprisoned in his robes howled and raged, struggling against the runic seals that bound them. Even Ulthalesh — the scythe hooked onto the portal's beam — its imprisoned souls ignited with the same fury and destructive will, screaming hysterically. A pair of eyes seemed to watch from within the gem — peering into his consciousness, his body, his will, his very soul — observing everything that happened in this trembling dimension. Then Aegwynn suddenly clenched her raised hand. Jace felt a violent ringing in his ears. The voices, the desires — something slammed into them. The gray-white sky seemed to take on new colors, as if a film over his eyes had been ripped away, and the outside world became real again... The magical storm that had nearly gone completely wild returned to the mage's control. At the same time, Jace felt the portal's power surge. The Eye of Sargeras, which he'd been holding back, was now pulled by some force from outside the dimension. But no matter how the power shifted — even Aegwynn's magic couldn't shake the devouring force of Ulthalesh's master. Like a beast biting into the Eye of Sargeras, he refused to let go no matter what, determined to consume it completely... In his warped vision — the unreal and real portal, the Guardian hidden by the magical wind, the cracked sky, the ground closing in from both sides, the churning lake, the swaying Blue Child and White Lady — nothing seemed to matter anymore. Only me and the Eye of Sargeras. "Jace!" "She was forced to prematurely manifest the unfinished portal! We've achieved our goal! But we need to get out of here! Or do you really want to be crushed between two dimensions?!" "I barely fought my way through this mess... I can still use the scroll! If not, we're dead!" "I could save myself and leave you and this damned thing to die! Don't make me risk my life for nothing!" "Let go of that thing! Don't you recognize me, Jace Sesso? Look at me! I'm Khadgar!" "They're all waiting for you! Windrunner, Thunderfist, the green dragon, the black dragon, Lord and Lady Sesso, and your damn maid — do you really not care about any of them?!" Jace snapped his head around, staring at the disheveled mage. For an instant, he saw — in those tired, blood-spattered blue eyes — a demon. A fiend with mad green fire and shadowy purple in its gaze. Like a chaotic arrow of malice, floating at the seam between the Twisting Nether and reality. "Sorry..." "We'll talk outside." Khadgar grabbed Jace's collar and flicked the scroll. Jace didn't even see it fully unroll before everything in his vision began to spin, like a toilet being flushed. He was glad he still remembered what a toilet — something from his past life — looked like.
