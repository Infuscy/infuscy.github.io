## Chapter 1829: South Bank

Fel-green lightning rolled deep within the swirling, dark gray storm clouds, casting intermittent eerie glows. This demonic phenomenon centered on Lake Lordamere was visible even from as far away as Aerie Peak.

A squadron of elite gryphon riders flew from the Hinterlands straight toward Dalaran. Grede, their leader, had been frowning the entire journey, constantly glancing up at the strange vortex's center.

Krasus Square was in chaos. Wounded dragonhawks and gryphons struggled and roared. Priests, mages, and messengers wearing the tabards of various kingdoms darted back and forth. The air was thick with the stench of blood, mingled with the metallic tang of arcane energy and the heavy reek of demons — a combination that made anyone who smelled it dizzy and nauseous.

On the other side of the city, closer to the lake, many buildings had already collapsed into the water. Waves relentlessly battered the broken walls and shattered roads. Cracks in the earth revealed a glow like blue arcane flames, though no clear fire was visible.

In the streets, groups of mages kept rushing toward that area, trying to use magic to repair what they could or hastily erect a dam to hold back the fierce lake water. But the rubble and stone held up by arcane energy seemed disobedient — chunks kept flying too high or crumbling out of control. Shouts, desperate screams, and cries for help echoed everywhere.

"High Thane Grede Thunderfist!"

Archmage Vargos called out. Grede pulled his gaze from the distance to look at the mage. He jumped off his gryphon and was about to ask where Jace Sesso was when he spotted a massive raven landing not far away. It transformed into a tall night elf man amidst a puff of smoke and strode quickly into Dalaran.

The dwarf stared at the guest — someone who definitely shouldn't be in Dalaran — disappearing through a doorway, then said to the mage beside him, "If even the elves are moving that fast, I reckon we're in real trouble this time."

"It's certainly dire, but there's still hope, High Thane," Vargos said. "Archmage Antonidas wants to know when the Wildhammer clan's reinforcements will arrive."

"The main force of the knights can get here before nightfall. We're just the vanguard, here as fast as we could." Grede pointed toward the eastern sky. "So what the hell happened? Why are demons suddenly everywhere? Did you know some of them have already reached the Alterac Valley area? A fear guard with a giant mouth on its belly landed right in Strahnbrad! What the hell's happening north of Dalaran — is that the demons' doing too?"

"The portal has already manifested, whether or not it was the guardian Aegwynn's doing." Vargos pointed at the dark anomaly in the sky. "It's located at the center of that vortex — Fenris Keep. Lordaeron's army is already engaging the flying demon legions in the capital city."

"The damage wasn't caused by demonic power. It's because the activation of that portal forcibly drew upon the ley lines. Dalaran, being a ley line nexus, suffered the worst of it. Fortunately, the magical barrier doesn't rely on ley line energy, so the city can still function as a forward fortress."

"What?" Grede grabbed Vargos's cloak and shook it, nearly roaring, "You're telling me that portal was activated using Dalaran's ley lines... I don't understand much of this, but I know you lot have been monitoring every single ley line!"

"And you guaranteed that if anything stirred, we'd know about it from your outposts immediately. But here we are — Kil'jaeden used your own ley lines to build a super-portal and is pouring hundreds of greater demons through it, and you still don't know how the hell this happened?"

"No, no, High Thane..." Vargos said. "That portal's ley line connection seems to have been one with Dalaran's from the very beginning. Even the Kirin Tor doesn't know why. Lady Aegwynn is intimately familiar with everything the Order of Tirisfal built in Lordaeron — far more than the Kirin Tor..."

"None of that matters now, damn it." Grede let go. "So this time it's the other way around, yeah? On Draenor, Jace and the others built a portal in secret, and we protected it from the demons. Now the demons built one in secret, and it's our turn to attack."

"Indeed..." Vargos nodded helplessly. "And just like last time, time is short. The portal is nearly complete. Its final purpose is to summon the Defiler. These demon legions are only here ahead of time thanks to his power, buying them time. So, High Thane, we must gather every fast-strike force we can and destroy that portal before it's too late."

"Is Jace Sesso in Dalaran?" Grede interrupted.

The mage shook his head. "Actually, Master Sesso was the one who alerted us. He and Archmage Khadgar were on Fenris Isle a few days ago and sensed something was wrong. A dreadlord seemed to have controlled some of the soldiers guarding Fenris Keep, covering up what was happening there. By the time the Kirin Tor realized—"

"It was too late." Grede lowered his head, gritting his teeth. "I have to find him. I have to find him. Damn it. What about the elves? The dragonhawk riders? Vereesa? The magisters..."

"Quel'Thalas's forces are already mobilizing — Farstriders, Sunfury, magisters... but all that still takes time." Vargos said. "Right now only a handful of dragonhawk riders are helping us relay messages and intercept the doom guard legions. Everything happened too suddenly."

"What about teleportation spells?" Grede stepped closer. "Portal magic? All those fancy runic circles? Don't tell me you lot could rig up a dozen portals to shuttle nobles and priests around for those boring hearings, but now that Azeroth's about to be torn apart, everyone's supposed to get around on wings or on foot!"

"The ley line network has been warped by that portal, High Thane." Vargos shouted. "From what Lordaeron's told us, teleportation still worked the night before the portal appeared. But afterward, mages who tried to teleport to deliver messages either went missing or couldn't complete the spell. The Violet Citadel's portal array is also down or scrambled. Ironforge, Stromgarde, and Stormwind probably still don't know what all this means. Our messengers are still flying over Khaz Modan."

"At least the Kaldorei druids can use the Emerald Dream to contact the powerful dragonflights." The mage looked east. "The red dragonflight could arrive any time. Flying here from Grim Batol would only take a few days, and this time they're on our side."

"The Emerald Dream, the Emerald Dream..." Grede muttered under his breath. "What about Sora? That green dragon? Where is she?"

"Hey! Hey, will you stop crying already!"

Viewed from above, the Silverpine Forest stretched for hundreds of miles, swept by fierce eastern winds, rising and falling in waves like a dark green sea that couldn't find calm.

Sazeno hung on to Sora's horn with one hand while wiping away the Tears of Elune streaming down his face — enough to douse the fel fire on his head — and shrieked, "No matter what, you've got to let me feel my master's presence, green dragon!"

"If you keep crying like this, drenching everything in arcane energy, how am I supposed to sense him? You're just wasting time! The ley lines here are already a tangled mess, like a knotted ball of thread. Demons everywhere, mages everywhere, and now you're adding to the chaos?!"

"Might as well not have had that banshee summon me here at all. I was doing just fine hiding behind Stormwind's walls! But no, look at that massive portal over there — holy mother of— if I wanted to join the Burning Legion, I would've just listened to the dreadlord back when I was summoned! Why go through all this trouble!"

"I should have gone with him to Fenris Keep," the green dragon said quietly. "I should have known it would be dangerous. I shouldn't have let him just—"

"Aiyah! What's the point of saying that now! The portal's already open, Dalaran's practically done for!" The imp shook his head. "Besides, the Jace Sesso you keep worrying about isn't dead yet. Look, that damn contract is still yanking on my poor, pitiful soul, refusing to set me free. Sigh!"

"And think about it — if he were dead, would that she-devil be running around Lordaeren so loyally asking about him? She'd have slipped off to the Twisting Nether to hide from the Burning Legion long ago. Between you and me, that's the real nature of that cowardly female demon..."

Sazeno snickered. When the massive magical beast beneath him stayed silent, he coughed and shut his mouth.

"Alright, I won't cry. You search more carefully." The green dragon swallowed hard, the motion nearly sending the imp flying.

"Very good, my lady. That's the spirit. I thought after living a thousand years you might have grown at least a little more mature than that little black thing." Sazeno stole another glance at the dark sky over Lake Lordamere. "No lies — I can feel we're getting closer to my master. Keep going!"

"Hey, if I find that reckless fool before Morofil does, you have to help me get that nathrezim's soul fragment. That's what we agreed on back in Dalaran."

"We did." Sora nodded. "I'll help you get it."
