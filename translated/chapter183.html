Chapter 183: The Buyout

After leaving the ward, Jace followed Grede back to their rented room to grab his luggage. The staff, of course, stayed behind — Jace had no intention of parading around with it before leaving Dalaran.

On the way home, he mulled over how he really ought to start practicing Mage Armor. If he'd had Mage Armor on him during the fight with Gorefiend, things probably wouldn't have gotten so ugly, right?

But Mage Armor was just so damn difficult. For someone who was normally pretty fired up about learning new spells, even thinking about it made him want to procrastinate.

Ever since leaving Stormwind, he'd memorized the incantation dozens of times but had never once actually cast it. Partly because it really was dangerous, and partly because he never seemed to have the time.

Especially after the fight with the black dragon in Southshore, when he'd been unconscious for two or three days — that had cost him a ton of time.

Tonight, he planned to ask Master Dushan for a favor.

He had to start practicing no matter what. This bizarre journey across the Lordaeron continent was already nearly half over, and today was already December 12th.

When he reached the door of their lodgings and knocked, it was flung open almost instantly.

Dushan stood inside. "Jace, why didn't you find a way to contact me? You left last night without a word, didn't even say where you were going."

"Last night's situation, Master — there was no way to get in touch," Jace said helplessly. He stepped inside, glanced back, and saw the tension on Dushan's face. "Master, what's wrong?"

Dushan hesitated a moment, then said, "They're saying the Dalaran vault was robbed."

"I warned them at the meeting," Jace said.

His master sat back down with a sigh, picked up his cup, and said, "If you hadn't been right, they probably would've just written you off as some young apprentice talking out of turn. But you called it more accurately than anyone — they're definitely going to come here and ask questions."

Jace knew Dushan was right. It was only a matter of time before the Kirin Tor tracked him down. And sure enough, Dushan hadn't even finished his cup of coffee before someone knocked on the door.

"Master Caro Dushan, is Mr. Jace Sesso back, please?"

The speaker's tone was stiff and proper — it had Kirin Tor written all over it.

Dushan shrugged helplessly, and Jace had no choice but to open the door.

He opened it to find an elf standing outside.

He wore a blue mage's robe with a gold-threaded sash and a long cloak, golden hair falling to his waist, with a rather striking gemstone necklace at his throat.

In both dress and bearing, he looked far more like a pure Quel'Thalas-schooled mage than a Kirin Tor elven member.

"Hello. I am Remi Stargazer, of the Relics Society. Is Mr. Jace Sesso here?"

"I'm Jace Sesso."

Jace turned it over in his mind, wondering what the Relics Society could possibly want with him.

It wasn't that the staff had been discovered, was it?

Impossible…

A whole flood of worries flashed through his mind, but he still nodded and asked, "How can I help?"

Remi glanced inside, greeted Dushan, then said, "Mr. Sesso, this is a house you share with someone, isn't it? Why don't we go find a restaurant and talk there?"

He noticed the bandages wrapped around Jace's shoulder and asked, "Your injury shouldn't be a problem, should it?"

"If it's just a meal… I should be fine."

After saying goodbye to Dushan, the two of them went to the Azure Encounter. Remi ordered a few things fluently in Thalassian, then cleared his throat lightly and looked at Jace.

"Mr. Sesso, let me explain my purpose first."

Remi's Common was near-perfect. Forget Jace's Brill-farmstead accent — even the Menethil royal house probably didn't speak it that well.

"You may have heard of the Relics Society."

"I have," Jace said.

Remi nodded. "You might think the Relics Society is an organization loyal to Quel'Thalas, or even to the Sunstrider royal house. On the surface, that's true enough. But our actual purpose isn't to serve anyone in particular — it's to seek out the various holy relics, artifacts, and even weapons left over from ages past, to unravel their secrets and knowledge, in order to achieve our ultimate goal: helping the Quel'dorei break free of their dependence on magic, once and for all. You may have heard of the affliction that's plagued our people for tens of thousands of years. Some call it magical addiction."

"I've heard of it too," Jace nodded.

Remi Stargazer spoke with such rigid formality that, even though all manner of pastries and dishes had been laid out and Jace was starving, he still felt a little awkward digging in. In the end he just lifted his cup and sipped some juice.

Remi went on: "I'm giving you this brief introduction because I don't want you to be wary of me, or even hostile. We may not be the same race, but I would never do anything to harm the alliance between elves and humans. I'm here on the introduction of the host of the Quel'Thalas embassy in Dalaran, Aethas Sunreaper, who recounted to me what happened at last night's Orcish-language discussion meeting."

The Relics Society. The Orcish-language discussion meeting. Aethas Sunreaper.

Even though this Remi had talked a lot without getting to the point, Jace had already more or less guessed what he was after.

"So you want that command from Teron Gorefiend, don't you," he said.

Remi Stargazer said, "Mr. Sesso is exactly as sharp as I imagined."

Sharp, my ass, Jace thought. Anyone could see that.

This guy's laying it on this thick — he's not about to hit me with some sugar-coated scheme, is he?

Still, thinking that didn't stop Jace from feeling rather pleased. The sugar coating had, in fact, punched right through his armor.

He glanced at the food on the table, swallowed, and said, "But it's just a letter. Everything written on it has already been fully translated by the masters at the meeting. With Master Aethas and his students' memories, they probably have it memorized down to the last word by now. I don't quite understand — isn't the Relics Society supposed to be an organization that searches for magical holy relics? Are you planning to use those few scraps of text to find all the remaining relics?"

"The remaining one relic," Remi corrected.

He popped a piece of cured fruit into his mouth and said, "Haven't you heard that the Dalaran vault has been robbed? Judging by the Kirin Tor's violent reaction, what was stolen must have been the Eye of Dalaran — that legendary relic of the Order of Tirisfal."

"Though, you shouldn't know about the Eye of Dalaran, Mr. Sesso. Don't go spreading it around, or you'll have trouble. I'm telling you as a gesture of good faith."

"As for the use of that letter… it's certainly not just the Orcish on it," Remi said, leaning back in his chair. "If you've taken any Enchanting courses, you'd know that powerful warlocks and mages, when they handle objects, inevitably leave behind traces of magical disturbance. Humans call it magical resonance, and the ability to sense that energy is called sympathy. It's an important ability for tracking down holy artifacts and ancient relics, Mr. Sesso. For instance, I can tell right now that you met one of my kin just two hours ago, and that your wound was inflicted by a death knight. I'm not a Farstrider — I don't have the senses to smell a forest troll from hundreds of miles away. I don't have eyes and ears all over Dalaran reporting to me at every moment. But I don't even need to ask whether I'm right — so you can see the power of sympathy."

Jace thought, this guy really does have something going on… I wonder if he can "sympathize" out whatever other problems I've got going on?

Remi didn't say anything further on that front. He continued: "Senior examiners of the Relics Society can read hidden information in a mage's letter that a linguist could never uncover — including, but not limited to, their mana, their mental state, their physical condition, their emotions… And from those clues, we can gather a tremendous amount of extra information, turning something that might have been worth very little into a priceless treasure."

"I see," Jace nodded.

"If you're amenable, name your price, Mr. Sesso," Remi Stargazer said, shifting gears so fast it caught Jace off guard. All that circling around, and now he was this direct?

Jace's mental price point was, of course, four gold — that was the judgment he'd made back in Stormwind. But since this Relics Society was clearly well-funded, he ought to squeeze them for a little more.

"I've never done this kind of business before, Master," Jace said. "You make the offer!"

"Ten gold," Remi said.

"Thirteen, then," Jace said flat-faced. Only after he said it did he suddenly realize — Remi had just said ten gold. Gold!

He'd decided in advance that no matter what Remi offered, he'd push for a little more. He hadn't expected Remi to throw out a number he'd never even dreamed of!

Remi Stargazer stroked his pointed chin with its hint of golden stubble and said, "Thirteen gold is already over the budget they gave me, sir. If you can't accept twelve gold, then this transaction will have to be called off."

"Twelve, then," Jace said, forcing himself to stay calm. "This letter's already become a liability anyway. I'm eager to be rid of it."

"That's certainly true," Remi Stargazer said. "Is that command still on you?"

Jace opened his bag, pulled out the curled-up orcish command, and spread it out on the table.

Remi picked it up, glanced at it, then looked at Jace. "This is it. No problem."

He set a pouch on the table and added two more gold coins to it from his own pocket. "Thank you very much for parting with such a valuable material, Mr. Sesso. Please, enjoy your meal at your leisure. I'll take my leave."

Jace watched Remi walk out of the tavern, felt the coin pouch on the table, then touched the wound on his shoulder and hissed — confirming all of this was real.

Then he looked at the gnome busboy wiping down tables up front and said, "Excuse me, sir, could you wrap up these pastries to go… thank you."
