## Chapter 1830: Fel Fire

A rainy night. Burning fel-green meteors sporadically squeezed through the black, churning clouds. The distant walls of Lordaeron's capital were faintly tinged with green. Magic flashed atop the walls — at this distance it was impossible to tell whether it was fel fire, shadow, arcane energy, or golden Holy Light.

At least Sylvanas's messengers had brought good news: King Terenas's soldiers, relying on numbers and home turf, were slowly retaking the walls. After the orc wars, the Wildhammer dwarves had trained a large batch of human gryphon riders for the Alliance kingdoms, and that training was finally paying off.

Below the walls, the Alliance army — presumably reinforced by the Silver Hand knights who had just arrived — was coordinating a counterattack on the harbor. Word was that Turalyon himself was leading the charge.

The pitch-black Tirisfal Glades made it impossible to see the northern shore of Lake Lordamere from this direction. But Vereesa knew her straitlaced brother-in-law — the kind whose jokes landed like cold rocks — was at least irreproachable when it came to slaughtering evil and fighting demon legions.

The black dragonhawk skimming low over the pines blended into the darkness perfectly. Vereesa pulled her hood's edge slightly forward to hide the glow of her ice-blue eyes as she stared ahead. She wasn't the only elf in the sky — a host of dragonhawk riders and Farstriders flew alongside and behind her.

Doomguards pouring out of Fenris Isle weren't just ravaging the watchtowers, villages, and towns around Lake Lordamere — they were also secretly building Legion portals in the forests to summon more demon soldiers into Lordaeron.

The most elite Wildhammer gryphon riders — or more precisely, the monstrous dwarf knights under Kurdran Wildhammer and Grede Thunderfist, fighters whose battle prowess rivaled young dragons — were already preparing to assault the portal on Fenris Isle.

Quel'Thalas's dragonhawk riders had to first take out the Ered'uin strongholds the demons had established along the shore, clearing the way for the dwarves' opening bombing run.

Word was that the bombs the dwarves carried were arcane explosives proposed by the Sunstrider Dynasty to wipe out Zul'Aman once and for all. The last Vereesa had heard, they were nowhere near being manufactured and hadn't been ready in time when Gul'dan invaded Quel'Thalas. She never expected their first deployment to be here, on Lake Lordamere.

But if they didn't use them now, there'd be no point later. That's what she told herself.

Up ahead, the green fel glow burning through the forest was clearly visible now.

The gryphon riders had detected this portal in the evening. They'd tried to destroy it but were driven back by demons, so they had to report to the capital, where Lordaeron's forces and the Farstriders were informed.

This was clearly one of the larger portals the demons had built in their first wave. That was why Vereesa had volunteered to lead the dragonhawk riders and Farstriders on a night raid.

A roar erupted from the forest — some demons had spotted them. But the Legion portal, covered in fel rune marks, was right ahead. Most of the demonic shadows moving around it clearly hadn't expected a threat from above.

"Let them taste the chill of death, Quel'dorei!"

Vereesa shouted the attack in Thalassian. Arcane arrows from the Farstriders carved trails of ice-blue magic across the pines, like a horizontal deluge intersecting with the rain from the sky.

Dragonhawk riders raised their enchanted lances and drove them at the airborne Ered'uin demons. These elite soldiers, inheritors of ten-thousand-year-old Highborne demonic knowledge, were nothing like the mortal guards of Lordaeron. Every strike aimed straight for the demons' hearts. The doom guard legion, caught off guard, was momentarily suppressed.

This was the perfect chance to destroy the Legion portal. Vereesa drew Belanore and loosed an arrow at a fear guard that had chased someone into the sky — its belly-mouth spewing fel fireballs in their direction. She then immediately aimed at the two Eredar who were channeling the portal's magic. Simultaneously, two magisters raised their arcane energy, ready to assist the ranger lord's assault and break the portal's construction ritual.

One Eredar warlock was struck through the heart and collapsed in agony. But the other Eredar abandoned the ritual at once, twisting around to hurl a strange arcane spell that ripped one magister's dragonhawk apart in a spray of blood.

The magister riding it plunged into the forest and went still. The Eredar's eyes gleamed cruelly as he pointed at the other magister — only to have an arcane bolt punch straight through his skull, slamming him into a portal pillar. A second arcane arrow pierced his heart, finally sending this demon warlock back to the Twisting Nether.

Vereesa's black dragonhawk swept over the portal. But the portal seemed to have already completed its construction. The remaining magister tried to disrupt the Twisting Nether channel within it, but with little effect. Soon doomguards crawled through to harass his casting, forcing him to break off and fight back.

Just then, the pine trees around them — nearly burned to death by fel fire — began to twist and writhe, transforming into shambling treants that grappled with the doomguards.

At the same time, thick vines erupted from nowhere, coiling tightly around the portal, slowly sealing it shut. The creaking strain was audible even high in the air.

Kaldorei. Though the sentinels of Mount Hyjal couldn't reach the eastern continent's shores yet, some druids who guarded the Emerald Gate for the green dragons had already flown to Lake Lordamere to fight alongside their Quel'Thalas cousins.

Under the power of those mighty roots, the portal's stone base began to tilt. Some demon guards crawling out were pressed back in by the vines. The portal's fel fire curtain flickered... but then a massive demonic hand reached out from within, seized a clump of roots, set them ablaze effortlessly, and tore them away.

Vereesa recognized that hand. It belonged to a pit lord.

The enormous demon tore through the roots, squeezing half its body out. Even the massive portal seemed narrow compared to its bulk.

It shoved several treants off the steps with one hand while the other pulled a polearm from the Twisting Nether, spearing through the root net and swinging directly at the night elf druid channeling natural magic. The druid, unable to dodge in time, instantly shifted into an enormous bear — only to have the blade bury itself in his shoulder. He collapsed into the mud with a pained howl.

"Weakling..." The demon laughed victoriously, tearing at the tangled roots trying to block or topple the portal. Vereesa loosed several arcane arrows that punched through its fel iron armor and embedded themselves in the monster's flesh. Demon blood sprayed everywhere, but the arrows couldn't kill it — couldn't even slow it down.

"Where the hell is its demon heart?!" The ranger cursed under her breath. But the demon seemed to hear her and roared in challenge: "No one can destroy the burning heart of Azgalor! No one!"

Vereesa aimed directly at the portal's crossbeam instead. A Dream Arrow blasted an arcane-white scorch hole through the fel-scorched stone, but not enough to sever it. More and more demons poured through the gaps the pit lord had torn in the roots, leaping down to surround the wounded druid and engage the dragonhawk riders and rangers.

The ranger ignored the demon's mockery and kept drawing her bow, frustrated that her prized magic arrows were doing so little against the portal and the grotesquely oversized demon.

As the demon was about to break free, a dark shadow suddenly dove from above.

"Look up, you stupid demon!"

With a sharp cry, the dark blur hurtled down wreathed in flames entirely different from fel fire — not at the demon, but straight into the portal's crossbeam!

The beam — already pockmarked by Vereesa's arrows — finally shattered.

"Needleflame!"

Vereesa shouted. The pit lord's eyes changed color.

His laughter became a roar. He tried to grab the black dragon, but the young whelp, though sizable, was surprisingly agile. A wing-flip sent her shooting back into the sky, easily evading.

The demon stopped trying to swat at the darting pests. His other hand grabbed the tilting doorframe desperately as he tried to haul himself through the collapsing curtain — but the residual roots, still refusing to let go, kept blocking him at every turn.

"Gnats! Flies! Vermin!"

The pit lord bellowed in Demonic, thrashing and struggling to force his way into the world. Even as Needleflame's acid breath burned his face down to blackened bone, he refused to let go.

Vereesa shot an arrow through the hand gripping the doorframe. His grip faltered, and his bulk lurched backward through the portal, undoing all his struggling in an instant.

The portal finally collapsed. The curtain tore.

Even then, the enormous demon refused to give up, trying to force himself through — only to be bisected by the abruptly severed Twisting Nether channel. His shrieking upper half tumbled down the steps. Fel-green demon blood flooded the corrupted ground like a burst dam.

The Quel'dorei scattered to avoid it. But the sky and the forest rang with Thalassian cheers of "For Quel'Thalas!"

The black dragon grabbed the wounded druid and lifted him clear of the spreading demon blood, saving him from being burned into corrupted slag. The druid stared at her in shock, as if unable to accept that a black dragon had just saved his life.

He survived. The forest wasn't so lucky.

Every tree the demon blood touched — living or dead — burst into green flame.

Even though the portal had been destroyed, who knew how long this place would take to recover.

"I didn't find Sesso. He... I'm sorry, Vereesa."

Needleflame called out from the air. There was no excitement in her voice, despite having just defeated a demon commander.

"Don't worry. Morofil and Sora might have found him already!"

She encouraged the little one, but as she watched the black dragon circle and fly west again, the ranger stared at the fel-green flames blanketing the ground and felt a knot of anxiety in her chest.

Not because she doubted the Alliance would win. She believed the demons couldn't destroy this world. Stromgarde's forces and Kul Tiras's fleet were already on their way. The Kirin Tor was regrouping. Ironforge's army hadn't arrived yet, but Alterac's Stormpike Regiment had already reached Hillsbrad. Since the Sons of Lothar returned, the Lordaeron Alliance had never been stronger or more united — not even at Blackrock Spire.

Nor was she afraid of dying at the hands of demons. She'd made peace with that possibility long ago, when the orcs broke into Quel'Thalas, when she first joined the Farstriders.

She lowered her head. *Jace...*

*To hell with corruption, twisting, dark magic!*

*To hell with everything. To hell with everyone. I just want you to live. Please, Jace...*
