## Chapter 1831: You Have Your Staff, I Have Mine

Vibrant emerald green doesn't always mean life.

Jace stared at the puddle of bright green blood before him — so potent it had ignited a patch of fungus near his foot, sending up black smoke. The thick, cloying stench of demon filled his nostrils, overpowering every other unpleasant odor in the dim, abandoned mine tunnel, including the coppery tang of his own nearly-uncontrollable shadow magic.

It was his own blood. He'd just coughed it up. By now, he'd stopped being surprised.

"That's what I'm telling you, Jace." Khadgar sat across from him beside a pit prop, staring at the blood on the ground. "You really shouldn't leave here. Actually, strike 'shouldn't.'"

"Our job is done. We disrupted Lady Aegwynn's attempt to secretly build that portal. Fenris Isle is exposed for everyone to see, and the portal isn't complete yet. You crippled the Eye of Sargeras — she won't be able to replace that missing power anytime soon."

"By the time the dragons arrive at Lake Lordamere, when the Kaldorei forces land at Whisperwind Coast, not to mention the aerial raids from Quel'Thalas and Aerie Peak..."

Listening to Khadgar ramble on, Jace felt another wave of dizziness wash over him.

The teleportation power released by that scroll was so incredibly strong that Jace half-wondered if it had bypassed the ley line or void channels entirely. For a moment, he'd felt like he and Khadgar might be hurled back ten thousand years in time.

But even with that kind of power, the scroll hadn't managed to throw them out of Lordaeron. Jace wasn't sure of their exact location, but the air outside smelled of familiar pines — somewhere in the Tirisfal or Silverpine mountain ranges.

Before his mind had fully cleared from the magical disruption, Khadgar had dragged him to this hidden spot. According to the mage, Jace looked even more terrifying than an actual demon now.

"If we paraded you through Dalaran right now, let all those nobles and princes and princesses who care about their pretty faces have a good look, the annual rate of fel magic crimes in Dalaran would drop by at least half." The mage's exact words.

At the time Jace didn't want to believe Khadgar's exaggeration. But looking at his own hands — covered in burst-open wounds, dried pus at the edges, dark green tumors bulging from fel swelling, large patches of blackened, necrotic skin like reptile scales — he decided not to argue about what he looked like to others.

And even in this state, he was doing much better than when they'd first crawled into this abandoned mine.

Also, even a scroll as powerful as that one hadn't freed him from this staff.

Ursales still lay there, its hollow eye sockets seeming to stare up at the ceiling — or maybe at him, at its master.

After devouring the power of the Eye of Sargeras, the bone seemed even more vicious and arrogant than before. Or maybe that was just his imagination. Maybe the staff had never changed at all.

"You stole part of the Eye of Sargeras's power, Jace. Aegwynn definitely wants you to walk right back into her trap so she can use you as fuel for the portal." Khadgar said. "She won't kill you — but that doesn't mean she won't capture you. Grabbing you to power the portal, then finding a way to destroy Ursales before finishing you off — that's probably her ideal plan."

"So we do the opposite. Avoid her scouts and every demon too. You staying hidden here is already a huge help to everyone out there."

"I didn't exactly graduate from the Wizard Sanctum with honors, Khadgar, but I'm not an idiot either." Jace hung his head. "I can feel the ley lines across the entire region converging around the portal on Fenris Isle. That power is going to help the Burning Legion finish the final step. That portal... is nearly complete."

"Demons are already pouring out of it, and you're standing here blocking me from leaving. I don't think you're worried about me becoming the portal's fuel, Khadgar. I'll say it again — don't treat me like a fool."

"Then what's my real motive?" Khadgar asked. "You think Tichondrius's soul possessed me?"

"You've lost trust in me, just like Aegwynn." Jace said. "You're afraid that once I'm out of your sight, I'll lose control and become an even bigger problem than the Burning Legion. You think the Eye of Sargeras's power has twisted my will. You want to be my jailer, my warden — seal me in this mine forever until you find a way to destroy Ursales, or kill me..."

"What the hell are you talking about? Jailer? Warden?" Khadgar cut him off sharply. "I'll let you leave. Just not yet."

"If you joined the fight now, the Eye of Sargeras's power could backlash and burn you out. Even if you got lucky, it would twist you into something no one would recognize. You look terrible now, sure, but at least I can still see you're Jace Sesso — and you're getting better."

"The fel is affecting your mind. Can't you feel it? You know the dark side of emotions better than anyone. You know demons better than anyone. You know this better than I do right now. Be patient. Control this power, Jace. *Then* leave this place. When we get to Karazhan, maybe I can find something in Medivh's books to purge this corruption. Maybe... you can even keep it."

Khadgar was right. Jace knew it.

He understood all of this perfectly. The power of the Eye of Sargeras — fel energy on a scale he'd never experienced — had nearly turned him into a fel-fire monster. Even his soul-link perception had blurred. He couldn't hear his imp's calls clearly anymore.

He tried to steady himself. Looking back, directly siphoning the Eye of Sargeras's power had been an incredibly reckless decision.

But what other choice was there to destabilize Aegwynn's nearly-complete great gate?

"Khadgar? Is that you? I heard your voice."

A voice echoed from the tunnel. Khadgar turned and asked, "Lady Sora?"

A golden-haired elf in green robes materialized through a shimmering veil of dream magic. She leaned against the wall as she stepped into the cave, her pale face illuminated by the arcane lamp.

The moment she saw the warlock sitting there, tears started streaming uncontrollably down her face.

"Master! You're actually here!" Sazeno jumped off the green dragon lady's shoulder and landed on a rock. "I thought a pit lord had swallowed you whole! Does this count as me finding you first, huh? Does it?!"

"Jace... I finally found you!"

Sora rushed over and embraced the warlock, completely ignoring the fel corruption on the ground and walls.

"You're badly hurt..." 
"I'll be fine."

Jace wanted to tell her to stay back, that the fel was dangerous, but when her hands touched his shoulder and the back of his neck, the rage burning in his chest — the wild urge to destroy that portal — seemed to calm, carried away by a faint, cool breeze.

The fierce heat still churned inside him, but at least it wasn't surging into every wound on his face, neck, and head every second.

"Be careful... this demonic power, Sora." He warned quietly.

"I'll heal it, Jace. I'll heal you." Sora said. "I'll do everything I can."

"I know, Sora." Jace said. "Thank you."

"I'm so glad you found him, Lady Sora." The mage said. "I was starting to think I couldn't convince him anymore. He's badly hurt — nearly died, you can see that — but he insisted on leaving here to fight the demons, convinced I was imprisoning him."

"But you're right about one thing, Jace. That portal is nearly complete. The Alliance doesn't have much time. And I don't think the Kirin Tor or Quel'Thalas can break Aegwynn's magic and destroy the portal quickly enough. While the demons are busy fighting the Alliance, I have to take on the role of that sharp spear and help them land the final blow. After all, my fight with her isn't over yet."

"You're going after Lady Aegwynn alone?" Sora asked in surprise.

"Not alone — I'll have the whole Alliance backing me. But..." Khadgar paused and smiled. "Anyway, I've dealt with a guardian before. I even put a sword through his heart. This time I've got something better than a sword. Jace and I took a beating in that dimensional barrier before, but that was because we weren't prepared. This time, I'll be much more ready."

"Atiesh." Jace said.

"I guess I do mention it too much." The mage muttered.

"But Atiesh is sealed in Dalaran's vault. Will they let you take it?" Jace asked.

"Dalaran is a weapon. It exists to fight the Burning Legion, Jace." Khadgar's tone turned deadly serious. "Given the situation, what they allow or forbid doesn't matter anymore."

"I can take you to Dalaran, Khadgar." Sora said. "All teleportation magic has failed. Flying from here to Dalaran would take at least half a day. If you can't find a gryphon or dragonhawk rider..."

"Don't worry about that, Sora." Jace said. "He's already left."

"Well, you caught me." Khadgar sighed. Looking at the green dragon, he said, "Watch this insane lunatic for me, Sora. Don't let him do anything stupid. This projection won't last much longer."

"I promise you, mage." Sora said.
