Chapter 1832: Forced to Stand Aside

Trying to open his eyes, Jace saw a thick canopy of dark green leaves blocking the night sky. Silvery light filtered through the gaps, flickering faintly — he couldn't tell if it was starlight or moonlight seeping through.

His body was surrounded by purple flowers and grass. He could grab a handful of blossoms just by closing his fist. The air was filled with a refreshing fragrance.

Not far away lay a sapphire-blue pool. A light mist shrouded this deep forest glade. For a moment, it felt like being back in Ashenvale — no searing pain wracking his body, no chaotic, burning power threatening to explode in his chest, no stench vile enough to make him vomit blood. Just... stillness.

But Jace wasn't surprised to find himself lying here. He turned his head and saw a massive shadow crouched nearby. As it slowly raised its head to look at him, its long tail curled around, gradually encircling his body. Even that made almost no sound.

"Somaliel," he murmured.

"Call me Sora, Jace." The green dragon stretched her neck over and laid her head beside him, fixing a single eye like a moonlit gem onto this fragile, tiny mortal.

"Sora... it's hard to call you that when you look like this."

Jace sat up and looked at her — the horns on her head spreading like a sapling, her massive wings folded like a skirt draped around her — and said dazedly, "I think... you've grown bigger since I last saw you."

"The Queen Ysera wanted me to grow up quickly." Sora said softly. "She gave me favor I didn't deserve, hoping I'd become one of the guardians of the Dream one day. I... must have disappointed her. But she never took back her blessing."

"Then that means you haven't disappointed her." Jace said.

"It's me healing you, not you healing me, Jace." The green dragon narrowed her eyes, her voice as light as a whisper.

She seemed to be smiling, but the enormous reptile's face didn't have that much nuance. Jace couldn't quite tell.

And from his experience dealing with dragons — especially black ones — being this close to a dragon's mouth usually meant smelling blood, sulfur, or charcoal. But next to Sora, he smelled none of that. Just a faint floral scent, indistinct, reminding him of the little room Sora kept in the Mage Quarter.

"A few years ago, before I received Queen Ysera's blessing, I had already slept in the Emerald Dream for a thousand years, but I was still just a whelp." Sora said. "I remember the way you looked at me back then, Jace. Like I was a monster. Like I was a snake with wings grafted on — not much different from one of those Hakkari children..."

"Did I really say that?" Jace asked.

"Then you forgot." There was a note of complaint in her tone.

"We didn't know each other back then. I was still wary of you. You're a dragon, Sora — completely different from us." Jace said. "But I swear, both the you from back then and the you from now are beautiful. Just... different kinds of beautiful."

"Mmm." Sora raised an eyebrow. "I thought you could only appreciate someone like Vereesa, or Morofil — someone closer to human form..."

"Morofil?" Jace gestured vaguely above his head. "You think I'd like a demon? Something with horns on its head, spikes on its back, bat wings, a weird tail, and hard scales on its shoulders and ankles..."

He trailed off, feeling like something wasn't right. He looked the green dragon up and down. Sora had already turned her massive head to stare at him with both eyes.

"Ahem. It's mainly the hooves." Jace quickly patted his boots. "Mainly those cloven hooves. I don't actually have that much of a problem with the other parts."

Sora's shoulders shook with laughter. She rolled slightly, stretching lazily like she was enjoying herself. Her body was so massive, yet even movements that large didn't seem to crush a single blade of grass beneath her. Instead, every living thing around her seemed to reach out eagerly, hoping she'd come closer, trying to embrace her.

But in that motion, Jace caught a glimpse of his own reflection in the jade-like scales sliding past his face — perfectly still, steady.

He looked... like a completely different person from the fel-rotted wreck he'd been moments ago.

He stood up at once. The green dragon started to speak, then swallowed her words, watching in confusion as he walked toward the pool.

Jace dropped to his knees at the water's edge and stared at his reflection. A clean, sharp, tall, sturdy man — no trace of demon, corruption, or decay. For a moment, he felt almost... unfamiliar.

"Is this me?" "Of course it's you, Jace."

"So I've recovered. And I look better than when I was healthy." Jace got up. "Let me wake up, Sora. I need to leave this cave."

"No. No." Sora shifted, stretching her long neck to his side and looking down at the reflection in the water. "This is... the you in my dream. You're recovering quickly, but you're far from ready to leave. What you see is different from what you really look like in the outside world. Don't rush to leave now. Khadgar asked me to take good care of you..."

"Grede. Vereesa. They're all fighting the Burning Legion." The warlock looked up, meeting the green dragon's eyes. "Knowing that, I can't heal in peace."

"You're holding the power of the Eye of Sargeras, Jace. You are the key factor keeping the Burning Legion's lord from descending on Azeroth." The green dragon said. "They have their fight. We have ours. Imagine if Vereesa and the others knew your condition — would they want you to leave, to risk yourself against the demons?"

"If they knew I let you go, they'd blame me for being weak, for not insisting you stay. Jace, don't make me make such a terrible mistake. It's the fel corruption making you restless, unable to calmly assess your situation. Listen to me. Listen to Khadgar. You'll recover faster, regain your strength, and then you can turn that raging demonic power back against itself."

Jace sighed helplessly and sat down.

Just then, the canopy above seemed to tremble. The ground shuddered. Sora looked up warily, not having expected this disturbance — especially since this was a dream she had woven.

Jace felt his eyelids grow heavy. The unnatural tremor made it impossible to stay peacefully in this dream.

He forced his eyes open again. The tranquil forest was gone. Instead, he saw the flickering arcane brazier Khadgar had left burning, its light dancing unsteadily not far away.

The mine had grown strange ferns — nourished by Sora's magic, he knew. But most of them had already been killed by fel energy or twisted into thorn-covered, half-dead weeds oozing green pus.

The dull itching and throbbing pain reclaimed his exhausted consciousness. Only the cool hand gently resting on his forehead offered any relief.

"What's happening outside?" Jace asked.

"The gryphon riders are attacking the portal." Sora said. "The shockwaves from the arcane energy reached the mine. The magical backwash is everywhere. I think the Alliance has retaken the initiative and pushed the line back to the island, giving the dwarves an opening."

"But the portal's still standing. I can feel it!" Sazeno hopped in from the cave entrance, shouting. "Those arcane bombs are powerful, but they didn't destroy the portal. I knew it wouldn't be that easy. Those Eredar, the Ered'uin demons, and the guardian..."

"Khadgar's stopped maintaining that projection." Sora looked at the arcane fire. "That means he's fully committed to his goal — whether it's retrieving the legendary staff Atiesh or finding the guardian to destroy the portal."

"Nothing's decided yet, Mister Sazeno. Don't be so quick to judge." "Heh, convincing me doesn't matter, Lady Green Dragon. You need to convince my master..."

Jace closed his eyes. He couldn't settle down. Maybe it was because he could only watch his friends fight from the sidelines, the frustration gnawing at him.

Or maybe it was because Sazeno was right — he was deeply worried about everything happening outside.

Another tremor rumbled through the cave. The strange, wailing sound of magic crossed the vast forest and reached the hills. For now, Jace could only steady himself and accept the healing. Sora had already poured nearly all her energy into helping him recover from the catastrophic magical chaos.

Even if it was only to rejoin the fight as soon as possible, he couldn't waste her efforts on pointless internal turmoil.
