Chapter 1833: Atiesh

"Make way! Make way, colleagues! Fellow mages!"

A chestnut mare galloped through the rain-soaked streets of Dalaran. Not a single Kirin Tor guard dared block her path — because riding her was the long-missing Archmage Khadgar.

"Archmage Khadgar?" "Archmage Khadgar is back... There's hope!"

"I sure hope so." Khadgar stared at the arcane light atop the Violet Citadel's spire, muttering, "Hope so. Damn it."

"Archmage Khadgar! Archmage Khadgar!"

Khadgar heard a familiar voice among the calls. He reined in sharply — his horse whinnied, nearly slipping on the wet purple-red cobblestones — and looked toward the source. A middle-aged wizard in a short green robe stood beside a doorway, looking up at him.

"Perfect timing, Master Vadras. I need to talk to you!" Khadgar dismounted and strode over. "I hope the agreement I made with Dalaran's alchemist still holds, even with the end of the world at our doorstep!"

"Destruction Potion." The green-robed wizard said. "I know what you're referring to, Archmage Khadgar. So what exactly happened? That portal..."

"I'll find time to explain everything after I destroy it." Khadgar wiped the rain from his face. "I need more than the Destruction Potion. I also need a powerful enough Arcane Protection potion — the improved recipe we brought back from Draenor. I'm counting on you, dear Detris. Don't cut corners this time."

"Like you said, the end of the world is almost here, Khadgar. I understand, I understand..." Detris Vadras sighed. "Come inside first."

Just then, the sound of young dragons echoed from the dark sky. The archmage knew that sound all too well — nearly twenty years ago, Dalaran had heard something similar. The red dragonflight, driven by orcs, had descended on Hillsbrad, spreading fear and despair.

But this time, the red dragons were on his side. He knew the reds, their queen included, had been severely wounded by the endless disasters and torments. They still hadn't recovered — the more powerful the dragon, the harder the fall, and the harder the healing.

But in this crisis, Azeroth... everyone, no matter their circumstances, needed to come to Lake Lordamere and do their part. The reds understood that better than he did.

Following the alchemist inside, the man turned and said, "Prismatic Miracle Elixir — the recipe your Sons of Lothar brought back from Draenor. I still have a few bottles, Archmage Khadgar."

He handed Khadgar a square bottle filled with a strange, bright purple liquid, then said, "As for the Destruction Potion — you know that since Blackrock Spire, Dalaran has been living under the shadow of plague and demonic infiltration. Considering the dangers from the Twisting Nether, we never successfully cultivated the Void Blossoms needed for Destruction Potion. We've made some progress recently, but this portal..."

"So you don't have it." Khadgar said.

The alchemist started to speak, but Khadgar cut him off. "I know Destruction Potion is extremely dangerous. You don't need to remind me, Detris..."

"No." The alchemist said. "I was going to say that Lady Ursula Delin of Stormwind is in Southshore. She was supposed to come to Dalaran for a conference on Destruction Potion, but the Fenris Isle incident stranded her there. Maybe she has samples..."

"Ursula Delin?" Khadgar shoved his Archmage's Ring into the alchemist's hand. "Find the fastest gryph— no, find a dragonhawk rider. Bring her to Dalaran, Detris. Show them my ring. Tell the elves it's a personal favor."

"Alright. I'll go right away." The alchemist took the ring and asked, "But what exactly are you planning, Khadgar?"

"I don't want to repeat myself. Destroy the portal! You think I was joking, damn it?" Khadgar turned and walked back into the rain, mounting his horse, then called back, "When she gets to Dalaran, tell her to wait for my message. I'll find her!"

Without waiting for a nod, Khadgar kicked his horse back onto the street and rode hard toward the Violet Citadel. He could feel the entire ley line network trembling. It felt like he was racing the entire Burning Legion. Which, of course, he was.

Antonidas was in the castle. He knew it. The Archmage of Dalaran was the city's last line of defense. He had to stay here and ensure the city could provide magical support for the entire Alliance.

Leaving his horse at the citadel's stables, Khadgar climbed quickly to the high terrace, where Antonidas was speaking with several Kirin Tor members, including Drenden.

From this height, looking toward the center of Lake Lordamere, even through the heavy rain and mist, the strange ring of arcane light was still visible.

"Archmage Antonidas!"

The Archmage turned, and along with the other mages, looked surprised to see Khadgar gasping for breath.

"The Kirin Tor has been searching everywhere for you and Master Jace Sesso, Archmage Khadgar." Drenden said. "Only you two can tell us what happened at Fenris Keep."

"Whatever happened, it's right in front of all of you now, gentlemen." Khadgar said. "Jace and I barely managed to interfere with Lady Aegwynn's plans. Now..."

He glanced at Antonidas and sighed. "I know you might have been a little disappointed to hear we were still alive, Master. But please hear me out."

Antonidas nodded. Khadgar continued, "I'm guessing you've already noticed — that portal used the Order of Tirisfal's dimensional barrier to bind the ley lines of the entire region, including Dalaran's. That means all our spellcasting is affected. But it also means... we can use those same ley lines against it."

"Correct. But Lady Aegwynn's and the portal's magical strength is nearly unassailable. We already tried to attack it earlier." Antonidas said.

"But you haven't tried Atiesh." Khadgar swallowed, then added with a hint of uncertainty, "Find a trustworthy, powerful enough mage..."

"To put it plainly — me." He jabbed his own chest. "Let me wield Atiesh's power again. The power of the Guardian. Maybe I can wrest back control of the ley lines."

"The gryphon riders are attacking Fenris Isle. Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider has even deployed his secret weapon. I can feel the portal and its magical barrier under unprecedented pressure. I just need to get that staff. When the gryphon riders launch their next assault, I can seize control of the ley line network."

"Are you sure, Archmage Khadgar?" Drenden asked. "Are you sure you can control the Guardian's staff?"

"Sure? What's there to be sure of?" Khadgar stepped forward, looking at the assembled mages. "I know it's worth a try. If you don't have the confidence, Master Drenden, then I'll do it. Or anyone else who does. But we have to try!"

Seeing the mages lost in thought, he pressed urgently, "We can't delay any longer. We don't have time to wait for all our allies to get here. The blue dragonflight hasn't been heard from. The reds only just arrived. The Kaldorei might still be at sea..."

"By the time everyone's ready for a final assault, it'll be too late. This portal is almost complete. Don't let the time Jace Sesso and I fought so hard to buy slip through our fingers, Master. He paid a terrible price for it. I was the lucky one. So I suggest we recall Modera and Vargos now. Call an emergency meeting..."

"There's no condition for an emergency meeting anymore. Come with me." Antonidas looked at Drenden beside him. "Master Drenden and I, along with the staff's guardian, Archmage Angela Dosantus, can open the vault for you."

"Because I know that even if everyone disagreed, you'd find a way to take it yourself — and you've got plenty of tricks to pull that off, especially now, with Dalaran at its most vulnerable. I only hope that when this is over, Khadgar, you'll take responsibility for the unforeseen consequences of your decision. Don't make me regret this."

Drenden looked at Antonidas, as if about to say something, but swallowed it and nodded in agreement.

Khadgar immediately made a vow. "Full responsibility. I'll bear all of it. You won't regret this, Master. Thank you."

Antonidas nodded slightly to Drenden, murmured some instructions to the other mages, and turned into the doorway.

Following the two Council of Six members down the spiral staircase, Khadgar felt his heart start to pound.

Truth be told, he didn't actually have the confidence to break into Dalaran's vault alone in a short time. But he had been fully prepared to fight anyone who tried to stop him to the death.

Because Atiesh... was the only solution he could think of. Use the Guardian's power against the Guardian.

He wasn't a Guardian himself. Holding Atiesh felt a little fraudulent. But Aegwynn was also at the end of her rope now. That evened things out. Sort of.

Antonidas, Drenden, and Dosantus chanted in unison, unlocking the Guardian's vault within Dalaran's treasury.

The white Violet Eye relief on the heavy stone door opened its lid as the gate swung wide, revealing the hall lit by arcane magic. Atiesh sat on a pedestal in the center, kept here in isolation.

Before being returned to Dalaran, this staff had always been with Medivh. It looked like nothing more than an ordinary oaken branch — remarkably straight, but otherwise unremarkable.

The raven carving on the staff's head occasionally flashed with eerie arcane light. The first time Khadgar had seen it, at seventeen, he'd thought the somewhat round little carving looked almost comical... completely unfitting for Medivh, the mysterious wizard feared by all seven kingdoms.

Until he learned that this was the staff Aegwynn had carried when she defeated the avatar of Sargeras. The staff of every Guardian. The staff of legacy.
