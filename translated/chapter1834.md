## Chapter 1834: Just in Case

It was very early, the sky dark and heavy. Normally, by this time, it should have been getting light.

The sporadic rain that had fallen for days carried a faint burnt stench. On Krasus Square, several dwarf messengers on gryphons headed off in different directions, carrying the Kirin Tor's orders for the next phase of the attack. The assault was set for three o'clock that afternoon — less than ten hours away.

The magical bombs crafted by the elves might not be enough to collapse the portal directly, but they could deal a serious blow to the island's magical barrier. And at that crucial moment, Khadgar believed that using Dalaran's position as a ley line nexus to "counter-bite" the portal, combined with the power of Atiesh, wasn't an impossible task.

The problem was: within ten hours, they needed to contact every gryphon and dragonhawk rider around Lake Lordamere and coordinate with Lordaeron's capital on the north shore.

With teleportation magic almost completely disabled and demons flying everywhere, even with the most elite Aerie Peak gryphon riders delivering messages, the conditions were brutally tight.

In all his years in Dalaran, admiring the lakeside scenery, this was the first time Khadgar felt that the sprawling Lake Lordamere was annoyingly large.

But Khadgar and the Kirin Tor couldn't afford more time.

The portal wasn't even complete yet, and it was already pouring countless demon legions into Azeroth. Those demons were in turn building their own portals along the shore, and with the magical advantage near the main portal, they could even construct Legion gates capable of summoning Annihilan-level demon lords.

Even on Draenor, the Sons of Lothar hadn't faced direct pit lord assaults more than a few times. That alone showed how much power Aegwynn had gathered at Fenris Keep using the Book of Medivh.

Who knows what kind of existence would step through once this portal was ready?

Watching the gryphon riders vanish into the gloomy sky, Khadgar silently prayed for their success.

Every link in this chain, every insignificant soldier, could be the deciding factor in the Alliance's victory or defeat — even the fate of Azeroth itself.

But Khadgar was prepared. If the Alliance forces on the north shore of Lake Lordamere couldn't coordinate with the Kirin Tor and his Atiesh...

In ten hours — or sooner, if the portal's power approached the danger threshold — regardless of whether there was a response, he would begin the ritual. He would fight with everything he had.

He'd never actually wielded Atiesh's power before. But he'd always believed that a way would present itself when the time came. When facing the Dark Portal on Draenor, he'd never unleashed the power of the Skull of Gul'dan either — but he'd still succeeded in closing the joint creation of Medivh and the entire Horde.

Destruction Potion... right, the Destruction Potion. He'd almost forgotten about it in the chaos. Two dragonhawks from the south had just landed on Krasus Square. Khadgar wondered if they'd brought Ursula Delin.

His original plan had been to steal Atiesh from the arcane vault, then find alchemist Ursula Delin on his way out to get the Destruction Potion. Now that everything had gone so smoothly, it had thrown off his carefully planned sequence.

Antonidas, Drenden — the Kirin Tor high-ups he'd always butted heads with — had turned out to be surprisingly cooperative. Azeroth really must be at its most dangerous point, the mage thought.

Khadgar walked out of the Violet Citadel, heading for Krasus Square. But Ursula Delin was already at the bottom of the castle steps, led by a dragonhawk rider.

"Ursula Delin..."
"Archmage!"

Ursula ran up the steps and immediately demanded, "Where's Jace Sesso?"

"Let me explain, Lady Delin." Khadgar coughed. Ursula didn't want to hear any of it. She cut him off, "They said you disappeared with Jace. You showed up. He didn't!"

"Don't make it sound like I deliberately abandoned him to save myself." Khadgar said. "I care about his life as much as you do! He saved my life more than once. He saved all our lives. Don't forget — when the portal at Auchindoun was about to be torn apart by the Scepter of Sargeras's warping power, who stood with you to the very last moment, completing the summoning ritual!"

Ursula let out a breath, seeming to calm down slightly. Then she muttered, "'Black Talon' Galders too."

"Yes. And Mister Galders." Khadgar nodded. "Before all this, we discovered the demons' and Aegwynn's plan and found their hideout. We tried to stop them from building the portal. We had to face Lady Aegwynn — but you know how impossible it is to defeat her. He was badly injured. Couldn't travel far. So I left him near the Lordamere docks to rest and came back to Dalaran alone."

"He's alive." Ursula stared at the mage.
"At least he was when I left." Khadgar said.

"Then I need to find him, Archmage." Ursula muttered, looking west. "Can you arrange a gryphon..."

"Every gryphon is precious right now, Ursula Delin." Khadgar said. "Of course, if Jace Sesso were at death's door and you carried a miracle cure that could save him, the Kirin Tor would gladly send an elite dragonhawk escort. But the thing is — he's got a green dragon taking care of him. I know you're one of Stormwind's best alchemists, but even you'd admit you're better at driving people crazy with your potions than keeping them alive."

"Say no more." Ursula frowned. "It's Sora, Somaliel, isn't it?"

"Yes."

Khadgar watched the female alchemist fall silent for a moment, then said, "No need to worry. Even though this demon gate crisis is more severe than the Dark Portal twenty years ago, the Lordaeron Alliance has united like never before. Or maybe you could say they're only united because death is staring them in the face..."

He trailed off, realizing the alchemist seemed completely unfazed, only glancing westward from time to time. It dawned on him — she didn't care about any demon gate.

"And don't worry about Jace Sesso. He won't be joining the next fight, Lady Delin. I've convinced him to rest and heal. Once he's well enough to leave the Lordamere docks, Lady Sora will bring him back to Dalaran for further treatment. I'll take you to see him then."

"Thank you, Archmage." Ursula said.

"Not at all, Lady Delin. You're an important ally to both me and the Kirin Tor." Khadgar flashed a quick smile. "So, the Destruction Potion? Don't tell me you didn't bring any either."

"Ah. I did. I was going to demonstrate it for the Dalaran alchemists." Ursula snapped back to reality and pulled a potion pouch from her bag.

"But I only have two bottles, Archmage."

"Now that is genuinely good news." Khadgar sighed in relief as he took the pouch, examining the two small vials. "This precious stuff isn't meant for prolonged combat anyway. I only need it to fuel one magical ritual."

"Perfect. Let Lady Aegwynn see just how many new little toys we brought back from Draenor." He muttered.

"Within the next day, things are going to get very chaotic." The mage's voice dropped as he addressed the alchemist. "The safest place right now is probably Dalaran itself — the barrier still protects it. Leave the city, and you could run into flying demons anywhere. So stay in the city. Don't go out. Until... it's over. Find the alchemy lab. You should know the people there — Detris, for one. He'll help you find somewhere to stay."

"I understand, Archmage." Ursula replied.

Khadgar nodded slightly to the elven dragonhawk rider and said in Thalassian, "Thank you, knight. You've been a great help. I'll report this to Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider personally."

He turned to walk up the steps, then stopped. He looked at the two potion vials in the pouch.

"Ursula Delin!"

He turned back, calling to the alchemist who was about to leave. "I haven't followed the research progress on Destruction Potion here in Dalaran. But from what Detris Vadras told me, the Void Blossom cultivation isn't going well. If I needed to brew another bottle of this right now, how long would it take?"

"Impossible under current conditions." Ursula shook her head. "It's not a raw material problem, Archmage. Destruction Potion has very strict magical environmental requirements. As long as this portal exists, even if every other condition were perfect, it couldn't be made."

The mage handed one bottle back to her. "You keep this, Lady Delin. I only need one bottle for the ritual. If it doesn't work, more wouldn't help anyway."

"What am I supposed to do with this one?" Ursula asked.

Khadgar swallowed. After a moment's hesitation, he said, "If something goes wrong... leave it for Jace Sesso."

"I understand." Ursula said.

"Hopefully... it won't come to that." Khadgar said.
