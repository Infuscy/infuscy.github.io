## Chapter 1836: Arcane Bomb

The portal's magical glow bleached the lake surface white beneath the night sky. From above, the floating debris — wrecked timber, demons, soldiers, innocent corpses, shattered ships — looked like half-buried bones.

Grede Thunderfist felt like he couldn't look directly at the portal. A moment longer and he'd be blinded. He kept his head tilted, doing his best to keep his gryphon on course.

"I know it hurts, lad." He patted Goljet's head in Dwarven. "But think about it — Jace Sesso might've been burned into a black cinder by this door already. That's way worse than what we're going through. Bear with it. We'll be there soon. Drop the payload and we're out!"

The ash-white gryphon let out a low, trembling whine. Not from fear — Grede knew that — but from the growing pressure of the ley lines, which made every mortal and beast in this area suffer terribly.

Fenris Isle had been torn apart by the ley line veins. Fissures glowing with purple-blue arcane energy spread across it like a spiderweb, reaching all the way into the lake. The densest, deepest cracks ran south toward Dalaran, disappearing only in the deepest part of the lake bed.

The catastrophic magical devastation on Dalaran's north shore was probably caused by the collapse of those ley lines.

Grede once heard a mage say that the ley lines were Azeroth's blood vessels, and arcane energy was the blood flowing through them. He'd never really understood that analogy. Today, seeing this, he finally got it.

At the edges of those arcane chasms, survivors were still trying to flee toward the shore. Their wavering silhouettes were lit by the flickering fel flames — at any moment, they could fall into the cracks or be casually crushed by passing demons.

Grede cursed under his breath and glanced back at the two elven magisters riding gryphons. By now, the arcane roar from the portal had made shouting completely useless. But they'd already prepared for this — light signals.

Aegwynn's arcane barrier was completely invisible to the naked eye. It only appeared when struck by a heavy blow — that was why the last bombing run had failed.

But the last run had let Quel'Thalas's mages figure out the barrier's rough range and strength. When the magisters raised their wands and gave a pale arcane signal, he would drop Kael'thas's magical bomb. Then the magisters would chant to detonate it. Simple and straightforward.

The magisters hadn't given the signal yet. So he and the gryphon riders just had to fly as high as possible with the bomb, like the elves had instructed before they approached Fenris Isle.

Among the ruins of Fenris Keep, red-robed Sunfury knights, Stormpike dwarves, and Silver Hand vanguard had already landed by boat and were locked in battle with swarms of demons. Since Lordaeron's harbor had been retaken, red dragons flying in from across the continent had reached Lake Lordamere, helping the Alliance's air forces hunt flying demons.

They hadn't fully gained air superiority yet, but they'd created enough of an opening for the transport ships to attempt a beach landing.

But against the endless stream of demons pouring from the portal, these fierce vanguard fighters — however terrifying in combat — would eventually be overwhelmed by the growing numbers of demons on the ground and in the air.

Grede and the Wildhammer knights with him knew it well. Like the red dragons and dragonhawk riders circling the island and tangling with doomguards, they were all answering the Kirin Tor's call for a temporary assault — drawing the demons' attention, buying the gryphon riders the opening to drop the arcane bombs and weaken the portal.

Otherwise, a single squad of gryphon riders charging straight at the portal... even the High Thane himself would be no more than a beetle diving into an anthill.

He glanced back at the magisters again. Both elves saw him look and raised their hands, signaling to keep going forward. The time wasn't right yet. They needed to get closer.

The portal was at the highest point of Fenris Isle — where the castle used to be. From the shore, it would take a few more minutes of flying. But even at this distance, the light was already so blinding that Grede wondered if he'd gone blind.

At least he could still make out the dark specks emerging from within the radiance.

"Demons inbound!" Grede roared. "Protect the elves!"

He cried out in the language of the elements, summoning lightning. Even though the portal's immense magic was nearly tearing apart the elemental energies of the Lake Lordamere region, thunder still pierced the thick clouds of fel and shadow, crashing into his Saffras and erupting in a spray of molten fire.

Seeing the signal to engage, even the gryphon riders who hadn't yet spotted the demons coming from the portal called down lightning, readying themselves.

A horde of doomguards howled as they charged. Grede counted a dozen at a glance. A gryphon rider at the front was struck by a shadow bolt and fell screaming. Flying hammers wreathed in lightning crossed paths with spiraling shadow bolts. Demonic roars and dwarven curses crashed over one another.

Grede yanked on the reins, climbing hard, swinging Saffras with the other hand. Among the Alliance's gryphon riders, you'd be hard-pressed to find another gryphon in all of Alterac that could lift both Grede and Saffras through this kind of ascent.

A laughing doomguard gathered a shadow bolt in its palm — only to have Grede slam into it with his hammer, turning it into a fireball that plunged into the burning pines below.

Grede looked back. The magisters were breaking free of the doomguard encirclement with a few gryphon riders. "Don't waste time, elf! Keep moving!"

More and more doomguards came from both sides. Even the gryphon riders couldn't hold them. One magister, trying to slow a demon's pursuit, was hit by a Cripple spell that entangled his gryphon, sending them both tumbling into the forest in a flash of light. The other magister was barely keeping up with Goljet. But Grede was too busy smashing surrounding demons with Saffras to protect the elf.

Dragonhawk riders in the distance seemed to notice the dwarves' trouble, but Grede had no time to track them. Suddenly, Goljet screamed. Grede felt the stench of shadow corrosion spreading from beneath him — Goljet had been hit by shadow magic.

"Gol—"

Before he could even finish the gryphon's name, Grede was sent flying. A doomguard caught him midair with one hand — but the dwarf punched the demon in the face, raised Saffras, and smashed it into a burning lump of scrap metal and flesh.

He wasn't in great shape either. He plummeted straight into the burning forest.

For a dwarf like him, scrapes and bruises were nothing. He wiped the fel-filthy mud off his face, coughed twice, and spotted the ash-white gryphon struggling into the forest ahead. He started running after it.

Goljet was in bad shape. The gryphon lay in a mud pit, unable to get up. The terrible roar of arcane magic overhead mixed with demonic howls. Doomguards could arrive any second.

"They're coming for me — for this bomb, Goljet. I'll take it and leave. You'll be fine. They won't care about a gryphon. Though you might be the biggest gryphon they've ever seen."

"Anyway, don't follow me. Find a way to escape. As far as you can."

The gryphon whimpered in pain, as if refusing to agree. That made the dwarf furious. He jumped up, grabbed the long feathers on Goljet's head, and twisted the gryphon's face toward his own. "You trying to mutiny, you idiot? I'm the High Thane now! Even Kurdran Wildhammer answers to me! And that goes double for you! So you do what I say — find a way to get yourself the hell out!"

The ash-white gryphon whined. Grede spat, hoisted the magic-sealed arcane bomb onto his back, looked around, and scanned the sky. The other gryphon riders and both magisters were gone.

"Elves... useless as always!"

Grede didn't bother with anyone else. Cursing loudly, he charged toward the portal.

Doomguards in the sky seemed to notice the glow from his warhammer. One after another, they descended to block his path. But these Ered'uin were no match for a true Mountain King.

In the air, riding a gryphon, he couldn't show his true form. Down here, he didn't have to hold back.

The dwarf had transformed into the likeness of an Earthen. Even Saffras looked like a smith's hammer in his hands. Even the felguards charging at him didn't look much bulkier.

"You spike-ridden, horn-headed, winged, hoofed freaks. I've been putting up with you long enough. Die! Die! Die! Die!"

Grede took down demons one hammer at a time, carving a bloody path through the ruins of Fenris Keep's outer wall. He pushed toward the broken ramparts. One felguard after another fell. He smashed one's chest in, tossed it aside, and looked up at the portal above him. He spotted an arcane projectile flying from somewhere — it struck a thin, nearly invisible surface and vanished as if it had never existed.

A felguard behind him slammed an axe into his head. Sparks flew. Grede turned, grabbed the axe blade, wrenched it away, and headbutted the Mo'arg's chest, caving it in. He kicked the demon down.

He spat. With no more demons in pursuit for the moment, he raised the arcane bomb with both hands, took aim at where the earlier projectile had disappeared, and hurled it with a mighty roar.

The bomb shot into the night sky like it had been launched from a catapult. Grede watched it go and muttered, "Sorry, elf. I'm doing this my way. Screw your instructions..."

"I know you're here... Thunderaan! You... now! Help me blast that bomb! Just one blast! Make it count!"

He shouted in the elemental tongue, though his grammar and word choice were a mess in his haste.

More demons closed in. Grede spun and smashed the nearest one, then drove Saffras into the ground, unleashing a pillar of flame that blew the rest apart. The shockwave sent nearby rubble and unshattered eaves flying.

A doomguard dropped from the sky and grabbed Grede by the shoulders, trying to lift him — but couldn't budge him.

"Even a black dragon can't lift me. Who the hell do you think you are?!"

The dwarf turned, looked up, and punched the demon's head sideways with a curse. Another punch to its chest.

The demon fell. Then — a bolt of lightning split the sky, striking something in the night that shouldn't have been there. A pinpoint of light appeared at the impact site. Then the world fell silent.

A piercing whine replaced every sound that had existed before.

The High Thane stared blankly as that pinpoint of light erupted into blinding white.

"Your turn now, mages..."
