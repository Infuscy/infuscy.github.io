## Chapter 1837: The Descent

A silent flash illuminated the distant, roiling black sky. Standing at the edge of an altar near the top of the Violet Citadel, Khadgar turned and shouted to the three archmages already standing within the arcane circle — Antonidas, Modera, and Drenden: "The Wildhammer knights have completed their task. Our turn now. The ritual can begin, archmages!"

The three Kirin Tor council members nodded silently. The guards stationed at the altar's edge gripped their staves nervously.

In the distance, the faint outline of the pine forest began to sway. Then the lake water heaved. The shockwave soon reached Dalaran. Many buildings already damaged by the portal's initial explosion had their roofs and tiles blown off. Panicked shouts filled the streets.

Compared to the first arcane bomb strike on the portal, the city's residents took this one a little better.

The mages closed their eyes and took their positions, raising their staves high. Arcane runes materialized around the circle, arranged like a rotating wheel — a kaleidoscopic barrier that was dizzying to look at.

Khadgar walked through the circle to the center of the three mages. He glanced at the quiet raven carving atop his staff, then slowly raised it higher.

"You know what you'll face if you fail, Archmage Khadgar." Modera opened her eyes slightly between chants. "Manipulating the ley line network is incredibly dangerous. Even Malygos's eldest son miscalculated once and was obliterated by the backlash — not even bones left."

"Don't scare me with bedtime stories from apprentice readers, Lady Modera." Khadgar looked at her. "If I fail, you won't be much better off than me. And every living thing for thousands of miles around will be even worse."

Modera was silent for a moment. "You're right."

"So let's say something nicer. Like 'good luck to us all.'"

Khadgar had already downed a bottle of refined Elixir of Wisdom and a strong Elixir of Fortitude. Casting a spell of this magnitude required a resilient body.

He closed his eyes and downed the last bottle of Destruction Potion. Compared to the thunderous roar that slammed through his mind as the potion took effect, its bitter, acrid taste was nothing.

The mage didn't waste the potion's fleeting effect. He raised Atiesh and began the incantation.

In the dark field of his closed eyes, pale blue threads blossomed like spiderwebs or capillaries, spreading outward. Khadgar seized one thread without hesitation, then another, then a third.

He picked out specific threads with precision and bound them together — the result of meticulous calculation.

Under the amplification of the Guardian's staff and Dalaran's ley line nexus, the threads offered almost no resistance. Pulling them was as easy as pulling a loose thread from fabric.

In over thirty years of life, Khadgar had never felt the ley lines so docile and obedient. It was as if every hard lesson he'd learned, every pattern he'd deduced, every trial and error, had been mere tricks.

Lake Lordamere churned with his movements. The entire city of Dalaran began to shake. Cracks opened in the streets and squares, releasing brilliant, multicolored arcane light. A sound like chiming wind bells echoed from Hillsbrad to the Alterac mountains, from the capital to even Deathknell.

For a moment, he felt elated — as if the entire continent rested at his fingertips, ready to be molded and shaped.

For a moment, he almost understood Lady Aegwynn and his own teacher. With power like this, who could let go so easily?

At the center of all those threads stood another manipulator.

Khadgar pulled at the threads confidently. The shining "ball of thread" thirty miles away began to destabilize under his tugging, fragile as a lamb to slaughter. One more squeeze and it would collapse entirely.

Then he felt another hand reach down from above and grasp that "ball of thread." Another hand casually seized every connected thread.

Khadgar's brow furrowed sharply. The Violet Citadel itself began to tremble. The Kirin Tor guards couldn't stay at the edge anymore — they fell back, steadying themselves against the castle walls and statues.

Looking north, the light of the arcane bomb had already died over the lake. But the ring of light hadn't reignited either — it had dimmed until it was barely visible.

Multicolored magical arrows streaked through the forest, piercing a doomguard's heart cleanly. The ranger gripped Belanore, her focus sharp as she picked off every demon that emerged from the portal.

Draw. Summon arrow. Loose. Draw. Summon arrow. Loose...

*Swoosh.* An arrow veered off course, drifting up into the sky as if uncontrolled.

Vereesa's heart was still. She immediately drew a spare elven arrow from her hip, nocked it, and began a brief incantation to enchant it with arcane energy — but even the incantation failed.

She looked up. The fel curtain of the portal before her suddenly blazed brighter. Behind it, black clouds churned over Lake Lordamere to the south. Shadow and fel lightning burst without pause. The ground shook. Nearby rangers and soldiers couldn't keep their footing. The demons around the portal roared, shrieking.

Before another collapsed portal, atop countless Mo'arg and Ered'uin corpses, Morofil smiled at the fel-fire eyes of her demonic sister.

"Hope to see you again soon, dear Lady Auraladeil. Honestly, today's fight was barely satisfying."

"A pity I couldn't tear off your wings again this time, my lovely Molly. Your demon wings are even more beautiful than I remembered..."

In the increasingly violent fel wind, the succubus's severed head dangled by its hair, swaying back and forth as she murmured triumphantly, "But he'll take them off for me as a souvenir soon enough. Can you feel him? He..."

Morofil's smile vanished. She stomped a hoof through the headless demon's chest beneath her. The head in her hand screamed as its eyes burned out, its face twisted and withered. She tossed it aside in disgust.

The demoness looked south. The fel wind flattened the pines in that direction. The Sayad was right. She could feel it.

On the walls, Turalyon was discussing the next phase of the counterattack with King Terenas when a sudden tremor interrupted them.

After days of shaking since the portal's appearance, everyone had grown used to it — the capital's walls were sturdy enough.

But soon both men realized this was different. The tremors grew more violent and showed no sign of stopping. The shockwaves rolling in from the portal carried a suffocating stench.

Ranger-General Sylvanas, stationed higher on the wall, leaned out and shouted down, asking what was happening. But her Farstriders, even her lieutenants, looked equally confused.

"What now, Marshal Turalyon?" the king asked. Turalyon could only shake his head.

On the dark sea, a fleet of Kaldorei ships cut through the storm, parting wave after wave.

On the lead ship's deck, a group of elves braved the gale to gather at the crescent moon decoration on the bow. The lead elf's face was pale in the swaying moonlight.

She stared east toward their destination. Dark. Oppressive. The entire continent lay shrouded in gloom.

"Are we too late, Priestess?" a priestess asked quietly in Kaldorei.

"Speed up." Tyrande Whisperwind commanded.

Southshore had been in chaos for days. Dawn hadn't broken in what felt like forever. Ships from every nation kept arriving, but no one could say what was happening beyond the Alterac mountains — not even the mages who'd traveled from there.

Today, a group of tall, blue-skinned strangers, stranger-looking than orcs, arrived.

An old man, clearly their leader, stepped onto the pier. The dockworkers and townspeople whispered among themselves as they watched. Then a violent tremor from the north shattered the spectacle. The entire harbor erupted in panic.

"He's coming, Prophet." A Draenei guard in silver armor said.

"Yes." The Prophet's expression was calm. He looked west, his cracked lips quivering slightly, but he said nothing more.

On a hillside, demon corpses lay strewn everywhere. A group of orcs stood in silent mourning around two fallen comrades. A young orc suddenly turned to look down the slope. A shockwave bent every pine tree low. Several orcs were knocked off their feet, tumbling into trees.

A long-haired orc carrying a bloody axe steadied himself, walked a few steps down the slope, then looked back at the younger one standing higher up. "The true battle has come."

"I know." The young orc replied.

"Don't think. Don't even think!"

Atop the Violet Citadel, the archmages could barely keep their footing in the magical gale, struggling to maintain the circle. Khadgar, at the center, gripped Atiesh with white knuckles, eyes clenched shut, teeth grinding.

He poured every ounce of his will into tearing at those threads. Even with the ley line nexus at his back, even with Atiesh in his hands, he felt like an ant trying to shake a tree.

"This is impossible." Khadgar whispered. "This can't..."

"If you'd spent ten thousand years traveling the Twisting Nether like I have, you'd understand one thing, mortal. Nothing... is impossible."

In his vision, a pair of fel-green demonic eyes snapped open. Khadgar felt the thousands of threads converge into one. The single thread remaining in his hand snapped.

The upper half of the Violet Citadel shattered without warning. A deep, rumbling crash followed. Soldiers, mages, and civilians on the square and streets below screamed and fled.

It wasn't over. To the north, the sky and lake were consumed by white light. A glacial wave, far more violent than any shockwave before, swept across the entire city of Dalaran and kept going, toward Hillsbrad.

"What..."

Jace awoke with a violent coughing fit, spitting out another mouthful of blood.

Staring at the slightly blackened fresh blood on the ground, he could feel the fel corruption, which had been mostly healed, worsening again.

The mine shaft trembled continuously. Sazeno sat beside him, shaking all over, his fel-fire eyes nearly extinguished, replaced by a murky demonic orb.

"It's over. It's over. It's over..."

"Jace!" Sora hurried in, her voice trembling as she pointed behind her. "Outside, outside..."

Jace scrambled to his feet and stumbled after her to the cave entrance. What he saw stunned even him.

The sky had cleared. The filthy clouds of fel energy and chaotic shadow magic that had blotted out the sun moments ago might never have existed. As far as the eye could see, not a single cloud marred the sky. Even in his memories — even from before he'd left Lordaeron over a decade ago — Lake Lordamere had never been this clear.

But beneath the sky, the entire land — the Silverpine Forest — was buried under a thick layer of snow. A blinding white as far as the eye could see.

Among the trees, ice spears taller than the pines stood everywhere, like swords pointing west — or more precisely, radiating outward from the lake.

The lake surface was completely frozen. In the distance, a half-sunken Lordaeron warship was locked in place.

At the center of this endless white, beneath the clean sky, nearly invisible at the farthest reach of sight, stood a giant, faintly blue figure.

The giant stared at his right hand as if none of this — not this entire world — had anything to do with him.
