## Chapter 1838: The Snow and Ice of Dalaran

"You... did you see that?" Sola stared at the lake, stunned.

"The Defiler," Jace said.

"That's... him?" Sola murmured. "Ten thousand years ago, it took the Dragon Aspects united with the Kaldorei, wielding the Demon Soul, just to drive him back..."

Her voice sounded so out of place in the white, deathly silence -- brittle and fragile.

"Khadgar failed," Jace said. "Looks like luck isn't always on his side either. We're going back to Dalaran together to find him, Sola."

"Your corruption hasn't fully recovered yet, Jace." Sola blinked, as though being yanked back to reality. She swallowed hard and said, flustered, "But Khadgar won't allow me to--"

Jace grabbed Sola by the shoulders, cutting her off, and stared into her moonlit eyes. "Get a grip, Sola."

"The Defiler is already here. If we don't do something now, there won't be anything left to do later. What does it matter if the corruption recovers or not? And I'm going back to Dalaran, not charging headfirst at the Defiler on Fenris Isle. Do you understand?"

Sola nodded quickly. "I understand... Jace, I understand."

She stepped forward and, in a burst of kicked-up snow, transformed back into her true form. Fear and overwhelming dread were nearly consuming her reason.

Truth be told, Jace wasn't exactly calm himself, even though he'd been mentally preparing for this day ever since he learned of Aegwynn's plan.

And what about Aegwynn?

Weren't you planning to summon the Defiler, enslave this demon lord, and blast me with your finger of death to finish me off?

Now's the perfect opportunity, isn't it, Guardian? Why haven't you acted? What are you waiting for...

The swirling snow obscured his vision, and the distant forest blurred into indistinct shapes.

The arcane shockwave from that sudden, overwhelming ice spell seemed to only now be fully registering, beginning to more profoundly affect the climate around all of Lake Lordamere.

The lake had frozen solid. Jace could guess why Archimonde had done it -- the portal had brought him through, and now more demons would follow, pouring into this world through that same portal and his summoning magic.

With the lake no longer an obstacle, endless demonic soldiers and legion commanders could cross this newly created plain more easily, launching their assault on the capital of Lordaeron and Dalaran.

All he had to do was... crush the toughest roadblocks with the most decisive force, as fast as possible, and leave the remaining mess to his underlings.

*Faster, Sola. Just a little faster.*

He thought it, but said nothing. Sola was already flying as fast as she could; the forest below was beginning to warp from the churning dream magic.

Sazeno cowered in the pack, trembling, not daring to make a sound, as if he still refused to accept reality.

In the final moment before that demon appeared, Jace felt the shockwave of another arcane bomb detonating -- was that the Wildhammer knights' last attempt? *Grede Thunderfist, you old bastard, did you join the final charge?*

*Morofil Wind, where are you?*

*Needleflame, are you okay?*

The vast sky was a cloudless gray -- not a gryphon knight, not a red dragon, not even the demons that had been flying everywhere. A single Frost Nova had seemingly scoured the entire Lake Lordamere region clean.

But it wasn't true quiet. He could feel it.

Soul-screams from afar filled his senses. Dead soldiers, civilians who hadn't managed to flee, demons -- endless slaughtered demons.

These souls, even freed from their flesh-and-blood shells, couldn't escape this place to reach the Shadowlands or the Twisting Nether.

Just then, he heard someone calling out -- the voice sounded like Common, as if searching for comrades, for other soldiers. It came from the forest below but quickly grew indistinct.

Jace had no idea who was shouting or what they were saying, but that single sound of the living gave him hope -- it wasn't over yet. There was still a chance to salvage this.

Dalaran was ahead. From a distance, half the city was buried in snow and ice. On the ground, caravans fleeing the city and ordinary people escaping on foot stretched everywhere, shouts and cries for help echoing endlessly.

By now, Jace couldn't tell which were the shouts of the living and which were the screams of the dead.

Only the lower half of the Violet Citadel remained. If not for the long staircase and the violet-robed mages gathered around it, Jace wouldn't have recognized the place at all.

Sola landed in the square before the slope. All around, mages and soldiers still able to move were working desperately to clear the frost-locked rubble and doors, trying to rescue those trapped inside.

The streets were chaos. Everyone was acting on their own. Thankfully, the magical barrier sustained by the Eye of Dalaran was still barely holding, though its power was now pathetically weak.

Jace grabbed a passing violet-robed mage. "Khadgar! Where's Khadgar?"

"I don't know. Master Khadgar... I don't know..." The mage shook his head frantically.

Fear had completely consumed him. Here was a mage who wanted to help rescue the trapped, yet he chose to pick up a pickaxe or whatever tool was handy to break ice instead of chanting spells.

He wasn't alone. Most of the mages seemed unable to cast any effective spells now. Only the Kirin Tor members could still draw on arcane power to crack the ice. But compared to what their robes signified about their status and corresponding mana, that trickle of arcane power was pathetically thin.

Sola used a nature spell to heal the mage's nearly frostbitten hands. He stammered his thanks and rejoined the rescuers. Then, from the other end of the street, a familiar voice called out.

"Jace? Is that you?"

Jace turned toward the voice and saw Ursula Delin dodging through the bustling crowd, hurrying across the street, nearly slipping on the packed snow.

"Ursula?"

"Khadgar told me you were with Lady Sola." Ursula steadied her hood as she ran up, nodding a quick greeting to Sola before continuing, "I saw a green dragon fly here and chased after it immediately. It really is you..."

"Khadgar told you? Where did he go?" Jace asked.

"Earlier, he went to find the Council of Six. He said he needed to perform a ritual -- using the ley lines' power to destroy the portal." Ursula was out of breath. "Before this ice storm hit, he was at the top of the Citadel with several of Dalaran's leaders. I know Archmage Antonidas was badly wounded. The others haven't been found yet. I'm afraid... it doesn't look good. It's not just you, Jace -- half the Kirin Tor is looking for them."

"I need to see Antonidas. Where is he now?" Jace asked.

"At... the academy, I heard. That place seems less damaged." Ursula said.

"Thank you, Ursula." Jace said. "Thank you. Let's go check it out."

"Oh, Jace." The alchemist called out to the warlock just as he was about to leave.

"Before he left, Khadgar told me to give this to you." She pulled out the potion of destruction.

Jace took the potion. "He doesn't need it?"

"He took one bottle. He said the ritual only needs one... If it fails, I'm to give the remaining one to you." Ursula said.

---

In a desolate snowy valley, the blue-robed mage coughed, lifted his head from a snowdrift, and spat out a mouthful of cold grit.

He pushed himself up with all his might, raising his snow-caked eyelids to survey his surroundings. Where was this? Alterac? Winterspring? Somewhere in Northrend? He couldn't be sure.

The ley-line surge that had blown the top off the Violet Citadel's spire hadn't torn him to pieces on the spot, which was already more than he'd expected. After a desperate struggle to save himself, he hadn't counted on surviving intact, let alone waking up in the same place.

Thank the gods for Atiesh. And thank Azeroth. And thank his rotten teacher.

Directly manipulating the ley lines really didn't end well. *You were right about that, Modera. I just hope you didn't get dragged to the same place I did by the ley lines, only to wake up and immediately mutter "I told you so."*

But just then, he spotted a female mage standing in the snow not far away, watching him. He couldn't help but let out a bitter, self-deprecating laugh.

"Hand the Guardian's staff over to its rightful owner, Khadgar. Let me complete the final step quickly. I don't want any more innocents to die."

"Innocents? I can't believe you still dare to say that word, lady." Khadgar leaned heavily on Atiesh as he forced himself upright, his voice cold. "What do you want it for? To summon the Deceiver here as well?"

"You've seen Jace Sesso devouring the Eye of Sargeras, young man." The female mage lifted her chin slightly. "After witnessing his true nature, do you still intend to protect him? He's already lost control of Ulthalesh. That ending is marching toward us step by step. Only I can stop it."

"Funny you should say that, Lady Aegwynn. We're of one mind." Khadgar raised Atiesh. "Only I can stop all of this. And that includes you."

*Damn it,* the mage thought bitterly. *I really shouldn't have left that potion with Jace Sesso. Last time I regretted something this much was when I let Ner'zhul and the Scepter of Sargeras slip through my fingers at the Temple of Karabor.*
