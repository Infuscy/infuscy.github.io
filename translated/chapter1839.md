## Chapter 1839: Unwilling to Give Up

A rumbling hum rolled in from the horizon. Jace didn't want to imagine what was making that sound.

The demonic horde's iron hooves hadn't yet reached Dalaran, and the corrupters' magic hadn't yet touched this place, but it wouldn't be long now. The only reason they'd bought even this sliver of time was because Solara had flown at full tilt to catch up.

Khadgar, Drenden, and Modera — three archmages — were missing. Kael'thas Sunstrider was surely with the High Elf vanguard, somewhere near Lordaeron's royal city or in Tirisfal Glades destroying demon portals. For all they knew, he and the red dragonflight were already locked in battle with the main force Archimonde had summoned — out on the frozen lakes, through the woodlands. No one could count on them reaching Dalaran in time.

Antonidas couldn't die now. If Dalaran lost its anchor, how could it stand against the Defiler?

The academy's corridors and gardens were thick with Kirin Tor guards and mages carrying wounded back and forth, and a crowd of priests had gathered to help. At least in this moment of despair, the Holy Light still answered their call, healing ordinary people hurt by demonic magic.

Jace and Solara walked and asked, walked and asked, but no one knew exactly which building or which room Archmage Antonidas was recuperating in.

Chaos reigned. The academy had taken lighter damage than other parts of the city, but that was only relative.

Then Jace spotted Jaina Proudmoore hurrying across the corridor in the other direction. He caught up and called out, "Lady Proudmoore!"

Jaina startled, turned, and blinked at the approaching Jace. "Sesso... Archmage Jace Sesso?"

"Where is Archmage Antonidas?" Jace asked. "Is he all right?"

"He's badly wounded," Jaina said. "A kaldorei master from Kalimdor is treating him. Down this corridor, the hall at the very end."

"Kaldorei?" Jace thought for a moment, then asked, "Vereesa and Grede? Are they here too? And the black dragon... did she return to Dalaran?"

"They haven't come back yet," Jaina said. "As far as I know, the Ranger-Lord is still near the royal city, attacking the portal with the Quel'Thalas legions. Grede... High Thane Grede Thunderfist led the bombing run on the portal alongside the Kirin Tor archmages. Lady Fydegnia... I don't know. I think she left with the Windrunner Lord."

Hearing that, Jace could barely breathe. Swallowing felt like an effort.

He glanced north, but Dalaran's purple-red brick walls blocked the view entirely — almost as if they were hiding the one reality he couldn't bear to face.

"Thank you, Lady Proudmoore. Thank you."

"You're welcome..." Jaina hesitated, visibly struggling to keep her composure. "Archmage Sesso, you met with Archmage Khadgar and Lady Aegwynn, didn't you? Why... why did she do this? I don't understand... Because of her grudge against the Kirin Tor and Dalaran? Because... but she was the Guardian."

Jace didn't answer. Jaina let out a faint breath, her face almost bloodless, then bid them farewell and hurried off.

Because... you...

Jace tried to push the voice out of his head. He followed the direction Jaina had pointed, reached the end of the corridor, spoke with the Kirin Tor guards and mages at the door, and entered the room.

Unlike the frozen chaos outside, this chamber was rare in its stillness and warmth. Just as he'd thought — none of them were here. Not in Dalaran either.

The old mage Antonidas lay in bed under thick blankets, his face pale and gaunt as a frozen corpse dug from some ancient Northrend tomb.

At his bedside sat a kaldorei druid. Deep green hair and a beard covered half his face, spilling over his shoulders and bare lavender chest. A pair of magnificent antlers rose from the braids atop his head, making him look like an ancient tree bathed in moonlight.

"This leader of wizards is at his end, Jace Sesso," the druid said, turning his head. His amber eyes glimmered faintly.

His Common had a heavy accent but was fluent enough to match a native speaker. Jace didn't need Solara's help to recognize who this was.

A long time ago, his younger brother had eyes like those — but Jace hadn't treasured them. This man before him hadn't been gifted such a blessing at birth, but now it fell to him to give those extraordinary eyes an even greater meaning.

"Master Malfurion Stormrage," Jace said quietly.

The archdruid nodded slightly. He didn't explain how he'd arrived, didn't question why Jace was here, didn't probe into Aegwynn or the portal. He said simply, "But their efforts were not in vain. The Ley-lines were shaken. The portal was nearly torn apart. But the Defiler stabilized it at the last moment and forced his way into this world."

"That means the Defiler is vulnerable now," Jace said.

The archdruid shook his head. "It means we can predict his objective. The kaldorei fought the Defiler ten thousand years ago. He failed then. With a burning desire for revenge, this time..."

"The walls north of this lake shelter tens of thousands of people. But those walls cannot stop the Defiler. The souls of those mortals will be enough to restore some of his power, helping him reach Dalaran as fast as possible, destroy it, and consume its magic. Once he takes Dalaran's arcane power, he will march north to Quel'Thalas and seize the Sunwell."

And finally — Nordrassil, the World Tree, and the Well of Eternity.

Malfurion Stormrage didn't spell it all out. He didn't have to.

"But we can't let him destroy Dalaran," the druid said, rising. "Once the Defiler consumes this city's Ley-lines, this world will have no way to stop him from reaching the Sunwell. Azeroth's strength is gathering, Jace Sesso. The legacy your human ancestors left here will help us all — using this very city to end the Burning Legion's ambitions once more."

"What if we evacuate everyone from the royal city in advance?" Jace pointed north. "Get them out. Weaken the Defiler's ability to draw soul-power. That gives us a bigger advantage in a decisive battle at Dalaran."

Malfurion Stormrage stared into Jace's eyes in silence. Jace didn't hesitate. "I'll inform King Terenas, help the people evacuate, and then return here."

He walked out of that warm room. Solara hurried after him.

"Jace! Jace! You know what the archdruid meant. He wants you to stay and fight the Legion with everyone else. There must be someone else who can notify the human king. He..."

"You heard what Proudmoore said too," Jace cut her off. "Grede and Vereesa are still up north. Grede especially — that idiot led the bombing run on the portal, and now he's missing. How do you expect me to stay here? How can I just sit around with everyone else waiting for news?"

The green dragon's lips trembled. Tears rolled down her pale, cold cheeks. Jace knew — faced with all this, she was barely holding on.

"Stay here, Solara." He glanced toward Krasus Square. "I'll bring them back soon. Needle Flame too. You know I'm always lucky."

"Why?" Solara grabbed Jace's arm. "Why not let me come with you? You can't abandon them — do you think I can abandon you?"

"Too many wounded here," Jace said. "They won't hold out until the Burning Legion arrives. You can help the druids tend to these mages..."

"Without me, those gryphons and dragons... can't get you north in time. You know that, Jace Sesso." Solara forced her voice steady, her expression deadly serious. "The druids have arrived. Other children of Ysera will be here soon. My strength is nothing next to theirs. But only I can take you to catch those demons. Let me come with you!"

Solara held his wrist in a death grip, refusing to let go. Finally, Jace sighed and nodded. "Let's go. Together. We'll find them."
