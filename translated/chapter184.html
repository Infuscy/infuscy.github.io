Chapter 184: As One Act Ends, Another Begins

When Jace returned to Dushan's doorstep in the evening, several mages in Kirin Tor robes were standing there talking to Dushan.

One of the mages, the one facing this way, seemed to spot Jace. Jace didn't know where he'd seen him before, but the man immediately pointed in his direction.

Someone asked Dushan something while pointing at Jace. Dushan followed the finger, saw Jace, and his face immediately took on a helpless expression.

He said something, and everyone turned to look at Jace in unison — exactly like that scene in The Matrix where a swarm of Agent Smiths lock onto Neo.

What is this, the secret police?

Especially after last night's suspicions about Kel'Thuzad, Jace felt the hairs on the back of his neck stand up.

He was now convinced that the only reason Teron Gorefiend knew his name was because Kel'Thuzad had told him. There was no other possibility.

Had it been because of what Jace said at the meeting that made Kel'Thuzad feel threatened? Or was he afraid Jace had guessed at some connection between him and the death knights, and wanted to shut him up?

Either way, did Teron Gorefiend really need to hunt him down across half the city?

It was all a bit too hasty… but then again, for a mage secretly studying necromantic magic and dark magic within the Kirin Tor, panicking at the thought of being exposed was only natural. Jace could understand that perfectly well.

But that only made Kel'Thuzad look more suspicious.

So should he run? Were these Kirin Tor mages sent by Kel'Thuzad to finish the job?

Running was pointless. No matter how fast Jace was, he couldn't outrun Dalaran, and these Kirin Tor mages would definitely catch him.

At this point, no matter what they were here for, all he could do was stiffen his neck and walk straight at them.

Still, the fact that none of them had immediately rushed over and tackled him to the ground was a good sign.

It meant that whatever their intentions, at least they wanted to talk.

Having thought it through, Jace cleared his throat and walked forward step by step, doing his best to look calm, innocent, and like he had every right to be there.

The lead Kirin Tor mage, a brown-haired man who looked quite young, looked up as Jace approached and said, "I am Wayne Halister of the Security Division. You are Mr. Jace Sesso, I take it? A pleasure."

"Yes, I'm Jace Sesso." Jace glanced at Dushan. "This is…"

"Master Caro Dushan isn't aware of the situation either," Halister said. "We're here under the Kirin Tor's orders to investigate leads related to a theft."

He glanced at the bandages on Jace's shoulder. "May I ask how that happened, Mr. Sesso?"

"I ran into a death knight last night," Jace said.

"Looks like it's healing well." The Security Division mage glanced at Dushan's house, then said to Dushan, "Master, may we go inside to talk?"

Dushan said quickly, "Of course, please come in."

Though these mages were wearing short robes under their long cloaks, they all carried themselves like criminal investigators.

Jace wasn't being grabbed or restrained by anyone, but he was practically herded into Dushan's house by the crowd.

"Does Mr. Sesso live on the first floor?"

"I'm on the third floor."

"Then let's talk on the third floor."

Jace's room, though nicely furnished, was cramped as hell.

He thought, thank the Light I didn't bring that staff back. Otherwise, no matter what these folks had come to ask about, there'd be no explaining it.

He'd barely finished feeling relieved when he suddenly remembered the warlock's bag he'd shoved under his bed.

He wondered if Dalaran recognized the concept of a search warrant, and whether he had the right to refuse a search… probably not.

A few mages stayed downstairs and by the door. Halister and another female mage came upstairs. Jace felt like he was being pressed into his own chair by the sheer force of their presence.

Halister leaned against the window and said, "The Security Division sent me mainly to understand the matter of that letter, Mr. Sesso. I imagine you can guess what happened. Last night's theft — it's very likely closely connected to what was described in that command."

Jace said immediately, "I knew it! At last night's meeting I strongly urged Master Kel'Thuzad to strengthen the vault's defenses. Those death knights must have been working with the black dragons, drawing off Dalaran's defensive forces outside while they snuck into the vault to steal what they believed to be one of the three holy relics. I saw it coming! And I did everything I could to make my point at the meeting!"

"Am I right?"

He stood up and stared at Halister, who was clearly caught off guard by Jace's reaction.

"Please calm down, Mr. Sesso."

"Master Kel'Thuzad simply didn't take the opinion of a lowly apprentice seriously! He may hold a high position… but that's no excuse for such arrogance, is it?"

"Please… calm down, sir. No one wanted this to happen. Master Kel'Thuzad is deeply regretful as well."

Jace Sesso sat back down in his chair, his fingers drumming ceaselessly on the table.

He was considering how to make himself look a little dumber without overdoing it to the point of looking like an act. If this man hadn't been sent by Kel'Thuzad, fine and well. But if he really had been sent by Kel'Thuzad to probe for information, then Jace needed to make the most of this opportunity.

Getting on the bad side of a member of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six was no light matter. Jace wanted Kel'Thuzad to ease up a little — stop thinking it was worth the risk to keep trying to kill a nobody like him just to protect his secrets.

"I'll try," he said quietly.

The two mages exchanged a glance. Halister asked again, "So, could you tell us why you went out so suddenly last night?"

"I wanted to find my friend — he was injured and hadn't recovered yet. On the way, I ran into an extremely powerful death knight who nearly killed me," Jace said. "I believe that was Teron Gorefiend himself. It had to be him."

"How can you be so sure it was Teron Gorefiend?" Halister asked skeptically.

Jace said angrily, "Then who else? No one else could have had that kind of power — he was a demon! If it weren't for those elven rangers, I'd be dead already. Deader than a death knight. You wouldn't be having this conversation with me at all, Respected Master Wayne Halister."

"Last night was an unforgettable disaster for every resident of Dalaran," Halister said, then pivoted. "Your friend is a Wildhammer dwarf, isn't he?"

"A Stormwind mage apprentice and a Wildhammer dwarf… wait." He turned to the female mage beside him. "The mage apprentice's name was…"

"Sesso," the female mage prompted. "It should be this gentleman."
