## Chapter 1840: The Emerald Dream

Above the frozen lake, demonic felguards and felhounds heading south had nearly blanketed the ice. A dark, shifting mass of helms, twitching tentacles, and faintly glowing fel-green eyes stretched all the way into the distant snow-fog.

In the sky, flocks of doomguards surveyed the land below — like overseers of the endless, lowly Mo'arg, or perhaps like guides for the Legion master's plan.

Near the portal, a colossal fel-green silhouette was just barely visible. An Annihilan lord, far larger than the Azgaz that had attacked Nethergarde Keep.

"Advance... destroy... Dalaran! Crush the mortals, pave the way for our master!"

The pit lord's voice boomed in guttural demonic, echoing so fiercely that even the pine forests below rippled. But to Jace now, understanding these incantation-like words was no challenge at all.

The demons marched in ragged formation, every one of them clad in heavy fel-forged armor, wielding massive scythes, spiked battle-axes, and spears burning with fel-green flame. Just at a glance, there were thousands — and that wasn't counting the shifting shadows further out.

Yet the surface of Lake Lordamere held firm as solid ground, with no sound of cracking or imminent collapse.

The demons bellowed in response to their commander's orders. At the roar, Sazeno's terror intensified, his trembling more violent by the second.

He couldn't help but whimper. "The Destroyer... the Destroyer... Mannoroth!"

"None of our business," Jace said. "He's not our target."

"That's the Destroyer Mannoroth, master!" the imp half-sobbed. "I really..."

"You should've listened to me from day one. From the very first day. But you always hesitated. Now it's too late. Too late. Even with Usaalas, you never tried... Can you protect this world now? Can those so-called heroes do it? The Ley-lines won't even obey them anymore. The mages are finished — can't even throw a simple Arcane Missile. The Defiler's about to enslave the Ley-lines. What good is Dalaran now? They can barely stop the Destroyer Mannoroth! Let alone, let alone..."

"Shut up." Jace's voice was cold. "Shut up!"

"Mmph?! Mmph?!"

Sazeno bit his lip and kept whimpering until Jace sealed the bag tight.

The Destroyer Mannoroth? One axe swing. That's all.

Grommash Hellscream was roaming through Lordaeron's forests right now. Maybe he'd find his chance to charge in and cleave the pit lord in two, just like in another timeline.

Sure, the Warsong chieftain hadn't drunk Mannoroth's blood, hadn't become a red-skinned fel orc with multiplied strength, hadn't killed the demigod Cenarius. Did that matter?

He was Grommash Hellscream. Mannoroth the Destroyer died by his hand. That's how fate wrote it.

Antonidas was dying. Just like in another timeline. Even Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage arriving in Dalaran couldn't reverse it.

Next, probably Terenas of Lordaeron too. Just like it was written.

And he, Jace Sesso — no matter how much he did, how many fates he changed, even if the Scourge never came, the Defiler Archimonde would still descend on this world and put an end to every story that shouldn't have continued. Right?

And it's all because of you.

"I can't get close to the portal, Jace."

Solara tilted her head. "The Dream's veil becomes fragile once we leave the forest. Out on the frozen lake, there's nothing left but demons and dark magic. We can't sneak past their detection to reach Fenris Isle."

"We don't need to search near the portal," Jace said. "If Grede survived the portal explosion and Archimonde, he'd leave the lake or be rescued. If he's not in Dalaran, he's in the royal city. If he's not up north... then he's probably dead."

"You... you're right." Solara banked northeast, toward the great forest and hillsides.

They were silent for a while. The air was filled with nothing but demonic roars, the howl of fel winds, the screams of tormented souls...

Then Solara spoke again, as if trying to drown out those ominous sounds.

"Don't worry about Grede, Jace. He's the High Thane. That hammer of his survived Ragnaros the Firelord pounding him into the dirt more times than I can count. Quel'dorei arcane bombs and a few demons won't kill him. He's probably waiting for us on the walls of Lordaeron City right now, and Ver..."

"I'm not worried, Solara." Jace said. "I've already prepared myself. The day I met Grede, he almost drowned in the sea. And since then... that guy's never exactly stopped trying to get himself killed. I'll go find them. Give it everything I've got. But if this is how it ends... I can accept it."

"Are you afraid of dying, Jace?"

"No."

"Me neither."

Solara's voice grew softer. But as a dragon, even a mumble was clear enough to the passenger on her back.

"I'm... not afraid of dying either."

"Really?" Jace gave a faint smile. "You were about to lose your mind back in Dalaran. Don't think I didn't notice."

"In Draenor, when you all went to the Throne of Kil'jaeden, I didn't follow." Solara's tone was calm. "In Auchindoun, I poured all my strength, all my regret, into every demon I killed. But when I heard that Prince Sabellian had betrayed you, I still... I wanted to take Grede and Vereesa and fly to the Throne of Kil'jaeden immediately. If those warlocks who knew you hadn't come up with a way to summon you back, I might have..."

"From that moment on, I swore I'd never let you face danger alone again. But I always... get left behind. And this time, that portal — I already sensed the danger. I should have gone with you and Khadgar."

She turned her head. "Do you still see me as a whelp who needs care and protection? Like Needle Flame?"

"If Needle Flame heard you say that, she'd faint on the spot," Jace said.

"I hate being seen that way too." The green dragon managed a smile. "So this time, I'm not getting separated from you. I'm never letting you leave me behind again, Jace. Even if death is waiting."

"Thank you," Jace said.

"In this group... there's no 'thank you,' Jace Sesso. Saying thank you means you still haven't really joined..." Solara tried to mimic Grede's accent, but her naturally elegant cadence forced into a dwarf's rambling came out hilariously wrong. Even she couldn't help laughing before finishing — it was what Grede always told her.

Remembering their tavern days, Jace found himself laughing too. But the smile quickly froze in the icy mountain wind and snow-dusted pines.

"Honestly, Solara, I always thought long-lived races would be terrified of death. I figured the more you'd experienced, the more you'd seen, the harder it'd be to let go."

"Maybe you're right." Solara beat her wings twice, cresting an Alterac ridge. "But I've seen things worse than death. And I... will never let myself fall into that again."

"The Nightmare," Jace said.

"Remember what we talked about in Frostsaber Valley?" The green dragon tilted her head. "About the Nightmare?"

"I remember." Jace smiled. "Stay optimistic. Do things that make you happy. Maybe it can resist the Nightmare's erosion. But I wasn't sure back then either — just a guess based on what I know about shadow."

"Queen Ysera said I've almost broken free of the Nightmare. Few of my kind have that luck. It's one reason she wants me as a Dreamwarden." Solara said. "So I think your method probably works."

As she spoke, Jace felt her heart pound harder — so hard that his seat vibrated beneath him.

She breathed out white mist and said softly, "The happiest I've ever been... is with you, Jace. Don't abandon me again. Never again..."

Jace felt something clench in his chest. Words failed him. Finally, he managed, "I know."

The green dragon lifted her head, trying not to let tears fall where the demon armies below might see. Then she drew a deep breath, spread her wings, and climbed higher.

They both knew — once they crossed the Alterac peaks, they'd see the high walls of the royal city.
