Chapter 1841: A Solitary City in the Snow

The royal city still stood atop its frozen high cliffs. In the darkness, its countless lights still glowed red — not yet swallowed by the fel-fire that had already consumed nearly half of Lake Lordamere. Even so, as Jace gazed at this city surrounded by tens of miles of snow-covered plains, it no longer felt familiar.

Once, this city had been the heart of the Alliance, of the entire Eastern Kingdoms. He'd grown up here. In his memory, it was prosperous, bustling — endless carriages and pedestrians, ships coming and going along the lakeshore. Every time he left and came back, left and came back, it was always the same.

But now, beneath the cliffs, beyond the walls — nothing living moved in the blizzard.

The city's harbor had been completely destroyed by the frost. Ships that had docked here were either smashed against the shore or frozen into the ice. Countless people were surely huddled behind those snow-covered walls, trembling. Icy fear had seeped into the city's very bones. The demons' black hooves hadn't yet touched this soil, but suffocating darkness had already surrounded them from every side.

As he drew closer, Jace grew more certain this darkness wasn't coming out of nowhere. Something was watching the city, and it wasn't hiding its intent.

Horns sounded from the walls — allies arriving. Not a single soldier was surprised by the arrival of a massive green dragon. Not a single resident opened a window or ran into the streets to look. In less than a week, this city had grown accustomed to fighting alongside the very dragons that had once spread fear.

Before Aegwynn's portal had fallen, most people in this city — soldiers included — might have lived their entire lives without ever seeing a single whelp. Or, for that matter, a single real demon.

Word of the green dragon's arrival spread quickly. Soon Jace spotted the young paladin hurrying over, raising a hand in greeting from a distance. Everything from the earlier council meeting felt like it had happened in another world.

"Your Highness."

"Archmage Sesso. Lady Solara." Arthas strode up quickly. His splendid golden armor was badly damaged, and he looked exhausted. He inclined his head briefly to Solara, then studied the warlock's eyes. "Your face..."

Jace touched his scarred, pockmarked cheek. The damage from Sargeras's Eye still hadn't fully healed. His eyes probably looked off too.

He remembered that when he'd rushed back to Dalaran, Ursula hadn't commented. Archdruid Malfurion hadn't mentioned it either. Jaina had looked startled, sure, but she hadn't brought it up.

"I was wounded fighting demons. It's nothing serious now, Your Highness — just the scars need time to heal. Either way, thank you for your concern. I need to see the King. I have urgent news from Dalaran to report."

"My father..." Arthas lowered his head. "He fell from the wall during a battle. He landed on the steps below — alive, but badly hurt. He can't get up. He drifts in and out of consciousness. If Archmage Antonidas has something to convey, tell me. I'll pass it to my father. And Marshal Turalyon — he's on the other wall, Archmage Sesso."

Of course. King Terenas had fallen too. Jace couldn't help thinking darkly — not executed on his throne by his own son, but struck down by a demon, falling from the wall. At least it wasn't quite so humiliating. So helpless.

"You don't know about Archmage Antonidas," Jace said.

"No," Arthas said. "What happened to him?"

"So not a single messenger from the south has reached the city since this blizzard started?" Jace asked.

"No," Arthas said. "But the blizzard hasn't been going that long..."

"Archmage Antonidas was gravely wounded disrupting the portal ritual," Jace said. "He's in critical condition and can no longer lead Dalaran. That's not all — two other members of the Council of Six are missing. If Prince Kael'thas of Silvermoon and Archmage Krasus of Northrend don't return in time, the current leadership of the Kirin Tor will fall to Archmage Vargus, or perhaps Archmage Othrys of the Violet Eye..."

"Dalaran was attacked too?" Arthas asked.

"Dalaran took even worse damage than here," Jace said. "The portal's power struck the Ley-lines."

"It matches Archmage Esdeath's speculation..." the prince murmured.

"Listen to me, Prince Arthas." Jace said. "We need to evacuate every civilian from this city. I mean entirely. Northward — to Brill, Valdemar, Darrowshire, all the way to Stratholme. As far as possible."

"Why?" Arthas's brow furrowed. He looked back at the city's lights, steadying himself against a merlon. "Demons are everywhere out there. Doomguard legions keep trying to breach the walls. Once you leave these walls, there's no protection. I don't want to say this, Archmage Sesso, but it sounds like you're sending these people out to die. Staying here at least lets us hold out — our stores can last a month or two. Before the blizzard hit, the Kirin Tor sent word that the entire Alliance army, including Ironforge's forces, are marching here. Why would we abandon the city now?"

"It's different now. Further resistance here is pointless." Jace said. "The Burning Legion has summoned its master to Fenris Isle. The Defiler Archimonde. When I flew here, I saw the army he's already summoned. The entire Lake Lordamere is covered in demons. Right now, as we speak, he may have finished his summoning ritual and started marching. Not a month or two — by this time tomorrow, this city could be leveled."

"What? The Defiler? Leveled..." Arthas was stunned.

"Yes," Jace said. "If you haven't heard that name, let me tell you — these walls, this entire city, are nothing to that demon king. If everyone stays and fights to the death, your soul and mine, the souls of tens of thousands in this city — they'll all become fuel for him to destroy the world. Dalaran won't withstand what's coming."

"Souls. Everyone's souls. I've heard that name. In books, I mean..."

Arthas seemed at a loss. Jace knew it was hard for this young prince, who'd never truly faced demonic threats or dark magic rituals, to accept so much at once.

So Jace pointed to the boundless frozen expanse of Lake Lordamere below the cliffs. "If you don't believe me, think about this — where did this sudden blizzard come from?"

"That was the demon's magic the moment he descended. With a wave of his hand, he shattered the assault of every legion surrounding Fenris Isle and paved the way for his army to counterattack. He didn't cast it a second time only because he knows the real threats are in Dalaran and the Sunwell. He's conserving power to deal with what matters most. And this city — our souls — that's the power he's gathering. How can these walls stop an enemy like that?"

"No matter how dangerous it is outside, it's worth the risk over staying here to die, Menethil," Solara added.

"Is Ranger-Lord Vereesa Windrunner and High Thane Grede Thunderfist in the city?" Jace asked. "And the black dragon — the one with the Ranger-Lord?"

Arthas pointed to a cluster of buildings below the wall. "The Ranger-Lord is there. She's tending to wounded Farstriders. Word of your arrival has probably reached her by now, Archmage Sesso. The black dragon is at the 'Dragon's Lair' in the north. But High Thane Grede Thunderfist... we've had no word of him."

Despite claiming he could accept any outcome, Jace's heart sank at the prince's words.

He steadied his breathing. "We'll stay too, Your Highness. With you, your father, the Quel'Thalas Farstriders, and the city's finest fighters — we'll hold the walls, cover the residents' escape from the demon king's grasp, and find a way out ourselves when it's done. Our stand will minimize innocent casualties and slow the demons' advance, buying time for Dalaran and the whole Alliance to gather."

"I understand, Archmage Sesso." Arthas finally spoke. "I'll relay everything to Marshal Turalyon, General Sylvanas, and my father."

"Thank you, Your Highness." Jace let out a small breath.

Arthas was about to turn up the steps when he paused and looked back. "Archmage Sesso, I... I want to apologize in person for what happened at the Sky Council that day. I believed the demons' lies. I doubted the entire Kirin Tor's decision because of it. I thought I was saving everyone, but I was just being led by the nose by those cunning demons."

"None of that matters now." Jace said. "Go inform Marshal Turalyon. This city could be attacked at any moment. We have to act now. May the Holy Light protect this city. And all of us."

"May the Holy Light protect us all." Arthas nodded.
