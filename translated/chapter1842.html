Chapter 1842: Drifting Souls

Fear. Unease. Despair. These had become the city's very fabric. The fervor of those guards who'd dreamt of serving the kingdom and the Alliance, who'd hoped to die protecting everyone — it had been drowned almost beyond perception.

Jace walked the steps along the walls, breathing in the reek of blood that filled every street and alley, every eave. Demon blood. Mortal blood. Hero's blood. And the lingering scent of bloodlust and helplessness in the face of death...

He didn't want to admit it, but these emotions were rapidly mending the fel wounds on his body — faster and more effectively than even Solara's natural magic.

But he knew this was exactly what Khadgar had always feared. What the Kirin Tor dreaded.

Not that it mattered anymore. The Kirin Tor had failed. Aegwynn had completed her portal. The Defiler had arrived.

Never mind how I'm doing, mage.

The warlock greedily drank in the city's darkness. He was recharging for the coming battle, preparing himself. The demons would be at the gates soon.

A faint thread of apathy drifted into his grasp. Its source was a corner of the street — a Lordaeron native huddled at the base of the wall.

Snow covered the ragged blanket draped over him. Lifting it, Jace saw the man was still alive. Beneath the blanket, he wore a fur-lined coat and a cloak — several layers of respectable, expensive clothing. It would take a while to freeze him to death.

"Go home," Jace said flatly.

"Home... my home is gone. The demons burned it down." The man kept his head down. "My wife is dead. My parents and children... I don't know. I hope they died without pain. Not like me, sitting here waiting for the end."

"No home? Pick any empty house." Jace grabbed his collar. "Don't freeze to death in the street."

"No soul in this city is going to escape. If you die here, your soul won't reach the Shadowlands. It'll become demon food. Best case, the demons turn you into a soul bomb and send you after everyone you ever knew — blow them all to blackened scraps. Worst case, you're tossed into the soul furnaces of the Twisting Nether to burn for ten thousand years."

At those words, the man slowly lifted his head. His features twisted in terror, as if his heart had been suddenly squeezed.

He stared at the warlock's eyes. His whole body began to tremble uncontrollably. Jace dropped him. The man immediately started begging, promising to obey, then scrambled into an alley.

Or... I could do this myself. Take the Defiler's place. Spare you all those horrifying endings.

Jace looked up at the night sky. Lonely, drifting souls flickered in and out of view.

They were visible now without any vision potion or enchanted goggles. He could even read their expressions, see how they'd met their miserable ends.

These souls were fragile and thin. But gathered by the thousands, they became a force even the Demon King would covet.

For me too.

"Jace." Solara's cautious voice came from behind. "I know not having news of Grede is hard. But just because we haven't seen him doesn't mean he's..."

"He's not dead." Jace lowered his head and turned to face the green dragon. "I can't smell his soul. He'll come back. Maybe he's already in Dalaran."

Solara murmured an "mm" in agreement. But looking at Jace's face, she found little reassurance.

Jace didn't want to probe deeper into her emotions. Facing the coming demon army — facing the Defiler who'd nearly helped Sargeras step through the Legion's gate ten thousand years ago — no one with any fight left in them could feel at ease.

Or maybe it was because of what she'd said earlier.

The thought sent another jolt of fel corrosion from Sargeras's Eye stinging through his skin. He couldn't help wincing.

Leaving the alley, he stepped onto the main street. Snow fell thick and fast. Icicles hung from the eaves. Torchlight and brazier glow reflected off every icy surface, glittering and dazzling.

A squad of Farstrider rangers hurried past. Their leader caught sight of Jace at the corner, stopped short, exchanged a few words with the others, and sprinted through the snow toward him.

The ranger and the warlock embraced in the middle of the street. She sobbed against him, shoulders heaving. He closed his eyes and said nothing.

"You're hurt. Your eyes, your skin... who did this to you?" she asked, wiping her tears.

"This amount of fel isn't enough to affect my mind, Ver. Just residual wounds and scars. Ask Solara — she's been looking after me the whole time." Jace said.

"I'm sorry, Ver. I couldn't fully heal Jace..."

Before Solara could finish, Vereesa stepped over and hugged her too, steadying her voice. "I just want you alive. Nothing else matters, Solara. Don't say that. I thought I'd never see Jace again. Never see you again."

"Prince Arthas told me there's still no word of Grede," Jace said.

"Before the blizzard hit — I know it's not really a blizzard, but everyone calls it that." Vereesa took a deep breath, looking at them both. "Grede led the gryphon riders to attack the portal, working with the Kirin Tor to weaken the magical barrier. A squad of Quel'Thalas dragonhawk riders was supposed to cover Grede's retreat after the bombing. Fenris Island was crawling with doomguards."

"But everything went wrong. The gryphon riders' attack hit snags. The arcane bomb broke the barrier, but the Kirin Tor mages couldn't destroy the portal. The Defiler... I didn't know what was happening then. I saw dragonhawks fleeing toward Lordaeron without their riders. I knew they couldn't help the gryphon riders retreat. Something had gone wrong. Needle Flame took me straight to Fenris Island. None of us could abandon that dwarf. But then the 'blizzard' came."

"Yes." Vereesa said. "Needle Flame protected me. But the shockwave blasted her out of the sky. The forest broke her fall, but she was badly hurt. I couldn't do anything. Luckily, the city had cleared a space for wounded dragons — the 'Dragon's Lair' under the north wall. A red dragon is trying to heal her. You should see her, Jace. She kept calling your name. And Solara's."

"We'll find Grede. Take us to the Dragon's Lair first." Jace said.

"Yes."

But then the horns on the wall sounded a long, mournful note. Alerting everyone. The Quel'Thalas horns answered in the distance.

"Retreat?" Vereesa asked. "Why?"

"Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage has reached Dalaran, Ver." Jace said. "He believes Archimonde will come for the royal city first — right after he finishes summoning his army. He wants to drain every innocent soul here to fuel his destruction of Dalaran and the Sunwell. This is the largest city on the continent — more people than Stormwind and Stratholme combined. If we don't evacuate..."

"But..." The ranger looked toward the north gate. "It's a frozen wasteland out there. Worse than in here. And the demons will be hunting us. Countless people will die."

"That's why we're staying. We'll hold these walls against the Legion. Cover the people's retreat north. Once reinforcements from Stratholme and Stromgarde arrive — and the main Sunfury forces — we'll have bought enough time to get civilians clear. And bought Dalaran time to rally, counterattack the portal, and defeat Archimonde."

Vereesa took two deep, steadying breaths. Her gaze hardened again. "I like that plan better."

"Me too."

Jace said it as he slit his own wrist. A trickle of purple-red blood ran out. For a moment, he'd worried it would still be fel-green.

"Hey, hey. What are you doing, master?"

Sazeno poked his head out of the pouch at Jace's belt, gripping the edge as his master drew a summoning circle. "I know you don't want to hear it, but I have to warn you — be careful, master. Don't perform a summoning here. The Defiler is watching this place. That massive portal is right outside — you can almost see it with your bare eyes! He'll crush your magic without breaking a sweat. He commands every demon's will. That succubus has probably already defected to the Defiler. She definitely has..."

Watching Jace ignite his own blood, Solara asked, "Then why haven't you gone to defect to the Defiler yet, Mister Sazeno?"

The imp said, shifty-eyed, "Because... Lord Defiler..." he wouldn't... "Could it be he doesn't think you're worth the trouble?" Vereesa asked.

"Er, don't say that, but..." The imp couldn't finish. Finally, he grabbed his master's raised arm. "Whatever happens, listen to me, master..."

But he wasn't wrong.

As Jace chanted the summoning for Morofil, the void's gaze grew clearer. Something was tearing at his connection to the Twisting Nether, scrambling the already near-nonexistent soul-link.

But he gritted his teeth, seized the fraying thread, and held on. The circle erupted in purple-red fel-fire. The Usaalas in his hand bled streams of strange hellfire from its eyes, screeching like triumphant, mocking laughter.

The encroaching interference burned away in an instant. A black hoof planted itself firmly in the center of the circle. The imp stood frozen, throat emitting only hoarse, choked sounds.

He craned his neck up, watching wide-eyed as Morofil folded her wings and crushed the Eredar heart in her hand into paste, tossing it aside. Her fiery demon eyes locked onto her master's increasingly distant gaze, her own feelings too complicated for words.

"What were you saying just now, Sazeno?" Jace asked.
