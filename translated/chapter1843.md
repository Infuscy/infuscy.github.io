## Chapter 1843: Dawn Has Not Yet Come

"The demons have already started marching on Dalaran, master. When I felt your summoning, I thought you'd be in Dalaran. I didn't expect you to bring me here... but as long as I'm with you, I can adapt to anything. Even if there are thousands of Burning Legion elites camped outside these walls — and the Defiler himself."

"I envy your optimism, succubus! I almost wish you were still that banshee desperate to escape the Legion's grasp. Maybe then you'd talk some sense into these suicidal lunatics, and we'd still have a chance to slip back to the Twisting Nether..."

"I'll return to the Twisting Nether. You'll just be ground to dust. Only real demons have souls with a chance..."

"Pah!" "Morofil."

Following Vereesa toward the stables, Jace cut off the demonic bickering. "Have you seen Grede Thunderfist?"

"No." Morofil asked, "Is he not inside the walls?"

Solara shook her head from behind. "None of us have heard anything, Lady Morofil."

"Then it seems a warrior has met his glorious end." The succubus tilted her head slightly. "Pity his soul can't be reforged in the fel furnaces. I wonder who his final vanquished enemy was..."

"Have you seen my sister? I mean Alleria." Vereesa turned to ask the demon.

"No, I'm sorry, Vereesa Windrunner." Morofil replied. "By the time I was summoned, there wasn't a living mortal near me. But I can at least tell you that among the fallen Quel'dorei I saw, your sister wasn't among them."

"Thank you."

Vereesa said it softly as they left the alley. The stables were across the street.

Morofil concealed herself from the gathered soldiers, officers, and church priests. Everyone here was preparing for the Legion's attack. A winged demon suddenly appearing would cause panic — no one had the energy to figure out if it belonged to Jace Sesso. In this moment of extreme tension, even the slightest disturbance could trigger an unpredictable chain reaction.

They mounted up and rode north. Jace checked his pocket watch — it was deep night, but the streets were full of people. Voices — talking, crying — came from every house. No one could sleep.

Looking at these familiar buildings, this street layout, the people passing by on both sides, the soldiers, riding through the middle with his companions — for a moment, Jace felt like a kid again. This life's childhood, anyway.

Riding a stolen horse with his childhood friends — no idea where they'd stolen it from — shouting with excitement as they tore through the streets. Everywhere they passed: curses and complaints, children crying because they were scared of the horses. Knowing he'd get a beating from his father when he got home never stopped him.

Was that boy you, Jace Sesso?

The royal city was huge. Crossing it on horseback still felt like a chore.

If he didn't know what the Defiler Archimonde was, looking up at this city's scale — its towers, its massive walls — Jace might have thought no army of demons could take this place from Lordaeron's royal guard, the Silver Hand, and the Church of the Holy Light.

It was deep into the night by the time they finally saw the north wall. The so-called Dragon's Lair was really just a hastily converted garden. Many wounded red whelps were resting here. Seeing Jace's party arrive, several lifted their heads to greet him in draconic. Jace had to tell them all to rest and prepare for the coming fight.

The black dragon was curled up in the middle of all those red dragons — a dark blotch amid the brilliant red. A far cry from the arrogant, all-fighting powerhouse she'd been when she first arrived at Grim Batol.

"Needle Flame?"

Hearing the warlock's voice, the black dragon flipped open her amber lids. Spotting Jace, she immediately opened her sticky mouth and shoved her head toward him. Only when he held her snout and Solara stroked her horns and cheeks did she seem willing to settle down.

"Damn you, Jace Sesso... We looked for you for so long, and you just show up on your own. You shouldn't be here. You should have stayed in Dalaran with Solara."

"Too late, little one." Jace said. "I'm already here."

Needle Flame coughed twice, a burnt smell wafting from her mouth. She couldn't help pressing her head between Jace and Solara.

"Look, my face still has fel scars, Needle Flame." Jace warned her. "Be careful you don't get contaminated..."

"I'm not that fragile." The black dragon lifted her head immediately, looking at Vereesa. "I think I've mostly recovered, ranger. That last time... I just didn't expect such a strong storm. A minor slip. Grede Thunderfist — the dwarf? Even if we started a drinking contest right now, I wouldn't lose."

"We haven't found him yet, Needle Flame." Vereesa said.

"I can go find him. I can fly up right now and take you, Jace Sesso. Don't look at me like I'm a terminal patient." Needle Flame spread her wings and pushed herself up, drawing stares from the nearby red whelps.

"I was thinking the same thing, Jace." Solara said quietly. "We might still have time to find Grede. The Burning Legion is about to attack this city. That Mountain King could be a huge help. If we're fast enough..."

Was there really still time?

The evacuation had already started. The demon army could descend at any moment. And they had no idea where Grede even was. Not a single clue.

Tirisfal? Silverpine? Alterac? Would they have to fly straight to Fenris Isle against the demon army's advance?

One day? Two? How long would it take to find a dwarf on the shores of Lake Lordamere? Two dragons searching separately might improve efficiency. But even if they found him — could they still make it back to the city? Could they regroup?

Needle Flame was just putting on a brave face. He could see it clearly. Solara had been cooked by his own fel energy in that cave for days. Then she'd flown at full speed, risking demon detection, and was now exhausted to the point of collapse.

Vereesa wasn't much better. The arcane light in her eyes looked almost extinguished. Fel burns covered her body. Her steps lacked the lightness she'd had before they'd separated in Dalaran. Days of continuous fighting on the north shore of Lake Lordamere had drained her.

In this state, if they split up — if either group got surrounded by demons, or cornered by a demon lord like Mannoroth and his legion — they'd be lucky to find Grede and regroup. Even self-defense would be a challenge.

But Grede...

"First, help evacuate the city's people." Jace said. "When we abandon this city and head back to Dalaran, we'll search for him then. It won't be much longer."

"Jace is right." Vereesa said. "Alleria is already out there looking for survivors. Maybe she's already found him. And if we draw the demons' attention here, pull their main force to the walls, we'll help those trying to reach Dalaran."

"Mm." Solara agreed, glancing at Jace one more time.

The black dragon fell silent too, looking at Jace and Vereesa. The excitement of their reunion evaporated instantly.

A paladin entered the garden. Jace turned to look — it was Turalyon. "Vereesa. Jace... Lady Solara."

"I'm truly glad to see you all safe. I wish I had time for a proper welcome, but..." Turalyon's expression was grim, his warhammer gripped tight. "The mages report that the demons are close. By their estimate, the Burning Legion's vanguard will reach the cliffs by dawn. The doomguards will arrive even faster. We have to speed up the evacuation."

"Evacuate everyone in one night... how is that possible..." Solara whispered.

"Do what we can." Jace said. "The fewer souls the Defiler controls, the better our odds. His main target is Dalaran. He won't waste time chasing refugees north. That gives the Alliance time to gather its forces."

"And the Defiler?" Vereesa asked.

"The mages can't see him. They don't have the power — or the nerve — to look further." Turalyon said.

"One more thing, everyone." He paused. "The King has just passed. Don't spread this yet. First, ensure the evacuation proceeds in an orderly fashion."

You saw this coming, didn't you, Jace?

Because it's all connected to you. If you hadn't insisted on picking up Usaalas, none of this would have happened. This world wouldn't have been pushed to the edge like this. Alleria hasn't been heard from in a long time. If she's dead, if her soul is devoured by the Defiler, I'll blame you for that too.

Look at yourself now. You just abandoned Grede Thunderfist — left him in the snow to die. If he were here and you were out there, would he make the same choice? You know he wouldn't. He'd drop everything to save your life.

The warlock slowly lifted his head, looking at the paladin not far away. Turalyon was standing there, watching him, waiting for a response.

"I understand, Turalyon. We'll help with the evacuation immediately. I've been ready for the demons to come at any moment."

"Us too." Vereesa and Solara echoed.

"Me too." The black dragon emphasized. "My wounds are healed, paladin."

"Thank you, all of you." Turalyon said. "It's my honor to fight alongside you."
