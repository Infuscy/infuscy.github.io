## Chapter 1844: Soul Devourer

No one knew exactly how many people were sheltering inside the royal city. Even the late Terenas himself probably couldn't have said. After the plague rumors, the portal's descent, and all the chaos that followed — fast and slow — people from outside had flooded in seeking refuge, while people inside had tried to flee south or to Stratholme. By the most conservative estimate, at least seventy to eighty thousand people needed evacuating.

Jace stood atop the wall, gazing north toward the city gate. Inside the city, streams of torchlight flowed through the streets toward it. The north wall had only one main gate and a few small openings. Everyone would have to squeeze through these narrow passageways.

He wondered how the soldiers were managing to convince this sea of people — exhausted by demons, death, and cold — to leave the homes their families had lived in for generations, to step outside into the unknown. How were they keeping order on this suffocating night before the end?

Thankfully, the Silver Hand and the church's leaders were here. They could make the citizens believe that abandoning the city wasn't just the King's will — it was the Holy Light's guidance.

Jace had never doubted Turalyon's power to rally this continent. In Lordaeron's darkest hour, he'd been the one to stand up and call for unity against the Horde. He'd been the one to personally defeat the Horde Warchief Orgrim Doomhammer, turning the tide of a nearly lost battle and leading the Expeditionary Force to strike back at the demon servants' lairs.

Now, over a decade later, he was still the one guiding everyone from these high walls.

Turalyon wasn't just a beloved leader and a radiant paladin. He was also the brother-in-law and mentor who'd looked after Jace all the way from Draenor to here.

So when Jace heard him say those words, for a moment he almost believed they'd really come from Turalyon's mouth — and not from the increasingly oppressive shadow torment that was feeding him hallucinations.

No wait — was this real or not?

Turalyon didn't know about Usaalas. When they'd discussed whether to search for Grede, he hadn't been there. He couldn't have heard Jace's decision. The paladin had no mind-reading powers.

Vereesa, Needle Flame, Solara... If any of them had heard the paladin say those things, they wouldn't have stayed silent.

Or maybe they were all quietly resentful that he wouldn't go save Grede.

You know the answer, Jace Sesso. You know you've never actually heard it speak. It's all your own mind. Your own desires.

But you'll keep deceiving yourself. Deny it. Pretend you're still struggling, that you still have the right to refuse.

"I kind of envy Grede."

Vereesa spoke suddenly from beside him. Seeing Jace not react — just staring motionless into the distance — she pressed on. "Imagine it. Maybe he's surrounded by demons right now, swinging his axe to his heart's content, killing as many as he wants. Maybe the demons can't even make him pull out Sulfuras. That fool treasures that hammer too much. Unless his life depends on it, he won't take it out. Like it's not a weapon, but some kind of art piece."

She blinked and said, "Do you think Alleria's the same? She's probably the same. Finding every opportunity to kill any demon soldier she can see."

"Remember when we first met? Back then... I missed Lirath so much. I was desperate to kill every troll in the world, every orc, every death knight, demon, every red dragon. I missed Alleria so much that when I found out you could open a portal to Draenor, I wanted your help no matter the cost."

"But now, Alleria is outside these walls. No word from her. And strangely... I don't feel sad. I don't miss her the way I did then, Jace. Because I'm here. Right on the front lines against the Burning Legion. I'm doing the same thing she and Sylvanas are doing — fighting for the same goal, making the same effort. Not as an apprentice who doesn't deserve a place on the battlefield. Not like those human citizens leaving the city, watching helplessly from the sidelines. I..."

Beyond the wall, in the pitch-black night, a scattering of green pinpricks had begun to appear along the horizon — merging into a single line.

She was right.

None of this mattered anymore. No need to agonize.

Just kill as many demons as possible. Hold them back at the wall. Let those vessels of souls flee as far as they can. Don't let the Defiler use them as weapons to destroy Dalaran, to destroy the Sunwell, to exterminate everyone.

That way, no one will be dissatisfied. No one will complain. No one will oppose.

"Demons incoming! Watch the sky!"

"Doomguards!"

Shouts rang out along the wall. Urgent horns passed from one section to another.

Vereesa wiped her tears and raised Belanur. Arrows shimmering with dreamlight soared into the sky, illuminating the countless demons closing in.

Each of these demons was a powerhouse in its own right, but they pressed forward like a bloodthirsty swarm of bats. Arrows, bolts, and Arcane Missiles sailed into the surging black tide high above — making almost no impression.

On another stretch of the wall, paladins and Lordaeron's garrison were locked in close-quarters combat with doomguards that had already landed. Scattered shadow bolts occasionally sent ripples of panic through the districts behind them. Further north, near the gate, the torchlight flickered even more chaotically.

Prince Arthas was among them, trading blows with demons. Even from this distance, he was unmistakable. His emotions were extraordinary — regret, pain, grief — and also, mixed with the soldiers' fury, a burning rage.

The soldiers' shouts and the demons' roars were drowned out by the dragons' cries. One after another, red whelps rose from within the city, lunging high overhead. Their bright flames — blinding in the night — illuminated the rooftops as they intercepted demons trying to dive straight at the fleeing crowds.

More and more red dragons joined, along with several green dragons. Quickly, they killed or knocked down the stray Eredar demons that had slipped through, then flew to the walls to support the fight.

Compared to the other whelps' flame breath and poison sprays, Needle Flame clearly preferred a simpler, more direct style of combat.

The black dragon flung herself straight onto the wall, taking out a section of crumbling battlements and several doomguards as she sent them all flying off the cliff. She rampaged like a young tiger crashing into a pack of stray dogs — battering with her great wings, tearing with claws and fangs, using her flame only to instantly incinerate the cowardly demons trying to evade her fury.

Her savagery, her rage, her bloodlust — even from hundreds of meters away on this section of wall, Jace could feel it burning like the breath of a black dragon in his face.

And not just her. Vereesa, who was nailing winged demon after winged demon with summoned arcane arrows. Morofil, fighting effortlessly in the air, outnumbered but treating it like bullying children. Solara, hovering behind the wall, calling on nature's power to dispel shadow corrosion and bring the breeze of recovery to the wounded — her calm, her passion, and...

Thousands of Lordaeron soldiers clutching shields, spears, and swords. Many of them had watched Jace grow up — veterans who'd known his father well.

Farstrider rangers forced into close combat, still lethal with their longswords. And their leader, Ranger-General Sylvanas, who tore the fel-heart from a fear-fiend spewing fel-blast with a single arcane arrow.

Every emotion they released — and every demon's madness, fanaticism, and brutality — gathered in the warlock's hands as he climbed to the top of the tower, coalescing around his will.

Jace Sesso raised Usaalas slightly. His gaze swept over the Eredar still folding their wings, descending, searching for targets. He sensed their shadow and demonic power.

Like that day in the wastes beyond Auchindoun, standing with Sabellian, watching the reanimated bones as he practiced Rain of Shadow.

But in his eyes, these flying demons seemed even more fragile than those draenei and orc skeletons. At least... they were more tempting.

As he thought this, the bleeding Usaalas began to emit an ethereal blue light from between the horns atop his head.

His chanting of demonic words fell like heavy footsteps, pounding the defenders along that stretch of wall. In the middle of battle, they couldn't help glancing up at the lone figure atop the tower, bathed in ghostly blue light.

"Egh egh..." Sazeno clutched his head in the nook of the battlements behind him, trembling. "Don't count me in. Don't count me in, master..."

Jace opened his blood-red eyes. The tip of the demon bone staff released a web of blue magical threads that instantly snagged every demon in the sky — covering half the night like a spider's web.

The burning fel-eyes of the Eredar high above dimmed and snuffed out the moment the threads touched them. They had almost no strength to resist.

Between the blue threads, screams and shrieks filled the air around the wall. Demon flesh and fel-forged armor crashed against the stone.

The warlock's eyes had turned ice-blue — like a Quel'dorei. But the difference was, looking into these eyes, you could somehow hear the curses and pleas of countless demons at once — not the chiming of arcane winds.

Come, then, followers of Sargeras. Offer yourselves to me — your souls!!
