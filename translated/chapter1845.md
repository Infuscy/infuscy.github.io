## Chapter 1845: The Encirclement

"Demons are coming! Demons are coming! Up in the sky — I saw them!"

"Impossible..."

"Why are they making us leave the city now? The demons will surround these walls, and if we run, we'll just be hunted down in the forest like stray dogs!"

"There's no monster in the world that could knock down these walls — even Orgrim Doomhammer couldn't do it..."

"Trust in the Holy Light. The priests will protect us. The demons can't get near them. The Light will shield them — and all of us believers."

"Which ones are dragons, and which are demons?"

"Stay in line! Don't crowd! The demons can't break Lordaeron's defenses. They'll be trapped at Lake Lordamere and freeze to death on the ice. Dawn is almost here. Reinforcements from Stromgarde and Stratholme are on their way. The Holy Light will sweep the shadows from our hearts again..."

The priests called out, but the people jostling to get out still made the grand city gate a bottleneck. Wagons loaded with wounded clogged the way. Wailing, cursing, urging, barking dogs. A five- or six-year-old child sat atop a lion statue on the gatepost, bawling.

Then the commotion died down by a notch as a faint blue glow flickered in the distance.

"Is it dawn? Is the sun coming up?"

"That's not dawn..."

"The Lightbringer! Great Lightbringer, do you see that?"

Doubting voices rose from the crowd. Priests and Quel'dorei reinforcements alike raised their heads to gaze toward the southern front. They must have all glimpsed that strange light.

Uther the Lightbringer didn't answer. He just stared up at the southern wall, where the battle raged, and that murky blue light bloomed like a strange flower, its petals and vines spreading across the shadowy fiends in the sky.

He knew who it was. Jace Sesso. The man who had saved the Alliance time and again, saved countless soldiers, priests, members of the Silver Hand and Kirin Tor. Even now, around him stood several high-ranking officers who'd returned from Draenor.

Every Lordaeronian knew that no matter how loud the rumors grew about that cursed demonic artifact, no matter how many people believed Jace Sesso was a demon in human form, no matter how much evidence was laid before them — the Sons of Lothar stood firmly against those slanders. Including Turalyon himself, a leader who loathed demons, orcs, and black magic to the core.

And now, this so-called demon incarnate stood at the very front, placing himself between the demon vanguard and the people of Lordaeron.

The mere touch of his magic sent demon shadows plummeting from the sky. Those fallen fiends — killing machines that most believed knew neither fear nor retreat — panicked under his spell, scattering in terror, not daring to cross the city to pursue the fleeing masses to the north.

Holy Light, perhaps I have been gravely mistaken. But I choose to trust that this man from Brill, not yet thirty, is no demon's emissary.

At the Fire Festival in Dalaran, I looked into his eyes. Here, I have witnessed his deeds. If all this still leads me to a false judgment, then strike me down.

"How many still need to evacuate?" the Lightbringer asked a paladin behind him.

"A little less than a third — maybe a quarter. If His Highness can hold on for another hour or two..."

The paladin hesitated. Uther immediately said, "Send three gryphon riders — avoid the areas where demons and dragons are fighting — to the south wall. Tell Prince Arthas, Marshal Turalyon, and Archmage Sesso to prepare for evacuation. The citizens here need adequate protection to escape the flying demons' pursuit eastward..."

Before Uther finished, a deafening impact split the sky. Not thunder, not a demon's roar. A tear of green light ripped open the black canopy — like a bleeding demon maw vomiting a burning green fireball, trailing rolling black smoke as it plummeted onto the palace district, erupting in a fel-green fireball.

A stone giant the size of half a watchtower slowly emerged from the flames. It turned toward the evacuating crowd, advancing step by step. Every building, tower, and shack it passed ignited. It punched through them casually, kicked them over. Within a minute, the central district was an ocean of fire.

Before the first infernal had finished its rampage, a second, third, and more fel comets followed.

One struck a monastery, blasting chunks of stone into the sky. Another landed in a street, setting the entire block ablaze almost instantly.

In the darkened city, where torchlight had visibly dimmed, giant after giant rose up — green cursed flames flickering across their forms — their engine-like roars echoing between the walls.

"Stay calm! Stay calm! The almighty Holy Light protects us!"

"Run! Run!"

"Infernal! It's an infernal! Infernal!" "Look up! Another one!"

A roaring whoosh came from above. Uther looked up and saw a fel comet angling down, smashing directly into the wall beside the gate. A cascade of stone blocks collapsed, opening a breach. The great bell atop the gate clanged and groaned.

An infernal the size of a small monastery straightened up from the rubble. Beside it, the ladder on the wall and the white banner of the Kingdom of Lordaeron ignited and burned to ash.

It seemed to know who was leading the defense. It bellowed, spraying fel-fire from its maw-like stone fissure, and charged straight at Uther. It kicked aside the small houses and stone steps in its path, raising its flame-wreathed stone fist, ready to pulverize this fragile mortal.

The crowd at the gate dissolved into chaos — weeping, fleeing in every direction. Not even the Holy Light could hold them now. No matter how the priests and paladins shouted, order was gone. Wagons overturned. Luggage abandoned. Countless people were trampled. Only one figure stood firm, staring down the flame demon — Uther himself.

He grasped his hammer two-handed, adjusted his grip. As the giant drew closer, the ground shook with each step. His golden beard and hair began to smoke from the approaching heat, taking on a fel-green sheen. But his gaze didn't waver — not even as the image in his pupils was swallowed by green fire...

A flash of gold. Uther's warhammer swept upward, shattering the infernal's descending fist. Another flash of gold — the infernal's leg, formed of flaming stone, shattered, and it collapsed.

Uther didn't hesitate. A third hammer blow cracked the giant's torso open. Raging demon-fire sprayed across the ground, igniting a few eaves and piles of hay. But the source of the flame dimmed. The infernal's corpse soon became a heap of dark green rubble.

"As long as you believe in the Holy Light..." He turned to the stunned crowd and thundered, "Stay calm, everyone. The church, the priests, and the Silver Hand... will protect everyone. Until dawn. Until victory!"

A cheer erupted. Uther cast one last glance at the blue magical glow on the southern wall. No amount of words or promises was worth a single display of real power.

At the gate, the evacuation quickly regained some order. The gathered torches and crowds steadied, if barely.

But the fel comets kept falling — more and more of them. The entire city was now dotted with green demon-fire. Infernals grappled with red dragons and flying knights, struggling across the cityscape.

They had to get as many people out as possible. Unlike Dalaran or Nethergarde Keep, this city had no powerful magical barrier to stop the Burning Legion's infernal rain. Any evacuation route could be blocked by an infernal at any moment. Smashing one or two giants wouldn't turn the tide.

Is it you, Defiler?

Are you eager to send this infernal rain, trying to stop the evacuation? Are you watching all this from afar?

You want to devour these souls. Devour as many as you can. You're starving for these pure mortal souls — just like every lowly demon in the Twisting Nether.

"Archmage Sesso! The Lightbringer Uther wants you all to retreat as planned. The north gate evacuation will be over soon."

Jace turned his head on the tower, watching the gryphon rider approach from behind the city. The Ranger-Lord on the ground below had heard the rider's message too — she looked up, seeking her husband's opinion.

Soon, horns sounded from Arthas and Turalyon's direction...

Jace nodded. Then another infernal comet, trailing black smoke, struck the north gate squarely. This time, the gate couldn't hold. The entire section of wall collapsed. The crashing of stone reached them moments later.

The gryphon rider in the sky froze, turning to stare.

Even from this distance, he could see the lights below scatter into chaos again. A few small gates in the north still stood, but the evacuation speed had taken a crippling blow.

To the south, on the front lines, more and more doomguards landed on the walls, shrieking and cursing in triumph. Felguard footsteps had reached the base of the cliffs. A few felhounds had already climbed the walls, locked in combat with the soldiers.

In the distance, forests were burning from demon-fire. The demon army was slowly encircling the city.

Nearly everyone inside these walls was now trapped.
