## Chapter 1846: Hell's Inferno

The infernal that had destroyed the north wall's main gate was so massive that even officers and veterans experienced with the Burning Legion were stunned. It was eventually brought down by red dragons and the gate guards, but the cluster of buildings, the wall section, and the gatehouse beneath it were already blazing with fierce demon-fire — glaringly bright against the dark pre-dawn.

The infernal comets that followed seemed endless, one after another slamming into walls and watchtowers, rising from the rubble to continue their destruction.

They not only ignited entire stretches of wall and districts, but also pinned down the bulk of the aerial forces that had been engaging the doomguards. The Eredar, previously suppressed, came surging back. The defenders on the south wall quickly found themselves caught in a pincer, barely hanging on.

With more and more felhounds scaling the walls, the mages — whose casting had already been severely weakened by the unstable Ley-lines — were even less useful. Facing these crazed fel-beasts, Lordaeron's defenders no longer even held the numerical advantage.

Disjointed horns sounded from the western wall as well. Demonic fire raged there. A Silver Hand commander — Jace couldn't see or hear clearly who it was, maybe Sir Gavinrad, maybe another Silver Hand member — was rallying soldiers to reinforce the western wall.

He couldn't see the eastern wall from here, but the waves of defeat drifting from that direction — the despair washing over, the confusion of not knowing how to stop the fighting — the surging brutality, bloodlust, and devouring hunger — the shadows writhing and struggling in the flames — meant demons had already climbed those high cliffs and were attacking from the east.

"Is there still a way to retreat, Jace?" Vereesa shouted, bow raised.

Jace watched a soldier lose his footing while fighting a demon, falling from the wall into the dark abyss below — swallowed by a sea of green demon eyes, his only response a burst of hysterical demonic laughter.

Above the cliffs, high on the walls, the red dragons, green dragons, gryphon riders, and dragonhawk riders were besieged by a sky full of doomguards — hard-pressed to even counterattack, let alone lead survivors through the encirclement, or fly as many people out as possible in the chaos.

The entire city was ablaze. What options were left?

"Master Uther ordered us to retreat north, Archmage Jace Sesso!"

Arthas shouted as he swung his warhammer, cutting a path through a pack of felhounds, retreating with several guards toward this position.

"The east and north walls are already swallowed by demon-fire, Your Highness!" a knight behind him called out. "Maybe we could risk pushing through — but most of the people will die inside. It'd be no different from dying at demon hands!"

"I don't need you to tell me, Marwyn! I have eyes. I can see!" Arthas crushed a felhound's skull as it lunged at him, shouting in frustration.

Jace clenched his right hand, already burning with soul-fire, suppressing the boiling emotions inside him. "If you plan to leave, Your Highness, the gryphon riders might be able to get you out. Lead the evacuees toward Stratholme as fast as possible. Don't get bogged down by what's happening inside the walls. Get out as many as you can..."

"Thank you for the advice, Archmage Sesso. But protecting the people — Uther will do that better than me. Much better!" Arthas heaved for breath, warhammer in hand, then roared, "As for me — I will not abandon the people here and flee!"

"I will stay and fight to the very end. Until I face the Defiler. Until I avenge my father. Avenge every Lordaeronian who died in the past few nights! Revenge! Damn demons! The Holy Light will burn you all to ash! I will not retreat!"

Arthas bellowed at the top of his lungs, each cry punctuated by a fierce hammer blow. His mental state was near hysterical. But the golden light pouring from his warhammer only grew more blinding.

Jace glanced at the prince between castings. This was something he'd never imagined hearing before coming to this world — the Prince of Lordaeron shouting about avenging his father.

Maybe this counted as a good change he'd brought to this world. Even if no one knew this world had been "changed" at all.

And this changed world was slowly walking back to where it had been before.

Then Vereesa suddenly looked up. Jace also sensed something strange in the sky above and looked toward the clouds.

A patch of dreamlike rainbow passed across the already fouled sky. Jace knew that power intimately. The power of the Dream.

A massive, pale pink shape spread its wings, revealing a deep green dragon beneath. A golden crescent moon crest adorned her head, forming a crown-like ornament with her branch-like horns. The scales on her belly and her wide wing webbing were a dreamlike pale purple. In this pitch-black world, she seemed like a fairy cutting through all filth.

"Queen Ysera!" a red whelp cried in draconic. "The Dream Queen has arrived!"

Jace knew — even though he'd never seen her before, only heard legends — he recognized her instantly.

Beside her, a flock of green whelps emerged from the Dream's veil, diving down. They sprayed choking poison mist across the demons on the walls. Sleeping breath soon covered half the city. Several infernals rampaging through the city were dispelled of their demon-fire by the green dragons' magic, cooling into immobile black stone.

Queen Ysera swept over the city and crossed the southern wall. The felhounds and felguards climbing the outside seemed to go mad, roaring and falling, crashing into the demons behind them. Screams echoed across the frozen abyss.

The green dragon queen glanced at Jace. Just one look — no audible words — and Jace heard her voice in his mind and heart.

"Dalaran is besieged by several powerful demon lords, including Mannoroth. The Twisting Nether magic of the Legion's gate, combined with the Ley-line threads seized by the Defiler, is empowering their wicked forces. Until more defenders arrive to break the Burning Legion's invasion, everyone must hold. I will help you evacuate this city, protect the innocent souls here, and weaken Archimonde's power as much as possible — buying you the time and opportunity to reach Dalaran and support the others..."

From the reactions of the paladins and defenders on the wall, it seemed everyone could hear the Dream Queen's voice.

Ysera's words grew faint as she flew away, but her brief appearance over this section of wall was enough to turn the tide.

The mortal warriors who had been on the verge of despair, fighting with everything they had, seemed to snap back into peak form in an instant. Their wounds healed at a visible rate. Fel-fire burns mended.

One by one, they raised their weapons again, shouting, "For the Alliance!" "For Lordaeron!" as they bravely surrounded the demons that hadn't yet been driven mad by the illusion magic.

The green dragons who had been fighting alongside the reds, seeing their queen in person, flew high to join their siblings. Countless demons still poured over the walls. Infernal comets still rained down. But the morale of the mortals within the walls had been rekindled.

"Do you see that? The fire giants inside the city are retreating before our flying allies' assault, Arthas!" Turalyon shouted over. "The Undercity! We can use the Undercity passages under the green dragon queen's cover. If we move now, we might escape before the demons discover the sewer exits."

The Undercity.

Jace had never explored the city's underground sections. They said it was built like a maze down there.

As an old player, he'd initially dismissed those rumors. Then he'd seen a rough structural diagram of Lordaeron's underground that a companion had procured from somewhere. He'd realized it was very different from the Undercity he knew from his previous life — the one ruled by Sylvanas. So he'd never committed to sneaking in with anyone.

But someone like Arthas or Turalyon — nobles who'd grown up in court — would know it well.

"I agree with Turalyon's idea, Your Highness!" Jace added.

"Then we act now. We have to act now." Arthas shouted into the distance. "Immediately inform all gryphon riders — all gates, especially the north gate — tell Uther, or whoever's in charge there, to relay the order. Rally at the Undercity entrance!"

"The Undercity entrance at the palace moat!" Jace corrected. "Some of those infernals have already stepped into the palace. Ten thousand people can't get underground through the lifts!"

"Right." Arthas added. "The canal Undercity entrance. Notify them!"

"At once, Your Highness!"

Gryphon riders took wing, heading in different directions. Vereesa whistled for a dragonhawk, vaulting onto it with practiced ease. "I'll go tell Sylvanas to rally the Farstriders and other Quel'dorei to meet you. Prince Arthas. Marshal Turalyon..."

"Light be with you, Ranger-Lord!" Arthas shouted.

"Light be with you, Vereesa." Turalyon called. "Tell Sylvanas — I'll be waiting at the canal bend near the Dalaran Embassy."

"I'll remember that, Turalyon." Vereesa nodded, then looked down at Jace. "See you soon, Jace. I'll be back."

"See you by the canal."

The Ranger-Lord flashed a quick smile, turned her dragonhawk, and plunged into the smoke and demon-fire toward the distant wall.

Arthas shouted for everyone to retreat as he strode down the steps, engaging felhounds and Eredar.

The dozen or so demons that had slipped through hadn't even exchanged a full round with the Lordaeron soldiers before they fell screaming in the shadow's magical light. Flesh and bones shriveled into blackened, corroded things.

Everyone looked back at the warlock. Jace just flicked his soul-fire-burning bone staff. "Don't waste time. Keep moving, everyone."

The night sky was suffocatingly dark. The torchlight flickering through the streets struggled weakly.

Lordaeron's forces followed the canal toward the Dalaran Embassy. That was where the city's largest sewer entrance to the Undercity was. After the infernal rain, many doomguards and fear-fiends had landed here. The streets were littered with dead demons, defenders, and citizens who hadn't managed to flee.

Every knight, priest, and soldier had a tight expression. Jace knew what that meant. It was entirely possible the Undercity was already crawling with demons. Maybe even the sewer exits had been sealed by the Burning Legion.

But no matter how dire the situation, this was the only choice left.

Even if all exits were blocked, retreating into the Undercity and making a last stand using its defensive installations would be the option that hindered the Burning Legion the most.

Maybe they could hold out until reinforcements arrived. Queen Ysera had come, after all.

Don't kid yourself. We're all going to die here. Every living thing. Every mortal soul.

Jace felt the last flicker of hope in his heart being snuffed out by a suffocating force. But this force wasn't coming from within — it was external.

His perception was spot-on. The Chain of Will growing hot around his neck confirmed it.

The Defiler was here.
