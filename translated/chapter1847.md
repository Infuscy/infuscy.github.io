## Chapter 1847: A World of Blood

Jace glanced back at the southern wall they'd defended earlier. All he could see was burning demon-fire, red and green dragons fighting doomguards, and a pitch-black sky.

But the suffocating despair gripped every heart, making it hard to breathe. Not just him — several soldiers couldn't hold on either, sobbing as they slumped against walls until a priest or a paladin brother dragged them onward.

If even Lordaeron's defenders were this broken, then the citizens who'd been herded here — those who hadn't yet escaped — were even worse.

If they weren't all terrified that the demons would hear the noise and descend on this canal, the arriving citizens would have been wailing and screaming long ago. Even so, Jace could still feel the barely-suppressed terror churning restlessly in the fel-tainted smoke.

Turalyon looked back at him. Jace knew the paladin could feel it too — that undeniable presence drawing closer.

A dragon's roar tore through the oppressive silence from the south. It was Ysera's cry. Soon, answering roars from the dragonflight echoed across the entire city sky. Even the weeds sprouting between the brick cracks — withered by the pervading fel energy — seemed to surge upward as if encouraged by the Dream Queen.

Not many beings in this world could push a Dragonqueen to this level of fury. Jace knew exactly whose arrival had provoked her. He could guess what was happening in the south.

"Hurry! Get everyone inside!" Arthas shouted to the knights beside him. "Don't waste the time these dragons are buying us. We'll hold the pursuing demons here. Once you're in, don't stop... Don't go into the western sewer — that's where the demons landed. Head straight for the eastern side. If we're lucky, there's a passage there that can get several thousand people out in an hour, into Tirisfal Glades. Maybe we can catch up with the people heading east toward the monastery. May the Light protect them... and us."

When he mentioned the numbers and the time, even the Lordaeron prince — pushing his will to its limit — swallowed nervously. At this point, no one knew what the city would look like in an hour.

But the knights answered firmly, then organized the soldiers to lead the people down the canal, following the steps and ramps into the Undercity.

Then a deafening impact came from the south — so loud it seemed to stop everyone's hearts. The earth shook violently. Buildings around them collapsed and tilted. Even sturdy towers shed chunks of wall. Many people couldn't keep their footing and tumbled down the slope into the canal water.

The soldiers left above fared little better. The ground split open, spewing foul fel-black smoke. Many fell or were burned before they could scramble back.

Jace steadied himself and turned toward the source of the southern tremor. Near the southern wall, a jagged, blade-like fel volcano had pierced into the sky, spewing vast quantities of fel lava. The demon horde's green eyes burned brighter in this light. Their roars grew even more piercing.

Soon, fel ash rained down like a storm. It burned and corroded any skin it touched. The refugees still above ground panicked even more, scrambling to push deeper into the Undercity. Not even the church priests and paladins could maintain order.

Though the buildings blocked his view of what was happening in the south, the blessings and healing the green dragons had granted were rapidly fading. Everyone's will teetered on the brink of collapse.

A hoarse but familiar dragon's cry came from overhead. Jace looked up to see Needle Flame descending nearby, carrying several wounded knights and rangers. At this point, the sight of a genuine black dragon barely drew a reaction from Lordaeron's soldiers or even the civilians.

After setting them down, Needle Flame turned and spotted Jace. "Vereesa told me to find you..."

"Get into the Undercity with everyone else, Needle Flame. Take human form!" Jace pointed at the ramp leading down.

"I can still fight!" Needle Flame spread her wings, sweeping away a cloud of fel-volcanic ash, and stalked forward angrily. "I'm not badly hurt. I can save more people..."

"That's enough, Needle Flame." Jace grabbed one of her horns. "Get out of here. The green dragon queen is buying time for our retreat — not so we can fight a decisive battle with the Burning Legion here. We still need to find Grede later. We'll need your help!"

"I know. I know..."

The black dragon spat out a plume of black smoke in frustration, then shifted into human form in the dust. She was about to descend the steps when Jace grabbed her again. "Where's Solara?"

Needle Flame froze. "I saw her helping the other green dragons evacuate Silver Hand soldiers trapped on the western wall, Jace Sesso. I can go find her now."

"Get inside!" Jace pointed forcefully at the crowd streaming down the ramp. "Do as I say!"

"But what about Solara?" Needle Flame shouted. "She hasn't come back yet!"

"Do you doubt her magic, Needle Flame? She's faster than you. Stronger than you. Listen to me — don't make her worry about you on top of everything else. Now get in there!"

Needle Flame ground her teeth in fury, but finally said, "Then you'd better hurry."

"I'll wait for Vereesa and Solara, then go in right behind you," Jace said.

She nodded, glanced one more time at the sky, and turned to follow the crowd.

But even as he said it, Jace stared up at the sky, listening to the green and red dragons' cries, his mind a blank.

He couldn't let Needle Flame go back out there.

Then a sudden surge of void-fury exploded from the south. Jace felt his consciousness tear apart with the rage. Thousands of souls screamed in unison, unleashing the hatred and fury they'd held when they were killed.

He looked south. Half the sky had been stained ice-blue by some kind of soul-storm. Just gazing at that dark magical power, hearing those souls howl and curse with mad fury, was enough to shatter reason. Being caught in it...

"Defiler... you will not advance another step!"

Ysera's roar thundered across the entire royal city. The sound hit like lightning, shaking nearby walls until they collapsed.

A group of Quel'Thalas elves arrived from the northern streets. Sylvanas and Turalyon exchanged a few words, then she immediately directed all Quel'dorei down the ramp into the Undercity. Vereesa crossed a collapsed section of rubble to reach Jace.

"Jace... are they all inside?"

"Solara and Morofil are still out there," Jace said. "Morofil I can summon back. Solara?"

"I tried to reach her through dream magic. Told her we were retreating here." Vereesa said. "I said we'd be at the Dalaran Embassy. Maybe she hasn't gotten the message yet."

"She hasn't," Jace said, shaking his head.

"I'll wait for her here. Try to reach her again." Vereesa said. "You go with the paladins. I heard some people say the demons have already found their way into the Undercity."

"Alright." Jace nodded and hurried down the ramp to the Undercity entrance.

The stench of decay wafting up from below mixed with the foul odor of fel energy and the reek of shadow. It felt like standing at the mouth of a poisonous cave, not an escape route.

"General Sylvanas." Jace nodded to the Ranger-General beside him.

Sylvanas nodded back. "Follow me, Jace. We have to get in there and clear out the demons, or else..."

She stopped mid-sentence, freezing. Her brow furrowed. Jace saw her face illuminated by the demon-fire light seeping in from outside — red as blood.

Then a wave of crushing despair hit him. The urge to give up all resistance flooded his mind and spirit.

Jace looked back outside. People still lingering at the entrance were clutching their heads and wailing. Some collapsed on the spot, unable to rise. Even a paladin was leaning against the wall in agony, the faint golden glow of his armor and the holy relic at his belt being eaten away by shadow-corrosion, darkening, cracking with black-purple lines.

Despair. Pure, absolute despair. Every step Jace took felt like walking toward his own final end.

He pushed past the weeping civilians and soldiers, struggling to get back out. The sky had turned blood-red. Everything around him — broken walls, rubble, still-standing houses and towers, and the weeping people collapsing in the shadow-corrosion — looked as if it had been drenched in a sea of blood.

Compared to this descending curtain of blood, the demon roars and magical explosions from the walls near and far seemed thin and muffled. Like hearing gunshots on the shore while drowning in the deep — frightening, but no longer relevant.

Finger of Death.

A thick, blood-red magical thread cut across the same blood-red sky. They seemed to merge — yet were clear enough to distinguish.

Then another. And another. They arced between every living thing in the sky, leaping and rebounding. They pierced countless flying creatures, tore through tower after tower of roughly equal height across the city.

The threads' movement seemed instantaneous — yet also stretched across endless years, torturing every witness. The profound darkness dredged up the deepest despair from every spectator's heart, cornering them in the dead ends, the last roads, the unsolvable problems of their entire lives, forcing them to endure that unbearable scrutiny once more...

Despair.

Through the haze, Jace glimpsed Vereesa collapsed at an intersection ahead. He sprinted to her and lifted her up. Bloody tears streaked the ranger's eyes.

Several Quel'dorei lay around her. Some still breathed weakly. Others were already still.

"Anyone who can still move — help me save them!"

Jace shouted behind him. The Lordaeron prince clutched his warhammer, struggling to rise at Jace's call. But the golden light in his hand had vanished without a trace. He looked like he might collapse at any second.

Sylvanas was the first to reach them, kneeling beside Jace and Vereesa. She grabbed her sister's shoulder and pulled a flask from her belt — a brilliant golden Sunwell water. In this blood-red sky, only that flask held any color.

"Don't feed her that here. The shadow energy will corrode the arcane power quickly." Jace grabbed the Ranger-General's wrist. "Take her below. Feed it to her there. The deeper, the better. Away from this shadow!"

Sylvanas nodded, holding her sister close. She pushed the flask toward Jace. "You drink this one, Jace. You look terrible. I have another. Don't worry about her."

"Thank you. Thank you." Jace took the flask, helping another still-breathing Quel'dorei nearby. Turalyon and another paladin arrived from the ramp to help rescue people too. A dull impact came from above — the Dalaran Embassy's tall tower. Jace blinked and looked up. A green dragon had just crashed into the tower's outer wall, falling onto the roof.

Blood-red dust and rubble swallowed everything. But in that instant, Jace saw the whelp's face. The eyes that should have held moonlight had gone pale. The emerald scales had lost all luster.

He was dead.

Not just this whelp. All across the blood-red city sky, the whelps that had been fighting demons were falling like rain. Some were already dead. Others were barely alive, struggling as they fell. Alleys behind him, the other side of the canal, the end of the street ahead, the direction of the church — thud, thud, thud...

"Jace!"

Turalyon was supporting a Farstrider at the edge of the ramp, shouting, "Come on! You can't help them. We'll hold the Undercity together. We'll buy Dalaran enough time to counterattack!"

"Jace! We..."

Sylvanas was shouting too. But the voice was no longer clear.

I've already abandoned the dwarf. Do I abandon Solara too?

And then who else will you leave behind, Jace Sesso? What have you even been fighting for?

What are you fighting for, Jace Sesso? What are you living for?

Never abandon me again, Jace. Never again...

I won't...
