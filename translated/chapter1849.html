Chapter 1849: Dawn

In the snowy valley, the female mage stared at the darkness devouring the sky and earth beyond the mountain peaks, her brow furrowed deep. She turned to look at the other mage leaning between icy spikes, clutching Atiesh, breathing in ragged, pained gasps.

"You feel it too, don't you, Khadgar? This is the man you've trusted all along -- your so-called savior. My teacher witnessed the same catastrophe once."

"If it weren't for you, Lady Aegwynn..." Khadgar coughed, then shouted in fury, "If it weren't for your arrogance in summoning the Defiler, for stubbornly pushing that impossible plan of yours, for destroying half of Lordaeron in the process -- how would he have ended up like this?!"

The female mage let out a laugh. "Countless entities in the dark void have their eyes on this world. Archimonde is just one of them."

"Even if it wasn't the Defiler, he would have had to face another moment of annihilation sooner or later anyway. A qualified guardian should know when to make sacrifices, when to... make decisions -- to resolve problems before everything slides into an irredeemable apocalypse. I've already failed once..."

"In a sense, you're even crazier than he is, Khadgar. Jace Sesso craves power, craves... control over everything, because he wants to crush the Burning Legion and take revenge on Sargeras. You could say we share the same goal. He just can't control his own power anymore. But what about you? What are you after?"

"You don't know him at all." Khadgar paused, lowering his head. "Trust... because I know the man. I fought alongside him."

"Enough. I'll give you one more chance. Hand Atiesh over to me. I will break this prophecy, before everything passes the point of no return." Aegwynn's voice turned cold. "We've wasted too much time already."

"I... haven't lost yet," Khadgar said through gritted teeth.

Within the royal city, the Burning Legion's assault was effortlessly torn apart.

The tide of darkness flooded over rows of houses and streets, climbing the walls. The demonic legions that had been madly pouring through the breach moments ago were now fleeing in full reverse. Above the city, the fel-green soul-mass swirled thicker and thicker, spiraling toward the crumbling central tower.

The warlock's eyes had been completely consumed by soul fire. Ulthalesh's skull seemed to have regained its flesh, transforming into a hollow devil's face, spewing soul-stuff and shadow flame from its eyes and half-open maw.

But just then, he found he could no longer weave this soul well.

The dark tide leaked through cracks that spread little by little, impossible to repair no matter how he tried.

Looking down, he saw the city walls around the capital collapse like a child's toy castle, falling off the high cliffs and tumbling into the forest below. At the same time, the still-standing houses, palaces, cathedrals, towers, and monuments within the city shattered into dust...

A giant figure appeared from behind the western wall as it crumbled. He wore magnificent golden pauldrons, his face solemn as a holy icon. His azure skin bore none of the demonic runes that marked other Legion members -- flawless, like a gem polished smooth by a master craftsman.

If not for the burning fel-fire in his eyes, no one would have taken him for a demon. He looked like Prophet Velen's twin brother -- a high-ranking Eredar sage, but younger, and far more absolute.

Even after his earlier direct confrontation with Queen Ysera, he seemed completely unshaken, as perfect as when he'd first set foot on Azeroth.

But even the Defiler, powerful as he was, frowned at this moment.

He had casually flattened the mortals' entire city, yet he could not destroy that black tower.

It stood there defiantly, like a stubborn beast refusing its shepherd, daring even to lower its horns and point them his way.

"I see. Ulthalesh... Sargeras has always been concerned about this artifact being lost."

Archimonde smiled and murmured, "He wanted Kil'jaeden to retrieve it quickly, or else destroy it. Pity -- I don't have Kil'jaeden's patience."

The Eredar raised his right hand and clenched his fist. Around the central tower, the shadowstorm gathered from the void, gradually forming dark claws that gripped the tower tight, squeezing tighter and tighter...

Inside the tower, everything turned black and white. Sola clenched her teeth in pain, refusing to make a sound. The shadows scraping along the outer walls tore at her spirit but could not truly touch her flesh or skin.

Because another force was gripping those claws like iron chains, prying them apart bit by bit.

The earth split open, spewing shadowy mist. The water in the canals had turned pitch black. As the two dark forces twisted against each other, no color remained anywhere the eye could see. The forests withered like dry insect carcasses on a gray wasteland; the hills festered like swollen, peeling wounds on necrotic skin.

"Is that all you've got, Archimonde?"

Jace laughed coldly. His voice barely carried through the howling, all-consuming storm of doom, but he knew the Eredar heard him perfectly well.

"Who do you think you are?" Archimonde stepped forward, staring at the barely visible demon-shaped speck atop the black tower. "You're nothing but a maggot feeding on Sargeras's power. Every ounce of your magic comes from him. Your own existence is meaningless."

"Then kill the maggot." Jace raised his violet-red demonic eyes, fixing them on the giant. "Can't do it? Or are you the same? A maggot feeding on Sargeras's power..."

The Defiler's unchanging expression finally twitched. He stepped forward again, his left hand gripping the tower -- the earth trembled -- and raised his other hand high. The sky and earth changed color. Blood-red fel-light stained everything once more.

Good. I've been waiting for this.

The Soul-Song in the warlock's arms suddenly glowed with pure violet light. Pure despair, gathered here.

Seeing that light, Archimonde hesitated. He recognized the fragment of an Atama Crystal -- a treasure the Eredar had sought for tens of thousands of years.

But he did not change his course. Because he saw the warlock's eyes had also been dyed blood-red. No one could stand before inevitable death. Not the creations of Titans, not anything that had walked through the Twisting Nether...

Not even himself.

A more brilliant blood-red light expanded outward. Tens of thousands of demons, the demon lords who led them, and on the other side -- the allied forces fighting the Legion within and around Dalaran -- dwarves, elves, dragons -- all raised their heads to witness this sight.

Varian Wrynn gasped for breath, his sword in hand. He looked back toward Stormwind, then again at the spreading blood-red light, gritting his teeth.

Behind the allied forces, Prophet Velen, maintaining his Holy Light barrier, noticed the same omen. He slowly closed his eyes, his lips moving in silent prayer.

Vick Sesso lifted his helmet's visor, tucked his sword under his arm, and tugged the reins to make Arna stop. The mare whinnied toward the other side of the Alterac peaks, as if calling out to someone. His heart tightened.

Hennie Sesso, tending to the wounded, couldn't help shuddering. Tears seeped from her eyes, but she didn't stop her work. She muttered "Light protect us" under her breath and kept wrapping bandages around a young soldier's wound.

Inside Dalaran, Ursula Delin and several Kirin Tor alchemists were startled by the sudden tremor and stopped their work to look outside. Seeing the sky drenched in blood-red, her legs gave way and she slumped against the wall.

In Dalaran's vault, Amy Marin gasped for air as she maintained the Eye of Dalaran's spell, her mana nearly depleted. A Kirin Tor mage immediately took her place.

At the high wall along the border between the Eastern Weald and Tirisfal, Erland Dumor, Sulenna Kael'don, Garalen Arrow, and "Blackclaw" Galders exchanged worried glances, but none of them spoke.

Beside them, on the road leading to the royal city, Stratholme's reinforcements, Stromgarde's Red Knights under General Danath Trollbane, and Quel'Thalas's Sunfury Legion marched in full force toward that blood-red glow. The fluttering blue phoenix banners, Arathi clenched-fist banners, and Lordaeron's white banners were all stained varying shades of red.

At the Cathedral of Light in Stormwind, the blood-red canopy stained everything. People in the square stared at the scene in confusion, none knowing what it meant. Morgan Ladimore held his terrified wife and two children close, whispering a prayer for his distant friend.

On a ship off the coast of Stranglethorn, a goblin collector and his alchemist friend looked up blankly at the suddenly red sky, murmuring about what it could mean.

High above Booty Bay, an Amani priestess stepped out of her cave, her face solemn as she gazed upward, wondering if this was connected to the northern disaster she'd heard of.

Aegwynn was channeling a razor-sharp arcane blade, ready to deliver the final blow to her opponent, when the collision of those two world-shattering forces made her freeze for an instant.

But that single instant was all Khadgar and Atiesh needed to seize their chance for a desperate counterattack...

Ulthalesh's surface flesh was torn apart by the all-consuming blood-red fel-light. Its horns snapped off. Its skull cracked open. But it kept laughing its mad laughter.

The Defiler stumbled back several paces in disbelief. His chest had been scorched blood-red. Dark discordant veins spread from there across his entire upper body, even to his neck and waist... The fel-fire in his eyes began to flicker. His lips started to tremble.

"...You cannot possibly. Master my magic."

You're right. I don't know your magic at all. I don't need to master it, Archimonde. Jace wanted to shout it triumphantly, but he couldn't speak anymore.

The black tower began to topple. Dim shadows scattered from his body. His robe, boots, pack, belt -- all burned away. The pocket watch shattered before his eyes, turning to dust.

And then, his flesh.

Dawn had come. But so had the end. Finally, it was over.

If he still had a mouth, still had lips, he would have smiled to celebrate this moment. All the unspeakable things along this road -- finally at an end.

Started by him. Ended by him. Was this what they called freedom?

No, not yet. My master -- well, former master. Almost. Too bad. But not yet...

In the depths of the unseen, a ghostly blue figure reached out toward him, until everything within his sight fell into nothingness.
