Chapter 185: First Come, First Served

"You and your friend took part in the Battle of the Black Dragon in Southshore?" Halister said in surprise. "My apologies, sir. I heard about your experience in Southshore — if I'd known sooner that you were that Jace Sesso… but our main purpose right now is to find leads related to the theft, and everything happened so suddenly. Forgive me for not having prepared earlier — I didn't get any sleep last night either."

He went on: "If even you felt the invader's power was unstoppable, then whether or not it was Teron Gorefiend himself, it must have been one of his capable lieutenants."

"Speaking of which, may I know the name of that dwarf — your friend?"

Hearing this, Jace thought, thank goodness I coordinated our story with Grede beforehand. Otherwise this could've been a disaster.

Still, thinking back on the earlier conversation, even if they hadn't coordinated, they probably wouldn't have been able to dig much more out of him.

He'd never mentioned the Eye of Dalaran to Grede, nor had he brought up his suspicions about Kel'Thuzad.

Actually, when he'd been at Grede's place, Jace had felt a twinge of guilt, feeling like he was keeping Grede at arm's length by not telling him a lot of things.

But now it looked like not telling him had been the right call.

In this world, there were some things he could only keep to himself for now. That was good for him and good for the people around him. Not until he truly had the power to change and control things — and that power couldn't come from anyone else. Only from himself.

"Grede Thunderfist. He's my best friend," Jace replied.

Halister thought for a moment. "Grede Thunderfist — yes, that is the dwarf warrior. Back to that command — the one you presented at the Orcish-language meeting, the order concerning Teron Gorefiend. Could you go over once more how you obtained it?"

Jace glanced at Halister and cursed inwardly. So this is where you were waiting for me.

If it hadn't been for that sudden flash of inspiration at the herbalist's door, when he'd thought to coordinate this with Grede, everything would have been exposed right there.

Had he known it would be this much trouble, he might as well have just told the truth straight up at the Orcish-language meeting.

What did he care whether Kel'Thuzad went to Southshire or not? So what if he turned it into a second Northrend? Duskwood was going to end up that way anyway.

As his anger flared, Jace thought of the Ladimore family again.

He thought of that boy his own age sitting by the campfire, telling him he wanted his wife and children to think their father was someone remarkable.

If time kept moving along its set course, they were going to face a terrible disaster as it was. Because of one herb-gathering trip of his, should they have to deal with Kel'Thuzad on top of that?

And his own parents still lived nearby… right. Couldn't let Kel'Thuzad poison his home environment.

Jace shook his head. "I don't want to relive the Karazhan experience again, Mr. Halister. Recounting it publicly at that meeting took every ounce of courage I had. You can go ask the other scholars who were there — as bystanders, they might remember it more precisely than I, the one who lived it. Making me tell it again won't change a thing."

Halister nodded slightly. "Ah, Karazhan — of course I understand what that means. Very well. I didn't come here to deliberately prod at a past you'd rather not revisit. Still, may I take another look at that command, Mr. Sesso? The Kirin Tor would like me to bring it back for further study."

"You're too late," Jace said. "I already handed that command over to the elves. Go ask at the Quel'Thalas embassy."

"Elves?" Halister asked. "Why give it to the elves instead of the Kirin Tor?"

Jace looked up. "So how much were you planning to pay?"

Halister hesitated and said nothing. Jace pressed: "Not even a single copper, then? So this is outright robbery?"

"Absolutely not, Mr. Sesso," Halister said at once. "The Kirin Tor fully respects your right as the owner to dispose of it as you see fit, but…"

Jace set the crumpled transcription copy on the table and spread it out. "This is identical to what was on that command. I copied it down by hand, word for word, and Master Kel'Thuzad himself certified that there's no difference. You can take this one. Free of charge."

Halister took it. "Thank you very much. I'll take it with me then, Mr. Sesso. This is extremely important to our investigation."

With that, he gave the female mage a meaningful look and hurried downstairs.

Did that count as passing?

Jace walked downstairs and, once he'd confirmed all the mages had left, finally let himself relax a little.

"Did you give that command to those people?" Dushan asked, eating bread as he spoke.

"No chance, Master," Jace said. "Remember that elf from this afternoon? He'd already bought the command off me."

Dushan laughed. "Actually, the moment that fellow said he was from the Relics Society, I'd already guessed what he was after. Your command wasn't much in itself, but after last night's chaos, it was bound to get a lot of people's attention. Selling it off was for the best — let them fight over it among themselves."

"That's what I figured too." Jace picked up a piece of bread. "When are you heading back to Stormwind, Master?"

Dushan said, "I'm setting out for Southshore tomorrow. When are you heading back? The academy officially resumes on January 21st — you need to be there by the 18th."

Jace said, "If it's a fast ship, the kind that doesn't stop at Menethil Harbor, is there any chance we could make it in ten days?"

"You'd still be better off leaving yourself some buffer," Dushan said. "How about I find you a fast ship on the 5th?"

"Thank you, Master. Will it be hard to find a ship right now?"

"Shouldn't be a problem. It's still early, and things are far less busy than they were in November. So still the two of you, then? Is Mr. Thunderfist returning to Stormwind as well?"

"Yes, sorry to trouble you."

"Noted." Dushan said, "Then I wish you both smooth sailing on the rest of your journey. This trip's been nothing but rough."

"And fair winds to you too, Master."

After dinner, just as Dushan was about to head back to his room to rest, Jace suddenly remembered the Mage Armor matter.

"Master, I'd like to ask you for one more favor…"

"What favor?" Dushan turned back.

"I want to practice a spell. Could you keep an eye on me so nothing goes wrong?"

"Tired. I haven't had any sleep to speak of since yesterday, Jace. And your shoulder's in that state — what are you pushing yourself for… you're not the only one exhausted, watching you is exhausting too."

And with that, before Jace could even reply, Dushan shut the door with a bang.

Jace figured he really had been pushing his luck. He leaned back in his chair and, having heard Dushan out, suddenly felt dead tired himself. And with one arm injured, practicing a mage spell was going to be ten times harder — this spell had pretty demanding gesturing requirements. If something went wrong and messed up tomorrow's travel plans, that'd be a disaster.
