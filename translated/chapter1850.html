Chapter 1850: Ash City

"He... was prepared to sacrifice everything all along."

"I'm not surprised, Lady Aegwynn."

Snow still fell, but the taint of corruption and filth had faded from it. It was pure again, as it always had been.

The female mage lay in the snow, her blood staining the white around her. A sharp icicle had pierced her chest, making every breath a struggle, a fight.

Khadgar watched her closely, not daring to relax for even a second.

His hands trembled violently, as if he still couldn't believe he'd just defeated someone. "Was I... wrong?" she asked in a low voice.

"There's no point talking about that now. It's dawn."

The sky was still a dark blue before him. The forest over there was pitch black, hiding whatever lay within. But the high peaks were already dusted with gold -- almost blinding in contrast.

Khadgar couldn't help raising a hand to shield his eyes. By then, the female mage in the snow had already gone silent.

Soon, the terrible ice spires all around glistened in the sunlight. Snow fell upon her, burying her little by little.

The cold wind howled. Snow swirled. As if none of it had ever happened. But he knew, deep down, that this world had changed forever.

Still, he had no time for sentiment. No time to rest, to let his body recover.

The mage looked at the Book of Medivh in his hands -- most of its dark magic drained -- and at the former Guardian's dense notes. Then he looked at Atiesh.

If his earlier perceptions and judgments weren't too far off the mark, he needed to move fast and find that warlock, or it would be too late.

A tawny-brown raven flew over the other side of the mountains. She watched the snowy peaks, knowing that layer upon layer of golden light would soon bathe the land, driving away the shadows shrouding the frozen lake.

Moss Thistlemane didn't know what that mountain was called, but she knew what lay below it -- Lake Lordamere. A name countless humans had cried out countless times in these past few days.

That night, under the Defiler Archimonde's enslaved ice magic, demons had poured through the Legion's portal in the middle of Lake Lordamere, bent on trampling this already battered land, stripping away the world's last vestiges of magic, and turning the mortal realm into a complete fel-dead wasteland.

But now, the portal's fel-light had dimmed. The demon legions on the ice, who had been repelling the allied counterattack, had lost their night-long evil frenzy.

The turning point had been that blood-red hue that had nearly swallowed the entire sky. She had been flying at full speed in response to Queen Ysera's call when she felt that suffocating shadow energy and nearly fainted, plummeting from the sky.

Thankfully, she'd held on. But her transformation had been slightly warped -- a pair of heavy horns had begun to protrude from her head again, throwing off her flight rhythm.

She didn't dare land and try to reshape the transformation. The slopes were crawling with demon soldiers, and she had to reach the Dream Queen as quickly as possible. She couldn't let demons slow her down.

And there was a foreboding feeling gnawing at her heart -- one that made her not want to be distracted by anything else.

The farther north she flew, the closer to the human capital, the colder the air grew. Not just because of the grim frost that covered the entire region, but because...

The forest below grew more desolate. The earth itself turned dry and gray. Lush trees were reduced to black branches. The scent of life grew thinner and thinner.

Moss, who had grown up in Kalimdor, had never seen such a barren wasteland. Yet at the same time, she felt a strange familiarity in the despair and loneliness that saturated the air -- as if she'd encountered it somewhere before. Or rather, in someone before.

And the source of that presence was at the edge of her sight -- a gray city reduced to ash, burned to nothing.

The shadow wreathed in gray mist at the center of those ruins -- just looking at it made her feel like she was plunging back into that nightmare from which she could barely wake.

Then a warm energy crept into her heart, pulling her back from that drowning pain. The raven looked toward its source. Amid the almost endless gray hills, a patch of fresh green fields and low trees stood out, surrounded by tall, black dead trees.

Moss immediately wheeled around and flew straight there, descending through the corrupted trees. Several Kaldorei druids and a group of emerald whelps were already gathered there.

Among them was the archdruid who had led her and the other druids through the Dream Gate to reach the Legion's portal and reinforce the Lordaeron alliance -- Malfurion Stormrage.

"Archdruid."

Moss hesitated as she surveyed the woodland. The thick grove and hills before her resembled a woman sleeping among the slopes, but the form was unclear. For a moment she thought she was still trapped in the strange visions the shadow energy had shown her.

"The Dream Queen..."

"Queen Ysera's spirit has returned to the Emerald Dream, Moss Thistlemane." The archdruid gazed at the lush, garden-like mound. "Though the human leaders in that capital succeeded in stopping the Defiler's mad attempt to drain the souls of the entire city... Ysera, still recovering from the Nightmare, could not withstand his power."

"Returned... to the Dream." Moss murmured.

"Now is not the time for grief," the archdruid said. "Though the portal, deprived of the Defiler's support, has collapsed, tens of thousands of demons have already set foot in this wounded world. They won't just lie down and die."

Moss suddenly looked up as if something had occurred to her. "Sommariel? Archdruid... I mean, is she here?"

Malfurion Stormrage turned to her. "She was in that city when Jace Sesso defeated the Defiler."

Jace Sesso... defeated... the Defiler?

Moss stood frozen. She couldn't help recalling that familiar presence, that strange power that had stained the sky and earth red. The possibility had crossed her mind, but she hadn't dared to dwell on it -- not until the archdruid, the leader of the Kaldorei, spoke it aloud.

"Please... permit me, honored Archdruid." She stammered, then steadied her breathing and lowered her head. "Please allow me to go into that city to find them."

"That place has been consumed by darkness, Moss Thistlemane. Until the land beneath is healed, even souls cannot escape its torment." Malfurion said. "We all wish to enter quickly, to find Jace Sesso and any possible survivors. But recklessness will only bring more needless death."

Moss shook her head. "Then they would be..."

Outside the royal city, gray-white ash and soul fragments drifted among the black tree carcasses and demon corpses strewn everywhere.

The Silver Hand, the Church, and Lordaeron's last defenders led thousands of surviving refugees out from the Undercity and here, shielded by the Light's blessing.

Though the hellhounds that had been prowling through the Undercity had vanished like dispelled magic, and though they'd yet to encounter a single demon standing after leaving the city, they never looked back, never stopped. The darkness that had nearly torn apart every living soul had almost dragged everyone into the abyss -- compared to that, even the demons hadn't seemed so terrifying.

The paladins' Holy Light had protected them from being drowned on the spot while they were still in the Undercity. But Turalyon knew the darkness hadn't failed to swallow them all only because of the Light's protection. It was also because its caster was not the Defiler, but...

Of course, it wasn't just the Silver Hand and the Church members. Everyone knew this blessing of protection wouldn't last much longer. No one relaxed or slowed their pace until they were completely exhausted, the entire column stretched beyond endurance, and even the priests could no longer muster their prayers. Only then did they stop in this woodland.

"Look! The royal city..."

Someone mustered the courage to look back. It was morning now. The fog and dust had thinned somewhat. But when they looked behind them, the magnificent walls that had once stood on the northern shore of Lake Lordamere had simply vanished. The entire city had been reduced to rubble.

"The Defiler is going to destroy the world! Tirisfal is doomed! We're all doomed!"

"At least Dalaran's still standing. You all heard what that dragon said last night -- Stratholme's legions are coming to save us!"

Turalyon was about to shout something to calm the crowd, on the verge of panic. But as the mist continued to thin, everyone fell silent on their own.

A horrifyingly huge figure emerged from the ruins. Even the words he was about to speak died in his throat.

The skeletal remains of a giant hundreds of feet tall knelt in the center of the ruined city, draped in tarnished golden armor. One hand still gripped a half-collapsed tower, as if trying to push it over. But his other arm had broken off and fallen, and his skull had also tumbled from his neck, smashing onto the roof of another palace that hadn't yet completely fallen. Its hollow eye sockets happened to face this way, as if its bone-deep hatred had not yet been spent.

You defeated him, didn't you, Jace?

"Has Master Sesso caught up?" Arthas asked the nearby paladins. "Turalyon?"

Turalyon was at a loss for words. Vereesa, leaning against a withered tree trunk, caught her breath and said, "He didn't follow us. You can feel it, Turalyon. He's still in there!"

She stumbled a few steps toward the royal city. "Vereesa!" Sylvanas shouted her name and chased after her, pulling her close.

The two elves collapsed into a patch of dead brush. Sylvanas held her sister's neck and face, whispering, "Don't be reckless, Vereesa. If you go back to that place now, you'll never get out. We barely made it out of there. I promised Jace I'd keep you safe. Do you want him to come back and find you even more badly hurt, or worse... and then blame me for it?"

"But, but... sister..." Vereesa could hold it in no longer. The entire forest fell silent.

"Little Jace! Green dragon!" "Little elf! Little one!"

"You'd better answer me, one of you, or when I dig you out myself, you won't be making any sounds for the rest of your lives, damn it!"

A dwarf walked alone through the mist-shrouded ruins of the road.

He stepped over a broken pillar, glaring at that ruined tower and the shadow of the dead giant, squinting.

"Well, you sure as hell did something this time, Jace Sesso!"

"Hah, what was it all for? Just so you'd have bragging rights later? So you could always one-up me over drinks? Yeah, you're really something. Did you ever think -- ending up like this, not even able to call back to me -- even if you'd taken down a Titan, you'd still need me to bail you out. Nothing to brag about there. It'd almost be embarrassing. You get what I'm saying?"

"I mean, you better just jump out, brush yourself off, and say something tough like 'That all you got?' 'Easy money.' Or I'll never respect you. Winning that ugly -- there's no glory in it."

"Tch. Still nothing?" "Talk!"

"Jace Sesso!"

"What about that big green lizard? You think I'm just some worthless lump of dirt? Come on out! Let me see how tough you really are. I'm still standing here!"

"Ugh. Cough, cough. What is that stench?"

"I'm getting tired. I'm begging you, say something..."

The dwarf muttered, flopping onto a nearby slab to catch his breath, rubbing his eyes hard. Suddenly, he thought he heard something nearby. He whipped his head around, cautiously raising his axe.

It was that idiot's cloak.

The dwarf jumped onto a high piece of rubble like a grasshopper. He spotted the brownish-red thing, grabbed the cloak, and brushed off the residual shadow clinging to it. Beneath it lay a pale green dragon, eyes slightly open, staring at him.

"Sola? Sola, that works too. Sola's fine. Hey! Hey!"

Grede shook Sola by the shoulder, waving his hand in front of her eyes. "Where's Jace, Sola? You're wearing his cloak. Where'd he go?"

"You don't recognize me? Fine, you're out of it. Don't answer. I'll get you out first."

The dwarf hoisted the green dragon onto his shoulder. He stared at the desolate ruins ahead, swallowed hard, and his lips trembled slightly.

He raised an eyebrow, wiped his eyes hard with one hand, and finally stepped off the collapsed wall.

Then, out of the corner of his eye, he spotted a shadow circling in the high sky. "Hey! Over here! Little black dragon, over here!"
