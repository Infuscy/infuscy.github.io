## Chapter 1851: One Good News, and One Bad News

The snow in Dalaran never seemed to stop falling, as if it refused to let anyone wake from that nightmare.

Amy Marin had just walked out of a meeting hall. She looked out at the snow-covered garden, then toward the corridor beyond, momentarily lost in thought.

"Lady Marin!"

Jaina Proudmoore waved from the other corridor. She glanced at the crowd filing out of the meeting hall, then walked over. "So? How is he? I mean, Master Jace Sesso..."

Amy shook her head. "No word yet."

"I heard from my father." Jaina looked toward the street beyond the garden. "He stopped the Defiler Archimonde in the Lordaeron capital. But..."

"The Kirin Tor is still searching for him, Jaina." Amy interrupted quietly, her voice steady. "We can't make any judgments until we find enough evidence. As far as I know, the situation in that city is worse than we imagined. We don't have the conditions to thoroughly search the area yet."

Jaina hummed in acknowledgment, then hesitated. "Actually, yesterday, before that demon lord froze the entire Lake Lordamere with ice magic, I met Master Sesso. He..."

Amy's eyes flickered. "What?"

"He looked terrible." Jaina said. "Like he'd been badly wounded -- hurt by demonic energy. And... I don't know how to describe it. I've never seen wounds like that. Lady Sola was with him. She was very worried too."

Amy was silent for a moment, not answering her student's words. Then Jaina noticed an archmage in pale blue robes approaching and immediately lowered her head. "Master Marin."

"...I won't disturb you further, Lady Marin."

After Jaina left, Amy turned to see her father. She could no longer force herself to stay calm.

She burst into tears in old Marin's arms, no longer caring about the passing mage colleagues, or the students and juniors in the garden.

"He'll show up. If he's alive, he'll find a way to come back to Vereesa, to Lord Grede Thunderfist, to Mr. and Mrs. Sesso. He wouldn't just disappear without a word, leaving all of us here searching for him with no leads. Unless... Father, I can't accept this. What can I do to find him?"

"I won't pretend to know Master Sesso better than you do, Amy." Old Marin held his daughter close, speaking softly. "But I think... maybe it's time we let him rest."

"Whether he chose to leave, or... I feel that perhaps this isn't a bad thing. When I first saw him at the Wizard Sanctum, I knew he was no ordinary young man. He was kind. He took responsibility. He was undeniably brave. But at the same time, he carried things in his heart that he couldn't share with others... And whatever those burdens were, they weren't a treasure to him. They were an unbearably heavy weight."

"But it's not fair." Amy's voice was muffled. "He just wanted... to live in peace with his family."

"Now that we've finally won, everyone who fought at Lake Lordamere -- humans, Quel'dorei, even the foreigners from another continent, the dragons -- all of them were helped by him or saved by him. Everyone knows he's a hero. Everyone hoped he'd get what he wanted. And now he's gone? That's not fair. I refuse to believe it ends like this."

"I promised him I'd find a way to make everyone here believe he was a real hero. But I didn't do anything. Until the very end, he was still facing the suspicion of the entire world, or even... Did he have to sacrifice everything just to get what he wanted? That's not fair at all!"

Old Marin tried to say something, but in the end, he couldn't find the words. He just let his daughter cry in his arms.

In a dark hall, shadow-flame burned, illuminating the silhouette of a purple figure on a dragon-claw throne.

The dark storms of the Twisting Nether never stopped scouring the terrible fortress built by Gul'dan and Blackhand with tens of thousands of orc laborers. Later, on Magtheridon's orders, Warchief Kargath Bladefist had further reinforced the Hellfire ramparts with demonic fel-iron, making them and the fortress beyond stand firm on this slowly cracking crimson land.

Even when rumbling sounds came from outside, and the walls and ceiling occasionally shook and rained down dust, the fortress's current ruler never worried about any damage to it.

But now, his expression was grim as he stared at the main gate, as if he knew the trouble heading his way was more dangerous than any obstacle he'd faced in these past few years on this broken world.

"Right this way, human." An orc guard in blood-red armor entered, struck his fist against his chest, then stood at attention. "He's here, Lord Sabellian."

"Leave us."

The figure on the throne nodded slightly. Once the orc guard saluted and left, he narrowed his amber eyes, watching the brown-robed figure enter.

The man carried a wooden staff and had a strangely shaped object wrapped tightly under his arm.

"Well met, Khadgar. I thought you didn't care for your teacher's style, but you look just like him now."

"People always become what they hate most, Prince Sabellian." Khadgar pulled back his hood. "I imagine black dragons aren't much different."

The black dragon shifted uncomfortably on his throne, leaning against the other armrest. "Tell me, then -- is there anything else between you and me that hasn't been settled?"

"I came to tell you he's dead." Khadgar said.

"I know." Sabellian said.

"You know?" The mage frowned. "Who told you?"

"Needleflame is my daughter." The black dragon lifted his chin. "Every torment she's endured, every bit of sorrow in her heart -- I feel it all. Now, hah -- I believe even if I died, she wouldn't be in this much pain. So it's not hard to guess what happened. Besides, given the way that man operated, there was never any chance he'd live out the lifespan Queen Alexstrasza's blessing granted him. That he ended up this way doesn't surprise me in the least."

"Still, I appreciate you coming all this way to confirm it for me, Khadgar. That counts as the first good news I've heard in years."

"Then I've got some bad news for you too." Khadgar said.

"Oh?" The black dragon laughed coldly. "You planning to resurrect him or something?"

"Yes." Khadgar said.

Sabellian's smile froze on his face. He was silent for a long moment before speaking. "Impossible... Are you serious? A mortal can never be resurrected after death. Though calling that arrogant bastard a 'mortal' is a bit of a stretch, he still can't escape that rule."

"I used to think so too." Khadgar said. "But now..."

"Someone once told me that if I spent tens of thousands of years drifting through the Twisting Nether like he did, I'd understand one thing: nothing is impossible. I don't like admitting it, but he had a point."

"Right now, this faces several difficulties. The biggest obstacle is that I can't reach his soul, Prince Sabellian. And that's the prerequisite for performing the resurrection ritual. So I need someone familiar with him -- and knowledgeable about the secrets of necromancy -- to help. Apart from you, the eldest son of Deathwing, I can't think of anyone else."

"Reach his soul?" The black dragon echoed, then something seemed to occur to him. "Hold on. He was the master of Ulthalesh. His soul is bound tight to that weapon made by the Dark Titan. That's the ancient demon's curse. No one can escape it. You want to contend with that scythe for his soul? If you're not careful, you'll be dragged into it too!"

Khadgar didn't answer. Instead, he opened the bundle he'd brought.

When Sabellian saw what was wrapped inside and sensed the power surging from it, his expression shifted involuntarily. His eyes widened, and he slowly rose from his dragon-claw throne.

"What do you think now? I need you to decide quickly. Every second we waste could cost us this chance."

"How... was this done?"

"Finger of Death."

"The Defiler? Even knowing how reckless and arrogant he was, his sheer audacity in challenging Archimonde head-on still far exceeded my imagination."

"He killed Archimonde, Prince Sabellian. The Defiler's corpse is in the royal city right now, very close to your father Lord Daval Prestor's former manor."

"What? Wait, no... How could he..."

The black dragon couldn't even form a proper question. Khadgar nodded. "I know it's hard to believe, but it's already happened. We can discuss the details later. Right now, neither he nor I have much time."

"Fine. You've got my interest." Sabellian composed himself. "Count me in. Let's see what tricks you have up your sleeve."

"Thank you very much, Prince. We need to return to Dalaran as quickly as possible and use their resources to complete the ritual." Khadgar said. "I already have a plan."

Sabellian stepped down from the throne. "Then what are you waiting for? Why aren't you casting a teleportation spell?"

Khadgar coughed, covering his mouth. "Do you have any mana potions? Apparently the Deceiver's barrier magic hasn't fully faded yet after all these years..."

"What?" Sabellian pointed at the mage's right hand. "You're holding the Guardian's staff, Atiesh, and you need to ask me for a mana potion to cast a teleport spell?!"
