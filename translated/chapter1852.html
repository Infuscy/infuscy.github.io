Chapter 1852: The Mage's Task

In the central square of the town south of Dalaran, ever since the mages' usual gathering spot -- the Wizards' Sanctum tavern -- was torn apart by the ley-line tremor, many had chosen to come here to rest and gather between battles. In the days since, aside from the wounded, the rotating soldiers, and the mages, many homeless people had also gathered here.

At least there were plenty of places here that offered some shelter from the elements. Close to Dalaran's magical barrier and the Alliance army, it also offered refuge while demons still roamed the area.

Evening came. Cold rain fell from the sky. A elven ranger hurried through the muddy streets, a mess of ice, filth, and sludge, scanning the awnings and tarps where refugees huddled.

For a Farstrider like her, picking out her companions among these hooded figures in the sharp contrast of light and dark wasn't difficult.

"How goes it, little elf?"

Grede Thunderfist watched the ranger sit down beside him and grinned. "How many demons did you bag today, eh? Fel hounds don't count."

"Didn't keep count." Vereesa answered flatly, quickly glancing across the table at the two silent dragons and the big yellow cat perched on a chair staring up at her.

The dwarf shrugged. "That demon called Mannoroth's trying to attack the Gilneans' Sivermane Keep, use it as a base to hold off the Alliance counterattack. But his buddies have pretty much all been driven back to the Twisting Nether by now. He's not long for this world either. So, little elf -- we won't have many demons left to kill soon. Gotta make the most of every chance we get."

"A lot of demons escaped into the Alterac Valley. A lot escaped into the village of Deathknell to the north, and the valleys beyond." Vereesa said. "Some of the Burning Legion commanders are still out there. They might try to summon more demons here. Anyway, after this mess, the clean-up work's going to be even more troublesome than hunting down the orcs after the war."

"So bottom line -- we'll never run out of enemies. You can count on that, dwarf."

"Too bad the biggest one got taken off the board early." Grede muttered, taking a gulp of his ale.

Silence settled over the table. Nobody seemed willing to pick up that thread. Finally, the dwarf broke it. "Uh... any new leads?"

"Vethras found the Chain of Will." Vereesa said quietly.

"That's great. That's... good news, right?" Grede pulled his chair closer to the elf. "Don't you guys have that thing -- sympathy, or whatever?"

"The Grand Magister's in Dalaran. Prince Sunstrider's in Brill. Have your sister talk to either of them, use the sympathy to track him down. He always wore that chain around his neck. Never took it off, not even to sleep, let alone to cast spells. What better lead could there be?"

Vereesa shook her head. "It's already been destroyed -- broken into several pieces. And there's still lethal shadow energy lingering on it. Just bringing it back to Queen Alexstrasza caused Vethras immense pain."

"Hah. Some lead." Grede spat contemptuously. "I learned all about the so-called power of the Elemental Lords back at Blackrock Mountain. I never expected much from those guys anyway. The Thorium Brotherhood probably makes better gear."

He looked at the people around the table. "Why're you all moping? Didn't you hear? Shadow energy shattered the chain. You don't honestly think there's any shadow magic in the world that could kill that warlock, do you?"

"His staff..." Moss asked, her voice hoarse, tentative. "You were the first one in there, Grede. Did you find his staff?"

"You have no idea how hard it was to get around in that place a few days ago." Grede shook his head. "I could barely get Sola out. If the little black dragon hadn't helped, I'd have gotten lost in there too, Moss. You think I had the energy to go searching for his staff?"

"That place was already full of demon bones. I mean full of them, everywhere you looked."

"Speaking of demons..." He turned to Vereesa and Needleflame. "I heard from Turalyon that those two demons were with you when you went into the Undercity. They -- especially the imp -- could definitely find him. Where'd they go? Why haven't I heard either of you mention them?"

"They were with us at first." Needleflame said softly. "Then all the braziers turned red. All the demons trying to surround us were running away. The paladins' light was getting dimmer and dimmer. One of the paladins collapsed from exhaustion. Turalyon decided to get us out of there as fast as possible instead of waiting to die. Vereesa's sister was watchful ahead, in case demons or other shadow monsters ambushed us, so I had to keep moving with Sister Vereesa Windrunner. You were in a bad state then, Vereesa."

"I know. I'm sorry, Needleflame." Vereesa said.

"I'm not blaming you." The little black dragon said. "I'm saying -- after that, the whole Undercity fell into chaos. I never saw Teacher Morofil again."

"Looks like those ugly snaggle-toothed orcs were right after all." Grede slammed the table. "Once a demon lord like the Defiler shows up, every demon summoned by every warlock betrays their 'master' and swears allegiance to the demon king instead. Is that surprising? Not at all. Demons worship power, magic, status. They'd never truly see mortals as worthy of their loyalty!"

"But right until the end, when everyone knew the demon king was coming, she never left me. She told me to listen to him." Needleflame looked at the dwarf. "She even held me back, didn't want me to go outside and get myself killed. What those orcs said -- it doesn't add up!"

"Then you tell me -- where are she and that imp now?" Grede asked.

"She... they must have gone to look on their own." Needleflame lowered her head.

"Even if she's the most powerful banshee in the world, even if she's better at tracking than any Farstrider, there's no harm in sharing information with the rest of us!" Grede leaned in, eyes wide. "You're just making excuses for what you want to believe..."

He stopped mid-sentence, like he'd been choked. Then he didn't continue. He slowly sat back down.

The black dragon didn't argue either. The ranger stared at the clay mug in front of her. The young druid looked down, as if regretting having asked the question in the first place.

"Sorry, Sola." Vereesa looked up at the green dragon. "We saw you were recovering well, so we wanted to have a meal together, talk about happier things. After everything that happened, we've all been..."

"I'm happy, Vereesa." Sola tried to smile. "I really enjoy being together like this with everyone. Seeing that you're all alright..."

"I guess I spent too long in that place. My brain's gone rotten or something." Grede stood up abruptly. "I'm going over to those mages to scrounge up some food for you all. Sorry, everyone. Tell me what you want to eat."

When nobody answered, he muttered to himself, "I'll just grab whatever, then. Hopefully they still have some of the smoked sausages Bronzebeard brought..."

The dwarf hopped off his chair and walked off. The remaining few sat in silence around the table, none of them knowing what to say next.

Then the ranger felt something was off. She looked in the direction the dwarf had gone and saw Grede stopped in front of a brown-robed mage.

The dwarf looked up warily, one hand already reaching for the dagger at his back.

Because the mage was carrying two staves -- one long, one short. And one of them looked just like the Guardian's staff from the paintings.

Aegwynn?

Vereesa was about to draw her sword when the mage quickly pulled back his hood, revealing his white beard. He glanced around, pressed a finger to his lips in a "shh" gesture, and then slipped over to their table under the stunned gazes of the dwarf and the others.

"What are you doing here?" Grede scurried back, whispering urgently. "Aren't you supposed to be dead?"

"Not yet, High Thane." Khadgar said, setting the strange short staff -- wrapped tight in magically enchanted cloth -- on the table.

He opened a corner just enough to reveal a hint of crimson magical light within.

"Hold on -- how did you get that?" Grede asked.

"This... this is..."

Moss couldn't even bring herself to confirm it. Vereesa felt like something was lodged in her throat, unable to speak.

Sola didn't dare look, but the moonlight in her eyes had begun to cloud over, growing murky.

Needleflame reached out to touch it, but Khadgar slapped her hand away and covered it up again.

"Not yet, little one." The mage said. "It seems everyone here happens to be free. I've got an important task that needs your help. And I think you'll all be very interested. However, you need to know this mission is extremely dangerous. If anything goes wrong, everyone could..."

"I'm in." Several voices spoke at once.

"Shh!" Khadgar's eyes went wide again as he scanned their surroundings. "Then follow me."
