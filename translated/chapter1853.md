## Chapter 1853: Always the Same Man

"What is this place?" Needlefire asked.

"The Guardian's Sanctum," Khadgar answered, leading the way ahead. "Earlier, Jace Sesso and I found the last remaining member of the Order of Tirisfal here — the one who could help us. That's how we figured out where Aegwynn had hidden her portal. Of course, in the end, we still failed to stop her."

"Half of the Violet Citadel has already collapsed, and this place came out completely unscathed?" Grede craned his neck, looking around. "At least I can't see anything wrong with it — no cracks or anything. So this castle isn't as fragile as I thought."

"It's not that the castle protected this place, Highlord," Khadgar said. "It's that this place stabilized the entire castle."

"I've heard of this place, but I don't understand why we're here," Vereesa said. "Aren't we supposed to... rescue Jace from the demons?"

"There isn't a demon left on Azeroth capable of taking Jace Sesso captive, Ranger-General." Khadgar turned back. "I barely made it out alive myself to find this broken staff fragment he left beside Archimonde's corpse. When I got there, nothing — man or demon — could have survived. So worrying that some demon might have stolen it is more or less pointless.

"But I am sorry for taking it without asking first. I hope you can understand my position. At the time, I had no way of finding any of you, or even knowing if you were still alive. The little mana I had left had to go toward completing the next phase of the plan as quickly as possible..."

"Where is he, Khadgar?" Vereesa asked.

"He's dead, Ranger-General." Khadgar stopped walking. "Archimonde's magic completely obliterated his body. He sacrificed everything to protect all of us."

Vereesa stared blankly at the mage. The others behind her remained silent too, as if none of them were surprised by this outcome — as if they'd only been waiting for someone to say it out loud.

"Alright, I'm glad we're all on the same page, because that means we can move quickly to the next stage — resurrecting this reckless bastard." Khadgar clutched the enchanted-cloth-wrapped Ulthalesh tightly. "Hopefully, if we succeed, he'll learn to value his own life a bit more from now on. Because as of that moment, his life isn't just a gift from Mr. and Mrs. Sesso anymore."

"Resurrect... resurrect?" Vereesa's brow twitched as if she'd just snapped awake, and she exchanged a glance with the equally stunned Mossa Woodmane.

"How do you resurrect..."

The look on Needlefire's face could hardly be called surprise anymore — it was alarm, as if she'd already connected this to something very wrong.

Sora's reaction was hard to call joy either. She looked nervously around the magic sanctum again, as if she was starting to wonder what exactly Khadgar was planning — or what methods he intended to use.

"Hold on, I don't follow," Grede said. "Can you explain this to us a bit more clearly?"

"Archimonde the Defiler clearly didn't show Jace Sesso or Ulthalesh any mercy. He unleashed the most powerful dark magic he could muster — Finger of Death — because he knew Ulthalesh's master was no easy opponent, and he doesn't like wasting time." Khadgar gestured down the corridor ahead. "Follow me. I'll explain as we walk."

"How do you know so much about it? I was at the foot of the Alterac Mountains and couldn't figure out what happened up there. Where were you at the time?" Grede asked.

"I was up there, on the Alterac peaks. While trying to destroy the portal, Archimonde's magic flung me into the ley lines. The collapse of the castle's top floor was caused by that — I'm guessing you've heard something about it." Khadgar said. "But I was lucky — perhaps because the staff protected me, I wasn't thrown too far. The problem is, the staff..."

"Anyway, when the Burning Legion attacked Lordaeron, I was forced to watch the whole thing from that mountain. But I was... badly injured. I couldn't intervene. I could only watch helplessly as everything unfolded. I couldn't act until Archimonde fell, the Legion's portal collapsed, and the ley line order was restored."

"Ulthalesh was his curse, but in the final moment, it undoubtedly became his 'sanctuary.'" Khadgar said. "If not for the staff's obsession with and greed for its master's soul, that dark magic would have erased him — body and soul."

"Then again, it's also because its master had grown so powerful by then — to a degree I find hard to imagine — that Ulthalesh was able to release its full power, withstand the Defiler's full-strength strike, and not be annihilated on the spot. So it's hard to say who was protecting whom."

Mossa swallowed, and asked quietly: "So the deathlands that swallowed that city, and the forests and hills outside it — that wasn't Archimonde the Defiler's magic? It was..."

"You guessed right." Khadgar said. "But Jace didn't do it intentionally. That's Ulthalesh's true nature. This staff was created to help the Dark Titan Sargeras's servants harvest souls from countless worlds. But its last trusted wielder betrayed that trust and turned around to challenge him and the Burning Legion."

"Jace's original intention in taking this weapon was to keep it from falling into the hands of other power-hungry wizards..." The mage glanced at Vereesa. "Like your magister, Dar'Khan Drathir, who would have brought untold disaster to this world."

"And throughout these years, he saw himself as the staff's warden, not its owner. He used every means to suppress its power and restrain its greed — and he succeeded. Everyone who knows about Ulthalesh is uniformly amazed at this new wielder's willpower. Even those who doubted whether he could keep it up... in the end, they had to admit..."

"I don't mean to say anything unpleasant," Grede said, looking around at the group. "But if what you're saying is true, Khadgar, then the fact that it turned out like this in the end — doesn't that mean something went wrong? Or... that he failed?"

"He didn't fail!" Sora looked at the dwarf. "He was still Jace Sesso until the very last moment. He wanted to save you, Grede. He didn't want to abandon you, or me, or Vereesa, or Needlefire — any of us!"

Her voice was already starting to tremble as she spoke, but she pressed on. "He knew that to protect all of us, he had to stop Archimonde."

"He knew that retreating and hiding would never let them escape the Defiler's grasp. At that moment... Archimonde's dark magic could have crushed me and the entire tower into powder. I was utterly useless in helping him stop the Defiler. But he still wasted his energy, wasted his mana on protecting me..."

"Turalyon told me the same thing." Vereesa rubbed her eyes. "He said if that dark magic had been Archimonde's handiwork, the few thousand of us who survived would have already been buried in the undercity. He could feel that even though the dark power seeping down was lethal to the touch, the guiding hand behind it didn't intend to devour him and everyone else in hiding."

"You hear what they're saying, dwarf?" Needlefire said. "That means he didn't lose his mind. He didn't become the staff's slave like you thought. Who do you think he is?! He was always himself!"

"I believed from the start that he was that kind of good-hearted human," Mossa said.

"Hey, when did I ever doubt him? I was just worried — because the way this mage tells it, it sounds like..." Grede sighed. "Damn it. Sometimes I wish he'd really turned into a full demon before he died. But no. Damn it."

Khadgar looked at Vereesa and Mossa beside her, and said: "Truth be told, I've had some doubts about this plan these past few days. The disaster that hit the capital and its surroundings has weighed on my mind too. I kept wondering whether bringing him back was really the wise decision. But now, all of you have strengthened my resolve. He didn't betray anyone's trust in him."

"Trust me — if this ritual succeeds, what comes back won't be a ghoul, or a banshee, or a wraith, or a shadow. It'll be a complete and whole Brill boy. But the flip side is — we only get one shot."

Everyone nodded. Grede couldn't help but speak up again. "One more thing. Since you saw clearly what happened up there, Khadgar — how did Jace beat the Defiler?"

"Ask him." Khadgar pointed ahead.

"What's it got to do with me?"

A black-haired man in a red robe stepped out of the shadows. Needlefire's eyes went wide as she watched him approach, looking alarmed. He folded his arms impatiently and leaned against a stone pillar.

"Don't lump me in with that kind of madman. Mimic Finger of Death? If Onyxia knew I had thoughts like that, she'd laugh at me for at least two hundred years."

"But he succeeded," Khadgar said.

"And that's why he's dead," Sabellian said.
