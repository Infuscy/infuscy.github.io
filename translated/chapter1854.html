Chapter 1854: The Guardian's Way

"Prince Sabellian?" "Baron Sabellian — what are you doing here?" "He's..." "The leader of the black dragons in Draenor, Deathwing's own son, Nefarian and Onyxia's half-brother. But don't worry, Mossa Woodmane — he's here to help us." "Uh, isn't he supposed to be in Draenor? Or has the demons' sealing magic already weakened..." "F-father..." "Don't call me father." The black dragon prince eyed his daughter sidelong. "Seeing you now, having practically forgotten your identity and pride, slowly sinking to the same level as these people — it pains me more than hearing news of your death." "Address me as 'Your Highness,'" he commanded in the dragon tongue. "I'm sorry, Your Highness," Needlefire said, bowing her head obediently. "What?!" Grede practically jumped in front of him, roaring. "You, this clown prince who leads a hundred vagabonds playing house with a bunch of orc refugees and demon deserters in a crumbled-to-dust pit — what right do you have to talk about 'levels'? We haven't even settled the score with you about Jace Sesso facing Kil'jaeden on his throne alone!" "A dwarven tribe that survives by digging holes and eating dung beetles in the cracks between Lordaeron and the Amani trolls has no right to mock those of us holding the line in another world," Sabellian said with a cold laugh. "They're called Hinterlands Golden Scarabs, you uncultured idiot! You dare slander my forefathers, I'll..." Grede's beard and hair looked like they were about to fly off his face in fury, until Khadgar stepped between them. "Easy, easy, everyone. Have you forgotten why we're gathered here?" "What help can he give?" Grede pointed at the red-robed man. "The biological son of the granddaddy of dark magic..." "What role a practitioner of shadow magic and necromancy could play in resurrecting the dark mage Jace Sesso, hmm?" Sabellian stared at the dwarf. "In this world, I suspect anyone besides you, Grede Thunderfist, could grasp the connection immediately." "You just promised, Archmage Khadgar," Sora said, also worried. "This resurrection ritual won't bring Jace back as a ghoul. I don't understand..." "I get it now." Grede looked at the mage. "Not a zombie, not a ghoul — but he didn't say anything about not becoming a death knight. Throw in a live black dragon and an old ally of Teron Gorefiend's, and now I've connected all the dots." "I'm not familiar with necromancy," Mossa said. "But an elder I know once told me that even if a resurrected undead retains fragments of their former memories, those precious memories and emotions would be twisted beyond recognition. Archmage Khadgar, in body or in spirit, it could no longer be called the same person." Vereesa frowned deeply, silent on the sidelines. The look she gave the black dragon prince was far from trusting, but on the other hand, she seemed to have steeled herself for something terrible. Khadgar looked at their complicated expressions and hesitated a moment before speaking. "I know you might not be able to understand this right now, but full resurrection magic does exist." "In the two thousand years the Order of Tirisfal existed, they didn't just work to fight demons and invaders from the Twisting Nether. They also researched countless other types of magic — including, naturally, immortality and even resurrection. The Guardians have never stooped to using necromancy to patch over their regrets." "But necromantic knowledge was still necessary in the process, because when it comes to bringing back the dead, finding the soul is essential." Khadgar looked at Ulthalesh in his hands. "And my knowledge of necromancy isn't enough to wrestle a soul away from this staff — even shattered and nearly broken as it is." At that, Sabellian smiled with mild satisfaction. Grede, Sora, and Mossa seemed somewhat convinced. As for Needlefire — she looked like she still had words bottled up inside, but after meeting her father's gaze, she couldn't bring herself to speak. "Trust me, all of you," the mage continued. "If I possessed the full power of a Guardian and could complete the whole process on my own, I would never have spent so much time finding him, finding all of you, while risking Jace Sesso being consumed by Ulthalesh — his soul is strong enough to restore the staff to its original state, even making it far more powerful than before you found it. By then, it would truly be too late." "Go on, Khadgar," Vereesa said immediately. "We'll follow your lead." Khadgar nodded and turned to look at the stone door behind Sabellian. He raised his hand and spoke an incantation. A delicate arcane veil rippled across the stone door, as if a lavender curtain were being drawn aside. The great door swung open on its own. The clean, metallic scent of arcane energy that wafted out suggested something on the scale of a massive teleportation ritual was taking place inside. The chamber fell quiet once the stone door stopped moving. There was no sound of chanting from within — only the faint, wind-chime-like ring of magic. As they followed Khadgar inside, even Sabellian couldn't help looking around curiously, not wanting to miss a single detail. Past the corridor beyond the door, they entered a round hall. Below the steps stood a small altar carved with arcane runes, shaped like a fountain — or perhaps some kind of monstrous statue. Looking at the strange object, Vereesa asked: "Is this the most secret, most central place in all of Dalaran, Khadgar?" "More or less," Khadgar said. "In a way, this place has a higher security clearance than the lower vaults where the Skull of Gul'dan and the Eye of Dalaran are sealed. Even most of the Kirin Tor don't know it exists." "You're bringing us in here... and using spells to bypass the secret mechanisms — are you sure you won't draw the attention of the Kirin Tor high council?" Vereesa asked again. "The Kirin Tor high council — who do you mean?" Khadgar said. "Right now, the ruler of Dalaran is Archmage Vargoth. Master Antonidas is gravely wounded. Drenden and Modera are missing. And the other two masters — Prince Kael'thas and Master Krasus — have bigger concerns than Kirin Tor affairs." "The incantation to open this door was given to me by Vargoth himself. Considering we fought together in Draenor for years, and that Jace Sesso brought him back to Azeroth and found me alive... that temporary dictator didn't hesitate for a moment." "Of course, he doesn't know I brought a necromancer into this place. But that doesn't matter either. I trust Prince Sabellian has always been quite skilled at covering his tracks." With that, Khadgar raised Atiesh in his hand and pointed it at the small altar. He began reciting another long incantation. As he chanted, the altar began to glow, clean arcane energy surging and flowing through it like real water. "The Guardian Crucible. What you see before you is the implement the Guardians of Tirisfal used to focus their power and inherit their responsibility. Of course, ever since Lady Aegwynn took office, this thing had lost its original purpose. Today, it finally sees the light again, to help me complete this ritual that only a Guardian can perform. Hopefully." As the others stared in astonishment, Khadgar walked up to the "Crucible" and murmured as if to himself: "You must have witnessed everything that happened on Lake Lordamere too. You know better than anyone who truly deserves the title of 'Guardian' of Azeroth. Please help me complete this ritual — help me bring him back." "So what do we need to do?" Sora asked. Sabellian stared at the Guardian Crucible. "Next, I'll perform a soul ritual that temporarily separates your souls from your bodies. All you need to do is drink the Soul Purification Potion Khadgar has prepared for you, and then sit at the corresponding positions in the magic circle I've drawn and wait." "Got it. Send our souls into Ulthalesh, beat the hell out of whatever demon's hiding in there, and rescue poor little Jace. Simple enough." Grede nodded. "I've never fought with my soul before, but I've been in a dream... close enough, right?" "No one can say for certain what you'll face once the ritual begins," Khadgar said. "Neither the Guardians' records about Ulthalesh nor the accounts of the resurrection ritual describe it very clearly." "All I can say is — the key is making Jace Sesso turn back in time. He's resisting Ulthalesh's assimilation. With his power, he's already strong enough to churn up all the other souls inside the staff. But he'll fail eventually. His will is being erased. He's forgetting who he is. That's why I needed to find you." "Logically speaking, finding Mr. and Mrs. Sesso would be the most effective approach." The mage pulled an old notebook from his pack and flipped expertly to a page. "But unfortunately, they're neither Guardians nor mages. Their souls couldn't survive that kind of ordeal. They might not even be able to withstand the Soul Purification Potion's effects." "More importantly — if that guy found out I'd used his parents for this kind of soul magic, even if he did come back, he'd lose his mind immediately. I'm bringing him back, not trading places with him." Hearing this, Sabellian couldn't help saying: "This might be the softest necromantic ritual I've ever participated in, and I've been alive a long time." "Prince Sabellian..." Sora sighed helplessly. "Alright, alright, little one." The black dragon looked at her. "What can I say — this is your style. Yours and Jace Sesso's. I'm getting used to it by now." "I can't say I can replace Vic Sesso," Grede said. "But send me in there, black dragon. I'll find a way to make that bastard remember he's got parents." "Same here," Vereesa said. "I'm ready too," Mossa said. "Are you sure you want to go with them, Needlefire?" Sabellian asked. "I'm sure, Your Highness." Needlefire nodded firmly. Grede looked at the black dragon prince, who was squinting with obvious displeasure. "It's not that I don't want to trust you, dragon. But can you tell me — why are you willing to help us? Help Jace?" The black dragon was silent for a moment, then looked over. "At the summit of Kil'jaeden's Throne, he... spared me, Grede Thunderfist. Consider this compensation. I don't like being in anyone's debt." "I urged him to devour all of Auchindoun — the ancient draenei spirits there would have been enough for Ulthalesh to shatter the Scepter of Sargeras with ease. Look at what's become of Lordaeron now... But he refused. He believed he could do it without the souls of the draenei." The hall fell quiet. None of them knew what had truly happened atop that demonic throne except the black dragon before them. "In the end, he failed. That proves my concerns were justified." Sabellian continued. "Even though the Scepter of Sargeras was on the verge of collapse, just like Ulthalesh now — failure was still failure. To stop the Burning Legion from using its power any further, the only options were to take it away in time, or find a strong enough soul to finish the scepter for good." "That's where we disagreed... He defeated me. Pressed the Spellweaver's blade against my eyes. I thought he'd choose the latter — use my soul to finish the job. I was prepared to accept that outcome. But he didn't. He let me live." The black dragon looked up. "Look at it from another angle — Ulthalesh is still here. Even if the Burning Legion threw everything they had at it, every scheme and ounce of energy, they still fell just short. That's not so different from your 'soft' style, when you get down to it." "And in Draenor, Jace Sesso at least won once. Maybe this time, it'll be your turn to claim total victory."
