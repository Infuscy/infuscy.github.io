Chapter 1855: The Boundary

It was quiet. So quiet it made you uncomfortable.

The sky was gray, with no sun and no clouds — just a thin layer of light that seemed to filter through from very far away, never quite reaching anyone's face.

A young man sat on a black rock on the shore. The sand beneath him was also black, fine as powder, making almost no sound when walked on.

He didn't know where this was, or why he'd come here. But the place had an indescribable feel to it — familiar and foreign at once. Like he should leave here and head for a new land. Or maybe he should stay, and wait for everything to end.

The waves lapped gently against the shore not far away. He couldn't smell the salt of the sea. The sound of the water pushing onto the beach didn't feel like splashing, either — more like someone calling out from a distance. It was the only sound in this dead-quiet place.

The reason he thought it was shouting was because there were vague shapes in the distance. On the shore. In the water.

They stood there, motionless. Neither approaching nor retreating, as if they'd been forgotten there. Like they were waiting for something — though they didn't know what.

Just like him.

Maybe he was one of those shadows too, the young man thought. Maybe those people looking this way felt the same about him.

The air was cold, carrying a damp chill that made him want to pull his coat tighter. But there was no wind to block.

Maybe I don't belong here.

Then he noticed someone walking toward him from the other side.

"We should go," the person said.

The stranger wore a gray robe and hood, his face hidden.

His voice sounded familiar somehow, but the young man couldn't remember anything about him. Whatever impressions he managed to dig up were fading into the distance, like the quickly dissolving memory of a dream after waking.

But the young man was certain of one thing — this stranger seemed sick, or badly wounded. Although the man looked tall and upright, every gesture carrying the air of an ancient ruler, he couldn't hide his inner frailty from the young man's eyes.

It was some kind of gift, maybe. Or an ability — like sensing what those distant figures were waiting for.

"Who are you? Where are you taking me?"

"I'm the one who brought you here."

The stranger answered, pointing to a small boat on the shore. "Come. I'll take you to the other shore."

"The other shore?" the young man asked. "What's there?"

"Many people like you," the stranger said. "They had the same goal as you, struggled in the same direction. Many of them lost their way in the process. And I... am your guide."

"What goal?" the young man asked. "I don't even know what I came here to do. But you seem to know."

"Revenge," the stranger said. "Against Sargeras."

Sargeras.

Hearing that name, the young man felt like he remembered something. This person really did seem to know a lot of things.

"Do you know who I am?" he couldn't help asking.

"You..." The stranger thought for a moment. "In the past, you faced many terrible enemies. Sargeras's powerful servants — those who had destroyed countless worlds — all fell before you. You crippled their magic, crushed their arrogance. But you were always alone."

The young man looked at the stranger across from him, hesitated, and shook his head. "No, I don't think..."

"No one could understand what you carried," the stranger said. "No one could share the pain you bore. You walked this path alone for so long that you forgot what loneliness even was. Those who once walked beside you were long gone."

The young man looked at one end of the beach, then the other. In the end, he could only nod.

"Would you rather stay here?" the stranger asked. "Or go to the other shore and meet them?"

"They were all like you once. But you are without question the strongest among them. They will welcome your arrival — because your coming will bring everyone one solid step closer to the final goal."

Following the gray-robed figure toward the boat, the young man felt the beach grow colder. The figures swaying in the distance grew restless too.

The boat was long and narrow, barely big enough for five or six people.

The stranger pushed the boat gently into the sea and lit a lantern at the bow, motioning for him to follow. The young man stepped into the water. The bone-deep cold drove him to scramble aboard.

But once he was in, the boat swayed back and forth as if it couldn't bear his weight, groaning ominously. Seawater was even seeping up through the bottom.

"What's wrong with this boat?" the young man asked. "Are you sure it'll get us to the other side?"

"Don't worry." The stranger picked up the oars and looked at him. "As long as you sit still, the journey ahead will be smooth."

The young man didn't answer. But remembering how cold that seawater was, he tried to sit up straight — and things did settle a little.

And so the little boat sailed out into the sea. The beach grew smaller behind them, gradually swallowed by thick fog.

Pure gray-black closed in around them. The faint blue magic lamp swung at the bow, lighting the water ahead — but it couldn't illuminate anything beyond more water.

That strange sound of waves hitting the shore was starting to sound more and more like human cries — full of resentment, hatred, and hostility, but also regret and sorrow. He felt like some of those voices were meant for him. He wasn't sure, though — because soon they grew distant too, fading away.

Then, little by little, near-total silence settled around them. The stranger ahead stopped rowing, but the boat moved more steadily.

But complete silence also made the slightest strange noise painfully jarring.

Suddenly the young man heard what sounded like a splash from deep within the fog. He turned sharply toward it. The stranger also looked around.

"Did you hear that?" the young man asked.

The stranger shook his head. "Stay calm. Don't let your mind wander, and we'll reach the other shore safely."

The young man didn't answer. He kept his eyes fixed on that direction. Something felt wrong.

Just as he could sense the fierce emotions in those chaotic cries, and the frailty the stranger was trying so hard to hide — he could also feel something special from that splash. Something real. Something connected to him. It was just...

Suddenly, the boat groaned again beneath him. He felt a bone-chilling cold at his feet — seawater was seeping in once more. The stranger at the bow sighed helplessly, steadying the flickering lantern.

The young man looked back at him, carefully gripping the boat's edge. That steadied things again.

But then, from the direction of that splash, another vague shout came. The young man turned to listen more carefully, and managed to make out some words.

"Help..."

"Help! Damn it, what is this place, huh?!" "Blrrgh... ah!"

"Help..."

"Someone's fallen in the water," the young man said, pointing. "Shouldn't we save him? Maybe he's lost here too, maybe he shares the same goal. He could follow you to the other side..."

"Why?" the stranger asked. "Those who watched you from the shore — you ignored their words. What's different about this one?"

"I... I'm not sure," the young man said. "But I feel like I should save him. If you won't help, then I'll..."

"Hold on! Make some noise — I'm coming to save you!" he shouted, even though he couldn't see where the person was through the fog.

The stranger's tone turned serious: "Don't let the things in this sea deceive your senses. If you do, not even I can bring you back."

"Stay, master."

"Master? What does that mean?" The young man frowned. "Jace? Jace Sesso, is that you?"

"You... help!" Jace Sesso... Who's Jace Sesso?

It didn't matter anymore.

Before the stranger could say anything else, the young man made his choice. He jumped off the boat, plunging into the suffocatingly cold water, swimming toward that voice. His figure was gradually swallowed by the darkness beyond the lamplight.

Behind him, only the solitary little boat remained, rocking on the water and slowly growing still — along with the dark silhouette aboard, watching coldly.
