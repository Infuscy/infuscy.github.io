## Chapter 1856: Turning Back

The black seawater cut into his skin like knives. His muscles couldn't tell if they were pushing hard or just twitching. The young man swam further and further, but slower and slower — as if his body no longer belonged to him.

"Hold on..."

He shouted as he tasted the bitter, salty water. The cry didn't carry far. Maybe for the same reason, he couldn't hear the drowning person's voice anymore either.

But some nameless force pushed him to keep swimming — a surge from somewhere deep inside, driving him forward, even pointing him in a clear direction: ahead, down beneath the surface, deeper, deeper still.

He was growing more and more certain that none of this was simple. The voices of those figures. The stranger. This drowning person. The boat. The sea. And the shore.

But he didn't have time to think about any of that now.

"Hold on..."

Shouting underwater was obviously stupid. At least he still had enough memory left to know that.

The ice-cold seawater rushed into his mouth, threatening to freeze his teeth and throat. Rolling bubbles reflected countless pale faces as they drifted toward the surface. Above, the gray sky looked almost bright through the immense depth of water.

Suddenly, the young man felt his hand grab something like seaweed. No — hair. A braid rough enough to cut his fingers.

The familiar texture made him seize on it without hesitation. He knew he'd found the drowning person — as if the shouting voice and this braid belonged to the same person by necessity.

But the drowning man seemed to want nothing to do with being caught. He wrenched at the young man's wrist, kicked and punched at his legs, shoving him away. This guy was too strong. The young man couldn't hold on. He lost his grip.

"What are you doing?! I'm trying to save you!"

He roared at the dark shape below him, even though he didn't expect anyone to hear him at this depth. But the stocky, solid figure seemed to actually understand. He looked up. The faint light from the surface caught his eyes, making them gleam.

"You're Jace Sesso! I'm Grede Thunderfist!"

"Remember this — you're... Sesso. I'm... get out of here... get out of here!" "Don't waste your time on me... you fool. Get out, get out!"

The shadow shouted as he sank — pulled down by something, faster and faster. The figure grew blurrier and blurrier.

The young man dove frantically after him, but couldn't catch up no matter how hard he tried.

"No... Grede Thunderfist!"

"Grede Thunderfist!"

The young man sat up abruptly on a cold stone floor. Everything around him was pitch black. He swung his arms — not through water, but through empty air.

"Jace, Jace... you're awake."

"Who are you?" The young man whirled to face the darkness. He could sense someone there. And she...

"I'm Vereesa. I'm... it's alright, Jace. I know this is hard to understand, but..."

The young man cut her off. "Are you talking to me? You mean I'm this 'Jace Sesso'? That man said the same thing. He called himself Grede Thunderfist."

"You found Grede?"

The woman who called herself "Vereesa" moved closer. The young man noticed her magical eyes glowing with pale blue arcane light. She was an elf — a quel'dorei, by the look of her.

It wasn't just her that felt inexplicably familiar and close. Even this place — this dark chamber filled with strange, mysterious black reliefs — felt like somewhere he'd been before.

He must have been here at some point. With this seemingly unfamiliar quel'dorei woman. What was going on? Why couldn't he remember anything?

"Yes, Grede Thunderfist." The young man pressed hard on his brow. "That dwarf fell into the sea. I drowned trying to save him too. I overestimated myself."

"Did you pull me back? Thank you. But did you see him, Lady Vereesa? There was someone with a boat who could take us off this strange island and find the others."

"Hold on, Jace. We should—" the elf hesitated.

"We should search together," the young man said immediately. "I'm sure he's still alive, and nearby. This might be some palace ruin near the coast, right? I can feel him close by. I have a special sense about these things..."

"Before I got here, I was on another beach. I remember what it looked like — the position of the rocks, the strange shadows. The problem is, Grede Thunderfist told me something important. My head's not working right, Lady Vereesa. I keep forgetting things. So I need to write down what I still remember before I lose it all."

He noticed some scattered luggage near the elf. For some reason, he felt certain there was something useful inside.

"Do you have paper or a scroll in your travel bag? I've learned some basic enchantment — I can write with magic."

"Yes," the elf answered quietly.

The young man took a sheet of paper from the elven woman. Looking at the beautiful mage-scroll paper — the kind only made in elven kingdoms — he hesitated for a moment before speaking.

"Once we save that dwarf, once we leave this place and get back to Stormwind, I'll pay you back, Lady Vereesa. These things are valuable. I'm sure I don't have much magical knowledge, but I can tell..."

"You idiot."

The elf let out a choked laugh, but it quickly turned into a sob. Tears rolled down her face.

She held him tight, weeping quietly. "I missed you so much, Jace. I just wanted to hear your voice again. I just wanted to see you again..."

"Vereesa..." the young man murmured, stunned.

The elf glanced behind her as if remembering something, quickly wiped her tears away, and said quietly: "I know you might not remember who I am right now. But listen to me — don't listen to that demon. Don't take any advice from that boatman. Don't get on his boat! He has you trapped here, but that doesn't mean you have to let him lead you around. He can't take you. He's at his weakest!"

"But even so, none of us have the strength to fight him. You're different. Because you're Jace Sesso — the most powerful dark mage and soul manipulator in the world. Even Archimonde the Defiler fell before you..."

"Archimonde the Defiler," the young man repeated slowly.

"Yes. That demon lord is dead — flesh and demon soul annihilated by your hand. Nothing left but an ugly, hollow skeleton. And you're still here." The elf gritted her teeth as she finished.

The young man still remembered that name. For some reason, it felt so close — as if it could step out of the shadows nearby at any moment.

But just as he was trying to piece together some clue from his already shattered memories, the elf woman's presence began to fade, receding into the depths of the darkness. Her sudden departure jolted him awake.

"Vereesa?"

He got up at once and chased after her. Through a corridor built by Dark Iron dwarves. He thought he'd emerge into a ruined city, onto a black plain carpeted in falling volcanic ash — but instead, he found himself in a strange place.

Before him was a gray coast wrapped in mist. Similar to the beach he'd been on before, but slightly different.

In the white fog, a lone wooden pier jutted out. A large crescent-shaped ship waited there, its portholes lit. Purple sails looked mysterious in the moonlight.

A tall figure stood on the shore, staring at the ship. She seemed disappointed.

The young man looked closer. This wasn't the gray-robed oarsman from before — it was a tauren woman with short horns, wearing a leather hide coat and carrying a wooden staff.

"Hello, ma'am. Did you see—"

"Jace Sesso?" The tauren turned to face him, pointing at the pier. "What took you so long? They're all waiting for you on the ship."

"Who?" the young man asked.

"Grede Thunderfist of the Hinterlands, and Vereesa Windrunner of Quel'Thalas, of course." The tauren woman said. "Take care of Windrunner — she's a fierce and kind ranger. And you're a good warlock. I know that."

"I... alright. Thank you for the advice." The young man nodded and hurried toward the pier.

But as he passed the tauren, he noticed the antler hanging from her belt. Something clicked.

"What's that?" he asked, pointing at the antler.

"Hmm." The tauren lowered her head. "This is Celebras's Horn. We barely escaped from Maraudon and ran into some forest keepers. Have you forgotten, Jace Sesso? Did you get poisoned by silithid venom..."

"Celebras's Horn..." The young man squinted, thinking for a moment. Then he looked up at the tauren. "You might think I've lost my mind. That I'm talking nonsense. But I think — you shouldn't be here. Go north. Find the leader of the forest keepers. Their leader. They'll help you. But be careful of the satyr demons. They'll use old legends to trick you, lure you into nightmares."

"Satyr? Nightmare..."

The tauren hesitated for a moment. The young man nodded seriously, then headed for the pier.

He didn't know why he'd said that. Why those things had suddenly come to mind. He just felt that if he didn't say them, this lonely tauren would be in grave danger someday.

"Wait, Jace!" The tauren suddenly called out. "Don't go any further. Don't get on that ship!"

"Why?" The young man stopped and turned around.

"I'm sorry — I was completely lost here..." The tauren shook her head. "But now I remember. They're not there. Come with me. I'll take you out of here. Don't board that ship!"

The tauren shifted into a large, powerful cat and bounded toward the mist-shrouded forest in the distance. The young man looked at the great ship, and couldn't help thinking of Vereesa Windrunner's words.

...Don't listen to that boatman. Don't get on his boat.

"I'm coming right now! Wait for me, Mossa!"
