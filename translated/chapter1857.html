Chapter 1857: The End

The young man ran the name through his mind again. But as he watched the great cat charge into a dark forest on the shore, he couldn't spare the time to wonder where he'd heard that name before, whether it belonged to the tauren shifter, or why he'd called it out. He only knew that this tauren knew things he didn't — and that she was connected to Vereesa Windrunner, Grede Thunderfist, and even Jace Sesso. The black forest was unsettling. It didn't look anything like the wilderness he remembered. Forests weren't supposed to look like this. In the depths of the woods, figures lingered, all staring toward him — as if they recognized him. And there was something else. The deeper he ran, the stranger the feeling creeping into his heart. Like this forest belonged to him. Not just because of those figures — but because this darkness, this desolation, was bound to his strange abilities by a thousand invisible threads. Maybe this place had turned from the lush, vibrant forest he half-remembered into this wasteland because of him. Did I do this? "Mossa? Where are you? What happened here? Why did you bring me here?" "Where did the other two go? Can't you just tell me?!" But the great cat was too fast. It vanished from sight before long. The young man didn't want to give up. Grede Thunderfist and Vereesa Windrunner had both disappeared on him just as mysteriously. He'd finally found someone else who was lost here, someone who knew him. He didn't want to lose track of her too. But as he ran, a tall tower emerged from the trees ahead. It stood on a low cliff, looking utterly out of place among the surroundings. What made it even stranger was the massive phantom lurking at the tower's top, faintly visible through the mist. The young man stopped shouting. He didn't know what kind of creature that shadow in the fog was, but at least it didn't look like something to mess with. The problem was — he was pretty sure the monster had already noticed him. "Leave this place." The shadow's voice was hoarse and low, accompanied by heavy breaths and hissing. But for some reason, the young man thought it sounded like a woman's voice. "Sorry, ma'am," the young man called up to the tower-top. "I'm looking for a tauren — no, a cat, actually. She's a tauren druid..." "Mossa Woodmane?" the shadow asked. "Yes, that's her... that's the name," the young man nodded. "Did you come with Mossa Woodmane?" the shadow asked. "Yes," the young man said. "If she's a friend of yours, can you help me find her? There are a lot of people lost in this place. We should stick together..." "No... wait. No." The shadow shifted. "Don't try to trick me into leaving. You foolish wandering souls. That demon has fooled you all. I'm not leaving this place. Never." "Why?" the young man asked. "I am the guardian of this tower," the shadow said quietly. "This is Ilgara's Tower. Its master trusted me. He wanted me to protect this place, to purify the corruption here. Until one day, this tower will restore the entire forest to life." Ilgara's Tower. Hearing that name, the young man felt a dizzying fog lift. He should remember this place. Ilgara's Tower. Stonewatch. Redridge Mountains. "Why has it become like this?" the young man asked. "That's none of your concern, wandering soul." The shadow lowered its head. The young man noticed it was a great green dragon — but her appearance wasn't nearly as savage or menacing as the dragons that could burn down a town with ease. Wait. The hellfire that burned through the city. The sky choked with rolling black clouds. Falling meteors. Screaming demon legions. The trembling great tower. You're hurt, Jace. A searing pain tore through the young man's chest. "Leave this place," the green dragon warned sternly. "You don't want to offend the master of this tower. The only reason you're still wandering here is because he's fighting the creator of this dark world. So be grateful for his strength. I will guard this place forever. Guard his home. Until he wins. Until he returns..." "He will come back," she murmured. "Because I know he would never abandon me. Never." "Sora?" Hearing that name, the great shadow stirred. She lowered her head again, studying this lone, lost soul more carefully. "You're... you..." "It's me." The young man looked at her. "I'm Jace Sesso." "Jace... it's really you!" "You're still here. You're still here..." The green dragon's voice shifted from a hoarse reptilian growl to a young woman's cry. The dragon-shape in the mist dissolved into pale pink dust, scattering into countless moths that flew higher and higher. "I'll be right down! Wait for me!" When the dust cleared, only an elf girl in green remained, high above. She turned and stumbled through the door at the tower's top. "No... no! Don't go in there, Sora!" Jace shoved past the swarming phantom moths, charging recklessly up the cliff and onto the stairs. He slammed the door open with a crash — but it was already too late. This wasn't Ilgara's Tower anymore. It was a dark corridor. A dungeon. Vereesa. Grede. Mossa. Sora. Why was everyone here? Why were they all trapped? Hadn't he... died? Had he failed? Had Lordaeron been destroyed by the Burning Legion? Had he given everything, only to fail to protect everyone? — Don't trust that boatman, Jace. Don't believe his words. He wants to take you away. Get out of here. Get out of here. Don't go near that ship. Jace leaned against the scorching black wall and rubbed his forehead. This wasn't the Shadow Realm. He could feel them — their souls still struggling around him, hiding, fighting desperately to get closer — just like the countless figures on the shore and in the forest. But they couldn't hold on much longer. Every second, their wills were being torn and twisted. Jace moved through the dark dungeon, past prison-like cells. He knew where they were. He could sense every corner of this enormous cage. He knew this place all too well. Jace pushed open an iron door ahead of him. A shadow inside turned its amber eyes toward him, then curled up warily in a corner. "Needlefire. It's me." "Jace Sesso?" The little black dragon still seemed unable to believe it. "I made it! I knew I would!" She threw herself at him, hugging him tight. "They all left, Jace Sesso. Only I... I remembered what Father said. I never let him find me. I hid here the whole time, waiting for you." "He couldn't catch me. Because he doesn't know whose daughter I am. He doesn't know whose blood runs through my veins. He doesn't know my name. Don't underestimate me. None of you better underestimate me!" "Right, right — they told me to tell you, don't forget who you are. Don't forget how many people still remember you. Don't forget... me. Don't forget Fidegonia!" "You promised Queen Alexstrasza you'd take care of me. You promised Father — no, His Highness — you promised him you'd take me to Stormwind, to Kalimdor! And you forgot all of it, you damn bastard! No matter how we called you, you wouldn't wake up. No matter how hard we tried, you couldn't remember. Damn you..." "I remember now, Needlefire." Jace held her close, her head covered in black ash. "I'll take you all out of here. I will." "That's not enough. There's one more thing. The most important thing. I have to tell you." Needlefire's eyes went wide. She grabbed Jace by the collar, pulling herself up, staring into his eyes. "Ulthalesh. His name is Ulthalesh. He's the demon! They all forgot, but I remember. I still remember." Yes. Ulthalesh. Jace closed his eyes slowly. Time to settle things between us. A soul-splitting scream echoed from beyond the corridor. Jace walked step by step to the other side of the dark room, pushed open the iron door, and entered another shadowy passage. Braziers on either wall lit up with dark blue soul-fire, illuminating his path — until he emerged from that cramped space into a demonic hall. Outside the hall, souls — demon, mortal, beast, even elemental — swirled around it like a storm. The howling of these trapped souls was enough to drive any mortal who strayed in here instantly mad. Jace watched them in silence, studying the hall before him. The cold, spike-covered chamber. This place felt familiar too — just like everywhere else he'd walked. After all, he'd been entangled with this ghostly domain for years. Finally, his gaze settled on the figure standing in the center of the hall. A pale phantom wreathed in blue magic light. "I've come."
