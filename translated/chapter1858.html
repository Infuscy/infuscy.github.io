Chapter 1858: Liberation

The Lord of Souls turned back. On that pale, humanoid face, there was barely any distinguishing the specifics of its features. Beneath those eyes that glowed with balefire, the countenance was not the sort of face one might find on a Nathrezim or a human or elven hero — it was also the face of a calm demon, a frenzied Eredar...

Its gray death-rune cloak was thrown open by the storm of souls. Beneath it, the exposed skin bore even more indistinct faces, as if thousands of trapped, miserable souls were screaming to break free from this body — and all of those souls had long forgotten their original forms.

"We've been tormenting each other like this for a long time, Jace Sesso."

Uthalesh watched him from across the hall. "But this is my world. This is my domain. No matter how much souls that enter here struggle, it's futile — whether you, or anyone. And I can feel it clearly: when you took me up, you already foresaw this day. You knew it all along."

Jace looked up, observing the soul-storm swirling beyond the hall's dark cyan pillars. "Now," he asked, "is this still your domain?"

As he spoke, the sound of chains breaking rang out from beyond the hall, one after another, echoing across the soul-realm's nether sky.

Uthalesh lifted his head slightly, his gaze following the strange noise. His pale face was as rigid as a stone carving. But Jace could already feel the millennia of soul-essence churning within him — the same fragility and powerlessness he had sensed beneath that solemn exterior before.

"You know very well why those drifting souls wander aimlessly. You know what those figures are crying out for. You know why all these souls hate me, Uthalesh. Because they've all been waiting for me to lead them in destroying you completely, and until now, I've always let them down. But now, you've lost control of this place."

Beyond the hall, a hazy soul-phantom crawled up onto the edge of the hall's floor. He wore silver armor, carried a longsword, his white tabard emblazoned with the crest of Lordaeron fluttering in the storm toward the void beyond, too blurred to make out clearly. But his eyes — those burning soul-eyes blazed like solid things.

Between the other pillars, hundreds, thousands, more and more souls of Lordaeron and Stromgarde soldiers, Quel'dorei hunters and knights, and dwarven vanguards climbed up one after another, gathering in clusters, wielding soul-forged blades, axes, screaming, and crowding toward the center of the demon hall in a charge — like a tide of nether rising from every side, flooding the central disc.

"Weaklings... I wouldn't even deign to reap scum like these."

Uthalesh sneered as he hurled the first Lordaeron knight aside. But the next elven dragonhawk rider drove her lance deep into his twisted form. A low howl vibrated through the hall, and for the first time, the Lord of Souls' face showed a flicker of anger.

"I am the King of Nathreza. The reaper of countless worlds in the Twisting Nether. I am the first necromancer, the Dark Titan's plague, and his doom. And you are nothing but —"

Uthalesh casually raised a wave of soul-energy that sent a swath of Lordaeron soldiers flying. He turned and set another group of dwarven warriors ablaze, scattering them into silent soul-ash. These ghostly soldiers were as fragile as toy soldiers before him.

The souls' resistance was still not enough to shake him. Jace descended the steps, his hands blazing with soul-fire. His spirit touched the madness and mania within Uthalesh's soul, then seized it decisively. A chain of nether formed from his willpower, wrapping around the demonic shadow besieged by countless ghosts — and igniting even its body.

The demonic shadow's form began to tremble uncontrollably. Seizing the opportunity, more and more mortal souls swarmed in. Among them were not just soldiers, but many mages, citizens, fishermen from Lake Lordamere. Some even climbed onto Uthalesh's body recklessly, stabbing at the Lord of Souls' form like madmen.

Tangled in Jace's soul-drain, assaulted by countless soldiers and mortal souls, Uthalesh's demonic shadow visibly dimmed and crumbled.

But not long after, he suddenly reversed his arm, yanking the soul-chain back hard. The trembling stopped at once.

"You... consume me?"

"Good... I can be Uthalesh. I can be Sathael. I can be any of the countless demon lords and death-lords who have held me. And of course, I can be you, Jace Sesso. In the future, I could even become Sargeras."

"I am the oldest demon in the Twisting Nether. I have existed for tens of thousands of years. And you — you are merely a single instant in this infinite universe. So I admit this is the right choice. Now, we will destroy this cage, release the souls together, and become eternal."

Uthalesh laughed. Jace could even hear his voice echo through his own mind.

The two soul-wielders drew closer. Only then did Jace finally see the demon-soul's face clearly.

Young.

Pale. And here, at the center of this soul-furnace, the face seemed far too ordinary.

It was his own face.

The smile grew more and more manic, like an elaborately prepared prank that had finally found its victim.

But Jace could not stop. He could not let anything distract him. He was on the verge of overwhelming the demon's power completely, of freeing all the souls from this cage and sending them where they needed to go. He could not let these declarations shake him now.

Then, abruptly, something hot expanded from within Jace's core — something he should not have been able to feel.

"You may be the oldest demon in the Twisting Nether," a voice dropped from high in the hall, "but I am the hunter in this Twisting Nether who is best at hunting demons. So what happens when my blade pierces your soul, Uthalesh?"

A demon wreathed in blazing crimson flame materialized between the dark cyan pillars.

At first glance, the demon looked like a reanimated skeletal dragon. Its head bore black horns that twisted upward like a crown, entwining high above. A blood-red tail lashed like a bone-whip.

She shook off the last few sealing chains still clinging with soul-fire, spread wings reduced to barely a few ribs, and landed among the gathering mortal souls. Her black hooves shook the entire demon hall with their impact.

Morophil?

Jace and Sazeno had seen this shadow in the soul-magic mirror guarded by the blue dragon Azuregos. He knew it intimately.

How could she...

The thought barely flickered through Jace's mind before it was overtaken by the Shivarra's movement.

The next instant, Morophil had already driven her claws clean through Uthalesh's soul-form from behind.

The demon lord who had been laughing moments before let out an ear-piercing shriek. Jace's soul-fire detonated in response. That all-too-familiar face twisted instantly into a hideous demonic grimace. Its entire body burned into even more fragments, scattering like wind-blown ash.

And in Jace's awareness, the demon hall — the entire cage that bound the souls — grew ever more solid.

Gradually, Uthalesh's mad curses and howls in Jace's mind grew fainter, faded away. But with them...

He casually seized the entire domain's soul-energy and tore apart the skeletal shell that was already on the verge of collapse — as easily as ripping paper, as easily as snapping a twig.

The coast capsized. The black reef, battered by bitter seawater, crumbled into stone dust and scattered into the sky.

The demon hall split open. The circular floor cracked and turned. The surrounding pillars collapsed. The dome burst like shattered glass. Countless souls cried out in joy as they left, riding the final currents of the dissolving storm, rising higher and higher, flying toward the light above.

Jace instinctively stepped forward and caught Morophil's black hoof, stopping her from drifting away with the other souls. His fingertips passed through the remnant flames. The touch was not cold — it was searing hot. As familiar as his own blood.

"How are you here?" he asked.

"Probably because I've soaked up so much of your life and mana that I've become the same kind of utter fool you are." The demon smiled. "I actually thought I could shatter the damaged Uthalesh on my own and free your soul. Or maybe I just made the mistake of being too impatient. If I'd waited a little longer, I might not have ended up so pathetic. Ah, but what can I say — I work fast."

"I don't understand —"

He started to say more, but the demon raised a hand to cut him off. "Let me go. Or is devouring Uthalesh not enough? Do you plan to swallow my soul too? Not that I'd mind a destructive game like that, but..."

Jace seemed to snap back to reality and released his grip. The crimson soul-form drifted upward, away, standing out clearly among the countless icy-blue phantoms.

"Ah, you're still as adorable as ever, Jace Sesso. Well then — see you in the Twisting Nether."
