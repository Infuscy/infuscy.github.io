Chapter 1859: Return

Recite after me, Jace Sesso.

Somendimon, somatiru, ropsidar...

Following that familiar voice, amidst a vision of pure white light, Jace repeated the incantation — as long as a thesis — without error.

He did not know what this spell was for. He could feel that it was Khadgar's voice, and Khadgar's mana — but mixed within it was an immeasurable amount of pure arcane energy, far stronger than Khadgar's residual power. It made Jace think immediately of the Guardian of Tirisfal.

But before they parted, Jace remembered Khadgar mentioning that he was going to Dalaran to retrieve Atiesh, the Guardian's staff. After that, using this staff, Khadgar had nearly torn apart Aegwynn's portal — which meant he could already command its power. So that was not so strange.

His memories had indeed fully returned.

Yet even with this arcane power flowing as abundantly as a river, speaking each syllable of the spell and activating the ley lines to drive the unknown magic it was meant to invoke was still barely enough.

So Khadgar was almost miserly with his words — no unnecessary chatter.

A single reminder, and then, whether or not anyone heard, whether or not anyone recognized his voice, whatever questions they might have — begin reciting immediately, unwilling to waste even the smallest amount of mana.

It meant the mage was serious. Completely serious. Jace knew that if Khadgar was this focused, he had better do what he was told first and ask questions later. After Draenor, Karazhan, and the subsequent adventures against the Nathrezim and Aegwynn, this had become something of a habit.

More importantly, right now he did not know what to do anyway.

The countless souls that had surrounded him moments ago had already departed for where they needed to go — the legendary Grey Realm, or the Shadowlands, or the Emerald Dream, or the Twisting Nether.

Struck by Uthalesh's Death Grip after losing the power to hold them, the demon lord's attempt to reap and absorb his final master had forced him to set those souls aside temporarily. Though they were trapped here, many had retained some or nearly all of their consciousness.

And those who stayed — especially the mindless ones who tried to devour other souls in the chaos — were gradually drowned in the flood of arcane power as the new master replaced the old. They were consumed entirely, without exception.

There was only him here now.

Verditi, thomrem, atelfar, sin, clop...

A stabbing pain shot through his fingertips. A burning sensation like skin being torn open spread to his shoulder — then his whole body — accompanied by wave after wave of tingling numbness.

Heavy. Unbearably heavy. Forget lifting a hand — even staring straight ahead became an exhausting labor. The world that had been so vivid and sharp began to blur. Light and darkness bled together into a murky, complicated gray.

At the same time, his joints grew stiff, as if something had seized them. He sucked in a sharp breath. Cold and heat poured into his body in alternation, as if powerful elemental magic had struck through him continuously. Sweat oozed from every pore, soaking into his parched, near-cracked skin — and only then did it seem the skin remembered the sensation of touch, which it had long forgotten, bringing with it a clear, biting pain like ants gnawing at his flesh.

He had long since stopped fearing sensations like these — even being run through by a blade or having his skin flayed open. But now, he felt as fragile as a child who had never fallen, never fought, protected in a greenhouse, just stepping into this chaotic world.

He squeezed his eyes shut. His eyeballs did not feel like organs helping him observe the world — they felt like two cysts wedged in his skull. Even a slight rotation tugged and tore at every blood vessel and muscle inside his head.

The air tasted of thick, metallic arcane energy, mixed with faint traces of shadow and fel.

The world before him was completely dark now. Jace could sense that he was in a corridor with no light.

This corridor — or perhaps the entire building — was shrouded in powerful magical energy, blocking most of the outside magical signatures, especially dark magic. So the shadow and demonic energies here were seeping from within.

This place was the Guardian's Sanctum. He remembered coming here with Khadgar before, searching for clues about Aegwynn. This ancient hall, built over a thousand years, had a different architectural style than the rest of Dalaran — it reminded him more of the Tirisfal Vault on the hill behind Deathknell.

But why had he ended up here?

Considering the drifting shadow and demonic energies, was this place also a soul-cage of one of Uthalesh's victims? A victim with countless connections to the Guardian's Sanctum?

Like that ocean, the Thaurissan Ruins, the Feralas coast, Ilgalar's Tower, and the Twilight Embassy in Shadowforge City.

Just then, Jace noticed a coat rack not far ahead, hung with cloaks and robes — some belonging to Quel'Thalas nobles, some to Ironforge dwarves, a large brown hooded cloak, and one that was his: Olastrasz's Hide.

Jace took the dragon-hide cloak off the rack and draped it over himself. Visions of the Tower of Menethil, the burning doom of Lordaeron's capital, flashed before his eyes again.

Why was this thing here? And why did it feel so... real?

"What the hell is going on, Khadgar?" The voice, tinged with anger, came from around the corner ahead. "You said you could do it. Now Uthalesh is destroyed, the souls are gone — so where is Jace Sesso? Tell me, where is Jace Sesso?!"

The voice was so piercing, so sharp — completely different from the sounds he had perceived until now. It felt like it was hammering against his eardrums, even sending a dull ache through his jaw. He could not even identify who was speaking.

"We know very little about this resurrection ritual, Ranger-Captain, but I can confirm that the ritual has been completed successfully. Every step, every detail — all finished cleanly..."

"A resurrection ritual finishes. The subject disappears. But it's a success?"

"Please, not that tone right now, Prince Sabellian. What we need now is patience."

"We've been patient for a day and a night, Khadgar."

"I see him, dwarf."

"I see him too, little black dragon. We all saw him. He was wandering around in there, circling endlessly under that staff's compulsion. But now? Where is he?"

"I've already started to forget what happened inside. Like a terrible nightmare..."

"Your souls very nearly got assimilated and devoured by the owner of those bones, just like Jace Sesso. To come out intact and reclaim your flesh is a mercy, Sommariel. Don't ask for too much."

"No — I'm saying, I really do see him."

Needleflame pointed toward the entrance. Every head turned to look. Jace stood at the doorway, looking inside. It was not just his companions — Khadgar was there, and even Sabellian.

This did not seem like any illusion at all. No stirring of emotions and souls. This was real, solid flesh and life-force — clearer than his memories by orders of magnitude.

They were all gathered around a gray, fountain-like sculpture. The runic circle and soul-shards beneath their feet had crumbled to dust. A pile of bleached, twisted bones lay there like ashes that had burned completely, as if a single gust of wind could scatter them into dust.

"Resurrection ritual," Jace said, looking at Khadgar. "What does that mean?"

"Jace?" Sora said, dazed. "You... you..."

Vereesa walked quickly toward him in silence, biting her lip as if using every ounce of strength to keep her emotions in check.

But just as she reached Jace and reached out a hand to touch him, Grede called out to stop her.

"Hold it! Elf!"

The dwarf ran up to Jace, staring hard into the eyes of this man who had suddenly appeared. Then he pulled a bottle from his pocket and thrust it forward stiffly.

"Drink it."

"What is that, Grede?" Mosa asked, worried. "Are you sure he's in any condition to be drinking random things?"

"Never mind what it is." Grede insisted. "Drink first. You have to drink it!"

"Alright, alright, Grede."

Jace took the bottle. But he did not care in the slightest what was in it.

He was dead. No question. His soul had been taken, just like every record about Uthalesh said it would be.

It was supposed to end there. From the moment he had climbed that tower and faced the Defiler, he had not allowed himself even a shred of expectation for what might come after. Let alone hope.

But now?

Time was still turning. Air was still flowing. This was Dalaran, the Guardian's Sanctum. Vereesa's nervous breathing. Grede's bloodshot eyes. Sora covering her mouth, not daring to make a sound. The scent of scorched ash from Needleflame's clumsy transformation spell wafting through the room. Mosa's fingers rustling against her staff...

And behind them, the black dragon prince staring curiously at that small gray statue, and the archmage watching his every move with a grave expression.

He drank whatever Grede had given him. It had no taste — like plain river water, cool as it went down, settling into his stomach in a way that was somehow reassuring.

When he had finished, he handed the bottle back. Grede's eye twitched. He examined the bottle for a moment, muttering, "This Stratholme holy water hasn't expired, has it? I got it from Turalyon, you know."

"I've told you a hundred times, you damned dwarf." Khadgar sighed. "This is not necromancy. This isn't a raise-dead curse! Not a death knight resurrection ritual!"

"Get out of the way!"

Needleflame dodged around Sabellian and shoved the dwarf aside, slamming into Jace and sending him tumbling onto the steps. His back and hips hit the sharp stone edges so hard that he nearly stopped breathing from the pain. Not to mention the black dragon's arms around his neck, gripping with enough force to practically strangle him to death a second time.

The pain, the suffocation — they were so real that they felt almost foreign. But he was back.

His nose stung. Vereesa's lips trembled as she threw herself over him and Needleflame. Sora watched from a few steps away, her legs shaking so badly she could barely move, moonlight-bright tears streaming down her cheeks.

Grede stared at Jace and the others sprawled on the steps for a long moment, as if he finally understood what had happened.

He turned to the tauren beside him and mumbled thickly, "Tell me this is real, Mosa Woodmane. Tell me this is real. Not some dream you and that green dragon cooked up for me — one of those where I wake up and find out I'm about to die..."

"It's real, Grede." Mosa was crying too as she held the dwarf's head. "It's not a dream."

"So... anyone remember to thank the archmage here? Maybe toss in a simple apology for the verbal abuse while you're at it?" Khadgar folded his arms. "I feel like my contribution was fairly significant..."

But even as he said it, he had to lower his head and blink rapidly.

"It really did work. Remarkable." Sabellian said. "I'm curious what this method was originally meant to resurrect, Khadgar. It looks like everything was nearly ready — just needed a suitable conductor."

Khadgar coughed a few times, as if pulled back to reality. "I know the Tirisfal Council has been disbanded and the Kirin Tor has taken heavy losses, but some things still cannot be shared with outsiders. I'm sorry I can't satisfy your curiosity, Prince Sabellian."

The black dragon did not seem particularly bothered. The mage's peripheral vision caught Jace looking at him — and he seemed to feel fine. Jace was indeed staring at him.

Because Jace already understood what this ritual was, and who it had originally been meant for.
