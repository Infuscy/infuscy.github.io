Chapter 186: Physical and Mental Changes

Upstairs, Jace eyed the warlock's bag, shut the door, closed the curtains, and said, "Sazeno, out."

The imp poked his head out of the bag. "Master, I'm about to lose all sense of time!"

"That's for the best," Jace said. "I want to ask you something. Are you sure that spell is Life Tap?"

"Life Tap?" the imp asked, puzzled.

"You forgot?"

Jace had only just asked when he realized he'd just picked two words from Common that corresponded to the Chinese and tossed them out casually.

No one in this world who spoke Common had given that spell a name yet, so naturally the imp had no idea what he was talking about.

"Ruhelawen…" Jace said. "That spell — remember? The one you pulled from that broken fragment. You said it was extremely evil."

The moment he so much as started saying the incantation, the shadow energy in the air seemed to surge forward in anticipation.

"I remember, ha!" The imp nodded. "Life Tap — you've already given it a Common name, how extraordinary. But actually, we prefer to call it Vitality Sacrifice. So, you sacrificed your own life force and converted it into dark magic, master! Ah, that's why I sensed you seem… a little different."

"Different?"

Jace hadn't actually felt anything at all, but since the imp had said it, maybe he really was different.

He felt a faint flicker of regret for having used that spell. But if he went back and did it all over again, what other choice would he have had?

"After I used that spell, I felt all the shadow power rush in and gnaw at my skin and body. But at the same time, it stimulated some kind of… power. I don't feel like I recovered any mana. It was more like a shadow backlash, turning me into a shadow creature."

"That's the result of forcing that spell, master," the imp explained. "A demon's will is far stronger than a human's. When they sacrifice their own vitality and offer it up as magic and shadow, even though they undergo shadow-ification, they can also control the power they've attracted, bending it to better serve them. Your will is stronger than an ordinary mortal's, but compared to a demon's… well… but that's not your fault! After all, your origins aren't exactly impressive!"

"Origins?" Jace asked. "You mean race?"

"Of course," the imp replied. "Races born in the Twisting Nether have a far greater resistance to shadow than races raised under Azeroth's protection!"

Jace asked again, "So I've been shadow-ified a little?"

"Maybe!" the imp said. "Since you say you used that forbidden spell!"

"Is there any way to reverse it?" Jace asked. "Or once I've been shadow-ified even a little, there's no going back?"

"From what I understand, master, since you can be shadow-ified, naturally you can also be re-fleshed." The imp shook his head. "But why would you want to be re-fleshed? If you plan on becoming a powerful warlock, wouldn't it be better to remake yourself into a shadow creature? Hehe!"

"What are you laughing at?" Jace asked, annoyed.

"I just find you interesting, master!" the imp said. "You want to grow stronger, but you keep putting obstacles in your own way. Is it just you who's like this, or are all humans like this?"

"Humans come in all kinds. I don't represent anything," Jace said. "I just don't want to become a different person. I don't know if you understand that."

"I don't." Sazeno stopped his fidgeting and stood in place. "Even if you're shadow-ified, aren't you still yourself? The Eredar were remade into Man'ari by the master of the Burning Legion, yet not a single creature in the Twisting Nether would say the Deceiver isn't the Kil'jaeden he was before."

Man'ari meant "twisted thing" in the Eredar tongue. The Eredar had originally used this near-curse of a word to describe demons, but after they accepted the Dark Titan's gift, they took to calling themselves by that very word — like a spiteful, mocking jab at their former selves.

"But," Jace said, "Kil'jaeden no longer follows his original ideals. He was once one of the Eredar's leaders, pursuing wisdom and probing the secrets of the universe. But after he accepted the Burning Legion's power, he became a different person overnight."

"Maybe because he'd already uncovered the wisdom and secrets he wanted, master," the imp said. "Even setting aside the Eredar sorcerers, I know that in a human's brief life, there are changes beyond counting — changes even greater than the ones Kil'jaeden underwent before and after meeting his master! And the triggers that bring about those human changes… are far smaller than the power his master bestowed! But can you say they're not the same person anymore?"

"But those are changes on a spiritual level," Jace said.

"So when it comes to physical changes — if an enemy chops off your arm, you're no longer Jace Sesso?" the imp pressed.

"Our views are different. It's normal you don't agree right away," Jace said. "Maybe I didn't make myself clear — what I hate is this kind of change. My goal is to change my present will as little as possible while getting as strong as I can. And what you need to do is help me in that regard. Stop arguing with me. Even if you win the argument, what good does it do? You have to listen to me anyway. And I feel like if we keep discussing this, it's going to turn into a philosophy problem."

"Understood!" the imp replied. "But master, I don't have any good solutions right now either. If you want to change as little as possible, then you can only try your best not to use that spell!"

"I know," Jace said. "That's enough for today. Back in the bag. We should be leaving Dalaran tomorrow."

The imp nodded sharply, then leaped into the bag.

Jace used his mouth and hands to cinch the bag shut, then carefully moved to the bed and lay down on his side. A shadow lingered that he couldn't shake. Had he really been shadow-ified?

The imp shouldn't be lying to him — he'd never told an outright lie before.

Jace thought about never using that spell again. But… if not for that spell, he, Windreaver, and Kafal would all be dead. So what did that make this?

Turning these messy thoughts over in his mind, he couldn't fall asleep until well past midnight. His mood had hit rock bottom.

How to cheer himself up?

Might as well take a look at those gold coins.

He opened the bag. The gleaming big-foreheaded Terenas IIs shone brighter than one another. Now he was even less likely to sleep.
