## Chapter 1860: A New Day

"Cheers!"

Under the lamplight, the streets of the town outside Dalaran were packed with cheering people. Everyone was drinking, singing, as if celebrating some grand festival.

Jace and his companions raised their cups and clinked them happily together. The beer was a bit weak — drawing the occasional grumble from a cluster of Wildhammer dwarves nearby, which did not quite match the festive mood. But the war was not over. From Tirisfal to Southshore, people had lost their homes, their families, their friends. Being able to drink wine not yet tainted by demonic magic during this rare break — that was enough to ask for.

Vereesa looked back at the crowd. "In a few days, when we tell the whole Alliance that Jace is back, all the defenders gathered here — I think everyone will be even happier than this."

"Let me remind you all once more." Khadgar held up a finger, lowering his voice. "Don't mention anything about resurrection, soul rituals, or anything like that. Not even to your closest friends. Just say we managed to find him. Unless you want to bring Jace endless trouble."

"Got it." "Remember clearly." The group acknowledged one after another.

The only one not holding a cup was Mosa, who had transformed back into her great cat form. A tauren was still a bit too visually overwhelming for these northerners who had seen too many long-horned, shaggy, giant-hoofed demons over the past few days.

She rested her cat head on the table and asked quietly, "Actually, I'm curious — what's the occasion today?"

Khadgar swallowed his mouthful of beer. "I just asked around. The Alliance army has defeated Mannoroth, the Pit Lord. Retaking Silverlaine Castle is pretty much a done deal."

"Who killed Mannoroth?" Jace asked.

"High Priestess Tyrande Whisperwind," Khadgar said. "You met her before when you went to Ashenvale, didn't you?"

Jace nodded thoughtfully, then asked, "And Archmage Antonidas?"

"Sorry to disappoint you, Sesso." Khadgar chuckled quietly. "Though hundreds from Dalaran to Quel'Thalas were hoping he'd kick it early — he's still not quite dead yet."

Grommash Hellscream had not walked toward his fate. Antonidas had not died as he was supposed to.

Tyrande Whisperwind and Malfurion Stormrage had come to the Eastern Kingdoms, united with the humans, dwarves, and Quel'dorei. The Sunwell had not been destroyed. The World Tree Nordrassil and the second Well of Eternity stood intact, silent upon Mount Hyjal.

Everything was different now.

Jace instinctively touched his chest for his pocket watch to check the time, then realized that after his resurrection, he had been given a set of military-issue clothes and trousers meant for Lordaeron soldiers. Other than the Olastrasz's Hide draped over him for warmth, nothing he had carried at the top of the royal city was left.

"What's wrong, Jace?" Vereesa asked quietly.

"Nothing. I just —" Jace shook his head. "How long has it been since I got... taken out?"

"'Taken out'?" Needleflame leaned over the table indignantly, sticking her head out proudly. "It was at the very top of that human palace! You used that unbelievable dark magic to destroy the Defiler!"

"The way you talk, you'd think you were the one who killed him." Grede laughed hoarsely.

"Whoever killed him, it wasn't you, dwarf!" Needleflame stuck out her tongue smugly.

"You little —" Grede gritted his teeth and reached for her hair. Needleflame ducked behind Sora with a *whoosh*. The dwarf glared at her, huffed a few times, then waved his hand and let it go.

Everyone around the table burst out laughing. Sora sighed fondly and pulled the black dragon close, patting her little gray face. "Ten days, Jace. Not that long."

"It feels like it's been... years." Jace stared at the cup before him.

But thinking back on everything that had happened in between, he realized it really had been just a moment.

"Ten days was long enough for Uthalesh to nearly erase your will." Khadgar said quietly. "From what they told me after returning, it was worse inside than I'd expected. We almost couldn't bring you back. If Uthalesh had become what it was meant to be, everything would've returned to square one — except half of Lordaeron would've been destroyed by demons..."

"Where's Aegwynn?" Vereesa asked. "Why hasn't she shown herself?"

"A lot of gryphon riders saw a mage when they were bombing the portal," Grede said, holding his cup. "But after I threw that bomb, nobody saw her again. She's probably been crushed like a kitten by the Defiler. That overreaching witch — it's what she deserved."

"I don't disagree with your opinion of her, Grede, but Aegwynn was never someone you could call 'overreaching.'" Vereesa said quietly.

Jace glanced at Khadgar beside him. The archmage just drank in silence, offering no opinion at all on a topic that clearly had no shortage of connections to him.

"Where did you go after the bomb, Grede?" Jace turned and asked.

"A group of orcs saved me." Grede grabbed some dried fruit and shoved it in his mouth, mumbling as he chewed. "The blast wave from that damned bomb threw me way, way out. Gorgeit found me, but she was hurt too — could only carry me through the forest, couldn't fly at all. Then I ran into a group of friendly orcs. Funny, right?"

"Mmph. A group of friendly orcs in Lordaeron's forest. That sentence alone would've gotten me hanged in the capital three times over — once for questioning the patrol's efficiency, once for orc sympathizing, and once for collusion with the enemy." The dwarf held up three fingers. "Lucky for me, the capital died before I did. So it's just a story with a boring ending. You should tell yours instead, Jace — what really happened that night?"

Everyone at the table looked at him with curiosity, waiting for Jace to speak.

Jace glanced toward Sora. She noticed his gaze and seemed to flinch away instinctively.

"By then, everyone had already retreated to the undercity. But Sora was still trapped on that tower. I thought I had to bring her back. I just didn't expect Archimonde to already be at the walls."

"At them?" Grede raised a hand above his head. "That wall wasn't even as tall as him!"

"I was following the Queen Ysera's orders, evacuating the humans in the city." Sora said, her head low. "But that one spell... Even though I wasn't hit like my sisters were, I still couldn't withstand the force radiating from it. As I was falling, I caught hold of the tower wall and fell inside. There were flying demons everywhere outside, so I planned to take human form, use the tower for cover, and escape. But two or three demons found me and chased me in. I managed to drive them back, but I didn't have the strength to go any further. I'm sorry."

"It's not your fault, Sora." Jace said. "Even if I'd known the Defiler was waiting for me in there, I would've come for you."

Sora's eyes widened slightly. Jace added, "Of course, in that case, I might've been a bit calmer. Maybe thought about tactics a little. Instead of just charging into the tower without thinking like I did that day."

"So you're telling me you ran straight into a pile of demons without a second thought." Grede said.

"Like you'd have had time for tactics in a situation like that, dwarf." Vereesa fixed her eyes on him. "You don't think about tactics even when there's no danger at all."

"Then you don't know me at all." Grede snorted. "If I never thought about tactics, your boyfriend would've been dead in some Elwynn mine years ago! Long before the Defiler ever got the chance."

Just then, Jace noticed a figure at the edge of the square watching them. The others were still caught up in their lively discussion about what had happened inside the walls. Jace set down his cup and stood. "I'll be right back."

"Hey, you haven't told us how you beat the Defiler yet." Grede called after him.

"I'll finish when I get back." Jace said.

"It's Prince Sabellian." Khadgar rested his chin on his hand, looking at the dwarf, and gestured vaguely in that direction. "Looks like he wants to talk to Jace."

Jace nodded. Needleflame had been excitedly telling Mosa about fighting off several demons at once — but at the mention of that name, she flinched all over. Her eyes followed Jace as he left the table, but she could not quite bring her gaze all the way to that dark figure.

"You look brand new, Sesso."

The red-robed noble by the broken wall spoke. The yellow eyes glinting in the shadow were as unsettling as ever.

"I remember the Light was kind to you, but you could never keep it from burning you. That holy water doesn't hurt you anymore."

"Honestly, I don't know why either." Jace said. "Did you need something?"

"I have to leave Azeroth." Sabellian dropped his smile. "I don't like this place. And not just because of my father, or my other kin. You understand."

"I understand." Jace said.

"You've taken good care of Needleflame." Sabellian said. "I'm grateful."

"She goes by Fidegnia now." Jace reminded him.

"Mm?" The black dragon frowned. "Who gave her that name?"

"Don't worry, it wasn't me." Jace said. "It was your sister, Lady Onyxia."

Jace expected Sabellian to explode at the mention of Onyxia. Instead, he just nodded calmly, muttered the new name a few times to himself. His emotions were mixed, but nowhere near as intense as Jace had anticipated.

"By the way — I'm grateful too, Prince Sabellian. For the soul ritual."

"It was nothing." The black dragon puffed out his chest. "A small favor. Your mana had already worn that staff down to a wreck. The rest of the work was not as difficult as Khadgar described. But — I suppose we're even now?"

"I never blamed you for anything." Jace said.

"Is that so?" the black dragon asked.

"Like you said." Jace replied. "One day we all have to face the problems we can't solve. That's when we find out what we're really made of. And as it turns out — I'm no different."

"No, no. You are different." Sabellian smiled. "But I'm glad you see it that way."

"There's one more thing I've always been curious about." Jace said.

"What?" the black dragon asked.

"That day, on top of the demon palace." Jace said. "Who did you really see?"

"The Deceiver, Kil'jaeden. Why?" Sabellian's eyes slanted toward him. "Are you saying I should have stayed and fought him to the death alongside a hot-headed fool like you? Ah, right — you did just kill Archimonde the Defiler with your own hands, so I suppose I have no right to mock your choices."

"That's not what I meant." Jace said. "Kil'jaeden appeared to me as my mother. Was it my mother you saw too?"

At this, Sabellian hesitated for a moment.

"No."

He swallowed. The arrogant, slightly contemptuous look in his eyes gradually went dull.

"I saw my... mother too. That's all. And afterward, I realized it was just the Deceiver's psychic projection. Nothing complicated."

"Well. See you around, Sesso."
