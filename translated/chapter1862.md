## Chapter 1862: Parting, Reunion

Rainy night. Dalaran. Sunset Square.

An elven ranger in simple green civilian clothes, a wizard in a dark gray long cloak, and a mage in a brown hooded cloak carrying a cloth-wrapped straight wooden staff arrived at the square. Across from them, between the stone columns, priests were gathered. The holy-light runes on their robes flickered faintly through the curtain of rain. They were praying quietly for the wounded resting nearby, their chanting audible from a great distance.

Jace did not want to draw too much attention tonight, so he had left the dragon-hide cloak behind. Too many people in Dalaran recognized that thing these days.

"They're just over there." Khadgar pointed at an alley ahead. "Madame Henny Sesso has been helping the church these past few days. Her lodging is down that way."

"This district has been a bit chaotic since the Defiler's arrival. The exact address might be hard to find, but I'm sure any priest from Stormwind can point you in the right direction. I won't tag along."

"Thank you, Khadgar. We'll go ask around." Vereesa said, heading that way. The archmage nodded. Jace looked at him. "So, this is where we part."

"It's about time." Khadgar said.

"What are your plans next?" Jace asked.

"First, I need to go back to Karazhan. Make sure everything's all right there." Khadgar looked southward. "The Curator and all the tower's arcane guardians are running normally, but I haven't been back in a while. Time for maintenance."

Jace watched Vereesa's retreating figure and could not help asking quietly, "Where is Aegwynn, really?"

The mage's hand around his staff tightened unconsciously. "Do you really want to know?"

"If you don't think I need to know, I won't press." Jace said.

"Fine." The mage pulled his hood down. "She's dead, Jace."

Jace's eyes widened slightly. "You killed her?"

"It wasn't a choice I had." Khadgar said. "And if I hadn't, I doubt I could have brought you back. So either way, I had to try. It's just..."

The mage sighed and did not continue.

"The way you resurrected me — it was the method she was going to use to resurrect Medivh." Jace said.

"Exactly." Khadgar was not surprised that Jace had figured it out. "You noticed some clues too, didn't you? In Kalimdor."

Jace neither confirmed nor denied it. Khadgar continued. "Aegwynn believed that after I killed our master in Karazhan, Sargeras's soul was driven back to the Twisting Nether. That meant she could use the last of her Guardian power to perform the resurrection spell — to bring her son back. Her real son, not the Burning Legion's master who had possessed him. But when she learned Uthalesh had surfaced, she put the plan on hold."

"You believed her judgment? About Sargeras's soul, I mean." Jace said.

"Regardless, the method she recorded really can bring a person back to this world." Khadgar spread his hands toward Jace. "She believed the Death Grip would destroy Uthalesh. It didn't fully succeed in the end, but I can guarantee you, Jace — if Uthalesh hadn't been wielded by you, the result would have been much 'smoother.'"

"So why wouldn't I trust her judgment on this? Maybe the Guardian Medivh's soul is still wandering somewhere. His mother had already touched his soul when she reached that conclusion. Maybe she was just one step away. I..."

"You're planning to find a way. To finish that step." Jace said.

"I don't know." Khadgar watched the mages passing by through the rain in the distance. "It's nearly impossible. But... I've succeeded once."

He frowned and stepped closer, lowering his voice to barely a whisper. "The key is — I really did see him that night, Jace. You know that. It's why I didn't want to keep it from you."

"I haven't decided anything yet. Haven't even settled on any idea. Everything we've discussed tonight must stay confidential. You can't tell anyone — not High Lord Grede Thunderfist, not Ranger-Captain Vereesa Windrunner, not Lady Sommariel or Lady Fidegnia, not the tauren druid from Mulgore, Mosa Woodmane... anyone. Or any demon. Anything."

"I'll keep it secret." Jace said.

"Doesn't matter. If you dare tell anyone, I'll tell the Kirin Tor that Aegwynn resurrected you." Khadgar leaned in. "Then neither of us gets off easy."

"That won't be necessary..." Jace scratched his forehead. "But if I need to reach you while you're traveling — how do I get in touch?"

"Jace Sesso needs help?" The mage's eyes went wide as he pointed at his own nose. "Then you'd better not call me. Thanks."

"What about mine and Vereesa's wedding?" Jace said.

"Ah..."

Khadgar seemed to catch on. He nodded slowly.

"Fair point." The mage thought for a moment. "Here's what you do — when the time comes, recite 'Khadgar, Archmage, time has come, come to the page' in Thalassian. That way, no exceptionally gifted child will accidentally blurt out the incantation and trigger the ley lines."

"What kind of chant is that... 'Khadgar, Archmage, time has come, come to the page.'" Jace recited it in Thalassian. "Can you feel that?"

"It won't work yet. But it will within three months." Khadgar snapped his fingers confidently. "Also — practice your Thalassian with the Ranger-Captain a bit more. It'll improve your odds. Honestly, I've never heard anyone make the elven language sound like Demonic."

"Got it, got it." Jace waved his hand.

"Jace Sesso." Khadgar smiled, rubbing his clean-shaven chin, eyes narrowing. "You're a remarkable fellow. Knowing you has been my honor. From Draenor back to here, from Karazhan to Blackrock Mountain, Violet Citadel, the Great Cathedral, and then to this square today... this entire story has been an irreplaceable treasure to me. Looking back now, I can't believe I was actually debating whether to bring you back just a few days ago."

"Same to you, mage." Jace looked at the Atiesh in Khadgar's hand. "And I think from today on, you are unquestionably the new Guardian of Tirisfal."

"What kind of dark curse is that?" Khadgar shook his head with a laugh. "The Tirisfal Council is already a shell of its former self. The age of Guardians is over, Jace. If the Dark Portal nineteen years ago didn't make everyone see that, then today they've seen it clearly enough."

"But that doesn't mean this world has no one to protect it. Look — from Mount Hyjal to Quel'Danas, from Lake Lordamere to Khaz Modan, to the countless mortals of Elwynn who have stood together. Even the Burning Legion cannot break this unprecedented alliance again."

"And whether you want to admit it or not, Sesso — without you, none of this would have happened."

Listening to the mage, Jace gazed at the raven's-head top of the staff wrapped in rune-cloth, catching a faint glimpse of the gleaming hidden eye within.

Khadgar smiled. He leaned forward slightly and tapped the staff gently against the wet cobblestones.

His form dissolved into a scattering of arcane feathers.

Jace blinked as if snapping back to reality, staring at the fading silhouette.

"Well then. Farewell, Jace. Take care of Mr. and Mrs. Sesso, Ranger-Captain Vereesa Windrunner, and yourself — especially yourself..."

By the time nearby mages noticed the arcane disturbance and looked over, there was not a trace of residual magic left in the air.

Thank you, Khadgar, Jace thought.

"Jace, over here! I found it!" Vereesa called from a distance.

"Coming." Jace waved.

"Khadgar? He left already?" The ranger looked around.

"He had urgent business." Jace said.

"Rare to see a mage more impatient than I am." The ranger said. "But I suppose only someone like him could have brought you back to us in time."

She rose on her toes. Jace wrapped his arms around her belt, leaned down, and kissed her gently. He breathed in the faint floral scent mixed with a hint of metal in her hair. For a moment, neither of them wanted to pull away. The souls that had been so bitterly cold and dark not long ago trembled at this fiery touch.

This feeling — he had once thought he would never experience again in a hundred lifetimes.

"What spell is this?" The ranger looked into his eyes, her voice soft. "Even in Quel'Thalas, no one's ever mentioned a resurrection ritual like this. Does the great library in Karazhan really contain every scrap of knowledge from every dark corner of the Twisting Nether? Including the dark magic you used to defeat Archimonde?"

"I only spent a few days in there. He studied for years. With the Guardian Medivh as his personal tutor." Jace said quietly. "Who knows what he learned in there. I don't understand how he did it either."

"Speaking of which..." Vereesa cleared her throat and lowered her voice. "You, uh, do you still have... well, Khadgar did promise he'd resurrect a complete you."

"Still there. Still there."

"Of course I know that! I meant —"

"Want to... try it?"

"Tonight? At Madame Sesso's place? Have you lost your mind?"

"Who said tonight?"

"Uh, well, fine. So... soon?"

"Soon." Jace nodded.

"Without that demon lurking nearby, it might go a lot smoother this time." Vereesa said dryly. "I've suspected for a while... never mind."

Jace laughed. "I'm surprised you agreed to let me summon Morophil back, Vereesa. I was worried her soul might get captured by Kil'jaeden again. She knows us too well. It'd be a massive threat."

"Ha. I don't believe that." Vereesa's lips curled.

She walked on, then let out a long sigh. "Fine. Your concern is valid. But even setting that aside — she's my friend, Jace."

"I can't deny what we've all been through together. She didn't have to protect me. She didn't have to help me. But she did. Even if she was hesitant sometimes, even if she was sarcastic now and then. Anyway — she taught me that not all demons are as mindless as the Burning Legion's killers. Like your little imp."

"Sazeno's probably run off somewhere. When I find him, I'm going to give him a piece of my mind." Jace said.

"Don't change the subject." Vereesa raised a finger. "More importantly — I am Vereesa Windrunner. I'm not just anyone. I remember telling you before: I can accept that countless exceptional, powerful women — Quel'dorei, human, dwarf, gnome, demon, even dragon — might admire you, might love you. Because you're Jace Sesso."

"But I cannot accept her saving all of us — mostly saving you — and then sacrificing herself like some hero in a story, leaving an indelible mark on your heart. Lucky for us she's a demon, so we still have a chance to see her again. This time, I will absolutely not let her get the better of me so easily. Absolutely not."

Vereesa let out a soft hum and picked up her pace, quickly leaving her lover behind. A few steps ahead, she turned and pointed to a small door at the side of the street. Light shone through the crack.

Jace did not know why, but looking at it, he felt dizzy.

Even he could not describe what he felt right now. Especially remembering the past few years — every time he had stood in front of his parents' door, by the canal...

It had been so long since he had last come to see them so easily, so freely, without a care in the world.

Thinking of that, he almost felt nervous. His heart thumped wildly, so loud it felt like someone was pounding on his chest.

Over a decade ago, when he was about to leave for Southshore, his father and mother had stood in the distance, watching his carriage drive away. It felt like he was only now finally coming home.

Thank you, Prophet.

"Madame Sesso? Are you home?"

"Vereesa?"

"It's me, madame. Guess who I brought?"

"Mom — look, did I get taller?"

"Who — wait. Jace?"

"Jace! Jace..."

The rain stopped. Beneath the witness of the White Lady and the Blue Child, mother and son held each other tight.

He had dreamed of this day a thousand times. But he had never truly dared to hope he would reach it.

Thank you, Master Marin, Sister Amy, Ursula.

And of course, Vereesa, Grede, Sora, Needleflame, Mosa, and Morophil.

Plus Sazeno — you little thing. Don't think you can escape my grasp.

Thank you, Azeroth.

And the beautiful troll ladies.

## Author's Postscript

Author: Another Torpedo

First of all, greetings and Happy New Year to all the readers.

It's finally finished. I thought I'd feel relieved after posting the last chapter, but there wasn't much relief. Maybe because it happened to fall during the Spring Festival — I've been busy the whole time, not much rest.

During the writing process, I often had things I wanted to say, planning to stuff them all into the postscript once the story was done. But when the ending actually came, either I'd forgotten everything, or I'd already worked it into the text bit by bit. So sitting here now, I don't even know what to write.

When I first started this book, I had no idea so many people would like it. Western fantasy, a Warcraft fanfic, road-trip adventure — these were all considered outdated or niche genres back then. So I'm very grateful to the editors and to the website for agreeing to sign this book.

My initial reason for putting fingers to keyboard was, besides hoping for some pocket money, really more of a self-indulgence. Like finding the game not realistic enough, so I wanted to write something more grounded. Daydream about storylines that went differently from the game. And at the same time, give some readers who enjoy fantasy adventures — or WoW players nostalgic for the leveling experience — something fun to read.

But even back then, I had already set the ending that doesn't quite match the opening's tone — leaving home to reuniting, walking into darkness to "gaining freedom," and then blowing up with Archimonde.

I remember not long after I started, a reader asked me who my favorite Warcraft character was. Yes — it's Archimonde. I wrapped a five-million-character dumpling just for that one dip of vinegar.

Looking back now that it's finished, I'm happy I earned my keep, and I went through a bizarre, wonderful long journey with a few protagonists. Seeing so many readers love this book, I'm honored too — just like Jace, I managed to accomplish my original goal.

Thank you all so much for your support along the way.

As for side stories, epilogues — I don't really have a clear plan yet. I've put all my energy into wrapping things up. Going to single updates until the end was really the limit of my ability. But I'll read the comments. Readers can remind me.

As for the next book... I haven't had time to think about it carefully either. If there is one, it should also be an adventure story. Maybe more lighthearted, without so much world-ending catastrophe and saving-the-planet stuff. This book started out intending to be cheerful, but the more I wrote, the harder it was to stay cheerful.

Oh, and one more thing — I really do think the female trolls in Warcraft are beautiful. You've been scolding me for two years, and I still think so.
