## Chapter 187: Setting Out Again

The next morning, Dushan set out early, heading west toward Silverpine Forest. Jace waited at the Azure Arrival with his luggage, and Grede walked in not long after.

"I got in touch with a Church caravan," Grede said, coming up to Jace. He was about to go on when he noticed the few pastries sitting on the cloth-wrapped bundle beside Jace.

Those things looked expensive. What kind of fruit was that, frost pear…

"Someone else bought them," Jace said, picking up half a strawberry round and taking a bite. He chewed and said, "Sit down. Let me show you a trick."

"What spell did you learn now?" the dwarf asked.

Jace gave him a look, then remembered that in Azeroth, "magic tricks" weren't really a thing people got excited about.

So he cut straight to the point. "Transmutation. Silver into gold."

"Silver into gold?" The dwarf laughed, slapped two silver coins on the table, and plopped down across from him. "Go on, then — turn both of those into gold coins. Wait, no — transmute them into gold coins."

"Only two? You're selling me short," Jace said.

The dwarf raised an eyebrow, then grabbed and piled twenty silver coins onto the table. Jace said, "Too many — it costs mana. Just put down ten."

"Fine." Grede took ten back. "Do it."

Jace eyed the twelve silver coins, then said slowly, "Actually, twelve should work too."

Grede stared at Jace's hand and lips with a half-smile. Jace snapped his right hand, his lips moved, and suddenly a blinding flash of light burst out. Grede couldn't help but squint.

"No casting spells inside, my dear masters!" the shopkeeper up front shouted. "If you keep muttering incantations, I'm calling the guards!"

"Sorry, sorry, it won't happen again."

Jace gave an embarrassed laugh.

When his gaze came back down, Grede across from him had gone stupid.

Jace glanced down at the twelve gold coins on the table and said with feigned nonchalance, "By the Light, it actually worked. I thought it was going to fail!"

Grede chuckled and slapped the table. "Give me back those silver coins, quick! The face on them's different!"

"You're no fun." Jace dug out the silvers and set them on the table. "Six-four split doesn't divide evenly. Take seven, I'll take five. How's that?"

"This is really the money from selling that Teron Gorefiend command?" Grede asked.

"Sold it to the elves," Jace said. "After that meeting — especially after the theft incident — that letter wasn't just a little orcish textbook anymore, Grede. Its value skyrocketed!"

"It sold for that much…" The dwarf scratched under his beard for a while before saying, "Then you keep that Gurubashi gold coin."

"Keep it for me and you'll be even worse off," Jace reminded him. "I'm already getting more than a six-four split."

"You think I'd really split it six-four with you?" Grede laughed, counted out seven gold coins, and stuffed them into his pocket. "Let's just muddle through with the split! Ahh, good times are coming! What should I buy? This is torture — we're leaving Dalaran today, and we've got this huge pile of money. But once we hit Alterac, Durnholde, Aerie Peak, there won't be anything decent to buy!"

"Save your spending. We'll talk when we get back to Stormwind," Jace said.

"Tell you what — let me buy you an outfit?" Grede said. "We're about to head up into the snow mountains. Climbing a snow mountain in winter, and all you've got left is that shirt and that cloak. You're not going to freeze to death up there, are you?"

"It completely slipped my mind." Jace pinched the collar of his shirt and felt the thickness. This outfit was already freezing in Dalaran in December, let alone climbing further northeast into the snow.

Grede said, "That Church caravan leaves at nine. We've got about an hour. We'd better hurry and buy something, or we'll have to borrow clothes from them on the road."

The two of them quickly gathered their things and went looking for a clothing shop. In the end, they spent five silver on a brown leather jacket and a pair of thick trousers for Jace. It felt like walking into a clothing shop in summer and running straight out into winter.

The weather had turned sharply cold over the past week or two. Silverpine Forest, which had still been a deep green when they first arrived, now looked positively haggard. With Winter Veil approaching, the dwarves were all setting out for home, so the post station south of the city was crowded with dwarves and gnomes. If Jace hadn't come to see it, he never would have known there were this many dwarves in Dalaran.

Probably because the dwarven village of Dun Baldar up in Alterac wasn't nearly as lively or well-stocked as Dalaran. So after the war, no small number of dwarves had come down the mountain to join in the fun on the human side.

Right now, Jace was draped in a brownish-red cloak, clutching a staff wrapped in a dark, tattered yellow cloth, a bag at his side, sitting on a boulder beneath a pine tree.

He hadn't trimmed his beard in days, and the little curls on his mouth made him stare absently at the group of Church of the Holy Light priests not far off. He really did look like some homeless hedge wizard.

"Grede," he asked, turning his head. "Can you smell anything strange?"

"What kind of strange?" Grede said, munching on a pastry bought from Remi Stargaze.

"Something like… corpses. Or shadow," Jace said.

Grede's chewing stopped dead. He glanced at Jace's bag again and asked, "What the hell did you hide in there this time?"

Jace pointed to the head of his staff. "This cloth is Teron Gorefiend's cloak."

"You bastard," Grede jumped. "Why didn't you say so earlier? I went and found a Church caravan! If those priests find out, won't they throw you off the wagon?"

"I doubt it. I'll just say I killed a death knight and put the thing to good use. And I'm not bragging — I really did kill a death knight," Jace said. "I just figured I'd avoid causing trouble. So can you smell it or not?"

"Can't smell a thing," Grede said. "You're something else, using Gul'dan's apprentice's clothes as a wrapping cloth. I'll have a hell of a story to tell after this."

Jace laughed. "Maybe someday Teron Gorefiend's ghost will be bragging to someone too — back in the day, Archmage Jace used my cloak to wrap his staff."

"Ha! You really do dream big!" Grede took a clean cloth and wrapped it around the thing with no eyes, laughing as he did. "Forget that — you should be thinking about how to win Windreaver's heart! All right, move your ass. Those Church priests are about to set off."

Hearing that, Jace's smile slowly faded. He lowered his head and checked all his luggage once more.

Including the proto-drake whisker hair he'd given to Amy Marin, all the books, the money pouch, the clothes, the dried provisions his parents had bought him, the pastries some rich folk had bought him — he confirmed nothing had been left behind, then stood up.

He dusted off his backside and cloak with one hand, watched the dwarf's back as he shouldered the big woven sack, and thought to himself: Three months ago, if I'd told you we were going to kill a real dragon, would you have thought I was talking out of my ass, Grede?
