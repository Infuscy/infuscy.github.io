## Chapter 188: The Snow Yeti Legend

The decision to buy a few extra outfits back in Dalaran had been a stroke of genius.

Traveling along Lake Lordamere for the better part of the day, Jace hadn't felt much of anything. But by the afternoon, when he looked up at the Alterac snow peaks and felt the wind hit his face, he was getting a bad feeling.

After passing the abandoned Warsong Gulch outpost clinging to the cliffside, the caravan followed a meltwater river that ran straight into Lake Lordamere up toward the summit.

The deeper they pushed into the Alterac valley, the more the snow drifting down from the peaks built into an unbroken blizzard. The air currents rolling around the mountains were fierce and fast, making the pine forests on the slopes moan without pause.

The whole caravan was struggling badly. Jace had been riding in the wagon at first, but eventually he had to get down and walk.

If not for his still-healing shoulder, he'd have had to help push the wagons just like Grede.

Then again, tagging along with the Church caravan hadn't cost them a copper in the first place. They'd only wanted the company.

The journey from Dalaran to the city of Alterac wasn't terribly long, but the road was packed with every kind of hardship imaginable.

If the two of them had been climbing alone and anything went wrong, they'd very likely have lost their lives. With a group of priests looking out for them, it was a lot safer. And the priests welcomed as many travelers as possible into the group — after all, these days, fewer and fewer people wanted to go to Alterac. Gazing at the narrow mountain path ahead, the surrounding cliffs and snow valleys, Jace thought that if an ogre or a pack of frost wolf orcs came leaping out right now, it'd be checkmate.

But aside from the frozen corpses they'd found on the hillside, at least nothing else unsettling had shown up so far.

The ogres didn't seem to have made it into these snow mountains yet. And the frost wolf clan of the Alterac ranges… were still struggling to survive. Most likely they wouldn't risk taking casualties by picking a fight with a traveling party this size.

At night, the caravan rested in a naturally formed cavern halfway up the mountain. It was only once they were inside that Jace noticed the walls were covered with writing.

Dwarvish, elven — and of course the most Common. But a lot of that Common writing was completely illegible to him.

The Alterac Mountains were one of the oldest dwellings of humanity. After the human tribes of Arathi were united, the first war of foreign conquest had been against the human tribes of the Alterac Mountains.

The war between the Arathi tribe and the Alterac tribe was one of the primary historical memories of human civilization. The most famous legend from that war was undoubtedly the duel between Emperor Ignatius, leader of the Alterac, and the great King Thoradin — a mythic battle that had established the basic borders of a vast empire.

Alterac had witnessed the rise and fall of humanity. It had once been, one might say, the holy mountain of all mankind.

It was only after the breakup of the Arathi Empire that Kul Tiras and the Kingdom of Stormwind, both far from these mountains, were no longer willing to emphasize their ties to their northern kin. And Lordaeron, Gilneas, and even Alterac itself had no desire to be bound by the concept of a unified great empire that this mountain range symbolized. Only then did that meaning gradually fade from memory.

For ten years, countless travelers had left their marks on this ancient mountain road between the Dalaran region and the city of Alterac. This cave seemed to have become something of a waystation.

"I hear Alterac's snow yetis live in caves like this, Jace," Grede said from his spot in the corner, lowering his voice. "You think this cave might've housed snow yetis before?"

"How would I know? I've never climbed an Alterac snow mountain," Jace replied quietly, shivering under his cloak.

Most of the people in the cave had already settled in, tucking themselves among their clothes and luggage, preparing to rest. Aside from the wind that blew in from outside now and then, there was almost no sound in the cave at all.

"In another ten days or so, when this road becomes completely impassable, dwarf," said a priest lying on his side nearby, speaking softly, "this place will become the yetis' home. We're only borrowing it temporarily, while they don't need to shelter here yet."

"The Kingdom of Alterac ruled this mountain for ten years. They never conquered the yetis here?" Grede asked.

"Conquer?" The priest coughed. "You can conquer a kingdom, but can you conquer the wild wolves of Khaz Modan?"

"So you think yetis are a kind of beast," Grede said.

"Snow yetis and wild wolves are certainly different," the priest said. "But compared to us, the difference between them might not be all that great."

"But from what I know," Grede said, shifting his backside closer to the priest with curiosity, "the ancestors of humans and yetis were ancient giants who lived in Tirisfal and Arathi."

"Nonsense." The priest sat up. "Snow yetis are not only covered head to toe in hair, they grow ram-like horns on their heads. The air they breathe out can freeze your hair solid… Humans don't possess a single one of those traits — especially not the horns and that near-magical frost-breath."

The dwarf said, "You gained wisdom, and your relatives gained stronger, taller bodies that let them adapt to the snow mountains and the wilds. That's not strange at all. It's just like me and my Dark Iron relatives — we're tall, they've got dark skin…"

The priest sighed in exasperation. "Have you heard of Emperor Thoradin, Wildhammer dwarf?"

The dwarf said, "Of course I have!"

The priest said, "When Emperor Thoradin fought his decisive battle against Ignatius on this mountain, he wielded the holy sword Doomgard and won final victory. This sword, also known as the Sword of the Giants, was a legendary blade forged by the giants who once walked the Arathi. And snow yetis — even to this day — have never forged a single true weapon. Could it be that as the giants evolved over generations, they became more and more stupid?"

Grede nodded. "That's a fair point… But change doesn't necessarily mean becoming stronger. For example, those giants who became short became you lot, and who became stupid became yetis, and so on."

"Ah… dwarf."

The priest sighed. He looked like he'd given up on this topic.

"Still, the reason yetis are still here isn't because the humans here never drove them off. It's because the Kingdom of Alterac… no longer exists. Yetis, wild men — they've all come back here. I hear orcs have also taken the opportunity to hide in the mountains. Some people might still believe this kingdom endures, but from what I've seen, the city of Alterac is on the verge of collapse. After the war, more than two-thirds of the people left — heading to the surrounding towns and villages, to Lordaeron, or farther places, like Elwynn and Gilneas."
