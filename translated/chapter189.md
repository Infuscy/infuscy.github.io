## Chapter 189: The Test of the Light

"So you've been to the city of Alterac?" Jace asked.

"Of course," the priest said. "After the war, I often traveled to this city with other priests, wanting to use our abilities to do something for the people here. But every time I went, it was more dilapidated than before. Clearly, believers running on nothing but passion aren't enough to save a kingdom. If a house goes unoccupied for a long time, even without any outside force deliberately destroying it, it will bit by bit decay, bit by bit collapse, until it becomes a pile of ruins. A city is the same, only more so — the impact of that kind of change hits you far harder than a single house would. A kingdom even more so."

"I admire your devotion, priest," Jace said quietly. "I believe that no matter how things turn out in the end, the people of Alterac won't forget your help."

The priest smiled. "If I may be frank — are you a mage?"

"More or less. I'm still an apprentice," Jace said.

"I sense an unsettling aura about you. I don't know if you're aware of that yourself," the priest said. "If you'll allow it, I can take a look — see if there's any hidden danger inside you."

"How?" Jace asked warily.

"The Light can see many things, sir," the priest said.

Hearing that, Grede turned his head and looked over with curiosity.

Jace said, "I was injured back in Dalaran. Burned by black dragon fire, and a death knight… My shoulder was stabbed by a death knight. If there's residue from these dark things lingering inside me, will it be painful?"

"As long as your heart believes in the Light," the priest said.

Oh, that just made it worse.

Jace glanced around at the priests in the cave. They were either already lying down asleep, or reading. Only this corner had any sound of conversation left.

If he planned to keep living in the human world, the Light was something he couldn't avoid. Jace knew that well enough. He might as well use this chance to see exactly what he was made of.

"Then I thank you, priest," he said.

The priest shook his head, placed his hand on Jace's right shoulder, and slowly closed his eyes.

Then he began to recite a prayer in a low voice: "O Light, who gazes from on high — this young scholar awaits your descent. Bear witness, amid chaos and darkness, to your guidance."

"Let him see clearly the invisible cage he is bound by, and grant him a steadfast mind, supreme resolve, and noble courage to struggle against all chaos and corruption… By the Light above, may your power manifest in my thoughts and my soul. May my mind align with your will, so that in your name I may deliver this young man from the high walls of anguish, suffering, confusion, and wrath. May the Light shelter the path forward for us all."

Grede listened from the side, mouth slightly agape, his one eye unblinking, watching intently. Jace couldn't tell what he was thinking.

Jace listened to the priest's words, and for a moment he couldn't think about anything at all.

Even when Teron Gorefiend had swung his warhammer and come at him while he was pinned to the wall, he'd still been thinking about how he could manage to stay alive after taking a hit, how he could find a way to fight back.

But while the priest prayed in that calm, still way, his mind went completely blank.

Only then did he notice that the priest's palm was giving off a gentle glow. The nearby priests opened their eyes and looked over, murmuring something — as if praying alongside the priest before them.

Suddenly, Jace felt a stabbing pain in his shoulder. He glanced at the priest. The priest's eyes were still closed, as if he hadn't felt a thing.

The stabbing pain was followed by a familiar burning sensation that spread from his shoulder to his heart. He didn't want the priest to notice anything wrong, so he could only grit his teeth and bear it.

This pain wasn't as intense as when he'd been treated back in Stormwind, but it was getting close to the edge of what Jace could keep quiet about.

The Alterac snowstorms were far more violent at night than during the day.

It screamed and howled outside the cave, as if urgently reminding the fragile living things resting inside just who the master of this place was.

Fortunately, just before Jace couldn't take it anymore, the priest stopped his touch and withdrew his hand.

Jace tried to relax his jaw and breathed in two slow breaths of icy air, which dulled the burning heat in his chest.

"Strange," the priest said. "The Light hasn't told me there's anything wrong with you…"

Jace knew full well that he was a shadow caster, and that he carried some power seized from the black dragon and shadow inside him. He'd even undergone the so-called "shadow-ification" earlier. And he'd just been in that much pain… How could the priest not have felt a thing? It was impossible.

"If there were a problem, what would the Light tell you?" he asked.

"It would reveal the corruption and disease you've suffered," the priest said. "But even though you yourself say you've been harmed by dark things, I didn't sense the response the Light should have given. Perhaps you've already recovered, and what I detected was only some unimportant residue. Or perhaps my understanding of the Light simply isn't deep enough."

"Isn't deep enough?" Grede asked, surprised.

"We are still learning everything about the Light," the priest said.

Jace touched the sweat on his hairline. A chill that made his scalp tighten spread through him, and it slowly calmed him down from that intense burning pain.

Looking at the priest, he asked a question he'd always been curious about.

"Priest, did you know there were walking dead in Dalaran?"

"The dead raised by death knights — of course I know about them."

"Is the Light's power very effective against these undead?"

"Of course," the priest said. "Warriors who believe in the Light are almost all driven by a righteous hatred, and the Light naturally answers their call — searing the flesh of these undead, purging the corruption that drives them." Jace swallowed and said, "So let's say an undead with free will appeared. What if he revered the Light — say, he was a devoted Church priest in life, and after death he didn't betray his faith. Would the Light descend upon him again?"

"If he truly holds to it, why wouldn't it?" the priest countered.

Jace was a little surprised. The priest hadn't even questioned why an undead creature would believe in the Light first.

"Then when the Light descends, wouldn't these undead Light believers be seared by it?"

"I imagine they would," the priest said. "But the Light would also answer their prayers. I suppose that would be the difficult part of being such an undead believer, sir. They would need a stronger will and a more powerful body than we do to carry out their faith. And the Light would only answer them more fiercely. I imagine that in the end, they would find a redemption more real than any living person's, through pain and rebirth."

"Of course, this is only a conjecture based on my own understanding of the Light. It's hard for me to picture exactly what a Light-worshipping undead would look like. Actually, hearing myself say this, I almost envy the kind of existence you've imagined."

Jace thought: this guy is something else.

If his guess was right, then those undead priests in the days to come would be plenty tough too.

Still, the discussion with the priest had cleared up a small question Jace had about his own encounter with the Light just now. As Jace understood it, when the priest had been examining the so-called darkness inside him, it must have been his shadow-touched part that got burned.

But this was only the Light's own nature causing him harm. The Light hadn't been triggered into a violent reaction by true hostility — it had simply, in the course of existing, collided with an energy it could barely tolerate.

In a way, it was a little similar to an undead priest's experience of coming into contact with the Light.

Or perhaps this wasn't the so-called "will" of the Light at all, but rather that the priest bore Jace no hostility, and so the Light power he'd summoned had no aggressive edge to it either.

Jace still didn't dare claim he understood the Light. He could only speculate about what had happened, based on his own understanding and the priest's answers.

Either way, he could breathe a little easier.

At least from now on, even if a priest tried to heal his injuries with the Light, he didn't need to worry about getting incinerated to ashes or anything like that.
