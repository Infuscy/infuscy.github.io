## Chapter 190: The City of Alterac

When they set out the next morning, the wind and snow had died down. The whole caravan picked up its pace.

Even though the road was much rougher than at the mountain's foot, everyone was a lot more at ease.

Not long after setting out again following lunch, a black city wall was already standing tall on the cliff in the distance. The wall had a rather terrifying gash in it, as if it had been blown open by some kind of bomb.

"Is that the city of Alterac?" Jace asked, pointing at the wall to Grede beside him.

"That should be it. There aren't any other castles or towns around here." Grede looked a while longer, then added, "Did you notice that breach?"

"I saw it," Jace said.

Grede said, "Look — there's no sign of any repair work up there at all. No scaffolding, no workers, no guards on watch."

He was right. Stormwind had been nearly overturned in the war, but by the time Jace had arrived, most of the damaged areas had been swarmed with people. Even when reconstruction wasn't actively underway, there were always people of every kingdom's accent on hand to assess the repair work or draw up plans.

In short, it had been a scene of a city rising from the ashes.

The city of Alterac was a completely different story. Gazing at it from a distance, those towers, those high walls — they looked no different from the ruins in the game. And in the game, Alterac had already been fully abandoned for over a decade and occupied by ogres.

Was this the price of betrayal?

During the Orc Wars, King Aiden Perenolde, seeing the Horde's army winning battle after battle and fearing defeat would bring upon him the same fate that had befallen Stormwind, had chosen to collude with the Horde — letting the Horde pass through the Alterac mountain passes to strike north into Westfall, and even Tirisfal.

After the Horde was driven back, the Stromgarde army, which had been locked in a quiet rivalry with Alterac before the war, had swiftly seized the city of Alterac under the banner of suppressing a revolt.

Just as Perenolde had wished, the city's walls hadn't been touched by orcs or ogres. But they had been razed by allies within a matter of days.

Afterward, no one in the entire Alliance was willing to help the heir to the Perenolde family that Terenas II had put forward rebuild the city.

Not to mention, the city was currently occupied by Stromgarde's Torbain. If the Lordaeron-backed heir Oriden Perenolde didn't take the throne, it would be annexed by the Kingdom of Stromgarde.

The nations of the Alliance were unwilling to see the descendants of a traitor receive aid. And they were even less willing to help the geographical heir of the Arathi Empire ascend the Alterac peaks once more — it almost carried the weight of fate, or an omen.

By evening, when the caravan approached the gates of Alterac, Jace saw those red banners fluttering atop the city walls. A large number of Stromgarde soldiers were gathered at the gate, dressed in heavy military uniforms, clustered in groups, subjecting every caravan and traveler that arrived to strict inspection.

At the same time, Jace could also see the occasional small group of travelers leaving. Some of them might have been heading out on errands. But some of them, perhaps, would never come back once they left.

Grede jumped off the wagon and promptly fell face-first into the snow. He brushed the slush off his face, stood up with a curse, and shouted toward Jace, "It's bloody freezing!"

"Don't you live up on Aerie Peak?" Jace asked. "No snow up there?"

"The Hinterlands are a damn sight warmer than this." Grede picked up his luggage and bundles one by one, then slung his hammer back over his shoulder. "It's not just cold here — it's dreary. Feels like nobody lives here!"

The moment he said that, a whole group of people nearby glanced over. Jace quietly reminded him, "Watch what you say…"

Still, what Grede said wasn't exactly wrong. Jace peered through the city gate — even just along the main street that led straight in from the entrance, it felt like there were barely any people at all.

Of course, it was cold, and it had already gotten dark. But for a kingdom's capital, having this few people on the main street by the city gate was beyond ridiculous.

The Kingdom of Alterac, despite once boasting the most formidable army of the seven kingdoms, had never been a large nation.

Dalaran aside, Alterac was the smallest of the other six kingdoms, in both population and territory. And Alterac was far from as wealthy as Dalaran, nor did it carry the same weight in influence.

Being abandoned by the other nations after the war meant that this place's decline was already a foregone conclusion.

"Did you hear what those travelers on the road were saying?" Jace said, leaning close to Grede. "They said all that's left here are old people who can't relocate, crippled veterans, and abandoned women and children. Anyone who could make it out over the mountains alone has mostly left. I remember you said earlier it wasn't as bad as you'd imagined, but take a look…"

Grede glanced into the city and said, "I got played too. Back in Stormwind there was this Stromgarde fellow who said he was going off to serve in Alterac, said it was cool and the scenery was lovely. I thought they'd turned it into some kind of resort."

The two of them chatted as they walked further in. Suddenly, a wild wolf darted right past them in front of their eyes, glanced at the people on the road, gave Jace a look, and then swaggered off into the distance without a care.

Following the direction the wolf had run, Jace saw, in the hazy distance, a stretch of burned-out houses mixed in with a few that were still more or less intact. But one thing was certain — neither kind was occupied anymore.

That said, the street wasn't entirely deserted either. In the corners, at the alley mouths, people huddled in dark, heavy clothing so thick you couldn't tell male from female, young from old, watching the outsiders coming through the gate.

Grede and Jace walked one after the other to the front of an inn, about to go in, when Jace suddenly realized someone was trailing right behind him.

The person was dressed in thick clothes and moved without making a sound. Grede had walked in on his own, and Jace hadn't been able to shake the tail.

"Hello, sir." It was a woman.

"Er, hello."

"Are you from Lordaeron?" The woman moved closer. Jace noticed her face was red from the cold.

"Yes."

"Want to try the warmth of the Alterac snow mountains? Just two silver."

"Sorry…" Jace said helplessly.

"One silver then, Tirisfal sir." She pulled open her collar, revealing a jutting collarbone traced with purple veins.

Only then did Jace get a clear look at her skin and features. She wasn't as old as he'd thought — it was just that her voice sounded hoarse, like someone well into her years.

But looking at how starving-thin she was, what kind of mood could he possibly have for that sort of thing?

Besides, back in Stormwind he hadn't dared go near women in that line of work. Let alone here.

Jace shook his head again. "We've still got a long way to go, miss. And I'm not exactly a rich man."

"Could you take me with you?" She took another step. "I want to go down the mountain. I'll do anything… sir, I can read, I can do laundry, I can even translate a little Thalassian. I really can."

With that, she immediately said several sentences in Thalassian. Jace didn't understand a single one of them, but it really did sound like Thalassian.

"Or just give me something to eat, my lord," she said quietly. "Please."

Even though she was saying please, her tone was absolutely ironclad.

Jace glanced at her from the corner of his eye and saw her staring fixedly at him.

"All right. Come inside with me. I'll get my pack."

She nodded and followed Jace to a corner of the inn.

Jace opened his bundle, took out a pastry, and handed it to her. Seeing those fingers like withered branches, covered in sores, he couldn't help but take out a silver coin as well.

The moment she pocketed the silver, her hand went straight for his belt. Jace grabbed her hand at once. "Don't — don't. Just stay here a while. Eat, then go."

"You paid. I won't take it for free."

"Then give the money back to me right now," Jace said, holding out his hand.

The woman glared at him. She didn't say anything else, just quietly put the pastry in her mouth, her other hand rubbing that silver coin the whole time.

Jace stood up. He thought, why does this woman have to be so damn proud? She'd have been better off finding somewhere to give him a whole session, instead of putting on this act of being a good person.

Though the inn still had quite a few people in it, nobody was paying any attention to what was happening over here. Jace went back to Grede's side and had just sat down when the woman appeared beside him at some point and gently kissed Jace on the forehead. Then she pushed open the door and fled the inn.

Jace was stunned for a moment. Grede just sighed — whether from helplessness or something else, it was hard to say.

"She actually knows a little Thalassian," Jace said. "If she went to Dalaran she might be able to find work. It's less than three days' journey — she could tag along with a merchant caravan and get out."

"I don't know," Grede said. "Maybe she's afraid to die. Look at those frozen corpses on the road. But it's hard to say, Jace. Everyone has their own hardships."

Jace knew Grede was right. Things he knew couldn't be expected to be clear to everyone. Things he found simple couldn't possibly be simple for everyone. He was really just feeling a bit anxious on that woman's behalf.

Grede went on, "A young woman with no ability to defend herself — if it were you, would you dare go wandering outside with a caravan of travelers on your own? The only reason she dared come up to you is because you look like a good person. It shows she's got a good eye for people, Jace — she nails it every time. You're an out-and-out pushover, that's what you are. I figure if she'd spoken a few words of orcish, you really would have taken her with you."

"How am I a pushover?" Jace cleared his throat and said in a low voice. "I know so much dark magic — I'm absolutely a dangerous character."

"A dangerous character who's a pushover through and through," Grede said.

With that, he leaned in and asked quietly, "Jace, you haven't ever been kissed by a woman other than your own mother, have you?"

"Yes!" Jace said firmly.

Grede pinched Jace's face and said, "Didn't expect you to look like such a slut on the outside but be so proper on the inside. Weren't you talking about messing around with trolls the other day? And now a human kisses you and your face gets hot enough to fry meat."

Jace swatted Grede's hand away. "Let go! It's just the cold."

He glanced back at the door. Aside from the wind and snow that blew past now and then, there was no one left outside.

A man in a heavy cloak walked to the door, slammed it shut with a bang, muttered something in complaint, and went back to the kitchen behind the inn.
