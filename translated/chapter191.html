Chapter 191: Remarkable Results!

The inn at Alterac had plenty of patrons, but nobody was in much of the mood for chatting or joking. There were only hushed conversations here and there, and even newcomers walking through the door drew no attention at all.

Ever since they'd come in, there had been an intermittent slap-slap-slap from upstairs. Jace hadn't paid it any mind at first, until Grede clapped him on the shoulder and pointed upward. Jace glanced up and caught a pair of thick, pale thighs draped over the stairs — with someone behind them, going at it hard.

But aside from the two of them, nobody else seemed interested in what was happening on the stairs. Probably because up in the snow mountains, everyone cut some slack for any means of keeping warm.

Jace walked up to the counter and ordered two large mugs of ale — one pale ale, one rye — then brought them back to the table. He slid the rye over to Grede. "Here."

"Ah, you actually remembered our standing arrangement about buying rounds without me having to remind you." Grede nodded in satisfaction. "You want a favor, don't you?"

"I do, Grede," Jace said.

The dwarf took a long pull, squinted his eyes shut, and said, "Mother, that's cold."

"I didn't order it iced," Jace said.

"Obviously…" Grede muttered. "All right, what's the favor?"

Jace said, "There's a new spell I need to learn, but I've never managed to get all the way through casting it from start to finish. The thing is, it's easy to mess up — like, say, burn too much mana and pass out. Tonight I need you to keep an eye on me. If I black out, pour a mana potion down my throat immediately."

He pulled a bottle from his pack as he said it. "This one. It'll soften the damage when I come to and help me recover faster."

"Sounds simple enough," Grede said.

"It is simple, but there's no way I can pull it off alone." Jace said, "I've wanted to try again ever since we left Stormwind, but I didn't get around to making my first real attempt until today. I've already got the incantation memorized cold — I just need to get through one complete casting."

"Then let me finish my ale first."

"This round's it."

"So that was the plan. No, this one doesn't count — when we get back to Stormwind, you're buying me a proper round."

"Fine."

After they'd eaten and drunk their fill, they booked a room. Alterac City was already in shambles, but for some reason the cost of lodging was a lot more expensive than Southshore's.

Then again, that was probably just what it cost to stay in a kingdom's capital. Jace had never actually stayed at an inn in a capital city before — he'd always either rented a place or slept in a tent.

Jace looked at the cramped little room and said, "We need to take fire precautions. Our luggage should go under my cloak."

"Fire?" Grede asked, puzzled. "What, like fireballs? You planning to set something off in this tiny room? That's a serious crime, lad. I'm not going along with that."

"It's not any kind of offensive spell, Grede!" Jace said. "It's Mage Armor — a protective spell that layers magical resistance onto your body. No offensive capability. It requires channeling the elements, so if something goes wrong it could start a fire. But don't worry — the first time I actually cast it, I didn't start a fire. I'm a lot more practiced with the incantation now than I was back then."

"When it comes to your magic, you're the expert," Grede said. "But if you're that practiced, why bother fireproofing the luggage?"

"In case something goes wrong." Jace wrapped his cloak around the luggage as he spoke. "If even a single stray spark gets out, we'll be fine — but what if it burns something important?"

"So I just sit here and watch you?"

"Pretty much. If you see anything like a little flame, put it out for me. I need to maintain my concentration to cast, so I can't help you. If I lose focus… then it'll cause even worse mana drain, and that'll end in failure. Might even trigger other problems I can't predict."

"I think I've got the picture." Grede folded his arms and nodded. "You can start. I'm ready."

"Good."

Jace caught himself slipping into Master Marin's tone out of habit. He spread out his notes on the Mage Armor casting process across the table in front of him, cast Arcane Intellect, took a deep breath, and drank about half a mana potion.

Grede sat bolt upright on the bed like a temple monk, eyes locked on Jace's every move.

"Are you done?" he asked.

"I haven't started yet."

Jace shot him a look, then stood in the middle of the room and mimicked Master Marin's opening gesture. A sharp pain lanced through his shoulder. He frowned — something told him this wasn't going to go smoothly.

But the moment that thought surfaced, he forced it back down. Because every time he'd believed he could do it, the spell had turned out surprisingly well; and every time he'd started worrying, the result had always been worse than what he'd worried about.

"Lokpotorie…"

Jace slowly closed his eyes. With Grede helping out this time, he planned to focus every scrap of his attention on the casting and let everything else fall away.

Last time had been his first attempt — he'd been distracted by curiosity at all the phenomena that arose when arcane energy manipulated the elements. Now he had no reason to be curious. Other than completing the cast, he didn't need to think about anything else.

"Ingrukoo…"

Jace continued his gestures, concentrating on feeling the arcane energy swirling around him while fluently reciting the incantation from memory. But soon he began to hear Grede softly going "Ow… aah… ouch… hey-ya!"

What the hell?

Jace didn't dare open his eyes. That kind of noise was already starting to disrupt his concentration. Until the spell was complete, or until Grede actually cried for help, he had no intention of abandoning the cast.

This time, he had to finish it!

"…Itaxii, bediinkatu, nazha-zoon!"

Jace snapped his eyes open. In that instant, all the arcane energy — along with the various elements it had swept in — converged at once like a burst of prismatic light slamming into his body.

And yet the impact brought no harm at all. Jace felt as though he'd been hit by blasts of air, some cold and some hot. The prismatic light shattered at random, forming glimmering crystals that settled onto his clothes, his neck, even his chin, and began slowly creeping up toward his face…

Then Jace felt a sharp pain in his shoulder. He clutched his arm — in his trance-like focus during the cast, he'd swung his arm without even noticing the pain.

But seeing the magical effect he'd produced, the pain didn't bother him quite so much.

The crystals' iridescent shimmer didn't dissipate. Instead, it gradually dimmed, forming drifting motes of colored light that floated above his clothes and skin.

Just as he was marveling at it, he noticed Grede had somehow already retreated into the corner.

"What… happened to you?"

"I didn't do anything!" Grede raised both hands.

"I'm asking why you're hiding in the corner…"

Grede gestured wildly with both hands. "Just now I suddenly saw some arcane energy — like little stars — flying in from everywhere, with red ones, yellow ones, green ones… things like flames. I couldn't tell what they were. They were swirling around you, following your hands in circles. A few of them landed on me — not flames, I don't know what they were! They hurt like hell! Crackling and glowing like the lightning off a knight's hammer. I had no choice but to get as far away as I could…"

Natural energy, maybe. Perhaps Jace's lack of finesse during the cast had caused a leak of natural force. But that was normal enough.

"So does that mean it's started?" Grede asked again.

"It's already finished," Jace said.

"Finally… finally!" Grede exclaimed. "That really scared the hell out of me just now. I thought the whole place was going to burn down. There's so little space in here — for all that energy you were throwing around, it was way too cramped. Nowhere to hide!"

"Then… next time we'll find somewhere more spacious."

Jace hadn't yet mastered the technique Master Marin had described — dialing back the intensity to produce a weaker version of Mage Armor. He just wanted to complete a full cast while he was at his best. As for whether he could scale the spell down to make Mage Armor more practical for real combat, he could work on optimizing that later in his Protection studies…

He just hadn't expected it to go this well!

Jace hadn't felt the slightest bit of fatigue during the cast. Only after it was complete did he feel the relief of finally being done.

Actually, that was itself a sign of excessive mana drain — just not to the point of near-depletion. As Jace cast more and more spells, he was getting familiar with what each different sensation meant for his mental state. It helped him keep track of his mana bar.

Jace knew the reason this cast had gone so smoothly had to be the Sunwell water in that flask, which had given him a significant boost to his energy.

But in the mana-source-related texts he'd read back in the library, the elves hadn't mentioned that even a tiny amount of Sunwell water could boost one's energy — or rather, raise one's mana capacity. They'd only said that carrying the flask on your person greatly benefited energy recovery. So why did he get this kind of effect?

Actually, Jace had already guessed at one possibility: the elves were born of the arcane and had lived under the Sunwell's radiance for ages, so whether noble or ranger, they all possessed mana far beyond most other races.

Mana growth probably had some kind of diminishing-returns effect, so even if they drank Sunwell water, they couldn't feel any obvious improvement — only its astonishing recovery effect. After all, the Sunwell was nothing more than a flask of Well of Eternity water poured into a spring. The magical power the Quel'dorei enjoyed now was nowhere near what their ancient ancestors had known beside the Well of Eternity, in Zin-Azshari.

Whatever the case… that flask of water had indeed given him — a human with a rather short mana bar — a remarkably noticeable boost. The last time, he'd started feeling fatigued halfway through casting Mage Armor. Now that he'd finished the whole thing, he felt like he might just have enough left to try it again!

But he knew, rationally, that he couldn't manage another full cast. Still, this already thrilled him beyond words.
