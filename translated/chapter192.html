Chapter 192: A Way to Blow Off Steam

The mountain journey had been surprisingly uneventful, which Jace found a little unexpected. Probably because the Kingdom of Stromgarde had stationed a heavy military presence here to consolidate its hold on the region. From Alterac City all the way to Durnholde Keep, just as Grede had said, there were red-coated guards and cavalry everywhere.

During the day, looking down from the mountains, you could see a slash of discordant red in the snowfields and river valleys at regular intervals — each one a Stromgarde camp or waystation.

In the morning they'd followed the patrol routes of the red-coated cavalry down the mountain road, and by evening they'd reach the waystation on the Coldwind River bank. From there, following the Coldwind River upstream for about three days, you'd reach the landmark on Lordaeron's eastern frontier — Durnholde Keep.

Durnholde Keep was so massive that Jace could hardly believe it was just a single keep. Of course, Lordaeronians generally agreed that Fenris Keep was the most magnificent castle in the entire Kingdom of Lordaeron — it's just that Fenris Keep sat on the continent's largest lake, Lake Lordamere, and the vast expanse of Silverpine Forest covering the entire western shore, plus the even greater Lordaeron capital in the distance, made it look like a child trying to break free from its father's embrace and dash for the grasslands to the west.

Durnholde, on the other hand, stood alone on the boundless riverland — the only towering thing in sight, with the mountains far off at the edge of the horizon, nothing but hazy silhouettes. The whole keep looked like a mountain giant dozing off as it sat between the streams.

The calm, broad Coldwind River was the largest of the rivers on this plain. It connected the continent's second-largest lake, Lake Darrowmere, to Baradin Bay, and served as the dividing line of the lowlands. This place had been called the Lowlands long ago, in contrast to the Arathi Highlands to the east, which stretched far farther than the widely known Hillsbrad — and that was Stromgarde's territory.

After the Lordaeronians seized Durnholde, they designated the area as Eastern Hillsbrad, but the highlanders stubbornly insisted on calling it the Lowlands, or even Arathi Lowlands, or Arathi Foothills — and to this day, they still hadn't settled on a name everyone could agree on.

But Durnholde's soldiers had a very simple name for this river plain: "down below."

Jace had heard his father mention "down there" since he was a child. He'd always assumed his father meant Tirisfal, outside the royal city.

So the time his father mentioned that Arathi's red-coated cavalry had gone "down there" again, Jace had actually thought the Stromgarde of this timeline was preparing a surprise attack on the Lordaeron capital.

The village beneath Durnholde Keep looked even livelier than Alterac City. When Jace and Grede walked into the inn that evening, it was like stepping back into Southshore.

Humans of every stripe, speaking in every regional accent, sang winter songs in every key. Travelers dressed as oddly as Jace and Grede didn't feel out of place in the slightest.

Jace hadn't even known there was a village half the size of Southhore beneath Durnholde, let alone that it was this bustling.

The moment he walked into the inn, he looked around curiously and asked, "Why are there so many people here? Wasn't this place hit hard during the war? Even if there are gladiator matches…"

Grede leaned back in his chair and said, "When there's money, everything changes fast. Even a Gurubashi star came here for an exhibition — you forgot?"

"Ah, right," Jace said. "I wonder if that panther's dead yet."

Grede pressed his lips together and said, "That panther's probably never fought an orc in its life. I'd say the odds aren't good."

Just then a waitress approached and asked, "What can I get you two?"

Grede said, "One large mug of goat milk for each of us. From an Alterac goat. I've been wanting to try it."

"What are you doing?" Jace asked.

"What do you mean, what am I doing?" Grede shot back.

"Why are you drinking goat milk?"

"Because it tastes good."

"I mean, why aren't you drinking ale?"

"Because I don't feel like it right now."

"Fine. You've been acting strange ever since we left Alterac City," Jace said. "If something's on your mind, just tell me. Don't bottle it up."

"I'm not you," Grede said. "I don't have a problem."

"You don't have a problem," Jace sighed. "Right."

"Then make both of them goat-milk ale!" the dwarf bellowed.

Jace shot him a look and decided not to push it. He just spread out his notes on the Mage Armor incantation and casting procedure across the table and kept on memorizing.

Before long, the group at the next table started talking about the arena.

From their conversation, Jace learned there was a practice match to watch tonight. Tickets were seventeen silver, and a whole group of them were debating whether to spend the money to watch for the night or just wait until next week for the real matches.

Listening to these people talk about Durnholde's pit fights, Jace itched to go, of course.

Because he knew who was living inside there right now — the future Warchief who would rebuild the entire Horde.

But when he thought about how the kid was probably just a real brat right now, even younger than Arthas by several years, the excitement kind of fizzled.

After all, back in the royal city, Jace had managed to see Arthas and Jaina in person — but those two really were just kids barely into their teens. There was no star-struck satisfaction in looking at them.

Arthas might look every inch a prince, but there was no connecting him to the young prince of later years, or the death knight of the Scourge, or the Lich King.

And Jaina was even more so — aside from that head of pale blonde hair that had a hint of her, she was entirely the look of a grade-schooler who hadn't started growing yet.

Maybe he'd transmigrated a little too early. Since coming to this world, the heroes who'd given him that "game" feeling seemed to be limited to Alleria Windrunner. And Wrynn and Kel'Thuzad could count as half. Then again, getting in required a seventeen-silver ticket, and it was only a practice match at that. Even if they got in, there was no guarantee they'd actually see whoever they'd come to see.

Jace figured the future Warchief of the Horde was probably still in basic training right now — no way a kid that young would be risking injury in the arena.

Across from him, Grede was drinking his sour, tangy goat-milk ale one sip at a time without saying a word. Jace, who couldn't quite get used to the smell, just watched him drink.

But in the end, he brought it up himself. "Hey, you think we should go watch the matches inside the walls tonight?"

"What matches?" the dwarf looked up.

"The pit fights, obviously. What else? But it's just a practice match, so it might not be that exciting."

"Sure! I've been pretty bored anyway."

Seeing how interested Grede was, Jace figured it might help lift his spirits, so he stopped hesitating.

"Let's go, then," he said.

"Does it cost money?"

"My treat!"

"Hah, now you're talking!"
