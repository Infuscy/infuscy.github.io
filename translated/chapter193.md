## Chapter 193: A Real Gladiator

The two of them followed the stone road all the way up toward the gate in the wall.

The place was already packed with people. Plenty of travelers were willing to pay to watch orcs beat each other senseless, and on the road you could already hear people talking about the matches from the past few days — one had featured a fierce Blackrock clan warrior against six Alterac brigands, and the scene had been a slaughterhouse.

At the entrance, Jace gave the ticket-seller a gold coin for two tickets. The man gave him two tokens and over seventy silver in change. Jace cupped the silver in his hands, thinking at least he'd have smaller coins to use later.

He hoped the practice matches would be worth watching — otherwise that was the only consolation he'd have.

In the little room by the entrance, a whole pile of coin purses sat there while several people crouched over them counting money. No wonder this place had grown so prosperous within a year or two after the war. The entertainment industry made money fast.

Once they passed through the small door beneath the main gate, a wave of cheers and screams hit them. The moment Grede heard that sound, the corners of his mouth curled up on their own.

Jace noticed that behind the crowd, the spectators up front were standing on the stone bridge or along the edge of the high wall, shouting down at the dirt yard below.

From his memory of the game, he roughly recalled the basic layout of this place. He looked up and spotted Durnholde Keep itself — that towering mass of architecture sat at the highest point behind ring after ring of towering walls, looking down over everything. The whole keep was ablaze with torchlight, like a gray palace.

"Hey, Jace, over here!"

Grede had already rushed to the front, too impatient to wait. He shoved several spectators aside and squeezed right up to the front, and Jace followed in his wake, benefiting from the dwarf's barbaric tactics to reach the front line in one go.

Just then Jace heard the sound of bones snapping. He grabbed the railing and looked down — a green-skinned orc was being dragged off the floor.

A trail of broken black marks was left beneath him. It didn't look like blood, and it certainly didn't look like green fel blood — more like a smear of mottled ink, unpleasant to look at.

"Filthy cur, ha!" a spectator beside them couldn't help cursing.

Then another seven or eight orcs carrying clubs walked out onto the floor. They didn't salute the surrounding crowd the way Jace remembered gladiator champions doing before a match — they just charged straight at each other and started brawling.

Grede raised his eyebrows and shouted back at Jace, "Last time I saw this many orcs at once… I still had both my eyes!"

One of the orcs was the first to be beaten down. He lay on the ground howling something, and the crowd only shouted louder. Jace seemed to be the only one there who could understand what he was yelling.

"You maniacs… you bunch of maniacs…"

The downed orc couldn't get back up before several fully armored human knights charged in and dragged him off. The moment those humans appeared, the orcs didn't seem all that enthusiastic about fighting each other anymore — they just stared at those humans until both they and the unconscious orc had disappeared at the edge of the floor, and only then did the brawling resume.

That scene made Jace feel a little worried for the master of this keep. No wonder it would get wiped out by the orc uprising someday.

"Should've given them a sword or an axe and let them fight for their freedom, ha!" a man shouted, and groups of men and women around him took up the cry.

A guard nearby pushed and shoved the agitated crowd and said, "If you want to see somebody die, wait a few days and buy a ticket for the real matches. If you can't afford it, stop yelling your heads off."

"Is this how you treat your honored guests, you Lordaeron bastards?"

"Watch or get out. Who the hell cares about your stinking money, you Redridge hillbilly? Your house rebuilt yet?"

"You rotten maggot! I'll tear your dog mouth open!"

"In a hurry to die? Want to go down there and play with those green-skinned beasts?"

Several soldiers heard the commotion and rushed over with their swords drawn. The whole group immediately tangled into a brawl, and within seconds it was over.

Jace looked at the bloodied man from the Kingdom of Stormwind being pressed to the ground with a sword to his head, and for a moment he didn't quite know how to feel about it.

It was like he'd been cursed twice over, and yet also had the wind taken out of his sails twice on his behalf. Empathizing with either side felt wrong.

While he was still watching the commotion up top, the fight below had already ended. An old orc crouched in the middle of the floor, holding a younger orc and asking him something over and over, but blood was already streaming from the younger one's mouth. He looked badly hurt. Soon a dozen knights dragged them away — the standing and the lying alike, all of them.

These dozen guards were all fully armored, wearing splendid armor and plumed helmets. They looked not much different from the Menethil royal guards in the capital — even more lavish than the noble knights in the moving pictures.

"That was satisfying." Grede grinned. "Enemies though they are, orc fights really are something to watch! These lads are all veterans of the real battles, every one of them vicious. I'm really looking forward to the real matches — shame the timing doesn't work out."

"The real matches might be so expensive you won't even feel like coming in," Jace said offhandedly.

"How much did you spend on the two of us this time?" Grede asked.

"Don't worry about it," Jace said. "Just enjoy watching the orcs fight."

Grede laughed twice and leaned back over the railing. Just then a Hillsbrad wild bear and an Arathi red raptor were dragged onto the floor, and an uproar erupted from the crowd.

Grede started shouting along with them: "Who hasn't seen those things, huh?! Pulled them out just to pad the runtime, have they? Everyone's time is precious, you know."

Actually, Jace had already started getting bored. The orcs' fighting spirit wasn't exactly high — they were more like a bunch of poorly trained, battle-worn defeated soldiers being dragged back onto a battlefield and forced to fight.

Maybe because he understood their language and knew a bit of their culture, Jace couldn't quite see these fellows as mere beasts the way the surrounding spectators did. He had to think back to the villages the orcs had slaughtered and burned in the Kingdom of Stormwind before he could finally find a bit of that "watching the spectacle" feeling again.

Of course, it might also have been because it was his money. That much silver — he felt like he ought to be seeing something more interesting.

Just as he was getting more and more bored, another roar came from the arena on the other side of the bridge.

"Let's go have a look over there, Jace!" Grede said. "This side looks like they're bringing out beasts again… Look, they're even trotting out a mountain lion to fill time! I could jump down there and fight four of them myself. What's the point of watching that, honestly!"

"Then let's go," Jace agreed. Grede grabbed him and pushed through a stretch of guards and spectators. Jace was already starting to feel the sting of the silver he'd handed over.

The spectators on this side were clearly watching more intently, and quite a few people had also rushed over from the other side along with them, making this area more and more crowded. Jace squeezed to the front with effort and looked down — there were four orcs brawling on the floor right now.

No, three orcs ganging up on one.

There were already several orcs lying on the ground howling. Most of the practice matches here were free-for-alls, no teams — which meant the remaining three had figured out who the toughest one was and decided to team up and take him first. You could tell the orc they were all targeting was younger and more confident. He was smaller than the other orcs, but both his eyes and his movements were distinctly different.

Jace watched him as he constantly shifted his gaze between his opponents, judging their positions, his footwork adjusting nonstop… the way he held his wooden club felt more human than orc.

One of the three orcs couldn't hold back and suddenly attacked — but the young orc sidestepped and brought his club down squarely on the back of the orc's knee, sending him to the ground. By the time the other two charged in, it was already too late.

A burst of low growls and the *clack-clack-clack* of wooden clubs — Jace, shoved by the crowd around him, hadn't even managed to see clearly how the three of them fought, and it was already over!

The young orc took a few steps back and looked up at the surrounding spectators. Jace thought he looked exactly like a real gladiator — not some pitiful slave forced to fight after being captured here!

But as if just to slap Jace across the face, a great many of the spectators started shouting: "Thrall! Thrall! Thrall!"

And with all that shouting, the orc only grew prouder, beginning to bow and play along with the crowd's chants…

"This lad's incredible! How's he so strong? The other orcs clearly can't keep up with his rhythm!" Grede shouted excitedly.

"I'll tell you something even more incredible, dwarf," a gnome beside them said with a grin. "You know how old he is?"

"How old? Fifteen?"

"Eight!"

"The hell do you mean, eight?!"

"Blackmoore's still got plenty of money to make…"

Amid the chorus of "Thrall!" being shouted over and over, Jace gradually tuned out of the Common he'd grown completely accustomed to.

In the Common spoken with an Alterac-region accent, "thrall" was how you pronounced the word for "slave."
