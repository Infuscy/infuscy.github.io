Chapter 194: Thanks to the Trollbanes

That was Thrall — the future leader and prophet of the orcs on the Lordaeron continent.

Right now, not yet ten years old, he was already nearly the size of an adult human man, with shoulders even broader.

Every movement, every shout of his seemed born for battle. He'd been forged by the master of this place into a killing machine.

Facing the roaring tide of the crowd, Thrall threw his wooden club to the ground and raised both hands, shouting his own name along with them again and again.

Unlike those other orcs, his eyes held only the thrill of combat — pure enjoyment.

He genuinely wanted to become a powerful gladiator, not escape from here to win his freedom.

Because right now, his world was here. His whole life was here. Everyone he knew was within these walls. The lord of Durnholde, Blackmoore, was his father.

Grede noticed Jace staring at that orc without a trace of excitement or thrill. He asked, "What's wrong? Do you know this orc?"

"Do you remember that orc shaman we ran into in Redridge?" Jace asked.

"Of course I remember — how could I forget!" The dwarf looked around for a moment, then asked, "He here?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "But I told him the son of the Frostwolf chieftain was still alive and was right here at Durnholde — that's why he let me go. This young orc might be that Frostwolf chieftain's son."

"So did you actually know, or did you just guess right?" Grede asked again.

Jace said, "How could I actually know? The lord of this keep had been buying and hunting orc slaves for a long time. I figured if that young orc was still alive, he might be here… so he sort of saved both our lives indirectly."

Grede glanced at the distant keep, then at the massive wall behind them, and said, "That orc shaman might already be dead under this place. I can't figure out how a single orc could sneak into this keep."

"I wasn't thinking he'd die here either," Jace said.

Grede clicked his tongue and said, "That bastard beat the hell out of the two of us — he deserves a bit of suffering! Orcs are all the same. But this orc right here, this kid — I can see something different in him, I'll give him that."

He looked down toward the arena. The young Thrall had already been led away from the center.

"He was raised by humans, wasn't he? Speaks the human tongue, moves like a human, fights like a human warrior. But humans will never accept him, and orcs won't treat him as one of their own either. You saw the way those older orc warriors looked at him? No different from how they look at the human guards. The orcs don't consider him one of them at all. I'm telling you, he's got a rough road ahead."

Grede was right. Thrall at this point only thought of himself as a human who'd grown up fast. He lived in a human keep, raised by a human wet nurse, educated by a human foster father, trained by humans, spoke the human language, and bore a human name.

As an eight-year-old, he knew a lot — but not quite enough. He still needed time to figure out who he really was, where he was going, and what he was meant to do.

Jace just knew that no matter how easily identity, life goals, and values could shift under all kinds of influences… race…

That was something Blackmoore perhaps understood, or perhaps hadn't yet realized — but at the very least, he must have felt he could keep Thrall under control.

The arena practice matches went on until midnight, and the crowd never thinned. Aside from travelers stopping here for the night, no shortage of nobles and merchants showed up either.

Jace could see that some people could cross the bridges guarded by soldiers and use the passages up along the walls to reach higher vantage points, watching the matches from a better angle without having to jostle and fight for position with the common spectators below.

But judging by the scale of these so-called practice matches, even though this kind of show was cheaper, the combined ticket revenue might not be any less than the official matches.

After all, all kinds of passersby — anyone who wasn't so poor they couldn't afford a meal — were willing to find an opportunity to come in and take a look, just to see some orcs and broaden their horizons.

To put it unkindly, this place was like the one and only zoo on the entire Lordaeron continent. It wasn't until midnight that Grede, who'd shouted himself hoarse, finally had his fill, and the gloom that had been building in him ever since they'd left Alterac City finally dissipated.

Ever since the snow mountains, Jace had suspected he was feeling that "the closer you get to home, the more nervous you get" kind of emotion.

Seeing that Grede had found a way to let some of that melancholy out, Jace felt relieved too.

The next morning, Jace walked out of the inn and saw a faint blue mountain silhouette at the far edge of the horizon — hazy and indistinct, like a distant patch of dark cloud.

Grede was already standing there, gazing in the same direction.

"What are you looking at?" Jace asked.

"Aerie Peak," Grede murmured. "I'm finally back!"

"That's Aerie Peak?"

"Yeah," Grede said. "The rest of the way we'll have to walk on our own. I just asked around — nobody's heading to the Hinterlands. My kinsmen might all have already made it back to Aerie Peak!"

Jace fixed his gaze on the stretch between those mountains and the rivers spreading out across the plain below, taking in an unbroken expanse of primeval forest that stretched all the way to the far east.

"Are there trolls in this forest?"

"We have the Trollbanes to thank for that — this isn't troll territory," Grede said. "But just in case, we can't afford to let our guard down." Jace looked into the distance again and roughly estimated the distance. At first glance it didn't look far, but if the going through the forest didn't go smoothly, it might take three days to get there.

"Our time's a little tight, Grede. Think about it — today's already the twentieth. Even if we reach Aerie Peak the day after tomorrow without a hitch, stay two or three days, and then head straight for Southshore, we'd barely make it by January fifth… Honestly, I'm not even sure that's enough. Maybe on the way back we should rent a couple of horses at Tarren Mill to pick up the pace — otherwise if we miss the ship, it'll cost a good deal more to find another one."

Grede said, "No need to stay two or three days. I just need to show up at one dinner and let them know Grede Thunderfist isn't dead yet. And I need to bring that gryphon rider's war hammer back and give it to his family, his brothers, or his comrades — to tell them I did my part to avenge him."

Jace nodded and said, "But don't forget Quel'Dani."

"Ah, right, Quel'Dani." Grede scratched his head. "Quel'Dani is north of Aerie Peak, but our city is on the south side of the mountain — which means we'd have to go around the mountain. That's another day and a half. You're right, Jace. I hadn't factored Quel'Dani into the plan, and we spent a bit too much time in Dalaran too."

"Then why'd you take a job that runs all the way through the evening of the thirteenth?" Jace asked.

"Don't bring that up!" Grede waved a hand. "I hit it off pretty well with that employer. But if it turns out Quel'Dani can actually fix my problem, Jace, there'd be a much faster way back to Southshore. Remember what I promised you when I asked you to come to the Hinterlands?"

Jace's eyes gradually lit up. "Of course I remember."
