## Chapter 195: The One We Really Ought to Thank

The vast primeval forest at the foot of Aerie Peak was untouched by foot traffic — even the dwarves who traveled here most often had never really managed to carve out a proper path through it.

But Grede looked perfectly confident, striding forward with firm steps, stepping over giant tree roots and trudging over uneven hillocks carpeted with muck-like layers of rotted leaves.

Jace barely kept pace with the dwarf ahead of him, and could only feel that his leg injury had healed quite a bit.

His shoulder, though, had also felt much better these past two days. Following the Dalaran apothecary's instructions for applying medicine and changing bandages, the wound had healed so fast it almost felt unnatural — like he'd been on some kind of steroid.

He looked up. The canopy of ancient pines and oaks blocked out the sky so completely that even in daytime it was pitch dark.

At night the two of them rested in low-lying caves screened off by dense tangles of tree roots, taking turns staying awake on watch. That night they finally finished off the sweets Jace had packed in Dalaran.

Because time was tight and this forest looked plenty dangerous, Grede decided to push through the night and reach the foot of Aerie Peak as quickly as possible before resting. Jace agreed.

Dwarves weren't built for all-nighters, but the thought of getting ambushed and caved in by trolls was enough to snap him wide awake.

The two of them made it through the night without incident, and by the early hours before dawn, Jace felt the air getting more and more humid.

"Is it going to rain, Grede?" he asked.

Grede had just climbed up onto a raised mound ahead and looked back, saying, "I don't think so. The Hinterlands is just a bit more humid than Hillsbrad, Jace. Nothing strange about that."

Jace looked at Grede — and suddenly caught sight of a terrifying shadow rising up behind him.

"Gre—" He pointed behind him. Grede slowly turned around. The giant silhouette emerged bit by bit from the shade of the pine trees, growing taller and taller, like a swelling gray cloud.

It was a monster over four meters tall. It tilted its head back to stare at the sky, glanced at one tree, then another, swayed its body, and took two steps forward before finally noticing the dwarf at its feet.

What surprised Jace most was that this thing was so enormous, so bloated and swollen — like a walking hill — and yet it made almost no sound when it walked. Only the faint snap of small branches underfoot, barely audible over the sound of the cold wind rushing through the trees.

Jace even suspected he'd gone a whole night without sleep and was hallucinating, or that he'd eaten something off the night before.

But Grede looked up too, eyes wide as saucers — which proved Jace wasn't the only one seeing this.

The dwarf didn't reach for his hammer. He just clutched that round shield, his body frozen in an awkward stance, his hind legs already trembling from the uncomfortable position. Seeing how he was behaving, Jace also rooted himself to the ground and tried his absolute best not to move.

The monster took another step forward and finally revealed its whole body from behind the cliff's edge.

It was gray-white all over, with a fringe of pale brown feathers around its neck. Atop its head sat an exaggerated parrot's head, its upper beak raised high — about the size of a medium shield — trembling up and down with each shake of its head, as if it were sniffing at something.

Behind its head, a pair of antlers like ancient trees spread out to either side, hung with what looked like woven ornaments. Dotted among these ornaments were dead branches and fallen pine needles, and every time it shook its head a few more would break off and drift down.

Was that a wildkin?

Only now did Jace understand why Dalaran's wildkin-feather quills were so cheap. He had a feeling that the feathers this single wildkin shook loose just standing next to Grede would probably last him a whole year.

And it was enormous!

The wildkin glanced down at the mound Grede was standing on, seemed a little afraid to jump straight down, so it picked its way around to a distant slope and made its way down the gentler incline. Along the way it even stumbled once, like a clumsy wild bear.

Its two feet might have looked like eagle talons, but they were so thick and massive that at a glance they more like a lion's — except covered not in fur but in layer after layer of scaly brown hide.

Once it had come down, it shuffled step by step over toward Jace. Grede kept his eyes locked on Jace, shaking his head nonstop — a silent warning not to make any move.

But the wildkin still spotted Jace.

When it drew close, it sniffed at him curiously, as if intrigued, then made a displeased *prrrt* sound.

Jace felt the creature's breath — thick and hot, like an enraged warhorse staring him down at point-blank range.

Its massive wings, tucked against its sides, reached forward slightly, as if wanting to touch Jace — but in the end they never actually made contact.

That was when he could see the vicious talons hidden beneath the feathers, each one longer and thicker than Jace's arm. Jace knew that if he took a single hit from one of those, there wouldn't be enough left to bury.

After all, this thing was more than twice his height. Why were the wildkin here so huge… he didn't remember them being this exaggerated in the game.

Gnoll chieftains, death knights, orc shamans — at this moment they all felt pathetically small. Jace finally understood why the night elves of ancient times had worshipped these creatures as envoys of the gods.

Just when the tension had reached its peak, the wildkin opened its mouth.

"Smells… bad, but…"

It… spoke? And in Common, no less?! And with a rising, questioning inflection?!

After it said that, it wobbled away deeper into the forest, until its silhouette merged once more with the black shade of the trees.

But what?

You could've at least finished the sentence.

Was this a druid in animal form? No — if it were a druid, why wouldn't it change back, and why would it be so clumsy… This had to be a real wildkin.

Just then Grede asked in surprise, "Why'd it talk to you and not me?!"

"So you knew it could talk?!" Jace shot back.

"Of course. Wildkin can obviously talk…" Grede said. "You didn't know?"

"I…" Jace said. "So it has its own intelligence? Then if we mean no harm, why were we so scared?"

"Wildkin are just bad-tempered, that's all, Jace," Grede said. "If you do something strange and get on its bad side and it smacks you one, it probably wasn't trying to kill you — it just has absolutely no concept of how fragile our bodies are."

"But a wildkin showing up is a good thing. Because trolls or other ill-meaning types would never appear anywhere near a wildkin. These massive creatures despise dark magic and malicious hexers… If it seemed to be in a decent mood, that means a wide area around here is clean."

"Uh…" Jace glanced at his own staff and asked, "You sure about that?"

Grede caught Jace's meaning at once and grinned. "A wildkin's a lot better at telling who's bad news than an Alterac woman is!"

"Don't bring that up. Anyway, that scared the hell out of me just now," Jace said. "It's not often I see an animal that big standing right in front of my face."

Grede said, "That one's a big one even for a wildkin. In Dwarvish we call them owl-bears — because even though the thing looks like a bird, its whole body is like a giant bear. Come on, let's move. If there aren't any trolls around… we just need to reach the foot of Aerie Peak fast and we won't have to worry."

After an encounter with a creature that size, the sleepiness had been scared clean out of both of them, and the road ahead didn't feel quite so endless anymore.
