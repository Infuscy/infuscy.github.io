## Chapter 196: The Hinterlands

The stretch of forest between Durnholde and the Hinterlands seemed endless. As Jace trudged along, he started to wonder whether Grede even knew which direction they were headed anymore.

Here, beneath the canopy that hung over the woods like a blanket of cloud, it was impossible to tell which direction the sunlight was coming from high above. The light that managed to filter through only formed scattered patches of shadow and glare.

The scenery had felt beautiful and wondrous at first glance, but after a while it gave him nothing but dizziness and a headache.

That said, the feeling of making his way through these woods was still rather relaxing.

The pine forests of the north were generally quite crisp, carrying just a hint of pleasant moisture — comfortable, not clammy.

If not for the risk of getting lost, it would have been a rather enjoyable woodland hike.

This wasn't Elwynn Forest with its damp, cloying mud and its swarms of nasty, misshapen insects and ants — a nightmare in summer, to say nothing of Duskwood further south, or Stranglethorn Vale.

The rain forest of Stranglethorn was a place that could torture a man to death without a single beast or enemy needing to show up.

The trees grew sparser. Jace followed Grede up a high slope. Ahead lay a stretch of flat, rolling hills; behind them loomed a vast primeval forest far denser than anything they'd walked through so far. Was this the Hinterlands?

And further off, the trees grew taller and taller, darkening like mountains, growing more and more gloomy and sinister, as if they could swallow a man's gaze whole and refuse to let it go.

The tallest colossal trees on the horizon — their hazy blue silhouettes towering and unnerving — made one feel oppressed even from this distance.

Jace nearly thought he'd caught a glimpse of the legendary World Tree.

Of course he knew there was no World Tree in the Hinterlands. That was probably Thaldoran, the forbidden sanctuary guarded by the green dragonflight.

A brick road ahead led northwest, stretching all the way to a sheer cliff that jutted up from the landscape. Countless stone walls, doors, and windows dotted its face. At the very top, a colossal eagle statue — easily sixty or seventy meters tall — was set into the mountainside, its head held high as it gazed into the distance.

"How in the hell did they build that?" Jace asked before he could stop himself.

"Sometimes I think this thing's a bit daft," Grede said, hands on his hips, craning his neck to look up. "But it is beautiful, isn't it?"

"If you dwarves ever decided to be practical about your buildings, you wouldn't be dwarves anymore," Jace said.

Grede laughed. "When I was a lad, every time I marveled at how huge this eagle was, my da would smile and say, 'You ought to go have a look inside Grim Batol.'"

"My da wanted to name me Grim Batol Dvergisson — meaning 'Heart of Grim Batol' in Common. But my mother thought it was too long and that people would mock me for such a strange name, so she changed it to Gredigrisson. After that, except for my da, almost nobody remembered that name. Everyone just called me Grede."

"Gredigrisson," Jace said. "Sounds like a Dark Iron name."

"Hah!" Grede pointed at him. "If the Wizard Sanctum's exams had a Dwarven Culture section, you'd pass, lad. That bloody name sounds like something a Dark Iron would come up with, which is exactly why I hate it!"

"But honestly," Grede went on, "I've always felt there's some kind of connection between me and Grim Batol — something I can't quite put my finger into. Don't you think? Look, my father wanted to name me after Grim Batol, and now Grim Batol is ruled by shadow. And on the flip side, I met you and you became my best friend — a warlock. Is that just a coincidence?"

Jace figured this was not the moment to go making sweeping judgments about another culture.

After a moment's hesitation, he only said, "Who knows."

"Aye, who can say." Grede lifted his head and gazed into the distance, his voice dropping to a small, choked murmur. "Even though we're here now… I'm getting more and more afraid to go up. Maybe we should just head straight to Quel'Danas instead. It'd save time."

Jace sighed. "Whatever happens, at least you need to return that hammer to where it belongs, Grede. Besides, you can't expect me to have come all this way with you just to turn around, can you? We're already here — don't worry too much about what they think."

"You're right. This isn't just about me anymore," Grede said. "Then let's go."

At the foot of the trail up to Aerie Peak sat a small village of only just a few houses. At the entrance, a red-bearded dwarf in a brown-red cloak was watching the two approaching strangers.

Beneath the cloak he wore a thick chainmail hauberk. He held a long-handled war hammer planted on the ground with both hands, looking rather like a short Norse warrior.

This guard didn't even glance at Grede. He stared straight at Jace and said, "Human. Shouldn't have come here."

Grede let out a sharp "Hey!" and strode up, unleashing a torrent of Dwarvish. Jace couldn't understand a single word of it.

The red-bearded guard fired back with an equally unintelligible stream of Dwarvish. He hopped down the stone steps and walked right up to Grede, pointing at Jace and saying something in an unhappy tone.

Grede laughed loudly a few times, walked over, grabbed Jace's hand and raised it, then pounded his own chest and said something else. The red-beard's eyes went wide and he roared something. Grede nodded, satisfied.

Jace could more or less guess what was going on — Grede was introducing him as his friend. It was probably quite a rarity for an Aerie Peak dwarf to be friends with a human, which explained the red-beard's shock.

The red-beard shook his head in disbelief, then looked at Jace and said, "Go on, human. You can pass."

"Thanks," Jace said.

The red-beard nodded. "Thanks. Good."

Jace looked at this dwarf who had probably just exhausted his entire life's supply of Common to communicate with him, and had a feeling the rest of his evening was going to be considerably more troublesome.

"Hey," he asked as he followed Grede onward, "what did you just say to him?"

Grede said, "He asked me what you were here for. I said you came here with me."

"Just those two lines?" Jace asked. "What was that bit where you pounded your chest?"

The dwarf hemmed and hawed for a moment, then said, "Well, I told him we helped the Lordaeronians kill a dragon together."

Jace asked in shock, "Why would you bring that up all of a sudden?"

Grede said, "You've got to let them know you're a dragon-slaying hero, not just some random human lad — that's the only way they'll accept you fast! If they didn't know you were a warlock, they'd send you up the mountain to feed the griffons. I'd have to brag about your staff too — tell them you took it from Teron Gorefiend himself… then they'd be even more convinced you've earned the right to climb this mountain."

"Ah… uh… then maybe you should keep your voice down."

"They can't understand Common anyway!" Grede said matter-of-factly, marching onward. Jace clutched his warlock's satchel tighter and silently prayed these dwarves weren't too sensitive about demons. If there happened to be any anti-demon runes around here, they were in trouble.

## Flags

- First appearance of Aerie Peak (鹰巢山) and the Hinterlands (辛特兰) as active story locations.
- Grede's full intended name revealed: Grim Batol Dvergisson ("Heart of Grim Batol"), later shortened to Gredigrisson — a name he dislikes because it sounds Dark Iron.
- The giant eagle statue of Aerie Peak described in detail — a landmark of Wildhammer dwarven craftsmanship.
- Thaldoran (瑟拉丹) referenced as the green dragonflight's forbidden sanctuary in the Hinterlands.
- Grede's connection to shadow (via Grim Batol's corruption) and Jace (a warlock) noted as a possible thematic thread.
- Wildhammer dwarves' distrust of humans established: "humans are a band of cunning bandits."
