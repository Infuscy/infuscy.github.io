Chapter 197: The Thunderfists

"Grede!"

"Grede…"

Though Jace couldn't understand a word of Dwarvish, Grede's name stood out clearly enough.

Quite a few dwarves recognized him and came over to greet him, and Grede only managed to respond half-heartedly.

From what Grede had described along the way, it sounded as though he was about to be cast off by his homeland and his father — that returning home would earn him nothing but cold stares.

But Jace had never expected so many people here to know him, or to care about him this much.

The dwarf returning home had become the focal point of the mountain stairways. Many dwarves came when they heard — some greeting him, some scolding him — reminding Jace of his own mother when he'd come home.

Some dwarves had clearly rushed up in agitation, even shouting angrily, while Grede only gave reluctant replies, his face tinged with embarrassment and shame.

Jace guessed they were asking why he hadn't kept in touch with his family, or why he'd left for so long without a word.

After they'd finished greeting Grede, their attention naturally turned to Jace. The dwarves crowded closer — some saying a few words in Dwarvish, some managing a halting greeting in broken Common: "Hello," "Where are you from," and the like.

Jace answered as best he could, but the other party seemed not to understand either, leaving Grede to explain for him one by one.

Caught up in the enthusiastic embrace of the local dwarves, the two of them had unknowingly climbed the dwarven-built stairways halfway up the mountain. From here, Jace looked out at the other cliff faces more clearly than he had from below.

Several enormous cave-palaces could be seen set into the mountainside, guarded by dwarven statues, their entrances flanked by half-open, massive stone doors.

Switchback stairways had been built from the foot of the mountain all the way up to each palace. Jace lifted his head and noticed griffons circling above the giant eagle-head statue at the very top, as if they'd made it their nest.

Perhaps none of the structures here could match the gates of Ironforge, but the clusters of palaces large and small across the cliff face, together with that colossal eagle carving, formed a different kind of grandeur.

Halfway up, Jace leaned on the railing to catch his breath. That was when he noticed how complicated Grede's expression had become.

"Grede… are you really this famous here? It feels like the whole city knows you."

"Not that famous," Grede said, pointing at the opposite cliff face. "See over there? Aside from a few griffon riders, nobody over there knows me. Lord Kurdran's home is over there — that's the Wildhammer family hall. This is where we Thunderfists live. They're all my cousins, more or less."

Jace followed Grede's pointing and did see a cluster of stone palaces on a far larger scale. Even through the screen of trees and rock, it was clear they were considerably bigger than here.

The Wildhammer clan was the largest branch of the Wildhammer dwarves — after all, outsiders had named the entire tribe after their clan.

"Do the dwarves here really not speak Common?" Jace asked, exasperated.

"Most of them don't," Grede said. "Those who've traveled abroad know a little, or the older ones — the thanes and lords and such."

"Where did you learn Common?" Jace asked.

"Stromgarde," Grede said.

"Why? What were you doing in Stromgarde?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "Back in the day, Stromgarde wanted us to send guards to patrol Thandol Span, to make sure the Dark Iron clan wouldn't harass their farmers. That was thirty or forty years ago. Back then, I was eager to rack up some merit so I could ride a griffon sooner, so I volunteered. You know, in those days on Aerie Peak… everyone thought humans were even less trustworthy than elves. If elves were a bunch of arrogant, selfish snobs, then humans were a band of cunning bandits. That's what most people thought at first."

Jace wasn't surprised by these stereotypes. "But why didn't Stromgarde send their own troops to patrol there?"

"Because Thandol Span was built by us and the Bronzebeard," Grede explained. "A hundred years ago, before the bridge was finished, Stromgarde didn't have to worry about the Dark Iron at all… back then, nobody could cross the chasm separating Khaz Modan and Arathi except griffons. So Thoras Trollbane figured we and the Bronzebeard should take responsibility for it."

"No wonder you know so well what weapons the Stromgarde guards use," Jace said, the realization clicking.

"I wasn't there long anyway. Every day I just wandered the road between Stromgarde Keep and Thandol Span with a sword, chatting with the soldiers and farmers there," Grede said. "My Common wasn't nearly as good back then. But after we'd been stationed there a few years, those of us who'd picked some up became the envoys for dealing with the outsiders down the mountain, you understand? Especially during the Orc Wars."

At that point, Grede was surrounded by another batch of dwarf guards who'd appeared from somewhere, still wearing leather and chainmail.

Jace could only step aside awkwardly and watch him get squeezed into the middle of a crowd of dwarves.

But then he noticed one dwarf grab Grede's hand and say something. Grede shook him off, displeased, and tried to break free and push ahead a few steps quickly.

The other dwarves seemed not to understand and pressed him with questions, but Grede refused to answer them anymore.

Jace could more or less guess — the other party had probably been asking Grede about his illness.

But after being shaken off, one of the dwarves spotted Jace and said, "Hello, Grede's friend."

"Hello," Jace said quickly. He haltingly managed a greeting in Dwarvish. The dwarf clapped his hands in delight and said, "For the Lordaeron Alliance!"

In truth they couldn't understand each other at all. After a few pleasantries, the dwarves let Jace go. Jace immediately hurried after Grede's footsteps, ducking into a great hall carved straight out of the mountain cave.

"Your friends here aren't quite what you described," Jace said, following behind him.

Grede strode quickly deeper into the hall. "How so?"

"They seemed pretty warm toward you…"

"Drop it," Grede said, his face hard. "I'm going to see the thane and ask him the name of this hammer's owner. Honestly, I don't even want to attend the feast tonight. I just want to get out of here as fast as possible."

"Well then, could you help me practice Mage Armor again tonight? I still don't think I'm proficient enough…"

"Fine," Grede nodded and kept walking. Jace rubbed his growling stomach and watched Grede's back, quietly sensing the real reason he was unhappy.

Everyone here who knew Grede knew about his illness. They all knew his pain.

So in a place full of Wildhammer dwarves, his identity as a Wildhammer dwarf naturally faded away entirely, leaving only one label: "disabled veteran." To him, Aerie Peak was a "torture chamber" built by a crowd of people who wanted to be kind to him and take care of him.

If an outsider mentioned his disability, he could curse them out, fight them, make them pay the price. But here he was as if his hands and feet were bound and his mouth sealed — unable to fight back in any way.

Grede had ultimately chosen to run away. That wasn't a bad solution.
