## Chapter 198: Another Attempt

The thanes lived high up. Jace followed Grede for a long time, turning through several maze-like grand corridors — now plunging deep inside, now emerging back outside — the one constant being the endless climbing of stairs.

After he didn't know how long, a surprisingly broad, flat ledge suddenly opened up before him. Jace looked to the right, and a giant eagle's eye immediately filled his vision… Grede had led him all the way up here.

It was late. The sun had turned a deep, dull red. Jace gazed out over the vast Hinterlands forest and felt a wave of loneliness wash over him. Looking west, he could no longer make out Durnholde from here, much less the Alterac Mountains. Without realizing it, he'd come so far from home.

Grede walked over to several stone doors and called out a few times. An old dwarf with a snow-white beard came out.

He looked at Jace, then at Grede, and said something in a low voice. Grede handed him the hammer and said something more.

The two conversed quietly for a long time. Only then did Grede nod, finally taking the hammer back and walking over.

"He had a wife. She's on the other side of the mountain. Let's go return this hammer to her."

"Aren't you going to ask the thanes whether you could become a thane yourself?" Jace asked.

"Have you not been listening to a word I've said? I already learned from them ages ago. It was completely useless," Grede said, pushing him. "Come on, don't embarrass me."

Jace planted his feet and said, "If you truly had no talent at all, how were you able to use that hammer to summon lightning? Have you forgotten Southshore?"

"I already told you, Jace." Grede grabbed Jace's cloak, looked up, and stared him dead in the eyes. "The war hammers of Wildhammer riders are weapons blessed by the thanes. Not every griffon rider can commune with the elements and charge a weapon with elemental power! Only the most senior riders can! And right now I'm obviously a cripple, understand? That's all I'm going to say. You can be as stubborn as you like — there's nothing I can do about it."

"I can tell you for certain that no thane blessed that weapon, Thunderfist," came a deep voice from behind them. It was the dwarf thane from before. Though his Common carried all manner of dwarven pronunciation quirks, it was more than clear enough for an outsider to understand.

"The owner of this weapon, Karden Greyfeather, was an apprentice of the thane master Dogovan Thundermar. He himself could commune with thunder and channel its power to charge his war hammer. That's why there was no need for a thane to specially bless his weapon — and also why he was sent to the Hillsbrad coast to assist the human patrols."

Grede was stunned for a moment, then shook his head and said something in Dwarvish. The thane pointed at the sky and said a single short sentence. Grede tried to argue something, but this time the thane spoke just one word and put an end to his babbling.

Jace understood that word. It meant "get lost." He'd heard it plenty of times in Stormwind. Of course here it couldn't possibly mean "get lost" — it was probably something like "go give it a try."

Grede stared at the thane for a long while, then looked at the hammer in his hand.

Jace said, "Trying doesn't hurt."

Grede pointed at Jace. "If it doesn't work and you dare laugh a single laugh, I'll throw you off this cliff. I mean it."

"Fine," Jace said.

Grede slowly lowered his hand and turned back toward the ledge. Jace caught the thane's eye. The thane's face was nothing but white hair and beard — impossible to tell what expression he was wearing.

It was almost dark. Grede walked slowly to the edge, carefully glanced down, and took a deep breath.

Then he raised his head to the sky, lifted the hammer, and after half a minute of silence roared: "Wind and thunder… spirits of the sky, I call upon you — grant me courage and strength!"

A long silence. Nothing happened.

Jace was a little worried he'd get struck by lightning too and tried to edge backward, but the sky remained perfectly still.

So Grede really didn't have any common language with the elements?

What happened in Southshore, then… could it be that the previous griffon rider, that Karden Greyfeather, still had residual power left in the hammer, and Grede had simply reactivated the leftover charge? And what about Dalaran?

The night they'd defeated the death knight, Jace clearly remembered Grede's hammer flashing with a spark of lightning in the rain. If Southshore was residual power, was Dalaran residual power too?

An elemental weapon that lasted two weeks? That was absurdly overpowered.

Grede kept the hammer held high, as if he simply refused to accept defeat. He didn't turn around — just held it there. His arms slowly began to tremble.

Jace glanced at the thane. The thane was watching him too, eyes narrowed to slits. Was he smiling? Had Grede done something wrong…

Then it hit him.

"Why don't you… speak Dwarvish?!" Jace shouted.

Grede glanced back at Jace and shouted, "What Dwarvish? What do you know! You speak the language the elements understand!"

"Right, I don't know, you do! Speaking Common to the elements, are you? The fact they haven't struck you dead is a miracle," Jace shot back.

"Shut up!" Grede bristled, pointing at Jace. "Now I'll do it again! You stay quiet!"

Jace knew what Grede meant by "the language of the elements." On another continent, the Kaldorei and tauren called it Kalimdor — a mysterious, ancient language used by the elements to communicate.

Grede took a few deep breaths, raised the war hammer high once more, and shouted at the sky in a stream of sounds completely unlike the crisp staccato of Dwarvish — more continuous, almost viscous in its flow.

Jace stared at the sky. It seemed as quiet as ever, but the air around the war hammer in Grede's hands appeared to warp and distort…

Suddenly a bolt of lightning crashed down from the sky and struck the war hammer. Light exploded, whiting out Jace's vision in an instant. He heard Grede cry out, then felt a shockwave-like gust of wind slam into his face. He staggered two steps, lost his seat, and sat down hard on the ground.

When his vision cleared two seconds later, he actually saw the war hammer trailing a chain of lightning as it spun up into the sky…

Damn it… how did he let go at such a critical moment?

But Jace didn't dare say a word. He knew better than anyone what Grede least wanted to hear… Still, as long as he could successfully summon lightning, everything else could be dealt with. If the hammer was lost, it could be picked up again.

But Grede charged two steps to the edge, locking onto the war hammer that was about to fall.

Just when Jace thought he was about to suffer another crushing defeat… the dwarf actually reached out his hand.

The war hammer suddenly erupted with a brilliant burst of electrical energy, as if riding a chain of lightning bolts straight back, and was caught firmly in Grede's hand — leaving only a single sharp crack of metal striking metal!

Jace was completely stunned. He'd only ever seen a scene like this in the movies. Grede coughed a few times. The hammer in his hand kept crackling with flashes of lightning, illuminating his face in stuttering bursts, forcing him to squint.

Even from this distance, Jace found the electrical energy in the dark twilight almost unbearably dazzling — and he wasn't even close.

"I did it, Jace…" Grede gripped the hammer and looked over, shaking it and shouting. "This is real!"

"Am I strong?! Am I strong?!"

"Grede, that was… incredible! I've never seen anything like it!" Jace shouted back, just as excited — even more excited than when he'd heard that Windrunner might have a way to cure Grede's illness.

And he was certain Grede felt the same. Gripping that war hammer crackling with electrical energy, he let out a string of strange chuckling sounds, snot bubbles forming — impossible to tell whether he was laughing or crying.

When he'd first been told he could come to Quel'Danas to find that botanist, he'd been far from this emotional… perhaps deep down he still didn't like the idea of having an elf help solve his problem.

Looking back at where the thane had been standing — at some point he'd already gone back inside. That old man had seemed to know exactly what was going to happen here, as if from the very start.

## Flags

- Karden Greyfeather (卡登·灰羽) named — previous owner of the war hammer, apprentice of thane master Dogovan Thundermar (杜戈万·桑德玛尔).
- Dogovan Thundermar confirms Grede has innate elemental talent — the hammer was never blessed, meaning Grede's earlier successes were his own power.
- Grede successfully summons lightning and recalls the hammer — a major breakthrough in his elemental abilities.
- The "language of the elements" is Kalimdor (卡利姆多语) — the same tongue used by Kaldorei and tauren shamans.
- Grede's earlier failures were partly due to using Common instead of the elemental language — a humorous but critical mistake.
- The thane appears to have foreseen Grede's success, suggesting he had more faith in Grede than Grede had in himself.
- Southshore and Dalaran lightning incidents recontextualized — they were Grede's own power, not residual energy from the hammer.
