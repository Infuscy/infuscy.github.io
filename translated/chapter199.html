Chapter 199: Whose Problem Is It, Really

On the way back, Grede was practically a different person, frantically telling Jace how he'd felt up on the cliff. He talked about how he'd wanted to commune with the elements before, how disappointed the thanes had been — he went on so long Jace could have recited it all from memory, even the occasional Dwarvish fragments that popped up.

Still, even though he'd used the war hammer to summon the power of lightning, something that belonged to someone else still had to be returned to its rightful owner.

Grede and Jace crossed several stone-carved walkways to another clan's great hall. Following the location the thane had given Grede, they found the wife of the griffon rider Karden Greyfeather.

Their conversation was completely unintelligible to Jace, but the wife wept bitterly. Jace couldn't bear delivering news like this to someone — having to tell another person that a family member was dead. He could only stand far off and stare blankly at the Hinterlands forest.

After a while, Grede walked over, let out a deep sigh, and said, "Finally delivered."

"So where are you going to get a new one?" Jace asked.

"You think I don't have a hammer?"

"You kept your own hammer? Why didn't you bring it with you?"

"I'm not a griffon rider anymore. Why would I still carry a griffon rider's war hammer?"

"Then right now…"

"To hell with it." Grede waved his hand. "Come with me. I'll get my hammer."

Returning to the Thunderfist great hall, Grede led Jace down a spiraling staircase, finally reaching the bottom of a dark cave and ducking into a low-ceilinged room.

It was far too short. Jace kept banging his head and had to keep his hood pulled up the whole time — otherwise he'd have walked out covered in bumps.

The space was dark and stuffy, reeking of a thick, damp-rot stench, like mildewed rags left to fester. The other rooms nearby seemed to have people living in them, but Grede's stood wide open. The strange thing was that nobody had touched it in all this time — a layer of dust covered everything. Aerie Peak's public order seemed to be in decent shape.

Once inside the room, Grede rummaged through the dusty clutter for a long while, then finally hauled out a war hammer.

This hammer looked less like a war hammer and more like a nail-driving hatchet — the pointed end even more exaggerated than the last one.

Jace's impression had been that griffon riders' war hammers were all a standard metal block, very proper and uniform. But having come here and seen them, all kinds of war hammers came in every bizarre shape and size — the pure block shape was actually the rarest.

"You damned thing," Grede said, hefting the hammer. "Last time I used you to try in the exact same place and got nothing. Chances are you and I just weren't a good match, and that's why I got my leg bitten by that bat!"

"Give me a spark right now if you've got the nerve…" Grede hadn't even finished his sentence when a bolt of blinding white lightning suddenly erupted from the hammer, bouncing all over the cramped space. Jace shrieked and bolted outside. Grede went "Aaah!" and flung the hammer away — but the hammer, charged with electrical energy, flew wildly around, chasing right after Jace's backside. Jace barely managed to scramble and roll his way up the stairs. He waited quite a while before things finally quieted down below.

"Hey, you dead down there?!" Jace shouted.

Just then a red-clad dwarf came running from above, carrying a shield. He locked eyes with Jace, short sword in hand.

"Human? Why?"

"Good human," Jace said immediately, standing straight.

Grede shouted a string of Dwarvish from below. The dwarf nodded, sheathed his short sword, and left, keeping an eye on Jace the whole time.

A few moments of footsteps, and Grede walked up carrying his own hammer, muttering something under his breath in Dwarvish. When Jace saw him, the dwarf's beard and hair were standing completely on end.

"Doesn't this prove it wasn't your hammer that was the problem — it was you, back then?" Jace asked.

"Shut up…" Grede bared a white tooth. "Then why didn't it work before, and why does it work now?"

Jace mulled it over for a moment. "Maybe becoming a truly powerful griffon rider requires going through a few things first. Don't you think?"

"So what did I go through?"

Grede sat down on the ground angrily, exhaled a huge breath, and a cloud of dust puffed out of his mouth.

"That depends on what you were like before," Jace said. "Before you were injured."

"Ah…" Grede leaned against the stone stair railing, looked up at the not-too-high ceiling, and said, "I was really different from now."

"Back then I… thought that one day I would definitely become the greatest griffon rider, a legend of Khaz Modan and the Hinterlands — like Khadgar Wildhammer, or a Mountain King… Even if the elemental powers didn't answer my call, I could still prove myself with my combat skills, my sharp senses, my immense strength. Maybe I overestimated myself. No — scratch the 'maybe.' I just wasn't that strong."

"Maybe the elements thought you were too much of an arrogant little shit back then, so they didn't want to answer your call?" Jace asked.

"Then the elements have the temperament of a child," Grede said. "And if I do something they don't like later, they can just refuse to answer my call again on a whim?"

Jace said, "Then tell me — why do those thanes, or the shamans of other races, all cultivate their temperament, learn the elements' moods, and maintain humility? Isn't that precisely because the elements have bad tempers? You can keep your patience around an owlbear and not provoke it — but when it comes to such powerful elemental forces, you're the one with no patience."

"If an owlbear is provoked, it'll really slap you across the face," Grede said.

"Then if you make the elements unhappy, they won't fight for you. Which price is steeper, I couldn't say," Jace said.

"Mmm…" Grede's beard twitched. He slowly lowered his head and thought for a while, then suddenly said, "What about you, Jace Sesso? What kind of person were you in the past?"

"I've only been alive eighteen years total. What 'past' could I have? Probably just a kid who didn't know anything," Jace answered offhandedly.

"Only been alive eighteen years," Grede grinned. "You don't sound like a seventeen- or eighteen-year-old when you say it. You sound like some old man lecturing a child. A normal person would say: 'I'm seventeen now, old enough, a grown-up! I'm going into the forest with you to kill trolls!'"

"Probably because being out here with you, if I don't grow up fast, I'll die easy," Jace said.

"Go to hell." Grede stood up and stretched. "Are you hungry, Jace? I'm hungry!"

"What do you think? I haven't eaten a thing since this morning," Jace said helplessly. "I've been looking forward to the big feast at Aerie Peak for days now, and you didn't even want to come."

"Then let's go," Grede said. "Come on, come on — eat for free, why not? It doesn't cost a copper!"
