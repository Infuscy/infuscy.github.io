Chapter 202: A Strange Sound on the Wind

"Hey, Jace, you still alive?"

"Alive, alive!" Jace raised a hand and sat up, feeling around for a moment. A small fire flickered on the stone seat before him, and his groggy field of vision slowly brightened.

"We should get going!" Grede said. "It's ten o'clock already — if we don't leave now, we won't make it to Quel'Danis before sunset."

"Really?"

Jace worked his mouth, licked his teeth — they felt a bit furred — and his head was still a little fuzzy.

"Last night… what the hell was that liquor last night?!" He grabbed Grede. "You said it was griffin drool?"

Grede frowned and glared at him. "What's griffin drool?! That was 'Griffin's Tears' — a local specialty! You won't find it anywhere else in the world! It's the most wonderful thing on this planet… you could die without regret after drinking it!"

"'Die without regret' is a phrase that has a lot more impact coming from the person who actually drank it, don't you think?" Jace said helplessly.

"Doesn't matter — I'll say it for you, and I guarantee it absolutely lives up to the praise."

Grede packed as he spoke. "Get your things together. We're not coming back after this."

"Not coming back? After Quel'Danis, we're heading straight to Southshore?" Jace asked.

"That's right. Save time." Grede nodded.

"If I'm understanding this correctly…" Jace sat up and rolled his neck, waiting for the dizzy, muddled feeling to pass before continuing, "you're planning to fly us back."

"You understand correctly." Grede said, "But even if we fly back, I can't exactly steal a griffin from Aerie Peak, can I?"

"So?" Jace asked. "You planning to catch one?"

"Catch one?" Grede stuck his neck out. "Catch what? Catch a griffin? You've seen griffins — does that sound realistic? Who's catching who? I'm planning to negotiate with a griffin, you see!"

"I don't really see," Jace muttered.

"Then just watch, you pasty little pink-skin. I don't expect you to understand the art of this kind of communication." As he spoke, Grede tied up his braids, threw on his fur cloak, cinched his belt, hung the loop of his hammer handle at his waist, and looked at Jace. "Well? Don't I look like a true knight?"

"You'd look more like one if you were a little taller."

"And you being so tall hasn't exactly made you look like much of a warrior."

"Pfft. Why are you dressed up like that?" Jace asked.

"When you go see elves, you absolutely cannot lose out in presence." Grede patted his bulging belly. "Those Quel'Danis folks dress like they're going to a wedding even when they head out to fight."

"That's a bit of an exaggeration…"

Jace finished packing before putting on his shirt. Staying in the dwarven caves was so warm he'd almost forgotten it was already winter outside.

Grede let out a laugh. "Wearing more is also to guard against wolves, Jace. When I used to patrol those parts, I went with six or seven fully armed dwarves — normal wolf packs wouldn't dare threaten us. Now there's just the two of us, and that's a different story. The wild wolves of the Hinterlands survive by snatching food from trolls — think about how vicious they must be… definitely not the small-timers from Elwynn Forest."

Jace picked up his staff by the wall and gave it a shake. "No wolf would dare threaten us. You just keep your legs steady and don't run off with the wolves when the time comes."

He strapped the staff to his back, tightened his belt, puffed out his chest, and smiled. "I'll try not to hit you by accident!"

Grede slapped his warhammer. "Chances are those wolves will be convulsing and heading to the Shadowlands before you even finish your incantation, Jace. Let you catch a glimpse of a true griffin knight's power."

The climate of the Hinterlands was a bit warmer than Hillsbrad's — Jace guessed the sea breeze from the east was being blocked by Aerie Peak.

Unlike Hillsbrad and Alterac, where it was nothing but uniform rows of pines and cypresses, the Hinterlands had more oaks, towering firs, and even absurdly enormous trees of some unidentifiable species.

Jace believed those supernaturally large trees up north must have some kind of blood relation to the World Tree — otherwise, how could they get so big…

The Hinterlands already felt like the edge of civilization as Jace understood it. A glance in any direction showed almost no trace of human activity.

He knew that if he kept heading east he'd eventually run into an Amani troll village or outpost, but trolls weren't the kind of race that liked to reshape nature on a massive scale, so compared to Lordaeron's territories, this place was still remarkably pristine.

At noon the two of them filled their waterskins by a small pool, then ate a bit of food under the crumbling wall of a troll ruin that was who-knew-how-many years old.

As Jace chewed on the bread he'd brought from Aerie Peak, he suddenly noticed something mixed into the sharp whistle of the wind blowing through the cracks in the wall — a sound that didn't quite fit.

Sometimes it was a broken, intermittent whistling. Sometimes it was a muffled, low moan.

He glanced into the crack in the wall but saw nothing.

"Hey, Grede, did you hear a strange sound?"

Grede stopped chewing and slowly raised his head. The two of them went quiet for a moment. "There is something… Jace, what do you think that sound is like?"

"Like someone talking with their mouth covered," Jace said.

"That specific?" Grede asked. "You got some kind of guess?" A mumbling sound in the Hinterlands forest…

"What are the odds this is someone tangled up in a spider's web?" Jace asked.

Grede stared at him for a moment, then nodded. "Pretty high!"

Jace glanced toward the nearby ruined walls. "Should we follow the sound and take a look?"

"Since we heard it." Grede said, "If it's an elf, fine — but what if it's a dwarf? We can't just leave them to die. We're not far from Aerie Peak. Though I hate to admit it, the odds of my own kind running into this kind of trouble are way higher than an elf's."

"The spiders here are connected to the trolls," Jace said. "They worship the spider god Shadra, right?"

"You even know that?"

"You told me. Did you forget?"

"Ah… I think I did mention it." Grede scratched his ear. "But the spider priests all live around Shadra'Zol. The spiders near Aerie Peak have already broken free from the trolls' control. The hunters used to come out regularly to kill spiders and drive them farther from Aerie Peak while they were at it. Now that the war's just ended, I reckon they've seized the chance to sneak back and cause trouble."

"Just like the gnolls in Elwynn," Jace said.

"Just like gnolls," Grede agreed.
