Chapter 203: The Shadra Cellar

The two of them set off right away. Grede drew the dagger at his waist and, holding his small round shield, rounded the wall. Jace gathered their things and followed behind.

They followed the faint sound drifting in on the wind, pushing deeper into the troll ruin. Jace looked at the collapsed cyan walls, the ancient carvings on them, and said quietly, "Careful — there could be trolls lying in ambush…"

"I know," Grede said from ahead. "But the trolls who built these structures probably don't even consider the forest trolls to the east as their kind. When the forest trolls came here, they were just as wary of elves ambushing them inside — they were on edge too!"

"This place gives me the creeps," Jace said.

"A human who faced down Teron Gorefiend is scared of trolls?" Grede turned around. "Let me tell you something, Jace. After Zul'jin allied with the Horde, a lot of trolls discovered their comrades-in-arms were a bunch of ghosts possessing human corpses, dark warriors who could raise the dead with a single incantation — and they were even more shocked and terrified than you are. The dead possessing corpses is practically a mythological tale to ordinary forest trolls, and not exactly a flattering one."

"Fine, but I've never actually seen a troll before. Being nervous about an unknown enemy is perfectly normal, isn't it?" Jace said.

The dwarf said, "Compared to trolls… right now the most important thing is to watch out for spider ambushes, especially the little ones." Hearing that, Jace immediately looked down to check his arms and legs to see if any spiders had crawled on him.

"The spiders of the Hinterlands aren't like the ones in Stormwind," Grede said, watching Jace's movements. "The smallest ones here are the size of a dog. If they ambush you, you'll know — don't worry."

Jace's eye twitched. He didn't see what there was not to worry about…

The ruin was shaped like a small, ancient altar. Because it wasn't very large, you could make out a square layout, and right now they were feeling their way toward the center of that square.

After rounding a wall, Jace found white spider webbing stuck to the surface — that basically confirmed their fears. This was a spider's lair.

He reached out and touched it. It was unbelievably sticky and tough; he barely managed to tear off a single strand. This was his first time touching giant spider webbing in this world, and it had left quite the impression.

Rounding another wall toward the center, the spider webs grew thicker and thicker. Though the two of them picked their way carefully, Grede ahead nearly tripped several times.

Near a wall close to the center, they found a hole in the ground — and the thing neither of them wanted to accept was that the muffled sound was coming from inside.

By now it was almost certain: this was the sound of someone sobbing with their mouth covered, punctuated now and then by a sharp whistle — the very whistle Jace had first noticed.

"I don't remember there being a hole here." Grede scratched his head. "I've been to this ruin twice, but… I never noticed an opening."

"Tell me, Grede," Jace said. "Could this spider be like that panther on the boat — using calls to lure us over, then hunting the two of us?"

"So what if it is." Grede said. "Congratulations, it worked. And here's wishing it a pleasant trip to the Shadowlands."

With that, Grede stepped down the stairs. Jace raised an eyebrow and followed him in.

Entering the pitch-black tunnel, a faint stench drifted over. Jace conjured a Light spell and walked ahead. Grede glanced at the glow and muttered, "Now I'm finally feeling a bit of what it's like to have a mage around."

"How many times have I saved your life with magic before this," Jace said.

"That's not what I call magic," Grede said. "Every time I've been saved by one of your strange incantations, I get this inexplicable sense of guilt — like to save someone, you have to kill someone first."

Jace snorted. "If I hadn't used it, you wouldn't be alive to feel guilty about it."

"Exactly." Grede said. "Which is why I haven't complained! As a friend, as long as you're not causing problems yourself and not hurting anyone, I've got nothing to say. But I've been alive for sixty or seventy years now, Jace — the process of changing my thinking is going to be a lot slower than you brainless young brats."

"Watch our backs…" Jace reminded him.

If Grede was right, this cellar should be extremely ancient. Jace felt it resembled that Atal'Hakkar Temple underwater in Black Marsh — except the images on the walls weren't snakes, but all manner of multi-legged creatures, not all of them spiders.

Perhaps this had once been a small Shadra temple, built by the ancient Amani trolls, abandoned as the Amani Empire declined irresistibly, and after thousands of years buried by dust and earth — transformed from a temple into an underground crypt, its original tower top becoming a small ruined courtyard.

Just then, the sobbing stopped.

The two of them halted at a fork in the tunnel, listening carefully. Grede held up his head and looked at the tunnel ceiling. "Hear that tap-tap-tap, Jace? Some of it is spiders crawling across the web. We've been noticed."

"Look at all this webbing covering the ground," Jace said. "They definitely locked onto us the moment we came in. Hey — where exactly is that person?"

Discerning the sound here was even harder — there was noise and echo everywhere. Just as the two of them hesitated, another weak sob came from close by. They both looked toward the tunnel it came from.

"Go!"

After descending a stretch, they arrived in a fairly large chamber. Broken statues and stone slabs were everywhere, and the whole room was filled with a hard-to-bear stench.

If Jace hadn't been tempered by several encounters with the foul reek of shadow and corpse poison, he probably would have thrown up on the spot. Even Grede was pinching his nose hard, fighting waves of nausea.

"Bringing back some bad memories there, Grede," Jace said.

"I'm charging for this." Grede looked at the split cocoons of spider silk on the ground. "I'm charging for this — even if I'm a Wildhammer dwarf. This trip is worth at least ten silver. If he can't pay, he's going to regret it."

"Agreed," Jace said.

He held up the Light and swept it along the walls and the base of the walls for a while, finally spotting a trembling cocoon of spider silk.

The cocoon was wrapped tight, without a single gap — yet the person inside had somehow survived this long without suffocating.

Though compared to a Wildhammer dwarf, this cocoon did seem a bit long.
