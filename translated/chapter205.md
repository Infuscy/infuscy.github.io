## Chapter 205: What's Coming Will Come

"Hurry up, Grede, use that lightning hammer of yours and do something!"

"My hammer…"

Grede stretched his arm as far as it would go but couldn't reach his warhammer, which had fallen not far away.

He tried calling down lightning — the hammer just flickered and shuddered with energy, releasing a pulse of power, but the hammer itself was wrapped up tight in spider silk.

"Do you know any fire magic, Jace! Light — can Light start a fire…"

"I'll try."

Jace recited the Light incantation again. He remembered that whenever his magic failed, he'd accidentally set something on fire — but this time, no matter how he cast it, the Light came out perfectly. It was soft and gentle, and it had absolutely no chance of igniting this tough spider webbing!

Before, when he was nervous, he'd misfire spells. Now that he'd practiced enough, when he was nervous and *wanted* to misfire on purpose, he couldn't do it at all.

The dense sound of spiders scuttling across the ground grew closer and closer. Grede cursed a few times. Jace said in a low voice, "I've got a really effective 'fire stone' that lights right up — but I'm warning you, don't blame me after we get out."

"THEN WHAT THE HELL ARE YOU WAITING FOR!!"

"Sazeno! Out!"

"Here! Here! This humble self finally sees the light of day again!" Hearing that voice, Grede, who'd been frantic with worry, went rigid.

"Who's talking?"

A dark-smoke-trailed, dark-gold little creature leaped out of Jace's bag, and a burst of flame ignited right on top of its head. Grede swore: "What the hell are you?!"

"Shut it, dwarf! I've been putting up with you for ages! Screw around and I'll roast your fat lips into sausages."

"How dare you insult me — who the hell are you… just you wait till I get down there… just you wait. Jace Sesso, you goddamn…"

"Quit the chatter… Sazeno, hurry up and burn this web away!"

"Don't save me! Let me die here! Ahhhh!!! I'm going to kill this foul thing!"

"You won't let me save you? Then I'll save you, haha!"

The imp snapped his fingers. A ball of flame instantly incinerated all the spider silk binding the two of them — and the fire spread far beyond, making the whole passage flicker between light and dark.

"You're welcome, you stinking dwarf!"

"Don't mind me — I'll thank you properly."

Grede slammed down onto the ground, immediately raised his lightning hammer, and smashed it hard in the imp's direction. Lightning exploded outward, blowing out all the surrounding flames in one go. A massive serpentine current swept toward Sazeno's position — only to see the imp vanish with a mocking laugh in a flash of light. The blinding white light and shockwave from the discharged energy slammed both Jace and Grede into the wall.

Jace hit the wall, rolled, and only slid to the ground after a full rotation. Smoke and dust from the fire and lightning explosion billowed everywhere. He pounded his chest and couched painfully several times.

"Stop fighting! Go… go… hurry! There are still spiders!"

That said, the sound of spiders had gone completely silent.

It wasn't hard to understand — if he were this nest of spiders, hearing a lightning-wielding madman and a fire demon duking it out in a rage at his doorstep, he wouldn't stick his head out either.

"Spiders… spiders, spiders, what do spiders matter."

Grede muttered as he stood up, flames still dancing on his body. The imp popped up again not far behind him.

The demon hopped about as it shrieked: "Come at me, you foul-mouthed idiot — see if I don't toy you to death."

Grede whipped around, snatched up the hammer from the ground, and hurled it in one fluid motion. The hammer spun through the air — and hit nothing, *clang*, smashing into the wall and dropping to the ground. The imp was already gone.

Grede was so furious his beard looked ready to explode. He scrambled over on all fours, snatched up the hammer, and roared back: "Where's that demon?! Where is he?!"

"Come on! Come on! Come on!"

The imp materialized not far from Jace. Jace grabbed it by the horns and shoved it straight back into the bag.

"Ah… don't put me back!!! Ah… that fat guy's so fun!!! Ahhhh… Master, I'm suffocating!!! …Mmph mmph mmph…"

Jace cinched the warlock's bag tight. The bag thrashed back and forth a few times before going still, slowly deflating.

Grede stared at Jace, breathing hard, as if he was about to have a heart attack on the spot.

He held it in for a long moment before finally saying: "That's solid muscle! Goddamn it — can't even tell fat from strong, you moron demon."

"Let's get out of here…" Jace said, holding the bag.

The two of them scrambled out of the passage and emerged outside. A flame was still dancing on the back of Grede's cloak — he pulled it off and stomped on it a few times.

He picked up the cloak, shook off the dust, and looked at Jace, then at the bag, then back at Jace.

"You're not going to say anything?"

"What am I supposed to say… wasn't the plan to not rush things?" Jace said helplessly.

Grede pointed at him, then dropped his hand and sighed. "You goddamn… you always find some new trick to pull on me. That was a demon, right? I shouldn't expect anything else from you warlocks, but when the hell did you get that thing?"

"Before we went to Redridge Mountains," Jace said.

"That long ago?" Grede said. "I was completely in the dark all these months?! It's been by your side the whole time, hiding in that bag, and I never knew?"

"Pretty much. That time in Redridge Mountains…" Jace said. "He saved me from that orc — which indirectly saved you too. And up at Stonewatch, without his help, I definitely would've been chopped to death by gnolls."

Grede pinched the back of his neck with a pained expression. "I actually traveled with a demon all this time. My gods."

"Honestly, Jace, you working with a demon doesn't bug me as much as the fact that you kept it from me the whole time… Those orc warlocks, some of them brought demons into battle with us too — it's not the first time I've seen these little things, so I've got some mental preparation."

Then he asked: "Wait — I need to be clear on one thing. He does follow your orders, right? You can actually control him?"

Jace said, "Haven't found any problems so far."

"So the stuff he was yelling at me — that was you telling him to say it?"

"What? No way…"

"Fine. Just don't let him out for now, all right?" Grede said, staring unblinkingly at the bag at Jace's waist.

"Got it… should we take some spider silk back?" Jace tried to change the subject. "If we're still heading to Southshore, spider silk sells for coin too — otherwise this really will have been a wasted trip."

"Ah… that's a good point." Grede said. "But we can't take much — it's getting late, and our bags aren't big enough to hold much anyway."

"Let's at least take some clean bits — not too much dust and dead bugs." Jace said. "Can we make it to Quel'Danis before dark?"

"Should be doable." Grede nodded and started sorting through the things in his bag.

"But come to think of it — I got saved by that demon, which means I'm now in league with a demon too, doesn't it? So we're not so different after all! Heh! I used to hear humans say — the Wildhammer dwarves up in the mountains may have bad tempers and don't deal with humans, but they hate evil with a passion and stand opposed to the darkness, no compromise. I was so proud of that. And now? Tsk tsk tsk — I've become an accomplice to an evil warlock in the woods too."

"A bit late for that realization, isn't it?" Jace said.

"You demon lord…" Grede gripped his dagger and gritted his teeth. "Hurry up and get that spider silk!"
