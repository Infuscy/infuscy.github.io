## Chapter 206: The Elven Refuge

Quel'Danil was an elven settlement nestled at the foot of Aerie Peak, so the straight-line distance between the two wasn't far.

It was just that the place was surrounded by a forest dense beyond anything natural. At dusk, Jace caught distant glimpses of what seemed to be faint purple rooftops, but the moment he entered the forest, not a trace of them was visible anymore.

As a half-baked mage who'd been exposed to arcane magic at the Wizard's Sanctum, Jace could feel clearly that a considerable amount of arcane power swirled around this place. It had unquestionably been modified by elves.

"The elves may have set traps here, Grede," he warned.

Grede agreed. "The Amani trolls rarely dare invade this forest directly. There must be a reason."

The two wandered through the forest for ages. Though they'd been heading in the direction of those purple rooftops the whole time, it felt like they'd been walking forever without getting any closer.

Just as Jace was about to suggest they try a different approach to find the way, he suddenly noticed a figure up ahead.

"Hello, we're lost," Jace tried.

"We are NOT lost!" Grede bellowed.

"And this doesn't count as lost?" Jace pointed at the sky. "It's almost dark out. Have you ever even been to Quel'Danil, or—"

"Of course I've been!" Grede licked his lips, then added, "Just… not by myself…"

The figure drew closer. It was indeed an elf — a woman, clad in a dark green short robe, a silver lance in hand, her eyes fixed warily on Jace's staff.

"Excuse me, miss, could you take us to Quel'Danil?" Jace asked.

"State your business, both of you. Even if we are allies now, that does not mean you may come and go as you please," the elf said.

"Go on, Jace, tell her who sent us," Grede said, slapping Jace's backside.

"Lady Vereesa Windrunner told us to come here seeking a botanist named Vinaryal Sunseeker."

Jace was a little relieved he could still remember the name.

"I see." The elf's gaze shifted back and forth between the two of them. "Give me your names."

"Jace Sesso."

"Grede Thunderfist."

The elf nodded. "Follow me."

"It really…" Grede looked astonished, leaning in close and lowering his voice. "That little lady's word actually carries weight!"

Jace glanced at him. "So you're only truly believing me now, is that it?"

"You've got to admit… you weren't a hundred percent sure either?!" Grede said. "That Vereesa Windrunner didn't exactly seem reliable to me."

"Do you have some kind of grudge against her…?" Jace narrowed his eyes. "Don't forget — if it weren't for her, your problem wouldn't even be in sight of a solution."

"Ah, I'm very grateful to her, truly grateful." Grede scratched his head. "It's just… I always felt like she wouldn't go to such lengths for us. But I'm believing it more and more now."

"Maybe she's just a good person!" Jace muttered.

He recalled that night when he'd used Life Tap — something far more terrifying than in any game — to pull back some strength and shadow energy. Vereesa, in the middle of fighting, had seemed to catch him in her peripheral vision.

At the time he'd been desperate to loose a spell at Teron Gorefiend, focusing all his attention on gripping his staff lest it be snatched away from him again. After that he'd been busy for days on end, no time to properly recall what had happened, and he'd almost forgotten the look of shock in her eyes.

Had she been horrified by the evil of that sudden burst of shadow energy, or worried that the shadow would torment him to death — or perhaps both.

At that moment, Jace remembered what Grede had told him back in the ancient troll ruins, and felt he shouldn't keep hiding everything anymore.

"Actually, there's something I haven't told you yet."

"You kept a succubus?" the dwarf asked.

"Hah?" Jace shot him a look. "No… I don't have any other demons. I mean that night — the night we dealt with Teron Gorefiend."

"I knew it," Grede said. "I knew there was no way the Windrunner would help us that much, going by what you told me."

Jace lowered his voice. "There's another spell I know. On top of Fear, Curse of Weakness, and Drain Life, this magic — even the demon you just met found it somewhat evil. It uses your own life force to trade for shadow power, to seize one desperate shot at a fight in the most extreme circumstances. That night I used that spell. I hadn't planned on telling you, Grede, but you said I shouldn't keep anything from you."

Grede fell silent for a moment, murmuring, "Trading life for shadow power… and even a demon finds it evil. Your description is giving me a bad feeling, Jace Sesso…"

"I know it's not good. But that was Teron Gorefiend, Grede." Jace said. "To make me take on a being like that, I was prepared to pay a massive price. Otherwise, I wouldn't be standing here."

"All right. So long as you survived." Grede said. "I've never faced Teron Gorefiend head-on, but I've heard his evil name enough times. If it were me, I'd have fought dirty and struggled for my life too. And since Vereesa was willing to help you, she must understand what you gave up. Not a bad little girl — knows how to repay a kindness. I'd thought that quality had long since gone extinct in Quel'Thalas."

Following the elf, before long they left the forest and came upon a finely-crafted path of laid stones.

Jace and Grede had been circling around and around in that forest with no idea how to find this place. At one point they'd half-expected to be trapped in there overnight.

So the so-called magical trap was probably the forest itself — a place you could never leave without an elven guide from Quel'Danil.

Compared to the outlandish buildings in Dalaran's elven district, Quel'Danil was far more understated — an elven village seemingly built from materials gathered locally in the Hinterlands.

The main structure of the village was a rather tall two-story elven wooden hall built at the edge of a pond. Below it, a square formed from finely-crafted wooden houses. In the square stood a stone-pillar shrine that emitted a faint fluorescent glow even at night, as if a swarm of fireflies surrounded it.

A sword-wielding elven guard whispered something to the elf who'd led them here, glanced at Jace and Grede, and stood aside to let them pass.

Jace noticed several beautiful wooden perches behind the hall. On one of them sat a gorgeous giant bird, its body even larger than a gryphon's.

It coiled its entire body around the perch like a tangle of gold-and-red vines. Its massive, clumsy vulture-like head sprouted two curved horns.

The way it hung from that perch inexplicably reminded Jace of the black dragon on that watchtower.

This was a dragonhawk.
