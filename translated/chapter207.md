## Chapter 207: The Residents of Quel'Daniel

The hawkstrider was gazing out toward the northern dense forest. Every now and then, some animal's cry from within would make its whole body shudder with a ripple of feathers, and it would try to climb higher, straining to stretch its already rather short neck, as if it wanted to investigate.

But every time it made a move, an elven guard beside it would shake his lance, signaling it to stay quiet. Sometimes the giant bird would even try to peck at the soldier's lance, angrily letting out a string of *whoosh-whoosh* sounds.

This was a creature unique to Quel'Thalas. Jace had read an account of it in one of Master Marin's bestiaries back in the study.

Some guessed they were hybrid monsters born of dragons disguised as elves, hidden away in Quel'Thalas, mating with giant eagles. Others said they were predatory birds native to Quel'Thalas, transformed into magical creatures by the Sunwell's energy radiation.

In any case, here Jace could feel far more viscerally than in the game exactly why this beast was called a "hawkstrider." In appearance, in movement, in sheer size — it truly did look like a hybrid of the two.

The appearance of two outsiders had clearly drawn the attention of some of the elves on the street. Two elves who'd been idly playing a bagpipe, along with their audience, stopped to look this way.

As Jace watched the elven woman head toward the hall, he heard a female elf whispering to her companion beside his ear, as if asking some strange question. Another female elf glanced at Jace, gave a soft laugh, and didn't answer. Before long, as if that earlier topic had fermented, a wave of ridicule spread among the male and female elves nearby, making him rather uncomfortable.

After stepping inside the hall, he said, somewhat annoyed, "The elves here don't seem to have many who can fight. There aren't many guards either. I wonder how they'd defend this place if the trolls really broke through the forest and attacked."

"That's only how they appear on the surface," Grede said under his breath. "Let me tell you a little story about Quel'Daniel. You want to hear it?"

"Go on."

"I remember when I was still young, an elven woman commander from Quel'Daniel was captured by trolls. They flayed her alive and hung her on a stone pillar over in those troll ruins to the east, trying to terrorize this place. After the elves of Quel'Daniel brought her down, they hung up a particularly beautiful, flower-carved elven cloth bag up there — stuffed full of fresh and shriveled troll dick."

"Troll… how do you know that so clearly?" Jace asked.

"Because the first ones to find it weren't trolls — they were a bunch of our scouts," Grede said. "The first one to open that bag was named Truden. I still remember his name. That poor bastard — a few years later he got a troll blade through the chest. Right before he died, his mind already fuzzy, he was still muttering that at least he wouldn't have to dream about that bag anymore."

Jace imagined the contents of that bag, and his thoughts spun into chaos for a moment. "Real or not…"

"A hundred percent real? I can't say for sure. The elves won't admit it, but that bag definitely wasn't ours." Grede leaned in and lowered his voice. "And that elven commander didn't die. She was saved. They supposedly used water from the Sunwell itself — just to deny the trolls their satisfaction."

After hearing that little story, Jace glanced back at the elves outside, chatting and laughing, and felt a chill run through his crotch.

Upstairs in the elven hall, an elf in a deep red long robe was adjusting his floral crown.

He was very tall — half a head taller than a guard standing beside him — and his whole bearing was robust and formidable. He had a powerful build, broad-shouldered and solidly built, not like an ordinary high elf. He was closer to Jace's impression of a night elf.

"Grede Thunderfist, sir, and Jace Sesso, sir." The elf turned around, waving a hand signaling the guards to withdraw first.

"I am Vinaryal of the Sunseeker Sanctuary. Lady Sylvanas Windrunner must have told you about me?" He glanced at the dwarf. "She told me to wait here for you during Winter Veil. I've already waited many days — I can't wait out the entire holiday."

"Truly sorry about that," Jace said. "Because from Dalaran to here, most of the way we traveled on foot… Only the stretch from Alterac to Durnholde did we have horses, so it took a very long time."

"Lady Sylvanas should have found two hawkstrider riders to bring you. But Dalaran… never mind." Vinaryal shook his head. "If you'd arrived two days later, I would have had to return to Silvermoon City."

"So then, Master Elf — what should we do?" Grede asked eagerly.

"Let me have a look first."

Vinaryal stepped forward, braced Grede's forehead with his hand, and unwrapped the eye patch bound tightly around him.

Being touched by a male elf clearly made Grede about to die of discomfort — he had the look of someone wanting to break free on instinct, but forcing himself to stay as still as possible.

"I don't have much experience treating dwarves," Vinaryal said, examining the dwarf's ruined eye as he spoke. "But I once treated two Bronzebeard dwarves. Two hundred years of separation wouldn't produce an essential difference between your two clans, would it?"

"I'm taller than them. Does that count as a difference?" Grede asked.

"I see no difference," Vinaryal said.

Grede glared wide-eyed at Jace. Jace shook his head helplessly. What do you want? You want me to help you beat him up?

Vinaryal opened a cabinet beside him and pulled out a green cloak. Jace noticed that the layers of decoration on the cloak were actually real blades of grass — and not a single one showed any sign of withering, as if they were growing naturally upon it.

He gave the cloak a gentle shake and draped it over his shoulders. Not a single dead leaf or scrap of twig fell. Those blades of grass looked softer than cloth or wool in his hands.

"Come with me," Vinaryal said, heading for a spiraling staircase.

The two followed him down the spiral staircase to a terrace of the hall, which now sat directly above the pond outside.

The elf walked down the terrace and led the dwarf to the lake shore. The place was quiet and deserted — not even guards lingered here. There was only the calm lake and, not far off, the serene forest.

He paced back and forth for a while, finally stopping on a slab of rock jutting out from the shore.

"Dwarf, no matter what happens in a moment, do not move. Lie here calmly," Vinaryal said.

Grede nodded, hopped onto the rock slab, and lay down flat, his hands pressed tight against his trousers, eyes squeezed shut — he looked just like a mummy.

Jace instinctively took a few steps back and sat on a bluish rock a short distance away, watching the situation.

"If we were in Eversong Forest, everything would be much simpler."

Vinaryal muttered what sounded like a soliloquy, raising both hands flat in front of his chest. Jace suddenly felt a cool wind rush past his ear. He didn't know if it was a coincidence, or if it really had something to do with the elf's gestures.
