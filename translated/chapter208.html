Chapter 208: Jace's Good Brother

The elf closed his eyes and began to chant the incantation. Jace noticed that the lake water had started to glow with a faint light — arcane power, without question — and in the forest, too, arcane glimmers wandered here and there.

By now the wind was blowing harder. Jace was certain these were air currents stirred by the elf's shifting magical power.

Before long, he began to hear a series of howls drifting from the forest — or maybe not animals, maybe the wind… He couldn't say for certain what it was, but it seemed as if the entire dense forest surrounding Quel'Daniel was growing restless.

Then a rather familiar sensation brushed past his ear and neck. It reminded him of the power he'd felt from that sheep when he'd tried Life Drain back in Elwynn Forest.

Could this elf be using the arcane to drain life force — and what was being drained was the entire surrounding forest?

The scale of it was honestly a little terrifying.

Looking again at Vinaryal, he'd already gathered a mass of milky-white energy in his hand. The energy rolled and churned in his palm, releasing a faint greenish gleam even in the night.

His expression could hardly be called relaxed, as if he were struggling against some even more powerful force. And the energy in his hand didn't look particularly obedient to his will either — rather than guiding this energy, he seemed more to be forcing it to obey his commands and execute his intent.

The botanists of Quel'Thalas were also a breed of mages. It was just that most other mages used the arcane to manipulate the elements, while they used the arcane to manipulate life.

When Jace had heard Kafal describe this group of people back then, he'd thought he understood. But only when he actually saw a mage who manipulated life force did he realize the difference was still considerable.

Life force was nowhere near as obedient as he'd imagined. It resisted the botanists' bondage wildly and ceaselessly. But clearly, it could not withstand the oppression of the powerful arcane energy these great mages wielded. With every passing moment, more and more life force submitted to Vinaryal's will.

The surface of the pond began to ripple — whether from the wind or from the magic, there was no telling.

The hawkstrider perched in the tree no longer cared about the goings-on in the forest. Its gaze was fixed on the botanist the entire time.

In contrast to the uneasy forest and the swirling air currents, it showed no surprise or panic from start to finish. The only thing moving on its whole body were those long feathers trembling in the fierce wind.

Looking at Grede again, he was already lying motionless.

Had the botanist pinned him down with some spell? Or was he really that calm?

Just as Jace was watching Grede's reaction, Vinaryal suddenly pulled his hands back and guided that life energy toward the top of Grede's head. The air around them quieted in an instant, and the forest ceased its restlessness.

That was when Jace heard Grede's snoring. He'd fallen asleep…

All right. Jace propped his cheek in his hand. Honestly, the time this was taking really was a bit long. How many more steps did Vinaryal still have to perform, and how much longer the whole process would last — he hadn't the faintest idea.

After that grand, sweeping display just now, the utterly still and waveless scene before him was also starting to wear on Jace's endurance.

After all, the afternoon's scares and shocks had really taken it out of him.

With that thought, he tilted his head and passed out on the ground.

Morning. Sunlight from the Hinterlands shone on his face. Jace opened his eyes and sat up — he'd actually fallen asleep by the water.

There wasn't a soul in the small square of Quel'Daniel. Jace stood up, checked his luggage to make sure nothing was missing, then walked groggily up to the front of that hall.

"Excuse me, where is Master Vinaryal Sunseeker?" Jace asked the guard before him.

"He left not long ago, human." The elf pointed into the distance. "Your friend is in that forest over there. Remember to return Lady Wrynneldess Swiftblade's bow — she's told me about it several times."

"Wrynneldess… all right, I remember."

Jace acknowledged it as he spoke, glancing toward the fence behind the hall. He spotted that the hawkstrider that had been resting there earlier was already gone. Following the direction the elf had pointed, he entered the forest. After just a few steps, he heard the thwip of an arrow burying itself in wood.

"Grede?"

"Over here."

Jace walked toward the sound and saw the dwarf holding a bow, aiming at a tree right beside him.

With a thwip, the arrow plunged into the trunk. When Jace went to pull it out, he saw the spot where the arrow had struck was already chiseled into a complete mess.

He looked at Grede and saw that he wasn't wearing his eye patch. He was just looking at him — with both eyes.

For a moment, Jace even felt as if the dwarf had become a little unfamiliar. He was especially unaccustomed to this guy looking at him with both eyes at once, as if a chunk had suddenly appeared on his head — extremely jarring.

"Bring the arrow here, Jace." Grede beckoned. "Let me show you something…"

Jace nodded and handed the arrow to him, asking, "How do you feel?"

"How do I feel?"

Grede laughed, then backed up more than ten paces, gazing at that tree. His expression suddenly turned cold.

He nocked and released without hesitation, almost without aiming. The arrow whooshed out with a scream of split air, gouging viciously into that already perforated spot. The whole tree swayed, looking like it was about to give out.

"If I ran into that orc right now, things might just turn out differently, Jace Sesso."

Grede's tone was even a touch vicious. He looked at Jace with a cold expression. "That bastard — I really want to fight him once more."

"You're still hung up on that, huh?" Jace said. "He might be dead for all we know."

"Best if he is," Grede said. "Otherwise I'll make him understand that a Wildhammer dwarf's hatred is far more terrifying than Gul'dan's curse."

Jace swallowed. "He doesn't understand it yet, but I'm starting to feel it."

Grede said, "Come here."

"What for?" Jace walked over.

"Bend down."

"Fine."

Grede threw his arms around Jace's shoulders and punched him hard in the back, nearly making Jace cough up blood.

Not only was the bastard's eye healed — his strength had increased quite a bit too.

"Good brother, Jace. Before I met you, I never thought I'd ever see out of this eye again — truly never thought it. Without you, I absolutely would not have made it this far."

He let go, grabbed Jace by the collar, and said, "From now on, no matter what trouble comes, no matter how terrifying the danger — orcs, ogres, black dragons, red dragons, any damn dragon — you only need to say the word, and I'll charge straight through the Dark Portal without a moment's hesitation."

"No need for such dramatics. I want to buy my parents a house in Goldshire or Stormwind — just help me cover some of the cost when the time comes." Jace said.

"Hack — cough! Cough!"

Grede was suddenly racked by a fit of coughing, sounding like he was about to cough his lungs out.

He propped himself against a small tree beside him, clutching his neck, and said with difficulty, "That elf… aah! Did he put shadow power into the spell? So he's actually a warlock… We've been duped, damn it!"

Jace sighed. "Is that really necessary…"

Grede looked up. "You're buying your parents a house and you need someone else to foot the bill? Then your sentiment loses its value, Jace. Your parents wouldn't be happy either."

"I'll definitely pay you back. It's just in case there's not enough time, to tide things over…"

"I know! Let's talk about it later… we'll figure it out! Now then — let's hurry up and find a griffin! Back to Southshore!"

"What about the Dark Portal? Didn't you say you'd charge through the Dark Portal too?"

"So what does buying a house have to do with the Dark Portal?"

"You… ugh. Right, your bow! Give the bow back to those elves."

"No need to return it! She owes me one."
