## Chapter 209: The Natives

After leaving Quel'Daniel, Jace trailed Grede to a gentle slope.

It was thick with fir trees, and sudden shrieks rang out now and then from within the forest.

Those must be griffins, right?

But Jace could only hear them, never see them. He spent the whole way looking up, following the sounds, searching for a griffin's trace — and saw nothing at all.

Grede walked out into a clearing, slung the bow over his back, and waved a hand signaling Jace to hang back.

Jace stopped not far off and watched to see what he was going to do.

The dwarf looked up at the pale sky, puffed out his chest, and blew an extraordinarily loud, long whistle.

The whistle wound through several turns, then let off a series of short bursts, ending in a sudden sharp note.

After blowing it, he took a deep breath and patted his chest, looking like he was getting ready to fight.

Before long, a rumbling sound came from the sky, like a motorcycle engine. A black shadow trampled across several tree trunks and flapped its enormous wings as it landed in front of the dwarf. It let out several sharp cries at Grede, but kept a wary distance the whole time, as if trying to scare him away. This was a rather large griffin — its head about as tall as Jace.

It was deep grey all over, with only the feathers ringing its neck tinged silver. It gave off impatient barking cries while keeping one claw leveled at Grede the whole time.

Though it wasn't as large overall as that white griffin ridden by the Kirin Tor mage, according to Grede, griffins living in the Hinterlands were generally smaller than those in Alterac. This Hinterlands griffin was already bigger than any of its kind Jace had seen before.

Grede said something in Dwarvish. The griffin grew visibly more agitated, shaking its head a few steps and backing up further.

Unexpectedly, not only did Grede not grow anxious or displeased at the griffin's reaction — he threw down his shield and hammer, roared a few sentences in Dwarvish, and actually started laughing as he roared.

The griffin let out a string of low *coo-coo-coo* sounds, then flapped its wings and charged straight at Grede. Jace's heart clenched at the sight.

But seeing Grede's delighted expression, he realized this was probably what Grede had been looking for too, so he didn't step in to help.

Grede met the charging griffin head-on. As it raised its upper body, he seized its two front claws with both hands, precisely on target!

The griffin shrilly shrieked in agitation, trying to flap its wings to batter the dwarf's head. Grede roared back, dodging the heavy wing strikes while grappling with the beast.

Such a massive creature — its center of gravity seemed less steady than the dwarf's. After a tussle, its hind legs slipped and it toppled sideways. But as its body fell, the sheer weight dragged the dwarf down and slammed him to the ground.

The griffin was clearly enraged. It beat its wings, stood up, and swiped with a claw. The dwarf curled up and dodged. The griffin then kicked off its hind legs and lunged to bite.

Grede nimbly dodged the pecking beak and threw his arms straight around its neck. The griffin began leaping in place on the ground, struggling to throw the dwarf off. But the dwarf held on tight — all that struggling was in vain.

Jace had never doubted Grede's strength, but he hadn't expected the griffin to have even more explosive power. It suddenly cranked its neck in, its whole body whipping around in a spin. Its neck snapped back like a spring and flung Grede clean off onto the ground a short distance away.

The griffin didn't press the attack. Grede climbed up from a pile of leaves, wiped his face, and glanced at his palm — he seemed to have been clawed somewhere.

Just when Jace thought he'd been beaten, the griffin backed up several steps, scratching at the dirt and fallen leaves with its claw while chittering in complaint nonstop.

Grede walked over, panting hard, his face and arms already bearing several cuts. He smiled and said, "Let's go, get on. She's agreed to carry me. But when we get to Southshore, we'll need to buy her some meat to eat."

"That simple?" Jace asked.

"Simple?!" Grede scowled and stomped a few steps toward him, clenching his fist. "Come here, stick your head out, let me show you my strength now, and then use that as a reference for how ferocious she is."

Jace laughed and dodged to the side. "Never mind, hah, sorry. I can't read how you communicate with griffins either."

"Quit stalling." Grede pointed over there. "Get on and try. You've always wanted to ride a griffin, haven't you? Now this fine lady is right in front of you!"

Hearing Grede's words, Jace looked at the griffin before him. Though he got the feeling the griffin's gaze toward him wasn't quite right, he couldn't suppress the excitement in his heart.

This was a real griffin… And more importantly, this was a griffin waiting for him to climb on!

Jace carefully walked a few steps closer. The griffin gave an uneasy *whoosh-whoosh*, its four thick claws — half cat, half eagle — scratching at the ground incessantly, seeming to want to back away.

He glanced at Grede. The dwarf's confident-looking smile seemed to have faded a little too.

"Go on, standing there like an idiot — the griffin's not gonna come carry you herself."

Jace nodded and stepped forward again. This time the griffin reacted more violently, letting out a continuous "ha-ha-ha" sound.

Given his many years of cat-keeping experience in his past life, this was definitely not a good sign.

But this wasn't Earth, after all, and griffins were a far cry from cats. He couldn't just back down because of some utterly irrelevant experience, could he?

Steeling his heart, Jace reached straight for the feathers on the griffin's back. He hadn't expected that just as his hand was about to make contact, the griffin's feathers would explode on end and it would leap aside, screaming at Jace without pause.

It puffed up its neck feathers and suddenly lunged at Jace. Jace hastily backed up several steps.

Fortunately, it probably only wanted to scare him — it didn't ram him outright. But this was more than enough to make its rejection clear.

"What… what's going on…"

Even as the words left his mouth, Jace wasn't surprised in the slightest.

After all, he was a warlock. Even a furbolg — an intelligent creature — could sense the taint on him. It just didn't think Jace meant any harm, so it hadn't made a move.

A griffin, though an exceedingly intelligent beast, was still a beast. It would instinctively reject anything unfriendly.

He'd actually taken this into account before coming to the Lordaeron continent. He'd just been delusional enough to hope the griffin wouldn't notice.


So in the end, he still wouldn't get the chance to climb on?

Jace felt a little disappointed. If he hadn't learned dark magic back then, would he have been allowed?

But if he hadn't learned dark magic, he wouldn't have made it this far either. Could he have survived to this day on nothing but the Light cantrip?

The dwarf said nothing. He walked over, grabbed the feathers on the griffin's neck, as if trying to tell her something. The griffin kept trying to shake free, backing up step by step, like a mare in a temper.

"Forget it… Grede, don't force her either," Jace said helplessly.

Unexpectedly, Grede burst out laughing.

"Hah! She wants to duel you too, Jace. You thought it was simple — why don't you give it a go yourself?"

"Can I beat her?"

"You still want to ride or not?"

At Grede's question, Jace's heart suddenly sank.

"In that case — bring it on, then!"
