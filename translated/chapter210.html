Chapter 210: Between Monsters

Jace looked at the griffin, set down his pack and cloak — damage to either of them would be a financial loss he couldn't afford.

He strode up to the beast with a swagger. Seeing his manner, the griffin stopped barking too, slowly folding its wings and letting out a low, rumbling oooh-oooh, oooh-oooh — like the engine of a sports car starting up.

He copied Grede's move and tossed his staff to the side.

Fighting a beast as tall as himself bare-handed? This felt like the brain-dead move of someone looking to die.

Unexpectedly, the griffin walked over to the staff, picked it up in its beak, and tossed it back toward Jace. Jace caught it. The griffin even shook its head with a touch of arrogance… Was this creature looking down on him?

Grede couldn't help but laugh. "She thinks you're too weak. She doesn't want to bully you."

Jace shot Grede a quick look, then fixed his eyes back on the griffin. Though he was a bit unconvinced in his heart, he also knew that if a monster this big was bent on killing him, one pounce and a claw would probably finish him.

Having a staff to fend her off — and a reliable demonic staff at that — was far better than bare hands.

But if it really came to a fight to the death, and he used shadow magic… whether Fear or Curse of Weakness, the beast probably wouldn't have much means to resist.

But would he really use those spells? Just to win? Jace understood that if he wanted this griffin genuinely convinced, he had to defeat her with pure physical strength — even if the odds weren't exactly high.

But if he really used shadow power, it might not only truly provoke her hatred — it could enrage every griffin in the forest. Grede might even think he was a man who'd stop at nothing.

Jace leveled his staff at the beast before him, ready to meet the charge. Seeing that Jace wasn't trying to cast and was instead fighting with it like a stick, the griffin clearly let out two excited cries.

The battle began without warning. It raised its upper body and beat both wings with a boom. Jace nearly got dust and leaves flung into his eyes. The griffin seized the chance and pounced straight through the gap!

This third-rate trick wasn't much different from tossing lime in someone's face. Jace had overestimated the creature's sense of honor a bit.

But Jace, who'd been scrapping in the streets since childhood, certainly wouldn't be caught out so easily. Hearing the boom of wind, he nimbly dodged to the side, and the griffin's pounce hit nothing but air.

He thrust his staff hard into the shadow within the dust, feeling it connect with something solid.

He shoved fiercely. The griffin clearly hadn't expected Jace to have so much strength. It howled and stumbled a step to the side.

But it quickly adjusted its posture and swatted the staff aside with a claw, its body lunging forward with the aid of its wings, trying to grab Jace. Jace gave way, watching the sharp claws rake past the side of his eyes — one careless move and he'd be blind.

The griffin's momentum was too great. In the blink of an eye it was already upon him, its back wide open. Jace prepared to land another hit on its hind legs — but before he could, the griffin's long tail, from God-knows-where, whipped across his neck!

This blow was even harsher than a lash on a sheep. It made his head swim and his vision blur, his whole neck to chest burning with pain.

The griffin sprang up, whirled around, and savagely snapped a bite — barely blocked by Jace's staff.

The beast clamped down on the staff and wrenched it hard. The staff was flung clean away on the spot. Jace thought: this is it.

But as he stared at the staff, even as the thought crossed his mind, the staff was suddenly wrenched back by some strange force, plunging straight into the dirt beside Jace's hand!

He reached out and grabbed it without thinking. The glow from the shadow gem atop the staff shone straight through the tattered cloth of Gorefiend's cloak!

The griffin let out a bizarre shriek and leaped away, barking at Jace like mad. Gripping the staff, Jace realized that in order to return to his side, the staff had probably triggered some shadow energy — and that had displeased the griffin.

Had this staff really taken him as its master?

Actually, he'd already realized this the moment Gorefiend failed to snatch it away with magic. Only today did he confirm exactly what this blood-stained bond meant.

But amid the excitement, he still set the staff aside.

Jace looked back at the griffin, both hands empty, signaling that he wouldn't use shadow magic again.

But the griffin, panting hard, was also giving off a low, warning growl — clearly somewhat enraged.

It actually raised a wing and bounded forward in a leap, both claws stamping down at once, trying once more to pin its opponent. Jace was of course prepared too, slipping between its claws to beneath its belly, then darting off to the side.

This huge, not-agile creature clearly couldn't quite keep up with the rhythm. It glanced under itself and, for a moment, had no idea where Jace had slipped off to.

Jace seized the chance to grab the griffin's wing and try to leap onto its back. The griffin shrieked, spun around, and flung its wing with the spin!

The wing swept in a far wider arc than Jace could hold on against. Having never received griffin-rider training, much less fought a beast of this size bare-handed, he lost his grip entirely and was flung out before he could react.

But the griffin didn't let up the way it had with Grede. It flapped both wings and charged in head-low!

Jace barely scrambled to his feet in the messy pile of shredded leaves when the griffin had already crashed before him. He threw himself to the side to dodge the blow, then jumped a step further to avoid the long tail that whipped around in the stumble — nearly falling flat. The griffin caught Jace's loss of balance, turned, and beat its wings hard. The blast of air slammed into Jace and almost knocked him off his feet. Seizing the opening, the griffin lunged and shoved Jace to the ground at last — it had finally gotten its way!

Jace was smashed into the dirt by the beast. If this had been a brick-and-tile floor, the impact probably would have cracked the back of his skull.

The griffin was clearly swollen with fury. Every feather on its body trembled. It hissed viciously, claws slamming down hard. Jace grabbed her claws directly with both hands.

Jace exerted every ounce of his strength to keep it from raking his face. If it caught his face, he'd probably be blinded and disfigured. The griffin's claws, spread wide, were almost as big as his entire front torso, each hook curved like an exaggerated scimitar.

And yet the two struggled against each other for a while, and soon the griffin stopped shrieking. Jace couldn't feel the force pressing down on him anymore.

It stared at Jace for a moment, flapped a wing, and leaped away. Blasts of wind sent dust, dirt, and shredded leaves into Jace's nose, mouth, and eyes. A round of coughing and sneezing took a while before he finally found his bearings and stood up.

This time he was truly spent.

But the griffin seemed to have no intention of attacking again. It let out a whoosh-coo, whoosh-coo cry and backed up several steps in a row.

Jace dusted off his body and face, gasping great lungfuls of air — air that no longer stank of griffin musk. "What's she doing?"

"She's convinced," Grede said. "A griffin only wants to know one thing — whether you can be a comrade, Jace. So long as she can trust you, she'll agree to let you ride on her back. There's no need to actually win."

"You could've told me earlier…" Jace said, drained.

Grede folded his arms and explained. "If I'd told you earlier, would you have fought her with everything you had? Griffins are combative creatures. If you don't give it your all, she'll get angry."

"Honestly, it's the first time I've ever seen a human match a griffin in raw strength, Jace. Of course, I have no doubt that great warriors like Danath Trollbane or Anduin Lothar could take this griffin down bare-handed — but you're far too young, and you definitely haven't undergone any rigorous strength training either. Or is it that Lordaeron men can tap into that kind of potential in a moment of crisis? Some thousands-of-years-old Alterac warrior bloodline, that sort of thing…"

"I suspect it has something to do with that black dragon," Jace said, propping himself on his knees. "That time in the tower, when I drained energy from that dragon to save you — it may have had an effect on me too, not all of it coming back up."

"Black dragon's blood?" Grede asked in surprise. "Why didn't you mention this at all before?"

"I'm not certain either," Jace said. "But like you said, I haven't undergone any particularly rigorous training, and I have no other means of strengthening my physical body. If it were me from two months ago, I absolutely wouldn't believe I could arm-wrestle this griffin."

"Ah… if that's the case." Grede nodded. "I've also heard tales of people seeking dragon blood to gain greater strength — but those stories always end with them finally finding the dragon blood, drinking it, and then dying. None of them come to a good end. Maybe it's because you're always using shadow power, so you're especially compatible with black dragon blood too?"

What Grede had heard were probably stories crafted or embellished by the ancestors, meant to admonish people not to blindly pursue every dark path for the sake of power — so they deliberately showcased some irreversible, horrifying consequences.

But the idea that drinking dragon blood would kill you was by no means an exaggeration.

Jace had read in the alchemy books at the Wizard Sanctum that dragon blood couldn't be drunk directly without specialized processing. Black dragon blood especially — if an ordinary person drank it, they'd die in a truly miserable fashion.

Even drinking the blood of other dragons would very likely cost you your life. And even if you miraculously survived, you'd be left with severe lifelong aftereffects, even disability.

When the dragons created the drakekin legions, that also required a specialized ascension ritual — not having the mortals who accepted the promotion recklessly drink the blood unprepared.

The only creature recorded in the books that had drunk dragon blood without severe consequences was the fin-fish of the Wetlands. The beast seemed to have evolved a fearsome stomach capable of digesting red dragon blood.

Jace had drained the energy from the black dragon blood through dark magic. If he'd been forced to drink it straight, his fate probably wouldn't have been much better.

He dusted off his clothes and said, "I'm not sure either. If I'd known, I'd have steeled myself and drained a bit more, instead of pulling my hand back the moment I saw that dragon go down."

Grede asked again, "Then if you kept finding black dragons to drain, would you eventually turn into a real black dragon?"

Jace glanced at Grede. He thought that if he kept this up, at best he'd end up as a mindless, dragon-headed humanoid freak taking orders from the black dragon — and then Nefarian would lock him up in a dungeon to be a raid boss for players to farm.

Of course, telling Grede all of this now really was too early.

But it did remind him of that dragon dream… The figures in the dream — whether Nefarian or Deathwing himself — represented the possibility that his mind really had been influenced a little by the dragon blood.

Right now, Jace still didn't have the slightest feeling of worship or anything toward black dragons. But that didn't mean that if he kept draining, nothing would change.

All he could say was, "How many black dragons are there lined up waiting for me to drain them? This one nearly cost me my life."

Grede laughed. "Whatever the case, Jace — with strength this great, maybe if you train for a while you could be nearly a match for a griffin rider."

"Is there any chance of becoming a real griffin rider?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "No no no — Aerie Peak only trains dwarves."

Jace said, "Hah, maybe in the future they'll train anyone for money. Tell me — one gold per person, would you train them?"

Grede steeled himself and said, "Absolutely not!"

Jace snorted and walked up to the griffin's side, reaching out to touch her feathers. The griffin cooed — but this time she didn't shy away at all.

So did she acknowledge his strength, or trust that he wouldn't use dark magic to harm her?

If only this griffin could talk.
