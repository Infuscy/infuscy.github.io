Chapter 211: Happy Flight

Grede swung himself up onto the gryphon's back. "Up you get, Jace. No saddle, just some straps I brought, so for the sake of your life, you hold on tight."

Jace climbed up. The gryphon beneath him felt like an oversize, high-powered massage chair — the slightest shift and he felt like he was about to topple over. Once they took off, wouldn't he just get flung right off?

"What am I supposed to hold…"

"Whatever you grab, just don't grab me!" Grede said. "Or we both say bye-bye, and I won't even be able to fish you out."

"This is way too hard." Jace hesitated. "How about you sit in back and let me hug the gryphon's neck? You're a rider — you're way steadier than me!"

"Worth a try," the dwarf said.

Jace switched to the front and wrapped his arms tight around the gryphon's neck. The gryphon rumbled once and the vibration tickled through his whole body. Grede cinched the straps and bellowed something in Dwarvish. The gryphon spread its wings, and Jace's head swam…

"Oh gods… oh gods…" So Grede couldn't handle it either!

Jace had already had a bad feeling about this — but the feeling didn't even survive two seconds in his head. Just when Jace thought they'd taken off, the trunks and canopies around them seemed to get yanked away, dropping and vanishing without a trace.

That alone nearly knocked him out. But then came an even more outrageous spinning… the whole world churned into a stew — green, blue, black, yellow…

He didn't dare do anything except cling to the gryphon's neck. He'd actually thought he'd get to enjoy the beautiful sights of the Hinterlands from up here. Now forget the scenery — the sudden acceleration made him feel lucky he wasn't just dropping dead from a burst blood vessel on the spot.

Grede was yelling. The gryphon was screaming. Jace tried to shout but no sound came out, the howling wind battering him so hard he could barely breathe, nearly suffocating him.

He barely recovered his senses and ducked behind the gryphon's neck to gasp a few breaths. Even through his haze, the ground flashing at the edge of his vision told him they were covering ground fast.

He didn't know how long the torment lasted. By the time the gryphon touched down, Jace had nearly lost all sense of space and time.

He slid off the gryphon, and when his hands touched the ground it didn't feel like landing — it felt like being scooped up by a giant flatboard, swaying this way and that, unable to steady himself no matter what. He drifted in a daze for a long while before finally finding the feeling of solid ground beneath his feet.

Once his senses returned to normal, the very first thing he did was vomit. Jace collapsed onto the dirt road and heaved wildly, couldn't care less about the crowd of onlookers and gasping bystanders. Just. Vomiting.

He heaved until his belly felt like it was being scooped out from the inside. When there was truly nothing left to throw up, he leaned against the nearby wall and stared blankly ahead — at the passersby pointing their way, the children gasping at the gryphon, the soldiers stopping to stare. The bizarre noises they made should have been deafening, but to Jace they sounded eerily muffled, like voices underwater.

Not just the people — the air itself seemed to have frozen. Everything he looked at seemed locked in place, totally devoid of motion.

Jace realized he still hadn't recovered from the mad spinning and speed of the flight.

Grede plopped down beside him, his gaze just as vacant and wooden.

The black gryphon bounded and leaped over to the two of them, letting out unending screeches. Grede could only barely keep trying to soothe it in Dwarvish, but the gryphon seemed completely unresponsive — now butting him with its head, now swiping him with its tail.

Jace swallowed and managed, "Where are we?"

Grede squinted, waving a hand to try and catch the gryphon's tail. "Obviously… Southshore."

"I wouldn't have recognized this as Southshore," Jace said.

"You see those charred houses? Over there?" Grede threw a punch at the gryphon's foreclaw, then pointed at a building's rubble nearby being cleared by several workers. "This is Southshore."

Jace nodded, watching the waves of nausea roll over the dwarf. "Aren't you a gryphon rider? Why are you this miserable too?"

"She hasn't had a lick of training, Jace Sesso." Grede cleanly caught the gryphon's tail. "You've ridden a horse — even an old pack horse isn't exactly a joyride, let alone a wild one. And we're riding a gryphon, one of the worst-tempered mounts in the world, not just wild but without a saddle… I'm not… doing that again. I've never ridden a wild gryphon that far — in the past I only ever tried it for fun over a short stretch. I thought I could just fly her straight to Southshore. I admit it now — that's not happening. One more time and I'm dead. This is a one-time deal. We're never doing this again."

"You're a trained dwarf riding an untrained gryphon," Jace said, then tried to poke his own chest for emphasis and poked himself in the lip instead.

"And I'm an untrained human riding an untrained gryphon."

"At least you get to hug its neck," Grede said, the gryphon rubbing its head against his face while he tried to grab its neck and could only be bounced up and down. "While I just get to swing back and forth. Count yourself lucky…"

"What's it… doing?" Jace asked.

Just then a cart rolled past. The gryphon suddenly jerked its head up and sent Grede tumbling to the ground.

The two horses up front both reared and whinnied in terror. The driver shouted in panic, "Quick — don't let that bird-headed monster look over here! You damned Wildhammer dwarves trying to kill me?!"

"She wants meat!" Grede scrambled to his feet, leaped up, and clamped his arms around the gryphon's head, covering her eyes. "We'd better get her some fast, or she's going to eat someone's horses…"

The two of them fussed for quite a while before they finally calmed the gryphon down, buying some raw meat from a butcher to feed her her fill.

After they sent her off, the next few days were just waiting for the ship to arrive.

The spider silk they'd gathered in the Hinterlands they sold to a cloth merchant in Southshore for ten silver. It took asking several shops before they realized that collecting spider silk was a professional trade — for an ordinary person, just cleaning off the impurities and dust was a considerable cost in itself.

Still, ten silver was enough to recoup a bit of their living expenses for the past few days.

Jace practiced the Mage Armor incantation, draining the last of his mana potions in the process.

By the last evening, he could basically cast a Mage Armor — lasting roughly five hours — successfully within twenty or thirty seconds.

But a problem came with it: the shimmering traces on the Mage Armor, even after fading gradually post-cast, never fully disappeared.

If he took his clothes off, the layer of arcane-tamed elemental shimmer would crawl faintly across his skin; if he put them back on, it would slowly float back up to the surface.

Indoors it wasn't that noticeable, but outside it drew attention like a beacon, making him look thoroughly strange.

Grede, meanwhile, completely let himself go, eating and drinking heartily every night. Fortunately, at this time of year in this little town, there wasn't anything so extravagant as to bankrupt him, so in the end he didn't spend all that much.

On the fifth of January, before dawn, they boarded the ship back to Stormwind. Jace Sesso stood on the deck gazing back at the coastline, thinking that arriving here over a month ago already felt like a past life.

Back then all he'd thought about was saving up enough to survive. The Scourge, demon invasions, the Black Dragonflight's rampage… all of it had felt like it had absolutely nothing to do with a little apprentice like him.

Now, he couldn't scrub those things from his mind anymore.

Jace glanced at the top of the staff cradled in his arms: through the gap in Teron Gorefiend's tattered yellow cloak-wrapping, the surface of that shadow gem was giving off a faint, eerie dark glow.

He tugged the cloth edge by the gap lower and headed toward the hatch.
