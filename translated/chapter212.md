## Chapter 212: End of Vacation

No matter how interesting a journey might be, the first trip could feel novel, the second might be full of fond memories, and the third… well, it got boring.

Let alone a voyage at sea, where all you saw was blue.

During the crossing, the thought crossed Jace's mind more than once: he hoped some kind of tentacled monster would surface to kill the time.

Of course, a second later he always decided that was probably for the best.

This was a Kul Tiras merchant vessel. It didn't stop at Menethil Harbor either, and it was considerably faster than that goblin ship — exactly what Jace had arranged when he'd asked Instructor Dushan to help him book a berth.

The only downside was that Grede and Jace were among the very few passengers on board. Eighty percent of the people on the ship were Kul Tiras sailors, and their Common was ridiculously hard to understand. The two of them simply gave up trying to communicate.

As if the journey wasn't miserable enough already, it became even more oppressive. Within a few days, the faint homesickness of leaving home had been completely melted away by the torment of the ship.

Good thing the ship was fast. They reached the port of Stormwind by the afternoon of the sixteenth.

When he caught sight of the familiar spire of the Cathedral of Light in the distance, Jace felt like he was laying eyes on family.

Walking down onto the dock, a surge of uncontainable relief rushed over him. He raised both hands, palms out, tilted his chin up slightly, and roared toward the golden sunlight reflecting off the cathedral—

"Ah… praise the sun… praise the Holy Light!"

"Long live the Holy Light…"

"The Holy Light is above!"

"The Holy Light watches over me."

Responses came from everywhere.

Grede walked down from the ship carrying his bags, glanced at Jace, and said, "What, you planning to go work for the Cathedral, so you're getting your practice in early?"

Jace shaded his eyes against the sun and looked out at the not-too-busy port, at the Stormwind soldiers in their blue cloaks. "I'm just thanking the Holy Light for giving us a smooth voyage!"

"You? Sincerely thanking the Holy Light? Pull the other one," Grede muttered. "I've known you a long time. You're the type who's 'the Holy Light is above!' when it suits him, and 'sod the Holy Light!' when it doesn't."

"Are you insane?" Jace glanced around to make sure no one had heard. "You don't want to be a priest anymore?"

"What kind of priest would I be now?" Grede patted the hammer hanging from his belt. "The Holy Light doesn't favor me, and the Holy Light's servants can't be bothered with me either. So I'm stuck on this old path! Go grab your books and whatnot — lugging them around this whole trip has been exhausting."

"Thanks a ton," Jace said, and hauled out his wooden box.

The two of them divided up the already-messy luggage on the dock, then sat on the wooden piles of the pier and rested a while.

The sun set fast — the sky had already darkened compared to a moment ago.

"Still up for a drink tonight?" Jace asked.

"I'm done. Tomorrow or the day after. Right now I just want to crash — I don't even want dinner."

Grede muttered a few words, braced his knees, and stood up.

"I'm heading home to see if that family's moved out yet. You head back too. See you around."

Hearing that, Jace felt his own wave of drowsiness hit. He could only wave and force himself to his feet.

After all, he couldn't fall asleep on the dock — not with the valuables he was carrying right now. No room for carelessness.

In the evening, Jace wandered back to his rented room in a daze. He opened the door, carefully sniffed the air inside — no strange odors. He checked every corner. Nothing wrong.

The house had been empty for less than two months. Aside from a layer of dust, nothing had changed at all.

Thank the Light.

In his past life, he'd left home for two months and come back to find the bathroom light still on. He'd wanted to check whether it had been turned on when he walked in and he'd forgotten, so he'd touched it — and burned his hand so badly it blistered. That agonizing memory still hung over him like a dark cloud in this life too.

He put his luggage in order, tidied up the room, did a quick cleaning, and lay down on the cold bed.

Thinking back on everything that had happened on this trip to the Lordaeron continent, it felt hazy — like he'd heard someone else tell the story rather than lived it himself.

He lifted his cloak and looked at the gouges clawed into it by that panther, and the patch where the dragon had burned it — the color was clearly uneven, as if someone had splashed a layer of ink across it.

He opened his shirt and looked at the hideous scar on his shoulder. He reached out and touched it — there was still a faint, lingering pain.

Even now, he still couldn't quite believe he'd actually driven off Teron Gorefiend…

If Gorefiend had abandoned his body in Dalaran, where would he go next? Flee back to the Dark Portal in his spirit form, or hide away in some unknown corner and wait for a chance to be reborn?

Would the bastard hold a grudge and find a way to come looking for trouble?

Returning to the Dark Portal should be a far higher priority for him than getting hung up on Jace.

As long as Deathwing or Kel'Thuzad helped him get the Eye of Dalaran, he'd immediately find a way to return to the Horde's current leader, Ner'zhul. That was the logical move.

"Sazeno, you can come out!"

"Master…" the fire imp poked his head out of the bag. "Where did that fat guy go?"

"You won't be seeing him for a while." Jace lay on the bed, staring at the ceiling. "Listen — next time you see him, don't say anything provocative. Don't deliberately piss him off."

"I know, I know!" the imp said. "It's just that guy was such a pain. If it weren't for him, Master would be way stronger by now!"

"I'm sure if it weren't for him I'd be dead," Jace said, folding his arms. "And you'd still be a slave and servant to whatever lord of yours, liable to be wiped out at any moment. You want to go back?"

"Of course not!" the imp said with a grin. "Ah, so that fat dwarf isn't completely useless after all! But Master, why did you call me out here?"

"To get back to work, obviously," Jace said. "Now I've got money to buy paper and ink. Your job is to write down everything you've memorized for me — help me master the demon tongue!"

"Got it…" the imp asked with a long face, "Starting work right now?"

"Tomorrow," Jace said. "Tomorrow I'll come back from the Wizard Sanctum and bring you all the tools you'll need. Then you can get started. You've got to be serious and persistent — meticulous! The day I use the demon tongue to master powerful magic, you'll benefit too. Your treatment will definitely be better than it is now."

"What treatme—"

"For now, the next time we go out, I'll see if I can let you walk around outside on your own instead of staying in the bag all the time," Jace said. "But you'll have to be even better at hiding yourself by then."

"Ah! Really?!" the imp said. "I get to move around freely too?"

"Freely? Don't push it. I'd like to be free too."

"No! I think Master is especially free!"

"Stop. Don't start talking about freedom again, I'm going to sleep." Jace turned his head to the side. "You want to stay out, stay out. Just don't leave the room, hide if anyone comes near, and don't wake me up."

"Understood, Master!"
