## Chapter 215: New "Trainee"

Jace brought Vereesa back to the Mage Quarter and had her wait in the gardens below the Wizard Sanctum for a while.

He'd been planning to look for Amy, but had no sooner started up the stairs when he spotted old Marin coming out of the tower.

"Master! Over here — me, Jace! Jace Sesso!"

Jace squeezed past clusters of mages along the spiraling walkway until he reached Marin's side. Marin finally managed to make out who was calling him.

He pushed his glasses up. "I was starting to think you weren't planning to come back. I heard something major happened in Dalaran? I haven't had the chance to go ask Caro Dushan about it yet."

"It was quite major, Master. I'll tell you the full story later," Jace said. "But actually, I wanted to ask — where is my apprentice's apartment? I should have my own apartment, right…"

Marin looked the student up and down. "Don't you not live in the apartment? Amy specifically asked you about it back then."

"I do! Back then I was still just an assistant — I barely counted as a mage at all."

"Had a change of heart? Good thing I haven't canceled it yet this year. Want to take a look?"

"Yes, yes."

"Fine." Marin pulled a small ledger out of his robe, flipping through it as he muttered. "This is the advantage of coming back a little early, Mr. Sesso — everything can be handled, everything can be arranged in time."

Hearing that the room was still available, Jace felt a huge weight lift off him.

If this hadn't panned out, he probably would have had to go to Grede — and the area where he lived had long since been packed full. There was no way he'd find a vacant room.

But getting Grede to house an elf in the Dwarf District? That'd be a volcanic eruption, a thunderbolt out of nowhere, a lump of sodium dropped into water.

Even if Grede bore Vereesa a debt of gratitude, none of the other folks in the Dwarf District would care who she was — and then it definitely wouldn't count as "safe."

Marin pointed at an address in the small ledger. "See it? The key — ask the old lady who watches the apartment. If she's not around, it should be a heavyset gentleman with a northern accent. And remember to go register at Archmage Andormas's office hall either this afternoon or tomorrow. There'll be quite a crowd — don't be late."

"Got it," Jace said. "Thank you, Master."

"Don't mention it. Just study hard and don't lose face for the King. Oh — where's your robe?" Marin adjusted his glasses.

"Bad luck. A death knight burned it."

The old mage heard that and went still, staring into Jace's eyes for a good while before speaking. "I really should hear the full story one of these days, Mr. Sesso."

"Yes, Master. I'll take my leave!"

With that Jace turned and threaded back through the clusters of mages to the gardens below, finding Vereesa in front of him. She looked like she was about to fall asleep — propping her chin in her hand, fighting to keep her eyes open — and only managed to lift her head when she heard someone approaching.

"Got a place to stay. I'll lead the way."

Hearing that, perked up quite a bit.

Jace led her following the address Marin had given him, walking deep into the Mage Quarter. The Slaughtered Lamb was just ahead on another side street — its sign flashed past.

The thought of that whole crew of shady characters waiting for him in the basement of that place made his heart sink.

Good thing his apartment wasn't in that neighborhood. They walked a bit further before finally arriving at this cream-colored building with green tiles.

It was much quieter here than around the Wizard Sanctum. Jace pushed open the building door — it creaked — revealing a narrow stairwell. Whether you looked up or down, it felt like there was barely enough room for a single person to squeeze through, as if the whole space had been compressed into a single thin crack.

Beside him, an old lady pushed open a small side door. "Are you an apprentice, or an apprentice mage?"

Jace made a little room for the small door. "I'm an apprentice, ma'am."

"Ring." The old lady beckoned with her fingers.

Jace showed her his ring. The old lady grabbed Jace's finger and squinted at it for a while before letting go.

She went back through the small side door, rummaged around for something, then shuffled back and pressed a key into Jace's hand. "Fourth floor, number twenty-one. Go on up. Ah — that's an elf? There are elves coming to study magic at the Wizard Sanctum too? Isn't Quel'Thalas far better?"

"We're all the Alliance of Lordaeron now, ma'am," Jace said. "Thank you."

With that, he led Vereesa single file up the stairs, one after the other. Walking behind him, Vereesa whispered: "The humans here are quite friendly toward the Quel'dorei… Before this, Alleria told me that humans outside Dalaran were all hostile toward us, and to be careful."

"And yet she's going to marry a Lordaeron noble?" Jace asked.

"You know about that too?"

Jace looked back. "Not just me — the whole Alliance knows."

Vereesa said with some concern, "I don't think that's a good idea."

Jace asked, "You still want to manage your sister's affairs?"

"I don't dare manage her. It just feels off," Vereesa said. "But it's not as if I have any say in it now."

"You can talk about it again once you can beat her — at the very least you'd have to beat Turalyon first," Jace joked.

"You're right — that's what I think too," Vereesa said seriously. "And I'm not sure I couldn't beat him. You saw me fight Teron Gorefiend, after all. You're a human too — tell me honestly, if it were that Turalyon fellow, could he have managed it?"

"I… I couldn't say." Jace glanced at her as he said it, thinking — did this woman actually plan to beat up Turalyon with her own hands? Was this little sister-in-law still that traditional?

Vereesa let out a cold huff — clearly displeased. Jace wisely swallowed his words and kept climbing.

In the dim fourth-floor corridor reeking of a stale floral scent, Jace counted his way along until he found the number, opened the door with his key, and a thick smell of books and paper came pouring out.

Though this place was even more cramped than his rented room, at least it genuinely looked like a simple study rather than a hole with no toilet. It even had a battered bookcase — though it looked like it might be older than the building itself.

Jace carefully sized up the lodgings. He felt that even with the imp, keeping him in the bag he could keep him locked up here permanently, same as in Dalaran.

But since he was going to learn the demon tongue, he couldn't take that risk — a demon standing outside copying things all day would eventually get noticed by the mages of all different skill levels in this apartment building.

"It's certainly a far cry from Quel'Thalas — but well, it's definitely livable." Jace handed the key to Vereesa. "When you leave, just return the key to Master Marin at the place you visited earlier — or to his daughter Amy. You met this morning."

"I remember. But… isn't this actually your room?" Vereesa took the key and pointed at the nameplate on the door. "It still has your name on it." Jace squinted over at it — the metal plate really did have a not-too-visible "Sesso" engraved on it, plus Jace's initials. He hadn't noticed it at all earlier.

"I rent a place outside — closer to the Trade District, more convenient, and the conditions are quite a bit better than this."

"I see." Vereesa ran a finger across the desk. "Actually this is better than I imagined by a long shot. These past few days I'd prepared myself to sleep in the outer forests."

"That won't be necessary — at least you'll have a place to lie down," Jace said. "Oh, right — so if anyone asks, I'll just use that fake name, Aiselwyn?"

Vereesa set her pack on the desk and thought for a moment. "Of course — especially don't tell any other Quel'dorei. Just call Aiselwyn… ah…"

"Sunfury? Sunblade?" Jace prompted.

Vereesa's face went white. She waved her hands frantically. "No — absolutely not."

"Goldthorn, then. I like the word Goldthorn. So it's Aiselwyn Goldthorn. If anything goes wrong, I really don't want to get those royal kinsmen riled up."

"Fine, I'm not too familiar with… so be it." Jace pushed in a desk drawer that hadn't been slid shut. "I wish you and your investigation every success, Lady Aiselwyn Goldthorn. I'll be off."

"And I wish your studies every success, Jace. Thank you truly." Vereesa said.

Jace nodded, pulled the door shut, and stared back at his own name on the door. What even was this… helping Sylvanas's own flesh-and-blood sister find a place to stay in Stormwind?

He was actually quite curious about a lot of things — like why she was using a fake name, why she'd come alone, and so on… but Vereesa seemed somewhat resistant to deeper questions, so he didn't ask.

Still, he had the vague sense that she seemed deliberately intent on shutting out all outside factors — that she wanted to use this mission to prove her own strength to her family, something along those lines.

Back in Dalaran, Vereesa had mentioned that the relationship between her two sisters was quite bad right now. Could that have something to do with this?

Thinking it over, Jace glanced at his watch. Oh no — it was already three in the afternoon. He still had to go register.
