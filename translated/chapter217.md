## Chapter 217: Is That the Job?

"What do you mean?" Grede asked. "What new body?"

Jace pressed on, "You know death knights are orc souls that have taken over human corpses, right?"

"I know that, of course I know that." Grede tapped his plate with a crab leg. "Ah! That's what you mean — so the old body's about worn out, and he's going to find a new one."

"Or he thinks the old one's too weak and wants a stronger one." Jace picked up a piece of bread, dipped it in the crab-leg broth, and popped it in his mouth. "Remember when we went there last time? The tomb's owner was a Troll War hero. A lot of the corpses there were veterans, knights, that sort of thing — plenty of them should be suitable for a death knight… Actually, this is something I ought to tell Vereesa."

"Why are you always thinking about telling Vereesa? I thought you said not to tell her. Are you getting ideas again?" Grede asked.

Jace slapped the table. "Again? Why 'again'? She asked me today. I treated her well because she did you a favor, didn't I? And I'm hoping she finds that death knight sooner rather than later and gets rid of him for the good of Stormwind. Unlike you — thinking day and night about screwing this and that, wanting to jump anything with a hole."

"When have I wanted to jump everyone? I have standards! But you, Jace Sesso — who was the one yelling about wanting an elven girl? I don't think you take a single word I say seriously!"

"Yelling about it means I have to do it? Can't I just yell for the fun of it?" Grede glared, looking like he wanted to blow up but didn't know where to aim it. He finally took a huge swig of beer. "I'll tell you something, kid — I think *you're* the one who's not right. So young and not thinking about finding a few pretty girls. Every day running off to chant spells with a bunch of shady old men!"

"Then find me one!" Jace shouted.

"Tonight!" Grede slammed the table too. The whole spread of food and drink shook.

Only then did the two of them notice a few nearby tables glancing their way. Fortunately, in the noisy evening crowd they weren't especially conspicuous.

Jace shrank back and said in a low voice, "I was just saying."

"Coward!"

"Yeah, yeah, you're right about everything."

"Ah, I get it." Grede laughed, pointing the crab leg at him. "You're the type who needs the first time to mean something."

"More or less," Jace said — then immediately realized something was off. He'd been tricked.

Grede snorted. "Ha. Finally admitted it. Still a little virgin, huh? No surprise at all. You spend all your time practicing stone-skipping — what girl's going to want you?"

He took a smug sip of beer, eyeing Jace sideways, already savoring his victory.

"Valinde Quickblade, right?" Jace said.

The moment he heard the name, the smile on Grede's face was sucked clean away.

"All right, truce, Jace," he said. "The battlefield's already bloody enough."

Jace sipped his beer and nodded.

"All right, real talk now," Grede said.

"Real talk? Got a death wish again?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "Don't you want to buy a house? A house takes money. Where's the money coming from — you don't want to go hauling cargo for other people again, do you?"

He tapped his eye. "And I urgently need a proper target to test my real strength. Jace, you owe me that chance."

"You could just find work at the warriors' guild," Jace said. "I'm about to start a new term soon. There's a lot to do. Buy clothes, buy paper, buy pens, buy books, buy…"

Grede wagged a finger. "This one's not far. Westfall Keep. A day trip there and back — won't take up too much time."

Jace set down his glass and stared at him for a moment. "You don't mean that job, do you?"

"That's exactly the job I mean," Grede said. "Another mercenary squad got themselves killed going to Lordaeron, but word is they took out a lot of gnolls. Only the boss got away in the end. The Riverpaw tribe has got to be weak right now. King Varian didn't even raise the bounty."

"He's not raising it because he's broke!" Jace said helplessly. "It's over, Grede. One trip to Dalaran and I can barely stand staying here anymore. Stormwind is so poor. I feel like a few noble elves from Quel'Thalas could come on over and cause… would cause… prices to skyrocket."

Jace suddenly realized he didn't know how to say "inflation" in Common. Peasant vocabulary problems, as usual.

"Many grains make a mound, Jace," Grede said. "And think about it — that Riverpaw boss has been robbing Elwynn Forest all this time. He must have hoarded a ton of good stuff, right? Wasn't the biggest haul of your life from some inconspicuous old farm in the south?"

"That's true enough, but how are you so sure the two of us can handle the Riverpaw tribe?"

"You beat the legendary Teron Gorefiend, and you're afraid of a mangy dog in Elwynn Forest?"

"How many times do I have to repeat this… that was because of Vereesa."

"Now you've got me." He pounded his chest. "Am I going to be worse than her? That skinny little elf who's thinner than a kobold? She should go eat thirty pounds of wild boar meat before comparing herself to me. If I'd been there, Gorefiend wouldn't have gotten away — I'd have smashed his face in! You really wasted that trip to Aerie Peak, Jace Sesso. You didn't learn a single useful thing in our grand stone halls. Forget about you — even I alone, given the crappy hammer skills that gnoll boss had the day we ran into him, I'd have taken him with one hand." He pointed toward the Stormwind gate. "Tell you what — if I can't take down that Hogger, I'll jump off the gate bridge. Headfirst. Won't even touch the water."

"Afraid I won't be alive to see that explosive a sight," Jace said.

"So. Are you in or not."

"Fine. We can go take a look."

"Then I'll come find you tomorrow morning."

"Wait… tomorrow morning? Hey… don't rush like that," Jace said. "There'll definitely be more than just Hogger. Even if some of them got killed, he won't be fighting alone — there's got to be a whole pack of Riverpaw guards protecting him. You don't think those mercenaries couldn't beat Hogger in a group fight? We need to at least work out a proper plan."

"Can they stop my hammer?" Grede said, shaking his fist.

"If they close in from all sides, each one with a crossbow, can your hammer fly out and spin around and knock them all down?" Jace asked. "What if they use the trees for cover? The two of us would be standing in the middle like a pair of live targets."

"So how stupid do you have to be to get caught in a situation like that?" Grede said.

"That riverside forest is gnoll territory… We have to account for that possibility," Jace warned.

"Use your Fear, Jace," Grede said in a low voice. "Scare the piss out of them — if they're wearing pants, that is… Make them never dare look at you again. Then whether they're hiding behind a tree or not doesn't matter!" Jace laughed. "What about you? You got pants?"

"Ah, uh…" Grede scratched his beard. "I should be more resistant to fear magic now than I was back then…"

"Want me to cast one on you right now to test?"

Jace raised a finger. Grede quickly covered his ears, then clamped his hands over his eyes, then shut his mouth tight. "Don't… don't, I don't want nightmares for days."

"Covering all that is fucking useless."

Still, bringing up Fear had given Jace an idea.

"This way, then. We set out tomorrow afternoon, rest at Westfall Keep for the night, and go check out the gnolls the next morning. Tomorrow morning I'll get you two Shadow Protection Potions."
