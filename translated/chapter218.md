## Chapter 218: Good Lad, Bad Lad

Early that morning, Jace arrived at the door of The Slaughtered Lamb carrying a bag with his cloak in it.

His plan was to head to Larson's clothes shop right after leaving The Slaughtered Lamb.

He walked into the tavern — and there wasn't a single person inside.

Tharle poked his head out from behind the bar. "Who's there?"

"Do you always just ask your guests who they are?" Jace said.

"You brat." Tharle pointed at the door. "You didn't see the sign? What time is it? Who opens their eyes first thing in the morning and runs to a tavern for a drink? A man with a drinking problem that bad can't even open his eyes at this hour."

"Fine," Jace said. "Are they all here?"

"Same with those lot," Tharle said. "No one's here. Except Ursula."

"Right," Jace said. "I'm here for Ursula."

"That's nothing good, Jace. She's been in the basement this whole time. Rarely goes out at night."

"Got it. I'm heading down, then. Hope business is good today," Jace said, and left the bar.

Tharle snorted and set out the mugs. "Welcome back, Jace Sesso."

Jace looked back at him, nodded, and kept walking toward the basement. Passing through layer after layer of iron cell doors, Jace glanced at the hallways and felt they ought to add some magic runes, or illusion magic like the forests around Quel'Danas, to disguise the place a bit. Otherwise it was way too easy to stumble onto.

Just as Tharle had said, the basement hall was empty. From the side corridor, though, came the bubbling of water and the faint clink of a wooden spoon against the side of a crucible.

Jace walked to the door of the alchemy lab and saw Ursula squatting in front of a boiling crucible in a small tank top, one hand holding a spoon and the other fixed on a page of a rolled-up book, reading intently — the very picture of a forest hag.

"Miss Ursula."

Hearing the voice behind her, Ursula whipped around. Seeing Jace, she let out a sigh. "I thought we were friends."

Jace asked, bewildered, "We *are* friends. Why would you say that?"

"Why didn't you say a word to me before you left?"

As she spoke, she turned back, stirred the pot twice, and poured in some unrecognizable liquid that killed the boil at once. She turned around again and leaned back against the shelf behind her.

Her black hair was soaked through and plastered to her scalp. Her whole body was drenched in sweat, as if she'd just stepped out of a shower and run back in wearing nothing but a tank top — everything showing through, the things that should and shouldn't.

Jace scratched his head. "I told Galders. He said some scary stuff — something about what he'd do to me if I didn't come back. I didn't dare hang around after that."

Ursula gave a bitter laugh. "Galders is just like that. So — how are you going to make it up to me?"

Jace sighed. "This needs making up for too?"

Ursula's eyes snapped wide. "I made potions for you. I told you how to brew mana potions… I even promised to teach you alchemy. And you leave for two months without a single word. You really don't take me seriously at all."

"Never mind, you little brat. Nothing to your name." She gave a light laugh. "So tell me — what are you here for? Not just to say hello?"

Once Ursula put it that way, Jace suddenly didn't know how to bring it up.

She really wasn't wrong at all. He'd asked her for help before, and when he left he hadn't even said goodbye. That really had been looking down on her.

"Sorry… Ursula. I was in the wrong."

"Say what you came to say straight out. Isn't that the spirit of the Dagger Society?" Ursula said, a hint of mockery in her voice. Then she began organizing the materials on the shelf.

Jace nodded. "Then I'll be straight. Do you still have any Shadow Protection Potions?"

"Five silver a bottle."

"Ah…"

"You still want it for free?" Ursula glared at him over her shoulder. "Or bring the materials. Something this simple, I'd make a few bottles for you at no charge — oh, right, you can make them yourself, so you didn't even bring materials… You really are trying to get it for free, aren't you? This time you're definitely doing something for me."

Jace leaned against the wall behind him. "So what do you want me to do? Tell me. I'll do my best."

"I need some gunpowder."

"Huh? Gunpowder? Gajin starting a rebellion?"

Ursula shook her head. "I don't want that crude, low-grade gunpowder. I want a special kind — to be precise, Dark Iron dwarf gunpowder."

"What's different about Dark Iron gunpowder?"

"I heard from Bronzebeard dwarves that the Dark Iron dwarves infuse their gunpowder with a special kind of fire element energy from the depths of Blackrock Mountain. It makes their firearms absurdly powerful. Right now I need a material that produces intense burning. I've tried several and none of them have worked well enough, including regular gunpowder. So I want to take a look at their secret weapon…"

"Isn't that too much?" Jace said. "You want me to go on a Shadowforge City grand adventure?"

He was thinking to himself: *I'm still figuring out how to kill Hogger, and you're sending me straight to Blackrock Depths. Isn't that every move a double crit, a triple devastate?*

"Of course not," Ursula said. "You just need to head up north of Redridge. You don't have to go out onto the plains — just find an abandoned Dark Iron dwarf camp and see if they left any gunpowder behind. Because of the Orc Wars, a whole lot of Dark Iron dwarf villages, camps, even mines were just abandoned wholesale. Now they're tangled up with orcs over there… even less time to reclaim lost ground. Perfect opportunity for you to scavenge."

"'Just'?" Jace said. "You think I don't know what that area's like? Fine. I'll give it a try. I can't guarantee I'll actually get any. But if I really do — you make potions for me free from now on…"

"Done," Ursula said.

Jace said, displeased, "So you know it's dangerous too! Otherwise why did you agree so fast?"

Hearing him say that, Ursula couldn't help but laugh. "It *is* dangerous. So what else are we going to do? How about this?"

She suddenly pulled on her tank-top strap. Jace watched, wide-eyed, as the right side popped out with a bounce, and his heart hammered.

Jace covered his right eye, wiped his face, and sighed. "Ursula, I really do value our friendship. Don't make us as messy as those people."

Ursula pulled the strap back up, looked at him, and smiled. "How can you be such a prude? What a sweet good lad."

Jace said bitterly, "This isn't a good-lad-or-bad-lad issue. I want potions! I didn't ask to see that — it can't count toward the potions!"

"Fine, fine!" Ursula turned around with a stern face, yanked open a cabinet, took four small bottles of Shadow Protection Potion and put them in a pouch, then added a mana potion and stuffed the whole thing into Jace's hands. "One extra."

Jace nodded. "Thanks. I'll bring you herbs later."

"Yeah, yeah, that's secondary. Remember what I asked — don't you dare secretly shortchange me like last time, or I'll…" Ursula lifted the ladle and shook it.

"By the way — it was pretty. Just a little small."

"Get out! Get out!"
