## Chapter 219: Extra Value

After leaving The Slaughtered Lamb, it was still early — there weren't even many people around the Wizard Sanctum yet.

Jace carried his cloak to Larson's clothes shop. It had only been open for a little while.

He took a few breaths, put the scene he'd just seen in the basement out of his mind, walked in, leaned against the counter, and set his cloak on top of it. "Mr. Larson, please have a look — how much to repair this?"

"Ha, I recognized you the moment I saw this thing. I remember you."

Larson picked up the cloak, shook it open, examined it, and sucked in a breath.

"Young man, did you get into a fight with a dragon?"

Jace asked, surprised, "How did you know?"

Larson gave him a sidelong look. "I was just exaggerating."

"It really was a dragon… I went to Lordaeron and ran into a dragon, sir. If not for this cloak, I'd be dead."

Larson asked again, "This scorch mark really came from dragon's fire?"

"Yeah. Have you heard about dragons being all over the Lordaeron continent?"

"I've heard a bit," Larson nodded. "I thought it was just a rumor. Light… This scorch mark will reduce the cloak's magical performance a little, but if I were you, I wouldn't use magic to remove this scorch mark."

"Why?" Jace asked.

Larson leaned in close and sniffed the scorched area of the cloak. "A lot of elven tailors know this — dragon fire, whether from a black dragon or a red dragon, leaves behind a special smell that doesn't fade for a long time. I haven't actually smelled something scorched by a black dragon, but this cloak has a fishy, gamey smell that no smoked dragon hide or any kind of leather carries. Something close to the scent of shadow energy corrosion, but not quite the same. Isn't this the smell black dragon fire leaves behind?"

"Probably. Everyone in Lordaeron who's fought black dragons says so," Jace said. "Does this smell have any special use?"

"Of course. Take red dragons as an example. For humans, this smell is clearly perceptible. For other animals — wild wolves, wild bears, that sort of thing — even more so. Fear of dragons is carved into their bones, generation after generation. The moment they catch this smell, they'll be frightened, even flee. Only the truly powerful, or the mindless low-ranking monsters, don't avoid the aura of a black dragon."

"There's that kind of effect too?"

Jace picked up the edge of the cloak in surprise. He himself didn't feel anything like fear, but since the clothes-shop owner had said so, might as well profit from it, right?

"Step back and think," Larson said. "Why remove a battle scar from fighting a black dragon? This cloak must be more expensive now than when it was first made!"

"That's true. I didn't think of that," Jace said again. "But how much worse is its fire resistance, exactly? I still need it to protect me, after all. Without this cloak, I'd probably have been burned beyond recognition."

Larson examined it carefully. "That would need proper testing to determine. Do you want to leave it here for a few days so I can test it? I estimate it wouldn't be less than eighty percent."

"Cost money?"

"Of course. I'd need to bring in an enchanter."

"Never mind, then." Jace took back the cloak. "My mage robe burned up too. I need money to buy another one…"

Although, back when Jace had no money, he'd always thought that once he had it he'd definitely buy himself better magic equipment, stronger magic weapons, to deal with all the demons and monsters out there.

But now that he was actually holding a few gold coins in his hand, he felt he spent less freely than when he'd only had a few dozen silver to his name.

After all, once he could see a sliver of hope for buying a house, he always felt he ought to save a little here, save a little there.

James Larson asked, "Are you looking to order now?"

"Do you have anything in stock, sir?"

"Yes, but not necessarily in your height… How tall are you? Six feet?"

"I don't know. I was measured here last time but they didn't tell me the number." Jace said the last time he'd known his height, he'd been fifteen. He had no idea how much he'd grown since.

"Then let me measure again." Larson took out a tape measure and measured him. "Between five foot eight and nine."

That was about 172 to 175 centimeters — meaning Jace was about to surpass his past-life height.

"Sir, how old are you this year?"

"Eighteen. Technically, ten days from eighteen."

"You've still got some growing to do, sir. How about I pick one to fit ten inches?"

"All right, thank you, Mr. Larson," Jace said. "This time I'd like something sturdier. Budget's about twenty silver, and not too long — something that won't get in the way when I move."

Larson thought for a moment. "I happen to have a set about the right size — a battle mage robe. Sturdier than a normal apprentice robe, but because the style is simple it doesn't look much different from an ordinary robe. It is long, but the hem is cut high, to make it easier for battle mages to move. Want this one? Eighteen silver and it's yours."

"The downside is that after spring you'll need another robe for summer, sir, because this one is a bit thick. Even without an undershirt underneath, it'll be fairly hot in summer."

Jace considered it for a moment. "Better get one I can wear all year round."

"Hold on, let me find one for you."

Larson went to the back. Jace glanced at his pocket watch, thinking how nice it would have been if he could have worn new clothes to registration yesterday. This old shirt he had on — would it make his future classmates' first impression of him take a nosedive?

Thinking this, Jace couldn't help shaking his head. Here was a powerful caster who'd killed dragons in Lordaeron, crossed spells with Gul'dan's disciple in Dalaran, wrestled a gryphon in The Hinterlands, and bantered with the little sister of Silvermoon's Ranger-General — actually worrying about what a bunch of kids thought of him. Really beneath him.

"Sir, take a look at this one." Larson came out, unfolding a brownish-yellow, faintly greenish old robe. "This is a robe I took in last Harvest Festival. Shorter than the last one, though not exactly a short robe. Its former owner was a Kul Tiras mage about your height. This robe is old, but it's woven from island spider silk and manaweave cloth, so the price might be a bit higher — but both the size and the features fit what you're after. The manaweave used in this robe is specially enhanced for elemental resistance, so it's hard to burn straight through. Difficult to ignite. If it gets slashed, since manaweave and spider silk aren't particularly rare materials, the repair cost isn't high either. What do you think?"

"The price is a bit higher — how much higher?"

"Twenty silver and seven."

"Let me try it on."

Jace put it on and looked at himself in the mirror. Felt kind of handsome, actually. And the main thing was, wearing this mid-length robe, he really did look more like a proper mage now.

That old simple twelve-silver short robe — fine in Stormwind, but the moment he'd walked into Dalaran he'd looked like an errand boy.

"So why did the previous owner sell it?"

"Ah… absolutely nothing wrong with the robe itself, sir."

"Can you tell me?" Jace's curiosity was suddenly piqued.

Larson said, "This robe was made by the mage's wife. Then they got divorced. So he sold it. Simple as that."

Jace nodded. Thank the Light it wasn't some bizarre reason like dying in a blast of dragon fire, or getting exiled for studying necromancy.

"This one, then."
