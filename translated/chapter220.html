Chapter 220: The Westfall Night

Potions, ink bottle, rations, waterskin, the bag with the spell pamphlet and a few scraps of paper — after checking it all, Jace sat himself down on the steps outside the little tavern by Stormwind's main gate, wrapped his cloak around himself, clutched his staff, snacked on dried fruit he'd bought inside, and teased the mare tied up by the stable while he waited for Grede.

Not long after, a dwarf came swaying around the corner, trudging along with a sack on his back, constantly tugging at his sleeves and yanking at his belt — like a zombie who couldn't manage walking right.

Grede had actually shown up wearing a suit of armor!

It wasn't the sculpted, statue-like heavy plate the dwarven warriors who'd expeditioned to Draenor wore, but it counted as a genuine set of "plate armor," especially on the shoulders and arms, where layer upon layer of iron plates overlapped — fairly intimidating.

He walked up to Jace, grinned, and pulled at the padded jacket stuffed inside his collar. "What? Never seen a tin can? They said this armor was easy to move around in. Why's it so tight here? Are Bronzebeards all just too short?"

Jace said, "You're just not used to it."

"True enough. I haven't worn armor this heavy many times." Grede rolled his neck. "Still, it'll do. Probably get used to it as we walk."

"Did you buy this in the Dwarf District? How much?"

"Borrowed it," Grede said. "Why would I buy something this expensive that I'd barely use? I just figured we might be facing crossbows and the like — a small shield alone might not cut it."

He picked up his hammer and tapped it against his shoulder a couple of times, laughing. "Pretty fun."

"Besides the big piece, what else did you bring?" Jace asked.

"I brought two healing potions. Shield, rations… not much else. Daggers and such — you planning to hunt or gather herbs while we're out there?"

Jace said, "If we see herbs, of course we gather them. There's plenty of Peacebloom and Silverleaf in Elwynn Forest. We can't just see them and pretend we didn't. Like you said — many grains make a mound."

Grede said, "But I didn't bring that big bag."

"Killing Hogger's still the number-one priority," Jace said. "Who knows, maybe where it's holed up there's a bag too — grain sacks, that sort of thing. That's not a problem."

"You get the Shadow Protection Potions?" Grede said. "Give me one."

Jace pulled out two and handed them over. "Keep one as a spare."

Grede pulled the stopper and sniffed. "This definitely isn't your brew, Jace Sesso."

"Why?" Jace asked.

"The quality's too high. This could go toe-to-toe with those sages up on Aerie Peak. Did you buy these from the mages brewing in the alchemy lab?"

Jace said, "I have my ways of getting high-quality potions. Don't worry about it. Definitely didn't cost me anything."

Jace was a little surprised. He knew Ursula was a remarkably skilled alchemist, but he hadn't expected Grede to rate her this highly. He really ought to learn properly from her in the future.

The two of them set out by cart from the main gate of Stormwind. One of the horses seemed to run into trouble along the way, which delayed them quite a bit. By the time they reached Westfall Keep, it had already gone dark.

Once winter set in, the Westfall area got bitterly cold. The barracks in the distance looked deserted — bleak and lifeless.

In the light of the campfires, only a few soldiers in padded jackets sat outside in a daze.

After eating, Grede fell asleep quickly. Dwarves needed plenty of sleep to handle the next day's rough and tumble. Jace, though, couldn't sleep at all.

He slipped out to the gate of Westfall Keep and spotted a bounty poster — the same kind as the notices on the boards back in Stormwind.

He'd actually never taken a careful look at what was written on this bounty.

The poster described the threat the Riverpaw gnoll leader posed to the roads running through the southern forests and the Westfall region. It didn't give the leader's name, only an approximate build and height, and stated that anyone who brought back a gnoll head of sufficient size could claim a bounty of sixty-five silver.

Sixty-five silver — more than double the Hogger bounty Ursula had told him about three months ago. Grede had said that when the last squad failed, Varian hadn't raised the bounty. Jace had thought it was still the original thirty silver.

So the king had raised the bounty before, and by a considerable amount.

Jace stared south into the pitch-black forest, thinking: How many people have actually tried to go kill Hogger?

Just then, he noticed a man wearing leather pants and draped in a thin coat, sitting on the wall of the keep, watching him.

"Interested, mage?"

"Yes. And you are?"

"I'm the magistrate here. Anything you don't understand?"

Jace asked, "I'd like to know how many mercenaries have tried in the past two months?"

"Three groups," the man said. "Quite a few mercenary squads have been planning to go in these past couple of days. Two groups already went in yesterday. They might achieve more, now that word's spread about how the previous squad hit the Riverpaw tribe hard."

"Is the Riverpaw tribe still large?" Jace asked again.

The magistrate pointed to the river surface off to the west. "Compared to the portion in Westfall, this one isn't big. The whole lot is about thirty to forty, scattered in different places throughout the southern forest. Probably fewer now. But what worries me is that they might be recruiting from other tribes."

He lowered his head. "You look different from those mercenaries who came through yesterday, so I'll tell you a bit more."

"A month ago, our patrol stopped a robbery targeting travelers. We killed two gnoll thugs and found that one of them was a Redridge tribe member from the east — its fur color was different from the other Riverpaw gnolls. Gnolls are stupid, but they're not beasts, sir. You can't underestimate the connections between their different tribes."

"If this keeps up, I'm afraid we'll have another Gnoll War on our hands — especially now, when Stormwind hasn't fully recovered. We can only rely on you mercenary companies. Our current forces are only enough to keep the main roads safe. We can't fight a full-scale war against the Riverpaw tribes on both banks of the Nafeltari at the same time. Imagine leading two or three hundred young lads who've never even seen an orc, fighting a guerrilla war in the forest against those bastards. It'd be an absolute nightmare! But if we do nothing, every few days there's another death at the hands of gnolls. The soldiers will reach their breaking point eventually."

Jace said, "Killing Hogger — they might just elect a new leader."

The magistrate gave a bitter laugh. "That's true enough. But at least it would set off infighting within the Riverpaw tribe — buy us a few months, even a few years of peace. After all, it's been all these years since the Gnoll War and we've only run into this one Hogger, right, mage? You might think that doesn't solve the problem, but for those soldiers, that's already no small thing."

Jace nodded, not knowing what to say.

The magistrate went back into the tower and brought Jace a simple map marking the approximate locations of the gnoll camps in the riverside forest. Jace kept turning over his earlier suspicion in his mind — did that mysterious death knight have anything to do with these gnolls? Thinking it over, what had been a simple bounty job had gotten a little more complicated. As long as they could kill Hogger, some things ought to become clear.
