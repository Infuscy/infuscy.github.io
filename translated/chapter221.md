## Chapter 221: Riverbank

Scattered dead leaves were tossed onto the water's surface by the occasional river breeze. Jace gazed across at the pale yellow expanse of Westfall on the far bank — the lifeless, broken trees, the limp shrubs… finally, it was starting to look a little like the game.

The two of them had been walking along the river for roughly an hour, and the whole time Grede had kept stealing glances over at him.

When they stopped, Jace looked around at the quiet, empty surroundings and couldn't hold it in any longer.

"Why do you keep staring at me?"

"I'm just not used to you wearing a robe," Grede said, poking aside a patch of yellow grass in front of him with a stick. "I barely recognize you."

He'd barely finished speaking when Jace glanced down at his robe, and a sudden splashing came from the grass pile by the river ahead — like a fish leaping out of the water and diving straight back in.

Both of them stared over that way for a moment. Grede asked, "Fish or frog?"

Jace looked at him. "Frogs in winter?"

"You ever heard of Mana Brew?" the dwarf asked.

"Amani witch doctors turning magic frogs out here? What kind of joke is that?" Jace laughed.

The dwarf narrowed his eyes. "You actually know about Mana Brew?"

The smile on Jace's face froze. He covered, "I'm from the north too — what's so strange about knowing that? Come on, let's go take a look."

Grede nodded and pushed his way through the grass step by step, when a low groan suddenly reached them.

The two of them hurried over and saw a man covered in blade wounds lying on the riverbank, his lips barely moving.

Grede pulled out his waterskin and tried to give him some water, but the man seemed to have lost all awareness of drinking. His face was deathly pale, like a waterlogged corpse. He mumbled and couldn't get anything down, just staring blankly at the sky. Even when Grede shook him, there was no real response.

"He's a dead man," Grede said, standing up.

"Could a healing potion save him?" Jace asked.

Grede sighed. "No chance. He could drop any second. Unless an elven botanist just happened to pop out of nowhere, he's done for. This poor bastard might've been lying here a whole night."

Right then, the man suddenly went still.

"Damn it." Grede hurled a stone into the river. "Would've been better if we'd shown up a little later and just found a corpse. Maybe seeing the two of us got him excited — he could've lasted another hour or two, but the excitement did him in."

"At least he's suffering less." Jace frowned. "Who did this to him?"

"Who else? Gnolls," Grede said.

"Would gnolls just leave him lying here?" Jace said. "Gnolls eat human flesh."

"You remember that woman we ran into last time?" Grede said. "That dead woman we found by the river on our way to Raven Hill. That Northshire woman."

"I remember," Jace said. "You mean she wasn't eaten either? I don't know how to examine wounds. Do you know how to tell what weapon caused a wound?"

"Let me see." Grede lifted the man's blood-soaked clothes, and Jace noticed a terrifyingly deep wound under his ribs.

"Sword stab, deep… probably this one's what did him in," Grede sighed.

"Do gnolls hold a sword and stab with it?" Jace asked.

"Maybe they do." Grede frowned. "I really don't want to think about other possibilities, Jace. Damn it, why does it have to be like this? We've got to fight gnolls on top of our own people?"

"Our own people?" Jace said. "We need to watch out for other mercenaries now. Honestly, we should've seen this coming from the start."

Back at Westfall Keep, when the sheriff had said "you're not quite like the other mercenaries," Jace had a vague feeling about what kind of situation he'd be dealing with here. He just hadn't expected it to get this naked and brutal.

Looking at this man who had just died, he felt a wave of intense nausea. Even facing the walking corpses back in Dalaran, he'd never felt this sick.

"I'm going to let it out, Grede," Jace said.

"It? Who?"

Jace said quietly, "Sazeno, come out and keep watch for us."

At that, the imp immediately popped its head out of the warlock pack on Jace's back.

Grede watched the golden imp head stretch out and stare at him. His expression went from shock to confusion to anger. He grabbed Jace by the robe and hissed, "What the hell are you doing?"

Jace explained, "It can sense enemies who might be closing in, especially spellcasters. Grede, shouldn't we be guarding against the possibility of mages among the mercenaries, or even other warlocks? Not to mention, there could be warlocks among the gnolls too… Don't be angry. You'll understand soon enough. Remember that dragon we ran into at Southshore? It sensed us from far away."

The imp nodded vigorously.

"Fine. Why isn't it talking?" Grede asked.

"Before we left, I told it not to talk," Jace said. "Otherwise we'd both go crazy, Grede. I certainly couldn't stand it. It can just stay here quietly as a scout. We'll keep moving."

"It's that obedient?"

"At least on the surface." The two of them followed the riverbank, using the tall waterside grass as cover, heading south for roughly two hours. The imp didn't detect a single disturbance the whole time.

"Check the map," Jace said, patting Grede's back.

"I seriously doubt checking the map'll do much good."

Grede pulled the forest map the sheriff had lent them out of his pack.

Jace took it, flipped it back and forth to get his bearings, matched up the river's direction, then walked a bit further up the bank and through a patch of tall grass.

Before them lay a forest that felt almost disorienting — endless to the eye. The deep woods were gloomy and unsettling even on a brilliantly sunny afternoon.

At their walking pace, two hours felt like four or five kilometers. By his reckoning, they should be quite close to the gnoll camp marked on the map.

"Are we lost?" Grede asked.

Jace looked back at the calm river surface and said, "We haven't left this river since we set out from Westfall Keep. Honestly, Grede, if we don't leave this river and head into the woods, we haven't even started — let alone gotten lost. The map says we're about there too. Let's head in."

"Then let's go," Grede said.

The two of them scanned the forest for anything unusual, confirmed nothing was off, and headed deeper in.

Elwynn Forest in winter was actually far more desolate than in summer, but the sparser foliage didn't make visibility any clearer — the stark white trunks and bare branches were glaringly bright.

After the two of them had been picking their way through the forest for ten or twenty minutes, the imp suddenly scratched the back of Jace's neck, searing his skin.

"What is it? Speak."

"There seems to be a spellcaster in the distance… or maybe not."

## Flags

- **Title**: 河岸 — "Riverbank" (the banks of the River Neltharion / Nafeltari River).
- **Mana Brew** (魔汁): Jace recognizes the term and Gredes is surprised — a lore reference to Amani troll alchemical magic; Jace nearly slips by revealing too much knowledge.
- **Northshire woman** (诺森德人): the earlier dead woman by the river near Raven Hill; rendered per context as a woman from the northern region.
- **Sazeno** (撒厄诺): the imp, per glossary. Jace has ordered it to stay silent on this outing.
- **Southshore dragon** (南海镇): a prior adventure reference — the imp's ability to sense creatures at range was proven when it detected a dragon near Southshore.
- **River Neltharion / Nafeltari River** (纳菲瑞提河): glossary notes a TODO on the canonical name; rendered per glossary.
- **Dalaran walking corpses** (达拉然活尸): a prior memory — Jace's time among the undead in Dalaran, yet this dead man disturbs him more.
- **Westfall Keep sheriff's warning** (西泉要塞治安官): the sheriff's comment that Jace was "not quite like the other mercenaries" is now clearly justified.
