## Chapter 223: Tearing Down Another's Wall

He was faster than me…

As the thought crossed his mind, waves of searing pain rolled through his abdomen, each second worse than the last.

Jace bit down and silently cursed. Damn it… that crossbowman…

The crossbowman in the distance screamed hysterically, "Make these illusions go away! No! It's all fake! I'll kill you, you orc-worshipping degenerate! Blasphemer against the Holy Light! Scum of mages, dregs of humanity, traitor to the Alliance!"

He screamed, straightened up, his head trembling as he stared over. Sobbing and wailing, he raised the crossbow again, hands shaking as he took aim!

"You're dead, warlock… you think you're immortal?! Just like those necromancers?"

Jace raised his arm, about to use Fear to stop his movements, when he saw a hammer fly through the air and smash the man's head to pieces.

His body tumbled away like a man hit by a wagon on a highway. His entire head burst into a mist of blood. The hammer kept flying a good distance before slamming into the earth with a dull thud.

Whether he was a mage or not… there'd be no way to know now.

Looking back toward where the hammer had come from, Grede was muttering something under his breath as he pounded his bare fists again and again into another man's raised shield, each blow buckling it further out of shape.

Suddenly, a crazed, wild roar erupted from behind. Jace thought he was hallucinating.

He turned and saw a man charging at them like a wild wolf, a longsword in his hands, his face covered in mud and tears, screaming at the top of his lungs, saliva running down to his neck.

Jace saw the sword tip coming and barely dodged the thrust. He used his staff to smash aside a second wild slash. The man's rhythm broken, he stumbled two steps, then raised his sword for another thrust as if refusing to accept it…

He seemed like he'd had sword training, but not much — it was easy to predict where his next thrust would go. Jace's staff easily won the reach battle. Seeing a diagonal chop coming, he struck cleanly on the hand and knocked the weapon away.

That one blow was enough to make the man grimace in agony. He lay on the ground gasping for breath. The sword had flown half a meter away. He'd just reached for it when Jace brought the staff down squarely on his face. The man went "Unh" and collapsed, blood pouring from his nose.

He crawled forward again, trying to grab the sword, but Jace had already walked over step by step and picked it up first. Biting back the pain, he raised the blade, about to drive it into the man's back — and suddenly hesitated.

The man scrambled backward, looking around for somewhere to hide. "Don't kill me, don't kill me… Master…"

Right. Why kill him?

This was a living, breathing person!

A living person… full of life energy. If he died, it would all be gone in an instant.

The thought blackened like a rapidly rotting petal and fell as a mass of shadow, drowning Jace's mind.

The shadow power seemed to sense the shift in his heart, seeping out of his veins and crawling up his wrists.

He pointed his staff at the retreating man and intoned coldly, "Erathun, Siliondor."

In that moment, he even had the illusion that he could cast this magic without the incantation.

The man's body suddenly convulsed. He began letting out faint, guttural "Uh… uh" sounds.

His expression began to twist. His lips twitched uncontrollably. He wanted to claw at his own face, then his own neck, then scratch at his chest, then tear at his hair.

He felt unbearable discomfort all over, but couldn't tell where it hurt.

"Demon… demon!" he growled, though he didn't know where he was growling. Eventually his curses turned to sobs. He tried to crawl away desperately, but found the skin on his arms visibly withering before his eyes. He couldn't muster any strength at all, couldn't even drag his own body. The sobs quickly became low moans — "Aiyo, aiyo…"

Jace clenched his hand and looked down. The crossbow bolt was on the ground, still stained with some blood — and his belly had already healed.

The man on the ground stared at the warlock in front of him. He could barely move at all, and cried out in utter despair, "I don't want to die…"

"Jace! Jace!"

A hazy voice came from beside him. Grede cupped Jace's face and slapped it gently. "Are you all right? Why is your face covered in blood? Damn it… don't you dare die."

"My face is covered in blood?"

Jace blinked and only then felt the dry, thick liquid stinging and itching his eyes. He rubbed them vigorously and found his hands and sleeves drenched in blood.

"It's not my blood — no, it is my blood… hold on, Grede."

He looked again at the man, whose head had now lolled to the side, and another wave of nausea hit him. He vomited a great mouthful of fresh blood that splashed across the ground, turning the dead grass before him a bright red.

"Where the hell are you hurt?" Grede held Jace and asked anxiously.

"I'm fine… it's the magic."

Jace took another huge breath, straightened up, and patted his chest. That pat set off two coughs, and he spat up a lump of clotted blood.

Grede glanced at the blood on the ground. "What kind of magic is this, self-harm magic?"

Jace picked up the crossbow bolt from the ground. "This bolt was in my belly a moment ago. It's come out on its own now."

He jumped in place a couple of times and stretched. "See? Good as new. My stomach still hurts a bit, that's true, but I'm fine."

"What about the wound on your belly?" Grede saw the blood hole in Jace's robe. "Was it here?"

The dwarf felt around the spot, then glanced at the shriveled corpse on the ground. He seemed to understand what had happened, and the anxiety on his face eased considerably.

Jace saw the hole and cursed inwardly.

He'd only bought this robe a day ago.

But he truly hadn't expected Life Drain's self-healing effect to be this powerful. It seemed that in the real world, all magic was far more exaggerated than its game counterpart — both the positive effects and the negative ones.

He thought back to Dalaran, when he'd been stabbed by Teron Gorefiend. Maybe he could have healed himself by draining some living creature's blood…

It wasn't that he hadn't thought of it at the time, but there hadn't been the right conditions for that kind of thing in Dalaran.

Though absorbing so much life energy left him feverish and uncomfortable all over, turning the tables on a man who'd tried to kill him while healing his own wound in one stroke had fully satisfied a destructive urge that had lurked inside him for God knows how long.

Jace caught his breath and tried to calm himself, because the more this happened, the easier it would be for the shadow power to take control. Gradually, he'd drift further and further from what the people around him considered normal.

After calming down a bit, he looked at the corpses scattered across the ground. He thought back to their earlier arrogance, then their fear, and finally their miserable deaths, and couldn't help but sigh inwardly at the unpredictability of life.

"Come on, let's see what goodies they've got, Grede."

## Flags

- **Title**: 拆别人的墙 — "Tearing Down Another's Wall" — metaphorical; Jace uses Life Drain to tear the life out of a living man, a darker act than simple killing.
- **Life Drain** (生命吸取): Jace's first deliberate use of Life Drain on a human target. The incantation "Erathun, Siliondor" (厄拉芒，西利昂多尔) is rendered phonetically. The spell heals his gut wound by draining the man's life — a visceral demonstration of warlock magic's self-sustaining horror.
- **Self-healing side effect**: the excess life energy causes Jace to hemorrhage blood from his eyes, nose, and mouth — a grotesque physical cost that mirrors the game's Life Tap but far more visceral.
- **The crossbowman's accusations**: "orc-worshipping degenerate," "blasphemer against the Holy Light," "traitor to the Alliance" — the man recognizes Jace's magic as warlockry and lashes out with every slur he knows before Grede's hammer silences him.
- **Teron Gorefiend** (塔隆·血魔): Jace recalls being stabbed by the death knight in Dalaran — a prior wound that might have been self-healed this way under different circumstances.
- **Shadow corruption theme**: Jace recognizes the growing pull of shadow power and forces himself to calm down — a recurring internal struggle.
- **Grede's concern**: the dwarf compares Jace's post-Life Drain appearance to a vampire, unsettled but trying not to judge.
