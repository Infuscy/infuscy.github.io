Chapter 224: Strange Coin

Jace looked at the corpse lying face-down on the ground — the one who'd tried to grab his staff. "Let's move fast. Don't wait around for gnolls to show up. From what they were saying earlier, it sounded like they'd just had a run-in with some gnolls. I think there must be a fair number of them around here."

"All right," Grede said. "I'll search the ones further out."

Jace searched the nearest one. Nothing in his pockets.

He looked at the sword the man had dropped. If he sold it, it'd fetch maybe two or three silver at most. But weapons were too hard to carry back, and lugging them around would just become a burden. Best to bundle them up and bury them somewhere inconspicuous so the gnolls wouldn't find them.

He picked up a sword that looked to be in decent shape and hung it at his belt, then went to search the man who'd shot him.

He turned him over and pulled open his armor. Jace suddenly noticed the outline of a coin imprinted against the man's shirt over his chest.

He felt around and pulled it out — a coin that wasn't perfectly round.

But the material and design of this coin were like nothing he'd seen before. It wasn't a troll beast totem, nor the portrait of any king he recognized — it looked like the head of some savage monster.

He couldn't tell if it was from the wear of war, but the coin was so badly worn that the head was hard to make out. Was this some Loa that the trolls worshipped?

He tucked it into his pack and moved on to the bald man's charred corpse. The body was all stuck together. Jace tried to search through his melted clothes for anything useful, but after a while the stench of burnt flesh was too much to take, and he gave up.

Just then, Grede came back.

"About thirty silver or so, no gold, Jace. Unfortunately the crossbow was burned up — unusable. But there's something here you'll like."

He held out a basic mana potion.

"There should have been three, but two broke," Grede said. "I remember these aren't cheap, right?"

"True enough." Jace took it. "So the crossbowman really was a mage!"

"Seems like it," Grede said. "He had a booklet too, but it was mostly burned up. Probably a half-pike like you."

Jace coughed in displeasure. "Shame. Never got to see what spells he knew."

Grede said, "Didn't trust his own magic. Would rather shoot a crossbow than throw a fireball or anything."

Jace said, "Maybe all he knew was Light — something to light the way when these guys went to take a piss at night. How good do you think a mage tagging along with a crew like that could be?"

"You're making it sound too pathetic." Grede shook his head. "What about you? Find anything?"

Jace held out the strange coin. "Take a look. What kind of coin is this?"

"Isn't this an orcish gold coin?" Grede said.

"Gold coin?" Jace looked at the money. Now that Grede mentioned it, it did have more of a golden sheen than a regular silver piece, but it wasn't quite a real gold coin either.

Grede said, "Look at this castle and this orc head on it — this is orcish money. We can sell it when we get back."

"An orc head?" Jace pointed at the horn on it. "Have you ever seen an orc with horns on its head?"

Grede said, "Maybe there are horned orcs. Anyway, they call coins with this design orcish gold coins — brought over from the other side of the Dark Portal. Someone in the Dwarf District was buying them for about a dozen silver each."

Jace looked at the so-called gold coin again. Connecting it to what Grede had said, a possibility suddenly occurred to him.

Could this be ogre money?

The ogres had once built a mighty empire in Draenor — Gorian. Back then, the orcs had been nothing but slaves in that empire.

And this horned head was very likely the head of an ogre — one of the empire's rulers.

The idea of ogres stamping their own faces onto coins was so absurd in Azeroth that Jace had dismissed the possibility at first. But if it was the twin-headed ogre sages who ruled the Gorian Empire, it made perfect sense. And the coin's material was more likely some Draenor precious metal — truegold, or khorium, or something of that sort.

"So these guys were bought off by orcs?" Jace asked.

"Maybe, but who knows," Grede said. "What if it's just some kind of lucky coin or something?"

Jace nodded and handed the silver coins to Grede to stow in his pack. The two of them piled up the remaining weapons, buried them in an inconspicuous spot, and walked a stretch further south before resting at the base of a cliff slope.

As he chewed his bread, Jace found it had lost all its flavor. His mouth was full of the taste of blood.

This blood was his own — the result of him draining his enemy's life force, causing his own blood to burst from his ruptured veins and overflow. If the life energy hadn't been repairing the damage at the same time, he'd have bled out long ago.

Though this was the fourth time, and he'd more or less gotten used to it.

Jace felt the bread tasted a bit off. He spat a bit into his hand and looked — the bread was stained with blood.

Grede, sitting on the dirt slope nearby, looked over. "Are you really all right?"

"Should be fine." Jace tossed it back in his mouth. "This isn't the first time."

"What did it feel like, killing that man with dark magic?" the dwarf asked.

Jace glanced at the staff in his hands. It seemed to be savoring the victory too, not quite ready to let go.

He'd been about to tell Grede just how satisfying it had felt, but in the end he wasn't heartless enough to be that honest.

"Honestly, right after you cast that magic, you looked just like a vampire out of legend," Grede said, looking at Jace's bloodshot eyes. "Of course I'm not saying you shouldn't have killed that bastard, or that you shouldn't have found a way to heal yourself, but…"

"Forget it. Maybe I'm overthinking it." He lay back on the slope. "I'm tired. I want to sleep. That last fight really took it out of me. Wake me in about two hours and we'll keep moving."

"I'm wondering if I should sleep too, even though I'm not tired yet," Jace said.

Grede lay back on his arm, eyes closed. "If you sleep too, we'll both get our throats cut and never know the difference."

Jace said, "Can elves go several days without sleep?"

Grede muttered, "I know some elven rangers can go eight days straight without sleep and still fight trolls on the ninth — like killing machines built by the gods of old. We can't do that. We're natural-born sleeping machines. Fighting is just a hobby."

With that, he began to snore. Jace had always envied the dwarf's ability to fall asleep so fast. Even without Grede saying it, Jace knew that the struggle against the ever-growing shadow power was a never-ending process — a battle with no end in sight.

His power grew stronger, and the battle would only grow fiercer. His will to master his own body had to grow with it. The slightest lapse, and all would be for nothing.
