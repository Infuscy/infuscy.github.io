Chapter 225: Cannon Fodder

In the shade of the slope's trees, Jace silently recited the Mage Armor incantation, debating whether to cast one for the coming fight. But here was the problem.

Mage Armor's magical effect was far too flashy, and it lasted so long that you needed dispelling magic to get rid of it.

In other words, if he cast it, Jace would be forced to become a walking lantern glowing in the forest for five or six hours. That basically made sneaking up on a gnoll camp an impossible task.

Before he'd learned Mage Armor, he never would have imagined he'd face a problem like this.

But there was no buff icon here for him to right-click away, and the incantation for dispelling your own magic was itself a rather difficult thing to learn.

Right as he was turning the problem over in his mind, he suddenly felt his hair get yanked hard — twice.

"What is it?"

He turned his head. The imp was frantically pointing at the top of the slope. Jace whispered, "Phase Shift!"

The imp immediately shrank into the warlock pack. Jace lay down flat against the slope. Just then the dwarf let out a snore, and Jace quickly grabbed his foot and shook it.

When Grede woke and saw Jace's expression, he tensed up immediately and drew his neck in, crawling over to Jace's side.

Jace pointed upward. Grede nodded. The two of them held their breath and listened. Sure enough, a clamor of footsteps reached them.

Accompanying the footsteps were raspy, rustling breaths and chittering sounds — like a pack of wild dogs roaming back and forth looking for food.

Both of them knew: they'd stumbled onto real gnolls.

Grede pulled his hammer over and held it before his eyes, his expression murderous, ready to slaughter. Jace waited a moment for a reaction from above. Confirming the gnolls hadn't noticed the noise below, he gripped Grede's hammer and shook his head.

He used both hands to make two little figures walking one in front of the other, then pointed upward.

Grede understood immediately.

As the gnolls' footsteps grew fainter and were about to disappear, Jace patted his pack and whispered, "Come out. Keep an eye out for any other gnolls approaching."

The imp patted its chest. Jace and Grede climbed up the slope and followed the direction the gnolls' footsteps had faded in.

These gnolls weren't walking in a straight line — they were patrolling through the forest. But the two of them were patient. A patrol would always return to its camp eventually.

As long as they kept following from a distance, they'd find at least one camp.

The sunlight grew deeper, the shadows of the trees in the forest grew longer, and the two of them finally followed the gnolls to the vicinity of a camp.

This camp looked less like a gnoll camp and more like a human one. Both the tents and the way the campfire was stacked looked like they'd been set up by humans.

This band of gnolls had most likely attacked a group of travelers or mercenaries resting here, then seized and fortified the facilities, treating them as their own.

Jace poked his head out from a distance and roughly counted the gnolls. He whispered to Grede, "Ten of them, at least. And I don't see Hogger."

"That's a bit too many," Grede whispered back. "And a lot of these gnolls have ranged weapons — bows and crossbows. They really know how to use them. I don't think it's safe. Are you planning to take this camp out?"

"We can wait for the patrol to leave first," Jace whispered. "Looking for Hogger is way too much trouble, Grede. Tell you what — if we let one gnoll escape, would Hogger come here to check on the situation? He's the leader of the tribe. If he sees his brothers being slaughtered and does nothing about it, he'd lose all his authority, right?"

"So you want to set up an ambush here," the dwarf crawled over to Jace and asked. "Then why don't we follow the fleeing gnoll straight to Hogger's home base?"

Jace whispered, "The sheriff told me the Riverpaw tribe has about forty or fifty on this side of the river. If there are ten here, then Hogger's 'main base' could have over twenty gnolls. Do you really want to storm a small stronghold with twenty defenders?"

Grede stroked his beard and muttered, "That would be quite difficult."

"Exactly." Jace kept his voice down. "If we wait for them to come to us, and there are too many, we can still choose to run. That's much better than wandering all over the forest looking for them."

Grede stared at the firelight over there for a moment, then asked quietly, "Will Hogger actually fall for it?"

"Gnolls aren't beasts, but they're not exactly geniuses either. I say don't overthink it," Jace whispered. "If Hogger's really smart enough not to come, then we'll find somewhere to rest for the night and see tomorrow whether to keep looking. Don't be too eager, anyway. I'm not really going to make you jump off a bridge."

"Then we do it your way." Grede let out a breath. "How do we fight here? Do I just charge in, or what?"

Jace grabbed at Grede's armor. "Is this reliable?"

Grede pinched his own cheek. "An arrow to this spot and the armor won't stop it either. It's decent, I guess. It came in handy against those idiots at noon."

"I've got another idea," Jace said. "Sazeno, you go up first, show your face, then run."

"That's it!" Grede immediately broke into a grin. "Let him go be cannon fodder!"

Realizing his voice had gotten a bit loud from excitement, he quickly covered his mouth.

The imp grabbed Jace's collar and shook it back and forth, its tongue lolling out. Jace grabbed it by the neck. "You don't need to say anything. Just do what I tell you. Wait for the patrol to leave, then Phase Shift over and light the gnolls' campfire for them. It's evening now, they should have it going anyway…"

"When they spot you, you run. Then we come out and hit them by surprise. If you screw up, you're the one who dies. If we don't show our faces, nothing happens to us. Understand?"

The imp nodded with a long face, its arms dropping to its sides as it sat down.

"I can't believe it, Jace — I'm actually starting to feel sorry for this demon," Grede said, then couldn't help but grin.

Still grinning, his expression suddenly changed. He pointed past Jace. "Hey, hey, look — they're leaving."

"Already?" Jace turned and saw four gnolls yelling something at the gnolls in the camp, then preparing to leave.

He felt like he could even understand what the gnolls were saying — something about food and evening. Did gnolls speak Common among themselves? Or some dialect of Common?

"It's up to you, demon," Grede said gleefully.

Translator Notes

Flags

- Title: 送死鬼 — "Cannon Fodder" — Grede's gleeful suggestion that Sazeno be sent in as bait, expendable and alone. - Mage Armor dilemma: Jace can't cast Mage Armor because its glow would give them away — a clever nod to game mechanics (visible buffs) clashing with real-world stealth needs. No right-clicking buff icons here. - Phase Shift (相位变换): the imp uses its Phase Shift ability to hide, per glossary. - Hogger (霍格): the target gnoll chieftain; not present at this camp. - Riverpaw tribe (河爪部族): the sheriff's intelligence puts forty to fifty gnolls on this side of the river — a serious force. - Human-style camp: the gnoll camp appears to be seized human infrastructure, suggesting the gnolls are more organized than expected. - Gnoll language: Jace thinks he can understand the gnolls' exchanges — food, evening — raising the unsettling question of whether gnolls speak Common or a dialect of it (picked up again in chapter 226). - Sazeno as bait: the plan — the imp lights the campfire, draws attention, then flees while Jace and Grede ambush. A morally casual use of the imp that even Grede feels briefly guilty about. - "Jump off a bridge": an inside joke between Jace and Grede — Jace promises he won't push the dwarf into a suicide mission.
