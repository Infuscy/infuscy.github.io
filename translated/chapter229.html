Chapter 229: The Dark Thicket

The towering gnoll leader bolted into the trees, leaving only a swaying silhouette weaving back and far in the distance.

Jace and Grede, still dazed from the bomb blast, gave chase with everything they had. That bastard had escaped from bounty hunters' pursuits more times than anyone could count — this time, he would not be allowed to slip away again.

Jace stuffed the imp into his pack and sprinted at full speed, but as he ran, Grede gradually fell behind.

He looked at Jace helplessly, while Jace's stamina was still holding — perhaps because of all that life energy he'd drained earlier, and more likely because he hadn't been trading blows with Hogger at full intensity for several minutes…

Even though he'd told Grede not to push himself earlier, now that Hogger was right in front of him, Jace didn't want to give up even the slightest chance either.

"Ah! Damn it!"

Grede howled from behind, while Jace ran faster and faster. His mind held only that shadow in the forest — he had to catch up!

He didn't know how long he chased, how many tree roots and sinkholes he crossed, but Jace felt he was getting closer and closer to that shadow. No question about it — Hogger was badly wounded too. He couldn't possibly keep running like a healthy gnoll for this long.

Hogger's silhouette grew clearer. Jace set his sights on him and cast Curse of Weakness again as he ran — this time he could feel his energy nearly spent.

Hogger took the hit, his legs gave out, and he crashed to the ground. Jace seized the chance and quickly downed a mana potion, tossed the bottle aside, and closed in. Hogger propped himself up and suddenly kicked up a cloud of dust with his hind legs, right into Jace's face.

Jace dodged to the side. Even though he avoided the bulk of the dust, the gravel that scraped across his face hurt like hell.

Hogger burst through the dust cloud and swung a vicious downward chop. Jace raised his sword to block — and the sword snapped clean in half from the impact!

Hogger howled in excitement and swung the cleaver in continuous horizontal slashes with his one remaining arm. Jace tossed the broken sword and backed up frantically, feeling each swing could cleanly take his head off — even the wind from the cleaver's arc stung his neck.

After several chops all missed, Hogger suddenly took a huge step forward and slashed horizontally again. Jace raised his staff and caught it head-on!

Hogger let out a resentful shriek and slashed from the same angle again. Jace blocked with the staff once more — this time the impact rang out with a "clang" that numbed his entire arm. Only then did realize what Grede had been going through…

And this was Hogger already affected by Curse of Weakness…

"Hogger… will never die…"

Hogger roared and opened his mouth as if to bite into Jace's shoulder. Jace nimbly dodged. Hogger snapped sideways again — Jace raised his staff to block his foul jaws. Seeing the staff caught in his teeth, Hogger chuckled a few times, then swung the cleaver in another horizontal chop while still biting down. Jace let go of the staff and leaped back a step, feeling the wind graze his belly — for a second he was certain he'd been disemboweled.

Hogger bit the staff and grinned, swinging the cleaver at him again. Jace reached out with one hand. The staff emitted a burst of shadow light and suddenly wrenched itself free from Hogger's mouth, tearing out several of his teeth in the process.

Hogger screamed, pulled off balance by the staff, and fell — even the cleaver dropped from his hand.

He struggled to get up, but this time he was nowhere near as strong and nimble as before.

But Hogger seemed even more frenzied. In the moonlight, the blood covering his face looked like black ink. Though his two eyes could no longer focus and his whole body trembled, he still raised his fists and roared, as if he meant to beat the warlock to death with his bare hands.

Jace knew that the last shred of strength this bastard was running on, propped up by nothing but sheer berserk will, had basically been spent with this fall.

He picked up the bastard's massive orc cleaver and chopped it into Hogger's forehead with all his might, ending the monster's life once and for all.

The cleaver lodged in the skull. Jace slowly let out a breath and backed up a few steps.

He stared at the Riverpaw leader's corpse, whose limbs were still twitching uncontrollably, and thought that twenty years from now, if any adventurers came here, no matter whose bounty they saw, it wouldn't be Hogger's.

The Stormwind prison would never hold a prisoner like this again.

At that moment, Grede came panting up from behind. "Did I miss anything?"

"You missed all of it, Grede." Jace stepped aside and pointed at Hogger's corpse not far away. "How about it — my kill."

"You absolute, shameless bastard." Grede braced his hands on his knees and caught his breath. "I'm completely spent, damn it… help me out of my armor, quick."

"I was just kidding…"

Jace helped Grede peel off his armor piece by piece and gave him a healing potion. Grede lay down on a soft pile of leaves with a grimace and caught his breath.

"So, Jace, do I look pretty banged up?"

"You look fine — bruised here and there, but nothing looks fatal."

"Fatal? You looking down on me?" Grede grabbed Jace by the collar. "How could a bunch of brainless idiots ever take my life, you're… I've wasted my time knowing you…"

Before he could finish, the dwarf had already fallen asleep, still gripping Jace's collar.

Jace listened to the tone of Grede's words and thought it sounded awfully strange — like the stubborn last words of a dying man in a movie.

He checked again for any fatal wounds, confirmed there was no blood on his back, and seeing that his breathing was steady and even, decided he'd just passed out from exhaustion — only then did he finally set his mind at ease.

After all, they couldn't shoot movies every day. Plant enough flags and eventually a few of them wouldn't come true.

Jace moved up next to Hogger's body and measured the size of his head — it was about as big as Jace's entire chest.

Carrying a head like that back was completely impractical. This single thing was heavier than all their luggage combined.

He remembered that in the game, the proof for killing Hogger seemed to be its hand.

Jace looked at that one remaining arm — sure enough, the hand was as big as his own face. Truly an absurd gnoll's hand. That would do.

He took Hogger's massive cleaver and chopped the hand off in one swing. He drained the blood, wrapped it in cloth, bound it several times to stop the seepage, and set it down next to Grede's luggage, then sat down to catch his breath.

He found a stone for the dwarf to use as a pillow for his head, and a tree root for himself to lean against. Staff in hand, he finally let his guard down.

His gaze slowly grew unfocused. At last, he had a moment to zone out.

Thinking back on that necromancer, Jace felt there were far too many questions that needed answering.

First — who had raised the necromancer?

Barring death knights, there was no second possibility. Jace felt the answer had practically been slapped right onto his face.

Chances are, those patrol soldiers had killed reanimated undead gnolls before too — it's just that they couldn't really tell which were undead and which were living gnolls, which created the illusion that the Riverpaw gnolls were multiplying the more you killed them.

Honestly, Jace hadn't expected that coming to kill Hogger would also mean dealing with a necromancer. After all, in the game, there were no signs of undead here at all.

So if the two of them hadn't come into this forest and taken Hogger down, what would have happened afterward?

According to the game's lore, although the Riverpaw gnolls had plenty of warlocks who wielded dark magic, they didn't have necromancers — let alone any rotting corpses like the Foulweald gnolls of Tirisfal Glades.

What had changed all this?

Did the Silver Hand come through here later? Or did Wrynn or Bolvar Fordragon conduct some kind of purge against the undead spread?

Or… Onyxia, the black dragon princess?

Given everything he had to go on, Jace could only guess blindly. There was no way to reach any reliable conclusion.

Right now he was still riding an adrenaline high, so his thoughts kept scattering in every direction. He wasn't sure if it was the effect of the Shadow Protection Potion or the life vitality he'd drained keeping him in a state of constant excitement — he felt no obvious desire to sleep.

Still, he lay down and forced himself to rest as much as possible. After all, gnolls were still prowling the woods. Not only did Jace have to protect Grede, he had to stay alert for any surprise attack at any moment.

Those gnolls could smell blood. They could surround him from every direction.

Frankly, after that last battle, Jace wasn't all that afraid of these brainless monsters anymore.

But it seemed his worries were somewhat unnecessary. After two or three hours, the woods remained quiet. The gnolls didn't seem particularly keen on searching for their fallen chieftain's body.

But just as Jace was beginning to let his guard down, a faint, off-putting stench drifted over.

This smell had nothing to do with the blood and animal reek coming off Hogger's corpse — this was the genuine scent of necrotic energy.

"Sazeno… Sazeno?"

How dare this little bastard ignore his summons? It had some nerve.

He took off the warlock's pack and looked inside — the thing was actually fast asleep in there.

Did demons even sleep? Had this guy been spending too much time with the two of them and gotten too into character?

Still, that imp had used no small number of spells helping Grede fight Hogger just now. Even now, faint red lines were still visible on Grede's body — that had to be the Blood Pact mark.

The imp's stamina was probably even weaker than Grede's, Jace knew. Two or three spells and it was running on fumes. "Wake up."

Jace patted the imp's face. The imp opened its eyes in a daze.

"What do you want…"

"Smell anything off?"

"I can't smell anything right now…"

"Then keep watch here. I'm going to take a look over there."

"Fine…"

"Don't fall asleep — don't you dare sleep before I get back! If anything dangerous shows up, you wake this dwarf up, understand?"

"Understood!"

He set the warlock's pack with the imp inside next to Grede, stood up, and looked in the direction the necrotic aura was drifting from. He walked some distance toward it.

Then he realized — this was exactly the direction Hogger had been running.

He hadn't gone far before he spotted a place that seemed to be hidden behind a thicket of withered trees. It looked like a secret camp — except without any of those familiar tents.

At night he couldn't see clearly. Jace could only vaguely make out some shadows swaying inside, their postures grotesque.

A gnoll camp? Why was there no campfire at night?

He opened his map and compared it with the direction they'd been moving. There didn't seem to be any camp marked here.

Jace didn't dare go any further. The arcane glow still shimmering on his body hadn't faded — if he went any closer, he'd definitely be spotted.

Better to go back, wake Grede up, and come back to investigate together.

Translator Notes

Flags

- Hogger's death: Jace delivers the final blow with Hogger's own massive cleaver, ending the gnoll terror of Elwynn Forest for good. - Jace takes Hogger's hand as proof of the kill — a callback to the game's quest mechanic, adapted to the practical reality of not being able to carry a head that size. - Grede collapses from exhaustion after the fight — his near-death "movie dialogue" moment is a false alarm, but it underscores how close the dwarf came to his limit. - Jace reflects on the Stormwind guard who held off Hogger for them — a lingering emotional thread and a desire to find that man. - Major lore mystery introduced: a necromancer in Elwynn Forest is completely absent from the game's canon. Jace begins theorizing — death knights, the Silver Hand, or Onyxia as possible explanations. - A new necrotic scent leads Jace to a hidden camp in the direction Hogger fled — suggesting Hogger was running toward something, not just away. - Sazeno sleeps through Jace's summons — the imp's stamina is genuinely depleted from the Hogger fight, and the Blood Pact marks on Grede confirm the strain.
