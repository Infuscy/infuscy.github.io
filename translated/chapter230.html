Chapter 230: The Fel Ground

"Wake up, hey, Grede."

The dwarf groggily lifted his head. "…What time is it now?"

"A little past two."

"A little past two and you're waking me up?!"

"It's not like we're at your place." Jace picked up his pack and slung it onto his back. "How are you doing?"

"Ah… right, we're not home yet, damn it." Grede propped himself up, lifted his blood-stained armor, and sniffed it, frowning. "Didn't think much of it when I took it off last night, but it's pretty nauseating now."

Jace said, "I wasn't asking about your armor…"

Grede stared at Jace. "How do I feel? Like I just came down with a serious illness. My head feels hollow, my heart feels hollow — all in all, just the normal feeling after drinking a healing potion."

"Are you still sleepy? I found something off. Can you stand and walk? Let's go take a look together."

The dwarf braced himself against a nearby tree trunk and pulled himself up. Once steady, he twisted his waist back and forth, his bones cracking and popping.

"I'm fine. What'd you find? What's off?"

"I can't really explain it."

Hearing that, Grede looked at Jace for a moment. The still-groggy expression on his face slowly turned serious.

The two of them returned to that eerie place screened off by branches and withered wood. Those swaying shadows were still inside. Jace wasn't even sure anymore whether they were gnolls or some other god-knows-what.

He whispered, "You smell that smell?"

"Smelled it. This place reminds me of Duskwood." Grede pressed his hand to his forehead. "What is this place? Are those shadows gnolls? Or is the place goddamn haunted?"

"I just saw them a moment ago. You think it might have something to do with that necromancer?" Jace said.

Grede said in a low voice, "I'd almost forgotten about that necromancer and those walking corpses… damn it, how does Elwynn Forest have such freaky shit? I already thought gnolls, murlocs, and bears were bad enough."

"One surprise after another, huh?" Jace patted his pack. "Sazeno, can you sense any casters here? Arcane, necromancer… warlocks, anything like that."

"Only corpses… the smell of corpses!" the imp whispered.

"Are there any gnolls in there? Can you sense any living people?"

"You're asking too much of me…" the imp said helplessly.

"Useless," Grede said.

The imp asked, "Then you tell me if there are any?"

"How should I know?" Grede said.

"Use—"

Jace clamped a hand over the demon's mouth before it could finish. "All right, all right, all right… you can shut up now."

Grede shot the imp a glance, then looked at Jace. "What's your plan? Storm the camp like before?"

"Storm it?" Jace sighed. "We don't even know what this place is. What if there's a death knight inside?"

"Really, don't scare me like that." Grede's eyes went wide. "But then again, death knights vary a lot too — go ask any soldier who fought in the Hillsbrad campaign! I refuse to believe a death knight hiding in a gnoll nest could be that powerful… didn't you kill one in Dalaran, huh!"

"Let's be careful," Jace said.

Grede said, "How about we let the imp set the place on fire first? Looks like it's all wood and stuff. If there's no reaction after burning it, we go in and have a look."

Jace said, "What if we burn up something important? Don't forget why we came here."

"True…" Grede said. "The bounty on that gnoll chieftain alone is pretty slim. But are you sure there's anything valuable in there?"

"Sure, not sure — I'm not Hogger. What good does asking me do?"

Jace adjusted his hood and stared at the camp for a moment. "I've got an idea. Let the slip in and take a look, see what's inside, and then we decide what to do."

"You sure it'll be fine?" Grede asked.

"Should be fine," Jace said. "If things go wrong, we just leave it and run."

The imp shook Jace's collar in protest. Jace whispered, "If you want to live, you follow my orders. Go in, see if there's anything important, and come right back out. Don't sneak anything out that makes the whole place go up like a bomb — just like last time in Westfall. If that happens, we'll have no choice but to leave you behind. Understood?"

The imp nodded vigorously, leaped out of Jace's pack, and darted toward the camp. Then, with a barely perceptible flash, it vanished.

Grede lay on the dirt slope, staring in the direction it had disappeared. Jace crouched down too, watching the camp's movements.

The camp remained quiet, with no disturbances — until the imp reappeared beside the two of them. Jace asked, "What's it like inside?"

"A lot of gnoll corpses that don't move… and some that do."

Jace glanced over in that direction again. "Not surprising. Besides that, are there any living gnolls?"

"None at all." The imp continued, "But there's a human corpse in the middle, master!"

"Human? A mercenary's corpse?" Grede asked Jace. "Should we go in and take a look? Is it moving too?"

"It's not moving, and it's been dead for ages!" the imp said.

Jace said, "Let's move in quietly. Careful."

The two of them crept forward tree by tree. As they drew close to this eerie camp, Jace peered through the gaps in the crooked trees.

Just like the imp had said, there were only five gnoll zombies in here, stumbling around looking none too bright.

There were at least a dozen gnoll corpses here, and what was bizarre was that all the gnolls were unusually small in stature.

Jace was wondering if he'd just gotten so used to fighting Hogger that every gnoll looked small to him now — when he spotted the human corpse he'd noticed earlier.

This intact human corpse was propped up to one side, surrounded by a pile of small gnoll corpses. It was wrapped in tattered, rotting rags. The corpse was nearly black and looked like it had been here for a very, very long time.

The whole place reeked of decay.

This place was a long way from Westfall Keep, Raven Hill, or even Sentinel Hill. And the nearest of those, Westfall Keep, was separated by a vast stretch of forest occupied by gnolls. It was possible that the soldiers of Stormwind had never discovered this place from start to finish.

"This is really completely fucked," Grede whispered.

This had already exceeded Jace's understanding of Azeroth. After all, in the game, Elwynn Forest didn't have such a bizarre place either.

"We take out these walking corpses and see what's inside," he said.

Grede nodded. The two of them found a gap to slip through and enter. The gnoll zombies noticed the intruders and began to screech — but the two of them moved with brutal speed, cutting down several right away, then working together to finish off the last few.

These gnolls were far less cunning than the ones that necromancer had reanimated earlier — just a pack of rabid lunatics.

This was a small enclosure ringed by withered, rotting logs and branches. The ground was covered in gnoll corpses of every kind, all piled up here — from fresh ones to ones that had been dead for ages.

The human corpse sat in the middle. Jace then realized it hadn't sat itself down and died here — the gnolls had fixed it in place with iron rods and nails and the like, just like a worshipped idol.

Jace covered his nose and moved closer to search the human corpse. Though it looked intact on the surface, up close it was clear the body was highly decayed and blackened. The skin had even cracked open. The inside was probably in even worse shape.

The state of this corpse reminded him of something very unpleasant.

Teron Gorefiend's mortal shell.

Could this be another death knight's mortal shell — the very one they'd been searching for?

"Come over here, Jace!"

Translator Notes

Flags

- A hidden camp deep in Elwynn Forest — unmarked on any map, far from any Stormwind garrison, and saturated with necrotic energy. - The camp contains only gnoll zombies and one ancient human corpse — no living gnolls at all. - The human corpse is propped up like a worshipped idol, pinned in place with iron rods and nails — the gnolls were venerating it. - The corpse is ancient, blackened, and highly decayed — Jace immediately connects it to Teron Gorefiend's mortal shell, suggesting this may be another death knight's body. - Grede's death knight knowledge surfaces — he references the Hillsbrad campaign and downplays the threat, but Jace remains cautious. - Sazeno scouts the camp invisibly and reports back — the imp's utility as a reconnaissance tool continues to grow. - The gnoll zombies here are mindless and far less dangerous than the necromancer's reanimated ones — suggesting a different, weaker source of necromantic power. - Jace's understanding of Azeroth is being challenged — none of this exists in the game's canon, and the implications of a death knight's presence in Elwynn Forest are deeply unsettling.
