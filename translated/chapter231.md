## Chapter 231: The Offering

"Come have a look!" Grede shouted, pointing at a makeshift tent cobbled together from rags.

Jace walked over. Inside the waist-high tent sat no small number of objects: a few coins much like the ogre currency from before, a black shortsword, and a messy pile of papers of varying materials.

The strangest part was that they were ringed by a circle of white candles burned halfway down. Where the hell had the gnolls even gotten those?

The dwarf ducked down and swept all the coins into his pouch. Jace picked up a candle and examined it — there were still bits of coarse hair stuck to the base.

Please tell me these were ripped off kobold heads.

He set the candle down and picked up the black shortsword, holding it up. "What do you make of this?"

Grede reached out, took the sword, and gave it a few experimental swings. "The weapon of the dead bastard outside?"

Jace studied the blade. There was something familiar about it — especially the way it felt in his hand. It reminded him of the long dagger that death knight in Dalaran had carried.

Either way, mine now.

He took the sword back, tied it off with a cord, and hung it from his belt, then gathered up the papers to look through them. Some of the sheets looked like... darkened human skin. The writing on them was cramped and dense — neither Common nor orcish, and it didn't read as demonic script either.

And they all looked like they'd been torn from a book.

A book made of human skin?

Jace flipped the pages back and forth. If this really was human skin, it was a touch too dark.

Sure, there were darker-skinned human ethnicities, but you're not telling me someone made a whole damn skin-book exclusively from those — that's some twisted shit, and besides, you couldn't even read the writing clearly.

If it was orc skin, it wasn't green enough, wasn't yellow enough. These pelts were strictly grayish-blue...

Draenei skin?

The Draenei were the eredar who had fled their homeworld of Argus rather than submit to the Burning Legion. Their leader was one of the three eredar lords, Velen.

They had eventually settled on Draenor, where they came into conflict with the orcs. With the aid of demons, the orcs defeated the Draenei and enslaved the countless ones who couldn't escape. Their fate had been... brutal, to put it mildly.

Thinking about it, Jace felt his scalp tighten. Given everything the orcs had done to the Draenei during their invasion of Azeroth through the Dark Portal, this was entirely plausible.

"Can you read any of this, Sazeno?" Jace asked.

"Some Nathrezim language, master!" The imp held up a sheet. "Necromantic incantations and their uses! But the rest... the rest I couldn't say."

"What else is there besides incantations?"

"The Nathrezim tongue isn't used much by demons other than themselves. I can pronounce it, but as for translating... I only know a small fraction of what's written here." The imp was frank.

Jace looked at the fel-green flames flickering over the imp's body, then at the withered grass and the tent all around them. "Get back in the bag, Sazeno. Stop wandering around out here."

"Yes, master!"

The imp scrambled up Jace's cloak and into the bag.

"What the hell are Nathrezim?" Grede asked.

"Dreadlords," Jace said, cinching the bag shut.

"Dreadlords?" Grede frowned. "Some kind of terrifying human lords? Landed gentry?"

Only then did Jace realize that since the War of the Ancients, dreadlords had never shown themselves openly on this continent. Aside from the most powerful mages, very few people had an accurate understanding of this breed of demon.

"You can think of them as a very intelligent breed of demon. The kind that knows a lot of dark magic — especially necromantic and shadow magic." Jace looked at the human corpse outside. "Gul'dan was able to create death knights. I suspect the Nathrezim had a hand in it. Look at that human body out there — I think it's the husk of a death knight."

"How do you know?" the dwarf asked.

"When Teron Gorefiend fled, he left his body behind. I got a look at it that night — same disgusting state."

As he spoke, Jace recalled the scene in that alley. There had even been a shambling corpse lying right beside Gorefiend's husk.

"You think these undead gnolls were raised by the necromantic energy of this husk?" Jace said. "The gnolls discovered what was happening, so they piled their dead comrades here to keep the resurrections going."

"You sure about that?" Grede asked.

"How else would there be shambling corpses here? You saw the ones the necromancer raised — clearly a different breed from these."

Grede pressed, "The ones the necromancer raised were smarter than these brainless zombies, too! So why would the gnolls pile corpses here and rely on this husk to raise a bunch of trash-tier mindless ghouls? Why not let the necromancer raise them all?"

"Fair point." Jace said, "So then — where did that necromancer come from?"

"What do you mean?" Grede asked.

"That necromancer was an undead itself, Grede. I figured that out while fighting it... that thing didn't fear anything, didn't feel pain. You had to smash its skull to put it down."

Grede scratched his brow and muttered, "An undead necromancer..."

The dwarf was right. If the necromancer could raise more powerful dead, the gnolls wouldn't be piling corpses here, counting on this husk to churn out a bunch of garbage mindless ghouls.

And judging by the shambling corpses wandering around here, even the Riverpaw tribe under Hogger's leadership seemed to have lost interest in what the husk could produce.

Then why had Hogger, in his dying moments, fled this way like a madman?

"If this husk can really raise the dead, is it possible Hogger wanted to come here and count on that thing out there to resurrect him after he died?" the dwarf asked.

"I thought of that too," Jace said. "But why didn't he head back to his own stronghold and let the necromancer resurrect him?"

"Maybe the whole Riverpaw tribe only had the one necromancer, Jace!" Grede said. "Remember — when I went to kill it, Hogger went absolutely berserk trying to stop me..."

"Only one... I just thought of a possibility." Jace said, "What if this husk raised an undead spellcaster — that necromancer — and so the gnolls piled all their warlock corpses here, hoping to raise another necromancer? Look at these gnolls — they're shorter than the gnolls outside. Warlocks tend to run small!"

"And aside from that one, all it raised were these trash little ghouls," Grede added. "Yeah, very possible. I think these gnolls are practically worshipping this human corpse. Look at the setup."

The dwarf's expression grew complicated. He looked at the small space ringed by dead branches and murmured, "Still — do you think this death knight was killed, or did it flee like Gorefiend?"

"Killed by gnolls? What are the odds..." Jace sighed. "Look, let's take this stuff and get out of here. This isn't a good place — we've stayed too long."

He crouched down and gathered up the remaining sheets one by one. "We'll find a safe place to talk."

Grede grabbed Jace's wrist. "What are you planning to do with these papers? We should burn this whole place to the ground!"

"I agree we should burn it," Jace said. "But these documents I'll give to—"

That was when he realized he couldn't hand these over to the mages of the Wizard Sanctum.

If he turned these in and word leaked to the Kirin Tor, would Kel'Thuzad find some excuse to lock him away — or have him killed outright?

Even worse — would he make Jace his apprentice?

He didn't want any more involvement with Kel'Thuzad. None.

"What are you hesitating about, Jace?" Grede grabbed his collar. "You're not thinking of learning the necromantic magic written on these things, are you?"

"Are you out of your mind?!" Jace said. "If I hand these over to the mages, I could get labeled a necromancy practitioner, Grede. I can't just hand them over willy-nilly. You have any idea how serious that is?"

"I want to trust you, Jace," Grede said. "If you're planning to become a necromancer, I won't let you live."

"Even for elves, necromancy is an untouchable taboo — let alone for us. I'd rather burn the two of us along with this godforsaken place than let this stuff leak out. You saw what that necromancer was doing back there... Damn it! Unless you want all of Elwynn Forest and Stormwind to end up like Duskwood? Isn't it possible the whole Raven Hill cemetery started out fine, until some mage just like you wanted to learn this orc-left-behind crap and caused the whole mess?"

"I won't learn it, you have my word. But this is critical evidence — we can't just burn it," Jace said. "What if it has clues about other Horde necromancers, or information about that death knight, and not just incantations and spells? After all, the imp can't say for sure what's on here. Burning it would just make the trail go cold! Even if it's only incantations... there'd still be usable leads. That elf — the one from the Relic Hunters — told me they can use magic to trace any lingering arcane residue on an object and find everyone who's touched it. Think about it — if we could get this into the hands of those elves... Right — so why not sell it to the elves?"

Grede blinked. "That's... let the elves deal with it. Give it to that Windrunner woman — isn't she looking for death knights? Let her look... Damn it, I don't want to deal with this stuff anymore. You know where she's staying?"

"At my house."

"What?!" Grede grabbed Jace's cloak. "Why?"

"I... have another place. Let go."

"What? You have a second house in Stormwind?"

"A Wizard Sanctum apartment... let go."
