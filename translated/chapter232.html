Chapter 232: The Legend of the One-Armed Kul Tiran Hero, Gnoll Slayer, and Archmage

After burning the entire place to the ground, the two of them stood watching the dreadful blaze. The scorched stink carried with it a nauseating reek of rot.

"I'm half afraid we've caught corpse-poison," Jace muttered, scratching his wrist. "When we get back I'm going to the Cathedral of Light for a check-up."

"Me too," Grede said. "Hey — you think there's any point going back to check on Hogger's corpse? Should we smash its skull up a bit more, make sure it doesn't get back up?"

"Makes sense."

The two of them ran all the way back to where Hogger had fallen. One wielding a cleaver and the other a battered sword, they hacked the massive gnoll leader's corpse to a thoroughly unrecognizable pulp before finally calling it done.

Compared to the bodies they'd seen at that altar back there, this enormous pile of mangled meat almost looked presentable.

Grede panted, "Promise me you won't learn these incantations."

"You have my word," Jace said. "Even if I wanted to learn them, I'd need the talent for it. And based on my track record with the arcane so far — it took me days just to finally cast Light competently, Arcane Intellect took God knows how long to get down, and Mage Armor... don't even get me started. Right now I can't even pick out which passages on these papers are the incantations. How the hell am I supposed to learn them?"

"I'll say it plain — if you try to learn necromancy, Jace," Grede said, "I'll kill you on the spot." Jace shook the papers at him. "I'll take these straight to Vereesa when we get back, all right? Back in Stormwind I'll go find her directly. No chance to learn a thing."

"Fine. I'll be watching you," the dwarf nodded.

Jace asked, "Then why don't you hold onto these papers yourself, if you're so worried I'll steal them?"

The dwarf immediately barked, "No! Keep that stuff away from me, damn it. I'm not carrying them."

"Then stop running your mouth!" Jace said, exasperated.

In truth, he'd never had any intention of learning necromancy.

It was a road that led nowhere you could control. Everyone who so much as brushed up against necromancy ended up dead and buried — or worse, couldn't tell the difference anymore. Everyone close to them wound up with a fate that wasn't worth looking at, and even in death they found no peace.

So he understood Grede's reaction perfectly well.

If not for that business in Dalaran, he might have handed these straight over to the Wizard Sanctum. But now he couldn't do that.

Either destroy the material, or give it to the elves. He didn't even want to try translating it. He wanted nothing to do with this stuff.

If Vereesa hadn't been in Stormwind, he might well have joined Grede in burning these papers right alongside that camp and the death knight's husk.

Midday, the two of them dragged their exhausted bodies out onto the main road at the forest's edge. Catching sight of the watchtower near Westfall Keep, it felt like they'd finally returned to a sane world.

"I thought we'd never make it back," Grede grumbled. "If this world really had a Shadowlands, I bet it'd look just like what's in there."

With that, he glanced over at Jace, who'd just leaned against a road sign. "Promise me — promise me for real you won't learn necromancy."

"I won't. That's the fourteenth time you've asked."

"And how am I supposed to believe you..."

"Tell you what." Jace raised a hand in the shape of the Silver Hand. "I swear on the Holy Light."

"No!"

"Then I swear on... on all my gold — if I learn necromancy, may I never afford a house in Stormwind."

"Ah... that's more like it," Grede nodded.

Jace figured, at least this was Stormwind. If he'd been back in his home province with his old two years of swearing, that oath would've been worth about as much as air.

Just then a patrol of soldiers came up the road heading toward Westfall Keep. Catching sight of the two of them, one of the soldiers called out, "How about it, gents — run into any gnolls?"

"We did!" Grede dropped the cloth sack containing Hogger's claws onto the ground. "Ran into an extra-large one." The soldiers hurried over, flipped open the bag, and every last one of them looked stunned.

"Is this Hogger's claw?"

"The coloring's right... it's really dead..."

"Hogger's dead! Hogger's claw is here! Only Hogger's claws could grow that big — the size of an orc's hand..."

"The hero of Westfall has been born — quick, come see, Hogger's dead!"

The two of them were swept up by the crowd of soldiers and pushed toward the watchtower. The whole way Jace clutched his bag tight, terrified those necromantic skin-papers might get squeezed out and spill in front of everyone.

At the watchtower, the marshal had put on a full suit of Stormwind armor to come out and greet them. He looked at Jace with wide eyes. "You... aren't you the mage from that night?"

"That's me, sir," Jace said.

"The two of you alone? I know there's a three-man team still out in the forest — I've got a bad feeling about them."

Jace and Grede exchanged a glance. So that seven-man party had been completely unknown to the soldiers here. Chances are the two of them had run into professional bandits posing as mercenaries.

The marshal said, "Regardless, the trade routes and the settlers around here — including over in Westfall — are going to have a few years of peace. I think I can speak for the people and soldiers of Goldshire and Westfall in thanking you two for what you've done... Honestly, I feel the bounty's a bit on the low side, but the king really can't spare any more."

The marshal handed the two of them the money sack, delivered an impassionate flood of praise, but there truly wasn't any extra reward to speak of. Jace thought: no wonder nobody had ever managed to bring down Hogger. The shit-to-reward ratio here was completely out of whack. They'd need to raise it to at least three or four gold to make sense.

"So much talk, and I still don't know your two names..."

"Grede Thunderfist," Grede said, and spelled out his Common name.

Jace hesitated for a moment, but considering the necrotic altar had already been burned to ash and the only lead was in his own hands, there wasn't much to worry about. He signed his name.

The marshal glanced at Jace's signature, frowned, and asked, "You're Jace Sesso? What's your relation to the one who saved Stonewatch? Or are you that person??"

Jace admitted it plainly. "I am."

"This here's the gnoll slayer of Stonewatch!" the marshal told the soldiers around him. "You all don't know about the gnoll attack at Stonewatch? I heard the king rewarded him with a royal-family heirloom sword!"

"Hold on—" Jace said. "He didn't!"

A nearby soldier said, "No wonder he could take down Hogger... I said it before — if anyone could kill Hogger, it'd be that mage from Stonewatch!"

"Is it really Jace Sesso?"

Hearing the commotion from inside the watchtower, plenty of heads poked in to look. Jace stood there blank-faced, thinking: what royal-family heirloom sword? He'd gotten no such thing.

"Really or not?" Grede looked at Jace in a low voice. "There really was a sword? Did you sell it yourself on the sly?"

Jace glanced at the crowd and whispered, "You think I'd sell a sword given to me by Varian Wrynn? Don't talk nonsense."

"He can't be Jace Sesso — I heard Jace Sesso was a one-armed Kul Tiran."

"You heard wrong. He's taller than a Kul Tiran, not actually Kul Tiran."

"This lad isn't nearly that tall, and both his arms are still attached!"

Jace sensed this was getting out of hand. He explained for ages and couldn't talk any sense into them. In the end, it was only with the marshal's help that he finally got the story of what had really happened at Stonewatch straightened out.

It was just a tale of a handful of Orc War veterans holding the fortress together with Jace — nothing like the legend.

Fortunately, the soldiers were used to hearing outrageous tales, so they had some mental preparation for the truth coming out.

Combined with Jace showing off the faint burn scars on his arm, and the corroboration from a few veterans who'd heard a slightly more reliable version of the story, they managed to sort out his account at Westfall Keep.

But Westfall was, after all, just a small outpost. Who knew what kind of absurd legends about him he'd run into down the road.

He took a proper bath in the Nafeltari River, scrubbed every piece of clothing he'd been wearing clean of the corpse-stench, changed into the old military shirt and trousers the soldiers had given him for free, and the two of them boarded a carriage for the ride back to Stormwind.

After all the warmth from the people of Westfall, Jace's mood had improved quite a bit. The necromantic mess — that could be dealt with slowly.

Drowsiness hit, and he drifted off to sleep, the rumble of wheels and the clop of hooves the last things he heard.
