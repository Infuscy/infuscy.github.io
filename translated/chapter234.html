Chapter 234: Innate Understanding

Jace looked at Galders's mysterious expression, then glanced at Sulenna. This incantation was clearly no simple matter.

"Will it take long? I'm actually here looking for Ersula Drenn."

Galders said, "She said she was turning in just a while ago — it's gone quiet over there by now."

"That early?"

"She's never kept normal hours. Want me to go wake her?"

"No, I'll catch her another day," Jace said.

Galders said, "In that case, come take a look at this incantation with me. Jagin and I were both hoping you'd give it a try."

"What incantation?"

"Don't know," Galders said. "That's why we wanted you to take a look."

Now Jace was genuinely interested.

"Cute and so talented, oh my." Sulenna shook her head. "I wonder how your parents made you, Jace — is there a trick to childbirth? I'd like to learn..."

Erland, clearly displeased, bared his teeth in a quiet hiss. "You crazy woman, drink a little less for once..."

Sulenna shot him a look and muttered something under her breath.

Jace followed Galders into the side passage. As they walked, Galders said, "I heard from Ersula the other day that you were back. Why didn't you come find me? You didn't look like you were doing too well."

"I was preparing things in the Mage Quarter these past couple days... ran into some trouble, but it's nothing now," Jace explained, then asked, "How did you even get that incantation? You really have no idea what it is?"

Galders said, "As for the source — don't ask. As for the incantation itself, Jagin and Sulenna and I have all tried it recently, but none of us could cast it successfully. It's no simple spell... it's too long, requires being able to hold intense concentration for a long stretch. Jagin figured you spend all that time around mages, so you might have some different techniques. He wanted to wait for you to get back and give it a try, see what exactly this spell is."

Jace said, "Don't count on me necessarily getting it to work."

"I won't. But if even you can't figure it out," Galders said, "I'll have to head to Dun Morogh and ask around."

"Do the warlocks over in Ironforge know this incantation?" Jace asked.

"I'm not sure either." Galders led Jace deeper into the passage as he spoke, stopped at his own room to fetch a book, then continued toward the warlocks' "practice room."

"Maybe they do, maybe they don't. We haven't been in much contact lately. For all I know those warlocks are celebrating Winterveil too."

Arriving at the practice room, Jace took the piece of skin-paper Galders handed him, tucked inside the book.

The sight of the paper immediately reminded him of the Nathrezim-script sheets in his bag, and in a flash the scene at that mysterious necrotic shrine came back to him.

"What's wrong, Jace?"

"Nothing." He looked at the magic circle that had been drawn at some point and couldn't help asking, "Was this drawn for this incantation?"

"Yes. It's a shadow-amplification circle we got off a dwarf, but... it doesn't seem to be doing much. Go ahead and try using it for now," Galders said. "Try reading it through. I'll correct your pronunciation."

"If I just read it, nothing's going to trigger, right?"

"If it did, that'd save us a lot of trouble," Galders said with a wry smile.

Jace nodded. The moment he looked at the Common-script characters he could tell they were phonetic transcriptions of demonic, and there were familiar sounds in there.

"Tatalini, Monta, Yinhatong, Tuliluokeseni, Tayatansayadini, Binotusi, Tarvisaite, Elemann, Wuerhaokesijisi."

It really was too long. Reading through it, Jace kept feeling like something about the incantation was off, and his delivery was halting.

But after listening, Galders said, "Not bad, Jace. I've got nothing to correct — I knew I should come to you. All right, let's begin the actual casting. The casting notes are in this booklet."

Jace took Galders's booklet and looked at the heavily edited notes. "Were these casting notes found along with the incantation?"

"No." Galders said, "These casting gestures I worked out bit by bit from other shadow magic. This version is the one that feels the most responsive. The rest... I'll have to rely on your powerful innate understanding, Jace."

"Innate understanding?"

"What's that look for?" Galders said. "Don't put on that innocent act with me, Jace Sesso. You mean to tell me you're completely oblivious to your own shadow affinity? Even Jagin can feel it — and I think in his eyes, you're the replacement for that fellow Morgas. And I've always thought you're no replacement, Jace. You're one of a kind."

"Don't say that. I'm just a member of the circle like anyone else," Jace said.

Galders nodded. "Of course. Every one of us has our own gift — it's just that most people fumble through their whole lives searching for it. And the day you walked into the Slaughtered Lamb, you put an end to that long search before it even began."

Jace couldn't tell if Galders was giving him a genuine compliment or just blowing smoke to get him to hurry up and try the incantation.

In truth, of course, he wasn't unaware of his connection to the shadows — he just didn't know where it came from.

When he tried to channel shadow magic, he could feel the shadow energy around him grow excited, almost manic. The arcane never gave him a feeling like that.

But then again, for mages with powerful arcane affinity, casting was a thrilling experience too.

After all, the arcane was addictive — and the four laws of magic were written right on the walls of the Wizard Sanctum:

Magic is power; magic invites corruption; magic is addictive; to touch the Twisting Nether is to fly into the flame like a moth.

Jace didn't have much personal experience with the arcane side of that. But with shadow... he felt it, truly and viscerally.

He'd actually always had a wild theory: as a transmigrator, he'd spent so much of his past life idly trawling every last corner of the internet for weird shit that all that grim, dark stuff had piled up in his head. Compared to the people around him, his negative-energy concentration was off the charts — like rotting meat drawing flies, it attracted all that dark energy straight to him.

How else could he explain his connection to the shadow?

Really, just like Galders said — everyone had their own gift. Was he just lucky?

"So, ready to give it a try?" Galders asked.

"All right," Jace said. "I'm just... not entirely sure about this incantation."

Galders stepped aside. "In Jagin's words, this is part of the charm of the dark arts, no? Two thousand years ago, the Arathi mages faced the arcane path in total ignorance too, but in the end they carved out a path different from the elves through their own exploration. Now it's our turn."
