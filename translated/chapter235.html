Chapter 235: A Lively Soul

Jace stepped to the center of the circle, kept the casting notes in mind, and began reading the characters off the paper.

At first he felt nothing, but by the middle of the incantation he clearly sensed something — a pull toward some unknown force... though the bizarre feeling faded faster than it had come.

He tried to reach into the shadow to reclaim that power, but it was like grasping at nothing. He rarely felt this kind of helplessness when releasing shadow magic.

By the time he reached the tail end of the incantation — the point where he'd been most expecting to feel the magic surge at its strongest — everything came to an abrupt halt, catching him off guard.

After finishing the recitation, Jace watched the faintly glowing circle dim, feeling as though his consciousness was still being pulled at by something, not yet back. It took a while for the daze to clear.

Zardeth folded his arms, pondered for a moment, then asked, "You didn't feel anything either?"

"Not exactly nothing," Jace said. "It seems like something responded to the incantation, but I couldn't tell you what. Did you two feel the same way?"

"More or less." Zardeth said, "The two of us felt very similar. Gakin... he has his own interpretation. As for Surena, I think something about her casting process was off, so let's not use her as a reference. First off — do you think this is a shadow magic spell?"

"Of course it is." Jace had been about to say it was because this was a Demonic incantation, but it occurred to him all at once that he probably shouldn't let these people know he had a channel for learning the Demonic tongue.

Even now, he didn't trust any of these people — not Zardeth, not Gakin, not Erlan or Surena or anyone of that sort.

If he had to call anyone a friend, it was probably only Ursula — the only warlock who showed little interest in learning magic...

"Let me put it this way," Zardeth said. "Do you think this is a straightforward offensive spell, or something that channels shadow energy to influence others, something like Fear..."

"You want the honest truth, Zardeth?" Jace asked.

"Of course."

"I think this is a summoning spell," Jace said. "When I was casting, I felt something similar to the sensation of connecting with another world that I get when summoning an imp — just not very strongly."

Zardeth smiled. "That's what I think too. Gakin keeps insisting it's some kind of direct-damage spell, but if that were the case it'd be way too long for what it does."

"I'm sorry, Zardeth." Jace looked at the paper. "As to what exactly this spell is, I'm genuinely no help."

Zardeth handed Jace a copy. "Take this back and study it carefully... If you hit a wall, we'll share notes. Gakin and I have been puzzling over this thing this whole time, and he's itching for a breakthrough."

"All right, I've got it." Jace put the copy in his bag and returned to the hall. Erlan had left at some point. Surena was leaning against the wall and asked offhandedly, "How'd it go? That was fast."

"Nothing came of it."

"Not much use, are you, kid." Surena sounded drunk, though her dark skin made it impossible to tell if she was flushed.

She stared at Jace, holding her ale. "Come have a drink?"

"No." Jace said coldly.

"What the hell is your problem, walking around looking like the world wronged you every single day? How many troubles could there possibly be? Jace, sometimes I really can't figure you out... Find ways to make yourself a little happier, a little more cheerful."

Hearing that, Jace looked at her again, walked over, picked up the half-full mug, clinked it against Surena's, and drained it in one gulp.

"Another?" Surena asked with a smile.

"No more." Jace licked the ale foam from the corner of his lips. "I need to head home and sleep. Thanks for saying all that."

After getting home that night and closing the door, Jace's sleepiness had more or less evaporated.

"Sazeno, hold off on whatever you're doing and take a look at this for me."

"Coming, master!" For Sazeno these days, anything that didn't involve copying text counted as interesting work.

"Take a look at this incantation... what does it mean, and how should it be read properly."

"Ah... master, you've been tricked." Sazeno laughed. "This isn't an incantation!"

"What?" Jace asked. "Then what is it?"

"Look." Sazeno pointed at the front. "This line says, 'it should be recited as follows.' After that, 'a lively soul is required.' And the middle part... ah, that's a real incantation — seems to be a spell to summon a demon from the 'Boundless Realm.'"

"What's the Boundless Realm?"

"How would I know, master? The Twisting Nether has thousands of worlds — some random place gets mentioned and how am I supposed to find it! This is definitely a note some other demon wrote to his master, and somehow you got your hands on it!"

Jace turned it over in his mind for a while. Could it mean the Void realm? A spell to summon a voidwalker?

No — that couldn't be right. Sazeno would know where the Void lords dwelt.

What was a "lively soul"? A soul shard?

But he didn't know how to drain souls right now...

"So, how do you get a soul? You don't need to sacrifice a living person to cast this spell successfully, do you?"

"Souls are a broad category!" the imp said. "I'd say a mortal is best, but animals would do too — a lamb, or a calf, something like that! Sacrifice it during the casting... and you'll need a magic circle, my master."

"Can you draw one?"

"Heheh, heheh." The imp grinned stupidly. "Sacrificial stuff like that, of course I can... but I have a condition..."

Jace grabbed it by the neck. "You're negotiating a condition with your master?"

"No deal! No deal! Whatever master says goes!"

"You've got some sense." Jace let go, sat on the bed and thought for a while. "Would a rat do? Rats are smart enough!!"

"Rats are smart enough," the imp rubbed its neck, "but their power is far too small, master. It would actually cause a catastrophic failure because the soul wouldn't channel enough energy!"

"You've heard about how, ten thousand years ago, the great master of the Burning Legion wanted to personally descend upon Azeroth? In the end, the painstakingly-made portal was blown up by a bunch of weak natives, and his head got suddenly caught in the closing portal passage! Hahahaha! Growing an extra pair of horns really is inconvenient, hahahaha!"

Jace stared expressionlessly as the imp cackled itself into a frenzy, slapping its knees and shaking all over like it was having a seizure. This time he truly believed it had absolutely nothing to do with the Burning Legion.

"Stop laughing. First teach me to smooth out the pronunciation — this Common-script reading definitely isn't accurate enough."

"Let me spell these words out first."

With the imp's help, Jace refined the incantation back into the Demonic tongue: "Inhajet, Tulioxenoni, Tayatansayidini, Binodoxis."

All that was left now was an animal sacrifice. A cow would be hard to manage, but a lamb should do.
