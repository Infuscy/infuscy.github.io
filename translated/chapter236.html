Chapter 236: The Best Candidate

It was the first day of term, but Jace had no classes on Monday. He arrived at Marin's study early, thinking that as an assistant he probably ought to do some cleaning at least.

In truth he'd been planning to slack off, but then he'd started thinking about that incantation from last night — wondering whether to buy a lamb at the market or swipe one from a farm outside the city — and before he knew it, he'd already gotten to work.

Not long after, Amy Marin walked in.

"Oh, you're here, Jace!"

"Amy." Jace asked, "So — how's that pen working out?"

"How could I bear to use it!" Amy said. "I'm afraid it'll just collect dust sitting there, let alone let it touch ink. Oh — I heard from my dad that you ran into a black dragon. Is that actually true?"

"It's true. In Menethil Harbor."

Jace told the whole story of the black dragon encounter from start to finish — naturally, the version Grede had given to the Menethil Harbor soldiers — and finished by saying he'd blinded the dragon's eyes with the Light cantrip.

Amy took a deep breath when he was done and shook her head.

"You don't believe me?" Jace asked.

"I'm just thinking — if I ran into a dragon… I'd probably panic and be completely useless." Amy sighed. "I hope when the time comes I can be like you, and have the will to stand against evil."

"You never know how you'll react until you're actually in it," Jace said. "Honestly, before I ran into that black dragon, I thought I'd freeze up too. But when it actually flew overhead, I just felt like I had to make it pay."

"Now I understand how you faced down all those gnolls at Stonewatch," Amy said with a smile. "So — is that how you met that Miss A'seluun? Meeting a Quel'Thalas ranger in the middle of a dragon-slaying battle? It's like something out of a poem."

Jace blinked.

Amy said, "I saw you having a meal with that elf together. Did you think the area around the Wizard Sanctum was some huge place? I saw her waiting for you there when I went out. I honestly couldn't say which surprised me more — you running into a black dragon, or you dating an elf."

Jace sighed. "We met in Dalaran. She came here to investigate the orcs and tracked me down because she thought I might know something. As for dating… I don't think she has much love for humans."

Right — he wondered if Vereesa had left Stormwind yet, and whether she'd returned the key.

"Has she been by the past couple of days?"

"No, actually — I haven't seen her since that day. You're pretty concerned about her, aren't you?" Amy leaned in.

"Don't you have class this morning?!" Jace asked suddenly.

"Oh… right!" Amy snatched up her book and muttered under her breath, "Damn it…"

The young mage dashed out of the room. Jace stared at the half-open door and thought, This woman is really nosy.

He was still staring at the door when Marin suddenly shoved it open with an armload of books.

"Good morning!"

"Master!"

Marin dumped the entire pile onto the desk with a thud and sighed to himself. "Another batch of dunces."

"So," he said, looking up. "You haven't come by the past two days to ask if there's anything you need to do, and now you show up on the first day of class? Don't forget you're still my assistant, Mr. Sesso."

"Sorry, Master. I've beeb pretty busy the past couple of days…"

"Busy killing those Riverpaw gnolls?"

"You know about that?"

"Of course I know! You've practically cemented that 'gnoll killer' reputation of yours." Marin sat down behind the desk.

Jace thought about all the ridiculous things he'd heard at Westfall Keep yesterday and slapped his palm to his face in embarrassment.

Marin picked up a letter tucked between the books and read aloud: "Mr. Jace Sesso, mage of the Wizard Sanctum… Ha — the Westfall Keep sheriff's thank-you letter spells it all out quite clearly. I didn't take you for the vindictive type — you get hit, you hit right back, eh? How much bounty did you collect?"

Jace said, "Sixty silver, split two ways."

Marin nodded. "Not a small sum. But considering the trouble it's brought you, it's a little on the low side."

"I thought the King would brood over it for another half a year at least. With it dead, the western half of Elwynn Forest should settle down for quite a while. The remaining gnolls will probably flee west across the Nafeltari River to join their kin — and that'll be Sentinel Hill's problem."

"The vitality of that Riverpaw gnoll pack has always been an issue. Actually, two weeks ago King Varian wrote hoping that Archmage Andormas would dispatch investigators, but we hadn't even had time to prepare. Since you took part in the fight, what do you think made those Riverpaw gnolls so special? How did they manage to defeat so many mercenary bands?"

"There were undead among those gnolls," Jace said. "We guessed that the necromantic energy of Duskwood was affecting the land north of the Nafeltari, Master. My comrade and I both saw it clearly."

Marin sighed. "So the gnoll zombie business is true? I'd thought it was just a rumor until now. Right now we've got mages in Menethil Harbor, some in Redridge helping the kingdom deal with gnolls, some in Duskwood investigating Karazhan's influence, and some in the Black Swamp searching for orc warlocks… There's simply no spare manpower to investigate the undead on the northern shore. Fortunately this matter is settled for now — so not only did the people of Westfall Keep, you saved those mages a trip too, Jace. I should get Andormas to buy you a new robe."

He pointed at the arrow hole in Jace's chest.

"Really?"

"Really. I'll go talk to him this afternoon. Why should my student clean up the Wizard Sanctum's problems and get nothing in return? Don't cover it — this spot here was torn by a gnoll, wasn't it?"

"Uh, right."

"You see? Like that. You shouldn't have to spend your own coin solving these problems."

Jace nodded emphatically. Couldn't have said it better myself.

Marin said, "I heard about your black dragon encounter in Menethil Harbor, Jace. I imagine you've heard enough praise already. Remember what we suspected back then — the black dragons coercing the red dragons on the orcs' behalf… I have a feeling the Wizard Sanctum is getting close to the truth. While their mages are investigating in the Wetlands, we can't slack off on our end either. I'm planning to send you to Redridge again. When are you free?"

"Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday."

"How about this Friday?" Marin said. "If you're not back by Tuesday, I'll get your leave sorted out."

"What's the job?"

"Someone claims to have found a black dragon scale. Right now the only person I can find who's actually seen a real black dragon with their own eyes is you. Go there, confirm whether it really is from a black dragon, and if it is, negotiate a good price and buy it — the Wizard Sanctum will cover the cost."

"Dragon scale…" Jace nodded. "Understood."

"I didn't memorize all the details at once. Tomorrow I'll copy out the letter and leave it at your apartment — alright?"

"No… I'll come by the day after tomorrow to get it, Master."

"Fine." Marin glanced at his pocket watch. "I'd meant to chat with you about that death knight too, but we've wasted too much time. We'll leave that for next time. Your Mage Armor incantation — that accident last time hasn't left you with a psychological scar, has it? Or would you rather take some time with the School of Abjuration coursework before trying again?"

"I can try now," Jace said. "I feel like I picked up a lot of new insights on this trip."

He recalled that he'd stolen the incantation himself… which meant he shouldn't be particularly proficient with it.

"Do you need me to demonstrate it once more?" Marin said. "You almost had it last time — go ahead and do it yourself."

The old mage gestured with a finger toward the middle of the room. Jace nodded and walked to the violet carpet.

He figured he should cast it slowly, doing his best to still look like he was getting the hang of it. He'd just started chanting when something struck him as wrong.

"Wait, Master… the incantation… I haven't memorized it yet."

Marin crossed his right leg over his left, hands folded on his knees, eyes half-lidded. "This is the first time I've seen an apprentice start chanting out loud only to realize they haven't memorized the spell. Let's not pretend anymore — let me see what you've been practicing on the sly these two months."

Jace scratched his head. "You saw through me."

He smoothly completed a Mage Armor. The arcane energy flowing over his body filled the cramped study with a beautiful shimmer.

Marin nodded. "I can see your mana has grown quite a bit since you left Stormwind, Mr. Sesso… though there are still two small problems in your incantation. I'm guessing you memorized it purely through Arcane Intellect right before you left, from hearing me chant it? Good thing — for a moment I thought you'd broken into my cabinet."

Now Jace was genuinely impressed.
