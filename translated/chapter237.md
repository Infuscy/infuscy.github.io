## Chapter 237: Arcane Talent?!

Marin pulled that book from his cabinet and flipped to the Mage Armor incantation. "Mage Armor is a dragon-tongue spell. If you haven't studied the dragon tongue, even if you replicate someone else's incantation word for word, you'll have problems you can't even detect yourself. This is why we use language study as our foundation before we ever start chanting spells."

"As a Lordaeronian you should know — if you want to sing a Tirisfal hymn, you have to learn the pronunciation rules of Old Tirisfal, not just transliterate it crudely into modern Common and sing it phonetically. Forcing your way through what is practically another language doesn't just sound wrong and miss the emotional nuance — it's terrifyingly inefficient if you want to learn more pieces. That's how it is for singers, and we mages deal with far more dangerous material."

"I understand." Jace nodded, a little sheepish.

"I have a question, Master. Is the dragon tongue they teach at the Wizard Sanctum the real dragon tongue?"

"Of course it's the real dragon tongue — you think they'd teach you a fake one?" Marin had just said it when he seemed to catch Jace's meaning. "Ah… you mean — the language of dragons?"

"Right. The language dragons speak." Jace emphasized.

Marin shook his head. "No. What the Wizard Sanctum calls the dragon tongue is what's been passed down through elven accounts. It's a vehicle for magic dating back to the ancient elven empire and even earlier — not literally the language of dragons. You could say there was some connection in the earliest days, but that's all. If you run into a dragon next time, don't try to talk to it in this tongue — you'd be better off just using Common. Supposedly all older dragons speak Common. As for the details, I'm not too clear myself, since I've never seen an adult dragon."

"Young dragons speak Common too," Jace said. "That black dragon in Menethil Harbor threatened me in Common, and it was pretty standard."

Marin nodded, surprised. "If that dragon was actually driven to the point of threatening you in words, that alone proves you played the leading role in that fight… Many records say the proud dragonkin, black dragons especially, look down on speaking the mortal tongues — unless they're pushed to the extreme."

"That said, Jace, whether young dragons speak Common is still a matter of considerable debate. Quite a number of scholars don't accept it. If you really did hear it speak Common, you should write it down and get a few witnesses to help you recall the details. That account of yours might just get stored in the Wizard Sanctum's library."

"I was wondering — what would happen if you tried to talk to a dragon in this dragon tongue?" Jace asked curiously.

"I don't know," Marin said. "But plenty of dragon-tongue textbooks mention that you shouldn't try to communicate with real dragons in this language, or it won't end well. I'm guessing some warrior tried it once, and it went very badly for him."

"Enough of that idle chatter — time is precious. Your Mage Armor looks like it's coming along fine. Once you've learned the dragon tongue, you can further refine your pronunciation. Oh right, Jace — one small detail. I noticed your Mage Armor shows particularly vivid resistance to shadow and fire. What's going on there? Is it related to the incantation phrasing?"

"I…" Jace hadn't prepared for that. "Is it? I didn't notice."

Marin waved a hand. "Well, it's normal for Mage Armor to have uneven resistance to different elements for all sorts of reasons. Who can perfectly balance the power of every element? Even a powerful shaman can't manage that. Let's talk about your next spell."

Jace, seeing that Marin had let it slide, quickly seized the thread and kept the conversation going. "Uh, I'd like to learn something offensive. Master Marin, you're an expert in fire magic, aren't you?"

"At the Wizard Sanctum, you could say that," Marin said. "You want to learn fire magic? Sure — learning a few Evocation tricks for self-defense isn't a bad idea. Shall we start with the most basic — Ignite?"

Ignite. Jace had heard of that spell before — it was basically a lighter. Given that the imp could already use Immolate, wouldn't learning this be a bit of a waste of time?

Seeing Jace hesitate, Marin said, "You've learned Mage Armor and now you're looking down on these low-level spells, eh? Alright then — what do you want to learn? Fireball? Fireball is a tricky spell to control. Don't let outsiders fool you with all their jokes about fireball-slinging fire mages like it's some kind of circus act… Amy's Fireball couldn't blow up that cabinet over there. Prince Kael'thas's Fireball might be able to blast the top off that tower outside — I mean that literally."

"Though if he really wanted to do it, he'd more likely just use a Pyroblast and level the entire Mage Quarter." The old mage raised an eyebrow.

"I don't doubt that at all… but doesn't Fireball need a long casting time?" Jace ask.

"Compared to Ignite and Light, yes, it's long. Compared to Mage Armor, it's not that long," Marin explained. "But it is a battlefield technique, and for a fast-paced skirmish it really is a bit slow."

Jace wanted something faster — something that could keep up with the close-range fights he kept getting into. These days he could barely keep up with a short incantation like Curse of Weakness in the heat of combat, let alone a Fireball that needed a few seconds of channeling.

"Is there something that takes effect instantly and deals direct damage?"

Marin nodded. "Scorch. Like you said — fast. But its destructive power is no match for Fireball, and it demands a high level of mental focus from the caster, precisely because the incantation is so short. Long incantations have their advantages — they make it easier for the caster to concentrate while chanting."

Jace said, "That one, Master. Scorch should be simpler than Fireball, right? Learning Mage Armor was honestly a bit too painful for me. I'd like something a little easier to build up my confidence — I don't have any particular talent for the arcane, after all…"

"Don't sell yourself short, Mr. Sesso." The old mage stood up and walked to the cabinet behind him, rummering through books as he spoke. "Your arcane affinity is absolutely not the worst among my students… more than enough to get you started, at any rate. And your language talent is outstanding even among my fellow instructors — I mean that sincerely, even if the way you stole my incantation didn't exactly fill me with joy."

"I'm really sorry…"

"I'm doing this for your own good." Marin pulled out a red-covered book and flipped to the first few pages. "Repeat after me — *Kibimofulosas*."

After repeating it a few times with Marin, Jace could already feel magical fluctuations when he chanted — did that mean he'd developed a certain degree of arcane affinity?

Maybe it was because he'd cast so many spells that he'd built up a connection to the arcane. Maybe it was that vial of Sunwell water… Either way, he could feel that he was a little different now.

Once he realized that, the excitement became impossible to contain.

But if he hadn't chosen the path of shadow in the first place, none of this would have happened.

He was becoming more and more convinced of his original belief: even knowing the future, even understanding so much about this world, without enough strength none of that advantage mattered.

He had to find a way to make himself stronger — only then could he seize the opportunities that came his way.

"Now, let me demonstrate."

Marin picked up a copy of *A Troll's Romance* from another bookshelf, tossed it into the air, murmured "*Kibimofulosas*" with a furrow of his brow, palms flung open — and the novel suddenly erupted into a swelling ball of flame in an unnatural blaze of heat and light that made Jace flinch. The book vanished entirely, barely leaving behind a scrap of ash.

"Ah, I've been wanting to get rid of that waste of ink for ages. A very simple spell, Mr. Jace Sesso. Now you give it a try."

Jace nodded. "What should I aim at?"

Marin stood in the center of the room and smoothed out the wrinkles in his robe from sitting too long. "Aim at me!"

"It won't hurt you…?" Jace asked, a little worried.

Marin gave a slight smile. "If you can hurt me, I'll write a letter to Archmage Andormas myself recommending you for promotion to apprentice mage and have you transferred straight to the School of Evocation. The School of Abjuration certainly can't afford to waste your talent, Jace Sesso."

"Fine…" Jace said awkwardly, and stood opposite Marin. "I'm ready."

Marin murmured an incantation Jace had never heard before. A mass of arcane energy turned red as flame and coiled around him — stunning, like a crimson lotus in bloom. A fire barrier, then?

No wonder Marin had said Jace could get promoted to apprentice mage if he could hurt him. Breaking through a fire mage's barrier with a fast-cast spell — that level of skill was probably only found among the top mages in all of the Kirin Tor.

"Go ahead, Mr. Sesso," Marin said.

Jace nodded, ran through the casting gestures from the book in his mind, referenced Marin's demonstration from a moment ago, and focused. "*Kibimofulosas!*"

A wave of exhilaration washed over him like nothing he'd ever felt. *This* was the power of magic? The sheer release of pressure was something none of his previous spells had ever given him.

Had he developed arcane talent after all?

As he chanted, he thrust his palm toward the old mage — and a streak of dark-red fire exploded across Marin's body, only to melt rapidly into his barrier as if it had never happened. Yet Marin frowned…

The barrier vanished in an instant. Marin stumbled a step and caught himself on the desk, hands scrambling for support and nearly knocking the whole pile of books to the floor. Jace was startled and rushed over to steady him.

"Master… what—"
