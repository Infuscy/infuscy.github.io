## Chapter 238: Black Fire

Malin tried to speak but couldn't get the words out at first — just groaned in pain. Jace suddenly realized what had happened.

This wasn't arcane talent at all!

He bolted out of the room and sprinted to the alchemy shop, shouting as he ran, "Shadow Protection Potion! I need a Shadow Protection Potion!"

The young Miss Lumere, seeing the state Jace was in, nervously set a bottle on the counter. "We only have the eight-silver one, sir… but you can take it now and pay later."

"Thank you! Thank you!"

Jace dumped the dozen or so silver coins from his pockets onto the counter, grabbed the potion, and raced back to Malin's study, forcing the draught down the old mage's throat.

Malin knew exactly what was wrong with him and cooperated fully. He drank it down, squeezed his eyes shut, sucked in a sharp breath, gritted his teeth, and lay still for a moment. "Close the door. Quick!"

Jace nodded, shut the door, and turned back. Malin had already lowered himself into his chair, supporting himself. He suddenly looked years older — feeble as a genuine old coot, all of his former confidence gone.

The two of them sat in silence for a long time. Malin's every breath across the desk was perfectly clear to Jace. *This is really it,* Jace thought.

He didn't understand why this had happened. He'd been casting a spell that used arcane incantations to channel elemental energy — why had shadow energy gotten mixed in?

Was his arcane apprenticeship over before it had even begun?

Apprentice mage, full mage, court mage — all of it reduced to empty words?

Just moments ago he'd felt like shadow magic was the thing that had carried him to where he was today, and now he'd fallen straight from the clouds into the abyss.

Was this the price, then? What Gakin had said — the necessary sacrifice on the path of dark arts?

Replaying the spell in his mind, he felt as though the arcane energy had been obeying his commands, gathering every scrap of fire element it could reach. But as he felt his mana surging, as the excitement swelled, a savage burst of shadow energy had suddenly appeared midway through and hijacked the arcane effort — then been ignited by those fire elements, producing a crimson explosion far more powerful than the spell itself had any right to be.

Yet through all of it, Jace had believed he was successfully channeling arcane energy. In his excitement, he'd overlooked the moment his mana had suddenly been converted — and only now, watching the mage get hurt, did he grasp what had really happened.

It seemed the so-called improvement in his arcane affinity had been an illusion from the start. From beginning to end, the thing that had been answering his call more and more eagerly was the maddening, chaotic power of darkness.

When he'd felt the mana gathering the fire elements, it was as if his blood itself was boiling, the shadow rushing to assemble with an urgency that nearly robbed him of his reason, the excitement pushing him past the brink — until he'd mistaken everything before him for what he wanted it to be, namely genuine arcane flame.

It wasn't until that vivid crimson dark fire had fully erupted and the old mage had crumpled in pain that he'd snapped back to his senses. It had all been an illusion woven by the shadow.

"Almost… took my old life, Mr. Sesso," Malin muttered under his breath.

"I'm sorry, Master. I didn't mean it," Jace said.

Malin pulled a piece of candy from his drawer, popped it into his mouth, and sighed. "Of course I know you didn't mean it — you're not an idiot! So what happened? Give me a reason not to call a hearing. Quick."

Jace said, "That black dragon… it hit me with some kind of dark magic. The Dalaran mages said I was badly injured, and I've been plagued by shadow energy ever since. But I thought it was just shadow damage — I didn't realize it would affect my spellcasting."

"Dalaran — can they verify this?" Malin asked.

"Yes. Master Dushan knows too." Jace said.

Malin nodded, clutching his chest. "Alright, Jace Sesso. I'll write to the Kirin Tor's Magical Defense Division and ask about this. I'll ask Caro as well. If they can both vouch for you… then I'll explain the situation clearly to High Sorcerer Andromath and inform him of your special circumstances, to prevent any further misunderstandings. But in the meantime, you attend your classes and behave yourself — and outside of class, don't cast a single spell anywhere in the Wizard's Sanctum."

"I understand, Master." Jace nodded.

Malin braced himself on the desk and pointed at Jace. "Right — give me every department, every person who can clear your name. I want as much proof as possible — documents, guarantees, and so on. The more we have, the more convincing it'll be to High Sorcerer Andromath. If you could get a letter of guarantee from King Varian Wrynn himself, then you'd have nothing to worry about."

"But King Varian doesn't know about my shadow injury," Jace said.

Malin pinched the bridge of his nose in pain. "That was a figure of speech, you foolish student… You're not seriously trying to find a lord or noble to underwrite your risk, are you?"

"A lord or noble — would a Windrunner do?" Jace asked.

"What Windrunner — Alleria Windrunner?"

"No. Vereesa Windrunner."

Malin frowned. "How would you be able to get a guarantee from her younger sister?"

"I…" Jace said. "In Dalaran, I helped her fight Teron Gorefiend. Teron Gorefiend attacked me with shadow magic too — wouldn't that also count as shadow corruption?"

"Hold on — what?" Malin narrowed his eyes. "Teron Gorefiend? Why didn't I know you'd encountered Teron Gorefiend? The Kirin Tor… ah — the Kirin Tor mentioned that when Teron Gorefiend attacked Dalaran, a mage apprentice survived and was rescued by a Farstrider ranger. That was you too?"

"Probably," Jace said.

"Mr. Sesso, did you go to Lordaeron to visit family, or to besiege Dalaran?" Malin sighed. "But Lady Vereesa… you'll have a hard time reaching her, won't you?"

He coughed twice, caught his breath. "If we had her guarantee, it wouldn't carry as much weight as King Varian himself speaking up, but given the current diplomatic climate and the Expedition's reputation… it would certainly count as significant enough. Can you really get one?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "I can try asking… I mean, writing to ask."

"If your letter can actually reach Quel'Thalas, that alone would speak volumes, Mr. Sesso… Then good luck." Malin let out a long breath. "I trust you, so I won't restrict your movements. Don't betray that trust. That's all for today — I need to rest. If the blasted shadow hasn't completely devoured your conscience, at least go next door and brew me a pot of hot tea — goldthorn tea, Sesso, like before. At least that way I'll die a little slower…"
