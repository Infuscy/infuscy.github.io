Chapter 239: Tea and Mead

Sitting in the alchemy room brewing tea, watching the water boil, Jace's mind was full of the magic mishap from earlier.

His hands were still trembling slightly from the rush of excitement.

What he couldn't understand was — he'd been chanting an arcane incantation. Why had it ignited dark fire?

How could shadow obey an incantation designed to manipulate the arcane?

Unless the shadow energy wasn't obeying the incantation at all — unless it was acting on its own, of its own volition.

It reminded him of that night at Stonewatch, when the shadow fire had suddenly burned hotter and hotter until it destroyed the whole sword.

If this was true — to use the game's terminology — then this wasn't a spell at all. It was an "ability."

The priests at the Cathedral of Light had mentioned that negative emotions could induce and draw in dark power.

But he'd just wanted to successfully cast an arcane spell — did that count as a negative emotion?

Still, agitation, excitement pushed to the extreme — extreme emotion was fuel for the shadow's expansion too. Most likely, that shadow energy had fused with the tiny amount of fire elements summoned by the arcane power he'd barely managed to call, producing that deep crimson explosion — a sort of knockoff Shadowflame.

It seemed that every time the shadow erupted with shocking power beyond his expectations, it came alongside the most extreme of emotions… whether fighting the black dragon, or battling Teron Gorefiend for his life.

Even that night when he'd used Fear to fight off Jagin's Fear.

Was it connected to the black dragon vitality he'd absorbed? Or was it, as Galders had put it, that he was just "naturally" like this? Or maybe both.

Because now that he'd calmed down, he knew that Scorch hadn't been a successful cast at all. Even by his standards of arcane manipulation, managing to produce fire on the first try was progress, sure.

But the real damage — eighty percent of it — had come from the shadow energy that surged out afterward.

So once he learned Scorch properly, could he use it to start a fire and then channel shadow into it to throw on another log — ending up stronger than the spell itself?

Just thinking about it, it sounded pretty practical.

The question was whether he could reproduce it.

Right now, all that shadow energy that appeared whenever his emotions suddenly flared up — none of it had been triggered deliberately.

Was there a way to trigger it reliably and at will? Deliberately work himself into an emotional frenzy?

No… that wouldn't work…

Lost in thought, Jace stared at the flame under the kettle and unconsciously reached out with his right hand.

His awareness slowly drifted toward the flickering fire…

Wait — he clenched his fist and pulled back.

Clink, clink, clink — the lid of the kettle rattling snapped his wandering mind back to reality. He squeezed his eyes shut and didn't even dare touch the tea ware at first, terrified that more baleful energy would seep into it.

Jace poured out the water, scrubbed the kettle half clean, put it back, and replaced it with a fresh one to boil again.

After brewing the tea, he sniffed it carefully. Confirming there was nothing off about the smell, he carried the pot out.

While Marin drank his tea, Jace stood beside him watching his skin, his eyes, and the rest of him carefully. Seeing nothing abnormal, he finally breathed easier.

That lapse in attention earlier had really been dangerous.

After looking after the old mage and getting permission to leave the study, Jace stepped outside and looked up at the sky, feeling as though he'd just walked back from the gates of hell.

By then, Grede was already waiting for him by the garden.

The two of them headed to the Blue Heron. Jace found a quiet corner to sit in. Grede glanced around to make sure no one was nearby, then leaned in close. "What'd that elf girl say?"

"I didn't see her."

"Huh — she went south?"

Grede scratched his head. "This is a pain, Jace. You didn't have any nightmares last night, did you?"

Jace said, "No nightmares, but I'm waiting for her to come back… Having to stay cooped up with those things in the meantime — just thinking about it gives me a headache."

Grede said, "Honestly, I think that place full of corpses must still have some demonic energy or something. I had a hell of a nightmare last night, damn it. After we came out yesterday I couldn't get those things out of my head no matter what. Couldn't stomach a bite of anything. Only started feeling a little better when I got up this morning. Figured I'd find you and have a few drinks to clear my head. I need something strong."

"I didn't have nightmares either, but I'm not doing much better — I keep thinking about that tent, those corpses," Jace said.

"Right? It's freaking cursed. You still want your usual lager?"

"I'll have the same as you."

Grede flagged down the barmaid and ordered two large mugs of mead, then let out a heavy sigh. "Hope this whole thing wraps up soon."

Jace looked at him. "I'm heading east in a couple of days."

"Eastvale?"

"Somewhere around Lakeshire, probably."

"Need any help?"

"I'm just going to check something for the boss. I'll be back in two days. Just giving you a heads-up so you don't come looking for me and wonder where I've gone."

"Watch yourself on the road, then."

"Hell, without you I'd have an easier time."

Grede shot him a look, then traced a few circles on the table with his fingertip. "I still don't get it — how did that death knight end up dead there?"

Jace said, "As far as I can tell, his body just happened to get found by those gnolls."

Grede asked, "Then why'd he do it? I mean — did he switch bodies, or abandon his own body? Like Teron Gorefiend?"

Jace said, "How would I know? Anyway, it sounds more plausible than 'the gnolls killed a death knight.'"

The dwarf turned it over in his mind for a moment. "Is that death knight actually one of Gorefiend's lieutenants?"

"Let's hope so," Jace said. "Otherwise it means there might be more death knights in the kingdom, and Elwynn's only going to get darker. Oh — I injured my teacher while learning magic this morning. It was…"

"Why'd you hit him?" Grede asked. "Charging too much tuition?"

"No, it was an accident…" Jace said. "I was learning a fire spell… He told me to cast it at him, said he'd put up a barrier and it'd definitely be fine. So I cast it, but he didn't block it — probably got ambushed by some shadow energy I let out by accident… I was pretty worked up at the time too. I'll have to figure out exactly what happened later."

Just then the barmaid set two mugs on the table. The dwarf watched her leave, then glanced sideways at Jace. "Shadow? Then how come nobody's come to arrest you?"

"I told them a black dragon injured me, so I've got shadow energy in me, and there are people who can vouch for me," Jace said, taking a swig. "As long as Vereesa is willing to write me a letter of guarantee, and the Magical Defense Division in Dalaran doesn't lose their minds and come after me on purpose, and Teacher Caro Dushan doesn't suddenly develop amnesia, I should get through this safely."

"I can vouch for you too, Jace." Grede thumped his chest. "Does a former gryphon rider's guarantee carry any weight?"

Jace looked at him. "It means a lot to me, Grede. Thank you."

"To you? So to everyone else it doesn't mean squat?!" Grede took a drink too. "Look down on me all you want… wait till I become the Mountain King, a legend of Khaz Modan. Forget a letter of guarantee — I'll write a letter to your little king and have him grant you a farm on the western open lands. Make you a little lord. You won't even need to save up for a house."

"I'll be waiting for that day," Jace said, raising his mug.

"Then you'll be waiting a while." The dwarf grinned. The two clinked mugs and each took a long pull.

Grede wiped the foam from his beard. "Oh — doesn't this mean you can go public with your magic now?"

"What kind of joke is that…" Jace lowered his voice. "Having shadow energy in my body that I can't fully control is one thing. Learning all those weird, miscellaneous tricks is another! Keep it down — don't go blabbing about it."

"Yeah, yeah, I won't say anything. Hell, I'm an accomplice too, aren't I?" Grede said between drinks. "Still, at least it's a good start. Maybe someday you'll legitimize warlocks in the Alliance of Lordaeron. With a good warlock like you around… 'good warlock' — honestly, it sounds even more ridiculous than 'orc scholar.'"

"Still less ridiculous than 'ogre thief,'" Jace muttered.

"Let's not talk about that far off," Grede said. "When exactly is Vereesa coming back? Did she mention it?"

"No," Jace said. "That day I walked her back to my room and she left without saying much — she was in a hurry to sleep, and I didn't want to bother her. But she said she was going to Duskwood to investigate. Think about how long it took us to make the round trip to Raven Hill that day — going to Duskwood from Redridge would be faster, but there's no way she could do it in a few days. And she still has to investigate the death knight. I'd guess at least a week or two. I'll go check again in a few days."

"So you're not charging her half a month's rent?" Grede asked.

"It's not my house," Jace said. "And I need a favor from her right now — I'm borrowing her elder sisters' reputation to scare these mages. If I hound her over a few silver on top of that, wouldn't that make me look like an idiot?"

"Fair point," Grede nodded. "But you really do clean someone out, don't you? Found one elf and you're milking her for all she's worth."

"What else am I supposed to do?" Jace said. "I finally caught one — if I don't squeeze her dry until there's nothing left, isn't that just wasting it? Besides, a letter of guarantee doesn't do her any harm. I'm not some bad—"

He stopped mid-sentence.

Because a guarantee was, in essence, a promise that the shadow energy he carried wouldn't cause further destruction, wouldn't create further chaos.

If his identity as a warlock were exposed in the future, would it implicate the people who'd vouched for him?

Thinking it through, the matter suddenly didn't feel so light anymore.

"Alright, actually, vouching for me does carry some risk. If she's unwilling, I can't really blame her."

"What kind of bullshit risk?" Grede suddenly flared up. "You fought your heart out for Menethil Harbor. You protected those soldiers at Stonewatch. And — and… you've done so much for these kingdoms, and those damn mages still think there's something wrong with you learning dark magic? I don't get it — if someone can kill with the Holy Light, why isn't someone allowed to save lives with shadow? What kind of logic is that?!"

Jace looked at the agitated dwarf and sighed. "It's because they haven't traveled with me for the past few months like you have, Grede. Remember how you reacted when you first found out I used dark magic?"

When Grede heard that, he froze, then slowly nodded.

"It'll take time," Jace said. "Changing their minds — I don't think it's any easier than you becoming the Mountain King."

"Changing a whole group of people's minds… doesn't sound like something a mortal could pull off," Grede said with a bitter laugh, raising his mug.

"But I'll still help you, Sesso — as long as you keep doing what you're doing."
