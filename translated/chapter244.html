Chapter 244: The Burning Black Blade

After the succubus turned invisible, Jace stared at the spot where a demonic figure had been standing moments ago, now completely empty. He had to admit — the special abilities these lesser demons had were a little frightening.

But perhaps it was precisely because these lesser demons possessed these unique means of hiding that so many warlocks chose them as minions. Having one at your side was far more convenient than those massive felguards, doomguards, and infernals.

Two thousand years ago, it was precisely because of the invasion of countless mid-sized demons who could easily conceal their tracks that mages had gradually lost the ability to resist, and Dalaran had nearly become a nightmare city overrun by demons.

As he walked through the streets, even though it was night, Jace deliberately chose places that mages and priests rarely visited — those out-of-the-way alleys and corners.

If shadow casters were still only shunned by the public, interrogated and expelled by magical institutions, then demon summoners — and demons themselves — were simply beyond redemption. In terms of how they were treated, they weren't much better off than necromancers.

He noted the houses on both sides of the street as he walked, thinking that he ought to rent a bigger room somewhere close to the city gate. That would make it easier to get out of the city.

Or rent a room in Goldshire and station both demons there instead?

After arriving outside the city without incident, he walked for a long time until he reached a pitch-black stretch of forest far from the farms and city walls. Jace found a sheltered slope and asked, "Are you still here?"

"Of course." The succubus materialized from a patch of shadow and walked up to a tree. "I didn't get the impression that the people on those streets were priests or priests. They had no mana to speak of. They couldn't defeat me either…"

"That's because I deliberately chose streets where we wouldn't run into those people, Morophil," Jace said.

"The master cares about my safety?"

"What matters more is my safety."

"You matter ten thousand times more than I do."

"I remember one of my rules was: no charm spells."

"That wasn't a charm spell."

"Smooth talk, too — please reduce the frequency," Jace said. "If you're living in Stormwind, I'm afraid you'll be discovered sooner or later. You're not like that imp…"

"Of course I'm not like it."

"Let me finish — it can fit inside a bag. You…"

"I can stay invisible the whole time. It won't cost me anything."

"I want to be more certain than that. How about I put you in Goldshire? It's a town over that way. Hardly any mages or priests ever go there hunting for demons." Jace pointed to the southeast.

"Can I refuse?" Morophil asked coldly.

Honestly, Jace didn't want to do it this way either. The long trip back and forth would waste who knew how much time.

And leaving a demon in Goldshire where he couldn't keep an eye on her at all — even if the risk was lower, the moment something went wrong it would be a catastrophe.

He stared toward the southeast and thought for a moment. Morophil said, "I don't believe anyone in this city could catch me. Even if they did use some high-powered Detect Invisibility magic and sensed my presence, I could slip out of their detection range the moment they did — leave them unsure of what they'd seen."

"Even though I've lost my wings, it doesn't mean I've lost my speed."

Jace asked, "So how fast are you?"

The succubus locked eyes with Jace. A small hand suddenly snatched the black sword from his waist. Her leg muscles tensed and she sprang up, her black hooves striking several small trees in midair in a few quick steps before landing back on the ground — the blade coming to rest right against the back of his neck. In the blink of an eye.

Was she some kind of… assassin? Or a rogue?

This made him even more curious about the broken wings on his back.

"I can sense fear, master. Is it yours?" the demon asked by his ear.

"Of course it's mine," Jace said. "The sword's on my neck. Isn't it normal to be nervous?"

"You still don't trust me." Morophil walked away to the side, disappointed.

"We've known each other two days. How am I supposed to trust you?" Jace took the short sword back from the succubus, glanced up at the twin moons overhead, and said, "All right, enough of this. It's already very late, so let's not waste any more time. Morophil, I want to know how your fire resistance is."

"You want to scorch me with fire magic?" Morophil asked in surprise.

Jace said, "What, you're a demon. Getting set on fire a little shouldn't be a problem, right? Do you know any fire-protection spells or anything?"

"I know a little." Morophil said, full of anticipation. "But why would I use them? Use your mana to torture me to your heart's content. I wouldn't use even a scrap of magical protection to diminish your love for me… not for anything…"

"You damn pervert, I can't believe this," Jace said helplessly.

Morophil sat on the ground, licked her lips, and narrowed her eyes. "I am a pervert. I'm the master's plaything… as long as the master—"

"Stop, stop… I'm starting."

Jace steadied himself and downed a mana potion. Instantly everything he saw became much clearer, and the dull, stifling fog of the whole day dispersed.

He glanced at the incantation he'd copied out, fixed his eyes on the succubus whose tail swayed without stopping, and spoke the words of the Immolate spell.

A drifting, unsteady sound of wind swept past. Morophil calmly blew a tiny flame strand off her hair and asked with feigned bewilderment, "Is that the master's fire magic?"

A flash of irritation rose in Jace. "That's not the point… it is shadow fire! Damn you, just you wait."

He repeated the incantation again, but the results weren't much better.

Morophil couldn't help but laugh. "I don't even have the heart to put out this little flame. After all, it's the bit you worked so hard to produce — far too precious…"

Jace was starting to regret bringing the thing out here to practice. But there was only so much help the imp could give him in this area, since it couldn't take much damage, and besides, this incantation wasn't in demonic.

Emotion… emotion…

No excitement, no agitation. Jace felt that practicing like this was far enough. After all, he'd long since given up expecting anything of his arcane talent. He couldn't stir up shadow power out here the way he had in the study.

"Is there any magic that could get me excited, or angry, or something… Do you know Curse of Recklessness?"

"Curse of Recklessness?" Morophil said seriously. "Curse of Recklessness is a spell that can cause a human to lose control. Are you sure that's the effect you want?"

Lose control?

Staring at the pitch-black, quiet forest spread out before him, the last thing he wanted to hear right now was the words "lose control."

"But if it's about getting excited…" The succubus licked her lips and stripped off all of her underarmor. Jace looked at the succubus's nearly smooth, flushed body under the moonlight. "What are you doing…"

Stark naked, Morophil stepped closer bit by bit, leaned against Jace, and whispered, "I know what you want to do, master. You want to use your inner self to channel the dark power. Plenty of demons are good at this. If you'd told me earlier…"

"In fact, that first night, I already sensed it, darling. How fervent and wild your shadow power is — how badly it wants you to accept it right away… yet you're like a sadistic warden, making them stare at your tempting body, going mad and roaring as they grip the cage formed by your will…"

Before Jace could figure out how to react to that speech, Morophil had already pressed her nose against his cheek. He could feel her powerful leg hooks slowly curl beneath him.

Right now, all he felt was his head growing hotter and hotter, his heart pounding more and more savagely.

The succubus whispered, "Don't… restrain yourself. At least not now."

Surging tide-like shadow energy erupted through his veins. Jace grabbed the succubus's chest and slammed her against a thick tree trunk. Staring into her burning arcane-infused eyes, he growled: "Kibimofloroth…"

Suddenly, her hair began to burn with dark-red flame. Morophil's eyes grew hazy.

"That's the feeling… master… this is yours…"

Morophil murmured, clutching at Jace's shoulders and back as if she were about to lose her mind. Crimson shadow flames flowed like blood, spreading across her shoulders, arms, chest, and lower belly, while at the same time searing Jace's skin. But for some reason, right now he wasn't afraid of these flames at all. On the contrary, he was stirred up more and more fiercely by the heat of those flames, more and more agitated.

Jace looked at his own skin. His arms were only scorched red, with some minor burns on his hands — they looked somewhat alarming, but in this moment he felt no pain at all.

Did this count as success? Using an Immolate spell to ignite shadow flames…

It was definitely a success. But reproducing it in other circumstances didn't seem quite so simple either. Still, he felt… with the succubus's help, he seemed to have found a trick or two.

As long as he tried again and again, he'd definitely make it… Morophil, wreathed in shadow fire, suddenly threw her arms around his neck and buried her head against his shoulder with a muffled groan, gently biting his skin with her fangs.

She panted a breath, as if enduring enormous pain. "Master… do you want to… right here, right now…"

"I've got magic classes tomorrow. I need mana to cast," Jace said.

"But you still have mana potions." The succubus reminded him mournfully.

"Unreasonably good point."

Jace spoke as he pinched the succubus's neck and forced her hard against the already blackened dead tree trunk, his whole body crashing against hers. The gloomy forest echoed ceaselessly with the demon's rapid, ragged breathing.
