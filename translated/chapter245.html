Chapter 245: "Irredeemable Depravity"

"Between different orcish clans, the Orcish language used differs as well. Research by the Kirin Tor has found that the majority of Orcish materials we know of are based on a common Orcish, while each clan has its own dialect — dialects that often differ so widely that orcs from different clans cannot understand one another."

A male student only a little older than Jace finished reading from his notes and looked at Caro Dushan expectantly.

Dushan nodded. "Indeed. The rapid development of the Orcish we know of really only began in the decades before the war, during the process of the many orcish clans integrating and founding the Horde."

"Orcish is still an extremely lively language, very easy to adapt foreign words into using their runes. The scholars of Stromgarde have discovered that some of the orcs living in the internment camps already transliterate Common words — the word 'arcane,' for example. Now then — can anyone give me an example of an Orcish word that has different meanings across different orcish clans?"

"Naghor," an older student said. "In the Dragonmaw clan, the word means 'loyal beast,' while in other orcish clans, it simply means 'dragon.'"

"Well said, Mr. Charlton." Dushan nodded.

"Are there dragons on the other side of the Dark Portal?" a male student asked.

"The dragons of Draenor are very different from the red dragons of Azeroth," a female student answered. "They say those dragons are far more savage and ferocious, but also much weaker. To us, those beasts can only be called dragon-like creatures. It's only because of the resemblance that, the moment the orcs arrived on Azeroth, they used Naghor to address the red dragons they saw here."

Dushan said, "Yes. Here I'd suggest you all seek out more words like this to deepen your understanding of orcish culture. Discussions like this one about dragons are also very helpful for improving your comprehension and application of the language. Now then — can anyone give me an example of a foreign word, other than Common, that has been absorbed into Orcish?"

"Does no one know?"

The students looked at one another. If everyone knew a few Common words the orcs had absorbed, as for absorbed Dwarvish or elven words… that was completely uncharted territory.

"Saizhidula," Jace said quietly. "Derived from demonic. Succubus — the term for a female succubus."

"Actually, I didn't know that one," Dushan said. "Where did you come by that information, Mr. Sesso?"

"I happened to hear the orcs mention it in Durnholde," he tried to explain.

"Excellent." Dushan nodded. "Then, based on this Orcish word, can you guess what the term for a male succubus would be?"

"If we follow Orcish grammar, it should be Saizhigakha, or Saizhidum." Jace rubbed his eye and thought for a moment. "Though I haven't quite figured out the Orcish rules for gendered inflection yet, Master. This is just my speculation."

Dushan said, "That's quite good. My inclination leans more toward Saizhigakha — of course, that's purely based on the patterns of Orcish I'm familiar with. I imagine the accurate answer could be found in the Grand Library of Dalaran."

"As a matter of fact, research on succubi by the Kirin Tor was already quite thorough a thousand years ago. In summary, succubi are a breed of demon that is untrustworthy, volatile, impulsive, and cruel. The friendliness they occasionally show toward humans has, on the contrary, made them far more dangerous than the surly Mo'arg demons."

"So in the early days of the restoration of the Kingdom of Dalaran — or rather, after the demon invasions had disrupted the stability of space itself — the Common word 'succubus' carried, for a very long time, the meaning of — irredeemable depravity. Especially where desire was concerned."

"Of course, that's just a little digression. Let's bring our focus back to this passage in front of us…"

Listening to Dushan's brief explanation, Jace thought again of last night — a mad demon venting itself without restraint upon another demon.

Right now there were no burn marks on his body at all. Not because he was immune to fire, nor because he'd gained some powerful magical resistance while being swallowed by shadow last night.

It was because he'd completely devoured the life force of a massive mature Bloodfang boar.

Even though he hadn't slept all night and couldn't remember the exact process of his spellcasting, in the end the scene hadn't been a pretty one. When class let out at noon, he dragged himself to the park and sat on a stone bench, pulling his outer robe tight, and stared into space for a long time.

He was starting to understand the dejection of those orcs now.

The boiling blood cooled. Reason returned. The fervor of strength and desire dissipated, leaving behind only a cold, sluggish body.

Then what about next time?

Would it be like this again?

Like an uncontrollable beast, driven by instinct alone, until he committed an irreversible mistake — hurting his loved ones, hurting his friends, hurting innocent people, the way he'd hurt Marin?

Did it really have to come to that?

He found himself back at this very question again.

Looking at the mages, apprentices, assistants, and merchants coming and going before him, Jace was so sleepy and so exhausted he could barely lift his eyes.

Every one of them was either chatting and laughing or calm and composed. Their clothes were clean, their strides brisk — as positive, sunny, and full of hope as the ever-rising Kingdom of Stormwind.

And he, a Lordaeronian, had just crawled out of a sewer trench still reeking of green robes. Worse — he was a Lordaeron corpse from after the Third War, merely wearing a layer of living human skin, hiding among the crowd and warily watching for something.

Last night had felt both like a dream and like a journey through hell. But he had succeeded — shadow fire, such a powerful offensive magic.

Afterward, he'd even casually loosed a blob of blackened, blood-colored flame at the boar's corpse, blowing the already withered and broken remains to splinters without a trace. It had no difficulty at all — as simple as spitting. He couldn't even remember reciting an incantation.

In that moment, he'd even felt that dealing with that lamb should have gone this way too — instead of carrying it out on his back and selling it to some fisherman he didn't even know. Why was life so complicated? Why couldn't he just use his power to solve everything cleanly?

The second blast of flame had been far more violent and terrifying than the one he'd unleashed on Morophil's skin. Thinking back on it now, it had been like a bomb going straight off.

Anyone passing by that spot after waking up today would probably think some dark anomaly had appeared yesterday. In any case, Jace had absolutely no intention of going back to that place.

But wasn't this exactly what he'd wanted in the first place? Wasn't this the purpose of his heading into that forest, the plan he'd had from the start — the plan he couldn't let a succubus disrupt?

Grasp the trick to releasing shadow, gain the ability to spread shadow fire. He'd gotten what he wanted. He'd won.

But at what cost?

If that didn't count as losing control, Jace didn't know what did.

"Jace?"

Jace looked up. It was Amy. "What's wrong? You look terrible."

"Uh, I didn't sleep well last night."

"Are you heading to eat now? My treat."

"Really?"

"What are you saying… come on, to the Blue Heron. Get up."
