Chapter 251: Toward the Highlands of Khaz Modan

After class that morning, Jace and Grede headed to the Pig and Whistle and found a corner table.

"Why'd you want another drink before we head out?" Grede said. "Don't tell me you've finally started to appreciate the magic of liquor."

"I never said anything bad about drink to begin with," Jace said. "It's just — I'm worried I won't be eating any proper meals where we're going. Figured I'd get a good one in before I leave."

"Just head straight to Lakeshire and eat there." Grede licked his lips. "I've still got raptor meat on my mind. Compared to that, the stuff here is honestly nothing special."

"I'm craving it too," Jace said. "But the person I'm looking for might not be in Lakeshire, so I'm not planning on staying the night and wasting time. The sooner we get moving, the better. Hope to be back by Tuesday at the latest."

"Heading to Stonewatch again?"

"Not exactly." Jace looked across the table at the bartender. "Sir, we'd like to order!"

"One pale ale, half a sweetbread, a Winter Veil roast, and a bowl of mushroom soup — black mushroom soup."

"I'll have a dark one. As much for the meat, a plate of blood sausage will do — hopefully they can manage a proper Thandol Span flavor," the dwarf said.

"Right away." The bartender nodded and left. Grede pulled a purse from his coat and set it on the table. "This is the money from selling those orc coins, Jace. You take your share first."

"That was fast?" Jace counted the coins as he spoke.

"Some of it hasn't sold yet. I'm giving you your cut first, I'll unload the rest slowly." Grede stroked his beard.

Jace glanced at him, nodded, and kept counting. The purse held a bit over sixty silver pieces total — roughly what Grede had originally estimated for an even split.

Grede stared at his face, looking him up and down. "There's something I'm not sure I should say or not…"

Jace tucked the purse into his coat. "Openings like that never mean good news. I'm about to head out on a trip — say something nice, would you?"

"I'm just looking out for you," Grede said. "It's just — I keep feeling like there's something off about you."

Jace's heart skipped a beat. Had Grede caught a whiff of something, or noticed something?

He wasn't wearing that green robe — just the wool jacket he usually wore when he went out, and he'd cleaned himself up pretty well. He couldn't smell anything off on himself.

Jace touched his face guiltily. "What's off?"

Grede wagged a finger. "It's not something on your face. It's you, as a whole. You've changed in the last couple of days. I'm just being honest about the keen perception I possess as a veteran amateur beast hunter of the Hinterlands, a former griffin rider, and a novice sage."

"Do any of those random titles actually count as official?" Jace cut in.

"That doesn't affect my intuition," Grede said. "Anyway — nothing's happened to these past couple of days? You really won't say anything? You couldn't make it to Redridge today even if you got up and left right now, so we might as well talk about it."

"So what exactly has changed?" Jace asked, testing the waters.

Grede set down his mug. "Let me put it this way — I feel like you've become a bit more positive. A bit more confident. You know that feeling, right? Like you just won a battle."

Jace stared at Grede in shock, thinking: has this dwarf's perception gone to shit?

Could he possibly be any darker than he already was?

Spending every waking moment with a succubus — that was dark among dark among dark. And yet this dwarf was telling him he seemed more positive?

But he didn't let it show. He just said, "Well, you guessed right. I figured out how to ignite shadow energy with fire and create a pretty powerful fire spell, Grede. On this Redridge trip, I'm planning to find some unlucky bastard to pick a fight with and give him a proper lesson with this."

"Not bad." The dwarf nodded. "Actually, I've got something important to tell you today too."

"What?" Jace blinked, with the vague feeling that this was no ordinary matter.

"Remember when I told you I wanted to become a Mountain King?" Grede picked up a blood sausage and bit off half of it, chewing as he spoke. "I wasn't just talking. Waiting around in a human city isn't going to get me on that path."

"I thought Mountain King was some honorary title," Jace said. "Are there actual requirements too?"

"Mountain King is an honorary title right now," Grede explained. "But there are requirements all the same. The Mountain King is the defender of Khaz Modan. I have to keep fighting and tempering myself to live up to what the ancestors demand of a Mountain King. But as things stand, I'm not satisfied with my own strength."

"Even though I beat Hogger, Hogger… like I said, if he'd fought me one-on-one, he wouldn't have been a match for me at all. The only reason he gave me so much trouble was because of that pack of cunning little pups."

"So beating him wasn't any great achievement. And in that forest, I saw my own limitations. I need to push myself harder and get stronger."

"What are you planning to do?" Jace asked.

Grede cleared his throat. "I'm heading to Ironforge to seek the Path of the Avatar."

"Avatar?"

"Mm. Where you're from, there's a more exaggerated term for it — the Descent of the Gods."

"God… God…" The bone in Jace's mouth dropped right into his mushroom soup.

"That's the one." Grede nodded. "A dwarf's bloodline is inherited from the ancient titan-forged — and that's not just empty talk. That blood hasn't vanished after tens of thousands of years of being passed down. It still flows in our veins. The Wildhammer clan's tradition doesn't emphasize this, but that doesn't make it false — it's just that we'd rather win through our own strength in the present than borrow our ancestors' names to make ourselves look important."

As Grede spoke, it was like he'd memorized the whole thing somewhere — neither his tone nor his manner was quite right.

But Jace knew this meant just how seriously Grede was taking this, not that he was putting on an act.

"So will you be gone for a while?" Jace asked.

"Not necessarily," Grede said. "I'm only going to Ironforge to seek the method of setting foot on this path. Even though I don't know much about it, I know it's a long, grueling process."

"Once I understand what I need to do, I'll come back. If things go smoothly, about a month. At most, three months — I can't say for sure. Have you heard the rumors that the Bronzebeard king is in talks with gnomish engineers about building a track between Stormwind and Ironforge?"

"Track?"

Jace hadn't dared to imagine that subway could actually be built here.

"Like the tracks in the mines — you've seen those, right? Actually, I heard it from someone else," Grede said. "Don't know how long it'll take to build. If it gets finished, getting there should be a lot faster. But no enjoying that luxury just yet."

"All right." Jace nodded. "So when are you planning to leave?"

"Is there anything you need my help with?"

"Probably not, no."

"Then I'll start packing after this meal," Grede said. "By the time you get back from Redridge, I might be close to reaching Menethil Harbor."

Jace looked at the dwarf and thought: he's already sixty or seventy years old. If you convert a Wildhammer dwarf's age to a human's, that's about thirty — the prime of his life.

If that "Path of the Avatar" really was a "grueling path" even by dwarven standards, then he really should be making plans sooner rather than later.

A few days ago, listening to Grede boast over a meal, he'd thought the title of Mountain King was something so distant he couldn't even see it on the horizon. And now, Grede was already preparing to walk that road.

"I support you," Jace said. "Hope you'll have found your direction by the time you come back. If there's anything I can help with, just say the word."

"Good brother," Grede said.

The two clinked mugs. Jace glanced sideways at the tavern door and felt that leaving here had just become a great deal harder.

Translator Notes

Flags

- Jace and Grede share a farewell meal at the Pig and Whistle before departing in different directions — Jace to Redridge, Grede to Ironforge. - Grede announces his intention to seek the "Path of the Avatar" (天神下凡 / Descent of the Gods) in Ironforge — a grueling dwarven warrior tradition tied to their titan-forged bloodline. - Grede explains the Mountain King title: currently honorary, but with real requirements — constant battle and self-tempering to live up to the ancestors' standards as defender of Khaz Modan. - Grede's motivation: beating Hogger wasn't a real achievement (the gnoll pack made it hard, not Hogger alone), and he saw his own limits in the Elwynn forest. He needs to get stronger. - Grede references the planned "track" (railway) between Stormwind and Ironforge — Jace is surprised the in-game Deeprun Tram might actually be built in this era. - Grede's self-estimated timeline: one to three months; he'll pass through Menethil Harbor on the way to Ironforge. - Jace's secret: he's been spending all his time with a succubus (Morofil) and has learned to ignite shadow energy with fire — a new, powerful spell he's itching to test. Grede misreads his shadiness as "positive confidence." - Jace is traveling to Redridge to find a ranger named Roenmes about a dragon scale — not to Stonewatch this time.
