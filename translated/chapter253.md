## Chapter 253: The Campfire

After establishing the Soul Link, even when Morofil turned invisible, Jace could sense her location almost by instinct. As for side effects, he hadn't felt any yet.

But since the imp knew about Soul Link and hadn't warned him about anything, that meant the succubus truly did have his interests at heart. Even if there were side effects, they should be far outweighed by the benefits.

That taste of blood… Jace knew it all too well. It was the taste of the blood flowing in his own veins — the taste he was forced to experience in careful detail every time he drained a living target.

Except now, Morofil had chosen to take harm for his sake. That fact was hard for Jace to feel at ease with, demon or not.

The next morning, upon arriving at Lakeshire, Jace rode over himself to tie up the horse, then went to make inquiries about Mr. Roenmes's family.

But when he went to the small house at the address the town hall had provided, there was no one home at all. Jace had a vague feeling this matter was about to get complicated.

Fortunately, a lawman recognized Jace as the mage who'd killed a bunch of gnolls at Stonewatch. He pointed Jace toward the path to the ranger's cabin and explained how to navigate the forks along the way.

According to the lawman, walking at a decent pace, it'd take about half a day.

After reuniting with the two demons, Jace looked up at the path leading into the mountains and remembered the last time he'd come here with Grede — fighting that orc shaman in the rain.

He wondered what the outcome would be when he ran into that shaman again this time?

Morofil looked at Jace, turning the black blade over in her hand, and said, "I smell a whiff of battle-lust on you, master. Am I right?"

"You can even sense that?" Jace asked, surprised.

"What the master thinks is none of your business!" the imp shouted.

The succubus lowered her eyes and glared at the imp. "If it weren't for the master protecting you, this scum would have died a hundred times over, understand? Learn to shut your mouth, you miscarried freak born of a demon mother."

"Ptui! Pink-skinned goblin-woman."

"No killing power," the succubus sneered. "Sounds like you're complimenting me!"

Jace sighed. "Last time, Sazeno saved my life down on that lake shore, Morofil. Stop trying to kill him all the time."

"Did you hear that, you self-unaware idiot!" the imp puffed up his chest. "I'm the master's real partner and servant — you're at best a toy for venting lust!"

"Is that so?" the succubus rolled her eyes at the imp, then looked at Jace. "Master, am I your lust-toy? I get the feeling—"

"You're all my friends," Jace cut her off. "All right, let's get moving. This place is too close to Lakeshire — if a militia patrol comes by and sees the two of you, they'll execute me on the spot." With that, Jace walked into the forest path.

"I don't want to be a friend, I want to be a toy…" Morofil muttered, catching up to his footsteps.

"Ah! Psycho!" the imp shouted angrily.

Last time he'd come here with Grede, the two of them had stayed in the foothills and never climbed high.

This time, Jace followed the path upward the whole way. After a while he reached an open clearing with no tree cover. Gazing into the distance, with the clear weather and winter dryness, the vast stretch of Elwynn Forest — growing darker the further south it went — was all laid out before him.

And the source of that darkness was a gray mountain range directly to the south, connected to the peaks he stood on. Jace knew that crossing that gray range would bring him to the Deadwind Pass — where the black tower of Karazhan stood.

He couldn't see any part of Karazhan's tower, of course. He couldn't possibly see it.

At this distance, the mountain silhouettes at the edge of his vision were at most thirty or forty miles away. And continuing south from those gray mountains, there were probably another hundred miles or more before you'd actually reach the base of the tower.

But the terrifying energy that Karazhan released — Jace could feel it even from here.

Void, Shadow, endless power leaking out from unseen places, bringing wave after wave of palpitations.

Then he noticed Morofil beside him gazing in another direction — to the southeast.

He followed the succubus's line of sight and saw the gloom behind the high mountains, too indistinct to make out clearly, spreading until it swallowed the horizon.

Though he couldn't see it clearly, Jace knew that was the vast Black Morass.

He'd once seen the approximate extent of the Black Swamp on an early-war battle map from the Orc Wars — a wetland wilderness comparable in size to the entire Kingdom of Stormwind, blocked to the north by the mountains of Khaz Modan, eventually stretching south all the way to the lands of the Gurubashi trolls.

Jace knew what Morofil was looking at. She was looking toward the direction of the Dark Portal.

Before the Dark Portal had appeared, neither humans nor trolls had been willing to venture deep into that swamp. But right now, that place had become the most terrifying battlefield on the entire continent.

Still, when Jace looked over he could see nothing at all — the Dark Portal was even farther than Karazhan, after all.

"Haven't had a proper meal all day. I'm going to eat something. You two find somewhere to rest — seems like there's still some way to go before we reach that cabin." Jace rummaged in his bag, pulled out the dry bread he'd prepared, and sighed.

"You don't like eating that?" Morofil said.

"What difference would it make if I didn't?" Jace sighed. "It's winter — there aren't any edible fruit on the mountain either."

"Do you want meat?" The succubus glanced toward a distant hillside. "Want me to get you some meat?"

"You can hunt?" Jace asked.

The succubus gave a disdainful little laugh and vanished right before Jace's eyes. He could feel her dart quickly into a distant thicket.

Right — magical invisibility. Was there any more terrifying hunting advantage than that?

Since she'd gone to find food, he ought to get a roasting frame set up here with the imp.

"Go help me find dry grass and dead branches, Sazeno!"

"Yes!"

Jace directed the imp while scanning the ground for forked twigs. He found two roughly the same thickness and stuck them into a relatively flat patch of open ground, kicked everything else away, leaving just the bare earth.

He definitely didn't want to start a mountain fire here in winter.

After setting up the roasting frame with the imp, Jace looked at the imp and said, "Step back."

The imp hopped onto a nearby rock and watched Jace. "Master, what are you trying to do with that stance?"

"Practice magic." Jace muttered the words, gazing at the fire pit. "Kibimoflosas."

*Bwoosh* — an explosive burst of flame blew the roasting frame apart on the spot. Jace stumbled back several steps in shock, watching the burning dry grass and branches with their scattering sparks fly everywhere…

"What are you doing! I gathered all that with such difficulty! Aaaaah — it's burning! It's burning!"

Though the imp complained with its mouth, it was also hopping around with excitement, yelling nonstop as it watched the burning twigs dance through the air.

Jace quickly stomped out the scattered sparks, terrified he'd actually start a mountain fire.

"Hurry and help… why are you yelling!"

The man and the imp fussed for a good while before finally getting the roasting frame propped back up. They used the remaining embers to light the fire. On such a cold day, it worked up a sweat in Jace.

Jace noticed that this time there was no shadow mixed into the flames at all — just pure fire element burning.

He'd actually cast a working Scorch spell?

Thinking back on those days of uncontrollable frenzy… aside from some guilt toward his teacher, the unease from before was gone.

Perhaps it was precisely because of this that he could so easily keep the shadow power in check, stopping it from devouring the fire magic he was igniting.

Thinking this, he pulled a burning branch from the fire and held it up to his eyes, watching the small flame at its tip. Gradually, as it flickered, it grew viscous — deeper and deeper red, finally turning as terrifying as blood, like a chanting devil dancing wildly in the darkening dusk.

Jace pinched out the dark-red fel flame between his fingers, crushed the burned end of the stick into purple-black ash and blew it away, then tossed the remaining half of the stick casually down the hillside, over the cliff.
