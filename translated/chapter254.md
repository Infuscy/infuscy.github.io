## Chapter 254: The Mountaineer

The sun hadn't set yet, but after staring at that fire for so long, it always felt as if it were already completely dark.

Jace gazed at the distant gray-blue lake and sat off to the side killing time by tossing stones at stones.

He hadn't been adding firewood for too long before Morofil finally emerged from the thicket.

She was carrying a wild rabbit about the size of a small fox, looking dejected as she came over to Jace. "Eat."

"I thought you were gone this long you'd come back with a whole cow," Jace said.

"So you're not satisfied?" the succubus asked, sitting down beside Jace.

"Not dissatisfied — just worried you'd run away." Jace glanced at the rabbit, which had been sliced nearly in half at the waist by a sword, held together only by the skin. "You already made it like this — might as well skin it while you're at it."

Morofil shrugged, looking thoroughly crestfallen, and grabbed the torn waist-skin of the wild rabbit. In a few tugs she peeled the hide off the lower half and tossed it aside, then skillfully ripped the skin off the upper half too — so deftly it didn't seem like the first time she'd done anything like it.

Jace looked at the skinned rabbit, considering that this succubus had never served a human before and presumably had no experience skinning rabbits. So what the hell had she skinned before?

From the corner of his eye Jace caught the imp also staring blankly at the succubus's movements. Its body wasn't much bigger than this wild rabbit — being grabbed and skinned like that would fit it just right.

The two demons and the one human fussed over the rabbit with more hands than needed, tearing it open and proasting it over the fire. Even though it roasted for a long time, with some spots burnt to a crisp, when he put it in his mouth there were still parts that seemed like raw meat he couldn't bite through.

Jace didn't have much outdoor roasting experience either, so he just made do. He figured next time he ought to bring some salt and spices — eaten like this it had no flavor at all, and on top of that it was fishy and gamey. It had nothing to do with the word "delicious."

But this was also one of Jace's rare outdoor barbecue experiences. It didn't taste good, but it had a flavor all its own.

The two demons had zero interest in the roasted meat. They just bickered nonstop, occasionally dragging Jace in to judge who was right.

He really wanted to just sit here and keep chatting forever — with these two demons, hearing every story they'd lived through in the Twisting Nether.

But it was getting late. They still had to set out.

Using the last thin rays of sunlight leaking past the mountain's edge, Jace spotted those few lonely houses up high. Several windows had already lit up.

He stuffed the imp into his bag and told the succubus to turn invisible, to avoid being spotted by mountaineers who might be on their way home.

This small settlement didn't exist in the game — of course it didn't. Just like the entire mountaintop of Redridge, it had next to no content in the game. Winding up the path toward the mountain cabins, from a distance Jace heard the rhythmic sound of chopping wood.

Behind a simple fence, a strong man was splitting firewood — probably just about to start a fire and cook.

"Are you Mr. Roenmes?" Jace called from afar.

"No." The man straightened up and wiped the sweat from his head, pointing toward the higher slope behind him. "Henbury went up the mountain the day before yesterday. His little son went with him. They haven't come back yet. They've probably gone back to Lakeshire — you can check in Lakeshire."

Hearing this, Jace felt his scalp prickle. He couldn't help but scratch it.

He looked in the direction the mountaineer was pointing. The pitch-black mountainside loomed like a crouching giant demon, and the faintly visible mountain path seemed to lead people straight into its abyssal maw.

"Did they say what they were going to do when they left?"

"Who are you?" The man walked a few steps this way, axe in hand. "What are you looking for him for?"

Jace raised the hand wearing his ring. "I'm a mage from the Wizard Sanctum. I heard he found a dragon scale and came here looking for a Wizard Sanctum mage to help take a look. I mean no harm."

"A mage?" The man kept walking closer. He rolled a thin blade of grass in his mouth, squinted at Jace's ring, and said, "Does look like a mage's ring. All right — he did pick up a pretty big scale. Bigger than the scales on a murloc's forehead, and reddish-brown, shaped like a blade. He said it was a dragon's scale — is that what the northerners call a red dragon? Would it sell for a lot of money?"

"I don't know the exact price, sir," Jace said. "But it ought to be quite valuable."

The man glanced at the mountain behind him. "That's what I said — otherwise he wouldn't be so obsessed…"

"Mage, you know a lot. What do you think — could there be more scales? Do dragons really exist in this world? Or is that something left behind by a Blackrock Mountain fire salamander? I've heard fire salamander scales are valuable too, but I've never seen a fire salamander on this mountain."

"Dragons undoubtedly exist, though they may be a bit different from what most people imagine." Jace asked again, "What about his wife? Does he have other family?"

"You mean old Roenmes's wife?" The man scratched his head. "His wife died a few years ago — the orcs. So, young mage — I've told you this much, is it worth five coppers?"

This fellow had a full beard all over his face. Only up close could you tell from his skin that he was probably around thirty. From a distance, at a glance, he looked like a man in his fifties.

"How big was that scale?" Jace asked again.

The man gestured a triangle with his hands. "About this big."

Jace glanced at the man's hand — the size of the scale he was indicating. Even on that young dragon at Southshore, there shouldn't be many scales that big on its body.

But considering he might exaggerate what he'd seen, even if it were two sizes smaller it would still be impressive.

Was there any other animal in the whole of Redridge that could have scales like that? Could it really be some kind of fire salamander? Unless it was a giant scale from a fire drake.

But fire drakes rarely came to the surface to make trouble — unless it was a special individual too big for cave-dwelling. Jace had a feeling this was most likely a scale that had fallen off a dragon that had flown over.

"So have you seen anything huge fly over?" Jace asked. "Or heard of anything?"

The man held out his hand. Jace handed him five coppers. The man said, "No."

"Thank you for the information, sir."

Looked like he really would have to keep heading up the mountain.

"You want to go up and find them, young mage?" the man called from behind. "I'd suggest you look tomorrow morning. I can also call a few buddies to go with you. Going up at night — going alone is very dangerous. Especially once you cross over to the north side of Redridge, what you might have to deal with won't just be nocturnal animals. Roenmes is an old mountaineer, but you're not."

Jace couldn't rest here. If Roenmes and his son had gone up the mountain the day before yesterday and were still trapped up there, they might be in danger by now.

Of course, he wasn't alone either.

"Thank you for the advice, sir. I'll be careful."
