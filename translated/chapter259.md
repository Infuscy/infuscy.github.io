## Chapter 259: Evidence

Henbury Ronmeis followed Jace back toward the ravine from before. He was much calmer than earlier — the influence of the mental curse seemed to have largely faded.

"Is he all right?" Henbury asked. "My son Cark — is he hurt?"

"Not exactly all right," Jace said.

"What happened to him?"

"Same kind of problem as you. But for now he's safe enough." Jace asked, "Do you have any memory at all of what you just did?"

Henbury said, "I felt like I saw a demon. I thought he was going to hurt my son, so I…"

"The demon you saw was me." Jace held up his injured arm. "And this is your handiwork, Mr. Ronmeis. I'm no philanthropist. If you're not planning to compensate me properly, I'll have a nice long chat with the Lakeshire sheriff about this when we get back."

At that, Henbury quickly said, "Mister Mage, I truly didn't know what I was doing. That woman cursed me, and that's why my brain was in such a fog. I'm truly sorry. I'll definitely compensate you."

"So what exactly happened?" Jace asked, examining the wound on his arm as he spoke.

Henbury said hesitantly, "Someone told me dragon scales could sell for money — several gold apiece. There was a treasure-hunting group that would let anyone who found a dragon scale join up. They said we'd search together up in the northern hills and split the profits, so we arranged to meet inside that cave up there. My son and I had just gone in when the heat became unbearable — we nearly passed out. But then a woman walked up to me, and she told me…"

He rubbed his head and continued, "She told me she could grant me salvation before the world was destroyed. She said even if the orcs stormed Lakeshire again, I'd survive. And somehow, I just believed her."

"That was a dragonkin," Jace said. "A half-dragon, half-human monster. I suspect she can use dark magic to influence people's minds — making you see her as a human. Your son was affected too. When I found him, that dragonkin was casting a spell on him."

"A half-dragon, half-human monster… why would she come after me?" Henbury said as they walked. "It has to do with that dragon scale, doesn't it?"

"I can't say for certain, but is that dragon scale still on you?" Jace asked.

Henbury said, "That woman — no, that monster — took it from me."

Hearing this, Jace figured he should search the dragonkin's corpse more carefully.

Still, the talk of "receiving salvation before the world was destroyed" was something Jace found very troubling.

Could it be the Twilight's Hammer? Probably not.

The Twilight's Hammer was a cult that worshipped the Old Gods, founded by the ogre warlock Cho'gall. It wouldn't develop into a full-blown cult organization for many years yet.

Right now, Cho'gall and the orc fugitives loyal to him hadn't even recovered from Gul'dan's death. They were already considered traitors even among the orcish Horde. They wouldn't have had the reach to establish ties with the black dragons so quickly and begin operating in the Redridge region.

Bringing Henbury to the mouth of the ravine, Henbury saw his son leaning against a tree, fast asleep.

"Cark?"

Jace had been about to tell him that his son might not have fully recovered yet, but Henbury had already rushed forward.

He slapped the young man's cheek. It was a long while before young Ronmeis slowly opened his eyes.

"No… no…"

The moment he woke, his first reaction was to shrink back in terror, scrambling and crawling around the trees. It was only after Henbury called his name several times that he seemed to come back to himself.

"Dad? Is that you?"

"Did I hurt you… Cark?"

"No… no…"

The two embraced tightly. The son broke down crying, and the father's face was a picture of guilt and anguish.

"I'm the one who told the sheriff about the dragon scale, Dad. I was afraid you'd get scammed by them…"

"You did the right thing, Cark. Otherwise we'd be dead for sure."

Old Ronmeis kept looking back at Jace and saying thank you over and over. Jace watched the two of them, thinking about what Henbury had said about his experience in Redridge. Who was the person who had lured him here?

If it wasn't the Twilight's Hammer, could it be the Dragon Cult?

In the game, the Dragon Cult was a bizarre organization on shattered Draenor made up of orcs, humans, and draenei.

They seemed to help the black dragons incubate and raise their young in the Blade's Edge Mountains. In return, the black dragons would find ways to bestow their "blessings" upon them — that is, turn them into dragonkin.

By the time players reached the Blade's Edge Mountains, the Dragon Cult had strangely gathered a large number of human followers. By all rights, at that point in time, the only Alliance presence that had entered the Blade's Edge Mountains was the night elves.

Yet the Dragon Cultists entrenched in the Blade's Edge Mountains had almost no night elves among them — they were mainly composed of orcs and humans. Where had they gotten so many humans…

Jace speculated that the Dragon Cult might have existed ever since Azeroth. They had entered Draenor alongside the black dragons, tasked with helping the dragons smuggle dragon eggs through the Dark Portal under cover of darkness.

After settling in the mountains of Draenor, they became the black dragons' helpers in incubating dragon eggs.

Wasn't this father and son's encounter exactly the way the local dragonkin recruited Dragon Cult believers?

Luring them to a certain place by some means, then taking control of their minds?

If this son hadn't slipped away in time and run into Jace, both of them would probably have been brainwashed into black dragon-worshipping cultists.

If they'd been sent back to Lakeshire — or even Stormwind — who knew how much trouble they'd cause. After the two had sobered up a bit, Jace led them to the dragonkin's corpse lying on the ground.

"Take a look. Can you make anything out?" Jace asked.

Neither father nor son could say a word. The son stepped forward and ventured, "It seems somewhat familiar… is this the monster that turned herself into a woman to deceive us? Light above."

"She didn't turn herself into a monster," Jace explained. "She simply used magic to deceive your minds. Her true appearance has been like this from start to finish."

Hearing Jace's words, young Ronmeis stared at the dragonkin for a long time without saying another word.

Jace walked up to the corpse and carefully searched its clothing and accessories.

Aside from some braciers forged by persons unknown, a dark red cloak too tattered for a human to wear, and some body coverings that didn't come close to fitting human proportions, there was nothing else.

"The dragon scale isn't on her." Jace turned and asked old Ronmeis. "Are you sure she took it?"

Henbury answered, "If that woman was her… then yes."

Jace could more or less guess what this dragonkin had come here to do. She had been preparing to eliminate the evidence of the black dragon's existence. That scale had probably been something dropped by a passing black dragon.

Perfect, then. She'd destroyed the dragon scale — but left herself behind. Wasn't that even stronger evidence?

"Lend me your dagger, Mr. Ronmeis." Jace held out his hand.

"All right." Henbury handed over the dagger, guilt still written on his face.

Jace took it, stepped on the dragonkin's neck, and — in front of the father and son's complicated expressions — rolled up his sleeves, gripped the knife in his left hand, and drove it between the rough scales. Slowly, he sawed the dragonkin's entire head off, propping the final bit of bone against a rock and stomping on it several times until it snapped. By now, almost no blood flowed from the head at all.

"The head's mine. The rest of the body is yours to do with as you please. I suggest you go back and report this to the Lakeshire sheriff and put them on alert." Jace caught his breath from the exertion. "I'm taking this back to Stormwind to report on the mission. Oh — do you have a cloth bag I could borrow?"

"I have one…" Young Ronmeis stared at Jace, then held out a cloth pouch. "I'm truly grateful you saved my father's life, mage. And mine, of course."

Jace took the pouch, handed the dagger back, and smiled. "Save the thanks. Now then — let's settle the medical bill for my wound, shall we, gentlemen?"
