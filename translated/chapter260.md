## Chapter 260: Ruins

After returning with the father and son to the small village up the mountain, Jace got eight silver and some bandages from them as compensation.

For one thing, these two were hardly wealthy folk. For another, Henbury believed Jace's arm had been run through by a dagger, but Jace himself knew it wasn't nearly that bad — because the Soul Link could shunt far more than twenty percent of the damage into his minions.

After resting a bit and eating some of the ranger's dried meat, Jace set out on the road heading further north. He still had one mission left to complete: finding dwarven gunpowder in northern Redridge.

Although it was already the middle of the night, Jace didn't feel drowsy in the slightest.

He didn't find it strange either. After a big session of Life Drain, it was always like this — a buzzing, wired sort of exhilaration, like he'd shot up a stimulant. His brain hummed nonstop, his ears throbbed with his pulse, and he felt like he had boundless energy.

It was just that the business of finding Dark Iron gunpowder — when he'd agreed to help Ursula, he hadn't felt anything was off about it. But now that he was here, he had a feeling it wouldn't go so smoothly.

This place was simply too far from Blackrock Mountain. Would Dark Iron dwarves really come all the way out here to set up camp and settle down?

After all, ever since the days of the War of the Three Hammers, when Blackrock Mountain had still been part of Redridge, this had been human territory. The Dark Iron dwarves, in order to deal with their cousins, had never provoked the Stormwind Kingdom too much — lest they end up caught between enemies on two fronts. So did he really need to help Ursula get gunpowder by pushing further and further north, all the way into the war zone between the Dark Iron dwarves and the Blackrock orcs?

Thinking of this, Jace took a more philosophical view of his chances on this trip.

He'd just follow the Redridge range. At the very least, don't push past this deadwood forest into the Burning Steppes. If he ran into something, fine. If not, he'd head back and tell Ursula he was powerless to help.

Worst case, he'd compensate her with some money.

And so Jace followed the ridge eastward, the tense search turning into a post-battle stroll.

He'd been ready to write the whole thing off as a wasted trip, but before the sky had fully brightened — when there was only a sliver of deep blue at the horizon — he spotted something not far off the cliff that looked like it had been arranged by human hands.

He ran all the way over. It was indeed an abandoned camp. Dust had settled thick over the left-behind goods — crates, bundles, and even some kind of frame whose purpose was anyone's guess.

If Jace really had to guess what the frame was for, he'd say a telescope — though for all he knew it could just as easily be a dwarven metal grill.

Still, after rummaging back and forth with his two demons, aside from some food that appeared to have rotted and molded, they found nothing related to gunpowder at all.

That said, this did seem to be a dwarven camp. Jace had found a worn vest that was broad and short — it simply didn't look like something a human would wear. But the vest was white and yellowed. Would Dark Iron dwarves like wearing vests in this color?

Maybe. But Jace had the feeling this was more like a Bronzebeard location.

Many of these things had already fused with the surrounding earth — either badly decayed or so tattered they were completely unidentifiable. It might have been an abandoned camp from many years ago.

Jace sat on a crate that something had chewed a hole through and felt he'd underestimated just how troublesome this mission was going to be.

Not to mention, he had no way of telling whether this was a Dark Iron dwarven camp. What if it really was left behind by a Bronzebeard expedition — say, they'd found some mine around here and were planning to go in and scout things out, resting here for a while… then even if they'd left gunpowder behind, it wouldn't be the kind Ursula wanted.

Right — was this an expedition camp?

Would there be a mine or a settlement ruin somewhere nearby?

The sky gradually brightened. Jace looked off toward Lake Everstill, and without realizing it he could no longer see the lake's surface from here. Even looking in the direction of Stonewatch, he couldn't see the tips of those watchtowers anymore.

They'd come pretty far east already. Stonewatch had vanished from sight. Jace stuffed a piece of dry bread into his mouth and glanced down the northern slope from the mountain.

The trees up here had grown sparse too. Everywhere Jace looked was fractured stone and exposed grey ridgeline, like a valley that had just been through a mudslide.

Jace braced himself on his staff and made his way down the mountain. He rummaged back and forth across the rubble slope down below, and by the time full daylight arrived, he finally spotted what seemed like a crack that might lead somewhere.

The crack was extremely well hidden. There were no footprints or paths outside — it didn't look like an active mine or a cave inhabited by anything else.

He wondered if the dwarven camp up on the mountain had been set up for the sake of this cave.

Jace poked his head in for a look and couldn't see a thing. Moonlight only reached the entrance.

He cast Light, but the succubus grabbed his wrist. "Master, let me go first? I can probably see more clearly in there than you."

"Then be careful."

"No problem."

"You see that, Sazeno? Look at how Morophil bravely takes responsibility by using her abilities."

"You two are so big, and I'm so weak — isn't protecting me just what you should be doing?"

"And you just said that out loud, so naturally…"

"Quiet!" The succubus took two steps in, then turned back to look at Jace with wide eyes. "Not you, dear, of course…"

Unlike that lava cave from before, this cave was deathly cold. And contrary to Jace's expectations, it wasn't natural in origin. After passing through some earthen tunnels with digging traces, his feet stepped onto a solid floor.

Relying on the faint arcane glow emanating from the succubus's eyes, he could roughly make out that this was a dwarven dwelling from ages past. The nearest walls even resembled the stone structures inside Aerie Peak.

It was so quiet inside that there was no sound at all — utter silence. Even the faint ringing in Jace's ears born of vigilance was crystal clear.

The succubus asked softly, "Shall we keep going?"

The air carried a faint, earthy smell and a barely perceptible dry staleness. Jace couldn't say what that scent meant. No one lived here, but animals might be sheltering inside.

As far as he knew, there shouldn't be any large mammals like bears living around here, and a cave this cold shouldn't have fire lizards either — or… spiders?

He felt the walls beside him — no leftover cobwebs or anything similar. Nothing like that outside the cave either.

Could it be some other kind of insect?

Jace couldn't tell whether this was a Dark Iron dwarven ruin or the remains of some other dwarven clan's dwelling.

He lowered his head and searched the ground for a while. In the shallow soil at a corner, borrowing the light from the succubus's eyes, he found several indistinct large footprints.
