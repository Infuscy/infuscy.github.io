## Chapter 261: For a Fellow Countryman's Sake

"There are footprints — whose feet are that big?" the imp squeaked in a hushed voice.

"A dwarf," Jace whispered back. "A dwarf's been through here recently."

"Is he still inside?" the imp asked.

"I don't know. Can either of you smell a living person?"

Both demons shook their heads.

If they were tracking a demon caster, the two demons' internal radar would probably be pinging like mad. Against a living human, they weren't much better than Jace himself...

"Careful, let's go in. If there's a Dark Iron dwarf in there, he's either using this place as a shelter or scouting it out. Either way, there might be something we want."

This ruin wasn't just an open space — it was an underground little settlement with walls, corridors, and rooms. Without being able to see the full layout, it was practically a maze.

Still, in the first few rooms Jace found some abandoned containers — iron boxes, jars, and so on. There might be gunpowder left in any firearms that had been left behind.

Whether it would meet Ursula's requirements was another question.

Pressing deeper, after following the wall of a corridor for some distance, the succubus suddenly stopped. Jace halted too.

"Shh..." the succubus chuckled softly. Jace noticed the fel fire in her eyes fading bit by bit.

She'd turned invisible. Jace understood immediately without needing to be told.

Someone was nearby.

A Stormwind adventurer? A dwarf?

...An orc?

The surroundings were dead silent — not a footstep, not even a breath. Jace didn't believe an orc would have the patience to play hide-and-seek with them in the dark.

"Stay put, human."

A hoarse voice came from behind him.

Both the accent in Common and the timbre of the voice were more than enough to give away his race. It was a dwarf.

The dwarf was trying to whisper, but in the near-absolute quiet, his voice rang out as clear as a brass bell.

"Don't do anything stupid. You know what a gun is? All I have to do is pull this trigger, and boom — uh, sorry, I didn't know you had company. How embarrassing, haha."

Jace turned around. Not far off, the dwarf was already pinned from behind, Morophil's blade pressed against his throat.

Under the glow of the succubus's eyes, the dwarf's own eyes gleamed a demonic crimson. His hair and beard were dark red, and his skin was swarthy enough to blend almost perfectly with the pitch-black environment — a Dark Iron!

The dwarf rolled his eyes to the side. "Is this succubus your wife, warlock? She's quite the looker. Prettier than any succubus I've seen in Shadowforge City. You're a lucky man. Think you can handle her at night?"

When Jace's expression didn't change, the dwarf went on, "Heh, look, this is a misunderstanding. How about we each go our own way? I never meant to hurt you, right? If I'd wanted to, I'd have—"

"You think you'd have had a chance to fire, short stuff?" the succubus murmured.

"Not a chance. My bad, my fault." The dwarf let the gun drop to the ground and raised his hands. "How about I give you something nice?"

"Sazeno, strip him of every last bullet."

"Yes, master."

Sazeno scurried onto the dwarf, grabbing and clawing. The dwarf twisted his waist and crossed his legs to keep the imp from reaching in, and the imp shrieked in frustration.

"At least leave me some, tall guy..." the Dark Iron dwarf looked at Jace pleadingly. "I mean it, I want to live! Without this gun I really can't scare off those orcs. I can see you're a good man — you wouldn't wipe me out completely, right? I mean, we're more or less allies against the orc invaders, aren't we? We're all Azerothians here! For Azeroth's sake..."

"No more wasting time. Morophil, let him unload the bullets himself," Jace said.

"You're not going to waste a beautiful succubus's precious mana on me, are you? I mean it, don't charm— ah... all right, darling, I'd do anything for you. Bullets, sure, anything's yours."

The Dark Iron dwarf started stripping off his own things — pack, bullets, dagger, luggage — at an astonishing pace, down to bare skin, even tossing aside his underpants and socks.

Jace looked at the white undergarment and felt his earlier assumptions about mountain dwarves had been a fair bit stereotypical.

"Running to the deepest part of the ruins? No problem!" The dwarf raised his hands and jogged off into the dark until not even the sound of his footsteps could be heard.

Only then did Jace realize why he hadn't noticed the dwarf approaching. The fellow had taken off his leather boots so he wouldn't make a sound walking, and had slung them from his belt to creep up on them.

"What would you do without me?" Morophil murmured.

"Why do you think I summoned you?"

"Master can't even say thank you?"

"Thank you for not slitting my throat and draining my blood while I sleep?" Jace conjured a Light spell. "Grab the bullets and let's go. Dark Iron dwarves have strong shadow resistance — he might snap out of it any moment!"

The three of them grabbed a string of bullets, hauled the firearm, and ran all the way out, scrambling up the mountain. They didn't stop until they reached the abandoned dwarf camp.

Jace examined the haul, especially the gun. Unlike the game's guns, which had absurd fire rates for the sake of gameplay, real-world firearms were neither accurate nor reliable, and reloading was slow.

A Dark Iron dwarf's firearm might be a touch more powerful than an ordinary one, but only a touch. Still, it should fetch a decent price in the Dwarf District.

And Jace's marksmanship was, frankly, terrible.

Still, the gunpowder in those Dark Iron bullets should be enough to meet Ursula's requirements.

Ah, this Redridge Mountains trip.

Jace gazed down at the vast stretches of forest to the south and the faintly visible watchtower peaks on the edge of his vision, and let out a long breath. "Morophil, weren't you asking me to thank you?"

Before Morophil could answer, Jace suddenly grabbed her arm and yanked her over, pinning her down. The succubus let out a soft cry as she hit the ground, unable to suppress a smile.

She lay on her back, looking up into Jace's eyes, and whispered, "Just let me die right here, master..."

"I'll do my best," Jace said.

"I'm still here, damn it!" The imp flung the string of bullets he'd been holding to the ground in exasperation. "One of you kill me first! I'd rather go back to the Twisting Nether and suffer than watch this!"

"Little thing, you'd better not look away. Let you see who the master's real—"

Morophil chuckled, but before she could finish, her expression suddenly changed and she bit her lip...

"Master, how did you... ah..."
