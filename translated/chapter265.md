## Chapter 265: Good Friends Help Each Other

Having arranged to meet Vereesa at his apartment that evening, Jace brought the sword and the papers to his door. Before knocking, he deliberately sniffed the sword one more time to confirm he really couldn't detect that fragrance anymore.

Morophil had told him that the succubus's scent would dissipate on its own given enough time. Unlike demonic aura and necrotic miasma, which lingered for long periods, succubi didn't like leaving traces behind either — but Jace didn't know how long counted as "long enough."

Still, after returning home, she didn't seem to have touched the sword again.

He knocked and entered, set the sword and papers on the table. "Here are the things. Have a look."

Vereesa picked up the sword and examined it closely. "This looks like a demon's work."

"What do you mean, a demon's work?" Jace tested.

"A sword used by demons." Vereesa said. "Death knights consort with warlocks and demons alike. It wouldn't be strange for demons to forge weapons for them, don't you think?"

Jace nodded. It seemed she hadn't detected the succubus's presence.

He picked up the dark brown pieces of fabric on Vereesa's table and gave them an experimental sniff. A faint corpse stench threading through the earthy smell made him frown. The memory of rummaging through a pile of gnoll corpses with Grede that day came flooding back.

The ranger set the sword down. "These very much feel like the same person's belongings, Jace — at least in terms of the necrotic aura." Jace couldn't pick out any distinguishing features, so he could only nod, but he didn't doubt a Windrunner's judgment.

Vereesa continued, "So it's possible he was defeated in the Great Graveyard and his soul fled? Or... he's dead. You saw that death knight's corpse. Are you certain it was the same as the husk Teron Gorefiend left behind?"

"Very similar," Jace said. "Is there a big difference between a death knight killed outright and a husk left behind by a fleeing soul?"

"There is some difference." Vereesa said. "A death knight killed on the spot would rapidly blacken and decay from the energy collapse. A husk that's been abandoned won't — the soul seizes most of the necrotic energy that sustained the body, leaving only a bit of residue that couldn't be taken in time. So the body looks as though it's been consumed into an empty shell. Without outside force destroying it, it won't change shape for a long time."

"Then I think it's more like the latter. But if it's just a husk... the residual energy still created a lot of gnoll zombies," Jace said.

"That means he's powerful — not some randomly resurrected orc warlock." Vereesa said. "The good news is that fits our profile of him even better — a lieutenant Teron Gorefiend trusted. Perhaps the owner of this sword is the one we've been looking for all along."

"But if it's a husk, that means his soul has already escaped, and tracking him becomes even harder. I don't know if bringing these things back to Quel'Thalas will be enough to convince Sylvanas."

Jace said, "I don't think he was defeated. I think he switched bodies voluntarily. The entire Wizard Sanctum — the entire kingdom — doesn't even know there's a death knight here, Vereesa. Who do you think defeated him? It definitely couldn't have been gnolls."

"The Great Graveyard is the resting place of many war heroes — from the Troll Wars, the Gnoll Wars, and even some who died during the two Orc Wars. When the wars first ended, Raven Hill wasn't as run-down as it is today. Some of the corpses there, especially those from the Orc Wars, still haven't fully decayed due to the dark energy influence of Duskwood. Aren't these exactly the kind of vessels a death knight would be looking for?"

"But a death knight needs a warlock to perform the ritual to enter a new body, Jace." Vereesa said. "Without someone else guiding his soul into a new vessel... he can't complete the body switch on his own."

Hearing Vereesa say this, Jace recalled the level-70 Shadowmoon Valley questline — how after Teron Gorefiend was slain by Turalyon, his soul had hidden away until the player found him, and in the end he'd tricked the player into completing a series of ritual preparations before he could be reborn.

Even Teron Gorefiend couldn't simply resurrect himself from a corpse, so his subordinates certainly couldn't either. Vereesa's objection was reasonable.

"That's true." Jace rubbed his lip. "Let me think. Would it be his orc warlock attendant? That seems perfectly reasonable, doesn't it?"

"I don't know." Vereesa shook her head. "I didn't find any orc tracks in the Great Graveyard. You know orcish warlocks are far less skilled at hiding their movements than trolls."

Since Vereesa had said as much, Jace didn't know what to say either. Then he thought of someone.

"While you were investigating Duskwood and Raven Hill, did you come across a man named Abercrombie?"

In the game, Abercrombie was a necromancer living next to the Great Graveyard. After his wife died, he'd gone mad and tried to resurrect her with necromantic magic, but his wife had turned into a terrifying zombie that attacked townsfolk and was eventually killed by the townspeople.

To get revenge on the townspeople, he'd deceived travelers into helping him gather materials... eventually creating a massive abomination that attacked Duskwood.

If there was anyone in Duskwood who might know something about the death knight in the Great Graveyard, it had to be this man — after all, in the game, he lived right next to the Great Graveyard.

"Never heard of him. What does he do?"

Jace said, "He seems to know a thing or two about necromancy, and he lived in Raven Hill too. We should go ask him."

"Is he still in Raven Hill? When I went before, I'd never heard of such a person in town." Vereesa said. "Well, I didn't meet many people willing to share information with me either."

Given the decade-plus gap from the game's timeline, Jace didn't even know if Abercrombie had become a necromancer yet, or whether he'd moved next to the Great Graveyard.

"I can help you find him," he said. "I want to track down this death knight too."

Vereesa's eyes widened. "That would be wonderful... it really is a lot of trouble doing this alone. But won't it interfere with your own affairs?"

"Well, I actually have a favor to ask you too."

"Go ahead, Jace. Though you do sound like you've been hesitating for a while."

"Earlier, when I was learning magic, I ran into a small problem. Now I need a reference — proof that I'm not a..."

"I know." The elf's long eyebrows twitched. "You want me to write a reference to your magic academy, proving you're not a warlock? But it's one thing to help you hide how frightening your shadow magic is — helping you lie is... uh..."

Jace let out a soft sigh. "All right... I know it's asking too much."

"Here's what I'll do." The elf leaned against the table. "I can't prove you're not a warlock, but at the very least I can prove you're harmless, right? I'll write that I saw you struck by Teron Gorefiend's dark magic and tainted by shadow energy, but that you have no intention of using the Shadow that plagues you to harm innocents. How's that?"

"That works." Jace said immediately. "Thank you so much!"

Vereesa nodded. "I'm helping you. If one day you do go down that path and hurt someone... it'll drag me down too, even in Quel'Thalas. It'll bring trouble. So I don't want you to treat this as something a casual letter can fix."

"But I'm still more than happy to help you out of this mess — regardless of whether you're going to help me investigate. And also because of this room." She stretched. "Ah... the night I came back from Duskwood and lay down here was pure bliss. It's been ages since I slept that comfortably."

Jace said, "I understand the weight of this guarantee, Vereesa. I know how dangerous the Shadow is."

Vereesa looked over. "I know you understand. That's why I'm willing to trust you — after all, we've fought side by side."
