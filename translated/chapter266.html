Chapter 266: The Mass Graves

The banks of the Nafeltari River had been all but stripped of their feeble green. Layer upon layer of withered, brittle branches — never mind the cold wind that blew through from time to time, the sight of it alone was enough to make a person's blood run cold.

Jace clutched his hood and red cap, both flapping wildly in the wind, as he studied the forest across the river. Vereesa, dressed all in pale green, glanced at him. "That hat of yours looks awfully awkward."

"Not a good look?" Jace said. "If it works, that's enough. You should be able to sense the magical power in it, no? Fire power. As a mage, I need every piece of equipment that can strengthen my magic."

"I can sense a little," Vereesa said. "But it really is… rather conspicuous."

"I'm a warlock, not some kind of thief." Jace looked toward the far bank. "Are you sure we should cut straight through the forest over there?"

"Of course. Following the main road to Raven Hill would take at least two or three days." Vereesa said. "I came through here once for a look around — nothing dangerous. Don't tell me you're afraid of a few beasts prowling around in the middle of the night?"

Vereesa had a point. The last time he and Grede had traveled to Duskwood, the two of them had been in a state where even handling a few gnolls nearly cost them their lives. Three or four months later, things were completely different.

More importantly, the elven woman beside him gazed at the forest across the river the way one might look at their own living room, and that put Jace considerably more at ease.

The docks along the Nafeltari River were mostly for fishermen and patrolmen crossing the water; ordinary commoners and farmers rarely chose to cross to the far bank from here.

Of course, these docks had been bustling once. Ever since the explosion at Karazhan, the place had grown much quieter.

Jace could see many sunken, abandoned boats beneath the docks. Further out, a few wooden boats were tied up that no one seemed to be looking after at all — by the look of them, they hadn't been moved in a long time.

The two of them rowed across. To reach the great cemetery, they only needed to cross this gloomy forest and walk another half day or so — this route cut two-thirds of the time compared to going around Sentinel Hill.

Jace planned to take Vereesa to Raven Hill first and search for traces of the necromancer scholar Abercrombie near the great cemetery. After all, in the game he'd lived in seclusion in a ruined house near the cemetery — Jace just wasn't sure whether it was the same here.

After venturing deep into Duskwood, the familiar eerie feeling swept over him in full force — even though it was only two or three in the afternoon.

"I sensed very few signs of beasts that time, Jace," Vereesa said. "Last time I came here, a few wild wolves tracked me — I felt like there were beasts watching me from everywhere in the forest. But now…"

"And you still said there was nothing dangerous here?"

"We do have two of us this time," Vereesa said. "And you have classes to attend — I wanted to make the most of our time." Jace said, "My classes all happen to be crammed into three days, so this time I happened to be free to come out with you…"

"Ever since we set out from Stormwind early this morning, I've been noticing a strange smell following us." Vereesa looked Jace up and down. "At first I thought it was some kind of illusion, but now…"

"What kind of smell?" Jace sniffed himself a little nervously. Please don't let it be the scent of a succubus, he thought. He'd been extremely careful when getting ready to go out today.

"It smells like a black dragon," Vereesa said with certainty. "Back in Silverpine Forest, that scent left an extremely deep impression on me."

"Ah… I'd nearly forgotten about that." Jace let out a breath of relief. "It's my cloak, Vereesa. When I was in Southshore, that black dragon spat on me — the smell's still there."

"The clothing shop owner told me not to wash it too hard — just leave the smell, and it'll keep the forest animals from coming near. Looks like it's working as intended, huh?"

"So that's what it is." Vereesa leaned in and sniffed Jace's cloak. "I can't believe you actually put up with this… Even though they always told me to learn to hide my scent in the forest, or else trolls might track me — but I don't think there are any forest trolls around here?!"

"No forest trolls, but the south is Gurubashi Empire territory," Jace reminded her. "Though those jungle trolls don't really come around much these days."

"Why are there trolls everywhere," Vereesa muttered under her breath.

Yes, trolls everywhere — you're all descended from the ancient dark trolls around the Well of Eternity too, Jace thought to himself as he watched her back.

If he actually said that out loud, their relationship would probably come to an abrupt end — and any thought of a letter of sponsorship could be forgotten.

After leaving the forest, Jace found the surroundings much more familiar. After all, last time he and Grede had circled around the great cemetery area for a while, so he'd gotten to know the local terrain.

When he saw those crooked, half-collapsed brick walls, and the damaged metal fencing faintly visible in the distance, Jace knew they weren't far from Raven Hill.

"Where does the person we're looking for live?" Vereesa asked, looking at the section of railing that had been knocked over to the south.

"Did you see any ruined houses last time you were here?" Jace said. "Honestly, I don't know the exact location either. I only heard from someone that he might be living in seclusion here."

"There's one on high ground inside the great cemetery, but no one lives in it." Vereesa pointed to the southwest. "It's on that high slope over there — can't see it from here, blocked by the forest behind the fence."

"Not that one — it's outside the cemetery. Have you been over there?"

Jace pointed to the southeast. On the game's map, that was where Abercrombie's secluded little house was located.

"There are graves over there too?" Vereesa said. "When the people in Duskwood Town mentioned Raven Hill's great cemetery, I thought it was just the area inside the iron fence. I never once went to look outside — damn it."

Realizing she'd said something inappropriate, she lowered her brows. "Sorry."

"Don't worry about it. I'm not exactly the most refined person myself." Jace said, "Let's head that way. Hopefully this trip goes smoothly — otherwise I'll really have to show you how rich the Common tongue's vocabulary of curses is."

Vereesa gave an embarrassed smile and fell into step behind Jace.

Just as he'd guessed, the east and northeast sides of the great cemetery were covered with stretches of mass graves. But the undertakers of Raven Hill hadn't simply abandoned the dead here — they'd built some simple fencing and there were even a few small houses where the gravediggers lived.

Even so, the place looked like it had been abandoned for many years.

This was probably where the future Abercrombie would live in seclusion. With undead wandering everywhere, the houses themselves dilapidated and rarely visited by the living, only here could Abercrombie study his necromantic magic and carry out his revenge plan.

Vereesa and Jace searched the place house by house. Not only were there no living people, there wasn't the slightest sign that anyone living had ever stayed here.

By now it was nearly evening. Jace stood on a high slope and looked out, watching Vereesa return from a distance. "Not a single person. Looks like the gravediggers have all left too."

"Not only are there no signs of the living — there aren't even many dead left," Vereesa said. "I took a look over there. A lot of the graves here have been dug up. Did those corpses rise?"

"I don't know either."

Jace felt it probably wasn't that simple.

After the population of Raven Hill dwindled, this massive pauper's cemetery lost its keepers, and all sorts of people came to dig up the graves — taking the burial jewelry off the wealthy, or the few insignificant but meaningful things on the bodies of others.

He'd heard plenty of such stories back in Stormwind too.

"Let's go ask inside Raven Hill," he said, pointing to the southwest.
