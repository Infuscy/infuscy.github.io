Chapter 267: The Warlock in the Tavern

Following the great cemetery's iron fence along, it wasn't long before Jace could see the outermost abandoned houses of Raven Hill. His sense of direction here was still sharp.

Night had deepened — though for Duskwood that only meant it had gotten slightly darker. When they arrived at the Raven Hill town square, the place somehow felt even more desolate than the last time he'd been here.

The tavern was fairly well populated for the evening, but compared to a stranger like Jace, the elf was clearly what drew attention. Quite a few patrons kept stealing glances at Vereesa between swigs of their drinks.

Vereesa, for her part, was perfectly composed. Compared to when she'd first arrived in Stormwind, she was noticeably more at ease with these cross-racial stares.

Jace walked up to the counter. The proprietor was still the same bald man. He glanced at the customer in front of him, looked at the elf, then looked back. "I remember you."

"Yes, I've been here before, boss."

The proprietor said, half-joking, "Brought a dwarf last time, didn't you? This time it's an elf lady. Next time you'll be bringing a gnome girl, I suppose?"

"Might be a troll bitch," a middle-aged man nearby chimed in.

A wave of laughter rose from a few men further off. The proprietor shot him a look, then stole a glance at Jace, his face clearly showing he felt a little bad about having opened that line of conversation.

Jace said, "Maybe a demon?" The moment the word "demon" left his mouth, the laughter throughout the tavern died on the spot.

The patrons' furtive gazes lingered on him — at the thin black rod wrapped in dull yellow cloth on his back, the gem-topped fire staff in his hand that looked as if it had been scorched, the dark brown cloak bearing fel-burn patterns, the crimson crown-cap beneath his hood — and the more they looked, the less this outsider seemed like he was joking.

There was an indescribable strange smell hanging in the air, seemingly drifting ever since the two customers had walked in. Now that the place had gone quiet, the odor was growing even more pronounced.

"I'm sorry, sir. I'm very sorry, elf lady." The middle-aged man lowered his head.

Vereesa nodded. Jace fixed the man with a stare, then set a silver coin on the counter. "Two rooms, boss. Just for one night."

"The last two at the end of the second-floor east hallway." The proprietor asked, "Anything to eat?"

"I'll have a block of dried cheese and two loaves of bread. One with a sprinkle of salt, the other with a spread of cream."

"Wait downstairs a moment, both of you."

The proprietor headed into the back kitchen as he said this. Just then, the inn door banged open with a thud, and the patrons all turned to look in unison.

A familiar young face appeared in the doorway. He held a longsword and wore the kind of white shirt commonly seen on Stormwind soldiers, with a leather jerkin over it — he looked the very picture of a town constable.

"Morgan?"

Morgan Ladimore's stern face suddenly broke into a grin. "Sesso? So it was you… Out there I thought some rogue warlock had shown up. Last time you came to Raven Hill, it didn't leave you with any nightmares, did it?"

"Even if it had, I'd still have to come." Jace said, "As it happens, there's something I wanted to talk to you about. Perfect timing."

"Boss, bring me an order of fried spider legs!" Morgan shouted toward the back kitchen. "Clean the hairs off properly this time — I don't want a mouthful of blood again!"

"Got it, already! You've been nagging about that piddly thing for two years!" came the proprietor's grumbling reply from the kitchen.

"Spider legs…" Jace looked at Vereesa. "Can you eat those?"

"Of course." The elf said, "Though I've never had them."

"Tastes no different from crab legs." Morgan glanced at Vereesa with a smile, then blinked. "An… an elf? Uh, I think I've seen you before. Haven't you been here previously?"

Vereesa nodded. "It should have been last week, but I didn't stay in town for long."

"Morgan, this is Aserwen Goldthorn, a ranger of Quel'Thalas," Jace said. "Aserwen, this is Morgan Ladimore, town guard of Raven Hill."

"Hello." Vereesa smiled and extended her hand.

Morgan wiped his hand on his clothes before shaking hers, wearing a faint, awkward smile. "Hello."

Once the three of them sat down, Morgan said, somewhat unnaturally, "To be honest, I've never spoken to a single elf in my life — never mind shaking hands. Frankly, a few years ago I didn't even believe elves existed…"

"We'll be seeing more and more of each other from now on, Mr. Ladimore," Vereesa said. "After all, we're of the same faction now."

Morgan nodded. "Let's hope so. So what are you two here for?"

Jace said, "We're here to investigate that werewolf business…"

"Werewolf? You're sure there actually is a werewolf here?" Morgan asked, startled.

Jace said, "We're not sure there actually is a werewolf — this area is too vast, and it's plagued by all manner of dark curses. But we suspect that the so-called werewolf the Raven Hill residents claim to have seen is actually a Death Knight. Have you heard about the Death Knights?"

"I've heard something of it," Morgan nodded. "Isn't that even more terrifying than a werewolf?"

"The Death Knight's target probably isn't this town," Vereesa said. "But I found some things the Death Knight left behind in the great cemetery. Jace and I suspect that someone here or in Duskwood Town helped the Death Knight… so we were hoping you might help us find some leads."

"Truth be told, there aren't many people left in this town," Morgan said.

Just then the proprietor set down the bread, cheese, and spider legs in turn. After he left, Morgan continued, "To imagine that anyone among the remaining folk here helped a traitor of the Horde…"

He glanced at the patrons inside the shop and lowered his voice. "Well, I can't rule out that possibility either."

"Then, are there any people who used to live in Raven Hill but have since moved to Duskwood Town?" Jace asked.

"A great many. A great many," Morgan said. "Far more than those who still live in Raven Hill, in fact. Come to think of it — do you two have a suspect already?"

Jace had to admit that Morgan Ladimore genuinely had the makings of a detective.

"There is one. Is there anyone in town named Abercrombie?"

"Abercrombie?" Morgan frowned. "I've never heard that name. Could he be living in Duskwood Town? Truth be told, I don't know many people there. My wife probably knows more."

"Still, don't you think that name is a bit strange? Sounds like it was cobbled together."

Those words of Morgan's reminded Jace — the quest mentioned that a decade or so from now, the people of Duskwood Town would know nothing of the name Abercrombie, the mayor included. So was this a false name adopted to conceal his identity?

"It is rather strange."

Jace glanced at the roasted spider legs on the table with great unease, feeling as if spiders were crawling all over him.

"Do you have any more leads? Like what he does, what he looks like, whether he's married…" Morgan said, picking up one of the legs as he spoke.

Jace thought for a moment. "He should be an alchemist originally, a doctor… and his wife seems to have died of illness. Well, or she might have been seriously ill and bedridden at home for years — I'm not entirely sure."

As he listened to Jace's words, Morgan's expression grew complicated by the degree.

"You must be talking about Mr. Thomas Waller."
