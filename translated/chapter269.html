Chapter 269: New Inhabitants of the Forest

The wagons coming from Moonbrook were mostly hauling grain, and the grain sacks squeezed Jace and Vereesa into a corner where they sat pressed right up against each other.

Unlike those elven mages who reeked of cloying perfume, Vereesa carried only the faint scent of grass on her. She was a far-ranger, after all — slathering on perfume in a forest crawling with trolls would've been a death wish.

Thinking back to Morgan's joke earlier, Jace only now realized that this whole trip out with Vereesa, he hadn't entertained a single improper thought about her.

It wasn't just the time crunch, or what had happened to Grede, or the warnings to be careful around elves.

Of course, he also didn't really believe that Vereesa Windrunner was the sort of depraved character Grede had made elves out to be.

Probably the bigger influence was Morophil — the succubus had been twisting his ideas about romance to the point of breakdown these past few days. Even when he ran into a perfectly normal person, everything felt off.

This probably couldn't be counted as an influence of shadow energy… could it?

Just as he was thinking this, the wagon suddenly came to a halt.

The people up front were saying something, but Jace had been lost in thought and hadn't caught it.

He raised his head and looked ahead — something seemed to be blocking the road. "Wolves? There are wolves?!"

The driver of their wagon called out.

"Wild wolves? Scare them off! Don't let them block the road, the goddamn beasts!"

Two men carrying steel forks jumped down from the wagon and headed forward. That was when Jace noticed that Vereesa didn't seem to be paying much attention to what was ahead. She raised her head and twitched her nose. "Something's wrong, Jace!"

Before her words had finished falling, several objects — too fast to make out what they were — came flying out of the grim forest on both sides of the road, streaking straight toward the two men with the steel forks. A shrill arrow suddenly struck one of the objects dead-on, while another slammed full-force into one of the men and knocked him flat. The man lay on the ground, mouth open, but no scream came out…

Jace looked closely — there was an axe buried in him!

What the hell had thrown an axe that fast, like a throwing knife?!

"Ogres!" Vereesa shouted at the top of her lungs. "Everyone, be careful!"

"Ogres?!"

"What?!"

The caravan instantly dissolved into chaos. Vereesa drew her bow and loosed several arrows into the trees. Roars and howls immediately erupted from the forest — lower and more savage than any wild bear's.

Suddenly a large tree by the roadside was smashed apart, and a massive shadow over three meters tall leapt out of the wreckage. The thing hung its jaw open, its teeth thick as a hippo's, its blank eyes staring straight ahead without registering anything to either side.

It swung a giant club that looked to have been carved from a tree trunk in a wide arc toward the wagon at the front. The driver was so frightened he outright flung himself backward. The blow smashed the wagon to splinters, and on the backswing it caught the rump of the pack horse that hadn't broken free of its harness in time, pulverizing its hindquarters into a mess of blood and flesh!

The howling of wolves rose on every side. Jace leapt out of the wagon, and from behind came several heavy, panting footfalls. He'd barely turned around when an ogre — large enough to fill his entire field of vision — screeched to a halt right in front of him, still gripping a small axe grotesquely out of proportion with its frame.

He looked up. An arrow was already buried in its forehead. It stared at Jace for a moment, let out a couple of dumb laughs, then toppled backward — the impact sent a tremor through the ground strong enough to make Jace's feet tingle.

A scrambling of footsteps came from beneath the wagon. Jace had barely looked down when a gray wolf came lunging out, going straight for his lower leg. The wolf's neck was bound with a thick collar, the kind forged by orcs, so tight that the skin beneath had rotted and the fur had fallen out — but this was no warg. It was a local forest wolf.

An ogre's pet?

Jace kicked it over. From above came Vereesa's shout.

"Jace! Over there!" Vereesa loosed an arrow on instinct, and it buried itself straight into an ogre's belly. The ogre froze for a moment, then stared at its own navel and started clawing at it frantically, all while howling something in its tongue.

Jace gripped the dragonman's staff. Off in the direction the elf had indicated, an ogre was raising its club and charging straight at him.

He swept his hand outward and murmured the Immolate incantation. A burst of crimson fire suddenly detonated on the ogre's shoulder — flesh and bone scattering into the air in an unnatural mist of pink.

The ogre shrieked in horror, staggering on the few strips of sinew that still connected its arm. Jace spoke the incantation once more, this time straight at its head — and blew the thing's skull clean off. It toppled backward into the roadside brush.

He had no time to watch these towering monstrosities fall. Behind him came another howling ogre. These creatures fought with absolutely no regard for subtlety — the bigger the presence they could project, the better.

Jace turned, meaning to hit it with a Fear spell — but the moment his eyes met its own, the fierce expression on the ogre that had already charged up to him instantly drained away. It stood frozen in place.

"Warlock… warlock… fear…"

They shouted warnings to one another in Orcish. Before long, other ogres further off heard the cries and took up the call.

"Warlock! There's a warlock!"

"Run!"

One ogre had already charged onto the road, axe still in hand, and was hesitating at the sound of its comrades yelling to flee — when an arrow punched clean through its forehead.

Its two dull eyes rolled upward to look at its own skull. It reached up, scratched at it, grabbed the arrow and tried to pull it out — and the moment it yanked, it collapsed.

Behind it, Vereesa had just nocked another arrow from her back, her breathing ragged as she looked at Jace.

"Jace… your eyes."

"What?"

Vereesa touched Jace's sweat-soaked forehead, and her eyes flared.

"Your eyes were full of shadow just now. Are you all right?"

"I… don't feel anything in particular."

Listening to Vereesa, Jace half-wondered whether he'd been consumed by shadow again or something.

But he truly didn't feel that bloodlust that came with total loss of control. All he'd had in mind was saving these caravan members — the people who'd given him a ride to Duskwood Town and who had no power to resist the ogres.

But thinking back on it just now, it seemed that before he'd even spoken the incantation, that ogre had already been hit by a Fear spell.

What was going on? Had that not been his Fear spell? Was there another warlock here?

He scanned the surrounding forest. There wasn't a single figure in sight.

Four ogre corpses lay around them. Aside from the one Jace had blown apart, each had several arrows lodged in vital spots.

The rest of the caravan members were still in a state of complete bewilderment. They hadn't even processed the fact that they'd just been ambushed by ogres.

"There are ogres here?" Vereesa asked.

"I don't know." Jace looked at the bodies around them. "Probably blocked by the Expedition on their way back to the Dark Portal and stranded here."

"Why were they so terrified of you?" Vereesa asked again.

Jace said, "You understand Orcish?"

"A little," Vereesa said.

"It was the two travelers we picked up in Raven Hill who saved us… it was that elf and that fire mage!" someone from God-knew-where shouted.

"Damn it… we'd really have been done for without them. Why were there ogres…"

"What are your names? I'm taking your names back to Stormwind, mage!"

"He's Jace Sesso," Vereesa answered.

"Master Sesso… I'll remember that name for life."

"Anyone hurt? Up ahead…"

"There is… Balasen took an axe to the shoulder. He's right under the wheel — pull him out first! Anyone got a healing potion or some herbs… he's badly hurt!"

"I've got some." Jace pulled out a bottle. "I have a little here."

"We really can't just take it for free from a master," the caravan leader said. "I'll buy it!"

In the chaos, he pulled two gold coins and a fistful of uncountable silver from his pocket and stuffed them into Jace's pack.

"That's too much…"

"That's not just for the medicine — it's my thanks to the master and the beautiful elf lady. Our whole group would've been wiped out without you two. If we'd fallen into the hands of those monsters, even surviving would've been worse than dying!"

Jace handed the healing potion to the leader, glanced at the coins scattered throughout his pack, and after a moment looked back. "Vereesa, you should get a cut of this."

"What are you talking about? Keep it all," Vereesa whispered. "I'm nearly out of arrows, Jace. If they hadn't suddenly run, I was about to grab you and flee. There were really too many of them — if I'd had to fight those brutes blade to blade, I couldn't guarantee a vital hit every time."

Jace swallowed. "Did you sense any other caster just now? A warlock or a mage or something."

"No," Vereesa said. "There was only you. You're not being modest enough to invent a nonexistent person to take the credit for you, are you?"

"No, I just…" Jace wasn't being modest either. He truly didn't know exactly how he'd scared those ogres away.

But the entire caravan only had one warlock, and that was him. If it wasn't him, then who?

"Help me pull the arrows out, Jace. Let's see if any of them are still usable," Vereesa said. "It'd take me forever on my own."

Jace nodded and walked up to a fallen ogre corpse.

Even in death, the creature was terrifying enough. Standing before it, Jace felt a profound sense of unreality, as though what he was looking at didn't actually exist.

Then again, ogres weren't native to Azeroth to begin with — it was no wonder they felt so alien.

If there really was only one warlock here, then it meant he'd just cast a shadow magic without speaking an incantation.

It reminded him of that wild night, when he'd apparently set fire to and blown up that boar's corpse without uttering a word of the spell.

Lost in thought, he tried to pull the arrow from the ogre's forehead, when from behind came a shout: "Quick, help the elf lady find her arrows! Move it!"

The caravan members quickly set about recovering arrows from the ogre corpses, wiping off the blood one by one and handing them to Vereesa, who looked a little embarrassed and kept saying thank you. Still, plenty of arrows were broken or ruined, and in the end they only recovered four or five that were still usable.
