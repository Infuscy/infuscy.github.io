## Chapter 270: Duskwood Town

The caravan pressed on for a while, leaving behind the spot where the ogre ambush had taken place, and arrived at a crossroads waystation in the afternoon.

After the Duskwood Town guards were briefed on what had happened, they dispatched several fully armed patrol riders to speed toward the scene.

Jace looked at these well-equipped soldiers and found it impossible to connect them with the Duskwood Town of the future — the one protected by nothing more than the Night Watch.

The caravan planned to rest up here properly and arrange to send the wounded away, so the two of them took a tent as well.

The cramped, makeshift tent was damp and stuffy, carrying the smell of something rotting.

Vereesa sat across from him, watching the humans passing back and forth outside. Before long, she noticed that Jace had been silent the whole time.

"What are you thinking about?"

Jace answered, "Why those ogres ran the moment they saw me."

Vereesa said, "If you ask me, perhaps your magic reminded them of Gul'dan's disciples. Whether orcs or ogres, they're terrified of those high-and-mighty casters. They haven't seen warlocks among the Alliance armies, so they might worry you have some connection to their masters. A lot of ogres look fearsome, but they're actually stupid and cowardly."

"Don't the Quel'dorei armies use shadow magic either?" Jace asked.

"I've heard of it, but I've never seen it with my own eyes," Vereesa explained. "To protect the Sunwell, Silvermoon and Quel'Danas strictly forbid the practice of shadow magic. Still, the Sun King hoped their research could help counter the forest trolls' sorcery, so shadow studies are permitted south of the Elandar River, away from the runestones. Professor Kefal would know more about it."

"He seems quite familiar with warlocks," Jace said. "I suspect he might actually know Quel'dorei shadow warlocks."

The elf sat up straighter. "Speaking of which — why did you learn dark magic in the first place? As far as I know, Stormwind expels shadow practitioners too…"

"At first it was just to protect myself," Jace said, counting the silver coins in his pack as he spoke. "I originally wanted to learn arcane magic, but it was obvious I had no talent for it. Later, as the enemies I had to deal with grew more troublesome, I just kept learning more."

"I've heard that scholars who delve into shadow grow increasingly reclusive, and many magisters believe shadow can make a person lose control, so they oppose any shadow research," Vereesa glanced over. "You seem perfectly normal, though — frankly, more normal than a lot of arcanists who've never touched shadow."

"Your magisters' concern is justified. Shadow does sometimes influence my thinking," Jace admitted honestly. "I've been fighting it all along."

Vereesa nodded. "So I suppose I don't really know you yet."

"It's not like that." Jace said, "Well — I think the Kirin Tor would be willing to vouch for me. If you're truly worried, I should get by even without your sponsorship — at the very least, I wouldn't get arrested." He pinched a gold coin between his fingers. "And you needn't worry that refusing to sponsor me means I'll refuse to help you — this trip's already paid for itself many times over."

"Hah — if there were no risk, what would be the point of sponsorship?" Vereesa said with a smile.

"Thank you," Jace said.

Before long, the noise outside grew hectic. Jace glanced out — the merchants were moving crates and goods into the shelter of the waystation's awning.

Was it going to rain?

Seeing the horses growing restless in the distance, he suddenly remembered something and leaned back in. "What was that magic you used on me in the wagon?"

"A dispelling ability," Vereesa said. "But only the relatively friendly kind — if it were someone else's curse… I'm afraid I couldn't dispel it."

"Could you teach me that magic?" Jace said. "I have a problem right now — some of my buff spells last far too long, and then there are these strange arcane glows clinging to me… I'd like to find a way to actively dispel them."

"But that's not an incantation…" Vereesa shrugged. "It's an innate arcane manipulation ability of the Quel'dorei — even their swordsmen who rarely touch arcane magic can do it."

So it's Arcane Torrent-style dispel, Jace thought. "Then I'll just have to go learn it at school like a normal person."

Just then someone stirred the tent flap.

"Is something the matter?" Jace asked.

"Master, elf lady — I wanted to bring you something to eat. Bread my mother baked…"

A hand reached in carrying a cloth-wrapped loaf studded with dried fruit and smoked ham bits.

"Have a taste, both heroes. It really is delicious."

"I'm certainly no hero," Jace said. "But thank you."

"You don't have to be one of the Sons of Lothar to be a hero," the soldier outside said. "You saved our lives. The Light preserve you!"

"Thank you — and may the Light preserve you too, sir," Jace said.

The footsteps outside grew more distant. Jace sat back down with the rain-speckled bread and noticed that Vereesa's expression had turned somewhat off.

He broke the loaf in half and passed a piece over. "What's wrong?"

Vereesa looked at the bread in her hand. "I wonder how Alleria is doing down south. Come to think of it — aren't they about to enter the Dark Portal? I heard the Horde armies there are already crumbling."

Just as Jace was at a loss for how to answer, a flash suddenly lit up the outside, and not long after came a rolling barrage of dull thunder.

A patter of raindrops began drumming on the tent above.

"It's really raining," the elf said, holding out her hand and rubbing the raindrops between her fingers. "Looks like we won't be setting out tonight."

"Even without the rain we couldn't travel at night," Jace looked at her. "We're not you."

"Fine." Vereesa drew her knees up. "But now I can't even go out for some fresh air."

"I'm not in the mood to go out," Jace lay back. "This morning's fight took too much out of me. I'm going to sleep it off — I'm turning in first."

"If you're not eating, the bread's all mine."

"Suit yourself. I'm not hungry."

Lying on the blanket, Jace felt he was more or less trying to dodge the topic of the Dark Portal.

The road from Raven Hill to Duskwood Town was absurdly long. It wasn't until the evening of the second day that the caravan finally reached the town's entrance.

Jace half-rose and looked toward the town's entrance at the head of the caravan, and at a single glance he could take in the bustling Duskwood Town — it really was a remarkably large settlement.

Through this town, he could see just how prosperous southern Elwynn had been before the Karazhan Cataclysm, and get a sense of why humans had kept expanding south until they encroached on troll territory and sparked war.

Outside the inn, Jace was checking his belongings while Vereesa kept staring across the street.

Jace glanced over curiously and noticed a girl in a thin dress leaning against a shop doorway, watching the passersby. When he noticed the elf on this side looking at her, she stared right back at Vereesa. A half-elf. The look in her eyes was hard to read — perhaps resentment, perhaps jealousy. In any case, not a friendly gaze.

"Are they the children of our kind and humans?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes." Jace picked up his pack.

Vereesa said, "Human society seems to find Turalyon and Alleria quite shocking, but whether it's Stormwind or here, there are always some of these… wanderers."

She seemed not to know what word to use for half-elves and could only describe them this way.

Jace said, "A Quel'dorei comes out for a romp, has their fun with a few men and women, then runs back home and leaves the children here. The reason those two caused such a sensation is that people had never seen someone put something like this directly out in the open."

"They are absolutely not that kind of relationship," Vereesa stressed. "Though I wouldn't say I approve of them either, I can assure you that Alleria is not one to trifle with feelings."

Jace said, "I believe it too. So isn't a elf who's serious with a human shocking enough on its own?"

Vereesa paused. "So this is how you all see us?"

Jace said, "I don't see it that way, but I don't represent humanity — just as you don't represent elves."

Vereesa looked as if she'd been rendered speechless. Jace said, "Come on, let's go in and eat. We still need to ask around at the town center later — it's getting late, and I'm hoping we can find this Mr. Waller today."
