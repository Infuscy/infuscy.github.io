## Chapter 271: The Hermit

Grand Hamlet — or Darkshire, as it would later be known — was far more lively than it would be in the days to come. The Night Watch hadn't taken over yet; Stormwind soldiers and town watch were everywhere, and even now light cavalry patrols could be seen heading north.

After all, this was another important stronghold against the Redridge gnoll incursions, supporting Lakeshire to the north. Darkshire was also the last major supply station before Stranglethorn Vale to the south, so plenty of hunters and adventurers stopped here, and there were folks selling things they'd brought out of Stranglethorn — goblins, even, came and went among them.

Looking at the menu board here, Jace could see exactly what people meant when they said "the people of Darkshire have adapted well to their new lives."

Worg meat. Mutant spider meat. These people had the nerve to cook beasts that had been tainted by shadow magic — heat sterilizes it, right?

Jace glanced into the back kitchen and decided the place was probably more depraved than the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb. At least Jarel didn't dare grill lamb that warlocks had experimented on. Though thinking about the fel boar meat on Hellfire Peninsula in the game… this was nothing.

Whatever. There'd always be people who'd throw caution to the wind for a new recipe. Being a warlock himself, he wasn't exactly in a position to complain.

That didn't mean he was going to eat that stuff, though. After sharing a simple meal of ordinary Stormwind fare with Vereesa, they set out before the town hall closed for the evening.

Darkshire's town hall was quite grand — far more impressive than Lakeshire's. There were even a couple of elves inside talking to the clerks when they walked in.

As they entered, Vereesa deliberately pulled her hood lower, avoiding the elves' gazes. But elves weren't exactly a rare sight in Darkshire, and when a ranger passed by, the other elves merely glanced over before losing interest.

Lord Ello Ebonlocke was a member of the Ebonlocke family that ruled these lands — a family with considerable influence even in Stormwind.

"Thomas Waller!"

The clerk repeated the name as she went into the back to search the registry. Jace and Vereesa waited outside for a long time with no result.

It was getting late. Vereesa kept her head down, cracking and eating nut after nut she'd bought at the inn, her eyes fixed on the town hall entrance the whole time.

Jace listened to her crunching grow more and more impatient — and then the clerk came back out.

She shook her head. "It seems Mr. Thomas Waller has moved away from Darkshire, sir."

"Where did he go?" Jace asked.

"I really don't know. Perhaps Raven Hill?"

"We just came from Raven Hill!" Vereesa said, displeased. "The people there all said Mr. Waller had settled in Darkshire. Can't you look more carefully?"

"Ma'am? Lady Alleria?"

A rough male voice came from behind them. Jace and Vereesa turned to see a man of about thirty standing there.

The man glanced at Vereesa's forehead and said, "Ah, apologies — I seem to have mistaken you for someone else!"

"No matter," Vereesa shot a glance at Jace.

"What does this elven lady need?" the man asked the clerk.

"These two are looking for a Mr. Thomas Waller's address, Lord Ebonlocke, but the registry only has his name, with no place of residence..."

So he was the mayor?

"Thomas Waller, that alchemist," the man said. "I know him. A few months back he moved outside town. His old house was bought by another merchant, so we struck his address from the records. Head straight south out of town along the main road, and at the first farm you pass, keep going a few hundred meters — you'll see his house. There's a path leading straight to it."

"Much obliged." Vereesa nodded and tugged at Jace, ready to leave. Jace asked, "Lord Ebonlocke... one more thing. Do you know why Mr. Thomas Waller moved out of town?"

"Not too sure about that, young man. You'll probably have to ask him yourself," Ebonlocke said. Then he looked at Vereesa and added, "Ma'am — do you know Lady Alleria Windrunner? I had the honor of meeting her here late last year. Your voice is very much like hers. Your features... as well."

"You're not the first to say so, sir. Thank you for your help. Goodbye." Vereesa smiled, then pulled Jace out of the town hall and into the street.

"If you'd dragged your feet any longer I would've been exposed! Didn't you see there were other elves in there?"

"This Waller fellow is no simple character," Jace said. "He's connected to necromantic magic. Don't you want to know a bit more?"

Vereesa blinked at Jace. "Alright, maybe I was being a little too nervous."

"Don't do anything rash when we get there," Jace said, fixing her with a stare. "His place might not be ordinary. Let's not let things get out of hand."

"We're not in any danger. Even if he's a necromancer, not just a doctor," Vereesa said.

Jace stared at her. "I'm not worried about you being in danger. Right now my concern is I don't want him ending up in danger. We still have questions for him."

"I know," Vereesa said.

"Let's get one thing straight," Jace said. "We can't come in talking about necromantic magic right off the bat. If he is involved, that'd blow it. We need to earn a little trust first — let him know we're not hostile."

"He's an alchemist, right?" Vereesa said. "Then we just say we're herb-gatherers. That works, doesn't it?"

"Have you ever gathered herbs?" Jace asked.

"No."

"Then let me do the talking," Jace said. "I've done a bit of that work."

"Then I'll leave it to you, Jace," Vereesa said. "Shall we go?"

Jace nodded. Walking out with Vereesa, he always felt like she was rushing him forward — not a moment's pause.

She had thousands of years ahead of her, and yet she was even more impatient than a human.

Then again, they really ought to make haste this trip. The ogre problem had already cost them half a day.

Following Lord Ebonlocke's directions, they came to the edge of town, passed a farm, and stopped at the first fork in the road.

A weed-covered flagstone path ran all the way to a cabin half-hidden by trees, its lamplight flickering in and out of view — at least that meant someone was still living there.

Jace walked up to the door and knocked gently. A suspicious voice came from inside: "Who is it?"
