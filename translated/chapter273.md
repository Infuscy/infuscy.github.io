## Chapter 273: The Man in Black

Waller clutched at Jace's clothes. Watching a scholar in his sixties break down and sob like this made Jace deeply uncomfortable.

Jace glanced back and saw Vereesa holding a candlestick, watching him — not the knife or dagger she'd brought. She'd understood the "smash" instruction rather more literally than Jace had expected.

"Stop crying, Mr. Waller. I'm not here to take anything," Jace said. "I just want some information. About the Great Graveyard."

This time, when Jace mentioned the Great Graveyard, Waller's eyes filled with something close to terror — far more than the slight change in expression the first time.

Eliza snarled nearby, her free hand clawing forward, trying to attack Jace or Vereesa. Jace glanced at her and said, "I'm going to let go of you now, Mr. Waller. You promise you won't attack me?"

"If you touch him again, it won't just be your wife who's in trouble," the elf said, fixing the alchemist with a stare. Her short blade twirled between her fingers like a light stick, then suddenly stopped, its point aimed straight at the old man's neck.

"Trust me — your head will leave your shoulders faster than you can strike."

"I know. I promise..." Waller glanced at his wife by the wall, wiped his tears, and coughed twice. "Eliza, be quiet for a while. I need to talk to the guests."

After the zombie heard this, though she still tried to tear free from the wall and claw at something, her voice was much quieter than before.

Jace watched the old man shuffle to the table and collapse onto a stool.

"You want to ask about the man in black, don't you?"

"The man in black?" Jace looked at Vereesa. The two exchanged a glance.

"A death knight?" he asked.

"Perhaps. I'm not sure, young man," Waller said. "The moment I saw that man in black, I knew this day would come. I don't know death knights, and I don't know the Horde. All I know is that the man in black wielded necromantic magic more powerful than I could have imagined. Watching him use his power, I almost doubted everything I'd learned in my life..."

Jace sat down across from him. "This is very likely the man we're looking for, sir."

Waller looked over. "You won't arrest me just for necromantic magic, will you? You're one of the Wizard Sanctum."

"You don't really have much leverage to negotiate right now, human," Vereesa said flatly.

He moved the finger resting on the table. "Indeed. I can make no guarantees on that. Go on — let me see just how involved you are."

Waller thought for a moment, then asked, "Can you promise... that the man in black will never come looking for me again?"

"That's beyond my power," Jace said candidly. "But our goal is to capture that fellow. So if you don't want to run into him again, cooperating is helping yourself."

The old man nodded, took a drink of water, swished it around, and swallowed. "My wife was gravely ill for many years. I spent every last coin I had, searched through every bit of herbalism and alchemy knowledge I could find, and still couldn't save her. Those last days, she coughed up blood every day, every..."

He covered his face and drew a breath. "After that, all I could do was try to keep her body intact, young man. Because I didn't think I'd failed. I thought I had a way to wake her up. This is a world full of miracles, isn't it?"

"Does this count as a miracle?" Vereesa couldn't help but ask.

"She's standing in front of me again, ma'am," Waller said. "She stood up. She called my name. She spoke to me. She can walk. She even longs for the outdoors..."

"You sure she longs for the outdoors, and not to go eat human flesh?" Jace asked.

Waller was stunned for a moment. His lips moved but he didn't know what to say. Clearly his thoughts were in complete turmoil.

"Let's not talk about that," Jace said. "So the man in black did this?"

"That night," Waller nodded. "About half a year ago — I can't remember the exact date. He came to my door and told me he needed a favor. The reward would be... getting my wife to stand up again."

"He wanted me to help him find a powerful enough dead body. I took him to the Great Graveyard... He ultimately settled on a cavalry captain who'd fought the orcs in Black Marsh and died here in Duskwood Town."

At that, he covered his face and sighed. "I was the one treating that cavalry captain at the time. I failed him — I couldn't heal his wounds, and I handed his body over to that dark-robed warlock. I must have been out of my mind. Because that man in black only had to chant a few incantations and my wife opened her eyes again after all that time. Even if he'd asked me to find a dead man who was myself, I'd have done it!"

By now, Waller's voice was nearly breaking into sobs. Jace asked, "You helped him perform the soul-transfer ritual, didn't you?"

"You already know everything, sir," Waller said, shaking his head. "You even know about the soul ritual — what's the point of asking me? I watched the whole process. I saw his original corpse collapse into the dirt mound. I saw his soul fly into another rotting body. I saw that body regrow its flesh, open its eyes, and speak to me in the man in black's voice. Slowly, I began to feel that I was doing something wrong. Something terribly, terribly wrong."

"That night I was nearly driven mad, sir. I didn't even take the extra gift the man in black left me. He said those things would let my wife gradually return to normal — like a normal person. Like... she had never died at all."

"What gift?" Jace asked.

"I don't know," Waller said. "I didn't dare stay in that place. It was raining that night, thundering, as if heaven itself knew I was doing wrong and was driving me back. All I wanted was to see Eliza again — not spend the whole night with those corpses. I'd had enough that night. Even a moment more and I'd have been driven mad on the spot. I knew I had to come back."

Thomas Waller rambled on about everything he'd seen that night — the dead who'd heard sounds in their graves and clawed their way out of the earth, the shards of soul-stuff flying through the air, the dark carcass toppling like a felled statue, the wailing wind and rain, the summer night cold as winter.

It was as if he'd been wanting to pour all this out for a long time but hadn't known who to tell.

As his rambling, delirious account drew to a close, Vereesa asked, "That night, when the man in black came to your door — was he injured? Did anything about his movements make you think he was hurt?"

That was a real question. Why would a death knight switch bodies for no reason at all?

Waller said with certainty, "He was dying. I could feel his soul struggling inside his body, just like every dying person I've ever seen, ma'am. So he chose me to perform that evil ritual. He wanted a new body to keep existing."

"After switching bodies, he seemed much more confident. At first I even thought he was going to kill me to keep me from leaking the secret. Back then I felt that as long as my wife could come back, paying with my own life would be worth it. But in the end he let me go. He called me a genius. He thought I... should serve the orcs."

Jace asked, "That being so, did he mention where you should go to find him?"

Waller slowly raised his head and looked at Jace. "He said if I wanted to gain eternal life like my wife, I should go find... Ganasrash. I don't know why, but I remember that strange name, as if it were seared into my mind by magic, as if there'd come a day when I'd need to remember it. I suppose that day is now. As for what that name means — I have no idea, sir. I've been completely honest."

"Ganasrash. Thank you. I know what that means," Jace nodded.

He glanced back at the female zombie, now quiet. "What about your wife, Mr. Waller?"
