## Chapter 274: Progress

Hearing Jace's question, Waller suddenly tensed.

He looked at his wife, then at the young man in front of him. "What are you going to do..."

Jace said, "I think you already know very well that she isn't your wife. What's here now is just an empty shell driven by necromantic energy, clumsily imitating the beautiful Mrs. Waller. Her true soul departed long ago with that illness."

"Don't say any more."

"Since you've cared for so many dying people, you probably suspect that souls are real in this world," Jace said. "If you spend the rest of your life with this mad puppet that's taken over your wife's body, then when your soul is reunited with your wife, how will you explain yourself to her? If this puppet harms an innocent person — do you think she'll forgive you? Allowing a vile magic to use her body to do evil?"

"No... no... but what am I supposed to do?" Waller asked, his eyes wild. "You want me to kill her myself? Take a blade to her head, or smash her skull in with that candlestick like you said?!"

Jace glanced at the zombie. "I can try to take away the power that's driving her to move like a machine, and leave only your true wife — the body that once housed her soul. That's at least a part of the person you truly loved. It gives you a chance to lay her to rest."

"How could that be possible..." Waller said, nearly in despair.

"Are you willing?" Jace asked.

Waller looked at his "wife," whose eyes kept twitching, and nodded with difficulty.

"You sure about this, Jace..." Vereesa murmured.

Jace glanced at her and whispered, "Relax. I've drained Gorefiend himself. Why would I be afraid of a woman?"

"Elaram, Silondor."

He chanted calmly, without channeling his staff's power, only reaching out his hand. Eliza's once-smooth skin rapidly aged. Her lustrous black hair began to gray and wither.

Waller broke into sobs, murmuring his wife's name over and over. When the magic ended and Eliza collapsed limply against the wall, he ran over and caught her body.

"Eliza, my love... you... you're so beautiful..."

Waller held his wife's frail body tightly and lowered his head. "I don't know why, sir, but I feel much lighter. This is how she looked when she left me — she never changed. The man in black made her young again, sickeningly young, and I could never accept it. She didn't look young like that back then... I must have been mad to agree to his demand."

Jace said, "Give her a proper funeral, Mr. Thomas Waller. The townspeople here, and the folks at Raven Hill — they'll all come to see her off."

The old man nodded. Jace and Vereesa left the cabin and stepped outside. It was already very late.

"You really plan to just leave this necromancer here? He resurrected a death knight," Vereesa murmured.

"We'll report it to the Wizard Sanctum when we get back."

"Why not tell that Eberlock?"

"Magic matters are the Wizard Sanctum's specialty," Jace explained. "And I'm worried the people of Duskwood Town would deal with him too harshly. He is a doctor, after all. Morgan told me that both Duskwood Town and Raven Hill need him right now."

"Let's hope the mages can come up with a proper solution — strict surveillance, or a few years in a cell, that sort of thing..." He glanced around. "Now it seems the so-called gift from the man in black is exactly what Grede and I found at that gnoll's place. This alchemist dropped the stuff and ran, and the gnolls scooped it all up afterward."

"Let's hope this fellow hasn't stashed any other necromantic items elsewhere. If the mages find any, the punishment he faces won't be nearly so lenient."

"Then what did he mean by Ganasrash?" Vereesa asked. "Is it a place name?"

Jace said, "Ganasrash is Orcish — it means the Shattered Hand clan. I think the Shattered Hand must still have remnants on Azeroth, and they're still loyal to the death knight we're after. He probably wanted Mr. Waller to find the Shattered Hand orcs."

Vereesa sighed. "Right now I feel like I'm being led around by the nose."

"This is already huge progress, isn't it?" Jace smiled.

"It is. Thank you, Jace," Vereesa said.

The Badlands — a stretch of wasteland east of the Burning Steppes. Like the northern reaches of the Redridge Mountains, it was a vast desert whose ecology had been obliterated by the blast waves from the Blackrock Mountain explosion, just not as extreme as the Burning Steppes.

In the game, there was an outpost there called Kargath's Garrison, named to commemorate the former Shattered Hand warchief — Kargath Bladefist.

Jace had heard that some Shattered Hand soldiers routed at the Dark Portal had fled north to the Badlands. Perhaps the reason Warchief Thrall had named that place so was not only to honor Kargath, but also to reassure the Shattered Hand remnants lurking nearby that they could submit to his new Horde, rather than join the Blackrock orcs.

If Ganasrash definitely pointed to a location, that was most likely the place.

"If we confirm the location, you still plan to go alone?"

"Yes. Sylvanas doesn't want humans involved."

"If you're gone too long this time, I won't be able to just sit by. I'll inform the King, and then it won't just be a matter of involvement — it'll be far more troublesome than things are now."

"So you're saying I'm definitely going to fail and cause you trouble, is that it?"

Jace was about to say something when a wave of nausea hit him. He ran into the woods beside the road, steadied himself against a tree, and gasped — then vomited a puddle of black and yellow pus.

"Damn..."

A searing pain ripped through his entire abdomen. This was the necromantic energy from just now interfering with him. Even though it was only the necromantic power of an ordinary zombie, he couldn't digest it.

Vereesa came up behind him, pulled a healing potion from her pack, and popped the cork.

Jace sat to the side, took the potion, and gulped it down. He finally felt a little better.

He caught his breath. "You're strong, Vereesa — I don't doubt that at all. But our opponent is no pushover either. Don't take pointless risks. Your sister surely wouldn't want you walking into danger."

Vereesa frowned, leaning against the tree trunk, saying nothing.

Jace caught his breath, slowly slid down to sit on the ground, and rummaged through his pack. "The biscuits I brought, the dried meat — where did it all go?!"

"I ate it all," Vereesa said, not bothering to hide her irritation.

"Then what am I supposed to eat?!" Jace asked.

Vereesa pointed into the distance. "There's an inn in town! Didn't someone give you plenty of money?"

"Damn it all..." Jace tossed his bag aside. A clatter of coins hitting the ground.

He stared down the dark road stretching into the distance and heaved a deep sigh.

After a while, Vereesa said, "Sorry!"

"So you finally know you should leave your travel partner something to eat?" Jace asked.

"No." Vereesa lowered her head. "You were right — I shouldn't go after that death knight alone. I know almost nothing about his strength, and nothing about the Shattered Hand. I'd definitely get the worst of it. If he really is what you say — someone even the orcs are willing to call the Death Wish... I don't know how it would end."

Jace glanced at the female elf and finally set his mind at ease.

"Alright, I'll accept your apology. Got anything else to eat?"

Vereesa looked down at him and pulled an egg from her pocket. "Here!"

"Where'd the egg come from?" Jace took it.

"A human gave it to me outside the tent," Vereesa said. "You were asleep at the time."

"You hid this all this time — were you saving it so I wouldn't starve to death?"

"I thought I'd eaten them all. I just noticed there was one left."
