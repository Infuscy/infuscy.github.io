Chapter 275: The Greedy Mercenary

Duskwood Town was called Duskwood Town only because there wasn't much difference between its days and nights — not because everyone actually liked being out after dark.

By midnight, the streets had pretty much emptied out too, aside from the odd night-patrol soldier and late-traveling carriage. Every window was shut tight, most of the lamps extinguished.

Jace looked toward the crossroads at the center of town — that fountain decorated in a style that looked like a tombstone — and the tall clock tower of the town hall in the distance. He was finally starting to feel a hint of the game's eerie chill.

A gaunt, lame man crossed the street shouting as he went, "The end is near!" "Glenton is about to be punished!" "The kingdom has been cursed!" and the like.

Neither the passing soldiers nor anyone else paid his shouting any mind. Only Jace, an outsider, was still willing to spare him a glance.

The tavern was still fairly busy. Jace sat in a window seat. That single egg had pretty much been burned up on the walk here, and his stomach was growling hollow again.

Vereesa sat across from him, sniffing her hood and cloak over and over, then finally yanked them off.

"It reeks of death!"

After pulling off her cloak, she undid the tie binding her hair, and her silver locks cascaded down — in an instant she seemed like a completely different elf.

"Have a drink?" Jace asked.

"No."

"We survived the ogres, and we finally tracked down the death knight's trail — we ought to celebrate, at least?"

"Alcohol clouds my judgment."

"What else are you planning to do?" Jace asked. "I mean, work that requires judgment."

Vereesa raised her eyes to look at Jace, then glanced back at the bar, her gaze sweeping across the patrons. "Alright — nothing strong."

Jace smiled. "When you come out with me, you've got to adapt to how things work around here, elf. Every time I survive Grede, I have a drink to savor the beauty of being alive."

With that, he called the barkeep over and ordered two mugs of mild ale, a wedge of cheese, a portion of smoked ham, and two pieces of dry bread.

"Honestly, Vereesa," he said, watching the comings and goings in the tavern, "I don't agree with you going alone, but I also seriously doubt the Kingdom of Stormwind can offer you much help."

"Neither the death knight nor the Shattered Hand clan are likely within the Kingdom's borders anymore. And the mages and the kingdom's army can barely handle the troubles inside their own borders, let alone help you hunt down a fugitive hiding abroad."

"What about you two?" Vereesa said. "With the two of you helping, things would be a whole lot better, wouldn't they?"

"Bad timing," Jace said. "Grede Thunderfist has gone to Ironforge — probably won't be back for two or three months. I'm saying you should reveal your identity and seek help from the other elves. That would go a lot better."

"Your sister never told you not to seek help from elves, did she?"

"Then all the hardships I've overcome so far would be completely meaningless," Vereesa said. "I took on this mission to prove to Sylvanas that I can handle the investigation on my own."

"The investigation is done," Jace said. "We're already very close to that death knight. After we track down where the Shattered Hand clan is operating, you can report your progress to the Ranger-General and have her send mages or Farstriders to handle the rest."

"I want to catch him myself," Vereesa sighed. "I can tell Sylvanas knows I won't be able to complete this mission. She just wants to teach me a lesson, or shove me out of her sight so I stop bothering her. She doesn't care about where that death knight went. She cares more about what the other artifact is."

"So I have to finish this — to prove to her that I'm not some little girl who deserves a spanking from her big sister, but a real Farstrider warrior, a Windrunner just like them."

The barkeep brought the drinks and food over one by one. Jace took a long gulp of his mug and popped a slice of cheese into his mouth.

"If your read on her is right, then what exactly is the conflict between you two that she'd let you take on such a big risk for something she doesn't even care about, and you're dead set on..."

"I'm sorry, Jace, but that's between us," Vereesa murmured. "I just want to know — are you willing to help me?"

"Of course I'm willing to help you," Jace said. "But this time it really sounds like a suicide mission. Don't tell me you don't feel it. That's the Shattered Hand clan, not to mention a death knight who appears and disappears like a ghost."

"Alright, you're right — I've heard of the Shattered Hand clan too," Vereesa said. "They say they're all gladiators and killers."

She took a sip of ale and frowned.

"Why isn't it sweet at all?"

"Should it be?" Jace said, placing a slice of smoked ham in his mouth. "I'll be honest — I already have a pretty good guess about where that death knight is."

"You know the place?" Vereesa asked.

Jace said, "There's an orc stronghold in the desert east of the Burning Steppes — it might be the camp of the exiled Shattered Hand orcs. I saw this place when the mages were investigating orc hideouts."

"Because it's neither Bronzebeard dwarf territory, nor Dark Iron dwarf territory, nor the Kingdom of Stormwind's borders — but those orcs can't make it all the way through the Kingdom of Stormwind back to the Dark Portal, let alone now that the Dark Portal has already fallen to the Alliance. They're pretty much stuck surviving in the wilderness there, with nowhere else to go. But the thing is, that's a genuine orc settlement, Vereesa. You want me to go storm that place with you? That's no gnoll camp."

"Of course not," Vereesa said. "In that case, we can go scout it out first, Jace — see whether the death knight is even there. It's been a few months, after all. Anything could have happened."

"Honestly, I'm a little suspicious of you," Jace said, rubbing his forehead. "You, woman... damn it, you just charge ahead, charge ahead, charge ahead, go go go — what am I supposed to do if you lose your head and attack? Help you or not?"

Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "That's only because you're so slow, always off thinking to yourself."

"I'm hungry and exhausted — what do you expect? You've been stealing my food the whole time, and you don't even need to sleep..." Jace said. "Enough talk — at the very least, I'm not making this trip for nothing."

Vereesa picked up her half-full mug and drained it in one go, then slapped the elven blade she'd lent Jace onto the table.

"Did you like that sword? Help me finish this, and I'll give it to you. This blade is called Celardibelor — in Common, it'd be the Sunblade."

Jace stared at the exquisite short sword, swallowed, and asked, "Wait — you only give it to me after we capture the death knight?"

Vereesa said, "Take me to that place and we'll look things over. If there's really no chance of success, then I'll do what you said and find other elves for help — and the blade is yours. If there is a chance, I have other rewards for you. Everything's negotiable. And since you're worried, I give you my word here — I won't act without discussing it with you first. You know that if it were up to me, I would've just eliminated the alchemist's wife back there. But since you had other considerations, I respected your wishes, didn't I?"

Jace nodded. "Then it's a deal. Let me head back and plan the route — potions and everything else need to be properly prepared."
