## Chapter 286: Some Kind of Warning?

When he woke again it was already afternoon.

Jace sat up and saw Ursula lying in a lower chair beside him. He was covered by a thick blanket — so thick he was almost sweating — and the blanket reeked of medicinal herbs. She, meanwhile, was bundled up in her own old clothes, curled tight against the cold.

Despite his constant griping about Ursula always sending him off to die, Jace had to admit she was genuinely good to him. Thinking that, her constant goading of him into grand adventures in Stranglethorn Vale didn't irritate him quite so much anymore.

He gently draped the blanket over Ursula, gathered his things, and left the cellar.

Given that his circadian rhythm was completely wrecked, Jace figured he ought to drink something — milk, maybe — to prep himself for sleeping that night. Wasn't milk supposed to make you sleepy? Or should he come back later and ask Ursula for a proper sleeping draught? He never wanted Molorfil putting him under with hypnotism again. The demon knocked him out with the efficiency of an anesthesiologist snapping a mask over your face, and something about that just felt wrong. He was terrified that one of these days he'd go under and never wake up.

After turning it over in his head, all he could do was head to the Blue Heron for something to drink and a bite to eat. Since the previous night the only things he'd consumed were a handful of nuts and some wine down in the cellar, and his stomach was growling loud enough to hear — especially now that he was thinking about warm milk.

He pulled his robe tight and slipped over to the Blue Heron. Inside, with the ease of long practice, he ordered salted cheese, dried bread slices, and preserved fruit, along with a large mug of heated goat milk, and found himself a spot to sit.

The food had barely arrived when he spotted a familiar figure in the doorway — Amy Marin.

Jace raised a hand to wave her over, but then noticed a man about her age following close behind, speaking to her in an intimate tone. Judging by the ring on his finger, he was already a fully fledged mage.

Amy came inside, replying offhandedly as she scanned the room. When her gaze landed on Jace's corner her eyes suddenly lit up. She turned back and said a few words to the mage behind her, then waved energetically in his direction. Jace waved back, and the young woman mage hurried over.

The man said something else, stopped in his tracks, then caught sight of Jace. The expression on his face was hard to read.

Once Amy sat down she asked with a puzzled frown, "Why do you smell like an alchemy lab?"

"Just came from one." Jace glanced over in the direction the man had left. "Was that your boyfriend?"

"Go to hell," Amy said. "That guy's been flashing that crappy ring around and harassing everyone, just because he became a full mage in his twenties."

"That young and already a full mage? Impressive," Jace said.

"Impressive?" Amy frowned. "I'm not even thirty yet. I'll have mine in a few years."

"Boyfriend, marriage — all that sounds like a headache. I'll think about it when I become a full mage... no, when I make it to Dalaran and join the Kirin Tor. Mages there don't start families until their forties or fifties, nothing unusual about that. I wouldn't stand out at all."

Jace stirred the dregs at the bottom of his mug with a spoon. "Well, Aegwynn didn't get married until she was eight hundred, so she really dragged up the average marriage age for every human woman mage by a huge margin."

"You little bastard." Amy reached out and smacked Jace on the forehead.

"Though, do you actually believe Aegwynn really lived for eight hundred years?"

"That's what everyone says," Jace mumbled.

"Just because everyone says it doesn't make it true." She said it louder, glancing toward the bar. "Miss, I'll have smoked ham slices and a small glass of lemon wine."

She waved a hand, got no response, and repeated herself even louder, irritation creeping into her voice.

Jace took a sip of milk, licked his lips, and glanced up at her. "He's already gone, no need to stay mad!"

"And I'm not supposed to be mad about being harassed by that creep?" Amy shot back. "Have you never been hit on by a terrible woman?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Terrible in ways you can't imagine."

That actually got a laugh out of her. She leaned in closer. "A mage too?"

"Old witch," Jace said.

"That tracks." Amy grinned. "You've got the look of the kind of fellow an old forest witch would fixate on."

"Heh." Jace gave a dry chuckle.

"Right." Amy said. "That bastard made me forget why I came over. Master Marin got a letter from the Kirin Tor. You haven't been around much these past couple of days, and he couldn't find you, so he hasn't had a chance to tell you."

At this, Jace grumbled under his breath. He'd had a run of bad luck lately, so part of him worried the Kirin Tor side of things had gone sideways too. But judging by Amy's expression it probably wasn't anything serious — otherwise she wouldn't be talking about it so casually.

"You actually look tense," Amy said. "You're not worried the Kirin Tor masters are going to give you a hard time on purpose?"

"No," Jace said. "I'm just worried they might be suspicious."

"Not at all," Amy said. "The letter paints you quite the hero — standing shoulder to shoulder with the farstriders of Quel'Thalas against a death knight, holding the line during the black dragon flight's assault on Dalaran..."

"In any case, it matched exactly what Lady Vereesa Windrunner wrote. Speaking of which, her handwriting is stunning — it looked just like those elven arcane runes I've seen before."

"Quel'Thalas nobility probably practice calligraphy from childhood, right?" Jace glanced at the smoked ham that had just arrived. "Mind if I have a slice?"

"Go ahead." Amy nodded.

Jace stuffed the ham and cheese between the dried bread and bit off half in one go. As good as the smoked ham was, the recognition from the Kirin Tor felt even better. At last, his shadow magic troubles were finally behind him.

Amy popped one of Jace's cubes of cheese into her mouth and asked in a low voice, "Don't you want to know who signed your sponsorship letter?"

"Wayne Halister?" Jace looked up. "That fellow gave me the full interrogation before I left Dalaran. He's some kind of investigator for the Arcane Defense Division, isn't he? If my case reached the Kirin Tor, he'd be the one reviewing it."

"No," Amy said. "Archmage Kel'Thuzad — the one on the Council of Six. I'm pretty sure you met him."

The bread nearly fell out of Jace's mouth. He clamped down and swallowed it in a panic.

"Scared you?"

The woman mage said it like a joke, but Jace felt a cold wind on the back of his neck.

What was Kel'Thuzad playing at? Was this a warning? Doleing out a little favor to keep him from meddling further?

Amy seemed to read his thoughts. "Don't get too worked up. He's one of the leading researchers into orcish dark magic — it makes sense he'd sign off on something like this. And he's met you before."

Jace managed a thin laugh. "So that's it. I thought maybe he'd taken a liking to me at that council meeting."

Amy took a sip of her drink and said softly, "Well, that's not entirely out of the question either."
