## Chapter 287: A Sense of Crisis

Whatever Kel'Thuzad's intentions, the Kirin Tor made no further moves during this period and sent no further notices.

After that, Jace attended his classes and rested as normal, and neither Andormas nor his master Marin brought up anything about the Kirin Tor.

Though he gradually set his mind at ease, he still couldn't rule Kel'Thuzad out of all this.

After all, the fact that Teron Gorefiend knew his name was just too bizarre. Other than Kel'Thuzad, who else had both the means and the motive to tip off a death knight about his name?

Maybe it really was some kind of reminder — now that everything had settled, Kel'Thuzad wanted Jace to know he still had influence over the Wizard Sanctum, telling him not to get any wild ideas, not to make trouble, to just keep his head down and get on with things.

But compared to Kel'Thuzad's murky suspicions from across Baradin Bay, Jace was actually more concerned about Gajin's situation.

He didn't know how Erland had disposed of that succubus's corpse, but word was that during the orc invasion of Stormwind he'd spent years hiding in the forest with a band of refugees. Chances were they'd grown accustomed to the demons that orc warlocks summoned and had their own ways of dealing with them.

That day, after class, Jace had just stepped out of the Wizard Sanctum when he spotted Vereesa sitting in the garden looking his way. When she saw him she waved.

Jace walked quickly into the garden and asked, "How did it go? You're not hurt or anything, right? Everything go smoothly?"

"Almost, but no," Vereesa said. "As for results, I gathered some things that I think should be fadeleaf... both the smell and the appearance match. I left them at the apartment."

"You work fast. Not even two weeks, wasn't it? How was Stranglethorn Vale?" Jace asked.

Vereesa pinched the tip of her ear and said, "An unknown thorny plant ruined my cloak. Otherwise it was fine — no particular trouble. There really are a lot of crocodiles in the swamps, but they were all quite reasonable."

Jace nodded, then suddenly felt something was off. "Wait — quite reasonable? Crocodiles?"

"Farstriders learn to communicate with animals," Vereesa said. "Panthers, deer, and so on. Crocodiles too, of course."

She looked at Jace's bewildered expression. "I thought it was incredible the first time I heard about it too. Of course, you can't have a conversation like with a person, but you can signal that you mean no harm, warn them that if it came to a fight they definitely wouldn't win — simple signals like that."

"Is that so simple...?" Jace asked, baffled.

They chatted about the Stranglethorn Vale encounter as they walked back to the apartment. The moment they stepped through the door Jace caught a whiff of damp earth.

Seeming to notice Jace's reaction, Vereesa said quickly, "Sorry — I haven't had time to wash the clothes I mussed up in the swamp. I only got back to Stormwind this morning, and I was in a hurry to bring you what I'd gathered."

Jace said, "If you ever stopped by my apartment, you'd know this is nothing."

Vereesa smiled. "Right — I don't even know where you live. I can only ever find you at the Wizard Sanctum."

"By the canal," Jace said. "I'll tell you once I have a proper, permanent place. The current one I could be moved out of any time."

Saying you lived by the canal in Stormwind was basically the same as saying nothing at all.

"All right," Vereesa said, and brought out the bundle of herbs she'd been carrying. Jace opened it and saw it was indeed full of withered, grass-like plants flecked with dry clods of dirt.

"You gathered a lot," Jace said, rifling through it. "I'll take these to my friend and have her take a look, see how many potions we can make."

"So, when can we set out?" Vereesa asked.

"It'll probably take some time to brew the potions. I also need to ask my master for leave. How about the day after tomorrow? Get a good night's sleep — my guess is you ran back here without sleeping for days, didn't you?"

"I was worried the death knight would leave that place," Vereesa said with a shaky breath. "The closer I get to that thing, the more on edge I become."

"No need to rush," Jace said. "He doesn't have many places left to go. Since he hasn't gone looking for the Blackrock orcs, I imagine he's got some kind of problem with them. After all, plenty in the Horde consider Gul'dan and his death knights to be traitors."

"I know, Jace," Vereesa said.

Jace nodded. "Then I'll take these herbs to the alchemist."

"Oh — I need a new cloak," Vereesa said. "The places we're going are either desert or wasteland, right? Redridge, and the Burning Steppes. I know the terrain and the environment there — there doesn't seem to be much greenery or plant life. My cloak stands out way too much, and it's torn besides."

"Yours looks much better. If you have a cloak in a similar color, could you lend me one?"

"Do you think I look like someone with two cloaks?" Jace said. "Just buy one. There are two clothing shops in the Mage Quarter, very close."

"Um..." the elf hesitated.

"You really don't want to exchange a few more words with our kind... What difference does it make from me? Fine — let's go together. I was planning to buy a new shirt anyway, and I can turn the old torn one into a scarf while I'm at it."

"A scarf?"

"Last time I went up north of Redridge," Jace said. "Dust and cinders everywhere — one wind and you're filthy. It's a completely different world from the south. I only went for one night that time. This time we might be up there for a long while, maybe even further north. I need something to cover my nose and mouth, or I'm afraid my lungs will be ruined by the time we get back. You?"

"I have my own, though I've never used it since coming here," Vereesa said. "Then it's a good thing we're going together."

That afternoon, Jace brought Vereesa to Larson's shop. The shopkeeper saw Jace and said, "Back again, Mr. Sesso? Mending clothes this time, or buying new?"

"A cloak, sir," Jace said. "For this lady elf."

Vereesa nodded. The shopkeeper glanced at the elf and showed a flicker of surprise. "It's been a long time since a real elf last came into my shop to buy anything. Any specific requirements for the cloak?"

"We need a cloak — gray or dark brown, with a hood, and made of fire-resistant material. We may need to make a trip north of Redridge," Jace said.

The shopkeeper went into the back. Jace asked, "Aren't you going to mend that green one here?"

"No," Vereesa said flatly.

Jace sighed lightly, resigned.

The shopkeeper came back with two — one pale gray, one dark brown. Vereesa put on the pale gray and felt it looked strange, then tried the dark brown.

Jace picked out a linen shirt for himself and glanced over in her direction. The elf stood a short distance away, tugging at the collar and turning this way and that. Her already slender figure looked even more willowy set against the broad cloak.

Jace's earlier impression had been right — Vereesa hadn't been wearing tall boots this time, and her real height did seem to be a shade taller than his. A rough estimate put her at about a hundred seventy-three to seventy-five centimeters.

He needed to have a sense of crisis. The false impression Grede had given him had seriously distorted his self-perception.

If he didn't start eating more meat, in a few years he'd stop growing altogether.

"This one fits quite well," Vereesa said.

"Thirty-one silver total, sir, including those two shirts."

Those words yanked Jace out of his ruminations about his parents' heights.

"Right... fine. That'll do."
