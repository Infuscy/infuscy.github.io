Chapter 288: Grounds for Leave

In the morning Jace went to Marin to ask for leave. Marin saw Jace and spoke first: "Where have you been these past few days, Mr. Sesso?"

"Did you need something, Master?"

"Nothing urgent, exactly." Marin glanced at the bookshelf beside him. "The thing is, ever since you got back from Dalaran, Amy's been too lazy to come help me tidy up. As a hired assistant, you can't just slack off for so many days in a row, can you?"

"That's not true," Jace said. "A few days ago, before class, I came in the morning and cleaned, but I left before you arrived."

"Is it my imagination? It always feels like something isn't quite clean." Marin set down the quill in his hand. "Right — I should let you know. The investigation into the necromancy incident at Grandshire has produced results. That matter you had me bring up with Archmage Dumas last time — that Mr. Thomas Waller didn't offer any defense at all. He admitted to the necromancy right away."

"The thing about wanting to resurrect his wife?" Jace asked.

"Yes."

"Nothing else?"

"The mages are still investigating."

Jace thought to himself that Waller clearly understood that getting tangled up with a death knight meant more than just necromancy — it could even implicate treason. At that point this would be beyond the Wizard Sanctum's authority to handle.

Marin said, "He and his wife, Mrs. Elizabeth Waller, had been a model couple at Raven Hill for many years. Mr. Waller had once studied alchemy courses in Dalaran."

Jace asked in surprise, "He's a Dalaran alchemist? Then why did he come here?"

Marin continued: "In the period after the Gurubashi Wars, he came to Southshire to survey the effects of troll sorcery on the farmland and plant life. That was where he met his wife. After the two fell in love, he decided to stay at Raven Hill and help the local residents free themselves from the influence of Gurubashi dark magic. Within a few years he'd become quite a well-known doctor in the south. Oh — and he's a man of Alterac."

A geography-and-botany expert from Dalaran?

So this was how the fellow had managed, from a pile of texts that even demons couldn't read, to independently research necromancy and abomination constructs that plagued Duskwood.

That death knight had been right about one thing, at least — Mr. Waller really was a genius.

"I couldn't detect any trace of an Alterac accent from the way he speaks," Jace said.

"He's spent far too long in the south," Marin said. "And it's said he's fluent in quite a few languages, including elvish and Dwarvish, even the troll tongue. Perhaps learning all those other languages has worn down his own accent quite a bit."

Jace asked, "So how are they going to handle him? Has Archmage Dumas made a decision?"

"Still discussing it," Marin said. "After all, his contributions can't be ignored. As for motive... his feelings for his wife — plenty of people at Raven Hill can vouch for that. You could say love clouded his judgment."

"Archmage Dumas's thinking is to keep him under house arrest for a time, under surveillance for now, while investigating the several locations connected to him — including the two residences he previously occupied at Raven Hill and Grandshire. I'm inclined to agree with his view, but I don't have much say in this matter."

"You did well, Mr. Sesso."

Jace nodded. Duskwood's future should be a little sunnier than in that other timeline, at least.

It was then he realized he'd nearly forgotten the reason he'd come.

"Master, I'm actually here to ask for leave. I may need to leave Stormwind for a week or two."

"Where are you going?"

"I'd like to... take some leave."

"What's wrong?"

Jace said seriously, "There's been a lot going on lately — the mess with the shadow magic, and that business with the dragonkin. Especially the shadow magic — it's put a lot of pressure on me. My spirits have been low this whole time, so I'd like to go somewhere to clear my head. Maybe head to Lakeshire, breathe some fresh air, change of scenery."

"That's understandable," Marin said. "Go ahead, just be safe and don't stay away too long. Though the war is over, and the gnoll trouble in Redridge has eased a lot compared to a few months ago, it's by no means a peaceful place. That said, since you're going to Redridge..."

"Is there a task at Lakeshire?" Jace asked.

"Not exactly a task," Marin said. "Didn't you mention last time that the dragonkin might already be planning to move into the Redridge area? Last time Master Andormas and I were speaking with those who serve the king about this matter, we learned they'd investigated related leads as well — except they thought it was just some baffling Black Dragon cultists operating around Lakeshire and the logging camp, and hadn't realized there were dragonkin."

"Black Dragon cultists?" Jace said. "You're certain there are dragon worshippers? Right at Lakeshire?"

"Frankly," Marin said, "our cooperation with the crown on this front isn't all that close — it came up only in passing, in casual conversation. Judging by the way those investigators talked, they didn't seem to take these dragon worshippers seriously either, which is why they brought it up so casually."

"So my thought is, if you get to Lakeshire, you could keep an eye out for any suspicious individuals..."

"I understand, Master. I'll keep watch," Jace said.

"Of course, rest is the most important thing. Don't let hunting down a bunch of fanatics interfere with your proper recovery," Marin said. "And I'm only reminding you — telling you that Lakeshire isn't as calm as it appears on the surface."

That was certainly true. Being able to run into town and coax woodcutters into heading north of the mountains to hunt for treasure, and even contact dragonkin to carry out mental domination — that went beyond covert activity and straight into doing whatever they pleased.

Marin went on: "I do think you need some time off, though you mentioned before that you wanted to work hard and test into apprentice-mage standing, so I didn't bring it up. I'll say this, Mr. Sesso — you're only eighteen. Though on the older side for an apprentice, you have a long life ahead. Don't push so hard that you bring trouble on yourself."

"Thank you, Master. I'll remember that," Jace said.

Marin imagined Jace would be staying in Redridge for a week or two, which is why he expected him to keep an eye on the dragon worshippers along the way — but Jace was actually only staying one night to rest, so there wasn't much he could do.

Still, Marin's words had reminded him of something: this trip couldn't focus solely on dealing with the orcs. The dragonkin needed watching too, since their claws had already reached the vicinity of Lakeshire — the chances of encountering them further out were even higher.

He'd need to prepare not just Shadow Protection Potions, but fire-protection supplies as well, since Vereesa didn't have nearly as many tricks for countering fire as he did.
