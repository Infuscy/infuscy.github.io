## Chapter 289: The Demon Trade

The Slaughtered Lamb had returned to its usual self — except for Zardeth.

He hadn't shown up again since that gathering. According to Surena, he'd already set off for Ironforge and might disappear for a month or two.

The small room off the practice chamber had quieted down for a few days after Gakin's death, but recently strange noises had started up again.

Jace walked over with the herb bundle and opened the door to find four black-and-white legs trembling up and down on the bed, and Surena seemingly biting and swallowing something.

"Erlan, there's something I want to ask you."

Hearing an outsider's voice, Surena scrambled to the edge of the bed in fright, wrapping the blanket tight and looking over. When she saw Jace she let out an "Eek!" and said, "Can't you knock?"

"I don't care what you two are doing. I'll finish my business in a minute and you can carry on," Jace said.

Surena brushed her hair back and leaned against the side, not bothering to cover herself with anything. Erlan ran over and pulled on a pair of trousers. "Goddamn it, Jace — if you'd come a few minutes later I'd have been finished."

"Consider it a favor — helping you last longer," Jace said.

Erlan shot Jace a sidelong look and curled his mouth. "I think I'm seeing you in a new light."

The two of them came to the central hall. In the rather chilly cellar, Erlan sat bare-chested in a chair leaning against the cold wall — gotta hand it to these fellows, their physical toughness was no joke.

"So, what is it?" Erlan asked.

Jace said, "I want to know how you disposed of that demon's corpse."

"The one you stabbed to death?" Erlan asked.

"Yes."

"Sold it."

"Sold it? To who?"

"Don't look so surprised," Erlan said. "I didn't haul it to a farmers' market. You don't think our little outfit is the only one in the whole Kingdom of Stormwind that deals with demons, do you?"

Jace nodded. "I do know there are some other warlocks."

"Not just warlocks. Down south in Darkshire I know there's a group," Erlan said. "And Mor'zul — you know Mor'zul, right? Gakin should have mentioned him, you two were that close. Then over west in Moonbrook I know a few people. Some of those folks even deal with dwarves — selling parts from demons' bodies for coin, or for alchemy experiments, but they don't care much about shadow magic itself."

"How much did you get?" Jace asked.

Erlan waved a hand. "Not much. A dozen silver or so. By the time I found a buyer, only the horns were left. The demon heart is the most valuable part — without the heart, the rest rots away fast. I just wanted to get rid of the corpse quickly. You're not here to demand a cut, are you? But fair enough — you did kill it."

He pulled six silver coins from his pocket. "Right — half should go to you."

Jace glanced at the six silver, then at Erlan, and reached out to take it. "Now that's more like it."

"You bastard." Erlan laughed. "Back when the orcs had just left, over in Redridge and further east, there were plenty of demon corpses. Never mind succubi — word was they found a creature even taller than an ogre. You think the other side of the Dark Portal is just crawling with these things?"

Jace said, "I wouldn't know. You thinking of going to see?"

"Hah — I'm not tired of living yet," Erlan said.

"Right," Jace asked. "If you got skilled at summoning, and summoned one and killed one each time, couldn't you just keep selling them for money?"

"You're not the first to think of that," Erlan said with a faintly mocking smile. "And someone's already tried it — didn't end well. The dwarves of Shadowforge City say performing summoning rituals too many times in a short span rots your brain. One of them didn't believe it; by the time he died his eyes had leaked into purple fluid. I don't know if it's true, but even that lunatic Gakin didn't dare summon several imps in a single day."

"You mages have a saying, don't you? Don't fuck around with the Twisting Nether — I reckon that's what it means."

Jace said, "There is indeed such a saying."

"That all?" Erlan asked.

"That's it."

"Don't forget to teach me a trick or two someday, Jace — for those few silver's sake." Erlan stood up. "Work from the Stonemasons is getting scarcer by the day. I need to find ways to learn more skills to keep myself alive — at least until payday, you know?"

With that, he hitched up his trousers and jogged off down the passage.

Jace sighed as he watched his shadow disappear, then walked down the opposite passage to Ursula's side.

Ursula said without looking up from her book, "I was wondering when you'd finally join the two of them."

"Joined what?" Jace asked — then caught on immediately. "Are they that open?"

"Erlan's said to have several others on the outside," Ursula said. "Surena probably doesn't care either way."

The alchemist looked up, puffed out her chest, and drew a breath. "So what is it this time?"

"Fadeleaf." Jace handed the bundle to Ursula. "See how much of this is useless weeds, or other herbs mixed in."

"You bought herbs and they still mixed in weeds?" Ursula said with a mocking laugh.

"They were cheap," Jace said, sitting down to the side.

Ursula picked through them for a good while, then finally straightened up and caught her breath. "There aren't any weeds or anything."

"Hmm?" Jace looked inside.

"It's all Fadeleaf," Ursula said. "There's also a stalk of Liferoot in here... this bundle is worth two gold at the low end, maybe even three. Jace, are you sure you didn't grab the wrong bag and bring out your secret savings?"

Jace hesitated a moment. "I didn't spend that little. That just means the herbalist I found is highly skilled!"

Ursula gave him a look. "What about the Liferoot — are you taking it back, or what?"

"Liferoot can be used to make a healing potion, can't it?" Jace asked.

"Two recipes," Ursula said. "One is Mighty Troll's Blood Potion. Besides Liferoot, the other materials are all cheap — I can cover them for you. This potion gives you troll-like regenerative abilities for a long stretch of time... of course, that's what the novel-writers say. I think the actual effect is far from as dramatic as a real troll's, but the results are visible to the naked eye."

"The other is a Greater Healing Potion — it can stabilize the injuries of someone who's been badly wounded."

"The second one, then," Jace said. "Do you have a Fire Protection Potion here?"

Ursula said, "No. I've made one, but never drank it myself. I imagine it works like a mage's fire-protection barrier? Honestly, I think an elixir that boosts elemental resistance — an anti-magic potion — should do part of the job."

Though Jace couldn't remember most potion recipes, he recalled exactly how to brew both Fire Protection Potions — after all, they'd been high-demand potions back when he used to play the game.

The lesser version required flame sacs; the greater version required Elemental Fire.

Flame sacs were harvested from fire-breathing beasts and dragons. Having been in this world for so long, Jace had yet to see a single ordinary beast that could breathe fire, and dragons were even rarer — the material was probably far more expensive than he imagined.

As for Elemental Fire... the difficulty of obtaining Elemental Earth had already been made clear to him back at the alchemy lab. Materials of that sort were beyond his ability to even picture — what they looked like, how they were stored.

"Fine, then," Jace said. "Let's leave it at that."

"Come pick up the potions tomorrow morning, Jace," Ursula said. "Invisibility Potion and anti-magic potion — I'll make as many as I can."
