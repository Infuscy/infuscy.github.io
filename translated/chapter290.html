Chapter 290: Eastvale

On the agreed day, Jace shouldered his pack, slung two staves across his back, and waited outside the Gilded Rose.

Vereesa was clearly carrying a good deal too — her small bag was bulging, and the quiver on her back held far more arrows than last time.

"How many arrows did you bring?"

"Twenty-seven. I've rarely brought this many out — it's a bit heavy."

She sat on the steps nearby, watching the crowds ebb and flow through the Trade District. "So you've made a plan? How are we getting to that wasteland? I looked at a map, but I couldn't find anything particularly detailed — I only know the general direction, and I'm completely in the dark about what exactly lies along the way. You said you've been there before, right?"

"I've been north of Redridge, but I never went into that wasteland," Jace said. "We'll find a carriage first — heading to Darkshire or Lakeshire, either works. We rest one night at the Eastvale waystation, then set out for Lakeshire the next day. We'll catch our breath, eat something, and stock up on supplies. After that, we climb the hills heading northeast."

"Through the hills?" Vereesa asked.

Jace glanced at a priest in yellow robes walking past and said, "The plains north of Redridge were still a battlefield a few years ago. There are still plenty of corpses lying there that nobody dares collect — precisely because it's so dangerous. Even now, orcs and Dark Iron dwarves are still fighting it out there. The hills are empty, and it's easy to avoid danger. It's a longer route, but safer."

"You know the way — I'll follow your lead." Vereesa glanced at the pack Jace was carrying. "What did you bring?"

Jace opened his large pack and took out the warlock's satchel containing Sazeno first, setting it on the ground.

He'd turned it over at home again and again and felt he should bring the imp along, to use as a last-resort trump card. After all, an extra helper was an extra helper — whether for kindling fires or the Blood Pact, both were useful.

Molorfil was out. That demon was powerful, true, but having it at his side always gave Jace the hallucination of catching some strange scent. Having it trail along behind Vereesa the whole way would give him more anxiety than he could handle.

"Seven Invisibility Potions — I'd planned to make ten, but the materials didn't quite add up in the end. Three anti-magic potions for the two of us to ration. Six Shadow Protection Potions, basic grade — enough to weather one Curse of Agony or two Shadow Bolts, I'd estimate. Two Lesser Mana Potions. Two Healing Potions, for treating wounds."

"One Antidote — though I don't know if it can guard against Necrotic Poison. The alchemist told me it's not very effective, so we'll keep it ready for scorpion venom instead. One Strong Healing Potion — if I'm about to die, remember to make me drink this one."

"Then bandages — mageweave bandages, enchanted. I got them from the Mage Quarter. These two Lesser Elixirs of Agility are for you. Drink them if you think you should, but don't drink them randomly — I hear you get dizzy when the effect wears off. And this gray potion whose name I don't know — don't you drink it. It's absolutely vile. That one's for me." After counting everything he stood up and stretched. "All right, Lady Vereesa — tuck a few bottles into your clothes. These potions are too heavy."

Vereesa looked a little glassy-eyed by now. She counted out four Invisibility Potions, one anti-magic, three Shadow Protection, one Healing Potion, and all the Agility Elixirs, and packed them into her own bag.

When she spotted the Antidote she looked up. "Scorpions? What scorpions?"

"A species of venomous scorpion that lives north of Redridge," Jace said. "Extremely dangerous. This Antidote can treat its venom. I'm suddenly curious — if we run into scorpions, could you talk to them, ask them to spare us? Does that animal-communication trick work on insects too?"

Vereesa stared at Jace for a moment. "Are you serious?"

"Sorry, I don't really know either... pure curiosity. When the time comes I'll just set them on fire."

Jace glanced at the fully loaded quiver behind her and said, "I also brought some sweet spices, salt, and the like. If you get hungry along the way we can roast meat — you'll be the one hunting, then."

"That sounds nice," Vereesa said. "Do you know how to cook?"

Jace cleared his throat. "Well enough. I've roasted meat before."

Vereesa narrowed her eyes. "How many times?"

"Once. And it wasn't cooked through," Jace confessed.

Vereesa couldn't help but laugh. "I'm about the same. Then we'll just have to learn from each other."

"Right. We improve together."

As he said it Jace straightened up and looked down the road at a soldier riding past on horseback, thinking about whether he'd forgotten anything.

"Dragonkin," he muttered. "We need to watch out for dragonkin."

"Red or black?" Vereesa asked.

"Black." Jace pointed to the small bag where she'd stowed the anti-magic potion. "If we really run into dragonkin, drink that first whether they've spotted us or not. It lasts a long time."

Vereesa's expression grew serious. She said quietly, "I understand."

The two of them took a carriage bound for Lakeshire. By the time they arrived at the Eastvale waystation it was already evening.

They found an abandoned tent near the camp — no idea who'd left it — swept it out a bit, and made it their resting place for the night.

Late at night, with the elf doing whatever she was doing outside, Jace lay curled up in the tent thinking about the days ahead. But he couldn't concentrate, because the nights out here in the wilds were so cold that even with every piece of clothing and the cloak he'd brought piled on top of him, he still couldn't get warm.

He was just about to force himself to sleep when, before long, he faintly heard a thin sound drift in.

The pitch gradually rose, little by little becoming clear, until he could start to make out the notes within it.

What instrument was that? She'd brought an instrument too? Curious, Jace crept up to the tent opening and stole a look outside. In the moonlight, Vereesa sat gazing down the road that led into the dim distance, a leaf with yellowed edges held in her hand, playing an unknown melody.

Its rhythm was slow and drawn out, the style much like that Song of the Highborne, yet the melody was entirely different.

He eyed the leaf in the elf's hand, watching the shape of her mouth and the movement of her hands, pondering how she coaxed such a lilting sound out of it.

If he tried, at best it would come out sounding like a fart. Even Grede could only manage a sharp whistle — after all, the limited free time he'd had in his past life had all been spent practicing skipping stones.

Just then Vereesa noticed him. Her gaze shifted this way, but she didn't stop playing.

Jace was struck still.

He seemed to understand now why so many heroes would sacrifice everything they had, even their lives, to protect this world — why, even before the gates of the Legion, facing a being like Sargeras who could shake the Twisting Nether itself, they still did not flinch.

This was what they were protecting — the forest before their eyes, the soldiers listening quietly in the distance, the cold night wind brushing through the branches, all of this beautiful and heartbreaking.
