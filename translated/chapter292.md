## Chapter 292: Travel Hardships

Despite having studied plenty of maps in advance and done extensive route planning, the terrain east of the Redridge Mountains turned out to be far more complicated than expected.

The two of them had followed the ridgeline for three days and two nights. By the time evening came, Jace reached a cliff and gazed out at the vast, unbroken forest below — split by several great rivers, stretching all the way to the edge of his vision.

What forest was this… the Swamp of Sorrows?

No, this wasn't a swamp. The forest near Stonewatch?

No, that wasn't right either. Stonewatch, the vantage point overlooking the Everstill Vale, had completely vanished from sight yesterday, and today there wasn't even a shadow of it to be seen.

After flipping back and forth across several map annotations, Jace finally realized this was a primeval forest of enormous size. After being defeated by the Kingdom of Stormwind, many gnolls had fled into this place — the game had no record of it at all.

According to the map, if they entered this forest and continued southeast, in about two weeks they'd reach the Swamp of Sorrows.

Once he'd gotten his bearings, Jace confirmed that where they stood now was the narrowest part of the Burning Steppes — a place the dwarves called the Cinder Vale.

He looked to the northwest, where volcanic haze and ashen earth still hung thick in the air, then turned to the elf beside him. "From here, we should head north."

Vereesa hadn't slept for two full nights. When she looked where Jace was pointing, he could tell her spirits were far lower than when they'd set out from Lakeshire.

"Tell you what — you sleep first, I'll keep watch. Because from here all the way to Blackrock Mountain, we probably won't get another proper rest."

"Then I'll leave it to you, Jace."

With that, Vereesa walked over to a wind-sheltered spot behind a rock, sat down, wrapped her cloak tight, and leaned against the stone. Her eyes slowly closed, and almost before Jace could finish watching her, she'd fallen asleep right there.

The thought that this elf in front of him would easily live another two thousand years sat a little heavy on his mind as he settled onto a nearby rock.

The reason humans in Stormwind discriminated against and ostracized half-elves wasn't just that they often stole and caused trouble from a lack of discipline — it was also that while half-elves were few in number, most could easily live to nearly two hundred years, far beyond the average human lifespan. Some said the longest-lived half-elves could reach three hundred.

In short, it was hard for people to accept these annoying creatures who weren't all so different from themselves but were impossibly long-lived, hanging around all the time.

And because the elves of Quel'Thalas lived so far away and had so little contact, there was nothing to be jealous of — which meant considerably less hostility.

But actually getting to know a living, breathing elf up close, that thousand-year lifespan really did weigh on Jace as a human.

He shook off the idle thoughts, pulled out his maps, and by the afterglow left before the sun had fully set, continued comparing the terrain to the north.

Fortunately, to get from the Burning Steppes into the Searing Gorge here, they didn't have to cut through the Dark Iron dwarf city inside Blackrock Mountain and then into the Cauldron — they could go straight over the mountains. Those peaks weren't any harder to climb than the Redridge range they'd already crossed.

If they didn't take this route, their only option was to go all the way to Menethil Harbor, circle around through the Wetlands, and cross Dun Morogh — which was practically traversing the entire length of Khaz Modan.

Once they crossed the lower section of the Blackrock Mountains to the north, they'd reach the Searing Gorge. After that, it would be a matter of finding the Shattered Hand clan. With Vereesa's skill at hunting orcs, combined with Jace's general knowledge of where the Kargath outpost was located, he figured it shouldn't be all that difficult.

Though compared to the game, this "Searing Gorge" on the map was absurdly huge, the Shattered Hand orcs wouldn't camp in the dead middle of nowhere with no village in sight, would they? They'd at least settle near water, or against a mountainside.

Jace scoured the various maps for rivers near the Searing Gorge. Plenty lay to the north, close to Bronzebeard dwarf territory, but the Shattered Hand forces wouldn't go that far out of their way to provoke the Bronzebeard.

Two rivers lay near the Dun Morogh mountains to the west of the map — quite possibly the right area. But there was also a Dark Iron dwarf fortress there, marked on one of the dwarven-drawn maps. The dwarves hadn't named it, but Jace guessed it must be the canonical fortress from the game — the Bastion of Agony.

Relying on his memory of the game map, he figured: if the Bastion of Agony was here, then a short distance west should be the location of Kargath Outpost. Though the game map and reality differed wildly in scale, the relative positions of key landmarks were more or less the same.

So their route from here was: head north from here across the Cinder Vale toward Blackrock Mountain, then follow the mountain range northwest, skirting the edge of the Searing Gorge toward Dun Morogh.

By his reckoning, it might take another week to reach the Kargath Outpost area.

Jace scratched his neck. This trip might stretch to three weeks, even a full month.

Would Master Marin blow him to bits with Arcane Missiles when he got back?

But aside from this route, there really wasn't a faster option — cutting straight across the battlefield was tantamount to suicide.

Originally, by his thinking, they should have gone a little further east, threading through the forest between the woodland and the edges of this cinder plain. The environment would've been better, finding food easier, and they wouldn't have had to worry so much about Dark Iron dwarves, Blackrock orcs, or — worse — dragonkin.

The only real threat would've been gnolls in the forest, but gnolls wouldn't have much reason to leave the forest, cross the river, and come looking for trouble over here.

But looking at it from here, that route would cost them two more days. At this rate, they might not even make it back in time for the apprentice mage examination.

By the deep of night, around two or three in the morning, Jace had grown so drowsy he was starting to lose focus, yawning between counts of their remaining food.

Vereesa had been eating very little these past few days — probably because that huge meal back in Lakeshire was still working its way through her. At any rate, at this pace, the remaining food should last them until they reached the Kargath area.

But for the return trip, if they didn't find any villages or dwarven settlements, they'd have to rely on hunting.

That was when he pulled out a healing potion, and a strange thought crept into his mind.

If he was starving, would drinking this hold him over a little?

"Jace, you get some sleep." Vereesa's voice came from behind him suddenly.

The silence of the mountaintop had lulled Jace so completely that the sound snapped him out of his daze.

"You're up? How was your sleep?"

"Fine." Vereesa stood up, stretched. "I'll take the second watch. You rest properly."

Jace nodded. The moment Vereesa said that, his eyes could barely stay open.

He crawled over to where the elf had been sleeping, dropped to the ground, and was out cold almost instantly.
