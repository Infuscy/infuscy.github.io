## Chapter 293: The Telltale Sign

In the haze of half-sleep, a savory aroma drifted over.

Jace opened his eyes to find a whole pool of saliva on the ground. He sat bolt upright and wiped the corner of his mouth.

Vereesa was standing on a rock some distance off, gazing into the distance. Beneath a massive boulder nearby, she'd already set up a wooden frame strung with what looked like a cooked small animal of some kind.

The wind had been blowing the other way, so the smoke and charred smell hadn't reached them. Only a sudden shift in the wind had carried the scent over to Jace.

Vereesa must have heard something, because she turned back around.

"Come try it, Jace."

"What is this?"

"A little brown-furred fox. Animals are pretty scarce up in the mountains." Vereesa walked over as she spoke.

Jace licked his lips, carved off a slice of meat with his dagger, and popped it into his mouth. After a straight diet of dried mushrooms and going days without much meat — not daring to touch the dried meat strips either — suddenly eating hot, freshly cooked meat was indescribable.

She'd even added salt and a bit of spice, which mostly masked the gamey, rank smell of the predator's flesh — though that was part of its flavor too, in a way. Jace was so moved he nearly cried.

"I thought you said you couldn't roast meat?" Jace couldn't help but ask.

Vereesa said, "The other rangers in the Farstriders used to secretly laugh at me behind my back, saying I couldn't cook at all. So… this is just barely edible given the limited conditions. You've spent so much money on this trip — I felt I should make something to make up for it."

"You're too kind." Jace licked the corner of his mouth. "This roasted fox is incredible, I mean it — no flattery whatsoever."

He ate plenty more. Vereesa watched him wolf it down with a faint hint of satisfaction on her face.

"As long as you like it." She stood up as she said it.

"Aren't you eating?" Jace asked.

"I already did." Vereesa glanced toward a nearby dirt pit.

Jace looked over and saw a huge mess of scattered bone fragments.

"What's that?"

"Rabbit," she said. "I caught a rabbit too."

"How'd you manage to catch so many animals so fast — and ones that run so quick, like rabbits and foxes?" Jace said half-jokingly. "You didn't use your gift for talking to animals to lure them to you, did you, and then roasted them?"

"What else would I do?" Vereesa said, chin lifted slightly. "I've got plenty of arrows with me, but I can't just waste them. If we run into a real handful, I don't want to be caught shooting and realize I'm out of arrows."

Jace laughed. "You're really something…"

Once they'd eaten their fill, they carved away the inedible parts, wrapped the rest in large leaves, and set off again. The leftovers would cover roughly a full day's meals.

On the way down the mountain, the air grew worse and worse. Both of them wrapped their faces in cloth and scarves, adding their hoods, leaving only their eyes exposed.

By the time they reached the foot of the mountain, the temperature had risen considerably. The clothing that had felt a little cold up on the mountain was already starting to feel stifling down here.

But the windborne grit carrying a charred, acrid smell kept Jace from daring to change — he could only grit his teeth and push north.

This looked like part of that same primeval forest where the gnolls lived. Except here, the trees had all been incinerated in Blackrock Mountain's great explosion. The few that still stood had long since been toppled by centuries of sandstorms and buried.

As far as the eye could see, only a handful of enormously thick, ancient trees remained — standing alone like the withered corpses of dead demons.

There was nothing here. No animals, no plants, no living things, no sign anyone had ever lived here. Only those dead, withered trees, and the sand and dust kicked up by the wind that swept through from time to time.

The two of them walked who knows how long across this wasteland of cinder and ash. Jace didn't even want to look at his pocket watch — only watching the hazy sun drift across the sky overhead until it slipped behind the mountain, and the sky slowly darkened. They walked all the way into the night, until they found a gully in a stretch of hills where the sandstorms raged less fiercely, and stopped to rest in a hollow that offered some shelter from the wind.

There were even a few stubborn plants still clinging to life here, which at least made Jace feel he was still on Azeroth and hadn't been teleported to some alien world.

They walked the whole way without speaking — after all, they couldn't hear each other's voices anyway.

When Vereesa pulled down her hood and face wrap, the skin around her eyes had gone a dark gray — she looked like a blue-eyed demon hunter.

Jace knew he couldn't look any better. More like a Defias bandit in a mask, probably.

"Do the Dark Iron dwarves really live in a place like this?" Vereesa said. "Feels completely unfit for human habitation."

"They live underground. Haven't you heard of Shadowforge City?" Jace said as he unwrapped the leaf bundle.

"Didn't they used to live on the surface too?" Vereesa said. "I know the history of the War of the Three Hammers. I heard the Dark Iron clan built a city on this plain."

"That was before the Blackrock Mountain explosion. The city was called Thandol Span — if it were still here, it'd be…" Jace glanced outside. "I don't know where exactly. Anyway, after the explosion, that city became uninhabitable."

As he spoke, he tossed her a front leg and tore into a bone himself.

Once his stomach was full, Vereesa began coating the heads of her arrows with a pale purple potion.

Jace asked, "What's that?"

"Arcane," Vereesa said. "I need to prepare for dragonkin. I don't feel safe around here."

Jace nodded. "We should definitely be ready for trouble. This is basically Blackrock Mountain already."

After a brief rest, the two of them had no choice but to press on.

There was no way to rest properly among these gullies — not just because the conditions were harsh, but because orcs or Dark Iron dwarves could show up at any time. Better to get back up the mountain and find a more sheltered spot to hide.

They walked through another entire night. By the time the sky was just barely brightening, they finally saw the grim shape of Blackrock Mountain.

This was part of Blackrock Mountain, but it wasn't pitch-black like in the game — it looked closer to the Redridge Mountains they'd left behind, since they were already quite far from the heart of that catastrophe.

The two of them climbed up along a naturally formed ridge. Jace coughed twice, the face wrap pulled tight, and glanced back. The Redridge Mountains they'd come from were already out of sight.

Suddenly, he felt a hand clamp over his mouth and yank him to the side, dragging him behind a black boulder.

Jace knew it was Vereesa, so he barely panicked.

He slid to the ground, straightened his hood, and looked to the elf pressed close against the side of the rock. She made a "shh" gesture, slowly circled around, and peered past the rock in one direction, then pointed to a spot outside the rock.

Seeing her movements, Jace crept over too, craned his neck, and stole a glance. In the distance, at a lower elevation he could barely make out, what looked like a few humanoid figures seemed to be moving.

Those figures were clearly tall and powerfully built — definitely not dwarves. Judging by their gait and bulk, not human either.

"Four orcs?" Jace whispered.

Vereesa made a noise of assent, gripping the bow on her back. She whispered, "Hide here a while and wait for them to pass — or kill them?"

"No point drawing attention," Jace said, leaning close. "There are too many orcs around here. Killing a few won't change anything. Better to let them fight the Dark Iron dwarves."

The elf nodded.

Jace tilted his head out for another look. "I can't see clearly. Can you make out their gear and weapons? Are these soldiers, or scouts, hunters, something like that?"

"Let me look." Vereesa studied them carefully for a moment. "They're dressed pretty simply — all cloth shirts or leather trousers. Hard to tell what they are."

"Though, three out of the four are missing their left hands."

## Flags

- **碎手氏族 (Shattered Hand clan):** The telltale sign — three out of four orcs missing their left hands — marks them as members of the Shattered Hand clan, a canonical orc clan known for ritual self-mutilation.
- **明显的特征 (The Telltale Sign):** Chapter title refers to the identifying mark of the Shathered Hand orcs — the ritual removal (not "shattering" but amputation) of their left hands.
- **碎手氏族仪式:** The Shattered Hand tradition: every qualified Shattered Hand warrior ritually shatters and then severs their left hand, replacing it with a fearsome prosthetic — sometimes an axe, sometimes a blade, sometimes an unidentifiable horror. Even more fanatical warriors cut off both hands; these either become the most savage vanguard or warlords of the clan, or quickly die on the battlefield from their own reckless fury.
- **索瑞森 (Thandol Span):** The Dark Iron dwarf city that stood on the plain before the Blackrock Mountain explosion. Jace says "索瑞森" — this is actually the name of the Dark Iron emperor (Thandol Span is the canonical name of the bridge; the city here is likely meant to be the Dark Iron capital). Flagged: the Chinese 索瑞森 is ambiguous — may refer to the Dark Iron capital city. Rendered descriptively as "Thandol Span" but this may be an error in the source; the canonical Dark Iron city on the Burning Steppes was not named. Flagged for lore verification.
- **黑铁 (Dark Iron):** Canonical dwarven clan. Rendered as "Dark Iron."
- **奥能 (Arcane):** Vereesa calls her arrow-coating potion "Arcane" — likely an Arcane-infused coating for enchanted arrowheads, preparation against dragonkin.
- **龙人 (Dragonkin):** Vereesa's concern — dragonkin (half-dragon creatures serving the black dragonflight).
- **迪菲亚 (Defias):** Canonical WoW bandit faction. Jace jokes he must look like a "Defias bandit in a mask."
