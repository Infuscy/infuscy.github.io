## Chapter 294: The Hunters Hunted

The Shattered Hand clan were the first to break free from ogre slavery. Under the ogres' abuse and torture, they'd grown bloodthirsty and half-mad. The warriors who'd risen up against the ogres were, even by orcish standards, a pack of psychopaths.

And the traditions established by those first Shattered Hand orcs had been carried on by their successors. Now, every Shattered Hand warrior reveled in cruelty — whether inflicting it on others, or on themselves.

Every qualified Shattered Hand warrior would ritually shatter their own left hand, then sever it, replacing it with a fearsome prosthetic — sometimes an axe, sometimes a blade, sometimes a grotesque weapon beyond recognition.

The more fanatical warriors would even cut off both hands. These fanatics either went on to become the most savage vanguard or warlords in the Shattered Hand clan, or quickly perished on the battlefield, victims of their own frenzied recklessness.

If a single orc was missing a hand, you might not be able to tell — after all, scars and missing limbs were far too common in the Horde.

But if three out of four were all missing their left hands, then there was no question: this was a Shattered Hand patrol.

As for the last one with both hands intact — most likely a captive, or perhaps a young initiate not yet qualified for the "Shattering" ritual.

Jace and Vereesa waited until the orcs had passed, then followed from a distance, using the scattered boulders on the mountainside as cover.

If this was a Shattered Hand patrol heading out on a mission, wouldn't following them lead straight to the Shattered Hand hideout?

Jace hadn't expected things to go so smoothly. To think they'd picked up the Shattered Hand orcs' trail in just a week — perhaps because they were already so close to the Searing Gorge, and the Shattered Hand orcs would send out scouts looking for food and supplies.

At any rate, this meant they'd taken a huge step closer to their ultimate goal.

Vereesa was excited too. Jace could tell she'd snapped right out of the dejection that had hung over her during the crossing of the Cinder Vale. After all, it meant Jace's earlier judgment had been accurate, and the chances of finding the death knight had just shot up.

The two of them followed the orc patrol's tracks over hill and dale from a distance. With this ranger by his side, Jace had absolutely no worry about losing the orcs' trail or being spotted by them. Vereesa's eyesight and her instinct for tracking were beyond his comprehension.

After a certain distance, Jace had essentially given up offering any help with the tracking. He couldn't see where the orcs were, nor tell which direction to follow — he just followed the elf.

By evening, Jace's initially taut nerves had relaxed. Their route wasn't hard to predict — they were definitely climbing higher, with every chance of going over the mountain.

The two of them followed higher and higher. Jace looked up to see billowing black smoke pouring from the Blackrock Mountains to the west, blanketing a vast stretch of sky all the way to the edge of his vision.

Then, the warlock's bag seemed to shift. The imp?

Vereesa was right ahead — what did this demon want to do?

Wait, hold on — Jace could feel it too, even without the imp's warning.

"Vereesa, do you feel—"

The elf turned back, seeming to sense something off as well, her expression tightening.

Almost simultaneously, the two of them dove into a nearby thicket. Barely ten seconds later, a great whoosh of wind swept down from up the mountain, and Jace felt his heart slam against his ribs.

He raised his head and looked ahead. A black drake skimmed low over the mountainside, streaking into the distance — hard on the trail of the orcs.

Had they been spotted?

Jace pulled out an anti-magic potion and downed one, then drew an invisibility potion — but hesitated, and tucked it back into his potion bandolier instead.

After all, if a dragon had spotted them on this hillside, even drinking an invisibility potion and running wouldn't carry them far enough. The enemy was flying overhead with a bird's-eye view.

Vereesa also drank an anti-magic potion. Jace stuffed a mana potion into his bandolier, and noticed that the hand holding the potion bottle was trembling slightly.

"Down the mountain?" he asked.

"Go," Vereesa said.

The two of them scrambled and slid their way downhill. Jace clutched the bag in his hands to keep the food and potions from spilling, all while watching the direction the black dragon had gone. Before long, dust had swallowed visibility again — he couldn't see a thing over there.

"You called it," Jace said as he wrapped his face cloth tight.

"Did I want to be right about that?!" Vereesa leaped down a boulder. "Why is that dragon chasing those orcs? Aren't the black dragons allied with the orcs? Those death knights… damn it!"

That was a wake-up call for Jace.

In the game, the black dragons were indeed allied with the Blackrock clan and the Dragonmaw — but they clearly weren't allied with every orc in the Horde. They'd ignored the Warsong clan wandering the continent, and left the Frostwolf clan in the north to their seclusion.

So what about the Shattered Hand?

Suddenly, a dragon's roar echoed from a valley on the other side, followed by a string of explosions. Vereesa immediately looked over.

Even though the echoes had gone hollow and ethereal by the time they reached here, Jace knew those sounds all too well.

That drake was attacking — spewing lava and flame. The target was very likely that same group of orcs.

After all, they'd been following for this long and hadn't seen a single other living thing… what other possibility was there?

Right now, he had no mind to ponder why the black dragon was attacking the Shattered Hand orcs. He only prayed that the blind bastard hadn't spotted the two of them.

Though the odds weren't high.

The two of them made their way down into the valley, somehow ending up in a stretch of crumbling walls and rubble. Jace could tell these were dwarf-style walls — perhaps the ruins of an ancient city left here from ages past.

Vereesa had already pulled ahead, vaulting over collapsed stone walls and pillars with effortless grace.

Jace was starting to struggle to keep up. With his face wrapped, he couldn't breathe right; unwrap it and he'd be gulping down dust. But if he slowed down, that dragon might wheel around and be on them any moment.

Watching Vereesa pull further and further ahead, he didn't dare call out — what if the black dragon hadn't found them yet, and a shout tipped it off?

With that thought, Jace gritted his teeth and pushed harder to catch up — and just then, a shadow swept over his head from above.

All that anxious worrying had been pointless. This black dragon had never intended to let them go.

The sudden shift from hunter to prey turned his stomach.

Vereesa's pace up ahead had slowed. She'd already drawn an arrow and nocked it — she'd made her decision.

Jace pulled out the black staff and gripped it tight, bracing against a black wall.

Since they'd been marked, this was the moment to fight for their lives.
