Chapter 296: Heavy Losses

Hearing Jace's voice, Vereesa froze. In front of her, the black dragon suddenly raised its head, its eyes locking onto the two of them. Black, blood-like evil ichor dripped from its jaws — not the golden, molten flame from before.

She spun and ran. Jace whirled around too, not daring to look back, sprinting straight for the distance.

But by now he was running on fumes. He had barely made it a few steps when a wave of dark, blurred, chaotic yet all-consuming energy surged up from behind.

It was more agonizing than any flame he had ever cast... even just sensing it was enough to strike fear straight into the soul.

The two of them ran God knows how far, only stopping when that mad energy finally dissipated behind them.

Jace turned his head. Swathes of the district and its buildings had been swallowed by black flame. Fires the color of pus and blood billowed up everywhere, as if everything had been reduced to dark, cindery gore.

This was a black dragon's Shadowflame... true black fire, the kind that could destroy anything and everything.

"Thank God..."

Jace tried to say something, but his mouth barely seemed to work. He could not hear a thing.

Only then did he feel the deathly chill across his back.

"Jace! No... don't..."

Vereesa was looking his way, her voice nearly trembling.

Jace looked down and realized his robe had been burned through in several places, a chunk of his cloak singed away, its edges still being eaten by creeping black shadow. The exposed skin had turned a blackish purple.

Well, he had been just a hair too slow.

"Shadow Protection... give me a Shadow Protection Potion."

He said it weakly, but even the sound coming out of his own throat was a muddle.

Vereesa immediately pulled one out, popped the stopper, and helped him drink it. By now Jace could not even feel pain — just numbness... cold... he could not even taste the Shadow Protection Potion.

No good. The Shadow Protection Potion was not going to save him.

He tossed the bottle aside. "I... have to go back!"

Jace shook off the elf and ran toward the dragon with his staff in hand. He thought he heard Vereesa shouting something behind him, but he could not make out the words at all.

His consciousness was fading fast, his mind crumbling. Only a raw instinct to survive kept him moving forward.

Mustering the last of his strength, he pushed through the burning Shadowflame until he reached the rubble from before. There, amid the black dragonfire, the whelp lay on its back, belly up like a playing-dead snake, its eyes round and staring, powerless.

"Human... interesting..."

The black dragon's voice was as clear as a drumbeat.

"Interesting?"

Jace muttered, extending his staff and beginning to drain the black dragon's life force. His arms were nearly shaking, his lips trembling, his mouth repeating that last word until he could not speak at all.

Before long, a violent pain shot through his arm and side, nearly knocking Jace unconscious — but at the same time a thrill rose from the bottom of his heart, because it meant his senses were coming back!

The black dragon thrashed violently but could no longer fight back, could only let him drain its life unchecked, until even the edges of its scales had begun to grey, the tongue in its mouth drained of color. The whole dragon caved in like a dried-out specimen, its wings scraping with a faint sound in the wind like tattered canvas.

Only when he had drained it to the point of exhaustion, on the verge of blacking out himself, did Jace finally ease up, taking several deep breaths to steady himself.

He looked at his arm again — still pitch-black — but the moment he touched it, it sloughed off like a sheet of dead skin, revealing flushed, healthy skin beneath.

The burning pain all over his body was nearly unbearable, but he knew, rationally, that he was out of danger and his body was slowly repairing itself. He just had to endure it...

The black dragon's withered eyeball moved one last time, gazing at the sheets of dead skin sloughing off. Its mouth slowly closed, then opened again, as if it wanted to say something.

He walked over and gave the black dragon's head a vicious kick. Only then did the dragon finally loll its head to the side and close its eyes.

"Jace!"

Vereesa grabbed his arm, then pulled her hand back the moment the touch felt wrong, afraid to touch him again. She just stared at the scale-like things peeling away in sheets, her face drained of color.

"I'm fine. At least I'm not dying," Jace said.

He tried to brush off the blackish-red dead skin. "It's just a damn shame about these clothes. Shit, I really..."

The clothes on him, inside and out, were burned through on half his body — robe, cloak, and shirt alike.

Whoever made these clothes surely never imagined they would one day have to take a black dragon's Shadowflame head-on. After all, even in the game, it took a cloak made from the scales of the black dragon princess Onyxia to withstand a breath like that.

Jace checked his bag — it was full of broken glass and the sharp smell of potions — but thankfully most of it had survived.

"You really... scared the hell out of me, Jace."

She swallowed, stepped forward, and studied the whelp again. "We killed it..." she murmured.

As she said it, the elf's figure swayed, as if she had lost her balance. Jace felt something was off. "Vereesa, what's wrong?"

Vereesa backed up against the wall, panting heavily. She reached down to feel her ribs, then looked at her hand — it was covered in blood.

"That bastard must have clawed me earlier. Damn it."

The Greater Healing Potion was still intact. Jace immediately pulled it out, popped the stopper, and held it to her lips. "Quick, drink this."

Vereesa frowned. "If I drink it now, the death knight—"

"Are you out of your mind? Drink it right now." He popped the stopper and poured it into the elf's mouth, then shoved a Healing Potion into her hand as well.

Out of the corner of his eye, Jace caught a glimpse of her flank — several vicious gashes opening and closing with each swallow.

After she had drunk it all down, the wounds finally seemed to show some sign of closing. Jace just hoped it was not his imagination.

Still, Vereesa's color did look a little better. She pulled her cloak tight and leaned against the wall beside her with a sigh. "I really should learn that spell of yours. It's incredible."

Jace tossed the potion bottle aside. "Forget it. If you learned it, what would you drain right now? Me?"

Vereesa laughed, then winced in pain.

Jace rummaged through the ranger's bag for netherweave bandages when a head poked out from behind the wall the elf was leaning on. The imp — it was still alive. Unbelievable.

He glanced at the coughing Vereesa, then at the imp, then flicked his eyes toward the warlock bag beside him.

The imp Phase Shifted out of sight. Vereesa lifted her head, seeming to sense something, but the pain had clearly stolen most of her attention.

Jace coughed to draw her eyes over, then held out the bandages. "You want to do it yourself?"

Vereesa nodded and took them.

Jace stood and looked around. "We cannot stay here too long. There is still plenty of unspent black fire around — not good for your recovery."

After a quick bandaging, the two walked to a relatively intact low building in another stretch of rubble further off. The ranger sat down leaning against a dwarven stone ledge.

By now her color had improved a lot, and this time it definitely was not Jace's imagination. Only then did he relax.

He brushed the Shadowflame ash off his cloak. By now the shadow had mostly faded, but staring at the burn holes and ragged tears, the more he thought about it, the more it stung.

"You seem pretty attached to that cloak." Vereesa looked up. "Is it expensive? Or does it mean something to you?"

"King Varian gave it to me," Jace sighed.

"Why would the King of Stormwind give you a cloak?" the elf asked, eyes wide. "Is he a friend of yours? Though I suppose that human king is about your age."

"A friend? Not exactly," Jace said. "I helped some soldiers deal with a bunch of gnolls once, and the king gave me this... It really ought to be mounted on a wall as a collector's piece. But I did not have anything else to wear. And it has saved my life more than a few times — without it, I would not be alive today."

"It's my fault. I should not have rushed over like that."

"My fault? Because you ran fast?" Jace shook his head. "If I could run as fast as you, I would not have gotten singed. It is not your fault. Who would have thought that bastard would go that crazy at the end? Anyway, we survived — that's what matters."

Vereesa glanced his way and gave a small "mm."

"I'm out of arrows now. How are we supposed to deal with the orcs and that death knight next?"

"You're still thinking about pushing north in that condition?" Jace said. "Either way, we have confirmed there really are Shattered Hand orcs here, and they are in that direction. We can come straight back next time."

The ranger said nothing, but it was clear she was not ready to let it go.

"How'd you burn through your arrows so fast?" Jace asked.

"A few must have burned up when the black dragon was chasing us." Vereesa held out her hand, looking at the black soot staining it, and sighed. "Otherwise I would not have run out at the very end."

"Whatever we do next, let's rest here for now," Jace said.

Vereesa gave a reluctant nod, then peered through a crack in the wall. "What is this place?"

"An old dwarven city, probably," Jace said. "Might be near the ruins of Thaurissan. Once you've recovered a bit, we'll move further off. I'm worried dragonkin might come this way to investigate."

"Alright," the elf said.

Jace dusted off a nearby stone ledge and sat down. "I thought I was a goner!"

"Me too," Vereesa said. "I thought once he left, he'd stay gone. Never expected him to come back and fight us. If it were not for this rubble, we might really have been done for."

Jace let out a long breath. "Completely forgot about the Invisibility Potions."

Vereesa shot Jace a glance and burst out laughing, then nodded along.

After a moment, she said quietly, "Honestly, I just wanted to kill it."

"Same," Jace said.

"But next time things get that dangerous—"

"Definitely remember to drink up," Vereesa finished.
