Chapter 297: Blackrock and Shattered Hand

Now Jace finally had time to think through the tangled mess between the Blackrock clan, the Shattered Hand clan, and the black dragons.

Now that the Blackrock clan had taken root in Blackrock Spire, and the two sons of the Horde's former warchief Blackhand were already raising the banner of the "true Horde" from within, they were actively reaching out to the other Horde clans that had survived on Azeroth — the Dragonmaw, the Bleeding Hollow, rogues and stragglers alike — trying to rebuild the Horde.

But not all orcs accepted them. The Bleeding Hollow clan, for one, considered the Blackrock's Horde nothing but a rabble. They had been looking for a chance to return through the Dark Portal and join the Horde now led by Ner'zhul, rather than remain on Azeroth following the sons of Blackhand.

And the Shattered Hand?

The Shattered Hand was one of the highest-ranking clans in the Horde. They, too, had likely sided against the Blackrock and rejected this new "legitimacy."

Conversely, Blackhand's son — the current Blackrock chieftain, Rend — would hardly welcome them into Blackrock Spire either.

They had to be worried that the Shattered Hand chieftain, Kargath, was among those wandering orcs. If he came to Blackrock Mountain, Rend's position as chieftain would be done for.

In both martial prowess and prestige, the sons of Blackhand put together could not hold a candle to Kargath Bladefist himself.

Of course, Jace knew that Kargath ultimately had not stayed on Azeroth — he had retreated through the Dark Portal with the Horde.

But the Blackrock clan, trapped here, would not necessarily know that. Could this have been one of the conditions of the Blackrock's alliance with the black dragons — that the black dragons drive off the Shattered Hand orcs wandering the Redridge area?

And the death knight?

The death knight's relationship with the Blackrock clan was terrible. The Blackrock felt betrayed by Gul'dan — after all, at the most desperate point of the Horde's war, Gul'dan had chosen to pull away a host of elite forces to seek the Tomb of Sargeras in search of "power to rival the gods," and Teron Gorefiend and his death knights were Gul'dan's trusted agents.

When Teron Gorefiend had come to Azeroth seeking three artifacts, he had sought aid from Blackrock Spire — and been refused.

Even setting that aside, the death knights themselves posed a major threat to the Blackhand brothers' rule.

So it made perfect sense that Angor Deathwish had turned to seek the support of the wandering orcs — south of Khaz Modan, he did not have many other options.

Then again, all of this was just Jace's speculation. Sitting here with nothing to do, what else was there to do but spin theories? After all, he still had not caught a single glimpse of the death knight, and he had no idea where in the Badlands the Shattered Hand orcs were camped.

"Jace, your..." Vereesa pointed at Jace's nose.

Jace wiped it with the back of his hand — blood.

"It's fine," he said, wiping it away. "This is normal."

"Your eyes too?"

"...Also," Jace said, wiping the blood that had already gone tacky at the corner of his eye.

Jace thought back to facing that whelp earlier. Beyond the anxiety, the adrenaline, the desperate resistance, and the feeling of being completely out of his depth — how many other emotions had there been?

Worry that Vereesa might get hurt. Fear that he might just drop dead at any moment...

After all, he had cast too much Shadow magic. In the past, it would have been impossible to avoid losing control of his emotions — rage, bloodlust, or whatever else.

And yet now, his heart was pounding with what seemed like uncontainable anger — but most of it was over the damage to his clothes, especially that cloak.

The rest was pure dread at how close he had just come to being swallowed by Shadowflame.

No loss of emotional control from the Shadow?

It did not seem all that obvious.

But Jace knew the Shadow was good at creeping up on you just when you thought you were fine. He could not let his guard down.

"You do not look like someone who is not worried," Vereesa said, leaning against the wall and looking his way. As she spoke, she reached for the food bag and pulled out a piece of cold cheese.

"You sure you're fine?"

"It's an aftereffect of that spell," Jace admitted. "And after fighting with Shadow magic, I need time to cool down. Not just physically — mentally... I need to cool down there too."

"You're worried you'll slowly lose your mind to the Shadow?" Vereesa asked.

"Of course. After all, if I lose my mind, won't I drag you down with me?" Jace said with a laugh.

"You did not learn Shadow magic after you met me," Vereesa said. "Is there some incident that made you this careful? Does it have something to do with that dwarf? Does he know you're a warlock? I know the Wildhammer despise the Shadow — Grim Batol was destroyed by the Shadow."

"He knows," Jace said. "But he is not too worried about me these days."

Vereesa paused. "I'm not surprised."

Jace nodded and leaned back against the wall with a sigh.

The elf chewed and swallowed very carefully — Jace could tell her wound still hurt. He felt around for his own food bag and found nothing but the strap. It must have been ignited by the Shadowflame. He was only noticing now.

Bad luck. Jace rubbed his face to snap himself awake. Vereesa held out a slice of smoked meat.

"You keep it. You're the one who's hurt," Jace shook his head. "I'll take a look outside, see if I can find anything to eat. I'm the mercenary, after all. You're the employer."

"And does this black dragon cost extra?" Vereesa asked.

Jace laughed. "Do you have money?"

Vereesa pressed her lips together. "Put it on my tab. We'll settle at the end. Do you know what's out there to eat?"

"Scorpions, maybe. Or whatever other animals manage to survive out here. At this point there's nothing to be picky about," Jace said. "You'll be alright here on your own?"

"You'd better worry about yourself," Vereesa said, giving him a sidelong look. "Careful you don't get stung to death by a scorpion."

"I've still got antidote."

With that, Jace hefted his staff and threw on his cloak. With a huge chunk missing from the shoulder and the right side, it felt distinctly wrong, but at least it still covered him.

He glanced at Vereesa's long blade. "Lend me that for a bit. For chopping wood."

Vereesa tossed it to him with a flick of her wrist; Jace caught it cleanly. Judging by the strength in her throw, she was just hurting from the wound — ordinary beasts probably still were not much of a threat to her.

He covered his face and walked out of the low ruins. The area was deathly quiet — not a single sound, no sign of life. A dead zone.

His clothes were torn, his cloak was ruined, two flimsy potion bottles had shattered, and his skin still burned. If he did not go back and drain some life from that black bastard to make up for it, all this suffering tonight would have been for nothing.

Night had fully fallen. Jace retraced his steps. The red flames that had been burning were out; the world in front of him was near pitch-black. The faint glow was not moonlight either — moonlight could never pierce the clouds here. That dim light was the lava glow from a volcanic peak however many miles away, keeping the place from being completely pitch-dark.

As he drew closer, the coppery stench of Shadow still hung in the air.

Jace conjured a Light and looked around. His energy would not hold much longer, so he just got a rough idea of where scales had fallen during the fight, dismissed the spell, and walked over to pick up the more-or-less intact ones from the ground, one by one, and stow them in his bag.

Marin had said black dragon scales were quite valuable. But the one Ronemeth had picked up — judging by the size the mountaineer had gestured — was clearly bigger than anything from this whelp. The ones on the ground here were mostly the size of poplar leaves.

Still, gather as many as you can — they would fetch something.
