## Chapter 298: Amateur Hunter

Jace gathered his way back to the black dragon's corpse. Looking at it again, it was barely recognizable after what he had drained from it.

On top of that, the sustained burning of Shadowflame had turned the body into a blackened mummy. The scales and skin had lost all their luster, clinging to the bones like a layer of black oil. Even the belly and chest, once thick with muscle, had withered badly.

The spread wings propped up like dead trees, trembling in the occasional night breeze — they looked as if they would crumble at a touch.

There was no way Jace could haul away dragon bones. He walked over and knocked off the largest claws and teeth — these ought to be good for making some kind of weapon. He remembered there were claw-type daggers in the game. Or they could be made into ornaments. Either way, they were probably the most valuable things he could carry off.

A whole bag of loot, heavy and solid. If he could not sell it for at least ten-odd gold, he would consider it a waste.

Leaving the rubble, he headed toward the hillside. As he walked, he searched for any trees that were still alive enough to chop. Vereesa's long blade was sharp, fast, and sturdy — it made short work of wood too.

Carrying a small bundle of branches and chunks of wood, he headed west, scanning everywhere for signs of wild game. Gradually the ground grew dark — black, twisted stones everywhere, tree roots like charcoal. Was he walking deeper into the Burning Steppes?

He looked up. Heavy black clouds blotted out everything. Lightning rolled through the clouds now and then, producing a few muffled, barely-there claps of thunder.

Down here, you could neither see the lightning nor hear the thunder — it was all drowned out by the fires everywhere and the complex, ambient rumble of Blackrock Mountain bleeding into the background.

The folks in Redridge had said fire lizards lived near the lava. Jace honestly had no idea what a fire lizard was — some six-legged monster like a basilisk?

He spotted what seemed to be a narrow, glowing lava fissure not far ahead. Jace ran up and glanced down — the light from the overflowing magma seared his eyes.

When he looked back up, the world outside had gone completely dark.

Once his eyes readjusted, something dark green seemed to twitch among the rocks up high.

Was that... a scorpion?

Looking closer, the green beside the pointed rock seemed to be an arthropod's claw, raised high. Jace climbed a few steps higher — the claw snapped back in an instant.

That claw was big — about the same as the giant sea crabs Jace had seen at the Stormwind docks. He guessed this scorpion was about the size of a wild dog. If it hit you with the stinger on its tail, it could probably skewer you halfway to death even without injecting venom.

Jace checked his bag, pocketed the single remaining antidote, and climbed higher. As he drew near, he ducked behind a scorching black rock, turned his head, and peeked out. The scorpion had not moved — it was still waving its claws.

Jace crept closer. Suddenly the scorpion stopped twitching.

From behind the rock, a pair of yellowish eyes appeared.

Those were not the scorpion's eyes — they belonged to a black boar that was tearing into the scorpion's flesh!

Jace had not noticed it at all. Its black fur blended perfectly with the ground.

The moment the boar spotted Jace, it charged with a snort, kicking up a trail of dust. Jace immediately cast Curse of Weakness. The boar's front legs gave out and it tumbled several rolls down the slope. Jace darted aside just in time to avoid being run down.

He watched the boar tumble down the hillside, then ran and slid after it in pursuit. The moment he caught up, the boar had just gotten its legs under it — and Jace drove the blade into its back.

The blade was terrifyingly fast — in and out like stabbing tofu. Jace could hardly believe it, given that this was a thick-skinned, thick-fleshed Blackrock boar...

The boar howled in pain and whipped its head back to gore him. Jace leaped aside.

He staggered crookedly back a few steps on the slope. The boar was not chasing — it just snarled at Jace, its legs shaking constantly, occasionally buckling to the ground and struggling back up again.

It was not that big — about knee-height to Jace — but its two tusks curled up like a pair of blades, plenty threatening. The boar seemed to realize it could not take on this bigger opponent and turned to flee down the slope.

It was running much slower now. Jace did not rush to catch up, because it was heading the way he had come from — straight toward the rubble.

He chased it for God knows how long before the boar finally stopped and collapsed beside a blackened stump.

Any longer and Jace would have developed feelings for the thing.

It breathed fast, glaring as if still unwilling to accept its fate. Jace slashed its throat. The boar gave a few hoarse squeals, its hind legs kicking wildly.

Seeing it still had fight left, Jace did not want to drag it out any further.

He sat down to catch his breath, gazing at the hazy ruins of the city in the distance. This time he was truly exhausted.

Though he could barely feel the pain from the Shadowflame anymore, he also did not have that boundless energy he got after draining life.

The burns the Shadowflame had dealt him were enough to kill. If he had not run over in time to drain the black dragon's life force to heal himself, he would probably be dead.

So after the black dragon's leftover life force pulled him back from the brink, there was not much surplus left to replenish his strength.

So had it strengthened his power, like last time?

He did not know. Just taking down a boar was not enough to feel any change in strength or mana. He would need proper testing.

That was the worst part of living here — no stat panel. Everything came down to trial, guesswork, and comparison.

But thinking back to fighting that whelp at Southshore — he and Grede, plus he did not know how many Lordaeron soldiers, had besieged it for ages before finally killing it.

And here, just him and Vereesa had done it.

So whatever else, he must have gotten a lot stronger along the way.

Not to mention this whelp seemed even stronger than the one at Southshore — after all, it could spew the Shadowflame that appeared in the game. The black dragon before had only mixed a bit of Shadow into its fire.

He had thought that was what Shadowflame looked like, but having seen the real thing here, he finally understood the difference.

Jace raised his head and gazed at the hazy peak of Blackrock Mountain in the distance, imagining the power of the Shadowflame Nefarian could unleash.

If a single whelp was this terrifying, let alone an adult dragon — or Nefarian himself.

By now the boar had stopped moving. Bright red blood pooled on the ground.

He walked over and gave it a kick to make sure it was truly dead, then started cutting off the head.

The boar's head did not look like it had much meat — all tusks and skin stretched over bone — but it made up half the body's size. The whole boar was too heavy. Jace had no interest in lugging that massive skull back, so he just cut the whole thing off.

After finally getting the head off, he was so tired he could barely stand. It felt harder than killing that whelp.

He wiped the sweat from his forehead. The distant volcano had doubled in his vision.
