Chapter 299: The Dark Iron City

Following the whelp's corpse as a landmark, Jace found the small room where he and Vereesa had hidden earlier. As if she had heard his footsteps, Vereesa poked her head out and waved, as though afraid he might not find the place.

Jace dragged half a boar up to the window, sighed, and sat on a broken stone beside it. "This hike kicked my ass!"

"Good work. But how'd you get this boar to obediently let itself be killed?" Vereesa asked, curious.

"Same as you. Through communication." Jace patted the boar's rump. "It just wasn't very friendly. Nearly gored me to death."

Vereesa laughed. Jace glanced at the bandage below her ribs. "How's your recovery?"

"A lot better," Vereesa said. "That Healing Potion your alchemist friend made is really effective."

Jace smacked his lips, thinking of Ursula drenched in sweat back in the alchemy lab, and realized he actually missed Stormwind a bit.

Gazing at the pitch-black dwarven ruins around him, it really felt like he had landed on an alien planet. Two completely different worlds.

And those two worlds were separated by almost nothing but the Redridge Mountains.

"Can we move out?" he asked.

"Pretty much."

"Then we leave once we've eaten."

Jace arranged the firewood he had gathered on the ground, then picked up some broken stones from around to build a small stove, propping a thin stone slab on top as a griddle.

"Any Mana Potions left?" Jace asked.

Vereesa shook her head.

"Damn it." Jace scratched his head. "Then I'll try to cast what I can. My energy's running low."

"Watch this." Vereesa snapped her fingers. A spark of arcane starlight caught beside the firewood and ignited a flame, setting the wood ablaze in an instant. Jace stared, dumbfounded.

"Did you learn a fire-lighting spell?" he asked.

"Fire elements are practically everywhere here. Igniting them with the Arcane is effortless," Vereesa said.

"Alright, alright, stop." Jace muttered, exasperated. "Effortless..."

The half-dead old trees in the Burning Steppes burned surprisingly well — the stones got searing hot without needing to add more wood.

Jace sliced piece after piece of meat off the boar and laid them on the stone. They sizzled and oozed fat at once; oil dripped into the charcoal and sent up white smoke. The smell of roasting meat held Vereesa's rapt, unblinking gaze for a long time.

Once they had eaten their fill, Jace sat to the side, watching Vereesa still going at it — a short blade in one hand, a strip of meat in the other, devouring everything in sight.

He leaned back against the wall, feeling a wave of drowsiness roll over him. But he could not sleep — it still was not safe enough here.

After eating, the two of them tidied up and set out at once, heading uphill. By now it was close to dawn.

As they walked, just as they were about to leave the ruins, Vereesa suddenly hesitated, stopping at a corner and looking back.

Jace nodded silently and glanced past the wall. Sure enough, dragonkin were walking down the opposite street toward where the whelp had died.

Two dragonkin mages and a tall dragonkin warrior in dark heavy armor. In their current condition, Jace and Vereesa had basically no chance of handling them.

Vereesa leaned close to his ear. "I'm worried the hillside over there is already full of dragonkin. Can you hear that? Voices, over there."

Of course Jace could not make out anything through the howling wind, but he knew that following Vereesa's lead right now was always the right call.

"Then we'll have to go back," he said quietly.

Vereesa nodded. The two turned back sharply. Before long, they could already see dragonkin approaching from the south.

Using the rubble walls as cover, they crept forward street by street. As they advanced, Vereesa kept glancing toward Blackrock Mountain.

"There seem to be some up in the hills too," she said softly. "We have to find another way out of here."

"I'll follow your lead," Jace said.

Judging by the ranger's performance tracking the orcs before, trusting her read of enemy movements a hundred percent was absolutely the smart play. In this regard, Jace was no better than an idiot compared to her.

Using the rubble as cover, the two headed west toward a larger stretch of the dwarven city. Along the way they encountered several two- or three-dragonkin patrols. They seemed to be searching purposefully for whoever had killed the whelp.

The scale of the city here seemed much larger than before. Jace guessed this must be the true ruins of Thaurissan.

Unlike the few broken walls in the game, this was a sprawling, sinister ghost city. The black, half-destroyed buildings, their upper levels blown away by the explosion, had not lost any of their imposing presence — if anything, they felt even more solemn and majestic.

It was not just the atmosphere that was sinister. Jace could actually feel a cold here that was completely different from before — and this was supposed to be the Burning Steppes.

Vereesa led Jace behind a stone building. Just as they were about to head out through a collapsed doorway, she suddenly stopped and pressed herself against the wall.

She glanced outside. "There are a lot of dragonkin ahead."

"A cordon?" Jace asked.

"I think so," Vereesa said. "Sneaking past from here probably is not going to work."

She looked up and pointed. Jace pressed himself to a crack and peeked out, spotting a dragonkin mage standing on a high broken wall, looking down on the street below.

"So, Invisibility Potion?" Jace said.

"Right." Vereesa pulled one out. "Then we'll meet up in that building in the distance. Once we reach it, we should be clear of these dragonkin."

"Yours might last longer than mine," Jace said, studying the building's position carefully. "This potion seems to feed off your own mana."

"I can try to drop my own invisibility," Vereesa said, and drank the Invisibility Potion. Jace drank his too, counting the seconds in his head.

Before long, he felt the light around him blur. He could not see anything clearly, as if he had plunged into water — a hazy, formless void. The building positions he had just memorized were completely gone... He could only move forward slowly and hazily on instinct.

But just then, he felt like he saw something.

Vaguely, it seemed like figures were shifting in the distance. Those shadows were so short — not human, not orc, and certainly not dragonkin.

They looked like dwarves.

Why could he not see Vereesa right nearby, yet those dwarves in the distance were so clear? In his daze, he felt the dwarves were staring right at him.

How was that possible? Invisibility meant outsiders could not see you — so why were those dwarves staring at him, their gazes tracking his every move?

Time dragged interminably in the invisible state, especially with those dwarven shades watching him. Everything slowed to a crawl.

Jace's heart started racing. A guess had already surfaced in his mind.

The light began to gather, the world in his vision slowly solidifying. The potion wore off. Not far away, Vereesa faded back into sight as well.

Jace noticed her face did not look too good either.

"Ghosts, right?" Jace asked.

"Probably." Vereesa pulled out the Holy Wizard Oil. "Damn it. One problem after another."
