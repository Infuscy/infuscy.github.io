Chapter 300: A Murky Malice

Vereesa coated both her blades with the Holy Wizard Oil and handed the bottle to Jace.

Jace took the bottle, glanced at the black staff in his hand, then strapped it to his back and switched to the fire staff to apply the oil.

Who knew what would happen if you smeared holy energy onto that black staff. Either way, he had a feeling nothing good would come of it.

He glanced outside. The street was empty. The air was bitterly cold, as if frozen solid.

"Can you only see those ghosts while under an Invisibility Potion?" he whispered.

"Seems that way," Vereesa said, leaning beside him. "What, are we going to chug Invisibility Potions all the way out of here?"

Jace said quietly, "The potions probably are not enough. Chugging them one by one only gives ten-odd minutes each. Are you sure we can run clear of this city in ten minutes?"

"No," Vereesa said. "But I have no idea where these ghosts are. I cannot sense them — they could be right beside us for all I know. What about you? You're a mage, Jace."

Apprentice mage, Jace very much wanted to stress.

But this was not the Mage Quarter, and this was not the time to hide behind his apprentice status.

That said, it was not like he felt nothing at all... Since a while ago, he had felt this district was unnaturally cold. Could this chill be caused by the ghosts' intrusion?

He remembered feeling something similar at the great cemetery in Raven Hill. It was a completely different cold from spending a winter night outdoors — a bone-deep ache that felt like it was burrowing into his brain.

"Maybe," he said quietly.

"What do you feel?" Vereesa asked.

"Cold. It's a lot colder here than over there." Jace looked around. "Seems related to the ghosts here. You do not feel it?"

Vereesa looked around. "Honestly, I cannot tell."

She met Jace's eyes. "Either way, whatever feels off, try attacking with fire magic. I think fire — especially fire magic affected by this oil — can drive off the ghosts."

"You take the lead then," Jace said. "See if you can find the quickest way out."

"I'm not familiar with dwarven cities either," Vereesa said. "But I figure if we just keep heading one direction, we'll get out eventually, right?"

Jace sighed. "I cannot tell direction at all. The buildings here all look too much alike, and the roads are not straight — it's like a maze."

Damn it. Had not this whole trip been about finding the Shattered Hand orcs and the death knight?

They had found the Shattered Hand orcs — so why had they ended up running into this Dark Iron ghost city?

The two left the abandoned dwelling and stepped onto the street. The dragonkin had completely vanished, and there was no sound of voices to be heard.

"Have we shaken the dragonkin?" Jace whispered.

"I cannot sense their presence anymore. A dragon's scent is pretty easy to pick out." Vereesa glanced back at Jace's tattered cloak. "They probably think we're still at the whelp's corpse. Let them look."

"This road curves ahead. Let's take a different one."

So saying, she moved along the wall toward another doorway. By now Jace had completely lost his drowsiness. After all, what could be more invigorating than moving through a dark, ghost-infested alley in the middle of the night?

The elf ducked into the doorway; he hunched over and followed close behind. This must have been an ordinary dwarf's dwelling — both the ceiling and the doorway were painfully low.

Just as the two were about to climb out the window on the other side, Jace suddenly felt something off behind him.

A cold closing in!

He spun around and began chanting Immolate. To keep from interfering with the Wizard Oil's effect, he held himself back from channeling his Shadow energy...

The spell completed. Golden fire erupted through the room with a piercing shriek!

Jace watched a pale phantom shatter and scatter in the expanding flames. A ghastly, skin-and-bones face-shadow lunged at him, then stretched open in an expression of agony and dissolved. The shriek did not fully fade until it was right against his ear — like a woman sobbing with her hands around his neck.

Despite all the terrifying battles he had been through, the sight still sent a chill down Jace's spine. He stumbled back a few steps and tumbled straight out the window.

Across from him, Vereesa's face had gone a shade paler too, her blade raised as she stared hard at the window.

Wisps of black smoke drifted from the window. That soul-chilling cold was gone.

"Did you see that? That thing — did you see it?" Jace asked, pointing at the window as he climbed to his feet.

"I saw it..." Vereesa was still staring at the window. She nodded. "You really came through, Jace."

"I really do not want to do that again." Jace glanced at the narrow streets on both sides and the dwarven buildings standing close by. "But there are clearly a lot more ghosts here. While we were on the Invisibility Potion... I saw at least two. Maybe three."

"Maybe not all the ghosts want to kill us," Vereesa said.

"Let's hope not," Jace said. "But I have already taken out one of theirs."

Vereesa swallowed and muttered, "Hopefully the friendly ones will not take it too personally. This should count as self-defense, right?"

"They're ghosts. You really expect them to be rational?" Jace said quietly.

The elf sighed helplessly, glanced toward the lower ground to the south, and said, "Let's keep moving." The two moved along the wall down a flight of steps. Just then Jace felt a wave of freezing cold hit him. Before he could warn her, Vereesa suddenly gasped and slammed into the wall beside her, as if shoved hard.

The elf reacted fast, swinging her blade back through the air. The sword carved a diagonal arc of golden light, and a pale phantom suddenly materialized out of nowhere, howling as it rose up the wall and gradually came apart.

In the end, only a tiny skeleton remained, wailing as it slowly faded.

Vereesa craned her neck with a frown. Jace walked over and noticed a faint purple handprint had already appeared on her skin.

"Do not let your guard down. Drink some potion." Jace handed her a Shadow Protection Potion.

Vereesa nodded, took the bottle, and drank. She leaned against the wall to catch her breath. "That ghost was just a child. Their hostility is stronger than we thought. Maybe they're protecting their city. Or maybe... they just hate all living people."

"I'll take point." Jace drank a Shadow Protection Potion too. "I felt that ghost attack you just now, but it came too fast."

Vereesa seemed about to say something, her lips moved, but in the end she just nodded.

Jace held out his hand. "Give me a blade. At the speed that thing came at us, I probably will not have time to cast. And my energy has not recovered enough either."

The elf handed him the longer one. With the blade in hand, Jace immediately felt a lot more confident. After all, the idea of swinging a stick at ghosts had never sat right with him.
