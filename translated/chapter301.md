## Chapter 301: The Siege of the Dead

The two of them walked south along the street for a while, and Jace realized this place was even larger than he'd imagined when he'd first arrived.

Especially looking down the streets that ran east — even through the haze of ash, he could feel the streets stretching on and on into the distance.

The scale of this old Dark Iron capital was probably no smaller than Stormwind itself. Of course, the Dark Iron clan that could build an underground city as absurdly massive as Shadowforge City — when constructing a great capital named after their own emperor, how could they possibly have held anything back? They would have spared no effort to make it as magnificent as possible.

It was only that, a little over a hundred years ago, the cataclysmic explosion of Blackrock Mountain had burned it to the ground in an instant, and from then on it had been all but forgotten by history.

Walking among the streets, Jace could even sense the pride and arrogance the dwarves had felt when they built this city. The walls here were as thick as the underground warrens of Aerie Peak. The toppled statues and decorations that lay across the streets brimmed with a beauty of raw power — the stone from the Redridge Mountains, stretching thousands of miles, must have been abundant enough for them to squander it so freely.

It was said that before the Blackrock Mountain explosion, the Burning Steppes had been a lush, sprawling wilderness.

Human travelers from a few hundred years ago had recorded that this was a place even more beautiful than the Arathi Highlands — the majestic red peaks and the boundless green fields formed a striking yet harmonious contrast.

When the Dark Iron dwarves had come here to settle from Khaz Modan, they must have felt this was a promised land they could cultivate for a thousand years.

But what the Sorcerer-thane Thaurissan wanted was no city of Thaurissan, no beautiful, fertile forests or plains — he wanted Khaz Modan. He wanted Ironforge.

As Jace stood gazing at a charred humanoid figure lying at the edge of the street, a fierce, bone-piercing cold hit him in the face.

He gripped the blade in his hand and pulled Vereesa behind him, staring ahead for a long moment.

This wasn't a sudden blast of cold — it was a wall of chill blocking his path.

Judging by what he'd sensed from those two wraiths earlier, there was no telling how many ghosts might be staring at him right now, and getting closer by the second.

"Maybe we shouldn't keep heading down this way. I think there are far more wraiths here than before."

Even as he spoke, he backed up and glanced down a passage to the west.

"This way, Vereesa. The situation's better over here."

After walking for who knows how long, they passed through a square. Gazing out at the layered, endless rows of buildings that seemed to have no far edge, Jace felt deeply that the prospect of leaving this place was growing more and more distant by the minute.

By now it should have been well into morning, but here, deep in the Burning Steppes, black clouds shrouded everything. Day or night, it all looked the same.

Jace looked up toward the highest peak of Blackrock Mountain, whose silhouette was now visible in the distance. Rivers of lava flowed down its slopes like blood streaming down the face of a dark-skinned monster — visible even through the blanket of ash.

The more he looked, the more dizzy he felt.

Fighting the black dragon, sustaining grave injuries, hunting, and now going a full night without sleep — Jace had severely drained his spirit.

Crossing the square, he walked a little further and felt the chill again, so he stopped and looked south. The moment he stopped, he even felt his body sway.

He glanced back at the elf and noticed that the wound beneath her ribs seemed to be seeping blood through the bandage.

"What's wrong?"

"What?"

Vereesa followed Jace's gaze to her own bandage. "Probably from just now, with that ghost… It's nothing, Jace. Don't worry about me. This wound can't possibly have healed this fast."

"Let's find a place to change your bandage. I need to rest a little too," Jace said.

Vereesa nodded reluctantly. The two of them walked along the edge of the square toward the north.

The further north they went, the more Jace felt the bone-chilling presence of the ghosts fade.

Up ahead, there seemed to be a relatively intact building. Its third floor had been blown away and the second floor was half-collapsed, but the ground floor was complete and looked solid.

"We'll rest here," Jace said, grabbing the doorframe. "I'll go in first and check it out. Stay behind me and be careful."

Vereesa nodded, and Jace instinctively ducked and stepped inside.

But once he was in, he realized the ceiling of this building was far higher than he'd expected — like a small palace.

Vereesa conjured a Light, and the radiance she called forth felt incredibly warm and gentle.

By that faint glow, he could see the interior walls. The reliefs and such, though hard to make out, were still relatively intact, not too badly damaged. But the overall style brought an uncomfortable sense of oppression.

Perhaps it was the black walls, perhaps the dust deposited everywhere — the stale, decrepit desolation made anyone inside feel their spirits sink.

The two of them walked deep inside and stopped at a corner. Once they stopped, the surroundings fell deathly still, nothing remaining but their breathing.

The rumble of the distant volcano, reaching this point, had become almost inaudible after being blocked by the corridors and walls — which only further set off the near-absolute silence.

Vereesa dismissed the Light, and at once the surroundings became completely invisible.

Even after a long time of adaptation, he still couldn't see his hand in front of his face. There truly wasn't a single ray of light that could penetrate in here.

"Can you still sense the presence of the ghosts?" Vereesa asked.

Though she had lowered her voice as much as possible, Jace still felt as though her words echoed throughout the entire space.

"No. It's like the ghosts have all kept far away from this place," he replied. "Maybe this was a place the Dark Iron dwarves couldn't afford to offend."

"You think this is some kind of temple too?" Vereesa asked. "But what do the Dark Iron dwarves worship?"

What do the Dark Iron dwarves worship.

Jace was stunned for a moment, because this was a rather critical question.

He conjured a Light. Vereesa squinted from the brightness and turned her head away.

Jace stood up and pressed the glow against the wall, studying the reliefs and murals here. He realized that with his knowledge, he couldn't seem to identify what these already somewhat stylized patterns meant.

They looked like burning flames, but saying they were swirling winds seemed to work too. As for saying they were the waves of the sea… that was a bit of a stretch.

Following the patterns of the reliefs little by little, Jace came to the entrance of a corridor even deeper inside. The dwarf figures here were scattered around the sides, not interwoven with the central patterns, so it was hard for Jace to judge what these things were through their interactions.

But the most likely answer was flames. Perhaps the lava within Blackrock Mountain, or perhaps Ragnaros himself — after all, this was the most historically consistent guess.

Was this some kind of fire temple?

But a faint unease rose in his heart, because the Firelord Ragnaros in reality served the deepest evil in this world.

Hundreds of thousands of years ago — or perhaps an unknowable length of time — in the primordial era, the elements had once ruled Azeroth. But soon those parasites from the universe, the terrifying void creatures later called the Old Gods by mortals, descended upon this place and enslaved the elements, using them as servants to corrupt the entire world.

Even after the parasites were defeated by the Titans of the Pantheon — some executed, some sealed away in the deep earth and the ocean depths — these elements that had been enslaved could never again free themselves from the influence of the Old Gods.

But the moment the Old Gods were involved, Jace couldn't help but think in the direction of shadow, of mental manipulation.

Thinking of those dwarf wraiths from before, he began to feel that these undead were guiding him and Vereesa toward this temple… or rather, besieging them.

Was he overthinking it?

But as sensitive as Jace was to shadow power, he couldn't detect any dark force in this temple. It seemed as though no magic existed here at all.

After all, this was an ancient holy site abandoned for well over a hundred years, nearly two hundred. Even if it had still held some power back then, there should be precious little left now.

As he pondered, Jace suddenly sensed a shadow in his peripheral vision.

He immediately swung the Light toward the corridor beside him and saw a faint, translucent figure standing inside. It was a human. Most importantly, he was wearing a set of cavalry armor. Judging by the style and the faintly visible emblem, it bore the lion head of the Kingdom of Stormwind, and the tassels on his characteristic full-face helmet drifted in the air.

Before Jace could say anything, the other party spoke first.

"Are you… from Stormwind?"

The wraith's voice was ethereal, like drumbeats, each one striking deep in the heart.
