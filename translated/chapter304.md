## Chapter 304: Whose Flesh, Whose Will

How could an orc warlock's soul invade a living person's body?

Because I am Teron Gorefiend! I do whatever I please! No one can stop me! Let alone you, Jace Sesso.

Even I couldn't stop me!

And yet I did it, Jace. Wake up to reality…

"Jace Sesso!"

A hard fist like stone slammed into his face.

Jace blinked. Cold rain slapped his forehead, his lips.

A dark shadow loomed over him. His skin was black, his deep-purple beard and the black hair cascading over his shoulders dancing in the wind — like a demon from the end of days. And Jace was nothing but a pitiful hare clutched in his massive hand.

"You're awake. I thought you'd never wake up, Jace Sesso. So who's in control here?"

The shadow laughed.

"How much of your mind is left? Why don't you speak? Or have you even forgotten my name? Is this the pathetic will of your feeble soul? You disappoint me. Is this the wretched will that dominates this body?"

"Teron'gor," Jace managed to murmur the name. The shadow fell silent for a moment — clearly not expecting Jace to say that name.

He suddenly raised his arm. Jace only felt his head take a vicious blow. The world spun. His body hung in the air, then crashed heavily to the ground again.

The shadow stood in the distance, like a black stone statue.

Where was this?

Black clouds rolled across the sky. The rain was cold as hail. His hands, his legs — all had gone numb from the cold.

Heavy footsteps splashed through the water, step by step drawing closer. Jace wanted to stand and resist, but his body had no strength.

He didn't know which bone had broken. He couldn't move.

Before his brain could even start turning, he was suddenly lifted up.

The shadow asked, "You think you were something impressive in Dalaran? Was that a real victory? If so, then what is this now?"

A bolt of lightning streaked across the sky, illuminating the shadow's face.

An orc with pitch-black skin, covered in scars and terrible violet runes. His eyes were completely white, as if clouded by severe cataracts, yet they contained terrifying shadow power.

He grinned, parted his lips, and said in a low voice, "Say it, Jace Sesso. Say you lost."

Jace laughed. The orc immediately grabbed his hair and wrenched it hard. Jace only felt as though half his scalp had been torn away, yet he felt no pain. He lowered his head and coughed twice, struggling to catch his breath and trying to hold on to his sanity — but it was as though he couldn't breathe anything in, and couldn't breathe anything out.

"Say you lost, Jace Sesso. I'll give you a comfortable death," the orc growled.

"You might as well give me the most painful death, Teron'gor," Jace said, turning his head.

The orc suddenly reached out and dug into Jace's eye sockets. Jace only felt his head spin, then a loosening, as though he'd lost something.

Jace felt he could see nothing. Before his eyes was only a field of blood-red.

Somehow, he felt no fear, no worry. Though he could barely resist the other's cruelty, it was as though everything around him was still not beyond his control.

Jace coughed twice and said, "I was thinking, Teron'gor."

"What… can't you kill me?"

"I could kill you more easily than crushing an ant," the shadow replied in a low voice.

"Then why haven't you killed me yet? Is it because you were never in control here to begin with?" Jace raised his head and asked.

Teron'gor seized Jace's neck and lifted him up. Jace's vision finally cleared a little.

He eyed the black shadow before him, stained red with blood. The other's anger was nearly enough to ignite flames — that heat almost swelled to the point of truly burning. The orc extended a finger, aimed at the center of Jace's brow, and softly chanted: "Torolas Asgolani…"

Jace could tell this was the incantation for Curse of Agony. It was somewhat different from the one Surena had chanted, but overall very similar.

But this Curse of Agony was even weaker than Surena's, because nothing happened at all.

Teron'gor's expression froze. Jace asked through coughs, "What is this laughable incantation?"

The orc roared again: "Torolas…"

"Torolas Asgeni!" Jace shouted the incantation first, loud and clear!

Teron'gor's arm suddenly shuddered. He let go and threw Jace to the ground, clutching his chest and coughing incessantly, backing up several steps, his face full of disbelief.

Jace sat on the ground and raised his arm.

"Fekt…"

Teron'gor's eyes suddenly went wide. His lips began to tremble. "No, no, Gul'dan, my lord, I… I… no… this can't be… Gul'dan, my lord, I don't know… no… impossible!"

He first clutched his eyes, then began frantically gouging at his eyeballs, howling wildly all the while: "Gul'dan is dead! There is no more Gul'dan in this world! This is an illusion… impossible. You can't toy with me with Fear, you damned… no, how could this be?"

Jace sat slumped to the side, watching him roll on the ground and struggle. When the orc stood up again, his face was covered in blood.

"You are not Gul'dan, you damned traitor, Angor. I should have chopped your head off long ago! You actually tried to use this despicable trick to deceive me into submitting to you? What do you want? You want my power? You think I can't see through these unconvincing illusions? Jace Sesso, Vereesa Windrunner — they're all phantoms you fabricated. You stole a peek at my memories? Let me out! Scum! Beast!"

He stood up and roared: "You have betrayed the Horde! Betrayed the Shadow Council! You cur! Admit your mistake now and you still have a chance!"

He swung his fists at the air back and forth, but hit nothing. The various curses gradually turned into crude Orcish abuse. Jace asked in a low voice, "Who told you the name Jace Sesso?"

"Jace Sesso… is it really you? I can feel your soul — it's indeed you…" Teron Gorefiend hissed. "Let me out, Jace Sesso. I don't want to be here anymore. Jace Sesso, where are you?"

He groped back and forth. The rage on his face had turned to bewilderment.

Before long, the orc collapsed to the ground and began vomiting incessantly, retching up pool after pool of black corpse-water. After the vomiting came relentless coughing.

"Tell me who told you the name Jace Sesso, and I'll let you out," Jace said.

Hearing this, Teron'gor raised his head. "You're lying. I know you're trying to deceive me. You won't fool me! I am you, and you are me! But why doesn't the shadow obey my commands? Why?"

"Because this was never yours to begin with," Jace said, spreading his arms. "Deep down, you know this yourself. Otherwise you wouldn't have wanted my will to submit. You wanted me to willingly yield this place to you. So who exactly is deceiving whom? You're deceiving yourself."

"Gul'dan is dead. I am the greatest warlock in this world! I know everything. It's not your place to lecture me, boy!"

Teron'gor's voice was hoarse beyond description. His hysterical howls echoed back and forth across the rainy night of the plains.

Jace said expressionlessly, "But you were never cut out to be a warlock to begin with, Teron'gor. Didn't you know that after all those years in the Shadow Council?"

"Bullshit!" Teron'gor screamed at the top of his lungs. He ran toward the sound, grabbing and thrashing about wildly, trying to seize Jace.

"You have no idea who I am. You have no idea what I've been through, you arrogant human brat!"

"Gul'dan never thought much of you to begin with," Jace continued, stepping aside. "The reason he made you the first death knight wasn't just because you were loyal to him — it was also because even if turning you into a death knight failed, it wouldn't be much of a waste. He felt that as a death knight, you performed far better than you ever did as a warlock!"

"WHO ARE YOU?!" Teron'gor turned toward Jace's direction and roared in Orcish. "WHO THE HELL ARE YOU?! You are not Jace Sesso. You're a Nathrezim, aren't you? Did Kil'jaeden send you to humiliate me? Hah, you demons! I saw through you long ago!"

Jace asked in Orcish, word by deliberate word: "Was it Kel'Thuzad who told you my name?"

"Kel'Thuzad?" Teron'gor's expression gradually returned to calm. He said slowly, "You'll never know. At least I can still make you suffer. No matter who you are… I won't give you the answer!"

The orc suddenly burst into laughter, until his hair began to fall out and his skin began to split. The pain turned his laughter into uncontrollable howling.

He collapsed to the ground, raised his hands high, and said, "Fine, you win. I just hope your soul is gnawed upon by thousands of felhounds! I hope you meet a fate even more miserable than Gul'dan's! I curse you!"

Even as he roared, his form rapidly withered. Finally, he was compressed by a powerful shadow force into a pool of black water. Jace only felt as though his skull was about to split. Suddenly, with a crushing tightness in his chest, his eyes flew open.

All around was a heavy, still darkness. Jace abruptly sat up. He felt as though there were all manner of delirious murmurs in his mind, accompanied by countless images rapidly fading.

A vast blue sky. Boundless grasslands. A stone monument atop a mountain, like something from a ritual.

Deep caves. Demon lords writhing with corpulent bodies. Countless roaring orcs.

A colossal gate flickering with terrifying fel energy. A fearsome warlock in a black robe, raising a massive staff strung with skulls.

Blackened corpses. Piercing howls. Wailing humans, elves. Frantically shrieking dwarves. A black-robed legion wielding war blades and great axes. The dead risen once more as zombies.

A massive black shadow on the cliff's edge and a demonic eye gazing upon him. Black dragon swarms flying across the sky. A violet city at midnight.

The tense face of a young human…

Pain… reluctance… a distant black dragon. A black demon grinning softly. A faint murmur… Anduin… Lothar…

These remnant memories flickered like a film reel. The vast majority of the images had flashed past before he could even see them clearly.

He wanted to remember these images, but could only grasp a limited few. The rest slipped away like running water, rapidly fading as Gorefiend's will burned away.

What impressed him most was the human standing on the high terrace of Dalaran, gazing down at him.

Recalling this image, Jace even felt as though he was staring into a bottomless abyss.

Who was that? Was it Kel'Thuzad?

Perhaps… that human had looked older and wore a long robe.

Very soon, this memory too began to fracture, to crack, and gradually vanish from his mind, leaving behind only a lingering concept for Jace to chew over when he looked back. No — compared to these images, what was more important were those murmurs!

After the images fractured, only the voices continued to play back incessantly. The most profound voices in Teron Gorefiend's memory, besides the commands murmured from the orc warlock's lips, were those incantations!

Jace immediately released an Arcane Intellect, wanting to remember these voices as much as possible.

He could distinguish the contents of those Eredun words. He had to recite them and engrave them into his mind, rather than letting them vanish with the destruction of Gorefiend's will!

He continuously repeated these murmurs, until even the voices in his mind gradually faded, like echoes receding into the distance.

At that moment, a sharp blade pressed against the back of his neck.
