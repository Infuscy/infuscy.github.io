Chapter 305: Which One Are You?

Jace turned his head. Before him, Vereesa stood holding a long blade glowing with golden wizard oil, looming over him with cold, unfamiliar eyes.

The light in her eyes flickered. Her face, neck, and collarbone were covered in wounds, but they seemed to be healing. The bandage on her abdomen had been changed.

"What have you done with Jace Sesso?" the elf said coldly.

Jace involuntarily swallowed. He realized that the incantations he'd just been muttering were probably what had caused the ranger to misunderstand.

"I am Jace Sesso," Jace said. "Teron Gorefiend is dead."

"You think I'd fall for that again?" Vereesa said. "I won't leave this body to you, monster. Let's see you try to cling to life this time!"

The elf raised her blade. Jace glanced at the gleaming edge and immediately said, "Wait! You still have that Blessed Wizard Oil, right? Give me a little and pour it on my skin. If I were a death knight, I'd definitely react."

Vereesa hesitated for a moment, then finally nodded.

She fixed her eyes on Jace and backed up to the pack, pulling out a bottle of the golden medicinal oil that was less than half full and handing it over.

Jace took it, poured a drop into his palm, then raised his hand. "See? Nothing hap—" Before he could finish, white smoke rose from his palm.

"Uh… ah, ah…"

A burst of agony sufficient to drive a person mad came from his palm. Jace howled and frantically wiped the stuff off on his sleeve, then a wave of violent nausea hit him. He ran to the side, braced against the stone wall, and vomited up puddle after puddle of black corpse-water.

"What happened…"

He kept muttering as he dry-heaved incessantly. Vereesa watched Jace's state and seemed to hesitate too — would Teron Gorefiend be stupid enough to grab holy energy and use it against himself like this?

"What the hell is going on?" Jace turned his head. "Why did I get burned? Am I a dead man now? But I'm still breathing."

He took two deep, forceful breaths, even coughing from sucking in too hard.

Vereesa glanced at Jace's palm, then touched Jace's neck — she could feel the surging of his blood vessels.

"You're still alive."

"Then… why? Why would this wizard oil burn me?"

"I suspect the corpse poison has infected your body," Vereesa said, looking at the black water on the ground. "You vomited a lot of it while you were unconscious."

Jace recalled the image of the orc vomiting wildly in his mind earlier. Chances were Teron Gorefiend had indeed forcibly occupied a part of his body — he just hadn't seized control. He was a powerful death knight who knew exactly how to manipulate soul force. So in the mindscape, Jace's soul had been wantonly trampled like helpless prey.

But in the end, it was inside Jace's mind. That world wasn't his to command.

"How long was I out?" Jace asked.

Vereesa said, "Maybe a day, or maybe a night. It's been dark this whole time. I couldn't tell. So what exactly happened?"

Jace said, "Teron Gorefiend burrowed into my head."

"I saw," Vereesa said. "But when you woke up, you were still muttering those… those words. They sounded like the ravings of a demon. I thought…"

"Those were things from his head," Jace said. "His consciousness was fading fast. I wanted to remember at least some of it before it disappeared, so I spoke them out loud to help me memorize them."

"What were they?" Vereesa asked.

"Incantations, conversations, some images. I can't say exactly. But those were things Teron Gorefiend remembered." Jace sighed. "Damn it, they faded too fast. I don't know if I remembered them clearly enough."

"If these memories didn't disappear, then you wouldn't be you anymore, would you?" Vereesa said, sitting to the side. "At least this proves you defeated him."

Jace nodded. Suddenly another wave of intense nausea surged up.

He turned his head and dry-heaved several times in a row. By now, the black foul liquid he was bringing up was much less than before. Only then did he realize the entire temple was filled with the corpse-stench of the filth he'd vomited.

"How are you? I remember you… seemed to be injured." He looked at the elf.

"I'm fine," Vereesa said. "All superficial wounds. As frantic as he was, he was still just a spirit. Dealing with him was troublesome, but he was nowhere near as powerful as he'd been in Dalaran. But if he had really taken over your body, I don't know what would have happened."

Jace let out a breath. He looked toward the warlock's bag in the distance. "Sorry, I need to write these things down quickly, or it'll be a disaster if I forget them later."

He got up and ran over, opening it and pulling out two sheets of paper and a gryphon quill — only to remember that the ink bottle had already shattered inside the potion pouch.

At the time he hadn't cared at all. He'd even felt a bit lucky that one of the broken bottles was the ink. After all, who would have thought he'd end up needing to write something on this trip?

Jace caught his breath, concentrated, and thought back to what he'd learned in Inscription class about how to write with arcane energy. He set down his finger, ready to write the contents on the paper — but the moment the arcane energy touched the paper, the entire sheet burst into flames!

He didn't even have time to smother the fire. The whole sheet burned to ash in blue flames in an instant.

Right. This kind of paper couldn't possibly withstand the power of arcane writing — it was the cheapest kind, after all.

"Damn it…"

Just as he was cursing under his breath, a roll of parchment of some unfamiliar material reached over from beside his shoulder.

"Use this," Vereesa said. "I brought it from Quel'Thalas. I was originally going to find someone at the Wizard's Sanctum to make some magic scrolls for backup. I ended up forgetting too."

Jace nodded. "Thanks!"

He took the parchment, unrolled it, and began imprinting everything he'd recited from Gorefiend's memory onto the paper with arcane energy. For accuracy, he used Eredun symbols — that is, Demonic script — as much as possible, annotating the unclear parts with Common. After learning Demonic script with Sazeno for a while, he'd at least remembered the most basic pronunciations and characters.

These fragments of Gorefiend's memory that he'd deliberately captured were far more vivid than he'd expected — they hadn't been annihilated along with the dissipation of Gorefiend's will.

Looking at the four or five dense lines he'd rapidly written down, he even felt a bit of dread.

He wondered how much he could have transcribed if he'd been made to memorize all this without Arcane Intellect and then write it from memory.

Once finished, he stretched and muttered to himself, "Good… good. At least I can still remember."

"This sheet of paper could sell for sixty silver," Vereesa reminded him.

"What did you say?" Jace turned his head.

Vereesa said, "I brought four sheets. I sold two in the Trade District, but the money still wasn't enough. Could this one cover some of the commission?"

Jace held the paper between his fingers. "No. I've got to go back and ask the ink-and-paper shop owner first. You say sixty and it's sixty? I'm not a fool. You sold two for sixty silver — if you had that much money, would you be living this badly?"

Though Jace pressed her question by question, Vereesa showed no sign of being unhappy at all. Instead, the tension that had been in her eyes this whole time, unable to dissipate, faded considerably.

She listened to Jace finish, then leaned against the wall beside her and took a deep breath.

"It won't help if you don't believe me, Jace Sesso. When we get back, I'll show you."
