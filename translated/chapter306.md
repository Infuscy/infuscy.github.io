## Chapter 306: Shadow Gem

Jace tied up his bag and collapsed against the pillar. After committing Teron Gorefiend's incantations to memory, it was as if all his drive and momentum had drained away at once, replaced by crushing exhaustion and a splitting headache.

The entire hall seemed a little brighter than before — he could make out vague shapes and objects now. Probably because even in the Burning Steppes, daytime was still brighter than night.

Perhaps it was because Teron Gorefiend's spirit had perished — the darkness that had shrouded this place had lifted with it. At the very least, the unsettling, eerie atmosphere the temple had radiated before was gone.

The strange patterns on the pillars behind him, the reliefs on the walls — whether they had meant to signify the Old Gods or the Firelord — including the collapsed dwarven statues shattered at his feet, had all reverted to ordinary cold stone, now carrying only the stench of shadow and dead flesh.

"Vereesa, you haven't slept in a long time, have you?"

"This whole night has been a nightmare."

"So get some rest. I'll keep watch."

"You couldn't make me sleep even if you tried." Vereesa stared straight ahead at the wall. "The moment I close my eyes I see that wraith — and you, Jace. You might not like hearing this, but while his spirit was tormenting you, you looked even more terrifying than Teron Gorefiend." Jace looked at her, unable to imagine how much terror she must have endured, sitting beside a body that had been possessed by a demonic spirit for hours — maybe ten hours or more.

If he had been in her position, he might have chosen to stab the man through with a blade, just to prevent Teron Gorefiend from taking his body — to permanently end any possibility of him rising again.

After all, he couldn't guarantee he could win a fight alone if the man came back.

If Vereesa had done the same, he would have understood.

Of course, back then he hadn't exactly been in any state to understand anything.

"Can you tell me — how did you see through him in the first place?" Vereesa asked.

This question caught Jace off guard.

He had just woken from that dream-like struggle and had been focused on memorizing as much of Teron Gorefiend's memories as possible. He hadn't had time at all to think about how to paper over what had happened before the fight.

So he could only improvise as best he could.

"He said he wanted to find Marshal Anduin Lothar's body, and that he was sustaining his existence through the altar here. Relying on a Dark Iron dwarf's shadow altar to sustain himself? I couldn't help but be suspicious. Like you said before, this isn't some normal temple — it's something dark that the Dark Iron dwarves worshiped. More importantly, I remembered his aura, Vereesa. The moment that thing walked up to me, I knew my suspicions were right."

Truth be told, Jace had expected their initial ambush to cripple him outright. After all, in the game, stripped of his body he'd been nothing but a seemingly powerless orc spirit who had to trick people into gathering his relics from life in order to be reborn.

He hadn't expected him to unleash such fel power in his rage.

"That staff." Vereesa glanced behind her. "The black one kept glowing — it didn't go out until shortly before you woke up."

Jace turned to look at the staff mounted on the wall. He touched his back and realized that his own staff had been taken off at some point without him noticing.

Actually, even his shirt had been changed. Come to think of it, that made sense — he'd been lying there vomiting necromantic residue for ages. His shirt must have been ruined, and leaving it stuck to his skin would have rotted the flesh.

"You couldn't lie properly with a staff on your back," Vereesa said. "So I had to take it off you — and that's when I noticed the gem on that staff. That's the gem from Teron Gorefiend's staff, isn't it? I remember it clearly."

Seeing that Vereesa had already figured it out, Jace could only nod and admit it. "Fine. That gem is indeed Gorefiend's. I found it near his body that day."

"I thought you'd recognize the connection between him and that gem," Vereesa said.

Jace said, "It always glowed whenever I cast..."

He glanced at the staff, and it was suddenly wrenched straight to him by a force of shadow, nearly slamming into his hand. His heart skipped a beat.

Jace shot a look at Vereesa. She seemed completely unfazed — probably because in Quel'Thalas, mages who could magically pull their own staffs to hand were a dime a dozen.

But what really startled him wasn't how forcefully the staff had flown over. It was the realization that the fire staff leaning nearby and the pack on the ground had shifted too, as if they'd all been hit by a sudden gust of wind.

What was this — Gorefiend's Grasp? In the game, Teron Gorefiend's spell was a mass-pull ability that dragged everything nearby to his side.

Did he have that ability too?

He certainly couldn't test it here, not with an elf beside him who already suspected he might have been possessed by Teron Gorefiend. And Vereesa had seen Gorefiend's Grasp firsthand. Whether or not he could actually use that power, trying it here would make things impossible to explain.

"Actually, I have another theory about your staff. Have you considered why Gorefiend was willing to leave this place with us?" Vereesa asked.

"Didn't he want to possess Marshal Lothar's body?" Jace said.

Vereesa said, "He claimed his spirit endured because of this altar. That could be a ploy to win sympathy — but it might also be the truth. During the fight, I could feel how strongly his power was tied to the altar in this hall. And I've read stories about evil spirits sustaining themselves through ancient, forsaken dark altars. If he left this temple, his necromantic power might decay very quickly."

"Teron Gorefiend wouldn't take that kind of risk," Jace said. "Unless... unless he had a way to keep sustaining his power. You mean this gem?"

"At least, that's what I think," Vereesa said.

Jace had to admit Vereesa's reasoning made sense. After all, in the game, Gorefiend couldn't leave that altar in Shadowmoon Valley at all. For ages he could only wait nearby for players to take the bait, and even then, he could only ask players to bring his staff and other artifacts back to the altar to hand over.

In other words, Teron Gorefiend had sensed Jace and Vereesa approaching — perhaps even sensed the staff and its shadow gem drawing near. So he'd used his necromantic power to drive the dwarven spirits to herd the two of them here, all so that Jace would carry the gem on that staff and bring him to Lothar?

Then he could seize the chance to slip into Lothar's corpse. Actually, he wouldn't even need to find Lothar's body — just finding any intact human soldier's corpse in a mass grave and crawling inside would have been more than enough to give Jace and Vereesa a world of trouble.

A death knight's combat strength with a body versus without were on completely different levels. Think about how in Dalaran, a single swing of his hammer had torn half a house apart.

If Jace hadn't seen through him, the consequences would have been unthinkable.

"Almost got played for a fool," Jace sighed.
