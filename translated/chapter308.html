Chapter 308: Maiden of Silverlight Lake

In March, fresh green had returned to the trees along the streets of Elwynn Forest and Stormwind City. Jace finally felt the faintest trace of warmth in the wind blowing in from the sea.

Gazing at the ship in the harbor — the Ronal's Blue Parrot — Jace tugged at his collar and asked, "Is that your ship? The one heading to Menethil Harbor?"

"Yes." Vereesa held the edge of her hood and turned back. "So this is goodbye again, Jace."

Jace nodded. "Then fair winds... All right, I can't help but ask — how are you going to get the money from the sales to me?"

Vereesa smiled. "I'll have a magister send it to you by teleportation magic, straight to your apartment address. How's that? Including the thing I promised you."

Jace said, "That's fine, but you've kept your lips sealed the whole way back — even now, you won't give me a single hint?"

"This thing is definitely far more valuable than the Sunfire Blade, and as a mage, you'll definitely love it," Vereesa said. "The reason I don't want to say it now is that, strictly speaking, it isn't mine yet."

"But I will get it. You can count on that."

Jace's mind was spinning through all sorts of random loot tables related to high elves and blood elves, but he couldn't think of any particularly likely possibility.

But since Vereesa put it so enticingly, he was rather looking forward to it. A Windrunner wouldn't mess him around, would she?

"Then I'll wait."

"You won't be disappointed, anyway." Vereesa took a couple of steps, then turned back. "If you ever need my help, remember to write."

"Will a letter from you even make it to the gates of Quel'Thalas?" Jace asked.

"As long as you write your name on it," Vereesa said seriously. "I guarantee it will reach me."

"All right, then," Jace said. "Thank you!"

"No — thank you."

Vereesa waved and walked toward the pier.

Jace raised a hand, watching the elf's figure gradually merge into the crowd boarding the ship. The pale green cloak became less and less conspicuous. He took a deep breath of the salty, fishy sea breeze, then let it out slowly.

Gazing at the dark bow of the ship, he couldn't help but think of the incantations he'd memorized from Teron Gorefiend. Actually, he still hadn't figured out which were Gul'dan's voice and which were Gorefiend's own.

After returning, he'd gone to the Cathedral of Light to make sure there were no necromantic spirits hiding in the corners of his body, then reported back to Marin. He'd been so busy that he hadn't even had time to find his imp to carefully sort through the contents.

Besides, these were terrible things involving Gul'dan and Teron Gorefiend — absolutely not something to experiment with in Stormwind. What if one of those incantations was something Gul'dan had devised, like the "Hand of Gul'dan," and after speaking it, a shadow meteor came crashing down from the sky and leveled a house? That would be disastrous.

"Jace!"

Hearing a shout from above that was nearly swallowed by the crashing waves, Jace looked up.

At the bow of the ship, Vereesa was waving vigorously and shouting, "Next time I come to Stormwind, I'll come find you!"

"Then remember to teach me elvish!" Jace shouted back.

The elf paused for a moment, then shouted back, "It'll cost you!"

"Fine. Not learning anything decent, then."

Jace said softly, then plopped down onto a nearby wooden post.

A powerful wave of emptiness and loss rolled in with the sound of the waves that once again filled his ears.

He stared at the lower sea surface, then suddenly remembered the lunch Grede had eaten before setting out. He rubbed his face hard, trying to shake himself free from this uncomfortable feeling.

There was still a lot in front of him.

The most important thing for him right now was the Apprentice Mage exam on March 22nd. Two weeks left — nothing was more urgent than that. Everything else had to be set aside, because he'd already fallen behind for too long.

As for the contents of Gorefiend's memories, he wasn't in a hurry to study them either. After passing the Apprentice Mage exam and getting a better grip on Demonic, he could carefully delve into them bit by bit.

After that business with Jagin, not just Galders but everyone else at the Slaughtered Lamb — he was much more cautious about these new things now.

Besides, he knew that what he'd written down was probably far more dangerous than summoning a succubus. If he couldn't guarantee safety, he'd rather keep that scroll buried in his pack.

For the period that followed, Jace threw nearly all his energy into the library.

Studying in the library wasn't just for the Apprentice Mage exam — it was also to pull himself out of the endless, chaotic atmosphere of shadow and necromancy that had pervaded those two or three weeks, and return as quickly as possible to a state of calm.

Marin had told him the Apprentice Mage exam didn't have that many profound, grueling arcane problems. After all, going from apprentice to apprentice mage was just part of getting started — it wasn't going to demand that you instantly have a command of every deep theory like an elite member of the Kirin Tor. So the focus should be on breadth of knowledge.

So the priority was reading introductory arcane texts written by the masters of Dalaran, memorizing the key points. There were a few days when he practically slept in the library.

As for that bag of dragon claws and scales, he planned to wait until Grede arrived and see if there was a channel to sell them for coin. After all, for materials that could be used to craft equipment, a dwarf would surely know more knowledgeable contacts.

That morning, Jace arrived at Marin's study as usual with a long bread loaf in hand. He took a bite and opened the door. Amy was sitting inside, her hands resting on a long, narrow box, looking at the door.

The box was a bit wider than a scroll case, quite exquisite. It was made of wood overall, reinforced with an unidentified emerald-green metal bearing a leafy pattern. Along the edges, elvish script inscribed with arcane energy formed runes.

"You're finally here, Jace." She squinted at the box. "Hurry up and open it — see what this is."

"What's it got to do with me? Just open it yourself."

Jace asked offhandedly, then realized — could this be the payment Vereesa had sent?

"For me?"

"Of course," Amy said. "You didn't know someone sent you something? This came from Quel'Thalas — a package from Lady Vereesa Windrunner. It's sealed with a magic passphrase. The note on it, written in elvish, says the recipient knows the passphrase."

"How am I supposed to know the passphrase?" Jace asked, taken aback. "No hint?"

Amy turned the note over a couple of times. "No."

"Jace Sesso?"

"No reaction."

Just then Marin walked in. He glanced at the box and asked softly, "Whose is this? Such a beautiful box."

"Jace's," Amy said. "From Quel'Thalas. We're struggling with the magic passphrase — it says here that Jace knows it, but Jace can't remember."

The moment Marin heard "magic passphrase," his interest was piqued. He stepped closer and examined it back and forth. "Judging by the strength of this enchantment, the passphrase is probably about a dozen characters long. Can't you think of anything?"

"About a dozen characters..." Jace frowned and tried, "Maiden of Silverlight Lake?"

With a pop, the box cracked open on its own. The lid lifted gently, little by little.

Amy's eyes went wide in shock. She couldn't help but ask, "What's 'Maiden of Silverlight Lake'?"

"An old elvish song," Marin said. "Silverlight Lake is Lake Lordamere. Long ago, when the elves settled in the Tirisfal Glades, they called it that."

Amy nodded. "I've never heard of that."

Not just her — even Jace, who lived on the shores of Lake Lordamere, hadn't known.

Once the box had fully opened, the arcane runes dimmed, and a faint scent of unidentified flowers and herbs drifted out.

Jace leaned in and peeked inside. He found one long pouch and two smaller pouches, one large and one small. Every pouch was beautiful enough to look expensive at a glance.

Amy glanced inside and said, "Oh, this seems pretty important. Is it really okay for us to be watching... shouldn't you take it back and open it yourself?"

"It's fine," Jace said. "Let's take a look here. I can't wait, and I have class later — no time to go home. Master Marin, could you watch the morning for me?"

"Of course." Marin stole a sideways glance inside. "I'm quite curious too. Didn't Lady Vereesa tell you in advance?"

"I'm guessing this pouch is money. I helped her out with something before, and she said she'd pay me."

That said, Jace was actually more interested in the long pouch.

He picked up the larger of the two pouches beside him and shook it — about a dozen coins inside. He opened it: all gold. He quickly tied it shut again.

Marin looked at the pouch, then glanced sideways at Jace and clicked his tongue twice.

Jace smiled, then took out the smaller pouch and felt it. Judging by the shape, it should be that shadow gem.

He slipped this pouch straight into his pocket, then clamped the bread between his teeth and reached in with both hands to lift out the longest one.

This, of course, was the one he was most looking forward to.

What on earth could it be...

After untying it, Jace drew from inside a slender straight sword wrapped tightly in deep blue elven silk.

Slowly unwrapping the silk from the hilt, which was engraved with deep blue draconic runes, an oval sapphire set in the silver ornamental guard gradually came into view.

As the silk continued to come undone, he saw a smaller teardrop gem on the silver between the guard and the blade. When both sapphires were fully revealed, Marin drew a sharp breath.

"Dad, do you recognize this sword?" Amy asked.

Actually, Jace thought he'd guessed what this was too, but he still couldn't believe it — after all, he'd never imagined it would be this.

But his heart was already pounding.

Until all the silk was unwrapped. As the silk slid past the blade, a series of pleasant arcane chimes rang out, as if carrying a wondrous melody of its own — though not quite so distinct.

Once the blade was fully released, a faint, unobtrusive blue arcane glow gently radiated from the steel and slowly swirled around it.

"I've only seen illustrations before — this is the first time I've seen the real thing. This is the legendary blue dragon sword," Marin said, his eyes fixed on the silver edge. "Song of the Blue Sky."
