## Chapter 309: Letter from the Elven Kingdom

"Pure arcane energy emits a white or violet glow, but when mixed with other types of energy, it takes on the characteristics and appearance of those energies — like the golden-yellow formed when mixed with fire elements, or... or..."

Jace glanced at the notes in his hand, closed his eyes, and said, "Gryphon Red. Gryphon Red — what color is that? Gryphons have a whole bunch of feather colors. How's anyone supposed to remember this? Damn it..."

He glanced down the corridor. At the end, several apprentices were waiting to go in for the exam. They were all younger than him — most of them fifteen or sixteen.

And he was already eighteen. The physical and appearance gap between him and them was almost immediately visible, which made Jace feel even more pressure.

If he couldn't pass the March exam, the next one would be in August — and that would bring him one step closer to twenty.

Becoming an apprentice mage at twenty wasn't anything to be proud of... Eighteen was fine. Passing the Apprentice Mage exam at eighteen was a fairly normal age. So let him pass this time.

Truth be told, Jace had no confidence at all. He hadn't prepared enough, and these past weeks he'd been plagued by all sorts of ailments. His life had basically shrunk to a straight line between the cathedral and the library.

Fortunately, there were plenty of immigrants from Duskwood coming to treat necrotic plague too, so he didn't stand out as much.

Damn it, his mind wandered again. He looked down at his materials, closed his eyes, and started memorizing again.

"Mr. Jace Sesso!"

"Coming!"

Jace looked up toward the exam room door, but no one was waving him in.

"This way," a voice behind him said.

"Uh." Jace turned around and saw Senior Mage Andormas standing there.

Jace hadn't seen Senior Mage Andormas in a long time, but he looked exactly the same as he had months ago.

And because he was standing among the young people nearby, he actually looked even more burly and robust — like an ox that had wandered into a flock of sheep.

"Come here a moment, Mr. Jace Sesso. I need to speak with you," he beckoned.

Jace said reluctantly, "Master, I'm about to take the Apprentice Mage exam. It's almost my turn. I've been preparing for a long time..."

Andormas raised an eyebrow. "That won't be necessary. Come with me, Mr. Sesso."

What did this mean... Was he going to make him take another spellcasting test?

Jace stood stunned for a moment, glanced back at the exam room, then had no choice but to follow Andormas.

Andormas led Jace through several turns in the Wizard Sanctum's high tower, finally arriving at an empty corridor. He brought him to an open doorway.

He glanced inside, then looked up and down the adjacent corridor to make sure no one was around, then leaned against the wall and said, "King Varian received a letter from Quel'Thalas — a personal letter from Lady Sylvanas Windrunner, Ranger-General of Silvermoon. You should know of her."

"I know of her, but she doesn't know me," Jace said.

"I doubt that very much, Jace." Andormas looked into the young mage's eyes and said, "After all, that letter mentions that you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner found Teron Gorefiend's fleeing spirit in the burned city of Thorasson, and it specifically thanks you for taking care of her younger sister."

Taking care of her own sister?

Try as he might, Jace couldn't imagine Sylvanas Windrunner writing something like that.

Andormas looked at Jace's expression and asked, "So it's true, then? Master Marin helped you take leave in the latter half of February. I heard you went to Lakeshire. So you actually went north? This trip?"

"Yes, Master," Jace said. "That trip."

"So why didn't you say anything? I mean, you went to hunt Teron Gorefiend — why didn't you seek help from the King and the Wizard Sanctum in advance?" Andormas said. "Did you have some other difficulty?"

"We weren't originally hunting Gorefiend," Jace admitted. "Lady Vereesa Windrunner believed there were leads on a death knight there. She wanted me to help her investigate. Who knew we'd run into..."

"So Teron Gorefiend came to you," Andormas said. "Is it related to that skirmish in Dalaran? I know you nearly got killed by him, and Quel'Thalas's Farstrider corps rescued you."

"Somewhat," Jace nodded. "But... it's actually quite complicated, Master."

"I'm just here to verify this, Jace," Andormas said. "Right now in Stormwind, only I and a few other masters know about this letter. Over at Stormwind Keep, only Lord Bolvar Fordragon and the King know. It hasn't been made public to anyone else. The reason for this is that you kept this matter hidden for over half a month for unknown reasons, and King Varian thought you had some reservations. Still, shouldn't something like this be reported to the King immediately?"

Of course it should have been.

But the reason Jace had hesitated for so long wasn't because he didn't want to tell anyone he'd killed Teron Gorefiend.

For one thing, he had no evidence. Once the King asked him what important items related to Teron Gorefiend he'd brought back, he couldn't exactly vomit a puddle of black sludge in the palace, could he? After all, he'd already given Gorefiend's shadow gem to Vereesa.

Gorefiend had perished, soul scattered to the winds. To this day, aside from that gem and the piece of paper he'd written down that could land him in prison, there was basically nothing. The body he'd used in life was in Dalaran, and the credit went to the Farstriders too — after all, in official terms, he was nothing more than an apprentice who'd been hunted and nearly killed.

Thinking of that body, he felt discomfort in his abdomen again. Even though more than half a month had passed, he still hadn't completely shaken off the impact that battle had left on him.

On the other hand, was the human he'd seen in Gorefiend's memories actually Kel'Thuzad? If he learned that Teron Gorefiend's death was connected to Jace, how would he react?

He naturally had no idea at all. All these tangled concerns, combined with facing the Apprentice Mage exam and the aftereffects and follow-up treatment from the necrotic residue, left him with no energy to deal with the pressure from the King or the Wizard Sanctum.

Several times he'd wanted to bring it up with Marin, but for various reasons he'd held back each time.

At the time, Jace had also considered that Quel'Thalas might write to the Wizard Sanctum or King Varian to explain the matter, so he'd thought it would be better to let the elves speak for him.

He just hadn't expected this day to come so soon. In truth, he hadn't mentally prepared himself yet.

"Lady Vereesa didn't want her movements to affect the kingdom," Jace tried to explain. "And in this operation, I was essentially hired help, so I had to follow her instructions too, Senior Mage Andormas. If I'd leaked everything in advance, I might not have gotten my payment. Of course, now that Lady Vereesa and her sister are willing to inform the King of everything, there's no need for me to hide it anymore."

"And I've been busy with the exam too, Senior Mage..." Andormas folded his arms and sighed. "I discussed it privately with those few masters, including Master Marin. Jace Sesso, we're considering promoting you to full mage."

"Full...?" Jace was taken aback.

Andormas said, "Just considering, of course. Because in the entire history of the Wizard Sanctum, there have been very few geniuses who became full mages before the age of twenty. And Jace, you clearly possess combat talent, and you clearly know how to deal with ludicrously powerful opponents. It's just that in arcane theory, you still have room for improvement..."

Jace coughed. "I understand... Master."

"So our current provisional decision is to promote you to apprentice mage first. Then, two years from now, when you turn twenty, you'll take the full mage assessment and we'll promote you from there."
