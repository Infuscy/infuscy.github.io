## Chapter 310: Unexpected Changes

"So I don't have to take the exam?" Jace asked.

"At least this exam is off," Andormas said. "If a mage of the Wizard Sanctum destroyed Teron Gorefiend, and he couldn't even pass the apprentice mage exam here... then we'd all be in trouble."

He took a pocket watch from his robe and glanced at it. "Besides, you've already missed it. I'll go back and communicate with the other masters — we'll have you pass. Ultimately, the promotion of young apprentice mages comes down to the masters' judgment anyway."

"As for the exam after that — how about this: could you write a detailed report on death knights during this time and give it to your teacher, Master Marin, for him to review? I don't think there's a single mage to this day — whether a teacher at the academy or any other mage — who has had an experience like yours. It's utterly unique. And with Lady Vereesa Windrunner as a witness to your experience, no one in the Wizard Sanctum can question your qualifications to become a full mage."

"I'll write it, Master," Jace nodded.

"There's one more thing." Andormas cleared his throat. "The King wants to see you. He asked me to find a suitable time. I'm thinking next Monday — the 25th. That's the King's wish as well."

"The King wants to see me?" Jace asked. "Is it..."

"He certainly wants to give you some encouragement and commendation, Jace," Andormas said. "Of course, I can't speculate. In any case, it won't be anything bad. You'll know when the time comes."

Jace nodded. The King wanted to see him — he certainly had no choice in the matter.

Looking at Jace's expression, Andormas sighed. "Am I being a little too casual about this?"

"When I first received this news, I couldn't imagine what was going on at all, Jace. Because in my impression, though you've been through quite a bit, you were still just a brave and excellent young mage. But this time, I and the other few masters are having a hard time even picturing it."

"Never mind the Wizard Sanctum — even for the archmages of the Kirin Tor, Teron Gorefiend was a terrifying enemy."

"I can't fathom how, after going through all of this, you were able to return to Stormwind and carry on with your daily life. If I had faced Teron Gorefiend as an opponent at your age, I would likely have been completely at a loss. If you need anything, you can talk to Ms. Cannon. You know she's counseled many mages who've had a hard time moving on from the horrific battles they experienced in the war."

Jace said, "Actually, back in Dalaran I had a feeling, Senior Mage Andormas — that Teron Gorefiend would never let me go. Many nights after that, I dreamed of this battle. Perhaps I'd already mentally prepared myself for this fight. And Lady Vereesa's payment was generous — I really did need that money."

The pouch Vereesa had sent contained sixteen gold coins, and the box itself, plus the pouches inside, could considerably improve Jace's life. After all, the shabby boxes at home weren't nearly this large, and the pouches weren't this sturdy — except for that warlock's satchel he'd picked up from the orc warlock's corpse.

Andormas said, "The Quel'dorei have always been generous with their coin, and she found the right person. Prepare yourself, Jace Sesso — this is definitely going to get out."

"I understand," Jace said.

Jace had brought some silver coins in his pocket for this exam. He planned to go to Larson's again and buy a mage robe.

Every robe he'd ever bought with the intention of wearing for life had died a quick death within a month, or even a week. The last time he'd brought his cloak and that Quel'dorei purple short robe to Larson's shop for repairs, Ivan Larson — James's nephew, who was minding the store — had just shaken his head and said it would be better to just replace them outright.

So this time, no matter what, he didn't want to buy anything too expensive.

Besides, it didn't seem like he'd be facing any dangerous situations in the near future. Grede didn't know when he'd be back, and everyone at the Slaughtered Lamb had been behaving themselves, so he didn't need an expensive enchanted robe either.

After walking into the shop, James Larson squinted against the midday sunlight outside, shielding his hand over his eyes. "Sir, what can I do for you?"

"Mr. Larson, don't you recognize me?" Jace asked.

"Uh." James tilted his head and tried a different angle. "It's Mr. Jace Sesso. Have you gotten taller? When was the last time you came in? I remember you've been in once or twice this year, right? Or have I reached the age where I can't remember things anymore?"

"Gotten taller?" Jace smiled. "Impossible. I was in a month ago, with that elf. You forgot?"

"Now I remember," Larson smiled. "Though at your age, you're not growing as fast as a ten-year-old, you haven't stopped yet!"

"So what can I do for you this time? How can I help?"

Jace looked at Larson in silence for a moment. He suddenly thought of the black dragon he'd drained to death in the ruins with Drain Vitality.

From then until now, he hadn't properly tested whether his power had grown stronger or whether his physical condition had changed.

Was Larson's feeling that he'd gotten taller really just an illusion?

He recalled that these past nights when he couldn't sleep, it wasn't entirely due to the nightmares and hallucinations brought on by the residual shadow and necromantic energy from his battle with Teron Gorefiend — there was also a physical discomfort.

"Still thinking?" Larson asked.

"I'd like to measure my height first, Mr. Larson, and then order a robe," Jace said.

Larson nodded and turned to head inside. "What's the budget this time?"

"Ten silver," Jace said. "Summer's coming soon. I'd like something loose."

"So just a regular robe, right? No special enchantments, no expensive materials." Larson came out with a measuring rod. "No travel plans these days?"

"None for now," Jace said. "Preferably brown or gray. Something ordinary."

Larson pointed to a platform beside him. "Then step up here, Mr. Jace Sesso. When was the last time you were measured? Was it here too?"

"Probably, but that was several months ago," Jace said, stepping up.

Staring at the measuring rod in Larson's hand, he could feel his heart pounding against his ribs. He'd never imagined he'd be this nervous about having his height measured.

"Five feet ten inches, Mr. Sesso. What was it last time?"

Hearing that measurement, Jace felt a roar in his head. He looked into the shopkeeper's eyes.

"Are you sure?"

"Of course I'm sure. It's right here, isn't it?" Larson pointed to the markings on the rod.

Jace nodded. "Last time was about the same. Maybe a little higher, or maybe last time it was measured short."

"Then you definitely weren't measured here." Larson smiled, put away the rod, and said, "Let me pick out a few that will fit you. Just a moment, young sir."

"Thank you, Mr. Larson," Jace said. Five feet ten inches was roughly 178 centimeters. When he'd arrived for term on January 20th, he'd only been about 173 to 174 centimeters.

If he could grow four centimeters naturally in two months, wouldn't that mean twenty-four centimeters in a full year?

So something definitely wasn't natural.
