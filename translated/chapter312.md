## Chapter 312: The Shadow That Cannot Be Shaken

That afternoon, Jace stood at the entrance to the cathedral square. At the end of the street before him, the high walls of Stormwind Keep were faintly visible.

By rights, although he felt somewhat unwell, compared to the patients coming from Duskwood he could practically be described as bouncing off the walls.

But that faint stench of rotting corpse just couldn't be dispelled.

Even having a priest purify him with the Holy Light could only drive the smell away temporarily. After a while, it would creep back in, faint but unmistakable.

At first Jace thought he just hadn't been purified thoroughly enough, but now he felt something was off about the whole thing.

He looked to the side, gazing far off at the still-repairing spire of the Cathedral of Light. Clear sky, pale clouds. Despite the crisp air and cool temperature, and the fact that Master Andormas had just promised to promote him to apprentice mage — a time when he should have been in excellent spirits — he always felt there was a layer of gloom in his heart. Even the height increase he'd discovered earlier had started to feel sinister.

A terrible thought slowly surfaced.

Was he going to rise as undead?

When Teron Gorefiend had seized his body, he had shoved Jace's own soul out of the body at the same time. Although Jace had won in the end, had he still been seriously affected? So now what was happening... was he possessing himself?

Jace turned around, crouched by the canal, scooped up water, and scrubbed his face hard, suppressing his increasingly outlandish theories.

He shook off his hands, sniffed his freshly changed clothes, confirmed there was no longer any of Morphiel's strange scent on them, and headed toward the cathedral square.

As usual, he came to the floor beside the cathedral where the priests treated corpse poison and infections. The moment he arrived, he could familiar stench of rotting corpses — most of it coming off the patients.

But the smell on him was quite different from that corpse stench.

Jace could feel it. The scent on him wasn't the simple smell of a decomposing corpse.

As several patients entered the room, Jace even found himself instinctively wanting to keep his distance — not out of fear that their diseases might infect him, but out of fear that his own bizarre problems might infect them.

In short, the ominous feeling in his heart was getting worse and worse.

"I remember you, mage," a young priest said. "Still no improvement?"

"I don't know if it counts as improvement. It's better than at the beginning, but I feel like it still relapses..." Jace said. "And I feel my problem is very different from the other patients'. Could it not be corpse poison, but some other rarer disease?"

The young priest leaned in and sniffed Jace, then shook his head. "It's already very faint. How about I try purifying you again, and you come back next time and we'll see? I think if there's improvement, that's good. If you're not confident about it, I can also find a more experienced priest to look at you."

"I'd still like to be more thorough, brother. I'm sorry," Jace said.

"Don't worry about it, sir," the priest said. "You're from the Wizard Sanctum — you're probably more sensitive to any possible evil magic than we are. I only joined the church after the Orc Wars, so there's still a lot I don't know."

With that, he spoke with the priests beside him and went back through the rear door.

Jace frowned, thinking about his encounter with Teron Gorefiend's ghost, then recalling the ghosts he'd encountered in the ruins of Thaurissan, considering whether he might have picked up something wrong somewhere else.

As he thought, a middle-aged priest with a severely receding hairline came to the front.

"Welcome to the Cathedral of Light, mage. I heard from my student that you've run into a rare trouble. I wonder if I might be of some help."

Seeing his hair, Jace immediately felt much more at ease.

"I caught this strange disease last month, brother. At first there was always a weird corpse-rot smell coming off me that wouldn't wash off no matter what. And I often felt exhausted, had a hard time sleeping at night, and would have nightmares. I've had priests purify me several times before — after each purification it would only work for a while, but slowly the smell would come back again. Although it's fainter now, something feels off to me. I feel more like it's some kind of magic." As the priest listened to Jace's account, his expression slowly grew serious.

"Does this problem affect your daily life? Such as walking, working, your research, spellcasting, and so on?"

"It affects them occasionally, but not much," Jace admitted. "I'm worried it might be some kind of chronic disease, something hard to root out. Is it possible I've come down with some particularly severe necromantic infection without even knowing it?"

"How should I address you, mage?" the priest said. "My name is Glenn Fading. You can call me Brother Fading. Please come with me this way. I think your problem may be more troublesome than you imagine."

"Jace Sesso."

Jace said it, but in his heart he was thinking: more troublesome than he imagined — what could that possibly be?

He already felt like he was about to turn undead. How much worse could it get?

Brother Fading led Jace to a side door upstairs. After opening the door, the brother brewed two cups of clear tea and handed one to Jace. "Try this."

Jace took the tea and drank a sip. It looked like it had been brewed strong, but he couldn't taste anything out of the ordinary.

"A special floral tea, Mr. Sesso," Fading said. "You can't taste it, can't you? Based on your description, plus the faint putrid fishy smell on you, plus your reaction to this cup of tea, I think you may have been cursed — not simply plagued or necrotically poisoned."

"Cursed?" Jace set down his tea. "What curse?"

Fading sat down across from Jace. "Could you tell me frankly what you came into contact with, or what accident you had, after which you started having this problem? Because I haven't encountered a case like yours in a very long time!"

Hearing this, Jace sensed that this priest was probably quite skilled.

"I took part in a battle to hunt down a death knight, brother. A fairly powerful death knight. Although he was already at the end of his rope... he was still ferocious."

"Just as I expected. I can confirm exactly what problem you've run into," Brother Fading said, looking into Jace's eyes.

Jace looked up immediately.

The priest said, "I was there when the Alliance army fought the Horde's invading forces on the continent of Lordaeron, Mr. Sesso."

"After the Horde suffered a setback at the Battle of Hillsbrad, they turned toward the Hinterlands to gather the Amani troll reinforcements. The Wildhammer dwarves met the orc legions there, while our forces needed to hold the Hillsbrad line to prevent the Horde from counterattacking — but we also couldn't sit by and watch the dwarves of Aerie Peak be ground down in a bitter fight."

"But right around that time, terrifying ghosts started appearing from time to time in camps, castles, and watchtowers. They used dark magic and their cold ghostly claws to wantonly slaughter soldiers in their sleep. Every night we'd lose a dozen, even dozens of young men. For soldiers who had just gone through the horrific Battle of Hillsbrad, this was a devastating blow to morale, and it seriously interfered with our plans to support our dwarven allies from the west."

"At first we didn't know why. Many soldiers lost their minds over it — so frightened that they'd rather desert and be captured and executed or thrown in the stockade than sleep in the camp."

"Lord Genn Greymane of the Silver Hand and we priests urgently investigated these incidents together, and finally uncovered Teron Gorefiend's plot. We surmised that in the final stage of the Battle of Hillsbrad, he realized the Horde might be unable to push westward any further, so he and the death knights secretly cursed our soldiers."

"This is a most vicious curse. For a long time it won't have any effect on a soldier, making people think they're healthy. But once a priest thinks you're perfectly fine and believes you can return to the other soldiers, your condition will suddenly deteriorate at a certain moment, and then this curse will kill you in your sleep. Immediately after, it transforms your soul into a furious wraith, sowing fear throughout the entire force."

"We call it the 'Death Shadow.'"

This curse reminded Jace of a certain boss mechanic from Teron Gorefiend's encounter in the game.

Gorefiend would randomly curse a player to die after a few dozen seconds, then turn their corpse into a skeletal construct that attacked their teammates.

Of course, this was the power possessed by the resurrected Gorefiend after the quest chain in Outland. This Death Shadow, on the other hand, directly drove a person's soul into a mad killing frenzy — and the timing wasn't as sudden as thirty seconds. It was a long, indefinite wait, designed precisely to give the cursed person time to return to peaceful crowds and create even greater panic.

At that moment, he remembered the words Teron Gorefiend's soul had spoken just before it annihilated.

So that curse wasn't just empty words — it was real.

Which meant this potentially lethal curse had already been on him for the better part of a month?

No wonder the young priests couldn't detect it. It had been so well hidden even under the watch of the Silver Hand paladins.

The brother continued: "It took us a very long time to figure out the characteristics of soldiers afflicted by this curse. The most obvious was the faint, indescribable smell of decay that couldn't be dispelled, plus nightmares frequent enough to be abnormal, and a loss of the ability to smell certain special plants."

"That smell could probably only be detected by the most sensitive mages, or the powerful spellbreakers of the high elves. Other people would be disturbed by all sorts of situations and simply couldn't make a judgment. For example, if a soldier had just defeated an undead creature summoned by a death knight, then everyone would assume the smell on him was caused by that undead creature — and the nightmares could also be trauma left over from battle."

"So the braver the soldier, the more dangerous they were. This terrible curse caused enormous obstruction to our pursuit over the following weeks and even months."

"The Battle of Hillsbrad was already three years ago, sir. Nobody would have expected to run into this difficult problem again."

With that, the brother sighed deeply.

Perhaps because he had mentally prepared himself, or perhaps because Jace had known from the start that Teron Gorefiend would never let him off easy, he didn't feel too much surprise or panic.

"Then, generally speaking, how long does it take from being cursed to the outbreak and death?" he asked.

The brother explained: "For some young warriors with weaker willpower, it's only a matter of days. As for the longer-lasting ones... it's hard to say. It could be several weeks or even months. So by the time Khaz Modan was liberated, Lordaeron's army was still dealing with this problem. Later, the Order of the Silver Hand discovered that using the power of the Holy Light for healing could slow down the outbreak time, but to this day the entire Alliance has no suitable way to cure it completely."

"Most people who were cursed have already died. Some, after long-term care, went mad from months or even years of living in fear. Some chose to end their lives — especially the combat heroes, who couldn't accept that one day they would turn into wraiths and attack the innocent."

"What?" Jace asked. "So, brother, if I die before the curse finally breaks out, my soul won't turn into a wraith?"

The brother nodded. "It should be so. The curse would become ineffective."

This was actually a bit surprising to Jace. In the game, if you got hit by Death Shadow, your corpse would immediately be transformed into a skeletal construct and start attacking nearby players.

If it weren't for gameplay and raid difficulty needs, later on, after Teron Gorefiend in the Black Temple chose to swear allegiance to Illidan Stormrage, his power would have been far greater than when he was in Dalaran.

"But there's no need to think about this right now, Mr. Sesso," Brother Fading said. "You can try to hold on as long as possible. After all, your condition doesn't look serious. Taking a step back, there are also a very small number of people who recovered for no reason at all. No magic, curse, or witchcraft can be one hundred percent effective. Moreover, to combat terrifying opponents like death knights, the Church has been searching for ways to treat the damage they cause — this curse is one of them. You can go to the Mage Quarter first and find a master to try to dispel it. The Church can help you slow down its progression as much as possible."

"Still, I hope you'll come to the Cathedral of Light every week to let us observe how your condition is progressing. After all, we're in Stormwind. I don't want to say this, but we should also prepare for the worst."

"I understand, brother. So can mages dispel this kind of curse?" Jace asked.

"Some can, some can't," the brother said. "I suspect it may have to do with the strength of the person who cast the curse. You haven't tried finding a mage before?"

"I always thought I'd been poisoned, or..." Jace frowned. "Anyway, thank you, brother. I'll go ask around the Mage Quarter."

Brother Fading nodded, placed his hand on Jace's shoulder, and murmured a prayer. Jace was in no mood to listen to his chanting anymore — his mind was a tangled mess right now.

Fortunately, perhaps because Teron Gorefiend's soul in its spiritual state was far less powerful than when he had a body, or perhaps because when he was inside Jace's head he was already in a severely suppressed state, the Death Shadow he had ultimately released wasn't progressing as quickly as Brother Fading had described.

But this was still Death Shadow... the final result was death. He couldn't rely entirely on luck to hit that tiny chance of natural recovery.

——Fine, you win. I just hope your soul gets gnawed upon by thousands of felhounds! I hope you meet an end more tragic than Gul'dan's! I curse you!

Teron Gorefiend's furious roar still echoed in his mind, and it grew clearer and clearer as the Holy Light seared.
