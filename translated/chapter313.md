## Chapter 313: No Way Out

The next day, Jace arrived early at Marin's study and sat there waiting until the old mage walked through the door.

"Master." He sprang to his feet with a clatter, shoving the chair behind him.

"What is it, Mr. Sesso?" Marin looked him over and asked with a hint of mockery, "Heard you're going to become a formal mage and you're so excited you can't sit still?"

"No." Jace steadied the chair.

"Right — have you tried that blue dragon sword this week?" Marin said curiously. "I've been looking forward to you telling me its true power. I've heard so many stories... if you really can't sense its power, you could lend it to me to try."

"Just a joke, Jace. But why do the looks so terrible? Run into some trouble?"

"Master, can you help dispel a death knight's curse?" Jace asked.

Hearing this question, Marin's face immediately went stern.

"A death knight's curse? Teron Gorefiend's curse?"

"I think it should be," Jace hesitated. "Can't you smell the corpse-rot stench on me?"

Marin frowned and sniffed. "There seems to be a little, but it's really not obvious. This doesn't smell like corpse-rot in the strict sense — it's more like some kind of dark magic. Is that the smell the curse gives off? Damn... if you hadn't mentioned it, I really would have had a hard time noticing. This reminds me of the time before in the Hillsbrad Foothills... wait, this is the Death Shadow, isn't it?"

Jace nodded. "Looks like the Master knows of it too."

"I've only heard of it," Marin said. "Let me try dispelling it for you."

Jace nodded and stood in the middle of the room.

Marin picked a wand out of the cabinet, pointed it at Jace, and moved his lips.

Jace only felt a ringing in his ears like an arcane blast. Waves of mana energy seemed to flow right along his skin all the way up to his brain. It felt as clear and bright as if he'd been washed head to toe with arcane energy.

Did this count as dispelled? Teron Gorefiend's curse?

Jace sniffed his clothes. There still seemed to be a little smell — but was this residual odor, or was the curse still there?

Marin shook his head. "Not really. I feel like I didn't run into anything at all. You are indeed cursed, but this curse is solid, like it's nailed inside. With my own mana, I simply can't budge it. The priests at the Cathedral of Light couldn't do anything either? After all, this is a curse inflicted by undead."

"The brother there said the Church of the Holy Light has no cure to this day," Jace said.

So was he going to have to rely on priests constantly purifying him to keep him alive for the rest of his life?

"Would the King have a solution?" Jace asked. "The day after tomorrow I'm going to Stormwind Keep to see King Varian. Would he have some way to cure this curse?"

Marin hesitated for a moment, then said, "The King's understanding of magic also comes from the court mages, and most of the court mages came over from the Wizard Sanctum, so I suggest you don't get your hopes up too high. Either way, he'd go to the Cathedral of Light on your behalf for help — but the problem is, you've already been to the Cathedral of Light. Are you sure the priest who treated you is authoritative enough?"

"He lived through the Battle of Hillsbrad," Jace said.

"In that case... the priests who experienced that battle are all elite among the elite by now." Marin blinked. "But at least for now it looks like you're still fine."

Jace said, "The priest said Holy Light healing and purification can alleviate it somewhat, but I definitely can't go on like this forever. Who knows what might happen."

Marin said, "I'll help ask around for you, Mr. Sesso. Maybe the Kirin Tor might already have a solution. The day after tomorrow, when you see the King, mention this to His Majesty. You were injured fighting Teron Gorefiend — he'll certainly be willing to provide some help."

"Right — have you considered asking Quel'Thalas for help?"

"I'll try everything," Jace said. "Then I'll go ask elsewhere first, Master."

"Sorry I couldn't be of more help. Good luck, Mr. Sesso. I've heard this kind of illness also has cases of mysteriously recovering — don't give up hope."

Jace nodded and came to the door of the study, gazing at the garden lit in sharp contrasts of light and shadow by the sunlight, thinking about what Marin had just said.

He had a point. In this situation, if he could get in touch with Windrunner, the elves might have a way to solve Teron Gorefiend's curse.

If even King Varian had no solution, then he could only write a letter to Windrunner and ask. Although writing a letter so soon after bidding farewell seemed a bit... but a life was more important than face.

Moreover, the fact that Windrunner had helped find someone to cure Grede's aftereffects from the bat poison had given him at least a little confidence.

But Jace also didn't want to sit around idly waiting these days — after all, several weeks had already been wasted. What other places hadn't he gone to yet?

Right. The alchemist.

In the morning, The Slaughtered Lamb tavern was empty. Jace looked at Tharle, who was sitting behind the counter in a daze, and asked, "Who's downstairs?"

"Ursula. She just came in over an hour ago," Tharle said. "Sulenna seems to be downstairs too, but I'm not sure about that."

"Did Galders come back?"

"Came back the day before yesterday, but he's not downstairs."

"Did he tell you about the situation in Ironforge?" the dark-skinned bartender said. "The warlocks in Ironforge, and those Dark Iron dwarves — they couldn't care less about Gajin's life or death. They think summoning a succubus and then getting yourself killed is the stupidest thing imaginable. They even said that maybe humans simply aren't suited to becoming warlocks. You hear that, Jace Sesso? That's how those bearded fellows see us."

After saying that, he glanced at Jace. "Still, don't let those people's words get you down."

"No, but those dwarves are a bit too cocky. They could use a lesson," Jace said. "Thanks, Tharle. I'm heading down."

Tharle nodded. Jace turned and walked into the basement, feeling his pace quicken unconsciously — after all, this was a matter of life and death.

"Ursula? Are you there?"

He called out a times before even reaching the alchemy lab door.

"I'm here, Jace."

Ursula, drenched in sweat, was answering while keeping her head down and tending her brew. The weather had gotten warmer than before, and the alchemy lab was getting harder and harder to cool down.

"How did the apprentice mage exam go? You didn't come at all yesterday — didn't go well?"

"That was fine. The problem is something else," Jace said, leaning against the cabinet beside him. "That long trip I took last month... in the end it didn't go so smoothly after all."

Hearing this, Ursula turned her head. "What's wrong?"
