Chapter 314: A Bizarre Potion

"I seem to have been cursed. A death knight's curse," Jace said. "The priests at the Cathedral of Light couldn't do anything, and my teacher at the Wizard Sanctum couldn't either."

"Jace, don't tell me it's that death curse that turns people into wraiths," Ursula said, her gaze piercing.

"Death Shadow." Jace gave a bitter laugh. "Am I really the last person in the world to know about this dark magic?"

Although he spoke in a relaxed tone, he watched Ursula's face visibly pale.

The alchemist put out the fire, blew away the smoke, and sat down to the side. After a moment of silence she said, "I know priests can slow down the outbreak time. Did you try having them help you?"

"I did," Jace said. "I'm guessing the reason my condition hasn't gotten worse over the past few weeks is because the priests have been helping me all along."

"Jace, I'm such a damn fool." Ursula sighed. "Last time you came to me and asked about poisoning, I never thought about this curse at all. I also thought it was just ordinary corpse poison."

Jace said, "Nobody thought of it. If I hadn't met a priest today who'd encountered death knights during the war, I'd still be in the dark. You don't have a way either, do you?"

Ursula didn't answer at first, just stared at the still-bubbling cauldron in silence.

After a while, she asked, "So this curse is going to kill a person, right?"

Jace glanced at her. "Yes, why?"

"Then what if you die first?" Ursula asked. "If you die ahead of time from some other cause, rather than being killed by the curse's power — will your soul still turn into a wraith?"

"The priest said no. Are you suggesting I commit suicide to save the innocent people of Stormwind?" Jace sighed. "I'll consider it, when I'm truly out of options."

"I'm saying you could try to trick this curse, Jace," Ursula said. "I know of a way to circumvent the death hexes of some Gurubashi troll witch doctors. Don't you want to try it?"

"What way?"

Jace asked, and at that moment he felt like he'd grabbed a lifeline.

Ursula said, "Goblin alchemists brew a very special medicine. I forgot its name, but I know it can create a perfect feigned death — it can turn a person into an undead creature. After all, the witch doctor hexes manipulated by the trolls rely on necromantic power, and the Horde's death knights also use necromantic magic. There shouldn't be any fundamental difference, right?"

She spoke as fast as if reciting a textbook from memory. Jace was dazed for a moment before he said, "Wouldn't that just kill me outright?! Think of a way to keep me alive instead!"

"Could you listen to me patiently?" Ursula said. "That medicine doesn't just kill you. Once the effect wears off, you wake back up — you might just sleep for a very long time. But after you wake up, all the death hexes will miss their target, Jace. Otherwise, how do those wealthy goblins who are obsessed with dealing with the trolls handle witch-doctor assassinations?"

"Hold on," Jace said. "You're not talking about Norgannon's Potion, are you?!"

"You actually know about it?!" Ursula said in surprise. "That weird, awful name — I thought I'd remember it for the rest of my life!"

In the game, Norgannon's Potion could turn a person into a skeleton. Jace had thought it was just some kind of joke item — he didn't expect it actually existed here too? Did such an absurd thing really exist?

But the one who knew how to brew this potion, Norgannon, lived in Gadgetzan in the southern part of Kalimdor. Was he supposed to sail across the ocean for this?

Of course, Jace couldn't just lay out all those details at once.

"Then where would I get it?"

"Booty Bay, of course," Ursula said. "The merchants in Booty Bay sell this potion, Jace. But the price is very, very expensive. If I were you, I'd bring plenty of gold coins."

"How much does one bottle cost? Do you have a rough estimate?" Jace asked.

"Frankly, I'm not too sure," Ursula said. "Only goblin alchemists know the recipe, and they never pass it on. I've even heard that its inventor only told a few close friends — not all goblin alchemists know it."

"As for what price the goblin merchants sell it for, I'm not too clear on that either, but it definitely won't be cheap. Judging by the effects of the other strange potions they sell, I'd estimate around ten gold."

"Ten gold?!" Jace sighed. In the game you could buy a stack of five for a few dozen silver — the difference here was really that big?

But thinking about it for a moment, he found that deep down he didn't feel it was too expensive. After all, this was something that might save his life.

Perhaps this was the price to be paid for obtaining a fearsome weapon like Azurewrath.

Considering that Windrunner and Grede had both praised Ursula's potions, and that Ursula also understood this curse and even knew so much about the problems between the trolls and goblins in the southern Stranglethorn Vale, he had to take the solution she proposed seriously.

"How long does a trip to Booty Bay take?" Jace asked.

"By sea, a voyage of a little under two weeks," Ursula said.

"Good lord." Jace sighed. "I might die halfway there before we even arrive, Ursula. Am I supposed to find a priest to accompany me? I can't possibly bring a priest I don't know to a place that dangerous — I can't tie my own hands by not using shadow magic."

Ursula said, "Just tell me whether you want to go or not."

Jace thought for a moment. He could write a letter asking Windrunner if the elves had a solution, then ask Marin to help him collect any reply. While that was happening, he could make a trip to Booty Bay and inquire about the situation.

"Of course I want to go."

"Then take this." Ursula turned around and pulled out a bottle of pale green liquid packaged in an extremely unusual material from the cabinet, walking over and placing it in front of Jace.

"Don't drink it all at once. Sip it slowly. One sip will gradually purify necrotic contamination over a period of time — it's very effective, much stronger than an ordinary priest's purification. If you're careful, this one bottle should last you two months."

Jace took it, opened the stopper, and sniffed. A faint scent of Goldthorn — a smell he was very familiar with, since he brewed tea for Marin all the time when he had nothing to do.

But behind that fragrance, there was clearly a hint of the scent of fresh earth after rain.

He quickly stoppered the bottle again and asked: "This... this is a Restorative Potion, isn't it?"

Jace still remembered asking the alchemy lab about Elemental Earth — how they'd had to write to the herbalists in Dalaran and Lordaeron to have it shipped in.

"You really do know a ridiculous amount of random knowledge," Ursula said with a sigh.

"This thing is too valuable," Jace said. "I..."

"More valuable than your life? If you die, where am I going to find such a capable helper?" the alchemist asked.

Jace looked at the bottle of medicine again. "Then how do I repay you?"

"Repay?" Ursula poked Jace's chest with her finger, saying each word deliberately: "Don't die, you hear me? Everything else is negotiable. I just can't guarantee this will be one hundred percent effective, Jace. If it works, don't forget to bring me back some fel essence."

"Even if it doesn't work, I'll bring you some," Jace said.
