## Chapter 315: Preparing for a New Adventure (Part 1)

Jace's knowledge of Booty Bay... frankly, aside from the game's content and a few bits and pieces mentioned by the people around him, there wasn't much left.

And Booty Bay wasn't Dalaran or Lakeshire — a relatively peaceful place where you could take your time figuring things out after arriving. This was a free port where all manner of people gathered: gladiators, mercenaries, pirates, merchants, wild mages. Charging in unprepared would definitely cost you.

Back home, Jace sized up his cloak, which had been burned nearly in half.

Although it could still barely be worn, this cloak was quite rare and distinctive. If he wore it, he might be recognized by others — especially since it was torn.

On this trip to Booty Bay, he'd best use a false name and hide his identity as much as possible.

Besides, there shouldn't be much fire magic in Booty Bay. Compared to other cloaks, this Ashenvale dragon cloak held no particular advantage.

So he might as well buy a new one — at least something less conspicuous.

"Right, I'm planning to take you two out on a trip," he said to the succubus.

"A trip?!"

Morphiel had been sitting on the bed, bored, scratching her horns. The moment she heard this, she sat bolt upright. "Has my eternal imprisonment finally ended?"

"Eternal... it's been all of two months, give or take?"

"Master, where are you going this time..." Sazeno asked weakly. "Don't let it be like last time. Don't make me fight black dragons... Master, let me live a few more days. I haven't seen enough of beautiful Azeroth yet. Oh, the ley lines..."

"We're not going to fight," Jace said. "I'm going to a goblin-run port to buy something."

"Mmm..." the succubus said softly from the side. "Sounds interesting. What does Master want to buy?"

"Actually, I have you to thank for this, Morphiel," Jace said. "If you hadn't reminded me there was still a corpse stench on me, I might have really overlooked my problem. In the end, the priests at the Cathedral of Light told me I've contracted a lethal curse."

"Lethal?!" The blue flames in Morphiel's eyes dimmed by half.

She grabbed Jace's shoulder, leaned in, and sniffed. "What the hell? What curse? How come I didn't know about it?"

"Probably a necromantic curse cooked up by a death knight. It might be rare in the Twisting Nether," Jace replied.

"Lethal — but Master is still alive, aren't you? So when are you dying?" Sazeno asked curiously.

Morphiel grabbed one of the imp's horns and lifted him up in one motion. "Whenever he dies, scum, I guarantee you'll die before him. Aren't you afraid of your former master? Don't worry — I won't let your soul return to the Twisting Nether."

"Ouch... ouch! I'm wrong!" The imp grabbed the succubus's arm, begging for mercy. "It hurts... it hurts! Morphiel, what good would my sadness do? Can it dispel Master's curse?"

"At least shut your wretched mouth," Morphiel said, and slammed the imp hard into the wall.

"So the port you're going to has something that can cure this curse, master?"

"It should," Jace said. "I have fairly reliable intelligence that there's a medicine there that can use feigned death to trick a lethal curse. I can only try it."

"Hah! Cunning mortal." The imp climbed back onto the table. "A lethal curse cast by a truly powerful demon wouldn't be fooled by such a clever trick! Have you heard of Archimonde's Touch of Death?"

"Are you asking for another beating?" Morphiel asked. "Does the Defiler's Touch of Death leave any room for trickery?"

"What I mean is, the Touch of Death can curse even the dead to death," the imp said. "A thorough, complete, traceless death — erasing you from every plane and every form of existence all at once."

"Unfortunately, I might not get to fulfill your wish to see me die that cleanly," Jace said. "The guy who cursed me clearly isn't as powerful as your 'Lord Archimonde.' Otherwise, do you think I'd still be sitting here?"

"I'm not wishing you dead, master!" the imp said. "I just wanted to see how much time we have left."

"Theoretically speaking, I won't die anytime soon," Jace explained. "I have ways to delay it, but I can't keep this up forever. Otherwise something will go wrong sooner or later. I'd best find a way to resolve it as soon as possible."

"Then when do we leave?" Morphiel asked.

"I haven't set a time yet. I'm going to the docks later to ask, but definitely within a week, Morphiel," Jace said. "Let me go over the rules for going out once more."

As he spoke, he took out the scroll on which he'd recorded Gorefiend's incantations and glanced at it. "We can't waste time on this trip either. Frankly, I've gone out on life-threatening trips more than once — it's just that this time the risk of death isn't out there, it's in me. So we still need to do what needs to be done. I want you two to thoroughly figure out everything on this scroll."

"Of course, only when there's no one around — like when we've found an inn, or there's no one else in the ship's cabin. If anyone else is present, or even approaching... you must hide. I'm not too clear yet on Booty Bay's attitude toward warlocks, let alone demons."

"We're already very used to all that, master," Sazeno said. "Got anything new?"

"Keep an eye on my things," Jace said. "If any thief or pickpocket dares to steal from me — no matter what it is, money, anything I'm carrying — if anyone dares to touch it..."

"Morphiel, you're quick. You're responsible for this job. I don't want to lose anything because of a trip to Booty Bay — especially this sword. Especially this sword. Understood?"

"Whatever you say, master," Morphiel said.

Jace drew Azurewrath from its sheath. The arcane radiance emanating from the blade even made Jace unable to help but linger on the edge for a moment.

Plus those two beautiful sapphire-blue gems — once your gaze fell on them, it was hard to look away.

An ethereal, hollow resonance reverberated softly through the room. Not only him — the imp and the succubus also stared straight at the sword, as if transfixed.

"Of course, I'll also be holding it the whole time."

As he said this, Jace thought to himself: perhaps this was what they called arcane addiction.

He truly hadn't felt such a deep attraction before, simply because he hadn't had much opportunity to get up close with a particularly powerful arcane source. That vial of Sunwell water had also been gulped down in one go — he hadn't had time to feel anything.
