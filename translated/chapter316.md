## Chapter 316: Preparing for a New Adventure (Part Two)

For this purchase of outerwear and cloak, Jace didn't go to Larson's or Duncan's — those two shops in the Mage Quarter. He bought a set of light-brown traveler's clothes, a dark-yellow mountain-lion-skin cloak, and a pair of black-gray runic leather boots in the Trade District, spending a total of seventy-one silver — most of it on that Alterac mountain-lion cloak, which was the pricey one.

Dress too rich and you drew attention; dressed too poor and you invited trouble. If he was heading to Booty Bay to buy a potion worth ten gold, then a plain outfit totaling only a few silver all told would look a bit odd too.

Carrying the cloth bundle of new gear, he walked out of the shop and glanced up — still early.

He planned to head to the Stormwind docks to ask about prices and schedules for ships to Booty Bay, but along the way he should stop by the Cathedral of Light. The priest had told him to come by about once a week to check on his condition, after all.

He knew the priests were worried that if he went too long without purification, he might suddenly turn into some kind of monster and wreak havoc on ordinary people in the city. Since he'd likely be away for the next month, it was better to check in now — otherwise the Church might come looking for him in the Mage Quarter, just to be safe.

He arrived at the small building beside the cathedral. There weren't many people around at this hour.

"Mr. Sesso, I heard about your situation from my teacher." The young priest from yesterday was still there. He spotted Jace and said, "Did you try having the Wizard Sanctum dispel it?"

"I did, but it didn't go well," Jace said. "I asked Master Marin for help."

"If it was Master Marin," the priest said with a soft sigh, "then the Church truly has no good way to cure this curse at present. But we've only known about death knights these last few years of the Orc Wars. Since we have ways to buy time, I believe a treatment will come soon. Don't lose hope."

"I know, Brother," Jace said with a nod. "Brother Fading asked me to come by the Cathedral of Light once a week to check on the curse's effects. But I have travel plans coming up — I might be gone for a month, Brother. Please pass that along to him."

"Will there be another priest traveling with you?" the priest asked.

"No, but I have another treatment method," Jace said.

"But... this is very dangerous, Mr. Sesso," the priest said. "Are you sure you want to travel, knowing you're under the influence of such a terrible curse? May I ask what treatment method you have?"

"Restorative Potion," Jace said. "A friend at the alchemy lab brewed me a bottle."

Hearing this, the priest's eyes widened and he nodded. "Well, I see. Then I'll let my teacher know. I hope everything goes smoothly."

"Thank you."

Jace noticed the anxiety in the priest's expression ease at once, and he knew Ursula had been right — the Restorative Potion's effects must be formidable indeed.

Compared to the cathedral square, the docks were quite busy at this hour. After several years of repairs, Stormwind had returned to normal operations, and commerce was thriving more and more.

Jace still remembered when he'd first arrived, the dock had been full of tools and materials shipped from every kingdom to aid reconstruction. Now all that had long since been replaced by goods of every kind, and the workers who'd been everywhere were gone, replaced by travelers and merchants of all sorts.

He came to the dockmaster's small office and waited a long time until the crowd thinned out. Making sure no one around him was someone he knew, he stepped up and asked, "When's the next ship to Booty Bay, sir?"

The dockmaster was a sturdy old man. Without turning around, he said, "None today. Come back the day after tomorrow — Monday or Tuesday."

"What time do they leave?"

The old man glanced at Jace. "Monday evening and Tuesday morning. The evening one's a goblin ship — fast, about ten days to get there. The Tuesday one's a Lordaeron merchant vessel, takes about half a month. Your choice."

He was meeting the king on Monday, and rushing to prepare for the trip right after might be tight — but since there was a third of the journey's time between the two, Jace felt he didn't really have a choice.

"What's the price?"

The dockmaster answered, "The goblin one's thirty-three silver; the Lordaeron ship's twenty silver." Jace asked, "I don't think it's that far from Stormwind to Booty Bay, is it? Why does it feel like about the same time as going to Southshore?"

"You've been to Booty Bay?" the dockmaster said, locking eyes with Jace. "You went to Booty Bay through Stranglethorn? Then you've really got some skills."

He pulled a map out of his office and spread it on the table. Jace noticed it showed nearly the entire Eastern Kingdoms continent, plus several islands — including Baradin and Kul Tiras, and a messy scatter of archipelagoes in between that he'd never heard of.

And on the far side of the Great Sea, there was another continent — but rendered far more crudely than this one. Only a small stretch of coastline was drawn; the rest was filled in with images of various monsters and demons, along with endless deserts and mountain ranges.

In other words, at this point the human world's knowledge of the other continent barely extended past the coast and a small stretch of inland — no one had yet ventured deep inside.

For explorers, after all, Kalimdor was decidedly a dangerous realm teeming with monsters and demons.

But what shocked Jace was that this map actually showed the night elves' sacred Mount Hyjal — it had even drawn the World Tree and the Well of Eternity atop the mountain.

This Well of Eternity was not the original one, but had been nurtured from two vials of water drawn from the ancient Well of Eternity. It nourished the World Tree Nordrassil, which granted the night elves the power of immortality. There was almost no chance a human could climb up there and witness these things firsthand. Jace guessed this was probably legend brought over by the elves of Quel'Thalas — to most human adventurers, it likely wasn't much different from the monsters drawn below.

"Are you even listening?" The dockmaster tapped the lower half of the Eastern Kingdoms on the map. "See this area here, Moonbrook? You have to detour around it. If you could fly, Booty Bay might be a bit closer than Southshore — but not by much. Can you fly? Can you afford to keep a gryphon?"

Jace shook his head.

The dockmaster carefully rolled up the map. "Then buy your ticket and stop dreaming, boy from the north. Right now even the slow boat to Southshore in the off-season is forty silver — but the fast ship south is only thirty-three. Isn't that already a lot cheaper?"

Jace tossed a gold coin onto the desk in front of the dockmaster. "Make change."

The dockmaster clutched the gold coin and rubbed it between his fingers. "Need to register a name and some identifying features. Your name first."

"Jack."

"Surname."

"Slovakia. Jack Slovakia," Jace said without a moment's hesitation.

The dockmaster's pen had just touched the paper when he hesitated. "Slo... I can't spell it, never heard that surname. Write it yourself. Any distinguishing features? Physical features? A young lad like you with a clean face..."

Jace opened his shirt to reveal the ribs burned by Shadowflame and the newly grown skin on his arm. "Will this do?"

The dockmaster stared at the rough, extensive scars, and his look at Jace changed completely.

"That'll do. More than distinctive enough."

After returning home, Jace started writing a letter.

He didn't write directly that he'd been struck with Death Shadow. Instead, drawing on what Brother Fading had told him, he mentioned that there were still people in Lordaeron living under the threat of this terrible curse, and asked whether Vereesa knew of any cure in Quel'Thalas — death knights had entered Quel'Thalas too, and their soldiers might be facing the same trouble.

That way, if his trip to Booty Bay resolved the matter, he wouldn't have made Vereesa anxious for nothing — and at the same time he'd learn what he wanted to know, keeping a backup path open.

The next morning, Jace went to Marin's and asked at the door, "Master, could you send a letter to the elves for me?"

"This is... ah, this is a letter for Lady Vereesa, isn't it, Mr. Sesso?" Marin asked.

"Yes, Master," Jace said. "I don't know any other elves in the city. If you know any elves, Master, could you have them pass it along? Please."

"Of course," Marin said. "It might just take two or three weeks."

"That's fine, Master," Jace said. "I'm planning to leave Stormwind for the next month, so even if Vereesa sends a reply I won't be able to receive it. If there is a reply, please hold onto it for me, Master."

Marin nodded. "Going out for something?"

"I'm heading south. A friend said they might know a way to deal with my curse, and I want to check it out."

"I see."

"So I was hoping you could keep covering my leave..."

Marin gave him a look. "You're an apprentice mage already, and you still plan to keep attending the School of Abjuration's apprentice classes? What leave could I even get you? You're not even on the class roster anymore... Oh, and the language and specialty courses too."

Jace nodded.

Marin said, "But you could still sit in on the Abjuration classes — nobody would stop you, just don't wear the apprentice mage's ring. Ah, the ring — I almost forgot. When are you leaving? I'm supposed to get your ring for you."

"Probably tomorrow or the day after," Jace said. "But I'll come find Master Andormas myself to get it when I get back."

"That works too," Marin said. "Frankly, I'm quite curious about what method your friend plans to use to help you. But since your friend has a way and I don't, it's not my place to say much. Of course, I trust your own judgment too, Mr. Sesso. All I can do is remind you to be careful, and wish you well."

"And if it actually works, you wouldn't mind letting me in on it a bit when you get back?"

"Of course," Jace said. "My friend never intended to keep it secret or anything."

"That puts me a little more at ease — most so-called secret recipes hide considerable risks," Marin said. "And remember that article of yours on death knights. It's hard to call it lucky, but having experienced Death Shadow will make your account all the more powerful."

"I haven't forgotten, Master," Jace said.
