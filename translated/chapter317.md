## Chapter 317: Mutual Harm

After leaving Marin's room, Jace let out a long breath.

He felt he'd done everything he could to prepare. All that was left was meeting the king tomorrow morning, really.

In truth, learning that he'd been hit with Death Shadow — that bizarre bit of magic — had instantly drained away any nervousness about walking into the royal palace to see the king.

When you were staring down death's door, what problem could possibly be worse than that?

It still was morning, and the rest of the day looked wide open.

Right — there was still one shadow magic he hadn't tried yet. That Nathrezim shadow protection spell Morphiel had dug up.

Jace ate lunch at the Blue Heron, then came down to the basement that afternoon carrying a paper bag of sweet potatoes, planning to use them as fuel to recover his mana. Once he'd burned through his energy and eaten everything, he'd head home.

He set the sweet potatoes on the hall table and took the slip of paper with the incantation to the practice room.

Passing the small room beside the practice chamber, he heard the creak of a wooden bed from inside. He'd thought the basement was empty — even Ursula wasn't in the alchemy lab, after all.

He entered the pitch-black practice room, lit the candle in its little basin, and pulled out Galders' notes on shadow magic gestures. He sat down in a small chair and recalled the memory fragment of Teron Gorefiend imprinted in his mind — the scene as it had been when he'd heard that incantation shouted.

Once he'd quieted down, all that remained was a faint, indistinct murmuring at the edges of hearing. But for Jace at this point, that sort of thing couldn't touch him anymore.

After all, what could be worse than what was at home? Sulenna and Erland — that was small potatoes.

Gorefiend's memories were like watching a film whose progress bar kept jumping on its own, with severe buffering at that. He could only just make out the most striking elements from hazy, unclear images.

And the most important thing: it was all in first person. Even if he remembered bits of the casting, he couldn't see the full gestures.

All he could recall was that Gorefiend seemed to clench his fist in front of his body — but that bastard cast nearly every shadow magic that way. Including that time in Dalaran when Jace had seen him reach for Kalfas.

In other words, to Gorefiend's level of shadow manipulation, gestures didn't matter in the slightest.

Actually, it was the same for Jace. When he cast shadow magic, his incantations were often pretty sloppy. Compared to arcane magic, which demanded he straighten his back and recite every syllable precisely by the book, shadow magic was practically doing whatever he felt like.

Still, it was clear he wasn't familiar with this protection spell yet, so for the first round of practice he ought to take it seriously.

Jace flipped through the several casting gestures Galders had recorded, and found one that made him do a double take — there was a Water Breathing gesture in there.

Galders knew Water Breathing? The incantation wasn't recorded here, though.

In the game, Water Breathing was a spell that let the caster breathe underwater. If the Water Breathing here worked the same way as in the game, did that mean he could learn it and head down to the docks to find work hauling in crabs and lobsters?

It took him a moment to remember he was already on the track to becoming a full mage — would people laugh at him if they found out he was hauling lobsters for coin?

Wait, he was getting way ahead of himself.

Of all these gestures, only the Water Breathing one seemed to be a self-buff. Jace decided to try that one.

He stood in the center of the room and closed his eyes, feeling the shadow energy swirling around him.

"Tomthokas, Veholisha, Vasdhaung."

He sounded out the incantation slowly, syllable by syllable, while trying to use the Water Breathing gesture to guide the shadow power gathering in response to the words — and found that the gesture slowly went off track.

He recited the incantation again. This time, beyond the opening gesture, he began following his instincts and the strength of the pull the shadow energy exerted, using his hands to steer it toward him.

After fumbling at it for ages and who knew how many attempts, Jace finally seemed to find the most comfortable, most natural feeling. He recited the incantation once more, following the guiding method he'd pieced together through constant trial — and the moment he finished, a violently flickering black-purple ring of light enveloped him, flashed intensely several times, then vanished.

Did that work?

Jace had seen the ring of light flicker clearly, but right now he couldn't think of any similar spell that existed in the game...

After spending an hour or two wrestling with this one incantation and a mess of different casting gestures, Jace felt his energy flagging.

Even if a spell wasn't successfully cast, just the process of guiding magical energy in between consumed mana.

Jace sat down and caught his breath. After quieting down and thinking for a moment, a spell from the game suddenly came to mind with an effect much like this one — the Shadow Shield a warlock got after sacrificing its Voidwalker minion.

And just like Morphiel had said, this kind of shadow magic could defend against both magical and physical attacks. The absorb shield from sacrificing a Voidwalker worked the same way.

But how was he supposed to test it?

He was thinking it over as he walked out into the hall when he saw Erland sitting at the table eating.

"Where's Sulenna?" Jace asked.

"Asleep." Erland picked up a sweet potato. "There's no woman in this world who can leave a room standing after being with me, Sesso."

"My sweet potatoes." Jace pointed at the half piece left on the plate. "You're eating my..."

"Ah..." Erland looked at the thing in his hand. "I thought Sulenna bought these."

"Wasn't Sulenna in your bed this whole time?!" Jace immediately snatched up the remaining half and took a bite. "No, you — come with me, Erland. Come make up for the wrong you've done. Consider it paying me back for my sweet potato."

Erland frowned. "Get lost, kid. I'm not gay, for fuck's sake. And more to the point, that bit of sweet potato isn't nearly enough to buy a round of me."

"Fuck off," Jace said. "Come help me test a spell."

"That's even less happening," Erland said. "Letting Sulenna test her little magic on me is one thing, but yours? No chance. I'm not done living yet."

"I'm not hitting you — you're hitting me." Jace pointed at the long-handled iron tongs leaning by the fire pit. "Bring those."

Erland hefted the tongs and gave them a swing. "You serious?"

"Come on..."

Jace led Erland into the practice room. "In a moment I'll cast. You'll see a dark ring of light appear around me, and then you hit me with these tongs. Obviously don't stab me with the pointed end — just swing them at me."

"Hard?" Erland asked. "Where?"

"Hit..." Jace looked down at himself, then patted his arm. "The arm's fine. As for force, take it easy. Don't go all out."

"No problem," Erland said. "So don't cripple you, just make it hurt, right? That's my specialty."

At the word "specialty," Jace shot him a look, swallowed, and said, "Right. I'm starting!"

Erland gripped the tongs in a batter's stance and nodded seriously.

The Stonemason's-enforcer build on this guy — all solid muscle and a savage frame — made Jace a little nervous. But for all that, he was still just a mortal man.

If this Shadow Shield couldn't even stop a full-force blow from an ordinary human, then it probably wouldn't be much help against the opponents he'd be dealing with in the future either.

With that thought, Jace smoothly recited the incantation and cast. A ring of light suddenly flared into existence, startling Erland into a step backward. He stood rooted to the spot, staring at the glow without moving.

"The hell... Hit! Hurry up!"

"Ah, right!"

Erland snapped back to his senses and swung the tongs with a gritted teeth — but halfway through the swing, the ring of light suddenly vanished?!

"Whoa?!"

The iron tongs whistled through the air and landed squarely on Jace's arm with full force. He howled and leaped into the air. Erland's eyes went wide in shock; he flung the tongs to the ground and moved to steady him. "How... how? You okay? How did the spell drop so fast?"

"Hold on, don't move." Jace tried moving his arm. A dull, throbbing pain rolled through.

He caught his breath for a moment, then raised his arm. "Good news — it's not broken."

"I've got to say, you're built pretty solid," Erland said, relieved. "I forgot to hold back."

"You son of a..." Jace sighed. "I said hit as soon as the ring appears. How come your reaction was so slow?!"

"Who knew you'd pull it off on the first try, and the way that spell looked was honestly a little scary." Erland scratched his forehead. "When I test with Sulenna, I have to wait while she chants for ages, and it only works once in a blue moon..."

"Fine," Jace said. "This time remember — react fast."

"You're doing that again? Fine, you're asking for it. Don't come crying later." Erland rolled his neck, a series of cracks popping. He rolled up his sleeves, picked up the tongs, and settled back into position.

"Just time it right," Jace sighed, silently hoping that once he got this spell down, its duration would last a little longer. After all, in a real fight, the opponent wasn't going to wait until he had his magical defenses up before striking.

He recited the incantation again. This time, the moment the ring of light flared, Erland struck without hesitation — one clean swing into the glow!

But the dark-purple ring suddenly blazed bright on impact, and with a shadowy explosion it expanded outward in a thunderous boom, blasting Erland clean off his feet and into the back wall. The big man slid down from the wall to the floor without making a single sound.

"Erland!" Now it was Jace's turn to panic. He ran over and propped Erland upright. Purple marks of shadow damage covered his face and arms. He tried to say nothing, but only managed a faint, breathless wheeze.

Jace darted out, ran into Ursula's alchemy lab, grabbed some things from the shelf where the healing potions and Shadow Protection Potions were kept, ran back, and fed them to him.

A while later, Erland finally gasped, stared at Jace, and murmured, "Jace Sesso, I'm gonna... fuck... your... fuck, fuck..."

"I didn't know either..." Jace said.

"You... did that on purpose." Erland suddenly reached up and grabbed Jace by the hair. "You've got it in for me. You've been waiting for me to die, haven't you?"

"Ow, sorry." Jace winced and caught the other man's wrist. "I'd only just learned this spell not long ago, but it really is too dangerous. Tell you what — I'll teach you how to use Curse of Weakness properly. How about that?"

"You dare bring up Curse of Weakness, you little shit..."

Erland's eyes bulged, and then he broke into a fit of coughing.

"Fine... all right, Curse of Weakness then," he said with a sigh, touching his face and sniffing his fingers with a frown.

Judging by the force with which he'd grabbed his own hair, Jace was confident Erland's injuries weren't too serious. So this was some kind of shadow damage-reflecting shield? Completely unexpected. And clearly, its maximum power was nowhere near fully tapped yet.
