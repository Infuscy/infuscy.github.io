Chapter 319: The Second Gift

"Speaking of Teron Gorefiend — your bravery puts us southerners to shame," Varian said with a sigh. "Perhaps you think I'm just saying the sort of pleasantries a king is supposed to say, but I mean it from the bottom of my heart."

"When I walked in earlier I was only making a joke, of course. I understand full well that death knights are terrifying enemies, Jace. I still remember the first time I heard reports that such dreadful monsters had appeared on the battlefield — I couldn't even imagine how to fight them. And yet you and Lady Vereesa Windrunner of Quel'Thalas hunted down their leader together — that undead sorcerer who brought terror to all of Azeroth. Frankly, when I first received that letter I couldn't believe my eyes. I thought he'd narrowly escaped the disaster at Dalaran and might come our way. Now all those worries have completely melted away."

"But I've also heard that death knights are still hiding in the uninhabited lands to the north, isn't that right? General Sylvanas Windrunner has reported this to us and to Ironforge as well. So for now we can't let our guard down. It's still hard to say we've achieved total victory in the war — not only the Dark Portal expedition, but even here there are orcish scouts and spies everywhere. There are even shadowy corners within our own kingdom's borders, no shortage of fanatics looking to recruit souls for the Horde."

After saying all this, Varian asked, "I heard from Master Andormas that you've become an apprentice mage, is that right?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Once you become a full mage, I'll formally invite you into this castle, Jace Sesso," Varian said. "If it were up to me, I'd have you come in right now. But you know there are many things I can't decide on my own — I have to consider the opinions of those outside, the opinions of those older than me, and the opinions of mages older than you."

"So — would you like to enter this castle they call a palace and help me in the future?"

"I'd be glad to," Jace said. "Actually, that's my goal too, Your Majesty."

"Wonderful," Varian said. "A lot of people think you're a Lordaeronian and that I shouldn't trust you. But we're the same age, Jace, and a few years ago I was forced away from home myself, only returning not long ago. So I think in a way we can understand each other. And this city must count as a second homeland to you by now, wouldn't you say?"

Jace nodded. Varian laughed. "Back at Stonewatch I thought there was a chance we'd become friends. Remember that time you bumped into me? That literally saved my life. Damn it, I really hate that kind of occasion — I still do, actually. Maybe I'll get used to it someday."

Jace smiled too, though he didn't know whether Varian's words were a habitual pleasantry or spoken in earnest. But at the very least, Varian's eyes were genuinely sincere.

He couldn't help measuring this young version of Varian against the High King of the Alliance he'd become later. Since the man he'd grow into was a brave and forthright leader, Jace didn't think the Varian before him now would be the sort of fellow who said one thing and meant another.

So when Varian said these things, they moved him all the same.

"Come." Varian stood up. "Follow me over here, Jace. I'll show you the gift I've prepared for you."

"A gift?"

"You don't think I'd just chat with you for a bit and send you off, do you?"

Varian was already heading for the door as he said it, not even giving Jace a chance to ask what it was. All he could do was follow curiously.

The king led him to the palace gardens. Jace had seen this place in the game, and Vereesa had mentioned before that during her time living in Stormwind she'd stayed here the whole while, never leaving even a step.

But the actual garden was far larger than in the game. Jace was craning his neck looking around in curiosity when Varian already vaulted over the stone railing and made for the middle of the garden.

"Over there, Jace."

Jace too climbed over the railing and followed the king behind a thick fruit tree. A black mare was standing there. When she saw Jace approaching, she snorted hard.

"This is her." Varian reached out and stroked the mare's neck. "What do you think — beautiful? Her name is Alna. She's three years old this year."

"A horse?" Jace was silent for a moment. "I can't afford to keep a horse. That's... too much of a burden."

"You think I'd make you keep her? That would be deliberately tormenting you," Varian said with a laugh. "I'll have someone send her to the Wizard Sanctum stables and let the stable master there look after her, on my coin. After that, you can ride her wherever you like, whenever you like. Of course, you'll need to take good care of her when you're traveling."

He glanced sideways at Jace. "As for whether she likes you or not — that'll be up to your own skill. I'll be honest, her temper isn't exactly good!"

Hearing the king say that, Jace couldn't help but twitch the corner of his mouth — this was really rather embarrassing.

And bad as her temper was, could it be worse than a gryphon's?

Still, thinking that he'd be leaving Stormwind tonight and wouldn't have the chance to walk this beautiful mare around for a while made his heart itch.

He stepped forward and tried to stroke the black mare's neck, but she snorted and shifted her body away to the side.

Seeing Jace's slightly taken-aback expression, Varian smiled. "That's perfectly normal. She doesn't know you yet!"

"Take good care of her, Jace. She's grown up. I can feel it — she wants to travel everywhere, not be cooped up in this palace forever."

"Thank you so much, Your Majesty."

"Mm. In a while I'll have her sent over." Varian said softly, "I'm sure she's worthy of a champion who defeated Gorefiend."

Jace nodded. He looked at this king only a year older than himself, and in the end still couldn't bring himself to speak of the Death Shadow clinging to him. After all, even if he asked — just as Marin had said — every avenue the king could pursue, he'd already pursued.

If things didn't go well at Booty Bay, he'd bring it up then. He'd already bought his ticket, after all.

That afternoon when he got home, Jace began frantically packing his luggage — coin pouch, spell scrolls, dried rations.

He put on the dark-red dragonkin cap, pulled on his casual clothes and outer robe, laced up his boots, hung Azurewrath in its rough leather scabbard from his belt, draped the mountain-lion-skin cloak over his shoulders, wrapped the cloth mask around his face, pulled up the hood, gripped his staff, and shouldered his pack.

"You two, it's time to head out," he said, looking at the imp and the succubus.

The imp scrambled up onto Jace's shoulder. The succubus leaned against his back and murmured, "Master, has anyone ever told you that you possess a very special charm?"

"No one ever has." Jace scratched an itchy spot on his bridge of his nose where a thread had been tickling him. "All right, stop with the nonsense. Vanish now and keep up with me."

"Understood." Morphiel whispered it in his ear, then slowly faded from sight.

"You too," Jace said to Sazeno.

"Yes, master!"

With that, Jace pushed the door open and walked up to the canal bank, looking at his reflection in the water. Looked like a blue-and-green mismatch from head to toe.
