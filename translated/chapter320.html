Chapter 320: The Place of Beginning and End!

By the time Jace reached the port it was already dusk. The sun still had its red head resting on the sea's surface, and the whole field of view was filled with gleaming golden-red afterglow.

It was still rather cold compared to a few days ago. Bundled up in all these layers, he still felt like he hadn't put on quite enough — though he could hide inside the ship soon enough, and once they got further south it ought to be warmer.

There was only one goblin ship at the pier. As Jace came up the dock ready to board, a shrill little voice suddenly shouted from the side.

"Hey! Trying to sneak aboard? You think I'm not here?!"

Jace looked carefully to the side and only then noticed a goblin standing on a wooden crate. Backlit by the sun, small and dark-skinned, he'd been completely invisible.

"I bought a ticket," he said.

The goblin jabbed a finger at him. "Then say your name."

"Jack. Jack Slovakia."

"Hmm... let me see... here it is — Slova... lift your clothes and let me have a look."

Jace lifted his cloak and pulled open his shirt. The goblin glanced and asked, "So it's you. What are you, some kind of necromancer?"

"Huh?" Jace looked at him. "Why would you say that?"

"Something feels off." The goblin tilted his head. "Ah, never mind. Whether you're a mage or some kind of magician, all I want to remind you is — don't go summoning any elementals, wood sprites, demons, or the like on board. And don't go raising dead things into undead monsters either, or you'll pay a supplement! If you harm a passenger, we'll throw you and all your messy companions straight overboard."

"Full fare?" Jace asked.

The goblin said, "Well, if you only summon a rat you can ride half-price. But you're not actually some kind of necromancer, are you?"

"No. Still, have necromancers ever boarded the ship?" Jace asked curiously.

"Don't remind me of that one. Ugh — next ones to board, I won't waste any more breath." The goblin sat back down on the crate. "Just remember what I said, that's all."

Jace nodded and stepped onto the plank. He could feel Morphiel, still linked to him by Soul Link, slipping aboard right behind him — but the goblin didn't notice anything amiss.

Probably because this goblin had caught a whiff of something not quite right on him. He really ought to drink some Restorative Potion soon.

This goblin fast ship didn't have any proper passenger cabins. The place where Jace would sleep and rest was nothing more than a corner under a staircase.

What he could do, besides eat, drink, and sleep on the spot, was find opportunities to stare at a blank sheet of paper, thinking about how to start writing down his experience of fighting Teron Gorefiend.

In truth, he genuinely didn't know how to begin.

After all, he could hardly write that he'd been targeted because his shadow affinity was too strong and because he was holding Gorefiend's own staff from when he'd been alive, could he?

Or write that Kel'Thazad had told Gorefiend his name and sent him to hunt him down?

After thinking it over for a long time, he decided to start with that very first death knight.

Fight like hell to kill a death knight, only to be marked by their master — that made for a perfectly logical opening.

And so, dazed and numb, he drifted through he didn't know how many days. By now Jace had been cooped up in the hold long enough that day and night had started to blur together.

That evening a storm seemed to blow up at sea again. From inside the hold came a discussion in a language he couldn't understand.

He leaned against the cabin wall, feeling the hull sway, and thought back to that night on the way to Southshore when he'd fought the Night Baron.

Who knew when Grede would be back. He'd already been gone two whole months — was Jace really going to have to wait until the three months he'd mentioned at most?

On the route from Menethil Harbor across the Wetlands to Ironforge, there were orcs of the Dragonmaw clan about. Safer and more stable than cutting straight across the Burning Steppes, storming Blackrock Spire through the middle, or crossing the whole of the Badlands, certainly — but it was by any means a completely clear road either.

Listening to the rumble of thunder and the crash of the ocean waves against the hull, Jace nearly forgot he was still carrying Death Shadow on him.

Measured against the power of the ocean, a death curse with no end in sight didn't seem so terrifying after all.

He took out the Restorative Potion and took a small sip, feeling the soothing comfort of it purifying his body bit by bit. He breathed in the refreshing fragrance from the bottle, doing his best to make himself forget the dark, briny sea.

After tightening the cap, Jace cradled Azurewrath in his arms, closed his eyes, and murmured, "Good night, Morphiel. Sazeno. Anyone who dares touch my sword — run him through for me, then throw him into the sea under cover of darkness."

"Rest easy, master," Morphiel said from beside him. "If there really is that big an idiot with that much guts, I'll deal with him without even waking you."

Though he found the night storm terrifying, Jace still fell asleep. Barring the two times he was jolted awake in the middle of the night by the hull lurching, he'd slept rather soundly.

When he woke again it was already noon the next day. Jace was just about to pull out his paper to continue writing when he heard a commotion from up on deck.

He climbed up to the deck and looked toward the bow. An unbroken stretch of green mountains filled the distant seaboard.

The cries of seabirds rose and fell. The sea breeze turned chaotic and uneven. He grabbed his hood and climbed higher, when suddenly a goblin shouted, "Look off to our starboard, honored guests!"

Jace glanced to the right and caught sight of a colossal goblin statue with arms spread wide, leveling its gaze toward the lands behind it. The goblin's expression was clearly meant to be a smile — but whether because the sculptor's skills were lacking, or because they'd deliberately wanted to create some sort of eerie atmosphere, it looked more like it was about to be strangled to death.

"Behold the majestic figure of our Great Patron, Revilgaz! You see him? The Great Patron Revilgaz is the rightful, lawful, and legitimate protector and most exalted master of all Blackwater Bay! Anyone who doesn't recognize this had better get off the ship — right now, while you still can, and save yourselves unnecessary trouble! After all, even sharks get their fill!"

The guffawing goblin strode to the bow and began introducing Booty Bay's "long and storied" history.

The bow pushed on through his chattering, rounding a cape thick with palm trees and entering Blackwater Bay — and the entire harbor burst into view all at once before Jace's eyes.

Layer upon layer of houses crowded and piled against each other, each one competing to be taller or wider than the next, together forming a great jagged expanse of brown and red interlocking along the curving shoreline. And yet, it had to be said, within the chaos there seemed to be a faint, almost imperceptible thread of order.

Without question these buildings had all been built by humans — they were identical in style to the houses at the Stormwind docks.

If he hadn't seen a pair of trolls and a dwarf standing on a ship not far away, chatting and laughing together, he'd have taken this for a prosperous human town.

Jace studied every house here, every street and staircase built plank by plank high and low, until the ship docked.

He craned his neck and stared for a while at a giant barracuda strung up overhead — about as long as the black whelp from the Thaurissan ruins — when a sudden explosive shout from right beside him gave him a fright.

"We're finally here! Damn it! Out of my way!"

A gnome in a red jacket slipped off the ship and bellowed, "Booty Bay! My dream, the starting point and the end of my life, the place of my beginning and end — here it is! From this moment on I'm a different man! Screw the Holy Light! Screw the Alliance! Screw the damn snowy mountains, wild wolves, and trolls!"
