Chapter 326: Some Kind of Mushroom

Bacchus Mishershield.

Jace didn't arrive at this alchemist's door until the afternoon. A wiry, brown-haired goblin was lounging in a chair beside it, glasses on, staring blankly out at the sea. Jace walked over and said, "Excuse me, are you Mr. Bacchus Mishershield?"

"'Mr. Mishershield'?" The goblin gave Jace a sidelong glance. "What do you want with him? Did Revilgaz go bankrupt?"

"What?" Jace was completely thrown by the response.

"I said, has he really fallen so far that he's sending your kind to collect his debts?" the goblin asked.

Jace cleared his throat. "No, sir, I'm not here to collect a debt. I just want to ask Mr. Mishershield some questions about alchemy."

"Hmph." The goblin said, "Of course you're not. After all, Booty Bay prints money — unless the combined Stormwind and Kul Tiras fleet is already on its way and he's planning to skip town with the loot."

"You said you wanted to discuss some alchemy. Then tell me: the dark plant born in the most venomous of lands, the coveted mystery material chased by every human, elf, and dwarf herbalist worth their salt..."

"Black Lotus," Jace said. "But that's an herbalism question, not alchemy."

"That'll do. The debt collectors probably couldn't even understand what I just said." The goblin took off his glasses. "You answered the question, so you've earned a truth most people never get to hear — I am Bacchus Mishershield."

Jace looked at him, deadpan. About as obvious a truth as there ever was.

The goblin coughed. "So, what did you come here to ask? Looking for an Invisibility Potion to sneak into some girl's wardrobe? I'll be straight with you, lad — if that girl suddenly flings the wardrobe door open, drinking the potion won't save you, because..."

"It takes a few seconds to kick in." Jace pulled out an Invisibility Potion and waved it in front of Mishershield's face. "And I'm not here for that, sir."

Mishershield squinted at the potion. "You brew this yourself?"

"No," Jace said. "The person who sent me here did."

The goblin nodded slowly. "And what are you to them?"

"Assistant and herb hunter," Jace said. "And to be precise, it's a 'her.'"

"A female alchemist." The goblin nodded. "Doesn't surprise me. Every female alchemist I've ever met has been terrifying. What do you want?"

"Noggenfogger Elixir."

"Noggenfogger, hmm." Mishershield asked, "Are you in some kind of trouble? I'm getting a nasty whiff off you — and I don't mean ordinary nasty. I mean fatal nasty."

Jace realized he'd come to the right person.

"That's our business."

"And of course it is. Everyone's got their own business." The goblin chuckled — a laugh that from any other race would've sounded like a sinister sneer, but from a goblin was perfectly normal.

"For the sake of your master alchemist friend, lad, I'll let you in on a little more." Mishershield went on. "That Noggenfogger fellow's formula has always had a serious flaw — there's a chance it causes memory loss after the fainting episode. I know he's been trying to fix that problem while also driving down the cost and the danger, so he can turn a bigger profit and attract more buyers."

"And I believe I'll beat him to a result, because he's got too many things on his mind — the Trade Cartel, Gadgetzan, the treasures of Zul'Farrak, desert racing, the cursed gold mine, pretty girls... I don't care about any of that. I'm a simple goblin alchemist, human. I only care about making more useful — and ideally more profitable — potions. And even the profit part is secondary to me."

"Noggenfogger's stuck in that town, unable to break free, while I'm far more free. Look at this harbor! Look at this whole continent! I have so many untapped materials I could use to improve that potion. Once I succeed in my experiments, I can call the new formula Authentic Mishershield Elixir."

"So you want me to help you gather materials for your experiments?" Jace asked.

"So do you want the potion or not?" the goblin shot back. "Do you want Noggenfogger Elixir? Or better yet — do you want something even greater than what Noggenfogger makes? For free? Then you help me. I'm betting an alchemist who could brew that invisibility potion doesn't partner with some idiot, and you do seem like a smart one."

Jace frowned. "Honestly, sir, I may not have much time left to help you develop a new potion."

"Hmph." Mishershield shot Jace a look. "Let me guess — that stench on you means some kind of death curse or poison? You had a serious accident in an experiment? And that's why you want Noggenfogger Elixir? Nothing to be ashamed of, lad. You're not the first person to come to Booty Bay for that reason."

"I do need it urgently," Jace said. "If you're willing to give me some of the finished product, maybe I can consider helping you out."

"The finished product?" Mishershield said bluntly. "I've got a little at home that I was using for experiments. But are you sure you want to drink it and risk memory loss? Just to stay alive?"

"How high is the risk of memory loss?" Jace asked.

"About twenty percent, from what I've heard." Mishershield said. "But you know how it is — some weirdos drink it, and even if they lose their memory they can't exactly tell you, can they? Maybe some people drink this stuff specifically to forget their embarrassing pasts. Some of them probably forget they ever drank Noggenfogger Elixir at all. Yeah, memory loss might actually be a feature. For all I know, Noggenfogger originally set out to make a forgetting potion. Maybe the fake-death part was just a side effect in the first version."

Either way, memory loss was absolutely a risk Jace couldn't afford.

If he lost the memories of his past life, he'd be casually throwing away his greatest trump card. Twenty percent was already too high. If that were the odds, he'd be better off waiting for the Church or the elves to develop a new treatment.

But living for years — even a decade — dependent on the Church's purifications or six Restorative Potions a year... if he ever got stranded in the wild with no way back to the city, he'd be dead before he could get help.

Or he'd need to find a priest companion to keep him alive at all times — and then his life would hinge entirely on that companion. Lose him, and he was done for.

So was that really any safer than a twenty percent risk?

"Tell me about your improvement plan, Mr. Mishershield," he said.

"Now you're asking." The goblin said, "Actually, I've already found the problem. The Noggenfogger Elixir formula has too many expensive materials thrown in to create a safe fake-death state. But one of them — the extremely rare Ghost Mushroom — while powerful, clashes with the Maglahmos Ghostmeat Flower in the formula. That's the root cause of the memory loss. But I'll give it this — its ghostly color and weird smell add a lot of mystique to Noggenfogger's product, which objectively drives up customers' willingness to buy it..."

"I've tried all sorts of hallucinogenic materials as replacements for the Ghost Mushroom — Hyjal's poison-cap mushrooms, purple-cap mushrooms, thick-stalk mushrooms, moonglow mushrooms, Ashenvale's blue-powder mushrooms, aquatic stinkhorns, felcone mushrooms, the oasis mushrooms of the Northern Barrens, the jungle fungi here, Black Marsh mushrooms... Honestly, most of the fungi from Khaz Modan and the Kingdom of Stormwind are fine to eat, but their medicinal value can't hold a candle to Kalimor's, and there isn't enough variety."

"There's still one type of dark-blue mushroom that a troll brought over from the sea that I haven't tried yet. This mushroom is extremely dangerous — its name, translated from the troll tongue, is 'Death.' I'm not optimistic about its effect, because I just want to improve Noggenfogger's formula, not turn it into some boring lethal poison. Why wouldn't I just buy a dwarven blunderbuss? Same effect, and a lot cheaper."

"And then there's Lordaeron and Northrend — I haven't tried looking there yet. I'm not too hopeful that cold places would have any exotic fungi, and the Orc Wars' spread into the Hillsbrad region a few years back interrupted my research... I haven't had the conditions to restart that program these past few years. All I need is one suitable mushroom, damn it!"

"Maybe you could help me with that," the goblin said. "Lordaeron's human territory, isn't it?"

"You've never tried the purple mushrooms from the Hinterlands?" Jace asked.

"The Hinterlands — alright, I haven't looked into that place much." The goblin raised an eyebrow. "Since you seem to know something, speak up."

Jace was a little surprised. In the game, the Noggenfogger Elixir questline had the alchemist Noggenfogger quickly completing his new formula once he got his hands on the purple powder ground from the Hinterlands mushroom, then launching a whole new business.

Jace had assumed that in this world, the purple mushroom for that quest was already part of the formula — but he hadn't expected the old formula to actually use Ghost Mushroom. Every recipe involving Ghost Mushroom was dodgy, so it made sense that the goblin's version could cause memory loss.

And this Mishershield hadn't mentioned trying the Hinterlands mushroom.

Jace tried to explain to the goblin: "The Hinterlands forest is wetter than the rest of Lordaeron, and it's home to the elves of Quel'Thalas and the Amani trolls. They've been fighting there for centuries, if not longer. The whole place is scarred with the residue of voodoo and magic, never mind the mysterious enchanted forests to the north."

"I know of a purple mushroom that grows in the ancient territory of the evil trolls — the same trolls who worship the spider loa Shadra. Just think about what their voodoo and venomous spiders would do to the fungi growing in those lands."

"Good lord, the way you put it..."

The goblin alchemist fell into thought, but by now Jace was already starting to worry.

Was he really going to have to go all the way back to the Hinterlands just to pick this mushroom? That'd be another few months gone.

Or was he supposed to help this goblin find a new Noggenfogger formula by writing a letter asking Vereesa to risk her life poking around in ruins crawling with Amani trolls — all for one goddamn mushroom?

Never mind whether the ranger would agree — even if she were willing, Jace thought it would be more absurd than just drinking the old-formula Noggenfogger Elixir. He simply couldn't bring himself to ask. But maybe an elven herbalist had already collected a sample of this mushroom? Right — that troll-coin shop owner had mentioned a troll alchemist from the north living in Booty Bay... although to the southernmost port on the continent, even Stranglethorn Vale counted as "the north."

"I'd like to know, sir — is the troll witch doctor in the port a Gurubashi troll, or... an Amani troll?"

"Oh... right! I almost forgot!" The goblin's eyes went wide. "That's an evil priestess who fled here from the Hinterlands a few years back. An alchemist too. Yes — go, go ask her! Let me lock up first... wait, damn it... I still owe her something. No, I can't go. You go! You go, human!"
