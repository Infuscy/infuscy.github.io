## Chapter 327: Lukongkani

Go meet a forest troll?

If it were a Gurubashi jungle troll, at least among them there were exile groups like the Darkspear — relatively peaceful, not so aggressive. But the forest trolls of the north were the real deal, so to speak. They embodied nearly every terrifying rumor about trolls: cannibalism, headhunting, flaying, dark magic, loa worship, foul rituals...

Still, since this forest troll had managed to carve out a place for herself in Booty Bay, she must've been playing by the rules. And if Mishershield had known her for years, it meant she'd integrated herself well enough over time.

Following the directions the goblin had given him, Jace walked down a narrow street gradually away from the coast, into a palm-covered hillside.

The buildings here grew ever more dilapidated. Though you could still make out their original human shapes, each had been patched up by its successive occupants in their own fashion, until every one had taken on a character of its own and was well on its way to abandoning any pretense of human architecture.

"A broken palm tree leaning against an earthen slope, with a dwelling beneath it."

Jace repeated the goblin's exact words to himself. Right in front of him was a snapped palm tree leaning against a slope, and below it a sinister-looking earthen hole just tall enough for a man to pass through stooping.

He stepped toward the cave mouth, half-hidden by rotting leaves, and pushed aside the dense tangle of eerie, white-hair-like roots hanging down from above, picking his way deeper in — until he was abruptly stopped by a wooden door fitted with a little sliding shutter window.

So it really was a "dwelling."

Jace raised a hand and knocked. "Hello? Is anyone there?"

No answer. He glanced outside — the sun was already starting to dim. Had the troll stepped out and not come back yet?

Just as he was wondering whether to leave and come back that evening, the door behind him gave a sudden creak.

Jace turned. Through the square hole in the door, a skull had suddenly appeared. He flinched, his hand shooting to the staff behind him, the first syllable of Immolate already on his lips.

But a step later he realized it wasn't a skull at all — it was a wooden mask carved and painted to look like a troll skull.

As a veteran player, Jace really shouldn't have been startled. Plenty of troll priests in the game wore masks like that. But the gloomy cave had really set the mood a touch too well.

"A human? What do you want?" The voice from behind the mask was as raspy as an old woman's. Through the eye holes, a pair of pale yellow, cat-like eyes peered out, set in light green skin.

"Speak up. I haven't got all day to play with you."

"I'm looking for a purple mushroom native to the Hinterlands — the kind that grows underwater. Lady Vilebranch..."

"I am *not* Vilebranch." The troll's voice went cold. "Not anymore. Call me Sylia. That mushroom you're talking about... would it be Lukongkani?"

"I don't know." Jace carefully recalled the quest details from the game. He remembered that originally this mushroom had been used by a chef as a food seasoning, so he said, "I think it's something that makes food taste better..."

Hearing that, the troll let out a loud laugh. "Of *course* it tastes better. Lukongkani is an incredibly powerful hallucinogenic fungus. After you eat it, if you want the thing in your mouth to taste good, then it'll taste as good as you want it to."

"Well then," Jace said. "Do you have that mushroom?"

"Of course I do!"

"How much?"

"Three gold apiece."

"Why so expensive?"

"You're welcome to go fetch it yourself from the Hinterlands, human." Sylia said. "I can even tell you exactly where the mushrooms are easiest to find. But I can tell you your fate in advance: your scalp will be stripped off and hung on a wooden wall in some Vilebranch hunter's dwelling as a trophy. Because if you're capable of finding that mushroom, three gold is hardly a price you'd refuse..."

Considering that Mishershield would probably want to run experiments with this formula, Jace couldn't exactly buy just one. Two or three caps, and the price would be absurd.

He'd come to Booty Bay prepared to spend as much as ten gold, but that was for buying potions — drink them and be done with it. The situation had turned out a little more complicated than he'd expected back in Stormwind. Blowing nine gold just on a few raw materials wasn't what he'd budgeted for.

So Jace leaned against the door and asked, "Could I trade something else instead? Some kind of work, maybe?"

"Work worth three gold?" Sylia laughed a few times, each laugh sounding like a cough. "Even in Booty Bay, money doesn't come that easily, young man."

"Tiger fangs, raptor feathers, basilisk eyes, gorilla sinews..." Jace said. "Whatever you need, I can try to get it for you."

Sylia fell silent for a moment. "What exactly are you — an alchemist, an herbalist, or a hired hunter?"

"I can be all of them," Jace replied. "If you need it, I can even be a mage."

He conjured a Light cantrip to brighten the dim cave, then casually dismissed it and leaned closer to the window. "So — any suitable work for me now, Lady Sylia?"

The troll forced out a raspy question: "First tell me — what kind of troublesome opponents have you dealt with?"

"Death Knights," Jace said. "I killed a Death Knight once. And I didn't bring any edged weapon — I took his own black dagger and drove it into his eye socket, and he collapsed like a pile of mud before he could even let his soul slip away."

Sylia's eyes shifted. "Hmm. Seems I underestimated you. I can tell from your eyes that you're young — that threw off my judgment."

"Since you're so capable, I actually do have a job that'd suit you. Do this for me and I'll knock two gold off — sell you a mushroom at a discount."

Two gold. The troll was offering a fair deal, especially considering the time and danger involved in going back to the Hinterlands, not to mention the travel costs.

"Tell me about it, then," Jace said.

"I had something stolen from me by a jungle troll." Sylia said. "It might not be worth much money, but it means a great deal to me. A mask."

"We used to be friends, that troll and I. But not anymore. She's a hunter, living in a cave by the sea outside Booty Bay. She's counting on the fact that I can't go out there after her... and I'm not a warrior built for close combat."

"How far?" Jace asked.

The troll said, "From the pass where you enter Stranglethorn Vale, follow the road through the valley heading east. Walk half a day and you'll reach the beach. Watch out for the panthers in there, human — though I doubt ordinary beasts would scare you. Anyway, once you're out of the rainforest, follow that beach north... in a few hours you'll see a narrow path leading into the jungle shade. The cave is in there, not far from the shore."

"I'm getting the picture. Does she live alone?" Jace asked.

"Yes. I'm certain she lives alone." Sylia let out a laugh, then her voice went cold again. "Because no one would stand to be around that ugly, stupid, arrogant bitch — except me, the fool who took pity on her."

She grew angrier as she spoke, until the last words came out as a gnashing, bitter condemnation.

"What does she look like?" Jace asked.

"Light blue skin, two metal rings through her nose, yellow hair like a patch of weeds. She's tall. Her tusks are short, barely poking past her lips."

"Tell me about the mask, then."

"The mask is blue-green, eyes shut tight, with a long nose — like a male troll's face." Sylia said. "If you find it, you'll recognize it at once."

"Blue-green, closed eyes, long nose. Got it." Jace nodded. "If she won't hand it over, or you can't find the mask anywhere — kill her." Sylia said. "Bring her head back. I'll make her confess everything."
