## Chapter 328: Rainforest

The next morning, Jace drank a dose of Restorative Potion and set out from Booty Bay. He'd never imagined his first time entering Stranglethorn Vale would be from the south.

Not long after leaving the gate of Booty Bay, the road was still fairly dry, and the surrounding valleys loomed hazy in the distance. Jace recalled the descriptions he'd heard back in Stormwind from people who'd been to Stranglethorn, and felt a twinge of unease.

Following the troll's directions and the goblin guard's pointers, he headed toward the coast. A few hours' walk brought him into the rainforest.

This was indeed the valley leading straight to the shore that Sylia had mentioned — Jace could see the rugged cliffs rising on both sides the moment he entered.

And in the game, there seemed to be just such a passage squeezed between two cliffs, running all the way to the eastern coast of Stranglethorn. But that valley was very short — you could see the exit and the ocean before you even entered it. Here, though, it was a vast tropical rainforest.

It was sweltering and humid. The endless green didn't feel the least bit lush and alive — if anything, it was a little *too* green, enough to put one's nerves on edge, as if any random leaf might take a bite out of you.

Anything more than a short distance away was obscured by wet mist and vapour. Jace could only roughly follow the direction of the sunlight, but this was a valley rather than a sprawling rainforest, so even if he veered off course he wouldn't go too far astray.

After walking for a while, Jace felt he was wearing a few too many clothes — especially the cloak. It did offer some protection against cuts in the rainforest, but at this rate he might pass out from heatstroke before any cut had the chance to get infected.

Thinking back to the natives in those old documentaries who'd pushed through rainforests wearing practically nothing, Jace figured that if he hadn't absorbed some of the black dragon's vitality — giving his body far greater heat resistance than before — he'd probably have collapsed long ago.

As it was, he was just uncomfortably hot.

"Morophil. Come here."

The succubus following behind came up to him. "Master, you missed me!"

"That's right, I missed you. Put this on." Jace draped the cloak over her.

"Thank you for your concern, Master, but I'm not cold."

"I know you're not cold," Jace said. "I know you don't mind the heat. Hold this for me."

The succubus clutched the cloak to her face and sighed in bliss. "Happy to oblige. I love the way you master smells — your sweat."

"Damn it," Sazeno muttered. "You're disgusting, Succubus. Humans are all stinking beasts."

Jace grabbed the imp by the hind legs off his shoulder and flung him to the ground. "Walk yourself."

The imp scrambled and scurried after him, yelling, "Ow, Master, you're a... not-too-stinky human."

"Didn't you always want to walk on your own, go wherever you pleased?" Jace said.

"But it's way too hard to walk here!" the imp complained.

"Shut up." Morophil suddenly spoke in a low, urgent tone. She looked at Jace. "Master, I think I heard something."

Jace stopped and listened. Sure enough, there was the sound of a branch snapping in the distance.

They took cover behind the upturned root mass of a massive tree. The imp immediately dove into the succubus's cloak. Morophil's face twisted with disgust, but she put up with it.

The three of them held their breath as best they could and waited. Aside from that one snapping branch, there didn't seem to be any other sound.

Jace listened with suspicion but couldn't make anything out. He was just about to speak when the succubus shook her head, pressing a finger to her lips to signal him not to make a sound.

The flames in her eyes gradually dimmed, until they were almost completely extinguished, leaving only the faintest glow. Jace could now see the grey, completely pupil-less demon eyeballs in her eye sockets — deeply unsettling to look at.

She pointed lightly behind the tree, frowning, and Jace seemed to understand.

The succubus slowly vanished, and the imp disappeared with her. Jace listened even more intently toward the tree line ahead, and now he could hear it — a faint sound of something pressing down on the forest-floor humus.

Someone's footsteps?

The sound wasn't very distinct, but the rhythm was slow, unhurried — like someone walking in exaggerated slow motion.

By now Jace realized the jungle had gone quieter than before, which was why he could hear it at all.

A troll?

No — Jace could sense Morophil right beside him. If the other party were a troll, the succubus would already have circled around to set up an ambush.

Just then, Jace heard a rushing sound behind him. It definitely wasn't a natural wind, but it didn't sound like the breathing of any beast either — it was far too massive.

Jace carefully leaned out and glanced behind him — and saw something that made his whole body flinch and his scalp break out in a cold sweat.

A gigantic scaly dinosaur head was hanging high in the air, big enough to look like a moving dark-green wall.

That absurd head was propped up by a comparatively slender neck, a single tiny yellow eye glancing left and right into the distance.

The creature slowly lowered its jaw and exhaled again, revealing a row of white teeth of varying sizes like blades. Jace reckoned the longest ones were nearly as long as Song of Azure.

Its two short forelimbs flexed and gripped at the air, then the beast turned and took a few steps away into the distance.

To Jace's astonishment, given its size, the sound its feet made was unbelievably faint. He also noticed how carefully it lifted and placed each of its feet — those colossal legs and that enormous clawed foot rising gingerly and setting down softly.

This wasn't a raptor. Compared to a raptor, it was simply too massive. Jace made a rough estimate: the creature had to be at least six or seven meters tall, and with its long, straight-held tail it was easily fifteen or six meters in length.

And the tall bony spikes rising from its back made its identity unmistakable — a devilsaur.

Stranglethern Vale actually... had a *devilsaur*?!

Devilsaurs were colossal creatures that lived in isolated rainforests. In the game, they existed in the rainforests of another continent — Kalimor. Stranglethorn Vale didn't have them.

Jace didn't know why there was a devilsaur here. Perhaps this had always been its habitat, and it had only gone extinct by the time the Horde arrived in the game due to overhunting — or perhaps there was some other reason.

In any case, it was here now, and Jace absolutely did not want to find himself being chased by a devilsaur.

By now he felt he was about to suffocate from holding his breath. The devilsaur must have caught some trace of their presence and followed it here, but by now it had lost the target.

His nerves stayed wound tight until he heard a thunderous footstep that made the whole ground shudder — proof enough that it had given up the hunt. As the footsteps gradually receded and the devilsaur's silhouette faded bit by bit into the green, Jace finally let out a breath.

Then he found a fat, bloated, monstrous bug latched onto his leg, its belly swollen with blood.

His scalp went wide with revulsion and his eyes bulged involuntarily. The succubus immediately materialized and clapped a hand over his mouth.

The imp hopped onto his leg, conjured a flame on his finger, and seared the bug. The creature shrieked and burrowed into the dirt, its rear spraying a viscous mixture of red blood and strange yellow pus.
