Chapter 331: A God's Guidance

Eighty silver was certainly not a cheap price.

But Jace didn't want to trek back into the jungle to hunt down a troll ruin where witchcraft had been practiced and fish some fel-essence bottle out of it, either.

He thought back to that hunter from before. She couldn't exactly be called strong, but her absurd agility in the jungle and the accuracy of her throwing weapons had left him with a lingering fear. If he ran into two or more of them, he'd definitely be the one to eat dirt.

"Then I'll take one."

"I've really hit the jackpot with a customer today." Silya turned and shuffled toward a nearby shelf, muttering under her breath. "Ah, it seems there aren't many bottles of that potent fel essence left. About to sell out soon."

"Human — how did that troll get this mask? And how did it break?"

Jace told the troll everything, from spotting her at the beach to chasing her to the cave entrance, then her throwing the mask down and escaping — screwing them over one more time. When he got to the part where the axe had split the mask clean in two, Silya actually let out a snort of laughter.

"She was afraid of you. Obviously. Because you're a powerful enough fighter — she sensed it, and so can I. I always knew that thief was a coward. Good for nothing except deceiving, extorting, and bullying the weak. You taught her a lesson, human. I'm delighted." As she spoke, she lifted a small bottle of purple viscous liquid and returned to face Jace.

"In Common, fel essence is a magical substance that can be extracted from certain beasts and trolls through shamanic ritual. The purer it is, the thicker it gets. Many Amani believe fel essence is the blood of the Loa, so the Loa's children flow with it too — bears, panthers, wind serpents, hawkstriders, raptors. And of course, us."

"So you don't actually know what it is." Jace took the bottle.

The troll looked at him and grinned. "Don't say that in front of other Amani witch doctors. They don't have my temper."

"Do you have any more of these mushrooms?" Jace shook the bag in his hand.

"Selling you one is already being generous. I need them myself, human, and I can never go back to the Hinterlands." Silya sized the young man up again, then lowered her voice. "However… unless you do me one more favor…"

Jace frowned. "You want me to go kill sea giants or something?"

"Nothing quite that lethal." Silya glanced toward the door, then leaned in. "To put it simply — you're going to take the place of that mask… and satisfy me properly. You broke my things. Don't you want to take responsibility?"

"Hold on." Jace shifted his backside backward, letting out a dry laugh. "Who taught you Common? Do you have any idea what you just said?"

"You want to know what that mask was for?" Silya reached up and pulled off the mask. Jace realized she didn't look nearly as ancient as her voice suggested — judging by the smoothness of her skin, she was even younger than the Gurubashi hunter had been.

"Then I'll tell you. Strictly speaking, it's not really a mask anymore. It was my other half. My mate, human. It brought pleasure to my life here, kept it from being so dull and empty. It's dead, and I don't hope for it to come back to life. Your arrival was its guidance — its final gift to me. It must be so… Prove my guess, human. If you're worthy of the power in that witch mask, I'll give you two caps of the mushrooms."

"A witch mask?"

"Yes. Witchcraft. Now you know."

So this thing was basically a…

Witchcraft. Maybe one that could even move on its own.

No wonder these two trolls who'd left their tribes had fought tooth and nail over it.

Then again, even within a tribe, something like this would probably cause a frenzy — how could a living male troll possibly compare to wicked Amani witchcraft for thrills?

Jace could barely wrap his head around how the damn thing was even used. He recalled picking it up and putting it on for a moment, and his mind had instantly tangled itself into a knot.

He caught his breath and watched Silya slide her dress down, unwrapping the sweat-dampened cloth from her waist and chest. In the dim lamplight, her smooth skin was still slick with glistening sweat.

By now, Jace could smell a beastly musk. But he had to admit — the female troll's figure was damn fine. Even if her arms were a little disproportionate, those powerful muscle lines, those slender but far-from-delicate limbs…

But those enormous flat feet and that narrow, tight chest — no matter how he looked at it, Jace couldn't work up any interest. Then again, it didn't really matter how big or small she was. This was a completely different species, through and through!

"To be honest, I've been holding it in for months. I was waiting for you to bring it back, and instead you brought back a piece of junk — a powerless, useless piece of junk. So even if you didn't come here guided by the gods, I wasn't about to let you off easy."

Silya stepped closer. It was clear she was trying her hardest to move gracefully, but it still came off as a little comical. Jace knew that if the one approaching him had been a male troll, he probably would have already lost it and gone berserk. But…

The troll drew up to him, and Jace felt like a prey animal pinned to the ground and sniffed over carefully by a big cat.

"You have strong power, and I like the scent you give off… And the thought of you beating that bitch so badly she could only tear off that mask in frustration to vent — it makes me itch all over."

She was nearly pressed against his face now, baring her teeth with a low growl.

Despite the whiff of bad breath, for someone like Jace who was used to dealing with every foul stench of death, decay, evil, and corruption, this smell was almost… gentle.

"My Common vocabulary is really quite limited… Fuck me, human," Silya whispered.

That finally got Jace to muster some feeling. Besides, who knew what tomorrow would bring? When you were this close to death, what was there left to fear?

"Then I'll say goodbye to that mask for you."

He reached out, grabbed the troll's arm, and wrenched her around, pushing her against the wall with her back to him. The troll's body stood no chance against Jace's strength. She stumbled two steps and caught herself on a nearby shelf to steady herself.

The witch doctor let out a growl, like a lioness enraged by a sudden grab from a male. She struggled on instinct again and again, only for Jace to seize her hands and feet each time. The whole shelf clattered as a handful of dried, specimen-like things tumbled down.

After enough thrashing to scatter and smash the once-neat shelf and table into wreckage, the troll was finally pinned by Jace, unable to move — reduced to a whimper.

"Later… try your best not to make a sound." Jace caught his breath, gripping her firm waist tightly. "Your voice makes me feel like something wants to take my head off."

As he spoke, he slid his trousers down. Seeing those long, pointed ears, he couldn't help but brush aside her hair and grab one, yanking the troll's head back sharply.

The troll groaned in pain but stifled it at last, panting and baring her fangs, lifting her eyes to glare fixedly at Jace.

Jace's other hand found roughly the right spot. He bit his lip and thrust himself forward. It was like squeezing into a narrow stone crevice — a painful grind.

The Silya beneath him narrowed her eyes and sucked in a breath, shaking her head and panting raggedly. In a broken, halting voice she said, "I'm starting to wonder… whether you really killed that death knight, or not…"

"You'll regret saying that, troll."
