Chapter 334: Aftermath

Coming back from the dead was no more pleasant than dying in the first place.

Jace felt like his whole body's skin had been shriveled up and was now being stretched back out — it was agony. His body was so fragile he couldn't stand at all. He could only slump in the chair Misershed had prepared for him, crippled, tilting his head to stare in one fixed direction like someone who couldn't take care of himself.

Before he'd fully recovered his speech, the goblin was already standing in front of him with a small notepad. "Race."

"Human."

"Place of birth."

"Lord… amere."

"Name."

"Jack… Slovakia."

"What the hell kind of name is that? Whatever — remembering a fake name you made up better than your real one really proves the effect!"

"Age."

"Eighteen."

"Huh?" The goblin frowned. "You didn't forget everything after eighteen, did you? That's impossible… there shouldn't be memory loss like that… something's wrong."

"I'm only eighteen," Jace said. "I was only eighteen when I met you, Mr. Misersheld."

"I've been dealing with a brat this whole time?!" the goblin said. "Open your mouth!"

Jace opened his mouth. "What for?"

"Ugh, these teeth — you really are a brat. Younger than I thought." The goblin pried his lips apart, glanced inside his mouth, and sighed. "Ah, well — everything went smoothly! Now tell me what trouble you've actually gotten yourself into. I've never heard of any time-limited lethal poison or curse-medicine."

"Death knight," Jace said. "I was hit by a death knight's Death Shadow curse. You heard of that?"

"Ah, I know the orcs cooked up some half-dead warriors, though I've never seen one." The goblin scratched his head. "But that curse… I've never heard of it. A person really should keep learning no matter what, or you can't keep up with the times!"

"Anyway, it's a lethal curse. And after you die, it causes even more trouble." Jace tried to stand. The motion made every bone in his body crack and pop.

"Biting zombies, or murder ghosts — that sort of thing." The goblin laughed. "It's not going to turn you into a super-rot ogre missile that levels two city blocks, is it?"

Jace froze. A related image flashed through his mind. Then he nodded. "Anyway — thank you, Mr. Misersheld."

The goblin put his hands on his hips. "Don't thank me yet. Are you sure a death knight's curse can be wiped out like this? I know nothing about it, so I take no responsibility. If it doesn't work, don't come looking for me!"

"Of course I won't hold you responsible." Jace nodded. "I don't have any way to test whether it's actually gone yet, but even if it did nothing, I wouldn't blame you, sir."

"Hey, you, kid." Misersheld said, "But you won't tell me your real name, and you won't tell me where you're from. If this potion really takes off and I want to give you the share of the money that's yours, what am I supposed to do?"

Jace said, "If you really mean to leave it for me, put it in a small chest and set it aside. I'll come collect it. And if you've got the face for it, keep it for yourself."

"You're an interesting one." The goblin smiled. "Then it's settled. If this potion really can beat that bastard Noggenfogger's inferior product… Hah! You'll get what's coming to you."

"I'll wait for your good news. Goodbye, Mr. Misersheld."

"Goodbye, Mr. Slovakia." The goblin nodded.

Walking down from Misersheld's house, Jace found a deserted corner and leaned against the damp wooden wall of an abandoned building, catching his breath.

The aftereffects of this new potion were certainly potent. But he'd made it through. No severe coma like Ursula had warned about, no memory loss like Misersheld had described.

He focused, recalling everything he remembered — from his past life to this one — stringing together the most vivid memories as best he could, making sure there were no serious gaps in between… Just then, he suddenly noticed a strangely familiar image slowly appearing beside him.

He warily shifted away. The image turned its head toward him.

Wait — wasn't this Molophil? The rough outline of a wingless succubus.

He rubbed his eyes, and the image seemed to vanish again.

As he focused once more, the image sharpened again.

The image seemed to notice Jace's attention too, pulling away from the wooden wall and moving to the side. Jace's gaze followed her…

The succubus suddenly materialized and pounced in front of Jace, glaring. "Why can you see me, master?"

"How should I know?" Jace said, equally bewildered. "I just saw what looked like a shadow here, and I thought it was you… Is this a soul link thing?"

"Impossible!" Molophil said. "Even the soul link only gives a general sense of each other. It shouldn't be possible to see this clearly. Did you drink a Detect Invisibility potion? No… I've been right beside you the whole time. You haven't drunk anything except that weird goblin potion. Could that potion detect invisibility?"

Jace shook his head. He remembered that in the game, Noggenfogger's Potion only did the growing, the shrinking, the lightening, and the skeleton transformation — nothing else.

Misersheld's potion seemed to have no effects beyond the skeleton transformation. It might also be that the sample size was too small to reveal anything. Or perhaps the goblin simply didn't care about those extra, non-dangerous "side effects."

Either way, a Detect Invisibility potion was a completely different thing. How could Misersheld's potion possibly grant that ability?

Right — where was Sazeno?

Jace lifted his cloak. Nothing there.

"Sazeno?"

The imp revealed himself from under the cloak. "Master?"

He walked out into the street, looking out over all of Booty Bay, focusing his mind, searching for that same feeling as he walked.

Before long, he sensed an indistinct shadow appear at the edge of his vision. That was the Sailor's House!

Was he sensing the imp living inside?

He followed the direction of the shadow and looked in… That's right — it was that imp.

Was this something like a demon hunter's spectral sight?

No — he couldn't sense living people or animals. He could only see demons.

This wasn't Detect Invisibility or spectral sight. This was Detect Demon!

He'd somehow picked up a warlock's Detect Demon ability from the game — except there was no minimap. It required focus to manifest in his field of vision.

But why couldn't he see the invisible Sazeno clearly? Right — Sazeno had mentioned ever since they'd met that his Phase Shift was far more powerful than an ordinary imp's, and thus cost more mana. Seeing him might require even greater focus, or stronger detection.

But how had he acquired this ability? It came out of nowhere. Could it be related to the experience of transforming into undead?

Jace avoided the crowded areas and found a corner beneath a wooden bridge. He sat and recalled everything he'd been through these past few days. Aside from drinking the potion, nothing else seemed particularly unusual.

Molophil couldn't help but ask, "Master, what's going on? You're making me uneasy."

"Me seeing things makes you uneasy?" Jace asked.

Molophil explained, "I just relax when I'm invisible because very few people can see me like this. I only focus and push my invisibility to the limit when facing Nathrezim. So I was afraid my invisibility had failed somehow."

"No, it's not your problem," Jace said.

"You don't think you can hide from a gaz'erene, do you?" Sazeno laughed. "I'd like to see you dodge those multi-eyed monsters' detection!"

"Being invisible in front of a gaz'erene is pointless," the succubus said. "Rushing up and stabbing out its weird eyes is far more efficient than sneaking!"

"Blowhard!" the imp shouted.

"Quiet, Molophil. Nathrezim — why would they be able to see you?" Jace asked.

"They can't see. But they can sense the presence of demons, master," Molophil said. "Like those ancient demon lords who've lived eons — they've mastered the ability to sense demons. When they detect you nearby, they'll… they'll look at me just like you're doing…"

At the mention of the Nathrezim — the dreadlords — a suspicion formed in Jace's mind.

Could this be a remnant power left behind by Teron Gorefiend?

But if it was Gorefiend's power, why was it only manifesting now?

Of course, Jace still remembered that in the ruined Dark Iron temple, he'd suddenly felt something like the activation of Gorefiend's Grasp — but it was extremely faint. It might have just been a surge of magical energy when the staff flew over.

Since returning to Stormwind with Vereesa, he hadn't sensed anything unusual.

So Detect Demon… why was it only appearing now?

"Right, Molophil," Jace said. "Smell me. Is there still that strange corpse-rot stench on me?"

Molophil leaned in close to his neck. "It's gone… master. Does that mean the curse that's been troubling you has disappeared?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "To be sure, I'd have to go back to the Stormwind church and have those priests check me over."

Even as he said it, Jace really couldn't sense that discomfort that had been haunting him all this time.

But he didn't dare get excited just yet. The last time he'd let his guard down, he'd overlooked a curse that could have cost him his life… The journey back would take ten days or more. The thought made him so anxious he wanted to teleport back this instant.

Right — there was one other way to verify. Tea.

But what kind of tea did the Cathedral of Light serve? Last time, he'd been so preoccupied with the Death Shadow that he'd forgotten to ask Brother Fadin what variety it was.

Did Booty Bay even have a priest?
