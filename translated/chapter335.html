Chapter 335: The Forgotten Monastery

Jace told the two demons to hide themselves and follow, then asked a goblin guard. To his surprise, Booty Bay actually had a priest who'd come from Stormwind.

Following the guard's directions, he reached the middle of the port and descended to the lower level. He put his mask on, told the demons to wait outside, and entered a narrow lane packed with refugees huddled in tents and ramshackle dens.

It seemed to be home to many people in tattered clothes, most of them listless. Many appeared to be suffering from various illnesses. The whole place was a slum.

A young man dressed neatly and moving briskly like Jace stood out sharply among them — but the people around him had the energy to pay him no mind, and they didn't care about his coming or going.

At the end of the lane was a run-down building, looking a bit like the little monastery in Tarren Mill from the game — a doorway, a simple aisle, two rows of benches, a pulpit. The whole monastery was just that one small hall.

And this little monastery didn't even have two rows of benches. Deep inside, fallen rubble and broken pillars were scattered everywhere. The pulpit up front was nowhere to be seen. A few children were hiding inside, staring at Jace as he stood in the doorway. Jace looked up at the collapsed, leaking roof and worried the kids might get hit by falling stones and chunks of wall.

"One moment, please!"

Before him, a thin man in a light brown robe and a sun hat sat on the steps by the door. He glanced up, gave Jace that instruction, then continued speaking with a wizened old woman in worn clothes beside him.

Jace nodded and waited at a short distance behind the old woman. Their voices were too low to make out.

While he waited, Jace observed the people outside. Only then did he realize every single resident here was human.

"Sir, have you come seeking help too?"

Jace turned. The old woman had left at some point, and the man was looking at him.

He wasn't much better off than the people living around him — gaunt and sallow, very different from the sturdy humans Jace had seen out on the docks.

But those were probably all pirates, or mercenaries like himself.

"Are you the monk of this monastery?"

"I'm a priest, yes. But do you think this counts as a monastery? Perhaps." The man asked, "Are you from Lordaeron?"

"Stormwind," Jace said. "What is this place, exactly?"

"I used to serve at the Cathedral of Light myself, sir." The man said. "But since you're from Stormwind, you should know — Booty Bay was built by humans a long time ago, and had close ties with Stormwind. This monastery has been here since early on. It was only abandoned decades ago, after trolls and goblins took turns occupying this town."

"Fortunately, the Church of the Holy Light doesn't seem to refuse priests like me from coming here. So I intend to help the struggling people here as best I can."

Jace nodded and asked quietly, "So how did all these people end up here? Are they descendants of the town's former residents?"

"No. The Orc Wars," the priest said. "When the war broke out, not everyone went north. Some — especially people from the towns outside Stormwind — fled too late. By then there were already reports of orcs pushing north, so they didn't dare sail north by sea."

Jace still remembered the Faolble family of Westfall — they'd been the ones who didn't escape in time and had to hide in the mines. The people here had at least made it to Booty Bay, but their lives hadn't been much better.

And during the war, the goblins had actually cooperated with the Horde. It was hard to imagine how these people had lived in fear in this place all those years.

"Doesn't the king know about this?" Jace asked.

"The king… I don't know. I was shocked too when I first arrived." The priest said, "Sorry to dump all this on you… really, sir. Let's talk about your problem instead."

"It's nothing," Jace said. "Actually, I came here to ask — is there a kind of flower tea that, once brewed, turns the flowers a pale green? With a distinctive smell."

The priest nodded. "I know that pale chrysanthemum. I have some of the tea flowers here. Would you like me to brew you some?"

Jace said, "That would be wonderful, monk. No need to brew it — I just want a few petals to smell."

At that, the priest's expression seemed to darken.

"One moment."

The priest stood and went inside. Shortly after, he came out with a small handful of petals and handed them over. By now, his face already carried a trace of sympathy.

Jace knew — the man had certainly heard of the Death Shadow.

He held his breath as he took the petals and examined them. Yes — this was the shape and color he'd seen in that teacup, just a bit more shriveled than the brewed ones.

If they had no smell, then this whole trip would have been for nothing.

But this hurdle had to be crossed sooner or later. Nothing to be afraid of. If it came to nothing, he'd just find another way.

He steeled himself, brought the crushed tea flowers close to his nose, and inhaled — too hard, in fact. He sucked some fragments in and couched, choking.

After a moment, he tasted something acrid, like gasoline — hard to describe precisely.

He frowned at the priest. "Does it really smell this bad?"

"Well, this flower isn't exactly pleasant to begin with."

The priest's expression relaxed considerably.

"But don't worry, sir. That means there's nothing wrong with you."

"I… I see. Thank you." Jace said, a little excited. He rubbed the flowers in his hand and brought them to his nose again. That sharp, choking scent had actually become a lot more pleasant.

He'd just stood up when he remembered something and pulled two gold coins from his pocket, holding them out to the monk. "Take these, priest."

"By the Light…" The monk's eyes went wide. "No, there's really no need, sir. Life here is manageable. Even as a donation, this is far too much — a few copper, or even silver, would more than express your gratitude to the Light."

"The church has helped me a great deal," Jace said. "Without you, I'd be done for — done for in ways you can't imagine. Consider it buying some food and clothing for the people here. And for these flowers…"

The monk looked at the two gold coins again, then at the crushed tea flowers scattered on the ground, and couldn't help but smile. "All right. Truly, thank you. I don't know what to say. To be honest, this will greatly ease the hardship here. We won't have to worry about it for a long time!"

Jace nodded. "When I get back, I'll find a way to let King Varian know — at least let him know there are still these people of the Kingdom of Stormwind trapped here."

"Really?" the priest asked. "You can actually meet the king?"

"Probably not. But I should be able to get the word to him."

"That would be more than enough. That would be wonderful."

Jace said, "But I have one condition, monk. Whether you eventually return to Stormwind or not, don't tell anyone you met someone like me here. Can I trust you?"

"I will keep what happened today locked away in my heart," the priest said.

Jace nodded and stood, glancing at the children staring at him from inside the ruined monastery.

"Then goodbye, monk. Thank you again for your help. It truly means a great deal to me."

"Don't mention it. It's just a bit of flower tea."

Stepping outside into the lane, Jace let out a long breath.

This trip had gone fairly smoothly, all things considered.

He gazed at the hazy statue of the Great Mogul Revilgaz on the sea at the edge of his vision, while in the corner of his eye he saw a shadow lean gently against his side.

His guess had been right. Part of Teron Gorefiend's power hadn't vanished completely with the destruction of his consciousness.

With the Death Shadow's hold broken, an ability like Detect Demon seemed to have been released, as if unlocked.

Perhaps it was because Gorefiend had poured his last strength into this curse. Perhaps it was because the curse had been suppressing Jace's body from accepting new power.

As for exactly how much power remained, he'd have to feel that out bit by bit from here on.

Possession… possession. Who's possessing whom now?
