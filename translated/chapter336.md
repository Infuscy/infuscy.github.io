## Chapter 336: The Shadow of the Dark Portal

By his count, it should be mid-April now.

He'd stepped off the ship that morning and gazed out at Stormwind Keep and the Redridge Mountains beyond — a sweep of green, nothing like the scene when he'd returned from Lordaeron.

Freed from the Shadow of Death's torment, Jace felt like he had boundless energy.

After getting home, he put away the scrolls, weapons, and the rest, then looked down at his battle report — a few hundred words at most — and deeply felt that a place like Booty Bay was no environment for getting real work done.

Still, he'd at least used the time aboard ship to sort out the pronunciation of every word on the scroll with the imp and the succubus, correcting plenty of fuzzy spots. In the next couple of days he'd find a sturdy scroll and recopy the whole thing with the cleaned-up text.

He changed into his regular in-city clothes, told the two demons to watch the house, and headed back out to the Mage Quarter.

Ursula happened to be out of the basement today, so he couldn't hand over the mojo. Jace went straight to Malin's, planning to report in to his teacher and mention the situation with Booty Bay and the Stormwind refugees while he was at it.

Naturally he couldn't come out and say what he'd actually seen in Booty Bay. He'd have to frame it as rumors picked up in Duskwood, or as meeting someone who'd somehow made their way back from Booty Bay. Then again, this didn't really concern the Wizard's Sanctum — reporting it through them didn't seem quite right.

Still, he'd bring it up in passing. He'd never gone to Stormwind Keep on his own initiative; he could even ask Malin for pointers on how to get an audience with the king.

He pushed open the study door. Malin wasn't there. Amy, instead, was tidying the bookshelves.

"Master Malin won't be in today, sir. If you're looking for him, come back tomorrow morning," she said, glancing toward the door — and then froze.

"Jace? You're finally back! Father told me what happened to you, your — oh my—"

"Amy…" Jace said with a smile. "I'm fine now."

"Really fine?" Amy set down what she was holding, came around the desk, and sniffed at his clothes. "I really can't smell anything… but Father said only a mage who's very sensitive to magic would be able to sense that kind of curse."

"I could smell it before," Jace said, spreading his arms. "Let alone you — I'm definitely fine."

Amy complained, "You were gone this whole time without a word. If I'd wanted to find you, I wouldn't have known where to look!"

"I was in a real hurry when I left, so I forgot to say goodbye. Sorry about that," Jace said.

"You think?" Amy sighed. Then: "Wait — did you get taller?" She stepped back and looked at the top of his head. "I feel like you've grown quite a bit."

"The demon's curse must have been stunting my growth. As a descendant of the Lordaeron generals, I shouldn't have been that short to begin with," Jace said, ruffling his own hair.

"You can still joke about something that was life-or-death," Amy said, exasperated.

"Of course I can joke — it's resolved!" Jace said with a grin.

"Come on, I'm treating you to a meal to celebrate," Amy offered.

"All right. Oh — did that letter go out? The one to Vereesa?" Jace asked.

"Vereesa?" Amy leaned on the desk and narrowed her eyes. "On a first-name basis with the Windrunner lady, are we? And that song — using an elven woman's melody as the password to break a magical seal… I'd say she's genuinely interested in you."

"What nonsense," Jace said. "That tune has to do with her family. Nothing to do with me."

"Except that letter never went out," Amy said, slapping the desk. "It's still right here!"

"Good thing it didn't go out. But what happened?" Jace asked. "Did Master Malin forget?"

"Of course he didn't forget. How could he forget?" Amy said. "He's been going to High Sorcerer Andromath and the palace regularly these past few weeks asking about the elven situation, but there's been no one returning to Quel'Thalas or able to help deliver a letter. Even the elves passing through Stormwind have all been rushed off to reinforce the Dark Portal. He was getting anxious — if you hadn't come back soon, he was about to contact Dalaran's couriers."

"Elves reinforcing the Dark Portal?" Jace asked. "Is the campaign not going well?"

"Dalaran seems to be mustering people too. A few mages from the Wizard's Sanctum left the other day," Amy said. "Father didn't tell me why, but the expedition clearly isn't going as smoothly as expected."

Hearing that, Jace couldn't help thinking back to the strange feeling that had come over him in the Redridge Mountains when he'd gazed toward the Dark Portal.

"When you see Master Malin, tell him not to send that letter. I've found a way to deal with it," he said.

"All right," Amy said. "He'll be relieved. Come on, I'm buying."

The two of them left and came to the Blue Recluse. The tavern wasn't too crowded at this hour. A young mage behind the counter was playing an unfamiliar tune on a flute.

"Do you drink wine, Jace?" Amy asked.

"Of course."

"Two glasses of vintage Dalaran red, then — grilled spotted yellowtail, a pumpkin pie, and a spicy wolf chop for this mage," Amy told the man at the counter.

"How do you know I like grilled meat?" Jace asked with a grin.

"I don't just know you like it — I know you're too cheap to buy it for yourself," Amy said, finding an empty table nearby and sitting down with a slow exhale. "If you ate a bit more often, you might have been even taller than this. I suppose I ought to take you out and feed you a few more meals while you're still young."

"Then I'll have to come to your father's place every day from now on," Jace said, sitting down. "Learn magic and get fed, too."

"You need to eat more on your own," Amy said. "Though it seems like these past months you haven't just gotten taller — you've filled out a lot. Have you finally started spending money on yourself?"

"I'm definitely better off than before," Jace said with a nod.

Amy hesitated a moment, then said, "I know you probably don't want to talk about it. I know plenty of people have probably already asked you this, but I still want to know…"

"I heard about your situation from Father. Teron Gorefiend… how did you actually do it? It's not that I don't believe you, Jace, but Teron Gorefiend was like a terrifying demon king to me. When word came that Teron Gorefiend had appeared on the battlefield, everyone would gasp — they knew it would be a brutal fight. I still can't forget the look on Father's face when he learned he'd shown up in Hillsbrad."
