Chapter 337: The Mage's Resolve

"A death knight is extremely weak once they leave their body," Jace said. "They're just a ghost. Even Teron Gorefiend — though he was terrifying too, sure. But wasn't Sylvanas… Lady Sylvanas helping? Without her it couldn't have been done. You don't think I could take down a Horde leader all on my own, do you?"

Amy laughed. "You say she did it, she says you did it — the two of you haven't compared notes?"

"She's being humble," Jace said. "With her power, she could drop three or four ogres in the blink of an eye. Those are monsters over three meters tall, think about it, Amy. Any one of those ogres could smash my head to pulp with a single punch."

"I don't believe it."

"You don't believe she can kill ogres?"

"I don't believe you're as useless as you say you are," Amy said with a smile.

Jace picked up a wolf chop and stripped off a layer of meat to chew. "Then there's nothing I can do about it."

Amy scooped up some pumpkin with her spoon. "You think I don't know? The strict Archmage Andormas is willing to let you become a full mage in two years, Jace. You'll be the first person since the war to become a full mage before the age of twenty."

Jace shook his head. "By his account there's still an exam, Amy. You know how hard it becomes a full mage. He's just giving me an opportunity. Who knows what things will look like in two years."

"You're not saying all that just to spare my feelings, are you?" Amy raised an eyebrow. "Everyone knows what's going on, Jace."

"Of course not. But it's uncertain all the same — it's too early to say," Jace said.

The waiter brought the grilled fish over, the curled scales still sizzling and popping. Jace noticed Amy drinking without pause, staring at the fish in front of her, the smile on her face fading bit by bit.

She seemed to have something else on her mind.

"What's wrong?" Jace said. "You look like you're worried about something."

Amy seemed jolted by the question. She blinked, glanced over, hesitated a moment, then said, "All right. I've been making a very important decision these past few days, Jace. I think I need your opinion — or maybe your encouragement."

"Go on."

"I… want to apply to Nethergarde Keep."

"Nethergarde Keep?" Jace asked in surprise. "You're planning to join the expeditionary force?"

"The expeditionary force? Of course not. I'm just an apprentice mage," Amy said, shaking her head slightly. "They'd never let me cross the Dark Portal. At most I'd be doing logistics — using my skills to support the fighting from Nethergarde Keep."

Jace looked at her and nodded, taking a swig of the wine the waiter had poured. "Why the sudden decision?"

"I need something solid — experience that can get me to full mage, Jace," Amy said, looking down. "Just studying, being a teaching assistant… heaven knows when I'll ever finish. I'm getting closer and closer to thirty. I can't keep going on like this. And no, it's not because I'm jealous of you or anything — you're actually a strong motivation for me."

Hearing that, Jace didn't quite know what to say.

The cold, bitter wine swirled in his mouth, and all he could think of was the murky, dark future that hadn't happened yet.

The Horde's current warchief, Ner'zhul, channeling the three sacred artifacts to open a teleportation spell — the tremendous twisting force tearing the entire Draenor apart. The expeditionary force, to keep Draenor's destructive energy from reaching Azeroth, forced to destroy the Dark Portal, severing the two worlds completely…

"Master Marin probably has some general idea of the situation on the other side of the Dark Portal. That he chose not to tell you — could it be precisely because he doesn't want you going to Nethergarde Keep?"

"His thoughts can't interfere with my decision," Amy said, her face hardening. "Don't tell me you're going to be like that old man and try to talk me into staying?"

"No," Jace said. "I know you're not content to stay in the Mage Quarter. I'm just reminding you."

"I knew you'd understand me," the mage said with a small smile. "It's just — if I go, it'll probably be a long time before I can come back to Stormwind."

Jace nodded, savoring a mouthful of wine. "At least you'll be far from that mage who's been harassing you."

Amy laughed. "That guy's nothing…"

"When are you planning to apply to Archmage Andormas?" Jace asked.

"Since the decision's made, tomorrow," Amy said. "I don't know if I'll get approved. Not every apprentice mage is allowed to go support Nethergarde Keep. In the end it might all come to nothing."

"Then I wish you luck," Jace said, raising his glass.

The two of them clinked glasses. Amy knocked back several big gulps at once, and even Jace couldn't help staring at her.

He realized that in the half-plus year he'd spent with Amy, he'd already come to see her as family. Thinking about the unknown situation at the Dark Portal and Nethergarde Keep, he couldn't help worrying.

Amy seemed to notice that Jace didn't look too happy, and asked in a teasing tone, "What, are you going to miss me?"

"Since it's your wish, of course I support you going," Jace said.

The mage nodded and took another sip, closed her eyes as she swallowed, and said, "Once I'm in the Swamp of Sorrows, I'll probably miss you."

Jace wiped the wine from the corner of his mouth. "I might end up at Nethergarde Keep too, Amy. Then there won't be any question of missing each other."

"You're going too?" Amy asked. "You can become a full mage even without going. Why take the risk?"

"It's just that ever since the Dark Portal appeared, everything else seems to matter a lot less to me," Jace said. "Of course I'm not saying dealing with gnolls or murlocs or bandits and brigands isn't important. It's just, compared to the Dark Portal standing there in the south… but maybe it's only because I'm not the king that I think this way." He finished his wine and called out, "Another round — mead this time!"

Even the black dragons were connected to the other side of the Dark Portal, he was thinking.

Hearing the waiter's acknowledgment, Jace went on, "Even if you hadn't said you were going, I've felt like I'd end up there someday."

He took the mead that was brought over, took a sip, and raised his finger to trace circles in the air. "The Dark Portal is like a vortex. Every time I look toward that direction, I feel like that place is little by little pulling me in."

Amy heard him and laughed happily, nodding without stop.

She stared at Jace for a while. Jace touched his face. "Am I drunk?"

"No," Amy said, propping up her reddening cheeks. "I just suddenly felt, Jace — Teron Gorefiend deserved to die at the hands of someone like you."
