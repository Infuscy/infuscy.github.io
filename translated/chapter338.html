Chapter 338: Ancestral Connection

The next day, Jace arrived early at the gates of Stormwind Keep. He'd never come to seek an audience with the king on his own and had no idea what procedure to follow, so he simply joined the crowd of people wanting to see the king.

He didn't know how long he'd been in line. People wanting to see the king kept coming without end. Somehow Jace kept getting squeezed toward the back. And some people would call out a few words and someone would take them inside — as if they'd made an appointment in advance. He had no idea there was even a way to make an appointment.

Just then, a voice called out from somewhere: "Mr. Sesso, is that you?"

Jace turned and saw a familiar face standing outside the line looking at him. Wasn't this one of Queen Tiffin's relatives?

What was his name… Jace could only remember he was a baron.

"Baron…"

The man came up to him. "You here to see the king too, Mr. Sesso?"

Hearing his tone, Jace suddenly recalled his name: Grey Ellerian.

Jace said, "I heard about something — those war refugees in Booty Bay. Do you know anything about that, by any chance?"

"Hm." The baron nodded. "I… all right, I know a little. There's been very little news about those who fled south during the war."

Jace said, "The war is over now. Right now they're being looked after by just one priest, and life is very hard."

"A priest?" Ellerian sighed. "Look at the situation of the refugees outside the city walls, my Mr. Sesso. Do you think their lives are any better than those people in Booty Bay? At least those people have a priest."

"Of course the king wants to bring back all his subjects who wandered because of the war, but for now… since they still have a way to get by where they are, coming back wouldn't necessarily mean a better situation."

"Normal people have a hard time living well in Booty Bay, Baron," Jace said. "Many of them were farmhands, hunters, miners from Moonbrook or Sentinel Hill before the war — it's hard for them to adapt to that place. And their hometowns need people now, don't they? Otherwise a few more of them would probably turn pirate."

"What you say makes sense, Mr. Sesso. We each have our reasons," the baron said helplessly.

"Then at least let the king know about this and let him decide," Jace said. "Maybe I'm oversimplifying, Baron — you're thinking more comprehensively — but letting His Majesty know… that's always the right thing to do, isn't it? He can hear more people's views too."

"All right, I'll go tell him," the baron said. "Even if I didn't, His Majesty would see you again sooner or later, and you'd definitely seize that chance to say it, wouldn't you?"

"Thank you, Baron," Jace said. "The people there very much want to go home. They'll thank you."

"Frankly, there are few people in this world who know what they really want," the baron said with a slight bow. "But rest assured, Mr. Sesso — I'll carry your message to the king."

Jace nodded and watched the man hurry into the palace gates.

It was nearly past noon, and he was getting hungry.

He followed the walls of Stormwind Keep all the way to the Old Town gate, threaded through the crowded streets, and came to the Pig and Whistle Inn.

He stared at the sign hanging there for a while, then suddenly noticed a familiar shadow sitting beneath it.

"Hey, Grede!"

"Huh?!" The dwarf looked up with a bread in his hand. Seeing Jace, his whole face kneaded itself into a ball that was impossible to read as angry or happy.

"You… where the hell have you been? I couldn't find you anywhere!"

"Went to Booty Bay," Jace said.

"Booty Bay?!" Grede asked with a grin. "Fun?"

Jace cleared his throat. "Inside?"

Grede hopped down the steps and came in front of Jace, wrapping his arms around Jace's waist and squeezing for all he was worth. Jace felt like he was about to stop breathing. After a couple of coughs he said, "I missed you too, Grede."

"You bastard," Grede said. "Haven't seen you this long and even your smell has changed!"

Jace smiled. "Compared to what I've been through these past months, a change in smell barely counts."

"I heard a little something," Grede said, squeezing into the packed tavern. "A young mage in Stormwind killed Teron Gorefiend. Even the Bronzebeard king knows about it. I'll tell you — I didn't even have to guess to know it was you. I knew that business in Dalaran wasn't finished."

"Especially when I heard there was a Windrunner involved — that much I'm even more sure of. You still couldn't escape that elf's clutches in the end, could you?"

Jace followed him to a corner. "The elf paid well. Why would I run?"

Grede sat down with a laugh. "Even without hearing that news, I could tell you've been through a lot, Jace. Not only has your smell changed — you look different too!"

"Do I?" Jace said. "I can't tell at all myself."

The dwarf raised an eyebrow. "Tell me about Booty Bay. What were you doing there?"

"Looking for a medicine," Jace confessed. "Teron Gorefiend left me a curse that wasn't easy to deal with. I searched everywhere and only heard that the goblins in Booty Bay sold a medicine from Kalimdor that could solve my problem."

Hearing "antidote," Grede's grin of waiting for a good show immediately deflated.

"Damn it, what curse? Then did you solve it? Did the medicine work?"

"Don't worry, it worked. Nothing to worry about now."

"All right," Grede said with a sigh. "Let me put it this way, Jace Sesso — not being able to help you deal with that death knight already makes me feel awful enough. If something else big happened to you and I couldn't help, then I might as well not have gone to Ironforge at all."

"What are you talking about…" Jace sighed. "Like I can't survive without you. Tell me — that thing about the Avatar, any leads?"

Grede said quietly, "My trip was probably a lot more boring than yours, Jace."

"Anyway, the Avatar isn't a spell you can just learn. I asked a lot of dwarves in Ironforge who've tried to walk this path or witnessed the Mountain Kings' explorations. I've basically figured out what kind of training you'd need to gain power like that."

"Remember what I told you — that our ancestors were things of the deep earth? And that when the gods created the earthen, it was in the temples where they lived. According to the researchers in those museums, those temples still hold the power of our bloodline."

"If a dwarf wants to reclaim this power that belonged to our ancestors, he has to go back to those temples and feel the bond of blood — until he's certain he's found that bond and returned to the earth again. Then he'll be able to train and strengthen his body constantly, like an earthen ancestor, until he's forged himself into something as hard and tall as a true stone statue."

"Honestly, I don't understand," Jace said.

"Don't understand what part?" Grede asked.

"From 'feel the bond of blood' onward," Jace said. "How do you feel it? What do you feel… I'm hearing it and getting confused, let alone the part about fighting like an earthen ancestor afterward. You mean — turn directly into an earthen? Or temporarily become an earthen? At that point are you still counted as a dwarf?"

"You're not a dwarf, it's hard to explain to you…" Grede sighed. "All right, I admit I don't understand either. The dwarves of Ironforge don't seem to understand either. But I've done my best to find everyone in Dun Morogh who knows about this — I just short of going straight to King Magni."

"In any case, all the Mountain Kings went through this stage, didn't they?" Jace asked. "They all went to some temple to find this bond of blood?"

"There's a temple ruin near Menethil Harbor, and a few more in Dun Morogh and Loch Modan. The dwarves in Ironforge say those ruins were buried with ancient power when they were first excavated. But they've been dug out by now — the ruins, the holy relics inside… they've pretty much been found. Those Bronzebeard dwarves who want to find the power of the Incarnation all say they can't feel any so-called ancestral bond in those ruins at all. I actually went to the Iron Ring Excavation Site in Loch Modan and stayed a few days. Honestly, I didn't feel anything."

"Did the Mountain Kings of the past all train in these ruins?"

"From what they told me, yes," the dwarf said. "So it's been a very long time since a dwarf who gained the power of the ancestors has appeared."

"Sounds like some kind of myth. However you look at it, it doesn't feel reliable," Jace said, shaking his head.

"Myth or not, I have to try," Grede said. "I have to find a lost, undiscovered temple ruin and take a look before I can give up, Jace. Even if it means going to Kalimdor, even if it means going to Northrend — it's all the same."

Jace of course knew there were still many undiscovered lost temples on Azeroth. In the game, plenty of dungeons were about exploring temples like this.

Like Ulduar in Northrend, or Uldum deep in the southern desert of Kalimdor — those were all creation temples left behind by the titans, places guarded by titan keepers.

But tens of thousands of years later, those places had more or less all developed problems — either corrupted by Old Gods, or occupied by all manner of strange creatures.

In short, for him and Grede right now, going to those places was pure fantasy.

Wait — what about Uldaman?

Uldaman was also a titan temple, just a lot smaller in scale than Ulduar and Uldum. In the game it was a five-person dungeon that wasn't even high-level. The temple ruins were located in the Badlands — that desert he and Sylvanas had planned to go to but never quite made it to.

Just now while talking to Grede, Jace had been carried along by the game's conventions and assumed Uldaman should have been excavated already. But considering this was still more than ten years before the game's timeline, there was a chance it was still a buried place?

"Are there ruins in the wasteland south of Loch Modan?" Jace asked tentatively.

"Heard there are, and they seem to be excavating already," Grede said. "But that's Dark Iron dwarf territory, Jace. What they're doing, we don't know."

So they'd started digging this early? But even more than ten years later, the depths of Uldaman still hadn't been fully explored by the Dark Iron dwarves — so their progress definitely couldn't be fast.

Then wasn't this an undiscovered temple?

"Do you know what's in those ruins?" Jace asked again.

"I…" Grede pursed his lips. "Of course I don't know. Those Bronzebeard dwarves don't seem to know either."

"Don't you want to go take a look, Grede?" Jace said. "Compared to temples that haven't been found yet — this one's right on our doorstep!"
