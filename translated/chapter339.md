## Chapter 339: An Amateur Archaeology Plan

"Since we're going there, we'll have to deal with the Dark Iron dwarves," Grede said with a nod.

Jace knew that compared to the dangers lying deep within Uldaman, merely the Dark Iron dwarves excavating outside barely counted as anything.

After all, even if it was a lot smaller in scale, it was still a genuine titan temple ruin, and the ancient guardians hiding in its deepest chambers, sealed away behind layers of stone doors, were no empty name.

Perhaps they'd grown weak from being sealed for tens of thousands of years, their original power lost — Jace didn't know what state they were in now.

But considering that dwarves could find the ancestral bond through the ruins in Loch Modan and Dun Morogh, Grede might not need to go all the way to the deepest part, open the titan's seal, and face the guardian himself.

"This one's not only the closest," Jace said. "And even if the two of us find temple ruins that Bronzebeard and Dark Iron scholars alike haven't found, we can't excavate a path inside. The Dark Iron dwarves are clearing the road for us."

The dwarf raised an eyebrow. "You're coming too?"

Hearing this, Jace asked in surprise, "Don't tell me you never even considered me from the start?"

"Of course I hope you'll help," Grede said. "But I don't know if it'll work if someone else is involved. I was planning to go alone."

"Don't tell me the ancestors are going to check the headcount the moment you step into those ancient ruins?" Jace asked. "If there's more than one person they'll block you at the door, or refuse to acknowledge you."

"Who knows," Grede said, scratching his beard. "I'm afraid I'll do something wrong again that doesn't match the old legends, and then won't find any bond, and it'll all fall through."

"Still better than dying in there," Jace said.

"Die?" Grede said. "When I was in the Wetlands I faced down a two-crocodile pincer attack without flinching. A few dark-skinned dwarves… how many legs can they have between them, twelve?"

"Can crocodiles shoot you in the head?" Jace asked.

"You think I can't blow theirs out?" Grede patted his waist, then glanced at the empty hook on his belt. "I'm not carrying it, but you know what I mean."

"Does your hammer fly faster than a bullet?" Jace asked.

Grede leaned back in his chair, looking this way, and sighed. "Then what am I supposed to do? Bring a rifle too? One shot draws a few dozen men. One rifle trading fire with a few dozen rifles?"

"I've still got some invisibility potions," Jace said. "Used them in the Burning Steppes. Aside from not being able to see a thing, they worked pretty well."

Grede sighed and spread his hands. "I know about that invisibility-potion stuff. The Dark Iron dwarves might not have found me otherwise — but after drinking it I'd stumble around blind for a few minutes and probably walk straight into their arms. That'd be better than me just walking in on my own."

"That's why you need coordination," Jace said. "I'll help you get past the Dark Iron dwarves and into that excavation site. If we find the ruins, you go in and do your thing, I'll keep watch outside. But I'm warning you — the temple ruins might be more dangerous than the outside."

"Not 'might,'" Grede said. "Definitely. But this is precisely part of the trial. Those excavation sites were dangerous too before they were opened up. So the more dangerous it is, the more it proves I've found the right place."

Even though that's what he said, Jace knew he had to go in with him — at the very least he had to warn him not to go too deep. Even with the seals, he should avoid provoking the remains of those "gods" from the ancient era.

"All right," Grede said. "So what do you want, Jace Sesso? You're not making any money on this trip!"

"I just don't want you to lose your life," Jace said, rapping the table.

Grede laughed. "Then I'll owe you one. Next time you need help, just call on me — no matter what it is."

"That works too," Jace said. "And once you've truly mastered that divine power, that's even more reliable. I come out ahead!"

"Of course," Grede shook his fist, then seemed to remember something and sighed. "It's just — not a single dwarf has succeeded all these years, and I'm a Bronzebeard dwarf… I really don't know how likely I am to make it to the end."

"You won't know until you try," Jace said. "Also — no matter what good things you find inside, they're all mine. Deal?"

"No problem," Grede said. "Anything that can be carried out is yours. So when do we leave?"

"I just got back from Booty Bay. Only yesterday," Jace said. "Give me a few days to rest and prepare."

"Perfect — I was planning to get a few more days of sleep anyway," Grede said, collapsing onto the table. "No rush, Jace. There's plenty of time."

"Oh right," Jace said. "I learned something new and was looking to try it out on you."

"What new thing?" Grede raised his head. "Powerful?"

"It should be powerful in theory," Jace said. "But I don't know if I'm using the wrong method or if my mana just isn't enough — I keep feeling this spell should be able to…"

"What spell? Speak," Grede asked in a low voice.

Jace nodded. "A shield spell. I'm told it can block weapons, arrows, and spells all at once — meaning it's like a wall, not just reducing damage the way Mage Armor does. But the problem is it lasts way too short a time. It barely appears for an instant and then vanishes. If someone sees me cast it and put up a barrier, they'll definitely choose to stop attacking, wait a bit, and then hit me — by which time my spell is already gone."

"But if you say its effect is weak… it's actually quite strong. If a weapon strikes it, it even deals damage back to the attacker. So if you're helping me test it, be careful — throw a rock from a distance or something, don't use your hammer."

"I'd never throw a hammer at you… but it does sound interesting," Grede said. "You're sure your incantation and casting are all correct? With magic, if any part of the casting isn't done properly, the effect gets drastically worse, don't they say?"

Jace of course hadn't failed to consider this possibility. But thinking back to the shadow magic before — even when he didn't pronounce it that precisely, or didn't bother with any casting gestures at all, the end result had never differed by much.

He didn't have too much time to keep developing this spell. But from his experience with those shadow magic spells, even if he trained this shield incantation to its ideal state, in the end it probably wouldn't last much longer either.

Going from one or two seconds and doubling it would only be three or four seconds — definitely not something you could treat as a sustained means of protection. Practicality-wise it couldn't even compare to Mage Armor, which at least partially defends.

After hearing it, Grede thought for a moment, then nodded. "Then you could use it like a shield."

"A shield?" Jace asked. "What do you mean?"

Grede gestured a few times with both hands in midair. "That means wait until the other guy's attack has already been launched and can't be pulled back, then you chant — and, from what you said, if this spell can damage the attacker back, isn't that making the other guy suffer the consequences of his own actions?"

Jace said, "But chanting takes time. By the time the other person swings a weapon or casts a spell, I can't get the incantation out in time — I've already been hit."

"Then that's a matter of proficiency, isn't it?" Grede said, folding his arms. "At first I also found communicating with the sky and summoning lightning very troublesome. But now I can establish contact with the elements very quickly, and the elements rarely refuse my summons, Jace."

"Even if you can't get the incantation out in an instant, you could use it against large monsters — like ogres, or… dragons, right?"

What Grede said made sense. Jace already had plenty of spells that he'd found a bit troublesome to chant at first but could now release very quickly. He really could catch those tiny gaps to chant, the way the dwarf said.

Jace said, "Then let's go practice outside tonight. How about it?"

"No problem. And it's been a long time since you've practiced swords, hasn't it?" Grede asked. "Getting rusty? I can see you're about to forget your swordplay teacher. Before we practice your new trick, we're testing blades first."

"Weren't the one who ran off to Ironforge yourself?" Jace said. "But speaking of swords — I've got a pretty special one right now. Perfect for a test."

"What sword? How special?" the dwarf asked.

Jace smiled mysteriously. "Too many people here — can't show you."

"Putting on airs," Grede muttered, scratching the corner of his mouth. "Get some food in me first — I haven't eaten lunch yet!"
