## Chapter 347: Preparing for the New Journey

After leaving Zardeth, Jace headed to the alchemy lab. Ursula was slumped in a chair with her forehead in her hands. She glanced toward the door at the sound of his footsteps and sat up with effort. "I thought you'd gone home."

Jace sat on a small stool in the corner of the room. "Actually, I came here to find you."

"Sorry, Jace. I don't think I'm up to brewing anything for you right now," Ursula said with a wry laugh. "I said what I said earlier, but the truth is, I don't really know of any formula that specifically strengthens resistance to mental interference. Aside from Shadow Protection Potion or an elixir that boosts shadow resistance, I'm out of ideas. My guess is that in the end, it comes down to forging your own willpower to keep a succubus like that in check."

"Even with potions, you still need to strengthen your own resolve," Jace said. "That goes for succubi and shadow magic alike. The moment the potion wears off, you become the warlock at his most vulnerable — that just creates more problems."

"You're right," Ursula said. "I wonder how the first warlock who mastered the succubus-summoning spell managed it."

"Probably got lucky," Jace said.

"Zardeth was certainly lucky too," Ursula said. "Having us right there, and more importantly, having a helper like you watching over him."

"Which is exactly why your own will matters that much more," Jace said. "You can't count on every warlock who learns the summoning spell having Zardeth's advantages — or your potions to help them."

"You're right." Ursula straightened up and lifted her chin. "Speaking of which — that Curse of Weakness you gave me last time, I've been practicing it with Surena, Jace."

"You haven't been relying on potions this whole time, have you?" Jace asked.

"Of course not. Drink too many and not only do you risk problems — my wallet couldn't take it either, Jace," Ursula said with a smile. "Once you get familiar with the feeling, it seems you can cast without the potion. Though I'll admit, the mood-affecting potions do produce more visible results."

"Right — what do you need? Tell me first, and I'll put it together once I've rested a bit."

Jace said, "There's something I wanted to ask you. Last time we talked about Noggenfogger Elixir — one of the ingredients is the dew gland from a bog beast in the desert. I was wondering whether the bog beasts in the Wetlands have something similar. The ones there certainly wouldn't need to store as much water, so the organ might be smaller, or even absent. But I figured it was worth a look."

"That's a good point," Ursula said. "Get one and we'll have a look."

Mentioning Noggenfogger Elixir swept away the gloom the alchemist had been feeling moments before. But she seemed to catch herself and asked, "Why are you being so proactive about this?"

"All right, the truth is…" Jace said. "I wanted to know the formula for Nature Protection Potion, Ursula. The ordinary kind — the sort that doesn't need Elemental Earth."

"Briarthorn and Liferoot — that's the most basic formula. There's a proprietary version I've mastered that also uses Wild Steelbloom and a touch of Blackmouth Oil," Ursula said. "I found that most nature magic hits with a powerful concussive force — enough to daze a person, even send them into shock… and my formula strengthens the drinker's ability to resist that dizziness."

"Mm… I don't need anything that complicated, Ursula," Jace said quickly. "Right now I'd be hard-pressed to get my hands on Briarthorn or Liferoot at all. Just the most basic version will do."

"Never mind the ingredients for now — just tell me how many vials you need," Ursula said.

"About four, to start," Jace said.

"Tell you what — get me two Liferoot and I'll handle the rest," Ursula said. "I did promise to make your potions free of charge, after all. Liferoot really is a bit pricey, and I'm running low myself… but I still have the rest in storage, especially the Blackmouth Oil."

"Then thanks, Ursula. That's actually what I came here for today," Jace said.

"And ended up dealing with a succubus along the way, didn't you?" Ursula said with a sigh.

"Luckily nothing went wrong," Jace said.

When the rain stopped that evening, Jace left the Slaughtered Lamb together with Erlan. The two parted ways by the canal, and Jace walked home alone.

The moment he opened the door, Morophil's brow furrowed.

"You went to see another succubus, Master. Who died this time?"

"Thanks to you, nobody did," Jace said.

"What a polite way to put it," Morophil said, leaning against the bed.

"I mean it literally," Jace said. "If I hadn't brought you into it, things might have gone badly. One more thing — you've got a new job from now on."

"Bwahaha!" Sazeno burst out laughing. "You're done for, Morophil. You have no idea what these past few months have been like for me! Now it's your turn to suffer!"

"What job?" Morophil shrank back against the wall. "It's not more copying, is it? Look at these slender arms of mine — I haven't got an ounce of strength, Master… writing back and forth a few times might twist them right off. How could you bear to torture me like that…"

"My arms aren't any thicker!" the imp hopped onto the table, holding up both hands. "Look! Look!"

"As for the specifics — you'll be in charge of torturing a few demons," Jace said, glancing at the imp and then back at the succubus. "All you have to do is force them to reveal their true names!"

"Huh?!" Morophil darted over. "Really?!"

The imp froze on the table, then slowly walked over, staring at Jace's face. "Does that count as a job, Master? Isn't that just pure entertainment?"

"Hah!" Morophil grabbed Sazeno by the horns and lifted him off the ground. "How about that now — who's Master's real favorite? Face it, you're nothing but a hairless quill… I'd suggest you know your place!"

"It's not fair! It's not fair!" the imp roared, thrashing in her grip.

"Hey, keep it down," Jace said, glancing at the door. "I have a question, Morophil."

Morophil tossed the imp aside. "What is it? I'm listening."

"To summon a succubus, you need a special object, don't you?" Jace asked.

Morophil smiled. "Ha, I know what you're getting at. You think some boring little 'lure' was enough to trick me into coming before you?"

"What really drew me in was your unique aura, Master. I'll admit, when I first laid eyes on you I was a bit disappointed — after all… it was just a boring human!"

She leaned against the table. "But now I have to say — the differences between humans are just as vast as the differences between demons…"
