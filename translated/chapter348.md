## Chapter 348: The Church's Concern

After two days of rest, the warlocks of the Dagger Circle had agreed to gather in the Slaughtered Lamb's basement that day to summon another succubus.

Jace planned to report to Marin that morning — after all, he hadn't seen his teacher since returning from Booty Bay.

The past two days had been overcast, but it hadn't rained all the way to the Mage Quarter.

Standing before the study, Jace pushed back his hood and saw Marin at the door in conversation with a man dressed as a priest.

The priest was an unfamiliar face, but he immediately reminded Jace: his illness was cured, but he hadn't given a report back to the Cathedral of Light.

He'd told the cathedral's priests that he'd taken a Restorative Potion and left town, but nearly a month had passed. He ought to go to the cathedral and explain in person.

But it looked like he wouldn't need to — this man had probably come to make inquiries.

"Master." He looked to Marin. Both of them glanced over.

"Mr. Sesso," Marin said, "Amy told me what happened. While you were away, the Church sent people twice to ask after you!"

The priest looked fairly young. He gave a slight nod. "I am Brother Martin of the Church. The Light bless you, Mr. Jace Sesso — it is good to see you well. But Master Marin mentioned you traveled south of the kingdom seeking a cure, is that right?"

"That's right!" Jace said.

"Let's talk inside," Marin said, ushering the two of them into the room.

The priest barely waited to sit down before speaking. "Mr. Sesso, I know Brother Fading briefed you on the death knight's curse. You also know that Archbishop Alonsus Faol has been seeking a way to cure this disease entirely… so we've been keeping a close eye on your condition. Since Master Marin mentioned you went looking to be cured, may I ask — what was the result?"

"By the Church's method of diagnosing Death Shadow, I believe I am cured, Brother Martin," Jace said honestly. "It's been a long time since I tried to purify myself with a Restorative Potion, and I haven't sought a priest's help either. By now, there is no trace of corpse-rot on me, nor any other discomfort."

"Have you tried smelling the scent of the meadow chrysanthemum tea again?" the priest asked.

Mentioning that tea, Jace felt he ought to tell the Church about the priest in Booty Bay as well. After all, that priest was one of theirs — it only made sense to inform them. Even if King Varian couldn't offer help, surely someone in the Church would be willing to step forward.

But the words about smelling the tea in Booty Bay had barely reached his lips before he swallowed them back.

Best not to mention that he'd been there…

"I have," Jace said. "A friend and I already tried it, Master."

"Then the specific method?" Marin asked, unable to contain himself. "How did your friend cure you? Never mind the Church — I'm very interested too."

"It wasn't really his method," Jace said. "He just bought a product from the goblins in Booty Bay… a magical potion that can turn a person into an undead."

The priest nodded. "I think I've heard rumors of that. There were stories that the goblins brought a drug over from Kalimdor, and that someone bought it at great price to resist the curse of death. But there have always been similar whispers about troll witchcraft and lost Highborne magic…"

"But Mr. Sesso has been cured," Marin said. "Shouldn't the Church procure some of that drug and attempt to treat the soldiers across the Alliance who still suffer from this affliction?"

"I'm afraid my master, and the archbishops of the cathedral, would place very little trust in such a drug…" the priest said.

Marin's fingers drummed on the table. "Lives hang in the balance. Especially when someone killed by this curse can turn into an even more terrible wraith and harm everyone around them. I see no reason to worry about side effects — even ones brought on by a strange drug like this."

"What loss is there, really, if a feigned death becomes a real one?"

"The Master makes a fair point," Brother Martin said with a nod. "I will report this faithfully to Brother Fading. Then I won't take up any more of the two mages' time. May the Light bless those still troubled by the curse."

"The Light bless you, brother," the old mage said with a nod.

"The Light bless you," Jace said.

The young priest rose and left the study. Jace suddenly remembered he hadn't mentioned the Booty Bay brother, and immediately ran out to the doorway.

"Brother Martin! One more thing — about Booty Bay."

The priest turned. "Booty Bay? Is it related to that potion as well?"

"No," Jace said. "Does the Church know that there's another priest trapped in Booty Bay, helping a group of the kingdom's drifters hide out there?"

"I'm not aware of that, no," Brother Martin said.

"I don't know exactly how many people are there — maybe dozens, maybe hundreds, maybe more. There are children too," Jace said. "I've tried to report it to the King, but I haven't received any response."

"I will remember this, Mr. Jace Sesso," Brother Martin said. "You are a good man, sir. I think the reason you were able to cure a fatal Death Shadow curse is that the Light saw the heart you show in caring for the weak."

"Thanks be to the Light," Jace said.

At the very least, Brother Martin seemed like a decent man. He ought to be able to pass this on to the Church, or at least remind them.

If Alonsus Faol had the coin to rebuild such a grand and towering Cathedral of Light, chartering a ships to bring their own people back shouldn't be difficult.

Back in the room, Marin raised an eyebrow. "Did you manage to get the formula for that potion?"

"I'm working on it with my friend, Master," Jace said, settling into the small stool that was his own spot. "No results yet, though."

"Perhaps I could help," Marin said.

"He's the one who bought the potion. I'll ask him," Jace said.

Marin nodded. "Shouldn't we move on to learning the next spell? Have you been practicing Immolate diligently these past two months?"

"I have, Master," Jace said. "I used Immolate to take down a dragonkin fire mage!"

That level — if not outstanding or masterful, then at least a solid, thorough command of the craft.

"Well said," Marin said, leaning back in his chair. "Then, after all this study, is there a spell you'd like to learn?"
