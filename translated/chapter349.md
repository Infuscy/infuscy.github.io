## Chapter 349: The Spell Most Worth Countering

More than a new spell, Jace was actually more concerned about a lingering problem right now — Mage Armor.

"Is there any way to tone down the arcane glow that Mage Armor gives off, Master Malin?"

Malin glanced at the books on the cabinet behind him. "If you can accept the trade-off, there is… another modified version of Mage Armor. It weakens the armor's resistance to lightning, nature, and fire elemental damage, but in exchange it strengthens resistance to shadow and chaotic magic. Most importantly, once this modified Mage Armor is cast, it becomes extremely difficult to see — in other words, as you put it, it no longer has that conspicuous shimmering glow."

"So it would suit a traveling mage like me better than the ones supporting soldiers on the battlefield."

Jace nodded. "Is it difficult to learn?"

"Since it's built on top of Mage Armor, it shouldn't be hard for you, Mr. Sesso." Malin pulled a gray-covered book from the cabinet. On its cover was written *Gondola Tenebrous and the Struggle Against Darkness*.

"Tenebrous's Warding Against Evil — that is, the modified version of Mage Armor. It doesn't completely lose resistance to fire and nature elements; it's merely scaled back appropriately to strengthen resistance to dark magic. The visual dimming is actually a side effect, but the difference is striking." Malin set the book in front of Jace. "Don't write in it, and remember to return it when you're finished, Mr. Sesso. And… when you're facing a fire mage or a particularly dangerous gnoll elementalist, you'd still better pull out the Mage Armor handed down by the elves to protect yourself!"

"Understood, Master," Jace said, taking the book. "As for a new spell… I want to learn Counterspell!"

"How far did you get in your Counterspell lessons?" Malin asked.

"Volume Two, I think…" Jace scratched his head. "All right, I seem to have skipped a week in between, Master. I'd say I've only just gotten my foot in the door when it comes to understanding Counterspell."

Malin sighed. "Truth be told, it's not a good look for an apprentice mage to still be unable to Counterspell. The silver lining is that you're rich in combat experience, Mr. Sesso. With any luck, you'll combine it with practical combat and pick it up faster than the ordinary mage students — faster even than the apprentice mages."

"Mr. Sesso, come with me."

Malin rose and walked out of the study. Jace followed him into the apothecary, where Malin unlocked a door that had always been locked. Beyond it turned out to be a small indoor garden.

Jace had never been here. He'd always assumed that tightly shut door led to a storage room or the like.

Malin walked to the center and said, "If you studied Volume One, you'll know that Counterspell is most effective when cast while the opponent is channeling a spell — it deals a heavy blow to the connection between caster and the magic they've summoned. If you cast it while they're in a normal state, it might cause a certain amount of minor trouble, but they'll recover quickly."

"I'll teach you the simplest Counterspell. Remember this incantation — Modestawening!"

Hearing the incantation, Jace felt it sounded quite different from the Counterspell the naga had used against him. Perhaps that hadn't been a Counterspell at all, but some kind of silence ability unique to the naga.

The means of interrupting spells were varied and many — even within the arcane domain alone, there were countless kinds.

"Modestawening," Jace repeated with a nod. "And the hand gesture?"

"Let me demonstrate." Malin raised his right hand. "Sense the magical disturbance caused by the opponent's casting, capture their resonance with the ley lines, and use the incantation to sever their connection to arcane energy… It is an extremely aggressive magic, demanding intense mental focus and draining a great deal of energy. So if your timing is off, if you cast too slowly, or if the opponent notices and pulls back in time, you will quickly be put on the defensive, Mr. Sesso."

With that, the old mage spoke the incantation and pointed a finger this way. Jace clearly felt the ambient magic suddenly scrunch tight, and under the influence of that arcane shock, his mind felt as though something had bound it, and the sounds around him turned muffled.

But that oppressive feeling vanished in a flash, and Jace quickly returned to normal.

"How do you feel?"

When Malin asked the question, Jace felt as though the voice was coming from underwater. He shook his head before he could say, "Fine."

"Good," the old mage said. "Now your turn, Mr. Sesso. I'll cast — you try to interrupt me."

Jace nodded. The old mage's lips immediately began to move, his hands gathering a somewhat indistinct arcane energy.

Malin's casting was so obvious, of course Jace sensed the magical disturbance — the grass at his feet even trembled from the energy he'd gathered.

He captured the arcane power coalescing around the old mage, aimed his hand at the focal point where Malin was gathering his power, and spoke the incantation…

The moment his spell's point of impact touched the power Malin was gathering, it was like reaching out and striking a solid stone wall. Had he been effortlessly resisted?

Just as Jace prepared to Counterspell again, Malin suddenly thrust both his hands forward…

Jace was overcome by an indescribable, surreal feeling he'd never experienced before — it was as though the world had suddenly grown larger, but… not that much larger. Or rather, taller!

For no reason at all, he suddenly lost his balance. He couldn't stay on his feet and hit the ground. When he tried to push himself up, his hands and feet seemed unable to coordinate.

Wait — he… No, this wasn't a matter of coordination. It was as if his hands and feet had vanished, robbed of the sensation they ought to have.

Jace tried to stand, instinctively kicked back with his hind leg, and toppled over again. He attempted to brace his body with his hands, but when he laboriously lifted them before his eyes, he saw two small, white, furry little hooves!

What the hell?! Polymorph?! I've been turned into a sheep?!

He screamed a long stream of profanity, but all that came out was a bleat!

"Polymorph is the spell most worth countering, Mr. Sesso." Malin crouched down and grabbed Jace's hind leg as he tried to flee. "Fail to stop an opponent's spell, and you'll immediately be thrown into exactly this kind of passive predicament."

With that, he gave Jace's back a firm slap, and the world was instantly swallowed by arcane mist.

In the chaos that followed, Jace felt as though someone had kicked him out of a cramped box. He ended up face-down on the ground.

It took him a good while to recover from the walking pattern of a sheep. He stood up, steadying himself as he eyed his own legs, and finally found his balance.

The old mage let out a long breath. "That's enough for today, Mr. Sesso. My energy is up to the task, but a lack of exercise lately means my body is starting to fall behind. Your attempt was more or less the right direction, but you'll need more practice."

"And read more — make up the lessons you've missed."

Standing off to the side, Jace's mouth opened and closed without stopping. When he finally felt his tongue was his own again, he said, "I will… Master…"

Through attempting to use Counterspell, he'd gained a deep appreciation for the gulf between himself and the great mage of the Wizard's Sanctum standing before him. If the opponent had been anyone but Malin, Jace would have tried using Fear to interrupt the casting.

He wondered whether shadow magic had a similar interrupt ability. After all, Fear wasn't a forced spell-interrupt — even a berserking gnoll could resist it, let alone undead casters who didn't know the meaning of fear.

Come to think of it, hadn't Teron Gorefiend used something like Shadowfury — the area-of-effect stun from the game — back in Dalaran to disrupt Vereesa's offensive rhythm?

Perhaps he should start from that direction. Arcane was, for him… still… difficult.

He wondered whether, boosted by Azuresong, he could Counterspell his master's Polymorph.

But if he was already wielding Azuresong, then Shadowfury or other shadow-interrupt magic should be even stronger…
