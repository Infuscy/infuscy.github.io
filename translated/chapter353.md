## Chapter 353: Souvenir

After handing the Living Root over to Ursula, Jace spent the afternoon learning some alchemy-brewing techniques from her. When night fell, he headed home with Molophil.

Once he was home, there was no escaping it — he could only let Molophil have her way with him. He wasn't going to be casting any magic tomorrow anyway.

Thinking back to how he'd burned the milk mug in front of Amy that morning, he decided he might as well not have mana at all — it'd be more useful in the hands of a succubus. So he gave up entirely and let her have her way with him. Just so long as she didn't knock him unconscious.

The next morning he woke up and glanced out the window from his perch on the sill. It was already nearly noon.

Molophil, sitting on the bed, said, "Master, I feel like your mana is much stronger than before. It's actually getting a lot harder to drain you dry…"

"Yeah?" Jace turned to look at her. "You sure?"

"Of course," Molophil said. "And it's obvious… Last night was really satisfying."

"I could tell without you using that weird method!" the imp hopped up onto the table. "I already noticed the master's mana getting stronger! He can cast more and more spells!"

"Hmph." Molophil gave him a sidelong glare. "My senses are clearly a lot more precise than yours. I can taste every last drop of the master's progress. Can you?"

"Yeah, you're good at everything, you're amazing at everything — so why don't you translate incantations for the master from now on!"

"Then you might as well head straight back to the Twisting Nether, you worthless piece of shit."

"Spite! You lazy slut who only knows how to seduce the master!!"

"You blob of yellowed snot!"

"ARRRGH!!!"

The two demons had picked up their old argument again, but Jace was used to it by now.

He thought back to that night in the forest with Grede when he'd been practicing his Shadow Shield — he really had felt like his energy wasn't draining as fast as before.

Maybe it was what Marin had said before: that stamina could be tempered through constant spellcasting. His journey with Vereesa to the Burning Steppes must have brought him a lot of growth.

"All right, all right, cut it out, both of you," he said. "Watch the house. I'm going out."

"Yes, master!" the imp scrambled to say. "I'll carry out the master's orders for sure!"

"You'd dare promise nothing else," Molophil said with disdain.

Today Jace planned to visit the general goods shop in the Trade District and see about picking up a few things he'd need for the trip to the Wetlands. The weather hadn't been great these past couple of days, and by the time he reached the Trade District a drizzle had started up again.

But Jace had his cloak ready and pressed on regardless. As he neared Old Town, he spotted a familiar figure doing squats on the steps outside a tailor's shop.

"Hey — Grede? Is that you? What are you doing out here in the rain?"

Grede pushed back his hood and saw Jace. "Well, look who it is. I was about to buy a new leather vest. The last one got chewed up by a croc — not really worth patching. Easier to just buy a new one. What are you up to?"

"Picking up some things. How'd the dragon stuff I gave you sell?" Jace asked.

"There's one dragon scale left. People say they can't even tell it's a dragon scale anymore," Greder said. "Level with me — did you suck that dragon dry with that weird magic of yours? They said this scale has no magic left in it at all. It's like a copper coin."

"Keep your voice down — we're on a public street…" Jace leaned in. "No way. Every scale I brought back was hand-picked."

That said, "hand-picked" was generous. Back then he'd gone a long time without sleep or food, and he'd just recovered from a real Shadowflame burn. His ability to appraise anything had definitely been compromised.

"Ah, well. What about the rest?"

Grede said, "Sold them for seven gold, Jace. Also — I made you a dagger. Look."

The dwarf pulled a shortsword carved from a dragon's fang out from behind his back and handed it to Jace. "Take a look. Isn't it beautiful, Jace? The quality of this fang… words fail. The bladesmith I know did it for free. He said it'd been ages since he'd worked with dragon fang. This fang is something else. I really couldn't bring myself to just sell it. Let's make it a keepsake. You killed that dragon — or at least you and Vereesa killed it together. Think about that dragon we ran into at Southshore. This is another legendary feat of yours… I know you don't care about the reputation of a dragonslayer. If it were me, I'd be bragging about it to the whole world already. But it'd be a shame to sell off something you worked so hard to get!"

"Of course, this was my own call. If you really want to sell it, this dagger's worth more than the fang itself."

Jace took the dagger and nodded. "Thanks, Grede. I won't sell it. Honestly, I'm not as indifferent as you think — I'm just used to avoiding trouble. This blade means a lot to me."

"Speaking of which, it's getting harder and harder to keep up with you, Jace," Grede said with a snort. "I should find a chance to solo a black dragon on the Burning Steppes. A full-grown one."

Then Grede burst out laughing. Jace stared at him expressionlessly. Even Grede couldn't keep a straight face after saying that.

After the laughter died down, Grede let out a wheeze. "Thinking about fighting a black dragon… even the Wetlands don't seem so scary anymore."

Jace tucked the blade behind his back, pulled his cloak tight, and asked, "Isn't the Wetlands close to Grim Batol?"

"Huh?" Grede said. "Well, you're not wrong — Grim Batol is in those mountains east of the Wetlands. But you're seriously underestimating how big that stretch of wetland is. Those mountains might be farther from Menethil Harbor than Menethil Harbor is from Stormwind. The dwarves call it the Greater Wetlands for a reason."

"That far?" Jace asked, surprised.

"Of course. And those mountains… they're another huge, rough stretch," the dwarf said with a sigh. Jace asked, "So getting from Menethil Harbor to Loch Modan must be—"

Grede said, "We don't go straight east the whole way. We head south along Khaz Modan — I mean the real Khaz Modan, the Khaz Mountains, the mountains Ironforge sits in. That road takes about four or five days to reach Dun Algaz. Once you're near Dun Algaz you should start running into Ironforge patrols. Once you're past that point, Loch Modan is in much better shape than the Wetlands no matter what."

"Do a lot of Ironforge soldiers hold Dun Algaz?" Jace asked.

Grede nodded. "Of course. Just last year Dun Algaz was still in the hands of a bunch of orcs making a last stand, Jace. The Bronzebeard dwarves didn't retake it until right before the Winter Veil. It's the key passage between Loch Modan and the Wetlands. When I was there, those dwarf soldiers were still fortifying the works, keeping the orcs who escaped from trying to counterattack. Still, the route between Menethil Harbor and Loch Modan is back in service."

"I asked a courier from Ironforge just yesterday. It should still be safe for now. But we'll want to be careful. The orcs could be hiding somewhere, waiting for Dun Algaz's defenses to weaken."

"When can you leave, Jace?" he asked. "I'm feeling pretty good right now. I should be able to travel."

"Then the day after tomorrow," Jace said. "I need to square things away with the Mage Quarter folk."

Grede said, "Then I'll go buy the ship tickets this afternoon."
