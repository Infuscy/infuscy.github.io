Chapter 354: Direction — the Wetlands

Now that the departure date was set, Jace had to speed up his preparations.

That afternoon he went to Ursula's and picked up the four Nature Protection Potions he'd brewed, plus two Healing Potions and two Mana Potions she'd thrown in for free. On hearing that Jace was also heading into desert terrain, she'd specially prepared a pouch of a secret-formula cooling drink to beat the heat.

When Jace left the Slaughtered Lamb he opened the pouch and took a whiff. A refreshing, minty-but-not-quite-mint fragrance hit his nose. Still, on an overcast day like this in the Mage Quarter, it wasn't going to have much to work with.

That evening he brought Alna out of the stable, planning to ride her around for shopping the next day.

The beautiful horse really turned heads — a full head taller than any horse he'd ridden before. Even standing before the cavalry mounts at Stormwind's main gate, she cut an impressive figure.

Alna seemed to enjoy trotting past those warhorses with her head held high. Jace rode her around the wall toward the gate, and before he could even take the reins and guide her in that direction, she bolted straight for them, making a beeline for the warhorses.

Jace was a little worried Alna would charge straight into the cavalry formation, so he quickly pulled her off to the side. Reluctantly, Alna slowed her pace and passed by the cavalry.

The soldiers didn't say much, but the warhorses all snorted and tossed their necks — probably trying to catch Alna's attention. Out on the street, Jace called a command for Alna to stop, and she obediently settled down to walk calmly at his side. She was noticeably more docile than the first time he'd seen her in the palace gardens with King Wrynn.

Jace figured that ride he'd taken her on through the rain a few days ago had let the two of them get used to each other. Alna must like the rain — she liked strolling through it. So did he, honestly. If only rain didn't make your clothes a wet mess to deal with, he'd like it too.

When he had nothing to do at home and it rained outside, he liked to come out shirtless and let the rain pour over him — as long as it was summer or autumn, of course.

After getting home, Jace stabled the horse downstairs for the night and had Molophil keep watch from the window to make sure nothing happened to Alna.

Jace had given up on the idea of using Molophil to charm her. Shadow magic like that affected people, and it surely affected animals too.

Ever since he'd used it on that Mage Quarter horse that one time, things had felt off.

With Alna, he certainly hoped they were building a real, growing familiarity — not forcing anything with magic.

Shopping on horseback through Stormwind was a lot more efficient, and having the horse carry everything he bought made things a lot easier too.

The main things he needed were dry rations for the ship, some paper and ink, and spare shirts and trousers. Since he was heading into swamps and desert terrain, he figured he'd probably want a few changes of clothes on hand. Thelsamar was a dwarf town — they almost certainly wouldn't carry clothes in his size.

And Menethil Harbor wasn't much of a port. Things might be out of stock. Best to prepare as much as he could here in Stormwind.

On the morning of departure, Jace brought his luggage down to the harbor. The weather had finally cleared. Looking out, not a cloud in the sky — that had to be a good omen.

The harbor was pretty quiet that day. He ran through a mental checklist — was there anything he'd left undone at home? The demons were all squared away, his mentor and the basement folk had been told, the horse was returned to the stable, the door was locked…

Then, in the distance, he saw a dwarf with a braided ponytail waving at him. Beside the dwarf sat a medium-sized sailing ship that was unmistakably Stormwind-made.

"I brought you two Nature Protection Potions, Grede." Jace pulled the potions out of the pouch Ursula had given him and handed them to the dwarf. "Keep them safe."

"Nature Protection Potions — against what?" the dwarf asked.

"Troggs," Jace said.

"I'd think some kind of iron-skin potion, or a shield potion — I can't remember the name — something that hardens your skin would be more useful," Grede said. "Troggs are brawlers just like us. Nature Protection Potions…"

"I'd say you're already plenty good at brawling, Grede," Jace said. "Have some special potions on hand for trogg magic. And don't think these are cheap just because there are only four bottles. Treat them with respect."

Grede rolled his eyes. "I know. These things need Living Root, don't they? Still, it sounds like you already know exactly what's in that excavation site. Don't tell me the Wizard Sanctum library has a complete record of what's inside ruins the Bronzebeard and Dark Iron dwarves couldn't even break into?"

"The library doesn't spell out what's inside. But are troggs a sentient species?" Jace asked.

"About as smart as gnolls, maybe a little smarter," Grede said.

"Well, if even gnolls have shamans, shouldn't you be worried that troggs — who are even closer to mountains and caves — have shamans too?" Jace said. "Do you have a short memory? Did you forget getting whipped with lightning by that orc at Lake Everstill?"

"All right, all right, I get it," Grede said quickly.

Jace looked him in the eyes, thinking to himself that he'd finally managed to bluff his way through that one.

"Better prepared than sorry."

Grede nodded. "I brought a few Lesser Healing Potions, mainly for the dangers along the swamp road."

"I promised my alchemist I'd look for a marsh beast while I was in the Wetlands," Jace said.

"Huh?" Grede said. "You're going after a marsh beast? I strongly advise against it. Did you already promise someone?"

"Not like I have to find one. It's on the way, isn't it?" Jace said. "She needs something from inside a marsh beast for a new potion experiment. Are marsh beasts that dangerous? Have you fought one before?"

"Not personally. I've only heard rumors," Grede said. "But you're actually planning to go into a swamp to hunt a marsh beast? I'd say it's more like it's waiting for you to come into its territory and then hunting you. Even when the Wildhammer clan ruled the Wetlands, my elders didn't dare go deep into the swamps to mess with those mysterious creatures. I think you should play it safe, Jace!"

Hearing Grede say that actually made Jace curious about what marsh beasts in this world really looked like.

In the game, marsh beasts were basically giant wobbling masses of green rot, hobbling along slowly — didn't seem threatening at all.

But of course, that was just the game. Thinking back to that wildkin in the Hinterlands that had been impossibly huge compared to the game, he decided he'd be better off listening to Grede's advice.

Grede hefted his luggage and glanced at the dwarf sailors on the pier. "If it were me, I'd ask around in Thelsamar — see if any merchants sell whatever it is you're talking about, the stuff from inside a marsh beast. If that really can be made into a potion, the Bronzebeards would be interested too."

Jace nodded. "Hope there is some. Hope it's cheap."
