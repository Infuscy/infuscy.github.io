## Chapter 355: Harbor in the Fog

The shipping lane from Stormwind Harbor to Menethil Harbor was, on the whole, far calmer than crossing Baradin Bay or heading south to Booty Bay.

After all, you could see land the whole way. There were no violent storms — at most the occasional light rain depending on the season.

On the morning of the eighth day of smooth sailing, Jace heard the captain bellowing from the deck that Menethil Harbor was just ahead.

Grede rubbed his eyes awake and sighed. "I can never sleep properly on a ship. It feels like I've been awake for a week straight."

"You snore louder than your hammer cracking skulls," Jace said flatly. "I'm the one who hasn't slept in a goddamn week."

"Nonsense," the dwarf snorted. "We had neighbors on both sides. Why haven't they complained to me all this time?"

Jace clapped Grede on the shoulder, punched his stone-hard belly, and tugged his black beard. "Look at you. Look at the look of you. Who'd dare come complain to a monster like you about snoring? Not complaining means a week of discomfort. Complaining might mean feeding the fishes."

"More nonsense. I've always been friendly and gentle toward humans."

As he spoke, Grede pulled on his new leather vest — though, new or not, after eight days at sea it looked as if it had been deliberately distressed. The two of them stepped out onto the deck. The sea was an endless white blur — they couldn't see a thing. Even the land that had been visible the whole trip had completely vanished.

"Is this normal?" Jace asked.

"Normal," Grede said. "It was just like this last time. Means we're about to reach Menethil Harbor."

"Oh, right, Jace — have you ever heard about the ghost ship in the fog? So one day there are people sailing at sea and the mist swallows them up, and then a black… crewless ship just appears out of nowhere…"

"All right, that's enough, zip it," Jace said, taking a deep breath. "I could handle it coming from anyone else, but when you say it, it feels like it's about to happen for real."

Around noon, the two of them spotted a hazy bluish-white light source high up in the distance. Jace could sense the magic woven into it. An arcane lighthouse?

As the ship docked, Jace swept his eyes across the whole port. Menethil Harbor was small — nowhere near Southshore. You could take it in at a single glance.

But unlike Southshore, the landward side of the port was ringed by a thick, towering wall. In the middle stood a fortress still under construction. Packed inside the walls were countless stone houses and wooden buildings in both human and dwarf styles, all crammed together. Soldiers, workers, and sailors were everywhere.

When Jace was a child, this place wasn't called Menethil Harbor yet. It was just a dwarf fishing village whose name he'd never learned.

During the Orc Wars, the Horde's army had invaded and used it as a temporary port for their push north. When the Alliance forces counterattacked, they'd seized it, torn down the orcish facilities, and rebuilt it as a military harbor.

Even years later, Jace could still see lumber and canvas left over from the demolition of crude orcish structures piled at the base of the walls. It reminded him of the glimpse he'd gotten of the Shattered Hand orc hideout in Teron Gorefiend's dream.

Jace focused his mind and swept the area with Sense Demons. Not a single shadow in sight.

In the end, he'd decided not to bring any of his demon minions to Menethil Harbor — not even the imp. It could keep Molophil company.

He was heading into a Titan ruin, after all. The Pantheon had built those temples to protect Azeroth from demons and Void creatures. Who knew what sort of terrifying consequences dragging a demon inside might bring? Completing Grede's trial came first.

Even though a section of the wall was still unfinished, the inns and taverns here were already built rather handsomely. The moment they stepped off the ship, Grede made a beeline for the inn like he owned the place.

In Jace's memory, the inn at Menethil Harbor was quite a famous spot in the game. After all, it would later become a vital transit hub linking the north and south and two continents. But try as he might, he couldn't recall the inn's name. Then he saw the temporary sign hanging by the entrance, written in both Dwarvish and Common, and it immediately tugged loose a memory from long ago.

The Deepwater Inn.

Ah… so that was the name.

Back at level 60 playing Alliance, he'd had his Hearthstone bound to this inn. That way, whether he was taking a griffin south to Searing Gorge to prepare for Blackrock Spire or sailing to the black dragon's lair in Kalimdor, it was all convenient.

Following Grede inside, Jace was greeted by the relaxed beat of drums. He glanced toward the sound and saw a crowd of dwarves packed together with Lordaeron soldiers in white vests. The dwarves were drumming on the tables, as if setting the rhythm for the humans with flutes and the other dwarves with stringed instruments.

Jace followed Grede to a seat by the window and waited for the waiter to make his way over.

Grede exchanged greetings with a few dwarves nearby. Jace asked, "You know them?"

"Can't I greet people without knowing them?" Grede said with a grin. "Most of the folks here are old veterans of the Orc Wars, Jace — myself included. That makes us brothers-in-arms!"

Just then, a golden-bearded dwarf behind Grede raised his mug and shouted, "For the Alliance, mage!"

"For the Alliance," Jace nodded back.

He looked at Grede and said quietly, "Pity I was still a kid during the Orc Wars."

Grede reached over and clapped Jace on the neck. "The hell you were — you killed Teron Gorefiend!"

Jace held up a hand. "Shh…"

Just then, a familiar melody drifted from a corner of the tavern.

A human soldier had picked up a flute and joined a dwarf with a stringed instrument to play a tune. This melody… Jace knew it better than anyone.

Every time he'd waited for a raid group to assemble, his character would always Hearthstone to this inn early, go AFK while waiting for everyone to show up and get their assignments sorted… and during that wait, this was always the melody playing.

The details might differ slightly, the instruments weren't quite the same, and these people's tempo was noticeably slower than the game's background music — probably because they were still getting the hang of it. But Jace was absolutely certain this was the same song.

Grede stared at Jace's dazed expression. "You've heard this tune before?"

Jace nodded. "Do you know what it's called?"

Grede said, "This place used to be called Deepwater Village before the orcs came. This tune doesn't have any special name. You can call it the Song of Deepwater Bay."

"Deepwater Bay," Jace murmured. "So does this inn have any special food?"

"Fish. All kinds of fish," Grede said. "And dwarven stout with a very particular flavor…"

At this he lowered his voice to a whisper. "But none of it is a patch on the glory of Thelsamar. I'm telling you, Jace Sesso — the moment you set foot in Thelsamar, you'll realize the first twenty years of your life were a complete waste."

"I haven't even lived twenty years yet," Jace said. "But the way you talk about it, I'm really looking forward to getting to Thelsamar soon."
