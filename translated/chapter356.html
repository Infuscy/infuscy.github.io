Chapter 356: Flames in the Deep

That afternoon, Jace and Grede spent the whole afternoon fishing off the dock. Grede had quite the haul — a handful of blackmouth fish of various sizes and some smaller catches, but the real showstopper was a fire-scale eel weighing a good ten-plus pounds, probably fifteen or so.

Granted, compared to the fat ocean fish like groupers or redgills, that wasn't much to write home about. But fire-scale eels were rare, and this one was long and slender — at that weight, its size was genuinely startling.

And it was bright red all over. Clutched in Grede's arms, it glistened like a flickering ball of flame. When the thing was hauled up out of the water, every dockhand in the vicinity stole a glance over in their direction.

Jace hadn't caught a single thing all afternoon. Watching the dark shapes drifting through the water from time to time, then looking at the fish soaking in Grede's basket, he was honestly tempted to just yank one up with Gorefiend's Grasp.

But after a glance at the dockworkers not far off, a powerful will to live won out over a powerful competitive streak.

If this had been the middle of nowhere with not a soul around, well — he wouldn't have held back.

When they got back to the Deepwater Inn that evening, it was a livelier scene than at noon. Jace even spotted two elves sitting in a corner on the other side, chatting in low voices. Laughter and shouting rose and fell in waves.

Grede dragged his eel straight through the middle of the tavern, drawing a chorus of gasps. He strode up to the innkeeper with his chest puffed out and bellowed, "Here, boss — roast this eel of mine, slice it up, and let everyone have a taste! And bring three mugs of Blackrock beer!"

Amid the cheers, Grede made his way back to their table. Jace raised an eyebrow. "Blackrock beer — is that the Dark Iron dwarves' brew?"

"Yeah, so what?" Grede said. "What, the Dark Iron dwarves can kill people, but their Dark Iron beer can't? Just because something good comes from the enemy doesn't make it any less good! Besides, not every Dark Iron clansman's got a screw loose — there are Dark Iron dwarves living in Ironforge, you know!"

At the mention of the Dark Iron dwarves in Ironforge, Jace thought of that group of dwarven warlocks Galders had brought up.

Just then, a dwarven waitress brought over two mugs of beer and said something in Dwarvish. Grede glanced at her and asked a few questions in Dwarvish. The waitress nodded with a smile and said something back.

Jace couldn't understand a single word, but he felt like he was practically reading their minds.

"You're on your own tonight, little brother Jace." Grede took a swig of his beer. "Clearly my reputation as a master angler has won that lady right over."

Jace glanced at the dwarf woman — whose age he couldn't begin to guess — then looked back and nodded helplessly. "All right. Have fun, I guess."

"What, jealous, lad?" Grede asked.

Jace said flatly, "I'm worried you'll drink too much. We're setting out tomorrow morning, and we need to make up time — this trip's already long enough as it is."

"Fair enough, I'll stop after these two." Grede nodded. "What about you — you not looking for a little fun?"

Jace thought to himself that he'd barely escaped from Molophil's clutches and had finally gotten a few days of peace — why the hell would he go looking for more trouble.

"We'll see about that, Grede." He glanced around the tavern. "Some mages from the Wizard Sanctum are in Menethil Harbor — they're here investigating the Dragonmaw orcs. I was going to try and find them tonight, ask about the Dragonmaw situation. Might as well ask if there's any danger on the road to Dun Algaz while I'm at it."

Grede looked surprised. "There are mages from your Sanctum here? Last time I came through, I didn't see a single one."

Jace nodded and scanned the tavern for a moment. Then he caught sight of someone in a corner — a tall, slender figure draped in a dark crimson cloak.

That cloak looked expensive as hell, its edges traced with arcane sigils, the fabric itself exquisite. One look and you could tell this was a mage.

Was that Morgas? Or some other mage from the Wizard Sanctum? Or perhaps a master sent by Dalaran to investigate the Wetlands orcs?

"You eat, Grede. I'm going to ask that man."

Grede took a sip of his beer, glanced in the direction Jace was pointing, and nodded.

Jace walked up to the bar and came up behind the mage. "Forgive the intrusion…"

The cloaked figure turned at the sound and looked at Jace. It was an elf.

Beneath the cloak he wore a fitted dark crimson robe, and it was clear the man was built like a brick wall. Add in the rather long red-hilted sword hanging at his hip, and Jace wasn't so sure this fellow was a mage after all.

Then again, this sort of getup wasn't exactly rare among elven nobility.

"Are you a mage?"

"I am, human. I come from Dalaran."

The elf's voice was deep — which meant he was probably quite old. It was hard to judge an elf's age from his face, but there were still tells in the voice. And his Common didn't carry even a trace of a Thalassian accent.

"I'm from Stormwind City. A mage of the Wizard Sanctum."

"I can see that." The elf's gaze drifted down, and he raised his long eyebrows. "Ah — you're Jace Sesso."

Jace blinked. "Uh, Master… have we met…?"

The mage said quietly, "That demon sword might escape the notice of others, but it can't fool my eyes. I didn't expect to run into you here — I've heard quite a bit about you!"

Seeing Jace's expression tighten, the elf smiled. "Don't worry, Mr. Sesso. As far as I know, the sword's original owner has already agreed to Lady Vereesa Windrunner's decision to give it to you. So you won't have any trouble — not from me, nor from anyone else."

Jace needed a moment to process this avalanche of unexpected information. "Thank you for telling me, Master. That does put my mind at ease."

"Then, is there some difficulty you're facing?"

Jace said, "Actually, I'm planning to travel to Loch Modan, so I wanted to ask about the orc situation on the road."

The elf nodded. "Based on your Wizard Sanctum's investigation of the orcs, if you're planning to pass through Dun Algaz, now is a good time. Remnants of the Horde may be hiding in the nearby mountains or deep in the Wetlands, but I don't think you need to worry too much. Ironforge's dwarf cavalry are patrolling the roads — the orcs don't want to expose themselves just yet."

"That's all we need to know, Master," Jace said.

"Then I wish you and your dwarf companion a smooth journey, Mr. Sesso." The elf raised his mug of mead.

"And you as well, Master."

Jace nodded and returned to Grede's table.

"Well?" Grede asked. "Any orcs? Not that I ran into any last time I came through."

"He said no — told us to make haste if we're going within the next couple of days."

"You trust that guy? Was he a mage from the Wizard Sanctum? Looked more like some noble knight, the build on him."

"He said he's from Dalaran. Probably Kirin Tor."

"Probably? You didn't ask who he was?"

"No. I got the impression he didn't want to tell me."

That said, Jace already had a pretty good guess in the back of his mind.

A Kirin Tor elf mage who knew the original owner of the Song of the Blue Sky — a certain blue dragon — who'd spotted the sword on Jace at a glance, and who was concerned about the Wetlands orcs.

He looked up toward the mage. The man was leaning against the bar, watching their direction, slowly raising his mug for a sip. Beneath the hood, his face was completely obscured.

If Jace's guess was right, that was Krasus — or rather, Korialstrasz, a red dragon disguised as an elf mage.

He was one of the members of the Kirin Tor's Council of Six, and the consort of Alexstrasza, the Dragonqueen. He'd come here to investigate the connection between the Dragonmaw orcs and the missing Dragonqueen.

And he was clearly already very close to the truth — Grim Batol.

If it really was Krasus himself, then of course he'd seen through the sword Jace was carrying. That was only to be expected.

Forget the sword — the man could probably see right through the fact that Jace was actually a warlock who dabbled in shadow magic whenever he pleased. He just didn't care whether Jace used arcane or shadow.

Just then, the waitress brought over a portion of fish. Grede held up his fork. "Come on, try this, Jace! I caught it myself!"

Jace nodded absently with his spoon in hand. After all, that was a real, living dragon over there.
