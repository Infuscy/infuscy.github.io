Chapter 358: Wetland Natives

At dawn, the mist rolled back in. Jace opened his eyes uncomfortably.

Sleeping here was miserable. It wasn't as hot as during the day, but the bugs — flying, crawling, all of them — made it impossible to get any real rest.

The strangest part was how few mosquitoes bothered Grede. They all swarmed around Jace instead. Was his blood especially sweet, or was a dwarf's thick hide too tough to bite?

Maybe that was exactly why only dwarves were willing to settle in a place like this.

On top of that, there were always strange sounds in the middle of the night — gurgling from the water in the distance, a sudden shriek from some unidentifiable creature far, far away, half-human and half-ghostly, jolting you awake in the dead of night.

Or toads that appeared from nowhere, croaking like they were crying.

Honestly, getting startled like this again and again was worse than if Teron Gorefiend's ghost had just dropped the act and charged straight at them to finish them off. At least that would be quick. This constant jumpiness — over time, it was unbearable.

When daylight came, they packed up and set out. Following behind the dwarf, Jace kept scratching his neck and arms. As they crossed a makeshift wooden bridge, Grede lifted his head and sniffed.

"What is it?" Jace called ahead.

"Something smells off." Grede said. "Move — get across. A little further down we'll hit the stone road."

Jace nodded and quickened his pace toward the far side of the bridge. Off to the side came a gurgling from the water.

He glanced over instinctively. The surface, which had been reasonably clear, suddenly burst into a spray of mud — and from the mud shot a dark-green monstrosity of a hand, straight at his face!

"What the—!"

Jace cursed under his breath and drew the Song of the Blue Sky, slicing the strange hand in two in a single stroke. The hand, wreathed in arcane fire, tumbled back into the water with a howl. He immediately broke into a run toward the underpass of the bridge — only to feel something wet and slimy grab him from behind, reaching over from the other side of the bridge. In the blink of an eye, a mass of weeds and mud enveloped his chest and neck.

He tried to turn and strike again, but his arms were already being bound fast by rapidly spreading vines!

"Jace!" Grede seized his war hammer. Lightning crackling along the head lit up the dim road in a blinding flash. He hurled the hammer straight at the dark-green arm holding Jace. The bolt blew the arm apart in a shower of dead branches and leaves — but the unbroken filaments and vines quickly filled the gap and regrew.

Jace grabbed the vines and pulled back hard, trying to tear the shattered arm free, or at least match the thing's strength — but the moment he put his weight into it, the plank beneath his feet gave way, and he was dragged straight into the water…

He held his breath on instinct. Splash. Gurgle…

The cold pool swallowed his vision. His clothes, his cloak, his pack — everything became dead weight in an instant. Every movement of his hands and legs felt tangled and sluggish, as if wrapped in layers of algae and water weeds.

The Song of the Blue Sky in his hand erupted in a blaze of arcane light, illuminating the depths beneath the dark water. Jace saw it clearly: a green monster covered in strange water weeds, staring at him with two faint, dark-red eyes, suspended in the murky, debris-filled water.

Jace tried to cast Fear, but the moment he opened his mouth, water rushed in.

His feet were bound tight by vines growing from the monster's body; his arms were wrapped just as firmly, barely able to move.

Then Jace blinked. Through the faint, distorted sunlight fractured by the water's surface, he saw a dark, fat shape plunge downward, swinging a war hammer and smashing it into the monster's head!

The creature's whole body shuddered. Its eyes burned redder. It let out a low, gurgling sound and tried to raise its other hand to claw at the dwarf above — but the dwarf swung again and shattered its fist.

Yet even as the monster's shattered wrist burst forth with fresh greenery and algae, reforming into a new hand before their eyes!

Jace seized the moment while the monster was regenerating and its strength faltered. He wrenched his arm free, pointed at the green monster's eye level, and a blood-red thread shot from his hand, cutting through the illuminated, dark-brown water and plunging into the creature's body near the eye. He wrenched back, tearing out a massive chunk of the monster's tissue and shredding it into the water.

The pull seemed to rip right through the monster's structural support. Its enormous body began to disintegrate. The vines around Jace's feet, the various monstrous hands assailing Grede — all of them trembled and loosened.

Jace felt like he was about to drown. He summoned every ounce of strength and swam upward until he broke the surface, throwing his head back and gasping for air.

He knew that even holding his breath calmly, he couldn't last more than a few minutes. What with the fighting and the panic, plus the fact that he hadn't taken a proper breath before going under, he guessed it had been maybe a few dozen seconds at most.

But it felt like he'd spent half a lifetime down there…

A moment later, Grede burst up beside him, struggling and flopping his way onto the bank. He collapsed flat on the ground, panting hard.

"You… hurt, Jace?"

"I don't think so." Jace spat out a mouthful of dark-green water. "But another few seconds down there and I'd be done for. How's the food?"

"The food bag in my hand — I dropped it on the ground, it's fine." Grede walked over to the bridge, picked up the pack he'd left there, caught his breath, and felt around his back. "Damn it, I've still got one."

He opened the pack on his back and took a look, laying out dried mushrooms and other rations. "The ones that were wrapped tight are fine, but these biscuits soaked through."

"Then forget them. Who knows what kind of poison's in this swamp water." Jace said, opening his own pack. His clothes were soaked through, but the elven pack was impressively waterproof — the papers inside were only slightly damp, and the scroll Vereesa had given him, inscribed with magical runes, was completely unaffected.

The bottles and vials in Ursula's potion pouch were totally undamaged. Reliable gear.

As Jace sorted out the things that were still reasonably dry, he said, "Definitely shouldn't have messed with that swamp beast. It's no joke."

"If you ask me, it shouldn't have messed with you!" Grede laughed. "That grabbing spell of yours looked like you ripped its spine clean out — I watched the whole thing fall apart with my own eyes."

Looking back down at the pool beneath the bridge, the once reasonably clear black water was now covered in a mass of dark-green algae and mud-like strange matter, floating on the surface. The air was thick with the sickly smell of rotting vegetation.

Grede asked, "You still planning to find that organ? The one you mentioned before?"

"What's there to find now." Jace stared at the chaos in the pool and scratched his head. "You expect me to dive in and fish around? I can't tell this mess apart from… anything…"

"Might take an anatomy expert to make sense of this thing's body structure." Grede said.

"Or an herbalism expert." Jace added, wringing out his shirt.
