Chapter 360: Thelsamar

The western shore of Loch Lake was still a peaceful, idyllic sort of place — not nearly as many monsters lurking around waiting to rush out and finish you off, like in the swamp.

Following the main road leading down out of Dun Algaz, the two of them pushed through the night, and by early morning, when the sky was just beginning to brighten, they could see stone houses linked one after another in the distance.

Thelsamar. This dwarven town's reputation in the Alliance was probably second only to Ironforge, and higher than Aerie Peak, Shadowforge City, Karanos, Dun Baldar, and all the rest.

Not just because of the Battle of Thelsamar that had happened here a few years ago — but even more because of the famous Thelsamar Blood Sausage, made from the intestines of a black bear!

"We're finally here." Grede said. "I really wish this trip was just a simple vacation, instead of heading south to the desert to hunt for god ruins. Then we could eat and drink to our heart's content for a few days and I could properly walk you through all the Bronzebeard dwarven delicacies!"

"As the culinary heart of Khaz Modan, it's been this way ever since the Dark Iron and Wildhammer clans still lived here! Someone once said that King Anvilmar's day went like this: wake up in Ironforge in the morning, eat his fill in Thelsamar by noon, drink his fill in Karanos by evening, and get carried back to Ironforge in the middle of the night! Today you get to enjoy at least a quarter of the kingly life — not bad, eh?"

"You must've done the whole tour last time you came through, right?" Jace asked.

Grede waved a hand. "Hah — I woke up in Ironforge in the morning, and by the time I reached Thelsamar it was already evening! Forget making it to Karanos…"

Following the dwarf up onto a high slope, Jace leaned against a nearby pine tree and fought back his exhaustion as he gazed into the distance.

Below the slope, the blue surface of Loch Lake sparkled, bright and dazzling in the morning sun, lifting the spirits.

A cool lake breeze carried the sound of dwarves greeting one another from below.

Compared to the perpetually snowbound peaks of Dun Morogh, the barren southern desert, and the muddy, humid Wetlands, Loch Modan was truly a fine place to settle down for good.

"Halt! You there!"

A dwarf in a blue Mountaineer's cloak shouted in their direction.

Jace was jolted by his gruff bark, thinking he'd done something wrong.

But the dwarf looked at Jace and asked, "Coming from the north — run into any kobolds?"

"No, sir." Jace said.

"Lucky you. Get into Thelsamar, human!" the dwarf said with a glare. "Move! Eat something good, get a good night's sleep!"

Grede nodded at the Bronzebeard dwarf and said a long string of things in Dwarvish. The dwarf burst out laughing, waving his hand. "Go on in, human — follow this highland dwarf! And try the Canisabella!"

"Canisabella?" Jace glanced at Grede.

Grede grinned. "A food, Jace. The Thelsamar folk are very fond of it…"

"Blood sausage?" Jace asked.

The moment he said "blood sausage," the Bronzebeard dwarf immediately wagged his index finger. "Thelsamar is more than just blood sausage, tall friend. Thelsamar's got plenty of good things…"

Walking through the streets of Thelsamar, Jace felt like every impression he'd ever had of the place had been completely wiped clean.

It was a remarkably lively dwarven town. The whole thing was built into a sunken hollow — most of the buildings were half above ground and half below. As Jace passed a stone house that looked small and modest from the surface, he glanced through the window and realized it was actually a two-level underground great hall, packed with dwarves having breakfast.

The Bronzebeards on the streets didn't carry the same wariness toward humans that the Wildhammer dwarves did, and most of them knew some Common.

Grede led Jace into an unassuming stone cave by the side of the road. After passing through a narrow stone tunnel, the view suddenly opened up — it was a tavern!

"Another human! Welcome! And one from Aerie Peak…" the woman behind the bar called out.

"Let me tell you, human — I smashed in four orc skulls on the Dun Algaz mountain road." A dwarf leaning against a stone pillar supporting the roof held up three fingers. "Four whole ones! You definitely haven't killed as many orcs as me. You're way too young. Definitely not."

"Definitely not, definitely not…"

Jace said quickly, thinking — how is this guy already this drunk in the morning?

"Don't mind that drunk." Grede pulled Jace forward and said something to the woman behind the bar in Dwarvish.

The two of them took an empty stone table beside the bar. Jace squinted. "God, Grede, I'm so tired I could fall asleep right here. Aren't you tired?"

"It's fine." Grede said. "I can't wait — I've got to let you try this Canisabella first."

Jace propped his chin on his hand. "It's not going to be some weird food that messes with my sleep, is it? All I want right now is a good night's sleep."

"It definitely won't affect your sleep." Grede said. "I think it'll even help you sleep!"

Before long, the woman brought out a platter of bulging white organs. Jace looked at the rough surface, then gave it a sniff — a strong smell of mutton kidney.

The woman set down two mugs of ale. Grede took a sip. Both dwarves watched Jace with expectant expressions as he frowned and sniffed at the food.

The boss took a knife and cut the plump sheep stomach pouch open down the middle. A mass of whitish-yellowish stuff gushed out, accompanied by a puff of steam and a rich aroma of garlic. The visual was actually pretty appetizing.

"Try it, Jace!" Grede pointed at the soft, paste-like stuff on the platter.

Jace picked up a wooden spoon, scooped a bit into his mouth. Hmm — a faint gamey taste, garlic… a hint of chili and milky richness.

"You can guess what this is." The woman leaned on the table and asked.

"Sheep brain with garlic and chili, pan-fried in butter… and I think some beer too." Jace licked his teeth. "And eggs, of course."

Grede looked at the woman behind the bar after hearing Jace's answer. She nodded vigorously. "That's more than close enough, human! Surprised there's still a human here willing to eat sheep brain."

"You must be starving, huh?" she teased.

"Of course not." Jace said. "I actually really like sheep brain…"

Grede nodded. "Well, you never mentioned that, Jace."

"Lordaeron and Stormwind both raise sheep too…" Jace reminded him.

"Sheep and goat taste very different." The woman said that, but she looked pleased.

She turned and called toward the kitchen, "Compliments of the house — a plate of authentic Thelsamar-style blood sausage for this young sir! I'm sure the recipe's lost most of its soul by the time it reaches Stormwind!"

A golden-bearded helper in the back kitchen answered and soon brought out a plate of what looked like blood-smeared sausages. A heavy smell of blood hit the air before the plate even reached the table.
