Chapter 361: The Herbalist's Advice

Jace woke up in the afternoon, the sky already turning dim.

He thought back to that so-called "authentic Loch Modan blood sausage" from the morning — at least it hadn't been black bear intestine sashimi stuffed with filling. The blood and meat paste inside were still half-raw, but they'd loaded it with seasoning. For someone who'd eaten raw hammerhead shark meat, the taste wasn't too outlandish — it was mainly the visual that was a shock.

As for the goat brain… the smell of goat had been overpowering when he ate it, but he'd loved brains in his past life and hadn't had goat brain in ages. Getting to taste an otherworldly goat brain with a rather unique flavor was actually quite satisfying.

Grede was still sprawled in bed snoring up a storm. Jace figured he'd take advantage of the remaining daylight and head into town to check the herbalist's shop or alchemy lab — see if anyone had marsh beast glands.

He left the inn and stepped onto the street. Even more dwarves were crowding the afternoon thoroughfare; Loch Modan's main street was packed tight. Plenty of dwarf hunters were trudging back from the lake, panting as they hauled in all manner of fish, wild boar, and other quarry.

One dwarf passing by Jace said, "You oughta try the beer-braised boar steak, human. Bloodfang boar meat's no match for Loch Modan's wild mountain pig — more fat. The boars here live good, lazy lives. Got food everywhere they look, so they're nice and marbled. And you've gotta admit, the beer here's got Stormwind's beat, right? And when it comes to roasting meat, Bronzebeard folk are way more professional than humans, no question."

The dressing-down came so fast Jace didn't even have a chance to fire back.

Still, he said honestly, "I'll give it a try, sir. Is there an herbalist in town?"

"There is." The dwarf pointed down the slope behind him. "Walk down there, follow the narrow path heading south, and you'll see a cave. The town's best herbalist lives there."

"Thank you, sir."

Following the dwarf's directions, Jace arrived at the cave. Inside, an elderly dwarf stood on a stool, dusting a bookshelf.

"Excuse me," Jace coughed. "Sir, I'm looking for a water-storage gland from a marsh beast. Would you happen to have one?"

"What do you need that for?" the dwarf asked, turning around.

"Making a special potion. I'm asking on behalf of an alchemist," Jace said.

"It's not that I don't want to help, human," the old dwarf said. "The marsh beasts in the Wetlands don't have anything like that — they can just draw water straight from the wetlands to sustain themselves. The creature you're describing, the one with the storage gland… its close relative is called a Shambler, or a Bramble Beast. They live in arid places and steal the moisture that other plants and beasts depend on, keeping just enough for themselves to digest and grow. Those monsters turn wherever they go into a dry wasteland!"

"Are there any of those creatures in the badlands to the south?" Jace asked.

"Not anymore," the dwarf said. "The Bramble Beasts in the badlands went extinct long ago. After all, they can't make their own water. Once they've completely turned a place into a wasteland, they're done for too. If you want one of those creatures now, you'd probably have to go to another continent."

Jace thought to himself that of course he knew you'd have to go to the deserts of another continent to find them — after all, this continent only had this one stretch of badlands.

But then the dwarf added, "I do have a Wetlands marsh beast spore here… you could try cultivating a small marsh beast yourself, human. Put it in an arid environment and don't give it a stable water source. It'll gradually grow into the shape you want."

"That actually works?!" Jace asked in surprise.

The dwarf said, "I once cultivated a poisonous marsh beast that looked completely different from the ones in the Wetlands. I reckon a dry-land Bramble Beast could be grown the same way! Of course, I can't say for certain. Aren't you and the alchemist who sent you here willing to give it a try?"

"How much?" Jace said.

"Twelve silver," the dwarf said. "Hold on, I'll get it for you."

A moment later, the old dwarf walked out carrying a glass jar the size of a milk powder tin, and patted it. "This is it!"

Jace looked at the pale green, mushroom-like globs inside and thought — this little thing was eventually going to grow into a massive behemoth that would come attacking him?

"How long until it grows?" Jace asked.

"If the environment's right, it might get as big as you in a few weeks to a few months," the dwarf said. "If you cultivate it correctly, the gland should be ready around then too."

Next Jace needed to head to Uldaman, and carrying this thing along would definitely be inconvenient, so he said, "Sir, I'll hold off on buying it for now — I'm heading south and I'm afraid I might smash the jar along the way. Could I pay half now as a deposit and pick it up when I get back?"

"That works too." The owner nodded, glanced at Jace's ring, and said, "Then leave your name, mage. We'll use the name on this ring as your claim token."

Jace pulled out a piece of paper, wrote down his name, set down the silver, and asked, "Sir, do you know about that ancient ruin the Dark Iron dwarves discovered in the badlands to the south?"

"Discovered by the Dark Iron clan?" the old dwarf said with resentment. "That was clearly an ancient ruin discovered by Ironforge's own explorers! Now that the Dark Iron dwarves have seized it, suddenly it's their discovery? Do you have any idea how many brilliant geologists and adventurers from Ironforge lost their lives excavating that mysterious temple?!"

"I'm sorry, sir…" Jace said quickly. "I only heard there was a Dark Iron mining operation there. Beyond that, I know absolutely nothing."

"For the sake of the Lordaeron Alliance, I'll let it slide." The old dwarf took the paper and continued, "The Dark Iron dwarves caught on that something was off about that place very early on. In any case, it's nothing like the ruins at the Digsite to the east or the excavation site in the Wetlands. The Dark Iron clan is taking that place very seriously."

Jace asked, "If Ironforge excavated that ruin earlier, did they leave behind blueprints or anything of the sort?"

"Are you with some kind of expedition too? Aren't you a mage?" The dwarf glanced at his ring again. "I remember humans only wear this kind of ring if they're mages."

"I am indeed a mage, sir," Jace said. "I'm working on a research project about the connection between ancient ruins and the arcane…"

"Well, that's quite a grand topic you've chosen. Go ask at the Digsite to the east, mage," the old dwarf said. "I'm just a doctor, but there are survivors there who took part in the excavation of that mysterious ruin. They know far more than I ever could."

Translator Notes

Flags

- Marsh beast cultivation: The Wetlands marsh beast spore can be cultivated into a Bramble Beast-like creature by raising it in an arid environment with no stable water source — producing the water-storage gland Jace needs. Jace left a six-silver deposit under his ring name; the spore is being held at the herbalist's cave in Loch Modan. - Ancient ruin contested: The Dark Iron clan seized a major ancient temple originally discovered by Ironforge's explorers at the cost of many lives. The Bronzebeard herbalist is bitter about it; the Dark Iron dwarves are treating it as a serious operation, not a minor digsite. - Digsite lead: Survivors of Ironforge's ruin excavation are at the Digsite to the east of Loch Modan — Jace and Grede's next stop for intelligence on the Badlands ruin.
