Chapter 363: The Old Expedition Member

These days, the archaeological development of the Digsite was a matter of considerable importance to Ironforge. The road from Loch Modan to the excavation site saw a steady stream of supply caravans.

Ever since the Orc Wars had wound down and the Horde's forces had been pushed back to Blackrock Spire and the Dark Portal, the area had regained its former bustle.

The excavation site was ringed by mountains. When they arrived in the afternoon, Jace walked up to the pit's edge and glanced down. Inside were bluish-green stone pillars everywhere, massive statues still being cleared, collapsed ones, and crumbling stone walls shored up with scaffolding and wooden planks… stretches of ruins and structural debris extending all the way to the cliffs in the distance.

"What do you think, Jace?" Grede shaded his eyes against the reddening sunlight and looked down. "They say the last Bronzebeard Mountain King found his bloodline here and mastered the legendary power of the Avatar."

"What was his name again…"

"Muradin Bronzebeard?" Jace asked.

"No, no." Grede shook his head. "It wasn't Muradin here — apparently it was Dun Morogh, though that's only rumor… the royal family keeps pretty tight-lipped about that."

"Actually, I suspect Muradin Bronzebeard mastered the Avatar inside Ironforge itself, because I heard a rumor that there's an ancient titan-forged ruin deep beneath Ironforge too… or maybe they're just born knowing how to reclaim the power of their Earthen ancestors."

Jace glanced at him and said, "Then why don't Thaurissan and Kurdran Wildhammer know how to command Earthen power? As leaders of the Three Hammers, are they weaker than the Bronzebeards? If you told me the High King Anvilmar's line, who ruled the Three Hammers three hundred years ago, were born knowing how to grow to giant size, I might half-believe it."

Grede stared at Jace for a moment and said, "You know what I hate about you?"

"That I can outfight you?"

"Gods, that's the second thing." The dwarf held up one finger. "Number one… you always say things that make me feel like you know dwarves better than I do in certain respects."

"Library work. Credit the library." Jace waved a hand. "Let's head down to the Digsite camp and ask around."

Grede, still unconvinced, followed him. "The Wizard Sanctum's library — it's not Dalaran, or… Ironforge's Great Library. And you're a human, not some gnome-built book-devouring machine!"

The two of them made their way down the slope to the camp. In the middle of several wooden plank houses, a group of dwarves sat speaking Dwarvish among themselves.

Grede walked up and said something to them in Dwarvish — those dwarves had apparently met him before, and came over to chat like old friends.

After a few minutes of conversation, Grede walked back shaking his head. "Nobody here knows anything about that ruin."

"What about the people who went?" Jace asked.

"They don't know." Grede said. "The dwarves here are all pretty young — twenty, thirty. They probably weren't even born yet when Ironforge discovered that ruin."

"Let's rest a bit first, then ask again later?" Jace looked toward the deeper part of the excavation site. "Seems like some folks haven't come back yet."

"Fair enough." Jace glanced at an empty wooden-shelf shelter nearby. "I could use a rest anyway."

The two sat in the shelter eating a bit. As the sky slowly darkened, it actually started to rain.

Before long, several paths leading to the excavation site saw dwarves running out holding down their hats. Jace gnawed on an enormous loaf of bread that felt like a Loch Modan version of a Russian black bread, watching each passing dwarf, searching for anyone who looked seasoned enough.

Then a brown-bearded dwarf walked up from behind the pair, came under the shelter, found a crate to sit on, and looked at them. "I heard you two want to know about that ruin in the badlands. They sent me over to ask."

He wore a white shirt and stood about as tall as Grede — tall enough for a Bronzebeard dwarf, in other words.

The fellow's skin was genuinely the color of polished copper, but he was soaked halfway through from the rain, his pants stained in patches of yellow and brown with mud.

His Common was thickly accented but remarkably fluent.

Grede said, "We don't just want to know about that ruin — we're planning to go take a look."

Jace nodded.

"I'm Daggot. And you two are?" the brown-bearded dwarf asked.

"Jace."

"Grede."

The two replied.

The worker named Daggot glanced at Jace and asked, "Human, you know that place is Dark Iron territory now, right?"

"We know. We plan to sneak in past the Dark Iron surveillance, Mr. Daggot," Jace said. "I heard you took part in the Explorers' League excavation of that ruin. I wonder if you could offer us some help?"

Daggot nodded and said, "I did take part in the excavation for a stretch, and worked right up until the day the project had to be shut down because of Stone Trogg sabotage."

"I was hoping to get a blueprint, or something like a map," Jace said. "We're willing to pay."

Daggot said, "I don't have any relevant documents. The expedition's findings are either in the hands of the Troggs — the Stone Troggs — or with the Dark Iron dwarves. Folks like me on the expedition were just lucky to walk out alive. The Explorers' League in Ironforge might have preserved some earlier blueprints, but they'd be useless by now. Even when I left, they were already outdated."

Jace had the feeling Daggot was holding something back, so he asked, "So, Mr. Daggot, what exactly did you have in mind coming to us?"

"Saying there's no route guidance at all wouldn't be entirely accurate," Daggot said. "After all, the layout of that Temple ruin…"

He tapped his head. "It's still burned into my brain, even after more than a decade."

"You'd be willing to guide us?" Grede asked.

"I heard you're a Thunderfist gryphon rider who fought in the Orc Wars?" Daggot asked.

"That's right." Grede nodded, then glanced at Jace and added, "Former gryphon rider, of course."

Daggot looked at Jace and asked, "And you're a mage? Your staff looks quite valuable."

"I'm a mage," Jace said.

The brown-bearded dwarf nodded and said, "I think you two are a lot more reliable than the last pair of dwarf mercenaries. You two clearly have some skills. Those two are probably vulture food by now. That said, I won't guide you for free, and I don't want money. I just want to find something I left behind in that ruin."

"You're sure it wasn't taken by the Dark Iron dwarves?" Grede asked.

"Even if it was taken by them," Daggot said. "As long as I can get back to the spot where I lost it and take a look — whether it's there or not — that fulfills my wish."

"We might need to use invisibility potions," Jace said. "We might also use some… not-exactly-honorable magic. Things that might… make you uncomfortable."

"Not-exactly-honorable magic? You mean sorcery, or dark magic?" Daggot looked at Jace and said. "I stopped worrying about those distinctions a long time ago. Otherwise I couldn't have become an Explorers' League member. Still, I'm quite interested in why you two are partnering up to head to that ruin."

Grede patted Jace's belly with his hand and said, "This mage here is off to explore the so-called origins of the arcane. He thinks the connection between the arcane and the Creator Gods must be extremely profound. As for me, like countless warriors of my kin, I'm on the path of tracing my own bloodline."

"So you want to become the next Mountain King, is that it?" Daggot smiled. "There are plenty of Bronzebeards with the same goal. But it's rare among Wildhammers."

After he said that, his smile slowly cooled and he said, "As long as you lay eyes on that temple, you'll know you've found the right place, Mr. Grede."

Translator Notes

Flags

- New party member — Daggot: A veteran Brownbeard (Bronzebeard) Explorers' League excavator who worked the Badlands temple dig until it was shut down by a Stone Trogg attack. He knows the ruin's layout from memory but has no documents. He'll guide Jace and Grede in exchange for being taken back to retrieve a personal item he left behind. - Badlands temple intelligence: The Explorers' League's original excavation findings are now held by either the Stone Troggs or the Dark Iron dwarves. Ironforge's blueprints are outdated. The Dark Iron clan is treating the site as a serious operation. - Daggot's combat credentials: He's physically sturdy, carries a copper walking staff, a small chisel, and a short blade. He's aware the ruin is Dark Iron territory and is unfazed by the prospect of "dark magic" being used. - Travel plan: The three rest at the Digsite for the night, then set out south for the Badlands the next day.
