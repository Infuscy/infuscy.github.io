Chapter 364: The Prerequisite for Entering the Badlands

The three men rested at the Digsite for the night, packed their food, and set out the next morning.

Along the way, Daggot kept a copper-colored staff in hand as a walking stick. A small chisel hung at his belt alongside a short blade. His sturdy build seemed a match even for Grede.

Members of the Explorers' League had never been simple scholars or archaeological specialists. There were even some among them who'd joined the League for the sole purpose of running into all sorts of ancient ruins and fighting creatures no one had ever heard of.

"I've always wondered, Daggot," Grede asked. "The king's brother, Muradin Bronzebeard — he's a member of the Explorers' League too, isn't he?"

"Not just a member — one of the leaders, same as his brother Brann," Daggot said as he walked. "What, you want to join the Explorers' League? The League does have non-dwarves, I'm not exaggerating. During our expeditions we've cooperated with all kinds of foreign guides — humans, gnomes, even trolls… so we'd certainly accept a Wildhammer brother too. But in practice, we still take orders from King Magni. You sure you can accept that?"

"Sounds pretty appealing, but I don't have any plans along those lines at the moment," Grede said. "I'm curious — how did Muradin master the Avatar? Did he find that ancient power in Dun Morogh, or in the ruins around the Digsite here?"

"Neither." Daggot glanced back. "He found it in the ruins of Northrend."

Jace asked in surprise, "He's been to Northrend?"

Daggot stopped walking and said, "The Explorers' League took an interest in the ancient ruins of Northrend a long time ago. Even from Wintergarde, they've already uncovered countless relics from gods-know-how-many hundreds of thousands of years ago — never mind the northern mountains buried under snow year-round. Lord Muradin found that power in the ancient ruins of Northrend."

Grede nodded and said, "No wonder he's so powerful. Northrend must have temple ruins on a far grander scale than the ones around the Digsite, and hold far more power left over by the ancient titans."

"That's the theory, but so far no one's made it deep into those mountains," Daggot said.

Following the road south through a gorge, Jace started feeling that something was off about the air.

He'd smelled this faint, scorched stench before when heading north through Redridge from Lakeshire.

Many dwarf geologists believed the Blasted Lands hadn't ended up the way they had naturally either — they'd been affected by the eruption of Blackrock Mountain.

The tall mountains of the Redridge range protected the humans around Lake Everstill, but the lower hills to the north hadn't been able to block the scorching waves from Blackrock Mountain that had been eroding northward year after year, until they finally slammed into the mountains of Dun Morogh and were stopped entirely. The already-arid grasslands in between had been turned into a lifeless wasteland.

After night fell, the three found a spot in a rock crevice within the gorge to rest. After Daggot arranged a sleeping spot, he looked at Jace and said, "You're a mage, right? With you around, traveling through the desert shouldn't be a problem."

"What?" Jace said. "Wait, what do you mean?"

"Water-conjuring spells, obviously," Daggot said. "You look like a fairly accomplished mage, with a staff like that, and I'd say you're pretty confident."

"Don't we have enough water?" Jace said. "Isn't that excavation site in the northern badlands, pretty close to the Loch Modan mountains?"

"It's not that far, compared to the overall size of the badlands," Daggot said. "But you can't count on us making it through this stretch of sand without a single hiccup… If we run into trouble — bad weather, a dust storm, a giant scorpion ambush, or some other worse accident… a couple of days' delay and our water's probably not going to be enough."

Grede sighed and said, "But the thing is, our mage here is clearly better at killing people than providing any extra support for the team."

"Fine." Daggot sat to one side and said, "I'm confident I can locate the ruin within a day or two if we push a bit."

"We could just steal other people's water," Grede offered. "Once we're in Dark Iron territory, we might be able to get our hands on their water supply."

Daggot said, "Best to avoid fights as much as possible. Don't let any of those Dark Iron run back to Angor Fortress for reinforcements — that would be a serious problem for us. We haven't entered the badlands yet, so we still have a chance to turn back to Loch Modan and figure out how to bring more water. Or something."

"Sleep through the hottest part of the day, travel at night — that'd save us some more," Grede added.

"That is indeed a good option, just slower," Daggot nodded.

"Enough, stop." Jace stood up and straightened his robe. "Give me one more chance. Let me try again."

"What?" Grede looked up. "You want to burn through another waterskin for us?"

"Burn a waterskin? What does that mean?" Daggot asked.

"This time I won't use a waterskin. I'll test it on open ground. All right? If it doesn't work, we go back to Loch Modan for more water."

Jace walked off to the side and found a large boulder with a depression in the middle, trying to steady his breathing.

"What's he doing?" Daggot asked Grede.

"Fire spell," Grede said helplessly.

"Bah." Jace gave him a sideways look and drew Skysong on the spot. Arcane light immediately illuminated the rock crevice.

Daggot blinked and asked, "What is this now…"

"Proof of a fairly accomplished mage," Jace said.

"There's… really no need for this, Jace. I was joking. You should take it easy with the testing."

Grede shifted his backside back a step as he spoke, looking around for stone cover, as if a firestorm was about to hit.

"There's a real need." With that, Jace pointed his sword at the hollow in the stone and intoned, "Vald, Tikar, Kalt!"

The moment the words left his mouth, the magic sword's light flickered. Jagged ice spikes burst from the middle of the stone, one after another, forming a head-sized cluster of ice thorns right in front of him!

Jace was so startled by the spell he'd cast that he took a step back. Even though the incantation was complete, the cluster of ice was still growing, stretching upward like bamboo shoots racing to the sky. The spreading cold eventually coated the entire rock surface in a layer of ice.

"How about that, Grede? A fire spell, was it?" Jace turned his head and looked at the two dwarves, stunned by the ice spikes that had appeared out of nowhere. "Go ahead — chip off a few chunks to fill the waterskins that aren't full yet."

Translator Notes

Flags

- New party member — Daggot (confirmed departure): Three men now traveling together: Jace, Grede, and Daggot. Daggot is a veteran Explorers' League excavator from Ironforge. - Muradin's Avatar origin — Northrend: Daggot confirms Muradin Bronzebeard mastered the Avatar ability in Northrend's ancient ruins, not in Dun Morogh or at the Digsite. Northrend holds far larger temple ruins and more ancient titan power, but no one has penetrated deep into the northern mountains yet. - Water crisis: The group's water supply is insufficient for the Badlands crossing if anything goes wrong. Daggot raises the danger; Grede suggests raiding Dark Iron water supplies. The group debates turning back to Loch Modan. - Skysong's frost spell: Jace proves his worth as a desert water source by casting a frost spell through Skysong ("Vald, Tikar, Kalt"), conjuring a rapidly growing cluster of ice spikes from a boulder — enough ice to refill waterskins. The spell exceeded Jace's own expectations. - Angor Fortress: Named as the Dark Iron reinforcement base to avoid alerting.
