## Chapter 365: Lord of the Badlands

Despite having a water source, the three men decided to play it safe — at least resting in the shade during the hottest midday hours and waiting for the sky to cool before continuing.

But apparently, in the Blasted Lands, they weren't the only ones with that idea.

During the day, the endless wasteland was barren and exposed. But once night fell, every so often they'd see small groups of three or four shadows trailing them in the distance. Some were sand wolves, some were packs of wild wolves, and some were wild boars that couldn't find anything to eat.

It wasn't that this happened once in a while — no, not long after entering the badlands, as dusk fell they'd already run into several such unwelcome parties. The different herds would even face off against each other over some strange "right to follow" until the three men slowly moved out of range.

After all, these beasts couldn't go picking fights with the Dark Iron dwarves, or even the orcs wandering the area. Naturally they could only come bully a traveling party that was relatively alone.

Of course, a traveling party bold enough to enter this place in a group of three was no ordinary bunch either. The herds that had struggled to survive in these badlands for over a century understood this too, so they just followed along, probing for opportunities — even chances to ambush.

Or they'd wait until the three ran out of supplies and collapsed in despair before closing in for the kill.

"Damn beasts," Grede muttered. "Come on up and try me if you've got the guts."

"There's a lot of them, Mr. Grede," Daggot said, looking over. "Don't engage them. If we get hurt in a fight, it'll seriously slow our pace."

Grede said, "If those cowards could actually beat us, they'd have tried charging us by now."

Daggot glanced ahead and said, "No need for that. I've already spotted several hill shapes I'm very familiar with, and our heading's right. I think by the time this night's over and dawn comes, we should be able to see the ruins outside the temple."

"Good thing we've got you as a guide, Daggot," Grede said. "I had no idea that ruin was so far from Loch Modan!"

Jace said, "The maps of this area are all drawn pretty vaguely. Probably not many people know exactly how big this place really is."

"That's true enough." Daggot climbed a slope as he spoke. "Both the dwarven and the human maps draw this place smaller than it is. After all, they're all just estimates — nobody's got the mood to actually survey the land. Even gryphon riders don't dare ride their beloved mounts toward the deepest parts. I'd say the ones who understand just how big this place really is are probably the Dark Iron clan."

At that moment Grede stopped and looked back. "Have you two noticed? All the animals have disappeared."

Jace nodded, gazing toward the southwest — deeper into the desert. The few shadows that had been trailing them were now completely gone.

There weren't any animals around, but right now he caught sight of dust billowing in the distance, a hazy, dark, pressing mass.

"Is a dust storm coming?" Jace asked. "Should we find somewhere to take cover?"

Daggot looked in that direction and said quietly, "The wind doesn't seem that strong, but over there… oh my gods…"

He'd just turned his head back when a gust swept through, whipping all three men's clothes and cloaks so hard they could barely stand steady.

But this wind didn't feel natural at all — it blew for a moment and then suddenly vanished. Yet the hazy wall of sand in the distance kept drawing closer.

"Something's wrong," Grede said. "I smell something strange in this wind."

"I smell it too," Jace said. "It's shadow."

"Shadow?" Daggot was taken aback.

Grede said, "Hide. Find somewhere to hide, damn it!"

The three immediately ran in the opposite direction with everything they had, heading for a stretch of sandy gullies dozens of meters ahead.

Grede dove first into the jumbled rocks inside the gully. Outside, the wind blew in fits and starts, but with growing force. Daggot frowned now — clearly he'd smelled it too.

Actually, smelling that scent, Jace already had a suspicion in his heart.

After all, this wasn't the first time he'd caught this particular shadowy, fishy smell. But considering the force of this wind… he really couldn't connect that suspicion to what they were facing right now. Jace glanced at Grede and saw Grede staring right back at him.

"Black dragon!"

The two said it in unison.

Daggot's eyes went wide and he whispered, "What are you talking about — a black dragon in the badlands?"

But if this was a black dragon, it absolutely couldn't be a simple whelp.

Whelps were strong, true, but there was no way you'd feel this kind of presence without even catching sight of the dragon yet.

Even now, a massive sandstorm had kicked up outside, and they still couldn't see a thing.

But the scent of this black dragon was indeed getting clearer and clearer.

The three pressed themselves against the jumbled rocks without making a sound. Just then, a fierce wind howled past in the distance, followed by another. The sand and dust stirred up by the wind suddenly intensified. Jace felt the large stones outside shuddering and knocking against each other.

Gravel and sand continuously hammered the massive boulders they were sheltering behind, making sharp cracking sounds. Jace could feel the stone at his back vibrating again and again under his hand.

If not for these stones blocking the wind, they'd probably have been blown away by now.

But these two blasts of fel wind just swept past and went silent.

Before Grede could even think about poking his head out to check, the sky suddenly darkened. Jace looked up and saw a vague black shape gliding overhead through the dust-choked sky. Straight, level, soaring vast wings covering a great swath of heaven, slowly passing over the three men's heads.

Grede clapped a hand over his mouth. Daggot huddled under the rocks without daring to move.

What the hell was this thing?! How big was it?!

Jace had a hard time gauging the creature's size, because he didn't know how high it was flying.

But judging by the shape of those wings, this was definitely not a whelp — this was a colossal adult dragon.

In the depths of his heart, Jace almost hoped this thing was Deathwing himself, or at least an ancient dragon like Nefarian.

Because if it wasn't… gods knew what the ancestors of this Blackwing flight would look like when they appeared. Would the very sky and earth really change color and the sun and moon go dark?

But he knew that was impossible. If it really were Nefarian, he couldn't possibly fail to notice the three of them hiding in the corner.

Or maybe he just couldn't be bothered to deal with them.

By now, the so-called sandstorm seemed to have settled.

The air was still a bit turbid, but much calmer than before. Daggot sat up, dusted the sand off his body, and asked, "Are you two all right?"

Jace spat the sand off his lips and shook out his cloak. "Fine."

As the three were sorting through their gear, a thunderous crash sounded from the side — as if some sand or rubble had collapsed.

Grede froze and looked back toward where the sound of cracking rocks had come from.

"I feel like I heard the rocks talking…"

"Et… thuk, Sa…"

"Duk… Tharusarak!"

Several low, muffled chants drifted from behind a massive boulder. A pair of burning eyes suddenly flashed into view, carrying a cluster of glowing, heated stones that floated into midair, spinning and assembling into a rubble golem bound together by fel fire!

An Earth Elemental?!

## Flags

- **Badlands predators**: Wolf packs, sand wolves, and wild boars trail Jace's party at night but hold back, probing for weakness rather than attacking outright.
- **Black dragon overflight**: A massive adult black dragon soars over the party at night, kicking up fel-tinged sandstorms with its passage. Jace and Grede recognize the scent; Daggot is shaken. Jace suspects it is not Deathwing or Nefarian — something older and possibly worse.
- **"Fel wind"**: The gusts carry a shadow/fel scent that Jace identifies from prior experience — a signature of powerful black dragon presence.
- **Earth Elemental encounter**: A chanting entity (speaking in what sounds like Titan/Dwarvish phonetics) assembles a rubble golem from heated stones using fel fire behind the party's cover — an ambush or territorial response triggered by the dragon's passage.
- **Daggot's navigation**: He recognizes the terrain and estimates they'll reach the temple ruins by dawn.
