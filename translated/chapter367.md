## Chapter 367: The Excavation Site

The three of them pushed hard for less than two hours. Before dawn, out on a stretch of open, flat sand, Jace spotted the base of a colossal cliff — and everywhere beneath it, toppled stone pillars and statues.

"We're here," Daggett said.

This was Uldaman. Jace still had a strong sense of déjà vu looking at the scene.

He and the two dwarves crouched on a high slope, peering from a distance. The Dark Iron dwarves in the excavation site seemed to be in a frantic rush to haul crates, tools, and such into the cave — bustling about, orders and shouts ringing out. It didn't look anything like the early-morning behavior of a race supposedly fond of their sleep.

"Don't they ever sleep?" Grede bared his teeth. "If I hadn't slept earlier to prep for traveling at night, I'd be too tired to keep my eyes open right now. Honestly, just looking at this pitch-dark sky makes me sleepy."

"Could it be because of that black dragon?" Jace asked. "Look — they keep hauling things into the cave, like they're trying to hide from something."

"But the black dragon already passed," Grede said.

"Maybe they think it'll come back," Jace said.

Daggett said, "Feels like there are fewer people here than the last time I came. Even if they're not worried about the black dragon, they're probably concerned about the orcs in the badlands. But we don't need to go in through the front, either. The troggs trying to get out of the ruins dug a lot of passages over the years. Two of them were modified by the Explorers' League — one leads fairly deep and is quite hidden. The other is more obvious and should've been easy for the Dark Iron to find."

"Can that back way get us past the Dark Iron dwarves?" Jace asked.

Daggett shook his head. "Once we're in, there'll probably still be Dark Iron waiting for us. We'll have to be careful — they may have dug a lot farther than we did back then."

Grede said, "Good enough for me. Still way better than charging in through the front."

Jace nodded. He knew the Uldaman dungeon in the game had a back entrance too, but it probably wasn't the same passage Daggett was talking about.

The three of them hugged the cliff face, skirting as far around the Uldaman excavation site as they could. Grede kept close behind Daggett, then turned back to Jace. "What was that dragon even doing here?"

Jace said, "If the black dragons are looking to collude with the orcs up in Blackrock Spire, it wouldn't be out of line for them to help the Blackrock clan intimidate the Dark Iron dwarves."

"What a fitting pair," Grede let out a derisive laugh.

Jace looked at the Bronzebeard dwarf. "When you left back then, how far had you excavated?"

Daggett answered, "We found the location of the great gate. We hadn't had time to go deeper before we ran into the troggs. Those troggs acted like they'd been living in there from the start — no idea if they built this temple, or if they're servants of the Creators."

"Servants of the gods," Grede said. "You mean those stone giants, like the ones standing around the Iron Ring excavation site?"

Daggett said, "I'm not sure, but with those enormous statues everywhere, you can't rule it out. I'd genuinely love to see a living stone giant someday — and I don't mean a mountain giant, mind you. I have no interest in running into one of those."

As dawn began to break, the three of them finally reached an area of folded, ravine-like earthen slopes. Daggett studied the cliffs here for a moment and said, "Good news: there are no Dark Iron dwarves watching here, which means they haven't found this path. Bad news: it looks different from the last time I was here."

Grede scratched his head. "Then should we go check out that other spot? You said there's more than one of these little paths."

"Don't rush — let me look around." Daggett walked into one of the ravines, looked around, then circled back out and went into another.

Jace and Grede kept watch outside, debating how they might find an opening to slip into the excavation site from the front, when Daggett suddenly called out, "Over here. Come on."

The two of them hurried over and found Daggett standing beside an entrance that looked like a well, holding its cover in place.

Walking up and looking down, they saw a iron ladder leading straight into the dark shadows below.

Though no sound came from within, if the Dark Iron dwarves hadn't found this place, it should still be safe inside.

"How is it that the Dark Iron haven't found this place in all these years?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "It might be blocked up in there by now."

Daggett nodded. "This passage does collapse easily, but I know where the problem is."

The three of them climbed down the ladder, moved through a narrow hewn tunnel, and reached the pitch-black bottom, stepping on soft sand. With only the faint light from the well opening above, they could make out very little of the cramped space.

Jace drew Skysong, and Arcane Brilliance lit the area — revealing the skeletons of two dwarves lying in the small room.

The Bronzebeard dwarf led the two through a dead-end passage, stopped before a pile of rubble, and said, "Let's move these stones together."

As they hauled the stones, they suddenly felt the pile in front of them loosen.

"Hey, put your sword away, human!"

Daggett whispered. Jace sheathed his blade, and the dwarf edged forward to shift a stone slab. After a brief cascade of sand, a faint, flickering light came through.

Jace peered through the crevice and saw a small, swaying brazier in the distance. In this darkness, even the tiniest glow stood out clearly.

Daggett said, "Move this bigger one and we should be able to squeeze through. I know these passages — I'll go first and make sure it's safe, then you two follow."

"Either way, have your Invisibility Potions ready." Jace handed one to each of the two dwarves. "Keep them somewhere you can grab them fast. Once we're in a spot we can't fight our way out of, these are how we get clear. Have you two drunk Invisibility Potion before?"

"A few times," Grede nodded. "But the last time was a long, long time ago."

Daggett said, "I drank them all the time before I joined the Iron Ring excavation. If I remember right, once you drink it, you basically can't see a thing."

"Can't see a thing?" Grede muttered. "I think I could still make out some stuff."

"Close enough," Jace said. "I suppose I'm the least experienced here — I've only had it once. But as long as you memorize the terrain in advance... you just push past the dangerous stretches."

Jace and Grede nodded. They helped Daggett slowly shove aside a large boulder below, opening a gap just big enough for a dwarf to squeeze through, and Daggett began to inch his way in.

Jace stole a glance outside, watching Daggett's shadow slowly move away. There was no reaction out there. Then he heard the faint sound of dwarven voices in the distance, though he couldn't make out the words.

After a while, Daggett came back and waved them over. "Most of them are busy organizing the stuff they hauled in outside. Let's move while we can."
