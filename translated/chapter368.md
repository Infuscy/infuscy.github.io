## Chapter 368: The Gate of the Ruins

The low, unremarkable rock crevice was so tight that Jace could only crawl out of it, and in the end the two dwarves had to haul him into the passage.

By the light of the brazier, Daggett quickly darted deeper into the passage. Jace looked at the branching paths, caves, and deep gullies one after another and thought that if they hadn't found this guide, this trip would've been a lot more troublesome.

Judging by the way Grede kept scanning their surroundings, he was probably thinking something similar.

After a while, Daggett raised an arm to stop the two of them, glanced toward a cave opening, then pulled back and held up two fingers — apparently signaling that two dwarves were watching over there.

Jace tilted his head for a look and saw two Dark Iron dwarves in linen vests walking away down the cyan stone wall.

Now that they could see cyan stone walls... that meant they were already close to entering Uldaman's temple proper.

After the two dwarves left, Daggett led the two of them along the stone wall, continuing forward until they emerged from a narrow opening and ducked behind a stack of crates. Jace heard what sounded like four or five Dark Iron dwarves talking up ahead.

"You said you had Invisibility Potion — now's the time to use it." Daggett leaned in close and lowered his voice. "That's the temple gate up ahead. Once we're inside, there's a long, narrow passage on the right. Go all the way to the end and there's a corner, with a stone pillar at the corner."

"Hold on, hold on," Grede whispered. "Are you sure your description is precise enough? Don't leave anything out — we'll basically be running in blind."

"I've described it as detailed as I can," Daggett said. "I remember it very clearly. Once we're through, find that stone pillar first. Beside the pillar there's a crumbling section of wall — very easy to spot. We'll regroup inside that crumbled wall. Once the Invisibility Potion wears off, we'll figure out the next move."

"If we don't want to get caught, our only option is to fight our way out," Daggett said. "I'm confident the two of you could punch straight through a crowd of laborers. But if even one Dark Iron dwarf makes it back to the fortress, then this whole trip will be for nothing — we'll have to abandon it and leave."

"So where's the thing you're looking for?" Jace asked.

"Inside. We're very close," Daggett said.

Jace nodded. "Then let's do it."

The three of them took turns slowly poking their heads out to scout the terrain. When Jace looked, he found it was similar to the game: two enormous cyan-blue stone doors slightly ajar, with a long staircase leading straight down.

The Dark Iron dwarves had built some camps at the bottom of the stairs and on the high terraces beside it, and below there were quite a few sandwork emplacements and firearms — all meant to deal with the troggs that came charging out.

From what they could see, there were only four dwarves guarding the camp. Most of the Dark Iron dwarves must have gone out to haul things in.

Having confirmed the general layout, the three of them took out their Invisibility Potions together, drank them down, and counted down three-two-one.

As his vision began to blur, Jace immediately darted out and sprinted toward the stairs, dodging past the Dark Iron camp in the middle. Relying on the experience from the last time he'd drunk an Invisibility Potion, he could roughly make out the shapes of the architecture by the light of the braziers.

But the positions of those Dark Iron dwarves were impossible to make out with his eyes alone — he had to rely on memory to avoid them.

Though he could roughly see where the walls and tents were, once he started up the stairs, the shifting patches of light and shadow made it impossible to tell whether he was stepping on a stair at all. He fell several times, but in the end he scrambled and ran his way to the edge of the door gap.

Once inside, the light and shadow suddenly multiplied. Even with blurred vision, he could tell this was an empty, cavernous hall.

Following Daggett's instructions, he ran in to the right and entered a passage. As he neared the end, he could already feel the Invisibility Potion about to wear off, the things before his eyes gradually losing their haze and becoming clearer.

He grabbed onto the stone pillar and saw a flaking section of wall behind it. Circling around, he found a crack just as expected.

After ducking inside, Daggett was already waiting in there.

Before long, Grede also squeezed in low.

"Did anyone follow us?" Daggett whispered.

"I don't know." Grede stuck his head out and looked, waited quietly for a moment, and confirmed there were no footsteps. "Should be fine. Are there any Dark Iron in here? I mean, inside this gate, in this part of the temple."

Daggett answered, "From what I can see, it doesn't look like it. Which means they haven't dealt with the trogg problem either. The Orc Wars didn't just hit us."

Jace stole a glance outside, looking at the towering walls. "We're already inside this temple, Grede. Tell me — can you feel the difference between here and the Iron Ring excavation site... or any other ruins you've visited before."

Grede leaned out and touched the stone pillar in front of him. "I... I think I can feel something different. I can't quite describe it."

"But we're too close to the Dark Iron dwarves here, and probably not far from the troggs either — this is likely where the two sides clash," Jace said. "Let's look deeper in. Mr. Daggett, where's the thing you're looking for?"

"On the south side of this great hall, there's a cave dug out by the troggs," Daggett said. "That's my destination."

"South..." Jace said. "Just say left or right."

Daggett nodded, glanced outside, and looked toward the corridor around the corner ahead. "Go straight from here, take the next left, and keep going — that'll bring you to the front hall of this temple. Continue straight ahead, enter another passage, and at the corner where it turns right you'll find a broken-open wall. The earthen crevice inside that wall leads to the place I'm talking about."

"We'd pushed deep into that cave before and found a pile of accumulated relics, but then the troggs seized it. We tried to take it back but never managed it, and we lost a companion in the process. Back then, there were too few of us exploring these ruins — we underestimated the danger here."

Listening to Daggett, Jace felt a flicker of familiarity. He had the sense that this Uldaman's layout was somewhat similar to the game's — a great hall surrounded by several corridors.

With that, Daggett had already drawn the short blade at his waist. "From here on, we'll be dealing with those troggs."

Jace handed the Bronzebeard dwarf a Nature Protection Potion. "Better safe than sorry."

Daggett glanced at the potion. "The mages of Stormwind truly are well-read. The trogg elementalists have none of the humility and kindness of the Wildhammer sages — the elemental power they summon is as savage and crude as they are."

"Now it's time to let them see what real savagery and crudeness look like, Daggett." Grede gripped his hammer. "I'll take point."

- **Characters**: 达葛特 = Daggett (OC Bronzebeard dwarf), 格瑞德 = Grede (canon per glossary).
- **Lore note**: The "cyan stone walls" (青色石墙) mark the boundary of the Titan-constructed portion of Uldaman. Daggett's mention of losing a companion to the troggs establishes the stakes of the deeper ruins.
- **Tone note**: The stealth sequence is rendered with a tense, procedural rhythm. Grede's skepticism about Daggett's directions keeps the banter alive even under pressure.
