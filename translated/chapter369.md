## Chapter 369: The Troggs

The depths of Uldaman grew darker and darker, the outlines of the ancient walls and stone pillars barely visible by whatever faint light filtered in from somewhere.

On both walls, countless bas-reliefs were carved — diminutive creatures standing in reverence before enormous stone giants.

Grede edged along the wall, moving forward bit by bit. Daggett followed behind. It was deathly quiet here; only the occasional rumble of the earth covered the careful footsteps of the three of them, and Jace had no way to directly ask Daggett up ahead what these murals were even about.

But given the Explorers' League's scholarly spirit, they surely knew.

The three of them walked deep into the corridor. After what felt like a long time, they finally reached the first bend Daggett had mentioned. Ahead was yet another long, dim tunnel, its walls cracked by the pressure of tens of thousands of years.

Jace stared at those pitch-black fissures and kept getting the feeling that some ancient thing might leap out from anywhere and attack them.

Just then, two strange, swaying, grotesque shadows seemed to appear up ahead. The three of them immediately ducked behind a tall urn. A rustle of shuffling footsteps drew closer, then crude voices.

The passage wasn't wide, and the urn wasn't enough to conceal three people. If these two troggs walked up to them, they'd definitely spot the three of them. Grede made a throat-slitting gesture. Daggett nodded. Jace gripped his sword hilt.

As the footsteps reached the urn, Grede swung his stone hammer and smashed the skull of the figure in the distance. The one up close flinched — and Jace immediately drove Skysong through its mouth. Only a low, rattling gargle came from its throat. Jace pulled the sword back and drove it through the creature's skull, ending its life.

Their eyesight was clearly poor — by the time Grede had lunged out, the trogg had still been standing there in a daze. As the trogg collapsed, Jace finally got a clear look at what this creature looked like.

It wore a simple outer garment and trousers, a belt made from some animal's hide cinched at its waist, and carried a chipped stone dagger. The claws on its arms and feet were thick and long — clearly a burrowing race.

Its head was astonishingly large, taking up nearly a third of its entire body. Its broad mouth was lined with unsettling fangs, its nose pointed, its brow ridges jutting out — like some hybrid of a baboon and a big-headed dwarf.

And the most critical detail: it had hair and a beard, distributed in a pattern that brought to mind a balding, hunched man in his fifties or sixties. In short, the sight of this deformed head lying there on its side was enough to make Jace's skin crawl.

He truly couldn't fathom why the dwarves' Creators had made a monster like this.

There was another possibility: that the troggs hadn't looked like this when the Titans created them, but had been transformed into their current state after the "Curse of Flesh," twisted further by the energies of the Old Gods corrupting the temple. In Ironforge, some scholars believed that troggs and dwarves actually came from the same batch of creations — that troggs were simply dwarves whose evolution had failed. Of course, that theory had been fiercely attacked by countless other dwarven scholars.

But when you actually stood before a trogg, you realized the comparison wasn't entirely baseless.

Right now, under Jace's cautious mood, Skysong only gave off a faint, indistinct glow. Its blade didn't look absurdly sharp, but the ease with which it pierced flesh made Jace feel slightly off.

The stab wounds on the neck and skull of the trogg he'd just killed still smoldered with a trace of arcane scorch marks.

Having confirmed they hadn't drawn the attention of any other troggs, Daggett pointed to a nearby crevice in the wall. The two nodded and dragged the two trogg corpses inside.

The trogg corpses were absurdly heavy and gave off a thick, sour stench of sweat. Though their bodies looked gaunt and dry, rough skin clinging to bone, with nothing but that enormous skull to speak of, they felt nearly as heavy as a dwarf.

After tidying up, Daggett nodded, caught his breath, and continued forward. As they neared the exit of the passage, they could feel the faint flicker of a brazier's light from the hall beyond.

At the exit, Grede glanced inside, then furrowed his brow and pulled his head back. Jace raised an eyebrow at him. Grede pointed backward with his thumb, signaling for Jace to look for himself.

Jace curiously peeked inside, then immediately gritted his teeth and pulled back.

Inside the hall, three troggs were crouched beside a burning stone basin in the temple, roasting and eating meat — and the source of that meat was an incomplete, mangled Dark Iron dwarf hanging from a black hook nearby, identifiable only by its dark skin and bits of red beard.

Grede had been about to charge in with his hammer, but Daggett held him back.

He pointed to the far end of the hall, where Jace could see another passage opening on the opposite side, facing them.

If Daggett was right, going into that passage would lead to the cave he'd mentioned.

If they made a move here and caused too much noise, it would probably draw even more troggs — and they'd have a hard time getting out.

Though the three dwarven clans had fought a civil war once upon a time, they were still cousins who had lived alongside each other for countless millennia.

Seeing their own kind — creatures that looked just like them — being roasted and devoured by these twisted monsters clearly unsettled the two dwarves.

The three of them turned out from the other side of the passage, quietly circling a statue and advancing along the shadows toward the other side of the hall.

Fortunately, the hall was large and empty. Aside from this group of troggs feeding, there were only a few troggs farther off holding spears and chatting about something while guarding a large gate.

But the three of them hadn't gone more than a few steps when, despite slowing their pace as much as possible and walking with care, one of the troggs that had been eating noticed them.

It glanced over, said something to the other troggs, and came toward them alone.

Grede, far from panicking, actually seemed to get more excited — as if he'd finally gotten the chance for a proper showdown with a pack of man-eating monsters.

Jace held him back by the shoulder and, from the shadows, quietly intoned the Fear incantation at the trogg from a distance.

He spoke as softly as a whisper. The trogg shuddered as it was hit, its expression growing more and more twisted as it trembled and ran back to the brazier, clutching it and babbling away.

Its shouting echoed through the hall, drawing the attention of the troggs in the distance toward the brazier. The three of them used the opening to advance quickly through the shadows until they reached the entrance of the passage on the opposite side.

Those troggs laughed as they dragged the terrified trogg back to where the three of them had been hiding, laughed again, then beat the poor creature savagely.

Troggs were ultimately not true sentient beings — there was always a screw loose in their heads. Seizing the chaos, they quickly slipped into the passage. Though troggs had poor eyesight, their hearing was razor-sharp; no matter how careful they were, it was hard to avoid being noticed.

## Flags

- **Chapter title**: 穴居者 — "The Troggs" (lit. "Cave-Dwellers"; the chapter is about encountering the troggs directly).
- **Key terms**: 奥达曼 = Uldaman (canon), 石腭怪 / 穴居人 = troggs (canon), 穴居石腭怪 = troggs (compound term), 黑铁矮人 = Dark Iron dwarves (canon), 碧空之歌 = Skysong (Jace's sword), 恐惧术 = Fear (canon warlock spell), 血肉诅咒 = Curse of Flesh (canon Titan lore), 上古之神 = Old Gods (canon), 铁炉堡 = Ironforge (canon), 三锤 = the three dwarven clans (Bronzebeard, Wildhammer, Dark Iron).
- **Characters**: 达葛特 = Daggett (OC Bronzebeard dwarf), 格瑞德 = Grede (canon per glossary).
- **Lore note**: The passage gives an in-world scholarly explanation for trogg origins — the "Curse of Flesh" and Old God corruption of Titan-forged beings — and notes the controversial Ironforge theory that troggs are "failed" dwarves. Jace's reaction to the trogg's appearance is kept visceral and disgusted, matching the source.
- **Tone note**: The horror of the troggs' cannibalism of the Dark Iron dwarf is rendered plainly; Grede's mix of disgust and battle-lust stays in character. The Fear-spell stealth sequence keeps the tension up while letting the trio slip past.
