## Chapter 371: The Shadow of the Snow Land

The three of them quickly left the cave and returned to the tunnel. Sure enough, three troggs were already coming toward them from the other end of the passage.

Grede and Jace made quick work of the three creatures, dragged their bodies into the cave, and continued along the tunnel in the other direction. After rounding several corners, they arrived at another chamber where six or seven troggs had made their home.

This cave was too far from the passage where the earlier fight had happened, though — they didn't seem to have noticed the commotion. They were just dozing off beside a fire basin.

Skirting along the edges into the shadows, Daggart pointed toward a large doorway high up in the chamber, signaling that they needed to go through there.

Jace had been uneasy about the stone Daggart was carrying the whole time. It was clearly not a good thing, and it didn't belong to Uldaman either.

They slipped through the doorway and it felt as though they'd walked out of the titan temple and back into the excavation site proper — forked roads and caves everywhere, earthen pits and gullies.

Daggart looked at the pit ahead and said, "Looks like the Dark Iron dwarves haven't dug much farther than we have. They've just been wasting time with these cave-dwellers. I knew they'd long since lost the patience for real exploration and discovery. I don't understand — if they've lost interest in recovering ancient knowledge, what's the point of fighting over the ruins of this temple?"

"We go this way. I remember there's another chamber over there," he said, pointing down a side passage.

Jace looked at the dead end on this side. So at this point in time, Uldaman hadn't been fully excavated yet.

Still, that put him a little more at ease. He hadn't been keen on dealing with whatever guardians or powerful stone temple sentinels lay deeper inside anyway.

They entered another passage. Deep ahead appeared to be yet another massive door, hidden in pitch-black shadow.

In truth, Uldaman was a complex of buildings buried beneath the earth. This ought to be a passage dug open by the troggs between different structures.

"I don't know how to describe this feeling, Jace." Grede looked up at the chamber ahead and said, "I think... it's right here."

"You're not the only one who feels that way," Daggart said. "All dwarves feel like they're coming home when they approach these buildings. It's as though the buildings, the walls, are speaking to you."

"Exactly," Grede said, looking at Daggart. "That's exactly it. These walls — it's like I've seen them somewhere before, I don't know when."

The three of them climbed the steps and approached the entrance to the hall. It was pitch black inside. Borrowing the faint light from outside, they could make out what seemed to be stone pillars within, but there was no sound at all.

Jace drew Skysong to illuminate the hall and found many toppled small statues and pillars scattered about.

"I can feel it, Jace." Grede leaned against a stone pillar and gazed into the depths of the hall. "I can feel it — this is the place."

"You sure?" Jace kicked aside a broken leg from one of the small statues, then noticed that all of them were shaped like dwarves.

Wait... could these be toppled Earthen?

Daggart said, "This is the hall where the Creator forged the dwarven ancestors, you two. We've found these shattered statues in the ancient sites of Dun Morogh as well. At first we thought they were just life-sized stone statues carved by ancient dwarves, but only later did we realize — these might be our ancestors themselves."

"So these are the so-called Earthen?" Grede looked down at a lifelike gray stone statue. "Incredible. I can hardly imagine these things ever moving."

"So what do we do next?" Jace asked.

Grede handed Jace all of his luggage and said, "If what those people in Ironforge say is true, I need to fast for a while. A day, two days... I don't know. I need to temporarily give up what it means to be a dwarf, and explore what it felt like to be an ancient Earthen."

"If you don't eat or drink, you'll be dead in four or five days," Jace said.

"Finish off the rest of the food. If I still haven't found anything by then, we head back," Grede said.

"Fine," Jace said. "I'll eat as slowly as possible. I didn't come all this way for nothing."

"Good thing we've got a mage who can conjure water," Grede said, clapping Jace on the shoulder. "That'll buy us some more time."

Jace remembered that there were still some lizards and scorpions and the like living in the deeper parts of Uldaman. He asked Daggart, "Are there any cave beasts in here? If we really run out of food, we could try searching deeper..."

"I'm not sure either, mage," Daggart said. "The deepest we've explored is around this area. Beyond that, we know very little. Truth be told, we don't know much about that last chamber either, let alone what's inside. But I'd say there must be some — otherwise how would these cave-dwellers have survived until now?"

Jace nodded and looked at Grede. "Do your thing. Don't worry about us."

"I need to be alone in here. Remember, Jace?" Grede said.

"Right. We'll head out."

Just as they turned to leave, Grede caught Jace's eye and mouthed a word toward Daggart's departing back — *Stone*.

Jace nodded and made a reassuring gesture, then fell into step beside Daggart.

Once they were outside, Jace glanced down at the stone clutched in Daggart's hand, its aura distinctly unsettling. "Can you tell me where you got this thing?"

"Northrend," Daggart said. "I found it in the ruins of a giant village."

"Giants' ruins?" Jace thought. Did he mean Storm Giants, or Vrykul?

Two kinds of giants lived in Northrend. The Vrykul were the progenitors of humanity. Most Vrykul, male and female, regularly grew over two meters tall, and the tallest among them could reach three meters.

The Storm Giants, on the other hand, were creatures of myth... In the game, Storm Giants were several stories tall, and every step they took made the earth tremble.

If it was a village of giants, he must have meant the Vrykul.

"Right. Northrend is home to a race of giants. Some of them are fairly friendly — you can communicate and exchange words with them. When I showed them this stone, they said she was a goddess from their legends, who lived in the cliff mountains of the far north."

"They told me that if you won her favor, your catches would grow abundant, and the prey in the forest would practically walk up to you on its own."

"Of course, there are too many myths and stories like that," Daggart said with a laugh. "At first I didn't take her seriously at all. But it's only now that I've realized — she holds a special meaning for me."

"She's been through so much with me. Adventure after adventure, again and again cheating death. Ever since I left her here last time, I haven't had the drive for work I used to, or that fearless, head-on drive I once had. You don't know how important that is to an explorer. I simply couldn't forget everything she'd given me... After I lost her, I tried to give up the thought of her and throw myself back into work, taking on other assignments. But I knew that someday I'd come back here and take her away, mage. And that day is now. I'm grateful to you — so grateful I don't even know how to express it. Once we get out of here, I'll take you to Kharanos, and whatever you want... money, gems, everything I have, mage."

As he spoke, Daggart grew somewhat agitated, a far cry from the calm, collected manner he'd shown the whole journey. And Jace grew increasingly wary, more and more certain that something was very wrong with this thing.

Because the woman's likeness on it, combined with the Vrykul and Northrend, connected it in his mind to something very, very, very bad.

"Does this goddess have a name?"

"Sara," Daggart said. "I could forget anything, but never her name."

Jace's suspicions were confirmed. This so-called goddess was almost certainly a phantom created by Yogg-Saron, the Old God imprisoned beneath the titan temple of Ulduar in the far north.

Sara. Back when he'd ground through the first phase of the Yogg-Saron raid hundreds of times, there was absolutely no way he could have forgotten that name.

At the start of the encounter, Yogg-Saron would transform into a seemingly kind maiden named Sara to help players fight the twisted servants formed from his own corrupted blood — when in truth it was only trying to deceive them, to keep them from knowing who their true enemy was.

And this so-called stone was very likely forged from the black ore formed from Yogg-Saron's blood, which had seeped beneath the entire continent of Northrend...

This thing had to be destroyed. Otherwise, left in the hands of this dwarf, there was no telling what disaster it might bring. But not now. If they destroyed it here, there was no telling whether Daggart would lose control — and then the entire journey would be for nothing.

"Sara," Jace repeated the name. "She is indeed a beautiful woman, Daggart."

"Of course," Daggart nodded, glancing at the stone once more. "I will never be parted from her again."

Take a good long look at it, Jace thought, glancing at him. Once we're out of here, you'll be saying goodbye to it forever.
