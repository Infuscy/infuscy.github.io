Chapter 372: Trial or Trap?!

In the pitch-black cave, strange skittering sounds were everywhere, along with the distant, muffled clinking of chisel strikes that had traveled far to reach here.

It wasn't just Dark Iron dwarves digging here — the troggs too were constantly carving out new tunnels.

Daggart had told him that many Explorers' League members believed this was actually a prison for the troggs — that these twisted, out-of-control monsters had been imprisoned here by the ancient keepers, and the arrival of the Explorers' League had given them contact with the outside world, which was why they were so actively digging tunnels outward to escape.

One important reason for this theory was that the trogg infestations in Loch Modan had all started shortly after the discovery of this excavation site.

Daggart hadn't mentioned the trogg invasion of Gnomeregan, though. Jace figured there must be several hundred kilometers from here to Gnomeregan — even digging straight toward it would take ages.

At first, listening to Daggart talk about the Explorers' League had made the waiting bearable enough. But after a while, drowsiness crept in, and the wait became far less interesting.

Jace didn't dare sleep. Whether it was the movements of the troggs outside, or that strange stone in Daggart's hands beside him, neither let him relax.

And of course, most important of all was Grede's condition. Every time he crept to the entrance of the hall and peered at the flickering shadows within, seeing Grede still sitting there motionless against a pillar, not knowing whether he was alive or dead, his heart would sink with uncertainty.

But he couldn't go in. He was afraid that entering might actually trigger some weird condition like "only a dwarf can complete this trial alone," or interfere with Grede's perception of his "ancestral bloodline," causing all his efforts to fail.

So every time he came to the door, he ended up turning back down again.

Watching his pocket watch and counting the hours, by the third evening he could no longer feel hunger or thirst. He had no appetite for food or drink either — just staring at the stone carvings around the hall's doorframe, feeling his consciousness on the verge of separating from his body through sheer drowsiness.

Daggart, off in the distance, had slept once in the middle, but his state wasn't much better. At this point he'd been clutching a dried mushroom in his hand for ages without eating it, his eyes fixed on the darkness on the other side of the cave, staring blankly without moving.

Jace couldn't help but ask, "Daggart, is this trial really something only one person can complete? No one else can take part?"

Daggart snapped back to attention and looked over. "From what I've heard, that's what the rumors say."

"Then does helping him get to this ruin count as assistance?" Jace asked.

"I should think not," Daggart replied. "After all, the Mountain Kings of old didn't dig open the temple by themselves either, never mind Muradin Bronzebeard — he certainly couldn't have traveled to Northrend alone to find the ruins buried beneath the ice and snow."

Jace nodded and asked, "Then what if he starves to death in there? He hasn't eaten or drunk anything for three days."

Daggart looked over and said, "I don't think we need to worry. I can go three days without food or water myself, never mind a Gryphon Rider. Not eating or drinking isn't about torturing yourself — it's about finding another way to communicate with this world. I think if the Wildhammer brother finds nothing at all, he'll come out on his own, rather than keep wasting time in there."

Jace nodded. "Fine. You're dwarves, you call the shots."

He plopped himself down on the steps when a rumbling sound suddenly came from all around, and he whipped his head back in alarm.

Wait — why is the door closing?!

Jace scrambled all the way up and charged at the door, but he was a step too late. The great door slammed shut with a bang, throwing him back against the outside.

He dusted off the debris that had fallen from the door's vibration, then kicked it — but the stone door was four or five meters tall and God-knew-how-heavy. His kick didn't budge it an inch.

Daggart ran over as well. "What's going on? Is this normal?" Jace asked.

He pressed himself against the door and listened carefully for any sound from within, but he couldn't hear a thing. The door was just too thick.

"I... I'm sorry, I don't know either," Daggart said, running his hands over the stone door. "It might be some kind of defense mechanism. I don't know what triggered it."

Jace's mind was a mess. He leaned against the solid stone door and ran through countless possibilities — he even considered the possibility that his own sitting down on the steps had triggered the door's closure.

Could this be part of the trial?

Why would a titan temple prepare a trial hall specifically for the dwarves who came later? Impossible.

Daggart was right — this had to be some kind of defense mechanism. Either he'd triggered it, or Grede had triggered it from inside.

Given that the idea of triggering a mechanism by sitting on the steps was absurd — he'd walked up and down those steps countless times over the past few days.

Something must have happened to Grede.

"Grede!"

"Grede! Are you all right?!"

He shouted and pounded on the stone door, but by now the entire stone chamber was thundering with a continuous rumble, as though some mechanism was in operation, completely drowning out his own voice — he couldn't even hear himself.

Jace wasn't about to just stand there and watch Grede get sealed inside. He backed up a few paces, took a running start, and hurled a fierce kick at the door. The stone door shuddered with a boom, but didn't move an inch.

He pushed against the door with all his strength until his vision started to blur — after all, he hadn't slept in days, and the drowsiness kept crashing over him, making it impossible to muster any real strength. Then the dwarf clapped him on the back and said, "Mage, mage, behind us — we've got company."

He turned to look toward the entrance and saw a group of troggs staring at them as they approached. Some were tall, nearly two meters high; others were short, practically the size of a scrawny dwarf.

Each of them was armed with various weapons — stone spears, stone chisels, stone hammers, and even Dark Iron dwarven weapons.

The thunderous rumbling of the stone chamber must have drawn them here.

Daggart steadied the bronze rod in his hand and pressed in close to Jace. "What do we do now?"

Jace yanked his cloak off and tossed it aside. He began chanting on the spot, and a brilliant crystalline armor enveloped him at once, radiating arcane light that illuminated the surroundings dazzlingly bright.

The approaching troggs hesitated, as though overwhelmed by the radiance, and none dared come forward.

He waved Skysong in his hand, releasing a Light spell to brighten the area even further, then glared at the troggs and roared, "Come on then, if you've got a death wish!"

How much of that the troggs understood was anyone's guess, but the ones in front clearly flinched back a step.

The troggs near him looked at each other, some staring straight up at the mage on high. Then a gray-haired, dark-skinned trogg stepped to the front.

He raised a long staff hung with numerous pendants and pointed it at the mage above like he was issuing an order!

"Surface-dwellers... all deserve to die!"

"You die first..."

Jace raised his hand and began chanting Life Drain. Before the old trogg could get his second sentence out, he shriveled up like a deflating sack — hair falling out, skin withering — and collapsed to the ground.

The surrounding troggs panicked at the sight. They tried to catch the old trogg but couldn't stop his rapid death at all, until he became a dried-out corpse lying stiff on the ground.

But Jace had already recovered a good deal of his spirits.

"Come on, then. All of them," he muttered under his breath.
