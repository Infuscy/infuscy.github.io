## Chapter 374: It Cannot Be Spared

Covered head to toe in the filthy blood of the troggs, so exhausted and sleepy he could barely keep his eyes open, Jace stared at the dwarf before him. Grede was like a statue carved from marble — every movement produced a grinding "gurr-gurr" sound of stone rubbing against stone.

Grede looked at himself, straightened his chest with effort, and opened his stiff nostrils to try taking a few breaths. As his chest rose and fell, his skin finally returned to its original color, and his frame gradually shrunk back down.

"That's the first time I've ever seen a dwarf undergo such a trial and gain the power of the ancestors," Daggart marveled. "Truly astonishing. Just now, you really looked like a genuine Earthen."

"I'm not used to this feeling yet..." Grede patted his stomach. "I'll need a lot more training, I think."

As the two dwarves discussed it, Jace peered curiously through the open door. He saw nothing but a mess inside — the Earthen statues from before seemed to have all shifted positions and toppled over.

He raised Skysong and walked in. At the far end of the statues, he found a toppled stone guardian.

This guardian was considerably massive, standing over two meters tall — but it was extremely wide, looking like a square box. Each stone-made arm was thicker than Jace's entire body, and its two feet were as broad as stone pedestals.

Why was this stone guardian here? When they'd first entered the hall, there had definitely been no such thing.

"This thing..." Jace looked back at Grede.

Grede said, "When the door closed, it was pitch black inside and I couldn't see anything. Then I felt the walls rotate, mechanisms rumbling everywhere, and then heavy footsteps."

He held up the empty bottle. "This Nature Protection Potion saved my life, Jace. This time I didn't forget to drink it. Several of these Earthen that stood up could cast lightning magic. Without that potion, I'd probably have been blasted unconscious right from the start and never had the strength to fight back."

Jace clapped Grede on the shoulder — and felt that his already impossibly tough shoulder was even more like a rock now, even though he no longer had that ashen-white look from before.

"Let's get out of here quickly. I haven't slept in days, and I don't intend to sleep in here either."

"You haven't slept this whole time?" Grede asked.

"How could I possibly sleep in a place like this?" Jace said helplessly.

They returned to the temple full of passages. The troggs here had mostly fled — not a single moving thing in the empty hall.

Jace figured these creatures must have some way of burrowing deep into Uldaman, otherwise how could they have fled so cleanly? It was just that the entrance hadn't been discovered by the dwarves yet. When they reached the gate leading from the Dark Iron dwarves' camp into Uldaman, they heard a hubbub of voices.

There seemed to be a lot of Dark Iron dwarves outside. They must have noticed the commotion inside as well, and probably thought the troggs were about to launch another breakout.

Jace said, "I only have one Invisibility Potion left, you two. If we split it three ways, that means the invisibility might only last twenty-odd seconds, and the effect will be weaker too. So we can only move as fast as possible and make sure we're out of here before they react."

"The good news is, running down from here is much shorter than the way in was," Daggart said. "And we know the way."

Grede whispered, "Then let's hurry. I can feel those Dark Iron dwarves coming up."

The three of them divided the potion into empty bottles, drank it in turn, and charged out the door as it took effect.

Jace could clearly see that his vision seemed sharper and the sounds around him were clearer too. When he darted past a figure, he distinctly heard the man grunt "Hmm?" and look toward him, then say something to a nearby dwarf.

"Hey, hey," the voices went on and on — clearly, when the potion's effect was fading, there must have been a faint, indistinct shimmer.

But most of the dwarves were fixated on the gate to Uldaman, and in the dim light of the fire basins, they couldn't be bothered to look closely unless you were right up close. Still, when the potion's effect wore off, Jace watched a Dark Iron dwarf staring right at him as he materialized from the darkness...

"What—!" The Dark Iron dwarf had just cried out when Jace cast Fear on the spot, cutting the dwarf's words short. The dwarf's mouth split open in a scream and he fled down a side passage. By now Grede and Daggart were also becoming visible.

"Let's go... don't look back!" Jace said.

The three of them immediately ran toward the crevice they'd used before, moved the stones aside, squeezed into the gap, and charged into the dark tunnel. They climbed the ladder up through the concealed trench and out.

Outside, it was still dark. Jace looked out over the moonlit wasteland, and a wave of drowsiness hit him so hard he could barely open his eyes.

But it was still dangerous. The dwarves inside could discover this passage at any time — and unlike last time, they hadn't bothered to cover it up when leaving.

They detoured northeast for over an hour. After their last encounter with the ambush by earth elements, they didn't dare rest among the jumbled rocks anymore, and eventually found the shadow beneath a broken cliff to catch their breath.

Daggart sat on one side; Grede and Jace sat on the other.

Grede glanced at Jace, and Jace knew — if they didn't deal with that stone, he'd never be able to sleep.

Especially with that stone right beside him, he kept having the feeling that if he fell asleep and woke up, he wouldn't be himself anymore.

Jace looked at Daggart. "I'd like to take another look at that stone, Daggart."

"You've already seen it," Daggart said. "I understand how you feel, but right now I'd rather not let her show herself again. I finally got my hands on her — I just want to bring her safely back to Thelsamar, back to Kharanos..."

"We came out of there in such a mess. What if she falls out along the way?" Jace asked.

Daggart froze for a moment and reached into his pocket. Jace moved with lightning speed, stepping forward and seizing his arm. Daggart started, then realized what was happening and clawed at Jace like a madman — but Jace twisted both his arms behind his back and pinned him.

"Bandit! Brigand! I never should have trusted mercenaries — you pretend to be innocent as lambs, but your hearts are darker than Blackrock Mountain, you thieves! I curse you!"

Despite Daggart's cursing and struggling, Jace's strength was absurdly great. Even after days without sleep, this burst of force was no match for an amateur fighter like Daggart.

Grede reacted quickly too — while the two of them struggled, he pulled the stone from Daggart's pocket.

"What the hell is this thing? Why does it smell like shadow? Daggart, aren't you going to come clean?"

"Smash her!" Jace shouted. "Smash her to pieces!"

"Smash?" Grede paused. "Just smash her like that?"

Jace shouted, "If we wait any longer... we might all be influenced by her, Grede. This is a corrupted fel artifact!"

"Then you're paying him back?"

"I'll pay!"

"Fine, then." Grede nodded, tossed the stone onto the ground beside them, and Daggart let out a roar when he saw it. "What has she done to deserve you destroying her like this?"

Grede paid him no heed. He drew back his hammer and struck — but to his surprise, when it came down, the black stone cracked the hard ground beneath it.

He looked at his own hammer to make sure nothing was wrong with it, then glanced at Jace. "This thing's pretty tough."

Saronite, congealed from the blood of an Old God, had been used by many factions afterward to forge weapons and armor — of course it was an exceedingly hard material.

Jace said, "Hit it as hard as you can, Grede. Try again. This thing can definitely be smashed!"

"No!" Daggart reached out and roared. Jace hauled him aside.

Grede set the stone on another stone slab. Jace watched as his body slowly swelled with flickers of lightning, his skin gradually turning gray, as though some kind of rapid albinism was spreading, until every inch of his skin had transformed — even his hair turned gray.

"Let's see how you handle this, little thing."

Grede's voice had become the grinding of hard stone against stone. He raised his hammer, unleashed a violent flash of lightning, and bellowed as he smashed it down onto the stone. A muffled thunderclap shattered the slab outright, the electric current spreading along the ground in all directions and illuminating the whole shadowed area. Jace blinked. And Daggart, pinned in his grip, had already screamed himself hoarse and was coughing violently.

After the lightning faded, Grede leaned over — the stone had become a scatter of black shards and powder, scattering in the breeze. The shadow power within it seemed to have been annihilated by his lightning. Not a trace of it could be felt anymore.

Only now did Jace finally feel as though he'd been released from a crushing weight on his spirit. The night before his eyes seemed to have grown quiet.

Daggart, still pinned by him, had gone limp too, shaking his head and murmuring, "I don't know why you did this..."

"It's over, Daggart," Grede said, walking over. "You should be clear-headed again."

Daggart looked up at the gray-white dwarf, who stood nearly six feet tall, his eyes filled with indescribable pain and fear. Jace set him on the ground, and he just slowly curled up, trembling without stop.
