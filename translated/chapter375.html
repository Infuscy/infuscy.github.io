Chapter 375: Like Waking from a Dream

Along the way, Daggart grew much quieter. He no longer said anything about that dark stone, but at least he was willing to properly guide them again.

Jace and Grede didn't say much either — they just kept watch over him the whole way, and slept in shifts, guarding against him losing his mind again.

After returning to Loch Modan, it seemed the lush trees and the moist lake breeze that blew over from time to time refreshed the spirits considerably. He finally became a bit more lively, occasionally saying something to point them in the right direction.

When they reached Thelsamar it was just past noon. From far away, the aroma of roasting meat and bread drifting through the whole town made Jace's stomach growl.

He looked at Daggart and Grede — they seemed to be feeling the hunger a bit too. After all, the food had basically run out the night they'd left Uldaman. The three of them had eaten next to nothing the whole journey back, until yesterday when they'd finally scrounged up another piece of bread from the pack, divided it three ways, and used it to tide themselves over a little.

At the inn, they didn't think about anything complicated like roasted meat. They ordered a few cuts of bread and cheese first to stave off the hunger. After several plates, Grede leaned back in his chair, eyes half-closed and head tilted to one side, looking like a man finally brought back to life.

"I've thought it through," Daggart said suddenly. "As long as I relied on that thing, I was never truly himself."

He speared a piece of Thelsamar cheese with the rind still on, stuffed it into a bread roll, and shoved the whole thing into his mouth. "I'm going to start a new life. An explorer can't always dwell on past great adventures. I have to look ahead."

"I'll compensate you, Daggart — name your price!" Jace said. "After all, it was a cultural relic."

"Compensate? I don't know how much she was worth," Daggart shook his head. "I never intended to sell her, and I never asked anyone else her value either."

Jace took the elemental stone, still wrapped in its glove, out of his pack and set it on the table. "Take this, Daggart. I know Elemental Earth is a quite valuable material too. It should make up for some of it, at least — after all, we smashed something you treasured. And you were a damn good guide. We couldn't have you running around for free either."

He looked at his glove and said, "Is it true, mage — that thing really was an evil object that could influence people's minds?"

Jace said quietly, "Think about who you were before you met her, then think about who you were after, and then think about who you are now, Daggart. You know I know a thing or two about shadow magic myself — I understand better than anyone how these things work. I think you actually understood it yourself long ago. You just didn't want to face it."

At that, Daggart looked toward the bar and called out, "I'll have a mug of Thunderbrew, barkeep. From Kharanos."

"I'll have one too," Grede raised his hand.

A moment later, the barkeep set down two mugs and glanced at Jace. Jace shook his head. "Thanks. I'll pass for now."

Daggart took a long drink and slammed his mug on the table. "I hate to admit it, but after that hammer blow from the Wildhammer brother, I felt... freed."

"Don't mention it," Grede said, raising his mug and waggling his beer-foam-dusted mustache.

"As for compensation — forget it, mage," Daggart said, shoving the glove into his leather coat pocket and setting the now-cooled elemental stone on the table. "I can't be bothered to find an alchemist to refine this thing anyway."

"You're right, mage. I could feel the evil hidden inside that thing, even after it was left behind in that temple. I just couldn't forget it."

"Think about the past me — even if I came back empty-handed from those ruins, I'd feel satisfied and happy from the joy of adventure and the knowledge I'd gained. When I think about it that way, that stone's influence on me was truly a terrible calamity."

"Tomorrow I need to get back to the Iron Ring dig as soon as possible. The brothers are still waiting for me."

Daggart drained his mug, looked at the empty glass, and said, "This was truly an unforgettable experience, you two. And you really are good people. I'm sorry for what I said earlier — I must have been out of my mind."

"Just a damned rock. Hell, maybe I need to find a woman."

With that, he tipped his hat to the two of them, took a bread roll, and left the inn.

"How do you know so much?" Grede said. "About that stone, and the woman on it."

"The library, right?" he cut in before Jace could answer.

"What else?" Jace said. "As a mage, am I not supposed to occasionally explain problems encountered on the road? Otherwise what was the point of me spending all that time in the Wizard Sanctum?"

Grede asked, "Then tell me — who was the woman on it?"

"A goddess named Sara," Jace said. "She could bewitch people's hearts and drive them mad. She might have something to do with certain shadow creatures — you know those shadow creatures? The monsters supposedly buried beneath the Amani Empire and Tirisfal."

Everything connected to the Old Gods would ultimately lead to madness, confusion, slaughter, and bloodshed — just like the dark weapon the witch Modgud had used to destroy Grim Batol during the War of the Three Hammers.

If they'd let this dwarf take that stone back to Kharanos, or even Ironforge, God knew what even worse trouble it might have caused.

After all, if Jace and Grede hadn't shown up at the Iron Ring excavation site, this old explorer would have been parted from that thing forever.

"I've heard the legends about those monsters too," Grede nodded. "No wonder he went crazy the moment he picked that stone up in the cave. It made my skin crawl just watching him. We really did save him once."

"I hope he truly feels that way," Jace said. "But let's talk about you. What exactly did you experience in that hall? I've been worried about that dwarf this whole journey — I've been dying to know."

Grede nodded. "For the first stretch, I didn't feel anything at all... Later, after passing out from hunger a few times and waking back up, I found I wasn't as sleepy or as hungry anymore."

"But there was something permeating that hall... or something buried in the earth, that could replenish my energy and lift my spirits again. It was like I'd never need to eat or drink anything ever again."

"Then why did you still want a bit to eat and drink?" Jace asked, pointing at the mug in his hand.

"It's different now than it was then!" Grede took a drink and continued. "After I realized my skin, hair, and eyes were all somewhat off, the door suddenly closed. Then all those statues came alive. I knew why — they were jealous of me. Jealous that I could reclaim the pride that had belonged to them! Haha! And then you know the rest — I stood up and took them down one by one, then smashed the huge stone statue that tried to ambush me, and completely conquered that stone hall."

Grede made it sound easy, but Jace knew it was anything but — especially when he thought about the wounds covering his body.

Given that the blow from that tall trogg swinging a hammer down on Grede's head hadn't left a single mark, God only knew what kind of battle had left those scars on his stone skin.

The dwarf murmured, "Everyone says Muradin's avatar was truly godlike on the battlefield, but I'm still far from that. I don't believe this god was about the same height as a human. So this is just the beginning. Just you wait — maybe one day I'll reach Muradin Bronzebeard's level. At the very least, I'm the first Wildhammer I know of who's been able to awaken this kind of power — even if my own kin aren't interested in the ancient creations or tracing their own bloodline."

Jace looked at him and said, "According to what those explorers said, even among the Bronzebeard, it's been a very long time since anyone's found this power."

Grede nodded. "Because it really isn't easy. Finding a temple buried this deep, breaking through the blockade of those monsters... I honestly don't know why you thought of coming here. Without you, I don't know how many lifetimes I'd have had to search for a chance like this."

"Even putting all that aside, the moment those statues in the stone hall rose to their feet before me, I thought it was all over. Because my body was stiff — like it had turned to stone... No, it had turned to stone. I could barely even stand up. Then I heard you shouting my name, and I thought — if I let those stiff-legged statues beat me, wouldn't you laugh at me? Damn it... how could I ever just give up like that."

Jace looked at the Bronzebeard dwarves gathering in groups to drink and chat and said, "Not only would I laugh at you — I'd carry you back to Aerie Peak and tell everyone exactly how you got wrecked by a bunch of stone men. Make you famous."

Grede laughed and reached over to clap Jace hard on the shoulder.
