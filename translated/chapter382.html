Chapter 382: The Specialty Materials Merchant

Despite having asked for the quietest room available, Jace still kept hearing all manner of shouting from the street throughout the night.

He hadn't slept well at all. Come morning, Jace threw on a thin shirt and hurried toward the residence of the Mister Cooper the innkeeper had mentioned.

Sure enough, just as the innkeeper had said, Cooper's home was noticeably more handsome than the other houses at a glance.

A servant of the Cooper household answered the door. After a brief exchange, Jace learned that the master of the house had gone to Stormwind to visit relatives.

Still, Jace wasn't particularly keen on dealing with these people anyway — as long as he delivered the documents smoothly, that would do. What truly intrigued him was Mosh'aru.

Did Mosh'aru have some connection to the warlocks here?

According to Ursula, Mosh'aru was obsessed with summoning a powerful demon to serve him — he even wanted to enter the Twisting Nether of his own accord to seek out a demon to enslave…

The two warlocks he'd happened upon last night had both been accompanied by imps, which was enough to suggest the warlocks around Moonbrook were fairly adept at summoning demons. Who knew whether that had anything to do with Mosh'aru.

So how should he make contact with the warlocks here?

Ah, right — Erland had mentioned before that there were people here who dealt in demon-hide goods. If they bought or sold demon organs, hides, horns, and the like, then even if they weren't warlocks themselves, they ought to have some connections to the local warlocks. They might even have ties to Mosh'aru personally.

If, as Ursula had said, Mosh'aru was wholly consumed by demonology, then how did he make a living? Did he wander around as a mercenary, same as Jace?

In any case, he might as well start by asking around the local alchemy shop or herbalist's.

Jace asked Cooper's servant for the location of Moonbrook's local herbalist's. After twisting his way through a maze of alleys, he found the place in a dusty, weed-choked back lane. The moment he stepped inside, he caught the scent of Silverleaf.

"What are you after?"

The shopkeeper was an elderly man, legs crossed, sitting in a chair against the back wall and watching the door. He turned his head, gave Jace a once-over, and said, "You're a mage from Stormwind? What, not enough herbs over your way?"

Jace glanced at the shelves behind him. The very row of racks beside the man's head already displayed several bottles of the standard Shadow Protection Potion formula.

"I need some specialty materials, sir," he said offhandedly. "Related to demons."

The shopkeeper licked his teeth. "You've come to the wrong place. This is an herbalist's, not a demon slaughterhouse. I don't know anything about that sort of thing."

Jace said, "I can assure you the Wizard Sanctum doesn't go after scavengers. The orcs summoned demons in such staggering numbers that filthy demon corpses are polluting land all across the kingdom. Mages are only too happy to let the hunters who collect those corpses put them to reuse."

The shopkeeper looked him up and down, then paused a moment. "Then what do you need?"

"Succubus wings," Jace said.

"You mean a succubus's wings? Sorry, I don't have anything like that." The shopkeeper waved a hand.

He'd even said "succubus" outright, and used the word "wings" — the very term demons themselves favored when describing their own wings. And yet he claimed not to know anything about it. Was he really taking this mage for a fool?

Jace set a silver coin on the table. "I know demon organs are sold here, sir. I'm collecting demon organs to experiment with a new anti-demon concoction. You know there are still stray demons near the Dark Portal causing no end of trouble for the Expedition's logistics."

The shopkeeper glanced at the silver coin. "I really don't have anything like that either. I'd love to do my part for the kingdom, but I'm not a mage — I can't conjure things out of thin air for you."

Jace produced another silver coin. The shopkeeper drummed his fingers against his knee, glanced at Jace, and said, "Honestly? I don't have them."

"But if you head out from here and follow this alley to the end, take a right, you'll see a standalone house on your right with its door always shut. It's a pre-war house — wasn't burned. There's a broken wheelbarrow out front. The people there sell what you're looking for."

"Much obliged."

Following the directions the shopkeeper had given, Jace felt he'd pushed deep into the most remote, out-of-the-way corner of Moonbrook.

Dilapidated wooden houses everywhere, coated in dust. It was true that most of the houses here hadn't been damaged by the war, but they didn't look much better for it — shabby and decrepit.

By the roadside there was the rotting carcass of some animal — couldn't tell if it was a dog or a wild pig — dried out to the point where it didn't smell anymore.

At a corner, he spotted the wheelbarrow without wheels that the shopkeeper had described. Before him stood a standalone two-story house. It truly hadn't been burned, but its roof had a hole torn through it.

Jace walked up to the door, tucked his ring into his pocket, and knocked. "I have something to sell."

The door cracked open, and a young woman peered out through the gap. "Who are you? I've never seen you."

"Let me see what you've got," she said.

Jace drew the dragon-fang dagger from his belt. "This is a demon's fang, isn't it?"

"It's already been forged into a blade…"

The woman sounded somewhat impatient, but she gave the fang a longer look, and her expression changed at once. Jace got the sense she could tell this was a dragon's fang.

"You selling this?"

"Yes. But I'd like to know what kind of demon this fang came from," Jace asked.

"A felhound's fang. Obviously. Only a felhound grows fangs this long." The woman pressed, "How much are you asking?"

"At least eighty silver," Jace said.

"Steep." The woman stared hard at the dagger, testing him. "Sixty. Corpses of that sort of demon are all over the swamp."

According to what Erland had said, demon parts like that sold for a few silver at most. Felhounds had densely packed teeth — even if this was an oversized specimen, there was no way it was worth sixty.

She clearly thought she could snap up a dragon fang at a bargain price, and was worried Jace might catch on, so she played it cool.

"No deal," Jace said. "You're too young. I need a properly seasoned demonologist to appraise this for me. Only if he tells me it's a felhound's fang will I believe it and sell. Are there others here? I don't think this looks much like a felhound's fang. I know felhounds… their teeth are fine and densely packed."

With that, he turned to leave. The woman opened the door and leaned out. "I can see you've got a cautious head on your shoulders. Since you don't trust me, I'll find someone who'll definitely convince you, and we'll do the business then."

"How do we do this?"

"Come back here at dusk tomorrow," the woman said. "Bring your dagger, and I'll have him come explain things to you."

Jace asked, "Here? I'd rather meet at the inn."

The woman said, "He won't go there. Don't even ask me why."
