## Chapter 383: The Demonologist

The next day, Jace set out as the sky began to redden, arriving at the alley. He focused his senses and swept the surroundings, making sure no demons lay in wait to ambush him.

Coming to a place like this at this hour was undoubtedly dangerous. But if he wanted to deal with a circle of warlocks — especially someone like Mosh'aru, a demonologist who couldn't show his face in the light of day — taking risks was unavoidable.

All he could do was be as careful as possible.

He found the house from before and approached. "I'm here."

But there was no response.

Jace glanced down the two side paths. This wasn't some back-alley double-cross, was it?

He had no desire to cause a bloodbath in Moonbrook, but he gripped his dagger tight, ready to cast Fear at a moment's notice.

The sky had grown late. It wasn't fully dark yet, but the streets on both sides had dimmed past the point of seeing clearly.

The crooked shadows of the lopsided houses reminded Jace of the time he'd chased that male succubus into the filthiest, most wretched heap of garbage in Old Town.

That unidentifiable animal carcass, in the darkening dusk, had looked as though it might rise up and bite him at any moment. He was about to leave — clearly, the other side had asked him to come in the afternoon but, for whatever reason, hadn't shown.

Probably the "demonologist" the woman had in mind had decided this meeting wasn't appropriate and had changed his mind at the last minute, standing Jace up. Or perhaps there was some other, less friendly intent at play.

Jace didn't want to wait any longer. He turned back the way he'd come, when suddenly something felt off.

He focused his senses and looked back and forth. Behind him, a low, squat shadow was slowly closing in.

He pulled his awareness out of his demon-detection sight and looked with his naked eyes. He saw a pitch-black thing crawling along the ground toward him.

Its footfalls were careful as it moved, but its body swayed with each step. As it advanced, it made a "khek-khek, khek-khek" sound, like coarse teeth grinding together — or perhaps a throat being choked.

When it was roughly five or six meters from Jace, it stopped. It hissed — some manner of threat — then opened its blood-red maw.

The creature had no eyes. Its mouth bristled with densely packed fangs. Black whiskers covered its head and jaw. Two backward-curving bone horns jutted from its shoulders, and its body was covered in long, black, hairy tentacles, their tips studded with needle-like spines pointed this way.

The skin hidden beneath the fur and whiskers showed a dark, blackish-red in the moonlight. With every step forward, its legs came into view — short, thick, crimson, alien-looking stumps of flesh, gripping the ground with a single massive, thick claw.

This was a felhound.

Even though Jace had never encountered this demon in this world, it looked enough like its game counterpart.

Despite the name, it bore absolutely no resemblance to a dog. It looked more like a mutant, oversized rat covered in spines and tentacles.

The demon didn't attack immediately. It was sizing Jace up — gauging his strength, perhaps, or going through some pre-assault ritual.

Felhounds were demon servants bred by the pit lords, meant to help those bloated, mighty lords track down sources of arcane energy across the universe to savor their near-insatiable hunger.

As such, this breed of demon was extraordinarily adept at tracking and hunting spellcasters. If it decided on an assault, and Jace tried to fight it with spells, he'd be asking for trouble.

And it wasn't just casters like Jace — even Eredar warlocks feared their spell-countering abilities.

For the warlocks here to keep a creature like this in town to deal with any mages who might come investigating — it was a perfect fit.

These monsters would devour the mana of anyone with magical talent, gorging themselves until they'd drained it completely, eating their victim into unconsciousness and memory loss. In severe cases, it could even cause permanent dementia and numbness.

But Jace was more than just a spellcaster. He took a step back, gripping the dagger's hilt. The felhound stepped forward. Its back tentacles stretched longer and longer, as though unwilling to let him leave.

"Whoever released this demon — you can show yourself now."

He called out to the surroundings, but no one answered.

"If that's how it is, don't blame me for what comes next." His voice dropped lower.

Still no response.

Jace watched the felhound, watching the tentacles raised high and swaying on its back. Suddenly, he reached out and, with his Fel Grasp, tore several of them clean off. The felhound, caught completely off guard, let out a shriek and was yanked half a meter toward Jace.

Its four thick legs scrambled to right itself, and it charged straight at Jace, maw gaping wide to tear at him. Jace sidestepped easily. The hound fired one last tentacle at him, which he caught by hand.

The spines at the tentacle's tip suddenly unfurled, like a fleshy flower opening to reveal the sucker within. Jace felt his very spirit tugged, as though hooked by something.

"Stop…"

Jace had already drawn the dagger. A man by the door shouted.

"No need to kill it," the man said. "Leave, Kirinm."

At the command, the felhound immediately abandoned its attack and scrambled to the side. Jace let go and released it into the shadows. Had that man appeared a second later, this last tentacle would have been severed by Jace's blade.

Jace glanced at the wound the felhound's barbed tentacle had carved into his hand. "The deal's off, sir. I understand your intentions — you wanted me to see what a real felhound looks like, to demonstrate your knowledge and capabilities. But I don't care for this method."

"You didn't come here for a deal either, did you," the distant figure said.

Jace looked over. "Who are you?"

The man shuffled toward him. Given how unsteady his gait and frame were, you could easily mistake him for a zombie.

He wore a threadbare white shirt and loose linen gray trousers that did nothing to conceal his withered form.

Up close, Jace noticed his sunken cheeks, protruding cheekbones, and a face and mouth covered in unkempt stubble.

The man swept aside the long hair covering one eye and fixed Jace with a hard stare. "Call me Mosh'aru. I daresay you won't find a single person in all of Moonbrook — nay, in all the Kingdom of Stormwind — more qualified than I to call myself a demonologist."

Jace said, "So that felhound from a moment ago — you summoned it."

The man nodded. "Without question. I am the only summoner in this town who can tame a felhound. And believe me — a felhound is not the most powerful creature I can summon."

Jace very much wanted to ask what the most powerful creature he could summon was, but figured the man wouldn't be willing to reveal the truth, so he kept his mouth shut.

"Could you teach me?" he asked. "To summon this kind of demon."

"Of course," the man said. "But not everyone can master this summoning magic. And I can see you're already well down this path. That ability you used against my felhound earlier — I've never seen anything like it."

"Since you know this path, you should know everything comes at a price. I can teach you to summon demons, but you'll need to give something in return."

Hearing the word "path," Jace couldn't help but think of Jagin and his obsession with the path of the dark arts. These two really were cut from the same cloth.

"Of course, I never intended to take anything from you for free, Mosh'aru," Jace said. "It's just that the spell from earlier — I'm afraid I can't offer that in exchange."

"I understand," Mosh'aru said. "But I'm certain you have something that could help me. And right now, I'm in desperate need of a capable collaborator."
