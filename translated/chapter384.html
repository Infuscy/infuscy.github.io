Chapter 384: The Hillside Cabin

An alley frequented by vagrants and refugees was hardly a suitable place to casually discuss demons or shadow magic, so Jace accepted Mosh'aru's invitation and headed for his nearby dwelling.

Mosh'aru didn't live in Moonbrook proper. He lived in a cabin on the hill behind Moonbrook.

That night, Jace followed this strange yet oddly familiar man — Mosh'aru — as they walked. Mosh'aru said in a low voice, "I could tell the felhound was wary of your power. I've never seen it so cautious in the face of a warlock or mage."

"If you don't mind, tell me about yourself. I should know, or at least have heard of, most of the warlocks in the Kingdom of Stormwind — or anyone connected to demons. But it seems I've neither seen you nor heard of anyone capable of hunting demons the way you do."

"You know Jagin, don't you?" Jace said.

At the mention of that name, Mosh'aru turned around.

"Jace Sesso — so you're Jace Sesso."

"So he's mentioned me," Jace said.

"Hah." Mosh'aru gave a hoarse laugh. "Jagin told me a few things about Stormwind and mentioned you as well. But you're far younger than I'd imagined."

"Regarding what?"

"He said the Slaughtered Lamb tavern was doing well, and that they'd found someone in you. He said you were, without question, an exceptionally gifted fellow. Let me guess — you've come to persuade me to come back? So Galders is dead too?"

"No," Jace said. "I'm genuinely interested in demons."

Mosh'aru nodded and continued climbing. "I have no interest in returning to Stormwind, but that has nothing to do with the people at the Slaughtered Lamb. It's because… what I intend to accomplish can't be done in Stormwind."

Jace nodded. The night forest was still somewhat chilly. Mosh'aru's frame was gaunt and looked exceedingly frail. When the cold wind blew, he hugged his arms tight, like some poor soul nursing a cold or running a fever.

"Why didn't you teach Jagin the incantation for summoning felhounds?" Jace asked.

Mosh'aru looked back at Jace, his expression noticeably colder.

"Look at the end he met — that reckless bastard who only cared for the thrill of the moment. I'll grant he had more talent than I do at learning shadow magic. But commanding shadow and commanding demons are two entirely different things. Not to mention, the ideas that popped into his head, or the mistakes he made, were simply beyond all reason. The way things turned out for him was well within my expectations."

"Felhounds can't hide themselves the way imps or succubi can. They're nothing but beasts — they won't help you think. For a beginner at demon summoning, they're extraordinarily difficult to control."

"Take an example: if you told a succubus to publicly kill a priest of the Church, she'd wonder if you'd lost your mind, and try to talk you out of it. But if you sent a felhound into Stormwind Keep to assassinate that little imp, it would charge straight through the front gate without a moment's hesitation."

"Although, Jagin was killed by his own succubus," Jace said.

"Don't put all the blame on the demons. Jealousy can drive a succubus mad. Chaotic emotions make them forget everything." Mosh'aru leaned against a tree. "I take it you already have a companion of your own, don't you? Then don't summon a second one."

The two of them passed through the woods, following the path upward, until they reached a cabin in a valley. A light still glowed in the window.

A succubus sat waiting at the door. She watched Jace with a smile and turned her head to say something to the approaching Mosh'aru.

Mosh'aru nodded and gestured for the succubus to leave. She turned and walked off toward a darker direction, but kept her eyes on Jace the whole way, right up until she disappeared around the corner.

"What did she say?" Jace asked.

Mosh'aru replied, "Dafren merely feels concerned. She thinks you… quite dangerous."

"And you're not concerned?" Jace asked.

Mosh'aru leaned against the door. "I am a man who deals with the Twisting Nether. In the face of such an unknown world, those who hold back out of fear will never go far."

Jace followed him inside. Though from the outside the cabin had looked ramshackle — converted from some hunter's or woodcutter's shack, even more dilapidated than the woodcutters' cabins in the Redridge Mountains — the interior was surprisingly tidy and clean. He swept his gaze across the space and noticed a bookshelf in the inner room off the hallway, lined with no small number of books and stacked papers. If these were old books collected from orcs, they were in remarkably good condition.

"Have a seat wherever you like," Mosh'aru said, gesturing to a few stools nearby.

Jace sat and cut straight to the point. "You said you needed a collaborator. What can I help you with?"

Mosh'aru sat to the side. "I've been searching for more powerful demons, Jace. Under the command of the orc warlocks, the demons that attacked the Alliance forces came in many varieties. Beyond the most numerous imps and felhounds, there were even powerful demonic warriors and winged demons that could fly."

"Ever since before the Dark Portal opened, I've been exploring ways to communicate with the demons of the Twisting Nether. The coming of the orcs should have accelerated my progress, but the Kingdom of Stormwind fell too quickly. During the war, most of my energy went into evading orc pursuit — I wasted far too much time."

"But I firmly believe humanity is no less capable than the orcs. We can certainly summon demons more powerful than these, to aid us in exploring and understanding the Twisting Nether."

Jace said, "The Dark Portal drew this world closer to the Twisting Nether. I've heard others say the same — that summoning demons has become easier."

"I don't see it that way," Mosh'aru said. "Too many fools describe the Twisting Nether and its demons with unwarranted confidence. If you ask me, rather than our world drawing closer to the Twisting Nether, it would be more accurate to say we've mastered more precise methods of making contact with it. Before the Dark Portal opened, the language of demons was an absolute forbidden art, locked away in the deepest vaults of the Dalaran library. Outsiders could glean only scattered fragments."

"But now it's different. Through orc records, we can learn far more about how to communicate with demons… There must be a reason these savage creatures are favored by demons more than we are."

"You speak demonic?" Jace asked.

"Some. Certainly not as well as you." Mosh'aru cradled a cup of water. "Otherwise, I should have spotted the problem in that incantation before you did."

"You mean the incantation for summoning a succubus," Jace said.

Mosh'aru nodded. "Exactly. That I managed to successfully summon Dafren — I have you to thank for that."
