## Chapter 385: Lesser Demons and Greater Demons

"Did Jagin get that incantation from you?" Jace pressed.

"No," Mosh'aru said. "Jagin learned the incantation from Galders. I was aware that summoning a succubus required a special token to catch their attention, but I never managed to obtain the precise method for summoning this particular demon. It was Jagin who told me the incantation."

"That's hardly surprising. Many orc warlocks still linger north of the Redridge Mountains, or deep in the mountains of Khaz Modan. As far as I know, the Dark Iron warlocks of Shadowforge City have also mastered the means of summoning succubi — perhaps they got it from the Blackrock orcs contesting territory with them, or even earlier. Who can say? Galders had any number of channels through which he could have obtained this incantation."

Jace nodded. "I only had a feeling that something in that incantation wasn't quite right. My command of the demonic tongue is far from proficient."

Mosh'aru said, "But I am not satisfied with lesser demons like felhounds. Even succubi fall far short of what I desire."

"Then what do you want to summon?" Jace asked.

"At present, I have mastery of a certain summoning incantation," Mosh'aru said. "But the materials required to summon this demon are no simple soulstone. I need a large quantity of spell components, and a sufficient mana medium to establish a connection with it."

"Are you certain you can control whatever you summon?" Jace asked.

"It is indeed extremely dangerous," Mosh'aru admitted. "I certainly cannot control it on my own. Summoning such a demon requires extensive preparation — enslavement magic, binding magic, a ritual circle to temporarily weaken the demon, incantations… I could never accomplish this alone. These workings require an exceptionally gifted shadow caster to help me complete them. I have been searching for such a person all along."

"I had originally believed Jagin could help me accomplish this. But clearly, he had far too many problems, and his end came as no surprise. After that, I intended to find a talented warlock here in Moonbrook, but I found no suitable candidate."

"So you're the leader of the warlocks here in Moonbrook?" Jace asked.

"Leader? No. After realizing they were of no help to me, I cut off contact with them," Mosh'aru said.

"I have no desire to call these people warlocks at all. They're nothing but bandits who can chant a few spells. Their talents… I can't be bothered to probe. After all, they summon demons and wield shadow power only for theft, murder, or the simple satisfaction of domination and lust."

"If it's purely about surviving, I'd much rather work with the people who deal in demon goods. At the very least, those folk only want to make a profit. They won't harm others — or themselves — over some deranged notion, and they won't drag me down with them."

"So, in the end — are you willing to join this plan?"

"I need time to consider it," Jace said.

"Of course, time is no issue," Mosh'aru said. "I know I'm not a patient man, Jace. But I also know great plans require patience to fulfill. I can wait for that. And once you agree, I will certainly give you a reward that satisfies you. For instance, I can teach you the method for summoning felhounds right now, Jace Sesso — as a gesture of my sincerity."

With that, Mosh'aru stood and selected a pale blue cloth bag from a nearby shelf. "Come with me."

Jace followed him to the backyard. The succubus was sitting there, waiting for him.

"Master," the succubus said softly. "Will you be needing me tonight as well?"

"Of course, Dafren — so you may better serve and protect me," Mosh'aru said. "But for now, allow me to discuss incantations with our guest."

The succubus gave Mosh'aru a reluctant look, then fixed her gaze on Jace. "Respect my master, warlock."

"Summoning a felhound requires not only a soulstone, but a soulstone infused with arcane energy." Mosh'aru took out a purple soulstone glowing with a faint blue light and set it on the ground.

"Listen to my incantation, Jace Sesso."

With that, he raised his hand and traced several circles in the air, chanting in a loud voice: "Moanan, Suojia, Tuluojiduosi, Luokexieni."

As he finished, Jace watched the soulstone gradually dim. The backyard, which had little light to begin with, grew even more sinister. Even the lamplight from the window behind them seemed to lose its warmth. A black rift appeared in the air before them, as though space itself had been slashed open, oozing black, viscous blood.

From that blood, a massive, swaying red rat-creature crawled forth, drooling purple saliva, barking "huh-huh, huh-huh" at Mosh'aru.

Jace gripped his dagger tight, ready at any moment to stop the felhound from attacking Mosh'aru — especially since Mosh'aru looked utterly drained after completing the summoning incantation. He could barely stand. Before the felhound, he looked as though he'd already resigned himself to being devoured.

But the felhound showed no intention of attacking him at all. Instead, it came to Mosh'aru's side, circling him excitedly.

"Once a felhound is summoned, it becomes completely obedient to its summoner at once? How is that possible?" Jace said in surprise.

"We each have our own gifts, Jace Sesso."

Mosh'aru spoke with a touch of pride, though his words came with labored, strained breathing. This summoning magic had clearly taxed him to his limits.

"Now it's your turn." He passed over a soulstone of roughly the same color and sheen from the bag. "Try it, Jace Sesso. Let me see how much of what Jagin said was true."

Jace took Mosh'aru's soulstone and mimicked his stance, moving to a distance. The felhound he'd just summoned immediately ran over in excitement. Mosh'aru gave a soft command, and it obediently circled back — truly like a well-trained dog. The strength of this man's control over demons was terrifyingly potent, entirely at odds with his frail physique.

Clearly, a felhound summoned by him wouldn't be nearly so friendly.

Mosh'aru looked at Jace. "You already have experience summoning a succubus. You should know how to feel the connection established with the Twisting Nether as you chant the incantation — to feel the presence stirred within that void."

"Repeat after me the incantation: Moanan, Suojia, Tuluojiduosi, Luokexieni."

Jace listened, then raised his hand and chanted in imitation of Mosh'aru. He felt this spell was extraordinarily taxing to cast — far more draining than summoning a succubus. By the time the rift tore open, Jace even felt as though his strength might not be enough to sustain the passage.

But he didn't need to sustain it any longer. The hound had already poked its head through.
