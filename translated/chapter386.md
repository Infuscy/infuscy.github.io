## Chapter 386: A Dangerous Enterprise

A massive, ferocious beast tore its way out of the rift. The moment it laid eyes on Jace standing before it, it let out a savage roar and lunged. Jace could feel its strength — the creature was built like an overfed breeding boar.

"What a… magnificent felhound, Jace Sesso!" Mosh'aru exclaimed loudly, stepping closer in high spirits. The felhound beside him growled nervously.

In the firelight of the courtyard brazier, the demon looked even more terrifying. Jace could see the lumps of red, scale-like protrusions hiding beneath its black fur — like scales, or like freshly cut flesh.

The moment it broke free of the rift's binding, it charged straight at Jace. Its jaws split open to reveal layer upon layer of jagged, inside-out teeth, a purple tongue slick with mucus whipping between them.

Jace backed up two steps, but the beast was terrifyingly fast. It closed the distance in a single bound, driving the two horn-like spikes on its shoulders straight at his chest — Jace caught them with both hands.

The force of the impact drove Jace backward a good distance. No wonder Mosh'aru had marveled like that — this thing was far more savage and violent than the felhound he'd encountered in the alley.

The felhound thrashed on the ground, realizing Jace's strength was no small matter and that it couldn't break free so easily. Then it whipped the tendrils from its back upward, reaching for Jace's neck.

Jace jerked his head up, dodging the first tendril's coil — but the second sank its fangs into his shoulder. He drove a hard kick into the felhound's skull, sending it reeling, then seized one of the horns on its shoulder with Gorefiend's Grasp and wrenched it backward with all his might.

Between the force of the pull and the felhound's staggered momentum, the entire upper half of the creature's head was torn clean off, along with one of its arms.

Fel blood sprayed in every direction, giving off a sickeningly foul stench. And yet the felhound still didn't die. The tendril-like hairs on the severed half of its head still twitched on the ground, and the tongue extending from the jaw still attached to the body still swayed. The whole body righted itself and charged at Jace once more.

Demons were hard to kill without destroying their heart — especially monsters like felhounds. But it was clearly gravely wounded now; it couldn't even charge in a straight line. Jace drew his dagger, severed the last tendril still trying to reach him, and drove the blade into its side again and again before the thrashing finally stopped.

After putting the monster down, Jace leaned over with his hands on his knees, catching his breath. Mosh'aru's newly summoned felhound approached the carcass and began tearing into its flesh. Only by comparison did Jace realize just how absurdly massive the felhound he'd summoned had been.

"It's dead," Jace said. "I wasted one of your soul shards, Mosh'aru."

At that moment, the felhound ripped open the demon's chest cavity with its tendrils and pulled out a heart — purple and green, already pierced by the dagger. The moment the heart was removed, the entire massive felhound corpse seemed to lose its luster all at once, fading and dulling.

The felhound trotted back over to Mosh'aru's side and laid the heart at his feet.

"It was hardly a waste," Mosh'aru said, picking up the battered demon heart from the ground. "A pierced demon heart still has value, not to mention the sheer size of this one. I'd say that soul shard was put to very good use."

"You really are as Jagin said — you possess extraordinary shadow talent. Working together, we could accomplish things I never would have imagined possible, Jace. You have to understand, even if you can summon a felhound this powerful, compared to what I have planned next, it's nothing."

"Still, it's gotten very late," he continued. "You can rest here. I have several empty rooms fit for a guest, and Dafryn will attend to you well."

Jace glanced at the succubus in the distance. She was gazing at him with lowered eyes, her stare full of hunger — hunger for mana.

Reading Jace's faintly suspicious look, Mosh'aru said, "You don't care for the idea? Your mana could make her stronger, she could find supreme satisfaction… and I would gain better protection, and you could enjoy yourself too. Really, I fail to see any reason to hesitate."

"You're not worried she might hurt you?"

"I'm not suspecting anything," Jace said. "It's just that I've already stayed far too long, Mosh'aru. Still, I appreciate the thought. Besides, I left my horse at the inn — I need to get back to Moonbrook tonight to prepare for tomorrow's journey."

Mosh'aru smiled slightly. "Then farewell, Jace. I hope you'll give my proposal serious thought — the rewards would be generous. Since you're not willing to accept her kindness, at least take this pouch of soul shards. They're not easy to come by in Stormwind." He held out the pouch in his hand.

"My thanks." Jace nodded, took the pouch, and turned to leave the solitary cottage in the woods.

As far as Mosh'aru was concerned, he couldn't bring himself to trust the man.

True, in the game he survived and became an exceedingly powerful demonologist. But Jace couldn't judge whether cooperating with him was safe based on that alone.

After all, in the game, Jagin survived too.

Back at the inn in Moonbrook, Jace kept speculating about what sort of demon Mosh'aru was trying to summon.

The only demon a warlock player could summon that was stronger than a felhound… was, plainly, a felguard.

Felguards were towering Mo'arg demons, clad in heavy armor and wielding massive weapons — ferocious and battle-hardened.

But though these demon soldiers were better in a straight fight than succubi, and far more capable of handling tough battles than imps and felhounds, in the grand scheme of the Burning Legion they ranked at best as shock troops. They'd even shown up among the orc warlocks who'd invaded Azeroth.

Would Mosh'aru, who spoke of the demon he wanted to summon in such fearsome terms, really be satisfied expending so much effort just to summon a high-end grunt that even orcs could command?

After all, he was pursuing achievements that surpassed those those orc warlocks. If it wasn't a felguard, then what was it?

There were simply too many kinds of demons. Jace racked his brains but got nowhere fast.

After all, the demons that could be summoned here were definitely not limited to the ones the game allowed warlocks to summon.

Breaking free from the constraints of game lore, even the gnome warlock Wilfred Fizzlebang had managed to summon a powerful Eredar lord. During the Scourge War, Kel'Thuzad had even managed to invite the Defiler Archimonde himself into this world.

So Jace was reluctant to involve himself in a plan of such limitless possibility and extreme danger.

Still, Mosh'aru clearly possessed a wealth of knowledge that neither Jace nor the others at the Slaughtered Lamb understood — especially the source of Mosh'aru's incantations and knowledge… that remained a mystery.

And that was what interested Jace the most.

He planned to try investigating this man's background a little further — to see where he'd gotten all this demonology. The books in his house certainly hadn't been written by him alone, nor could he have collected them single-handedly, and those stacks of parchment must have had a source they were copied from.

In short, Mosh'aru definitely hadn't come up with all this by holing up in that cottage and thinking it through on his own.
