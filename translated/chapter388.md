## Chapter 388: Mor'zul's Past

Early in the morning, Jace washed the clothes he'd worn dirty and hung them out to dry, changed into a thin shirt, and headed for the Slaughtered Lamb with the felhound summoning incantation he'd gotten from Mor'zul.

Most of the clothes he'd worn last summer hadn't survived this winter — either lost somewhere on the Lordaeron continent, or destroyed by the various monsters around Stormwind. Now that the weather was getting hotter, it was about time he bought a couple of new summer outfits.

Along the way, aside from the matter of picking up new daily necessities, he was also thinking about how to explain things to Zardeth.

Zardeth definitely wouldn't want him to be in contact with Mor'zul. Of course, he could always say he'd gotten the incantation from an undisclosed source — after all, Zardeth had said the same thing about the succubus incantation.

"Mr. Sesso!"

As he passed the door of the Blue Recluse, Malin came out of nowhere, holding a bag of long loaves and looking over. "Have you delivered those documents I gave you? Try not to drag it out too long."

"I've already delivered them and come back, Master," Jace said.

"So fast?" Malin raised his brows. "Well, that's that, then. My thanks for the trouble."

Jace added, "However, the candidate for mayor happened to come to Stormwind to visit relatives while I was there, so I didn't hand them to him personally — I gave them to his household servant."

Malin asked, "Hasn't Mr. Cooper been elected mayor yet? I remember there weren't many candidates there — I thought he'd already gotten his wish. Giving them to his servant is fine, Jace. He's a businessman, and he's been even busier since taking on Moonbrook's affairs. He didn't come to Stormwind just to visit family — he should also be scouting for new teachers for Moonbrook's newly rebuilt school."

Hearing Malin say that, Jace suddenly thought of Moonbrook's "famous" teacher from the game — the legendary Mr. Stalvan Mistmantle.

He wondered whether the man had arrived in the Kingdom of Stormwind from Silverpine Forest yet, or whether he'd already gone off to Moonbrook to teach?

Jace nodded and asked, "Is there anything else you need me to do, Master?"

Malin stepped closer. "Not for the moment, Jace. Perhaps there'll be news from Redridge in the near future — the king is investigating the movements of the Wyrmcult, and we need to be ready to assist the royal mages at any time. In the meantime, you can try to keep attending the lectures. Absorb what you can."

"I'll do my best…"

After bidding old Malin farewell, Jace made his way to the tavern. He grabbed a loaf of bread from Jarel, took a bite, and headed down to the basement — only to find that not only was Zardeth absent, but Ursula and Surena were nowhere to be seen either.

It was understandable that Zardeth wasn't there. He still remembered what Surena had told him last time — that Zardeth had been coming far less often ever since he'd summoned his succubus.

He didn't think Zardeth was the type to lose himself in nightly revelry with that succubus. He was probably just busy with other matters.

But the fact that there was no sound at all coming from the alchemy lab was unusual.

Just as he was about to go upstairs and ask Jarel about everyone's whereabouts, he heard a murmur coming from the practice room. Curious, he went to take a look — it was Erlan, practicing an incantation there.

"What are you practicing? How come you didn't hire someone to use as a target?"

"Jace." Erlan glanced over. "I'm practicing summoning an imp. For some reason, I just can't find that summoning feeling. It's strange."

Jace stepped closer. "Summoning magic is a connection, Erlan. You need to communicate with something far away. An imp's power isn't that strong — even without much experience, I'd think you'd find that feeling quickly enough."

"Lots of the warlocks in Moonbrook can summon imps. You should be able to as well."

"Now that you mention it," Erlan said, "I shouldn't lose to those guys… They showed me their imps. Damn it all. What's more, Ursula succeeded too… and Surena could summon one long ago — she just doesn't like being around a demon all the time. By that measure, I'm the slowest one here."

"Zardeth told me something similar, Jace, but I just don't know what that feeling is. Could you be a little more specific?"

Jace looked at him and thought for a moment. "It's like you've touched something very, very far away. No — it shouldn't be described as very far. It's hard to judge the distance… It's as if you've built a bridge with your mind."

"When you feel that bridge, remember to use the incantation to call the person on the other side over, Erlan."

"I think I'm starting to get it. I'll try again."

Jace nodded. "Has Zardeth been around lately?"

"He came by the day before yesterday. Are you looking for him?" Erlan asked.

"I've gotten hold of a summoning incantation for a new type of demon. The casting gestures and the key points for subduing that demon are all right here." Jace patted the documents in his hand. "I want to call a meeting and share this incantation with everyone."

"What new demon?" Erlan asked curiously.

"Felhound. Have you come across this kind of demon?"

"I've seen a live one," Erlan said. "When the ogres occupied Stormwind, plenty of ogre warlocks brought felhounds with them to hunt down mages lurking around, trying to sabotage their operations. You got your hands on an incantation to summon one of those things?"

Jace nodded.

"You've really got some damn nerve," Erlan said, bumping Jace lightly with his shoulder. "Hey — are you going to have that lady Morophil come watch us summon this time?"

"She'll definitely come. I need to make sure nothing goes wrong and that the summoner isn't harmed." Jace looked at his half-smiling expression. "What, do you need something from her?"

"Nothing important," Erlan smiled. "Don't tell Zardeth I said this, but yours is much prettier than his. I've been wanting to get another look at her."

"I'd suggest you direct all that pent-up energy of yours toward normal women," Jace said flatly. "It'll do you good."

"What, you're not happy I complimented your Morophil's looks?" Erlan leaned against the wall, picked up the paper with the incantation, and sighed. "I wonder if I could summon one that's just as pretty? Ugh, I've already lost the patience to practice summoning imps…"

"If you can't even summon an imp, don't even think about a succubus, Drudgemoor," Jace shot him a sidelong glance. "Let's talk about the felhound problem. This time I don't want to be the first to summon one. And if you ask me, Zardeth isn't a good fit either. I'd prefer it to be Surena or Ursula who summons the felhound."

"Why?" Erlan asked.

Jace said, "A felhound can't turn invisible like a succubus, so it'll have to stay in the basement. The first felhound — I want it to serve as a guard for our place. Relying solely on the tavern's disguise and Mr. Jarel's cover — isn't that a little too unsafe?"

Jace continued, "Felhounds can counter mages — drain their mana and make them pass out. And they're extremely skilled at hiding themselves from mages' detection. If you ask me, a demon like that is perfectly suited to protecting our basement."

"Gakin set up some magical arrays at the door before… but I suppose they weren't much use," Erlan said. "Your reasoning makes sense. But why have those two summon it?"

Jace explained, "You, me, and Zardeth — the three of us don't come here often. Surena and Ursula are here all the time. Don't you think it's more appropriate for them to look after the felhound?"

Erlan scratched his head. "Surena can't even stand imps — you think she'll accept raising a demon dog?"

"You push her to improve," Jace said. "But if she really refuses, then we'll have to hand it to Ursula."

"Me, push Surena to improve?" Erlan looked as if he'd just heard something astonishing.

"Do your best," Jace said. "One more thing, Erlan — you mentioned Mor'zul to me last time. I want to know where Mor'zul gets all that demon knowledge. Since he can get his hands on it, we ought to be able to as well."

"How would I know? That guy lives on his own and doesn't really interact with outsiders," Erlan thought for a moment. "If you want to know where he is, I might be able to help you dig around. But a question like that — I'm afraid even the demon scavengers who are in close contact with him wouldn't know."

"Ah, wait." Erlan looked at Jace. "That guy was once madly infatuated with a woman. He doesn't really bring it up much anymore, though."

"A mortal woman?" Jace asked. "Or a succubus?"

Erlan laughed. "He may know a lot about demons, but he's not a demon himself, Jace. He wasn't born looking like the dead man he is now, either. He was young once, just like you. And from what he's said before, that woman was a warlock too."

"Do you know her name?" Jace asked.

"If I knew, wouldn't I just tell you directly?" Erlan said. "I think you should ask Zardeth. He knows far more about these warlocks than I do. I can help you ask around too — though tracking him down these days isn't easy."

"Forget it. Zardeth doesn't like him," Jace shook his head. "Don't mention it to him. I'll see if I can fish for it myself, bit by bit."
