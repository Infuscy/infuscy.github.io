## Chapter 388: Mosh'aru's Past

Early in the morning, Jace washed the clothes he'd worn dirty and hung them out to dry, changed into a thin shirt, and headed for the Slaughtered Lamb with the felhound summoning incantation he'd gotten from Mosh'aru.

Most of the clothes he'd worn last summer hadn't survived this winter — either lost somewhere on the Lordaeron continent, or destroyed by the various monsters around Stormwind. Now that the weather was getting hotter, it was about time he bought a couple of new summer outfits.

Along the way, aside from the matter of picking up new daily necessities, he was also thinking about how to explain things to Galders.

Galders definitely wouldn't want him to be in contact with Mosh'aru. Of course, he could always say he'd gotten the incantation from an undisclosed source — after all, Galders had said the same thing about the succubus incantation.

"Mister Sesso!"

As he passed the mouth of the Blue Heron, Marin came out of nowhere, holding a bag of long bread and looking over. "Have you delivered those documents I gave you? Try not to drag it out too long."

"I've already delivered them and come back, Master," Jace said.

"So fast?" Marin raised his brows. "Well, that's that, then. My thanks for the trouble."

Jace added, "However, the town magistrate candidate happened to come to Stormwind to visit relatives while I was there, so I didn't hand them to him personally — I gave them to his household servant."

Marin asked, "Hasn't Mister Cooper been elected magistrate yet? I remember there weren't many candidates there — I thought he'd already gotten his wish. Giving them to his servant is fine, Jace. He's a businessman, and he's been even busier since taking on Moonbrook's affairs. He didn't come to Stormwind just to visit family — he should also be scouting for new teachers for Moonbrook's newly rebuilt school."

Hearing Marin say that, Jace suddenly thought of Moonbrook's "famous" teacher from the game — the legendary Mister Stalvan Mistmantle.

He wondered whether the man had arrived in the Kingdom of Stormwind from Silverpine Forest yet, or whether he'd already gone off to Moonbrook to teach?

Jace nodded and asked, "Is there anything else you need me to do, Master?"

Marin stepped closer. "Not for the moment, Jace. Perhaps there'll be news from Redridge in the near future — the king is investigating the movements of the Dragon Cult, and we need to be ready to assist the royal mages at any time. In the meantime, you can try to keep attending the lectures. Absorb what you can."

"I'll do my best…"

After bidding the old Marin farewell, Jace made his way to the tavern. He grabbed a loaf of bread from Tharle, took a bite, and headed down to the basement — only to find that not only was Galders absent, but Ursula and Sulenna were nowhere to be seen either.

It was understandable that Galders wasn't there. He still remembered what Sulenna had told him last time — that Galders had been coming far less often ever since he'd summoned his succubus.

He didn't think Galders was the type to lose himself in nightly revelry with that succubus. He was probably just busy with other matters.

But the fact that there was no sound at all coming from the alchemy lab was unusual.

Just as he was about to go upstairs and ask Tharle about everyone's whereabouts, he heard a murmur coming from the practice room. Curious, he went to take a look — it was Erland, practicing an incantation there.

"What are you practicing? How come you didn't hire someone to use as a target?"

"Jace." Erland glanced over. "I'm practicing summoning an imp. For some reason, I just can't find that summoning feeling. It's strange."

Jace stepped closer. "Summoning magic is a connection, Erland. You need to communicate with something far away. An imp's power isn't that strong — even without much experience, I'd think you'd find that feeling quickly enough."

"Lots of the warlocks in Moonbrook can summon imps. You should be able to as well."

"Now that you mention it," Erland said, "I shouldn't lose to those guys… They showed me their imps. Damn it all. What's more, Ursula succeeded too… and Sulenna could summon one long ago — she just doesn't like being around a demon all the time. By that measure, I'm the slowest one here."

"Galders told me something similar, Jace, but I just don't know what that feeling is. Could you be a little more specific?"

Jace looked at him and thought for a moment. "It's like you've touched something very, very far away. No — it shouldn't be described as very far. It's hard to judge the distance… It's as if you've built a bridge with your mind."

"When you feel that bridge, remember to use the incantation to call the person on the other side over, Erland."

"I think I'm starting to get it. I'll try again."

Jace nodded. "Has Galders been around lately?"

"He came by the day before yesterday. Are you looking for him?" Erland asked.

"I've gotten hold of a summoning incantation for a new type of demon. The casting gestures and the key points for subduing that demon are all right here." Jace patted the documents in his hand. "I want to call a meeting and share this incantation with everyone."

"What new demon?" Erland asked curiously.

"Felhound. Have you come across this kind of demon?"

"I've seen a live one," Erland said. "When the ogres occupied Stormwind, plenty of ogre warlocks brought felhounds with them to hunt down mages lurking around, trying to sabotage their operations. You got your hands on an incantation to summon one of those things?"

Jace nodded.

"You've really got some damn nerve," Erland said, bumping Jace lightly with his shoulder. "Hey — are you going to have that lady Melfiel come watch us summon this time?"

"She'll definitely come. I need to make sure nothing goes wrong and that the summoner isn't harmed." Jace looked at his half-smiling expression. "What, do you need something from her?"

"Nothing important," Erland smiled. "Don't tell Galders I said this, but yours is much prettier than his. I've been wanting to get another look at her."

"I'd suggest you direct all that pent-up energy of yours toward normal women," Jace said flatly. "It'll do you good."

"What, you're not happy I complimented your Melfiel's looks?" Erland leaned against the wall, picked up the paper with the incantation, and sighed. "I wonder if I could summon one that's just as pretty? Ugh, I've already lost the patience to practice summoning imps…"

"If you can't even summon an imp, don't even think about a succubus, Dordmol," Jace shot him a sidelong glance. "Let's talk about the felhound problem. This time I don't want to be the first to summon one. And if you ask me, Galders isn't a good fit either. I'd prefer it to be Sulenna or Ursula who summons the felhound."

"Why?" Erland asked.

Jace said, "A felhound can't turn invisible like a succubus, so it'll have to stay in the basement. The first felhound — I want it to serve as a guard for our place. Relying solely on the tavern's disguise and Mister Tharle's cover — isn't that a little too unsafe?"

Jace continued, "Felhounds can counter mages — drain their mana and make them pass out. And they're extremely skilled at hiding themselves from mages' detection. If you ask me, a demon like that is perfectly suited to protecting our basement."

"Jagin set up some magical arrays at the door before… but I suppose they weren't much use," Erland said. "Your reasoning makes sense. But why have those two summon it?" Jace explained, "You, me, and Galders — the three of us don't come here often. Sulenna and Ursula are here all the time. Don't you think it's more appropriate for them to look after the felhound?"

Erland scratched his head. "Sulenna can't even stand imps — you think she'll accept raising a demon dog?"

"You push her to improve," Jace said. "But if she really refuses, then we'll have to hand it to Ursula."

"Me, push Sulenna to improve?" Erland looked as if he'd just heard something astonishing.

"Do your best," Jace said. "One more thing, Erland — you mentioned Mosh'aru to me last time. I want to know where Mosh'aru gets all that demon knowledge. Since he can get his hands on it, we ought to be able to as well."

"How would I know? That guy lives on his own and doesn't really interact with outsiders," Erland thought for a moment. "If you want to know where he is, I might be able to help you dig around. But a question like that — I'm afraid even the demon scavengers who are in close contact with him wouldn't know."

"Ah, wait." Erland looked at Jace. "That guy was once madly infatuated with a woman. He doesn't really bring it up much anymore, though."

"A mortal woman?" Jace asked. "Or a succubus?"

Erland laughed. "He may know a lot about demons, but he's not a demon himself, Jace. He wasn't born looking like the dead man he is now, either. He was young once, just like you. And from what he's said before, that woman was a warlock too."

"Do you know her name?" Jace asked.

"If I knew, wouldn't I just tell you directly?" Erland said. "I think you should ask Galders. He knows far more about these warlocks than I do. I can help you ask around too — though tracking him down these days isn't easy."

"Forget it. Galders doesn't like him," Jace shook his head. "Don't mention it to him. I'll see if I can fish for it myself, bit by bit."
