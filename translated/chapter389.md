## Chapter 389: The New Summoner

Mosh'aru had been madly infatuated with a woman?

Jace honestly couldn't remember if there was anything like that in the game. Regardless, Erland had provided an important clue — this female warlock might know quite a few of Mosh'aru's secrets.

But considering that this female warlock's very existence was itself a secret, he had no idea which would be harder to uncover — finding her, or finding the source of Mosh'aru's demon knowledge.

Whether Galders knew or not, Jace couldn't even see Galders right now. He'd have to wait until the gathering a few days from now.

Over the following days, on one hand Jace familiarized himself with the incantation pieced together by the imp and the succubus, and on the other he actively attended the inscription and dragon-language lectures.

Fortunately, he'd built a foundation in inscription. The subsequent lessons were all about the possibilities of different writing techniques — not too much sequential dependency — so he could still keep up. But dragon language had already opened a gap between him and his classmates.

As for Orcish, he remained among the top tier of the class. After all, during this period, by studying the demon-language materials translated by his imp and cross-referencing them with the original Orcish-demon bilingual books, his vocabulary had grown quite quickly.

As for that incantation, Jace did try reciting it a few times at night in an empty clearing in Elwynn Forest. He could clearly feel that this was absolutely not a spell that would activate from recitation alone — and that it could channel terrifying shadow power. Not only that, he could even feel himself resonating with some strange force, though he lacked a stronger guide to direct it.

Gazing at the lush summer trees, he felt he couldn't conduct the test here. If he was going to try, he'd be better off going to Duskwood — preferably near the Raven Hill cemetery, a place full of corpses and plague, where the trees were half-dead. Alternatively, he could go north of Redridge.

If it was a spell like Shadowfury, it could destroy or wither large swaths of vegetation, or even cause shadow energy to leak and spread. Damaging the forest ecosystem was one thing; on a personal level, it would also be more likely to draw the attention of the Wizard Sanctum.

Last time, when he and Melfiel had blown a crater in the forest, the fire elemental damage had been more intense, so it had looked more like an explosion caused by an accidental fire.

Given that there were also fire-loving kobolds active in that area, it hadn't drawn too much attention in the end.

But this time he couldn't take a similar risk again. He had to find a place where no living thing would willingly go to conduct the test.

Mosh'aru was right — while living in Stormwind was more convenient, it really did hamper further research and testing. Perhaps Jace should follow his example and find an abandoned house outside the city to live in.

But then he wouldn't be able to communicate with the warlocks in Stormwind whenever he wanted.

Buying an abandoned cottage far from any settlement, or an unused cabin in the woods — as Grede had said, the cost of renovating wouldn't be any cheaper than hiring someone to fix up a place in Stormwind. In short, it was a huge sum of money.

Before he could buy a house in Stormwind and bring his parents over, he shouldn't even think about it. Gold was truly not easy to save.

When the day of the gathering Erland had notified everyone about arrived, Jace came to the basement on time that afternoon. When he got there, Galders and Ursula were already sitting in the main room waiting.

"Jace," Galders said. "It's been a while. Where did you get your hands on a felhound incantation?"

He still looked the same as he always had — as though spending all this time with his succubus hadn't changed him in the slightest.

"Not through any normal channels."

Jace raised his brows as he said it. Galders smiled and didn't press further.

"Did Erland tell you my thoughts?"

"He did," Galders said. "Having Ursula perform the first felhound summoning — I agree she's the most suitable. Her process of summoning an imp was remarkably smooth. That's enough to show that Ursula… you simply lack confidence."

Ursula, arms folded, leaned back in her chair. "I'll do my best not to let you two down."

"The way to subdue a felhound is rather special, Ursula," Jace said. "Felhounds can't speak, so it can't tell you its true name. And I doubt a demon like a felhound even has an innate true name to begin with."

"According to the summoning record I obtained, the way to make it recognize you as its master is to use a pit lord's method to apply a magical brand to it — a naming ritual, you could say. You'll need to think of a name now, then translate it into Annatheran — the language of the pit lords. There aren't many choices here, because aside from pit lords and felhounds, there are hardly any other beings who master this language. There are only about a dozen options here…"

Jace handed Ursula the page inscribed with a dozen Annatheran words and their corresponding Common translations and pronunciation guides. Ursula studied it for a moment. "I'll take this one — 'Viscous Venom.' It should be pronounced — Bosadim."

"Then that's the one. In a moment, follow the incantation on this page, add the name you've given it, and use shadow energy to carve the inscription into its soul."

"It sounds troublesome," Ursula sighed.

Jace recalled Mosh'aru's behavior in that courtyard — the felhound had leaped out and immediately treated him as its master, while everyone else had to go through such a troublesome set of procedures. Truly a natural demon affinitive.

"We'll help you control that felhound, Ursula," he said. "Give you plenty of time to try. Bring a few mana potions."

"If it really doesn't work out, we can always kill it and have Erland sell it for money — there won't be any loss in the end. I still have a few soul shards here, enough to summon a felhound. Don't put any pressure on yourself."

"I understand, Jace," Ursula said.

Galders looked toward the basement entrance. "While those two still haven't arrived, let me tell you what I've been up to lately, Jace."

"I don't know if you've ever seen an orc warlock, Jace. Though the robes they wear are ugly compared to a mage's, most of them are enchanted with their own power — they can strengthen their connection to their demons, or reinforce their shadow magic and fire incantations, much like the enchanted robes worn by the mages of your Wizard Sanctum that bolster arcane ability."

At that reminder, Jace thought of that elven robe Larsen had talked up to the skies — only for it to be carried off in one bite by that black whelp in the ruins of Thorium.

"I've been looking for a tailor willing to work with us," Galders continued. "Someone who has experience crafting enchanted robes and who also understands the need for discretion in our line of work."

"Sounds like you already have someone in mind?" Jace asked.

"More or less — I'm in negotiations," Galders said.
