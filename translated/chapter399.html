Chapter 399: A Farewell That Doesn't Count as a Farewell

Back home, Jace told the two demons, "It looks like we've found the cause of the anomalies here. Something's gone wrong at the Dark Portal."

"A gate that massive was bound to run into trouble — I'm not surprised in the slightest!" Sazeno poked his head out from beside the bag and said. "So, did that gate collapse on its own?"

"Worse than that," Jace said. "It seems a whole host of demons are attacking that gate. And correspondingly, the garrison there is in desperate need of reinforcements. I have to head out. This means I'll be gone for a while — maybe a long time, maybe not, who knows?"

"Then we should go help you!" Sazeno said. "Take me along, master!"

Jace said, "Unlikely. We're getting there by mage teleportation, and the place is crawling with priests, mages, even paladins of the Silver Hand. And we'll be fighting demons coming through the Dark Portal. I think you two had better not add to the chaos. One wrong move and you'll be treated as enemy combatants and wiped out."

"So I'm supposed to stay here and guard the house for god knows how long?" Morophil asked. "While my master is out there fighting for his life against some lowborn mongrel that crawled out of a forgotten corner of the Twisting Nether? This is precisely when I should be helping you…"

Jace shook his head. "I don't want to go alone either, but there's really no way. There's nowhere for you to hide there. If you're exposed to our own side, both you and I are dead… maybe you'd find a way to escape, but I'd definitely be a goner."

He changed into a shirt he normally wore for casual strolls around Stormwind — one without so many obvious mage traces — and continued, "If you're really bored, you can take the imp down to that basement. Remember to hide yourselves well. If that Erland Dudemore tries anything with you, cut the thing off. Other than that, there's nothing else worth noting."

Morophil smiled. "Understood, master. But if you die, I won't keep treating your friends as friends."

"After I'm dead you can do whatever you want, but at least promise me you won't go around killing people in the city," Jace said.

"This demon woman won't promise you that, master!" the imp shrieked. "Once you're dead, she'll have no restraints left and nothing to look forward to — she'll do whatever she wants, and even I'll be done for. At least take me with you… I don't want to die by her hands! I'd rather let the Dark Portal's terrible magic tear my weak little body apart! My invisibility is strong, really strong, I definitely won't be…"

Jace said, "No need to emphasize your invisibility. I can't take the risk. For all we know, the finest mages of the Kirin Tor will be there. How many phases can you shift through to escape the gaze of the Eye of the Arcane?"

"Ugh…" the imp wailed and flopped onto the table.

"If I die, I won't have time to go around killing people here, master," Morophil said flatly. "I'd need to hurry up and find out which demon killed you, then set off immediately and hunt him down to every possible corner of the Twisting Nether until I've crushed his demon soul, and only then consider what else there is to do… ah, just thinking about it is a hassle."

Jace fastened his buttons, glanced at her, and nodded. "I'll try not to put you through that trouble, Morophil."

In the afternoon, Jace went to the Dwarf District. The weather hadn't improved in the slightest — still overcast, and the streets of the Dwarf District were also steeped in that same scent he'd smelled in the Mage Quarter.

But without that strange-smelling rain, it was considerably better than the morning.

He knocked on the door of Grede's rented room. From inside, Grede shouted a whole string of things in Dwarvish toward the door, probably nothing pleasant.

After opening the door, Grede stuck his head out and glanced up, then looked further up.

"Oh, it's you? What are you doing here?"

Jace cleared his throat. "I'm going away for a while, Grede. Since we set out tomorrow, I came to let you know."

Grede asked, "So… it's true? About the Dark Portal."

Jace asked, "You know about it too?"

"It's all over the place here," Grede said. "Come in, Jace."

The dwarf pulled Jace into the room and closed the door, scratching his scalp. "Last night… the kitchen at the Pig and Whistle apparently burst into a ball of green flame. I think it was fel fire, but I couldn't be sure. Good thing it was put out quickly, but they haven't opened since."

"Besides the Pig and Whistle, the tanner's shop in Old Town also caught fire."

"There were some problems over in the Dwarf District too, but I don't know exactly whose house burned. In any case, none of it was normal fire. Some folks are saying demons are coming, saying the Dark Portal's fallen and demons have gotten out… I wasn't really paying attention. Does the Wizard Sanctum have confirmed word?"

"Word delivered by gryphon riders, Grede," Jace said. "There's a major problem at the Dark Portal. Nethergarde Keep needs reinforcements. I'm guessing it won't just be mages — probably more soldiers will head that way too. Redridge, Darkshire… reinforcements may be sent from here as well. It's just that word only arrived these past two days, and it'll take time to muster. But the mages are setting out."

"Damn it, can't you take me with you?" Grede asked. "When do we leave?!"

"Tomorrow morning," Jace said. "We'll be sent over by mage teleportation. At first it'll probably just be mages going."

The dwarf nodded and thought for a moment, then said, "Don't you worry, lad. I'm not about to let you go fight those demons alone. I'll definitely go."

"If Nethergarde falls, Stormwind falls. If Stormwind falls, how's Ironforge supposed to hold? Then it'll turn into another Orc War, won't it? This time I absolutely can't screw up again — at least I can't get mysteriously sent home and miss half the great battle!"

"I knew this thing was far from over… Those orcs would never just admit defeat so easily. The demon lords behind the Horde, those even more powerful monsters, they'd never accept it. I'll find a way to go, Jace. Don't forget — Kurdran Wildhammer is with the expedition too. The Wildhammer would never abandon their brothers, and I'll be joining those lads. Maybe a bit later, but I'll definitely go."

"So this doesn't count as a farewell," he patted Jace on the waist and said. "It just means you're going first and I'll be a bit slower. After all, even riding a gryphon there would take a couple of days — definitely slower than a teleport. And that's not even counting that there's no gryphon for me to ride right now."

"Word's already reached Ironforge, Grede," Jace said. "The Bronzebeard king might be mustering troops to march on the Black Swamp as we speak. If you really mean to go, there'll be a way."

"Even better," Grede said. "Since you're leaving tomorrow, how about we go grab a bite now? Oh… damn it, the Pig and Whistle's closed because of that business. How about eating at my place? I've still got plenty of cheese and bread in storage — and a few kegs of dark ale! Just bought them a few days ago."

Jace glanced at the dwarf's shelf. "Got any meat?"

"Smoked jerky and sausage — definitely won't be missing," the dwarf said.

"Then what reason do I have to say no…" Jace said, licking his lips.
