Chapter 400: Teleportation

June 20th, inside the Wizard Sanctum. A great many mages had gathered here.

Jace had drunk a bit too much the night before, but fortunately he hadn't been late this morning.

He was standing in a part of this mage tower he'd never entered before — the Mystic Hall. A colossal teleportation gate stood right before his eyes, radiating arcane light without pause.

Three elven mages from Quel'Thalas held sapphire staves, facing them across the way. Three teleportation masters of the Wizard Sanctum cast together at this giant gate, preparing to activate it.

This teleportation gate hadn't been opened since the Orc Wars. Half of the Wizard Sanctum's surviving teleportation masters after the war had joined the expedition.

At that moment, a chill wind of magic kept whipping the robes and hair of everyone in the hall into a frenzy. Old Marin beside him took out his pocket watch and glanced at it.

"It's nine o'clock. The teleportation array at Stonard should have already activated."

"Aren't we going to Nethergarde Keep, Master?" one of Marin's students asked.

"Stonard isn't far from Nethergarde — just a stretch of hills, maybe a few dozen miles," Marin explained. "Word came last night that, for reasons that couldn't be determined, Nethergarde probably can't establish a stable teleportation channel. To guarantee safety, the teleportation point can only be set up at Stonard behind it. More teleportation points will be established soon — that's also one of the tasks for the mages heading there."

Jace glanced at Marin. He knew of Stonard's existence — a garrison post the Horde had established in the Black Swamp.

After it and Dreadmaul Hold to the west were captured by the Alliance, the establishment of the expedition's base — Nethergarde Keep, the "Watch upon the Void" — had become possible.

Just as he was speculating about the situation on the front line, the teleportation gate suddenly released a faint white light. Everyone here couldn't help but blink.

When the light faded, a layer of magical curtain like a reflection on water actually appeared in the middle of the gate.

The image within the curtain… seemed to be a deep green jungle, but it flickered unsteadily again and again.

"The teleportation channel has stabilized," one of the elven mages called out.

"Then, Master Marin," Andormas shouted. "Now is the time!"

Marin nodded firmly and walked to the teleportation gate. Jace watched his silhouette reflected in the arcane curtain — he no longer seemed like that old teacher who could barely manage to tidy up his books, but rather something closer to the rumored Archmage Marin, a mighty mage who could command arcane and fire with a wave of his hand.

He gripped his robe, which was flapping in the magical wind, turned around and called out, "Mages, you may now pass through the teleportation gate."

Jace and the other mages beside him nodded. Several middle-aged mages wearing full mage badges held their robes and raised their staffs, stepping forward first. They said something to Marin beside them, then resolutely charged into the teleportation gate, sinking into it like leaping into a lake that shimmered with light.

Before this, Jace had more than once imagined what it would feel like to enter a teleportation gate. Every time he thought about it, he found it hard to imagine just what the sensation would be. As for long-distance jumps, merely thinking about them in his mind gave him a sense of nervousness.

It was even more so now. His mind kept running through several important rules from the teleportation gate usage guidelines he'd read — for example, no repeated entry, no retracting a limb that had already entered the gate, no probing the boundaries of the gate, no passing through the gate while polymorphed.

At this moment, the novice mages also came to the teleportation gate. Jace raised his staff and followed their steps. Marin comforted the young students ahead, then looked at him and asked, "Mr. Sesso, why is your staff wrapped so thickly?"

"The magical gem isn't fixed on very firmly, Master," Jace explained casually. "I'm a little afraid it'll fall off."

"Don't dwell on these small matters, Mr. Sesso," Marin said. "You're the youngest one here. The others will protect you."

"I know, Master."

Jace said this while staring at the blurry, chaotic arcane mirror before him. Marin smiled and nodded. Jace immediately stepped forward into the teleportation gate.

When he reached his hand in, it felt as though his hand had been instantly yanked who-knows-where… but this sensation, which nearly triggered his body's instinct to protect itself, only lasted a brief moment, because immediately after, his entire body sank into that strange arcane curtain.

He felt as though he'd been completely swallowed by the ley lines he'd always longed to reach, shoved and kicked into a constantly spinning, colorful deep lake.

The sensation was like suddenly flying up into the sky in a dream, but lacking that sense of distance — like being dragged back and forth by a viscous, glowing, scorching vortex, except after being dragged for ages it didn't seem to have flown very far.

But in the end, he was still spat out by this vortex all at once, breaking away from the water's surface, staggering a few steps and nearly falling.

Jace steadied himself. When he could make out the things before him again, he still felt dizzy from the inertia of that bizarre rotation.

Sight, hearing, the skin's sense of touch — each returned to normal one by one. A wave of muggy, humid air immediately enveloped Jace. He felt as though he'd plunged straight out of an air-conditioned room into a sauna. It took his eyes a long time to adjust from the various lights of the Mystic Hall to the gloom here.

Just then, a red-robed mage who'd come out of the teleportation gate in the distance let out a wretched scream. The other mages all looked over.

His hair was burning with green fel fire and he'd thrown himself to the ground. No matter how much he beat at it, it wouldn't go out. Several of his companions reacted quickly and immediately smothered the flames with a blast of ice wind. But that mage's head had already been burned covered in blood blisters, the flesh on his face melted and stuck together. He could only howl weakly.

"Damn it." A mage maintaining the teleportation gate shouted, "The channel here has also been interfered with!"

"Have the demons already reached Stonard?" Marin, who had just arrived through the teleportation gate, asked.

"Demons are everywhere, Archmage!" a Stonard mage who was hauling mana gems nearby said. "In the sky, on the ground…"

"Mages of the Wizard Sanctum, remain calm. He will receive the best treatment. The finest priests on all of Azeroth are here. Now follow me!" Marin said.

Stonard was full of stone buildings and ramparts. The enormous, dark-brown hide tents the orcs had laid out still remained here, but above them flew the white-blue banner of Lordaeron, forming an extremely stark contrast.

The Alliance soldiers hadn't done much to modify the orcish buildings, but had put them to use on the spot. After all, the war was far from over — it wasn't yet time to rebuild an Alliance town like Menethil Harbor.

To Jace, these orcish-style bunkers, fortresses and tents had been seen countless times in the game, but he hadn't seen many intact ones here, so he actually had the illusion of having returned to the game all over again.

Especially when he gazed out beyond the wooden walls into the deep, yellowish-glowing swamp forest.

Jace followed Marin's steps to the gate of the largest stone fort in Stonard not far away. Several sturdy soldiers in shirts and a mage in a tattered blue outer robe looked at the mages who had just arrived.

Marin walked over quickly. "I am Archmage Eden Marin of the Wizard Sanctum."

"Commander of Stonard," one of the tall soldiers stepped forward and saluted. "Knight-Captain Mash Selbury. Welcome, mages."

The old mage asked, "What exactly happened at the Dark Portal?"

The knight-captain answered, "The Dark Portal's energy became extremely unstable about four or five days ago. We immediately sent word to the commanders on the other side of the Dark Portal, but before a reply could come back, countless demons appeared in that world."

"Why did the demons appear at this time?" Marin asked.

The mage said, "Draenor… the world on the other side of the Dark Portal has developed enormous cracks, some kind of spatial rift, giving demons the chance to teleport here. We suspect it's related to the three artifacts stolen by the black dragon. The Book of Medivh, taken from the traitor in Alterac, contains spatial magic beyond ordinary imagination. The Horde very likely used that book, along with the other two artifacts, to summon demons, attempting to sever our link with the expedition."

Jace knew the Horde's Warchief wasn't trying to summon demons, but there was no question that the demons had poured in through the countless portal tears he'd opened — tears enough to rip all of Draenor apart.

Perhaps Kil'jaeden had seized the opportunity. He would never allow the Horde to fail so easily and then abandon all of Draenor. He wanted Ner'zhul to use orcish lives to tie down Azeroth and buy him another chance to invade this world.

But it was clear that Ner'zhul, at this moment, cared far more about a new lease on life for the entire Horde. From the very start he'd opposed selling their lives to demons. Now that he'd reached the highest position and obtained the most important artifacts, winning the chance to lead the Horde toward a new life, he would never let it slip.

"Then we need to retake the Dark Portal and restore contact with the expedition," Marin said.

After he asked, the entire hall fell silent for a moment.

"That is our ultimate goal — if it can be achieved, all the better," the knight-captain said. "But at present, first, more mages are needed to defend Nethergarde Keep. Second, we must secure the link and the route between Stonard and Nethergarde Keep."

"It must be emphasized that these demons were not summoned by the orcs, but are a true demon army, Archmage. They don't care about casualties or death — they simply throw themselves at our patrols and supply columns by any means, regardless of cost. None of us were prepared, so for now we must rein in this chaos before discussing further action."
