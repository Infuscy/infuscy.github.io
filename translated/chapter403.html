Chapter 403: No Time to Lose

In the depths of the dark forest, the group had been pushing forward at a forced march for less than an hour when Jace caught sight, in the distance, of dark energy writhing like serpents deep in the black marsh. A summoning spell?

By now the ranger had also noticed the dark magic in the distance. He said to the captain: "We seem to be very close, Captain. I can feel the fluctuations of shadow magic."

"The ritual has probably already started, Captain," Jace said. "This is exactly what shadow energy looks like when an orc warlock summons a demon — the signature is very distinct. It's like a channel connecting to the Twisting Nether. And their purpose in using living souls is to forge exactly that kind of connection."

Captain Quenton said: "Then we have to decide right now whether to act. Every minute we wait, we could be facing more demons."

He looked at the elves and said: "Farstriders, I need one of you to circle around to the north, find a good vantage point, and scout the situation. If you confirm we can attack, send a signal. We'll ambush from the thicket to the west and wait for your word."

"I'll go," the female elf said. "Doril, go with the captain."

The male elf nodded and said: "Take this mage with you. Don't get close to those demons alone."

"So, mage, you're with me?" the female elf said.

"No problem," Jace nodded.

He kept pace with the elf. They pushed through a stand of low trees draped in vines, drawing closer to the place radiating shadow energy. The ground here was slightly higher — just a bit further and they'd have a clear view of the depression below.

But even now, they could already hear what sounded like countless shrill voices chanting incantations in Demonic.

Jace could even make out the Demonic whispers clearly. Just as Sazeno had said, summoning incantations really were easier to decipher.

Souls wait here, called forth from the Twisting Nether…

"This is definitely the place. The ritual here uses souls as casting material, and it appears to already be underway," Jace whispered.

"Let's get a little closer and take a look," the female elf said.

Jace nodded, and the two of them crept closer. Jace focused his attention on observing the demons within. It appeared to be an abandoned murloc village — low shacks everywhere, with a huge pile of murloc corpses heaped in the middle.

Near those corpses, small, hunched shadow-imps were everywhere — a pack of them — and the summoning ritual was being conducted by this group of imps.

But behind this pack of imps, a burly demon shadow stood watching over them.

By now, the sobbing within the camp could be audible — but only one person was crying. There was no sound from the others. If there were many captured soldiers, then by now they had either already died, or they'd cried themselves too exhausted to make a sound.

But what kind of demon was that thing?

The two of them withdrew to a more distant spot. Jace whispered: "There may be over twenty demons in there, but most of them are imps. There's only one that's the real problem — the biggest one. You saw it?"

"A Mo'arg," the female elf nodded. "We've encountered one of these before. Every time one of these shows up, it brings a pack of weaker demons with it."

Although the Mo'arg demon was tall and strong, its status was far below that of the Eredar. It was just that these imps kept chanting their incantations nonstop, yet no demon had actually appeared at the center of their summoning ritual. Were they summoning some even more powerful demon?

Jace said in a low voice: "Their ritual has been going on for some time. I think we have to launch our attack as soon as possible, elf. Otherwise, in a few minutes we might not be facing more imps — we could be facing a demon lord far stronger than that Mo'arg."

"If you're sure about that, mage…" the ranger said quietly.

"One way or another, this is when they're at their weakest. Every minute they could be bringing in more reinforcements. If we want to save those people, we can't afford to hesitate."

The elf nodded, murmured an incantation, and fired an enchanted arrow that glowed with arcane light high into the sky.

The moment that arrow went out, a disturbance rippled through the shadow ritual in the distance. Shouts of "For Azeroth!" and "For the Alliance!" rang out from the distant thicket — they were attacking.

Jace and the ranger quickly pulled back, preparing to circle around and join the assault — when the snarling of felhounds came from several directions.

Damn it… how many felhounds are there?

Jace had caught glimpses of quite a few small demon shadows earlier. He'd assumed all those hunched little figures were the imps he could see plainly — he hadn't expected a whole pack of felhounds lying in wait elsewhere!

He immediately spoke the incantation for Tenebrous' Fel Protection — that modified mage armor that traded elemental resistance for fel resistance. A wave of dark-glowing magical flame rippled across his body, then vanished without a trace.

The elf, seeing the young mage buff himself with a magical barrier, nocked an arrow and said: "Head north — there's no felhound noise up there! Go link up with the captain. I'll hold these demons off for you… You're not planning to fight felhounds head-on here, are you, mage?"

Jace drew Azure Song and said: "That's exactly what I'm planning."

The elf's eyes flicked to the sword and lingered for a moment. Even if she didn't recognize it immediately, the arcane radiance alone was enough to give a high elf like her pause.

But the situation didn't leave her time to ask questions. A whole pack of felhounds was already closing in rapidly from three directions.

"Be careful with your magic — best not to use it," she warned.

"I know," Jace said.

The elf nodded and turned her attention away, rapidly nocking and firing. She dropped two felhounds with successive shots, then drew the two crossed longblades from her back and spun, hacking apart the tentacles of another felhound in all directions, before driving a blade through its head.

Jace, drawing on his previous experience fighting fel hunters, knew these things fed on mana through their tentacles. As long as he didn't cast spells and get stunned by their counterspell, his sword and strength would be enough to handle two or three.

He wielded his staff in one hand to keep the approaching felhounds at bay, and hacked at the reaching tentacles with his sword in the other. Working in tandem with the elf, they quickly killed five or six. As the remaining few hesitated, the elf casually shot one dead.

Just then, a burst of golden light exploded in the distance. The aura of shadow magic had thinned to the point of being barely perceptible — the ritual had probably been disrupted. The cries of humans and demons echoed through the forest.

"They've already engaged the demons — we have to get over there and support them." The elf lifted her bow, fixed her gaze on the longsword in Jace's hand for a moment, then looked up at him and asked: "Are you all right?"

Jace sheathed the sword, gripped his staff, and said: "Not injured. Let's go."

The elf took the lead; Jace followed behind. Every so often he caught flashes of golden light flickering in the distant forest. He thought that there were no priests in the party…

Could that Captain Quenton be a paladin of the Order of the Silver Hand?
