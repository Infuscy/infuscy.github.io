Chapter 417: Officially on the Books

After returning to Nethergarde, Jace was sent to the priests to have his wound dressed. Though the stab hadn't been shallow, it was only an external injury.

Thanks in particular to the black dragon's blood, Jace's constitution was absurdly resilient. After soothing it with a healing potion and having the priests channel Holy Light to cleanse the residual fel infection, he felt he didn't even need to take up a bed.

Still, considering it was a wound left by a rare shivarra, the priests wanted him to stay under observation for two days.

That evening, lying in bed, he listened to the soldiers recount how a few days earlier, when demons had attacked Dreadmaul Hold, the first wave of mages sent to reinforce Nethergarde had rushed over to help defend the fortress — and how the Wizard Sanctum's Master Marin had unleashed a spectacular wall of fire that swept across half the battlefield, engulfing a horde of Mo'arg demons that had been trying to scale the outer walls.

Listening to the soldiers' astonished tones, Jace finally remembered he ought to go see his teacher.

The next morning, Jace saw that most of the people lying there had been ravaged by fel corruption or were gravely wounded and disabled. He really couldn't justify staying any longer, so he got the priests' okay and left.

In the morning he went to the mage tower, heading to old Marin's room following the directions Amy had given him, and knocked.

"Master Marin." When the door opened, Jace glanced inside and saw the old man. He seemed to have lost even more weight than before — his cheekbones were practically jutting out.

"Master… are you all right?"

"I'm fine, Mr. Sesso." Marin beckoned him in. "Come, sit. I heard from Amy that you'd come here. I'm not surprised in the least. You were never going to be content staying at Stonard doing auxiliary work — sooner or later you'd find a way to slip over here."

"I didn't slip in, Master — Mr. Barry Frede approved my application…" Jace said as he entered. The archmage's quarters were cramped as hell — even narrower than the apartments in the Mage Quarter. Practically a tiny box. With the two of them inside, there was barely room to move.

Jace found a chair and sat. Marin sat on a small bed that looked barely long enough to stretch out on, his gaze a little vacant.

"Teacher… maybe I shouldn't ask, but you don't seem to be in great shape," Jace said gently.

"Quite right," Marin said. "I overestimated my own strength a little. When I went to reinforce the Alliance army at Dreadmaul Hold the other day, I still thought I was in my thirties or forties. But clearly my mana hasn't kept pace with my age — I can't cast with the same ease as Master Antonidas of the Kirin Tor, even at that age. This is the consequence of mana overexertion. A moment of glory that exacts a terrible price, Mr. Sesso. Look at the state of me — and consider myself lucky."

"More and more mages are arriving here, Master," Jace said. "You can rest easy for a while."

Marin fell silent for a moment. Then a horn sounded outside — the call announcing the arrival of allies.

"You can feel it, can't you — this place is slowly dying, Jace Sesso," the old mage said suddenly. "The power radiating from the Dark Portal is tearing this world apart bit by bit. These past few nights I've been able to hear the earth and sky howling in agony."

This wasn't exaggeration or personification. Jace could hear it too. The magical winds blowing from the Dark Portal weren't mere air currents — from the very beginning until now, the pressure Azeroth endured from the destruction of another world had never let up.

Just last night he'd heard that a watchtower guarding the Dark Portal to the south had collapsed. The demons hadn't even attacked it — the structure simply couldn't hold itself up anymore.

If this continued, it wouldn't be long before the destruction started spreading to the walls here, and then to places even farther away.

"But we don't have a choice, do we, Teacher?" Jace said. "No matter what, we have to hold the line."

Marin's gaze shifted to Jace, and then he nodded. "You're absolutely right, Mr. Sesso. Just an old man's sentimentality. So there's no way I'm lying here peacefully — I'll just remember to bring a few more mana potions next time."

After that, the mood lightened considerably. The two of them chatted a bit about the mines. It was plain to see that old Marin was clearly unhappy about his daughter fighting an opponent like a shivarra — he kept putting her in danger — but between the words, a barely concealed pride and satisfaction still shone through.

Leaving the mage tower, Jace went to get some extra bread when he saw a company of goat riders leading columns of dwarf warriors into the square. The dwarves stood in the middle of the plaza, visibly a head shorter than the humans watching around them — but broader, sturdier.

Every one of them wore a heavy helmet, chain mail or plate armor, carrying axes and shields, wielding hammers and swords — like a band of fully armed Viking raiders.

Columns of marksmen followed behind them, many carrying muskets with bores so wide they looked like small cannons.

Jace, as always, scrutinized every dwarf in the crowd, trying to figure out which one was Grede. But as with every previous time, faced with a mass of neatly bearded, curly-haired dwarves in full kit, telling one face from another was damned near impossible.

Then he noticed a few gryphon riders sitting on the steps beneath the wall, watching the Bronzebeard dwarves and chatting among themselves — one of them laughing with a timbre so distinctive it was impossible to ignore.

Jace crept over and studied one of the black-bearded dwarves for a moment, then smacked him on the back of the head.

"Who the fuck dares hit me?!"

The dwarf ripped off his goggles and looked up. The moment he saw Jace, both sides of his beard curled upward — it was Grede!

"Well, hell! I went to the mage tower and couldn't find you — I thought the demons had chopped you to bits already!"

"When did you get here?" Jace asked.

"Last night!" Grede stood up and pulled Jace aside. "Where the hell have you been? Weren't you staying in the mage tower?"

"I haven't been in the mage tower the last couple of days…" Jace said.

Grede said urgently, "You're a celebrity now, Jace Sesso. When I went to the mage tower, everyone at the Wizard Sanctum knew your name — though they didn't know where you were… I wonder when I'll ever have that kind of fame in Aerie Peak or Ironforge?"

"You already know Avatar. Just transform in the middle of the Ironforge hall and you're set," Jace said.

"Hah." Grede said, "I only recently mastered that power — I still need practice. The Mountain Kings in Ironforge aren't just the Bronzebeards. If I put on a show there for no good reason, and a transformation two heads taller than me shows up, then what? That's not the kind of fame I'm after."

Jace laughed. "So why didn't you come with the Ironforge contingent?"

Grede slapped Jace's arm. "I'm a gryphon rider — how could I walk here with those lot?!"

"Ow!" Jace yelped in pain. "I just started to recover, and you undid all of it with one slap!"

"What happened?" Grede only now noticed the bandage on Jace's shoulder.

"A demon stabbed me." Jace gritted his teeth and touched the bandage over his wound. "Luckily it's not too serious. I should be fine in a few days. Why do you think I wasn't in the mage tower?"

"Ha, my apologies… So you really have fought demons?" Grede asked.

"Of course." Jace said. "And I've already killed quite a few demons. At least… seven or eight."

"Bullshit," Grede said immediately. "There aren't that many demons for you to kill."

"You might not know it, having just arrived," Jace said. "But once you take a gryphon and fly a lap, you'll know exactly how many demons there are to kill, Grede."

"Really? Sounds blood-pumping just hearing it." Grede hiked up his wide leather trousers. "Compared to demons, orcs and trolls are boring. This is the fight I've been waiting for, you know?"

Just then, a dwarf riding a black goat and clad in heavy armor and a cloak appeared among the crowd. He wore a black great helm with two horns jutting from its crown, and a brown-black beard spilled from beneath it like a cloud of smoke billowing from rage.

"Is that Muradin Bronzebeard?" Jace asked, squinting. "I can't see clearly."

"No," Grede said. "Muradin should be in Lordaeron. If he were coming, he'd come with the Lordaeron army. This Bronzebeard is an Anvilmar — one of the last few remaining scions of their house. See that helm? That's the crown of the last High King."

"So his grandfather ruled the Three Hammers," Jace said.

"Grandfather, maybe," Grede said. "Father, grandfather, or great-grandfather — I'm not sure. But I know there are only three or four Anveelmars left in this world. If they keep this tradition of throwing themselves onto every battlefield to prove their lineage, it won't be long before there's not a single one left."

"Hey, didn't you just say you were a former gryphon rider?" Jace asked.

"I've re-enlisted in Ironforge's Gryphon Rider Corps," Grede said. "We're under the Bronzebeard king's dispatch, but there are plenty of Wildhammer dwarves among us too. We're continuing the alliance from the Orc Wars, Jace. So I'm officially a gryphon rider now."

"Congratulations, Grede." Jace clapped the dwarf on the chest.

"I won't forget I owe all of this to you." Grede nodded, looking at him. "My good brother, rest easy — even if you were buried in the middle of two hundred Felguards, I'd risk my life dragging your corpse out."

"Why not drag me out alive?"

"The fuck do you think — you get surrounded by two hundred Felguards and you're walking out alive?"

"Maybe!"

"Alive or dead, either way!"
