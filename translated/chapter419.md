## Chapter 419: Elves and Dwarves

Whatever the truth behind General Alexandros Mograine's words, at least one thing he'd said was right: the army of Quel'Thalas arrived at Nethergarde that very evening.

The horn of allied arrival sounded. Jace came to the observation deck of the mage tower and looked down. A long column of elves in blue and gold rode white warhorses and colorful springboks toward Nethergarde, wagon after wagon of supplies trailing behind them. Most eye-catching of all was an ornately decorated transport wagon carrying a gorgeous treasure chest.

What was inside naturally piqued Jace's curiosity — but he was far more interested in the elven commanders riding at the front.

From this height he couldn't make out their features, but from what the elven ranger at Stonard had let slip, Sylvanas Windrunner was surely among them.

The thought stirred a flicker of anticipation. He'd been in this world all this time and had never actually laid eyes on this heavyweight figure. He absolutely had to see what the Ranger-General looked like before she became the Banshee Queen.

He wasn't the only one interested. Quite a few mages, hearing the horn, prepared to head downstairs and see the elves — and by now the square was already packed. Jace shoved his way through the crowd, trying to fight to the front for the best view, but in the end the current of bodies pushed him to the back.

Even then, he could hear a few Bronzebeard dwarves beside him complaining about why there hadn't been this many people to welcome them.

In truth, it had nothing to do with whether dwarves were less welcome — it was that when the dwarves had arrived, several positions were under demon attack at once, and a good number of soldiers had been dispatched to reinforce the defenses.

If Jace hadn't been resting at the priests' quarters, he'd have had a mission too — which was why he'd felt guilty for a while, since his injury wasn't all that serious.

Just then, Grede squeezed through from the dwarf side over to where Jace was. He clapped Jace on the chest. "So? How's the wound?"

"Almost healed," Jace said. "You here to see Sylvanas Windrunner too?"

"Of course!" Grede laughed. "A lot of people say Alleria and Sylvanas look alike — who knows if it's true. I've got to see for myself!"

The gate rose. After a guard of honor carrying blue phoenix banners marched past, a pale-blonde woman in blue feathered armor rode into the fortress on a dark green springbok.

The springbok's plumage was almost painterly in its beauty, and it walked with a stride so haughty it bordered on swagger — exactly like the Quel'dorei who'd raised them.

Several of Nethergarde's generals stood with a leader of the Farstriders, calling out something from a distance — probably words of welcome. Jace thought he heard one of the generals mention Sylvanas's name, and the blonde woman nodded in response. No doubt about it, then — that was Sylvanas.

Compared to Vereesa, Sylvanas's features were noticeably sharper — she really did look more like Alleria. Her arcane-infused eyes, glowing blue, were narrower but deeper, and when she looked toward the human ranks, you could feel the pressure rolling off her.

To Sylvanas's left was an elven man Jace didn't recognize. He wore a Farstrider's cloak and golden mail, his dark-gold hair pulled back into a high ponytail. He walked beside Sylvanas without saying a word, maintaining a polite smile the whole time.

"Who's that guy?" Jace asked Grede. Grede shook his head.

"Lor'themar Theron," a red-robed mage beside them said. "The Ranger-General's lieutenant. A powerful Ranger-Lord."

Lor'themar Theron, indeed…

In another timeline, after the Scourge invaded and the Sun King perished, he was appointed by Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider as regent of the Sin'dorei and Quel'Thalas. And after Kael'thas himself died, he naturally became the true ruler of Quel'Thalas.

This trip to Nethergarde hadn't been for nothing — he'd gotten to meet several legendary heroes. With this many big names here, Nethergarde should be in good hands, right?

Just then, Jace spotted a familiar figure among the Farstrider ranks behind Sylvanas: Vereesa.

She was watching Sylvanas's every move, and only after the Ranger-General finished speaking with Nethergarde's generals did she glance off to the side.

The elves who'd come here were quite numerous — mages, rangers, spearmen with shields, and even Spellbreakers wielding sabers and shields. Considering Quel'Thalas had barely recovered from its recent devastation and still faced the threat of the Amani, being able to spare a force this size to reinforce Nethergarde was no small thing — especially since there was already a substantial elven garrison here.

The elven column, led by several human knights, made its way to the fortress heights at the rear of Nethergarde — presumably the quarters set aside especially for the elves.

The enormous, ornately decorated treasure chest was led by a group of purple-robed Kirin Tor mages toward the mage tower. Jace watched as the chest was opened at the tower's base, and a massive object wrapped in silk was carried out by a team of soldiers and mages and brought inside the tower.

What on earth was that… Jace looked up toward the top of the mage tower. A magic gemstone to supply mana?

"Which one is Vereesa?" Grede's question broke into his thoughts.

"You've met her before," Jace said.

"Once, in Dalaran — how am I supposed to remember what she looked like?" Grede said impatiently from the stone step he'd claimed, the only elevated spot in the area, now occupied by dwarves.

"And she was so far away, bundled up in that hood," he complained.

Jace pointed to the silver-haired elf riding a red springbok. "That one."

"Ah…" The dwarf nodded. "Got her. She didn't wave to you — you're not sore about it, are you?"

Jace said, "There are this many people in front of us and we couldn't even squeeze to the front. How's she supposed to see me?"

"She's a Farstrider," Grede said. "Haven't you heard of elven rangers' hawk-eyes? She never misses. As long as she knows you… she won't overlook you."

"You've got too much time on your hands," Jace said. "You'd better head over to the gryphon roost and ask if there are any new orders. Your gryphon riders are always so busy — don't miss some important mission and get chopped up by your commander as a deserter."

Grede said, "If there's really a mission, they'll blow the horn for me… Seriously, though, I've been wanting to find a chance to thank Lady Vereesa for getting my eye healed. If you go find her, make sure you call me along."

"I'd love to," Jace said, pointing up at the intersection. "But look over there — the elves have already cordoned off that intersection. You think they're going to let some nobody like me through?"

Grede looked over, thought for a moment, and said, "Just tell them you know—"

"You really want me to say that?" Jace sighed.

"Doesn't sound like you, ha!" Grede laughed. "Then let's find another chance. Let's go have a drink at the tavern, Jace? No idea how crowded it is right now. I haven't had a single drop of alcohol since I got to Nethergarde — it's driving me crazy."

"Let's go," Jace nodded.
