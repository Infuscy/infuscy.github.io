## Chapter 420: The Worst of It

Grede looked at Vereesa and cleared his throat. "I, Grede Thunderfist, have long heard of the great name of the Windrunner family. To receive your help in healing my injury — to give a dwarf who'd already lost all hope of a future the chance to take to the skies again… it is an honor beyond measure."

Jace watched Grede recite the whole thing like he was reading from a textbook, and thought: wasn't this the same guy who'd been full of prejudice against Vereesa? And now he turns out this polite the moment they meet?

Vereesa said, "Aerie Peak has been Quel'Thalas's most important ally for a hundred years, Master Grede. Even if you weren't Jace Sesso's friend, if you came to me for help, I would have done everything in my power."

"Then I suppose we're friends now," Grede said. "Pity there's no wine to be had — otherwise we could toast."

"Since a demon assault is likely coming soon, I'll pass on the drink," Vereesa said. "Intelligence says the demons are massing a great army to attack Dreadmaul Hold once again. Have you heard?"

Jace said, "General Mograine already expressed much the same thing. I imagine the soldiers here are more or less prepared."

Vereesa said, "It's a good thing we arrived before the fighting started — otherwise Nethergarde would be facing God knows what kind of pressure… Perhaps it's not quite proper to say this, but I'm actually looking forward to the battle starting soon. At last I'll get to face the true evil hiding behind those orcs."

Grede raised an eyebrow. "Same here, Lady Windrunner. You may be an elf, but I feel the Windrunners have a lot in common with us dwarves — I, for one, can't wait to unleash my fury on those monsters!"

"Well spoken." Vereesa smiled and nodded. "Here's hoping we defeat the demons soon and reopen contact with the expeditionary force."

Jace took a sip of ale and looked at the elf, wondering how to tell her that Khadgar would probably end up destroying the Dark Portal.

But he hesitated.

What if what Mograine said was true — that the expeditionary force was about to defeat the demons and seize the Dark Portal, and come back through from the other side?

Because he'd killed Teron Gorefiend here, and Gorefiend hadn't been able to cross to the other side of the Dark Portal to lead the death knights against the Alliance army… so everything was going more smoothly…

But feeling the ceaseless rumbling of the earth, Jace felt that Gorefiend's death didn't seem to have made things any better.

Kil'jaeden must have foreseen Ner'zhul's failure earlier than anyone thought — otherwise he wouldn't have been in such a hurry to exploit the spatial tears Ner'zhul had torn open to send a demon army into the collapsing Draenor.

Still, Jace wasn't sure whether Azeroth had faced a demon invasion at this point in the other timeline — but at the very least, it felt like Khadgar's process of closing the Dark Portal hadn't been this fraught.

Or perhaps Teron Gorefiend's role in the whole affair had never been as pivotal as imagined — that the ones truly pulling the strings all along were Deathwing and Kil'jaeden.

But in any case, he felt he couldn't just stay silent — especially not with Vereesa right in front of him.

"Your sister is the Ranger-General of Silvermoon, Vereesa," Jace said. "Has she received any word from the other side of the Dark Portal? I mean, definite word. Of course, if this involves some kind of secrecy… you don't have to tell me."

Grede whispered, "So, Jace — you doubt that General Mograine too, don't you?"

"I never said that," Jace said.

"My sister hasn't told me anything about that side. But last night…" The elf paused. "She said she hoped I'd be mentally prepared to face the worst of it."

Hearing that, Jace's heart clenched. Did Sylvanas know that Alleria Windrunner might be trapped on the other side?

Now he was almost certain that the people here knew about Khadgar's plan. What other "worst of it" could there possibly be?

"What do you mean?" Grede asked blankly. "What worst of it?"

Vereesa said, "She didn't say exactly what would happen, but I can more or less guess what she meant. All I can do now is kill as many demons as possible and try to make things a little better, right…"

As she said this, the elf's expression and tone were unnervingly calm.

Jace looked at her and, in the end, said nothing. Whatever he might have wanted to say felt superfluous.

"Then I suppose we've reached a consensus on that front, the three of us," Grede said. "Kill as many demons as possible — including young Jace here, the youngest of us and the one who ought to be looked after most."

"Young Jace," Vereesa laughed. "Is that what he usually calls you?"

Jace said flatly, "Little brother, young man, young Jace — I'm used to all of it."

That made Vereesa laugh even harder. She leaned to the side. "Finally, someone who understands what it's like in Quel'Thalas!"

"Didn't things get better after you got back to Stormwind?" Jace asked.

"Much better," Vereesa nodded. "Otherwise they wouldn't have let me come to Nethergarde. Strictly speaking, my Farstrider training isn't fully complete yet — I've essentially graduated early and become a full-fledged Farstrider."

"Then congratulations are in order," Jace said. "You've become a Farstrider, and Grede Thunderfist here has reclaimed his wings as a gryphon rider!"

"Definitely worth celebrating!" Grede took a small sip of ale and muttered, "In times like these we ought to find a place where wine isn't in short supply… hell, even Goldshire would do!"

"Then when we get back, we should all get together and have a proper feast!" Vereesa said.

"Thelsamar," Jace said.

"Thelsamar, yes!" Grede agreed eagerly.

The two of them swapped stories about their time in Thelsamar until Vereesa's mouth was watering. Before they knew it, the horn on the wall had sounded — signaling the changing of the watch.

They came to the tavern door. By now it was late. Grede said his goodbyes and headed for the wall.

"Thank you for the gift, Vereesa," Jace said, looking at the elf beside him. "Just these past weeks alone, I don't know how many times it's saved my life."

"I told you you'd love it," Vereesa said. "Take good care of it — and let it take care of you!"

Jace said, "I know."

"Until next time, Jace Sesso."

"Until next time."

He watched the ranger's slender silhouette walk away, and noticed that the square of Nethergarde seemed to be bathed in moonlight.

But from here you couldn't see either of the moons — the black clouds never lifted. If not for the torches on the walls and the braziers beside the castle, you could barely see a thing in the middle of the night here.

Jace looked up and saw that, at some point, a magic gemstone glowing with arcane light had appeared atop the mage tower. Under its radiance, the fel stench that permeated the whole fortress seemed to have been driven back considerably. Maybe tonight he'd be able to sleep a little more comfortably, he thought.
