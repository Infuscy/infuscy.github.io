## Chapter 421: Emergency Departure

In the small hours of the morning, Jace was jolted awake by a blast of horn calls.

He bolted upright. Almost the moment he heard those horns, he knew what had happened.

Something had gone wrong at Dreadmaul Hold.

He threw on his clothes and descended the spiral staircase. The agitated cries of gryphons echoed in from outside, reverberating through the passage. The clank of armored soldiers rang out in every direction, and the chains hauling up the gate crossed and clattered as if keeping ragged beat for all of Nethergarde Keep.

When he reached the mage tower, a Kirin Tor archmage had already arrived and was shouting: "We need mages who can ride gryphons!"

Several young Lordaeron mages in white and blue robes stepped forward, and a group of Dalaran apprentices in purple robes came up as well. The archmage did a quick count and furrowed his brow at the end.

He dispatched the mages to the walls and said in a low voice, "Still not enough. Is there anyone else who can ride a gryphon?"

"Ride them solo?" Jace couldn't help but ask.

"Of course you'll ride along with a dwarf knight, sir of the Wizard's Sanctum," the archmage said. "Have you ever ridden a gryphon? If you have and you're still standing here, that means you at least didn't fall off."

Jace hesitated. "I've ridden a wild gryphon before…"

"Then you're already better off than me," the archmage said. "Quick, up onto the walls. The gryphon riders will tell you what the mission is."

Jace nodded and ran up onto the wall with the other mages. He glanced west — the dark horizon seemed to be burning with green fire already.

That was the direction of Dreadmaul Hold.

"Human mage!" A gryphon rider reached out and grabbed him by the collar, snapping his gaze back. "Up to the front — the flying demons up there are causing some trouble, and mages can help. Go find a gryphon rider who's waiting for you and mount up with him. Remember — look for the white ones, the gray ones, the big ones!"

"Alterac gryphons," Jace said.

"That's right!" The gryphon rider slapped him on the hip. "A lad who knows his stuff. Go on, move!"

Jace nodded and made his way forward, passing a white gryphon that already had a mage seated on its back. Then he heard several shouts up ahead.

"Is Jace Sesso here?! Can you hear me?"

Hearing Grede's voice, Jace pushed forward urgently, squeezing past mages and dwarves toward the sound, and grabbed Grede's arm hard.

"I'm here, Grede."

"I knew you'd come, Jace." Grede stepped up onto the gryphon's roost. "Come on, say hello to Gorjet!"

The enormous gray gryphon tilted its head and glanced at Jace. Jace nodded. "Hello, Gorjet."

Gorjet nodded and flapped a wing. "That'll do," Grede said. "Come on, Jace, lad. I haven't slept much tonight, but I reckon the battle'll be over before I get tired enough to fall asleep."

"Is that really what you say before heading into battle, Grede?" Jace said. "At least say something to put me at ease!"

"Your rider can do Avatar." Grede pounded his chest. "Feel better now?"

"You're going to turn to stone in midair?!" Jace asked.

Grede settled onto his gryphon with a laugh. "That's enough out of you. So many questions!"

Jace laughed too and climbed on. Grede helped him fasten the straps. "Take care of this gray-feathered brother, Jace. If I die, he can still carry you back. But if he dies, we're both finished. Understand?"

"I know," Jace nodded.

"And one more thing." Grede turned back and grabbed Jace's arm, lowering his voice. "Don't use Fear. At least not the mass version. And… don't use your Shadow Shield!"

"Understood!"

"Good. Then let's move out, Jace," Grede said. "I've always dreamed of showing you my true skill — showing you me fighting in the sky as a gryphon rider. I've been thinking about this day for so long!"

"Gorjet, can you understand me?"

Gorjet nodded and let out a low growl, as if it understood exactly what he was saying.

More and more mages gathered on the wall. Gryphons great and small spread their wings, and enormous gryphon feathers drifted all across the top of the wall.

The cavalry below the gate had already set out. Watching those warhorses galloping across the black blighted ground, the gryphons grew even more restless. Some even turned their heads to peck incessantly at the dwarf riders on their backs. The tension atop the wall had reached a breaking point. Every living creature, man and beast alike, stared at the green fire on the far horizon.

Time to sortie…

"Move out! Move out!"

The gryphon rider captain bellowed the order in Dwarvish. A dwarf up on the parapet immediately sounded a long, drawn-out horn. The smaller black Hinterlands gryphons were the first to leap from the wall, one after another.

Grede pulled the reins, and Gorjet immediately let out a rumbling growl and plunged off, then beat its wings powerfully and soared into the sky.

The gryphon riders swooped into the night sky, heading straight for the emerald horizon. Jace gripped the straps on Grede's back, clutched his dark staff, and fixed his eyes on the sky ahead.

"Our objective is to help the support force clear the threats in the sky, gryphon riders and mages!" the captain shouted from up ahead. "Hold your formation. Remember to assist your comrades. Don't fly into a demon encirclement alone! Keep your gryphons in check!"

The gryphon riders roared their acknowledgment. One dwarf bellowed, "I want to charge into Dreadmaul Hold and kill more demons!"

"You'll have your chance, lad! You'll have your chance!"

The riders flew toward the battlefield in high spirits, but the farther they went, the more the air scorched.

It wasn't just the heat. The closer they drew to the demon army, the more the fel scorching became almost impossible to bear.

Jace swallowed to wet his parched throat. Gradually, he realized he was growing less and less afraid of the enemy, and more and more eager for the combat ahead — preferably drenched in blood, limbs torn apart…

But he knew this was the influence of fel. If shadow made one paranoid, delirious, breeding resentment, then fel brought an explosive, fiery fervor — agitation and recklessness.

And it wasn't just him. Jace could feel the gryphon beneath him pressing forward with ever greater urgency.

Then the dwarf captain banked west.

Even as he turned, he roared orders in Dwarvish. Grede heard the command and immediately pulled the reins. Jace felt the enormous gryphon beneath him suddenly accelerate in the captain's direction, beating its wings furiously. For a moment Jace felt as if his lower half had vanished. The black forest below quickly blurred into obscurity. Were they gaining altitude?

But he had to admit — compared to that terrifying wild gryphon ride in Hillsbrad before, this was practically the difference between a field tractor and a car on the highway. Even the climb was full of smooth power, not the kind of violent course change that could knock a man off his feet.

"Get ready, Jace."

Grede said it low. Jace strained to look ahead. In the gloomy night sky, points of light seemed to be appearing, one after another.

What was that? The demon fire atop felbats' heads?

From here Jace couldn't make it out. The gale whipped up by the gryphon's charge made the feathers flutter and roar. Beyond those specks of light, Jace couldn't see much else.

But he focused and tried using Sense Demons… and then he saw them — terrible black shadows filling the sky.

"Brothers, for Khaz Modan!"

Lightning suddenly erupted from the gryphon rider captain's hand up ahead, illuminating a swath of dead treetops. The lightning spun as he swung his arm hard, lunging straight toward a shadow behind a point of fire.

When the lightning struck the shadow, it triggered a violent explosion. The flash illuminated his target — a doomguard!
