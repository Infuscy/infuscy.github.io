Chapter 423: The Use of the Crystal

After returning to Nethergarde Keep, Jace learned that most of the griffin riders had been wounded to varying degrees. Facing a dozen or so Doomguards, the nearly hundred-strong griffin rider legion had lost more than twenty men — and quite a few of those were cases where only the griffin came back, its rider nowhere to be found.

Jace sat in a corner of the healing hall, listening to the dwarves nearby talk about the battle. In the pitch-black night, he hadn't realized the losses had been so enormous.

And he remembered that when the griffin rider captain had shouted the order to withdraw, there had been at least twenty or thirty flickering demonic shadows in the sky. He didn't know how many more griffin riders it would take to break a horde of Doomguards that large.

If they couldn't defeat them, how could the army marching overland to Grimhammer Hold possibly fight the Legion without support from the sky?

He wiped his face hard. All he could do was hope that more griffin riders could be sent to the battlefield — or the elves' dragonhawk riders… or that the Dark Portal could be destroyed sooner, to put a quick end to this battle.

But… Azgoth had already arrived.

Who was Azgoth?

Jace had only heard his voice, never seen him. But that roar, powerful enough to shake the entire region, was more or less enough for him to guess what kind of demon it was.

Only a pit lord had that kind of terrifying presence.

Grede had suffered some shadow burns, but nothing serious — about the same as Jace.

However, Jace had torn open the wound on his shoulder during the battle and been infected by fel once again. Though the injury was brought under control once more, the priest had given him a stern warning not to leave Nethergarde Keep to fight demons for at least two weeks.

Who knew what would happen in two weeks.

And at this point, pain was no longer a particularly troublesome sensation for Jace.

Grede sat beside him for a long time without saying a word. Jace didn't know what had put him in such low spirits.

"The Doomguards are powerful, Grede," he tried. "If we hadn't encountered so many demons before… I might not even have dared to act. And the griffin riders defeated an entire group of Doomguards, didn't they?"

"I'm not grieving because of their deaths, Jace," Grede said, his voice calm. "To die fighting on the battlefield… that is the ultimate aspiration of every Wildhammer knight, especially when facing so powerful a foe."

"I'm waiting for the next battle. I'm waiting for the next chance to get my hands on those damned demons. And this time… I will absolutely not run, Jace. I will kill every demon in my sight. I will…"

At that, he drew a deep breath, clenched his teeth, and let it all out again, as if struggling to pull his emotions together.

"I will avenge those fallen brothers. Watch me, demons. Watch me, Burning Legion. From this moment on, I await their coming to Nethergarde Keep. This time I won't have to flee, because there's nowhere left to run. I will fight to the very end, against the most powerful enemies, to the very last minute, the very last second."

Jace knew what he was implying.

"Azgoth." Grede gave a cold laugh. "I heard that name. Come, Azgoth. I will make you remember my name. I will make you proclaim my name to your own world…"

Two days later, there was actually some light in the sky — but ash snow fell, covering all of Nethergarde Keep in a layer of gray dust.

Every so often, soldiers and wounded retreating from Grimhammer Hold and the surrounding outposts came through the gates. Every griffin rider who flew back looked exhausted. From time to time, a sudden howl would echo through the castle — like a roar of pain while a wound was being treated, or the sound of someone breaking down after losing a comrade.

Jace had been helping transport supplies and carry the wounded. Grede had once again ridden his griffin out of Nethergarde Keep, reportedly to join an operation to intercept the demons.

From dawn to dusk, Jace's heart was in his throat. The image of that griffin — with no dwarf rider on its back, still madly gnawing at those fel bats — kept appearing in his mind. He couldn't stop recalling the sight of griffins crashing into the forest, slamming into buildings. He should have gone with them. At least then he could have helped in the fight, instead of waiting here for uncertain news.

But mages were no longer permitted to mount griffins and ride out with the dwarves. Although this kind of coordination had been quite effective against the Doomguards leading the fel bat swarms, the Alliance had lost a great many mages over the course of these days of battle.

Most importantly, at this point, no matter how many more Doomguards or fel bats they killed, the overall situation could not be turned around.

There were rumors that the commander of Grimhammer Hold had been mentally dominated by an Eredar sorcerer, which had led to the rapid collapse — or that the generals at Grimhammer Hold had underestimated the demon assault and so hadn't reported to Nethergarde Keep in time…

Some said the demons had orc allies who knew Grimhammer Hold's weaknesses, so once they gathered their forces, they could bring it down quickly.

In short, rumors were flying these days. For Jace, who didn't even know who was commanding Grimhammer Hold, there was no way to tell which were true and which were false. All he could do was try his best not to be influenced by the gossip and focus on his own work.

That evening, Jace was sent by the church to the mage tower to transport restorative potions. He arrived at the alchemy lab with a list in hand. A crowd of people picking up potions had gathered at the narrow stone doorway.

He pushed forward a bit and saw Amy there too.

"Amy, you're here too!"

"Jace." Amy nodded.

Jace sensed that Amy seemed to have something on her mind, so he asked, "What's wrong?"

"A Kirin Tor archmage told me earlier that I've been chosen to maintain Nethergarde Keep's magical shield," Amy said. "Many of the full mages in the tower received the same order."

"Nethergarde Keep's magical shield?" Jace glanced back up at the crystal atop the mage tower, which was always glowing with a faint arcane light. So this was the weapon Nethergarde Keep relied on to ward off the Infernals.

"You didn't know about the magical shield?" Amy asked.

"I had no idea," Jace said. "Looks like they already consider you a full mage, Amy. Keep at it."

Amy smiled, then said softly, "But that also means the demons will reach Nethergarde Keep soon, right?"

"I should think so," Jace said.

"Are you scared?" Amy asked.

"A bit," Jace said. "If they push any farther north, they'll enter the kingdom's borders."

"I mean facing the coming battle," Amy said.

Jace hesitated for a moment, then said, "Maybe a little scared. Honestly, I don't know what I'm feeling right now."

"Perhaps there isn't always enough time to prepare when facing hardship," the mage said.

"There are always surprises," Jace said.

"Maybe the real difference between me and those heroes is that even when surprises come, they can bring two hundred percent of themselves to face them."

"The biggest difference between you and a hero is that your surprise hasn't come yet, Amy," Jace said. "Trust me, I'm a good judge of character. You've still got a long road ahead."

"I hope so too," Amy said earnestly.

Jace smiled. "Anyway, try not to overthink it. Once the demons come to the walls, I'll definitely be up front helping with the defense. When that happens… since you're one of the mages supporting the magical shield, you'd better channel that magic well, or I'm done for."

"I'll be completely focused, Jace," Amy said.
