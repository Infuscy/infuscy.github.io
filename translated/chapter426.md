## Chapter 426: The Dark Portal…

As the Alliance army began to advance, the leading Eredar let out a snicker, then raised his hands and began channeling a fel summoning. Immediately, the Eredar around him made the same casting gestures in unison.

As the Eredar wove their spells, the demon army lurched forward amid a chorus of roars, their pace quickening by the moment. They surged past the Eredar warlocks' casting formation to the front, while overhead, swarm after swarm of fel bats appeared — a black, churning mass sweeping across the bleak sky, their shrill shrieks and the thunder of their wings nearly drowning out every other sound on the field.

Just as Jace thought the Alliance vanguard was about to be harried from above, several flights of gryphon riders dove in from different directions, scattering the lead swarm of fel bats in an instant. Then a flock of dragonhawks shrieked past overhead, climbing higher to soar above the gryphon riders locked in combat with the fel bats and streak straight toward the Eredar warlocks!

The sky began to rumble. Malevolent green lights flickered to life within the clouds… just as he'd feared — the Eredar warlocks were channeling Infernals!

Golden light flared along the Alliance vanguard. The paladins' thundering prayers rang out clearly even from where Jace stood. Rank after rank of Felguards faltered the moment they were bathed and seared by the Holy Light, and that moment of hesitation was instantly devoured by the Light of the Order of the Silver Hand. In the distance, a mass of knights in white and blue charged down from a high slope, plunging into the black tide of demons like a spear cleanly piercing the demonic battle line… Coordinating with the paladin-led vanguard to squeeze from both sides, the demons' front ranks had already begun to buckle!

The gryphon riders quickly seized the initiative in the skies. Sure enough, the demon-wings of the Doomguard appeared in the air behind the fel bats, and the gryphon riders decisively turned to meet those enormous flying monsters.

Just then, the crystal atop the mage tower let out a piercing shriek. Jace looked higher, watching green Infernal meteors slam into a thin, shimmering blue membrane in the sky above and shatter, raining down as a dull shower of broken stone.

The sight sent a surge of morale through every soldier on the field. Shouts rose all around. A company of Farstriders was passing by up ahead, and at a single command from the leading Ranger Lord, every one of them loosed at once — a volley of arrows wreathed in arcane hues arcing across the sky and raining down into the distant demons, drawing a blur of muffled wilds and savage howls.

More arrow volleys flew in from every direction. Jace followed the battle mages around him, flanking the charging dwarves toward the demon lines. Up ahead, the dwarves bellowed and swung their great axes into Felguards twice their height, vanishing for a moment as if swallowed by the demons — but amid all manner of screams and curses, Felguards began dropping one by one, the wet crunch of axes cleaving through bone and flesh carrying over the din.

The battle mages in front of Jace were clearly well-trained. At a command, they spread their formation, conjured mana shields, and plunged into the demon ranks.

The battle mages behind them — the ones like Jace, gathered from the Wizard Sanctum and other mage circles — naturally followed suit, trying to conjure some kind of shield to protect themselves. But Jace didn't know any proper shield magic, so he could only advance along with them, empty-handed on that front.

He stabbed a Felguard that had lost both legs to a dwarf's axe and was still crawling along the ground, then cast Immolate to blast the wings off a fel bat that was trying to dive at the mages from above.

Before long, Jace felt like he was finding his rhythm in the fight. When the demons closed in on the dwarven axemen up ahead, he charged into the Felguard ranks alongside the other battle mages. The dwarves fought with insane ferocity, like absolute madmen. The battle mages barely had to do more than cover the dwarves' flanks and swat away the fel bats diving at them from above.

When there was a spare moment, the battle mages could even chant Fireball or Arcane Missiles to support the troops further ahead.

Still, even so, they were already among the few real mages left on the battlefield — because Jace knew most of the mages, including even the mages of Quel'Thalas, were all inside the mage tower right now, sustaining the mana shield generated by that arcane crystal.

By now, Jace could almost feel the edge of the mana shield. The Eredar warlocks in the distance kept trying to bombard the shield with every manner of spell, but to little effect. The Infernals in the sky ceaselessly hammered the top of the shield, but the shattered rubble already blanketing the field told the story — not a single Infernal meteor had broken through the shield into the Alliance ranks, let alone inside the walls of Nethergarde Keep.

As the battle dragged on, the demons seemed unable to shake the Alliance lines — but Jace sensed another problem.

There was no end to the Felguards and Mo'arg berserkers running in from every direction to reinforce the ranks, but the Alliance had no steady stream of fresh soldiers marching out of Nethergarde to replace the fallen and wounded.

The Eredar warlocks still stood there without a care, channeling their spells to bombard the shield from not far away. From the very beginning until now, only one Eredar warlock seemed to have been taken down, and the dragonhawk riders no longer had the strength to break through the Doomguard's layered defenses to strike at them.

How much longer could the mages in the tower hold?

But Jace didn't have the strength to pay attention to anything else, either — the little focus he had left was barely enough to deal with the demons right in front of him.

He lifted his eyes. Gryphons and dragonhawks tangled in the sky with flying demons far larger than themselves, and it was hard to say they held any advantage. By now, every corner of the battlefield seemed locked in a stalemate.

A Felguard in front of Jace was swinging a massive black greatsword, hammering again and again at a shield-bearing dwarf. Jace drove his blade straight through the Felguard's back from behind. The Felguard coughed, dropped to its knees, and the dwarf split its skull with one axe blow.

He caught his breath — and then heard a low, rumbling growl drawing closer.

"Felhunter!"

A battle mage shouted a warning to the mages around him. Nearly every mage stopped casting at once. Jace watched a Felhunter dart between a Felguard's legs and charge straight at him!

More dark shapes slipped between and past the other demons, their intent clear — they were targeting this whole group of battle mages!

The dwarven warriors up ahead saw the Felhunters slipping past them to strike at the mages behind, and they swung their axes in pursuit of the darting, dashing creatures — but they couldn't pull many from the speeding pack. Soon the Felhunters were leaping onto the mages one after another, cutting off the magical support in this sector at a stroke.

Jace ran his sword through the chest of the Felhunter that had tried to pounce on him. He stepped to the side and swung at another Felhunter that was about to ambush a fellow mage, hacking off one of its unfurling tentacles, then drove his blade into its back.

A sharp pain lanced through his own back. He spun and slashed, severing two tentacles that had already pierced his body, then thrust his sword into the Felhunter's mouth.

Jace winced and clutched his waist from the pain — but another Felhunter's growl was closing in from behind. He barely managed to turn and reach for his sword when a throwing axe came flying in and buried itself in the Felhunter's skull, sending it tumbling across the ground.

No one went to retrieve the axe. The direction it had flown from was even more chaotic — a dwarf was sitting on a Felguard's head, pounding its skull with his fists over and over.

Just when Jace thought they'd be forced to fall back into the fortress and make a last stand, a mage beside him murmured: "The Dark Portal… look, the Dark Portal…"

Jace looked up toward the Dark Portal and saw that green curtain — that curtain that had always stood firm, that had never once wavered — flickering…

Was Khadgar's plan about to succeed? He couldn't tell if what he felt was excitement, or relief, or a clutch of despair gripping his heart.

But he had no luxury for sentiment right now. Endless waves of demons were still pouring in, and the Alliance was clearly struggling to hold its early advantage.

Jace took a deep breath, raised his longsword, and fixed his eyes on a Felguard that was chasing down a shield-bearing soldier, sword swinging. He walked toward it, step by step.
