Chapter 430: The Savior's Story

For a moment, Jace wished he would just die and be done with it.

But he quickly suppressed that thought with what little consciousness he had left.

He couldn't die!

Couldn't die…

But it really, truly hurt.

Jace wanted to reach out and touch something, to give himself a little comfort where it hurt — but he couldn't move, and he couldn't possibly touch anything anyway, because his entire body felt like it had been crushed and torn apart.

Thick, viscous liquid — he didn't know what it was — was oozing from everywhere on his body. Wet, hot. He had an intense urge to wipe it away, but he couldn't move.

Pain so deep it made him cold. Pain so deep it made him burn. Pain so deep he wanted to stretch his back, even just shift his eyes… anything, as long as he could move.

But he couldn't move.

He should have seen this coming. The moment he traded his life for shadow, he should have known.

He'd barely endured it once before. Now he couldn't even remember how many times he'd done it in front of that demon.

I can still endure it, he told himself. Damn it, I can't hold on anymore… It felt great in the moment, truly great — and the pain now was truly, truly real.

Jace wanted to scream with all his might, but his mouth wouldn't obey him, and his throat wouldn't either. Something viscous seemed to be clogging every passage in his body — his mouth, his blood vessels, his nose…

He didn't know how many times he'd lost consciousness and woken again. He felt as if many figures were swaying before his eyes, and those figures all seemed to be doing their best to torment him.

He was like a monster laid out in an alchemy lab, and these people were planning to run some terrible experiment on him.

For a while, Jace began to hate those people more and more. He wanted to kill them all, to stop them from violating him — but he couldn't.

At some point, a hazy shadow gradually took form. It was completely different from all the others — like a stretch of lake warmed by the sun.

That shadow reached out a hand toward him. Jace had a feeling that if he took its hand, he could be free of all this.

He just had to lift his hand…

Was this death?

Had death come for him?

No… wait…

That hazy shadow was like a whole dark sky of stars, blanketing everything in his mind.

No, Jace tried to say. No…

He recognized the face of shadow, and he pulled his hand back with effort, refusing to let that chaos draw closer, until…

Until…

Until he opened his eyes.

He stared at the wooden beams above him, blank for a while.

A blanket lay over him. An arcane magic lamp hung from a rack beside his feet.

The narrow room was a touch cold. He tried to sit up, but a sudden wave of pain forced him back down.

It was a very ordinary little bedroom — it reminded Jace of his apartment in the Mage Quarter. Tidy enough, at least.

After a while, he tried to sit up again, slowly. This time it went much more smoothly — it didn't hurt as much.

He was wearing a grey shirt and a pair of linen trousers. His previous mage robes, shirt, hat — all gone.

Azure Song… my Azure Song.

Damn it. He got out of bed, but his legs nearly gave way and he almost collapsed. He caught himself on a nearby table and stood.

Neither his sword nor his staff were anywhere to be found — nor the medicine pouch Ursula had given him, which he'd always kept on his person.

Jace looked at his hands. They were much thinner than he remembered — bones wrapped in skin, looking like an anorexic's.

Even more horrifying were the scars covering them — puncture wounds, lacerations…

He swallowed, barely able to believe this was his own body. He touched his face — just bones beneath the skin.

He lifted his shirt and looked at his chest and stomach — covered in laceration-like scars too, and places still wrapped in bandages that ached faintly underneath.

Jace hobbled to the door, bracing himself against the wall, and pulled. The door opened easily — he'd been expecting…

A mage in Kirin Tor purple stood in the corridor. When he saw Jace, he immediately gripped the short sword at his belt.

He looked vaguely familiar. Jace had the feeling he'd seen him somewhere — maybe on the battlefield, maybe in the mage tower. Probably on the battlefield. He was probably a battle mage.

But no matter what, Jace couldn't place him.

The two stared at each other for a moment before the mage spoke: "You're finally awake… but by order, you shouldn't be out here."

"Where is this?" Jace forced the words from his lips.

"Nethergarde Keep," the mage said.

Jace nodded and closed the door again, shuffling back to the bed on the wall's support.

At least it wasn't demons guarding him. That was a small comfort.

He wasn't surprised by the attitude of the mage outside.

After what he'd done — in front of so many Alliance soldiers, with so many mages, paladins, priests, and rangers watching him casually hurl a shadow-star at someone…

Thinking about it, he couldn't help but suck in a cold breath.

He instinctively patted the pouch at his chest — and found his pocket watch gone.

Of course it was gone. All his clothes had been changed. The room was spotless. He'd been stripped clean.

Just then, he heard voices outside. The door's soundproofing was quite good — the voices sounded close, but he couldn't make out the words.

A while later, the door opened.

Malin stood in the doorway, looking over at him. Jace felt like he was even thinner and weaker than before, as if he'd aged ten years in one stroke.

Jace couldn't read what his expression meant. Was he here to pass judgment on him? Death sentence? Life imprisonment? Exile, like Kel'Thuzad after his necromancy research was exposed?

Right now Jace's mind was a tangled mess — like a kid who'd just gotten into a fight in class, watching the homeroom teacher march onto the scene in a rage.

"Mr. Sesso…" Malin paused. "You died three weeks ago beneath the mage tower of Nethergarde Keep!"

Jace stared at the old mage for a long moment, then said after a beat: "I understand."

"Jace Sesso is a hero of the Alliance. The savior of Nethergarde." Malin sat on a nearby chair. "In his final moment, he sacrificed himself, destroyed the demon lord Azgus, and shattered the demons' intent to push further north."

"The Dark Portal has been closed. The Sons of Lothar and the other soldiers of the expedition remained in the other world to protect all of Azeroth, sealing the window of demonic invasion forever."

"Mr. Sesso, along with the countless defenders who gave their lives beneath the walls of Nethergarde to stop the demons from defiling this world… will be remembered forever by the entire Lordaeron Alliance."

After finishing, Malin took a deep breath.

"So — how are you feeling now?"

"I…" Jace looked at his hands. "What are these wounds?"

"When you were first brought back, you looked like a corpse devoured by shadow." Malin said flatly. "At first, many people thought you were a victim of that spell, not its caster."

"Of course, the many mages in the mage tower witnessed the entire process…"

"A vial of water from the Sunwell, provided by General Sylvanas, couldn't fully heal your injuries. It took the help of many priests, plus naturalists from Quel'Thalas, before your condition was finally stabilized."

"According to the Sunstrider royal house's consultant, the senior botanist Frewin, at that time you looked more like a demon corpse with its heart ripped out than an injured human."

"Why did they save me?" Jace asked.

"Of course they had to save you." Malin said. "Not a single person in Nethergarde wanted you to die. Everyone wanted to bring you back. Ah, yes, that's right — your magic did frighten a lot of people. I know many were worried about what you might do once you woke up."

"After all, the Orc Wars ended only three years ago. People still have lingering fear of shadow power and demons. Countless are still in the pain of losing loved ones and friends, my good student."

Jace looked at the scars on his hands and nodded, saying nothing.

The old mage said: "Now — don't you think it's time you told me about those spells?"
