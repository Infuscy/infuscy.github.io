Chapter 446: A Dangerous Topic

In the morning, Jace caught a ride on a merchant caravan heading to Silverpine Forest.

The wagon rolled along the smooth highway built by Dalaran and the Kingdom of Lordaeron, steady and reassuring. Jace lay atop a cargo crate, staring up at the cloudless blue sky, listening to the rumble of wheels on stone and breathing in the distinctive scent of the pine forest. It had been a long time since he'd felt this relaxed.

He was mulling over what he'd say to his parents when he reached Lordaeron when the chatter from a few men on the wagon behind him drifted over.

"I heard there are orcs on this road…"

"But the fare's pretty cheap."

"That's because there haven't been orcs around here in ages."

"So that Orgrim…"

"Probably dead, most likely. After he fled, there hasn't been a peep out of him all year."

"Maybe he's holed up in some cave at the foot of the Alterac Mountains, building up his forces."

"I'd believe he's building up a pile of dung…"

"Maybe a pile of maggots…"

"Shh, we're not far from the internment camp. Don't let the orcs hear you, hah!"

"When they were burning and looting in Stormwind, they should've seen this coming. I say that internment camp never should've been built in the first place. Should've burned the whole lot!"

Jace took a bite out of a roasted flatbread and listened to them, feeling a touch of genuine worry.

After the Alliance Expedition had taken the Dark Portal, more and more orcs who hadn't managed to flee back through it had scattered across the various kingdoms of the eastern continent.

With the Kingdom of Alterac effectively destroyed, and the powerful Kingdom of Lordaeron and the Stromgarde Kingdom looming on either side — with no Dark Iron dwarves, Blackrock Mountain tribe, or other truly dangerous factions in the area — the Alterac Mountains had become a convenient hiding spot. Many orcs had chosen to take refuge here, and some had even joined the Frostwolf clan directly.

If they wanted more abundant food, they'd inevitably have to leave the mountains for these pine forests, to raid or hunt…

At night, the caravan passed an old watchtower fort and gradually entered a stretch of mist. Every wagon hung up a small lantern, and the wagons ahead bobbed through the fog like a pair of dim, swaying yellow eyeballs — deeply unsettling.

The road grew much rougher than before, littered with broken bricks and potholes.

The roadside lamps, at least, continued one after another. Though most of them had gone dark, as long as a few still burned, they at least reminded passersby that this place was still maintained by normal people.

"Does the fog come in often here?" Jace asked the man next to him.

"It's a peninsula. Haven't you been here before?" the passenger facing away from Jace replied, his Common carrying a faint Gilnean accent.

"I have," Jace said. "But the last time I passed through, the fog wasn't this thick."

"That's true enough — the fog's been getting worse," the passenger said. "I heard a saying: the mist over the Gilnean peninsula shifts with King Genn's mood. When he's in a foul temper, the fog grows thicker and thicker, so no one can figure out what he's thinking. When he's happy, the fog lifts, and everyone's spirits lift a little with it."

"I hadn't heard that one," Jace nodded. "But by that logic, King Genn must be in a terrible mood right now?"

"Anything involving money is never good news, lad," the passenger chuckled softly.

He didn't elaborate, but Jace knew exactly what he meant.

King Genn Greymane of Gilneas had never supported the Alliance's "internment act" or the plan to build Nethergarde Keep. From the very beginning, King Genn had openly threatened to withdraw from the Alliance to oppose both proposals.

He'd always believed the internment camps were a joke, and Nethergarde Keep an extravagant waste of money — but many guessed he refused to pay because Gilneas sat at the edge of the world and had no reason to worry about orc or demon invasions.

Before long, handsome black brick houses began to appear along the roadside.

The wagons arrived at a broad intersection lined on both sides with tall, narrow two- and three-story buildings standing like black bamboo shoots. A wider building flew the black-anchor banner of the Kingdom of Gilneas.

The caravan stopped. The lead driver called out, "We're at Cinderstone Village! Rest up for the night, we move out tomorrow… Be at this square before eight o'clock. Don't be late!"

The caravan's guards and hands answered in unison. Jace hopped down to the ground and immediately felt how damp everything was.

Cinderstone Village was a mining village too. From here you could see the road leading to the mine, but the mine and its tunnels were invisible — the thick night fog obscured everything, with only a few faint, scattered lights visible.

The place was nowhere near as large as Moonbrook, probably about the size of Raven Hill, but livelier than Raven Hill — which made sense.

Jace followed a few of the caravan riders into the village inn the moment he arrived and heard the noise inside.

"…You tell me — if there'd been no Nethergarde Keep, what would've become of the kingdoms by now? It's gotten so you don't dare go out at night with just wandering orcs about. Add demons on top of that, and I wouldn't even want my house!"

"Hah. The kingdom is about to build a wall far stronger than Nethergarde Keep — more reliable, more magnificent. When the time comes, Gilneas won't need anyone else's protection at all, and we won't need to pay for the safety of Lordaeron and Stormwind. We Gilneans can protect ourselves! Do you have any idea how much gold and materiel the Kingdom of Lordaeron demands from our king every year? That money is scraped off the backs of miners and farmers like us, bit by bit! You coward — you don't dare take the field yourself against invaders, so you hide behind other people's money and lives as a shield."

"Coward… Why not look at the Perenoldes of Alterac, those cowards. Before the war they boasted they were the fiercest defenders on the whole continent, but when the orcs came to the foot of the mountain, they surrendered without a single fight… You're hollering so loud now, but you might be the first one to kneel at the black hooves of those monsters when demons show up!"

"How can you say that about something that hasn't happened? Have you ever served in the army? Fought naga? Naga might be too much for you — have you fought murlocs? Hah…"

"Fine, then let's talk about what has happened… Three years ago, if not for the Lordaeron and Arathi armies holding back the orcish horde in Hillsbraddock, think of the disaster that would've befallen Quel'Thalas. Can you fight better than those elves? Is King Genn's wall a match for the elven magical barriers?"

"Magical barriers? What are you going on about? Gilneas doesn't have mages? Don't we have our own magic? Wait — I'm realizing you're not just talking big, you're a thorough goddamn traitor. Not only do you think we're inferior to the Lordaeronians, you think we're beneath those preening elves too… What you're lacking isn't education, it's a lesson!"

"My point has always been that Gilneas must not leave the Alliance! What… You want to fight? Who the hell do you think is afraid of you?"

"Go to hell…"

The men suddenly threw themselves at each other. Tables and chairs crashed, cups flew — and Jace had just taken a room and was holding a drink when it started. He slowly turned his head at the sound behind him and watched quietly as a large group of people grappled and tore at each other, trying to physically persuade one another. All the while, he ordered a plate of smoked meat strips from the boss.

The innkeeper calmly set the plate in front of Jace and didn't even try to intervene — he just sighed. Clearly, he was used to it.

Now Jace understood why that Gilnean on the wagon hadn't wanted to keep talking.

Here on the Gilnean peninsula, discussing politics was genuinely a dangerous affair.
