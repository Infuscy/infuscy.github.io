## Chapter 449: The One Who Should Not Have Appeared

Inside the room, Jace gazed out beyond a few small buildings at the familiar fields beyond — unchanged after all these years — and felt a hollow ache in his chest.

The succubus sat beside him. "That innkeeper recognized you, master. I could tell…"

"Could he?" Jace nodded. "Probably…"

"The two of us will stay here for now. When you have time, come check on us. Other times, don't come out. If you've nothing to do, keep working through the incantations. There are only two left — find them and there's a reward."

"No problem, master!" the imp clapped.

"I have a question," Jace said, looking at Morophil. "Isn't there any way in this world to summon a specific demon from the Twisting Nether at will? I mean — a fixed demon. Like, some special contract between you and me, or some special magical link… or a special summoning incantation meant specifically for you and this little thing."

"Mm." Morophil nodded. "It's certainly possible, master. How else would the Burning Legion summon Azgoth at the War Altar? But I don't know how to do it… Perhaps only the highest-ranking members of the Burning Legion, or the Eredar summoners responsible for such things, would know the specific method."

Jace nodded and turned it over in his mind for a moment. The imp asked, "Is the master finding us a bother?"

"A bit," he said.

"Hah… How cold and heartless," Morophil sighed with theatrical self-pity.

That evening, Jace came out into the streets of Brill, planning to buy a new jacket on the way.

After all, he was going to see his parents tomorrow. The clothes he was wearing had been through a sea voyage and a bumpy road trip, with no time to wash — they honestly reeked. Walking into the house like that, he'd definitely be scolded.

Fortunately, it was still summer. Buying a decent-looking linen shirt would be enough — no need to overcomplicate things.

He found a tailor's shop, picked out an inconspicuous light-brown jacket, and spent a silver on it. He also bought a new pair of shoes to change into.

Shedding that set of dirty clothes was a change of mood in its own right. Walking out the door, he looked at the familiar buildings around him — the town hall, the various shops and houses, the trade station sorting grain and vegetables, the patrols on horseback passing by with the white-robed mages following them… It was as if he'd returned to the days before the Orc Wars broke out.

Might as well wander around and see what had changed in Brill since then.

As he walked, Jace realized he wasn't really in the mood for a late-night stroll after all.

His mind kept drifting back to what Morophil had mentioned earlier — demons summoning Azgoth at the War Altar…

During the Scourge invasion, Kel'Thuzad — by then already a lich — had used the Book of Medivh to summon Archimonde and destroy Dalaran. Later, on Sunwell Isle, the Eredar summoners had used the power of the Sunwell to summon the Deceiver Kil'jaeden (though they'd only managed to summon half of him).

Therefore, summoning a specific demon was certainly possible. The key was the method.

After returning to Stormwind, he should go find Mosul and probe him on this sort of thing.

But the thought of Mosul's plans — summoning an unprecedented demon to Azeroth — gave him a headache.

After all, there had already been one Dark Portal.

Still, if he wanted Mosul to help him figure out a way to summon a specific demon, Mosul would surely expect him to help complete this summoning in return.

Wait — wasn't it possible that the demon Mosul was summoning was itself a named, specific demon?

Jace hadn't dug deep into Mosul's plans at the time, and naturally Mosul wouldn't have shared the details with someone who wasn't cooperating with him.

But thinking it over now, Jace was getting curious.

He didn't need to help him complete the entire summoning — he just needed to understand the process. And at the end, if it looked like Mosul was about to succeed, he could engineer some accident to make it fail.

After all, summoning magic was a long, drawn-out ritual with a less-than-stellar success rate — planting a few imperfections would be effortlessly easy.

Of course, this time Jace wouldn't go to Moonbrook to find Mosul alone. Aside from bringing Sazeno and Morophil to protect himself, more importantly, he planned to investigate that female warlock in advance and see if he could find some leverage against Mosul.

The legendary female warlock Mosul had once been infatuated with, who had helped him learn boundless demonology, and who had now gone silent.

Thinking such thoughts as he walked, Jace reached the edge of town and caught sight of the iron gates of Brill's large cemetery.

The cemetery had been expanded several times since the war and now resembled a park, handsome in its own right. The original dirt paths had all been paved with stone. Flowers had been planted in the empty spaces between the graves — nothing like his childhood memories.

This was probably the place that had changed the most in Brill.

Jace wandered all the way to the newer section, where many heroes who had fallen in the Orc Wars were buried. Because many at the time hadn't believed they'd ever return home, they had been laid to rest here after death — so there were no shortage of southern nobles' names among them.

He found the handsome graves bearing the Stormwind lion crest, checking them against the noble surnames he'd heard while in Stormwind — Lesco, Elgadin, and the like — to see if any of them belonged to those families.

Just then, he noticed a handsome headstone not far away that looked freshly carved, topped with a small stone figure of a cloaked man holding a highsword aloft.

Jace walked over curiously and glanced at the lines inscribed on it. A sudden ringing filled his ears.

*Here lies Jace Sesso, a young and fearless battle mage, who fell before the Dark Portal after slaying the demon commander Azgoth. He stood with the Alliance Expedition to defend Nethergarde Keep and the whole world, and died of his wounds.*

*"From the darkest chaos springs the brightest light. You made me believe this, my dearest friend and companion."*

*— Sylvanas Windrunner, Captain of the Farstriders of Quel'Thalas.*

Jace stared at those final lines for a long while.

Though the grave was new, constructing it must have taken a week or two — especially the engravings, the runes, and that little statue.

It had been a not-short amount of time since he'd "died" — nearly two months. Even the flowers someone had placed here, whoever they were, had long since withered completely.

The dust had settled on the Dark Portal. The euphoria of victory had slowly faded, and the grief of losing loved ones and friends had gradually eased.

But Jace still felt as if it had only been a few days ago…

He rubbed his eyes.
