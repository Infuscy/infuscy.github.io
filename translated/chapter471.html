Chapter 471: A Creation from the Other Side

Pokodiaz whipped off his outer robe and darted into another cabin in a flash.

But Jace watched him leave, turning his words over in his mind.

So Dasuomola Dark-Eye might be on Kalimdor — another continent?

Was he really going to chase after such a shadowy figure all the way to Kalimdor?

Yet having followed the trail this far, Jace had already realized that this Dasuomola might be a remarkably skilled demonologist. If he could find her, there was a chance he wouldn't need to seek out Mosul to learn about demons at all — he could simply go to her directly.

But whether she was friend or foe, how powerful she was... all of that remained uncertain.

In any case, since he'd gone to the trouble of coming to Booty Bay, he might as well get to the bottom of this first. As for whether to go to Kalimdor... they'd cross that bridge when they came to it.

Judging by how booming the Trade Cartel's commerce was, making the trip probably wouldn't be as hard as he imagined.

When Pokodiaz came back out, he was carrying a box. What was inside caught Jace a little off guard.

He'd assumed that a demonologist like Dasuomola Dark-Eye would use a dagger as crude and twisted as any demon weapon — some ghastly beast's fang, perhaps, or a dead, withered branch, or a spike carved from the bone of a demon or evil beast... But up close, he found the dagger remarkably refined — far more so than most daggers or short blades made by humans.

The slender, pale-green hilt bore faint, indescribable patterns that seemed to depict something, the whole thing shaped like a leaf.

It reminded Jace of the Sunblade that Vereesa had once given him. Of course, compared to the Sunblade, this knife was far more ordinary.

"This is... a dagger that belonged to Dasuomola?"

"Yes," Pokodiaz said. "At least, that's what the trolls claim."

"This dagger — you got it from trolls?" Jace asked.

Pokodiaz said, "Surprised, aren't you? Even among the sand troll witch doctors, the legend of Dark-Eye persists. This warlock might have lived far longer than we thought."

With that, the goblin collector closed the box and handed it to Jace. Jace nodded and said, "Thank you, Mr. Pokodiaz. I'll put this to good use, and once I have results, I'll come straight back to find you."

"Compared to my other collections, this thing is really nothing, human," the goblin said. "But its key significance lies in its connection to Dasuomola Dark-Eye. Though as the trail of clues gradually hit a dead end, I came to value it less and less. Without a doubt, you've now given it a second life."

After leaving the goblin collector's sailboat with Mierschild and returning to the beach, Mierschild said, "I don't want to pry into whatever deal you two struck, Mr. Slovak, but at least remember our agreement — the Karazhan matter."

"I can't guarantee I'll get you the necrotic essence," Jace said. "If I decide the mission exceeds the level of risk I'm willing to accept, I'll turn right around and bring your things back to you."

Mierschild coughed and said, "Fair enough. But at least go and take a look, Mr. Slovak. Go see for yourself — it's not that hard. At least I know a good number of laughably stupid mercenaries have made it out alive, and even came back with something to show for it. Clearly, you're not that stupid yet."

"Got it, got it," Jace nodded.

He figured it'd be much safer with Grede's help, and with that kind of money on the line, Grede ought to be interested too.

He'd also want to prepare some holy oil capable of harming undead spirits. He wondered if the Reliquary people had anything like that.

He couldn't very well write another letter to Vereesa just to ask for a bottle of mana-enchanting oil...

Where was Grede right now, anyway? Back in Stormwind, or still at Nethergarde Keep?

By the time he made it back into Booty Bay proper, it was already evening. Jace didn't bother looking for a place to sleep — he was still buzzing from having just found an object that might have been touched by Dasuomola Dark-Eye.

Considering that elves didn't need much sleep, they were probably buzzing at night too. He casually asked a goblin guard sitting on the edge of a wooden plank about the elven quarter in Booty Bay, and was told that a group of elves lived in a rather handsome human-style detached house on an upper level.

That night, Jace climbed along the wooden planks to the upper level, walking in the direction the goblin had pointed. All the while he scanned the multilingual signs along the road by the moonlight on the sea, trying to pick out Thalassian script.

But it seemed every language was represented here except elven. He searched for a long time without spotting any, until he came upon a three-story detached house with no sign at all.

Not only was it clean and tidy, but it was decorated like the purple-tiled tower where the archmages of the Wizard Sanctum lived — standing out a little incongruously in the middle of chaotic Booty Bay.

Of course, because the entire port was already such a mess, it wasn't exactly jarring either. After all, any building plopped into this chaos was just adding to the disorder.

Jace walked over and knocked on the door. Before long, came the sound of a chair being pushed back from inside, and a golden-haired elf in a sailor's shirt and linen trousers opened the door and peered out.

"Human?" the elf muttered a word in Thalassian. Jace understood that word perfectly well.

"You've got the wrong door, human," he switched to Common. "This isn't a place you should be."

"Is this the Reliquary?" Jace asked.

Hearing that, the elf raised one long eyebrow. "It is. Who told you to come here looking for the Reliquary?"

Jace produced the Windrunner family token and handed it to the elf. The elf took it, his mouth falling open in surprise. "Lady Vereesa Windrunner's ring. Did Lady Vereesa send you here?"

Jace froze for a moment, nearly doubting his memory of what Vereesa's letter had said.

What did he mean he couldn't tell if it was Sylvanas's or hers... wasn't it obvious at once?!

"That's right," Jace replied.

"Come in, then, sir." The elf's tone became considerably more respectful at once.

Jace nodded and stepped inside. The quiet interior was simply but exquisitely furnished. A peculiar herbal fragrance filled the entire room, instantly setting it apart from the salty sea air outside. It made Jace feel as if he'd leapt straight from Booty Bay to Dalaran.

Then he noticed the lighting — a flame kindled by a small floating arcane crystal.

"I am Sirith Sunchaser, field examiner of the Holy Reliquary stationed in Blackwater Bay. Since you are a friend of Lady Vereesa Windrunner, feel free to ask me anything, sir."

Jace nodded. "Just call me York."

"Mr. York," the elf nodded.

Jace set the box on the table. "At the moment I'm tracking down a warlock's trail, Mr. Sirith. I was wondering if the Holy Reliquary could help me examine an item this person handled personally and find some clues."

He carefully opened the box. The elf's eyes went wide the instant he saw the dagger.

"This belongs to a resident of Eldre'Thalas," he said.

"Eldre'Thalas?" Jace asked in surprise.

The elf explained, "Eldre'Thalas is an ancient Kaldorei Empire city, and one of the empire's most important and oldest cities. Its history stretches back at least twelve thousand years."

Of course Jace knew what Eldre'Thalas was.

Because in the game, it had another name — Dire Maul!
