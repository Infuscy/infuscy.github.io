## Chapter 472: The Reliquary's Clue

"Where did you find this, Mr. York?" the examiner Sirith Sunchaser asked, carefully bracing the lid of the box.

Jace said, "This item originally belonged to a warlock named Dasuomola Dark-Eye. Have you heard that name?"

"Ah, Dasuomola Dark-Eye." Sirith Sunchaser frowned. "Of course I've heard it. But for now the Holy Reliquary has little interest in her. Some shadow-studying magisters in Quel'Thalas believe she exists only in the oral tradition of certain dark magic practitioners, and there's little evidence that she truly exists."

Jace asked, "What about the books, then?"

"Possibly a false name. That's quite common," Sirith said offhandedly. Then he glanced at the dagger again and said, "But if this dagger is hers, does that mean her true identity is that of a Kaldorei? Or even an ancient Kaldorei noble — what you call a Highborne."

At that, the elf hesitated for a moment. "If that's the case, then it means perhaps we should be taking her existence more seriously."

"I don't know," Jace said. "I didn't take this from her personally."

The elf immediately asked, "Then how do you know this item belongs to her?"

"I can't reveal that information," Jace said with a helpless smile.

Sirith said, "I understand. So I assume you want to use this dagger to trace Dasuomola Dark-Eye's magical trail, correct?"

"Yes," Jace said.

"Then let me try to help you have a look."

Sirith drew a silk cloth from his waist and, using it as a barrier, carefully picked up the dagger between his fingers.

By now Jace noticed the arcane light in his eyes beginning to flicker — each flash brighter than the last. He murmured an incantation under his breath, and when he finished, he closed his eyes. By this point, even the arcane crystal responsible for lighting the room had dimmed considerably.

After a while, the arcane crystal brightened again. Sirith opened his eyes and stared at the dagger in silence for a long time.

"Something's off," Sirith tilted his head. Then he asked, "Are you truly certain this dagger belongs to Dasuomola Dark-Eye?"

"I..." Jace took a deep breath. "I think I can trust my source."

"The magical traces on this dagger feel familiar to me. Inexplicably familiar," Sirith said. "I... perhaps I should report this to my superiors."

"Familiar?" Jace asked. "What does that mean?"

Sirith said, "This might be the belonging of a figure the Reliquary has been tracking for a long time. In other words, this dagger may not belong to that Dark-Eye at all, but to someone else."

Jace asked, "Then is it possible that Dasuomola and the one you've been tracking are the same person?"

"I wouldn't think so..." Sirith had barely spoken before falling into thought again. Then he said, "Well, I can't be certain either."

He examined the dagger from every angle, then asked, "May I send this back to Silvermoon City, Mr. York?"

"You won't use some magic to dismantle it, will you?" Jace asked.

"Of course not," Sirith said. "The Holy Reliquary understands how to build lasting, stable cooperative relationships with our allies and potential allies. So without your permission, we would never harm it. I only wish to send it to headquarters, where more senior Reliquary members or magisters can conduct a deeper examination and cross-reference. After all, I'm just an examiner — I wouldn't dare make a judgment call lightly. But my instincts tell me the clues on this will be of interest to headquarters."

"Then how will you let me know the results?" Jace asked.

"Where do you live?" Sirith asked.

"Stormwind," Jace said. "The Mage Quarter, the Wizard Sanctum. You can send the results and the dagger to York, or to Archmage Malin. I'm his apprentice."

Hearing that, Sirith studied Jace for a moment, then used magic to pull a quill pen from a nearby small cabinet. He drew out a piece of paper and jotted down a few notes.

"That will be all. Thank you very much for bringing us this dagger, sir. When we send the item back to you, it will come with a reward."

"A reward?" Jace glanced at the gold-rimmed blue magical gem emblem hanging in the room. "That's not bad. About how long will it take?"

Sirith said, "I'm not sure. Because we need to examine the residual magical traces on this dagger, we can't use teleportation magic to send it back to Quel'Thalas — that would interfere with the examination results for a long time to come."

"But since it's a request from a friend of Lady Vereesa Windrunner, and may involve some rather significant connections, I'll give this item special treatment. I can find a dragonhawk rider returning to Quel'Thalas to send it back as quickly as possible. It's still far slower than teleportation magic, though. Please bear with us."

Jace took a deep breath. "All right, then..."

After leaving the Holy Reliquary's station, Jace found a quiet corner to rest for a while, but sleep wouldn't come no matter what.

Carrying all manner of valuables on him, and without Morophil and Sazeno by his side, he realized there was no way he could rest easy on the streets of Booty Bay.

He looked toward the center of Booty Bay — that narrow passage leading into the refugee settlement.

Might as well go over there and take a look first.

It was already very late. Even Booty Bay, teeming with all manner of nocturnal races, was far quieter than during the day.

Arriving at this narrow alley, he was met with snoring rising and falling everywhere.

Making his way carefully to the innermost monastery, Jace noticed the priest from before was still there. At the moment he was holding a brown-covered book, reading by candlelight.

Noticing Jace's arrival, the priest looked up at him and asked, "Nowhere to stay, traveler?"

The priest clearly hadn't recognized him — after all, last time he'd been wearing a mask, and had been a bit heavier.

"Yes," Jace said. "I'd like to stay here for the night."

The priest said, "Sleep easy, sir. I'll keep watch for you."

"Don't you need to rest?" Jace asked.

"I had a nap this afternoon, so I can read a little longer tonight," the priest said. "And this book is quite interesting — I'm right in the thick of it."

Jace leaned against the monastery wall behind him, watching a few refugees sleeping comfortably in front of him. He noticed the children who'd been living here before were all gone.

"Hasn't the Kingdom of Stormwind sent ships to pick up the humans here?" he asked.

"Yes," the priest said, turning a page. "Two groups of ships have already come — one the king's, one the church's. They took the children, and many who were eager to get home to see their loved ones. A few who didn't make it in time are left, along with some who aren't willing to go back. I plan to stay here and help them until the last one leaves. The church says another ship is coming next month and told me to be patient."

"Sounds pretty decent," Jace nodded. "Well, I'll rest now."

The priest nodded without looking up from his book. "May the Light grant you peaceful sleep, sir."

That's what he said, but Jace still couldn't settle down. It seemed even with the Light's help, there was no way he could sleep peacefully in this chaotic place.

But at the very least, he could lie down here for a while.
