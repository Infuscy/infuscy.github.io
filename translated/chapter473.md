## Chapter 473: A New Place

The next morning, Jace packed his things, picked up Mithershield's equipment case, and headed for the port to catch a ship back to Stormwind.

Despite finding the fastest ship available, they still ran into a moderate storm midway through the journey, and it took roughly nine days to reach King's Bay in the end.

After getting home and putting his things down, he didn't even have time to catch his breath before hurrying off with the valuables from his place to the Slaughtered Lamb. He walked in, set everything on the counter, and looked at a somewhat bewildered Jarel Moor. "Did you find me a place to stay, Jarel? I've only got a few days left on my lease..."

"Of course," Jarel said with a relieved sigh. "I thought it was some serious trouble. I found you a place, Jace. And it's close to the Mage Quarter — just not cheap. Thirteen silver a month. It's in an alley on the south side of the Park. To get to the Mage Quarter, you just cross a canal bridge that's already been repaired, about half an hour's walk."

"Thirteen silver," Jace muttered. "I'm not some noble studying abroad in Stormwind."

Jarel laughed. "Nobles don't rent either, Jace. This is just a stopgap, given how urgently you asked. If you really think it's too expensive, I can wait around for a cheaper place for you. The rent on that little building you were in has already gone up to seven silver for new tenants. Your landlord just hasn't gotten around to raising the price for existing tenants yet, but it won't last long — things will definitely change after this year's Winter Veil."

"Fair enough. Thank you, Jarel Moor," Jace said. "Can I move in right away?"

"I already paid the deposit and the first month's rent — forty silver total," Jarel said. "This was too good a chance to pass up, so I took the liberty."

Jace counted out forty silver pieces and set them on the counter. Jarel wrote down the address on a scrap of torn paper and handed it to Jace. "Hope you enjoy your new home."

"Have Erlan and Surena come back yet?" Jace asked.

"They're back," Jarel said. "They already made a trip to Moonbrook and sold off the materials that Jalane and Ursula didn't need. Quite a haul."

As he spoke, he glanced toward the drinking customers in the distance and lowered his voice. "Erlan got at least seven gold. Surena... I don't know about her. She's always had her own special money-making tricks."

Jace said, "Seven gold... that's really tempting. They were only gone a month or so, weren't they?"

"Less than two months," Jarel said. "And from what I heard, they didn't run into any powerful demons. These days, the demons that can still survive in the Black Morass are basically just imps that don't draw much attention. The Alliance forces at Nethergarde Keep and Stonard have been mopping up demon remnants hard."

"According to Erlan, worrying about demons is less of a concern than worrying about gnolls and orcs... He said a member of a scavenging party ran into an orc group. There are still orc tribes hiding in the forests of the Black Morass. Also, there are other small, deformed freaks who came through the Dark Portal — have you heard of them? Those people who call themselves draenei."

Jace knew that the draenei Jarel was referring to weren't the real ones, but rather the curious Lost Ones they'd encountered in the forest on the way to Nethergarde Keep with the Stromgarde cavalry.

Many of the degenerated, mutated Lost Ones... still believed themselves to be draenei, refusing to acknowledge that they'd become monsters that even their fellow draenei couldn't bear to look at. After some of them learned a bit of Common, they went around proclaiming themselves draenei, so that later, when a tall, blue-skinned otherworldly race with almost no visible distinction from the eredar appeared calling themselves draenei, many in the Alliance were rather taken aback.

"I heard about them at Nethergarde," Jace said. "Is anyone in the basement right now?"

"No one, except Basadim. Ursula stepped out not long ago — she might not be back until evening," Jarel said. "Erlan and Surena will be here tonight. You can talk it over with them in detail then."

"Thanks, Jarel," Jace said. "One more thing — could you do me a favor and tell my current landlord that I may have died at the Dark Portal, so I won't be renewing my lease."

"No problem at all," Jarel said.

"Remember to get the deposit back," Jace reminded him.

With that, following the address Jarel had given him, Jace carried his luggage and headed north along the street toward the Mage Quarter.

On the way, he passed the apartment building where he'd lived as an apprentice. He wondered if the name on it had been changed by now. Thinking about how he'd never actually lived in that lovely little room, he felt a bit of regret.

Exiting from the north side of the Mage Quarter and crossing the canal, within a few minutes he arrived at the city gate leading to the Park.

Before the Horde captured Stormwind, the Park hadn't been incorporated into the city proper — only a few nobles lived here, so it was more like a forest with a few manors built in it.

After Stormwind's reconstruction, the Park was incorporated into the city walls as a new district. Some wealthy merchants and nobles eagerly bought up buildings and land here, and the funds the crown received became part of the money used to rebuild Stormwind.

In any case, no matter how rich Jace became, he would never consider settling here.

Because he knew that when Deathwing reappeared in the future, this area would be the first to face the fury of the black Aspect rising from the sea...

The flames he brought with him would easily destroy most of the Park, blowing it straight into a crater.

Aside from this area and the city gates, the rest of Stormwind remained largely intact, barely surviving the Cataclysm of the level 85 era.

He wasn't sure whether the same thing would happen here. In any case, he had no interest in taking that risk, and he certainly wasn't going to pick a fight with an existence like Deathwing. Not only would he not buy a house in the Park — he'd buy as far away as possible. Like Old Town, or the east side of the Trade District...

Passing through the Park's city gate, Jace glanced inside. He could see the dense, lush forest and the pretty little buildings behind it. It looked like it even had better greenery than the Mage Quarter.

No wonder that when the night elves later joined the Alliance and moved into Stormwind, they settled there — even building a moon well inside.

By then, housing prices in the Park would surely rise to an absurd height. It wasn't Jace's problem anyway. One blast of black dragonfire and there'd be nothing left...

Following the address Jarel had written, Jace arrived at an alley just outside the Park, found the building where his room was, and the room on the second floor.

Still a single room, slightly more spacious than the last one. The single bed felt a bit bigger too.

Only now, looking out the window, he couldn't see the canal anymore. He had to lean out and look beyond the alley to catch a glint of the canal's water.

And then, at the edge of his vision, the tall tower of the Stormwind Stockade — as if constantly reminding Jace not to try anything shady.

Judging by this, the new rental was quite similar — in both room size and layout — to the "sickroom" Jace had stayed in at Nethergarde Keep.

He was becoming more and more convinced that Nethergarde Keep had also been built by the Stonemasons.
