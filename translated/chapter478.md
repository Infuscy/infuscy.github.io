## Chapter 478: Scavenging Companions

The carriage pulled into Lakeshire at noon the next day. Jace noticed that the Stormwind soldiers at the Lake Everstill bridge were even more numerous than before, and there were even some Quel'Thalas forces stationed here. From a distance, he could see the blue phoenix banner snapping in the wind beside the Stormwind lion flag.

So not all the armies beyond the Dark Portal had returned home immediately. Some soldiers remained stationed here, searching for demon traces to keep them from entering Elwynn or crossing over the Redridge Mountains.

At the same time, they could provide support to the soldiers in the Black Swamp to the south.

The carriage stopped on the main street in front of the Lakeshire inn. By now the sun was about to disappear behind the Redridge cliffs behind the town, casting everything in a gloomy light. Jace kept a close eye on Gaxulis, who was following Sulenna, worried that he might be too drained to maintain his invisibility — which would mean their whole warlock crew getting exposed in broad daylight.

But thankfully, Gaxulis was clearly no ordinary succubus. While he wasn't much for close-quarters combat, his magic was reliable — his invisibility was remarkably stable, even less noticeable through Detect Demon than Morfell's presence.

The three of them walked into the inn. Erland swept his gaze across the room and spotted three people sitting in a corner.

"Dunn, Hubouri, Volis!"

Hearing Erland's shout, the three of them looked over.

"Dedemore! Over here, good to see you again!" A stocky female dwarf with a head full of red braids stood up, waving and calling out with a grin.

"Missed me, didn't you, darling Volis? What would you ever do without me!" Erland laughed as he walked over.

The female dwarf immediately doubled over laughing at that. Jace noticed a faint, blurry little shadow on the table in front of her. Could the dwarf warlock Erland had mentioned earlier in this group be a woman, then?

The other two were both human, so that settled it…

"Captain Obrik didn't come with you?" asked a bearded middle-aged human beside her. A crossbow and a pouch of bolts hung from the pillar behind him. He wore thick dark-brown leathers and a heavy hood — he looked like a proper "demon hunter."

"He's still got some business to take care of in Stormwind, Dunn. Probably won't arrive until tomorrow morning. We're just resting here for the night," Erland said.

"Then I'm sharing a room with you and Sulenna, Erland." Volis grabbed Erland by the shoulder.

Jace sat to the side. The middle-aged man — Dunn — seemed to notice him for the first time. "And this is?"

"Urk." Jace nodded.

"Mm." Volis said. "Pleased to meet you, Urk. Are you a warlock too?"

Hearing this female dwarf casually say "warlock" right out of the gate, Jace glanced around a bit unsettled. But in the noisy tavern, nobody seemed to be paying attention to what their group was talking about.

Besides, he was using a false name, wrapped in a cloak with his hood up. Even if a demon suddenly materialized beside him, he'd have plenty of time to bolt and dodge suspicion. Nothing to worry about.

"Yes," Jace said.

"Not just a warlock — the strongest warlock among us," Sulenna said.

"Even stronger than you, Sulenna?" Dunn asked.

"Don't let his age fool you…" Sulenna said softly. "Words won't convince you."

"You'll find out when the time comes," Erland said.

"Pleased to meet you all," Jace said. "I'll do everything I can to help this team."

"Welcome." The muscular man who'd been silent across from them looked up. "Hubouri. Hubouri Martinlos. I can tell you're from Lordaeron — I'm Lordaeronian too. I'm a friar of the Monastery of the Holy Light."

Jace hadn't expected this broad-shouldered man with iron pauldrons to be a priest. But Jace noticed the necklace around his neck — the pendant hanging from it was probably a Church of the Holy Light amulet.

The "Monastery of the Holy Light" he mentioned was, in fact, the grand cathedral that, after the Kingdom of Lordaeron's destruction, had been seized by the Scarlet Crusade — founded jointly by Lordaeron's remnants and the surviving members of the Order of the Silver Hand — and renamed the Scarlet Monastery.

"Dunn Felimon." The bearded man in black shook Jace's hand. "I'm from Gilneas — or I will be, for not much longer. I'm from Pyrewood Village, outside the wall the king built. You might not have heard of the place."

"Baron Silverlaine's lands," Jace said.

The man in black — Dunn — smiled. "That's the place."

"Volis Firestone." The female dwarf said, "I'm from Ironforge. Like you, I'm a warlock too, so we'll have plenty to talk about."

The female dwarf's friendliness took Jace a bit by surprise. After all, Galders had said that dwarf warlocks were mostly arrogant and unwilling to engage with human warlocks in good faith. He'd probably been talking about Dark Iron dwarves, for the most part — this Bronzebeard woman seemed perfectly pleasant.

"So then — can you summon a succubus, lad Urk?" Volis asked in a low voice.

"I can," Jace said. "Actually, she's right beside me right now."

"Ah…" Volis nodded. "I can't sense her at all. Your succubus is as good at hiding as that handsome Gaxulis. Speaking of which, since you have a succubus, why not join our evening gathering? Mr. Dedemore, Miss Kayldon, Gaxulis, and I…"

Sulenna cut in at once. "Urk and his succubus don't care for that sort of gathering, Volis."

"Ah, I see — my apologies." Volis smiled.

Sulenna gave an embarrassed laugh and looked at Jace. "I'm really sorry — I was just drunk that day… So I mentioned it offhandedly, thinking maybe we could all have fun together sometime, and Gaxulis probably took it seriously."

Jace shook his head. "It's fine, Sulenna. It's not that I don't want to have a good time with you all — I'm just not like Erland, who'll bed anything that moves. I have a hard time with the idea of him and a male succubus in a gathering like that."

Of course, that was far from the only reason — Jace just didn't want to make things too awkward.

Hearing that, Sulenna finally breathed a sigh of relief. "I see, then."

She smiled. "Jace, order whatever you like — dinner's on me this time. Consider it an apology for all the trouble I've caused you and Lady Morfell."

"Finally, a warlock who passes for normal." The friar across from them looked at Jace and nodded. "Gives me a little more hope that you warlocks might one day truly step into the sunlight."

"You'd better not put your faith in us, Hubouri," Erland said. "What if you trust us shadow-dabbling types too much and your Light stops answering? We need your help! Finding a priest willing to tag along on these little adventures isn't easy, you know."

"So long as the Light has never abandoned me," Hubouri said, picking his hammer up off the floor and setting it on the table. "But even if that day comes, as long as it answers now, that's enough."

"I think Dedemore means healing wounds and curing ailments, my priestly brother," Dunn said, taking a swig of ale.

"As long as the enemy dies fast enough," Jace said, "injuries and illness are prevented."

Dunn nearly spat out his drink, swallowed it with difficulty, and burst out laughing. "So I count as half a doctor too, then?"
