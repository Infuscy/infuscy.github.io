Chapter 480: Demon Carcasses

Once Erlan and the others finally came downstairs, the group ate breakfast and lunch together, then set off heading east.

Seeing the exhausted looks on the whole crew, Obrik was clearly less than pleased. Apparently this wasn’t the first time these fellows had put on an orgy the night before.

Once they crossed past Stonewatch and entered the forest to the east, the path became indistinct. But now, without Stonewatch’s patrolling cavalry or Lakeshire militia sentries, the warlocks’ demon servants could each materialize one by one.

Amidst the dappled light of the forest trail, Erlan’s imp Gerkik kept up a lively, blow-by-blow account of the previous night, with Volis enthusiastically chiming in to add all sorts of details.

From their retellings, it was clear that Gakres had been the star of last night’s show — that fellow seemed to have boundless energy, as though he could never tire.

But Jace knew the truth — this fellow, just like Morophil, was drawing out everyone’s energy through the act. For a demon like that, as long as he had an endless supply of vigor, there was no way he could tire.

The other men besides Erlan clearly weren’t all that interested in the details of that gathering, but they were paying close attention to the Morophil trailing alongside Jace.

Jace knew they didn’t have any designs on his succubus — there wasn’t much desire in their eyes. It was something closer to wariness.

Perhaps it was simply that men were generally more attuned to the threat a female succubus posed.

After walking for roughly three days, that afternoon as they marched into a dense stretch of forest, Jace suddenly heard what sounded like running water.

Back when he’d traveled eastward along the Redridge ridgeline with Vereesa Windrunner, he remembered that the first time they’d spotted a river in the forest, they’d already reached the eastern side of the Redridges, close to the gnoll-infested woods.

At the time, he’d debated whether to detour along that river to resupply or cut straight through the Burning Steppes valley to reach the other side.

Reaching the riverbank, Obrik glanced at the rushing water before them and pointed to a gravel bar in the distance. “Let’s rest over there for a bit.”

“Won’t that make it easier for gnolls to spot us, Dunn?” Surena asked.

“Take a look at this pack of demons, Lady Surena Caledon.” Hunter Dunn pointed at Gakres and the several imps over there. “If you were a gnoll, would you pick a fight with this crew?”

Erlan clapped Hubouri on his thick shoulder. “If I were a gnoll, seeing the size of this priest would be enough to make me think twice.”

Volis chuckled, then glanced back at Gakres. “I’m suddenly curious — why has Gakres been so quiet the last few days? Wasn’t he dead set on getting our priest before?”

Hubouri frowned. “Stop making those jokes, Volis.”

“I’m genuinely asking.” Volis looked at the incubus. “What’s going on? What happened?”

Gakres glanced at Morophil and visibly hesitated. For the first time, Jace finally caught a glimmer of the fear other succubi felt toward Morophil emanating from this one.

So he hadn’t been confident because of his own strength — he’d simply been clueless.

He’d probably held quite a high position serving that matron before.

Dunn clearly noticed Gakres’s reaction. He bumped the female dwarf with his shoulder and glanced sideways at Morophil sitting over here. Morophil smiled back at the both of them.

“It’s clear I hadn’t properly understood my place before,” Gakres said quietly. “I apologize to everyone I’ve hurt through my arrogance — especially my master… and Mr. Yurk.”

Volis, along with the hunter and priest beside her, looked visibly surprised. They seemed to want to say something but didn’t know what.

“Gakres nearly got that thing of his chopped off by Lady Morophil, just for trying to seduce our boy Yurk.” Erlan threw an arm around Jace beside him. “He’s behaved himself a lot more now, right, Surena?”

“He really went too far,” Surena said. “This is my fault, really — I am his master, after all.”

“Thank you very much, Lady Morophil. You’ve truly done me a great service,” the priest said quietly.

By now, the way he looked at the succubus beside them had loosened up considerably compared to the previous days. For a priest, that kind of change didn’t come easily — so Jace could imagine just what sort of trouble Gakres had been causing the men here before.

“You’re welcome, Mr. Hubouri Martinlos.” Morophil nodded.

Even though these two days of travel had gotten everyone a bit more familiar with each other, Jace still found these bizarre exchanges somewhat uncomfortable — and he wasn’t the only one.

“Finally, I’ve seen it.” Dunn coughed. “I was looking forward to this very scene during our last trip — a priest thanking a succubus. I’m writing this conversation into my novel.”

“Remember to change my name,” Hubouri said.

“And don’t write my true name either,” Obrik said, “if I appear in your story.”

“You really think anyone reads the crap I write?” Dunn laughed.

“No matter what it is, someone will appreciate it,” Volis said. “If you’d ever been to the Great Library in Ironforge and read the travelogues those Explorers’ League folks wrote, you’d know — sometimes I seriously question the mental state of those explorers…”

Jace was, after all, the newcomer to this group, and still wasn’t used to jumping straight into their banter.

He picked a bit of broken bread from his bag and stuffed it in his mouth, listening as they chatted about the adventure novel Dunn was writing. But it wasn’t long before the bread seemed to have gone off. This bread was bone-dry — how could it have spoiled so quickly?

Jace quickly realized the bread hadn’t gone bad — the wind had shifted, and from the southeast came a faint, vaguely familiar stench. He couldn’t place where he’d smelled it before.

“Do you smell that?” He lifted his head and sniffed the breeze coming through the trees. Hearing Jace, the hunter Dunn also put away his smile and sniffed.

“That’s demon carcass,” he said.

“There are demon carcasses here too?” Obrik said. “We’ve only just entered the gnoll woods.”

“I smell it too.” Erlan stood up, sniffing here and there, then looked toward the distant riverbank the wind was blowing from. “But it’s a little different from the demon-corpse smell we’ve encountered before. Do you feel it?”

“There’s shadow in this, everyone,” Hubouri said.

Prompted by the priest, Jace suddenly realized why this smell was familiar.

It was the smell of demon carcasses scorched by shadowflame — it reminded him of the time he’d fought alongside Grede on gryphonback against the doomguard and their felbats.

That time, he’d bombarded the doomguard in midair with a fireball laced with shadow.

The shadowflame had burned away its flesh, and the smell that had risen was exactly this.

Someone was using shadow magic to attack demons?

Or… someone was using shadowflame to burn demon corpses?

“Let’s go take a look,” the mage Obrik said. “We’ve rested about enough, and the closer we get to danger, the less we should be relaxing like we did back in the lake valley.”

Everyone nodded, gathered their things, and followed Obrik toward the direction of the stench. Skirting a stretch of dense forest along the riverbank, they came to another open clearing — and before long, they spotted something blackened on the gravel bar.

“Looks like a right stupid Mo’arg,” Sazeno said.

“Was he killed by other warlocks?” Volis asked.

“Doesn’t look like it,” Sazeno said. “To me, it looks like he got hit by shadow after death!”

“Who would burn a demon corpse? That’s throwing money away!” Erlan said in surprise.

Hubouri pulled his mask up over his nose, unhooked the hammer from his belt, and glanced over at the carcass. “I’ll go see what’s going on.”

“Hold on.” The hunter was eyeing the dense forest behind the demon. “Something feels off.”

“A bait carcass?” Jace asked.

He remembered the panther called the “Night Baron” that he’d encountered on the boat during his first trip home — that creature had seemed to want to use a half-dead wounded man as bait to lure their group of hunters into a trap.

“Bait?” Obrik asked skeptically, then immediately caught Jace’s meaning.

“You mean… someone’s using this as bait, luring scavengers like us over, then robbing us?”

“Hasn’t this kind of thing happened before?” Jace asked.

“Maybe, but I’ve never heard of it,” Obrik said.

“Maybe it’s not as simple as a robbery,” Erlan said. “It could be the gnolls’ doing — after all, only those dimwits would come up with the idea of casually burning demon corpses. For all we know, everything we’ve brought with us isn’t worth as much as the tattoos and armor on this demon carcass! And don’t forget — they have shadow mages among them.”

Hearing this, the female dwarf warlock looked toward the demon carcass. “From what I understand, gnolls have always been too afraid to cause trouble. They keep as far from us, the demons, and the army as possible — after all, they can’t afford to offend anyone.”

“But what if they suddenly stumbled onto a windfall?” Erlan said. “If one gnoll tribe tried it and got away with something, the other gnoll tribes would follow suit…”

“After all, their territory has been violated too.” The priest nodded. “I think that makes sense.”

“In that case.” Dunn traced an arc in the air, pointing into the distance. “Let’s circle around from here, see what’s over there — whether there’s an ambush…”

“We don’t need to go looking for a fight. We didn’t come here to pick a fight with gnolls, everyone.” The mage Obrik said. “This carcass has been burned to a crisp — it probably won’t be worth much. We’d better leave this place and continue south. Let’s get into the Black Morass first.”

“The key thing now is that gnolls are probably already waiting for us along the road,” the hunter said.

“Didn’t you say gnolls wouldn’t attack us with our demons?” Surena asked.

“I did think that — before I saw this demon carcass.” Hunter Dunn said. “But now… the situation here is changing by the minute. We can’t always rely on old experience to deal with new circumstances, Lady Caledon.”
