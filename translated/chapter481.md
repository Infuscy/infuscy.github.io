## Chapter 481: More Questions

The group crept along the treeline bordering the rocky riverbank, heading east. Hunter Dunn kept his eye on the distance and the ground in turn, pausing now and then to examine the trunks they passed.

Gnolls sometimes left marks on trees, or so he'd said — but after moving through several stretches of forest, they hadn't found a single suspicious marking.

After they'd been walking for a while, he turned to the lead mage Obrik beside him with a puzzled look. "Something's off, Obrik. I can't find any fresh sign of gnolls passing through here. I know gnolls pretty well, so by all rights…"

"Do gnolls even live on Gilneas?" Erland asked.

"Not on the peninsula, but there are plenty in Silverpine Forest," the hunter said. "And the gnolls of the Lordaeron continent are better versed in dark magic — they'd already mastered a great many dark incantations long before the orcs ever arrived."

By now the sky had begun to dim. Proceeding with caution, they'd already covered a long distance to the south.

Obrik spoke up. "Perhaps that charred corpse from earlier wasn't the gnolls' work after all."

"Then who did it?" Volis asked. "A bunch of shadow-magic users who had no use for a demon's corpse…"

Priest Hubouri said, "The only ones in this forest who'd want demons dead and couldn't care less about harvesting their remains are the soldiers down from Nethergarde Keep to the south. But how could there possibly be shadow-wielding mages among those troops?"

Obrik said, "Jace Sesso killed the demon lord Azgoth with a single devastating shadow meteor."

"That's nothing more than a rumor," Hubouri said. "And even if there's one man in the Alliance army who's studied dark magic in secret, that doesn't mean Nethergarde is full of them…"

"Rarely is a rumor entirely without foundation…" Volis said.

Erland cast a meaningful glance toward Jace. Jace shot him a hard look.

"Who knows?" Dunn said. "Honestly, I'm hoping it was Alliance forces."

After confirming there was no gnoll ambush lying in wait, the group relaxed a little and continued south.

But just when the mood had settled back into something like a normal journey, that evening they came across three more demon corpses piled together in a hollow.

The puzzling thing was that these too had been burned to a crisp by shadow magic — shriveled and mummified, like dried corpses. This was less a trap and more some kind of eerie sacrificial ritual.

"This time I'm certain the gnolls didn't do it," Obrik said.

"Why?" Erland asked.

Obrik gazed at the enormous charred demon corpse lying at the bottom of the pile. "That's a Doomguard. How many gnolls does it take to kill a Doomguard?"

Volis said in surprise, "Burned like that, I couldn't even tell. Don't those demons have wings?"

"The tattered black things underneath are the wings," Hubouri pointed out, "and they don't belong to the two smaller demons."

"The other two are Hellhounds," Erland said. "Let's go take a look at the big one, the Doomguard… we might find something."

Obrik nodded and pointed at Dunn. "Hunter, you and Urk keep watch up top. Hubouri, Erland, Volis, and I will go down and examine these corpses."

"Understood," Dunn said.

Jace glanced down into the pit and asked Dunn beside him, "What kind of valuables can you still find on a Doomguard corpse like this?"

Dunn said, "These demons aren't simple monsters. They like to wear magical accessories, and sometimes they carry rather fine weapons. After a burn like this, most things are probably ruined — especially their armor — but I'm sure there are some magic items that can withstand the flames. Find even a few and it's worth the trouble. As for those two Hellhounds… don't get your hopes up. Hellhounds aren't worth a thing even when they're intact, let alone burned like that."

"At least these fel horns are still whole," Erland called from below. "Shadow itself couldn't burn them through — they're practically the same as fresh ones."

"I'll chop these horns off." Volis hefted a woodcutter's axe and was about to swing when Obrik immediately grabbed the haft. "Careful… ma'am. Don't hack at them like that. Use a blade and cut them free."

"The magic that destroyed these demon corpses was immensely powerful," Gaxulis said to Sulenna. "And they deliberately burned the bodies like this, mistress."

Sulenna mused for a moment. "Whoever they are, why burn these corpses? Did the gnolls find them harmful — polluting their territory?"

"But I agree with Obrik," Hunter Dunn said. "How could gnolls possibly kill a Doomguard?"

"What if there were hundreds of them?" Sulenna asked.

"If there were a gnoll tribe that large, they could march straight west and take Stonewatch," Dunn said. "Why hide in this forest? So… could it be orcs?"

Sulenna said, "But the orcs have plenty of warlocks. They should need demon corpses more than anyone…"

In truth, Jace also strongly suspected the orcs.

At this point in Azeroth, everyone in the Alliance took it as fact that orcs and demons were thick as thieves — that the mighty demons had helped the orcs invade Azeroth, and the orcs in turn used lesser demons as accomplices to burn, kill, and pillage.

But in reality, many of the orcs who had come to Azeroth had begun to reject the demons' control as the fel blood in their veins gradually quieted. That they were also hunting demons in this forest was no surprise.

But why burn the corpses to this extent?

Erland hauled a bag of demon horns over to Jace's side. Jace peered inside and immediately caught the half-dead stench of charred demon — nothing at all like the surging fel energy a living demon gave off.

"A Doomguard has a lot of horns… running into a burned-up one like this could still fetch several gold coins," Erland said. "There seem to be a few magical gem ornaments down there too. Obrik's working on prying them off — that'll be another purse's worth."

"Not bad luck," Dunn said. "We haven't even properly entered the Wetlands yet…"

"Will we get arrested by the Lakeshire guards for hauling this stuff back?" Jace couldn't help asking.

"No problem, Urk." Dunn turned his head from the side. "There've been folk who walked right into Lakeshire carrying a demon's head held high. The Alliance army doesn't forbid scavengers from dealing with demon corpses — just don't let your demon servants parade around in front of them in broad daylight."

Jace nodded, then noticed Morfell staring fixedly into the depths of the forest, her expression wary.

"What is it?"

Morfell said, "There seems to be… fel energy, mistress."
