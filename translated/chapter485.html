Chapter 485: Ownership of the Spoils

After the orcs withdrew, all that remained was a charred felguard corpse.

A demon that had stood as sturdy as a black stone statue moments ago was now a lump of blackened cinders shimmering with a purple glow. Its armor, its gear — nothing of any worth left to salvage.

"These orcs truly don't know the value of things," Obrik said, watching the burning demon corpse with regret.

That orc warlock just now had indeed been powerful. Summoning such a potent felguard had cost him no more than a couple of coughs — it felt as though he still had strength to spare to summon another.

If he hadn't been looking out for his clansmen's lives, if he'd thrown all caution to the wind and fought tooth and nail, he could very well have given their whole squad a world of trouble.

"At least we still have a sword," Dunn said, pointing at Morophil's blade.

That Cataclysm's Edge.

Morophil was quite a bit shorter than Jace, but this red greatsword was even taller than Jace. The felguard had wielded it one-handed with ease, swinging it freely, but the succubus could barely manage to grip the guard with both hands and prop it on the ground — wrapping her fingers around the hilt was a struggle.

Still, using the sword as a point of comparison, Jace felt as though Morophil had grown a little taller. A rough guess put her at about a meter and a half — though that might have been an illusion, and he had no ruler on hand to check.

After all, he himself had grown a bit compared to before, so by contrast it was hard to notice the succubus's change.

"This sword is mine." Morophil puffed out her chest. "As the spoils of the Mak'gora victory."

"Tell me, Miss Demon," Dunn said. "Do you really think that blade suits your build? You'd be better off selling it and splitting the coin with your master. Look at you — you can barely swing the thing."

The succubus lifted the hilt with both hands, whirled it in a great arc, and brought it down on the ground with a heavy thud. Then she asked, "Where exactly is the difficulty, hunter? Don't forget how this demon died…"

"This is indeed part of our spoils," Obrik said, turning to the others. "How did we handle the money the last time we ran into a situation like this?"

Surena said, "We've never run into trouble like this before, Obrik."

Hubouri nodded. "Last time, in the area to the west, we ran into a few imps. And before that, to the northeast, we ran into a band of gnolls."

"The spoils weren't worth a copper, so we took nothing — the packs were all used for demon goods," Volis murmured. "Those bits of trinket jewelry we found that the gnolls had collected — we threw them away afterward, because I was the one who carried them the whole way… damn it all…"

"So this sword really is special," Obrik said. "If we can get it to Ironforge or Stromgarde, someone will certainly pay a high price for it. What do you think, Erlan? You're the one who handles selling our goods."

"I'm not sure — probably around thirty gold coins," Erlan said.

Gakres seemed about to offer an opinion, but Surena shot him a look and he swallowed his words.

Dunn drew a sharp breath. "Now that's a proper price."

"How much did you sell that last felguard's sword for, Mr. Drudgemoor?" Hubouri asked.

"Right — that black sword with the skulls on it was a fine-looking blade too," Obrik said.

Erlan said, "That one was slightly shorter than this one. Sold it at Booty Bay for fourteen gold coins. But in my opinion, I'm also in favor of the sword going to Lady Morophil."

The hunter glanced at him sideways. "Have you been charmed by that succubus?"

"I have no such interest," Morophil said.

The hunter sighed lightly. Surena said, "I side with Erlan's opinion too. After all, if not for her and Yurk, I thought we'd have to fight that whole band of orcs. Do you want to go up against that orc warlock who summoned a felguard so effortlessly?"

"Of course you side with Drudgemoor's opinion," Dunn said, pointing at the members of the Dagger Society. "And you're all Stormwind folk from the same hometown… what nonsense. I don't want to argue with you, but if it sells for thirty gold coins, each of us gets an extra four gold. I'm willing to give Yurk a bigger cut — I'll take two gold, let him take ten, something like that."

"Regardless, this fight was carried by the succubus alone," Hubouri said. "The rest of us couldn't help in the slightest."

"If you'd stepped up and offered to duel that felguard just now, I'd have supported the sword going to you too, Mr. Dunn Felimon," Erlan added.

"Ah… damn it," Dunn said helplessly. "That's actually a fair point."

"Here, I'll put up my commission to share among everyone," Jace said. "In exchange for this sword. How about that?"

"That settles things neatly — I'm for it," Dunn said, straightening up.

"There's absolutely no need, Mr. Yurk," Hubouri said. "This is what you and your demon servant rightfully earned. I feel I contributed nothing at all to these spoils, and I have no intention of taking a share of this money."

"Then your share goes to me," Dunn said.

"Suit yourself," Hubouri said.

"I can go without," Obrik said. "Running into this band of Bleeding Hollow orcs and coming out whole and unhurt — I'm already more than satisfied. As for how to handle this sword, whatever solution you all agree on, I'll accept."

"I'd like some," Volis said. "Getting a share would of course be best."

"I'll pass," Erlan said. "I agree with our tall priest here."

"I'll pass too," Surena said. "I'm grateful to Lady Morophil as it is."

Obrik said, "Then, Mr. Yurk — take four gold coins from your final commission. The hunter and our Volis each get two gold coins. You take the sword. How does that sound?"

"No problem," Jace said.

"What about the priest's share? I thought you said it was mine?" Dunn asked.

"You might as well settle down, Dunn," Hubouri said helplessly.

The hunter heaved another long sigh. "I actually thought you meant it."

The group appeared to have reached an agreement. Jace said, "Thank you all."

He gazed at the Cataclysm's Edge propped in Morophil's hands. This was absolutely no demon weapon that a few gold coins could settle.

Paying so little was definitely taking advantage of the fact that these mercenaries still didn't know enough about the weapons demons used.

If, in the future, these hired swords ever had the chance to enter Draenor — or the shattered Outland… they would realize that demon weapons came in several grades too.

The group packed up and prepared to move on. Dunn was still crouched by the felguard's corpse, trying to find anything else useful.

Erlan caught Jace's eye when the others weren't looking and gave him a meaningful look. Jace understood — the man had definitely lowballed the price on purpose.

Morophil came over to Jace's side, dragging the red greatsword with a satisfied expression. It was hard to call the picture coordinated, yet there was a strange sense of harmony to it.

After all, that sinister crimson went rather well with her skin tone. And the greatsword's length paired with her height created a rather off-kilter contrast.

"Thank you for spending big, master, to help me win such a gorgeous prize," Morophil said. "From now on I promise to serve you even harder…"

Jace lowered an eyebrow. "Sounds like you're the one coming out ahead either way."

The succubus smiled. Jace said, "Be that as it may, you're strong, Morophil. If I'd really had to step in, I can't guarantee how it would have turned out."

"I know I'm strong, master." Morophil hugged the sword with a soft laugh, then said, "Before the fight, I was fairly confident already — but I didn't expect you to care so much whether I lived or died…"

"Of course I wanted you to win. Otherwise we'd still have to deal with that band of orcs, plus that felguard," Jace said.

"But from where I'm standing, you weren't worried about handling the orcs — you were worried about me." Morophil narrowed her eyes slightly. "I'm very good at reading my master's mind. Still, I owe my victory to my master's concern, and the energy you replenished in me… without that, how could I have become this strong?"

Morophil muttered this to herself, close enough for others to hear. Volis smiled — of course she knew what "replenishing energy" meant to a succubus.

"And because you slipped on a Curse of Recklessness too," Jace murmured.

"Ah, you noticed that," Morophil said, the corner of her mouth lifting. "That felguard… probably never felt it itself."
