## Chapter 486: The Treasure-Hunting Hound

The scavenging party continued south for several days, following Obrik deep into the swamp. Along the way they found several felguard corpses and a doomguard corpse.

The demons of the Burning Legion all bore tattoos of one kind or another. Back on the battlefield Jace hadn't paid much attention to those fel markings — he hadn't been involved in handling the corpses after the fighting, after all. It was only now, coming into direct contact with these demon carcasses, that he finally had a chance to examine them up close.

Beyond those, there were also some less valuable felhounds, or the bloated, flesh-heavy corpses of Mo'arg that held nothing worth much. The scavenging party had already gotten too proud to bother collecting those demons' blood and organs.

By now Jace was starting to find the surroundings familiar. They couldn't be far from Stonard.

Everyone in the group was far more tense than before. After all, they were drawing close to the Blasted Lands. The dangers they might face here likely wouldn't be a scattered handful of demons anymore.

Erlan said the treasure they'd gathered this time was worth more than double their last haul — especially that doomguard corpse they'd found. Its pendants and the spell-marked skin it carried would be worth a good deal of money.

And by this point, Priest Hubouri and Erlan were both loaded down with bags and packs, Jace and the female dwarf were helping carry some too, and Obrik himself had his own substantial load. Only the hunter had stayed light all this time, helping navigate the way — though he too carried a pouch stuffed with no small amount of jewelry.

The whole party was heavily burdened now, and the pace of travel had slowed. The sinister aura emanating from the demon weapons and hides never stopped swirling around them, and over time it wore on them terribly.

At midday the group was resting on a hillside. Obrik noticed morale had dipped and said, "I have fairly reliable intelligence that there's something left behind by an eredar warlock in this area. A little farther east and we'll be there."

Hearing "Eredar warlock," Dunn perked up a bit. "I can keep going."

"Our food's running a bit low — enough for maybe two days," the female dwarf said.

Obrik said, "After we finish up to the east, we can rest at the Stonard garrison for a while. According to the map, we're already close — two or three hours' walk at most."

Jace cautioned, "There's a lake at the far eastern edge of the swamp called the Pool of Tears, Master Obrik. I trust you know what's in there."

"The ruins of a demon temple," Obrik said. "But the place I'm investigating is a good distance from where that legendary temple is supposed to be."

He spread the map out in the middle of the group and said, "The spot I've identified is only about twenty or thirty kilometers from here, while the Pool of Tears is far off to the east. As long as we move fast enough, we can reach it by evening, take a look around, search the place, and the moment we have results we head straight back to Stonard. Even if we rest the night, we can arrive by tomorrow afternoon. Food and supplies are more than sufficient."

Dunn nodded and said, "If we really can find relics of an eredar warlock, that would be best. I'm just worried the Kirin Tor got there first and cleaned everything out."

"Right now the Kirin Tor's attention is on the Blasted Lands," Obrik said. "They hold Nethergarde Keep, after all — which gives us our opening. This intelligence wasn't easy to come by... I promise you, if we run into any danger we can't handle, we leave immediately."

"Isn't that what we came for? To go deep into the Black Morass — not to turn back halfway," Hubouri said. "Let's get moving."

The others had nothing to say to that, since the priest was right. And Jace was being paid for this too — if they hadn't been planning to deal with more troublesome opponents, they never would have thought to bring in a helper like him.

After all, for dealing with gnolls or the beasts of the swamp, Dunn and Hubouri could handle it easily enough.

Jace was confident that even a mediocre orc squad could be easily ambushed and wiped out by the others in this group — with warlocks who commanded Fear, warlock servants who could enthrall, a full-fledged mage who could control the battlefield, a crossbow expert, and a formidable priest who could hold his own.

As long as they didn't get too close to the Pool of Tears, Jace wasn't worried about running into trouble in the Black Morass.

Long ago, the Atal'ai troll priests who had been driven out of Zul'Gurub for attempting to summon the blood god Hakkar had come to the depths of this swamp and built the Temple of Atal'Hakkar to worship Hakkar, continuing their attempts to summon the evil god within.

The stirrings of the evil god drew the attention of the green dragon queen, Ysera, who sank the temple into the lake, forming the Pool of Tears.

She had also sent green dragons to try to stop the trolls' efforts, but not only did this fail to completely halt the trolls — the dragons who ventured deep into the temple were instead corrupted by the evil god's power, their minds lost, trapped within.

Some green dragon guardians wandered outside the temple, but as long as they didn't get too close, as Obrik had said, they shouldn't cross paths.

The group continued east. By evening, Obrik gazed out at a stretch of low-lying marshland ahead, glanced at the map, and murmured, "Should be right about here. But..."

"Run into a problem?" Jace asked. "Who told you an eredar warlock died here?"

Obrik said, "I know a gryphon rider from Ironforge in the Dwarven District. He told me that on a previous patrol he'd encountered a hidden eredar warlock right here. He and two other gryphon riders killed the eredar, then returned to Stonard. Not long ago at all."

"Should be right about here — he said there was a stretch of low-lying marshland, and then..." The mage frowned. "Would the animals here eat a demon's corpse? Maybe. At least they wouldn't carry off the things he left behind."

"Sazeno," Jace asked the imp on his shoulder, "can you locate a mage's position? Can you track the location of magic items?"

"You'd be better off finding a felhound for that!" Sazeno said.

"A Felhound," Obrik asked Jace. "Can anyone summon a felhound to help?"

Surena clearly wasn't keen on it, and Erlan didn't know how at all.

Jace figured if it came to it, he'd just summon one himself temporarily — but just then the female dwarf stepped forward.

"I can," she said. "My last felhound died in the Wetlands. I never summoned another one after that — it's not convenient, walking around human lands with a felhound in tow."

"Then you can try summoning one now, if you still have the mana, Volis," Erlan said. "We have experience dealing with felhounds — we'll keep watch for you."

"Then you'd better keep a good eye on me," Volis said, and immediately began incanting the felhound summoning spell. Once the felhound materialized, the warlocks quickly cornered it, helping Volis bind it with the naming spell to complete the summoning.

Once the binding was done, Volis looked at the felhound and said, "Honestly, I have no idea how to command a felhound to find magic items. I only know how to make them help me kill a mage."

Obrik coughed. Volis hastily added, "I mean, troll witch doctors... you know there are frost trolls in Dun Morogh — real trouble."

Sazeno immediately said, "You can learn a little Demonic from me, dwarf warlock!"

He said in Demonic: "Seek enchanted treasures, seek the corpse of the Man'ari!"

Then he explained the meaning of both phrases in Common, and helped Volis practice. Once Volis had gotten them roughly right, she called the felhound's name and repeated the commands.

Sure enough, the felhound immediately began sniffing around with its tentacles. These monsters had been enslaved by the demon lords in their homeland of Annihilan to seek out arcane sources, so they were naturally adept at tracking warlocks and magic treasures. They quickly picked up a trail and bounded off excitedly.

"Follow it!" Obrik said.
