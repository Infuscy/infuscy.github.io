## Chapter 487: Depths of the Dark Marsh

The Felhound ran fast — and kept getting faster, as though its master's command couldn't compete with the lure of its quarry.

The group tore along behind it, lugging their bags and packs, swords great and small, splashing through stream after stream and bog after bog, until they were all gasping for breath. At last they followed the Felhound to a dark ravine veiled by mud-caked trees.

The Felhound let out a rapid string of "kukuku" sounds, its two tentacles whipping back and forth in excitement.

Volis stared blankly for a moment. The male succubus Gaxulis said in a low voice, "It senses arcane energy inside. That's the sound it makes to report to its mistress when it's found something good."

"So our target should be in there," Obrik said.

"Hsssh." Dunn frowned. "There's something else up ahead."

"What?" Volis craned her neck to peer inside, but given her height — looking up on tiptoes did her no good whatsoever.

Dunn waved for everyone to keep low, then made a shushing gesture toward the Felhound and beckoned for them all to follow him forward.

Jace crouched and followed Dunn through the undergrowth step by step. After covering some forty or fifty meters, the hunter moved beside a bush, steadied himself against a tree, and carefully raised his head to glance into the distance. His expression immediately shifted to startled surprise.

Dunn ducked back down and looked at the group behind him. "A demon?" he asked quietly.

Jace pointed to himself. Dunn moved aside to let him take a look. Jace raised his head and immediately understood why Dunn thought he'd seen a demon.

It was a massive, humanoid figure clad in dark-brown armor, but the bare skin visible beneath the armor was a bright, almost luminous green, not unlike fel energy.

But no — that wasn't a demon. That was a dragonkin. A green-scaled dragonkin!

The lower half of the dragonkin was sunk into the bog, so all they could see was its upper half reared high — which was why Dunn, not terribly familiar with dragonkin, had mistaken it for a demon. But the golden-spined ridge on its back and its reptilian head left no doubt as to its identity. Jace could not possibly be mistaken.

Green dragonkin weren't all that different from black dragonkin apart from color — if anything, the library texts said they were slightly more slender overall.

But the black dragonkin Jace had encountered before had been a female mage, whereas this green dragonkin was a powerful guardian, clad in heavy armor and heavily built — far taller and more muscular than that black dragonkin mage.

There shouldn't be any dragonkin this far out. According to the map, they were still nearly a hundred kilometers from the Lake of Tears. Black Marsh was vast — no less expansive than the great Wetlands to the north. Why would a dragonkin happen to be here?

But then, as Jace looked closely at the staff clutched in the dragonkin's hand, he understood.

That staff was clearly not the dragonkin's own. It was crafted in the Eredar style, its head a cluster of pale purple shadow crystals, and the dragonkin was greedily siphoning the shadow energy from within.

The demon carcass had drawn this dragonkin — a guardian of the Lake of Tears — here. Except these green dragonkin had long since been corrupted into madness by the evil god's power. The very guardians the queen had sent to disrupt the evil god's summoning ritual had become its defenders.

He ducked down and whispered into Obrik's ear: "A dragonkin. Holding an Eredar staff."

Obrik nodded. "Then kill it."

Hubouri said, "The legends say green dragons are benevolent creatures of the dream..."

"I know," Obrik said. "But the green dragonkin here are unquestionably maddened monsters. A year ago a Kirin Tor mage investigating orcs in this area was eaten by a dragonkin. Don't hesitate."

"Dream and reality always have their differences, priest," Dunn said.

"Let's hope that staff fetches a good price," Erland muttered. "I didn't come here expecting to run into this sort of thing."

"Dragonkin aren't dragons. Nothing to fear," Volis said. "Once, malevolent red dragonkin tried to aid the orcs in assaulting Dun Algaz. In practice, these creatures aren't much tougher than a tough orc."

"Then let's move. Fast," Obrik said. "Its armor is heavy — use magic as much as possible. Casters, this one's on us. Dunn, go draw its attention."

Dunn nodded. Jace signaled Morfell to Phase Shift and close in. Watching her drag a greatsword into the shadow was deeply unsettling, but over on the other side Gaxulis had already slipped into stealth on Sulenna's orders.

However Gaxulis fared, Sulenna and he worked well together.

The group crept closer to the dragonkin, which was frantically draining the power from the shadow gem. Its attention seemed utterly consumed by the staff's magic, blind to the world around it.

Dunn had stood up on the far side without so much as a reaction from the dragonkin.

"Hey!" Dunn shouted. The dragonkin suddenly looked that way. A crossbow bolt hissed through the damp, heavy air and buried itself in the gap of the dragonkin's waist armor.

The dragonkin clutched a green glaive in one hand and cradled the Eredar staff with the other as it rose to its full height, its enormous armored lower body hauling free of the muck.

It charged silently toward Dunn. Sulenna shouted out the incantation for Curse of Agony. The dragonkin heard the commotion from this direction and whipped its head around — but the curse had already taken hold. It hissed in pain, its teeth grinding audibly, the arm clutching the staff even beginning to tremble.

The dragonkin's throat bulged, purple venom dribbling from its mouth as though it meant to spew it toward the warlocks.

Suddenly a shout rang out from behind it... All they could see was the red greatsword lifted high — Morfell herself was completely invisible! But the blade came crashing down, slicing the dragonkin clean in half at the waist!

The priest, who'd been about to shield the warlocks with Holy magic, stood frozen in the bog, staring at the towering dragonkin split in two and toppling before him.

The front half of the dragonkin howled and thrashed wildly against the ground, while its rear half began convulsing uncontrollably. Morfell pounced again, swinging the blade and hacking the humanoid upper half clean off, then struck once more, cleaving the dragonkin's head from its body.

After those two strikes, none of the pieces so much as twitched.

Only now did the dragonkin's venom-drooling mouth get sealed by a sheet of frost — Obrik had just lowered his casting hand.

"Wh-what the... fire?" He stared blankly for a moment, glancing at the panting Morfell, then at the cross-section of the dragonkin's corpse, scorched black by the demon blade's crimson glow.

"Back when Dethmoor introduced Mr. Urk to me at the tavern, he spoke particularly highly of the Lady Morfell," he said, lowering his staff. "He felt that this succubus alone was worth more than ten gold in commission. I thought he was just boasting."

"Having seen it now, he wasn't exaggerating in the slightest. I hope we'll have the chance to work together again in the future, Mr. Urk. Lady Morfell."

Jace looked at the full-fledged mage and nodded, somewhat dazed — he'd just been winding up a Shadow Bolt of his own.

"When have I ever lied to you, Obrik?" Erland said with a touch of pride.

"Wait, everyone." Dunn picked up the Eredar staff left behind by the dragonkin and gazed toward the deep valley shrouded in thick mist. "There's more movement in there..."
