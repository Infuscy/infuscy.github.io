## Chapter 489: The Withered Forest

The forest had been swallowed by yellow mist — mushrooms everywhere, and more still growing. Jace knew everyone here would have been poisoned to one degree or another.

Up ahead, Volis had slowed her sprint, but the ground-shaking footsteps behind them showed no sign of easing.

Jace swerved around a mushroom that appeared to have died off quickly, and as he did, he glanced back over his shoulder — and saw a swollen eye swaying in the deep forest shadows, fixed on him.

The green scales around the eye were covered in white-matted, festering sores. The shadow lurched forward like a mass of rotting mud in constant decay...

The green dragon had lost one wing. Its remaining wing hung high and lifeless, like the tattered sail of a ghost ship adrift in the fog. Jace understood now why this dragon didn't exist in the game — the creature clearly didn't have much longer to live. In another decade or so it would have sunk into this corrupted Swamp of Sorrows without a trace...

But fighting such a head-on battle — a monster that spread poisonous mushrooms across the whole forest — would still be plenty stupid. He gripped his staff and pushed to keep pace with the others. The mushrooms growing around them were already thinning. Safety was just ahead...

He didn't know how long he'd run. His sprint had turned mechanical, his legs moving on their own without feeling. The brawny priest beside him was running himself into coughing fits, his face waxen yellow. The dragon behind them had fallen silent — no more unearthly howls, no more incoherent snarling, no more rumbling footsteps, no more snapping trees... It was only when Volis, at the front of the pack, tripped and plunged into a pond with a splash, followed by shriek after shriek of panic, that the swamp finally registered how quiet it had become.

Dunn hauled the dwarf out of the muck — she'd nearly drowned in knee-deep water — and leaned against a tree, breathing hard.

Erland plopped down beside them, counted heads, then let out a relieved breath. "What the hell was that thing?"

"A green dragon," Jace said. "A real green dragon."

"Some kind of mushroom demon?" Sulenna asked.

At the word "mushroom," Jace thought back to the fungi that had been sprouting everywhere in the reeking forest, and his stomach suddenly turned.

He wasn't the only one. Obrik, who'd been silent the whole time, finally couldn't hold back any longer and crouched by a nearby stream to vomit. Hubouri said, "Those mushrooms were poisonous."

He pulled two opaque bottles from his pack. "Anyone feeling unwell? I have antidotes here."

"I've got anti-toxin as well," Erland said, coming up to Jace. "How are you holding up? This anti-toxin was brewed by Ursula."

Jace shook his head. "I'm fine. Go ask Volis — I think she picked quite a few of those mushrooms."

Volis was indeed in bad shape. Ever since being pulled out of the water she'd been lying there, eyes fixed wide on the ceiling of the canopy, gasping for breath and muttering something under her breath.

After drinking two bottles of anti-toxin she finally began to recover, blinking rapidly. Hubouri stayed at her side the whole time, murmuring prayers and pressing his hand to her forehead, neck, and chest — it reminded Jace of the time he'd spent in the Cathedral of Light, when the priests had purged necrotic poison from his body.

Just then, a low, squat shadow emerged from the underbrush — the Felhound Volis had summoned. The thing was still alive!

Then again, Felhounds had absurdly high resistance to every kind of magical affliction. Running through a field of poisonous mushrooms should've been no worse than a stroll for it.

Except this Felhound was dragging something behind it — the Eredar warlock's skull!

The skull was enormous, bigger than half the Felhound's body, and the creature had carried it all this way without being overtaken by the corrupted green dragon.

Obrik moved to snatch the skull from the Felhound's jaws, but the creature refused to give it up.

"That hound will only hand treasure over to its master, Master Obrik," Gaxulis said. "We'll have to wait for Lady Volis to recover and command it herself."

Gaxulis's voice was still remarkably steady. A demon was a demon, after all.

"Benevolent dragons of the dream, huh. I reckon I'll be having nightmares for a few nights after this," Dunn said, sitting beneath a mud-soaked tree and catching his breath.

Hubouri, who'd just gotten Volis to sleep, looked up with a dazed expression at the hunter. "I'd only heard the legends myself."

"We have no supplies left at all. There's no time, and no conditions to wait for Volis to wake up," Obrik said. "We rest a moment, then we have to move immediately toward Stonard. And there's no telling whether that creature might come after us again at any moment..."

With that, he drank a mana potion, clutched a cloth bag, and began to incanting. A faint arcane glow gathered in his hands. As he cast, the bag swelled again and again.

"I don't eat conjured food," Hubouri said. "I'd rather go hungry."

"Just to give you all a bit more strength to keep walking," Obrik said, pulling out a steaming loaf of bread from the bag. Dunn eyed the bread and couldn't help swallowing.

"Why overthink it?" Dunn took a bite of the bread, then said in surprise, "There's raspberry jam in this."

"I like raspberries," Obrik said, glancing at the others. "Anyone else want some?"

"You know raspberry season ended months ago, right, Dunn?" Hubouri reminded him. "Harvest Festival was two months back."

"I not only know raspberries don't grow right now — I even know normal bread doesn't bake itself out of a mage's hands, you numbskull," Dunn said with a frown.

"I'll have a piece," Jace said.

Obrik nodded and handed Jace a loaf. Jace took it and found the quality remarkably good — springy and firm, stuffed with jam and topped with a layer of nuts. It was nearly on par with the bread sold in Dalaran.

None of the adventurers here didn't know that conjured food had no real nutritional value, and the stamina it provided was just an arcane effect — but this was no time to be picky.

Obrik passed the bread to Sulenna and Erland as well. The aroma of fresh bread wafted everywhere, easing the tension little by little.

"What about the Felhound?" Sulenna asked, biting into her bread. "We can't bring it back to Stonard with us."

"When the time comes, the priest and I will go in and find supplies," Obrik said. "The rest of you can find a place to rest outside with Volis. That way there's no need to worry about the succubus being spotted by the Kirin Tor mages inside."
