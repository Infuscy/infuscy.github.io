## Chapter 490: Generous Rewards

A sleepless night and a long march. By the next morning, the few who'd remained outside Stonard were exhausted.

There were even signs of rain. Dunn and Erlan skillfully rigged a makeshift shelter from vines and sodden branches — a stinking, ramshackle lean-to — just in time to keep the coming downpour from turning the whole group into drowned rats.

Jace, Surena, and Erlan were holding up fine, but if Volis, in her current state, got caught in the rain, she'd likely be bedridden with another serious illness.

The group huddled beneath the shelter. Dunn stood some distance away, staring in the direction of Stonard, lost in thought. Surena murmured, "I've never seen a dragon before."

"Neither have I," Erlan said.

"I bet Jace has," Surena said.

"I have," Jace said. "But never a green one."

A few choice curses rang out from Dunn in the distance — he was getting impatient. Jace asked, curiously, "What's got him so worked up?"

"He's always like that," Surena said.

Erlan hesitated, then said, "Dunn's out here earning money to heal his eldest son. His boy fought in the Battle of Hillsbrad and was wounded in the fighting. He still hasn't recovered."

"Didn't Gilneas refuse to join the Alliance army?" Surena asked.

"It did, but a small contingent joined the Alliance," Erlan said. "But that's got nothing to do with his son. His son didn't go as part of the Gilneas force — he enlisted in the Lordaeron army. So you see the problem, Surena. He can't go to the king or a lord asking for money to heal his boy. He has to earn it himself."

Just then, Dunn was walking back toward the shelter. Erlan lowered his voice. "Don't bring this up around him."

Jace and Surena nodded.

Before long, Dunn dropped down beside Erlan with a grunt.

"Those two — Obrik and Hubouri — aren't going to run off with that staff, are they?" he complained.

"The rest of the goods are still right here with me. What've you got to worry about?" Erlan said, patting the bags beside him.

"I shouldn't have let him take that staff," Dunn said. "Everything should've stayed here where we could all keep an eye on it."

"Well, in the end it all gets handed over to me and Obrik to find a buyer," Erlan said.

"I know you're a good lad, Erlan Drudgemoor," Dunn said.

With that, he glanced at Jace, lips pressed tight in displeasure, and licked his teeth.

"Obrik picks people he can trust," Erlan said, draping another cloak over the snoring Volis beside him. "Anyone who feels off, he won't take. Trust his eye for people."

Dunn took off his hat and scratched his head. "As long as I get a few gold out of it in the end, like last time, I don't care about the rest."

The group ate a bit more of Obrik's conjured bread. Now that it had gone cold, Jace could clearly sense the faint arcane tinge in it — was it about to expire?

Fortunately, it wasn't long before the priest and Obrik appeared in the distance across the bog. The two of them ran through the rain in their cloaks, carrying several bags of baked flatbreads and a small bottle of strong mead.

Everyone took a small swig, divided up the flatbreads, and prepared to head west, back toward Darkshire.

Given their current food reserves, retracing their steps across all of the Black Morass and cutting through the gnoll-infested forests to reach Lakeshire at the foot of the Redridge Mountains was hardly realistic. And with the weather as it was, hunting game in the forest wouldn't be easy either.

What's more, their bags were already packed to the brim with demon materials. Even if they came across more carcasses, they had no strength left to carry them.

Gakres, powerfully built and able to replenish his magic at will, naturally took on the task of carrying Volis.

A few days later the group reached Deadwind Pass. They rested for a while beside a dry riverbed a few hundred meters from the main road.

Jace gazed down into the valley below and thought that after he returned to Stormwind, if Grede was back, he'd come see if they could harvest some Essence of Undeath from this area.

From here he could see, at the very edge of his vision, the ruins of a still-intact village. It was tiny — maybe seven or eight houses tucked into a small mountain hollow.

That village probably hadn't been destroyed directly by the Karazhan Cataclysm. More likely, the environment within Deadwind Pass had grown ever more hostile after the explosion, tainted by shadow energy, and the people living here had all moved away.

After all, even Duskwood, on the other side of the mountain, had been reduced to that state — this place would be far worse.

To find ghosts who'd perished to the dark energy, he'd probably have to press on south, deep into the valley.

Just looking at it from here, he could feel the heavy, brooding darkness within.

After resting in Deadwind Pass for a while, Volis finally woke up. She banished the felhound and kept the eredar skull. Obrik found an enchanted pouch to wrap it in, containing the evil energy it radiated.

Back in Darkshire, the group booked a large room and tallied up the fruits of the expedition: the loot stripped from one charred and one intact doomguard corpse, the felcloth and armor fragments taken from several felguards, a demon serrated blade, and a fairly intact demon helmet.

On top of that, there were several sheaves of Mo'arg and doomguard rune skin, fel horns, a dimmed felguard heart...

The longer they counted, the more excitement and glee spread across everyone's faces. The exhaustion of days of hard travel was swept away in an instant.

Erlan did a quick calculation and said, "Even without counting Obrik's eredar staff or that eredar demon's skull, each of us should get at least eight gold just from splitting the spoils. Counting the blade, Volis and Dunn could get ten gold..."

"And more than that, for sure!" Dunn said, immediately excited.

Obrik said, "After we get back, Erlan and I will head straight to Booty Bay to ask the price on these two items, then go to Dalaran and inquire there."

"Mr. Yurk, I'll set your commission aside separately. In the end you should receive more than fourteen or fifteen gold — and that's after deducting the four gold advance..."

Jace nodded. "Thank you all."

Obrik clapped him on the shoulder. "No need to thank us, Mr. Yurk. I think you and your demon handmaiden far exceeded my original expectations. You've more than earned this price. I wasn't just being polite before — I hope we can work together again!"

"There were far fewer intact demon corpses this time than last," Surena said with a sigh. "If we come again next time without heading south of the Blasted Lands, we probably won't find a thing."

"I'll find some other way to make money," Obrik said. "There's always opportunity."
