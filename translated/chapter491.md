## Chapter 491: Just the Last Materials

After splitting up with Obrik's group following a meal in Goldshire, Erland, Sulenna, and Jace each took a portion of the demon materials they needed to sell and returned to Stormwind on separate days, so as not to draw too much attention.

After all, while Stormwind didn't explicitly ban the trade of demon materials, three people hauling a pile of them into the Mage Quarter and marching openly into The Slaughtered Lamb was a bit too brazen.

They agreed to meet in the basement. On the appointed day, they laid everything out across the floor. All they had to do was wait for Jalen Ailro to arrive, and they could sort out the materials needed to make the robe.

Winter had set in and the temperature had dropped fast. Down in the basement, Sulenna was shivering.

"Should we light the fire?" she asked, unable to help herself.

"It might affect these demon materials, Sulenna. Want me to fetch you another cloak to wrap up in?" Erland asked.

"Forget it. Let's just get on with it," Sulenna said reluctantly.

Jace looked over the demon skins laid out on the ground — different colors, different textures, each marked with different runes — and asked Erland beside him, "These things should fetch a pretty penny. How are we splitting the money?"

"A pretty penny, no question," Erland said. "But don't worry. We'll just take a little less on our end. I'll make up the difference and see to it that the other four get their full share. The three of us can work it out among ourselves easy enough."

As the three of them sorted through the felcloth they'd taken from the Felguards, Jalen came down the passage. He saw the rows of demonic sigils laid out on the floor and asked in surprise, "Are all these ours?"

"Take out your recipe," Erland said. "Jace said there are some patterns on it. We'll match them against the patterns and pull out the ones we need for the robe. The rest I'm going to sell. Let's get to work — it really is freezing down here."

Jalen nodded, took out the original orcish recipe, and flipped to the page on demonic sigils. Everyone gathered around to cross-reference it against the materials they'd found.

According to the illustrations in the recipe, crafting this robe required a total of two sigils.

None of the sigils on the Felguard skins matched. But they found both of the required sigils among the fel runes on the Doomguards.

"Typical. The orc warlock chieftains of the Shadow Council always did have a taste for the finest things," Erland said with a sigh. "I wonder how much demon hide bearing these two symbols will fetch."

"It's not that Doomguards are a higher rank than Felguards," Sazeno cut in at once. "It's that Doomguards are better at casting, human! The demonic marks on their bodies — many of them bolster shadow power, or strengthen shadow resistance, and so on. In any case, they're far more relevant to the kind of robe a warlock wears!"

"So why don't the orcs go after the fel runes on eredar?" Jace asked.

"They wouldn't dare provoke the eredar, would they?" Sulenna said.

"No," Sazeno said. "Eredar sorcerers consider it beneath them to use these demonic sigils to augment their power — they see it as an insult to their magical talent! Only eredar warriors use the fel-rune power of other demons to strengthen themselves!"

"Eredar warriors?" Erland asked, surprised. "The eredar have warriors? Aren't they all mages?"

"You know precious little about the eredar…" Sazeno said. "There are plenty of eredar warriors in the Burning Legion! They can even serve as vanguard. You may not have encountered the fearsome eredar Wrathguards — they're a more terrifying existence than Felguards. The armor of those eredar is engraved with fel runes that bolster their strength!"

"The eredar love battle," Morfell said, cradling the Cataclysm Blade. "But compared to the other demons of the Twisting Nether, their physical forms are not powerful. eredar warriors, after all their fel augmentations, are simply the result of an arrogant race trying to prove itself superior to all others in every conceivable way."

"Have you fought eredar before?" Jace asked.

Morfell replied, "One of my former masters wanted me to test the combat strength of one of his sisters — a winged eredar Doommaiden, immensely powerful. I was easily defeated by her, and then slain. Fortunately, the battle took place far from the Twisting Nether, so I wasn't completely destroyed…"

A silence settled over the basement. To Sulenna and Erland, the way Morfell so casually brought up her own previous death was both unsettling and uncomfortable.

"So, what are we still missing?" Jace asked Jalen.

"Necrotic essence," Jalen said. "I have no way of getting that."

"What about fire essence?"

"Galders said he could get it cheaply from dwarves," Jalen said. "I don't know the exact price, but I imagine if it's Dark Iron dwarves, obtaining fire essence would be far easier than on our end."

Jace nodded. Given Galders's familiarity with the Dark Iron dwarves, getting fire essence out of Shadowforge City would indeed be much simpler than having goblins track it down in Kalimdor.

He wondered what price Galders would charge when the time came… If the robe ended up being for him, he'd probably still need to chip in some money to make up the difference.

"So we're only short the necrotic essence to craft a Shadow Council orc warlock's robe," Erland said with a nod. "That orc warlock we ran into in the forest — I wonder if the robe he was wearing was that kind of Shadow Council robe?"

"You ran into an orc warlock in the forest?" Jalen asked.

"Yes," Sulenna said. "And he summoned a Felguard. We don't know the incantation for summoning a Felguard."

"Unfortunately, when that orc was chanting the incantation, he was too far away and his voice was too soft. We couldn't make out a thing," Jace said.

"You're not thinking of summoning a Felguard right here in our basement, are you?" Erland said, looking up. "I'm not even sure we have enough room for him to stand up straight."

Erland had a point. Conjuring a Felguard in the middle of Stormwind would be far too dangerous. If they lost control of it and it charged out of the basement, that would be the end of everything — none of the doors, locks, or bars down here could hold back a brute like that.

Then again, they didn't even know the incantation yet, let alone the conditions required for the summoning. Worrying about it was getting ahead of themselves.

Still, judging from the orc warlock's casting process, summoning a Felguard hadn't seemed difficult for him. But just because it was easy for him didn't mean it would be equally simple for the warlocks here…

Jace looked to the tailor. "I have a way to get the necrotic essence now, Jalen. But it might take a little longer. Don't worry about it for now."
