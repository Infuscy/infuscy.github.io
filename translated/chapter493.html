Chapter 493: Kalimdor and the Call of Gold

Jace was a little surprised that the Reliquary could use magic to discern the age of an artifact, but the thought only lingered in his mind for a moment — after all, using magic to investigate an item's current holder was clearly far more outrageous.

Seeming to sense Jace's reaction, the elf went on, "You may be surprised that a noble city destroyed ten thousand years ago was still crafting weapons as recently as a century ago, but we have sufficient evidence to suggest that the rule of Eldre'Thalas may have endured into the present day, Mr. Sesso. It's quite possible that this Highborne warlock has been hiding in that very city, and has survived to this day by the same token."

Jace knew this, of course. The ruins later known as Dire Maul, deep within ancient Eldre'Thalas, were still home to a great many ancient scholars. Even their leader, Prince Tortheldrin, ruled over the remnants of these surviving Highborne more than ten thousand years later.

Jace asked, "So is that Highborne warlock the so-called Dasuomola Dark-Eye?"

"Very likely," Lam Flameweaver said. "Beyond the arcane traces left by that Highborne, we've also detected traces of centaur magic on this blade, along with faint marks of troll witchcraft. If the Dame Dasuomola you mentioned truly handled it, and she is not the Highborne we're looking for, then she is either a centaur or a troll. But would you really believe the reputation of a centaur or troll warlock could spread across the whole of the Eastern Kingdoms?"

Lam Flameweaver had a point. It seemed that if they wanted to find this Dasuomola Dark-Eye, they would have to set foot on another continent — Kalimdor.

Jace had the feeling this trail was about to dead-end here. He'd been counting on finding this warlock so he could skip Mosul entirely and learn more powerful demon-binding abilities straight from her.

But then, how had Mosul made contact with Dasuomola? Or was his demonological knowledge truly something he'd figured out on his own, and was Dark-Eye nothing more than a legend beyond his reach…

Just then, the elf handed Jace the blue box containing the dagger. "Mr. Sesso, would you be interested in a trip to Kalimdor?"

Jace, still turning other matters over in his mind, looked up sharply at that.

"Huh?!"

"Accept the Holy Reliquary's commission to travel to Kalimdor and investigate that Highborne. The rewards would be exceedingly generous."

As he spoke, he produced a small purse and handed it to Jace. Jace shook it — it felt nearly empty.

Lam said, "This is the reward for providing a lead to the Reliquary — two gold, esteemed Mr. Sesso. If you can bring back sufficient additional evidence…"

Jace took the purse. Two gold and you still needed a pouch like this?

"Why not send elves? When it comes to tracking arcane traces, the Holy Reliquary is far more expert than I am."

Lam Flameweaver admitted frankly, "The Reliquary has always been short-staffed. That's our greatest disadvantage in competing with the Explorers' League of Ironforge… Simply stationing examiners in every region to gather evidence has already stretched our numbers to the limit."

"And an expedition like this requires both formidable fighting spirit and a sense of adventure. Quel'dorei who possess those qualities tend to be nobles of Silvermoon City, and such people are generally not interested in gold… However, we will try to communicate with General Sylvanas Windrunner and find you a Farstrider ranger qualified to track arcane traces to accompany you."

At the mention of a Farstrider ranger, Jace's eyes lit up.

Noticing Jace's reaction, the elf blinked and said, "But it… probably won't be Lady Vereesa Windrunner."

"Why not?" Jace asked.

"The royal court is a touch concerned about the relationship between you two," the elf said with an awkward smile.

Jace took a deep breath, sighed, and asked, "So how much can you offer?"

"Is Mr. Sesso looking for money, or some other form of compensation?"

"Money."

"If you return from Kalimdor empty-handed — ten gold. Enough to ensure you and your companions don't lose money on travel expenses. If you find relevant leads, like this dagger — at least twenty gold. If you find three or more related leads and turn them over to the Holy Reliquary — sixty gold."

"What if you bring her back alive?" Jace asked.

The elf said, "Bring her back alive… I don't know. The Reliquary never considered that possibility, nor did they tell me how much to give if you managed it. I wouldn't dare give you an answer on the spot, Mr. Sesso — but it would certainly be over a hundred gold. No, certainly far, far more than that. Bear in mind, she's a Highborne who's lived for over ten thousand years. She may have served Queen Azshara herself, lived through the great demonic invasions, perhaps even laid eyes upon the Dark Titan Sargeras's eye within the Dark Portal, witnessed the terrible Sundering… Bear in mind that the eternal Sun King ruled Quel'Thalas for only four thousand years, Mr. Sesso. You're quite a powerful shadow mage — the whole Kingdom of Quel'Thalas doesn't doubt that — but I think you shouldn't push yourself too hard."

Jace said, "I'm just asking about the various possibilities."

Lam nodded. "Of course. One more thing I must point out — that's the total compensation. If you plan to set out with an expedition party of several people, you'll need to work out the money split among yourselves."

"So the Farstrider you assign me will also need a cut?" Jace asked.

"You'll certainly have to negotiate with him or her," the elf said.

"Let me think it over and get back to you. How about that?" Jace asked.

"Of course. I'll be right here waiting for your reply, Mr. Sesso." Lam Flameweaver gave a slight bow and turned to leave the garden.

Jace sat on the ornate stone bench, watching the emissary's receding figure, feeling as though the elven Farstrider they meant to plant in his party was like a splinter he couldn't pull out — lodged firmly in his hand.

Because without an elf, there was no guarantee they could accurately track arcane traces, and he certainly had no such ability.

If he went looking for an elven mercenary out in the world, it would be far less reliable than accepting the Farstrider ranger assigned by the Holy Reliquary. After all, they would be sworn to the Sunstrider royal house and unlikely to cause any real trouble.

So Jace had to find Grede and give up another share of the money. And if Grefe wasn't willing to go, Jace hardly wanted to bring a stranger along on such a long and dangerous journey.

Let alone split the money with a stranger.

Then there was the matter of writing another letter to Vereesa, to ask about the situation within the Farstriders and find out exactly what sort of elf they intended to send to help him.

Right — had Grede come back yet, damn it?

He'd better go check the Dwarf District.
