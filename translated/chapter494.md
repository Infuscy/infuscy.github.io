## Chapter 494: Money Is All You Need

"Grede?"

Jace pounded on the door twice, stood waiting a moment, then pounded twice more.

"Grede?"

Still not back?

He was about to head downstairs to check The Pig and Whistle Inn when, with a bang, the door at the corner below the stairwell suddenly flew open.

"Hey, you've got the wrong door, Jace," Grede said, leaning against the doorframe.

Jace looked at the dwarf, whose hair and beard were matted together from days without washing, and said, "You're back."

"Of course I'm back," Grede said. "I reckon Nethergarde Keep can't afford to keep gryphons anymore — nor us Gryphon Riders. The area around the Dark Portal… has been pretty much cleaned up. So I came back."

"When did you get back?" Jace asked.

"Aren't you glad I survived being swarmed by a pack of demons and made it safely back to civilized society?!" Grede demanded.

"That's perfectly normal for you, isn't it?" Jace said, sitting down on the stairs.

"Heh, true enough. Jace Sesso can finally assess my abilities accurately," Grede said, running a hand through his curly, tangled beard. "Fancy a drink?"

"Let's go."

The two of them ambled across the canal and made their way to The Pig and Whistle Inn in Old Town. By now it was already evening.

The inn was packed. On a winter's night, everyone was bundled in padded jackets and heavy robes. The great hall reeked of firewood and ale, smoke and vapor hanging thick in the air.

Jace coughed twice from the sting, rubbed his chilled, stiff face, and found a seat. Grede dropped into the chair opposite, shook his face vigorously, and licked his lips.

He leaned back against the wall and said, "Ever since I got back from Nethergarde, I've slept for days on end. Finally got a proper rest."

"Did you get any kind of reward?" Jace asked, eyeing the barmaid hurrying over.

"What reward?" the dwarf asked.

"For killing all those demons," Jace said.

"What reward is there for that?" Grede laughed, then paused. "Ah, now that I think about it, there really should have been some kind of reward. I took a few wounds myself… but then, I am the hero who stopped the Pit Lord Azgoth. I am the 'Mountain King' who defended the walls of Nethergarde Keep. If I don't show them a few more tricks, they'll think my title of hero lacks its proper luster."

Jace couldn't help but laugh. "The real deal, Grede."

Grede leaned across and clapped Jace on the cheek. "Don't be modest here, lad. Everyone knows who killed that monster."

"What can I get you two handsome lads?" A young barmaid Jace had never seen before leaned in close.

"I'll have a glass of Northshire red wine, miss," Grede said.

Jace raised his eyebrows in surprise. "What's got into you?"

"I drank too many strange things at Nethergarde. I'm trying to find the taste of Stormwind again," the dwarf said, rapping his knuckles on the table. "You?"

"The same," Jace said. "And I'll have a pumpkin tart."

"Make that a plate of roast wild boar too," Grede added.

"That'll do it, then," Jace said.

The barmaid nodded and left. Grede glanced at the girl's backside and said, "Speaking of pumpkin — you went back to Lordaeron, didn't you, Jace?"

"I did," Jace said. "But the Kirin Tor clearly don't want me showing up in the capital city."

"They don't want you alive," Grede said, turning to fix Jace with a stare. "That's only natural, Jace. Actually, I'm reluctant to bring this up, because I worry you'll be unhappy to hear it, but I think it's better that you know. Those soldiers at Nethergarde Keep who fought in that battle — or, more precisely, those who witnessed you kill that Pit Lord — while they were grateful for what you did, they were genuinely more afraid of your dark power."

"That's also why the mages who run Nethergarde Keep let you leave as early as they did. Too many people at Nethergarde know you. If you kept hanging around there day after day, they'd be uneasy too. Believe me — during the time you were unconscious, there were probably no small number of people quietly praying you'd just die like that."

Grede took the wooden mug of deep-purple wine the barmaid had brought and raised it. "First round's on me, little Jace!"

Jace nodded and clinked mugs with Grede, taking a sip of the faintly bitter, faintly sweet wine. "I know."

"How are your parents?" Grede asked.

"Doing fine," Jace nodded.

"That's all that matters. At least you saw them. I thought those mages in purple robes would've stopped you halfway there, at the very least. At least it shows those fellows still have a touch of humanity in them."

Seeing Jace remain silent for a long while, Grede glanced at the pumpkin tart the barmaid had set down and said, "You didn't come here because you've got a job you can't handle on your own, did you?"

"More precisely…" Jace said. "Two jobs."

Grede coughed. "Ah, can't a man just come drink with his best mate for once?"

Jace held up five fingers. "One's a job collecting necrotic essence, Grede. Listen — five gold for collecting a single vial of the stuff. All you need to do is find a few wraiths… and you're done. The goblin in Booty Bay already gave me the equipment for collecting it."

Grede listened to Jace rattle all this off, then swallowed the wine in his mouth in one gulp. "That much money?"

"Come with me to Deadwind Pass. I'll keep a little of the necrotic essence for myself… and you take the lion's share, just like last time we went looking for Grave Moss."

"Sounds good," Grede laughed, then his expression cooled. "Did you say Deadwind Pass?!"

"Would it be worth that much if it weren't?" Jace asked. "If all we had to do was deal with a few ghosts in the haunted grounds of Raven Hill, would anyone pay us this much to hire us?"

"You've got a point," Grede said, scooping up a forkful of pumpkin tart. "The last time I was on gryphon patrol, I flew over the outskirts of that valley. It really didn't look like a normal sort of place, Jace. Do you have any preparations?"

"None at the moment," Jace said. "But not many people know anything about that place either. First I plan to find a map of Karazhan Tower from before it ran into trouble, and use it to get a sense of where the settlements are… Also, if we're going to deal with wraiths, we'll need to get some holy water from the Cathedral of Light. There are far too many people there who know me, so this is a job you'll have to handle."

"No problem," Grede said. "Also, that goblin equipment you mentioned — it's for extracting necrotic essence, right? Are you sure it'll actually work? Goblin contraptions…"

"That's exactly why we need the holy water," Jace said.

"All right, all right," the dwarf nodded. Seeing Grede agree, Jace took another mouthful of wine to rinse his mouth and said, "There's a second matter, Grede."
