Chapter 495: We'd Have to Meet Eventually

"What is it?" Grede stuffed a piece of wild boar into his mouth, chewed a few times, and said, "I get the feeling this one's going to be more serious than that valley business."

"Would you be interested in going to the other side?" Jace asked.

"The other side?" Grede looked as though he didn't understand at first. He blinked — then his eyes went wide. "You mean… Kalimdor?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Right now there's a group of elves — the same Reliquary that bought my command letter before — planning to hire me to go to Kalimdor to find someone. Even if we don't find her, just tracking down leads would earn us dozens of gold, Grede. But now they want to plant an elf ranger in the party… to track arcane traces, so we can't do without him. If that fellow's fond of gold, I'd have to give him half. If you join up, I'd only have to give him a third. Makes sense, right?"

"Why not Vereesa?" Grede asked. "That woman doesn't care much about money."

"Vereesa probably can't come," Jace said.

"Why not?" Grede asked. "I don't believe she'd be unwilling herself."

"Probably because they need her to fight trolls," Jace said. "In any case, someone doesn't want her coming. Don't worry about it — just say whether you're in or not."

"I'm in!" Grede said. "Exploring a legendary, mysterious continent — how could I say no? And there's gold to be made on top of it… Anyway, I've got nothing going on over here. When do we leave?"

"After we get back from Karazhan," Jace said. "In the meantime I need to make some preparations and put my affairs in order. This trip could take a long time. Even the sea voyage to Kalimdor might take several weeks. In other words, this journey will take at least half a year."

"But the reward is dozens of gold," Grede said. "That's enough right there."

"Provided we actually find leads. Otherwise they might only be willing to cover our travel expenses," Jace added. "So you'd better be prepared for that."

"Then we'll treat it as a free holiday," Grede said. "I'm an optimistic man."

Jace nodded.

But what weighed on him more was whether to bring those two demons — especially Morfell.

If he didn't bring them, he'd never rest easy leaving The Slaughtered Lamb behind. With Morfell keeping watch, at least there'd be no mishap with demon summoning. Even if the others tried to summon a Felguard without him, Morfell should be able to handle it.

After all, any Felguard they managed to summon certainly wouldn't be stronger than that orc warlock's.

But Morfell… she was, after all, a powerful fighter who didn't need a cut of the money. Heading into a strange, vast continent, facing the threat of a demonologist who'd lived for over ten thousand years — having a demon-specialist like Morfell along would make things a great deal safer.

The question was, would Grede and that elf traveling with them accept a succubus at their side?

"After this meal, I've got someone I'd like you to meet, Grede," Jace said, rubbing the corner of his mouth with a guilty conscience. "I think hiding it from you much longer isn't a good idea."

Grede laughed, pointing at Jace with his empty mug. "Ah, at last. So you were going to tell me about the succubus, weren't you?"

"You knew all along?" Jace asked in surprise.

"It really is a succubus?!" Grede froze. "I was joking."

Jace slapped a hand over his face. "It's a succubus. Sorry, Grede."

"Damn it…" Grede said. "You really are following in the footsteps of those black-magic bastards in Shadowforge City, step for step, Jace Sesso. Not a single step missed."

He slumped over the table with a sigh, then raised his head and said quietly, "Well, we've already got one demon. What's one more? Come on, let's have a look."

Grede followed Jace to his new lodgings on the north side of the Mage Quarter. Grede looked up and down the alley. "This place is even harder to find than where you lived before. You didn't move here just to hide that demon, did you?"

"I summoned that demon a long time ago," Jace said, climbing the stairs.

"When?" the dwarf asked.

"A few weeks after I got back from Lordaeron," Jace said.

"You mean that time the two of us went to Lordaeron together?"

"Yes."

"What the hell… And you hid it for this long? I kept noticing a faint, strange smell on you ever since — is that the scent of a succubus? Ah! That's it — that time at the Dark Portal, when we were killing those succubi, I kept thinking the smell was familiar but couldn't place it. So it was you, you bastard… At least now I don't have to worry that a succubus has scrambled your brains. Then again, maybe she already has… But if she has, it's too late to do anything about it now."

Jace swallowed awkwardly. "Sorry."

Reaching the door of the rental, Jace felt for the handle. Grede was already tensed up, eyes locked on the door.

The moment they stepped inside, Grede grabbed the doorframe and peered into the empty room. "Where's the demon? Where? Show yourself!"

Sazeno popped his head out. "Master, why did you bring this dwarf into our home?!"

Grede let out a sharp "Hey!" and raised his fist at Sazeno — then let out a breath of relief. "So it's this little bastard. Damn it, you scared me!"

Jace swept his eyes across the room and couldn't detect the succubus's location. He tried saying, "Morfell… you can…"

Just then, Grede spotted the crimson greatsword leaning against the wall. "I saw two hulking Felguard captains in heavy armor carrying swords like this one near the Dark Portal, guarding the Storm Altar… How do you have one of those?"

"Because of me." Morfell materialized suddenly behind the dwarf. Grede yelped and spun with a punch. Morfell easily slipped past his attack and appeared behind him again.

Grede whirled back around, locking onto the succubus's form. He seized the opening to catch his breath, then coughed with studied nonchalance.

"No need… to be so tense," Jace stepped between them.

Sazeno immediately barked, "Let me introduce you two. Mr. Grede Thunderfist, a good-for-nothing who ran down from the mountains. Miss Morfell, a shameless masochist with no bottom line!"

"Morfell," Grede glanced at Jace. "Sounds like a barmaid's name in a Shadowforge City tavern. She was a succubus too."

"You've been to the Blackrock Bar?" Jace asked.

"And you know it's called the 'Blackrock Bar'?" Grede shot back.

"It's normal to have heard of it," Jace said.

Grede glanced at the succubus again. "Of course I've only heard of it. What do you take me for — do you think a Wildhammer dwarf would sneak into Shadowforge City?"

"You can't tell Eredun apart," Morfell said. "It's only natural that all Shivarra names sound about the same to you."
