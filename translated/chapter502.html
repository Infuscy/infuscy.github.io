Chapter 502: The Right Man for the Job

“A felhound?” Grede said. “You sure…”

“Sure.” Jace took the soul shard Sazeno handed over with a grimace, raised Gorefiend’s staff, and focused his mind on the summoning ritual. Purple shadow-energy runes wove themselves together, forming naturally beneath his feet. Wrapped in the shadows of Deadwind Pass, the summoning went remarkably smoothly.

Just then, the imp suddenly pointed behind Jace and said, “Hey, fatso, I can feel that mage! He’s to the east, to the east! He’s coming! Protect my master!”

Grede looked east and saw a shadow raising its hand, channeling glowing frost magic. He swung his war hammer and loosed a bolt of lightning straight at it. The blast blew the shadow to pieces — but Sazeno glanced over blankly and said, “Well… that was just a mirror image.”

At that moment, a rift of shadow split open right beside the dwarf, and out of it lunged a massive felhound. This calf-sized monster had four long tentacles, and its black bristly fur stood out like spikes, thick and powerful. The instant it hit the ground, its claws dug deep into the earth and it let out a savage, guttural snarl.

The felhound had barely turned to meet Jace’s gaze — before it could even show hostility — when a naming incantation in Eredun was already spoken to completion.

“Your name is Dazarm.”

Jace didn’t hesitate. It was the first name on the Eredun felhound-naming list Mor’zul had given him.

Grede stood half-raising his axe, staring at the felhound as if unsure whether to treat it as the enemy or keep his attention on the mage. In the middle of his bewilderment, Jace spoke in Eredun: “Find the undead arcanist, Dazarm. See through the illusions. Seek the truth!”

His Eredun was fluent, laced with that peculiar demonic cadence — after all, it had been taught to him personally by two demons. But to Grede, hearing it made his skin crawl.

“You’re getting more and more like an eredar, Jace.”

“Can’t help it,” Jace said. “The harder the enemy I’m dealing with, the harder I have to become… for people to accept.”

Grede nodded. “That’s how it is, all right.”

Receiving Jace’s command in Eredun, Dazarm immediately bolted toward the forest to the south, like a raging boar, growling in low, rumbling bursts as it charged.

Toward the road!

Just then, another ghostly figure appeared in the woods to the side — but the felhound completely ignored it, running in a single direction. Before that phantom could even loose a frost spell, Jace blew it to nothing with a simple Scorch.

This thing really could tell the mage’s real body apart!

The felhound ran with terrifying speed. Fortunately, the two of them were in good enough shape to just barely keep up. But the undead mage was in a completely different situation.

After being hit by Curse of Weakness, his only means of moving fast was to seize openings to Blink, then hide himself among the woods and the ever-present shadows of Deadwind Pass. Now that he had a felhound on his trail, there was nowhere left to run.

They hadn’t been chasing him long before Jace spotted the limping figure up ahead. He immediately raised his staff, aimed in that direction, and spoke the incantation for a Shadow Bolt. The twisted black bolt that flew out seemed almost sentient — weaving past tree trunks, soaring over brush, twisting and spinning as it bore straight down on the hobbled target ahead!

The mage seemed to sense the shadow power closing in from behind. He spoke another incantation, and a faint flash of light shimmered around his body… Then the felhound let out a screech so piercing that Jace winced.

Its several tentacles flashed with arcane light all at once. The mage seemed to Blink into the shadows — but was immediately shoved back out of them with a howl, stumbling a few steps and crashing to the ground.

His Blink… had been countered?!

Jace had never imagined that a spell like Blink could be countered by a felhound!

Dazarm charged straight in without a moment’s hesitation. Even as the undead mage lay on the ground, he shrieked out incantation after incantation. Arcane Missiles flew at them in rapid succession — and were swallowed down whole, one mouthful at a time, by the felhound.

In the end, the mage howled in despair, drew a dagger from his belt with an arm as dry as kindling, and raised it high, as if to make his last stand. The felhound pounced straight at him, tore his head off in a single bite, then cracked open his chest and skull, greedily devouring the last dregs of arcane power left inside the undead body…

Jace took a deep breath. He could feel that the fight, on top of the felhound summoning, had drained him badly — even in this shadow-dense place.

Watching this scene, he remembered the time in the Black Morass, fighting that pack of felhounds alongside the elven ranger.

If he hadn’t made a point of training his combat skills, he probably would’ve ended up just like this.

“Why did he come back as a zombie?” Grede asked, looking at the corpse.

“The dark power of Karazhan raised all the corpses in Raven Hill, Grede.” Jace stepped forward and picked up the rotted, falling-apart Kirin Tor robe. “This place is full of ghosts… and no shortage of undead.”

“Master! Master! This machine is reacting!” Sazeno pointed at the box Grede was carrying — the one holding the Undeath Essence Collector. Jace looked over and, sure enough, saw a stronger arcane light flickering from its seams than before.

It could harvest this mage’s necrotic energy?

Grede immediately set the machine down on the ground. Jace stuffed an empty bottle into the output nozzle, and the imp started cranking the handle right away. Grede thought he was doing a sloppy job and joined in himself. Before long, the nozzle actually spewed out a pale purple mist — far more than that lonely soul from before, by a wide margin!

Jace watched the corpse that the felhound was tearing into rapidly deteriorate. The skin, which had only been deathly pale before, was now shriveled up like a dried-out mummy.

He wondered how much Essence of Undeath this undead mage’s remaining spirit could yield…

He tossed the tattered robe aside and noticed that the mage had been carrying a cloth bundle.

He opened it. Inside were several booklets. One was probably the mage’s journal, but it had been soaked through with putrid corpse fluid and fouled beyond recognition — the writing on it had been messy to begin with, and was now illegible.

Another was blank — probably carried around to write notes on.

The last one was different. Even though it had also been soaked in corpse water, it hadn’t been stained or ruined. The pages inside seemed to be protected by some kind of magical ward, intact and unharmed — or perhaps they were simply made from special materials.

At any rate, it was obvious at a glance that it was something else entirely compared to the other booklets.
