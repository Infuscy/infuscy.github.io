Chapter 503: The Difference Between the Game and Here

Jace opened the booklet. Inside were records written in crooked, cramped script — some in Common, but the greater part in Eredun. Even with Jace's command of the demonic tongue, he could tell that this booklet roughly noted down a great many locations and personal names, along with brief annotations on each.

Beside these entries were extremely crude diagrammatic symbols, presumably used to mark the relative geographical positions of these elements.

Jace could even make out that a considerable portion of it described locations around Grim Batol, as well as the war between the Dark Iron dwarves and the Blackrock orcs near Blackrock Mountain… It even mentioned the Tirisfal region under the rule of the Kingdom of Lordaeron — Jace's homeland.

But why would a mage write in Eredun?

Or perhaps this booklet hadn't belonged to this mage at all?

Jace glanced at Grede, who was working the handle for all he was worth, then at the small glass bottle that was now about a third full. A faint thrill stirred in his chest.

The thrill didn't come from the fact that a single mage's necromatic energy could yield this much essence. What mattered more was that he had a feeling this booklet was no ordinary thing.

The Kirin Tor had established the Violet Eye to explore Karazhan, and the most important goal of their exploration was to recover the countless treasures amassed here by Medivh and his mother, Aegwynn… Could this booklet have something to do with those treasures?

"Sazeno, come here. Help me figure out what this passage says."

The imp hopped over. With the hellhound noisily crunching on the corpse in the background, he pressed the book flat on the ground and studied it carefully for a while. "This handwriting is absolutely terrible. This definitely wasn't written by a demon!"

After staring a bit longer, he said, "Ah… hmm… This passage is recording the trail of an ancient artifact. It's got something to do with orcs and dwarves, and a dwarf civil war from hundreds of years ago. The person who wrote this was trying to find this thing — but obviously, it's recorded way too crudely. There's not a single useful detail. I can tell he never had a hope of finding it!"

"The evil witch Moargard's wicked weapon." Grede looked up. "You mean this mage was trying to find that legendary evil weapon?!"

"Moargard! Moargard!" The imp pointed at one of the words. "So that's what this word means. Hmm, looks like you know quite a bit about it too, fatso!"

"Of course I know plenty, you wretched little demon," Grede glared at him. "That thing destroyed my ancestors' homeland!"

Jace knew more than the person who wrote this book, and more than Grede put together.

It was obvious. This passage pointed to the remains left behind by the ancient entity Xal'atath — the Dark Empire blade that had unleashed endless slaughter and terror.

But anyone who knew less than Jace had even less hope of finding this dagger. Still, with this information, Jace at least understood what this booklet was for.

A treasure-hunter's guide?

Could this thing have belonged to Medivh's dark servants — those fallen, undead treasure-seekers?

That would more or less explain why the book hadn't been ruined by necromatic energy. After all, it had been carried by undead themselves!

So this Kirin Tor mage had found this record — and then died misfortunately…

Or had he died first, and then found this booklet? After all, this mage had still been chasing all manner of treasures even after death. Otherwise he wouldn't have tracked Jace and Grede all this way, following the Flow of Ice Crystal's power.

But that didn't matter now.

Jace flipped forward a few more pages of rambling, groundless speculation about Xal'atath, and then he came across the record of another treasure. This one was called the Calming Gem of Neltharion, described as a pure blue, enormous magic stone used to amplify magical power…

Its place of safekeeping was the Wizard Sanctum. The mage guarding it was one Alfred Vanderlin. According to the record, he was an apprentice of Neltharion who had died in the Orc Wars, so the gem was currently unattended…

Alfred Vanderlin. Jace seemed to have some faint impression of the name. Wasn't the discounted seven-gold fireproof short robe at Duncan's tailor shop this very mage's?

He wasn't sure anymore. But the name of this gem had been crudely crossed out, with a "Found" mark beside it.

So these treasure-hunters in Medivh's service had actually broken into the Wizard Sanctum and stolen the treasure of Medivh's father, Neltharion?

Well, that wasn't exactly shocking. After all, in the game, the "Calming Gem of Neltharion" was loot from one of Karazhan's bosses — the Shade of Elan. That more or less said everything.

Since it was already inside Karazhan, Jace didn't dwell on it. He flipped further forward, past several other familiar names of weapons and equipment that had also been found — including a black dagger called "Undesired," an enchanted set of armor and a longsword belonging to the House of Wryn, and a corrupted ring said to belong to the demon "the Flawless One," possessing terrifying power.

Then he turned to a page marked with a special blood-demonic symbol. On it was written only a single name in Eredun, with no Common annotation whatsoever.

"U… sa…" he tried to sound it out.

Ursuulas?

Jace knew this name. It was the artifact of the Affliction warlocks in the game — a fearsome soul-scythe.

Ursuulas was the name of a Nathrezim dreadlord who had ruled over a demon world tens of thousands of years ago, and this weapon had been forged from his soul…

He also knew, of course, that this weapon had already been found by Medivh's treasure-hunters and taken for their own.

In the game, to obtain it, you had to venture into the underground crypts of Karazhan and defeat the powerful leader of the treasure-hunters.

Before this, Jace had never even entertained the thought that he might one day enter Karazhan and find this warlock's artifact. But of course, back then, that delusion had been about as absurd as the notion he'd had right after transmigrating — bedding every beautiful woman on Azeroth.

Well, it wasn't much different now, either.

He flipped further back. The entries regarding this demon scythe were already in the last few pages of the handbook.

But it suddenly struck him: Ursuulas's name hadn't been crossed out.

That meant… at this point in time, this weapon had not yet been found by these treasure-hunters!

The game knowledge Jace possessed was from ten, even twenty years in the future — the late-game timeline!

He felt his heartbeat pounding in time with the sound of Grede cranking the handle. He glanced at the bottle of necromatic essence that was nearly full…

To find this demon weapon before Medivh's servant-hunters, he would have to understand demons even better than they did.

One step at a time.
