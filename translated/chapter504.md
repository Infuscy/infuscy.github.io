## Chapter 504: Darkness of the Black Tower

Ulthalesh was a cursed scythe. The Dark Titan Sargeras himself had forged this dark weapon, imbued it with the soul of the dreadlord Ulthalesh, and bestowed it upon his servants most skilled in the dark arts — the eredar warlocks.

Countless eredar warlocks who had wielded this weapon had died for one reason or another. And though Ulthalesh was unimaginably powerful, a rumor still circulated among the eredar: that this weapon devoured its own master's power… to strengthen itself.

Actually, before he'd learned that these hunters hadn't yet obtained Ulthalesh, Jace had never imagined he would ever have anything to do with such a weapon.

Jace knew this was no tool like Azuresong, which sat obediently in your hand and did as it was told once you picked it up. This was an artifact with a soul.

Or rather, an artifact with countless souls.

Of course, he had no idea what the scythe Ulthalesh actually looked like in this world, where it was hidden, how powerful the souls it had devoured truly were, or what fate awaited its wielder.

He only knew that he had to handle any information related to it with the utmost caution. In this world, many terrible things could bring calamity down on you simply by knowing about them.

And besides, this valley where Karazhan stood was no place to ponder these questions properly.

He and Grede set out again. The next village wasn't far from Karazhan anymore — by their rough estimate on the map, only about sixty or seventy miles.

And yet, when they reached the outside of this village, Jace had the distinct feeling… he could already see that tower.

He knew that even standing on a mountain, with the air clear and the light bright and nothing in the way, looking toward the horizon, seeing twenty or thirty miles would already be impressive.

But every time he looked south, he always felt that tower was right there, right in front of his eyes, watching him.

Even though all around was a thick, inky blackness, and the trees were like the weeds in a filthy river channel, covering the skin of the gray mountains — that tower… seemed to be at the very highest point.

Jace even felt like he was dreaming. He seemed to have experienced all this before, as if he had seen this tower from somewhere, and had the same feeling — that it was watching him.

And he wasn't the only one. Even Grede, and even Sazeno, felt it too.

By now, the imp who was always quick with his mouth and full of chatter had grown uncharacteristically silent. Grede's movements and reactions had become sluggish. And though it was the dead of winter, his forehead was always beaded with sweat.

Jace tried several times to ask if he felt something was wrong, but couldn't bring himself to open his mouth — as if he didn't want to initiate conversation, didn't want to waste the mental effort of discussing anything related to Karazhan.

After hunting a few ghosts in this desolate village, Jace and Grede sat down in front of a collapsed chapel of the Light, both gazing south without a word.

Grede took a piece of bread from his pack, gnawed off a bite, chewed twice, then stared at the bread with wide eyes.

"Tell me, Jace Sesso — what am I holding?" he asked.

"Bread," Jace said.

"Did you bring any Shadow Protection Potion?" Grede asked.

"No," Jace said. "I thought you brought some. I haven't seen my alchemist recently."

"Mine ran out earlier. Then… give me some of the priests' holy water." The dwarf said, "My holy water's all used up too."

Jace nodded and handed half a bottle of holy water to Grede, who gulped down every last drop.

"What's wrong with you?"

"I…" Grede closed his eyes after drinking, then looked at his bread again. The rigid expression on his face finally eased a little.

"Just now, when I looked at what was in my hand, it looked like a child's finger, Jace. I thought I'd lost my goddamn mind."

"Let's get out of here." Jace stood up. "We've got three and a half bottles of Essence of Undeath now, right? That's enough. Let's go, Grede."

"Because of Karazhan?" Grede asked.

"Yes," Jace said. "I'm feeling pretty damn uncomfortable too."

"You haven't had any hallucinations?" Grede asked.

"Not yet. Probably because my resistance to shadow is a little stronger." Jace held out his hand. "Give me the Flowing Ice Crystal, Grede."

Grede lifted the beautiful arcane ice crystal in his palm. He was just about to hand it to Jace when he stared at it, blinked, took a deep breath, and only then truly reached out to pass it over.

Jace took the crystal and tried activating its power on the dwarf in front of him. As the light permeated his whole body, Grede jolted as if someone had dumped a bucket of water over him from head to toe.

"This thing… it really works." Grede patted his head and put his helmet on. "The scenery around us looks completely different, Jace. Just now, everything in the distance looked like it was turning into a black blur."

"This money isn't easy to earn," Jace said. "Otherwise the goblins wouldn't be paying us so much to come here for Essence of Undeath. Let's move. Back the way we came… as far from Karazhan as possible."

When they drew close to the main road between the Black Morass and Elwynn, Jace banished the felhound back to the Twisting Nether. He couldn't bring a calf-sized beast like this back to the human world. It was a decision that had to be made.

But ever since Dazarm had been summoned to deal with the mage, the felhound had been helping Jace and Grede find their way through these two or three days of valley travel. It was precisely because of this that they hadn't gotten lost inside. After spending even this short stretch of time together, Jace felt he'd already developed quite a bond with this demon. Especially since a felhound's intelligence wasn't as high as other demons' — or at least it didn't show as much — and it couldn't speak, which actually made it easier to build trust.

When he banished it, even though he knew the felhound's soul would return safely to the Twisting Nether and that a weaker demon could be reborn again soon enough, there was still a hint of the feeling of killing a friend with his own hands.

He wondered when he'd ever find a way to summon a bound demon permanently.

After returning to Darkshire, Grede slept a full day and night before finally feeling like his spirit had recovered. On the road back to Stormwind, he kept muttering that he was never going to Deadwind Pass again.

Having gone through this journey, Jace was beginning to truly understand why, when he'd told those mages back in Dalaran that he'd gotten lost in Deadwind Pass and couldn't remember anything, they'd been so ready to believe his nonsense.

After all, even a mage of the Kirin Tor had gotten lost in there. That he, an unknown little apprentice at the time, had managed to wander his way back out really could be called a miracle.

As for what he remembered, what he could explain clearly — nobody had any expectations of that.
