Chapter 506: The Alchemist's Secret

"Jace... Jace, how did you... what is..."

Ursula looked down at the demon's corpse, eyes wide with shock.

"Help me carry this inside," Jace said.

Ursula nodded, and the two of them hauled the satyr's body into the hallway. Jace tossed it aside carelessly, and Ursula rushed back into the inner room, returning with a Shadow Protection Potion and a strong Healing Potion, which she fed to Jace.

Blocking out the lingering pain and damage, then adding the healing potion, he suddenly felt a good deal better.

"It's him, Master! It's him, Master!"

The imp hopped and shouted at the end of the hallway, staring at the demon's corpse. Ursula glared at him. "Shut up!"

Jace sat in the chair, head tilted back, catching his breath. "So what the hell happened, Ursula? This demon's connected to you, isn't it?"

Ursula nodded.

"He nearly killed me," Jace said, staring at her. "Just a little slower on the incantation and he'd have driven that dagger straight through the back of my skull."

He threw down the curved, beast-claw-like dagger. It clanged against the floor.

"I'm sorry, Jace." Ursula stared at the weapon, dazed. "He must have come to kill me. You saved my life!"

"What?" Jace said.

"Well... I don't think I ever mentioned..." She lowered her brows. "I suppose I told you about my father's death?"

"You did," Jace said.

"He was murdered," Ursula said.

"Why?"

"I don't know. He was... just a medicine seller. A perfectly ordinary merchant, with maybe a dash of elven blood. But later, through some half-elf friends, I tracked down the identity of the man who hired the killer — a magic materials merchant in Stratholme. And I wanted... revenge!"

"So that's the real reason you wanted to become a warlock?"

"At first, yes. At first... I thought the warlocks at the Slaughtered Lamb could help me. But once I got inside, I realized Jagin and the others didn't want to get involved in taking lives — it'd draw too much unwanted attention. So I gave up on asking them for help..."

Hearing this, Jace felt the demon blood on his clothes sticking to him unpleasantly. He sniffed at the stench on his shirt. "I need some new clothes. Or a cloak or something."

Ursula nodded. "Leave your clothes here, Jace. I can clean the demon blood off them."

"Some water would be best too," Jace said.

"No problem." Ursula went back inside, pouring water as she spoke. "Later, I got in touch with some Eastweald folk. They said they could help me take care of that merchant."

"So you gave all those earnings of yours to them to kill for you?" Jace asked.

Ursula brought the water over. "But clearly they didn't do a very good job. The merchant had more pull than I thought. He... simply refused to die. I heard those Eastweald folk even lost two of their own, and soon they didn't want to help anymore — wouldn't even give the money back."

"I'm guessing that Stratholme fellow must have figured out my identity and where I live. Probably through those Eastweald people. Someone told me — he's sent people to kill me now!"

"A few days ago, the imp told me there was a half-elf always lurking around here, and he might be a warlock. I got scared and didn't dare go out — just used the concealment runes my father taught me to write on the door, to keep him from detecting me and the imp with some spell. I could only pretend I wasn't home, wait for him to think I'd left and leave on his own. But he seems to have stuck around..."

"I just didn't know he was a demon. If Besadim had been here it would've been a lot better, but I couldn't bring it back — it can't be seen on the streets of Stormwind... What kind of demon is this? I've never seen one."

"A satyr," Jace said. "This isn't a demon that came from the Twisting Nether. It's a mortal twisted by demonic influence into a demon. Most of them were originally elves."

"I've heard the satyr stories, but I never thought they were real. Maybe the Lordaeron tales about half-elves turning into devils at night aren't completely baseless after all..."

Ursula stared at the crumpled corpse as she spoke. Jace scooped some water from the basin and washed his wounds. "I'm going away for a while after this. Are you sure you'll be all right? Want me to leave Molophir behind to protect you?"

"I don't want you or anyone else from the Slaughtered Lamb getting mixed up in this, Jace. I treasure you guys too much — you're my friends." Ursula said. "Anyway, this is my problem. You came to see me today and got hurt on my account — I already feel terrible... After this, I'm planning to move into the Slaughtered Lamb and lie low for a while. With Besadim and everyone else's help, you don't need to worry about me."

Jace nodded. She brought over a towel. "Let me wipe you down. Is the water too cold?"

"It's fine."

Jace grunted. The Curse of Agony from earlier still had his ears ringing, and he really didn't feel like moving.

He took a deep breath and slumped in the chair. As Ursula cleaned and bandaged his wounds, she asked, "You said you're going away for a while. Where to? Lordaeron?"

"No," Jace said. "Kalimdor."

"You're going to Kalimdor?" Ursula started in surprise, then said, "I..."

"You want me to bring something back for you?" Jace asked.

"Uh..." Ursula gave an embarrassed laugh. "All right, I had the thought. It's a rare opportunity, after all."

Jace said at once, "I can't promise I'll be able to bring anything back this time, Ursula. Kalimdor's huge. I've been hired to find something myself — I might not have the time to go out of my way for you."

"If it's on the way, help. If not, forget it." Ursula wet the towel and wiped the demon blood off Jace's body. "When are you leaving? I'll write you a note..."

"Probably within a week or two. Fine, write it." Jace sighed.

A faint, familiar scent of herbs drifted through the room. Ursula smiled. Jace watched the stray hairs behind her ear, her pale neck and collarbone, and felt a tightness in his chest.

Right then, he felt something he probably shouldn't have been feeling — even the pain from fighting the demon earlier had been suppressed by this impulse.

He sat up to cover himself, but at that moment Ursula suddenly turned her head and locked eyes with him.

"I must be going crazy, Jace."

"What?"

He'd barely registered it when she kissed him without warning, wrapping her arms tight around his still-damp body.

A long while later... Jace finally caught his breath and licked his lips. "Ursula..."

Ursula made a soft "mm," her gaze holding his as she stood, quickly pulling off her shirt and trousers and tossing them aside. Jace couldn't help but take in her figure — the alchemist looked almost fragilely thin.

Jace took a deep breath, lifted her entirely onto the bed, kicked off his own trousers, and leaned over her, eyes on hers.

The air was freezing, but the Ursula beneath him was flushed and burning hot, like a little furnace.

"I can't take this anymore."

She whispered it, her legs already hooked around his waist. Jace felt a damp warmth — cool outside, hot inside — press against his lower belly.

"Hurry up, you little brat... You think I didn't notice yours earlier?"

"You did that on purpose," Jace said.

"What are you talking about?"

Ursula frowned, then suddenly gasped, her eyes rolling downward. A breath caught, and she couldn't help but moan, then lost herself entirely in the rhythm of the thrusts.
