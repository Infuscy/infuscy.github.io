## Chapter 508: Elven Tolerance

> "Jace,
>
> Thank you for your letter. I have received the token as well — I'm glad it was of use to you at the Reliquary.
>
> So you've decided to accept the Reliquary's mission to Kalimdor?
>
> Actually, among the Farstriders, there aren't many rangers willing to adventure alongside a special caster like you and your special companion. I know a lady from House Goldthorn who would be a good fit — she recently finished her garrison duty on the southeastern front of Eversong Woods, so she's free right now.
>
> If you're willing, I can find a way to contact her and get her to join your party. Reply before you accept the Reliquary's envoy.
>
> —Vereesa Windrunner"

The Gilded Rose was quite lively at noon. Jace finished reading in a low voice and handed the letter to the dwarf in front of him. "What do you think 'special companion' means here?"

"Whatever it is, it's not me." Grede set down his ale, took the letter, and glanced at it. "Does a Wildhammer dwarf in Quel'Thalas need to be spoken about so euphemistically? I bet Vereesa already knows you can summon demons... Those elves just don't want to call you out on it. You think Quel'Thalas's void researchers know less than you do? But who's this lady from House Goldthorn?"

"You forgot?" Jace asked. "Aethwyn Goldthorn... that was the alias Vereesa used last time she came to investigate the death knights."

"How the hell would I remember that? I'd forgotten you even told me." Grede asked, "So are you sure? Are you sure she means she's coming herself?"

"What else?" Jace said. "Her intentions are pretty obvious. That alias was one she made up on the spot when I first met her."

Grede cleared his throat. "If Vereesa really comes in person, do you think she could handle traveling with that succubus?"

"Didn't you just say the elves already know I can summon demons?" Jace said.

"Other elves might be fine," Grede said. "But Vereesa..."

"She?" Jace asked.

Grede lowered his voice. "I'll be straight with you. Are you sure she doesn't have feelings for you, Jace?"

"Why do you care about that too?" Jace said with a laugh. "And didn't you always insist elves and humans were just fooling around?"

"While you were unconscious, she was worried sick," Grede said. "Honestly, I was a little moved by how she cared for you. Even if she really is playing you, she's putting on one hell of a performance."

"So you're not worried about me?" Jace asked.

"Of course I'm worried about you!" Grede muttered. "Ah, maybe, maybe I'm reading too much into it. Because even though she's an elf, she's still a woman. But didn't you say before you wanted to find an elf? Here's an elf right in front of you, and now you don't want her."

"I don't know." Jace rested his cheek on his hand, thinking for a moment. "Actually, those high elf nobles in Quel'Thalas are further away than you think. They think I'm about to steal away the Windrunner family's youngest sister. A lot of elves have told me... they want to stop me from seeing Vereesa."

"But like you told me before, Grede — I can only live fifty or sixty years, seventy or eighty at most. Even if she does have feelings for me, and we got together — what happens when I'm old? She'll still be as young as she is now, barely aged a day. Am I supposed to leave a will telling her to go find someone else and remarry?"

He frowned as he said it. "Damn, I don't even know what I'm saying. Anyway, right now she's a comrade I'd walk through fire with, a partner in life and death. She hasn't said anything, and I've got nothing to think about either."

"Well, on that front, she really is one hell of a companion," Grede nodded.

That's what he said.

But right now, Jace was thinking about that embrace at Nethergarde Keep.

It was hard to say he felt nothing at all.

Come to think of it, Grede had hugged him plenty of times, and every time it was a lot tighter — tight enough to kill.

Compared to a dwarf, if you really wanted to say Vereesa had no such intentions, that could also be explained.

When it came to the lifespan issue, Grede slowly lowered his brows too.

"You know, Jace — if every race lived about the same length of time, the world might have a lot fewer things to agonize over."

"Trust me, Grede. Even without agonizing over lifespan, there'd be plenty else to agonize over," Jace said. "There's no shortage of things to agonize over."

Grede said, "Other things can at least be changed. Even looks and build can be disguised with magic. Changing lifespan is a whole different matter — especially stretching a human's years to an elf's length."

"Let's not talk about this aimless stuff," Jace said. "I'll write back and tell her to come. And I'll tell her she'd better be mentally prepared for my special servant. Don't say I didn't warn her."

Grede laughed. "Now that you mention it, I'm looking forward to seeing her face when she meets your Molophir. And while we're at it, we can see exactly how she feels about you, little Jace."

Jace picked up his ale and took a drink. "So now you're not worried about elves playing me?"

"Hah! I'm not worried at all!" Grede grabbed one end of a sausage, bit off the other end. "Now I'm the one trying to talk Vereesa out of getting close to you."

Jace rolled his eyes at him, but had to admit — he felt the same way.

That business with the troll was genuinely the most absurd thing he'd done in two lifetimes. Even if it really did come down to losing his life next time, he wasn't doing that again.

Misersheld had said it — what happens in Booty Bay stays in Booty Bay...

But he hadn't lost his memory. Did he really think he could just leave it there because he wanted to?

It had been months, and he still dreamed about that scene from time to time.

As his command of shadow power grew more effortless and his awareness of shadow corruption's emotional influence grew sharper, a lot of the choices he'd made were things he could no longer pass the buck on.

All he could say was — novelty wasn't something just anyone could casually try. There were things in this world that few humans attempted, and for good reason.

Jace rubbed his face hard, trying to wipe the image of that cave from his mind. Then he asked, "What about you?"

"Me what?" Grede asked.

"After breaking up with that elf, aren't you planning to find someone else?" Jace asked.

"Find who?" Grede said. "Is single life not enjoyable enough? Is being unattached not carefree enough? Am I really so bored I'm looking to make myself suffer? Don't you worry, Jace Sesso — I'm done wasting energy on that stuff."

"You're just heartbroken, Grede of House Thunderfist," Jace said. "Wait until you meet a lady who truly cares about you, who loves you, who heals the wounds you—"

"Give it a rest, you little brat." Grede squinted. "You think you know me better than I know myself?!"

Jace laughed and took a drink. "We'll see!"

"We'll see!" Grede rapped the table with his fingers. "Five gold says I'm right."

"Who the hell bets you... and that much money," Jace muttered.
