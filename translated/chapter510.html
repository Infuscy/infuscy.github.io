Chapter 510: Farewell Stormwind, Once More

After the gathering broke up, come evening, Galders was preparing to leave the Slaughtered Lamb when Jace stopped him. "I'm heading to Kalimdor, Galders. I'm guessing about half a year... possibly longer."

"Kalimdor?" Galders said. "You mean the continent the goblins talk about?"

"Right," Jace said. "I have enough evidence to prove... Dasmora Darkeye might be on the continent of Kalimdor."

Galders said in surprise, "You're going to track down that legendary warlock woman yourself?"

Jace said, "Might be able to bring back some pretty powerful demonic knowledge materials, Galders. It'd be a huge boost to our entire Dagger Circle. I think it's well worth it. At the same time, I can find out whether Dasmora and Mocur have any connection."

"You're leaving for half a year?" Erland, who was packing up nearby, asked. "What about the money from your last trip? I feel like I can sell most of it within two months — demon materials have been selling great lately."

"You hang onto it for now. I'll get it when I come back," Jace said.

"Well, if you insist." Erland grinned.

Galders said, "By the time you're back, there might be a few more spells to learn. I heard the dwarven warlocks in Dun Morogh picked up some eredar incantations. I'm planning to go ask around. We've made good money lately — I'm sure those fellows will be happy to share something once they're paid."

Jace nodded. "Just be careful when testing new spells."

"If it's too dangerous, we'll wait until everyone's here to try it," Galders said. "Don't worry."

After parting with Galders, Jace handed over the prepaid rent to Tharle Mor and asked him to pay it on his behalf. Back home, Jace began preparing what to bring to Kalimdor.

But in reality, aside from the incantations he needed to familiarize himself with and some demonic-language materials, there wasn't much point in bringing anything else.

Whether it was potions, food, or other consumables, they'd probably all be used up long before the voyage to Kalimdor was halfway over. Bringing these things was less useful than bringing some gold to find goblins to buy from on the other continent.

Thinking of that, he could already picture the bunch of them getting mercilessly gouged by goblins.

Just then, Molophir hefted that red demon greatsword. "Can I bring this with us, Master..."

Jace was just worrying about what to bring when he saw Molophir wanting to take the sword. He took it from her and hefted it. "You sure you can stay properly invisible carrying this greatsword?"

"Of course. Didn't you see in that swamp last time, Master?" Molophir asked.

"Then why, on the way back, did you have us wrap it up and carry it into Stormwind?" Jace countered.

"Well, the mages in Stormwind... aren't quite the same as the beasts in the swamp," the succubus said, leaning on the sword.

Jace sighed. "This trip to Kalimdor I need you to help track demons that might be connected to Dasmora Darkeye — not use this sword to hack at people, Molophir. And this journey of ours is best not weighed down by something this heavy. Do you have any idea how far we'll be traveling... Unless you can promise me you'll carry this sword yourself the whole time without it affecting your invisibility at all."

Molophir licked her lips. "Well, I can't promise it won't affect my stealth — the demonic power in this sword... but using it is really satisfying."

Looking at her expression, sometimes Jace had a hard time telling whether it was bedroom activities that satisfied Molophir more, or battle and slaughter that really got her going... He still remembered the fanatical look on the succubus's face when she'd cleaved a dragonman's spine in a single stroke with that giant blade — no different from how she looked after a night of tumbling with him.

But then again, for the vast majority of bloodthirsty demons in the Twisting Nether, slaughter was the most direct outlet for desire — carnal pleasure was trivial by comparison. A succubus who'd never served mortals before coming to Azeroth being more enthusiastic about battle was perfectly normal.

Jace looked at Molophir's slightly disappointed expression and comforted her. "You said being with me would make you stronger bit by bit. Maybe one day you'll be able to carry this weapon and turn invisible just fine — grow a little taller, get a little stronger."

Hearing that, the succubus, who'd still been a little dejected, immediately brightened. "Then you'll need to work harder, Master..."

"Before the Master can reward this sayad, he should probably give me back my soul shards first..." the imp complained from the side. "In that valley, you went and snatched the shards that were supposed to be mine for no reason!"

"There'll be plenty of soul shards once we're in Kalimdor," Jace said.

The imp went "ahh" and muttered, "Now that you mention it, I'm actually starting to look forward to this long, weird journey!"

After finishing up his affairs in Stormwind, Jace and Grede arrived at the port that morning, sitting on the pier to rest before boarding.

Winter Veil again this year. Jace felt the holiday atmosphere was even more lively than last year. Quite a few dwarves were celebrating inside and outside the inns and taverns near the port — even the early morning was quite a scene. It wasn't hard to imagine what it'd be like at night.

"I haven't heard you mention going back to Aerie Peak this year," Jace asked, looking at Grede.

"You want me to go back once a year?" Grede blew out his beard. "Those folks aren't that important to me, hah. I feel like I'm basically a Bronzebeard dwarf now — eating Bronzebeard food, speaking with a Bronzebeard accent, and most importantly, drinking Bronzebeard ale."

"Even joined the Ironforge gryphon rider corps," Jace added.

"Yeah, damn it, that's true." Grede looked at the goblin merchant ship docked at the pier heading to Booty Bay, then looked back at Jace. "You've got new clothes on again today?"

"That's right." Jace patted his new robe. "What do you think?"

"Looks like a worn-out Dalaran robe," Grede said. "Except without that eye. Reminds me of the clothes on those Dalaran mages' corpses we found in the Blasted Lands."

"Can't you say something nice?" Jace sighed.

Grede rubbed the sleeve of the robe and actually widened his eyes. "Hey now, this robe... good material, seriously. So what level of tailor used material like this to make something that looks this worn?"

"It's got demon cloth woven in," Jace said. "And it's been seared with flame essence. Probably why it looks the worse for wear."

"So it's a robe custom-made for your sort," Grede said. "Now that you mention it, I remember intercepting Horde troops heading to Caer Darrow in the forest between Hillsbrad and the Hinterlands — I saw an orc warlock wearing a robe about like yours. That bastard even had a felguard minion! The difference is those robes glowed with fel light when the orcs cast spells. Does this one do the same?"

"Shouldn't," Jace said, tugging his collar. "The enchanted sigils are all stitched on the inside. Even if it glows, you shouldn't be able to see it."

"Right, where are your demon minions?" Grede asked in a low voice.

Jace patted his warlock's satchel. "In here."

"I mean the other one. You know what I mean." Grede's voice got even quieter.

"Right next to you on your right," Jace said.

Grede jolted and glanced to his right. "Goddamn it..."

Jace felt a lot more at ease — Molophir's stealth skills at this level should be enough to fool most opponents in Kalimdor.
