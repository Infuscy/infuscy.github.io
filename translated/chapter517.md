## Chapter 517: A Cost Both Great and Small

In the game, Death Coil counted as one of the warlock's ultimate moves — an absurdly long cooldown, but a striking effect. It could be cast instantly, slam into the enemy and send them into a stupor-like fear, deal hefty damage, and heal the warlock for the same amount.

It was, you might say, a bottom-of-the-deck magic that could turn defeat around and rewrite the board.

Judging from Morophil's description, the effect here was probably even more formidable than that. And compared to a Shadow Bolt in this world, it seemed to have another trait that wasn't especially prominent in the game but was extremely conspicuous here — homing!

World of Warcraft was a game where you selected a target and fired off an ability. Aside from ground-targeted skills, most of them tracked. But here, Fireballs, Shadow Bolts, Frostbolts, Arcane Missiles — all of them required aiming, or at the very least conscious guidance to redirect.

And this was a spell that could track, control the enemy, and deal damage all at once. No wonder just listening to the incantation, Jace felt its aggression was terrifyingly high.

But a magic this powerful — wasn't it a bit too strong? If you could cast it nonstop, wouldn't it be the ultimate offensive spell?

Surely it had some kind of cooldown mechanism, or some other annoying requirement to cast?

"I've heard of Kol'kamien," Sazeno said. "I know why this incantation has Nathrezim language for talking to the dead in it…"

"I know too," Morophil said with a smile. "Because the most powerful Kol'kamien uses shadow to sacrifice a demon soul. Only a demon servant's soul… will, for its master's power, madly devour everything of the enemy's until they die, then return all that vitality to its master."

Jace nodded. So that was what "Death Coil" meant in this context.

In the game, player death knights could fire off Death Coils too. It was just that, despite the higher cast frequency, it didn't pack the same punch as a warlock's.

So death knights were actually using the ghostly energy of undead creatures — or the withering souls of ghoul servants — in place of a demon's spirit essence. They'd inherited the Death Coil of the eredar or the Nathrezim dreadlords by another route, which was why its power was far weaker.

"Master's not gonna use my soul to harass someone someday, are you?" the imp said, pouting. "My soul's a teeny, faint, barely-there little blob. Using it to hit people would be pointless. Don't you do anything rash, master. Use this súcubus's instead — her soul's way stronger!"

Morophil gave the imp a withering look but said nothing.

Knowing Morophil's usual temperament, she would normally be falling over herself to emphasize just how willing she was to die for Jace.

But the thing was, a demon's death wasn't all that serious. Having your demonic soul sacrificed, though — that was a completely different matter.

Perhaps precisely because of that, Morophil finally hesitated for a moment and didn't rush to pledge her loyalty.

"Wouldn't a Soul Shard work?" Jace asked.

"Too weak. The effect it produced probably couldn't match a Life Drain or a Shadow Bolt of master's," Morophil said. "But if master really reached a life-or-death moment…"

She pursed her lips and continued, "Then there aren't many other options, are there? Since I'm so much stronger than this waste. And who knows — maybe after my soul gets used up, it'll fly back to the Twisting Nether on its own. I've never tried turning into a Death Coil myself."

With that, the succubus managed a strained smile.

Jace said, "Your whole self being here is more useful to me than a single Death Coil, Morophil. Don't forget I can summon a felhound too. Demons like those that are all over the Twisting Nether probably don't care much about their own souls."

"Anyway, this isn't a spell you can cast safely. I don't have a clear idea of how to use it either. I'll think about it some more. Right now I need to sleep first."

Sazeno let out an enormous sigh of relief. Morophil nodded.

That morning, Jace was lying on the corridor deck, reviewing the Death Coil incantation the imp had written down for him, quietly memorizing it.

The incantation wasn't long, but the pronunciation was tricky — part of it wasn't in the demonic tongue at all, and the way of producing the sounds felt deeply unnatural. Jace had spent several more rounds breaking it down and drilling it with the imp before he more or less got the hang of it.

As a human, producing all sorts of bizarre sounds that demons of wildly different builds and structures could make was already pretty unbelievable.

Just then, Jace caught a figure out of the corner of his eye. He looked over — it was Sylvanas, walking over this way.

"Jace." She handed over the Flowing Ice Crystal wrapped in a blue handkerchief embroidered with quel'dorei-style patterns. "I feel much better!"

"You sure you'll be alright from here on?" Jace asked, taking the crystal.

Sylvanas said, "I'm not one of the Magisters. My dependence on the Sunwell isn't that severe."

Jace said, "About that demon, Sylvanas… I'm sorry I didn't tell you earlier. But you know I couldn't spell everything out in a letter. And don't worry about what that súcubus might do — I've been fighting alongside her for a long time. You can trust her."

"I'm not worried," Sylvanas said. "Most warlock demon servants obey their warlocks without question. Even if the warlock ordered them to die, they'd charge ahead without a second thought. So don't count on breaking the bond between the two with some simple trick…"

"Is that out of some combat manual?" Jace asked.

"More or less," Sylvanas said. "After the Orc Wars, every defender of Quel'Thalas needed to know how to deal with warlocks. What's more, even the Amani trolls have learned from the orcs how to use demons in battle."

Jace slowly nodded. The elf continued, "You're a comrade worth trusting, and you have your own way of fighting. Think back — at first we couldn't even accept Wildhammer dwarves as allies, but in the end we came to recognize their valor and reliability."

She paused there, then said flatly, "Besides, even if we're good friends, I can't meddle in your private life."

Now she'd said it like that, Jace didn't know how to continue. After a moment, he asked, "So when you get back this time, can you become a Ranger-Lord?"

The moment Ranger-Lord came up, the shadow that the succubus had cast over Sylvanas's face dissipated quite a bit.

"I'm not sure, but a mission like this will add to my credentials as a Farstrider. It'll put me ahead of those old rangers who spend every day east of the Sun Temple mixing reconnaissance with slacking off on patrol, so they'll stop harping on my age."

"Hunting Teron Gorefiend and fighting in the Dark Portal campaign aren't enough?" Jace asked.

"Combat ability is only one side of a Farstrider, Jace," Sylvanas said. "Exploration and investigation are the other side. So I have to complete this mission — even if it means cooperating with a demon."

Jace glanced at her. "Since you don't mind, then I can set my mind at ease."

"Of course I don't mind," Sylvanas said. There was something almost petulant in her tone.

*So do you mind or don't you?*

Naturally, Jace wasn't bored enough to just come out and ask.

Sylvanas quickly added, "If there's nothing else, I'll be going."

Jace hadn't even processed whether there was anything else before the elf turned and headed back the way she'd come.

He turned back to look at the deep blue sea, its surface throwing back a blinding white glare under the brilliant sunlight. He figured this had gone way better than he'd expected. The fact that the Farstrider they'd sent, this Aethwyn Goldthorn lady, could cooperate with a succubus — wasn't that exactly what he'd hoped for in the first place?

Even if her temper had clearly gotten worse.
