Chapter 518: Riptide Bay

Even though Jace, Grede, and Sylvanas had spent the whole voyage worrying that the massive Clever Traveler might be attacked by naga or some other sea monster — the kind of kraken marked on maps, or tide-calling vengeful spirits — the journey across the Great Sea, beyond being excruciatingly long and boring, beyond a bout of food poisoning that swept through most of the human passengers on board including Jace for no discernible reason, and beyond two fairly harrowing storms, had produced no other trouble whatsoever.

The goblin captain pinned the blame on the crew's parrot shitting in the food storehouse, so he chopped the parrot's head off and tossed it into the sea, and that was the end of that.

Nobody quite understood why parrot droppings were that toxic, but nobody wanted to make a fuss in the middle of the Great Sea either.

By the sixth week of sailing, on the thirty-seventh day, Jace still felt like he couldn't quite keep food down. Dizzy, queasy, nauseous, with a constant dull ache in his belly.

But the good news was that the oppressive shadow saturation out in the deep Great Sea had dissipated.

The natural magical presence, suppressed all this time by the heavy fathomless water and the boundless shadow, had returned. He felt like even his breathing came easier.

Logically speaking, he was a warlock, and his prior experiences had proven he had plenty of shadow affinity. But the shadow of the Great Sea had no interest in being "affiliated" with him, because it all had a master.

And that master's name, when out on the open sea, brought nothing but discomfort even when thought of silently in the back of one's mind.

Today the sailors all seemed to be in good spirits. Chatter and laughter were everywhere. In a month on board, Jace had even picked up quite a few goblin curses.

Standing on deck, gazing to the side in the direction the ship was heading, the view was still the same monotonous ocean — though the color seemed a little different. It had lightened somewhat.

Then, the cry of seabirds drifted from the distance.

Jace looked up and realized that several white birds of some unknown species had already gathered around the mast.

Did that mean they were close to a continent?

Before he could find a goblin sailor to ask, the first mate up front suddenly bellowed something in goblin tongue, and all the sailors took up the shout. Then the first mate yelled over to the cluster of human passengers: "If this is your first time in Kalimdore, honored guests, you may come to the bow now — because the next moment will change your entire perception!"

"Behold the legendary mysterious continent! Behold this unknown, even disbelieved-of end of the world! Behold this ancient land that has survived for ten thousand years! This is no world-shaking wonder you can see for a gold or two."

Jace couldn't help but follow the crowd to the bow and look out into the distance. At the very edge of the horizon, a thin line had appeared — stretching left and right without visible end.

The goblin first mate pointed at that thin line and said to the crowd craning their necks, "Look, and don't doubt your eyes. Not all miracles arrive with thunder and fanfare. The pivotal moments that rewrite your life often begin this plainly."

After that, he surveyed the stunned crowd with satisfaction, pacing back and forth in triumph. That was when Jace asked, "Then will we reach Steamwheedle Port soon?"

"No," the goblin first mate said. "Probably still a few days. After this we'll need to head south for another two or three days before reaching Steamwheedle Port. To avoid the islands held by trolls and naga, we sail as far north as we can — but if we go too far north, we'll get pulled off course by the Maelstrom bit by bit, and we might not even be able to get out. You know about the Maelstrom, human?"

"I know," Jace said.

"Lots of humans think the Maelstrom is a rumor, but I'm telling you — the Maelstrom is real." The first mate patted Jace on the lower leg, then gave his robe a strange look and added, "Anyway, we can't make a straight run to Steamwheedle Port. Up ahead is Riptide Bay. When we get close to land, don't lean on the deck railing, or the murlocs will drag you into the sea!"

Hearing the first mate's words, several passengers had already drifted away from the edge of the deck and toward the middle.

Grede and Sylvanas also came to the bow. Grede couldn't contain himself — he charged to the highest point, squeezed through the crowd, and stared into the distance, terrified of missing a single detail. Sylvanas came to Jace's side, took a deep breath, and said, "The kin who still remain on this land did not get along well with my ancestors."

"You're worried about running into them?" Jace asked.

"A little," Sylvanas said. "Thinking about it, I feel uneasy."

"It might also be the temperature, the humidity, the scent… both the smell and the magical aura are completely different from the east. It's all too unfamiliar…"

Jace glanced at the dwarf gripping the railing and craning forward and said, "At least the people are still the same bunch."

Sylvanas smiled and nodded.

Jace asked, "Didn't Quel'Thalas — or the Farstriders, or the Reliquary — tell you how to handle things if you met the elves here? Like following some set of diplomatic protocols your predecessors worked out?"

"Don't make contact," Sylvanas said. "They don't want me making any contact with the kin here. Of course, they mean those who still call themselves kaldorei, the crowd outside Eldre'Thalas that has nothing to do with the ancient noble we're tracking. But I'm still worried about unpredictable conflict breaking out."

It wasn't just Sylvanas who was worried. Jace, in truth, felt a twinge of dread in his gut too.

Night elves, though few in number, were spread across the whole of Kalimdore.

Though their homeland and sacred mountain were in the far north, there was also a thousand-year-old military fortress deep in the desert of Silithus in the south, and an island off the coast of the Feralas jungles where Eldre'Thalas lay — and they certainly patrolled and scouted the deep jungle too.

But once you ventured deep into Kalimdore, it was hard to say you wouldn't run into them.

And these night elves harbored an absolute, bone-deep hatred for demons — nothing like Quel'Thalas, where rejection still carried a hint of ambiguous, barely-there ambiguity.

So if they caught sight of Jace and his demons, conflict would probably be unavoidable.

He could only hope that when the time came, if they really did run into any, Morophil and Sazeno would react fast and vanish before a night elf patrol spotted them.

Still, once they reached Steamwheedle Port and Gadgetzan, Jace figured it would be best to get the goblins' read on the night elves' situation first and steer well clear of their likely patrol routes.

Of course, he couldn't hold out much hope either. After all, asking goblins to have a clear picture of night elf patrol routes was a tall order.

Translator Notes

Flags

- Riptide Bay (狂潮湾): No canonical WoW name confirmed for this bay on the Thanalan coast. Rendered descriptively as "Riptide Bay." - Steamwheedle Port (热砂港): Canonical WoW name for the Trade Cartel port near Thanalan — used here instead of a literal "Hot Sand Port." - The Maelstrom (大漩涡): Canonical. Referenced by the first mate as proof the sea is dangerous. - Kaldorei (卡多雷): Night elves' name for themselves, per glossary. Sylvanas distinguishes the kaldorei from the quel'dorei and the Eldre'Thalas ancient noble they're tracking. - Eldre'Thalas (埃雷萨拉斯): The ancient night elf city in Feralas — the fic's quest target. Kept as canonical. - Great Sea shadow master: The unnamed presence whose shadow saturates the deep ocean — deliberately left unnamed in the original. Preserved as an intentional mystery. - Parrot food poisoning: Comic beat preserved; the goblin captain's scapegoating is played for laughs.
