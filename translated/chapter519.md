## Chapter 519: Steamwheedle Port

The *Clever Traveler* sailed along the coast for three days. Jace watched the already unbroken barren slopes grow drier and drier, the cliffs, crags, and boulders gradually flattening out. Then, after surging past a steep high cliff one morning, the view was entirely swallowed by yellow sand.

By noon, the ship was sailing into a small bay ringed by low hills. Here, at last, Jace could make out a scattering of yellowed, half-dead palm trees crowding the shore one by one. And among those palm trees, squat mud-brick buildings huddled in layers behind the trees and boulders. Before he'd even spotted a person, Jace could already hear a hubbub of goblin voices from the shore.

The Thanalan Desert was hot and dry, and even the sea air felt like the head-on blast of a black dragon's snarl, making it hard to breathe.

Jace had long since taken off the mountain-lion-skin cloak he'd worn the whole trip to block the spray and rain, undone a few buttons on his robe, and bared his chest to cool off for a bit. Beside him, Grede, who was completely immune to sunburn, and the other dwarves had long since stripped off their shirts, baring brown chests and shoulders — each one looking like a great tumbling clod of dirt.

Sylvanas, whose skin already carried a faint pinkish tint, was now flushed red from the heat, sweating all over, with silver hair clinging to her forehead in strands.

High elves, who spent their lives in the Evermoon Forest with its climate curated by the Sunwell, probably weren't quite accustomed to an extreme like this.

Compared to Booty Bay, Steamwheedle Port was like a village of a few hundred people. When the *Clever Traveler* entered this harbor, she was like an elephant squeezing into a pigsty — carefully, slowly, trying not to touch any fragile buildings, small piers, or other docked boats.

After a clanging of bells, a crowd of goblins gathered around the ship, ready to help unload at any moment. Jace stood at the exit ramp, looking into the port. Goblins aside, he could already spot quite a few trolls and even humans moving about inside.

"I'm a little thirsty," Sylvanas said softly.

Grede glanced at the heat-shimmered landscape in the distance. "I can imagine how hard water is to come by here."

"I'm still a mage, more or less," Jace said, patting the Song of the Sky at his waist. "You two can set your minds at ease on that score."

"Ah, I remember now — that sword wasn't given to him for nothing, Sylvanas," Grede nodded. "Without that sword stabilizing his arcane energy, you'd best see his Conjure Water for yourself. Scarier than a Dark Iron powder keg, I'm telling you!"

"Fuck off," Jace said with a frown. "Hurry up and get off the ship! The people ahead have already moved!"

Sylvanas couldn't help but smile and followed the two shoving-each-other men forward.

Though Steamwheedle Port didn't look all that big, it was considerably larger than its in-game scale. At least from the dock, you could immediately make out neatly stacked crates and, behind them, rows of cargo tents, inns, and other mud-and-stone buildings of uncertain purpose — and naturally, no shortage of shops of every kind.

The main road running through the middle of Steamwheedle Port from the dock also led straight out into the depths of the desert. A signpost by the road pointed in that direction, bearing words in both goblin tongue and Common: "Gadgetzan — 44 miles."

"Look at this — Gadgetzan's still a long way off," Jace said, pointing at the sign. "Let's rest here today."

Grede looked up at the inn rooms higher up. "Don't know if the inns have any vacancy. This port looks small and packed."

The three of them climbed the stone-ladder steps and ducked into one of those squat, shed-like buildings. Jace looked at the goblin proprietor tilted back behind the counter. "Got any rooms?"

The goblin took off his glasses, looked Sylvanas over, then looked Jace over. "There's one in the back. And there's an empty one in the west building."

"Two rooms," Jace said, glancing back at the others. "Sylvanas, you want a room to yourself?"

"Am I going to… stay with… you…?"

Grede stared hard, his eyes darting toward Jace's side. Jace knew exactly what the bastard meant. He definitely didn't want to share a room with Morophil.

"So, how about you two share?" Jace asked.

"No!" the dwarf and the elf said in unison.

"Argh, forget it!" Grede waved a hand. "I don't have all those long-eyebrow hang-ups. I'll share with you, Jace Sesso!"

Sylvanas looked away to the side. The goblin proprietor stared at the three of them with an utterly baffled expression as they sorted out the rooms, then scrutinized Jace a second time, frowning as if he couldn't figure out what was off.

"How much?" Jace asked.

"Five silver…" The goblin held up all five fingers on both hands. "Per room. So two rooms for one night is ten silver."

"Highway robbery?!" Grede bellowed. Even Sylvanas jumped.

The goblin grinned a bright, professional false smile. "Welcome to Kalimdore, everyone."

"Let's take one room," the elf said with a frown.

Jace asked, "You sure?"

The dwarf gaped at the elf again.

"I'm sure," Sylvanas said.

"Then one room," Jace said, looking at the proprietor.

The proprietor gave all three of them a look of contempt, his gaze lingering especially on their clothing and weapons, then nodded reluctantly and pulled out a piece of paper to write.

"How do you get to Gadgetzan from here?" Jace asked.

"Follow the caravan tomorrow morning," the goblin said, looking up. "Of course, riding along costs money. But you can tag along behind as free security, and nobody'll stop you."

"Not bad," Grede nodded.

Following the goblin up to the room on the second floor, they saw that the bed, the floor, the table — all of it was covered in sand that no amount of sweeping could clear.

Sylvanas managed to clean the only bed in the room with some effort, and a gust of wind from outside immediately blew in more sand from some unseen nook.

Though the little inn at Steamwheedle Port couldn't hold a candle to Stormwind or Booty Bay, at least there was a proper bed and a small building that blocked the wind and rain.

After all, once they reached Gadgetzan and set out again, there was no telling if they'd ever get to lie on a bed again.

Grede spat a few times to clear the grit from his lips, dusted the sand off his clothes, and said, "This reminds me of the time we went to that Dark Iron excavation site, with that dwarf — Dagg'thori. You remember?"

"I remember," Jace said. "But back then we either slept in ditches or holed up in caves. I wasn't expecting sand everywhere even in a proper inn now."

"It's not Dalaran," Grede said, pointing at Jace's waist. "Quick, use that magic sword of yours to conjure us some water, Jace. That's the priority right now!"

Hearing that, Sylvanas licked her somewhat dry lips too.

## Flags

- **Song of the Sky (碧空之歌):** Jace's sword, referenced as the arcane stabilizer for his Conjure Water. Kept as established translation.
- **Gadgetzan 44 miles (通往加基森四十四里):** The signpost uses "miles" in the English rendering — the Chinese 里 is literally "li," but the fic treats goblin/Common signage as using Common units.
- **Marin Noggenfogger (马林·诺格弗格):** Canonical WoW goblin NPC, head of Gadgetzan Water Company. Name kept canonical.
- **Room-sharing comedy:** The three-way room negotiation (Grede refusing to room with Morophil, Sylvanas and Grede both refusing to room with each other) is preserved as written — played for humor.
- **Dagg'thori (达葛特):** Grede's reference to a Dark Iron dwarf from a prior excavation. Name rendered per the fic's convention.
- **Booty Bay comparison:** Sylvanas's heat discomfort vs. Evermoon Forest climate is character-consistent worldbuilding.
