## Chapter 520: Wasteland and Beasts

Early the next morning, before dawn, the three of them came to the outskirts of Steamwheedle Port. Several caravans were already preparing to set out. Grede charged to the front — he'd already drunk his fill of water from Jace's magic, slept a full, replenished sleep, and was primed for a new-continent adventure. He couldn't have been more fired up.

Vereesa, on the other hand, hadn't slept the entire night. When Jace had gotten up in the middle of the night, he'd found her over there, eyes fixed on this side, leaning motionless against the wall. Those normally gentle arcane-infused eyes had looked just like a wolf's in the dead of night.

Still, for her, going a night without sleep wasn't any great hardship.

Up ahead, Grede suddenly yelped as if startled. Jace turned back and saw what looked like small mountains rising up from the front, the ground trembling faintly along with them.

Grede backed up several paces. Vereesa and Jace both stopped where they were, watching as two enormous monsters lifted themselves up before them, slowly opening their mouths to take a long, leisurely, deep breath, a thunderous low drumbeat rumbling from their throats.

These beasts, with their dark skin and hair-covered backs, had skin covered in cracks and scabs like stone. They were lashed with coarse and fine hemp ropes binding who knew how many packages and crates, yet when they stood up they didn't seem to find it especially taxing.

Combined with legs as thick as an elephant's and a forked horn that curved upward from the tip of the snout, they looked a bit like rhinoceroses — but with a long dinosaur tail dragging behind them.

"What the hell is that?" Grede asked, moving to Jace's side.

"Kodo," Jace said.

"Do they eat people?" Vereesa asked.

"Not a chance," Jace said. "There's no way goblins could tame a man-eating monster as a pack beast, right? But I thought only tauren raised these things."

The dwarf set his mind at ease. He looked at Jace, then at the two hulking beasts. "Clearly, even the stuff in libraries has its limits."

The goblins whooped and hollered at the powerful kodos, urging them forward. Jace, Grede, and Vereesa followed along, hoping to tag behind a transport caravan and get a free escort-and-guide squad.

As they set out, Jace noticed that some goblins, upon getting up, habitually started collecting small water bottles from the ground and stuffing them into their packs. Jace found a broken water bottle and looked at it. It bore three words in goblin script. One he recognized as "Gadgetzan," but the other two were a mystery.

"You two know what this is?" Jace passed it to the elf and dwarf beside him.

"Ah, I know this. It's Gadgetzan Water Company," Grede said, wiping the sand off the bottle. "I heard about it last night. Every goblin in Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port drinks water drawn by them. The head of this company's called Marin Noggenfogger. He's Steamwheedle Cartel too, so this company basically runs Gadgetzan."

"A desert city being controlled by a water company — that tracks," Jace said.

"You see those mercenaries up ahead?" Vereesa said. "They're fully armed, carrying weapons. There's got to be something dangerous in this desert."

"Whatever dangerous beasts there are, they'd be scared off by these monsters," Grede said, watching the swaying kodo tail ahead.

Jace said, "These kodos the goblins can tame probably don't have much of a temper. If a real charge happens, they won't be lifting a finger to help the goblins and the mercenaries. We'd better stay sharp!"

The goblin race wasn't a monolith either. It wasn't just the Bilgewater Cartel, the Steamwheedle Cartel, and the Venture Company running the show. There were other factions too — the Southsea Pirates, for instance, had grown out of a goblin criminal organization that had sprung up around Steamwheedle Port in opposition to the Steamwheedle Cartel, and later expanded thanks to the many immigrants from the Eastern Kingdoms.

And in the desert, there was no shortage of sand bandits looking to snatch water resources or other valuables.

Despite all these worries, the road between Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port was, in the end, vigorously secured by the Steamwheedle Cartel. There were plenty of waystations and supply points along the way. Aside from the hyenas that could always be seen watching from afar in the sand, there was nothing else that qualified as a threat.

By evening, Jace could already make out from a distance the square-shaped town sitting low in the middle of the vast sands.

The legendary Gadgetzan — capital of the Steamwheedle Cartel. The town, enclosed by earthen walls, was bustling. Both the west gate and the north gate had steady streams of pedestrians and caravans flowing into the distance.

Columns of billowing black smoke rose from the town. Machines were turning everywhere. Buildings of varying heights stood scattered about in disorderly fashion. Purple and dim-yellow lights lit the narrow streets between the buildings in the dusky red evening, giving Jace the feeling that he'd stepped straight out of a fantasy desert and into the entrance of a steampunk world.

The kodos were guided to the pens outside the city. Everyone else joined the goblins at the trading post and workers of other races in hauling goods into town.

Standing near the entrance to Gadgetzan, watching the constant flow of every race coming in and out, Jace tried sweeping the crowd with Sense Demons — and immediately found that there really were demons mixed in among the people… Most of them were imps, but there were individual succubi and other even stranger demons too.

Vereesa pulled her hood tighter and said softly, "I seem to be the only Quel'dorei here. This town makes me feel a little uneasy."

Hearing her, Jace realized that no matter how mixed and chaotic this place was, there truly wasn't a single elf in the crowd.

The dwarf glanced at the elf. "Nothing to worry about, Vereesa. Look at the succubus right next to you. Is there anything in Gadgetzan more terrifying than this she-demon?"

Vereesa glanced over at the succubus. Morophil was staring right at her too, and there seemed to be something provocative in her gaze — as if mocking the elf's caution.

"Fair enough. You're not wrong," the elf said with a sigh.

As she said it, she glanced at Jace again. Jace could only nod in agreement. He hadn't expected his dark magic to slowly turn into a source of psychological comfort for his friends.

"Then let's go!" Grede swaggered toward the iron gate of Gadgetzan.

Jace and Vereesa followed him. As they drew near the walls, a hoarse blend of goblin tongue and Common — the sound of machines — began to filter through.

"Sincere greetings from Gadgetzan and the Steamwheedle Cartel! Welcome to the heart of the scorching desert! Sincere greetings from Gadgetzan and the Steamwheedle Cartel! Welcome to…"
