Chapter 521: The Desert City

Gadgetzan was orders of magnitude livelier than Steamwheedle Port — though perhaps that wasn't a fair comparison to begin with, since Steamwheedle Port was really just part of Gadgetzan anyway.

Walking the streets here, Jace felt it was even more bustling than Booty Bay.

And the goblin-to-other-races ratio here was far higher than in Booty Bay. Booty Bay might be run by goblins, but it felt like most people there were outsiders — humans, dwarves, trolls, even gnomes.

Here, though, non-goblins were only a small fraction. The majority of residents were goblins, and they came in staggering variety — short, tall, dark-skinned, light-skinned. There were even a number of tall, powerfully built goblins walking the streets who stood nearly as high as Grede; the smaller goblins eyeing them looked more terrified than they would be seeing a troll.

In the game, Gadgetzan had been little more than a walled-off desert village. But this Gadgetzan had the genuine feel of a city — a tangled, easy-to-get-lost maze of intersecting streets, the metallic reek of machinery and kerosene fumes hanging in the air everywhere, a cacophony of noise roaring from one end of the street to the other.

You could tell the goblins had been operating here for at least a few decades; many of the buildings had aged to the point where they looked like they might topple over at any moment.

Still, though Gadgetzan seemed like the kind of place made for having a good time, the three of them had no mood to go gallivanting about. They were here on a mission and couldn't afford to waste too much time.

After no small amount of effort and directions from passersby, they finally found a small inn. By the time they reached the door, Jace and Grede had already been unable to resist pulling off their boots and shaking the sand out of their cloaks.

This was the one thing the place still had in common with the game version — the sand. It was on the streets, on the buildings, on the people around them… sand, everywhere.

Wynne stood off to one side looking perfectly unbothered. Jace glanced at her. "No sand in your boots?"

"When you enter a desert, shouldn't you tie off your boots?" the elf said, lowering her head. "When we went hunting the Shattered Hand clan and that death knight, Instructor Kefar told us as much. You two went through that wasteland too — you'd think you'd have picked up a thing or two."

Grede and Jace exchanged a glance. Neither replied. They just kept quietly pouring sand from their boots.

Inn prices in Gadgetzan were far more reasonable than in Steamwheedle Port. Two silver bought you a room for the night. Figuring this might be the last civilized spot they'd find before pushing deeper into Kalimdor, the three of them took three rooms and planned to have a proper night's rest.

In the middle of the night, Jace came down to the front desk of the inn and looked at the goblin innkeeper. "I'd like to know… sir, about the route north."

"Just follow the road, cross the pass, and you'll be at the Shimmering Flats, sir," the goblin said without looking up, his Common fluent and practiced. "No need to plan any route. Plenty of people on the road, and this whole area is safe. The worst you'll run into are basilisks that turn you to crystal."

Seeing Jace at a loss for words, he added, "No worries, that's treatable. Marin Noggenfogger has all sorts of remedies — provided you haven't been completely turned to crystal by the time someone finds you. You take my meaning."

Jace cleared his throat. "I'm not talking about the Shimmering Flats. I mean… Maraudon. Do you know Maraudon, sir?"

"Maraudon?" The goblin put down his newspaper.

Jace produced the route map he'd copied out by hand from the Reliquary and laid it out for the goblin innkeeper. "See here, sir, here's what I was thinking…"

He pointed to the Shimmering Flats in the north, then traced along the narrow stretch of Thousand Needles toward Feralas…

When his finger was halfway through Thousand Needles, the goblin rapped it hard with his pipe. "Not here, human."

"What's wrong with here?" Jace asked.

"You trying to cut straight through the centaur khanates?" the goblin said with a laugh. "You're not the first adventurer in the last few years who's wanted to reach Maraudon, but you're the first one I've seen draw up a route based on nothing but a map and zero common sense."

Jace stared at the map in his hand for a moment. "So we go north?"

"You'll have to go through Agama'gor." The goblin pointed to the high cliffs north of Thousand Needles. "Go up through here, and pray the quilboar haven't blocked the road. At least the quilboar don't patrol as hard as the centaur. At least I've seen people take this road and come back alive."

"Would we run into elves on this route?" Jace asked.

"You mean the forest elves of Kalimdor? Not much chance." The goblin smiled. "Well, maybe a slim chance. But even if you did run into them, they wouldn't do anything to you. They'd just watch you from a distance until you disappeared — or they did. I've never seen it myself, mind you, but that's what everyone says. At least I've never heard of those elves going out of the forest to kill anyone."

"There's really no way through the centaur-held parts of the Needles?" Jace asked.

The goblin raised an eyebrow. "A hundred percent? I can't say that. Maybe you'll charge straight into a tribe of centaur who all happen to be blind."

The goblin burst out laughing. Jace was in no mood to laugh. He just looked back down at his map.

Seeing Jace's expression, the goblin's face slowly cooled. "I'm just telling you this and you probably won't believe me. Go see for yourself, human."

"I'm just thinking about the quilboar, sir. It's not that I don't believe you." Jace offered an apologetic smile. "If we head to Agama'gor, besides the quilboar, do I need to worry about hostile tauren tribes?"

"Hostile or not, the tauren are nearly extinct!" the goblin said. "I'd wager the few tauren in Booty Bay and Gadgetzan combined already outnumber the ones still in the wild. Or maybe they're hiding away in some cranny somewhere, barely clinging to life — I don't know. The tauren I know never talks about his homeland."

"All right." Jace nodded and set two silver coins on the desk. "One more question, sir."

The goblin pocketed the coins, raised an eyebrow, and gave a little nod, his tone softening at once. "Go on, then. I've got nothing better to do anyway."

Jace asked, "Have you ever heard the name Dasmora Dark-Eye?"

"Uh…" The goblin squinted, thinking. "No… not really. What kind of name is that?"

"Might be an elf," Jace offered.

"There are barely any elves in Gadgetzan," the goblin said. "Not the elves from the other side, either — and certainly no Kalimdor elves. Your friend there's already done plenty to raise the elf concentration in this city all on her own. I mean, haven't you noticed how everyone out there keeps staring at your friend?"

Jace thanked him, found a stool nearby, and sat staring at his map, lost in thought.

If Thousand Needles was a dead end, then heading north into the mountains called Agama'gor, as the goblin described… that meant they'd have to cut straight across a vast grassland, then enter the Stonetalon Mountains, and from there reach Desolace, where Maraudon was.

Jace felt he couldn't rely solely on game knowledge to plan the route anymore. Best to find a reliable guide.

But even the goblins… they only really knew the area around Gadgetzan. Any farther — whether to Feralas, the Barrens, or even Stonetalon — they didn't seem to know much more than he did.

One step at a time, then.
