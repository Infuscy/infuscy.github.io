Chapter 523: The Shimmering Flats

Considering that once they left Gadgetzan and headed north, they probably wouldn't encounter many people they could talk to, or settlements of any kind, the three of them packed a fair amount of salt and spices on top of a small supply of long-lasting rations — dried meat and dried fruit for emergencies — ready to live off the land for a good while.

That evening, Jace and Grede also stopped by Marin Noggenfogger's alchemy shop and spent a gold coin stocking up on Nature Resistance Potions and antidotes to fill their potion satchels, plus some bandages for emergency first aid.

Grede always carried plenty of healing potions on him, so between the two of them, staying alive was more or less covered.

Early the next morning, they left Gadgetzan with a Gadgetzan patrol heading for the Shimmering Flats. After crossing a pass to the north, they caught their first glimpse of the vast salt flats.

The Shimmering Flats — true to the name. The moment they came around the mouth of the canyon, a blinding, mirror-white expanse stretched out before them, perfectly flat and endless, a surface so glaring it was genuinely unpleasant to look at.

The visibility here was terrifying. There was nothing on the salt flats at all — no plants, no animals, no terrain features like rocks or anything…

Just flat, endless white. In the distance you could make out a line of figures walking — goblins or some other race, who could tell — slowly advancing. You knew they were moving, but at that range it looked as if they were marching on the spot.

Gadgetzan's rocket-racing track sat right on the Shimmering Flats. From here the patrol would head east toward the raceway.

There probably wasn't a race on at the moment, and Jace's group had zero interest in watching those dangerous machines tear around at top speed anyway. They said goodbye to the patrol and carried on west.

Looking west, at the very edge of the horizon you could make out faint silhouettes of mountains obscured by cloud or haze — at least that gave the people trudging across this white death something to aim for.

Jace listened to the crunch of salt crystals under his gaze as he stared into the distance. He even felt as though the surface of the salt flats curved slightly — he couldn't tell if it was a trick of the eye or if he was actually sensing the curvature of Azeroth.

After walking for he didn't know how long, Jace spotted a small sign standing in the distance.

It took ages to reach it. The sign read: You are about to leave Gadgetzan's jurisdiction. Good luck.

Jace looked over at Grede. Grede just flicked the sign with his finger. "Finally won't have to see those green-skinned runts anymore."

"Don't know how much longer I can stand this godforsaken place," Wynne wiped her sweat and glanced at Morfell, who looked perfectly serene at Jace's side. "I'm actually starting to envy these demons."

"Compared to places I've been, this is quite the pleasant climate, elf," Morfell said with a smile.

At that, Wynne's expression darkened. She drew a breath. "I've been to the Burning Plains too. Compared to there… this is nothing, right, Jace?"

"Of course. The Burning Plains have lava flowing right across the surface."

Jace humored her, but of course he knew full well that the places demons endured in the Twisting Nether were orders of magnitude worse than the Burning Plains.

By now he'd expected Sazeno to start running his mouth again, but the imp just clung to Morfell's neck, wearing a half-smirk as he watched the elf and the succubus. He didn't say a word.

So even he knew who was safe to mess with and who wasn't. Morfell threatened to kill him every day, but with his master right there, he didn't dare actually start a fight that might cost a demon its life.

This elf, though — that was another matter entirely.

On this kind of alien terrain, your sense of distance was always off. Jace had figured they'd reach the mouth leading into Thousand Needles before nightfall, but by midnight it still seemed impossibly far away.

By now neither the dwarf nor the elf was complaining about the heat — after all, it was still winter, and at night it was bitterly cold.

After a long walk they found a gully and huddled in it together, warming up for a while.

"I'm done in, lads," Grede spread his cloak on the ground, flopped onto it, and shut his eyes. "I'm sleeping first. You lot who can stay up all night can argue over who keeps watch…"

"That'd be me, then," Wynne said.

"No need." Jace pointed to the succubus reclining behind him. "With her here, let her keep watch."

"Delighted to be of service," Morfell said, lifting her eyes to the elf with a smile. The elf shot her a look. "No. I will not trust a demon to stand guard. That's—"

"Don't fight it, little elf," Grede mumbled, eyes still closed. "Look how drained you are. Get a proper sleep and enjoy the perks of traveling with an evil warlock. Demons don't blink for tens of thousands of years."

"I can't…" Wynne said quietly.

"Then you two co-operate," Jace said, looking at Morfell. "No fighting."

Morfell leaned on Jace's shoulder and looked at the elf. "Then let's work together, Lady Windrunner."

"There's nothing ladylike about it."

Wynne murmured that, got to her feet, wrapped her cloak around herself, and walked out to the edge of the gully.

"What was the point of all that," Jace said, exasperated.

"Master thinks I talk too much again? Wasn't I just being friendly, as master instructed?" Morfell blinked. "I thought I expressed it quite precisely."

"Express a little less," Jace said. He threw down his cloak, lay back, and was snoring within seconds.

Late in the night, Jace felt as though he'd barely shut his eyes before something deep in his subconscious seemed to be calling to him… it was Sazeno's voice.

"Jace Sesso!"

Jace opened his eyes and looked around. Morfell was crouching some distance away, looking back at him. He was about to speak when the imp immediately gestured for him to stay silent.

After his eyes adjusted to the dark, he noticed that several dark shapes had appeared above the edge of the gully. The creatures were craning their necks, staring down at the two figures lying below.

Jace shook Grede beside him. The dwarf jerked upright with a start, and the dark shapes all swiveled to stare at the small dwarf at once.

Surrounded by these strange things, Grede leaned close to Jace and whispered, "What are these things?"

"Basilisks," Morfell said. "A lot of them. Master, what do you want to do?"

"Where's Wynne?" Jace asked.

"The elf went out to find food!" Sazeno said.

"So she's actually pretty comfortable leaving you two demons alone…" Grede said.

"You've got more immediate problems to worry about," Jace gripped the hilt of the sword at his side. "These basilisks can really turn people to stone!"

"I know that!" Grede shouted, hefting his hammer. Suddenly, a whistle sounded from outside the gully. At the sound, all the basilisks puffed up their stone-like scales and turned toward it in unison.

The whistle wound through a few turns, playing a simple melody, and a number of the basilisks immediately turned and left, as if retreating on command.

"What's happening?" Grede asked. "Who's blowing that whistle?"

Jace could already tell who it was — he'd grown quite familiar with that whistle by now.

Wynne poked her head in from outside. "You two alright?"

"You really know your stuff, little elf," Grede laughed.

Jace asked curiously, "Do the beasts of Kalimdor understand whistles from another continent too?"

Wynne slid into the gully, stood up, and dusted off her clothes. "Now I know they do."

Then the dwarf noticed there was still one dark shape up above that hadn't left. "Uh, why didn't that one run?"

"I told it to stay a while," Wynne said. "It's going to be our breakfast."

"An evil Quel'dorei indeed," the dwarf muttered under his throat.

"Then don't eat it!" Wynne said, annoyed.

"We're eating!" Grede immediately laughed. "How could we say no to that!"
