## Chapter 524: Into the Needles

The next day, by afternoon, the haze in the distance had cleared considerably. The air and visibility sharpened, and Jace noticed a single colossal stone pillar standing alone in the center of his field of view.

It didn't look like a finished pillar, the kind you'd find at a temple — more like a chain of boulders stacked to a terrifying height in a precarious balance. The topmost stone seemed to sway back and forth in the Shimmering Flats wind, as though it might come crashing down at any moment.

Before long, as they drew closer to this pillar, a thinner, taller one appeared in the distance… and then a taller one still, looming even farther off.

Jace scuffed at the cracked earth underfoot. This was no longer pure salt — it was mixed with no small amount of soil and stone.

He uncapped his waterskin and drained the last mouthful, licking his parched lips. "We've reached Thousand Needles!"

Grede craned his neck, staring up at the giant pillar beside them. "This really is a beautiful place. It's like Kalimdor is a world out of a dream."

Vereesa was looking at another pillar. She pointed into the air. "Look — what's that?"

Jace followed where Vereesa was pointing and saw a shadow glide across the surface of the pillar. Looking higher, he caught sight of something indistinct slowly gliding across the sky.

"Is that… a dragon?" Grede asked.

Vereesa shook her head. "I don't know."

When the thing flew into a patch of clear sunlight, Jace could finally make it out.

Grede wasn't wrong to think it looked like a dragon. The creature had two enormous, bat-like wings and dragged a long, slender tail behind it. But the biggest tell was that its forelimbs were the wings themselves — it had only two hind legs dangling behind.

So even though the glare of the sun made it hard to make out in sharp detail, Jace could tell this wasn't a real dragon. All dragons — male or female, young or grown — had two wings and four legs.

If Jace's guess was right, this was a wyvern.

Wyverns were a native creature of Kalimdor, living in the dry valleys of Thousand Needles and the Stonetalon Mountains. A two-winged flying beast somewhat resembling a male lion — though both males and females had manes of neck fur.

A decidedly mysterious creature. After the orcs arrived, they learned from the tauren how to communicate with these beasts. The tauren called them wind riders and regarded them as sacred companions — much as the Wildhammer dwarves regarded gryphons. The orcish New Horde later inherited that belief.

Thinking of that, Jace looked at the dwarf and shook his head. This was a creature even more mysterious than a kodo beast. If he knew too much, it'd seem suspiciously odd.

"That's not a dragon!" Sazeno said. "Just an ordinary flying beast, at least. I don't sense any magic from it. Maybe it's a bit like the gryphons you dwarves like!"

"Wonder if you can ride it," Grede said, pointing up.

"What I am sure of is it definitely doesn't understand Common or Dwarvish," Vereesa said, adjusting the quiver at her hip. "Best not to draw its attention. A creature like that is clearly a lot smarter than a basilisk. My beast commands probably wouldn't do much."

Jace and Grede both nodded. Hugging the pillars, they advanced in the shadows, staying out of sight of the silhouette wheeling overhead.

That thing was enormous — even from here it looked to be the size of a wild horse or bigger. If it really stooped to attack, even if they could beat it easily, someone was bound to get hurt.

And of course, as someone who'd played the game, Jace knew wyverns generally lived near their nests. Their nests could hold dozens of others of their kind. If you provoked one, you might end up drawing a whole swarm of them looking for revenge — and that'd be a proper mess.

After walking for a while in the direction away from the wyvern, the three of them had pushed deep into Thousand Needles.

The wind howled through the canyon like a wail of ghosts. Sand and grit stung their faces. By this point Jace and Vereesa had to keep their hoods up and pressed against the wind just to move forward, and even Grede had to put on a hat — even his stone-like skin was starting to struggle.

That night they rested beneath a short pillar. After confirming there was no movement around them, they settled in to sleep there. After a few bites of roasted basilisk meat that was a bit on the tough side, Jace was starting to feel drowsy. He'd barely shut his eyes before a muffled roar from somewhere far away jolted him half-awake.

Grede, who'd already fallen asleep, opened his eyes too. Vereesa was already peering alertly into the distance.

The three of them waited in silence… until the sound came again. This time Jace felt it wasn't an animal's roar — more like a conscious being wailing.

"What's going on," Grede said.

"Go take a look?" Jace asked.

The three of them packed up and crept toward the source of the sound. The miserable, bellowing howls set Jace's heart on edge. The closer they got, the more unnerving it became.

As they drew near they began to hear scattered footsteps — and not normal footsteps, but the clop and strike of hooves against the ground, along with low "hoo" and "hah" sounds.

They all thought of the same thing at once.

Centaur.

They came to a rise and looked toward where the sound was coming from. In the moonlight, three cavalry-like figures holding spears were circling a blood-soaked beast at a leisurely pace in the middle.

Those three "cavalrymen"… had human upper bodies, and their entire lower halves were the black bodies of horses. Centaur!

Vereesa had already seized her bow. Grede stared hard at the centaur and whispered, "What are they doing?"

"That beast was caught, and now it broke free," Vereesa said. "See those broken ropes? It's something like a panther. These centaur are bleeding it out and waiting for it to tire."

"That's no ordinary beast, Miss Quel'dorei," Morophil said. "That's a shapeshifter."

"A shapeshifter — a druid?" Vereesa asked, surprised.

"A druid out of legend?" Grede was stunned too.

Vereesa pressed. "How can you be sure, demon?"

"If I can't even tell that much…" Morophil raised an eyebrow. "This shapeshifter might fool those half-horse mongrels. It might fool you. But it certainly can't fool me!"

"Maybe it never fooled these centaur in the first place," Jace said quietly. "Otherwise they wouldn't be this careful. That formation is no way to deal with an ordinary beast."

"Master has a point too," Morophil smiled.

Vereesa drew an arrow and looked at the succubus. "If this is true…"

"She's telling the truth, elf!" the imp said. "I can feel it too."

"Of course." Morophil smiled and leaned against Jace's shoulder. "After all, I can't let your poor judgment fool my master."

Vereesa furiously drew a breath, then had to bite it back. She turned to Jace. "So what do we do?"

"Of course we save him! What's there to hesitate about?" Grede hefted his hammer, keeping his voice low. "We can't just stand by and watch that druid get bled to death by centaur, can we?"
