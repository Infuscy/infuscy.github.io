Chapter 537: The Stonetalon Shrieks

Jace didn't know much about the Stonetalon Mountains region.

When he'd played the game, he hadn't done many quests here. He'd only come back later for achievements and map completion, wandering around for a while — but that had been purely quest-clearing, running back and forth, so he hadn't formed much of an impression.

His only memory of this place was that the roads were a nightmare — tiny paths, constant forks, endless canyon crossings, with hostile factions lurking everywhere. You could die without even knowing what hit you.

Following Musa toward the Stonetalon Mountains, the terrain gradually grew rugged. The hills multiplied, and they had to watch both sides for ambushes by beasts or other enemies. By nightfall, the surrounding slopes were already covered in tall pines.

In the middle of the night, the group rested against a tree. According to Musa, they should reach the tauren village by morning.

But just as everyone was about to fall asleep, there was a sudden flapping of wings overhead.

At first, Jace thought it was just some bird roosting in the mountains — or another wyvern. The Stonetalon Mountains were full of all kinds of flying creatures. That was how it was in the game, to say nothing of the real Stonetalon range, which was far larger than the game zone.

But hearing the sound, Musa clearly grew wary. She immediately stood up, facing the direction the noise was coming from, and said something to Vereesa.

Vereesa frowned after listening — she didn't seem to understand either.

But Musa immediately grabbed her arm, pointing urgently upward. This time, Vereesa unslung her bow, nocked an arrow, and drew without hesitation...

Seeing the elf suddenly draw her bow, Grede and Jace tensed and stood, looking in that direction. Vereesa aimed for a moment, then loosed. The arrow whistled upward, and there was a piercing, inhuman shriek from the sky — it sounded like a woman's voice.

Since neither Jace nor Grede had expected whatever was flying overhead to make a sound like that after being hit, they both jumped at the scream.

But Jace quickly realized what they were dealing with.

"Harpies," he said.

"Harpies?" the dwarf echoed. Immediately, something fell from the sky. Musa reached out and caught it. Another savage shriek followed, right above the treetops over their heads.

A dark green, giant bird-like creature dove straight at Musa. Vereesa was already drawing a second arrow. Grede's hammer flew out and struck the creature square in the back — even without lightning enchantment, one blow crippled it. It hit the ground, shrieking and thrashing, spewing words no one could understand.

Grede pulled his hammer back and walked over. When he saw the creature's face, he froze.

The creature's lower body was a pair of deformed bird legs, but its upper body was completely that of a naked elven woman — including the head.

Its spine had already been shattered. It vomited blood continuously, glaring at the dwarf and cursing, trying to flap its arms — or wings — at its opponent. But after a while, it went still, leaving behind a fierce yet beautiful face.

These were indeed harpies, one of the native inhabitants of the Stonetalon Mountains.

Jace had never seen a harpy before. Unlike the game, where they wore at least some rags to cover their chests and other vital areas, this one was completely naked. She only had some ornaments made of bones and teeth from unknown animals around her neck, arms, and waist, providing minimal coverage — basically nothing. On her head was what was likely a simple crown denoting her status.

Not far away, another harpy was still thrashing on the ground — the one Vereesa had shot. Its neck had been pierced through, and it probably wouldn't last much longer. Its struggles were clearly weakening.

Then Musa's shout came from the side. Vereesa immediately ran over. Musa was calling out to something in her arms. Jace noticed it was a small tauren calf — not even as big as the dwarf.

The little calf hung its massive head limply. Its fur and the corners of its mouth were covered in blood and dirt. It didn't respond to any calls. It was probably already dead.

"Musa knows this child," Vereesa said. "A resident of that village."

"Could that village be in danger?" Jace asked. "The direction those two harpies came from — is that the direction of the village?"

Vereesa immediately asked Musa. After listening, Musa wiped her tears and nodded to Jace.

"Then how can we sleep here?" Grede said. "Let's get to that village and see! If it's not too far, we should be able to make it by morning, Jace."

"Grede's right," Jace said.

Vereesa said a couple of things to Musa. Musa looked at the three of them and nodded firmly.

With their decision made, the group immediately packed up and set off.

Musa wrapped the little tauren's body in her outer robe, tied it up, and carried it on her back. Besides the body, she carried everyone's food and even most of Grede's luggage — yet she didn't walk slowly at all. Eager to reach the village as quickly as possible, she even moved faster than before. The others following behind could barely keep up.

"Those harpies reek of shadow," Sazeno reminded Jace from his shoulder. "Master needs to be careful — they're far fiercer than they look!"

"Do these she-monsters like snatching children?" Grede asked as he ran.

"Could be some kind of ritual, necromancy, or simply taking trophies to intimidate other enemies," Vereesa said. "Remember the Amani trolls in the Hinterlands? Just as bloodthirsty."

Jace knew the legends — that harpies liked capturing outsiders as prisoners. This tauren child had probably died from severe wounds sustained in battle. Otherwise, his fate would have been far grimmer.

The group ran through the pine forest for God knows how long, and the sky grew darker and darker.

But shortly after crossing a ridge, Jace noticed a firelight in a mountain hollow up ahead. There were also faint sounds of roaring and snarling from the mountainside, though they were too distant to make out clearly.

Based on the village's geographical location, the approximate distance into the Stonetalon Mountains, and the surrounding environment, Jace figured that must be Sun Rock Reach.

Before Deathwing's Cataclysm, the Horde had only had one proper settlement in the Stonetalon Mountains — and it had been mostly defended and inhabited by tauren.

Musa said something, pointing toward the firelight. Without needing Vereesa to translate, Jace knew exactly what it meant. That was the place.
