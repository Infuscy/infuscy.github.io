Chapter 539: The Tauren's Predicament

"Musa! Musa!"

Grede patted the tauren's waist, but she just glared at Jace angrily, refusing to move. Beside them, Vereesa held her forehead, wincing in pain, breathing heavily.

Actually, it wasn't just the tauren and the elf — even Morfil, who'd been following behind, looked terrible. The imp had already fled into the warlock's pack, trembling.

"Damn it," Grede wiped the sweat from his forehead and looked at Jace. "We still didn't run far enough. That incantation of yours was way more terrifying than last time. If I hadn't been mentally prepared, I wouldn't have been able to take it either."

"What spell?" Vereesa asked, turning her head.

"Fear. Sorry," Jace said.

Hearing Jace's words, Vereesa gave a bitter laugh. "Fine. I... damn it, I don't know what I saw... so it's some kind of mind-disrupting magic, right?"

Jace said, "The goal is to disrupt fighting ability with fear. There really wasn't time to explain."

Vereesa explained briefly to the tauren. Musa took a few breaths, and her expression relaxed somewhat.

Jace didn't know what Musa had seen, but at least the tauren had kept her sanity — that already counted as remarkably resistant to Fear.

The harpies in the sky scattered in all directions, shouting at each other as they fled. The path ahead suddenly lost many of its obstacles.

"Let's move before these harpies recover their senses," Jace reminded them.

Grede nodded and took the lead, charging up the slope. The others followed the dwarf to the mountain pass leading into the village.

Jace glanced inside. In the middle of a small valley was a small pool. Several tauren tents of hide and coarse cloth, along with totem poles, surrounded it. A small bridge crossed the narrowest part of the pool, connecting both sides...

This was definitely Sun Rock Reach.

Then Jace saw a tall, blood-covered tauren turn around, swinging a totem pole to knock down a harpy trying to attack him from behind. Several harpies were tearing at the hide flap of a tent. Wails and cries came from every direction.

A blue-feathered harpy called out from above. The harpies still engaged heard the command and flew upward. Some even reluctantly tried to grab something else to take with them. Grede roared and hurled a lightning-trailing hammer. It smashed the slowest one to a pulp. Scattered charred fragments flew into the air — there was no telling what it had looked like before.

"They're retreating," Vereesa said, looking upward.

Jace nodded and signaled Morfil to go invisible.

Musa shouted something and ran toward the tauren clutching the totem pole. The tauren's legs gave out and he sat down on the ground. It took Musa considerable effort to help him up.

While Musa healed him, the tauren living in the village emerged one by one from their tents and caves to put out the fires burning in their homes. Jace counted — there seemed to be only about a dozen tauren living here.

Though there were both adults and children, the child Musa had been carrying appeared to be the smallest in the entire village.

By the riverbank lay the body of a tall tauren warrior. The body was covered in blood, but there didn't seem to be any fatal wounds. Perhaps he'd fought until he simply couldn't fight anymore, and died of exhaustion.

Though a single powerful tauren could handle God knew how many harpies, their numbers were small. Faced with the harpies' constant, long-term harassment, they would eventually be unable to hold out and fall.

Two young, strong tauren warriors, themselves covered in wounds, dragged the body to shore. After using the pool water to extinguish the flames, the remaining tauren all looked over toward Jace's group in silence. It was an unnervingly quiet gaze.

A large tauren holding a long staff approached. He wore a heavy kodo-leather garment, stitched with many patterns similar to those on the totem poles.

He was probably very old. Quite a bit of his brown mane had gone gray. He had only one horn — the other had been broken off. But that single horn was so massive that Jace felt just holding up his head must have been exhausting. Unavoidably, his head tilted slightly toward the side with the horn.

He didn't speak. He just nodded deeply, studied Jace for a moment, then looked toward Musa in the distance and let out a soft sigh. Musa seemed to understand his meaning. After settling the injured tauren warrior, she walked over.

Jace had a feeling this elder could already smell the shadow on him. Even in a calm state, he could make any random tauren in Gadgetzan smell the scent of demons — to say nothing of how much shadow magic he'd just cast. There was no hiding it.

However, this elder didn't seem intent on dwelling on the issue of dark power. He said a few words slowly to Musa. Musa relayed it to Vereesa, and Vereesa said, "They're very grateful to us outsiders and want to express their thanks, but there's nothing left in the village to give."

"As long as we can stay the night," Grede said, looking at Jace. "Right, Jace?"

Jace nodded. Vereesa relayed the message to the tauren. The old elder nodded with effort twice.

He said something to Musa and pointed to a cave in the distance — the very place where many tauren had taken shelter earlier. It seemed the elder intended to let Jace's group stay in the cave.

Musa led the group to the cave. Though it was dark and narrow inside, the floor was covered with thick fur blankets — the kind that made you sleepy just looking at them. Beside them, a small clay burner filled with something Jace couldn't identify burned quietly, filling the room with a soothing, comforting fragrance.

He'd thought that after leaving Gadgetzan, he'd never find a place where he could truly sleep peacefully again. To have a cave like this for a proper night's rest was something Jace hadn't dared to hope for.

Although they said they had nothing to give, tauren still brought a lot of food into the cave — what looked like wings from some enormous bird, a giant bird leg about as long as the dwarf, and a large pile of strange fruit none of them had seen on the journey, but which looked plump and juicy.

The group, who'd been eating bitter little fruits and cloying lizard meat the whole way, naturally salivated at the sight of all this food. But considering the village had just been raided, none of them were in a hurry to dig in.

Musa said a lot to Vereesa. Vereesa told the other two: "A tauren envoy from another tribe that lives by the sea came here before, trying to persuade this village's tauren to join them — to unite against the threats of the centaur and the quilboar. But the tauren here were unwilling to leave the sacred site of the Stonetalon Mountains, so they refused. But now, it looks like the elder is having second thoughts."

"Is that the Grimtotem tribe Musa left?" Grede asked. "That name alone doesn't exactly make you want to join."

Vereesa asked Musa. Musa shook her head and pronounced an extremely difficult name in the tauren tongue, then explained in Thalassian to Vereesa.

"That tribe is called Bloodhoof," Vereesa said. "Their leader is Cairne Bloodhoof."
