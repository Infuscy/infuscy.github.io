Chapter 540: The Invitation

The tauren were a quiet people, especially the older ones, who could go half a day without stringing together a few words. By comparison, Musa already came across as quite the lively young woman in this village.

What's more, the residents only spoke the tauren language. The handful who knew a few words of Thalassian were far worse at it than Musa. So after the group arrived, they had no contact with the other tauren at all. Even those who brought food and water came in silence and left in silence.

This made Jace curious — when and where had Musa learned the night elf's language?

But after the fierce battle with the harpies, he didn't have the energy to dwell on unimportant questions.

Vereesa tossed and turned, unable to sleep. Even when she managed to drift off, she'd be jolted awake by nightmares. She didn't say why, but Jace knew whose fault it was. It had been a long time since he'd tried casting mass Fear — the spell's power had genuinely exceeded his expectations.

His own lower back had been aching. He had Grede take a look in the cave — it was a bruise, probably from where that arrow had struck him.

Grede had it much worse. He'd been clawed several times by the harpies. It hadn't been obvious during the fight, but now his face and neck were covered in claw marks and streaks of blood.

Of course, for a gryphon rider, being clawed by birds was hardly worth mentioning. But Musa still tended to his wounds seriously.

The dwarf sat in front of Musa, chest out and head high, completely still, acting as if nothing bothered him. Meanwhile, Musa — who was nearly twice his height — worked with remarkable gentleness, cleaning the wounds little by little and applying herbs that looked like Wild Steelbloom or Silverleaf.

Jace asked Vereesa, who was cleaning her weapons nearby, "Doesn't Musa use nature magic to heal? Did the earlier battle drain too much of her energy, so she doesn't have the strength to cast?"

Vereesa said, "The tauren believe their magic comes from the blessings of the Earth Mother. Using it casually when the situation isn't urgent is considered rude and disrespectful. And now that they're in the village and have enough herbs to treat the wounds, there's no need to call upon nature's power again."

Jace nodded. "You've talked with her so much these past few weeks — you're practically becoming a tauren expert."

"Of course," Vereesa said, looking up. "The tauren are part of Kalimdor. If Quel'Thalas ever intends to return here in the future, whether the tauren become allies or enemies, we'll need to understand them as thoroughly as possible."

"You're thinking pretty far ahead," Jace said.

"Don't forget, my goal is to become an Aranalath — what you call a Ranger-Lord, Jace." Vereesa set her things aside, leaned against the stone wall, picked up a fruit, and bit into it, looking at him. "Gathering intelligence on Kalimdor is an important part of this journey."

Looking at this elf in front of him, Jace suddenly thought of how, in the game's future, after Kael'thas Sunstrider announced that the survivors of the Sunwell would rename themselves Sin'dorei — blood elves — Vereesa and many other elves who refused to accept fel magic had left Quel'Thalas one after another. They founded the Silver Covenant and continued to call themselves Quel'dorei. Vereesa eventually became their leader.

Right now, he really couldn't connect this young elf in front of him with the image of the Silver Covenant general from the game. After all, the two of them were already far too familiar with each other.

The next morning, Jace was still lying comfortably on the fur blanket, not wanting to get up. Grede and Vereesa were still sleeping beside him, which gave him even less reason to leave his bed.

Then came a heavy footsteps from outside the cave. It sounded like a tauren approaching.

Jace looked up and saw a massive shadow — over three meters tall — blocking the cave entrance. On his back was a giant totem pole. In his hand was a long spear. He wore heavy leather armor from head to toe. Fully armed.

What the hell? Was he coming in to purge evil?

Jace instinctively glanced in the direction Morfil was hiding. Had the tauren finally detected the demon's presence?

Impossible... unless these fellows actually knew Detect Demon — a skill that even most orc warlocks didn't have. Were they really going to charge in and kill their saviors just from smelling a scent?

Still, Jace prepared to flee. Just as the tension in his mind was at its peak, Musa walked in.

She saw Jace and said in heavily accented Common: "Invite, Jace. Ceremony. Dead."

Jace seemed to understand what she meant.

Were the tauren inviting them to a funeral?

Just then, Grede opened his eyes and sat up. Seeing the three-meter giant in front of him, he snapped awake and even reached for the axe beside him.

Vereesa woke up too. Musa immediately explained the situation to her. Sure enough, just as Jace had guessed, the ceremony to send off the dead from last night was about to begin, and the village elder wanted them to participate as well.

After Musa left with the tauren warrior, Jace turned toward where the succubus was hiding and said, "Stay in this cave, Morfil. Don't come out."

The succubus hidden in the shadows shuddered — a tremor through the soul. Jace knew she understood.

"Attending a tauren funeral?" Grede straightened his clothes. "Do I look presentable?"

"You've got quite the warrior's look, Grede. Don't worry," Jace said, pulling on his robe and deliberately checking the clasps. He had no intention of revealing the demonic runes beneath his robe during the tauren's solemn ceremony.

"The tauren are a people who deeply respect warriors," he added.

"Then I'll take your word for it." Grede patted his belly and strode out toward the cave entrance after Musa.

"Honestly, I'm a little nervous," Vereesa said, adjusting her hair. "What if we do something that breaks protocol... will they blame us?"

Jace looked at her. "If a tauren envoy who'd traveled a long way scratched an itch or swagged his tail at an elf funeral, would you consider it a desecration and throw him out?"

"We would absolutely never allow a tauren to set foot in Quel'Thalas," Vereesa said.

"Right, you elves have got some issues. I'm sure the tauren are far more forgiving," Jace said, picking up his staff and belting on his sword. "If they're welcoming strangers from afar, they won't expect you to do everything perfectly. I think as long as you respect the ceremony in your heart, that's enough."

"All right, I more or less get what you mean," Vereesa said, giving him a sidelong, slightly annoyed look. "But I did remind you — remember to call us Quel'dorei."
