Chapter 542: A Warlock's Companion

Jace said, "I remember when I first met you, you were prejudiced against both humans and dwarves. If it were the you from back then, you probably wouldn't be so interested in tauren beliefs and culture, right?"

"This is, of course, for the sake of intelligence gathering," Wynne explained.

Jace laughed, and the elf immediately breathed a sigh of relief. "Alright, I suppose I have changed a lot, Jace."

"But that's also because of me."

"Because of me?"

Wynne said, "Since we're talking about when we first met... I remember the first time I saw you, in a Dalaran tavern — a human casually wearing simple apprentice robes. I didn't really understand what Master Kafal had to talk about with someone like you."

"When he mentioned that you and a dwarf had slain a black dragon, aside from being a little annoyed that you'd stolen my chance at glory, I didn't feel much of anything else."

"Kafal said you were a warlock — a rare mage secretly studying dark magic hidden within human society. So at first, I didn't have a good impression of you. But you used that evil Life Drain magic against someone even more evil — Teron Gorefiend. And I knew what would happen. You'd rot and die from the necrotic energy inside Gorefiend's body, but you did it anyway."

"I had no idea what the consequences would be at the time," Jace admitted.

Wynne said softly, "Regardless, I saw you pull that staff impaling your own shoulder straight out, then transform shadow power like you did beneath the walls of Nethergarde Keep — fearlessly fighting a terrible evil, turning the tide for Nethergarde Keep and the Alliance. I... I don't even know how to describe it."

Jace said, "At Nethergarde Keep, I was trying to save my master and his daughter. They were both in the mage tower — Amy Marin. You remember her, right?"

"Of course I do. She's a very talented mage," Wynne said.

"They treated me like family," Jace said. "I couldn't just stand by and watch them be destroyed by demons. I simply couldn't. As for whether I was trying to save Nethergarde Keep or the Alliance... honestly, I wasn't thinking that far ahead. Mostly because I didn't have the time to think..."

Looking at Wynne's slightly dazed expression, Jace smiled and said, "Besides, that demon general was way too arrogant, don't you think? Someone had to teach him a lesson."

The elf's lips moved as if she wanted to say something, but in the end she fell silent, just smiling and nodding.

The two of them walked down the mountain together. Wynne gazed out at the mist-shrouded valley, and as they walked, she suddenly said, "I'm curious — when you go back after being alone with a member of the opposite sex here, won't your companion get jealous?"

"Companion?" Jace asked, startled.

"That succubus demon," Wynne said. "Don't play innocent with me..."

"Uh, her... alright." Jace stepped down a tall ledge. "She considers me her companion? Maybe. But the reason she stays with me is that being near me makes her stronger. Isn't that how demons who follow warlocks think?"

"Stronger?" Wynne thought for a moment. "Ah, right — mana feeding. I know about that."

She put a strange, deliberate emphasis on the word "feeding." Jace coughed. "I need her strength and her knowledge of demons. She needs my mana to keep growing."

"Isn't there more to it than that?" Wynne said. "A lot of warlocks treat their succubi as their lovers, their wives — at least, a lot of Dark Iron dwarves do. Other dwarves even take it for granted that a succubus is a warlock's beloved. I know some trolls and Quel'dorei are more or less the same."

Jace glanced back at her. "You know more about warlocks than I expected. You really did your research."

"Of course," Wynne said, her tone serious.

"Alright." Jace tightened the collar of his robe. "It's just that a lot of warlocks don't get to meet many outsiders while studying demons and shadow. And for their own safety, it's hard for them to entrust their secrets to anyone. So a female demon companion gradually becoming their closest partner — that's pretty normal. I can understand it."

"What about you, Jace Sesso?" Wynne asked, catching up.

"I don't know," Jace said casually. "I haven't even lived twenty years yet. The future might be long. I might lose my life tomorrow. Or maybe Morfell will die on the road. Or maybe we'll accompany each other for decades, and then I'll grow old and die, and she'll return to the Twisting Nether to find her next master. Who can say?"

"Decades?" Wynne stopped dead in her tracks.

"Yeah." Jace looked back at her. "What's wrong?"

"Uh, nothing." The elf swallowed and shook her head. "I just... spending decades with a demon — it's really hard to imagine. Even a harmless demon."

Jace nodded. "It really is hard to imagine."

For the rest of the way, the two of them didn't speak. The ranger had gone remarkably quiet all of a sudden, and Jace didn't know what to say either, until they reached the bottom of the cliff.

In the empty space at the center of the village, someone had brought over a kodo at some point. Two Stonehide residents were strapping saddles onto it one by one, fastening them tight with leather belts. Mosa was helping out too.

Seeing the two of them return, Mosa called Wynne's name and waved them over.

She said something to the elf. Whatever it was, it had dispelled quite a bit of the gloom Wynne had been carrying on the way back. She excitedly told Jace, "The village elder is willing to let this kodo take us to Desolace."

"Really?" Hearing the news, Jace got excited too. He truly hadn't expected he'd ever get the chance to ride a kodo.

Actually, back in Tanaris he'd been ready to spend some money to have a goblin put him on a kodo's back for a stretch of the journey, just for the experience.

But at the time, that money would've been wasted for no good reason — especially in front of Wynne and Grede. Back then, money was truly spent a little less with every passing day, and they had no idea what situation they'd find in Gadgetzan. Every bit of money was precious.

But after leaving Gadgetzan, Jace had assumed he'd never get to ride a kodo.

And now, wasn't this giant beast right here?!

Just then, Wynne noticed two quivers made by the tauren hanging from the kodo's body, already packed full of intact arrows. Among them were not only some of her own, but also some harpy arrows — similar in length and weight to elven arrows.

"The tauren even helped me find my arrows. I was planning to go look for them myself this morning. What a bother for them." The elf reached out and stroked one of the quivers. Then she pulled out a delicate black-feathered arrow that was different from the rest.

"This one isn't mine, and it's different from the other harpy arrows too."

She took the arrow and asked Mosa about it. Mosa pinched the arrow and sniffed it, then said something. Jace clearly caught the word "Kaldorei" in what she said.

"Our kin live in the mountains to the north, Jace," Wynne said. "This is their arrow. It was probably picked up by the harpies."

"So we're close to the Kaldorei homeland," Jace said.

"Ashenvale," Wynne whispered. "A place that always sounded like something out of myth and legend is right before our eyes. Good thing we don't need to go there."

Wynne's emphasis on "feeding" carries suggestive undertones. - Kaldorei: 卡多雷 — "Children of the Stars," the night elves' name for themselves. - Ashenvale: 灰谷 — the ancient night elf forest region in northern Kalimdor. - Wynne's character development: This chapter marks a significant moment in her arc — she's gone from prejudiced and aloof to genuinely curious about other cultures, and openly reflective about how Jace changed her.
