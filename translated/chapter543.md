## Chapter 543: Desolace

The whole of Stonetalon was an upland mountain range rising out of west-central Kalimdor. Entering it meant climbing; leaving it meant descending.

Sitting on the kodo's back, Jace swayed with the beast's shoulder blades as it walked. At first it was a bit uncomfortable, but after a while it started to feel like sitting in a rocking cradle. He was even getting a little drowsy.

Grede, lying on the bundles of herbs, potions, and food made by the tauren that they'd brought from Stonehide, was already snoring like thunder — loud enough to hear from a long way off.

Wynne, sitting beside him, couldn't stand the noise. Every so often she'd smack him, which would silence the snoring for a bit, but it only lasted ten minutes or so before the racket started up again.

They'd set out from Stonehide in the afternoon, walked around to the back of the mountain, and descended the slope. The sun hadn't yet set when they arrived at a dense pine forest.

Jace remembered that in the game, this was a place called the Cauldron — a forest that had been ravaged by fire. Gray earth, charred trees, ash drifting everywhere, angry fire elementals roaming within, and harpies hiding in the depths, lying in wait for any druid — tauren or night elf — who tried to heal the land.

But right now, Jace couldn't see any trace of raging fire elementals. The surrounding forest was perfectly peaceful. There was no smell of burning at all — only the fragrance of pine and cypress. Occasionally the call of some unknown bird would sound, and it was definitely not the noise of harpies.

The kodo strolled through the valley. It seemed like it would never get tired, advancing the whole time. Sometimes it would stretch out its tortoise-like long neck and take a bite from a tree or bush passing by the path. Sometimes it would uproot an entire tree, trunk and all, and drag it along, chewing as it walked, taking a good hour or two to finish it all.

After spending a night resting on the kodo's back, the next day Jace was groggily patted awake by Wynne.

The sky wasn't fully bright yet — it was probably only four or five in the morning. Wynne pointed at a stone pillar in the distance. "Look."

"What?" Jace rubbed his eyes and looked toward the stone pillar jutting up from among the trees. Only then did he realize this pillar didn't belong to tauren architecture, nor was it the work of harpies or centaur or any other race.

The pillar was already overgrown with all kinds of climbing plants, but you could still tell that the surface beneath was pale blue. You could even make out ancient patterns on it.

This was a relic of the ancient Kaldorei Empire.

Ten thousand years ago, the Kaldorei Empire had once ruled the most prosperous region at the center of the continent. After Empress Azshara defeated the Zul'Gurub troll empire, she expanded the empire's borders again and again. So even ten thousand years after the empire's destruction, its few remaining ruins were still scattered across the entirety of Kalimdor.

Still, in Jace's memory, there were very few elven ruins within Stonetalon. But Desolace... this vast plain, which had still been a beautiful grassland a few thousand years ago, had once been home to a great number of Kaldorei cities, and so had left behind many ancient city ruins.

Did seeing this stone pillar mean they were about to arrive at Desolace?

The kodo still advanced steadily. Jace realized the terrain here was already very flat — not like yesterday, when he'd felt like they were going downhill the whole time.

The pine forest grew sparse. Large stretches of earth and exposed boulders appeared. Jace raised his head — he could even smell a hint of desolation and death here.

This wasn't the smell brought by undead creatures or necrotic energy. It was an unsettling feeling caused by the increasingly sparse breath of life.

Even in the Barrens, the lone trees struggling to grow on the grassland, the beasts hiding in the grass and gullies — all of it still gave a strong sense of vibrant life.

Let alone Stonetalon, which was home to countless harpies and wyverns and even inhabited by night elves — that place radiated the warmth of life force from the inside out.

But here, even the things that were struggling to catch their breath seemed sickly, half-dead.

By evening, even Grede — who could fall asleep any chance he got — couldn't sleep. He and Wynne both stared out at this gray wasteland, alert to any danger that might appear at any moment.

If in Stonetalon the dense pine forest had at least been able to somewhat conceal the kodo's silhouette and footsteps, then in this dead land, the kodo beneath them was like a moving tower, clearly visible for miles in every direction.

The kodo finally stopped next to the massive skeleton of one of its own kind. The rhythmic swaying that had lasted for two or three days suddenly ceased. Jace even felt a bit uncomfortable, unaccustomed to the stillness.

The tauren said a few quiet words to Mosa. It seemed the kodo could only take them this far — ahead was the centaur patrol zone. The kodo would easily be besieged and would have trouble protecting itself.

"Centaur again," Grede said with a frown. "Sounds like the centaur are about to take over the continent."

"That's exactly what's happened," Wynne said. "Otherwise the tauren tribes wouldn't have been driven to such a state. Mosa says that long ago in Stonetalon, tauren and harpies rarely clashed, because back then the tauren tribes were very powerful. The harpies in the high mountains would rather harass the Kaldorei than risk going south into the mountains to harass the tauren settlements. Now, everything is different."

Jace slid off the tauren's back, straightened his clothes and luggage, and looked up at the tauren. He said in the tauren language, "Owa, xi."

This was a phrase of thanks. Jace had only just learned it a few days ago from the tauren at Stonehide.

The tall tauren rider nodded and said quietly, "Ishi, ni, alo, pula, a."

After speaking, he blew a low horn, and the kodo obediently turned around and walked off into the distance.

"What does that mean?" Grede asked.

"A very commonly used blessing. You can take it as, 'May the wisdom of the ancestors guide your path,'" Wynne explained.

"So the tauren's beliefs are quite similar to those of our Aerie Peak," Grede nodded. "I ought to teach him a couple of phrases in Dwarvish too — how to express gratitude to our elders and ancestral spirits."

Wynne got into an argument with the dwarf over the differences between the tauren and the Wildhammer dwarves. Jace, meanwhile, watched the kodo gradually disappear from view.

Sitting on the kodo's back, he hadn't felt it, but this monster's walking pace was actually quite fast. The tamed kodo of the tauren were even a bit larger than the kodos the goblins used for hauling goods, with an even wider stride.

This stretch of road that had taken them two days to cover — if Jace and the others had walked it on foot, who knows how much longer it would have taken, not to mention the time spent finding their way through the forest.

"The rest of the road, we'll have to rely on ourselves," Jace said.

Mosa walked over and pointed to a wasteland to the southwest. "Maraudon."

## Flags

- **Chapter title**: 凄凉之地 — "Desolace," the canonical WoW zone in central Kalimdor.
- **The Cauldron**: 焦炭谷 — in the game, this area in Stonetalon is a burned wasteland. Here it's still a healthy forest, showing the timeline is pre-catastrophe (or the game's state hasn't manifested yet).
- **Kaldorei Empire**: 卡多雷帝国 — the ancient night elf empire, destroyed ten thousand years ago during the War of the Ancients.
- **Empress Azshara**: 艾萨拉女皇 — canon WoW character, the former night elf queen who caused the empire's downfall.
- **Zul'Gurub**: 祖尔古鲁布 — the ancient troll empire that preceded the Kaldorei Empire's expansion.
- **Mashan'she / 马珊帐**: the tauren name for Desolace before its corruption — their original homeland.
- **Theradras / 瑟莱德丝**: the earth elemental princess whose awakening drained Desolace of life (canonical WoW lore; she is the final boss of Maraudon).
- **Maraudon**: 玛拉顿 — the sacred centaur tomb and dungeon in Desolace (called "Maraudine" in later chapters' naming convention).
- **Language notes**: Jace uses the tauren language (Owa, xi / Ishi, ni, alo, pula, a) — these are constructed-language phrases, not direct translations.
- **Pacing**: This chapter is largely atmospheric travel writing — the transition from the lush Stonetalon to the dead Desolace is the emotional core.
