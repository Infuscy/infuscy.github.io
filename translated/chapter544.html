Chapter 544: The Sacred Valley

Deep in Desolace was a gray wilderness. Almost no living plants remained. The withered dead trees that barely managed to stay standing reminded Jace of the Burning Steppes.

The biggest difference was that the Burning Steppes had been utterly destroyed by a single great explosion, but in the centuries since, whether in the cracks of the mountain stones or the corners of the ruins, there had been tenacious plants struggling to survive at every moment, battling against hellish heat and the black clouds that blocked out the light year-round.

But here, it seemed as though everything had lost the desire and the power to live. Even without the harsh environment of bitter cold or dry, scorching heat, even without flames sweeping the earth — there was only corruption and desolation.

Here, Mosa could barely even use Life magic to maintain her spirit or grant restoration.

Thousands of years ago, Desolace had also once been a gentle grassland — just as that tauren in Gadgetzan had said. They called it "Mashan'she," the original homeland of the tauren.

The tauren tribes who had lived here back then had been content and at peace, and so they had tirelessly pursued communication and communion with nature.

Until one day, a tauren priest heard whispers coming from deep beneath the earth. He believed this was the call of the Earth Mother. After communicating with the other priests, they tried to enter the deep underground to commune with the Earth Mother — but in the end, they awakened Theradras, the earth elemental princess who had slumbered for an unknown length of time.

After Theradras awoke, in order to restore herself completely, she ruthlessly drained the vitality of the entire Mashan'she, pulling all life into the depths of those caverns to nourish her own power. And the once-fertile grassland transformed into its current state in the span of a few short decades.

Add to that the later appearance of the centaur, and the tauren had no choice but to abandon this place, fleeing to the great grasslands of the Barrens and the peaks of Stonetalon.

The group walked along a dry, gray ridge of earth. Looking down, everywhere they saw were the skeletons of giant beasts that had died who knows how many years ago — perhaps kodos, perhaps thunder lizards, perhaps some other terrifying reptilian creatures. Even Mosa couldn't identify them all.

Late at night, while the group was resting beside a ravine, Wynne suddenly grew alert. She made a "shh" gesture, stopping everyone's idle chatter.

Grede, who had been chewing a tauren-made vegetable cake, also froze mid-chew. Jace's gaze slowly shifted toward the elf.

After falling quiet, they heard one coarse, absurd-sounding shout after another, seemingly coming from very far away — by the time it reached them, it had become faint and thin.

Accompanying these bursts of shouting was the chaotic sound of hoofbeats.

Jace followed Wynne and poked his head out beyond the slope. In the moonlight, very far off, four centaur silhouettes were chasing each other as they ran, shouting amongst themselves.

Judging by their relaxed demeanor, they didn't look like they were on patrol — more like they were out for fun, or perhaps engaged in some kind of race. Utterly out of place with the atmosphere of this dead land.

Mosa stared at those centaur. Although she didn't show any particularly obvious expression on the surface, Jace could still feel the intense hatred in her heart.

Actually, back when they'd encountered a centaur settlement in the Barrens, after Mosa had mentioned that the centaur also found the demons lurking within Maraudon to be a headache, Jace had been considering whether it was possible to communicate with the centaur — to try to get the centaur to allow them to enter Maraudon, rather than sneaking in, or being forced to fight their way in...

First of all, fighting their way in was absolutely not an option.

Maraudon was the centaur's sacred tomb. Things couldn't possibly work like they did in the game, where you triggered one group's aggro, killed them, and the centaur further off would look on with indifference — and you could just push forward bit by bit like that.

Here, if they attempted to force their way into Maraudon, what would greet them would surely be a surging flood of centaur pouring out. If they couldn't escape, the outcome would be miserable.

But with Mosa by their side, Jace had never known how to bring it up — after all, she had a deep-seated hatred for the centaur.

After packing up their luggage, the group continued heading southwest. After walking for about two days, at dusk, following Mosa's lead, they arrived at the entrance of a valley.

There were already quite a lot of centaur here. From a distance they could even hear the centaur's movements — there were loud howls, wanton shouting, and the sound of hoofbeats echoing within the canyon. This was unquestionably a very large settlement.

They hid behind a hill outside the canyon and observed the area for a while. Mosa confirmed that entering the valley and walking to the end would bring them to the Maraudon sacred tomb.

Wynne took out a scroll, placed the goblin collector's dagger on top of it through a layer of cloth, and softly recited the incantations on the scroll.

Her eyes flickered between brightness and dimness with the incantations. After the spell was complete, she gently closed her eyes. After a moment, she said, "Jace, that goblin collector you know was right. Since coming to Kalimdor, this place has the strongest resonance with the magical traces on the dagger."

"So far he hasn't been wrong about anything," Jace nodded. "If the source of his dagger is also correct, then the owner of this dagger is Dasmora Darkeye. That would let us confirm that she is the same Highborne that the Relics Society is searching for."

Wynne nodded. "The question now is, how to pass through the centaur's layers of defenses on the outside and get in."

"This valley looks very big," Grede said. "We could use the cover of night and sneak in from up high on those peaks."

Sneaking into Maraudon wasn't a completely impossible choice. Pocodiaz wasn't the only goblin who'd tried to send mercenaries to infiltrate Maraudon. In the game, goblin explorers had already brought their own squads past the centaur outside and successfully entered, wanting to mine the precious gem veins inside.

It was just that after going in, they were ambushed by demons and other dangerous creatures lurking within. In the end, only the leader survived — but at least they'd gotten in.

But this was still an extremely dangerous choice. Especially since not everyone in the group could turn invisible. The invisibility potions Jace had brought on this trip absolutely weren't enough to safely get four people through or out of the interior of this canyon, whose length you couldn't even begin to guess at.

Let alone the fact that they were completely unfamiliar with the roads here. Once they entered the invisibility potion's state, they'd be completely blind.

"I think to protect this sacred tomb, the centaur wouldn't fail to set up sentries on the high ground," Wynne said. "We'd probably be spotted as soon as we got close."

"Are they that smart?" Grede asked. "Don't tell me they've built a fortress in there?"

"Don't forget they even have their own inherited language, Grede," Wynne said. "Since this is the centaur's most sacred place, they're certainly going to protect it quite thoroughly. Look over there, Grede — can you see those faint earthen walls behind the mountains? This isn't some small centaur village."

Grede glanced over and said helplessly, "Alright, I can't see that clearly."
