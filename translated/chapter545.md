## Chapter 545: How to Talk to Centaur

At that moment, Jace glanced at the tauren beside them and said, "Perhaps we should try making contact with the centaur. Find a pretext and see if they'd be willing to just let us in."

Wynne thought for a moment, then turned and explained Jace's meaning to Mosa beside her. The tauren immediately said something in a huff, speaking fast.

"She said she just knew a guy who cooperates with demons like you wouldn't have a single shred of principle," Wynne said.

Jace looked at Wynne. He couldn't help but feel like she was using Mosa's words to express her own thoughts.

"Well, what does Mosa think, then?" he asked.

Wynne relayed everyone's words back and forth. Mosa thought for a while, looked up in the direction of the valley, and said she agreed with Grede's idea — but they didn't necessarily have to go in from up high. They could use the complex terrain inside to gradually sneak into the valley.

She felt that Grede and Wynne were both excellent hunters, skilled at concealing their tracks. And she could shift into her great cat form, making it even easier to hide her figure.

In Jace's opinion, he could simply stay outside Maraudon with the two demons and wait for their good news. Let the three of them handle the infiltration and investigation.

After hearing Wynne relay Mosa's suggestion, Grede immediately said, "You want to leave the guy in our group who's best at dealing with demons outside in the cold while we're dealing with a dangerous demon inside? No offense to Mosa's opinion, but..."

"That's exactly right," Wynne said. She exchanged more words with Mosa, and the tone of their conversation grew heavier, even taking on the feeling of an argument.

Since meeting in the Shimmering Flats, Jace had truly never seen Mosa and Wynne argue.

Jace took advantage of what seemed like a pause in their argument and said, "Actually, I'm not just some idiot who can only chant spells... But I still think we should try first to see if we can communicate with the centaur. I want to put sneaking in — that kind of move that shows our cards — as the last resort. If they refuse to let us in, then we try sneaking in. If sneaking in fails, we think of another way."

Mosa sat to the side, still looking a bit angry. Even if she couldn't understand, she definitely sensed that the conversation was gradually moving in a direction she didn't want.

"Why don't we talk about that pretext, Jace — how to get the centaur to be willing to let us in?" Grede asked. "We can't exactly buy tickets, can we? We don't have anything the centaur need to trade for it."

"Who says we don't?" Jace said. "Our swords and our spells — aren't those things we can trade? We could claim to be a group of demon hunters. We heard the centaur are having trouble with demons, so we're volunteering to go into the sacred tomb and help them deal with the demons — in exchange for a suitable reward."

"Ah!" Grede nodded. "That actually sounds... but what do you plan to ask the centaur for as a reward?"

"Treasure, coin — whatever they can give us," Jace said.

"Jace is right. The reward adds credibility," Wynne nodded.

"The reward itself is also important," Jace said. "If in the end we find nothing in there, we can't exactly go back empty-handed, can we?"

"Alright... then what about language?" Wynne said. "I wonder if any of the centaur know the tauren language, or the Kaldorei language."

She looked at Mosa beside her, sighed, and asked something. Mosa was silent for a long time before finally opening her mouth and explaining something to Wynne.

Wynne said, "Mosa says some centaur priestesses who command wind magic have inherited the ancient Kaldorei language. Perhaps we really would have a chance to communicate with them."

Just then, Mosa said another sentence. This sentence was clearly not Thalassian. Then she explained it again in Thalassian.

Wynne expressed her gratitude, then repeated the sentence from just now over and over, word by word, trying to memorize it. Then she said, "This is in the centaur language — 'Looking for someone who speaks Kaldorei.'"

"Since she knows the centaur language, why doesn't Mosa just translate for us?" the dwarf asked.

Jace looked at him. "Obviously. You think the centaur would communicate with a tauren?"

Wynne said helplessly, "I imagine Mosa doesn't particularly want to say much to the centaur either."

"Tell her for me," Jace said to the ranger. "Bringing us here already — we're very grateful. But we're few and they're many. Taking a non-conflict approach is to our advantage. If Mosa doesn't want to enter Maraudon with us, I understand too. After all, I imagine the centaur wouldn't let a tauren into their sacred ground either. And if they see her, they might be even more hostile toward us. Mosa would also be in danger."

Wynne explained Jace's meaning to Mosa. The tauren looked at Jace, then looked at Grede, and suddenly shifted into her great cat form. She stepped forward, step by step, coming to the dwarf's side.

"So she's agreed to go in with us?" Grede asked.

"A Wildhammer hunter with a mountain lion as a helper — that's pretty normal, isn't it?" Wynne smiled.

"That's true!" Grede immediately burst out laughing. He patted Mosa's back, and Mosa gently nudged the palm of his hand.

"But these little horns..." Jace tried to reach out and touch the great cat's head. Mosa bared her teeth fiercely and took a step back.

"Sorry..." He reluctantly pulled back his hand. "I just mean — won't these horns be noticed by the centaur and give us away?"

"Simple." Grede took off his helmet and placed it entirely over Mosa's head.

Unexpectedly, this demon guard's helmet, which was a bit too large for the dwarf, sat perfectly on the great cat's head, covering those two horns exactly.

Wynne laughed. "A strange dwarf leading a great cat wearing a helmet — strange plus strange, and it actually feels normal. Since that's the case, let me go talk to them."

"The two of us will follow behind you with our cloaks up, playing your hired foreign mercenaries," Jace said. "You can pass yourself off as a Kaldorei."

"Really?" Wynne asked, her voice full of doubt. "The magisters said the Kaldorei who stayed here are much more robust and tall than us."

Grede said, "Do the centaur really distinguish things that carefully? Don't forget — the elves of Kalimdor have been hiding in the shadows this whole time. We haven't encountered a single one so far! The centaur probably haven't seen many elves with their own eyes either. As for the ancient Kaldorei of the olden days — I've heard the legends about how they looked too. Anyway, they're more or less the same as you. Look at you — long, pointed ears, pale hair draped over your shoulders, wearing fine clothes, eyes flickering with arcane light, speaking the elven tongue, walking with that affected elven grace. Definitely no problem."

After hearing Grede's whole speech, Wynne's expression was extremely complex. She didn't even know whether she should agree with him or immediately argue back.

"More or less that meaning," Jace nodded.
