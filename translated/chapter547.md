## Chapter 547: A True Windrunner

Vereesa didn't answer. She turned back, tilted her head up slightly, and stared straight into the centaur priestess's eyes as she said something.

Before the priestess could reply, the sanctuary guards behind her lifted their spears and grumbled something in the centaur tongue. Grede's hand flew to the hammer at his belt, and in an instant every guard had leveled his spear, shouting what were probably warnings or insults.

Then, unexpectedly, the centaur priestess spoke in Common: "No need to be nervous, outsiders."

Grede blinked. He hadn't expected a centaur to speak the language of another continent.

Jace knew they couldn't be the first outsiders to have communicated with the centaur here. The goblins were probably already doing business with these people.

That said, the priestess clearly didn't know much Common either. After that one sentence, she went back to speaking Elvish with Vereesa — likely explaining what the sanctuary guards had just said.

When she finished, Vereesa nodded and said, "They want to see what we're made of — to confirm we're a group of fighters who can actually stand against demons, not a band of thieves pretending to be demon hunters."

"Well, that's just wonderful. Better than I hoped, damn it," Grede said, bouncing lightly on his feet. "Give me a chance, Vereesa. Let me smash these half-beasts' skulls in."

"No need for you to do the fighting," Vereesa said, patting the dwarf on the shoulder. "They mocked my surname. They think I'm unworthy of the name Windrunner. I'm going to show them what's what."

Grede asked, puzzled, "They know your family?"

"They probably worship the wind, or a wind god, or something like that," Jace said. "Mosa mentioned it earlier."

Vereesa said, "Right. They think my family name is a jab at their ridiculous made-up gods. Well, it doesn't matter. They won't be laughing for long."

Jace looked at the elf. "So you're sure you want to do this?"

Grede grinned and looked over. "Are you sure, Jace Sesso? Worried a member of House Windrunner might lose a one-on-one?"

"Yes, Jace, you really wound me," Vereesa said, frowning.

Jace drew a breath. "All right, Vereesa. Just be careful."

"I know." Vereesa gave Jace a look, then turned back around.

She pointed at the guard captain behind the priestess — the one who'd been laughing the loudest — and said in Elvish: "Bandu Thoribas."

The priestess glanced at the captain and said something in the centaur tongue. The guard immediately let out a wild cry and shook his spear furiously.

Even among the towering sanctuary guards, this captain was the most powerfully built of them all. He was covered in scars, his black hair hung over his shoulders, and his skin was nearly red-black. His shoulders, his chest, his entire horse-body was covered in symbolic tattoos — it barely even looked like a horse's body at all, more like a bull's. The centaur were a race that reveled in dueling and displays of strength. Seeing that a fight was about to break out, no small number of centaur coming and going through the valley gathered of their own accord, forming a ring around them.

The centaur guard strode into the center with a rhythmic, almost dance-like step. Plenty of centaur roared in their own tongue, cheering the guard on. Grede swept his eyes over them and started shouting encouragement for Vereesa at full volume. Hearing the dwarf's yelling, the centaur roared even harder, wave after wave of shouting drawing more and more attention.

Jace watched the gathering crowd of centaur and actually felt himself relax. The fact that the centaur were so enthusiastic about dueling probably meant they took the outcome of a duel seriously.

He glanced at the strapping centaur who stood a full head taller than Vereesa, and found himself genuinely worried about how this would turn out.

After all, the centaur was holding a war spear with a sharp point — not some practice wooden staff or blunted lance. As heated as the centaur were, this was a duel that would cost someone their life, or at least end with one side badly wounded.

And yet Vereesa Windrunner studied the centaur from a distance, then casually tossed her bow and her two sabers aside.

The gesture was unmistakably, outrageously provocative. The centaur crowd was immediately whipped into a frenzy — howls and low bellows rising from every direction, onlookers stamping their forehooves or waving their arms and weapons.

Jace watched the priestess's reaction. She remained quite calm, merely glancing at the centaur guard. The guard himself was clearly less worked up than the crowd around him — at least he hadn't been hotheaded enough to throw away his spear in a fit of rage.

Jace glanced at Grede beside him. The dwarf was even more fired up than before, shouting Vereesa's name over and over. Even Mosa beside him was swishing her tail — hard to tell if it was a lion's tail or a cow's — intently watching the scene unfold.

The centaur guard pounded his chest and raised his spear. The roaring suddenly shifted into a unified "Hoo! Hoo! Hoo!" — and even Grede, caught off guard, fell silent.

Vereesa simply stood where she was, shifting one step back. The centaur guard tensed every muscle in his body!

His opponent suddenly burst into motion. Four hooves kicked up a cloud of dust, his spear lifting as he charged like a savage knight launching a headlong assault. The guard was huge, his stride long — three or four steps and he was on top of the elf…

Vereesa nimbly stepped aside, dodged the spear point, leaped, and spun — her foot slamming squarely into the back of the centaur's neck!

It happened so fast it barely seemed human. And of course, she wasn't human. The centaur was caught even more off guard. The kick staggered him, and he lurched forward, plowed straight into the crowd, and crashed to the ground. Just like that, the arena fell silent.

Vereesa landed steadily and glanced back with contempt at the scattered centaur mob. The guard captain lay there. He never got back up.

He was dead.

Even from this distance, Jace could see the centaur guard's head twisted at a grotesque angle, his mouth and nose full of blood. His four long black legs stuck stiffly into the air, rigid as boards — and they even gave a twitch just then.

"It's over? But… there won't be any trouble, will there?" Grede murmured, scanning the stunned centaur civilians and soldiers.

"I don't know," Jace said — and his hand was already on his sword hilt.

It wasn't just the two of them and all the centaur who were stunned. The moment Vereesa kicked that centaur to death, Jace had even felt Morophil's emotions shudder through the Soul Link from afar.

He couldn't tell whether she was shocked by Vereesa's skill or on guard against a sudden centaur attack.

But the unrest they'd been bracing for never came. After a stretch of silence, Jace noticed the female centaur priestess tilt her head upward.

She was looking toward the heights — up at the cliff, where a single centaur silhouette stood. At this distance, Jace couldn't make out his build, but he stood a full head taller than every other centaur around him, powerfully built as a dark statue.

Was that the leader of the Maraudine?

Jace was still studying the centaur's outline when the priestess had already stepped into the center of the arena.

"Windrunner," she said in Elvish, speaking the name. Then she said something more to Vereesa.

Vereesa nodded, crooked a finger, and walked toward the valley entrance. The watching centaur parted to make a path for her. The whole valley had gone terrifyingly quiet.

Jace and Grede exchanged a look, hurriedly picked up Vereesa's bow and blades, and followed at a trot with Mosa in tow.
