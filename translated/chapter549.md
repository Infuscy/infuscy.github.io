## Chapter 549: The Gloomy Tomb

Stepping through the tomb gate of Maraudon, Jace realized that even after reaching the deepest part of the Valley of Spears — even after walking into this place — he'd still been harboring a trace of contempt for this centaur sacred ground.

Maybe it was because the centaur really didn't look all that intelligent. Or maybe it was that, despite the grandeur and gloom of the architecture here, it was still a far cry from the buildings of humans and dwarves — after all, he was a Lordaeronian at heart.

And of course, it was mostly because of his past-life experience…

Maraudon, as an instance, wasn't exactly low-difficulty — but in the old versions, mages who knew the tricks could use the instance's terrain to pull mobs and power-level low-level characters. That had turned this remote, bizarre underground secret into a leveling hotspot as busy as a marketplace…

Jace had of course tried power-leveling on his own low-level mage alt. He could do it, sure — but not well enough to actually make a business out of it, and he didn't have the patience for that anyway.

But the deeper they walked, the more these scattered memories distorted, one by one.

Jace stared at the pitch-black passageway, the braziers burning on either side, the towering centaur statues carved into the walls beside him — and unconsciously grew serious, no longer able to connect memory to reality.

After all was said and done, this was the source of the weakening and death of the entire Mashan'she region. And Princess Theradras, deep in the heart of Maraudon — while she'd become a famous old meme in the Warcraft community on account of being, frankly, way too grotesque-looking…

Here, she was the only daughter of Therazane the Stonemother — a genuine elemental lord, a terrifying being capable of deciding the life and death of an entire plain. Whatever else, Jace had no desire to go and provoke her on this demon-hunting trip for no good reason.

Following the centaur guard step by step into the depths, darkness, souls… it was like a symphony inaudible to mortal ears, growing ever more distinct — pounding at his consciousness, at his heart.

"It's bigger than I imagined," Grede said.

The words echoed back and forth through the deep, cavernous passage. The centaur nearby all glanced over, and some of them Jace had mistaken for statues in the shadows actually turned their heads.

Quite a few centaur warriors dwelt within the sacred tomb. Most of them were more finely dressed and handsome than the centaur outside, taller, more powerfully built. Jace had noticed that in centaur tribes, the more attractive, the stronger, and the more well-proportioned a centaur was, the higher their status tended to be. That the centaur guarding this sacred ground stood out so much was only natural.

Grede looked them over, swallowed, and went on: "Even though you and Mosa talked about what this place might be like before… this reminds me of Ironforge. If the Bronzebeard dwarves heard me say that about Ironforge, they'd thrash me for it."

Jace didn't take the bait. After all, compared to the palaces the centaur had built outside, the depths of Maraudon were far, far larger. Grede would see that soon enough. The guard led them deep into the palace, and the cavern walls gradually lost their traces of artificial construction. Tree roots hung down from above, and the floor tiles were missing here and no longer as level as when they'd first come in.

They rounded a corner, passed through a stone gateway, and entered a broad cavern.

Before them lay a dark, deep ravine. Jace glanced down — at the bottom was a shallow pool, home to some kind of beasts he couldn't quite make out. Frogs, maybe. Lizards. Or some other damned thing.

And on the far side of the ravine was a natural cavern that had undergone almost no centaur modification at all — purple crystal clusters everywhere.

The centaur pointed at the other side, said something, then turned and left through the stone gateway. It seemed the guided tour ended here.

Jace tried to reconstruct the map of the game from memory. He could sense some familiar directions, corners — but as for being precise about it…

"Which way do we go now?" Grede said. "This place is a complete mess."

Vereesa looked at the crystal clusters and said, "Everything about this place radiates hostility toward us. The plants, the water… even these stones and this soil — none of them welcome us."

"Well, good thing nothing here welcomes me either! The elements in this place are probably howling at me to get the hell out of here!" Sazeno said. "Which means the demons hiding in here don't have any environmental advantage over us!"

"Let's hope so," Vereesa said. "But these demons have been here for a long time. They must have their own way of surviving — otherwise the centaur here wouldn't be at a loss for what to do with them."

"I'm guessing the centaur don't dare enter Maraudon not because they lack the strength to get in, but because they don't dare," Jace said. "Remember the legend of Maraudon — the story of the centaur ancestors of Mashan'she."

"The earth elemental princess drove the centaur out of this place, right?" Grede sighed. "And these idiots actually abide by this so-called ancient pact — even though their souls being threatened by those demons is a real, ongoing problem!"

"Let's go take a look over by those purple crystal clusters," Vereesa said. "The magic within those crystals might guide us to the trail on this dagger."

"I bet the goblin collector's mage never made it this far," Grede said excitedly. "So every step we take… probably means we're one step closer to the clue the Reliquary wants about that Highborne. It gives me chills just thinking about it."

"Careful, Grede. We're dealing with demons here," Jace cautioned.

"It's not my first time dealing with demons either, is it, Jace?" Grede said with a laugh.

Jace knew the dwarf was starting to brag about his Dark Portal exploits again — but the demons here were nothing like the demons at the Dark Portal.

The demons hiding in Maraudon weren't just one — there were many. A group of satyrs skilled at lurking in the shadows. And they could only be far more powerful than the half-elf satyr Jace had encountered in Stormwind.

The leader of the Maraudon satyrs, Vyletongue, might have been transformed from a night elf — or even a Highborne. If it was a Highborne, that meant he had been hiding in Kalimdor for thousands, even tens of thousands of years.

Vereesa's lips moved. She closed her eyes and murmured an incantation. When she opened them again, the arcane glow in her eyes was noticeably brighter.

She drew the mysterious Eldre'Thalas dagger, cradled it in a magical cloth, and studied the cavern before her carefully. "The source of the magical trace is indeed in Maraudon — but the resonance I'm sensing here is quite faint. I need to get closer."
