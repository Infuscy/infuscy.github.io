## Chapter 550: The First Clue

The depths of the Maraudon caverns were dark and complex. By this point, the traces of centaur construction had all but disappeared. Even though Jace was quite familiar with Maraudon from the game, walking through it here, he could barely recognize the place.

Honestly, he felt that even if you handed him a detailed map, he could still get lost in an underground palace like this.

Then again, the crystal clusters were everywhere — Vereesa could use the faint magic emanating from these purple clusters to memorize the patterns of their distribution and judge where they'd gotten to, which kept the party from being completely lost.

When they'd first entered the sacred tomb, Jace had already sensed that the burial chambers the centaur had built were far more complex and vast than the stretch of centaur graveyard outside Maraudon's entrance in the game.

Pushing into these purple-crystal-covered deep caverns, he felt even more strongly that he had truly stepped into another world — a mysterious space utterly disconnected from that game of his past.

He remembered standing at the Lushwater Oasis, looking toward the hills on the far side, and even wanting to go over and see what the legendary Wailing Caverns actually looked like.

Back then, he'd still thought that since he knew his way around Wailing Caverns, he wouldn't have to worry about getting lost in there…

Having now seen what Maraudon was really like inside, all he could say was: thank God he hadn't been stupid enough to go wandering in there looking for trouble.

The deeper they pushed into the caverns, the thicker and heavier the shadow power became. Then Grede stopped in his tracks, gazed at the faint, distant glimmer of purple crystal light, swallowed, and let out a shaky breath.

"I think I should drink a little Shadow Protection Potion," he murmured.

"We didn't bring many Shadow Protection Potions," Vereesa said. "And each one only lasts an hour. Try to tough it out on your own as much as you can, Grede. How are you feeling right now?"

"All right, I guess," Grede said.

"Wait," Jace said.

Perhaps because the voices made the silence that followed all the more deafening, Jace now seemed to hear the sound of rushing water.

"Do you hear that?"

"Water?" Grede asked.

"Yes." Vereesa touched the cavern wall. "Let's follow the sound and take a look."

Following the sound of water, they entered a narrow passage and emerged into another, broader cavern thick with purple crystals. Here, Grede's expression grew even more uncomfortable.

Mosa behind them coughed — it seemed she too was feeling the pressure of the shadow power.

Jace glanced back at the tauren. Vereesa had already moved ahead, since the sound of water was very close now.

One after another, they squeezed through a crack-like stone gap — and a vast karst cavern suddenly opened before them. A stream flowed through the middle, running deep into an unfathomable black abyss.

Morophil looked upstream. "We should head uphill."

"You sure?"

Vereesa asked — she'd already been observing the downstream side for a while.

Morophil said, "This water has been touched by demons. I can feel the fel energy and shadow breath in it — the scent of demons. They're of the Burning Legion…"

Vereesa relayed the situation to Mosa. Mosa scooped up some water, drank a little, spat it out, then looked uphill and nodded to Vereesa.

Then Grede suddenly raised his head and looked toward the cavern ceiling.

"What is it?"

"What?" Vereesa asked.

A rustling sound came from above — and this time everyone heard it. Jace gripped his staff. Grede had his battle axe in hand. Mosa took two steps back and picked up a chunk of broken rock from beside her.

Without warning, a section of the cavern wall behind Mosa burst open. A gray thing smashed through the dirt and cracks, thrusting its head out and hooking the tauren with antenna-like mandibles near its mouth.

Grede was quick — he hurled his stone axe, sending it spinning straight into the face of the insect's carapace and splitting it open on impact!

The giant bug swayed its head, writhed and thrashed for a bit, then dragged its three-to-four-meter-long body out of the crevice entirely. Mosa grabbed Grede's axe, and as a burst of yellow pus sprayed out with it, she swung the axe and hacked the insect cleanly in two. Only then did she let out a breath.

Vereesa said, "We've started drawing this cavern's attention. We need to be even more low-profile than before."

Mosa seemed to sense that the bug might have been drawn by her cough earlier, and looked somewhat guilty.

Grede walked over, patted her arm, and pointed uphill. "Let's keep going. Straight up."

The tauren nodded. Jace said to Morophil, "From here on out, you take the lead. Go invisible and move ahead. If you see something, come back and let us know in advance."

"She goes invisible — can you tell where she's heading?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," Jace said.

Vereesa gave the succubus a skeptical look. Morophil was already heading uphill, melting into the shadows as she went.

The four followed the succubus's footsteps. As they climbed higher, the karst cavern narrowed somewhat. They hadn't walked for more than ten minutes before they had to wade into the water to move forward.

The water was bitterly cold — stepping in was enough to make you shudder. And Jace had a feeling it wasn't just the cold; whether it was the demonic corruption mixed in or what, he even felt the skin the water touched start to itch.

As they walked, Grede suddenly sucked in a sharp breath — nearly shouting.

But he managed to clap his hand over his own mouth.

Morophil up ahead sensed the commotion behind her and stopped. Jace looked over at the dwarf. Grede stared at Jace, pointed downward at his own feet, and whispered: "There's something here."

With that, Grede bent down, felt around, then seemed to grab something. He gave a hard tug and pulled up half a skeleton.

"That's all that's left."

"What is it?" Jace asked. "The upper half of a centaur?"

Vereesa frowned. "No. These are the bones of a Quel'dorei."

"You're sure it's a Quel'dorei?" Jace said. "Not a Kaldorei? Or a human?"

"There are powerful arcane traces on these bones, Jace," Vereesa said. "By my experience, these should be the remains of a mage… trust me, I've examined plenty of bones that've been picked clean like this. Even if he's not one of my own people, he's a Kaldorei mage at the very least."

"A Kaldorei mage?" Jace said. "The elves here strongly reject contact with the arcane — there can't be any mages."

"Perhaps the remains of an ancient Highborne noble," Vereesa said. "In any case, I want to take a sample from this dead man — it should serve as a clue to bring back to Quel'Thalas."

"That's not bad at all," Grede said, holding up the half-skeleton. "This is worth good money, you know."

"Could this be Dasmora Darkeye?" Jace asked.

"It's a male," Vereesa said, extracting a tooth from the broken skull as she spoke. "Our target is a woman — but there may be a connection between them."

Then Jace felt Morophil's emotions suddenly spike with tension — she was sending him a warning through the Soul Link.

"Hide. There's something ahead," Jace said immediately.

The two who'd been gathering the remains quickly dove for cover. Mosa too transformed into a panther and slipped into the water, hiding to the side.
