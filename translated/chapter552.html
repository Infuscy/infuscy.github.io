Chapter 552: Dangerous Life Force

Leaving the corridor for the passage outside, the burning Shadowfire braziers bathed the whole cavern in a ghostly blue.

Inside the palace, chittering and giggling could be heard everywhere. Sazeno pricked up his ears, listening, then looked at Jace and jabbed a finger hard at his own chest.

Jace understood — he was trying to say there were other imps here.

After confirming no one else was in the cavern, Musa muffled her footsteps and followed the treant. Jace had to admit that, while she might not have been all that powerful a druid, as a hunter she was top-notch — a figure that tall, walking on hooves, and she could still keep her sound to nothing.

The others hung back keeping watch, guarding against patrols approaching from other directions. Musa called softly to the treant from behind and spoke to it in a low voice.

The treant stared at Musa for a moment as if it didn't understand what she was saying. Musa drew a deep breath and began to chant a spell.

She swept her arms wide, calling on the Life Force. As she cast, the treant seemed to remember something — its big amber eyes flew wide, and the branches and bark across its body rustled and clattered.

Jace noticed the Shadow energy in its eyes being devoured bit by bit by the surging Life Force, slowly retreating.

When the spell was done, Musa pressed on, speaking to the treant. The treant seemed to finally understand something. It tilted its head, wrapped in brittle branches and withered leaves, looked across the cave wall, then turned and trotted off toward the depths of the cave.

Musa came back and said something to Windrunner, who explained, "This treant will try to wake the other treants. I think what we need to do next is wait here for things to change inside."

Before the others could react, a chorus of chittering drew closer. Morophil immediately melted into the shadows. The rest of them backed away a few steps in the other direction — and a bright-green imp came bounding recklessly in!

It shouted in demonic, "I heard voices! I heard—"

It didn't finish. Musa materialized beside it in great-cat form, bit its head off, and tossed it aside. Corrupted black blood sprayed out, giving off a thick metallic stench.

"Let's find another spot to hide," Jace said.

The others nodded and quickly moved to a quieter stretch of passage on the other side. By now, the cave was already rumbling with the muffled roars of treants.

"This thing isn't actually an imp…" Sazeno said, tugging at Jace's robe in surprise. "That wasn't an imp! But it looks just like us! I'm sure there's got to be some connection between us!"

"That wasn't an imp?"

Jace asked it, and now that Sazeno had pointed it out, he had indeed noticed — this little creature had no demon horns on its head.

"It really isn't," Sazeno said. "It's a descendant of a Life Elemental, master. It's been badly warped by satyr fel energy and Shadow, but I think it was originally a native creature… like that satyr we just killed!"

"Let's leave this cave first, then debate your taxonomy, demon," Windrunner said in a low voice. "We've got plenty of problems right now!"

She was right. Jace could hear satyr shouts of command echoing through the different tunnels, and the chittering of those little imps from before had gone quiet.

"Well, this is a proper mess," the dwarf said, eyeing the three-tunnel fork ahead. "Which way do we go?"

Jace cast Sense Demons and glanced into the cave. Demons great and small were everywhere, their demonic presence drifting in chaos all around.

The sheer weight of shadow pressed down on him so hard it felt like being back at the Dark Portal, and he had to force himself to relax.

But which way now?

Jace studied the ruins before him — things that looked as if they'd been built by elves and centaurs alike, now swallowed by tree roots, mud, and rubble from age and neglect.

Flagstone floors of bluish-green stone gave way to earthen-floored tunnels one after another, each carrying the shouts of demons, making it nearly impossible to tell which way they should go.

Windrunner suddenly pointed to the tunnel on the right. "This way."

"How do you know?" Grede asked.

"I can sense the magical trail resonating with this dagger — it's this way," Windrunner answered. "Now that we're here, my sense of it is very clear."

With that, she hefted her bow and headed that way. The remaining two men and one cat exchanged glances — they truly had no other choice.

The group pressed deeper into the cave, passing a burning Shadowfire brazier, and came to a wall lit by fel light and covered in demonic runes. The evil flame seemed to be affected by the unstable magic permeating the whole cave — with no wind at all it flickered back and forth, making the entire chamber feel utterly chaotic.

"Find the source of the disturbance! The Princess will not tolerate any interruption!"

A shout in demonic suddenly rang out from above, as if drifting from very far away.

Jace looked up — and only then realized there was another level here, higher up. The cracked stone floor jutted outward like teeth, and with the flame's light and shadow dancing, they'd charged straight in without noticing.

Muffled roars and furious demonic curses came from above. The sounds of chaotic fighting, shouting, branches swaying, and blades scraping across metal washed over them — and finally, a treant toppled from above and crashed right in front of the group.

Splinters and leaves exploded everywhere. It stared straight at Jace, its eyes — shot through with Shadow and gold — flickering out bit by bit, turning into nothing but an ordinary piece of dead wood.

"I smell something different…"

A voice in demonic drifted from above. Jace locked eyes with Morophil; she read his meaning at once and nimbly scaled the earthen wall, vanishing from sight halfway up.

Windrunner followed right behind her, climbing the slope. The demons above seemed to hear the movement on the wall and poked their heads over — but Windrunner reached out, grabbed a hoof, and hauled one straight down.

It crashed at Grede's feet, and the dwarf split its chest open with a stone axe. The Shadow energy barely had time to flare before it was snuffed out.

Another satyr above drew its longsword at the sight and slashed at the arm Windrunner was using to grip the ledge — but before the blade could fall, the succubus who'd already climbed up behind it ran it through the heart. It mumbled "traitor" in demonic with effort, then shuddered and tumbled forward down the slope.

Musa nimbly scaled the slope in cat form. Jace slung his staff across his back, took two strides to the height, grabbed Windrunner's hand, and hauled himself onto the ledge. The two of them then hauled the deadweight Grede up together.

No sooner had they reached the height than Grede shouted, "There's one there!"

Everyone looked where he was pointing, into another tunnel in the distance — a satyr was just turning to leave.

Grede had no time to swap to his war hammer, so he hurled the axe in his hand — but the distance was too great. The satyr barely dodged the throw and turned to flee deeper into the tunnel.

"Damn it," Jace cursed under his breath and immediately gave chase with Windrunner.

At that moment Musa suddenly shifted back into humanoid form and spoke a quick spell. When the group rounded the corner, the satyr that had already gotten a dozen meters ahead had its hands and feet tangled by roots spearing out of the cracked stone wall. It struggled to break free, but Windrunner put an arrow straight through its heart. The satyr went limp at once, and the roots dragged it straight into the wall crack — the corpse was even crushed out of shape, black blood oozing out.

That was clearly Musa's magic. But for a druid who cherished nature and upheld the Balance, that final flourish from the plant roots had been unexpectedly savage.

Jace looked back at the tauren, who had just caught up. She was staring at the scene, surprise plain on her face.

She said something to Windrunner. It turned out she had only called on the plants here to help block the demon — the way the plant had then torn the demon apart and crushed it hadn't been her doing at all.

Jace had a faint feeling that tapping into the Life Elementals deep within Maraudon was a deeply dangerous gamble.

Clearly, Musa had realized this hazard too.
