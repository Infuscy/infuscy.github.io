Chapter 557: The Satyr's Plan

Once they'd finished preparing the flayed tattoos and bagged Vile'tharn's head, the group hauled everything back to the cluster of temples beside the first pool they'd found. The place was already thick with demon corpses.

Looking at the bodies, Jace thought that if Erland Dudmoor were here, he'd be excited enough to lose sleep. All that hide and all those horns, if they could gather them up and find a buyer, would surely fetch several gold. Unfortunately, they simply didn't have the carrying capacity — even Musa didn't have unlimited stamina, and she was already loaded up with no small amount of gear.

All they could do was hope the satyrs had stashed enough valuables somewhere in the temple, or hidden clues linking back to that Highborne woman — something they could take back to the Reliquary to justify a bigger payout.

From here they could hear the murmur of Windrunner and Musa talking in the temple's depths. Following the sound of their voices, the three of them headed deeper.

Jace remembered that in the game, the interior of this temple had been nothing but a single bare, monotonous hall. Here, though, there were a good many side chambers, all connected by corridors.

These halls and rooms were furnished no less with Highborne-style fixtures — and you could tell the materials varied, as if they'd been scavenged from elven ruins in different places and brought here. Had Vile'tharn really been trying to deck this place out as his own palace, dreaming of a return to the days of the Kaldorei Empire?

"Come over here, Jace." Windrunner's voice drifted out.

Jace followed the sound and found Windrunner and Musa standing before a stone bookshelf packed with all manner of books. Even he could feel that no small number of them still bore traces of enchantment — some kind of preservation ward meant to keep the pages from the ravages of time.

The trouble was, every last one of them was written in the elven tongue. Highborne works, most likely.

"Were these enchanted with Dasmora magic?" Jace asked. "Is this the magical trace you sensed earlier?"

"No," Windrunner said. "The enchanters on these books all seem to be different people. At the very least, they have nothing to do with Dasmora."

Jace asked, "So what are these books about?"

"Ancient Kaldorei relics, artifacts, collectibles…" Windrunner said. "Vile'tharn must have collected these books to aid his own artifact hunting."

Just then, Musa came over from another room carrying an enchanted small chest. There was something inside; whatever the enchantment, Jace couldn't sense through it.

Musa said something to Windrunner. Windrunner shook her head, then said to Jace, "The magic on this chest doesn't match the dagger."

"This chest was sealed by the satyrs here with their own enchantment, master," Morophil said.

Windrunner said, "The chaotic magical energy in this temple is throwing off my trace-detection a bit. Maybe I just haven't learned the technique well enough."

"We'll split up and search a little — pull out anything suspicious and bring it to you one by one to examine," Grede said.

Windrunner nodded. Jace said, "Let's see what's in this chest first."

"How do we open it?" Grede asked.

"I'll try dispelling the enchantment." Windrunner closed her eyes. At a faint, ethereal chiming of arcane energy, her power clashed with the ward on the chest in a discordant buzz.

"It's tough," she said, frustrated.

"Then let's do it the clean way! If we can't open it, whatever's inside might as well not exist!" Grede swung his lightning-charged warhammer straight at the chest with a thunderous crash that made the whole great hall shake.

Of course the chest's enchantment couldn't withstand a thunderstrike. It burst open on impact, its contents spilling across the floor.

This… was a whole pile of soul shards.

The souls inside… were most likely the spirits of centaur heroes across the ages — the very reason the centaur so despised these demons.

Jace could feel the character of the souls within those shards. Ever since he'd stepped into Maraudon's sacred burial grounds, he'd sensed that wild, chaotic… unbridled urge to break free of their cages, surging with the silent howls of the souls in every direction. When Grede smashed the chest open, the sensation hit even harder.

"What do the satyrs want with these souls?" Jace asked.

"With this many soul shards… it has to be some kind of powerful summoning ritual, master," the imp said.

Jace thought of the chaotic meteor he'd channeled at the Dark Portal, powered by the souls on a battlefield — a spell he never could have gathered the strength for on any ordinary day. He murmured, "Some fearsome magic, maybe."

"We need more clues," Grede said. "Guessing off these soul stones alone gets me nowhere. I'm going to keep searching the other rooms."

"Windrunner, look through these books… see if there's any pattern," Jace said.

"Understood," Windrunner nodded.

"You two, look for anything in Demonic," Jace told the two demons.

"Over there." Windrunner pointed to a room down the corridor. "There are scrolls and books in there that Musa and I couldn't read. Probably Demonic."

"Thank you for your gracious guidance, Quel'dorei," Morophil said with affected courtesy, then left the room together with the imp.

Windrunner ground her teeth in discomfort and shot Jace a look. "Was that another one of your strange orders?"

Jace rubbed his temples and sighed. "She's just deliberately trying to get under my skin and make me miserable. Fits my image of a Sayaad perfectly."

Windrunner walked to the next bookshelf, pulled down a book, and flipped through it as she spoke. "But it's not my first time working with a disagreeable partner on a mission. It's nothing. I can adapt."

As she said it, she sounded more like she was trying to convince herself.

"After all, it's a quality a Farstrider needs. In the future I'll have to work with countless members of the Magisters, or those Holy Light-worshipping priests. You know how those Magisters look down on Farstriders…"

Windrunner trailed off, then seemed to catch herself. "Wait — why are there so many books about Eldre'Thalas magic on this shelf?"

"Eldre'Thalas magic?" Jace asked. "What's that?"

Windrunner set down the book and scanned the rest of the shelf. Suddenly it clicked. "This entire shelf is all about Eldre'Thalas. Not any specific spell — the city's entire magical defense array… the distribution of its arcane towers, the locations of its ley-line nodes, its magical prisons…"

The moment Windrunner said it, Jace thought of the satyrs lurking deep within Dire Maul in the game.

Yes — the place that would later be known as Dire Maul, part of Eldre'Thalas, was occupied by demons in the game. But Jace wasn't sure exactly when they'd moved in.

"You mentioned Eldre'Thalas demons to me before," Jace said to Windrunner. "According to the Reliquary's intelligence, are there satyrs in that ancient city?"

"There are plenty of signs of demon-summoning among the Kaldorei in Eldre'Thalas — that much is certain," Windrunner said. "But from the investigations by other mages before us, there have been no traces of satyrs there. At least, that wasn't in the intelligence the Reliquary gave me."

"So the satyrs here want to attack Eldre'Thalas?" Jace muttered to himself.

Windrunner said, "From what I know, when the mages of Quel'Thalas investigated the place, they didn't dare get too close to Eldre'Thalas' walls because of the sheer power within — they could only grope around by the magical traces emanating from it. So the idea that this handful of demons could directly assault that sealed city? I seriously doubt it."

One thing was clear: neither the satyrs here nor the Reliquary of Quel'Thalas knew much about the current state of that city.

Eldre'Thalas, in recent decades, had become a place teetering on the edge. The Highborne inside could no longer sustain themselves and had reached the brink of collapse and madness — otherwise they wouldn't have been so quickly overrun by a band of ogres who'd crossed the sea and seized more than half the city.

"Let's keep looking — and check what Grede's side found too." Jace ran a hand over the enchanted bark scrolls piled on the shelf. "Spell me how to write 'Eldre'Thalas' and 'satyr' in the elven tongue. I'll help you search. Things can't be this simple."

Windrunner looked at Jace and nodded.
