Chapter 558: The Demon Statue

After searching the shelf scrolls and books together with Windrunner for a while, it was clear Vile'tharn had a keen interest in Eldre'Thalas' magical defense system — relevant keywords turned up everywhere across the volumes.

But there was indeed no mention of any other satyrs.

At that moment, Morophil and Sazeno returned to the room carrying a bundle of Demonic scrolls of every description. Jace could see no small number of them were scraps of cured hide and bark — fragmentary enchanted items that looked like letters.

These things reeked with the peculiar stench of demons, far worse than anything the satyrs carried on them. When they were first hauled in, Windrunner had frowned and glanced over.

Jace, though, was long since used to it. He immediately leaned in to scan several of them. If they wanted to figure out what these satyrs were really up to, these letters would be far more useful than anything else.

Windrunner kept sifting through the elven-tongue books for clues while Jace crouched down with the succubus and the imp to comb through the letters.

From the other end of the corridor came the clatter of Grede and Musa, probably rummaging through some demon storehouse for anything suspicious — or valuable.

Most of the letters were satyr correspondence, exchanging news about Highborne ruins they'd scouted across Desolace. It was clear Vile'tharn had dispatched no small number of satyr seekers to hunt down Highborne artifacts in the field.

The artifacts mentioned in these letters were mostly various arcane implements. Jace wasn't interested — he had neither the time nor the manpower to go tracking them down, and making a special effort would be a pure waste of time.

He'd originally hoped to find something linking these satyrs to the ones in Dire Maul, but there wasn't the faintest trace of that either.

From these letters it was clear Vile'tharn had some urge to explore Feralas, but the night elf sentinels patrolling the region had stymied his attempts several times without much success.

"Master!" Sazeno suddenly picked up a broad hide scroll. "This… this is an incantation for a ritual. A really complicated ritual incantation. Could this be what those soul stones are for?"

Jace took the scroll and looked it over. The incantation was absurdly long — like a reading comprehension passage — and the language was so complex he could barely make heads or tails of it.

"All of this?!" he asked.

"Every word of it. Frightening, right, master!"

There was a strange note of pride in the imp's voice, as if he'd written the incantation himself.

"This is a summoning ritual that requires multiple casters to perform together, master. Different casters are responsible for chanting different passages." Morophil pointed to one section as she spoke. "And this isn't a simple summoning incantation. Look at these phrases — these are expressions used to summon a target under extremely complex conditions."

"What kind of extremely complex conditions?" Jace asked.

"Say the demon you need to summon is in the deepest reaches of the Twisting Nether…" Morophil thought for a moment. "Or say the being you want to summon is sealed by magic, and you intend to use a summoning ritual to break the seal on it."

"Sealed?"

The moment Morophil said it, Jace thought almost immediately of Immol'thar in Eldre'Thalas.

Immol'thar… was a void demon that had been summoned into the city by the Highborne nobles of Eldre'Thalas ten thousand years ago.

After the explosion of the Well of Eternity ten thousand years ago, though Eldre'Thalas had survived — preserving most of its pre-cataclysm splendor and the bulk of its population — they had lost the power supplied by the Well of Eternity and urgently needed a new source of magic.

The elves of that era had been so dependent on arcane energy that, without a fresh supply for an extended period, they would fall into weakness and madness, and some would even wither and death.

To keep the city alive, the rulers of Eldre'Thalas had taken it upon themselves to summon a powerful demon from the Twisting Nether — Immol'thar, a colossal void hound. After binding it in a seal, they used its power to continue fueling the entire city.

After thousands of years of having its energy siphoned, the demon had thoroughly corrupted every living being, every dead being, and even the city itself, inside and out… Countless elves had gone mad and died. That was the principal reason Eldre'Thalas was on the verge of collapse.

"You mean these satyrs want to summon Immol'thar from Eldre'Thalas to Maraudon?"

What in the world was that supposed to mean? Jace immediately dismissed the notion as insane.

Without the colossal, millennia-old sealing stone array and the network of arcane towers that Eldre'Thalas had built — a feat bordering on miraculous — to bind Immol'thar's power, these satyrs, with their pitiful strength, would be summoning Immol'thar straight to their own deaths.

Then what was this incantation for?

Just then, Grede and Musa came over carrying several chests and a large cloth sack stuffed with a mountain of things.

The tall and the short of them set the haul on the floor. The tauren seemed somewhat repulsed by the evil objects inside; Grede couldn't care less and started pulling things right out.

There were demon weapons, some peculiar spell components, a whole jumble of odds and ends. Jace looked at the scattered mess and felt it was genuinely hard to sum them up in a few words.

When a magic-tinged orb of a sinister blood-red hue was pulled out, Windrunner suddenly looked over.

Grede noticed her reaction and looked up. "What is it?"

"That magic orb," Windrunner said. "Let me see it, Grede. I think I'm sensing something."

"A magical trace resonance?" Jace asked.

Windrunner nodded.

Grede immediately handed the orb to the elf. Windrunner cradled it in her hands, and Jace leaned in for a closer look. It was a strange sphere that seemed to enclose a ball of flame, and within the flames there appeared to be a shadow in the shape of a figure.

No… that wasn't a human-shaped shadow. It was the image of a blood-red demon.

A demon statue?

A demon statue was a spell component used to summon a specific demon.

Combined with that long, complex incantation from earlier, and Morophil's reading of it…

"Doomguard," Jace said. "I think this incantation is used to summon a Doomguard."

Grede asked, "You mean those flying demons we killed at the Dark Portal?"

"Ered'ruin," Sazeno said. "That's a vast race, fat man! Don't you go thinking the cannon fodder the Burning Legion sends to die can be compared to the most powerful individuals among the Ered'ruin! You've never laid eyes on the power of an Ered'ruin lord! Ha!"

Grede said, "You think I'm looking down on those demons? The ones the Burning Legion specifically sends to die can already take on three or four gryphon riders each. So what would the most powerful of them be like?"

"The real question is what this statue — or rather, this Doomguard — has to do with the female warlock we're looking for," Jace said.

Everyone turned to Windrunner, who was holding the demon statue. She frowned and said, "I don't know. The connection between this statue and the magical trace on that dagger is extremely close. It's possible that warlock has touched it with her own hands…"

"Either way, it's unquestionably another important clue to hand over to the Reliquary," Grede said. "Bring this lot back and they won't give us fifty or sixty gold?"

Windrunner looked at Grede, then back to Jace. "Actually, I think the link between this statue and her may be much stronger than a simple touch. Jace — don't you think this might have been used by Dasmora? Say, she wanted to summon an Ered'ruin demon… but didn't succeed? Or something of that sort…"

"Keep looking. See if there's anything similar." Jace pointed at the other junk on the floor.

Grede immediately bent down to pick through it, and Windrunner joined in — but before long, Musa suddenly held up a hand to stop everyone.

She looked toward the doorway. A wet, squelching sound was coming from the corridor — splch, splch.

Jace had heard that sound before. It was a treant.
