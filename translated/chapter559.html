Chapter 559: Who Is the True Master of Maraudon

A treant came staggering up to the doorway and looked in at the group staring back at it. Its golden eyes, which ought to have been blazing bright, were now all but invisible — nothing but two faintly glimmering hollows.

Musa tried speaking to it a few sentences. A rumbling sounded from deep within the treant's trunk, and for reasons unknown, an intensely uneasy feeling crept into Jace's gut.

If Vile'tharn was dead, then after these treants corrupted by demons were freed, whose orders would they follow?

The treant opened its lips — split, ragged things like torn bark — and let out a twisted, eerie voice, like the hissing of some bloated insect. It kept repeating one phrase, over and over, until everyone present's scalp crawled.

"No communicating!" the tauren said in Common.

Jarring and hard to parse as it was, the others understood the meaning the instant they saw the treant's frenzied state.

The treant no longer listened to Musa. It was utterly unmoved.

Limping and lurching, it hurled itself into the room and lunged straight for the nearest of them — Grede. Grede snatched up the hammer he'd set aside and swung, shattering the treant's reaching limb to splinters.

The blow sent a shudder through the treant's whole body. The last glimmer of light in its eyes went out completely. It let out a feeble, ragged cry, wailing as it collapsed beside the dwarf, and soon stopped struggling.

By the time it had reached this room, the treant was already covered in wounds. Its battle against the demons had nearly drained the last of its life force.

"Pack up. We're leaving. Now." Jace scooped up the Demonic scrolls and booklets from the floor and stuffed them one by one into his pack.

Grede stared at the treant's corpse, then looked at Jace. "What do we take?"

"Anything with magical energy, and… I don't know." Jace hesitated. Windrunner quickly grabbed several items and shoved them into her pack, then picked up another chest, wrapped it in a cloth bag, and handed it to Musa. "Take these for now."

The imp shrieked and yowled as he kept shoveling the soul stones they'd just piled together into the warlock's pack. Jace simply grabbed a whole fistful and stuffed them in.

"There's more treants outside, damn it — they're all coming!" Grede said, twin axes in hand. "Now the demons are dead, we're the bad guys!"

"What about these books?" Windrunner asked.

"Take the maps of Eldre'Thalas… plus the general locations of the magical defenses, and whatever interests you. We can't carry much more, Windrunner. Quickly — we need to get out of here."

"Understood." Windrunner tucked the demon statue into her sling pack and grabbed a few more books that still held plenty of residual magic. Just then came the sound of an axe chopping deep into wood from outside — Grede had already engaged the treants in the corridor.

Hearing the dwarf's commotion, Musa hefted several bundles, strapped them to her body, and charged straight out the door. Jace and Windrunner swept their eyes across the room one more time, confirmed there was nothing else of value, exchanged a glance, and hurried out after her.

The situation was worse than Jace had imagined. A countless host of treants of every variety was already shuffling into the corridor — a horde straight out of some zombie apocalypse, complete with the ones dragging themselves along on half a torn-away trunk.

Most of them were survivors of the battle against the demons. Earlier, when Jace and the others had pursued Vile'tharn, the satyrs must have realized something was wrong in the temple and immediately broken off their fight with the treants to rush to their lord's aid — only to be held off outside by Grede.

Now these abandoned treants… had somehow come swarming back to besiege Jace's group.

Grede swung his axes up front, chopping and hewing without pause. You could tell he was running on fumes — his axe work was clearly far less fluid than when he'd been surrounded by satyrs. Musa held a satyr longsword and guarded Grede's flank. Jace and the others who'd caught up drew their own weapons and hacked and smashed at the rotting treants, finally cutting a path out of the corridor and back onto the high platform by the pool.

Down below, more treants could be seen stepping up the flagstones one after another, along with walking plant-things that looked not unlike ravenous flowers. Jace stood at the height and chanted the Fireball incantation, hurling the bolts of flame forming on his staff one after another into the mass of twisted vegetation gathered below. Fire roared up violently, spreading swiftly from plant to plant and tree to tree even across the water's surface.

The weakened plants toppled in swathes. The whole space was shrouded in black smoke. When the treants and plants caught fire, they actually screamed and roared — sounds so close to the shrieking din of surging Shadow that Jace had good reason to believe… the cores of these creatures had long since been corrupted by Shadow. Some of them, he suspected, had even been birthed from Shadow itself… Shadow and life had never been mutually exclusive. The Emerald Nightmare, born of corruption by the Old Gods, was proof enough of that.

The fouled pool, the roiling plants, the roaring flames and the heavy smoke pouring off the water's surface, the darkened hall — the group pushed through the thrashing, struggling twisted vegetation, picking their way carefully toward the cave they'd come in by. Musa gazed at the creatures, and her eyes held a trace of panic.

"Wake…" she struggled to say something, but the Common words at her disposal were few. She had no time to explain to Windrunner, so she could only trail behind them, forcing out one halting word after another.

"Wake… underground… I feel it."

"Something's woken up?" Jace asked, turning his head.

Musa opened her mouth but did not answer. She couldn't seem to articulate it clearly — or perhaps she wasn't certain enough to say. But Jace knew, in this moment, he was practically asking the question already knowing the answer.

Or rather, he was reluctant to face what was happening in Maraudon right now.

Do not disturb the Princess…

Jace recalled what the satyrs' tame fel imps had reported to Vile'tharn — that they would not allow outsiders to disturb the Princess's slumber. A savage roar suddenly erupted from deep underground, and the whole cave shook violently. Jace felt his whole body jolt. He couldn't tell whether he'd been startled by the deafening crash or whether the violent tremor had knocked him off his feet — but he didn't have the energy left to parse it out right now.

Grede was thrown off balance by the shaking and caught himself against the wall beside him. He turned his head and shouted, "The hell's going on, Jace?!"

A dreadful, rumbling roar filled the cave, making it nearly impossible for Jace to hear Grede's voice — even though he knew the dwarf was shouting at the top of his lungs.

The treants that had been crawling toward them all stopped in their tracks and turned toward the cave's depths, beginning to mutter and chant — like some kind of prayer, or confession…
