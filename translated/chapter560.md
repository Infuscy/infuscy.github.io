## Chapter 560: The Mad Cavern

Amid the treants' deep, rumbling chant, the stone walls, the cave, the earthen cliffs grew more and more unstable. Then, up ahead, the lintel over a stone gate suddenly gave way, bringing down a great cloud of dust and a cascade of rubble. Windrunner stared at the gate. "Is it going to collapse?"

For a heartbeat, Jace felt as if his heart had stopped.

He recalled the terrain he'd seen when they first entered this demon-infested warren — the broken floors jutting out over the cliffs, the stone columns that had no business being where they were, the stone gates set into walls with no doorway, the flagstone walls that cut abruptly across the middle of the path, and the murals carved above them…

This elven ruin might well have stood above ground many years ago. The topography within Maraudon must have changed who knows how many times over to have been ground into such a state.

Just then, beside them, the treant roared again that word that defied explanation — the same word it had kept repeating ever since it first entered the satyr palace where they'd been hiding.

This time, Jace heard it with absolute clarity.

Theradras!

The group broke into a dead run, leaping off the high cliff and charging toward the original fork in the path — only to find the way ahead completely blocked by fallen rock.

"Damn it all!" Grede swore. Musa let out a shout and waved everyone on. They all followed her into another tunnel.

The deep, savage roar kept rolling up from the depths, from beneath the earth. Jace felt as if the whole world were boiling over. Grede was running not far ahead of Jace when an earthen wall suddenly toppled over in its entirety.

The moment the rubble and dirt hit the ground, it surged back up in a swell of churning magic, clumping together into a gigantic mud-and-stone elemental monster.

The creature swayed its body and slammed straight into Jace, sending him flying into the mud wall beside the path. He coughed violently. Behind him, Morophil went soft in the knees and only kept herself from falling outright by grabbing a root.

Clearly her Soul Link had absorbed no small share of the impact for Jace. Judging by the state Morophil was in, had he taken that hit head-on he might have been knocked unconscious on the spot.

The stone giant paid no heed to the warlock and the demon behind it and lunged straight for Grede ahead. A cloud of falling dust completely blinded the view. Jace, just getting back to his feet, had no time to cast a spell to intercept it — and the whole cave was roaring and shaking. Grede seemed to have no idea the colossus was closing in behind him.

Jace shouted at once: "Grede, behind you!"

Grede turned at the sound and landed a hammer blow that staggered the giant back a step. Then he bellowed and called down a blast of lightning that lit the entire cave a searing white, slamming it into the monster's chest and caving in its stony torso in one blow.

Shattered, the monster collapsed in a rumbling heap, rubble and dust flying everywhere. But the scattered stones, rather than settling, reformed right where they were under the rampaging elemental energy, becoming a swarm of gnomes- and imps-sized rock creatures that now stood between Grede and Jace.

Morophil snatched one of the stone creeps lunging at Jace and hurled it behind her — but another clambered up her arm and punched one of her horns with a dull crack. The blow staggered the succubus; she reeled a few steps and nearly went down before she caught herself and hurled it away.

Jace swung his staff to bat off one of the stone creeps trying to pound his legs. Another rolled up beside him, and he instinctively kicked it away — and his foot throbbed with pain from the impact.

Gritting through the leg pain, he took two steps forward only to be blocked by several more stone creeps up ahead. But Musa came back from the front, brandishing a club that was either a branch or a root and swatting several of them away in one sweep, shoving Jace ahead of her.

Confirming the others had all made it behind him, Jace, the dwarf, the elf, and the demons smashed and scattered the stone creeps at their feet. Musa took the rear, facing the ever-growing swarm of stone creeps pursuing them and chanting an incantation. A tangle of thorny brambles speared up out of the earth and stone, binding the packs of creatures tight — even forming a wall of tangled roots to hold the line behind them.

Jace glanced at the root wall and felt only the slightest ease of mind before he saw it sprout several new tendrils at terrifying speed, wrapping them around the tauren who'd cast the spell. Musa tore free of the roots coiling around her arms, drew a satyr blade and hacked through the crazed roots and brambles, kicked aside a battered stone creep with a hoof, and only then did she stride forward to catch up with the group.

Musa led the group as they fled the cave, shaking off the stone creeps and the maddened root-tendrils that filled the tunnels, until they made it back to the water cave they'd passed through before. But the four of them searched every which way for a long while and couldn't find the crevice that led back to the centaur sacred burial grounds.

All the while, the water cave was collapsing too. Stone columns cracked and toppled into the water one after another, forcing them to pick their way through on edge. The once-rushing underground river had become an intermittent trickle.

Then Musa looked back upstream. The rumbling tremor wouldn't stop. Before long, the stream had dwindled to nothing but a thin, rushing thread.

Everyone knew, up top, the way in had most likely caved in. The route in from the satyrs' side was probably blocked too.

Grede stared downstream into the black abyss, lit only faintly by scattered amethyst crystals, and swallowed.

Jace coughed, clearing the grit from his throat and mouth. "There has to be another way out of here. Those satyrs were clearly in contact with the outside — I can't see them coming and going through the centaur burial grounds whenever they pleased. I can track demons. Windrunner, Musa — you can both help. We can definitely search for any passage the satyrs used."

"Then we're counting on you, Jace," Grede nodded. "I'm about to be completely turned around in here."

That said, what Jace was actually thinking of was the cavern of the orange crystals — the other entrance to the Maraudon instance in the game. That was a purely natural cave, overrun by giant lizards and elemental creatures, a place the centaur never ventured into. Only — if the cave's structure had shifted in a way that kept them from reaching the orange-crystal cavern, what then?

And where did this underground river flow?

Jace stared into the dark depths below and listened to the deep, muffled moaning drifting up from within. He had an ugly suspicion: this river most likely ran all the way to the deepest reaches of Maraudon — to the pool beneath Zaetar's Tomb, guarded by the earth-elemental princess Theradras.

If the orange-crystal cavern couldn't be found, then the open-air deep pit of Zaetar's Tomb, or that underground pool connected to God-knows-where, might be their only way out.
