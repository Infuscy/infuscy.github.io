## Chapter 561: The Essential Spell

"I still want to know — what exactly is Princess Therazane? Who's her father?"

The whole way down the underground river, Grede asked questions as he walked.

They'd been on the move for nearly half an hour. All they'd heard were the occasional rumbles, the strange iron-ball rolling and gurgling from somewhere behind God-knew-how-thick a wall, or the crude snoring of some beast deep in the cave…

At Grede's question, the others all turned over, as though they'd nearly forgotten what a normal voice sounded like.

"Therazane isn't the princess of any kingdom," Jace said. "She's the daughter of an earth elemental lord. You've heard of those ancient elemental lords, haven't you? The fire lord the Dark Iron dwarves summoned — you haven't forgotten that, have you?"

Grede blinked. "Hold on. So you're telling me that Firelord is a real, genuine monster? The disaster at Blackrock Mountain was its doing?"

Jace asked, puzzled, "That's what you told me yourself — the Dark Iron dwarves dug the Firelord out of Blackrock Mountain."

"I… I thought that was just an excuse they made to cover up their own mistake," Grede said. "They cracked open a volcano, caused a terrible disaster, and then pinned the whole thing on some god from legend."

"The Firelord Ragnaros is real," Wynne said from the side. "The Kirin Tor has more than enough evidence to prove it."

"So this Princess Therazane, then," Grede quickened his pace to catch up to Jace. "She's the daughter of some King of the Earth, or… Mother of the Earth…"

"No. Not the Earth Mother." Somehow Mossa had caught Grede's drift. She murmured the words in Common, and her expression had already gone distinctly ugly.

Jace knew why. A thousand years ago, it had been a group of tauren priests who'd wanted to commune with the Earth Mother who awakened Therazane from deep underground — and that had set off centuries of tragedy for the tauren people.

"The Earth Mother is the tauren's faith," Jace corrected. "She has nothing to do with the Therazane we're facing."

Though who knew, really? After all, the Earth Mother was a figure from tauren mythology — there was no such definite being in reality.

Some said the Earth Mother was a member of the Pantheon. Some said the Earth Mother was Azeroth itself. Some said the Earth Mother was the same deity as Elune, whom the night elves worshipped. And of course, plenty of people suspected that the Earth Mother was in fact the Stone Mother Therazane — the princess's own mother.

But facing a true believer in the Earth Mother, Jace figured it was best not to spout off wild guesses.

"Fine." Grede nodded. "Either way, is there any chance we could talk to this princess? Tell her we mean no harm and ask her to let us out?"

"I'd love that too," Jace said, glancing at him. "But I'm afraid we've already missed our chance. Look at this cave, Grede. Look at all these demons and monsters serving her. Do you really think she's the reasonable type? We can't let her catch us. In here, she's a genuine god."

Following the underground river down, they hadn't encountered a single fork along the way.

There were even several collapses en route, forcing them to squeeze through narrow crevices. It wasn't until Jace noticed the foul stench growing heavier, the swamp's rot slowly creeping in, that a faint glimmer of light appeared ahead.

"An exit up there?" Grede asked.

"Shh!"

Wynne signaled for everyone quiet. She glanced at Mossa, then took the lead, moving up to the source of the light and peering out before pulling back.

"A natural cave," she whispered. "Or a canyon. That light… I think it's sunlight filtering in."

Hearing that, Grede visibly brightened. He whispered, "So we're out of this cave?"

The words had barely left his mouth before the smile froze on his face — because Jace's expression had gone rather grim.

Wynne noticed Jace's reaction too. The truth was, Jace had been hoping he wouldn't see sunlight, wouldn't see this so-called "canyon."

He'd much rather they'd stumbled into some unknown fork and found sinister orange-yellow crystals scattered everywhere, strange lizards covered in orange-yellow crystal shards dashing about, and raging stone elementals charging back and forth — and then thrown themselves at the things in a fight to the death.

Because that would've been the orange entrance. And once you saw those things, it meant the centaur holy tomb couldn't be far off.

But the moment he saw this dim canyon, it meant their group had actually reached the very depths of Maraudon.

The whole underground palace had settled by now. Apart from the occasional earth collapse, it was terrifyingly quiet compared to the earth-shaking chaos from before.

Standing at the cave mouth, slick with damp vines, Jace could even hear the sound of a waterfall echoing from somewhere far off in this ravine.

Not far outside the mouth was a broad, shallow pool. Thick bushes and algae ringed the water's edge, and the distant cliff wall was covered in vegetation — a dense shroud of green over the entire cavern.

A blurry sliver of sky overhead let down a light that made one's head spin, turning the whole place hazy and dreamlike, a sight out of a dreamscape.

Wynne exchanged a few words with Mossa, and her expression wasn't much better either.

"We may still be a long way from the exit," Wynne said. "Mossa can feel that the life elements here can barely communicate at all. Those plants haven't reacted in the slightest to her presence…"

"Never mind Mossa — I can feel the malice of everything here growing thicker too," Jace said. "Clearer and more definite than outside the cave mouth, at least. This is definitely no time to let our guard down."

"Damn it all," Grede said. "Don't tell me we're actually going to run into that Princess of the Earth?"

"Let's rest here a bit," Wynne said, looking at Jace and the others. "Eat something here, then go see what's actually in this ravine. I have a feeling we're going to run into a lot of trouble next."

Everyone agreed, and each found a rock to sit on.

"Sazeno," Jace said. "Hand me that booklet — the one we brought from Stormwind, the one Senluola copied. I did a simple binding on it. Remember? It's got some of Galders's recorded incantations."

"No problem, master." Sazeno lifted his head from the warlock's bag and handed the booklet to Jace.

After traveling through Maraudon's caves for so long, the pages were even a little damp.

Jace flipped through it until he found the page with the Water Breathing incantation.

## Flags

- **Therazane / Princess Therazane** (瑟莱德丝公主): the earth elemental princess who dwells in the depths of Maraudon; canonical WoW figure. Rendered as "Therazane" to match the canonical Maraudon boss name.
- **Earth Mother** (大地母亲): the tauren object of faith; Mossa reacts sharply to Grede's loose talk connecting her to Therazane.
- **Stone Mother Therazane** (石母塞拉赞恩): Jace's speculation about the Earth Mother's true identity — possibly the princess's mother. Note: the source uses two distinct Chinese transliterations (瑟莱德丝 for the princess, 塞拉赞恩 for the Stone Mother) that both map to "Therazane" in English; they are distinguished throughout by title (Princess vs. Stone Mother) rather than by name.
- **Firelord Ragnaros** (火焰之王拉格纳罗斯): the fire elemental lord the Dark Iron dwarves summoned; Wynne cites the Kirin Tor's proof of his existence.
- **orange entrance / orange-yellow crystals** (橙黄色水晶): Jace's game-knowledge reference to the orange-hued section of Maraudon that leads to the centaur holy tomb.
- **Water Breathing** (魔息术): the underwater-breathing spell Jace is about to study; per the established rendering from Chapter 317.
- **Senluola** (森萝拉): the copyist who transcribed Galders's incantation booklet; not in glossary.
- **Galders** (加尔德斯): the warlock whose recorded spells are in the booklet; per glossary.
- **Sazeno** (撒厄诺): Jace's imp; per glossary.
- **Mossa** (莫萨): the tauren druid traveling with the group; not in glossary.
- **Wynne** (温蕾萨): the Windrunner ranger; per established rendering in Chapters 522–525.
