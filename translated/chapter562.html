Chapter 562: Earth-Song Falls

Water Breathing — the name said it all. It was breathing in the demonic fashion.

Of course, Jace had no idea whether demons actually breathed like this. Judging from Morfell, demons didn't seem to breathe at all.

But whatever it was called, it was an incantation that let the caster breathe underwater. And this was the version Galders had already refined, cross-referencing the demonic-language materials Jace had given them — it ought to be far more reliable than the version they'd heard secondhand from orcs or demons and scribbled into Galders's spell-collection book on a whim.

Out at sea, Jace had kept his mind on the incantations he'd copied from Teron Gorefiend's memories, eager to puzzle out the last few lines.

Well, now he knew the next line was Death Coil. He hadn't had the chance to practice and test it yet, and there weren't many lines left to figure out anyway — probably just some of Teron Gorefiend's ramblings with no practical use.

After arriving in Kalimdor, one crisis after another had kept him from giving Galders's spell booklet a second look either. But now Jace realized they'd reached the very depths of Maraudon. If he didn't learn it soon, something terrible could happen.

This sanctuary's lowest level had a number of lightless underground lakes, and those lakes were home to all manner of horrible monsters. The water feeding those lakes was a network of underground rivers — rivers just like the stream they'd been following down.

So many underground rivers converging into the deepest lake, and that lake never flooding Maraudon to the brim — there had to be an outlet to the outside.

The far west of Desolace was a coastline. Perhaps because it was near the sea, nourished by the life energy and waters of the ocean, it at least looked far more normal than Desolace's interior — broad stretches of grass and sparse woods on the rocky shore. These underground lakes were very likely connected to the sea.

And that was their way out.

So Jace had to find a way to learn Water Breathing as quickly as possible, to help everyone find a path through the deep water — and maybe fight enemies there too.

Jace was already quite familiar with the hand gestures for Water Breathing as summarized by Galders, since he'd used the casting motions before.

He looked at the demonic characters Senluola had written down and read them softly, thinking back to how Master Marin had taught him to channel magic into himself when casting Arcane Intellect.

Grede glanced over, watching Jace mutter the same thing over and over. He chewed on a dried mushroom and asked, "What are you doing?"

"Farha, henkinteyeth…" Jace murmured, then looked up at Grede. "Actually, Grede — come here with me."

Grede stood up blankly. The two of them pushed through the vines at the cave mouth and came out to the poolside outside. Jace scanned the broad open space, the mist-wreathed stone walls in the distance, the vast sheet of water.

Jace splashed water on his face to shake off the exhaustion and weariness of the journey, then plunged his head into the pool.

Breathing on purpose, underwater… Jace had a bizarre feeling like he was trying to kill himself. But he pushed past the psychological hurdle and tried to take a gasp.

He'd expected water to flood straight into his nose and chest and drown him, but the sensation was more like the shadow magic twisting the water into breathable air.

So did it work on alchemical principles? Transmutation — elemental water into elemental air?

After a few breaths, Jace lifted his head and tried breathing normally for a while.

Staring at the silent cliff face ahead, at the thick vines and bushes on the wall, there was no time left to ponder how this spell actually worked.

Jace looked at Grede. "I'm going to cast a spell on you… then dunk your head in the water and try to breathe."

"Breathe underwater?" Grede said. "You actually know a weird magic like that? I've only heard of a potion that does that."

"Try it and you'll see," Jace said.

"You just learned it?"

"Just now."

"Damn it…" Grede muttered and came to the water's edge. "Whatever. Go ahead."

"Farha, henkinteyeth!" Jace intoned the incantation and cast it at Grede.

"I think I can taste something strange — shadow magic." Grede frowned and touched his nose. "I dunk my head in now?"

"Now," Jace said.

The dwarf crouched by the pool and buried his head in the water. A moment later a burst of bubbles rose. He gurgled, then lifted his head. "It works… it actually works!"

Just getting the dwarf sorted wasn't enough. To be sure nothing went wrong later, Jace had to make sure he could cast it successfully on everyone else. He called Wynne and Mossa over and cast it on one after the other — the effect was good on both.

Wynne wasn't surprised by this sort of magic in the slightest. In Quel'Thalas, all kinds of creative uses of spells were commonplace. What worried her was the possibility of having to dive into the water to fight.

After all, you couldn't shoot arrows underwater, and your movements would be badly distorted.

Mossa had been quite resistant to shadow magic, dark energy, demonic power, and the like. When Jace cast Water Breathing on her and she felt that gloomy magic, she wasn't too happy either.

But after taking a few gulps of water and lifting her head, shaking the water from her horns and fur, Mossa's expression made it clear she'd fallen in love with the feeling.

Though shouldn't a druid be able to turn into a seal or a sea lion and swim through the water with ease? This tauren hunter-half-breed druid probably couldn't manage that.

After trying Water Breathing on every member of the group who needed to breathe, Jace felt a touch of fatigue. Between helping Grede fight off the satyr assault earlier and going head-to-head with Viletan… he could feel his mana was now severely depleted. The two or three hours in between hadn't been enough for the Frostflow Crystal to replenish all of his mana.

"Any mana potions left?" Jace asked.

"The one you gave me earlier — there's still one bottle left, Jace." The dwarf handed over a vial. The ranger patted her pocket. "I've got some here too."

"Wynne, give me one," Jace said. "Grede, save that last bottle for Mossa. Let's divide up the Nature Resistance Potions and the Invisibility Potions, seal up our luggage, and move out."

The group followed the sound of the waterfall forward. It was remarkably quiet here — apart from the water and the ethereal wind sighing from high above, there was almost nothing to hear.

Grede kept craning his neck toward the faint light up high, scanning the cave walls around them as if he meant to climb straight out of here.

After walking along the pool for a while, the sound of the waterfall grew louder. That was when Jace noticed something crouching at the edge of the pool ahead.

The others spotted it too. Everyone fixed their eyes on it, only relaxing slightly once they'd crept closer.

It was a giant blue-green claw turtle, about the size of a farm-bred breeding boar. Its shell looked like an enormous boulder. But it was lying there, its body rising and falling with the rhythm of its breathing, which had made them think it was another stone elemental lying in wait to ambush them.

The claw turtle was gnawing on the plants at the water's edge, completely unguarded against the intruders behind it. It paid them no mind at all, as if they didn't exist.

In the game, spotting a claw turtle like this meant you were close to Zaltar's Tomb — the grave Princess Therazane guarded.
