## Chapter 565: The Goddess's Invitation

Theradras shifted down a step, steadied herself by bracing one arm against the cave wall, then released another arm to grab a jutting ledge beside her. She let go of the other two arms bracing against the far wall and scrambled straight onto one of the cliff faces.

"Scrambled" wasn't quite the word — she slammed into it. The impact made the sinkhole shudder once more.

Jace noticed that the moment Theradras's hand left that wall, the wildly growing plants withered rapidly, snapped-off branches and shattered leaves raining down.

The plants didn't wither entirely — they were still far more lush than when Jace had first entered. But the moment they moved a little closer to the elemental princess, the reaction was intense.

None of them dared to make the slightest move. As Theradras descended to this height, Vereesa was already half-covered by the crazily growing vines beside her. Jace's legs were completely tangled up too.

Mosa and Grede on the other side weren't faring any better — a bush beside them had already buried both them and the nearby boulder. Though was that perhaps a good thing? Better concealment, at least.

But did Theradras actually rely on her eyes to see? She didn't have physical eyes at all.

After pressing against the cliff wall, Theradras seemed to stop moving. A silence that set everyone on edge blanketed the whole space.

Jace shifted his eyes toward the bottom of the bushes opposite. Grede raised his eyes, then glanced toward the tunnel behind Jace.

The group exchanged looks. The dwarf slowly lowered his body, shook off the bushes surrounding him, and crept toward the cave mouth under cover of the dense grass. Mosa followed him low, stalking. They made it all the way to the two of them without drawing the slightest reaction from above.

Once the dwarf and the tauren had moved up a bit, Jace and Vereesa tore free of the plants binding them and followed.

Besides making one feel life's fervor and unyielding tenacity, the power radiating from Theradras also carried chaos, disorder, and madness.

Under the influence of that twisted surge of life, the surrounding weeds and shrubs stretched upward in a frenzy — but they were too far from Theradras up high to draw on the bloated power that the plants around her absorbed. So they were like chicks straining their necks to cry out and getting nothing for it — stretching straight toward the top, rubbing and crushing each other with faint rustling sounds.

But it was precisely these raised plants and their subtle noise that allowed Jace's group to hide themselves.

Yet just about when they'd moved close enough to dash into the cave, a mass of stone slowly descended from above and blocked the entire cave mouth.

Grede, at the front, looked up at the boulder that had dropped from God-knew-where for no clear reason.

High up on the stone, overgrown with weeds and tree roots, two slabs of rock shifted apart, revealing a faint wisp of pale-yellow breath of life within.

A mountain giant!

Before it even opened its eyes, it had simply squatted beside the cliff wall — it was practically part of the wall itself, impossible to make out!

Then, the other several boulders in the sinkhole began to shift as well, stretching, straightening up, their eyes glowing from between the cracks in the rock as they looked down at Jace's group, now trapped.

Grede had already gripped the stone axe on his back. Vereesa kept her eyes fixed on Theradras above. Mosa watched the movements of the mountain giants around them in turn… but Jace knew that in this situation, fighting head-on would be nothing short of suicide.

"D…tu…ta…dahn…thus…"

The mountain giant before them spoke words in a language none of them could understand. The mountain giants above echoed something too, as if they were conversing.

"Bah…du…dah…tu…"

Then a different voice spoke.

High above, Theradras opened her mouth, and an even more surging breath of life billowed outward from that giant maw.

She spoke a few words, then fell silent. Jace guessed this was the Kalimdor tongue — the language the elementals used to communicate among themselves, the same root as the language Grede used when calling down lightning.

"Outsiders." Now Theradras was actually speaking Common.

Her voice was like the grinding of boulders, mingled with the undertone of a grown woman whispering.

"Forever, guard, Terramok, soul, Zaetar, stay, companion, endless… life, blessing, mother."

Even though Theradras was forcing out what she wanted to say one broken word at a time, Jace and the others understood her meaning at once.

This was also why the mountain giants hadn't launched an attack right away.

Theradras wanted more people to come here, to help her guard Zaetar's soul. In return, as thanks, she would… that last line — could the Stone Princess be saying eternal life?

If they lived here, nurtured by that terrifying power of life, Jace had no doubt Theradras had the means to keep them alive for a grotesquely long time.

Jace thought — perhaps the beasts living here had all accepted Theradras's invitation to become guardians. The snapjaws, the diemetradons, the giant hydras…

Even the satyrs.

But to Theradras, they — Vyletongue included — were no different from pets.

After all, staying here was pure imprisonment. No different from a life sentence without parole. Jace could never accept it. If he were kept here, what would happen to his house in Stormwind? What about his parents?

Grede, who couldn't stand the slightest restraint, would absolutely never accept it. Vereesa had already lived long enough, and she had so many responsibilities in Quel'Thalas. And Mosa loathed the hideous monster before her — how could she possibly accept such an exchange?

Jace just took one look at them and could tell they hadn't given Theradras's proposal any serious thought at all. Like him, they were busy figuring out a way to slip out of this place…

"Sorry," Grede called up. "Ma'am — no, Your Highness! I should say — we mean no harm. We came here only to explore, foolishly intruding on Your Highness's domain…"

The dwarf's voice echoed through the sinkhole. The many mountain giants stared at him, giving no response whatsoever.

"We don't wish to harm the soul of the demigod Zaetar, Your Highness," Jace said at once. "We don't wish to disturb the peace here."

"That hydra… was only a counterattack made in an emergency," Vereesa added immediately.

Grede glanced at Vereesa, then smiled. "Your Highness, we'll leave here at once and guarantee we won't cause any further disturbance."

Theradras spoke slowly, uttering a single word. "Dead."
