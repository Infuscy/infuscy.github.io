Chapter 567: A Legendary Hunter's Great Dream

The group drifted, clinging to one another, for God knows how long. They were submerged who knows how many times before finally reaching a spacious chamber with exposed ground they could actually stand on.

The moment they saw solid ground, without a single word between them, they all swam toward it with everything they had. Then they collapsed onto it, lying in exhausted silence for a long time before they had the strength to crawl up and peel off their soaked clothes to spread them somewhere dry.

Grede stripped off all his clothes crudely and tossed them aside. The tauren spun around in the distance, flinging water off her body. Wynne laid her outer robe and cloak to one side, leaving only a thin blue Quel'Thalas-style undergarment with faint gold trim. She crouched in the corner of the stone wall, sorting through her pack.

The ranger's figure was slender and lithe, yet her arms and legs bore the lines of lean muscle — as flawless as a work of art.

Jace watched her damp silver hair spill over her shoulders and felt as though this couldn't possibly be something that existed in reality.

He drew a deep breath and forcibly turned his head away, telling himself not to think too much and make things awkward — but his heart still raced.

Yet the moment he shifted his attention to Grede, who was stroking his beard up ahead, Wynne behind him let out a soft sigh.

"What's wrong?" he turned back and asked.

"The food." Wynne tossed a waterlogged pack in front of her. "The food we brought from the tauren village… it's all pretty much ruined. Either waterlogged or gone somewhere."

The elf's long eyebrows, wet with water, drooped down past her cheeks. Combined with her helpless expression, she looked exaggeratedly dejected — even a little comical.

Jace looked at her and barely stifled a laugh. Of course that didn't escape Wynne's eyes. She noticed something was off about her own appearance, forcefully wiped her eyebrows, flicked the water away to restore them to their proper shape, and looked off to the side.

Jace pulled a small bundle from the robes spread out nearby, untied it, and took out a strip of dried meat, handing it to the ranger. "Here. I'm not hungry yet. You eat first."

"Where did you get this dried meat?" Wynne took it, visibly surprised that Jace still had surviving food on him.

"Bought it in Gadgetzan." Jace lay back. "Emergency rations — that's what they're for."

"Thank you, Jace." Wynne smiled and immediately took a bite. Then she noticed, at the bottom of the cave on the other side, a demon with a pair of fel horns staring motionlessly at her with burning arcane eyes — and her smile vanished.

"Master, I've completely exhausted my strength," the succubus said softly from the shadows. "You know how much magic it takes to leap that high, not to mention all that invisibility earlier… even if invisibility doesn't cost much, using it too frequently takes its toll."

Jace asked, "Is there any other way for you to recover mana? Look at me — do I look like I have mana to spare for you?"

Morfell licked her lips and leaned closer. "Of course I want to be considerate of my master, but only your mana is the most special… the other methods don't interest me anymore."

"Then we'll talk about it when we get out," Jace said.

"With that crystal around, I'm sure I'll recover quickly," Morfell said, her voice growing softer and her tone more intimate. Beside them, the ranger silently gnawed at her dried meat, biting harder and harder.

The dwarf lying to the side turned his head and glanced at the elf and the demon, then looked back up at the pitch-black ceiling of the cave and cleared his throat. "Hey, did you remember to save any emergency rations for your good buddy, Jace?"

"That was the last piece," Jace told Grede. "You should've said something earlier."

"Hah, even if I'd told you yesterday you wouldn't have given me any…"

As the dwarf spoke, Wynne, chewing her dried meat, glanced sideways at him.

With that, Grede's stomach growled loud and clear.

Musa pulled a spindle-shaped yellow fruit from the waterskin she'd tossed aside. She tore off a section of the rough peel, revealing bright red flesh, and handed it over. Grede took it with a smile, thanked her, and bit off half in one go. He handed the rest back to the tauren.

Musa shook her head, indicating she wasn't hungry. Grede happily ate the rest, red juice all over his face. He licked his lips back and forth with his tongue and even sucked on each finger several times — clearly it was pretty tasty.

One little fruit certainly wasn't enough to satisfy Grede's appetite, but after eating it, he still lay down contentedly on the wet rock and let out a long, satisfied sigh. After all, this was no time to expect more.

He closed his eyes for a moment, then suddenly opened them. "Do you all remember what that princess said back there?"

"Of course," Wynne said.

"I don't think I'll be able to forget what she said for a long time," Jace said from the ground.

"She mentioned a word," Grede said. "Taramurk. Does that ring a bell?"

"It does," Wynne said.

"Taramurk," Musa repeated the word.

"You know that name?" Grede shifted his head to look at Musa.

Musa patted the stone wall beside her, then waved her hand. "Here. Taramurk. Everything. All of it."

"This place is Taramurk?" Grede sat up in surprise.

"What's special about that name?" Jace asked.

"Hah." Grede leaned against the wall. "Have you ever heard of the legendary hunter Nessingway?"

Jace looked at Grede and thought for a moment. Of course he knew Hemet Nessingway — after all, that was the most famous hunter crew in the entire game, hunting from Stranglethorn Vale to Nagrand, then slaughtering their way up to Northrend, and even more dangerous places…

But he figured that if he said any more, there'd be no explaining how he'd learned it from library books. So he simply shook his head.

Grede leaned a little closer. "There's something you don't know? Well, that's only natural. Nessingway is a dwarf — you mages probably wouldn't be interested in a hunter like that. But among our people, his stories are still pretty widespread. Especially my father — he told me a lot of his tales. Apparently his life's dream is to hunt the most powerful prey in the world."

"Deathwing?" Jace asked offhandedly.

Grede frowned. "You're really fucking boring, Jace Sesso."

"All right, all right, go on." Jace sat up too, leaning against a stone pillar nearby, chin in hand.

The dwarf scratched his beard, glanced warlock at the young warlock, then continued: "I heard a story from my father, about the Lorgrim Deeper of the Taramurk mountains…"

Hearing the name Lorgrim Deeper, Jace jolted as the memories came flooding back.

"Yes, that's the name."
