Chapter 569: The Coast of Desolace

Grede roared at the imp: "How the hell is anyone supposed to remember everything?! You cowardly little thing have done nothing the whole way but hide inside the bag playing dead, of course you remember it all!"

"Ouch… ouch, I won't say anything!" The imp shrank its head back into the bag.

"Well then, Jace, are you going?" Wynne asked. "Call it a favor to me. I really want to catch up to this target."

"Actually, I've been worried you wouldn't want to go," Jace said with a smile. "After all, I was the one who wanted to find Darzha Mora herself in the first place, Wynne."

"Wonderful." Wynne smiled. "I knew you'd definitely go."

Jace said, "If we can find her minion, we're one step closer to finding her herself."

Morfell said, "Using a summoned creature to find the summoner is work us succubae used to do all the time, Master. As long as the summoner is still alive, finding her won't be difficult."

Wynne nodded at the demon, then turned her gaze to Grede.

"Ah, who says I've got nothing better to do all day? Fine, let's go!" Grede said. "So where exactly is this place?"

"Feralas," Jace said. "The place we should have gone to begin with. If the Thousand Needles hadn't been occupied by the centaur tribes, we would have come here through Feralas — it would have been much closer than crossing the entire grassland."

"I know it's in that jungle, Jace," Grede said. "But that jungle isn't some little oasis — even on the goblin's map it looks enormous. How are we supposed to find anything there?"

At that, Grede's words reminded Jace of the night elf-held island off the western coast of Feralas — Feathermoon Stronghold.

He wondered how hard it would be to enter Feralas from the south of Desolace through the mountains while avoiding the night elf patrols. Even the satyrs hadn't managed it — what were the odds of their little team sneaking through?

"Nobody knows the exact location, but we have a general idea, Grede," Wynne said. "I don't think a city can hide that easily. After all, the scholars of the Explorers' League have already seen the walls of Eldre'Thalas before."

"What about Musa?" Jace asked. "What does she think?"

Before Wynne could explain to Musa, the tauren thumped her chest directly. "Eldre'Thalas. I fight more demons."

"Ha! If you really get promoted to Ranger-General someday, Wynne, don't forget Musa's contribution!" Grede said, punching the tauren in the waist. Taking the blow, Musa didn't budge an inch — she even chuckled.

Wynne backed up a step as she looked at Jace. "I certainly won't forget — and that includes you two."

"We're basically mercenaries here for the money," Jace said. "But Musa asks for nothing, helping us wholeheartedly."

"She asks for nothing?" Wynne raised an eyebrow and glanced sideways at the dwarf. Grede cleared his throat loudly. "Anyway — we've reached a consensus. Our next goal is that ancient city."

His words echoed through the deep cave, accompanied by the rushing of water beside them. Only then did everyone snap out of their plans for the near future and realize they hadn't even gotten out of Maraudon yet.

Walking all the way from the pool beneath Zaelta's Tomb to here, Jace could sense the hostile, surging life energy around them rapidly weakening.

It reminded him of entering Desolace from Stonetalon Mountains — watching the vegetation grow sparse, bare boulders and bleak earth replacing forests and shrubs, green peaks turning into gray and brown dirt slopes.

It was the same now. The algae or whatever unidentifiable plants clinging to the cave walls grew fewer and fewer. Only beneath the riverbed did patches of water grass remain. The water carried life energy out from the depths of Zaelta's Tomb, and it hadn't yet been completely swallowed by the already-dead Desolace.

But Jace's mindset had shifted too. Before, the vanishing life meant growing danger. Now, the waning life meant they were getting farther and farther from the horrors within Maraudon — a small comfort in the boundless darkness.

After walking for God knew how long, Jace was already exhausted and hungry enough that his stomach was touching his back. But since he had no sense of time, he didn't know if he should sleep or if he should eat something.

The others kept moving forward as if they weren't particularly tired. So he didn't want to show weakness and dent his companions' morale — he could only tough it out and keep going.

Then, after an unknowable stretch of time, a chill breeze blew from ahead, jolting Jace out of his numbness.

"Wind!" Grede reached out. "Are we at the exit?!"

Wynne immediately ran ahead. Jace, Grede, and Musa also broke into a sprint.

The clatter of the group's footsteps echoed through the cavern. The sound, which had reverberated everywhere at first, grew quieter and fainter… until a faint silver light appeared up ahead, too dim to make out clearly. Jace even wondered if he was so desperate to leave that he was hallucinating.

Wynne squeezed through a crack in the rocks up ahead. Jace followed behind her, pushing aside layer after layer of tree roots and dirt that reek of salt and brine. After a sudden lurch, nothing was in front of him. He swayed two steps forward and nearly fell into the water. Steadying himself, he saw before him a vast, empty coastline bathed in moonlight.

Behind him was a dense forest. Further off, cliffs and peaks stacked upon one another. In the night, they actually looked like several colossal mountain giants huddled together in various poses. Staring at them long enough, he even began to worry they might suddenly stretch their limbs and start speaking.

If he hadn't just come out of there, who would have guessed that an elementalist lord who'd been slumbering for a thousand years lived beneath this place?

"At any rate, we survived, Jace." Grede looked out at the sea, hand on his waist.

Jace nodded. Wynne had already sprinted to a distant spot on the beach.

"What's she doing?"

Grede shook his head. The tauren also watched blankly as the elf's figure grew smaller and smaller.

Before long, Wynne drew an arrow. Arcane light ignited at the arrowhead, reflecting against the twin moons over the dark coastline — even appearing somewhat mournful in its beauty.

Witnessing a scene he might never see again in his life, Jace almost didn't want to find out why she'd drawn her bow.

The arrow released with a whizzing sound, tracing an almost flat trajectory in a faint purple line before vanishing into the sea. The elf set off again, chasing the point where the arrow had splashed down — then dove into the water with a splash.

After a moment of silence, she suddenly burst from the surface, raising above her head a crab that looked bigger than a lamb.

The arrow still stuck in the crab's body, and the arcane light on it hadn't fully faded — illuminating the shell with a shimmer of water, irresistibly tempting.

"Jace!" the elf shouted from the distance. "Come here!"

Jace and Grede exchanged a glance, swallowed hard in unison, and charged toward her.

Translator Notes

Flags

- Feathermoon Stronghold (羽月要塞): the night elf outpost on the western coast of Feralas. - Explorers' League (探险家协会): the dwarven scholarly organization (here the text says 魔导师团 / arcanists, but the reference is to explorers who found Eldre'Thalas). - Darzha Mora (达斯莫拉·暗眼): the warlock they are hunting. - Eldre'Thalas (埃雷萨拉斯): the ancient high elf city (Dire Maul). - Sazeno (撒厄诺): the imp. - Morfell (莫洛菲尔): the succubus. - Stonetalon Mountains (石爪山): the mountain zone between Desolace and the Barrens. - twin moons (双月): Azeroth's two moons. - Quel'dorei (奎尔多雷): high elves. - Musa (莫萨): the tauren companion. - Wynne (温蕾萨): the high elf ranger.
