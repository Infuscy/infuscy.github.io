## Chapter 570: Emissary of the Forest

After eating their fill and drinking in the woods by the beach and resting for the night, the group set out again the next morning.

This place wasn't as utterly dead as the deep reaches of Desolace. Actually, it reminded Jace of the coast of Dun Morogh — the stretch one passed sailing from Stormwind to Southshore in Hillsbrad. No people, desolate, but at least there was some normal green.

Jace guessed it was because this place was simply too close to Maraudon. Even though Theradras had drained all the life from Mashan'she, the power she herself preserved still influenced this stretch of mountains and coast ceaselessly, ultimately leaving behind an ecosystem that looked more or less normal.

Jace knew this place. He even remembered there was an isolated island and bay to the north, occupied by naga.

But in the game, he'd never considered why this place looked so much more normal than the interior of Desolace — it just had struck him as a bit off.

Having made a full circuit through Maraudon, truly feeling Theradras's terrifying power, and then seeing this landscape with his own eyes, he actually felt a sense of satisfaction, as if he'd found an answer.

According to the map, crossing a plain and entering the mountains to the south, then passing through a canyon, would bring them not far from Feralas.

But before that, they needed to head northeast, following the mountain foot all the way back to the Maraudine tribe's home — the Valley of Spears, the entrance to the Maraudon burial site — and deliver Vyletongue's head to the centaur in exchange for the reward they'd promised.

"We can't exactly tell the centaur about that crack in the rock behind Maraudon, can we?" Grede said as they walked. "If we do, the location info we sell to Nesingwary won't be worth a damn."

"Nesingwary probably doesn't speak centaur, does he?" Jace said.

"Don't be so optimistic," Grede said. "Nesingwary may not be a member of the Explorers' League, but he's a man who's traveled the whole world for the hunt and the challenge. I have no doubt he's set foot in Kalimdor. And one thing's certain — he definitely speaks the elven tongue. Just look at how the little elf in our party talks to the centaur, Jace."

Jace nodded. "Then if we don't tell the centaur, how do we explain how we got out?"

Grede said, "That's easy. We just say we have no idea what happened — the demon must have cast dark space magic. He probably meant to throw us into the Twisting Nether, but our luck was too damn good, and by some fluke we escaped from Maraudon instead. A blessing in disguise."

"Would the centaur believe that?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "I think the idea that a dangerous demon used strange magic to cause an inexplicable phenomenon satisfies them more than the notion that there's a back door under their old grandmother's burrow. Besides, their brains aren't exactly sharp, are they?"

"Fair point. And no matter what, Vyletongue is already dead," Jace said. "Nobody's going to tell them the truth."

The two of them chatted as they walked. Vereesa was gazing toward a distant stretch of forest.

"What's over there?" Grede asked.

"I want to record this place," Vereesa said, looking at Jace. "Give me a blank scroll, Jace, and a pen and ink."

Jace took out a pen and scroll, then fished the ink out of his potion bag and handed it over. Vereesa walked toward the forest as she spoke: "I think this would be an excellent place to establish a ranger outpost. Abundant food, fresh water, one of the few spots in this dead land with green vegetation for cover, and the greatest danger won't leave her lair to make trouble abroad."

Vereesa was right. Later on, the Darkspear trolls would build a village here as the Horde's outpost in Desolace.

"Vereesa!"

Mosa called out, pointing to a small rocky slope up high. There was actually a white figure standing there, watching the ranger.

At the sight of the figure, Vereesa immediately grew wary. Grede and Jace both gripped their weapons. But the figure simply stood there, unmoving — not fleeing, not assuming a fighting stance, as if their actions were of no concern to it at all.

What made Jace's heart pound even more was that, aside from Mosa, none of them had noticed the fellow standing there in plain sight at first.

If Mosa hadn't pointed, their group might have left without ever realizing someone had been watching them.

Jace couldn't sense it and that was understandable — he was more attuned to shadow and fel, less sensitive to arcane, and his nose for beasts, humans, and the like was far worse than Vereesa's. He couldn't even match Grede.

But the two of them had no reaction either — that was truly strange.

At that moment, the white figure lowered its head slightly and shifted its body a little. Only then did Jace notice this didn't seem to be a normal humanoid creature — its humanoid upper body sat atop a four-legged lower half.

"Centaur?" Grede whispered.

"Not a centaur," Jace said.

This was a dryad — a daughter of Cenarius.

Mosa seemed to recognize the creature's form as well. She bowed her head slightly in greeting. The dryad looked at the tauren, then appraised the others in turn.

Then Jace felt it — a rather hazy, ethereal woman's voice drifting toward him like music.

"You carry the scent of demons upon you, travelers. Tell me your purpose here, or we will not let you leave easily."

She spoke Common, and quite standard Common at that. But she'd pointed out the demonic aura right off the bat…

Fortunately, to avoid being spotted by centaur patrols from a distance, Jace had already ordered Morophil to move ahead invisibly. Otherwise, coming face to face with this dryad would have meant no knowing what might happen.

Jace said immediately, "We've just escaped from the depths of the corrupted Terramok, daughter of Cenarius. There are many demons inside."

Vereesa pulled the satyr's head, wrapped in a magic cloth, from her pack and held it up by its long curved horns. "Perhaps it's because of this. The demon Vyletongue had been lurking in the depths of Maraudon for many years. We were entrusted by the centaur to go in and slay the demon, and only with great difficulty did we get out."

"It's not that simple." The snow-white dryad tilted her head, looking toward Jace. "The demonic aura on you is far stronger than this head, human."

As she spoke, more dryads — verdant from head to toe — appeared behind her. By now six or seven had poked their heads out, with perhaps even more still hidden.
