## Chapter 581: The Sentinel Commander

Shandris Feathermoon's appearance was much like that of any other night elf sentinel. Jace, who knew almost nothing about night elves, could barely tell them apart, sensing only a faintly different air about her.

Her skin was on the darker side, with two deep-blue facial markings running beneath her eyes. Judging by her bearing and the muscles visible on her bare arms, she was unquestionably a seasoned and powerful warrior — yet her expression was gentle, approachable.

Compared to those great heroes who could command the elements with magic, slay demon lords with their own hands, and face down entire armies without flinching — heroes who could rewrite history through their strength alone — she seemed, on the surface, nothing particularly remarkable.

And yet she was genuinely an ancient elder who had lived for ten thousand years.

During the War of the Ancients she had been a young elf maiden whose experience and combat skill might not have matched even Vereesa's today, but now she was one of the very few beings left in this world who had witnessed the Sundering with her own eyes.

Compared to her, even the Sunstrider King Anasterian — whose lifespan was already beyond human imagination — was merely a junior who had appeared who-knows-how-many years later.

At first she seemed slightly wary of Jace's dark power, then her attention shifted to the dwarf and the tauren beside him, but soon enough she focused entirely on Vereesa.

"Vereesa Windrunner, Ranger-Captain of the Farstriders of Quel'Thalas." Vereesa worked free of her bindings with a sentinel's help. "It's an honor to meet you, General Shandris Feathermoon."

"The Sentinel Corps has long received reports of descendants of Dath'Remar Sunstrider's followers," Shandris said. "But I have never actually spoken with a true Quel'dorei, so I am equally glad you have come here, Ranger-Captain Vereesa Windrunner."

After that, the sentinel general offered a greeting in Elvish. Vereesa was a little surprised, but returned the same phrase in kind.

Shandris said something to the sentinels around her, then turned to the now-unbound Jace and the others. "Please, come inside and speak, guests. I have many questions — about Celebras, and about Maraudon."

Jace exchanged a glance with Grede, then looked at Vereesa. She still seemed caught up in the nervousness of speaking with distant kin from another world for the first time. She quickly straightened her clothes and cloak and fell in step ahead with the night elves.

They entered the wooden house at the very heart of Feathermoon Stronghold. Stepping onto the aged but still-sturdy stairs, running his hand along the pillars built from ancient timber that might have been thousands or even tens of thousands of years old, Jace felt as though one careless move on his part might damage a priceless antique.

Calling it a "wooden house" was almost misleading — it was more like a massive pavilion, at least on the ground floor, which was entirely open. The upper level, reached by stairs, appeared considerably more enclosed.

The interior furnishings matched the exterior in their utter simplicity and directness — no excess decoration, nothing whose sole purpose was to please the eye.

Vereesa standing in the middle looked distinctly out of place. The leaf patterns embroidered on her cloak and outer robe were probably more elaborate than every decorative motif in the whole building combined, and as a Farstrider ranger she was already dressed more simply than any mage or noble by an order of magnitude.

Vereesa explained to Shandris the purpose of their visit, including the whole process of entering Maraudon to investigate demons and then escaping — while naturally leaving out a great many details concerning Jace's demonic servants.

When she finished, Shandris Feathermoon let out a long sigh. "The children of Cenarius have long wished to enter the centaur-guarded sacred tomb to seek traces of his eldest son, Zaetar, and they hoped for our help. But for ages we have respected the High Priestess's counsel and had no contact or involvement with the centaur. Though the forest guardians have shown full understanding of this, I have always felt a measure of personal regret. So in helping Celebras to some degree, I thank you as well."

Now Jace roughly understood why Mosa had been largely silent and in poor spirits ever since her capture.

Of course, being captured by a band of sentinels was hardly cause for cheer, but compared to Grede, Vereesa, and himself, Mosa seemed inexplicably cold toward these night elves.

Ten thousand years ago, night elves and tauren had been allies against the Burning Legion, but millennia had passed, and under the crush of the suddenly risen centaur the tauren had been nearly driven to extinction — yet the night elves had never truly extended a hand.

Of course, this was not because the night elves truly disregarded their former allies.

Their numbers were already scarce, and with a great concentration of night elf elites mustered in the Silithus desert to the south guarding the Scarab Wall, committing to another racial war spanning half of Kalimdor with no end in sight would have stretched the night elves impossibly thin.

Then again... Silithus.

Jace shouldn't have forgotten that coming to Kalimdor carried another rather important task: finding a Qiraji silithid for Ursula.

Silithus was a vast desert on the southwestern edge of Kalimdor. Compared to the Tanaris desert where Gadgetzan stood, Silithus's living conditions were orders of magnitude harsher; aside from the Qiraji lurking in the deepest reaches of the desert, almost no other living things existed there.

A thousand years ago the Qiraji had suddenly swarmed forth, and to protect Kalimdor from being consumed by their armies, the night elves and the bronze dragons together had paid a tremendous price to halt them, erecting the Scarab Wall to seal the Ahn'Qiraj hive.

But clearly they had also known that this seal would one day fail, and so they built Cenarion Hold, which still stood guard over it to this day.

At this moment, there was probably no group in the entire world that understood the Qiraji better than the night elves.

Just as Jace was considering whether to ask General Shandris Feathermoon about the insects, Vereesa brought up Dathmora Darkeye.

"Dathmora." Shandris repeated the name softly. "I know that name, but I'm afraid I cannot offer you much information, friends."

"Any scrap you can share, lady elf," Grede said eagerly.

Shandris glanced at the dwarf. She did not take offense the way Vereesa did when someone called her "elf." "For a long time now we have been tracking the Kaldorei who fled Eldre'Thalas — especially members of the Shen'dralar."

"Shen'dralar?" Grede asked. "What's that?"
