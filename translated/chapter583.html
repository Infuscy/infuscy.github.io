Chapter 583: Nothing Is Eternal

After eating his fill, Grede leaned back in his chair and asked, "I've always been curious, Jace. That Rexxar — the half-orc we met earlier — is his father an ogre, or his mother?"

"They're a tribe," Jace explained. "A mixed-blood group of orcs and ogres. He's one member of that tribe."

"That fellow really is something, Jace," Grede said. "I look forward to meeting him again and having a friendly bout to see who's the better man. His axe-throwing isn't a whit worse than mine!"

"Provided that bear of his doesn't interfere, damn it," he added.

Jace let out a belch. "Just don't make excuses when you get knocked flat, that's all."

"As if!" Grede said. "I'm a Wildhammer warrior — I'd never stoop to shamelessness! Though this Wildhammer warrior is getting a bit sleepy now. D'ye think they'd make us sleep in the street?"

"Surely not..." Jace glanced outside.

Of course not. That night the sentinels led Jace's group to the upper floor of another large wooden building. There were no rooms or corridors to speak of — the broad hall was lined with neatly arranged wooden beds.

But to call it some rough barracks would be wide of the mark. Each bed came with a dressing table and a chair, and even an elegant wardrobe in an antique style. On the wardrobes appeared — rarely for these quarters — a hint of vine-shaped ornamentation, and the dressing tables even held mirrors made of some material Jace couldn't identify.

Perhaps this was simply the night elves' way of living.

Grede flopped onto his bed and immediately began to snore. Mosa lay down on the bed beside him. Though the solid wooden bed didn't collapse outright and its length could just barely accommodate her hooves, the tauren tossed and turned for a while without finding comfort, and finally rolled onto the floor and fell asleep.

Jace stole a glance in the mirror. Not having seen his own reflection in some time, he now sported a face full of curly short beard and stubble, hair tangled like weeds, a face covered in all manner of grime, plus the bandaging on his shoulders. The whole picture was of a survivor who had finally crawled out of a trench after god-knows-how many months.

He couldn't help but let out a chuckle. Vereesa, who was standing by the terrace railing, glanced over.

Jace wiped the grime from his face and looked at her. "Do I look like a lunatic?"

"Like a Farstrider just rescued from a troll village," Vereesa said with a smile.

"Fair enough." Jace cleared his throat. "Aren't you sleeping?"

"I've had plenty of sleep these past two days. I'm not tired," Vereesa said. "And haven't you forgotten — I'm a Quel'dorei."

"But I don't think it's that simple, Miss Quel'dorei. I get the feeling something's weighing on you." Jace walked over and leaned against the doorframe.

Actually, Jace had more or less guessed what Vereesa was thinking. When Shandris Feathermoon had mentioned the dilemma now facing the inhabitants of Eldre'Thalas, Vereesa had grown somewhat distracted.

"All right. I was thinking... if the Sunwell..."

She swallowed hard, her expression one of acute discomfort.

Just as Jace had suspected.

"Did the people of Eldre'Thalas remind you of your own homeland?"

"In Quel'Thalas this is not a topic that can be discussed," Vereesa said. "The addiction. Many Quel'dorei refuse to acknowledge its existence, but that is nothing more than self-comfort. Everyone knows that once the Sunwell's energy is exhausted or it is destroyed, for us it would be the end of the world."

"Before the Horde broke through our defenses, the vast majority of young Quel'dorei had never contemplated that possibility. The Ban'Thallow was impregnable, Silvermoon would never fall — after all, the Amani Empire had not shaken any of this for thousands of years. But the orcs... I don't know, Jace..."

Jace swept his gaze across the dark, oppressive forest and the grim towers looming in the night. "Even the Well of Eternity could not last forever, Vereesa."

Vereesa clearly hadn't expected Jace to say that. She audibly sucked in a breath, turning to look at him. There was a hint of reproach in her eyes, but far more a complicated emotion he couldn't begin to parse.

Jace said, "I only have a few decades to live. If I were you, I might not worry about the Sunwell. But you'll live a thousand years, and you want to become a Ranger Lord — maybe one day a Ranger-General. If even a Quel'dorei like you doesn't consider the safety of the Sunwell, then the Sunwell is certainly doomed sooner or later."

"But we are the Alliance — Lordaeron, Stormwind, and Quel'Thalas. Before I die of old age, if one day something happens to the Sunwell, I will be in Quel'Thalas. Provided, of course, you open the gate and let me in."

"We will!"

Vereesa said softly. She lowered her gaze toward the sea wreathed in mist, the faintly glowing arcane lights on the pier, her silver hair veiling her face in the wind.

"Grede will come help too," Jace said, leaning on the railing. "I'm speaking for him."

The elf asked through a suppressed laugh, "And will you charge for it?"

"If there's a fight, we certainly can't work for free," Jace said. "But I imagine by then I'll have bought a house, so I'll give you a friends' discount."

Vereesa finally let out a laugh and gave Jace a gentle shove. Jace smiled too, but his smile faded soon after.

He didn't know how the ranger imagined that moment, but right now his mind was filled with hideous skeletal warriors, armies of zombies, black knights astride death chargers, wailing ghouls covering the hills, abominations swinging blood-stained war-axes, plague mushrooms sprouting through corrupted forests...

And amid the massive undead host, a gray-haired prince holding a runeblade stared at him expressionless.

A decade or so from now, the Scourge would erupt, Lordaeron would fall, and King Terenas II would be impaled upon Frostmourne by his own son, Arthas Menethil, on the throne.

The entire kingdom would swiftly perish at the hands of this patricidal prince. After converting Lordaeron's subjects en masse into the undead Scourge, he would immediately march north to storm the Kingdom of Quel'Thalas.

He would kill Vereesa's sister, Ranger-General Sylvanas, and in a final indignity transform her into a banshee to enslave. After breaching the walls of Silvermoon, he would ruthlessly slay Sunstrider King Anasterian in single combat.

In the end, he would destroy the Sunwell and with it all hope of Quel'Thalas.

If this had been a few years ago, thinking of all this would only have made him want to get his parents out of Lordaeron as quickly as possible and hole up in the relatively safe Stormwind to wait for it all to be over.

But now, at this very moment, looking at Vereesa leaning on the railing beside him with that faint smile still on her face, he couldn't shake that thought from his mind no matter what.

— I have to stop all this.

"What are you thinking about?" Vereesa asked.

"Nothing." Jace shook himself back to reality and rubbed his face. "I'm probably getting sleepy. Doubt I've got the energy to keep you company here all night."

"It's all right," Vereesa said. "Go sleep. Thank you, Jace. I'd never get the chance to talk about these things with another Quel'dorei — not even Sylvanas... probably not, anyway."

Jace nodded and stepped inside. Vereesa suddenly spoke again. "By the way — how do you think I did today, facing that sentinel general?"

"A qualified ranger captain."

Jace gave a thumbs-up, turned, and flopped straight onto the bed. Vereesa smiled and pressed her lips together softly.
