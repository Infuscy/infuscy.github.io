## Chapter 584: Feathermoon Stronghold's Thanks

He woke early, before dawn had fully broken. Outside was utterly quiet.

Gray-white mist seeped into the dark forest. Unidentified birdsong echoed from deep within the shadows.

He stepped onto the terrace and looked down. Feathermoon Stronghold was nearly deserted; only a single sentinel stood alone before the wooden house where the sentinel general was quartered.

The sentinel looked up and noticed him, gave a slight nod, then turned her gaze back toward the distant pier.

Though Vereesa had said the night before that she didn't need sleep, she was slumbering peacefully over there now, calm as though she had set her worries to rest, no longer as lost as she'd been the previous night.

Jace looked at her and recalled the thoughts their conversation had stirred the night before, realizing that his mindset was already very different from what it had been.

He still remembered in Dalaran, when the realization struck him that Deathwing and Teron Gorefiend were about to descend, his first instinct had been to say what needed saying, step aside, and leave the danger to people far bigger than he was.

Even earlier, the first time he'd gone to Duskwood with Grede, facing Morgan Ladimore, he had weighed every word carefully before speaking.

Back then he had worried that one ill-considered move on his part might steer the entire world's course in an unknown direction.

Now, after so much had happened and so much time had passed, he seemed to care little about such things anymore — or more accurately, he had long since cast them from his mind.

After all, when making important decisions, he had never had the luxury of that much time and mental space to consider so many angles.

When crisis arrived, there were only two choices. Do it, or don't. Can't do it, and you can't. No time to fret over this or that.

But had he reached the point where he could afford not to care?

Not by a long shot.

Jace could give everything to protect Vereesa and Grede before him, to protect his mum and dad, and everyone he cherished, to keep them from harm. But as for anything larger...

He certainly had this or that notion in his mind, and he did indeed have a foreseeable future in his head, but to rely on these plus a surge of passion was still nowhere near enough.

His fingers tapped back and forth on the railing. Jace glanced back at Azuresong and Gorefiend's staff lying on the table nearby.

He knew that no matter what he intended for the future, right now he needed to keep his feet on the ground and build his strength step by step. Only then would change be possible.

And this way, even if everything ultimately had to come to pass, at least he would be able to do something — to stand by Vereesa's side and face it together, like he had told her, rather than stand by helplessly doing nothing, or hide in a corner of the world and let time slip by.

In the morning, Jace and the others were resting on the ground-floor hall. Outside was still quiet.

"She's coming, she's coming," Grede said, patting Jace.

Jace turned and saw Shandris Feathermoon approaching alone.

"General." Vereesa stepped to the hall entrance. "Might we know when we can leave Feathermoon Stronghold?"

"At any time, ranger captain," Shandris said. "But before you go, I have one request."

"Please." Vereesa nodded.

"Could we keep Vyletongue's head?" the general asked. "The satyr have been a blight upon the Kaldorei and all of Kalimdor for many years, friends. Confirming the death of a satyr leader would mean a great deal to us and to the rest of the Kaldorei. If his head were sent to Astranaar, it would greatly boost the morale of the Sentinel Corps while striking fear into the hearts of the satyr lurking in the shadows. That would matter greatly to us right now."

"We are mercenaries, General Shandris Feathermoon," Jace emphasized.

"Of course your efforts will not go unrewarded," Shandris said to Jace. "The Sentinel Corps here has a suit of armor dating from the War of the Shifting Sands, forged from the carapace of a Qiraji silithid."

She said something quietly to the male elf guard beside her. He took a box from an approaching sentinel and set it on the table, opening it.

Inside the box lay a suit of ornate emerald-green armor, its patterning quite beautiful. Jace knew that by the night elves' standards, they would never put so much unnecessary decoration on a suit of armor — this was likely the natural color of the insectoid's own carapace.

The night elf guard introduced it: "Long ago, many elite warriors who fought in the War of the Shifting Sands owned a suit like this, but a thousand years later many have been lost or stolen. Now the Qiraji lurk beneath the earth and rarely emerge; we can hardly encounter silithid leaders capable of providing such precious material anymore, so every suit that remains is a treasure worth keeping."

A silithid-carapace suit of armor? Jace couldn't immediately recall whether such a piece existed in the game.

The sentinel general said, "You helped the sentinels hunt down a satyr leader, and you also helped Celebras reestablish contact with the lost keeper Zaetar, who had been missing for years. This gift is the Kaldorei's thanks."

Seeing that the group seemed hesitant, she added, "You may consider whether to leave Vyletongue's head. If you have other uses for it, I can understand. The Sentinel Corps does not insist."

"If you wish to leave Feathermoon Stronghold, come find me at any time, guests."

With that, she gave the group a slight bow and left with her guard. Grede kept his hand on the edge of the box, clearly coveting the armor, yet he remained silent for a long while.

"I thought she'd give us money," he said, looking up. "So what do we do with this armor now?"

"Grede, you wear. You fight," Mosa said.

"I was thinking the same," Vereesa said. "This armor is too heavy for me."

"You don't like it?" Jace asked.

"I do like it... it's just that the moment I change form, any good clothes get ruined," Grede said, tugging at the loose leather vest he was wearing now. "Otherwise I'd have been wearing this kind of thing all along — cheap and practical. And if I forget to take it off before transforming, at least it won't be completely torn apart in one go; I can keep wearing it after I return to normal!"

He rubbed the tattered hem of his clothes. "All right, probably one more time forgetting and it really will be ruined."

Jace asked, "What do other Mountain Kings do with their armor when they awaken their Avatar?"

"Why do you think families like Anvilmar have heirloom enchanted armor and helmets? Of course they're made to adapt to their enlarged forms!" Grede scratched his neck. "I certainly can't afford a craftsman to make something like that, so I can only wear very loose clothes, or remember to strip before I grow."

With that, Jace recalled that indeed every time the dwarf transformed to fight, he was half-naked.

"This enchanted armor can adapt to a shapeshifter's form," came a night elf woman's voice from nearby.
