Chapter 606: Enslaving Demons

There wasn’t anything of value up on the second-floor platform. It looked like their only options were to gather whatever edible things were still left in their packs and eat them, or to head out to the edges of the garden to scavenge for food.

Here in Eldre’Thalas, the difference between night and day wasn’t all that obvious, but there was still a slight variation in light and dark — more noticeable out in the garden, perhaps from sunlight leaking in from somewhere.

After returning from the passage, Jace spread everything he and Vereesa had pulled out of Dasmora’s stomach, along with the satyr scrolls taken from Maraudon, across the ground.

“Sazeno, come look at these things.”

“Yes, master!”

The imp crawled out of the pack and squeezed under Jace’s arm, poking his head out. He glanced at the Dasmora scrolls and said, “Ered’ruin script… honestly, master, I can’t read a word of it. These barbarians’ language hardly has any proper written records — there’s nothing worth an outsider learning.”

“If I wanted to learn this language, would I actually have to capture a doomguard?” Jace asked.

Sazeno glanced at the other scrolls and stopped on the ritual circle. “You could try using this. Isn’t this perfect — a circle for communicating with other demons. I bet Dasmora wanted this ridiculous circle to contact Archimonde! Ha! Contacting a being as powerful as Archimonde would be difficult, I’ll grant you, but contacting a doomguard? That shouldn’t be a problem.”

“Stop wasting my time,” Jace said coldly. “If you can’t read it, just say so. Go sort through those satyr scrolls — separate the ones whose meaning you understand from the ones you don’t.”

“Yes, master…” the imp said dejectedly.

“Now that Dasmora’s soul shard is in my possession, that means she has no chance of returning to the Twisting Nether to be reborn.” Jace looked at the imp as he said it.

“Yes, master.” The imp nodded, holding up a leather scroll.

“Is the spell that summoned Dasmora completely useless now?” Jace asked. “Could I swap in the name of a different fixed demon — just change the name?”

“Not a chance, master.” The imp’s burning fel eyes went wide. “If it were that simple, any demon who knew the Demonic script for ‘Archimonde’ could just swap it in and summon Archimonde here? That’s absurd… Demons of different types, different power levels, different ranks, different states — the summoning incantations all differ.”

“But at its core, this is still a doomguard summoning spell. With some modifications, it might work for summoning other doomguards…”

“We’ll deal with that later. Is this demon-binding circle any use?” Jace asked, pointing at the elven anti-demon circle scroll the satyrs had prepared.

“It is,” the imp said. “It’s just that for a demon lord like Dasmora, this circle seems a touch overpowered.” Jace asked, “You’re saying the people who created this circle originally intended to weaken something even stronger than Dasmora?”

The imp explained, “Who knows what sort of monster these Kaldorei originally meant to bind. I hear that during the War of the Ancients, the Burning Legion’s vanguard was led by demon lords like Mannoroth the Destructor — never mind Archimonde the Defiler eventually descending in person…”

That sounded promising. If he ever had to face a demon lord like Azgoth, a circle like this could serve as a contingency — a trap held in reserve.

He scratched his chin, thinking of Mor’zul’s ritual to summon a great demon. This circle might prove useful when that time came. Then again, Jace didn’t want Mor’zul’s success to come too easily, so how exactly to use it would need more thought. Who was Mor’zul trying to summon, exactly? Another doomguard? Was this his own idea, or Dasmora’s demand? Could it be the Burning Legion emissary mentioned in the scrolls?

Jace glanced again at the orders for contacting the Burning Legion emissary and picked them up for a closer look. They were written entirely in Ered’ruin as well — completely illegible.

He was gradually realizing he’d hit another wall with the language barrier.

Whether it was the scrolls mentioning the necromancer or the ones about the Burning Legion emissary, they all contained large amounts of Ered’ruin. He wondered if the mages of Dalaran knew anything about this language. Perhaps he could ask Amy Malin to help dig up some relevant materials — after all, she’d joined the Kirin Tor now…

If Amy couldn’t help, he might have no choice but to try using that Demonic Figurine to summon a doomguard on his own. With the demon-binding circle and the demon-enslavement magic to assist him… In any case, this was something that had to be handled with extreme care. A doomguard was no ordinary demon.

As for other avenues, speaking with the Highborne of this city might yield more knowledge, but that Kaldorei mage had already fled who-knew-where.

The rest had either hidden themselves in the western part of the city or behind the gate on the far side of the garden. Even though the demons in this sealed underground palace had been cleared out, they refused to come out and see for themselves. It looked like they intended to barricade themselves in until the ogres broke down the door, or until a decade from now when the satyrs invaded and slaughtered them all.

The demon-summoning incantation had become somewhat moot with Dasmora’s death, but at least the demon-enslavement spell was something real and solid.

Using this time while Mosa recovered — since there was no leaving this room anyway — Jace could consolidate everything he’d learned during his time in Kalimdor.

“Hand me that demon-enslavement scroll, Sazeno,” Jace said, holding out his hand.

“Yes, master.” Sazeno dragged over the tattered satyr-written bark scroll and placed it in Jace’s hand.

“Does my emotional state affect this demon magic?” Jace asked the succubus some distance away.

Morophil answered candidly, “My understanding of how mortals manipulate their own emotions to influence their magic only became this clear after I came to know you, master. If you don’t try it, it’s hard to say for certain.”

“Then I’ll try.” Jace glanced at Mosa and Grede, who were sleeping soundly over there, told Vereesa to take the two demons to the second level, and headed into the passage.

Arriving at the empty platform he and Grede had searched before, he placed a small soul shard on the ground and said to the two demons watching from the side, “I’m going to summon an uncontrolled felhound. Keep watch for me — if my demon-enslavement spell fails, make sure you restrain it.”

“How am I supposed to restrain a felhound, master?” Morophil asked. “One wrong move and it’ll drain me dry. If the spellcasting doesn’t go smoothly, it’d be more merciful to just kill it outright.”
