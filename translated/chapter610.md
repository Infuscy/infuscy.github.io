## Chapter 610: Fishing

Vereesa wiped the raindrops from her face, looked at Jace, then at his hands. "Where's your fish?"

"Didn't catch any," Jace said.

Vereesa, with a hint of complaint in her voice: "Then why aren't you fishing properly? What are you walking back for? Where's the fishing rod and the worms you caught — did they fall in the water?"

Why was she in such a weird mood?

Jace glanced toward the camp behind her. The deep jungle revealed nothing.

"I heard a noise from over there, thought something happened at camp. The fishing rod's still over there… but what are you doing here? What was that noise?"

Vereesa frowned. "Don't go back. I'll go fishing with you."

"Where's the succubus?" Jace asked.

"Guarding the camp — isn't that your order? And she's well suited to help keep watch there right now. I'm not." Vereesa cleared her throat, put her hand on Jace's shoulder and turned him around to face the pond. "Go."

"So what exactly—"

Jace hadn't finished before Vereesa shoved him forward, making him stumble several steps, nearly tripping on a tree root. "You're still pretending you don't know?" Vereesa asked.

"Should I know?" Jace walked while turning his head to ask.

"Grede Thunderfist," Vereesa said. "He definitely told you he likes Mosa."

"Ah, sort of. Wait, hold on—" Jace stopped and turned back. "So he's with Mosa right now?" Vereesa's tongue moved unnaturally in her mouth as she stared at Jace and asked softly, "What else could it be?"

"Impressive," Jace said. "I didn't think he'd actually say it."

He pushed through the bushes. Vereesa asked from behind: "Did you tell him to do it?"

"Why would it be me?" Jace said. "I did tell him to be honest with Mosa, but I never told him to just jump straight into action."

"Hmph." Vereesa said gloomily. "You warlocks… Don't think I'll believe anything you say."

"At least Mosa definitely didn't refuse. If she'd been unwilling, Grede absolutely couldn't have—" Jace swallowed the second half of the sentence, then asked: "But I really didn't expect things to move this fast. Is Mosa's body fully recovered?"

"I think so. She picked Grede up like it was nothing, as easily as lifting a little rabbit." Vereesa said, barely containing her laughter. "Grede shouted at me not to tell you, but I never agreed to that."

Jace heard this and turned back: "Was Mosa the one who initiated it?"

"Yeah," Vereesa said. "She looked really happy, while Grede seemed completely at a loss. Didn't he say he's been through a lot before?"

"He hasn't been through a tauren before," Jace said.

Vereesa couldn't help but burst out laughing, but only for a moment, then sighed deeply. "Mosa still can't come back with us."

Jace nodded. "I'm not surprised. Even if she wanted to come with us, Grede wouldn't agree." Vereesa: "You really know that dwarf well."

The two arrived at the pond. Jace picked up the fishing rod; the worm was already gone.

He sighed, dug out another worm and threaded it on. Another muffled groan came from camp; Vereesa's pointed ears twitched awkwardly.

Jace tried not to imagine how a dwarf and a tauren managed it, cast the rod back out, and set it by his feet.

Vereesa sat beside him, watching the rain ripples on the water. "If the Kaldorei use their hippogryphs to carry us, what about Morophil? Can she escape Shandris Feathermoon's sight? Maybe in the forest, but at Feathermoon Stronghold…"

"She can sneak past the centaur camp invisible. Neither she nor Sazeno are the problem — we are," Jace said.

Vereesa asked in surprise: "Make her run that far? Out of this forest… and then cross half of Stonetalon?" Jace glanced at Vereesa in surprise — she was actually considering whether a demon gets tired?

Vereesa seemed to realize how strange that sounded, quickly added: "After all, doing it this way, who knows how much time regrouping will waste."

"She'll always find us," Jace said.

"Right — would they be willing to take us by boat to Gadgetzan?" Vereesa asked. "I think hiding in a ship would be much simpler than hiding on a hippogryph's back. After all, when dealing with that half-orc before, Morophil managed to escape the sentinels' encirclement."

"I've considered it," Jace said. "But the Kaldorei definitely wouldn't want to expose themselves in front of outsiders, let alone an entire sailing ship."

Vereesa: "I've looked at the map. In the end we just need to stop at the coast of the Tanaris Desert. We'll head north ourselves to find Steamwheedle Port — no need for them to appear in front of the goblins."

"That might actually work," Jace said. "It depends on how much they value the infiltration of those silithids into this jungle. I hope they agree — otherwise I really don't want to cross the Stonetalon Mountains again and deal with those harpies."

"Neither do I," Vereesa said softly. "I'm already eager to go back and report."

"Right — would finding Dasmora get you promoted to Ranger Lord?" Jace asked.

"I think… I don't know." Vereesa answered. "Ranger Lords mostly earned their promotions through merit in battles against the Amani trolls, but there's an exception — Nathanos Marris, that human. After Sylvanas became Ranger-General, she directly appointed him as Ranger Lord."

"Why didn't your sister just appoint you too while she was at it?" Jace asked.

Vereesa shook her head: "She already angered the Rangers and the royal family with that appointment. Promoting me from Ranger-Captain in such a short time on top of that…"

"Still not as fast as Nathanos," Jace said.

"Well, that's true," Vereesa said.

"I remember at Nethergarde Keep, you participated in hunting an eredar mage," Jace said. "At Mandel Outpost, the night before the final battle… and the final battle itself, you and Sylvanas held the front line throughout. Even facing Azgoth, you persisted against the demonic siege. Aren't all these battles enough for you to be promoted to Ranger Lord? Is the Burning Legion really less formidable than the Amani trolls?"

"How do you know about the Mandel Outpost operation?" Vereesa asked.

Jace licked his lips. "That day I happened to be on the wall on guard duty, and I overheard the rangers passing by talking about it."

Vereesa gave him a long look, then gazed at the water. "Actually, you're not the only one who thinks so. Halduron Brightwing says the same. He feels that my performance in front of the Dark Portal was more than enough for promotion. Perhaps it's precisely because of Sylvanas's willfulness that the royal family feels the Rangers have already given the Windrunner family too much favor. So if I can't become Ranger Lord this time, I can accept it — but no matter what, I have to prove I deserve it, at least more than that human does."
