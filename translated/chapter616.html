Chapter 616: Back to a Familiar Place

When Jace had left Stormwind, winter had not yet passed. Now that he was back, there were already signs of autumn creeping in. He stepped off the ship onto the solid ground of the harbor.

Back in the capital of Lordaeron, and then during his life in Stormwind, he'd always taken the feeling of walking on stone-paved roads for granted — as though it were just part of daily life. But now it felt like something he'd forgotten long ago.

The spire of the nearly finished Cathedral of Light caught the sun's glare. Jace stared at the magnificent structure for a while. Grede glanced at him and said, "What are you doing? Planning to thank the Holy Light for keeping you safe on the journey?"

"No, just spacing out," Jace said, lowering his head. "And even if I were thanking anyone, I'd thank you first."

"Don't get sentimental all of a sudden," Grede cleared his throat. "But I'm not feeling too great myself. I kind of miss Musa."

"Me too," Jace said. "When you head back to Kalimdor, I'll come with you to visit her."

"Of course — as long as there's nothing going on over here," he added.

"If you hadn't tacked on that last bit, I'd have believed you genuinely wanted to visit Musa," Grede said. "You'll always have trouble on your hands here, Jace. You can't escape it. Unless you ditch all this mess and flee to Kalimdor with me, you'll be stuck here forever."

"There'll always be a chance," Jace said.

"Let's hope so." Grede hefted his pack. "I'm heading home, Jace. I'm going to get some sleep. Remember to drag me out for a drink tomorrow. Oh, and give my regards to Lady Morophil — thank her for all her help along the way. I know she's a demon and probably couldn't care less about my greetings, but I'm not a demon. Some things still need to be said."

"She can hear you, Grede," Jace said.

"I know." The dwarf slung his bag over his shoulder and trudged toward the gate of the harbor, hauling his other parcels and box one step at a time.

Back home, Jace opened the door to find everything already coated in a thick layer of dust. He gave it a quick cleaning, put his things away, and sat in the chair over here, leaning on the table to rest a while.

Morophil sat on the bed, one hand braced on the windowsill. "Master, are you really planning to try summoning an Eredrath?"

"Why not?" Sazeno hopped onto Morophil's shoulder. "With a powerful Eredrath's help, master's strength would increase dramatically. And there are many things he could accomplish that neither you, nor I, nor even our current master can manage…

"Eredrath demons all possess an extraordinary talent for Void magic. In wielding Shadow and fel, they're no weaker than the Eredar themselves — after all, they're a species born of darkness! Succubus, I'll admit you're a powerful killer, but the mana you and I can muster is far too limited."

"Regardless, summoning an Eredrath is an extremely dangerous affair," Morophil said. "I don't want master losing his life over this. What would I do then? It wasn't easy finding someone like—"

"Don't worry too much. If I really do summon one, I'll make proper preparations, and I won't do it alone," Jace said. "Remember that Cho'gall? The one who likes summoning powerful demons — we might be able to work something out with him. But that's a matter for later. Right now, the most important thing is for me to go to Erland Dudemoor and collect the money from our last scavenging run, then go look at the house."

"There's one more thing, master." Morophil flicked her eyes toward those bundles. "Remember Vilethorn, the demon lord you encountered in Maraudon? Those powerful demon runes bestowed upon him by high-ranking members of the Burning Legion… it would be a waste not to put them to good use, wouldn't it?"

"You promised me you'd let this little imp transfer the rune's power for me," she added with a smile. "A demon's memory is long, after all."

"What's in it for me?!" the imp demanded.

"Your soul shards were all drawn out by me, imp," Morophil said. "And now you're giving me lip about returning the favor?"

"As if I didn't do anything?! As if I didn't lift a finger?!" the imp shot back, offended.

Jace looked at the two demons, then picked up his robe. "Transferring runes onto clothing — he can manage that. But transferring power onto a demon?"

"There's no great difference," Morophil said. "Just prepare a few consumables for this magically feeble little thing — mana potions, soul shards, that sort of thing — then channel Vilethorn's power into the runes I've had carved on me."

"Wait," Jace said. "But you don't have any runes on you."

Morophil lifted the elven cloth bundle containing the Blade of Eternal Darkness and licked her lips. "That's why I need you to carve them for me, master."

"Me?" Jace took the bundle and drew the dagger from inside. After a moment's hesitation he said, "But I have absolutely no experience with carving. What if I draw them wrong?"

"If you draw them wrong, I'll just heal over them," Morophil murmured, leaning close to Jace's face. "I won't mind the pain. Cut as deep as you like, master. The deeper the better, as long as it's your hands doing the—"

She grabbed Jace's wrist and drove the dagger into the pale-red skin of her own neck. Jace forced his arm back. "Sazeno could do this just as well."

"What is that thing, anyway?" the succubus said coldly, moving aside.

"I wouldn't do it even if you asked me, filthy banshee!" Sazeno shrieked.

"All right, keep it down," Jace said. "I'll help you, Morophil — I did promise, after all, and I do want you to grow stronger. But not these next couple of days. I have a fair bit to do. And if we're going to do this, it'll have to be at the Slaughtered Lamb. Before then, I'll need Ursula to prepare the mana potions and everything else, and I'll need a stable environment to learn how to handle the blade."

"As long as master remembers," the demon said with a smile. "The rest is of no consequence to me."

That afternoon, Jace took a proper bath in the canal, changed into his everyday clothes, and finally looked like a normal person again. Only then did him lift the heavy insect head and head toward the Mage Quarter.

The three of them had lugged the thing all the way back and complained the entire time. Jace didn't want it staying by his side a second longer.

Entering the Mage Quarter. The last of the scaffolding that had still been up before was now mostly gone. Otherwise, there wasn't much change around the Wizard Sanctum.

Students came and went as usual. The Blue Heron's entrance was as lively as ever. But Jace could barely spot a single familiar face.

Some of the people he'd known had traveled to distant lands to continue their studies. Others had gone to Dalaran to further their learning. Those who'd stayed at the Wizard Sanctum rarely shuttled between classrooms on a rigid schedule like children anymore. And of course, some had remained forever at the Dark Portal.

Arriving at the entrance to the Slaughtered Lamb, Jace saw the dark-skinned man still behind the bar. There weren't many customers at the moment, but he was whistling a tune as he wiped down the tables, seeming to be in decent spirits.

Looking up and spotting the figure at the door, Tharle Mor frowned, squinted, then his eyes lit up. "You're finally back, Jace. That was quite the trip — nearly half a year, wasn't it?"

"About that," Jace said. "How's everything over here?"

"All good, going smoothly, Jace," Tharle said. "With your demonic materials to help them, that group's progress has indeed been fast. After that, Galders, Erland, and Sulenna managed to get hold of some more incantations from outside. They've been studying them together and have already gotten a few down pat — all while waiting for you to bring back the demonic knowledge from the other continent… So how did it go, Jace? Everything work out?"

"Overall it matched my own expectations," Jace said. "Is Galders downstairs?"

"No, he should be down south handling some business right now — the Duskwood area." Tharle wiped a clean cup and poured a small measure of wine, sliding it over to Jace. "Ursula's downstairs. She's moved in inside, actually. I'm not sure about Jaylene — she might have left at noon. I was busy all through lunch and didn't notice."

"I see. Thanks, Tharle." Jace downed the wine and headed downstairs, but Tharle suddenly called after him: "What are you carrying in your hand?"

"A gift for Ursula," Jace said. "Brought it from over there."

"If it's food, don't bring it into the kitchen," Tharle warned.

"I won't," Jace said.
