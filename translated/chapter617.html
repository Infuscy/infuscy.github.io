Chapter 617: Secret Potion?

Walking into the basement and catching that familiar, faint chill and dampness, Jace glanced into the main hall — and was surprised to find Ursula not in the alchemy lab, but sitting in the middle of the hall nibbling on a dried-fruit-like snack while reading a book.

She seemed even thinner than before. She'd probably been living on edge this past half year, worried those killers might come knocking again. She couldn't have been having an easy time of it.

Seeing someone come in, Ursula raised her eyes, hesitated a moment, then asked, "Jace?"

"Ursula," Jace said. "I'm back."

"Finally! I've been counting the days waiting for you." Ursula tossed the book aside, walked over, and gave him a hug, then stepped back to look him up and down. "I feel like you've grown taller — and gotten a bit tanned… Kalimdor really is a harsh place."

The taller part didn't surprise Jace — he was about to turn twenty now, so even without siphoning any blood, putting on a little height over half a year wouldn't be strange. But the tanning did give him pause.

Was it the Shadow?

The elite orc warlocks of the Shadow Council all had skin of a dusky grey-black, rather than the orcs' original brownish-yellow, or the green of fel corruption.

Then again, he'd been trekking across Kalimdor for so long and spent all that time at sea too — getting a bit tanned would only be natural. But he couldn't shake the notion of Shadow contamination from his mind no matter what.

Just then Ursula's gaze drifted downward. She spotted the bundle in Jace's hand and said, "If I'm not mistaken…"

"You're not."

Jace set the bundle on the table. Something inside clunked against the wood. Ursula tugged at the rope cinched around the wrapping. "Dead?"

"This is just the head," Jace said. "The insects over there are each bigger than a boar, Ursula. I'm lucky to have escaped with my life — you expect me to bring one back alive?"

"Of course not. You've done well, truly."

Ursula untied the bundle as she spoke, glanced inside, then quickly covered it back up.

"Give me a moment to process this fellow's face, Jace. The actual alchemy experiments — we'll do those in a few days." Jace said, "Your alchemy experiments are your own affair, Ursula. I'm only concerned about my handsome reward."

"Speaking of reward." Ursula turned and pulled a small leather purse from a cabinet. "I've been keeping this for you — the money Erland left for you. I don't know how much is inside; see for yourself."

Jace hefted the pouch, opened it, and saw fifteen gold coins and twenty or thirty silver.

"Why so much?" Jace said. "Back then the lead mage — the one named Obryk — told me we'd each get about eight or nine gold when the split came…"

"You'd have to ask someone else about that," Ursula said. "But I heard the Eredar skull sold better than expected, Jace. Overall… everything sold quite well, actually. Lately there's been no shortage of mages needing demonic materials — whether from Quel'Thalas or Dalaran. And precisely because of that, their funds are far more ample than before, so Galders, Erland, and the others have been much smoother in tracking down other incantations."

If that was the case, plus the commission Obryk had promised specifically for Jace from the start, that meant the entire scavenging run had pulled in a total of twenty-one gold.

If this Kalimdor trip hadn't ended up finding Dasmora, it would've felt like he'd have been better off just scavenging for money. Of course, according to what Obryk and the others had said at the time, the "demon resources" around the Blackmire would've pretty much been picked clean by scavengers by now — there wouldn't be any profit left to squeeze. Money-making opportunities came and went in a flash.

Jace tallied up the gold in his possession, plus the loose silver, and found he already had a full seventy gold. By Grede's reckoning, that was more than enough to buy a house near the Trade District outright, renovations included.

Maybe he didn't even need to wait for the Quel'Thalas payment to come through — he could go ask at the registry right now. But he'd have to get Grede to come along; dwarves knew more about this sort of thing.

"Wait." Jace said, as if suddenly remembering something. "This is Erland's payment — not your reward."

Ursula gave an awkward laugh. "Sorry, Jace. I'd originally prepared a secret potion for you, but as you know, I've really been strapped for cash these days."

"Since I couldn't expose my whereabouts, I couldn't run my old potion-and-herb business like before, so I sold off some of the ingredients meant for that secret potion, which set things back a few months. I've got to eat, after all — I didn't very well want to live off the Dagger Society's funds. If I did, I'd have even less standing before Galders and Jaylene. Still, I've tracked down all the ingredients again."

"All right, I understand. So what's this secret potion?" Jace asked.

"An extremely powerful potion. For a spellcaster like you, it's an improvement of more than one grade," Ursula said. "But the alchemy setup here doesn't let me brew anything like that. I've already contacted an acquaintance to help me find a more complete, higher-grade alchemy lab."

"I'll definitely have it done for you in time — it'll be worthy of the risks you took. For now, take this Greater Shadow Protection potion. Think of it as an apology for the delay."

She pulled an exquisitely crafted crystal bottle from the cabinet and handed it to Jace. The Shadow energy inside couldn't be felt through the bottle, but the color alone was deep enough to make Jace's scalp tingle.

Ursula said, "This Greater Protection potion was brewed from the leftover ingredients of that secret potion. So think about how precious that thing must be."

Jace said, "You might as well just tell me outright. You're just making me itch with curiosity."

Ursula said, "I don't want to say it too clearly before I've actually succeeded. Telling you I'm working on it is already because I feel bad about the delay. Don't push it, Jace. Give me two weeks — two weeks at most."

"Then I suppose that's how it'll have to be," Jace said, helpless.

Stepping out of the Slaughtered Lamb, Jace felt much more at ease. Nothing had gone wrong there, and it seemed Galders had been tracking down more incantations smoothly. Once he got back, Jace would be able to learn a few new spells himself.

Walking through the gardens of the Mage Quarter, he recalled that Shadow-control spell of the Eredrath he'd learned before leaving Kalimdor — he didn't even know what spell it corresponded to in the game.

He'd used it once, nearly throttling an insect's neck with the spell, but Windrunner's arrow had come too fast for him to tell just how much restraining effect his magic had actually inflicted on the creature.

He wanted to find a chance to try it again and confirm whether his incantation could actually take effect successfully. But obviously he couldn't test this magic on a living person.

Thinking it over, he found his mind drifting to Teron Gorefiend's Death Coil.

Truth be told, Jace had been avoiding this spell all along. Using a demon servant's soul as an energy source to strike at enemies had always struck him as too great a sacrifice to bear — he wasn't Gajin, after all.

So even though he'd long since memorized the spell inside and out, he'd never actually had the chance to try it.

When facing Dasmora, he'd almost wanted to use the soul of the dying hellhound to test-drive the magic. But precisely because he'd never cast it once and had no feel for it, he hadn't wanted to waste a precious casting opportunity on a spell of unknown effectiveness — so in the end he'd chosen Gul'dan's Hand.

But whether to use it or not was one thing, and whether he could do it was another. He absolutely had to find a chance to summon a hellhound and test whether this spell actually worked.

And then there was one more important spell: Enslave Demon.

He needed to try it again in an undisturbed setting.

Wait — was it possible to enslave a demon first, then use that demon's soul to fuel a Death Coil?

The thought alone was enough to get him excited. He could hardly wait to find a demon and try it out right then and there — except that by now he'd already arrived at Master Marin's office.

Jace pulled out the pocket watch that had stopped running ever since Maraudon and glanced at it. He'd come here to ask Master Marin whether that gnome Amy knew was still around the Mage Quarter, to see if the fellow could fix the watch for him again.

After all, every bit of savings helped — a pocket watch wasn't cheap, and he was about to buy a house. He'd still need to eat, too.
