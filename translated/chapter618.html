Chapter 618: The Gnome and the Watch

Outside Master Marin's study, Jace listened for voices within to be certain no one else was there before knocking. "Come in. It's not locked."

He pushed the door open and poked his head in. Marin glanced toward the door. "Mr. Sesso, there's no need to be so cautious — not even a word?"

"Sorry, Master." Jace smiled and stepped inside. "It's been a while."

"It has indeed. About two months, I'd say?"

"Half a year."

"That long?" Marin said. "I suppose I've had a lot on my plate lately and time's flown. Where did you go?"

"The matter with the Reliquary of Quel'Thalas, before. Kalimdor," Jace said.

"Ah, right, I remember now," Marin said. "Did you remember to finish that death knight report during that time?"

"Ah…" Jace smacked his forehead. "Completely forgot."

Marin looked at Jace. "So as long as Archmage Andormas doesn't grant you the title of full mage, you have no intention of writing it at all, is that it?"

Jace gave an embarrassed laugh. "Mainly it's that things in Kalimodor piled up one after another, and halfway through my papers all got ruined, Master. The conditions simply weren't there."

Watching Marin smile without saying anything, Jace asked, "By the way — how's Amy doing lately?"

Marin said, "She's well, everything's going smoothly. It's just that since joining the Kirin Tor and starting her own studies she hasn't written to me much. Last time we were in touch was over a month ago. But I imagine you didn't come here just to chat with me about her?"

"That's about the size of it, Master," Jace said. "Remember how Amy helped me find someone to fix that pocket table? That mage she knows — I'd like to ask him for help. I'm happy to pay. He's the only person I've heard of who can fix watches."

Marin closed his book. "You mean the gnome, Mr. Thorolum."

"Is he still in the Mage Quarter?" Jace asked.

"He is," Marin said. "He's still studying at the Wizard Sanctum as far as I know. I haven't heard any plans of his to advance to full mage. He lives at… I should have it here somewhere. Let me find it."

The old mage rifled through the volumes in his cabinet, muttering as he went. "As for whether he charges, I truly have no idea — you'd be better off asking him directly. I know there are a few gnome mages studying magic at the Wizard Sanctum who do indeed earn their living by repairing small odds and ends or helping craftsmen in the Dwarf District with tricky jobs. He may be the same. But I imagine it'll still be cheaper than buying a new one."

"Ah, here it is." He pressed the book flat and set it in front of Jace. "You can copy it down." Jace pulled out a scrap of paper and took the pen. "Thank you, Master."

"You're welcome, Mr. Sesso," Marin said.

Leaving the Mage Quarter, there was still plenty of daylight left. Jace glanced at the address on the slip of paper — Old Town. He should make it there well before dinner.

The location marked on the address was an old three-story building, clearly pre-war. If he was buying a house, he definitely wouldn't buy one like this.

Jace climbed the exterior stairs to the second floor, came to the door, and knocked softly. "Mr. Thorolum?"

"Who is it?" came a childlike voice from inside.

Jace said, "I'm a friend of Amy Marin's. I heard you're quite skilled at fixing pocket watches, and I was hoping you could do me a favor."

"Uh… hold on, a moment, it's not quite—"

A clatter of metallic noise came from within. Jace immediately said, "Shall I come back some other time?"

"No, no, that's not what I meant. Just a moment."

Jace stood by the door and waited. Not long after, the door was flung open with a bang, and a gnome with a small brown handlebar mustache poked his head out.

He wore a short white coat — not the sort of mage's robe commonly seen at the Wizard Sanctum. He also wore two gold rings Jace had never seen before, possibly of some Gnomecity connection, or perhaps a family crest.

The gnome looked him up and down. "A friend of Amy Marin's? Your name."

"York."

Jace produced his Wizard Sanctum ring. The gnome glanced at it. "You're Jace Sesso, aren't you?"

"Uh… you know about me?"

"Last time I fixed your watch in the Blackmire I knew you weren't dead. But I won't make trouble for you — don't kill me to keep me quiet."

"That's not possible, Mr. Thorolum…"

"Hah, a joke. Come in." The gnome pushed the door open. "Though you're not quite what I pictured. I imagined you'd be more solidly built, maybe a bit more rugged-looking — the sort of beard, perhaps. You don't look at all like a powerful mage who defeated the pit lord Azgors at the Dark Portal."

The gnome put a deliberately sarcastic edge on the word "mage," and combined with the joke he'd just told, Jace knew he was clearly aware of what magic Jace had actually used to deal with that pit lord.

By appearance and voice alike, Thorolum was a fairly young gnome. Jace reckoned he couldn't be much older than himself.

His room was simply furnished, no different from an ordinary magic student's. That said, the bookshelf held quite a number of small gadgets whose exact purpose was unclear — likely engineering-related, as he couldn't sense any particular magical power from them.

But compared to his care in arranging the room, the fellow seemed rather indifferent to matters of cleanliness. Dust seemed to float everywhere. With the windows thrown wide open and the afternoon sunlight streaming in, the drifting particles stood out clearer than ever.

"Did you bring the 'regular customer'?" the gnome asked.

"I did."

Only then did Jace realize he'd been rather too interested in someone else's living quarters. He quickly pulled his attention back and gently set the pocket watch on the table.

The gnome picked it up, glanced at it, and began disassembling it as he spoke. "Got a bit wet? Slight damage, but nothing major. Just give me a moment."

Jace said, "What's not a major problem to Mr. Thorolum is utterly beyond me."

The gnome laughed and deftly took the watch apart, laying out each tiny metal ring, gear, and minuscule component one by one on the table.

Jace had expected him to bring out tweezers and the like for a painstaking repair, but instead the gnome produced a slender copper rod from behind the cabinet and began chanting, summoning the Arcane.

Arcane energy trickled in through the dim-yellow window, the crack beneath the door, and between the motes of dust, swirling around the pocket watch and its parts in time with the gnome's incantations and gestures, guiding them to interlock, to assemble… Was he really just an apprentice mage?

It was said that many gnomes possessed a considerable talent for the Arcane — it's just that they preferred to combine the Arcane with mechanical engineering and the like to produce innovations, or to enhance the performance of their creations, rather than to kill and destroy.

Jace watched, dazzled, and saw Thorolum suddenly snatch the fully assembled watch, clench it in his palm, then smile and hold it out to Jace, opening his hand.

"There you are, Mr. Sesso. Time has begun to move again."

"That's incredible," Jace said, taking the watch.

The gnome waved a hand dismissively. "Just a small jest — after all, the march of time isn't affected by a mere pocket watch."

"Uh." Jace gave a slightly dazed nod, then suddenly seemed to remember something and pulled out his purse. "Right, shouldn't I…"

"Absolutely not. Amy would be furious with me," Thorolum said quickly. "She's helped me a great deal at the Wizard Sanctum — helping her friend is only natural for me. What's more, you're the hero of Nethergarde Keep. Even if I had nothing to my name, I wouldn't take your money, let alone while I'm still reasonably comfortable."

"Are there many people at the Wizard Sanctum who know I'm alive?" Jace asked.

"No — perhaps only the few who live at the very top. If not for Amy, I wouldn't have known either," the gnome said, leaning against the small table. "I've heard quite a lot about you — from Amy, and from other mages. Trust your own choices, Mr. Sesso. Don't let too many unnecessary factors cloud your judgment. Those who support you are certainly more numerous than you think."

"What factors?" Jace asked absently, running his fingers over the watch.

The gnome waggled his fingers and drew a deep breath. "Many things… the Kirin Tor, for one. Those who suspect you. Or messy politics, that sort of thing. You take my meaning. Still, I have an appointment to keep this evening, so…"

"All right. Thank you, Mr. Thorolum."

"It truly is nothing. Then good evening to you, Mr. Sesso." Good evening?

Jace found this gnome rather odd, but once he'd left the room and come outside, he realized the sky really had darkened. Perhaps the night in Stormwind came faster than in southern Kalimodor — he'd had no time to adjust.

Thorolum… the pocket watch…

In the quiet, an uncomfortably wrong suspicion bubbled up in his mind. Don't let unnecessary factors cloud your judgment, Mr. Sesso.

Then the gnome's words chimed in his heart like the tick-tick of a clock hand, dousing his doubts. Indeed — best not to think too much about it.
