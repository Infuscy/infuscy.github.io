## Chapter 619: Everything as Usual, Yet Not Quite

When Jace woke up the next day it was already nearly ten. He jolted upright, and only when he saw the grey-white tower of Stormwind's prison standing out clearly in the distance through the window did he remember — he no longer had to attend classes or go tidy up for Malin.

He ran his fingers through his haphazardly scraggly stubble and bird's-nest of curly hair, and couldn't help but recall the feeling of Vereesa holding him at the docks of Booty Bay.

He twisted his mouth awkwardly and sucked in a breath. He rummaged through his pack for a knife or dagger of some sort, but after a long search all he turned up was the Blade of Eternal Darkness. The Shadow energy drifting faintly above it was no longer as brazenly obvious as when it had first been dragged from a demon's belly in the ruins of Eldre'Thalas.

"Does master intend to carve my runes now?" Morophil asked.

"No," Jace said. "Shaving."

The succubus arched an eyebrow. Jace was already out the door.

No suddenly appearing giant bears, half-orcs, no gnolls, giant insects, thunder lizards, harpies, wind serpents, kodos, hyenas, lions, or ogres charging out of nowhere. No night elf sentinels watching him from who-knows-where. Just a few Stormwind guards chatting as they strolled along the opposite bank of the canal, and a flock of little blue birds chirping on the high eaves too cowardly to swoop down and attack him.

Then again, they had no need to attack him either.

Jace shot the birds a glance, walked to the canal's edge, crouched down, and set about shaving by his own blurred, wavering reflection in the water. No need to rush, no need to watch his back. Quiet, patient…

"Ow…"

He touched his cheek and felt a spot of stinging warmth — blood.

He shut his eyes and steeled himself for a good while before opening them again. Good, it was red. That was fine.

"What are you crouching there for?"

"Ah?"

Jace looked up. The Wildhammer dwarf in the sunlight looked like a pitch-black boulder crouched beside him. "Shaving."

The dwarf said, "What's there to shave? Grow it out like me — start looking like a proper warrior."

Jace said, "It catches food. I can't be bothered."

"Fair enough." Grede braced himself on his knees and stood up. "Done? Let's get a drink?" Jace rubbed his rough chin, still stubbled with prickly whiskers. "Done. Let's go."

The two of them strolled along the canal all the way to the Gilded Rose and found a quiet corner to sit in. Jace glanced at the busy serving girl. He'd never much cared for that girl — the first time he'd come here to eat with Vereesa, she'd found a way to fleece them both.

But after dealing with all sorts of goblins in Booty Bay and Steamwheedle Port, he'd realized she was small-time by comparison. His old disgust didn't feel so strong against that backdrop.

He turned his gaze back to Grede across the table. Stripped of his armor, helmet, cloak, and heavy outer garments, Jace thought the dwarf looked like he'd lost quite a bit of weight.

Tearing around Kalimdor on such a long trek, with no guarantee of food or drink along the way — any normal person would've slimmed down a fair bit. But judging by the feedback from people who knew him, he himself seemed to have actually filled out a bit compared to before he'd left Stormwind.

If there was any variable in that, it was the vitality he'd siphoned from Dasmora at the very end. "There's something I'd like your help with, Grede."

Grede's eyes widened. "So soon? Taking on another job?"

"No," Jace said. "I'm planning to go look at houses in the next couple of days. I'd like you to come with me — I don't know much about this sort of thing."

"I've never bought a house either," Grede said.

Jace said, "Two heads are better than one. We'll notice more problems than going alone, don't you think?" The dwarf nodded. "Fine. When?"

Jace said, "In the next day or two."

"You're not waiting for Vereesa's money to arrive?"

"I've scraped together enough for now. And I'll look while I wait."

"No problem. Just give me a word when you're planning to go. Same as always."

"There's one more thing."

"Out with it," Grede said.

Jace watched the serving girl bring over a small mouse-tail fish and set it on the table, along with two mugs of apple wine. Unable to resist, he scooped up a chunk of fish with his spoon and stuffed it into his mouth, then took a gulp of the ice-cold wine, exhaled, and spoke.

"Help me bring my parents here."

"Sure," Grede said. "After you've bought the house?"

"Right," Jace said.

The dwarf gave a "mm," scooped up a piece of fish soaked in rich broth, pursed his lips around it, closed his eyes to savor it for a moment, and only swallowed and opened his eyes to ask: "Have you thought about this, Jace? If I bring your parents to Stormwind, the Kirin Tor might follow you here and drive you out of the city. Or perhaps they'd stop me from bringing your parents here in the first place — keep you from seeing them every day."

"Isn't that a bit much?"

The words were out, but after a moment's thought Jace decided Grede's scenario wasn't entirely impossible.

"Then I simply won't see them. As long as they leave Lordaeron, that's what matters. In any case, let's buy the house first. The Kirin Tor can't very well confiscate my house, can they? Even if they bar me from entering Stormwind, at least my parents will have a place to live."

"I'm just speculating. As long as you're prepared," Grede said.

"What's wrong, Grede? You don't seem to be in very high spirits," Jace asked.

"I'm thinking about Mosa," Grede said. "What if she gets caught by the centaur again?"

"She once had nothing," Jace said. "Her tribe, her family — all gone. She lived for revenge alone. Now she has you. That changes everything. She'll take good care of herself."

"Maybe," Grede said.

Jace said, "I can tell — this time you're serious."

"If you'd told me before we set out that I'd develop feelings for a tauren, I'd have smashed your skull in," Grede said, dazed. "But Mosa changed everything."

"Maybe I sensed it when I saw her fighting those centaur to the death — I just wasn't willing to admit I could be attracted to a cow or a lioness back then. Now I can't say for sure either."

"Who can say for sure," Jace said with a smile.

After eating, they left the Gilded Rose. By the time Jace had gone to the Trade District market to buy some daily necessities and returned home, it was already evening.

Jace casually grabbed some dry bread, stuffed a handful of cheese crumbs into a cloth bag and tossed it into his warlock's bag, and looked at the succubus who'd been smiling at him the whole time. "Come on, Morophil. Don't forget to bring Vyletongue's demon skin."
