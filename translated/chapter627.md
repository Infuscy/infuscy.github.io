## Chapter 627: This Is the One

Lying in bed that night, the thought that he'd be going with the agent to pick out a house tomorrow set Jace's heart racing.

He glanced at the Quel'Thalas chest under the bed. It had originally been Vereesa's, used to hold the Song of the Azure Sky; now he used it to stash his money, and he wished he could seal it shut with some kind of ward.

"Guard this box well, Morophil," he muttered. Hearing the succubus's acknowledgment, he drifted off to sleep.

---

"The house you see before you is the property of the Lescovar family, built during the Troll Wars under King Baratheon's reign, so its style is somewhat older compared to the other houses in the Trade District. Yes, this is a pre–Dark Portal old house, and it doesn't meet your exact requirements…

"…but first of all, the war did not reach this area, so the house can be fully renovated and repaired. I estimate the craftsmen's fees and materials won't exceed ten gold at most. Once the repairs are complete, given the Stonemasons' standard, the quality will be on par with any new construction in the Trade District, Mr. Yurke."

The man before him, wearing a pale yellow linen shirt and gray trousers, was the real estate agent introduced through the registry — Dereett Blaze. He looked to be in his thirties, about Jace's father's age, but already more than half bald.

Over the past two days, Jace had followed this man up and down the canal to view several houses. The candidates weren't many. The two-story standalone before him was the second-to-last that met the criteria. He hadn't been satisfied with the previous ones — either too far from a church, or the houses themselves were barely passable.

He'd heard the last one was a bit closer to the Cathedral of Light, but it was located in the Park. The moment Jace had heard that, he'd ruled it out in his heart.

So for him, the one in front of him was the final option.

It faced the canal directly. Through the wall opening beside it, you could walk straight through to the Trade District. Looking west, you could see the massive white wall of the great cathedral standing behind rows of high walls. Gazing further out, you could take in the lower Stormwind Harbor at a glance, flocks of seagulls gliding silently past the sails — truly a splendid view.

But this house cost eighty-two gold, and with renovation expenses on top, who knew how much further the price would climb. And that was already after a discount for being an old house.

The figure made Jace dizzy. The man was still talking — recounting the history of the house, how this area had miraculously escaped the orcs' rampage when they stormed Stormwind — while climbing the tall stone steps, but Jace couldn't focus on any of it.

He pointed to the right. "The stables are over there, quite lovely. I'll show you in a bit. For now, let's go inside first."

"Hey, you look a little dizzy," Grede asked from the side. "You think he's telling the truth? About this house being spared by the Holy Light during the orc sack — are the Lescovars really that pious?"

The dwarf walked to the doorway, stretched his arms to gauge the width, then jumped and reached up to touch the top of the doorframe. "I don't think a full-grown orc could squeeze through a frame this size. They'd have to knock out the wood on both sides to get in. This looks perfectly intact, and the bricks don't show any signs of smashing… Maybe he's telling the truth, or maybe the owner already renovated the whole place."

"Who did he say lived here again? Someone related to Gregg Lescovar?"

"I wasn't really listening," Jace said. "I can never keep these noble names straight."

"Come in and have a look, gentlemen!" Dereett Blaze called from inside. Jace answered and walked in.

It was dim inside — probably because it was already evening. After passing through a narrow hallway, he entered a rather spacious living room.

What surprised Jace a little was that the living room still had some furniture: a fireplace, a rug, a handsome empty display shelf, a few carved wooden chairs that clearly weren't cheap, a cabinet leaning against the wall, and even some unnamed paintings hanging up.

Jace glanced at the agent waiting for them inside and asked, "If I buy the house, do all these things come with it?"

Mr. Blaze smiled. "If you buy the house, yes. And you needn't worry about the quality of this furniture at all. Someone lived here until last month, so as you can see, every piece of furniture, every corner of every room, is quite clean and intact."

"Not bad at all," Grede said, stroking the fireplace across from the chair. "If the dimensions weren't all wrong, I'd buy this house myself."

Jace had to admit, he was tempted too.

He thought of his parents' old house in the Old Craftsmen's Quarter of the Lordaeron capital. It was two stories too, but no matter how much they cleaned or tidied, both floors always felt like a run-down old warehouse.

In his childhood memories, home had always seemed pitch-black and gloomy.

But this house he stood in now already had a bit of the flavor of those Western historical dramas from his past life — the kind of townhouse a wealthy minor noble or merchant would live in.

"Shall we go upstairs, Mr. Yurke?" Mr. Blaze asked. "Let's go up."

Grede charged into the stairwell and clomped up to the second floor. Jace nodded as well.

"There's a basement downstairs. It's not large — about the size of that little room off to the side of the living room, Mr. Yurke."

Mr. Blaze pointed into the darkness beneath the stairs. Jace was more surprised that even the stairwell itself was quite tidy. Every time he'd traversed the stairwell at his parents' house, he'd felt like he was making his way through some ancient, dark dungeon.

Upstairs, there were two lovely bedrooms on either side of the hallway, with finely crafted wooden beds, bedside tables, and wardrobes. One even had a vanity and a mirror. Jace caught a glimpse of himself in the mirror and felt like his clothes didn't belong here at all.

His mother would love it here, he thought.

He could even picture Hennie sitting here looking in the mirror, carefully tidying her hair, laughing and saying she was already too old for young men to come courting, that her best youthful years had been wasted on Jace and Viktor…

Looking out the window beside the vanity, he could see straight to Stormwind Harbor — blue sky and sea… This really was a good place.

He could also see a good half of the Park to the left. If only Deathwing wouldn't destroy it in the future.

No, on second thought, maybe it'd be better if he did destroy it. After all, if it wasn't the Park, it'd be somewhere else — so better to let the Park go, at least this house would survive.

"I'm satisfied," he cut off Mr. Blaze's description of the view.

"Not going to see the next one? I don't know if you remember what I told you yesterday — that one's in the Park, right over there, that red roof. See it, Mr. Yurke?"

The agent pointed at a splash of red among the trees outside the window. "The Park has become the top choice for Stormwind's new rich, especially those Quel'Thalas elves."

"This one," Jace said. "I'll take this one."

"Your opinion is naturally the most important," Mr. Blaze smiled. "Then, a five-gold deposit will take this house off the registry's for-sale list, Mr. Yurke. Note that if you pay the deposit and then decide not to buy, that money will not be refunded. After that, I can also help you arrange the renovation work. I recall the landlord mentioned there's a beam in this house with some concerns about its lifespan. That spot will pose a safety risk if it's not repaired."

Jace thought — if the renovation work was also going through the Stonemasons' guild, maybe he could ask Erland Dudmoor. Wasn't he with the Stonemasons?

But considering the kind of people Erland knew, and the path the Stonemasons' guild might take in the future, Jace hesitated. He didn't really want to tie his house to Erland and his lot. Most importantly, he didn't want to drag his parents into it.

"Then let's do that," Jace nodded.

Dereett Blaze produced a contract and laid it on a nearby table. "You can read it carefully, Mr. Yurke. Once you sign, I can contact the Lescovars to arrange a time to meet for the transaction. How does that sound?"

Jace took the contract. "And the renovation costs?"

"You can pay those after you've bought the house," the agent said.

Jace nodded. His current funds weren't enough to cover the renovation — he'd have to wait for the Holy Reliquary's money to arrive. He looked over the contract, confirmed there were no issues, and signed his name.

Dereett Blaze picked up the contract. "I must mention one thing — I'm well aware that you mages often live under assumed names. And Yurke, a dwarvish name, is probably also an alias, isn't it?"

"Will that cause any problems?" Jace asked.

Mr. Blaze said, "As long as you have identification from the Wizard Sanctum, there's no problem at all. The leaders of the Mage Quarter — Master Andormas, for instance — can vouch that you are Yurke, and this contract is valid."

"You mean this." Jace produced the Wizard Sanctum's sigil.

Mr. Blaze took it and looked it over. "This is exactly what I meant. Then there's no issue at all, Mr. Yurke. I'll contact the Lescovars first thing tomorrow to confirm the transaction date."

"Understood. Thank you for your trouble, Mr. Blaze," Jace said.
