Chapter 628: The Reward

After signing the contract and paying the deposit, Grede and Jace went to a nearby tavern for dinner.

From the moment the two left the house, through entering the tavern, sitting down, Grede hadn't stopped talking about how beautiful the house was, and even Jace had started to feel a little giddy — he'd really picked a good place.

"But I still don't understand why you didn't even go look at the Park house," Grede said, propping his cheek. "That Mr. Blaze wasn't lying to you, Jace. Plenty of wealthy Quel'Thalas folk are planning to buy in the Park. Don't you want your parents to have a place in that beautiful forest-in-the-city?"

"And run into a dozen elves giving me the cold shoulder and looking down their noses at me every time I step outside?" Jace turned a silver coin back and forth on the table as he spoke. "Remember when we went to Quel'Daniel's cottage? Think about how those elves treated us."

"What about our little elf — wasn't she perfectly fine?" Grede raised an eyebrow.

"Hah." Jace said, "The first time I met Vereesa you weren't there. She looked at me like I was some Stranglethorn wild gorilla. And from what I know, she's already one of the more outward-friendly ones in all of Quel'Thalas."

"When was I not there?" Grede asked. "Weren't we in Dalaran?"

"The Skyblue Meeting tavern," Jace said. "You'd just gotten into a fight with Kael'fas Brightwing and run off to do something — probably order food — and right then Vereesa walked in."

As he spoke, the waiter brought the two light ales they'd ordered when they came in. Grede raised his mug. "Ah, right, I remember now. Kael'fas Brightwing, that self-important idiot…"

"Never mind, it's already settled." He took a sip of foam, then asked, "Remember that frothy drink we had in Booty Bay? I wonder how goblins get so much foam in there — it was damn satisfying to drink."

"I remember. Probably some kind of aerating machine," Jace said.

"There's a machine for that?" Grede nodded. "Mages sure know a lot. That drink was great in every way except it cost a damn fortune. Oh right — do you have enough money? How much was that house again? I forgot what the guy said."

"Eighty-two," Jace said. "I am a bit short right now, but isn't the Kalimdor commission arriving any day now? Once it's in hand, I'll have more than enough. Even if we just go by the Holy Reliquary's minimum price for actionable intel — eighty gold — and Vereesa doesn't take a share, that's forty each. I'll have over a hundred gold by then, Grede."

"Over a hundred gold." Grede nodded. "Think about when we first arrived in Stormwind — scraping together a hundred silver was a nightmare."

"That's because you were a cripple back then," Jace looked at him. "With your skills, you never had to worry about a few silver, did you, Thunderfist?"

"Hah." Grede laughed. "Without you, I never would have sorted out my own problems, Jace. Tell you what — if the Quel'Thalas money hasn't arrived by the transaction date, I'll spot you some."

"Generous of you."

"You think I'm like you?" Grede snorted. "Money's a great thing, but I've got more than enough for now. That last trip to the valley near Karazhan — I mean, the wraith essence business… the money I made there's enough to keep me living large for a long while. So I won't need that much gold in the short term, Jace. I can lend you some, help you through the tight spot."

"Thanks."

Jace raised his mug, and the two clinked glasses.

Two days later at noon, Dereett Blaze showed up at Jace's door. He told Jace he'd already spoken with the Lescovars — the transaction would take place at the house in a week, signing the contract. The exact date: the third of October.

It had been nearly a month since Jace had parted ways with Vereesa in Booty Bay, and by the time the agreed date arrived, enough time would have passed. If you calculated from when Vereesa and Jace had returned together from the Burning Steppes to Stormwind, and then she'd gone back to Quel'Thalas to send the Song of the Azure Sky over, this timing was definitely sufficient.

So Jace agreed readily. Honestly, he didn't need to worry — even if the money was delayed, Grede could help out, and with the dwarf's funds added in, it would more than cover it.

Over the following days, Jace traveled back and forth to the Mage Quarter every day. For one thing, he'd check in with Ursula to see how that mysterious potion was coming along; for another, he'd stop by Marin's to check whether any packages or chests had been sent to him.

Two days before the meeting with the Lescovars, Jace arrived at the Mage Quarter that morning and was heading toward the Slaughtered Lamb when he suddenly heard shouting behind him.

"Hey, Mr. Yurke! I say, slow down!"

It was Marin's voice, but Jace's brain didn't register right away that "Yurke" meant him. He'd already walked some distance before he turned back.

He spotted Marin amid the coming-and-going crowd. "Master, sorry — it's too crowded here, I didn't hear clearly."

"No matter." Marin hurried over. "Last night a mage from Quel'Thalas delivered a box to me and asked me to pass it on to you. Last time those elves sent a chest by teleportation; this time an elf brought it in person…"

He leaned in and whispered, "So, last time it was the Song of the Azure Sky — what is it this time?"

Jace touched the sword sheath at his waist. "Just money, Master. The Holy Reliquary's payment for the mission to Kalimdor."

"You never mentioned what mission you were sent to Kalimdor for." Marin straightened up, hugging his long loaf of bread, and continued in a low voice, "Last time you were in such a hurry to find that apprentice mage to fix your watch, and it was already late, so I didn't keep you. I wonder — do you have time to come by and tell me more about Kalimdor? You know, in the Kingdom of Stormwind, nearly everything anyone knows about Kalimdor comes from pirates. Even the Stormwind Royal Library is eighty percent secondhand accounts like that. The last time a senior Wizard Sanctum member personally went to Kalimdor for a thorough investigation and intelligence gathering was… probably before I became a full mage."

"Whenever I have time, Master," Jace said.

Marin smiled helplessly. "Then come with me first and pick up your things."
