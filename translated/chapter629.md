## Chapter 629: The Mundanity of Life

Following the old mage to his study, Marin pointed to a blue-flowered cloth-wrapped chest sitting on a bookshelf nearby. "That's it." Jace took the chest down — it felt heavy, considerably heavier than the box he used to store gold at home.

The weight alone was enough to get him excited. He set it on the table and opened it to find several bags of coins inside. He opened one of the bags, and it was packed full of gold.

"These are the less commonly seen Stromgarde gold coins." Marin picked up one of the coins and examined the warhorse imprinted on it. "Excellent quality. The elves don't use these coins often — they mostly store them for trade with us — so the gold that comes from their hands is always top-notch."

His lips moved as he murmured a spell, then his eyes widened. "A hundred and… sixty, Mr. Sesso. My goodness, what on earth did you do for the Holy Reliquary?"

"There's that much?"

Jace pulled out a letter from beneath the money bags and opened it. It was from Vereesa.

"The Holy Reliquary is thrilled with everything we found in Eldrethas. They had suspected that Dasmora might be a Burning Legion demon but couldn't find conclusive evidence. What we found helped them fill in a crucial piece. Enclosed is the reward — count it carefully. Remember to give my regards to Grede and Sazeno. Also, Miss Atherwyn Goldthorn is being considered for promotion to Ranger-Lord. Damn it, my Common still isn't good enough to express my feelings. Hope to see you soon — I'll teach you a few beautiful Thalassian words you can use here. Are you happy for me, Mr. Yurke?"

Reading that last line, Jace was happy — but he also realized just how important obtaining Dasmora's information must be to Quel'Thalas.

Looking at the money, and thinking of what Vereesa had said about "filling in a crucial piece," he couldn't help but wonder — did the Holy Reliquary also know about Ursales?

But according to Shandris Feathermoon's account, Dasmora must have known the locations of many ancient magical artifacts. As a demon that had remained on Kalimdor for ten thousand years, traveling everywhere in search of artifacts to help open a Legion portal, it would be natural for it to know the clues to many related treasures. The Holy Reliquary was probably planning to start with those scrolls and Dasmora's fel heart to track down these artifacts.

If that was the case, a reward of over a hundred gold wasn't particularly outrageous.

Looked at that way, splitting the money fifty-fifty with Grede and using the rest to buy a house was just about enough…

Of course, this house would still need a lot of money for subsequent renovation, maintenance, and furnishing. He'd also need to see if his parents had any additional requests. So this remaining sum couldn't be touched for now.

Things went far more smoothly than Jace had imagined, proceeding exactly according to his original plan.

Perhaps the Kirin Tor's attitude wasn't what he and Grede had anticipated — that they'd stop him from seeing his parents. They just didn't want him showing up in Lordaeron.

After all, his parents had no connections in Stormwind. Nobody here knew them. Who would care if he met with them? At any rate, the impact would be far smaller than in the capital.

Thinking this, Jace grew more and more looking forward to the day his parents moved into that house.

The next two days dragged by agonizingly. On the appointed afternoon, Grede and Jace waited at the house early for the agent and the Lescovars to show up.

Unsurprisingly, the Lescovars hadn't sent any family members — they'd sent several servants. Of course, those servants were dressed more handsomely than King Varian himself.

When Jace set the bag of gold coins on the table, listening to the clinking cascade from within, his mind flashed continuously through Lordaeron to Stranglethorn, from the Burning Steppes to the Black Morass, the depths of Eldrethas and Maraudon…

Vereesa, Grede… the imp and the succubus, the dead felhounds, friends, and one foe after another. The Lescovars counted the money, and when they finished, the way they looked at Jace had changed.

"Congratulations, Mr. Yurke." One of the Lescovar servants handed over the key ring with both hands. "This house is yours. May you and your family live happily here."

Until everyone had left the house, until only Jace and Grede remained sitting in the living room chairs in a daze, it still didn't feel real.

Jace Sesso had bought a house within Stormwind's walls — a house that belonged to him, no, to Yurke. It was like a dream, unbelievably smooth.

Jace had imagined running into a scammer agent, or these nobles or their servants being extremely hostile — unwilling to trust him because of his foreigner status or his inexplicable alias, or worried that the gold coins he produced were fake and throwing up all sorts of obstacles…

Or that the money he'd painstakingly saved would be mysteriously emptied by some dark-magic thief using strange spells at the last day before the transaction — something not even Morophil could stop, because over a hundred gold would be enough to attract the attention of the most fearsome thieves in some Stormwind underground guild, right?

Though Jace's knowledge of that world came only from a few mages, or scraps from Erland Dudmoor.

He looked at the deed and contract in his hands, confirmed the name on them, then looked at the key ring — he hadn't expected it to actually happen, just like that.

Grede, sitting in the other chair, slapped his thigh, snapping Jace out of it.

"Hey, Jace, what are you thinking about? Shall we go for a drink?"

"Ah — yes, let's go now."

Stepping outside with Grede, Jace could still feel his heart pounding — whether from the joy of having just bought the place, anticipation for the future, or something else.

Perhaps the six months of constant tension on Kalimdor had conditioned him to expect strange dangers at every turn, and he still hadn't been able to shake off that state.

Maybe daily life was supposed to be this plain and uneventful.

Thinking this, he locked the door and turned around — only to see a figure in a dark red cloak standing by the canal, looking straight at him.

Backlit by the evening sun coming from the direction of the sea, Jace couldn't make out his face, but he felt an inexplicable sense of familiarity. "Good afternoon, Mr. Jace Sesso."

The figure removed his hood, revealing shoulder-length pale golden hair — a rather tall elven mage. Hearing his voice, Jace suddenly remembered…

The man finished speaking, glanced at Grede, who was already somewhat on guard, then smiled and shifted his gaze back.

"Remember me? We met at the deep-water inn in Menethil Harbor. Now, there's something I'd like to discuss with you alone."
