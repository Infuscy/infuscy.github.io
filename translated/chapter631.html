Chapter 631: Quel'Thalas

That night, returning home, he opened the door. The succubus was sitting on the bed. She'd been about to speak, but seemed to sense something and ultimately stayed silent, quietly getting up to make room for Jace and leaning against the table. The imp poked its head out of the bag, staring intently at its master's every movement. Enter the room, get onto the bed, lie down facing away. It glanced at Morophil. Morophil glanced back at it. Neither was willing to ask anything. Jace stared at the wall, his mind a tangled mess. Should he tell Grede? Should he tell Vereesa? For some reason, when it came to him, the Grim Batol expedition had become a one-man mission. Of course, even in Rhonin's case, in the beginning Vereesa and the dwarven leader Falstad Wildhammer hadn't been part of the mission either. Vereesa was only responsible for escorting Rhonin through the dangerous Wetlands, and Falstad's appearance had been almost accidental. Rhonin, like him, hadn't been allowed to reveal the details of the mission to anyone. At first, everyone had thought Rhonin was simply going to the Wetlands to scout orcish traces on the Kirin Tor's orders. But Krasus hadn't mentioned an escort to Jace at all. Was it because he hadn't agreed yet? No, that wasn't it. The Vereesa of another timeline was still a rookie who'd just completed her Farstrider training, stuck in Quel'Thalas for years — not even close to a Ranger-Captain yet. She'd missed the Orc Wars, missed the Alliance's counterattack. The reason she'd escorted Rhonin was precisely to prove her abilities on this mission — to prove she was a true Windrunner, not some useless noble scion who only knew how to hide in Silvermoon when war and hardship came. But on this side, she was already about to be promoted to Ranger-Lord. She had nothing left to prove. Vereesa Windrunner — Ender of Gorefiend, the Scourge of the Burning Legion at Nethergarde Keep. Not to mention she'd just completed the Holy Reliquary's arduous mission. If she pulled off another extraordinary feat, even Sylvanas's position as Ranger-General wouldn't be secure. Even granting that — was a Ranger-Lord the kind of role that should be doing this kind of dirty work? The Farstriders and the Quel'Thalas royal house would never send a Ranger-Lord on some escort mission. Not only that — Krasus had specifically instructed Jace not to leak this matter to anyone, even those closest to him. Actually, even if Krasus hadn't said so, Jace would still have been hesitant about whether to tell Grede and Vereesa. This was, after all, a mission that was practically a death sentence. The enemy wasn't some reclusive warlock who'd fled to who-knows-where, or an orcish necromancer hiding in the shadows — it was Deathwing himself! Rhonin's success had been a series of coincidences… and now, almost every element was different. Could he achieve the same outcome? Bringing the two of them along — wasn't that just asking them to die with him? Thinking this, Jace buried his face deeper into the pillow. Then could he refuse? If he didn't do it, who would? Rhonin again? In another timeline, without Vereesa's help and trust, whether Rhonin could even make it through the Wetlands was questionable to begin with. Even if Rhonin were sent now, his escort certainly wouldn't be Vereesa. And where the hell was this timeline's Rhonin anyway? Dalaran? Jace was completely at a loss. He didn't know how long he'd lain in bed staring, but Jace didn't even dare look at his pocket watch. He probably wouldn't sleep tonight, but he knew that insomnia would do him a world of harm the next day. "Morophil." "My lord." "Put me to sleep… wait." "Yes?" "If I go to my death, will you come with me?" Morophil laughed softly. "Dying isn't that heavy a promise for a demon. But even so, I don't want to be parted from you." "Then I'll hold you to that. Put me to sleep." "Yes, my lord." Under Morophil's magical influence, he quickly grew drowsy. Jace didn't resist her power, calmly closing his eyes… Before long, a faint whispering seemed to linger in his ears, and then something gave him a jolt — he snapped his eyes open. A hand… ice-cold. Jace instinctively pulled his hand back. The sound of rushing water reached him. He realized he was lying by a stream, his hands submerged in crystal-clear brook water. Where was this? He turned to look around warily, but his limbs felt stiff, his head ached and spun — no matter how he tried, he couldn't move. Someone, help… The ice-cold water flowing over his legs made his whole body shiver. It was precisely because of this that his legs seemed frozen in place, beyond his control. He wanted to call for help, but all that came out was a faint "hah… hah…" — which quickly turned into a violent fit of coughing. "Jace… Jace!" After a series of splashes, a pair of slender arms wrapped around Jace from behind. The voice said softly, "Careful, Jace. Don't fall." This was Vereesa's voice? It was slightly different from the Vereesa's voice Jace knew — this one was a touch more composed — but the voice was unmistakable. "You've been spacing out again. You do this a lot lately — dreaming strange things. Don't think too much. It's not good for you." Jace turned his head and saw Vereesa's silver hair draped beside him, but his neck ached terribly — he couldn't turn far enough to see her face. Just then, his peripheral vision caught sight of the brook beneath his feet. In the rippling water, Jace saw that familiar face. Vereesa's gentle expression was as always. She was wearing a dark blue long dress, sleeves rolled up, her snow-white arm tightly wrapped around… looking utterly happy. And the person she was holding — this strange old bastard — reminded Jace of the twisted gray dead trees on the Burning Steppes. Staring at that bark-like human face, Jace swallowed hard, his throat burning as if on fire. What made him feel worse was that the reflection's neck had also wriggled, its eyes squeezing shut like a sick alligator. This was him. Jace Sesso. Without a doubt. "Did you catch any, Jace?" Vereesa asked softly in his ear. "The children in the garden over there are still waiting for you to grill fish for them." Jace rubbed his eyes, glanced at the golden silk cage in his left hand — empty. He craned his neck with difficulty and looked toward the faintly visible wooden garden wall in the distance. "No. Not yet." His voice was hoarse and weak. "What's wrong? You seem upset." Vereesa rested her head on his shoulder and asked. The gurgle of the brook, the clamor of children — Jace's feet ached from the bone-chilling stream water. He lowered his head. "I'm just a little tired. Go keep those elf children company. I'll try again." "No rush, Jace." Vereesa smiled. "The children and I are all very patient. Those fish will listen to you. Also… remember not to cheat with shadow magic." Jace nodded, watching her wade to the shore and bound off like a nimble little deer. He stared for a long time, until the fluttering blue figure disappeared among the flowerbeds, before he couldn't help but swallow again. Just that simple motion sent a tearing pain through his throat, making him frown involuntarily. He had a powerful feeling — he was dying. This decaying body could barely hold its soul anymore. As a warlock who wielded dark magic, he knew this clearly. But what the hell was this? Was he just… so old that he was having episodes of memory loss? Dementia? Like Vereesa said — he was hallucinating again, daydreaming again, talking nonsense again? Jace tried to recall where he originally was. His brain creaked like gears in some Titan temple that hadn't moved in hundreds of years, shuddering and shaking down sheets of dust. Was this Stormwind? The Park? No. Golden forests, a profound sky the color of egg white. Beneath wispy floating clouds, dense canopies of orange and red, and among the shade, faintly visible spires of blue and gold, beautiful feathery eaves. Quel'Thalas.
