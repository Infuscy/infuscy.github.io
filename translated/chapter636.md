## Chapter 636: The World Has Moved On

The rain came down hard, veiling the whole garden in a haze of water.

The tower amid the downpour stood like a gaunt gronn giant, the faintly glowing arcane energy at its peak like a single half-open eye staring down at the lone warlock below.

By now there wasn't a soul in the Mage Quarter — the rain was simply too heavy. That was perfectly normal.

Jace stood there for a long while, watching the rainwater streaming through the brickwork beside the garden merge into a rivulet. So it's just me, then?

The stone wall before him felt cold and wrong beneath his fingers, unfamiliar. Only the door in front of him gave him any measure of ease: this was Marin's study.

"Master?" he called, knocking.

He wasn't sure why he needed to go in, but something told him he should.

No answer from within. He pushed the door open, slipped inside, and pulled it shut behind him. The room fell instantly quiet.

None of the metallic tang of burning arcane energy. No old-book smell. Just a faint, unpleasant fishiness — and the shapeless blot of shadow sitting behind the desk.

The only light came from the high window, pale and wan, rain hissing against it in layered ripples. That wasn't Marin.

"You wanted to find me," Jace said.

The figure behind the desk spoke. "You wanted to find me. I can feel that your heart is full of questions." The man's words coiled around Jace's throat like iron chains, tightening with every phrase.

Questions in my heart? Can he actually read minds?

No — he'd already confirmed the man couldn't read minds.

Having spent so long around Morophil, Jace was long accustomed to having his thoughts guessed through his emotions.

Of course, this time he was facing Deathwing himself, not a succubus who indulged his every whim. It was only natural he'd feel rattled. Jace ran his hand along the bookshelf, tracing the spines one by one, trying to steady his nerves.

Then he spotted that book — *The Troll's Romance*. Marin had casually incinerated it right in front of him when teaching him the Scorch incantation.

The black dragon hadn't lied. He really had come into Jace's dream…

Or perhaps this was just another of his tricks — a ploy to make Jace sink deeper, to make him believe every word step by step, weaving a convincing scene out of Jace's own memories to lower his guard…

No. He couldn't pull memories. That much had already been confirmed.

Jace realized he was wavering again. Staying completely calm… was impossibly hard. But he had to try.

Maybe he should attempt to sense the emotions that shadow was radiating. He needed to seize even a sliver of initiative.

But he could feel nothing. The man's shadow was a churning mass of mad black ink… linger on it too long and he'd be swept into the vortex himself, lose his mind, sink into the mire.

"I sense something different about you today," the man said abruptly.

Jace turned to look at him. What was off about him?

Had he failed to control his attitude, his emotions, some gesture? Should he not have picked up that book?

Letting his thoughts spiral this far was driving him mad, but he couldn't lose control. The most critical thing was making the other party believe him.

"I've accepted my role," Jace said.

"That is exactly what I wanted to hear," the man said.

The heart Jace had been holding in suspense eased a fraction. Just then, a question surfaced — one he'd been meaning to ask for the past couple of days but hadn't known who to ask.

"I do have a question for you."

"Go ahead."

"That orc warlock — Nek'roz — the artifact he carries. I want to know how to use it."

"This is not the time." The man stood, casually pulled a book from the shelf, and flipped it open. "The method of using it cannot be recorded on paper like so much other knowledge, nor can it be taught by word of mouth… even the most gifted caster must hold it in their own hands and feel its power for themselves. When you take it from that orc, I will guide you. I will teach you how to use it to make my self-righteous brothers and sisters grovel at your feet."

Jace nodded. "I'll kill that orc."

"And make sure you give him a painful death," the man said. "He actually dared to try to use my own thing against me — the pathetic worm. He cannot comprehend that it is my creation, as my own child. What child would ever bring itself to harm its father?"

"But don't let that distract you from the most important mission. Retrieve my property — and those dragon eggs. The eggs are what matter most."

"I understand." Jace paused, then said, "Also… about immortality, I need to confirm some details."

Upon hearing this topic, even within that mass of shadow, Jace could feel the man's sudden interest. The emotion struck Jace like an unusual ripple breaking the surface of that chaotic ink — and for Jace, it was intense enough.

Why? Did he actually intend to grant Jace immortality? Not some shoddy deception, not some word-game trick. As if this were the question he'd been waiting for.

"I don't want the kind of immortality that dragonkin receive," Jace pressed on.

The man frowned.

"I'm disappointed. You don't value my promise. You think of me as a clown who only knows how to play tricks. I thought our communication was honest."

Deathwing genuinely seemed displeased. Jace did his best to maintain his composure, but the string in his mind… was wound so tight it trembled.

He couldn't withstand Deathwing's wrath. But he had to ask.

As a warlock who had fought dragonkin, as a mage who had studied dragonkin lore at the Wizard Sanctum, as someone who had personally killed a dragonkin — he couldn't possibly be unaware that dragons could elevate mortals into dragonkin.

Dragonkin, blessed by dragon's blood, also lived far longer than ordinary mortals. Immortality could take that form — but Jace couldn't accept that form, and Deathwing surely knew it well enough.

So if he didn't ask this one extra question, it would instead seem suspicious — at least that's how Jace saw it.

Honestly, even within this dream, Jace truly found himself wondering whether he even wanted to accept Deathwing's terms anymore.

So was this question meant to convince Deathwing, or did he genuinely want to know the answer? He couldn't tell himself.

But perhaps that was for the best. If he could fool even himself, what could the other party say?

"I won't doubt you again, master," Jace said.

The shadow fell silent for a long while, then let out a low, amused snort. "That will do for now."

Jace nodded, turned, and pushed the study door open to leave. He didn't know why he performed such a meaningless action in his own dream, but he did it out of habit.

Outside, the sky had already cleared. The sunlight was a little harsh, as if the rain had never fallen. Hurried students cut across the garden path toward the entrance of the Wizard Sanctum.

Wait.

No. This wasn't a dream anymore.
