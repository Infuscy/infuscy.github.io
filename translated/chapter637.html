Chapter 637: The Crux

Jace remembered now. He'd come to Marin's study earlier — his teacher had gone off to lunch, so he'd waited a bit.

He hadn't been sleeping well for days, and the drowsiness had crept up on him. He'd dozed off against the wall, and in that short time Deathwing had slipped in through the gap?

There didn't seem much point in going in to ask Marin now. He'd originally come here to ask about the Demon Soul and Grim Batol, wanting to find out how much the Wizard Sanctum knew — after all, using a magical artifact here wasn't a matter of the mouse cursor turning into a gear and a click.

Now Deathwing had given him his answer in the teacher's place. Still, he should say goodbye to his teacher.

He turned and knocked on the study door. No sound from within.

"Mr. Sesso?"

Jace turned to find Marin holding an old book, looking right at him. "Ah, Master."

"How long have you been waiting here?"

"Not long."

Marin nodded, pulling out his key as he studied Jace. "Not sleeping well lately? I can give you some Goldthorn tea — helps restore the spirits. Much healthier than a mana potion. No charge."

The old mage pushed the door open and went in. Jace stared into the dim room, took a breath, and stepped inside. He'd meant to refuse, but right now he actually wanted to take some.

"If it really works that well…" he said, glancing at his teacher seated behind the desk. "I'll give it a try, Master."

"It really does work." Marin pulled a small string-tied packet from a drawer. "Someone told me you've learned Conjure Water already, haven't you? Cold-steeped has its own charm — takes longer, but at least you don't need to light a fire. Given the particular qualities of your fire spells, best not to go lighting fires willy-nilly."

Jace gave an awkward laugh.

Marin asked, "So have you finally found time to talk to me about Kalimdor?"

Jace took the tea packet. "Yes."

"Wait here." Marin set down his half-eaten biscuit and hurried out, leaving Jace alone in the room. Jace hadn't planned on coming to talk about this, but somehow he'd ended up agreeing anyway.

Still, did he have anything else to do this afternoon?

Not really. The ship tickets were for the day after tomorrow, and Ursula had arranged to pick up the medicine tomorrow. Before he knew it, Marin was pushing the door open again with a pot of tea.

"Right then — from when you arrived at the Steamwheedle port. The place the goblin cartel occupies is called the Steamwheedle port, isn't it?"

And so Jace gave Marin a rough account of everything he'd experienced in Kalimdor — from centaurs to tauren, from the satyrs of Maraudon to the Highborne of Eldre'Thalas, wandering orcs, gnolls living in the deep woods, the strange insects spreading beyond the desert, and the night elves dwelling on their secluded island.

Right up to the moment he'd parted ways with Musa Woodmane on that shore.

Recalling that moment again, picturing the lone tauren on the coastline, Jace actually felt a weight lift from him. Compared with Musa Woodmane's circumstances, what he himself faced was nothing.

An endless flight for survival amid the malice of countless villains, day after day facing the life and death of his people, all while bearing the responsibility of passing on the last embers of his tribe's nature magic.

By comparison, all he had to do was enter Grim Batol and play out a story he already knew by heart. For others it might mean glory in success or a heroic end in failure — for him, it was simply walking someone else's road a second time.

After bidding his teacher farewell in the evening, Jace grabbed a quick bite and went home. He lay in bed, turning the matter of the Demon Soul over in his mind.

He knew the Demon Soul controlled the Dragonqueen. Find the way to control the Demon Soul and he could liberate the Dragonqueen. Achieve that, and no matter how many enemies filled Grim Batol, none of it would matter.

Deathwing surely knew this too, which was why he would never willingly teach Jace how to use the Demon Soul. Because once Jace killed the Dragonmaw leader Nek'roz and seized the Demon Soul, that alone would be enough to bring the entire Dragonmaw clan to heel — there'd be no need to learn how to actually use it.

The orcs weren't fools either. They could see the device that controlled the Dragonqueen already in someone else's hands, the survival of the entire fortress held in another's grip. Any further resistance would be pointless.

Thinking this far, Jace was almost certain that Deathwing would cook up every excuse in the book to keep him from truly mastering the Demon Soul. He claimed he didn't care about the Dragonqueen's fate… but who knew what he was really thinking.

The Demon Soul had once been cursed by the other Dragon Aspects so that its creator Deathwing couldn't touch it. Had it not been so, Deathwing certainly wouldn't have gone to such lengths to trick mortals like Nek'roz or Jace into wielding the artifact against the other dragons — he'd have done it himself.

He itched to wipe out every other Aspect in the world and leave the black dragons ruling alone. But that was fine — Jace had his own methods.

He glanced at Morophil beside him. If Deathwing wouldn't tell him, then he'd let that Master Nek'roz tell him personally. With that thought, he forcibly suppressed the surge of triumph in his spirit, careful not to let Deathwing detect the violent shift in his emotions.

From that sudden dream intrusion at noon, Jace had nearly confirmed his judgment: Deathwing, like Morophil — or himself — could infer another's thoughts through their emotions. It wasn't true mind-reading, but it was dangerous enough.

And Deathwing's understanding of Shadow surely far exceeded his own and the succubus's. How deep could his guesses go?

No amount of caution was too much.

After slowly calming down, the next key question was how to shatter the Demon Soul — to return the Aspects' power that Deathwing had tricked out of them with such effort a thousand years ago back to the Aspects themselves, and gain the capital to defeat Deathwing.

Without the Aspects' power, all the other Aspects combined were no match for Deathwing — even wounded as he was on Draenor and not at his peak, the Aspects were far weaker by comparison.

Rhonin had used one of Deathwing's scales to cut open the Demon Soul — a scale that Deathwing had originally given Rhonin to keep him under his control throughout the whole affair.

Deathwing had poured the power of the other four Guardian Aspects into the artifact so that they couldn't so much as budge it. He'd cunningly kept his own Aspect power for himself — but in doing so, had turned his own body into the very weapon that could destroy the artifact…

Jace had no scale. He touched the black sigil on his arm. He wondered if this could serve some purpose? This was all completely different.

He felt he couldn't just follow the old route step by step, retracing it exactly.

Maybe here, perhaps there — by the time he reached the end, the deviations might be enormous, or he might have gone completely in the opposite direction.

Well, one step at a time then. He had no way back anyway.

He hadn't even reached Grim Batol yet, and Rhonin hadn't obtained that scale before setting out either.

These details about the Demon Soul — let alone Rhonin, even Krasus probably didn't know them this thoroughly. This was his key advantage, and he absolutely could not afford to overlook it.

"Put me under, Morophil."
