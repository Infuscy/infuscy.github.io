Chapter 638: The Wetlands, Once More

The next afternoon, Jace arrived at the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb. The hellhounds at the door obediently moved aside at the sight of him, even hurrying ahead toward the alchemy lab as if to lead the way.

Spend enough time around demons like hellhounds and you slowly forget how ugly and vicious their faces are — you start to see their endearing side. Jace could only hope this wasn't the Shadow rotting his sense of aesthetics.

In the alchemy lab, Ursula was busy sorting bottles and pouches. Before she even saw who it was, she called out, "Jace, is that you?"

"It's me." Jace came to the doorway. "All set?"

Ursula slapped two medicine pouches into Jace's chest. "It's all here — mana potions, Fire and Shadow protection potions, invisibility potions… restorative potions, that's the lot, isn't it? I also specially prepared two padded pouches for you, so you don't accidentally smash anything. A lot of these bottles are expensive crystal — the jostling on the road could easily cause problems."

"Plus the refined wisdom elixir, and that strong Shadow protection potion I gave you earlier…" She hesitated, then went on. "I don't mean to go looking for trouble, but are you really not going to tell me what you're off to do? The things you've prepared make it feel like you're going up against an army. It's terrifying."

You're dead-on, Jace thought. "I'll tell you all about it when I get back."

"Fine. Won't ask, then, since you still won't tell me at this point." Ursula helped Jace tuck the potion pouches into his robes and straightened his sleeves. "Whatever it is, I hope these potions of mine are some help to you. If you don't come back, I'll feel like it's my fault — that I wasn't good enough and got you killed. I'll regret it for the rest of my life."

Jace said quietly, "Maybe I'm just heading to Dalaran to sell potions."

Ursula patted the coin pouch at her waist. "Then you'd be losing a hell of a money-making opportunity… Want me to take the rest of the money back? Otherwise I'm afraid you'll starve to death halfway through your smuggling trip."

"I won't starve to death even if I do die." Jace pulled a scroll from the demon bag slung over his shoulder and handed it to Ursula. "Keep the money. Just hold onto this for me."

"What is it?" Ursula asked.

"A ritual circle," Jace explained. "I brought it back from Kalimdor — a circle that can severely weaken a demon's power. If Galders and the others ever lose their minds and try to summon a felguard, or something even stronger, I figure this circle should at least give everyone a fighting chance."

"Galders will be thrilled to see this. This really is a gift when it's needed most." Ursula took the scroll and unrolled it a little to glance at it. "My demonic isn't quite up to snuff, but Erland did mention before that Galders was planning to try summoning a more powerful demon servant. The reason he never went ahead with it was that he wasn't confident he could handle whatever he called over."

"I promised Galders I'd bring the results of the Kalimdor trip back to him. Consider the promise kept." Jace said. The alchemist rolled the scroll back up, thought for a moment, then asked, "So how long are you actually going to be gone?"

"That's the thing," Jace said. "I don't know how long. So anything I can help with, I'll help with."

"You're really making me feel uneasy, Jace," Ursula said.

"Watch your own hide first," Jace said. "Don't make me come back and hear you got dragged off to Stratholme or who-knows-where up north, and now I've got to go rescue you."

"Stop talking nonsense." Ursula glared.

"When have I ever come to harm? Last time the whole Alliance thought I was dead, and I still came back alive in the end." He cinched the demon bag tight. "Don't worry, Ursula. We'll see each other again soon."

With that, he bolted straight out of the Slaughtered Lamb.

That was the last one. No more familiar faces to see, nothing more to entrust to anyone.

Back home, he brewed a pot of tea and settled in for the night. Once tomorrow came and he stepped onto the ship bound for the Wetlands, he wouldn't have to think about any of this.

At the Stormwind docks, Jace had been crouching on the pier watching the sea since before dawn. Since returning from Kalimdor, the days had grown colder one by one.

He hoped that when he finally had to face Deathwing's flames, it would be deep winter — that the mountains around Grim Batol would be blanketed in heavy snow. That way, at least, he'd feel like Azeroth was on his side.

Thinking that, Jace felt he was losing it a little.

Morophil was sitting a dozen meters down the dock, of course — fully invisible. But even from here Jace could sense her presence.

A succubus he'd once been utterly unwilling to trust had now become the only companion at his side. Whatever the demon's original motive had been — power, mana — none of it mattered anymore.

Ah, right. And Sazeno.

If he survived this, he'd never say another harsh word to the little imp again. Thinking that, he hugged the demon bag in his arms a little tighter.

At that moment, he began to understand why so many warlocks eventually fell that final step — why they threw their lot in completely with the demons, let Shadow and fel energy flood their bodies, and even transformed themselves into demonic monsters no different from satyrs or man'ari eredar.

When everyone around you only suspects you, and all you have at your side are the powers of darkness and those steadfast creatures of the Twisting Nether who follow you without question — it really does shake your original convictions.

Thinking this, Jace glanced toward the city gate near the harbor, his eyes flickering over every dwarf who passed.

He didn't know what he was hoping for — hoping Grede would suddenly show up? Then what was the point of all those things he'd said at dinner a few days ago?

Before long, the crew were already urging the passengers aboard. Jace came up onto the deck, his gaze still unwilling to leave the white tiles and grey walls of Stormwind.

Two gryphon riders mounted by dwarven knights swept past near the spire of the Cathedral of Light, heading toward Stormwind Keep in the distance.

Since the War of the Dark Portal, the Ironforge gryphon cavalry had been stationed permanently in Stormwind. Jace had watched the gryphon nests near the Valley of Heroes take more and more shape.

This world was getting closer and closer to the Azeroth he'd known in his past life — the Cathedral of Light rising step by step, the statues of Turalyon and the other Sons of Lothar, the city walls crowned with gryphon nests.

Everyone was fighting to protect what mattered most to them. He could hesitate no longer.

With that thought, Jace braced himself against the hatchway and walked down the stairs, listening to the crew's thickly accented shouts above as the ship's hull rocked once.

If this actually succeeded in the end, if he didn't pluck every last feather off the Red Dragonflight, he wouldn't be called Jace Sesso.
