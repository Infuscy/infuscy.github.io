## Chapter 639: Don't Know, Don't Care

The sailing ship did not have an easy time on the sea. Battling miserable weather, it took a full fortnight to reach its destination, and by the time it docked at Menethil Harbor it was already late October.

The cold came on fast, and Khaz Modan was naturally chillier than the Kingdom of Stormwind to begin with. Wrapped in his heavy void robes, Jace still felt the cold wind starting to cut right through.

This journey reminded him of the first time he'd traveled alone to Booty Bay… carrying the deathly shadow left behind by Teron Gorefiend, not knowing when he might suddenly drop dead and turn into a vengeful spirit.

Especially since this time he'd also brought a restorative potion along — all the ingredients present and accounted for.

Though both trips had carried the threat of death, Jace could clearly sense that his state of mind was very different.

He didn't know why, but last time he'd also been worried he might die suddenly and become some kind of vengeful spirit… yet it hadn't weighed on him nearly as heavily as this time.

The ship docked at the harbor. Jace walked down the gangway onto the pier, where a fine drizzle was still falling from the sky — the feeling of it on his skin was indistinguishable from a thick, wet fog.

He gazed at the mist-shrouded walls of Menethil Harbor, the already massive fortress taking shape, the coming and going of travelers, the jarring empty spaces where orcish ruins and leftover supplies had been cleared away.

He roughly understood why his mindset was different this time.

If he died, the Dragonqueen Alexstrasza would likely meet her end in Grim Batol.

And after that, just as Krasus had said — Menethil Harbor, the whole of the Wetlands, all of Azeroth itself — there was no telling where any of it would end up.

It wasn't like last time, where if he died he could always just become a vengeful spirit, at worst drag a few unlucky souls down with him, and in the end either dissipate into nothing or get cleansed by some priest or paladin. The most impact it would have on the world would be one more ghost story out of Booty Bay.

By now the sky had darkened a little. Jace walked toward the Deepwater Inn half-distractedly when a sudden flapping of wings beside him snapped him right back to full alertness.

A massive grey gryphon was crouched right beside the gryphon nests not far from the inn — right on the path from the dock to the inn. At that moment it was raising its broad wing high, stretching it upward, then shaking it a moment later.

The reason it wasn't crouched inside the nest was that its body was simply too large — it made the small yellow Hinterlands gryphons inside look like a clowder of scrawny little cats.

This was a rare Alterac gryphon, beautiful as a proud young dragon. It craned its high neck and stared at Jace, its eyes unreadable.

For some reason, Jace felt as though looking at it was a little… familiar.

As Jace drew closer, the gryphon let out a series of puttering sounds, like a steam locomotive starting up, and stretched its neck slowly toward him.

This forced Jace to take a step back. He was on his way to Grim Batol to save the Dragonqueen — he could hardly go and get his belly torn open by this gryphon in Menethil Harbor after all that careful planning about saving the world.

The gryphon, seeing that it had startled Jace, let out a couple of guilty-sounding heavy breaths and drew its neck back in, flattening its feathers as if to show it meant no threat.

Morophil, behind him, showed no sign of going on alert — which told her she didn't consider the beast a threat either, confirming Jace's judgment.

Could this big fellow actually recognize him?

Though it felt inexplicably familiar, Jace genuinely couldn't remember where he'd seen this gryphon before.

"Keep your distance, mage!"

A Wildhammer dwarf in a red jacket pointed at Jace. "Keep back! If you want to get to the inn, go around the other way — don't come past here! Our big friend here can easily hurt someone even when he's being friendly, especially humans like you with no experience!"

Jace nodded and made his way to the inn entrance from the side. Before going in, he glanced back at the gryphon — and found it still staring at him. He turned and entered the Deepwater Inn, rounding the corner of the entryway, and a wave of dwarven laughter and shouting hit him in the face.

He leaned on his staff and stood in the doorway for a moment. Inside was already packed to the gills at this hour — not an empty seat in sight. Chances were he wouldn't even get a room tonight and would have to find some sheltered spot outside to sleep.

Then Jace felt Morophil lightly tap his shoulder. Had she lost her mind?!

Dwarves were everywhere out here, patrols of guards outside, and from where he stood he could even see a couple of mages behind the counter — if demonic aura was detected here, he'd probably be… right on the spot, no question.

Wait.

He caught sight of a familiar face in the corner behind the counter. That black-bearded dwarf.

The dwarf was staring right at him, tapping the table with a finger to alert his companion sitting across from him in a deep green cloak. The figure turned around, and the arcane glint in their eyes was clearly visible even in the shadow of the hood.

She rose to her feet without expression, pushed past the soldiers, sailors, and mages beside her, and walked straight over. Watching her, Jace felt his heart hammer.

"How are you two here…"

Vereesa didn't answer. She drove a fist straight into Jace's jaw, sending him stumbling back several steps until he hit the wall… Jace's vision went dark and he nearly blacked out on the spot. It took a fierce shake of his head to claw his way back to consciousness.

He'd never crossed blows with Vereesa before. That one hit felt like taking a cannonball to the face. He should have known — the arm strength of a ranger who could casually pierce a young dragon's scales with an arrow.

Now, trying to steady himself against the wall, he was seized by the collar by Vereesa, who yanked him close.

The tavern seemed to fall silent for a moment, then grew lively again, as if no one cared to pay attention to the commotion over here. In the firelight, Jace stared into Vereesa's eyes — tears had already blurred the arcane glow.

"What is this, Jace Sesso?" she asked, her voice low. "Did you think you'd hold us back?" Jace wiped the blood from his split lip with the back of his hand and shook his head.

Vereesa leaned in, fixed her eyes on him, and went on in a low voice: "I threw myself into danger beneath the walls of Nethergarde Keep to pull you from the siege of felguards — so that you could come here alone without a word and throw your life away? Do you have any idea what Grede and I went through for you?"

Jace shook his head.

"I tried to talk her out of it several times," Grede said, looking helpless. "I meant — best to follow you first and see what you were really up to. But she refused. She insisted on coming head-on to hit you. There aren't even rangers like that in Quel'Thalas…"

Jace forced his mouth open against the searing pain across half his face, let out a breath, and managed to glance at Grede. "But I do think you had it coming too," the dwarf added, scratching his beard.

Jace looked at the two of them in front of him, swallowed the saliva and blood in his mouth, and swallowed down the surge of emotion that had nearly broken free and spiraled completely out of control.

"You don't know what I'm doing."

"Just playing the lone hero, is all." Grede grinned. "Damn it, lad — learned to take on private jobs and hide it from your own best friend?"

"Don't know, don't care," Vereesa said coldly. "All I know is that whatever you're going off to do, you're not dying alone."
