## Chapter 640: No Escape

Back at the table, Jace rubbed his face. He had to admit that last punch had been a solid one.

Seeming to read that the expressions on this side of the table weren't too cheerful, Vereesa said quietly, "I'm sorry. I shouldn't have hit that hard."

Jace shook his head. He certainly wasn't upset because he'd been hit. Facing these two familiar faces — one wearing a look of pure schadenfreude, the other laced with fierce worry beneath the reproach — the crushing loneliness that had dogged him all the way here, so strong it had felt almost impossible to fight, had been melted away without effort. But in its place came a fresh tangle of conflict.

They didn't know what he was doing, and of course they didn't know who the enemy was. Was he really going to bring them into Grim Batol like this, let them face the threat of Deathwing with no mental preparation at all?

But thinking further, the reason Krasus had told him not to tell anyone was that he feared Deathwing's eyes and ears were everywhere — that no matter who he told, Deathwing might learn of the operation's purpose.

The reason he himself hadn't wanted to tell anyone was, first, he didn't want others to worry about him, and second, he was afraid Grede or Vereesa would stubbornly insist on following him into a danger that he alone should have to bear.

Now, facing these two in front of him, both reasons had lost all meaning.

Deathwing didn't just have a crystal-clear picture of his every move — he even knew that Krasus was coming for him. He even had a clear read on everything Jace said, everything he did, every feeling in his heart.

And then, Vereesa and Grede were already here. He couldn't very well sneak out of Menethil Harbor in the middle of the night and flee to Grim Batol, could he?

He wasn't confident he could escape Vereesa's pursuit in the vast Wetlands — even if she were a half-tracked tracker, she'd only look inadequate next to the night elf Sentinel corps' manhunt.

If she wanted to, she could catch him within two or three days, no problem. She wouldn't even need to sleep at all — it might not take two or three days.

"So now you're going to explain everything properly, right?" Grede said.

"You might regret hearing it," Jace said.

Grede glared, sprang up, leaned across the table, and cracked a palm strike against Jace's forehead. The blow made Jace's head spin and his eyes swim…

…But at least it was better than Vereesa's punch.

"I don't think you've had enough," the dwarf muttered.

"Stop, stop." Jace held his head. "I'll talk, I'll talk."

He glanced at Vereesa. Hearing that he was finally going to explain what this trip was really about, the ranger's ears perked up. A elf's curiosity was truly impossible to hide. Jace also knew she couldn't genuinely not care.

"Grim Batol."

"I knew it." Grede rapped the table and looked at Vereesa. "See? Didn't I say so? Those Kirin Tor folks — just itching to get their hands on our Wildhammer homeland!"

"I heard the Kirin Tor suspects Deathwing is still on Azeroth," Vereesa said. "Could it be that he's hiding inside Grim Batol, protected by the Dragonmaw orcs and the Blackrock orcs? The Kirin Tor means to send you alone to scout and confirm?"

"Deathwing?!" Grede practically mouthed the word, staring at the elf. "Why didn't you tell me this earlier?"

Vereesa said, "I only overheard Lady Sylvanas and the Archmage discussing it. I wasn't even sure how clearly I'd heard. Besides, if I'd mentioned that Jace might have been ordered to go looking for Deathwing, would you still have followed me here?"

"Why wouldn't I?" Grede said. "You think I'm some coward who shrinks from a fight?" Vereesa said quietly, "I honestly couldn't tell…"

The dwarf grumbled, "You pointy-eared folk — don't know a damn thing and still run your mouths."

The elf slammed the table and went for the dwarf's mouth. The dwarf caught her wrist; his other hand went for her ear. In the end, Vereesa's arm was longer, and she seized his beard first… Jace coughed. The two of them glanced around, thought about the topic they'd just been discussing, and decided to let go, stand down, and retreat to their respective corners to cool off.

"For Musa's sake," Vereesa said under her breath. "I'll spare your life."

The dwarf straightened the braids in his beard, glanced at Jace, and in the end just: "Pah."

Only then did Jace notice the bruises and lacerations all over the elf's arm and across Grede's neck and face — these two had probably already thrown down more than once.

The tavern was getting more crowded. By now plenty of people were standing along the walls and by the windows, drinking and talking as they went. The whole hall was deafeningly chaotic. Fortunately, the crowd around Jace's table wasn't too bad yet.

"Not to go looking for Deathwing," Jace said, keeping his voice as low as he could. "To find the Dragonqueen. The Kirin Tor suspects the orcs have imprisoned the Dragonqueen inside Grim Batol, so they handed this matter to me."

By the time he said this, he knew he had to face the trial of the black dragon sigil.

"If possible, rescue the Dragonqueen — that's what Master Krasus of the Kirin Tor hopes I can accomplish."

How to make the truth sound like a lie — this was still an unfamiliar territory for him. But Jace was no stranger to the craft of lying, and he could feel the difference between when he was lying and when he was telling the truth. Otherwise he never could have fooled a succubus who could read the currents of a person's heart.

He only hoped Deathwing wouldn't take him too seriously — that he'd see Jace as nothing more than a warlock who wanted immortality and power above all else, a fool smitten by the promise of love, and wouldn't invest too much effort in digging into whether his words were true or false. That way Jace could be a little more at ease, instead of being crushed under extreme pressure from start to finish.

He still had Krasus to deal with. Krasus and the Kirin Tor were probably close to seeing through the disguise of the Alterac noble Daval Prestor — they'd be on the verge of storming his mansion any time soon.

Go deal with him quickly. Stop giving me trouble.

That was a real red dragon. Jace was just a small warlock skulking in the shadows where the light couldn't reach. No threat at all.

"Is that all?" Grede raised his eyes. "Me — a veteran of the Second War. Among the Wildhammer dwarves still alive, my experience fighting orcs is, if not second to none, then firmly among the top — hold on, don't bring up Redridge, that orc wasn't like ordinary orcs. The point is, you kept the truth from me just because you were going off to deal with a pack of orcs? Don't you think that's a little too insulting?"

"I've always regretted not truly fighting in the orc wars, Jace," Vereesa said. "Killing a death knight and a few Amani trolls who joined the Horde was far from enough to quench my fury. Not to mention now I have a fresh grudge against them — the Dark Portal… I have even more reason to go with you than Grede does."

Grede said unhappily, "You know where my name comes from, little elf?"

"What about it?" Vereesa asked.

"Other than Jace, I've never told another soul." Grede took a swig of ale, belched, and said, "The name my father gave me — Grim Batol Xifeels. Tell her what that means."

"The Heart of Grim Batol," Jace said.
