## Chapter 656: The Missing Orcs

The afternoon in the Bathor Mountains was cool and clear. Jace stood on the hillside gazing into the distance, enjoying a rare moment when he didn't need to rush to do anything important. Below him, a large group of Dragonmaw orcs searched everywhere through the mountains and river valleys, while young dragons flew back and forth overhead. He watched them bustle about, feeling not a ripple of disturbance in his heart.

Morfil leaned against Jace, a smile perpetually fixed on her face, likewise watching the orcs come and go in silence without a word.

Last night, a six-man Dragonmaw patrol had vanished—no bodies, no sign of life. It was hard to say whether the orcs here were searching for their missing comrades or for the killer.

As the sun neared the horizon, deep shadows spilled across the hillside. Suddenly, a burst of angry shouting from nearby shattered the tranquil atmosphere.

Jace looked up the slope. Nekros was gripping his son by the neck, his white hair flying and spit spraying as he screamed at him. Judging by the expression on young Nekrosh's face, the words being exchanged were anything but pleasant.

Mid-tirade, the old warlord seemed to notice eyes on them from below. He glared over, shoved the strapping young orc aside, and hobbled on his crutch down the slope until he stood right in front of Jace.

Without ceremony, he seized the front of Jace's robe with one hand and roared, "Your schemes! Your doing!"

Jace asked evenly, "So you've confirmed it was the Blackrock orcs?"

"Don't play games with me." Nekros gripped tighter, snarling. "A patrol goes missing the very day I arrive in Grim Batol—what are the odds of that?"

The warlord's strength was astonishing. He nearly lifted Jace clean off the ground, and Jace was no scrawny orc himself these days.

Jace lowered his eyes to the warlord's scarred, powerful wrist and said, "It's precisely because the Warchief foresaw the danger closing in that he dispatched me to Grim Batol in haste to assess the state of the Dragonmaw clan, before the situation spirals further out of control. You've got the causality backwards."

Nekros's features twisted, his lower jaw jutting forward, the yellow tusks nearly jabbing his own nose. But after a moment he released his grip and stepped back.

Jace straightened his robe. "Did you find out who did it?"

Nekros coughed. "Still looking. Our patrols, our dragonriders—they're doing everything they can. I won't let anyone waltz in here, kill my people, and stroll out again. Whoever it is dies here."

"Deserters, maybe." Jace looked toward the distance. "There are orcs here who came from Lordaeron and Khaz Modan. Naturally, there are also those who've left. That's normal enough."

At that, Nekros flared up. "We found blood. Dragonmaw blood. They were killed, outsider—are you insulting my clan? Which clan are you from? I want to know if your clan holds battlefield honor in the same regard. Maybe you serve Blackrock Spire, maybe you're the Warchief's right hand, but you are no Blackrock. Your skin, your accent—nothing about you matches theirs. No Blackrock would ever spout such rot! Tell me—which clan are you from?"

"Shadowmoon," Jace said.

At the word Shadowmoon, Nekros visibly hesitated. That was the clan of the true Warchief on the other side of the Dark Portal—Ner'zhul—and the clan of Teron Gorefiend. A great many of its members belonged to the Shadow Council, and even within the Horde they were shrouded in mystery.

Jace knew his Orcish could never be as fluent as a true Blackrock's, so he'd committed to the performance and fabricated a simple backstory that wouldn't be easy to disprove.

There were no Shadowmoon orcs left on Azeroth. And if there were, they were hiding in some hole somewhere, unwilling to show themselves. No one was going to step forward and expose him.

Before he'd fallen under Gul'dan's tutelage in the dark arts, the Shadowmoon clan had followed the ancient shamanic traditions. The Dragonmaw's warchief, Zuluhed, was an elder and a seasoned shaman. If he were here, he might have seen through Jace's act, but Jace sincerely doubted that this old warrior-turned-warlord-makeshift-warlock had any deep understanding of the enigmatic Shadowmoon.

Still, Nekros was a suspicious sort, so Jace had prepared other explanations to fend off his questioning. It looked like he wouldn't need them after all.

The warlord asked nothing further, and the aggression drained from his expression.

He could probably read emotions the way a succubus did, gauging whether someone was lying. And Jace was an expert at that—even Deathwing hadn't been able to see through his lies.

"Now you know that the total defeat of the Horde is absolutely unacceptable to me," Jace said in a warning tone. "I won't let your pride, or some inexplicable hatred of black dragons, sabotage my grand plan to help the Warchief reopen the Dark Portal, Warlord. Your Dragonmaw clan wants to stay here forever—but I can't. I need to return to Draenor, to Shadowmoon Valley, my homeland, even if it's been utterly destroyed by demonic power."

"So," Jace said, cradling Gorefiend's staff as he spoke, his eyes never leaving the lower slopes, "are you going to keep watching them search pointlessly until dark, or are you going to ask for my help?"

"Let's see what clever tricks you have, then."

Nekros stepped aside with his crutch. Jace glanced at him and headed down the slope.

When he reached the spot where the Dragonmaw orcs had found the blood, Jace surveyed the scene. He had to admit that Grede and Vereesa had taken "leave no trace" almost literally. There were only a few drops of blood on the grass blades, some bloodstained dust further off, and nothing else whatsoever.

All these Dragonmaw orcs searching far and wide, and they couldn't even find the bodies?

Then again, considering Vereesa's experience dealing with trolls back in Quel'Thalas, it wasn't that surprising. When it came to tracking, trolls were far more skilled than orcs. A ranger who could handle trolls would find orcs a simpler proposition.

"Now that you've seen it all, Yoruk—what do you think did this?" Nekros asked, wearing an expression that said he was waiting to be entertained.

Jace looked around. "Could it be the red dragons? Are you certain every red dragon in Grim Batol obeys your orders? Something this clean, with the bodies completely vanished—short of them all being snatched up and eaten by the dragons flying overhead, I can't think of a more likely explanation."

"That's nonsense. Grim Batol's hold over the red dragons has been battle-tested," Nekros said through gritted teeth, but clearly he'd grown uncertain.

Morfil glanced at Jace with a smile. It wasn't a succubus's signature fake smile—it was genuine.

Jace knew what she meant. Nekros was wavering more and more, drowning in self-doubt. That was fine. The main course hadn't been served yet.
