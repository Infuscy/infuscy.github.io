## Chapter 657: The Dragonmaw Clan's Trump Card

"So that's your conclusion—the red dragons did it?" Nekros said, displeased.

Jace shook his head. "As masters of magic, I believe you'd agree we possess far more methods of investigation than ordinary scouts."

He stepped into the middle of the path, raised his hand, and spoke an incantation. It was a tracking spell he'd heard Vereesa chant countless times back in Kalimdor—used to trace sympathetic residues.

But given his arcane talents, even if he pronounced the incantation perfectly and got the gestures right, he still might not be able to cast it at all, let alone pull it off with this slapdash performance. So when he finished, not a wisp of arcane energy had gathered, and his disguise remained completely uncompromised.

But the spell was genuinely impressive-sounding, especially to someone like Nekros, who'd fought Alliance mages and knew what arcane incantations were supposed to sound like. To him, it rang true.

When he'd finished, Jace walked straight ahead to a shady patch of forest, bent down, and searched for a while. Then he pulled two elven hairs from his sleeve and held them between his fingers.

Jace turned back, holding up the silver strands that glinted faintly in the sunlight. "An unexpected find."

"What do you mean?" Nekros asked.

Jace explained, "I assumed something this clean had to be the work of a Dalaran mage—Dalaran's magic is endlessly varied, after all—so I meant to use a spell to detect residual magical traces. I never expected to find this."

"What is this?" Nekros leaned in to look at the two long hairs, then said, "Elven?"

"You fought through the campaigns in Lordaeron, Warlord," Jace said. "I have no doubt—these are elven hairs. Elves are a race that lives around a wellspring of magic, nourished by the arcane. Their traces are especially visible under Detect Magic."

"An elf mage?" Nekros said. "So the elven kingdom really has gotten involved. There shouldn't be any elves around Grim Batol…"

"You should have thought of this day, Warlord, when you sent the red dragons to assault Silvermoon alongside Gul'dan," Jace said.

"You seem unhappy about my training the red dragons to aid the Horde," Nekros said.

Jace gave a derisive laugh. "You don't care for reckless use of black magic, and you don't care for black dragons—I've got no quarrel with that, Warlord. But the red dragons are likewise an unsustained source of power. In a sense, they're no different from black magic and black dragons in what they offer Blackrock Spire. One day you'll run out of dragon eggs, unable to secure more aid from the red dragons. By then the Horde will have grown accustomed to the support of mighty dragons, and once the dragons are gone, we'll weaken rapidly. What good is rebuilding the Horde then…"

"What I mean is, when you ordered the red dragons to attack Quel'Thalas, you should have expected that one day the elves would take their revenge when you couldn't mobilize so many dragons—even something like this… an ambush. An assassination."

He continued, "Furthermore, in my assessment, it's unlikely a single elf mage could have handled six orcs without using arcane energy. There are clearly no arcane traces here, so this was likely the work of Farstrider units. These rangers ambushed six Dragonmaw soldiers in one strike and left only two hairs behind. That alone proves Quel'Thalas has sent its most elite Farstriders to Grim Batol on a reconnaissance mission. These Dragonmaw soldiers weren't killed because they were incompetent—they were killed because they'd keenly sensed the rangers' presence, forcing the elves to silence them in the night and cover the evidence of their movements. This may not have been simple revenge."

"You think this means the Alliance is about to launch an offensive, don't you?" Nekros said with a cold laugh. "From start to finish, that's the only point you've been trying to make. You think I don't know? If you ask me, you're just believing what you want to believe."

"I'm an emissary of Blackrock Spire, Warlord. I'm only here to deliver a message," Jace said. "What I believe doesn't matter. What you believe, what the Warchief believes—that's what matters."

The creased smile still lingered on Nekros's face, but it had grown strained.

Jace went on. "I'll report everything here to the Warchief exactly as it is. The mighty Dragonmaw legion, the massive gates, the towering walls, the sound defensive works—excellent. It reminds me of Hellfire Citadel."

The Dragonmaw warlord's expression grew uglier by the second. Everyone knew that terrifying fortress, built with demonic aid—even with the black dragons' help, it hadn't held out against the Alliance Expedition's assault for three days.

Morfil said softly from the side, "Master, you've forgotten—Warlord Nekros has so many plump, healthy dragon eggs. Once they hatch, once they grow into whelps one by one, they'll be in time to help Grim Batol withstand the Alliance's offensive…"

"Shut up, demon." Nekros fixed his eyes on the succubus. "You have no place speaking here." Morfil glanced at Nekros and turned her head away in contempt.

"Be that as it may…"

Jace reached out and brushed Morfil's cheek. When the succubus met her master's eyes, she smiled again, delighted.

The soul link between them allowed Jace to read Morfil's expression more keenly. Through that brief exchange of glances, Morfil had already been exerting her influence without Nekros noticing. As to whether it had taken effect… from the hint of satisfaction in her expression, it probably had.

"I need to set out for Blackrock Spire at once. Grim Batol doesn't seem particularly keen on my staying. Still, Warlord, I sincerely hope we'll have the chance to fight side by side again someday."

With that, he turned and walked toward the wooden gate down the slope.

"Perhaps you've forgotten one thing—there's a reason Grim Batol has held out this long, Master Yoruk." Nekros called after him.

Jace turned back. The Dragonmaw warlord was staring him down.

"As long as the Dragonqueen lives, the Dragonmaw clan will never be defeated."

"The black dragons say she's already dead by your hands," Jace said. "Though I'm surprised you still have so many dragon eggs. I admit the situation is far better than Blackrock Spire anticipated."

Nekros asked, "Are you going to believe the ravings of a mad black dragon, or your own eyes?"

"You'll take me to see her?" Jace asked.

"If it will persuade the Warchief to reconsider his relationship with the black dragons," Nekros said.

"I can't give you a hundred percent guarantee," Jace said. "But if she truly is alive and still under your control, Warlord, then I will do everything I can to speak on behalf of the Dragonmaw clan."

"Tomorrow morning," Nekros said. "My men and I need time to prepare for the audience."
