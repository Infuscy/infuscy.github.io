## Chapter 658: Taming Dragons

That evening, back inside the gates, Jace could already sense that the imp was much closer.

Though he still couldn't see it with Sense Demons, it was clear that if he could feel its presence here, the imp had already infiltrated Grim Batol.

All day, many patrols and dragonriders had been out searching for the killer or the traces of the missing orcs—they must have seized the chance to slip in.

But Nekros's mention of needing another day before they could see the Dragonqueen was a hard pill to swallow. Another night without sleep, and Jace might truly become more exhausted, his mind drifting—though that was still far better than being dragged into Deathwing's dreams.

He wondered if activating the Flowing Ice Crystal once would restore his energy, at least enough to offset two nights' worth of fatigue.

When he reached the room where he was resting, just as he was thinking about how to deal with Nekros inside the Dragonqueen's prison, he suddenly heard a faint "Jace Sesso…"

He spun around, thinking some orc had recognized him—but then he realized that wasn't an orc's voice. It was the imp, calling his name again.

Morfil had already told them where the Dragonqueen was being held, so this wasn't to inform Jace of her location. And the call was clearly weak—they must be in trouble.

Jace could sense the imp somewhere on a lower level of the city, but to pinpoint its location with Sense Demons, he'd need to get closer.

Two Dragonmaw guards were stationed outside his room. It would be hard to slip away and help directly. Nekros must have figured that a single orc guard might fall to Morfil's charm, so he'd posted two.

"I'd like to see your dragon eggs again," Jace said.

"Speak to the Warlord for permission," one guard said to the other.

"Zog—Zog." The guard pounded a fist to his chest and trotted toward the Cadros Anvil. "We'll wait here. Go nowhere, emissary."

The guard stepped aside, eyeing Morfil with a strained expression, as if constipated, clearly terrified the demon would seize the chance to charm him.

Jace had no interest in wasting Morfil's precious mana on this fellow. After a while, the other guard ran back and nodded. "The Warlord has granted it."

"Then lead the way, guards," the first one said. "We'll take you."

"Very well," Jace said.

Following the two Dragonmaw orcs through the city and down the stairs to the lower level, Jace kept sweeping the area with Sense Demons. Finally, in a dark dwarven chamber, he detected the imp.

That must be where Vereesa and Grede were hiding.

Not far from there, a downward passage led straight to the hatchery corridor. But from this dwarven room all the way to the entrance of that corridor was a broad platform where a group of Dragonmaw orcs was currently training a whelp to accept a dragonrider.

There was nowhere to hide here. The walls had only a few tall stone pillars that provided minimal cover, but for an elf and a dwarf to dart across openly, even with the pillars for concealment, it would be hard to avoid the notice of the orc trainers.

That was probably the trouble they'd encountered. Drinking invisibility potions here—even two each—would never get them through the corridor, into the hatchery, and then into the Dragonqueen's chamber.

They had to get into the hatchery and lie in wait until tomorrow, when the doors opened.

Amidst a chorus of displeased snarls, Jace searched for somewhere to hide or an opening for the two of them to slip through. Then he focused on the whelp.

This whelp was in considerably better shape than the one Jace had seen before—sturdier and more obedient—but overall it was still a weak creature. It was clear the whelp wasn't exactly thrilled with the orcs' training either.

"Are all your whelps like this?" Jace asked.

"All of them are full of fighting spirit, emissary," one of the guards said. Jace thought it needed a good deal more fighting spirit than that.

He glanced back at Morfil, then at the whelp. Morfil narrowed her eyes slightly. Jace knew she understood.

"I wouldn't call that fighting spirit. That's extreme discontent and rage." Jace chuckled. "I'd hate to imagine what surprises await on the battlefield riding a mount like that."

The guard looked over. "We Dragonmaw are masters of beast training. The Blackrock clan is better at hiding behind their fortress and thick walls. Let the professionals handle what they're good at, warlock—don't lecture us on things you don't understand!"

"You understand nothing about taming beasts, just as we understand nothing about dark magic," the other guard said.

"Quite right," Jace nodded. "I'm only describing what I see."

The words had barely left his mouth when the whelp, which had only seemed somewhat displeased a moment before, suddenly let out a savage shriek and swung its spindle-like tail, smashing a trainer behind it clean off his feet!

The sudden move startled even Jace, let alone the two guards. The trainer plummeted into the abyss. Everyone cried out in shock, then chaos erupted…

The trainers, the soldiers, the laborers—all rushed forward trying to restrain the whelp with ropes and grappling hooks, but the beast went berserk, savaging everyone who came near. It even belched a jet of flame that swept across half the platform. The fire nearly caught Jace, and he stumbled back several steps to avoid it.

This was no time to use any magic to protect himself. The moment he cast a spell, that whelp would no longer be the most eye-catching thing on this platform.

The Dragonmaw orcs shouted as they fought to subdue the whelp. Flames and black smoke choked the entire space. Jace watched the beast send several more people flying into the stone walls or tumbling into the abyss, and even the two guards watching Jace had no choice but to rush over and help.

Jace straightened his collar, steadied himself, and whispered, "Curse of Recklessness, right?"

"Yes, Master—but the effect is far more potent than I expected," Morfil tilted her head.

"The more terrifying, the better." Jace glanced back at the dwarven chamber high up where Vereesa and Grede were hiding. He could already see two inconspicuous shadows darting between the stone pillars toward the passage leading to the hatchery.

Jace looked around again to confirm the two shadows hadn't drawn any attention, and was just feeling relieved that his little trick had worked—when a series of heavy, thudding footsteps echoed from inside the hatchery, and a massive dark figure gradually emerged…

It was a giant over four meters tall, dragging a thick dragon's tail. It had a short, stubby, deformed dragon's head, several uneven horns crowning its skull, and feet that were sturdy dragon claws. It stepped into the firelight with a heavy, solid gait. Beneath a dark-brown metal breastplate, its body was covered in crimson scales that gleamed with a fierce light.

A drakonid?!

The Dragonmaw orcs of Grim Batol had a drakonid guarding the Dragonqueen's chamber? Had it been summoned by the orcs to help?

By the time it appeared, Vereesa and Grede had just darted to the gate—less than ten meters separated them!

Jace swallowed and gripped his staff. Morfil's eyes widened with worry as she fixed her gaze on the scene. The two shadows had also frozen at the sound of the footsteps and scrambled behind a stone pillar—but the creature's strides were too long, its pace too fast.

As the drakonid walked, it lowered its gaze and glanced at the spot where the two shadows were hiding. But it made no further move. Instead, it walked straight toward the rampaging whelp.

It roared something in Draconic, seized the whelp's neck with both enormous hands, and slammed it hard into the ground. After a tense struggle, the whelp soon whimpered and submitted—the Curse of Recklessness was probably wearing off too.

The situation seemed to be under control, but Jace's heart was still pounding.

The two shadows had vanished. They must have slipped into the hatchery. After dealing with the whelp, the drakonid turned and slowly walked back into the passage, as if it hadn't noticed the intruders.

Had it spotted Vereesa and Grede?

That glance had been unmistakable… The moment the drakonid emerged, its eyes had gone straight to where the elf and dwarf were hiding. Jace saw it clearly, and Morfil must have noticed too. But the drakonid hadn't reacted at all. It had simply answered the Dragonmaw orcs' call and gone to deal with the whelp.

The red dragons were on their side, Jace thought to himself.

By the time the two guards returned to Jace's side, they were covered in black soot—one's clothes still carried sparks. "Does this happen often?" Jace asked.

"Not at all!" one of the orc guards said. "That was truly bizarre. But no matter—it's been dealt with. Come, emissary, we'll take you to the hatchery."

"I'm not much in the mood anymore." Jace pointed at the whelp, now bound by a dozen orcs, with fingers that trembled slightly. "You'd… better keep these whelps on a tighter leash. You're seeing the Dragonqueen with Warlord Nekros tomorrow anyway—you can have a look then just the same."
