Chapter 659: The Life-Binder

The black gate loomed shut before them. Warlord Nekros stood with a grave expression, his eyes fixed on the door seam covered in scorch marks and gouges.

Draped across his body was a heavy chain of black iron. Jace knew what that was—demonic chains. It was with those terrible chains, forged of elementium, and the power of the Demon Soul, that Nekros enslaved the dragons.

But where was the Demon Soul? Jace still couldn't sense it, but he was certain it was on Nekros's person. He remembered that the Demon Soul and the chain existed together.

"Open the gate," he said in a low voice.

The tall red drakonid, upon hearing the command, walked to the gate and pressed both hands against it, pushing forward step by step.

The moment a crack appeared, the life force that poured out began to scorch everyone present. Nekros couldn't help but shield his eyes and retreat several steps. Missing a foot and forced to rely on his crutch, he nearly toppled over from the wave of searing heat.

Jace could feel it—this fervent life force carried little warmth, little passion, only an ominous agony that made one reluctant to draw near.

The hall, already somewhat stifling from the dragon eggs, grew hotter in an instant. Jace undid his collar and followed Nekros and several armored Dragonmaw soldiers inside, step by step.

Once through the gate, Jace realized this hall was even larger than the hatchery outside—but it was dark and lightless, and nothing could be seen.

Beside him, Morfil's expression was one of evident agony. Clearly the magical radiation in this place was excruciating for a demon like her. Jace couldn't help but swallow nervously, because he knew what he was about to witness.

The smell of wildfire burning leaves filled his nostrils. In the dark hall, a sphere of flame spiraled into being—like a giant blazing ruby suddenly igniting in the shadows. The ruby flickered once, slowly rose, and then a searing maw tore open beneath it.

Brilliant lights flickered to life from all directions, illuminating the hall. Every orc couldn't help but take several steps back. Even Jace felt a chill run through him. Only Nekros stood firm at the very front.

One by one, magical gems began to glow and slowly float into the air. As Jace adjusted to the glare of these dazzling treasures, he finally made out the gems—and the silhouette they enclosed.

It was an enormous dragon's skull. The head alone was considerably larger than an entire whelp outside. The massive horns atop it were thicker than the hall's stone pillars, hung with golden rings thicker than house walls. Every magical gem and diamond embedded in them was larger than Jace's own head.

Jace felt the back of his neck tingle. Before this incomparably vast magical beast, he felt that merely standing upright was a tremendous challenge.

Alexstrasza, the Dragonqueen, tilted her head, observing her visitors with one eye. The hall grew brighter and brighter. The scales all over her body radiated a warm crimson light that played off the strange rocks in the hall in a riot of colors, finally driving back all the darkness.

But the chains binding her also stood out all the more—heavy, massive metal links draped even over her head and neck, each one empowered by the Demon Soul's power.

Only now could Jace take in her full form. Even bound tightly by chains, twisted into a posture of agony, she was still vast as a temple. Jace had no doubt her body could level the entire Stormwind Keep in a single charge.

Perhaps only a place like Grim Batol or Ironforge—those miraculous dwarven underground citadels—could serve as her prison.

At that moment, Nekros slowly raised his right hand, and Jace saw it.

It was… a rather ordinary golden disc. It lacked the brilliant radiance one would expect of a terrible artifact, released no fearsome power whatsoever.

It was exactly the opposite of what Jace had imagined. He couldn't sense a shred of energy from it. It was like an ordinary golden plate—even duller than a golden plate. Had Jace not known it was the Demon Soul, he might have taken it for a small brass gong you could cup in your palm.

Long ago, to keep Deathwing from finding it, the dragons had apparently placed a special enchantment upon it to prevent it from radiating any magical energy at all. Perhaps that was why Jace couldn't sense its power.

But when Nekros raised it, the Dragonqueen's enormous pupil visibly trembled. She was afraid.

Alexstrasza parted her massive lips, and Jace heard a rumbling, thunderous murmur. "You need not show me that thing again."

The Dragonqueen spoke Common. Her voice, though clearly feminine, was deep as an echo from the abyss.

"I did not come here for you today," Nekros replied in Common. Then he turned to Jace and asked in Orcish, "Are you satisfied?"

Every orc in the entire prison hall—the Dragonqueen included—turned their gaze on him. That was when he noticed the Dragonqueen's eyes fixed on his waist, or more precisely, on the sword at his hip—Skysong.

"Astonishing, Nekros," Jace said in Orcish. "I don't have the words… this miracle."

Even as he spoke, he tried to sense how close Sazeno was, but the Dragonqueen's power was too overwhelming. Standing in this room, he could barely sense anything at all, utterly consumed by the heat.

Had Vereesa and Grede made it in? Were they ready to fight? Jace had no idea. He wanted to glance back at the gate, but felt that such a gesture would be unbearably conspicuous at a moment like this, so he could only pretend to be awestruck by the Dragonqueen's appearance.

But he truly was awestruck. Locking eyes with the Queen, he could barely breathe. Had he really come here to save this ancient being? Did she even need his saving?

"Look well, Yoruk." Nekros stepped back and climbed the steps to what he judged a safe distance. "After you leave here, you may never again have the chance to witness such a being. Her temper is remarkably calm today. You're fortunate."

Jace nodded, his mind racing. Then the Dragonqueen suddenly fixed her gaze on him, slowly closed her eyes, and opened them again, shifting her gaze downward.

What was she hinting at? Was she trying to communicate with him?

Jace could scarcely believe that this godlike being was trying to warn him of something, but he followed the Dragonqueen's gaze downward. Then the dragon shifted one of her massive wings, which had been pressed tight against her body. The motion alone whipped up a whirlwind carrying the scent of wildflowers that spun violently through the hall…

Beneath the shelter of her enormous wing, Jace caught sight of something green—a corner of Vereesa's cloak!

Nekros roared, raised the golden disc, and began chanting a spell. The object finally glimmered faintly, and every chain on the Dragonqueen's body creaked and groaned with an unnatural sound. Alexstrasza let out a pained groan. Her head, which had only barely lifted a moment before, crashed to the ground in agony, and the entire hall shook violently.

When everything finally settled, Nekros stood with his eyes wide, gasping for breath. "I must be cautious," he said, turning to Jace, his face drenched in sweat. Jace nodded and pulled out a Refined Wisdom Elixir to drink.

"What is that?" Nekros asked.

"I'm frightened too. I need a potion to steady myself," Jace said.

Though suspicion lingered on Nekros's face, he didn't appear to be well-versed in Azeroth's alchemical ingredients. He could only watch the magic swirling within the elixir and found it hard to accept Jace's explanation at once.

But Jace had sounded so convincing…

Then he watched, dumbfounded, as Jace produced another flask and took a sip of the pale green liquid inside. "And what's that?"

"Restorative Potion," Jace said, stoppering the flask. "And you're drinking this because…?"

"Because I am."

Jace suddenly raised his staff and shouted the incantation for Fear, accompanied by a piercing rush of shadow!

Wails, roars, and curses erupted everywhere. Nearly none of the orc soldiers had any clue what was happening. The hall was instantly filled with howls.

The orcish disguise was torn apart by shadow. An orc guard who had finally shaken off the Mass Fear swung an axe at Jace—only to take an arrow straight through his throat and collapse.

Vereesa emerged from beneath the Dragonqueen's wing, drew her bow, and sent an arrow precisely into a gap in an orc guard's armor. A bolt of lightning tore free from beneath the red dragon's wing and slammed into the area near the gate, blasting the soldiers who had just charged in in every direction. Grede roared and charged out, his axe cleaving through one orc before shattering another's helmet.

"Jace, behind you!"

Hearing Vereesa's warning, Jace spun around. An orc bearing a two-handed sword was swinging at his head. He raised Gorefiend's staff and blocked, knocking the orc aside. An arrow immediately punched through the orc's skull.

"Bastard shapeshifter!" Nekros clutched his head and struggled upright with a grimace of pain. Jace was somewhat surprised—at this range, he'd recovered from the Fear spell impossibly fast.

He turned back to hit Nekros with a single-target Fear. He'd barely begun the incantation when Nekros outpaced him, extending his arm toward Jace and roaring the words for Fear. They both cast almost simultaneously, but Nekros's Fear was swiftly cleansed by the still-active Restorative Potion. Jace's Fear spell landed again, and Nekros burst into tears and sat on the ground—but through gritted teeth he tried to force himself back to his feet.

The warlord's willpower was absurdly strong. Even empowered by both the Refined Wisdom Elixir and the blood magic staff, the Fear spell was barely holding him. But Jace immediately cast Asphyxiate, pinning him to the ground. Nekros fell to his knees, clutching his throat. This time he couldn't even grip his crutch.

"Charm him—quickly, now!" Jace shouted as he turned back. A flood of orcs was charging in, only to be cut down batch after batch by Vereesa's volleys of magic arrows. Grede, wielding a war hammer, had already struck down several of Nekros's elite guards and charged over to block the Dragonmaw soldiers trying to reach Jace. But more and more orcs were pouring through the gate…

Morfil had already appeared beside Nekros, shadow coalescing in her hands. After a violent fit of coughing, Nekros said in a low voice, "I am yours… I give all that I am, my lady."

Though his tone was abject, the hatred etched on his face was unmistakable. "Give that object to my master," Morfil commanded.

Nekros, trembling, unhooked the chain from his shoulder and arms, then handed the Demon Soul bound upon it to Jace. When Jace took hold of it, he felt as though his arm had gone beyond his control.

Was this the Demon Soul…?

The terrible artifact that had repelled the Burning Legion ten thousand years ago and nearly annihilated the Blue Dragonflight. Deathwing's treasure…

Perhaps nothing in this world was more powerful than it.

Suddenly, everything around him seemed to fall silent. Even Vereesa's piercing arrows whistling past his ear became inaudible. Grede's roars grew fainter and fainter.

He had to save the Dragonqueen, Jace told himself.

He already held the Demon Soul. Deathwing should have taught him how to use it. Should he sleep?

Maybe he should. But… he shouldn't. He absolutely must not…

Jace blinked. A deep drowsiness washed over him. He forced himself to focus and looked again at the small disc in his palm. This was Deathwing's treasure. If he didn't enter the dream, he would never master how to use it.

No—Nekros was already under his enslavement. The warlord could teach him how to use the disc.

But how could this fool possibly understand it better than Deathwing? In his dreams, Deathwing would surely teach him precisely how to use this disc.

No—he couldn't sleep. He must not enter that dream again. Open your eyes! Open!

Jace held the disc, and a powerful sense of despair gripped his heart.

Perhaps he should stop agonizing and hand the disc to Deathwing, along with the dragon eggs outside. Only then could he survive—live forever. Otherwise, he would be punished in apocalyptic flames.

He was only a mortal. He shouldn't bear such a heavy burden.

If he gave it to the Dragon Father, he could gain immortality, be with the ones he loved forever. He could rule the new world alongside the Dragon Father, seek a new dawn.

Jace felt a sore, swollen ache in his arm. He pulled back his sleeve and saw the precious Black Dragon's Mark radiating an indescribably beautiful light. He could almost see his future within that light.

He could see Neltharion's nearly flawless face within it, as though all the torment, repression, and pain he'd endured along the way had been building toward this moment of awakening.

The Dragonqueen panted, one eye barely able to watch him. The agony Nekros's spell had inflicted on her still hadn't subsided.

He had already seized control of the situation. With command over the Demon Soul, the entire citadel now belonged to him—Jace Sesso, King of Grim Batol.

Jace Sesso—the name sounded so unfamiliar. "Jace, my only fear is that you will no longer be yourself." The voice suddenly echoed in his mind.

Vereesa—was that you calling me?

The ranger stood atop a distant stone pillar, loosing arrow after arrow with unerring accuracy at the orc soldiers trying to close in on Grede and Jace, dropping them one by one.

Her expression was resolute, her movements clean and precise—a ruthless, efficient engine of death. And yet, in the brief pauses between volleys, she still glanced this way.

——Death is nothing to fear. My only fear is that you will no longer be yourself.

Jace drew Skysong with his left hand, raised it, and drove it straight through the Black Dragon's Seal on his arm!

A roar followed that nearly burst his eardrums. He screamed at the top of his lungs but heard nothing. He knelt on the ground, enduring the searing pain as he slowly pulled the sword free. The Black Dragon brand had been torn apart by the wound, losing its original shape. Blood that welled up swallowed all the firelight, drowning that indescribable prismatic glow. Black shadow-blood seeped from the wound…

He raised Skysong, now stained with his own dark blood. If there was any way to destroy the Demon Soul, this was it.

——I will give you one last chance, Sesso…

A chant-like murmur echoed in his mind.

——Did you think you could destroy the Demon Soul so easily? I must admit, there was a moment when I truly believed you would be loyal to me. But in the end, you chose the other path. I am deeply disappointed, because I truly valued you.

——But your will is far stronger than I imagined. You are a gifted mortal, so I am willing to give you one last chance. Do not attempt to break my treasure…

As the black brand on his arm burned away from the puncture, twisting into a misshapen, corroded scar, Deathwing's cursed voice grew fainter and fainter.

Jace, gripping his bloodied blade, inched it carefully toward the golden disc. When the tip touched its surface, a sharp crack rang out.

Skysong had split.

The Blue Dragon sword had only grazed the surface lightly, yet a crack had already appeared. Its wavering arcane light rapidly dimmed, while the disc hadn't budged. The blood on the blade slid off the disc's surface without leaving a single trace.

Was it over? He'd failed.

Yet having accepted that fact, Jace felt an extraordinary calm.

Perhaps it was because he no longer had to bear Deathwing's cursed brand, no longer had to hide everything from everyone.
