Chapter 661: The Sun in the Night

"Master, master! Wake up! We're done for!"

Jace coughed a few times and came to, immediately feeling a wave of searing pain roll through his whole body. He couldn't tell if something was broken, torn, or if a wound had ripped open.

He was pinned between several massive boulders, unable to move. Sazeno was crouching on top of a tilted stone slab, staring at him — then slapped him across the face again.

"Stop hitting me! I'm awake! What the hell happened? Are we in the Shadowlands? Or was that all a nightmare?"

"Unfortunately, master, neither. The black dragon's here, and you're still alive. Hurry up and get moving — do you want to watch all your friends die?"

"They're alive?"

"I'm guessing… Master, at least you're alive, right? You're the most fragile of the bunch, so logically, the odds are they survived too!"

Fine, fine. The Demon Soul?

Jace groped around him frantically. He remembered that after Alexstrasza was freed, Molofier had handed the disc back to him. But right now he couldn't move at all, let alone reach into his robe's pockets.

Jace asked, "How long was I out?"

"Not long, master, a few minutes? I don't know — Grim Batol collapsed like a sandcastle!"

"Help me…" Jace tried to wrench his arms and legs free, but he had no strength to spare.

"I'll go find someone, you wait here!"

The imp scurried off in a trail of fel fire, and Jace shouted after it, "Hey, you little—!" But short of going for help himself, there wasn't much else he could do.

High above, roars and snarls echoed endlessly — Jace had no idea how many red dragons were swarming Deathwing, but he knew they had no chance of winning.

What he needed to do now was find a way to destroy the Demon Soul and return the Aspects' power… or at least return it to the Dragonqueen. That might buy enough time for the other Aspects to arrive.

That was the original plan, wasn't it?

I wonder if any of Deathwing's scales had fallen to the ground? With that many red dragons attacking him — some of them adults, plus the Dragonqueen herself — surely at least one of them had landed a hit, taken a swipe, and knocked something loose?!

Thinking that, Jace suddenly felt a surge of motivation. He gritted his teeth and forced his arms free, shifted his legs — though nothing seemed to be broken, he was still crushed tight.

Honestly, the miracle was that he hadn't been crushed to death directly beneath the collapsing Grim Batol.

Then a pang of anguish twisted in his chest. How were Vereesa and Grede? Had they survived too? This wasn't the time to think about that — every second he wasted, even if they were alive, could be their last. Jace bit down and tried to push the boulder beside him, when suddenly the boulder shifted on its own.

"Jace! You son of a—! …Are you under here? This little imp isn't jerking me around, is it?"

"Grede!"

"You really are… hold on, I'll get you out!"

"Where's Vereesa?! Where is she?!"

"I don't know — we'll find her together!"

"You're not hurt, are you?"

"A Wildhammer dwarf can't get hurt in Grim Batol!" Grede bellowed, and heaved the boulder aside. Jace saw that he'd already summoned his Avatar form — he looked a lot taller now.

Without Jace even noticing, Grede's Avatar had grown far larger and more powerful than when he'd first manifested it in Uldaman. This must have been what he meant about needing to strengthen his communion with his ancestors through trial after trial, honing his power and reinforcing his Avatar form.

He grabbed Jace by the shoulder with one hand and, with the other, wrenched away the stones pinning Jace's lower half, hauling him out like a kitten and setting him down nearby.

Jace immediately patted himself down, then found the disc…

"Thank God," he muttered. "Still here, still here. Now we need to smash it, no matter what it takes — it's our only chance of surviving this… Grede."

Grede nodded and said, "I don't get what this thing's got to do with that black dragon, but you're rarely wrong. Speak — do we smash it, or…"

"We need to find a scale that fell from the black dragon," Jace said. "Only a scale from Deathwing can cut through it." Thinking of the cracked Azurewrath, he swallowed hard.

"Right, there are a bunch of dragon scales over there, Jace — right over there, let's go!" Grede pointed behind them. "I just came from that way, isn't that right, demon?!"

"Right! I saw them too!" Sazeno yipped, bouncing around excitedly.

"Let's go!" Jace stuffed Sazeno straight into his robe and walked out of the ruins with Grede. When they emerged from the rubble, the sight before him sent a chill down the back of his neck.

Grim Batol was finished, no doubt about it — half the mountainside had been sheared away… Rivers of lava flowed between the valley and the boulders. It should have been morning or noon, but the sky had gone completely dark. Countless red streaks wheeled through the air around a massive black shape in the center, and cold laughter rolled down from above, nearly indistinguishable from the peals of thunder.

"How the hell did I survive…" Jace muttered to himself.

"Maybe the Dragonqueen protected us a little," Grede said, leaping over a cracked stone seam. "Vereesa and I were blown all over the place by the wind — before we hit the ground, I felt her cast some kind of spell on us. I couldn't explain it. But she can barely protect herself now, so don't count on that. Good thing there's nothing left of Grim Batol to collapse — we shouldn't get crushed by rocks again, ha!"

The dwarf ran toward where the dragons were fighting. Jace kept pace and couldn't help saying, "I'm sorry, Grede. I lied to you the whole way here. I didn't tell you that I'd gotten on Deathwing's bad side."

Grede laughed. "That sigil — I could guess what it meant. A black dragon's claw, I know that much. Vereesa could feel it too. I figured if you could've told us, you would have. Since you didn't, you must have had your reasons."

"Don't dwell on this stuff, Jace. If I were in your shoes, I'd move heaven and earth to help you. So would that Vereesa girl. Even those demons would."

The imp in his robe shrieked, "Not me! I don't wanna die! Getting the hell out of here is the right move — stop trying to do something heroic! We're still alive, there's still time!"

"Shut the hell up — when those red dragons are done for, we're all dead! You think you can escape Deathwing's grasp?" Grede cursed as he jumped down a step.

The imp wailed, "Oh mama, why did I pick a master like this…" Jace slammed a hand against his chest, making the little guy yelp.

Watching Grede's back as he walked ahead, Jace felt his nose sting. But this wasn't the time to get emotional.

"I don't know how you provoked that black dragon, Jace — he was shouting your name just now. Don't know if I heard that right," Grede said.

"Doesn't matter."

Jace kept walking as he spoke. Then a young red dragon, wreathed in black fire, plummeted from the sky. Before it hit the ground, it had been burned down to nothing but bones. It splashed into the flowing lava without a sound.

"Right here, Jace." Grede picked up a scale. "Will this work?"

Jace took the black scale in his hand and quickly realized it wasn't a black dragon scale — it was a charred red dragon scale, with one small patch still bright red.

"Not quite. It needs to be black… a dark brownish-gray with a purple sheen."

"Got it."

The two kept searching. Then a bubble suddenly rolled up from the lava behind them, and a blob of living magma, hot as a slime, surged out with a rumbling flow of molten rock and lunged toward them. Then more and more crawled out of the lava rivers elsewhere. Grede cursed, hefted his war hammer, called down lightning, and smashed the nearest one — blasting the living magma to pieces.

But more and more of them were closing in, and they spat burning flames, nearly splashing onto Jace and Grede again and again. They had to move elsewhere to keep searching for a usable dragon scale.

But after wasting ten minutes like this, watching more and more young dragons fall from the sky — even a strong adult had crashed into the rubble, triggering another explosion of flame — the two of them still hadn't found a single scale from Deathwing.

What's more, Jace hadn't found a single one of those glowing, crimson-crystal scales — the kind he'd seen on the Dragonqueen's body — among any of the red dragon scales either.

Goddamn it.

Even though the heat here was enough to make a man pass out from heatstroke, Jace still felt a cold despair clench around his heart.

Then he suddenly felt a sharp, itching pain in his abdomen. What was this? Wait — Soul Link?

Molofier was alive? And injured? What was she fighting? Could Nekros have broken free of Molofier's charm amid the destruction of Grim Batol? That warlock…

"They seem to be in danger, Grede. We need to help them — these scales… I don't think we're getting anywhere."

"Then let's go!" Grede replied. Jace glanced up at the sky — the shadow that blotted out the heavens had seized the red silhouette, like a dark cloud swallowing a dimming sun.
