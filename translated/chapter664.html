Chapter 664: The Wounded Earth

The Batol Mountains were quiet in a way that sent chills through the heart. The lava had congealed and blackened into hard crusts. The bubbling lava grew ever more feeble. The earth elementals and fire giants had long since collapsed and shattered, no longer moving. The flames in the air were pushed apart by the cool breeze blowing in from the Wetlands.

The clouds of ash and burning smoke were no longer as thick as before. Beam after beam of sunlight strained to pierce through the black, sooty haze and fall to the ground. The Grim Batol before them was like a shattered castle that had endured a great earthquake — one could barely remember what it had looked like before.

Dragon corpses lay everywhere. Orc corpses. The stench of burning, the breath of shadow, the reek of death — spread across the entire mountain range.

Deathwing's corpse had dimmed, a black hill on the horizon, faintly glowing with a dark red fire. Jace only looked at it for a while before he felt a growing pain from his eyes to his chest and had to look away.

Not far off, a dragonkin limped toward the distance. Jace watched the direction it was heading — toward the remains of the red dragon lord Tyranastrazz.

That gigantic ancient dragon was now only half a body lying in a stretch of ruins. He was one of the eldest of all dragons — among the dragonflights, probably only the Spell-Weaver Malygos was older than him. He was also the Dragonqueena's companion of the longest years.

Had it not been for the Dragonqueen's capture, which had imprisoned him in Grim Batol to be tormented by the Demon Soul all this time, he wouldn't have been killed so easily.

Following that dragonkin's footsteps to Tyranastrazz's head, up close Jace felt it was almost bigger than the house he'd bought not long ago — so stunning it stole the breath from his lungs.

Circling around the great skull to the other side, a high elven woman in red was squatting before Tyranastrazz's now-closed eyes. Beneath her had spread a patch of land covered in bright green grass, golden and red flowers. Mixed with Tyranastrazz's dragon's blood, it nourished a vast stretch of the surrounding area back to what it had been before the shadow and lava tainted it — even more vibrant with life…

Jace's group stopped at the edge of this green patch as they approached. He extended his shoeless, badly burned foot toward the green ground, a little hesitant to set it down directly.

"Come closer, my friends," the woman called gently.

The few of them exchanged glances and walked in. Jace watched the wounds on his feet rapidly heal, the skin restoring to clean and bright. Grede found the cracks on his arms and body from the earth elementals' blows quickly closing too…

The imp shrank back into Jace's clothes, too scared to show his head. Molofier stood outside, never daring to set her hoof inside. The woman in red raised her head and glanced at her. "You won't be harmed here, Succubus."

The succubus stepped in hesitantly. Strangely, as a genuine Twisting Nether demon, she truly wasn't repelled by this radiant green ground.

"Perhaps you can tell — I am Alexstrasza. This is another form of mine," the woman said softly. Her voice was far gentler than in her dragon form, and her speech was smoother, easier to understand.

Jace understood now why the dragons took mortal shapes to speak with them. Faced with Alexstrasza as she had been in Grim Batol, he simply couldn't have stayed this calm.

Then the Dragonqueen stood up. Grede's eyes widened as he watched her straighten her frame little by little. Jace only then realized she was at least two meters tall.

Slightly different from the game's depiction, her crown with horns wasn't nearly as prominent, and her clothing was much plainer. Her bright red hair fell all the way to her waist, seeming to merge into her crimson cloak — though there was a subtle, hard-to-place distinction between the two.

Jace guessed her mortal appearance probably had something to do with the strength of her power, or her mood.

Even so, even with exhaustion written all over her face and her complexion pale, Alexstrasza's mortal form was still so beautiful that even Vereesa stared a little dazedly.

Jace looked at the massive red dragon's skull beside him. "My condolences, my queen."

"This is the ending Tyran wanted. I know that," Alexstrasza said softly. "He was old, and he suffered endlessly in Grim Batol — all because of me… Even without joining this battle, he wouldn't have lasted much longer."

"In the days before, I had stopped hoping I might one day escape that castle. When I felt you arrive, I thought even if it just gave me one chance to face Deathwing head-on… to take control of my own fate, I would have been satisfied. I never imagined you would stay to fight alongside me, and I never imagined you truly would…"

"Tyran felt the same. He told me the same words. And you gave him that chance to prove himself the greatest warrior of the Red Dragonflight. He still had a flicker of breath left — he got to watch Deathwing perish. He told me he was grateful to you, and so am I. So I will not grieve, because I know he was thrilled and stirred in his final moment. And he wouldn't have wanted me to be sad."

Still, gazing at this dragon's body — lifeless, yet still nourishing the land around it — the group was silent for a long while. "About Krasus," Jace said. "There's something I want to tell you, my queen."

"Krasus?" Vereesa couldn't help but ask. "He…"

"His true name is Korialstrasz," Alexstrasza explained. "He is also my lover and companion. He wasn't trapped in Grim Batol — he'd been outside all along, searching for my trail, looking for a chance to rescue me."

Vereesa's eyes widened. "So he's a dragon. No wonder I felt like he'd lived even longer than the Sun King. Did Deathwing tell you his true identity, Jace?"

"Yes," Jace said. "But something went wrong with him at Deathwing's hands. I'm afraid he may have already—"

"He isn't dead." Alexstrasza raised her head. "I can still feel his presence."

Jace was stunned. Could what he'd seen been an illusion woven by Deathwing?

No, that wasn't right. In Deathwing's dreams, Krasus hadn't died either. So was he still trapped somewhere?

Jace didn't want to think about these things anymore. Deathwing was dead. Nothing could hold him anymore. Perhaps he'd already returned to the Kirin Tor, only unaware of everything that had happened here.

"It must have taken a promise from Korialstrasz to get you all to come here?" Alexstrasza asked. Jace said, "He promised to help restore my name."

"As a spellcaster who embraced the shadows, you must have endured a great deal. This is something you rightfully deserve." The Dragonqueen nodded. "And the rest? I will help him honor his promise."

"He didn't say anything else," Jace said.

There was also that orb — he suddenly remembered, then patted his chest. Damn, he'd lost that too. But it didn't matter anymore. He didn't need to turn into an orc again.

"And the rest of you?" the queen asked.

"I don't know Krasus… I mean, Korialstrasz. I came with him." Vereesa pointed at Jace.

"I came with them," Grede said.

"I… didn't have a choice," Molofier said.

The imp clung tightly to Jace's robe, trembling inside, not daring to say a word.

The Dragonqueena lowered her head. "I'm sorry. My own mistake, yet you risked your lives for it without asking anything in return. But please don't blame him — he has nothing left to promise, either."

"Here's what we'll do." She took a golden gemstone necklace from her own neck and, looking at the dwarf, said: "This is a pendant Neltharion… Deathwing… gave me before he betrayed us."

She personally fastened the necklace around the dwarf's neck. Grede was stunned for a moment. "This is too precious. It's a treasure he left for you, my queen. Um… was Deathwing a good person before?"

"He was once the most upright among us," Alexstrasza said. "But that was I don't know how many ages ago. This object suits you well, Grede of the Thunderfist clan. I can feel that you are on the path to seeking the power of your ancestors. May this necklace guide your way."

Grede held up the golden pendant and stared at it for a while. His lips moved, but in the end all he said was: "Thank you, great queen. I don't know what to say. Honestly, I wasn't expecting anything."

"Don't refuse it. It would only make me blame myself more. This is what you deserve."

As she spoke, Alexstrasza also removed an emerald pendant from her ear and fastened it on Vereesa, saying: "This is a pendant my sister, Ysera, gave me long ago. She hoped it would let me vanish into the forests and observe their free growth without disturbing the life within."

"But clearly, I am terrible at hiding myself. Even with its help, I still often frighten those innocent creatures. So I've actually wasted the power this trinket was meant to have… Even Ysera urged me once to find a friend more nimble than me, one who knows better how to lurk in the forest, and give it away. Now that person is standing right in front of me — Vereesa Windrunner."

"Queen Alexstrasza… I…"

Vereesa held the pendant between her fingers, barely able to speak.

Then the Dragonqueen looked at Jace — but didn't immediately take any ornament from herself.

Jace held back from looking too greedy… but Alexstrasza seemed to see right through him anyway.

"Don't be impatient. Your gift hasn't arrived yet, Jace Sesso. Let's wait here a moment. I've had my children go find it."

"Find?" Grede asked. "What is it?"

Vereesa looked over as well. Jace was, of course, instantly hooked with curiosity.

Before long, a whelp descended from the sky. In its mouth it carried something small and black, indistinct, slowly coming to the Dragonqueena's side. The queen reached out and took the object.

Though it looked like nothing more than an ordinary metal chain, up close Jace recognized it at once. This was that… chain of the Demon Soul.

Its true nature revealed in Ch. 665. - POV: third person. Alexstrasza's dialogue kept gentle, regal, and warm.
