## Chapter 665: The Most Important Promise

The Demon Soul's chain — shouldn't it have been shattered along with the Demon Soul? How had this thing survived a direct tail-slap from Deathwing without a scratch?

Alexstrasza said: "This primordial chain has no proper name. All I can confirm is that its history is older than my own existence. Malygos believes it to be a Chain of Will forged by the Elemental Lords in ancient times to resist the corruption of the Old Gods."

"To this day, I am no longer able to verify whether that tale is true or false. But it does possess astonishing power of will. Long ago the black dragon held it, until Deathwing's servants used it to bind the Demon Soul. Joining the two objects together granted the Demon Soul the power to control dragons."

"From this day onward it is free — freed from the Demon Soul. Wear it on your person, and the spiritual corruption you can resist will far exceed your imagination. Of course, you will also possess a fearsome power to suppress the minds of others. So this is both a powerful and a dangerous artifact. Neither its creators nor its former owners used it to resist corruption — instead, they used it to make an already sinister magical artifact even more terrifying."

"This is precisely why it needs a suitable keeper. I believe you are the most fitting. You defied Deathwing. You faced his overwhelming domination with a mortal's body. That has already proven how unshakeable your will is."

"Remember — do not tell any outsiders that you carry this chain. Of course, its appearance is unremarkable. Primordial metal doesn't catch the eye the way gold or thorium does, so hiding this secret won't be difficult. As long as the people present don't bring it up, no one will know it's on you, and no one will take an interest in it."

Vereesa and Grede realized this seemed to be directed at the two of them. They nodded in unison.

The Dragonqueen continued: "I consider this a suitable reward — but it is also a responsibility. However, if there comes a day you feel you no longer wish to bear this thing, you may return it to me. But do not discard it carelessly in the wild. That would be extremely dangerous."

Jace took the chain. He immediately felt a sharp tension tighten around his neck. He gripped the chain and adjusted for quite a while before slowly easing that tightness.

"I can safeguard this thing, my queen." He cleared his throat, then asked: "But… could you let me… safeguard it for a long time?"

The Dragonqueena smiled slightly. "I know what Deathwing used to tempt you into serving him — eternal life."

"True." Jace lowered his head, a little embarrassed. "That's what he said."

Vereesa looked at Jace in surprise, her eyes instantly reddening. Grede was also taken aback.

"I cannot promise you eternity, Jace. I'm sorry," Alexstrasza said. "But I can grant you the essence of the Life-Binder. That should at least let you live longer than most. Can you accept that?"

Jace drew a sharp breath. "Yes."

"Brace yourself. Accepting my blessing may feel a little… hot." Alexstrasza walked over and embraced Jace, pressing her hand to his forehead.

Jace only felt himself surrounded by a wave of searing flame. The fire gradually seeped through his skin and flowed into his veins — but not to the point of pain or torment… He only felt a spirit and fervor his body had never known before. Sweat poured out of him from head to toe, and soon he could barely breathe.

After a while, the queen gently wiped the sweat from his forehead. "I can tell you've studied fire magic. Remember to be a little more careful when you wield fire from now on, Jace. The fire elementals will be more… enthusiastic than you imagine."

Enthusiastic?

What did that mean…? How enthusiastic? As enthusiastic as shadow?

Jace was still savoring the fiery dragon essence surging inside him and the queen's words when Alexstrasza had already turned to Molofier beside him. "As for you, demon — I know you wouldn't like most of the things I could give."

Molofier said: "You didn't crush me to death on the spot. I'm already more than satisfied."

Alexstrasza said: "I won't care about what you are, where you came from, or why you came to Grim Batol. You saved me, and I am equally grateful. However, your power is heavily influenced by your pact and your master. I imagine that when he benefits, you will share in it."

"Indeed, noble Life-Binder," Molofier said.

Vereesa suddenly spoke: "I have one more request…"

"Speak, Windrunner," Alexstrasza said with a smile.

Vereesa carefully drew the damaged Azurewrath from Jace's waist. "Can this still be repaired?"

"Ah… this treasure. Its power was destroyed by the Demon Soul." The Dragonqueena said with remorse: "I watched it slowly approach that dreadful object, yet I didn't cry out to stop it… Because in my heart, I also hoped — hoped that this blade, which had broken Deathwing's brand, could truly stand against the power of that artifact. In that moment, my selfishness devoured my reason."

"The reason it could break through Deathwing's brand is that this sword harbors the hatred and sorrow of the blue dragon. So I don't have the power to repair it. But I can help you find a blue dragon to bring this weapon back to Northrend and attempt to reforge it. If you trust me, leave it here."

"Thank you so much, my queen," Jace said.

Vereesa placed Azurewrath in the queen's hands. Alexstrasza gazed at the sword and let out a soft sigh. "Next, I will stay by Tyran's side. I will remain here and mend the dark wounds inflicted on these mountains. But I do not have the power to restore the marvel forged by the Wildhammer clan."

"No need to trouble yourself, my queen. My kin and I will return to rebuild here with our own hands," Grede said. "Someday!" The red dragon smiled. "When darkness no longer haunts Grim Batol… I will come to Aerie Peak and bring you the good news."

Grede nodded, then couldn't help but cough a couple of times. The Dragonqueena said: "These lands have become a scorched dark zone unfit for ordinary people to enter. I imagine none of you want to linger any longer, do you?"

"I left something here, my queen," Jace said. "My staff."

"Your staff fell deep within Grim Batol, Jace," the Dragonqueena said. "Searching for it now would be no different from walking into your own death. But don't worry — I will find a way to retrieve it for you."

Even though the red dragon said that, without the staff that had been with him all this time, Jace couldn't help but feel a hollow ache in his chest. "Then let's go," Grede said, rubbing his eyes. "I really can't stay in this place any longer…"

Jace nodded. Alexstrasza said: "Deathwing destroyed a vast stretch of land. There are still far too many dangers on the road out of the Batol Mountains. I can have my child carry you out of here."

"Really?" Vereesa turned back. Grede asked in surprise: "I get to ride a dragon?!"

"Actually, rather than 'riding,' I would prefer to call it a partnership," the Dragonqueen corrected the dwarf.

"Ah… right." Grede suddenly thought of those orc dragon-riders and added: "Sorry."

At the queen's call, a beautiful red whelp landed on the grass, lowering its body slightly.

Compared to the whelps in Grim Batol before, who had looked savage and impulsive, ready to tear something apart or devour a few living people at any moment, the whelps here now were like a completely different species — docile and friendly.

Whatever the Dragonqueena called it, this time they were really going to ride a dragon. Jace stared fixedly at the whelp before him, thinking: no matter what, it was goddamn worth it.

"Where are you heading?" the Dragonqueena asked.

"Thelsamar!"

All three of them said it in unison.

The Dragonqueena asked, puzzled: "Of course, that's no problem. It's not far. But why?"

"Because there's lots of good food," Vereesa said immediately. "I haven't been there myself — they told me."

"There's just a lot!" Grede shouted. "We're going to Thelsamar!"

"Then Thelsamar on the shores of the Loch." The queen walked to the whep's side, stroked its neck, and murmured something. The whelp nodded.

Grede was the first to clamber eagerly onto the whelp's neck and settle in. Then Jace and Vereesa climbed on too. Molofier approached a little cautiously, but the whelp didn't seem to show any disgust either, so she jumped on reassuredly.

The whelp's body was naturally far steadier than a griffon's. Sitting on it, aside from the hard scales under his backside being a bit uncomfortable, there was nothing else to speak of.

"If you run into trouble you can't handle, remember to come find me in Grim Batol, my friends," the Dragonqueena said with a smile. "I remember, my queen!" Grede roared excitedly.

Just as Jace was still feeling the whelp slowly sway its enormous body sideways, a pair of arms reached around from behind and locked around his waist.

"There's nowhere else I can hold on, Jace Sesso. Don't let me fall," Vereesa whispered from behind.

"I'll allow you to hold onto me," Molofier behind them all touched the horns on her head. "I have far more places to grab onto than the master does, Quel'dorei."

"I'll allow you to shut up, demon!" the dwarf up front shouted.

The succubus snorted coldly. The imp couldn't help but giggle — then suddenly yelped as the succubus yanked him out of Jace's collar by the tail.

Jace looked at the fingers laced together on his waist, the pale skin still smeared with a little soot, and felt his heart pounding.

Then the whelp raised its wings and beat them slowly, rhythmically. The body lifted off the ground, rising bit by bit. Jace gripped the horn on the dragon's back tightly with his left hand, feeling the ranger behind him holding on tighter and tighter.

"Mama!" Grede couldn't help but shout. "This is way more impressive than a griffon!"

The whelp let out a serpentine shriek — there even seemed to be a note of displeasure in it, as if unhappy that Grede had compared him to a griffon.

Jace carefully covered those hands with his right hand. Then the whelp suddenly spread its great wings and lunged forward, riding the gale in an instant and bursting through the layers of soot-black haze…

*I won't let go,* he thought in his heart.
