Chapter 666: Signs at Banthor Mountain

The young dragon seemed not to have remembered the location of that watchtower. When it suddenly came into view, the dragon startled and abruptly climbed higher to clear the tower's line of sight, nearly shaking the riders off its back.

Then Jace remembered: in the eyes of the entire Alliance, the red dragon was still a mount of the Dragonmaw orcs.

Sure enough, before long they heard the shouts of griffin riders behind them. Grede flew into a cursing rage after hearing the crack of lightning on a war hammer, but the young dragon quickly gained altitude and finally shook off the pursuit of those griffin riders just before it froze everyone to death.

Although he felt somewhat sick about it, it was hard to blame those griffin riders — after all, the Orc Wars had only been over for a few years, and Khaz Modan had been one of the regions hit hardest by the Horde's frontal assault. It wasn't until the flight smoothed out that Grede remembered even he himself, not long ago, had been gnashing his teeth at the sight of red dragons.

So when they were still about a mile or two from Thelsamar, the young dragon quietly descended into a stand of pine trees. He couldn't get any closer — any nearer and he'd easily be spotted by the hill wardens around Thelsamar, and then getting away wouldn't be so easy.

After seeing off the young dragon, the few of them stood in the pine grove, looking back at the cooking smoke near the distant lakeshore.

By now it was nearly evening. The dwarf-built round-topped stone houses were all lit up. Knowing that it was Bronzebeard dwarves living in those buildings, not Dragonmaw orcs, was rather reassuring.

"Will they chase me out?" Vereesa asked. "I'm an 'elf,' right? They don't like outsiders like me."

"How many orcs did you kill in Grim Batol, Vereesa?" Grede asked.

"Thirty-seven," Vereesa said.

"Not bad," Grede said. "You killed thirty-seven orcs in such a short time — just say that when you go to the tavern. They'll worship you like a god. As for the length and thickness of your ears, that won't matter anymore."

Jace said, "I can't even remember how many orcs I killed."

"Don't even mention it," Grede turned his head to look at him. "Vereesa can say she pierced the necks and eyeballs of those orcs with arrows — though it doesn't sound as heroic as an axe or a hammer, it's not far off. But you, my little Jace…"

"Used terrible magic to drain the orcs until they looked like goblins," Vereesa added, raising her eyebrows.

"That's exactly what I mean. No one in a Bronzebeard tavern wants to hear that kind of story," Grede said. "Strictly speaking, they don't like the protagonist of that kind of story sitting right next to them instead of locked up in a Dalaran prison."

Jace sighed. "Fine."

The three of them chatted about the events in Grim Batol as they walked into Thelsamar. The Bronzebeard town was as lively as ever in the evening. No sooner had they entered the tavern than a dwarf patron caught sight of Vereesa and immediately came over to ask, "Hey, elf, do you speak Common?"

"Of course," the ranger replied. "What is it?"

"Good," the dwarf continued. "You're from the north, right? Do you know Grim Batol…" At the mention of Grim Batol, Jace and Grede, who had just sat down at a nearby table, looked over.

"What exactly happened there?" the Bronzebeard dwarf said. "This morning I heard from a patrolling brother that the whole place has been destroyed, blown up like it was hit by a meteor — all fire, and… it looked really ominous. He told me that the Dark Portal might open another one deep inside Grim Batol. Judging by your outfit, you're one of those Farstriders, aren't you? You must know something."

"Uh," Vereesa glanced at Jace and Grede beside her, her eyes a little troubled.

This dwarf glanced at the table the elf kept looking toward, then noticed Jace and said, "Mage, are you with her?"

"Yes," Jace said, sitting over and leaning in. "Something did go wrong at Grim Batol — seems like the red dragons they were raising lost control. We were planning to go investigate, but it was too dangerous. Looks like a disaster caused by the Dragonmaw orcs' dark magic — there was simply no way to get close. So we're planning to go around telling everyone to stay away from Banthor Mountain on the east side of the Wetlands for now."

"So that's what happened," the dwarf patron said, frowning. "How terrifying. Grim Batol was already a cursed place. When the orcs went there, I knew they were asking for trouble! Well, I really thought a second Dark Portal was coming. Does that mean we don't have to worry about orcs anymore?"

Jace said, "A lot of Dragonmaw orcs escaped from Grim Batol. There may be some stragglers getting close to Menethil Harbor or here, but that fortress… there are no orcs left in it."

"So we still need to be careful," Grede said.

"More precisely, we need to be more careful about isolated incidents; we just don't have to worry about a full-scale invasion," Jace said.

"Thank you so much, mage," the dwarf raised his eyebrows, shrank back to his table, and then got into a heated discussion with his companions. Jace nodded, then looked over to see both Grede and Vereesa giving him a strange look.

"What?" Jace said. "Let's get some food."

"Did you make that up on the spot, or did you have it prepared?" Grede asked.

"Uh," Jace said. "I don't know, probably made it up on the spot."

"Vereesa," Grede said softly. "You really dared to take a guy who opens his mouth and talks nonsense and shows absolutely no sign that he's talking nonsense?"

Vereesa said, "As long as he doesn't lie to me, I don't care who else he lies to."

Grede leaned over and said, "Have you forgotten how this guy talked to those orc soldiers in Grim Batol — exactly like a real orc? If it weren't for that sword, I couldn't tell which was him and which was…"

"Now that you mention it…" Vereesa nodded.

"So where did I talk nonsense?" Jace said. "What I just said — which part was wrong?"
