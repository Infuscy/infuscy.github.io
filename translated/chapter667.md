## Chapter 667: The Algaz Specialty

"The red dragons lost control? All right… the Dragonmaw orcs' dark magic? I suppose that technically counts too." Grede hesitated, then pressed his voice low: "Then what about the black dragon? Leaving it out on purpose doesn't mean it's not a lie, does it?"

"You're not thinking of telling the townsfolk here that Deathwing suddenly showed up again, are you?" Jace said, propping his cheek on his hand and keeping his voice low. "He's dead anyway. Why make everyone live in fear?"

Vereesa looked at Jace and smiled. Just then the dwarf waitress leaned in and said, "Ah, dear allies — a human gentleman, an elven lady, and a handsome Wildhammer lad — the whole party's here! What can I get you?"

At "handsome lad," Jace glanced at Grede in shock.

"Blood sausage of Thelsamar!" Vereesa said immediately.

"No problem — looks like the fame of our blood sausage has already drifted over to the beautiful magic kingdom." The waitress's face creased into a broad grin as she turned to the other two.

Jace said, "Last time I was here you mentioned Thelsamar had a lot of other dishes too… like soup? Last time I didn't get to try any soup. Could you recommend something?"

"The most famous here is the Algaz Goat-Milk Chowder," the waitress said. "Would you like to try it? It's made with Algaz Pass goat cheese, Thunderbrew ale, eggs, local black-boar ham, mustard and pepper, and baked into a rich chowder… If the elven lady likes things sweet, you can add sweet milk too — wonderful for dipping slices of bread."

Jace's stomach was already growling from hunger. He'd eaten too many rotten apples and rancid smoked meat back in Grim Batol; just listening to the description was almost unbearable. Vereesa, sitting beside him, kept swallowing involuntarily.

Vereesa said, "I don't want it sweet. I want the authentic Thelsamar flavor."

"One bowl each!" Grede tapped the table with his finger. "And bring a big plate of bread — like you said, none of that coarse, hard black bread… and none of that squishy sweet stuff either. The best kind you've got!"

"And some roast meat," Jace added. "What's a specialty roast here? Last time we had bear meat and beer pork chops. Anything you'd be interested in, Wen?"

"Bring that bear meat again, seriously," Grede said. "I think we absolutely have to let the elf try it."

"Right, bear meat! Sounds novel," Vereesa said. "There are plenty of bears in the Wetlands, but none in Quel'Thalas — I wonder what it tastes like."

Jace nodded. "Then what about drinks?"

"Thunderbrew ale from Kharanos, obviously. Last time you came I drank it with Dagot — you didn't get any," Grede said, slapping the table. "Six mugs of Thunderbrew ale to start!"

The waitress said with a beaming smile, "All right, just a moment, sirs and miss!"

"Dagot?" Vereesa asked, watching the waitress leave. "Who's that, one of your dwarf friends?"

"You could say that," Grede said. "Last time we came through here we met a Bronzebeard dwarf from the Explorers' League. He had a strange object he'd brought back from Northrend — a woman's head. That thing had his head all muddled; he was talking nothing but nonsense."

As he said this, Grede's expression cooled from the excitement of picking out beer and food, and Vereesa didn't press further.

"So, Jace — the Dragonqueen said Deathwing came to you." He glanced at Jace's arm. "How did he put that mark on your hand?"

"In a dream," Jace said.

"In a dream?" Vereesa asked, surprised. "What kind of dream?"

Jace watched the dwarf at the next table get up and leave, made sure no one around them could hear, and rubbed his forehead with his palm. "Just a jumbled-up mess of a dream. In it, he said he could let me live forever. In exchange, I had to go into Grim Batol and get the Demon Soul for him, then put the Dragonmaw orcs at his service — help him protect his dragon eggs, that sort of thing."

"Then he put this mark on my hand. I think he can use it to hear what I say and see what I see… and once I fall asleep, he can torment me however he pleases."

"So that night you just had a dream Deathwing gave you?" Vereesa asked. "That night it rained, at Tol'rona?"

Jace said, "Yeah, that's the one. He said he'd already captured Krasus."

"Gods." Grede shook his head. "What have you been through? Damn it all."

"I suspect someone in the Kirin Tor sold me and Master Krasus out — tipped Deathwing off," Jace said quietly. "I think it's Kel'Thuzad, but I don't have a shred of evidence. Deathwing didn't leak a single detail about it in the dream, and honestly, I didn't dare ask him either. I'm planning to stop in Dalaran on the way back to Lordaeron to see my parents, and feel things out."

"Kel'Thuzad," Vereesa said. "He's a member of the Council of Six. Between you and him, the Kirin Tor might not side with you…"

"Krasus will side with me," Jace said. "I'm going to find a way to go to him first and tell him about this. But you're right — besides him, the Kirin Tor will probably not speak up for me. Until I find solid evidence, I have to pretend I know nothing at all to the other Kirin Tor members."

Just then the waitress brought over a plate of blood sausage, glistening and dark, and said in Dwarvish, "Good appetite to you all."

"Let's not talk about boring stuff like that and ruin the mood," Jace said with a smile. "Hurry up and try it, Wen."

Vereesa hummed in acknowledgment, speared a slice of blood sausage, put it in her mouth — and froze.

She stared at Jace in front of her, then glanced at Grede beside her, chewed with difficulty, frowned, and swallowed the whole thing in one gulp.

"How is it?" Grede asked with undisguised glee.

"It reminded me of the last time a troll knocked out one of my teeth," Vereesa said, drawing a sharp breath. "Exactly the same taste."

"Sounds like you don't like it — which means the two of us are diving in!" Grede went in with his hands, and Jace immediately grabbed a slice too. The two of them devoured the entire plate of blood sausage in no time.

No sooner was the plate emptied than the Algaz Goat-Milk Chowder was served.

Jace lifted his bowl and couldn't help taking a big gulp first. The orange-yellow chowder was thick, almost paste-like. After swallowing, a salty, roasted-milk flavor spread through him, fully satisfying the exhaustion and weariness of the journey — and filling the emptiness left after the Refined Wisdom potion had worn off… especially when he bit into the bits of smoked ham inside. The burst of meaty aroma was as satisfying as finding a priceless diamond buried in a pile of gold.

Vereesa had milk foam from the tip of her nose to her lips. Grede lifted his bowl and gulped it down. Jace stared at the sliced bread on the table and said, "Hey, hey — the bread!"

"Oh, right!"

The three of them put down their bowls and lunged for the bread at the same time, dipping slices into the bowls and stuffing them into their mouths, then using the last slices to wipe their bowls perfectly clean…

"I want another bowl," Vereesa said, holding her empty bowl. "Save room for ale, little elf!" Grede said.

"I've got plenty of room," Vereesa said.

"Three more bowls of chowder, then, boss!" Grede said with a loud laugh.

Roast meat, ale, milk chowder… the three of them ate and drank until they were stuffed, and before they knew it, evening had come. Grede's third mug of ale was still unfinished when he suddenly slumped face-first onto the table.

Jace, who had just taken a swig of ale, set down his mug and glanced at him. "How'd he get drunk so fast?"

"He's not drunk — he's exhausted. He hasn't slept this whole time underground, Jace," Vereesa said helplessly. "Don't forget, he's a dwarf."

"Right."

Only then did Jace remember that he himself hadn't slept in several days either — though thanks to the potions, and having had blood drawn back in Grim Batol, he wasn't feeling all that tired at the moment.

"Let's take him back to the room together," Vereesa said.

Jace nodded, downed the rest of his ale in one gulp, and together with Vereesa hauled Grede down the stairs and into the room they'd booked.

They unclasped the dwarf's cloak and tossed it aside, set down the remaining luggage, and hauled the stone-heavy fellow onto the bed. By the time they were done, both of them were slumped to the side, breathing hard.

"Are you heading back to Stormwind with him tomorrow?" Vereesa asked.

"Yeah," Jace said. "Are you going back to Quel'Thalas?"

"Mm. I need to report this to Sylvanas as early as possible," Vereesa said.

She stood up and wrapped her cloak tight. "I want to wander around a bit and see Loch Modan. Last time I came through here with the Farstriders to hunt down that death knight… remember him? Angor Deadwish — we only stopped here briefly, but it's a beautiful place."

"Are you coming?" she asked.

"Let's go." Jace stood up, pulled the imp out of his coat and set him on the ground. "Watch this room, Sazeno — you and Morophil together. Protect that gold necklace around his neck."

"Got it!" The imp hopped onto the bed, then onto the stone table, and called toward the door, "Get in here, Sayad! You're not going to make me protect such a terrifying treasure all by myself, are you?"

Morophil wound her way in from the shadows beneath the lamplight and said coolly, "Have fun, my dear master."
