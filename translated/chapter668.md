## Chapter 668: Starlight on the Loch

The Loch at night, dotted here and there with the lights of dwarf houses in the distance.

Beyond the single street of Thelsamar, scattered stone cottages spread across the lakeshore. They didn't look like much until the lamps were lit at night — without that, they were nearly invisible, blending into the cliffs and hills.

Jace and Vereesa came to the forest by the lakeshore, gazing at the starlight reflected on the water as they slowly walked.

"Actually, I'm grateful to Morophil," Vereesa said.

"Because she saved you?" Jace asked.

"Of course I'm grateful for that too, regardless of whether it was on your orders," Vereesa said. "But that's not what I mean. I mean going to Kalimdor. If it weren't for her, I might never have figured out what exactly I felt for you."

The arcane flames in the elf's eyes dimmed a little; Jace could even make out her real eyes now."I'm sorry," he said.

"I'm not about to compete with a demon servant for anything, Jace," Vereesa said, flicking a long eyebrow. "Don't forget where I come from."

"Why don't we talk about you instead? Since when did you start taking an interest in an 'elf' from Quel'Thalas?" Jace thought for a moment. "Remember Eastvale?"

"Ah — how could I forget? I knew it." She smiled slightly.

The two of them sat by the water's edge, leaning against each other, gazing at the heart of the lake bathed in moonlight. Vereesa rubbed the emerald earring at her ear. "I can't wait to go back north and show this thing to them, let those folks get an eyeful…"

"Oh, right," she suddenly turned and said. "If you're going back to Lordaeron, why don't you come to Quel'Thalas with me while you're at it? Let me tell them that Jace Sesso is mine."

"Will Sylvanas kill me?" Jace asked with a smile.

"Are you afraid of her?" Vereesa asked.

Jace very much wanted to say that the Sylvanas in her head and the Sylvanas in his head were definitely not the same person. But then again, even if it were that Sylvanas, so what?

"Then before we meet, remember to let me drink a bottle of Sunwell water to wet my throat — in case she gets impatient and suddenly stabs me, I definitely won't be able to dodge."

Vereesa tilted her head. "If I'm going to let you drink a bottle of well water, you'd better guarantee she'll stab you the instant she sees you — otherwise wouldn't that be a waste?"

Jace's face stiffened. "How do you guarantee that?"

"You idiot." Vereesa turned her face away and looked toward the water grass not far off.

Jace understood what she was hinting at in an instant. He felt his heart hammer once, hard, and a wave of dizziness washed over his mind.

He tentatively reached out and took hold of the elf's cloak, tugging it to the side. Vereesa flinched as if shocked and lightning-fast seized his wrist. "Here?!"

"Are you scared?" Jace asked.

Vereesa bit her lower lip and glanced toward the distant lights of Thelsamar. "Who's scared of whom…"

The two of them fumbled eagerly and clumsily out of their cloaks, robes, and clothes. Vereesa breathed deeply, until at last only a layer of patterned elven breast-wrap remained. Her hand gripping the hem began to tremble violently.

"I'm a little…"

"What is it?"

"I don't know, I…"

"This isn't your first time, is it?"

"You bastard — how could it be my first time? Do you think I had zero appeal back in Quel'Thalas?"

"What's that got to do with anything? You're a Windrunner — how many dared mess with you?"

"Ugh… fine. So what if it is my first time?"

Jace looked at her expression and almost couldn't hold back a laugh. Was it that in Quel'Thalas, having no experience at all was something to be looked down on?

"Are you trying to laugh?!" In half a second Vereesa had drawn the Blade of Eternal Darkness from the robe beside her and pressed it to his throat. "If you breathe a word of this to any other Quel'dorei, I'll carve your stupid face to ribbons!"

With the shadow-imbued blade pressed against his throat, Jace shook his head in terror.

Vereesa frowned and tossed the dagger aside. "Then stop talking and hurry up!"

But no sooner had she lain down than she hesitated and said quietly, "Damn it, I feel like I can't breathe."

Jace braced himself on the grass, leaning over Vereesa, who lay on her back on the cloak with her chest heaving. He looked into her eyes. She stared at him for a moment, then averted her gaze; even her lips were trembling.

"Then how about this." He slowly lowered his body and kissed her flat, firm little belly, breathing in the faint scent of fire still lingering on her skin, and a light fragrance of grass and flowers.

He kissed lower and lower. Vereesa realized something was off and abruptly clamped her legs around his head, trying to grab hold of his hair.

"Don't… Jace, the last time I had a proper bath was in Quel'Thalas. The lake is right here — at least let me…!"

"…You, don't… ah…"

"Jace…"

"I like you, Wen." Jace raised his head and looked at the elf, whose upper body had curled up in shyness. "I had to say it."

Vereesa flushed, let out a breath, and smiled. "Well then, I like you — you must have known that all along." Jace hesitated for a moment, then said quietly, "Actually, I was never quite sure."

At that, the smile on Vereesa's face slowly faded. Her gaze grew more and more distant. She just looked at Jace like that, and tears suddenly welled up and spilled over.

"…I'm really afraid you'll die, Jace. I'm really afraid that one day you'll suddenly be gone. I'm so scared — what do you want me to say?! I'm sorry, Jace. I'm truly sorry…"

She bent forward and clung to Jace tightly, pressing against his hair and forcing back her sobs. "But even if the Dragonqueen doesn't bless you, I've already decided — I'm yours. Forever."

The two of them held each other tightly for a long time. Jace said softly, "You don't need to be afraid anymore. I'll stay with you to the very end."

"Remember your own words." Vereesa wiped away her tears and smiled again. "These past few days have felt like a dream. You stood in the center of that hall, driving Azure Skysong through your own arm. My heart ached… but at that moment you were like a hero out of a story — the master of Grim Batol. I think the Dragonqueen thought so too. Maybe you didn't notice, but she was stunned too. Heh — only that dwarf, too busy fighting orcs to care."

Jace couldn't help laughing too. "Ever since the war ended, he's finally had a chance to fight orcs to his heart's content. I suspect he followed me into Grim Batol just for that."

Vereesa was silent for a moment, then fixed Jace with a stare. "Then you followed me here just so we could chat about that 'Mountain King'?"

"Shall we continue?" Jace asked.

Vereesa glanced down, and her face flushed hot again.

"Mm." She tilted her head forward and tasted his lips. "I can't wait."
