## Chapter 669: The Plan to Go Home

Puff, puff…

"What…"

Jace opened his eyes to find an enormous black nose sniffing right in front of him. A brown bear!

Jace leaped up and bolted straight into the lake in front of him. He patted himself down — nothing but a single layer of clothing. He had no choice but to scoop up a pebble from the bottom and point it at the bear.

He silently readied a Fear spell. The moment that brown bear made any move to charge into the water, it was going to learn what's what.

Day had broken. The sun was overhead. The distant mountains stood quietly between wisps of white cloud. The brown bear just sat on the shore, watching him without moving. Then, from a short cliff nearby, came a burst of laughter.

"Vereesa?"

Jace caught sight of the ranger in the green cloak up above and finally let out a breath. "Did you find this bear?"

"It was right around here, so I asked it if it wouldn't mind waking you up." Vereesa stood up, whistled once, and the brown bear barked back at her and obediently left.

"You scared me half to death," Jace said. "I thought it was about to eat me."

"If I hadn't had a chat with it, it probably really would have eaten you." Vereesa hopped down from the cliff and came over, handing Jace a yellow fruit. "You didn't even mutter a spell at it. Here, try this."

Jace took a bite of the fruit, then walked to the shore to pull on his robe and cloak. "About to."

Vereesa wrapped her arms around him from behind and said softly, "Last night… it was so good I can't even describe it, Jace."

"Same here," Jace said with a smile.

"I want to follow you back to Stormwind," the ranger said, leaning against Jace's back, her voice getting smaller and smaller. "Then we'd have more chances to…"

"…But it would waste too much time. It's such a pain."

"We'll see each other again soon," Jace said. "Don't forget — I still have to go to Quel'Thalas with you."

"Then when are you planning to set out for Dalaran?" Vereesa asked.

Jace thought it over. "Handle things back in Stormwind first, then leave. I'm guessing I'll reach Dalaran around Winter Veil."

"Less than a month," Vereesa said. "Since it's the dwarves' Winter Veil, are you coming with Grede?"

"I'll have to ask him," Jace said.

"Then let's meet at Dalaran for Winter Veil — how about it?" Vereesa asked.

"Then it's settled," Jace said. "But before going to Quel'Thalas, I want to go back to Lordaeron to see my parents first. The Kirin Tor had them under surveillance before — had the place watched tight just to keep me from getting close. It's different now. I have to go see them at least once. That'll probably take an extra three or four days. After I come back from Quel'Thalas, I'll bring them back to Stormwind with me."

Vereesa said, "I'm going too."

"You're going too?" Jace asked in surprise. "You want to meet them?"

"What? Do your father and mother hate Quel'dorei too?"

"Too?"

"The first time I met you, it felt like you really didn't care for us," Vereesa said.

Jace teased, "Then shouldn't you ask yourself why you were treating me so badly?"

Vereesa said, "I was tracking a black dragon for two months with Instructor Kael and a few Farstrider apprentices when a bunch of Lordaeronians took us out of the blue. My Farstrider training was about to be delayed — and then the guy who killed that black dragon was right in front of me. You expect me to be nice to you and Grede?"

"What do you mean 'out of the blue'? That black dragon had flown all the way to Southshore — you're not allowed to fight back?" Jace said. "And you say you don't hate humans?"

"I didn't know how badly Southshore had been damaged back then," Vereesa sighed. "And the me back then was like that — only caring about my own affairs, never minding anyone else. I hardly ever left Quel'Thalas, never talked to anyone outside the Quel'dorei. I learned Common only because Farstriders taking part in Lordaeron Alliance missions had to. The orcs had already been defeated and the trolls had gone into hiding — if I didn't take on missions outside Quel'Thalas, I'd have no chance to distinguish myself."

"If it weren't for taking on Teron Gorefiend together with you, and meeting a human like you, I'd probably still be like that. Looking back now, I barely recognize the person I was back then. Before that, I had no idea the outside world was like this…"

Jace teased, "I thought you and Kael came chasing Teron Gorefiend to save an innocent human mage apprentice like me."

"For revenge," Vereesa said. "But we did save you too, didn't we?"

"That's true," Jace said.

"It's just that Gorefiend was way more powerful than I imagined," Vereesa shrugged. "Without you, Instructor Kael and I would have been done for. I'll admit that."

Jace said, "You charged straight in without any idea of what your opponent was capable of." Vereesa smiled. "I just knew I had to kill him. It was that simple."

What a twist of fate, Jace thought to himself.

"Come back with me, Wen. I'll take you to meet them — you'll like them," he said.

"I can't wait!" Vereesa let out a breath, then glanced at the sun overhead. "Ugh, it's already pretty late — shouldn't we be heading back to Thelsamar?"

"Let's go."

The two of them chatted the whole way about everything that had happened — from how Vereesa had escaped Quel'Thalas to track down that death knight in Stormwind, to everything that had happened after… By the time they got back under the tavern, Grede was still sprawled on the bed, snoring away.

Morophil, sitting beside him, moved her nose slightly and slowly shook her head. "I never imagined the scent of love could cause me such pain. Who says a Sayad is a demon attracted to love tokens? Right now all I want is to flee this miserable atmosphere…"

"Quit the act," Jace said, throwing his cloak onto a chair to the side. "Talk about it like it's real — publish a poetry collection, Morophil. Help me earn some royalties."

"Why couldn't it be real?" The succubus smiled and looked over. "Can't I love you, master?"

"Whether it's real or not, it's too late," Vereesa said, leaning against the wall with a smile. "He's mine now."

"Love's gone and the mana's gone too?!" Morophil said, visibly displeased. "You already have the Sunwell — and you still want to steal mine? Besides, master, you said your mana would be mine forever. You're going back on that now?"

"I never said it wasn't yours," Jace said.

Vereesa said, "That's between you and him — nothing to do with me."

"Looking at it that way, at least I'm not left with nothing, am I?" Morophil threw herself at Jace and hugged him. "When we get back, let me taste the Dragonqueen's blood, master. Let her dragonfire burn me to dark cinders…"

"Disgusting," Vereesa said coldly. "Damn demon — get away from him right now, don't make me regret this!"

"What are you two fighting about…" Grede smacked his lips and mumbled. "Don't wake me up. Tomorrow we still have to kill that… that… death… right, Deathwing. Shut your mouths — remember, we have to kill Deathwing… let me get some proper sleep first… my necklace, so beautiful…"

Everyone went quiet for a moment, making sure the dwarf wasn't going to mumble anymore. The imp wore a wicked grin watching the scene. Jace slowly pried the succubus's arm from around his neck…

Vereesa glared at Jace, forcing her voice low. "One Sayad is enough, Jace Sesso. If I find out there's a second…"

"I can vouch for my master on that one, Windrunner — you won't be seeing a second," Morophil said, twirling the black dagger in her hand.
