Chapter 671: Return to the Basement

Back in Stormwind, the first thing Jace had to do was go take a look at that house he'd just bought from the Lescovar family.

He cut straight through the Trade District to the canal up north, and by the time he arrived the afternoon was already wearing on. The walk had to make him admit that at least from the city gate through the market to here, the distance wasn't all that far — he probably wouldn't even need half an hour to get to the Cathedral. Location-wise, this was deeply satisfying. It was the house he'd hand-picked, after all.

The Park district was frankly terrible — not particularly close to the Church District, even farther from the Trade District market, and never mind the city gate. The only thing it had going for it was being near the harbor…

And, most importantly, there were far too many elves moving in. Jace didn't like living around elves. He thought about Windrunner and felt that this particular act of self-justification was hopelessly unconvincing.

Well, he'd bought it. That was that. The Park was right on the water — if Kul Tiras and Stormwind ever went to war one day and the fleet blockaded the harbor, wouldn't the seaside Park district be in serious danger?

Perfectly reasonable logic. Thinking this, Jace opened the door and stepped into his new home.

Morophil appeared from beside him and settled into the wooden chair in front of the fireplace, crossing her legs. "So this is master's new house? It really is big. And beautiful."

"It still needs renovating," Jace said. "Once we clean the whole place up and fix everything that's old or damaged, it'll look even better. Oh, right — I still need to go to Master Dushan and get my gold back. It's a bit late today; I'll go bother him tomorrow morning."

"Are we sleeping here tonight, darling?" Morophil asked, propping her chin on her hand.

"No," Jace said. "This house is for my mum and dad to live in someday. You sleeping here would be bad for their health."

"How heartbreaking," Morophil sighed.

"Have you forgotten you're a demon?" Jace asked, fixing her with a stare.

"She's gotten too into character, master!" Sazeno darted into the fireplace, glanced up, then turned his head and said. "She's practically started thinking of herself as a mortal! Hah!"

The succubus murmured, "If master let me have a taste of the red dragon's essence, I might just start thinking I'm a female red dragon myself. I wouldn't mind. Miss Morophil is just that free-spirited a succubus."

"The Life-Binder's essence." Jace stared at the pile of firewood sitting in the fireplace, who knew how long it had been since it was last lit, and slowly extended his right hand.

Right — he couldn't use fire magic in this house. Who knew what might go wrong?

Jace curled his fingers back into a fist. "Let's call it a night for now. I'm going to save my mana to try out a spell in the basement. I'm not deliberately turning you down, Morophil. Regardless, I'm grateful. Without you, the lot of us never could have pulled off this Grim Batol run."

"No need to thank me. I simply followed the commands you gave me, master." That smile never left Morophil's face.

Jace nodded. "Let's get out of here. Back to the old place. Sazeno — back in the bag."

"Why doesn't master buy his own house in Stormwind?" Sazeno said as he scurried over. "Preferably one with an underground hall — a grand chamber for summoning powerful demons, and cells carved with demon runes to lock up creatures from the Twisting Nether…"

"Plus an enchanted study where you can learn all manner of strange magics, and neither fire nor Shadow would so much as scorch the walls or doors… That way you wouldn't always have to go test your spells in the forests outside the city, or sneak around practicing in that little basement."

"What you're describing isn't a house. It's a castle," Jace said. "Given my current finances, that's a grander ambition than I can realize, Sazeno. And even if I bought a small building like this one, it couldn't withstand demon summoning and dark magic either. It wouldn't be all that different from the single room I'm renting. Forget it."

Sazeno clambered up Jace's chest and leaped into the open purple bag. "You slew a great black dragon and saved the red dragons! The boy king of Stormwind ought to grant you a castle!"

"He dares to offer and I'd dare not accept," Jace said. "The upkeep alone on a castle — unless the Dragonqueen is willing to pay for it with the gems off her crown, I couldn't possibly afford it. Anyway, for now I'll focus on getting this house sorted out first."

That evening, after bringing the two demons back home, Jace changed into a set of plain clothes and headed to the Slaughtered Lamb carrying his void robe.

This time the void robe genuinely needed a proper repair. The fact that it had only sustained minor damage after being swept by Deathwing's shadowflame and spending all that time in the burning Grim Batol had already shocked Jace.

Still, minor damage was minor damage. While traveling, blending in with a bunch of weary travelers, it might not have been noticeable. But if he was going to Dalaran — if he was going to see his parents — showing up in this thing would be entirely inappropriate.

This time he planned to buy a few normal sets of clothes for the trip to Lordaeron and leave the robe here with Gaylene for repairs.

Entering the Mage Quarter, he found it as calm and unchanging as when he'd left a month ago. News of the red dragon probably hadn't reached here yet.

Grede had flown him back here at speed on a griffin. The Khaz Modan messenger carrying word of the Grim Batol orc situation probably hadn't arrived any faster — after all, there weren't many dwarves in Ironforge more skilled at griffin-riding than Grede.

Outside the Slaughtered Lamb, Tharle was wiping down the door. Seeing Jace approach, he said, "Oh, Jace? Feels like you haven't been around in weeks."

Jace watched him working as he spoke, looking perfectly unruffled. He thought about it — he'd only been gone a little over a month, but it felt like a lifetime.

"Business up north," Jace said. "I'm heading to the practice room to test a few spells. By the way, has Galders tried summoning a felguard?"

"Felguard? No," Tharle said. "Galders figured that without you here, they shouldn't go dragging that powerful a demon into Stormwind. But they did summon another one, and they managed to bind it using your magic circle. It's locked in one of the basement practice rooms right now. None of them have been able to enslave the thing, though. But it's trapped in the circle, and Galders sealed the door with Dark Iron sealing runes. Plus Ursula's hellhound Basadem keeps watch on the situation, so things haven't spiraled out of control."

"Honestly, Jace, I don't even know if you'd call it a demon. I haven't seen it myself, but Ursula says it's basically just a mass of shadow."

A voidwalker?

These lot had actually summoned a voidwalker?

Voidwalkers… could be described as masses of Shadow "elementals" — chaotic energies bound to the physical plane.

They were quiet and steady by nature, capable of repairing their damaged forms by drawing in the Shadow around them. As long as they weren't suddenly obliterated by a powerful force, they were hard to deal what you'd call a fatal blow.

Jace had never seen a voidwalker here before, so he was even a little curious…
