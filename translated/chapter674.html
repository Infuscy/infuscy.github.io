Chapter 674: Effects Beyond the Flame

The next morning, standing in the Mage Quarter garden waiting for Master Dushan to appear, Jace was still thinking about yesterday's business with the Scorch incinerating that hellhound.

He couldn't summon demons again in the short term. According to Erland Dudemoor, letting the Void corrode your brain into Shadow slurry by summoning demons back to back… was no laughing matter.

But as he looked at the winter-yellowed flowers and plants around him, he actually felt a faint impulse to swallow everything in flame… As he dwelled on it, Jace actually caught a whiff of something burning.

What the hell?

That rattled him. He quickly touched his hair, then saw that the cuff of his linen shirt had somehow burned a hole through itself, and there was even a tiny flicker of flame still on it.

Jace immediately pinched out the flame with his fingers. He felt a searing heat on his fingertip — and that searing heat… actually brought a flicker of pleasure. What had Alexstrasza done to him?

Just as he was marveling at the fire-elemental affinity the Dragonqueen's essence had brought him, a familiar figure passed by not far off.

Caro Dushan would come to the Blue Heron around midday for a cup of coffee and some reading, especially right after his morning lessons. Jace had often spotted him in the past hurrying off in the direction of the tavern after class, and today was no exception.

Jace followed him into the Blue Heron. Dushan had already bought his coffee and two croissants, and was sitting in the corner poring over a stack of parchment materials. Jace quietly settled Dushan's tab with the tavern owner, then came up to the Orcish teacher's table.

"Good afternoon, Master Dushan."

Caro Dushan glanced up at Jace. "Oh, it's you — Jay… right, that name of yours…"

"Jace Sesso, Master," Jace said. "You've even forgotten my name, Master."

"I didn't forget your name." Dushan pointed at the seat across the table. "Sit down, quick. I forgot that other name of yours — the Dwarvish one." Jace pulled out a chair. "That one doesn't matter anymore, Master."

"Haven't seen you in ages — you've changed a lot, Jace. Young people really are a different person from one day to the next." Dushan picked up a croissant. "Want some?"

"I already ate at the Gilded Rose," Jace said. "Actually, I'm here today to…"

Dushan was taking a sip of coffee when his expression suddenly changed. "Right, right, of course — your money's still with me. That dwarf friend of yours, Grede Thunderfist, asked me to hold onto it for you. I'll take you to my place later to get it."

"Thank you so much, Master," Jace nodded.

"How have you been? Have you been practicing Orcish consistently?" Dushan asked, then added, "I think Orcish is going to become more and more important in the future. It'd be a real shame to let it drop just like that."

"I have," Jace answered honestly. "I was practicing Orcish diligently just the other day."

"That's truly rare," Dushan said. "Actually, I've been helping my students apply for visits to the internment camps. You know there are orcs in the Kingdom of Lordaeron who've been bribing humans with stolen money to help them contact the internment camps and orcs imprisoned elsewhere, trying to stage breakouts — so it's been getting harder and harder to get these opportunities lately. Being able to talk to real orcs would improve your language skills remarkably fast."

"Couldn't agree more, Master," Jace said.

That's what he said, but he had no desire to get another one of those intensive language-improvement opportunities. Thalassian might be all right, but Orcish was a hard pass.

Following the teacher back to his residence behind the Wizard Sanctum, Jace collected his coins — a small box packed full of more than sixty gold pieces — along with the contract and the deed. He felt like his life was coming back together again.

Next on the list was buying a decent set of clothes. Jace carried the money box to the door of Larson's Clothing Shop and walked in. Mr. James Larson was propping his chin on his hand and dozing off in the afternoon. Seeing someone come in, he forced his eyes open with effort.

"Good afternoon, young sir. Are you a mage?"

Old Larson clearly didn't recognize him again. Jace said, "I'm looking for a normal set of clothes — not a robe. A loose silk or mageweave shirt and jacket, plus a pair of matching trousers. Frankly, I don't know much about fashion, Mr. Larson. I'm going home to see my parents soon. I want them to know I'm doing all right and not to worry about me, but without being too flashy."

"I understand." Larson came out from behind the counter. "May I measure your height?"

"Go ahead."

Actually, Jace had come partly to get his measured height. The way Ursula had looked him up and down in the basement had made him want to find out just how much the Dragonqueen's essence had changed him.

After all, last time he'd absorbed some black dragon blood he'd seemed to grow a little taller. And this time he'd been blessed directly by a red dragon…

Going to the corner of the room, Larson took a measuring stick and measured him. Watching the old gentleman squint at the markings, Jace even felt a little nervous.

He glanced at the mirror to the side. He saw his mess of hair, his face covered in scars and burn marks — still thin, but with a lot more color than before, looking much better than the last time he'd checked.

The sight before him reminded him of those veterans he'd met in Southshore when he'd first left his parents — soldiers recovering in town after the Battle of Hillsblade, every last one of them looking about the way he did now. Survivors who'd struggled to live on, out of the blood-and-fire-scarred hills and mountains the red dragons had burned.

"Just under six feet one inch, sir — but I can count it as six feet one, because it's very close."

"You're sure?"

"Of course I'm sure."

If that was accurate, then he'd reached a hundred and eighty-five centimeters!

Jace was stunned. No wonder Ursula looked him up and down every time she saw him. He'd grown more than ten centimeters in the past two years.

Of course, this rate of growth wasn't that different from when he was twelve or thirteen and had first started shooting up. But after he'd first arrived in Stormwind, his growth had already become barely noticeable year by year. These past two years of height gain was clearly supernatural…

Still, he'd realized one thing: that little bit of black dragon whelp blood had made him shoot up five or six centimeters, while the Dragonqueen's essence had only made him grow another five centimeters. That whelp blood was absolutely nowhere near as powerful as a Dragon Aspect's essence.

Setting aside the fact that he might still grow a little at around twenty years old, it was possible that the effect of power on height had diminishing returns for him. Even if he received a more extreme blessing, he probably wouldn't make it to two meters.

"Let me find a few suitable outfits for you to try on, sir."

He changed into outfit after outfit, in every color and every style. Jace turned back and forth in front of the mirror. He wasn't sure if it was because he'd been through so much in Kalimdor, or because he'd received the red dragon's blessing, but even his shoulders looked broader. Was that the result of his muscles being tempered?

He should go find Grede and practice swordsmanship again, see what the results were.

Forget it — he wasn't going to go pick a fight with Grede, that humanoid top made of metal and rock. Just remembering the way the dwarf had casually yanked off the boulder that was pinning Jace down made his scalp tingle.

In the mirror, he was wearing a light cyan mageweave shirt under a sky-blue wool jacket, a pair of light gray silk trousers, and a pair of black leather boots.

"I'll take this set, Mr. Larson."

"No problem. That'll be two gold and thirty-two silver, young sir."
