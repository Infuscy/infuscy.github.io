Chapter 675: Different Ways of Achieving It

Stepping out of Larson Clothiers, Jace glanced across at the Park.

Since he was already in the Mage Quarter, he might as well go ask his teacher about fire magic.

Although the course he was enrolled in at the Mage Quarter was an Abjuration course, Master Malin had such a good relationship with him — he should be willing to teach him how to control fire too, right?

Malin was the most powerful fire mage in the Wizard’s Sanctum, no question. Even in Dalaran, only the most powerful and renowned fire masters could outclass him — and even Andromath acknowledged that.

Since he was facing the problem of his fire powers being a little out of control, going to Malin for help was the most natural thing in the world.

Jace knocked on the door, confirmed Malin was inside, and pushed it open with the money box in his arms. Malin looked over at him. “Mr. Sesso, you’re dressed rather splendidly today.”

Compared to the last time he’d suddenly vanished for half a month, everyone’s reaction to his return this time was decidedly more subdued.

“Master.” Jace set the box down on a small chest to the side. “I’m actually here to learn… some other spells.”

“Oh, so you finally want to learn something new,” Malin said. “But it’s not free, my good student. Hurry up and hand in that death knight report. By now the remaining death knights in Azeroth have all been wiped out by the Farstrider forces of Quel’Thalas. And I can hardly expect the Silvermoon magisters traveling with the Farstriders to write reports for the Wizard’s Sanctum. You’re our only hope on this one.”

“Working on it, working on it,” Jace said, scratching his head. “I’m heading to the Lordaeron continent in a little while. When I’m back, I’ll definitely hand in my assignment to you by early next year.”

“That’s more like it,” Malin said, relieved. “At last I can see a glimmer of hope. Go on, then — what do you want to learn?”

Jace had been about to ask Malin on the spot how to control fire more naturally, but hearing him say that, he suddenly wanted to learn something else.

“Blink — is that a possibility?” Jace asked.

Malin shook his head. “First, you’d need to enroll in the school of Transmutation and achieve a certain standing before the Wizard’s Sanctum would allow me to teach you a dangerous spell like Blink. Besides…”

Then Jace saw a complicated expression surface on Malin’s face. He knew it for what it was — the look of someone who wanted to say something harsh but didn’t want to discourage the young man in front of him.

Probably the old arcane-talent issue again.

“You can’t just learn an advanced Abjuration spell like Mage Armor and think you can master Blink with ease, Mr. Sesso,” Malin said, helpless. “Mage Armor is, on the whole, a spell where hard work makes up for — what I mean is, a spell where diligent training yields results. That’s why I was willing to try teaching it to you. But Blink — without a fair degree of Transmutation-school magical talent, the learning process itself could produce a catastrophe. This isn’t like Counterspell, where even if I teach it and you don’t learn it well, nothing terrible will happen. Even Fireball’s failure is more manageable than Blink’s.”

“If you hurl an uncontrollable Fireball around my room, the absolute worst that could happen is that you blow the room to pieces and destroy all my possessions in an instant. But I’m confident I could weave a fire-protection barrier on the spot and save both our lives. Compared to that, everything else is secondary. But if you tried to Blink through that door behind you… I have no means of stopping half your body from fusing with the wall or the door in a most horrifying fashion. And frankly, I fear not even Archmage Antonidas himself could manage it.”

“All right… Master,” Jace said, reluctantly accepting the old mage’s reasoning.

Malin smiled and lowered his voice. “But as I understand it, many demons can slip into the shadows to move swiftly, achieving an effect similar to Blink — though not as clean and decisive. I don’t know enough about Shadow, but you take my meaning, Mr. Sesso. We each have different talents and different things we’re good at. There’s no need to force yourself to master everything with equal ease.”

Was the old mage talking about Shadowstep?

The power wielded by the satyrs in Maraudon.

Right — Morophil seemed to have some kind of Shadow-movement ability too, but her so-called Shadowstep was more like turning invisible and using her quick movements to close in on enemies.

In any case, with Malin saying that, Jace did feel that he should go ask Morophil or Sazeno if there was a chance of learning the satyrs’ ability.

The moment he thought about Shadow, Jace felt a lot more confident. Actually, just now when he’d brought up Blink, he’d been a little apprehensive himself.

It was just that sometimes he really did feel he moved too slowly in battle. Although compared to ordinary people his age he could already run pretty fast, compared to Vereesa he fell far short.

He remembered the way that ranger had charged toward that burning orc watchtower with a dagger in hand — that speed was simply unnatural… And that had already been after Grim Batol had endured wave after wave of orc assaults, after surviving several catastrophes wrought by Deathwing, after fighting Nekros’s demonic form in battle multiple times — her stamina had already been drained significantly by then.

“What about fire magic, then?” Jace asked. “Master, I learned Scorch before, and at the Dark Portal I learned Fireball from another Evocation-school mage, Mr. Ferby Golobo. But I feel my control over fire is completely lacking, so I’d like to ask the Master for some guidance.”

“Fire magic — that, of course, I can help with,” the old mage nodded, glancing at his pocket watch. “But I’ll need to attend a meeting with High Sorcerer Andromath shortly — it seems to be about Grim Batol, so this afternoon is out. You can come back another day when you’re free, and we’ll talk about fire magic then.”

“Grim Batol?” Jace asked.

Malin said, “News came from the dwarves recently that terrible omens have appeared in Grim Batol. Ironforge hasn’t issued an official notice yet… or perhaps they have, and that’s exactly why Andromath is calling us over — to discuss their intelligence. I’ll tell you about what’s said at the meeting next time you come. After all, you were involved in the investigation of the Dragonmaw orcs, Jace, and you absolutely made an outstanding contribution. You must not be left out of this.”

Jace was about to say something, then didn’t know where to start. In the end all he said was, “All right. I’ll come back another day.” Malin glanced at Jace, seeming to detect something off in his expression, but said nothing.

“Then I wish you a pleasant afternoon, Mr. Sesso.”

“And you too, Master.” Jace nodded and left the study.
