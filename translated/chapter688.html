Chapter 688: The Magic Inn

Coming out of the Violet Citadel, Jace felt as though he had gained some kind of liberation.

The citadel was protected by all manner of complex magic — spells, runes, and wards. After he realized the Kirin Tor wasn't exactly friendly toward him, that arcane energy had only grown more and more oppressive.

For some reason, Archmage Kel'Thuzad hadn't been waiting for him inside — especially considering that it was he who had strongly recommended Jace for the "reconnaissance mission" to Grim Batol at the time.

Jace guessed that now that Deathwing had fallen, Kel'Thuzad had lost his biggest backer and was probably keeping a low profile.

Grede was waiting for him at Dalaran's largest inn — the Magic Inn. Jace planned to head over first, then use the ring Antonidas had given him to visit the Great Library.

As he quickened his pace down the Violet Citadel's steps, he thought about the date of Winter Veil.

Although King Wrynn's summons and the business in the Slaughtered Lamb's basement had delayed his departure to Dalaran by a few days, the gryphon had been more than ten days faster than traveling by ship. So all things considered, Jace and Grede had arrived here a full week before Winter Veil — right around the time they had agreed to meet Vereesa.

Jace guessed Vereesa probably couldn't arrive this early, since she likely still had plenty to attend to in Quel'Thalas. They could just stay in Dalaran for a few days and wait for her.

"Jace."

"Huh?"

Jace stood on the steps and looked down — the elf was already waiting for him below.

Except today she wasn't wearing her usual ranger's battle attire and deep-green cloak. Instead, she was wearing a light-purple Quel'Thalas gown, looking more like a mage than a Farstrider.

"I heard from the guards that you'd gone to the Violet Citadel, so I came here to find you." Vereesa brushed aside her silver hair, glanced down at her outfit, and asked, "What, you don't recognize me just because I changed clothes?"

"You look beautiful, Wen."

As he said it, Jace glanced at the several layers of unknown fabric on her shoulder that had been enchanted for a sheer effect, and immediately felt he'd been wasting his time at Larson's.

"I'm not used to this outfit either," Vereesa said. "Sylvanas prepared it for me. She said if I was going to Lordaeron, I had to dress nicely."

"You told her?" Jace asked.

"Of course," Vereesa said.

"Everything?"

"Yes."

"What did she say?"

"You can ask her yourself when you see her."

"..."

"Scared?"

"Hardly." Jace cleared his throat. "So tonight, are you planning to do more things she doesn't know about?"

"We can't do it outside this time," Vereesa said softly.

"What, you think I have some kind of fetish? That it has to be outside?" Jace laughed.

"Who knows what weird things you do with that demon every day." Vereesa's voice suddenly went cold. "Don't mention her. We barely escaped." Jace sighed softly.

Vereesa couldn't help but smile. "Where's Grede?"

"The Magic Inn."

"Then let's go find him. He must have been waiting for you for ages." Vereesa leaned against Jace, then suddenly frowned and looked down at the cloak in Jace's arms.

"What is this garment? It smells like..."

"The day at Grim Batol," Jace said.

"Exactly." Vereesa said. "That's just what I was about to say."

Jace explained, "It's a gift from a red dragon emissary, Wen. A cloak made of black dragon skin and scales, which a red dragon then enchanted, so the magical aura is probably quite complex."

"A red dragon emissary, in Dalaran?" Vereesa asked.

"Yes," Jace said. "Vaelastrasz. He's the one relaying messages for the Dragonqueen, informing everyone about what happened at Grim Batol." Vereesa immediately asked, "Then what about Master Krasus?"

"I don't know," Jace said. "I described the dream Deathwing told me to him. He thinks Krasus might be trapped in Blackrock Spire, so he's going to check it out."

"That means there's no one who can help you..."

As Vereesa spoke, an arcane servitor drifted slowly past — a vaguely humanoid magical construct. Masters skilled in arcane magic sometimes summoned these arcane energy beings to act as servants, helping them with their work or conducting magical experiments.

Could these servitors help their masters eavesdrop on the conversations of passersby?

Vereesa had grown up in Quel'Thalas and often visited Silvermoon, so she should know quite a bit about these arcane aberrations. "Let's talk inside the Magic Inn," Vereesa said, and Jace nodded.

True to its name, the Magic Inn's lobby didn't look like a place for dining or lodging at all — it was more like the ground floor of the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower. Arcane candle-lamps enchanted to float and illuminate on their own drifted everywhere. Magic brooms followed close behind guests, erasing the footprints they'd just left, one by one... Against the walls, instead of food ingredients or ale barrels, there stood oak bookshelf after oak bookshelf.

Jace deliberately pulled a copy of Alterac and the History of Frost Magic from the shelf. Inside, it actually documented the early development of frost magic in Dalaran, rather than being a decorative book with nothing written in it.

Grede was sitting beside a bookshelf flipping through a book. His expression was impossible to read — serious or bored. Jace and Vereesa sat down beside him, and only then did the dwarf look up.

"You actually have the mood to read a magic book?" Jace asked.

Grede held the book up for Jace to see. It was entirely in elvish — Jace could barely recognize a single word. "Do you know what's written here, little elf?" he looked up and asked.

"Quel'Thalas banquet etiquette," Vereesa said, propping her chin on her hand.

"Sounds fucking boring." Grede shoved the book back onto the shelf and looked at Jace. "So, how did Antonidas speak to you?"

Recalling the expression on the Lord of Dalaran at the time, Jace frowned. "Maybe I shouldn't have mentioned... that Deathwing had found me. He thought I was suspecting a member of the Kirin Tor Council of Six."

Grede asked, "You didn't even mention a traitor. Why would he jump to that conclusion?"

"Because before I left for Grim Batol, only the members of the Kirin Tor Council knew about this." Jace said quietly. "As long as I implied that someone might have leaked word to Deathwing, wasn't that tantamount to pointing at one of those six? Maybe I was too impulsive. Antonidas's reputation out there made me think I could trust him, but..."

"Dalaran is Dalaran," Grede said.

Vereesa nodded. "Is there anything I can help with? Perhaps when we go to Quel'Thalas, we can contact Prince Kael'thas and let him know about this."

"Tell him?" Jace was taken aback.

"I won't directly state our suspicions," Vereesa said. "I'll just tell him... that before you left Menethil Harbor, without telling anyone else, Deathwing already knew about it and tried to harm you with dark magic. Since Antonidas could understand what you meant, he'll understand too."

If Jace weren't a transmigrator, he might have thought this was a good idea.

But thinking of how Prince Kael'thas Sunstrider would repeatedly ally himself with demons and the Burning Legion afterward... whatever his original intentions, it all made Jace unable to rest easy entrusting him with resolving this matter.

"Let's wait for Vaelastrasz's news first," Jace said. "I still feel the red dragons are more trustworthy. And as a member of the Kirin Tor Council, Kael'thas might also side with his own people, just like Antonidas."

"That's true," Vereesa said. "The Sun King has already grown quite dissatisfied with him repeatedly standing on Dalaran's side."

"If the red dragons' side is slow in coming back, then we'll consider approaching Prince Kael'thas. At that time, I'll be counting on you, Wen."

"No problem." Vereesa smiled.

"I'm actually a little surprised there are no mages among the dwarves." Grede shook his head. "If there were, maybe they could help you too, Jace."

"Don't the Dark Iron dwarves have plenty of mages?" Jace said.

"They can go fuck themselves, they count as... dwarves!" Grede muttered under his breath, then said, "Oh right, Jace, did you tell Vereesa that I'm going back to Aerie Peak?"

"You're not coming with us to Lordaeron?" Vereesa asked.

"If I go to Lordaeron and then to Aerie Peak, it'll cost a lot of time, little elf," Grede said.

"Then today is December 9th," Jace said. "How long does it take you to get to Aerie Peak?"

"From here to Aerie Peak by gryphon, probably two or three days," Grede said. "I just need to attend the banquet on the day Winter Veil starts. I won't stay long, so I'll leave on the 17th or 18th. If I fly to Lordaeron to pick you two up... I should arrive around the 20th."

Vereesa cleared her throat. "My Ranger-Lord promotion ceremony is on December 23rd, Grede. If you still want to attend, you'd best fly toward Quel'Thalas immediately after finishing your wine..."

"It's confirmed?" Grede and Jace asked simultaneously.

"It's confirmed," Vereesa declared. "I won't be a Ranger-Captain of Quel'Thalas anymore — I'll be the Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas. That's how it is!"

Grede shouted toward the innkeeper, "Dalaran's finest wine! One glass for everyone at this table, on the house!"

Vereesa smiled. "I really wish you could have seen their expressions after hearing about Grim Batol — especially Sylvanas. Even her mask-like face couldn't help but look surprised, and that alone satisfied me more than driving a blade through Teron Gorefiend's throat."

"But what makes me happier is that I have you." She leaned against Jace's shoulder. "That's what matters most."

Jace smiled too. Grede cleared his throat. "There's still a person here, you two damned fools. Hurry up and drink!"

The Magic Inn's cellared Dalaran red wine was undoubtedly the most renowned vintage in this magic capital. Jace actually couldn't tell any difference between it and ordinary red wine. And even if he could, the taste and feel of the wine itself had long been rendered insignificant by the joy of the three of them being together.

The Magic Inn's guest rooms were far more beautiful and refined than the dormitory Jace had been assigned in the Wizard Sanctum. Jace even felt the decor here was more exquisite than Master Marin's study — aside from having fewer books.

"If the ceremony is on the 23rd, then we might only stay in Lordaeron for a day before setting out. From the royal city to Quel'Thalas... that's a very long journey."

Vereesa said, "Don't worry, Jace. We don't have to walk. After leaving Lordaeron, we'll return to Dalaran. From here, dragonhawk riders can carry us to Quel'Thalas, so we can even stay a few days longer."

"I can ride a dragonhawk?" Jace asked.

"Compared to a real dragon, a dragonhawk doesn't count for much, does it?" Vereesa said. "Of course you can ride one."

"So in the end, you're still letting me ride." Jace said with some satisfaction.

"What?" Vereesa asked.

Jace touched the golden magic lamp on the table. "Remember at Redridge? I asked if I could ride a dragonhawk. You said impossible."

"Your memory is clearly inaccurate. I said outsiders aren't allowed to ride them." Vereesa raised her arm. "So, are you an outsider, Jace Sesso?"

With a snap of Vereesa's fingers, a ball of blue flame suddenly flared to life on the lamp beside Jace without warning, brightening the room considerably.

"Shouldn't we turn off the lights now?" Jace asked.

"It was too dark last time," Vereesa said. "This time I want to see every bit of you clearly."

Jace tossed his jacket aside, walked to the bed, and gently pushed the elf down onto the pillows, kissing her forehead. The smile on Vereesa's face vanished all at once, and she drew a deep breath.

"Every night in Quel'Thalas I think of you, Jace. I envy that Sathayde. Damn it, envy her so much I want to drive a blade through her neck."

"Next time we see her," Jace said softly.

"She'll be delighted," Vereesa said, staring into Jace's eyes. "That's what's most infuriating."

Both of them smiled at the same time. Jace slowly reached downward, and as his fingers brushed across her lower abdomen, Vereesa suddenly sucked in a breath.

"Could you..." she looked up. "Kiss it again?"

"Do you like it?" Jace asked.

Vereesa nodded.

Jace slowly shifted downward, burying his head between Vereesa's legs. The elf shuddered, arched her back, and gripped the light-purple sheets... "Jace..." she leaned against the pillow and asked softly, "Do you not like this?"

"What?" Jace looked up.

"I don't know..." Vereesa lowered her eyes and looked at him. "I can feel it. You seem a little... unhappy. Am I overthinking it?"

"Magic perfume," Jace laughed. "It can't even affect my mind, Wen. A little magic like this is really..." Vereesa's face flushed red. She turned her head and buried it hard in the pillow. "I should have thought of that."

Jace continued, "But the real reason is, I don't like the perfume. I like your scent. Unfortunately."

"You..." Vereesa muffled a words, then suddenly sucked in another breath.

"Ah! Mmm..."

"Jace, now... come inside."

Jace climbed up and kissed her lips. His lower body slowly pushed forward. The elf slowly closed her eyes, her lips pressing tighter and tighter. "It's so hot, Jace."

"Does it hurt?"

"No, hotter..."

Jace buried his head into her already sweat-damp shoulder, nuzzling the wet silver hair and kissing the base of her ear. The elf wrapped her arms tightly around her lover's shoulders, stifled moans escaping her throat, on the verge of no longer being able to contain her voice...

He plans to fly there for Winter Veil, then rejoin Jace and Vereesa in Lordaeron around Dec 20. - Dragonhawk (龙鹰): Vereesa confirms Jace can ride one from Dalaran to Quel'Thalas.
