## Chapter 701: The Dragonhawk Journey

Early morning at Krasus's Landing, a crowd of dwarves over by the griffon roost were making a racket — no idea what they were going on about.

Jace rested his hand on the hilt of Azure Skysong, feeling the magical energy trickle bit by bit from the blade into his palm. He could even sense it — this wasn't just arcane power seeping into his skin and intertwining with the hot blood in his veins before circulating through his whole body.

The sword's power far exceeded what it had been before; he could feel it with absolute clarity. The jolt of the Dragon Aspects' scale-energy was a constant reminder of that fact.

Just then, Vereesa waved at him from the middle of a cluster of dragonhawk riders in colorful tabards. Jace glanced at the elven knights watching him, then at the massive crimson dragonhawks perched on their roosts, and his heart started pounding with nerves.

He wasn't sure if it was because he was about to become one of the very few humans permitted entry into Quel'Thalas, or because he was about to ride this grotesque monster that was part dragon, part eagle, and part serpent, and let it carry him into the sky.

Or maybe it was because he was about to meet the parents, and that naturally left him feeling a little unsteady. He reckoned that last one was probably the bigger factor.

Jace came over to the dragonhawk riders. Vereesa said, "I'm not much of a dragonhawk rider myself, Jace, so we'll each ride with one of the knights. Dragonhawks can't carry three or four people like griffons can — two is already pushing it."

Jace nodded. Vereesa mounted up behind a female elven knight on one of the climbing dragonhawks. A male knight beside him said, "Good morning, Jace Sesso. I'll be the one taking you to Quel'Thalas."

The dragonhawk rider's Thalassian accent was quite thick, and his word choice and grammar weren't exactly precise, but he spoke fluently — not a hint of the hesitation that came with being unpracticed.

"I've never ridden a dragonhawk," Jace said. "Anything I should know?"

"We'll definitely keep you safe — no letting you fall off its back. Unlike griffons, dragonhawks flap their wings over a much wider arc in flight, so it might take a little getting used to."

As he spoke, the dragonhawk rider blew a whistle. A vivid-red dragonhawk climbed over from the roost on its talons, a broad double saddle already strapped to its back.

Even though the dragonhawk's wings were enormous and its head looked like a gaunt, sharp-beaked dragon, its body was far less powerfully built than a griffon's. With the double saddle cinched to its back, it looked like someone had strapped a hot-air balloon basket onto a lion.

The elven knight nimbly climbed into the saddle and reached a hand back. Jace grabbed it, hauled himself up onto the roost, and settled into the seat behind the knight. With the dragonhawk rider's help he fastened the "safety belt" — Jace ran his fingers over the silk bands binding his waist and legs, and even these things were as exquisitely made as the kind of enchanted cloth belts he could never have afforded back in Stormwind.

Once settled, he looked over at the female knight. Vereesa was sitting in the back making an "all good" gesture at him. Jace nodded and gave her a thumbs-up.

Their dragonhawk was the first to raise its scarlet great-wings and gain height, and then Jace watched their view suddenly eclipsed by the wings of the dragonhawk beneath him.

The elven knight murmured something softly in Thalassian. The dragonhawk gave a low rumble in response, and with a scrape of talons on the roost, it beat its great-wings once and leaped into the air.

Jace had expected the sudden takeoff to be far more violent than a griffon or a drake, but it wasn't so — the dragonhawk's launch was remarkably gentle, and before he felt much discomfort at all, they were already very high up.

The dragonhawk dropped altitude to skim the surface of Lake Lordamere, churning up a sheet of mist, then beat its wings again and climbed higher. Jace's whole lower body suddenly felt weightless; the wind scoured him so hard he could barely keep his eyes open. He had to pull his cloak tighter, covering his nose and mouth, trying to shield his eyes too.

"Do we go north of Alterac or south?" Jace asked.

"We try to avoid Stromgarde's territory, so we'll enter the Eastweald from the north. It might be a bit colder, but it's faster," the dragonhawk rider replied.

The dragonhawk swept over the pine forests along the shore of Lake Lordamere. Looking to the right, all Jace could see was a white expanse of Alterac Mountains.

"But it's hard to believe a mage who killed Teron Gorefiend and held back a demon general's advance at Nethergarde could be so young," the dragonhawk rider said, glancing back. "When I first received Lady Vereesa Windrunner's orders to come here and wait for Jace Sesso, I thought you'd be a middle-aged human with a full beard — famous human heroes of the past tended to look like that."

"I suppose when the War of the Dark Portal comes, more and more young people have no choice but to step up and face it all," Jace said.

"That's certainly true in Quel'Thalas as well," the dragonhawk rider said. "Lady Vereesa Windrunner, for instance, is the youngest of the Farstriders. Before the Dark Portal, a ranger her age could barely have become a ranger-lord — even making ranger-captain would have been rare."

"The youngest, is she? How old is she? …Never mind, I'd rather not know."

The dragonhawk flew into the eastern hills and mountains of Tirisfal Glades, and from here Jace could almost make out a faint white dot on the northernmost peak.

That was the Holy Light Monastery, Lordaeron's largest church building. The cathedral inside was said to be far bigger than the historic royal capital's cathedral.

But because of its remote location, and the fact that almost only members of the Church and the Order of the Silver Hand lived there, that cathedral's fame fell well short of the one in the heart of the capital. But after the Kingdom of Lordaeron was destroyed, its fame would rise sharply — until no one remembered the capital's cathedral anymore, and only the Scarlet Monastery to the north.

King Terenas II telling him to go study at the Church probably meant heading to this monastery.

As the chief fundraiser for Stormwind's reconstruction and the driving force behind the building of the Cathedral of Light, Archbishop Alonsus Faol, though a Lordaeron native, traveled to Stormwind frequently.

After all, he was already counted among the Alliance's leaders, and at the same time the master of the Cathedral of Light.

Jace couldn't ignore a king's command, but Terenas hadn't said he absolutely had to go somewhere specific to receive the Church's teaching. Going back to Stormwind and waiting at the Cathedral of Light for the Archbishop to visit should be fine too — he was a court mage advisor to the Kingdom of Stormwind, after all. That was perfectly reasonable.

By now Jace had gotten used to the rise and fall of the dragonhawk's body in flight. Once you adjusted to the rhythm, dragonhawks were much more comfortable to ride than griffons — a gentle pace, smooth movements, none of the sudden braking or sharp turns. Like a wandering fish traveling through the sky.

The great river ahead, originating in the northern Tirisfal mountains and winding through hills and forests, was the Barrier River. Cross it and they would reach the Tirisfal Pass. From here on out was a place Jace had never once set foot since arriving in this world — the Eastweald, the Plaguelands of the future.
