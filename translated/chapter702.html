Chapter 702: The Gate of Magic

The Eastweald was a vaster stretch of forest, large and small farms and villages scattered between the great woods and the great river. Dragonhawks passing over these farms and roads revealed transport caravans, travelers, and patrolling cavalry everywhere.

Whether looking out from here or from the rumors he'd grown up hearing, Jace had always felt the Eastweald was a more prosperous place than Tirisfal or Hillsbrad. The reason the royal capital had become Lordaeron's seat of power was probably more a matter of history than anything else.

Looking south, where several great rivers intertwined at the edge of view, there sat a modest-sized town — that should be Andorhal.

Ursula had tasked Jace with finding a sayad demon in Andorhal. After leaving Quel'Thalas, he'd come here to meet with that Dark Iron dwarf.

The dragonhawk pushed northeast all day, stopping to rest at evening on a Farstrider outpost set on a high slope deep in the forest that the high elves maintained. Jace had been dead on his feet — and just as he was about to drop off, Vereesa suddenly shook him awake again.

The Twin Moons were still hanging overhead. The only comfort was a mana potion.

But Jace wasn't a high elf — what difference was one mana potion going to make? That said, when high elves gave a gift they were generous all right: they'd handed him a Major Mana Potion without a second thought, and after drinking it he genuinely felt a good deal more alert.

When the dragonhawk flew on into the gray light of morning, Jace spotted watchtowers standing in the valley ahead, a canyon road running deep into the far forest, white and Lordaeron-blue flags planted all over the slopes. As they drew closer, he could faintly make out the walls and church spires among the deep woods and valley floors.

It was a town of considerable size — or rather a fortress — no smaller than Andorhal, and certainly much bigger than Goldshire.

The dragonhawk skimmed past its southern edge, denying Jace a full view, but judging by its location, that should be Hearthglen in the game. Then again, settlements of this scale were hardly rare in the Eastweald. The reason the Kingdom of Lordaeron could be called humanity's most powerful kingdom pretty much came down to this stretch of land.

When the dragonhawk flew on toward late afternoon, it gradually approached a great river. By now Jace was running on fumes, especially with the steady rise-and-fall rhythm of the dragonhawk's back lulling him half to sleep.

Beyond a stretch of pine forest, a vast expanse of white walls and red tiles suddenly spread out before him.

A magnificent bridge spanning a broad river, a giant city gate and statuary, a patchwork of streets, plazas, tall buildings and white towers, a deep-red-spired church in the distance, and blue-and-white flags fluttering in the wind as far as the eye could see…

The sight jolted Jace fully awake. For a second he'd almost thought he was looking at Stormwind!

This great city was nearly on par with the rebuilt Stormwind, especially with the mountains rising behind it — and even a distant harbor.

Of course, unlike Stormwind's harbor, this one had to pass through a narrow inlet pinched between cliffs on both sides before reaching the open sea. That should be the North Sea, the narrow channel between Northrend and the mainland of Lordaeron.

"Stratholme."

The dragonhawk rider turned to look to the left, murmuring the name under his breath.

This was Stratholme, capital of the whole Eastweald, the Kingdom of Lordaeron's second city. Before Stormwind was rebuilt, you could fairly call it the second city of all seven human kingdoms.

This was the place his father had been born and raised. Jace had actually always wanted to come see it — though he'd never had the chance. Traveling overland from the capital and Brill wasn't nearly as simple as riding a dragonhawk.

The in-game scenes and models were a pale, simplified shadow of the real thing seen with your own eyes. Just looking down at this magnificent harbor city from above, it was hard to imagine the way it would look after the Scourge razed it utterly, burning for years in that dreadful fire.

Of course, seeing Stratholme also meant they were already very close to Quel'Thalas. The city sat at the northernmost edge of the Kingdom of Lordaeron; push on a bit further and he'd probably see Eversong Forest soon.

In the game, the southern reaches of Eversong Forest had been thoroughly corrupted by the plague of the Scourge, turned into a gloomy stretch called the Ghostlands. But over a decade ago, although the orcs had burned it, it hadn't been severely corrupted or tainted — so Jace was still looking forward to seeing Quel'Thalas's original appearance, before the Scourge invasion.

The dragonhawk descended at the foot of a mountain range to the northeast of Stratholme, coming down in a high-elven foothill village.

"This is the end of our journey — Quel'Lithien." The dragonhawk rider helped Jace down off the dragonhawk's back. "From here, the Farstriders will lead you to the Gate of Quel'Thalas, Jace Sesso."

"Thank you very much." Jace nodded.

The dragonhawk rider straightened up and gave a slight nod, then said in Thalassian, "Safe journey."

After he finished, he went off with the other knights to tend to the dragonhawks that had just landed. Vereesa came over to Jace and said, "You could take that phrase as a fine start to learning Thalassian, Jace."

"Adarelasha," Jace murmured, repeating the words. "It sounds a lot like the farewell the elves used to say back at Feathermoon Stronghold."

Vereesa reminded him, "Remember — don't bring that topic up once we're inside Quel'Thalas."

"Understood," Jace said in Thalassian.

"That'll do." Vereesa affirmed it in Thalassian as well, then switched back to Common: "Once Grede gets here, we can go in together."

Jace nodded and looked up at the elven wooden houses on the heights. They reminded him of Quel'Daneth, but compared to Quel'Thalas's stronghold in the Hinterlands fighting the forest trolls, this place was far more modest.

Suddenly, a griffon's cry came from above — and Jace knew exactly whose voice that was! It was Gorjhet!

"Grede?" Vereesa looked over too. "He's already here?!"

"Jace! Vereesa!"

The dwarf came leaping down the stone steps the elves had built several at a time, bounding up to the two of them and bellowing, "You're finally here! I couldn't stand another day in this place!"

He glanced over at the two dragonhawks that had only just landed and added, "So you really did ride dragonhawks after all? Then how come you're this many days late? It's been absolute torture waiting for you. The morning after the Winter Veil feast ended, I rushed straight up here — I thought you'd be waiting for me!"

"We stayed a few extra days on the Lordaeron side," Jace said. "My parents aren't ready to move to the new house in Stormwind just yet, Grede, so I figured I'd stick around a bit longer and keep them company."

"Ah, that's how it is." Grede scratched his beard. "Well, that's a legitimate excuse at least."

"Honestly, you should have come to Lordaeron with us, Grede," Vereesa said.

"Why?" the dwarf asked.

Jace gave Vereesa a curious look. She continued, "Come with us to meet the King. If they saw a Wildhammer dwarf standing before the throne, those nobles wouldn't dare spout so much nonsense."

"What did they say?" Grede asked, looking back and forth between them.

"Have me go study at the Church of the Holy Light," Jace said with a grin.

"What's wrong with that?" Grede laughed and clapped Jace on the back. "Brother Jace — sounds pretty good, actually."

"But the reason the King wants him to go study at the Church is that they don't consider a hero who uses dark magic to be a true hero," Vereesa added.

"What?"

At that, Grede's look — which had been pure spectator enjoyment — froze solid.

"What kind of reason is that… those kings ought to be thankful my hammer can't throw that far! This is exactly why the Wildhammer didn't want much to do with the humans back in the mountains before the Dark Portal. Even before the War of the Three Hammers, back when dwarves still regarded magic as a symbol of ill omen — when Emperor Thaurissan and his followers used magic against the ice trolls lying in wait among the snowy mine-caves, helping the people of Khaz Modan escape danger — we still regarded him as a great clan leader."

Here Grede coughed and said no more, since everyone knew what had happened afterward. Emperor Thaurissan and his clan's magic had soon brought the dwarves an unprecedented "ill omen" — which, if anything, had proven the whole "magic is a symbol of ill oomen" thing right after all.

After resting one night in Quel'Lithien, the next morning the elven folk had hawkstriders ready for their two long-distance guests, preparing to lead them into Quel'Thalas.

Jace rode a brown-black hawkstrider. The mounts the high elves bred stood a good head taller than the ostriches he'd known in his past life, and riding one was far steadier than he'd imagined.

The high elves' domesticated mounts all seemed remarkably placid — whether dragonhawks or hawkstriders, they were more comfortable to ride than griffons or horses. It made Jace suspect that this race, raised beneath the Sunwell, had worked some magical alterations on these beasts.

The hawkstrider column, led by two spear-wielding elven knights, set out from Quel'Lithien, following the broad road through the pine forest northward.

This was already the northernmost edge of the entire eastern continent. The royal capital of Lordaeron had been bitterly cold, and it had already snowed once before Jace's arrival. But coming here, not only was there no sign of snow — Jace even spotted broad-leaved trees on the pine-forested hillsides on both sides, the kind that ought to belong to the Stormwind region.

And what was even more obvious: the further north they went, the milder the climate became.

The wind blowing in from the valley ahead made Jace feel as though some danger were approaching — though of course he knew this was just his habitual wariness triggered by absurdly dense arcane energy, not anything inherently sinister about the power itself.

The Sunwell…

When she felt that force coming toward her, Vereesa visibly took a deep breath, as though finally set free.

Once they were deep in the valley, the column quickly came onto a handsome flagstone road. Magic streetlamps stood at intervals along both sides, and even in daylight they shimmered with a faint blue arcane glow.

Around midday, a white gate wreathed in gold filigree appeared at the edge of view, the golden phoenix Sunstrider royal banner drifting above it like a truly living bird in flight.

The gate itself was already breathtaking enough to pass as a palace — he couldn't imagine what it would actually be like to see Silvermoon.

Before the gate, a row of elven mages holding golden staves and clad in gold, white, and blue robes were watching their approach.

They barked commands in the elven tongue Jace couldn't understand, halting the riding column around them, several of the mages glancing from time to time at the two non-elves in the group.

Vereesa said something to them in the elven tongue, and the mages nodded one after another.

"Hey, what are they saying?" Grede leaned over and whispered.

"No idea," Jace said.

The dwarf glared. "Didn't you learn their language on the way here? Or did you slack off again?"

"It's been a few days — how am I supposed to learn enough to understand them talking at that speed in that time?"

"At least Thalassian is simpler than Demonic!" the dwarf hissed.

"Shh." Jace held up a finger.

The exchange between the elves was brief. After it was done, Vereesa made a reassuring gesture to Jace. Jace still had his reservations, but he could only play it off as nothing.

The mages bowed respectfully to Vereesa, then went over to the other riders, watching the few of them on this side while murmuring incantations Jace could barely hear.

The way they looked at Jace and Grede was noticeably colder than the Farstriders back at Quel'Lithien — perhaps because they were guarding the gate of Quel'Thalas and were supposed to be stern, or perhaps this was the true attitude of the elven kingdom's inhabitants toward outsiders.

Almost every mage who passed, though, stared for a good long while at the blue dragon sword at Jace's waist.

Once this "inspection" of sorts was finished, one of the mages looked at Jace and reminded him in Common, "Summons of the Twisting Nether may not be brought north of the Aldre River within the runestones. This is the law of Quel'Thalas — I imagine the Ranger-Lord already warned you."

As he spoke, he glanced at the pack at Jace's waist. Jace said at once, "I know."

"Good." The mage stepped back. "Welcome to Quel'Thalas, Jace Sesso the Dragonslayer."

- Eastweald (东威尔德) established as the prosperous Lordaeron region the party flies over; rendered per convention (ch. 152, 161, 506). - Andorhal (安多哈尔) sighted to the south — Ursula's prior contract: find a sayad demon there, then meet a Dark Iron dwarf. - Stratholme (斯坦索姆) seen from the air — Jace's father's birthplace; Jace contrasts its living grandeur with his knowledge of its future destruction by the Scourge. - The Sunwell (太阳之井) first sensed as a physical, overwhelming arcane presence. - Quel'Lithien (奎尔林斯) — the high-elven foothill village and jumping-off point into Quel'Thalas.
