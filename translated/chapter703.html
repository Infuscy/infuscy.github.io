Chapter 703: Windrunner Village

"Belor, ana, di'el."

"Belor, ana, ti'el."

"Listen closely: di'el."

"Ti'el."

"Still not quite right." Vereesa smiled over at him. "It's not that hard to tell apart, Jace — is it a Brill accent problem?"

Beside them, Grede was making all kinds of strange noises, even throwing in Dwarvish, trying to command the hawkstrider — testing whether the beast would quickly fall under the control of a griffon rider. Meanwhile Jace tried the phrase a few more times over on his side, and no matter how he tried it, it just didn't sound right.

"It's a little tough — ever since I was a kid people told me I had trouble telling those two sounds apart. Same in Common," he admitted.

"For now, just keep practicing slowly," Vereesa said. "It's a greeting, used to ask someone you know well how they've been lately. Try not to use it with elders or people you're meeting for the first time."

Jace asked, "Then what should I say if I meet Lady Sylvanas?"

"Sinu amanore," Vereesa said. "You can say that — it's roughly 'a pleasure to meet you.'"

Jace tried to repeat it. He must have sounded fairly ridiculous, because one of the Farstrider riders ahead glanced back with a smile already on his face.

Vereesa looked displeased at the rider ahead and said in Common, "If I were you, Farstrider, I wouldn't try mocking a human who fooled a Dragonmaw warlord in Orcish back in Grim Batol. He only started learning our language in earnest a few days ago."

"My lady, I apologize," the rider replied. "My apologies, Mr. Sesso."

"Knath'ir also mocked my accent," Jace said. "Good thing there are plenty of clans among the orcs, and each clan's Orcish accent is a little different — made it easier to slip through."

"You still haven't told me exactly what you did in Grim Batol," Vereesa said, narrowing her eyes.

"If I had to sum it up — I survived on the strength of a few strands of your hair," Jace said.

"Hair?" Grede turned his head. "What do you mean?"

As Jace, Grede, and Vereesa chatted about everything that had happened in Grim Batol, the hawkstrider column had walked out of the valley, and a sudden blaze of brilliant light shone toward them.

Jace faltered, dropping the back-and-forth with Vereesa, because he was looking at a sunset he had never seen before…

The Eversong Forest ahead was a golden ocean, mountain peaks rising from it like surging waves, rippling up crests of pale red and emerald green, each one like an island…

Clean white towers, and eaves of gold, blue, and red like birds' wings towered among the islands and waves, rising high to adorn all that brilliance and splendor.

The sun was slowly setting in the north… wait, the north?

No — that wasn't the real sun. That was the radiance overflowing from the isle of Quel'Danas: the Sunwell. The moment Jace realized it was the light of the Sunwell, he could barely breathe.

He suddenly understood why the Quel'dorei revered the Sunwell so deeply. It was a truly visible deity, nurturing and watching over everything and everyone on this land — close enough to reach out and touch, clear enough to see with your own eyes, thick enough to smell with every breath.

"This is my home, Jace," Vereesa said.

The hawkstriders walked down the broad road from the high slope, their view gradually blocked by the rising canopy of treetops.

This was the southern half of Eversong Forest. In the game this place had been destroyed by the plague of the Scourge, but here, as far as the eye could see, grew trees whose shapes resembled dancers — gray-white trunks with leaves of gold and vivid red.

The tender green grasslands along both sides of the road stretched deep into the forest. No matter where he looked, everything seemed spotless, pure, and sacred…

But not everything was so harmonious. Among the woods Jace could also see no few charred tree trunks left behind, though most had already been covered over by other new growth, blending perfectly into their surroundings. These must be the remnants of the orc invasion of Quel'Thalas five years ago.

The hawkstriders walked at ease over the flagstones, heading northwest. After a while, the sky gradually darkened, and Jace made out, visible through the tangled branches ahead, the faint blue glow atop a white high tower, shining like a beacon to guide their way.

"We're almost there," Vereesa said, pointing at the tower. "That's Windrunner Tower."

"Will I meet Lady Sylvanas there?" Jace asked.

"She should be waiting for you there," Vereesa said.

Jace asked curiously, "Is Lord Nathanos Marris also in Quel'Thalas?"

"No," Vereesa said. "Since the orcs' defeat he returned to Daromir — at least as far as I know."

Jace asked in surprise, "So right now I'm the only human in all of Quel'Thalas?"

"Most likely," Vereesa said. "There were mages from Dalaran who visited Silvermoon representing the Alliance some time ago — if they're not still there, then you're the only human here."

"Then I'm definitely the only dwarf here," Grede said.

"I'd bet on that," Vereesa said.

Even though the real sun had already set and it was well into the night, Quel'Thalas seemed unwilling to go fully dark. The sky held steady at a dim twilight, the radiance of the Sunwell to the north shrouding the entire magical kingdom in light, making the Eversong Forest at this hour feel all the more mysterious.

The trees gradually thinned, and the white tower's form grew clearer. Before them appeared domed sanctuaries of all sizes lining both sides of the road.

This must be Windrunner Village. Windrunner Tower stood on the high slope in the distance, its arcane blue light at the very top dazzlingly bright from here.

Although in Quel'Thalas the Farstriders ranked a step below the magisters, the Windrunner family was unquestionably one of the kingdom's core houses — second only to the Sunstrider royal line.

Yet as the seat of the Windrunner family, the Windrunner Village before them was remarkably quiet. Looking across, Jace could only make out a scattering of elves in green guarding these ancient homes and halls. Only around Windrunner Tower at the highest point did there seem to be any number of elves gathered.

Despite being back home, Vereesa's expression was anything but happy. Grede sensed the dejection she wasn't showing outright and had kept silent from a while back.

Jace could more or less guess why. During the Orc Wars, Windrunner Village had been one of the places hit hardest. The combined orc and troll forces had stormed through quickly, routed the garrison, and slaughtered the defenseless residents here without mercy. Vereesa's younger brother had died in that battle.

Looking at it now, Jace could no longer find any trace of war in this village. Everything was serene and peaceful, as though the Horde's armies had never come here at all — yet the expression on every resident's face said it all.

The group dismounted by the hawkstrider pens and continued into the village. Before long they came to a domed hall at the foot of Windrunner Tower, where a golden-haired elven woman was already waiting for them.

"Elder sister…" Vereesa said.

That was Sylvanas.

When Jace had met her at Nethergarde Keep, she'd been wearing the battle-dress of a Ranger-General and looked every inch the martial commander. Now, though, she wore only a silver long dress, and outwardly she looked no different from any other elf in Windrunner Village.

Up close, Jace noticed she didn't actually resemble Vereesa as much as others had claimed. Her features were more distinctly "elf" — less gentle than Vereesa, and quite close to her CG likeness.

Of course, that was only the face and features. After all, she was still alive right now. Her complexion wasn't the rotting, corpse-like gray of the undead, but full and ruddy. Her eyes weren't the dark crimson of death and shadow, but a bright arcane blue.

"Vereesa," Sylvanas said softly, then looked at the two beside her.

"General Sylvanas Windrunner, I am Grede Thunderfist." Grede stepped forward and bowed slightly. "Lord Falstad, leader of the Wildhammer clan of Aerie Peak, sent me to convey his thanks. The firm alliance we forged in the Hinterlands a hundred years ago is unbreakable."

Sylvanas nodded slightly and said, "Lord Falstad's kinsman, Kurdran Wildhammer, crossed the Dark Portal alongside Ranger-Captain Alleria Windrunner to campaign in Draenor, griffon rider. Our alliance has become far more sacred than at any time in the past."

"Indeed it has," Grede agreed.

With that, he let out a breath, stepped back to the side, and glanced at Jace.

"General Sylvanas," Jace said. "I am Jace Sesso, from Stormwind."

"Of course, Jace Sesso." Sylvanas shifted her gaze from Grede to Jace. "I'm grateful to you for looking after Vereesa. Several times she's defied my orders and slipped out of Quel'Thalas on her own — if you hadn't been at her side, she'd probably have gotten herself killed long ago."

"That's an overstatement," Vereesa couldn't help saying. "I've saved him plenty of times too."

"You know the truth of it yourself, Ranger-Lord," Sylvanas said coldly.

Vereesa fell silent. Jace said, "Without Vereesa, neither Grede nor I would be alive today."

"He's right about that, General Sylvanas," Grede said.

Sylvanas looked at Jace and sighed. "Let's not keep score, then. It's very late, and you two probably haven't eaten anything yet."

She glanced at Vereesa, who was standing to the side looking put out, and asked, "Don't be angry — what would you like to eat?"

"Salmon," Vereesa muttered.

"Then salmon it is." Sylvanas turned to Jace. "I've prepared some simple food to fill your stomachs. I know you must be exhausted and need rest. Tomorrow, once you're awake, I'll have someone prepare a more lavish meal. Though it may not be quite to your taste, Grede Thunderfist."

"I've gotten used to human food in Stormwind — time to challenge something harder," Grede said.

Sylvanas smiled. "I don't think it counts as much of a challenge."

"Just making a joke…" Grede hurriedly smoothed things over.

Watching the Ranger-General turn away, and recalling that smile, Jace still found it a little hard to reconcile.

After all, in his impression, Sylvanas was someone who never cracked a smile, whose every word was hatred, vengeance, killing — a hardline figure through and through.

Of course, he knew the Scourge hadn't arrived yet. The Ranger-General of Silvermoon hadn't been murdered by Arthas, hadn't endured that death knight's string of psychological torments, and hadn't witnessed the destruction of her homeland, her people, and the Sunwell… She was vastly different from the Sylvanas who would come later.

"When I saw you at Nethergarde, you still seemed like a child," Sylvanas said. "Even though I knew you and Vereesa had defeated Teron Gorefiend together, even though I saw with my own eyes you use that magic to destroy the pit lord Azgos's body — the way you lay there, you looked like nothing but a child."

"To you, everyone's a child," Vereesa murmured.

"Ah…" Sylvanas turned back with exasperation. "Vereesa, are you really planning to keep fighting with me in front of Jace this whole time?"

Vereesa, brimming with fury, said, "Jace went into Grim Batol alone to face those orcs. He freed the Dragonqueen, he killed Deathwing — damn it, do you really want to be like those foolish humans and be completely blind to what all of that means?"

"Of course I know what it means," Sylvanas said. "The Dragonqueen's envoy has already come to Quel'Thalas. She brought the Dragonqueen's apology — that matters enormously to the entire kingdom. She also mentioned her gratitude to the three of you, Jace Sesso in particular. And Deathwing — Silvermoon's ancient records still document the grievous crimes he committed against the ancient Kaldorei empire, and they still record the terror of his blotting out the sky. That was the era of the Well of Eternity! You think I don't know these things, Lady Vereesa Windrunner?"

Vereesa stewed for a moment, then let loose a long string of Thalassian in a jumble. Sylvanas furrowed her brow and argued back in Thalassian. Jace and Grede stood to the side in a daze, not knowing what to say, until the expressions on both rangers' faces softened somewhat — they seemed to have fought their way to some conclusion, though it also didn't seem like anything was actually resolved.

"I'm sorry, Jace," Sylvanas said. "I wanted this meeting to feel more like a family gathering, but I've completely ruined it."

Jace said, "Arguably, this feels even more like a family gathering."

Vereesa couldn't help laughing. Sylvanas shook her head helplessly.

"In any case, the day after tomorrow is your promotion ceremony, Vereesa. At least let's not be like this during the ceremony."

"I'll try," Vereesa said.

"Don't 'try.'" Sylvanas said. "Last time I asked you to try not to leave Quel'Thalas without permission, that's exactly what you promised — and then you went straight to Kalimdor! Afterward I had to clean up the mess for you, and explain myself to the Reliquary. You thought your disguise was perfectly convincing, didn't you, Miss Goldthorn?"

"Sorry," Vereesa said quietly.
