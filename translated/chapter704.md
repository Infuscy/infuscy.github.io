## Chapter 704: A Sort of Familiar Ground

The next morning Jace woke very early, sitting on his bed staring blankly at his surroundings.

Even though the bedroom in Quel'Thalas was exquisitely beautiful, like something out of a fairy or angel's garden — the soft deep-red mattress comfortable enough to lie on in any position, layer after layer of purple curtains shielding the doorway and giving a full sense of security, a faint fragrance filling the room that helped him fall asleep without being as crude as Morophil's hypnosis —

He still woke up very early.

Fresh pastries and fruit had been laid out on the table. He popped a grape-like purple fruit into his mouth — sweet and sour with not a trace of bitterness, even carrying a scent like orange. He had no idea what it was.

Just as Sylvanas had said, the elves had prepared a lavish breakfast for him. Aside from the pastries and fruit, there were all sorts of condiments on the side, looking as though they'd been pounded together from natural ingredients.

But for some reason, he didn't feel much appetite to touch them.

Leaving the room, he stepped onto the soft grass and neat flagstones. In the sunlight, Windrunner Village had shed the layer of melancholy gloom from the night before, though it was still as quiet as an abandoned settlement — just a good deal cleaner and tidier than your average ghost town.

At least the beautiful hawkstriders of every color by the pens were being looked after, lending the place a touch of life.

Grede was sleeping in another house — no snoring to be heard from over here. Vereesa and Sylvanas and the other Farstriders were probably busy with something at Windrunner Tower, after all; they didn't even need to sleep at night.

Standing here alone looking over Windrunner Village, Jace felt an indescribable something in his chest.

Of course he felt sorrow and grief for what this place had suffered at the orc's hands — after all, these were Vereesa's family. And of course the desolation here left a lonely ache in his heart — it was hard not to be affected when you were standing in the middle of it.

But more than that, he seemed to have some unspeakably strange feeling about this place.

Ever since arriving here last night, looking at the trees, the houses, the fences — the whole impression had struck him as somehow familiar. He was certain it had nothing to do with any scene he'd seen in the game.

He closed his eyes and felt the wind, heard the pleasant melody of some instrument playing from some distant place, and the barely discernible murmur of voices drifting down from Windrunner Tower above, the sound of running water.

Yes — it was the sound of that stream.

Jace knew now where he'd seen this place before.

Almost without thinking, he followed the sound of the water, passing through a stretch of forest, moving further and further from Windrunner Village, until he came to an unnamed garden.

Beside the garden was a small stream — the very stream he'd seen in Deathwing's nightmare.

He pulled off his boots and slowly stepped into the water. The icy shock made him feel as though that terrifying man had seized his soul once more, his whole body tensing violently.

Almost in a trance, he drew Azure Skysong — blazing in the sunlight like brilliant crystal — and raised it toward his right arm. But the scar on it had already faded to no more ordinary than a typical burn scar.

Jace looked around, making sure that man wasn't closing in like a phantom — no Shadow power, no dreadful visions. His reflection in the water was still the familiar one: a head of messy but thick, dark hair, a young face, a body not especially strong but certainly not frail.

This wasn't a dream.

He stood there hesitating for a moment — then heard someone calling from behind him.

"Jace, what's wrong?"

Jace turned his head. Vereesa was standing right behind him; he had no idea when she'd followed. "Why are you holding that sword?"

"I…"

Jace looked at the runeblade already in his hand and was at a loss to explain the gesture.

"What exactly did Deathwing do to you, Jace?" Vereesa stepped closer to the water's edge. "Are you still not going to tell me clearly?"

Jace hesitated a moment, then looked down at the stream beneath his feet and said, "The night Krasus found me, Deathwing seized my dream and brought me to this place."

"This place?" Vereesa asked. "Why?"

"In that dream I was a useless old man who could barely walk," Jace said. "I felt even older than a normal human elder — moving around like a skeleton, nearly falling apart. I fell into the water and almost drowned before I could crawl out. Look — the water only just reaches my knees, exactly like in the dream."

"Was I there in that dream too?" Vereesa asked.

"I was." Jace nodded. "You looked a lot like you do now."

Vereesa stared at Jace without speaking.

Jace gave a self-mocking smile. "Of course, even then I could tell it was a fake you."

"I see. Come here, Jace." Vereesa held out her hand.

Jace sheathed Azure Skysong and waded back to the bank step by step. "Am I the fake one now?" Vereesa asked.

"No," Jace said.

Vereesa took his hand and looked up into his eyes.

"The me in the dream would never have dared look at you like that. She'd be afraid you'd see through the disguise. She'd definitely avoid your gaze, because she knows you, Jace Sesso — a powerful Shadow master who sees through every illusion and falsehood, who shatters every lie woven by dark magic…"

She was right. Even now, Jace couldn't remember what that dream-version of her looked like. He'd only ever seen the phantom's reflection in the water — blurred and chaotic.

"No matter what terrifying things happen from now on, don't face them alone anymore," Vereesa added.

"I won't," Jace said.

Vereesa rose on her toes and kissed him. The touch of those soft, warm lips was so real that Jace's heart began to pound. No more hiding, no more ambiguous whispers.

Deathwing was dead, he told himself. The brand had been pierced through. The nightmare was over.

He met her lips and held her tight, losing himself in the taste of her tongue and the edges of her teeth — until the two of them lost their balance, yelped, and tumbled together onto the bank by the water, laughing happily.

After a while of laughing, Jace asked, "Where's your sister?"

"She went north," Vereesa said. "To prepare for my promotion ceremony."

Jace glanced north toward the light of the Sunwell and said softly, "Then…"

"Then…" Vereesa raised an eyebrow and pushed Jace down onto the pebbles.

"Here?!" Jace exclaimed from his back.

"What, are you scared?" Vereesa asked, provocative as ever.

## Flags

- Jace wakes early in the Windrunner Village guest quarters — the elven comforts can't quiet his unease.
- The stream (溪流) — Jace recognizes it from Deathwing's nightmare (the dream Krasus interrupted). He tests reality by drawing Azure Skysong on his own arm; the brand-scar has faded to a mundane burn.
- Jace finally tells Vereesa about Deathwing's dream-invasion: an aged, broken Jace drowning in the stream, with a false Vereesa beside him.
- Vereesa's reassurance — the real her meets his gaze without flinching, unlike the dream-phantom. A turning point in their intimacy.
- First unambiguous kiss between Jace and Vereesa by the stream; Jace internally marks Deathwing's death and the end of the nightmare.
- The chapter ends on a playful, intimate beat — Vereesa pushes Jace down onto the pebbles, teasing.
