Chapter 717: A Lord?

Jace arrived in Moonbrook at dusk the following day. He handed Arna over to the inn's stables, gave the stablemaster two extra silver to take especially good care of her, and made his way toward the house on the back hillside of Moonbrook where he remembered Musul living.

The last time he'd come here had been a while before the Fire Festival, the hottest time of year in the kingdom. The whole of Westfall had been baking like a flat skillet, and the trees on these slopes were a dense, dizzying wall of dark green that made his head spin.

But now, coming here again, he found the whole hillside turned golden. The trees had lost most of their leaves, and the bare branches crisscrossed in a dense tangle — a far cry from the place in his memory, and not easy to get his bearings at all.

Then again, Musul's house sat on a high terrace. As long as he kept heading uphill, even if he went too high it would be easier to look down and correct his course than to look up. So Jace just needed to find paths that climbed.

"The demon-concealing wards here… belong to a remarkably summoner, Master," Morpheus said suddenly from behind him. "I can tell that the traces here — both the dead demons and the people who died because of demons — have been hidden thoroughly enough. If you hadn't told me a demon summoner lived here, I might well have overlooked it."

"But you caught the scent anyway," Jace said.

"Always remember who I am, Master. Always," Morpheus said with a smile.

"Soon nobody'll know what the hell you are — you freak who drank living demon blood. You don't even count as a demon anymore," Sazeno said with a laugh.

Morpheus didn't rise to the bait at all. She said softly, "A vile mange-ridden wretch has no right to define what a demon is." Jace patted the demon bag. "Now's not the time for bickering."

The imp grumbled and retreated into the bag. Jace looked back and forth through the trees and said, "Dead demons are nothing — Musul's had dealings with people who traffic in demon organs. But people who died because of demons? Someone here was killed by a demon?"

"I can't say for certain, Master," Morpheus said. "Both kinds of traces have grown very faint. Perhaps they died from drinking demon blood, or perhaps they were backlashed by void energy while summoning a demon…"

Jace nodded and kept climbing, over ground cover and exposed roots, but his hand tightened on the hilt of Sky's Song at his waist.

By midnight he finally spotted a faint light up ahead. Following it, he found Musul's house.

He'd barely reached the front of the house when the door opened. Musul's slightly hunched figure stood in the doorway — at least he was still alive. So the dead ones must be someone else.

Jace knew this house was guarded by various invisible demons — who knew how many imps or shivarras were lurking around the grounds, and a hellhound was peeking out from behind the wall, baring a tuft of fanged fur, watching this way.

He wasn't at all surprised someone had been killed here. Maybe it was one of Magino Junot's mage subordinates, or maybe it was scavengers looking to harvest the hearts, skins, and horns from the demons here for coin.

"Jace Sesso," Musul said. "Many warlocks say you're dead, but some say you're alive. I believed the latter — and it turns out I was right."

"I came to see how your summoning ritual is going," Jace said.

"Without you, I can barely move forward." Musul gestured him in. "Please, come in. It's been over a year since we last met. If I don't give you a proper welcome…"

At that moment Morpheus materialized at his side. When her eyes opened, the sudden flames that blazed in them lit up the empty ground by the fence gate.

She came to Jace's side, wrapped her arm around his neck, and smiled toward the doorway — in the dark of night she looked like a woman wreathed in fire, hard to say whether she was terrifying or alluring.

"You brought your companion," Musul said.

Before Jace could answer, Morpheus cut in: "One of them."

The master of the house looked at the succubus and swallowed, his throat bobbing. "Not welcome me, Mr. Musul?" Morpheus asked.

"No," Musul said. "I'm just marveling at your… power. You're no ordinary shivarra." Morpheus said, "Because my master has no interest in ordinary shivarras."

Entering the house with Morpheus at his side, Jace felt none of the wariness he'd had last time.

Her expression alone told him that no matter how many demons Musul had hidden in this house, even if they all attacked at once they'd have no chance of defeating the two of them.

Still, Jace had to admit that Musul's knowledge of demon summoning ran far deeper than his own. If he wanted to summon a doomguard, he'd need Musul's help.

After all, the man had been studying demons since before the orcs had come through the Dark Portal.

"Davelin, brew that tea I bought from Moonbrook last time."

Musul called out as he sat down in the opposite chair, looking at Jace, who had just sat down, and the succubus standing behind him. "What made you change your mind?"

Jace asked, "Change what mind?"

"I mean," Musul said with a slight smile, "it's obvious you were unwilling to help me with the summoning ritual at first. After all this time, you've come here — you must have changed your mind. Why were you unwilling to help me then, and why are you willing now?"

"Honestly, back then I couldn't even summon a felbeast," Jace said. "You wanted me to help you summon a great demon — I wasn't willing to take that risk."

"In my view, summoning demons isn't a step-by-step process," Musul said. "Nobody says you have to practice on imps before summoning a succubus, or that you have to summon a mo'arg imp as your servant before you can summon a felguard."

"Summon demons according to your needs. If you want a little helper who can hide in the shadows and assist you with secret experiments, summon an imp. If you need to satisfy your desires, or need someone to talk to, or need an accomplice to persuade others — summon a succubus."

"Then why did you summon a great demon?" Jace asked.

"I'm different," Musul said. "If you go by what most people say, I summon for the sake of summoning. That probably doesn't sit well with the beliefs of those warlocks in Stormwind — or most warlocks, for that matter. But that's my pursuit. If I had to name a concrete goal, I have many questions I want to ask a demon that powerful."

"If I help you, I need you to share your demon knowledge with me," Jace said. "And when I plan to summon a demon, you have to come help me."

"Of course," Musul said. "If possible, I'd even want to take part in every powerful demon summoning ritual across Azeroth — including the orcs'… As for sharing my knowledge, that's exactly what I pursue. But clearly not everyone is suited to receive it. After we complete my summoning ritual, I'll share everything I know with everyone who took part in the ritual, in pursuit of a higher goal."

"Everyone?" Jace asked.

"Yes. You and I aren't the only ones taking part in this summoning," Musul said. "I've found several people over this time. Through the Dark Portal incident, I got to know some other demon researchers who want to walk this path. And we've already made two attempts — neither went well. I think there are problems with both the incantation and the ritual process. We need to try further."

"You haven't even told me what exactly you're trying to summon," Jace said.

Musul glanced at Morpheus, then said quietly, "A lord of the Burning Legion."

Jace's heart clenched. Hearing that, he even wondered if Musul was trying to summon Archimonde or Kil'jaeden.

Of course, "lord of the Burning Legion" was actually a very broad term. Even Das'mora counted as a minor chieftain — in the eyes of the imps and hellhounds she summoned, she was naturally a lord.

"Since you've decided to join us… this is a summoning incantation I found from a demon scholar in Kalimdor." Musul took a scroll from a shelf behind him and handed it to Jace. "Do you know Kalimdor? It's a place full of terrifying demons left over from the ancient war ten thousand years ago. The demon scholars there must be far more experienced than ours, of course. After the Dark Portal… someday we'll surpass them."

Musul had to be talking about Das'mora.

Jace took the scroll. He was a little worried Musul was trying to summon Das'mora herself — that would be ridiculous.

Still, after opening the scroll and giving it a quick look, this incantation, though complex, wasn't as complex as the one for summoning Das'mora.

But that didn't mean the being it summoned was weaker than Das'mora, because that incantation had too many phrases devoted to freeing Das'mora from the demon prisons and seals of Eldre'Thalas. If you were simply summoning an unbound demon from the Twisting Nether, you wouldn't need so many extra lines.

"Can I take it?" Jace asked. "I want to read it carefully and have a look."

"You can," Musul said. "This is only a copy. Use your abilities to see if there's anything wrong with it. You understand Demonic, don't you?"

"I hope you copied it without mistakes," Jace said. "Otherwise it'll waste a lot of effort."

"I've copied it to the best of my ability," Musul said.

"Then I'll head back to the inn in town first," Jace said.

"You can stay the night here," Musul said. "I have enough guest rooms — for you and your companion…"

He glanced toward the next room and called out loudly, "Davelin, where's the tea? Where have you gone? Why isn't it here yet? Does it really take this long?"

"Master… it's almost ready, almost ready, just a moment more…"

A flustered female voice came from inside. Morpheus gave a soft laugh.

"I won't impose on you, Musul," Jace said, standing. "Maybe we're making your 'companion' uncomfortable here. I'll come find you whether there's results or not."

Musul glanced further inside, then stood as well. "All right, Jace. I'll be waiting for your word."
