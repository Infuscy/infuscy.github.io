## Chapter 718: The Likely Summoning Target

Jace returned to Moonbrook in the middle of the night. He went to the inn's stables to check on Arna, then went back to his room.

He spread the scroll out on the desk and examined the runes on it carefully by the guttering candlelight. Sazeno hopped onto the table and stared at it curiously. Morpheus leaned in to look and said, "This incantation requires a tremendous amount of energy, Master."

The imp looked up. "Maybe that's exactly why that Musul failed. If these warlocks didn't prepare enough for the summoning, the ritual would drain their own energy! And the Twisting Nether doesn't give a damn about anyone's life the way you do, Master."

"Maybe that's why I could sense warlocks who died because of demons," Morpheus said.

The imp nodded with a grin. Jace asked, "Can you tell from this incantation what demon it's actually summoning?" Morpheus said, "I can't tell, Master. This kind of targeted summoning incantation is laced with too many phrases aimed at the individual."

"The succubus is right," the imp said. "Maybe a mo'arg general, maybe a Nathrezim…"

"Couldn't be Nathrezim," Morpheus said flatly. "I'm familiar with the incantations for summoning Nathrezim, little one — they're very different from most demons."

"Well, since you put it that way…" The imp scanned back and forth over the runes. "This demon does feel quite large, so it needs a powerful summoning. A Nathrezim probably wouldn't need a summoning method like this."

"Quite large?" Jace felt his eyelid twitch. He muttered, "It couldn't be eredar, could it?"

Of course, what he was thinking of were the two eredar leaders, Archimonde and Kil'jaeden — both of whom had mountain-sized, terrifying forms in the game and in the lore.

But even he felt that Musul and a bunch of half-baked warlocks absolutely couldn't summon either of those two to Moonbrook. The conditions required to summon them were far too demanding.

"Eredar aren't that tall," the imp said, looking at Jace. "Ah, I know what you're thinking, Master — you're thinking of those tall eredar mages who invaded here through the Dark Portal. Ha! You were fooled by their magic. Those fellows used enlargement spells to bulk themselves up like that. They're not actually that tall. Eredar love to put on airs like that… that's their nature. Clever, but far too arrogant — they think they're not just masters of magic but should be the vanguard of the army, and that they can strut across a battlefield like that without getting hurt…"

Sazeno had a point. Jace did recall descriptions of Archimonde using magic to exaggerate his size during the invasion of Azeroth.

"Couldn't it be an annihegan?" Jace asked. "A pit lord?"

Both demons fell silent for a moment. Jace said, "If Musul's trying to summon a pit lord, not only will I not help him — I'll have to stop him. That's not something we could ever handle."

The imp had been working his way through the sentences on the scroll one by one. He suddenly said, "I seem to recognize some familiar phrases, Master. I want to look at the incantation we found in that satyr's cave! My memory's not what it used to be."

Jace nodded and laid out the copies of the scrolls the group had found in Vilethorn's lair.

He placed the Das'mora summoning incantation beside it in particular. The imp went back and forth comparing them, then shook his head. "It's not in these."

"What about these?" Jace laid out copies of the scrolls they'd found inside Das'mora's belly as well. The imp studied the scroll marked "Necromancer" — the one possibly connected to Ul'solus — for a while, and Jace's eye suddenly caught the diagram on it.

This wasn't the Kaldorei demon-binding circle they'd found on the satyr. It was the circle Das'mora had used to contact some demon.

Because Jace and the imp had drawn the diagram together by comparison, it was etched in his memory. When the imp said he recognized Musul's summoning incantation, Jace had some impression of it too. When his eye caught several runes on the circle, they suddenly connected with a few phrases on Musul's incantation.

"Here." Jace pointed to the runes on the circle, then to Musul's scroll. "These few places?"

The imp put down the scroll in his hands, hopped over, and followed Jace's finger back and forth. "Indeed… indeed, Master."

"So what Musul wants to summon is really the demon lord Das'mora was trying to contact?" Jace asked.

"Very likely, so it seems," the imp said.

"Don't say 'likely,'" Jace said. "Can't you read what these circles say? Aren't these all Demonic characters?"

"These are indeed Demonic characters," Morpheus said. "But they're phonetic renderings of the ancient ered'ruin language, Master. So it's very hard to make out the exact meaning. It's like… 'anal anarathas' in Common…"

Ranger-General.

Jace glanced at her. "I see. Then, is there anything wrong with Musul's incantation?"

"I think there are some problems with the phrasing," the imp answered. "Maybe the previous copyist didn't notice they should use symbols to separate the clauses. We understand any incantation without needing punctuation in Common, but for you summoners, this kind of mistake would greatly affect the spell's power! Of course, I'll also correct the incantation based on the runes Das'mora left on these scrolls… Master."

Jace glanced out the window. "It's the middle of the night now. How long will you take?"

Sazeno said excitedly, "Give me a half-mana soul shard to satisfy me, Master — I can speed things up for you! About two or three days."

Jace helplessly took a shard from the small pouch in his demon bag and tossed it to the imp. "I'm thinking — if this incantation contains ered'ruin language, then isn't Musul summoning a doomguard too?"

"Very likely, Master," the imp said. "A large-bodied doomguard…"

Jace muttered to himself as he thought. Then something suddenly occurred to him. Doom Lord.

They were the rulers of the ered'ruin — terrifyingly enormous in size. They weren't as heavily built as pit lords, but they stood far taller. These lords' strength dwarfed that of ordinary doomguards by an enormous margin. Though they looked similar in appearance, they were practically a different race.

In the Burning Legion they were high-ranking leaders — even some lower-ranking annihegan commanders served under them. The most famous and powerful Doom Lord in the game was Kazzak.

After the Scourge summoned Archimonde, the Burning Legion launched a full-scale invasion of Azeroth, and this Doom Lord was one of the commanders who followed Archimonde to Azeroth. After the Legion's defeat, he seized the Dark Portal and later, on Kil'jaeden's orders, reopened it to begin the Burning Crusade. He then returned to the shattered Outland to lead the Legion's forces in a struggle for territory against Illidan Stormrage, who styled himself the Lord of Outland.

Within the Burning Legion, this Doom Lord's standing was second only to Archimonde and Kil'jaeden.

Summoning a demon lord like that would be no less terrifying than summoning a pit lord. "Could this be a Doom Lord summoning incantation?" Jace asked.

Morpheus said, "I don't think Musul has the ability to summon such a demon. Not even a pit lord, Master." The imp said, "I suggest Master first look at what preparations he's made for the summoning…"

Jace nodded. "Let's get this incantation fixed first. I'll go ask Musul more about it."
