## Chapter 719: Ample and Reliable Summoning Preparations

Two days later, in the afternoon, Jace brought the incantation the imp had organized and recopied to Mor'zul.

This incantation, which read like a proper essay, was something he could barely manage to pronounce with his Demonic skills — he could only understand fragments of its meaning.

Arriving at the house, this time Dafren finally showed herself. As a succubus, her expression was a touch too stiff. She carried a plate of pastries to the table while constantly watching Morophil's movements from the corner of her eye.

Mor'zul was clearly extremely dissatisfied with her performance and tried his best not to look at her. Jace placed both incantations on the table. Mor'zul picked them up and examined them.

"This is what?"

"My demon servants and I corrected your incantation based on reliable source material, Mor'zul," Jace said from his seat. "I suspect the source you copied from was also the work of people who don't understand demons particularly well — there were some inaccuracies."

"Possibly. But what's your reliable source material?" Mor'zul asked with some curiosity. "Where did it come from — the Wizard's Sanctum, or the Kirin Tor?"

"Have you been investigating me?" Jace asked.

"Forgive me. I needed to understand you as thoroughly as possible — after all, I'm responsible for what we're about to accomplish," Mor'zul said. "You distinguished yourself at Nethergarde Keep. Did they invite you to join the Kirin Tor? Or the Violet Eye… I know the Council of Six is always looking for people interested in Karazhan's secrets to join the Violet Eye, to satisfy their curiosity by going into that black tower to die. If you're smart, you'd know better."

It seemed Mor'zul's investigation hadn't been thorough enough — or rather, it had been done too early. He still didn't know about Grim Batol.

"Do you know Dasmora?" Jace asked.

Despite having investigated everything about Dasmora behind his back, this was the first time Jace had spoken the name directly in front of Mor'zul.

Mor'zul's expression shifted in an interesting way. He froze for a moment with no obvious reaction, then said, "You asked those people at the Slaughtered Lamb about me, didn't you?"

"I needed to understand you as thoroughly as possible — after all, I'm responsible for my own safety," Jace echoed Mor'zul's earlier tone. Then he continued, "Using my connections in the Kirin Tor and Quel'Thalas, I found some rather extraordinary things related to Dasmora. And these were all written by Dasmora herself. I believe you know about sympathetic magic — the method by which mages can trace the caster through the marks an arcanist leaves behind. Everything I have has been verified through sympathetic magic to be runes and circles written in her own hand… And based on these, your incantation has a few small errors."

Hearing Jace say this, Mor'zul's expression soured.

"Dasmora was a master of arcane magic too, wasn't she, Jace?" He still tried to find a hole in Jace's words. "As I understand it, sympathetic magic tracks arcane energy more accurately. Tracking other forms of power — whether shadow or fel, those chaotic, disordered forces — isn't as effective…"

Jace said quickly, "I have ample evidence that Dasmora had been dwelling in an ancient Highborne city. You know what the Highborne relied on to maintain their power."

"Highborne." Mor'zul repeated the word Jace had mentioned. This time he couldn't hide his emotions. If Jace had to describe it with a feeling… it was like telling him to his face that he'd taken his wife.

Still, the other man quickly recovered his composure. "I'll show the incantation you corrected to the others. You're the most powerful among us, Jace. I value your opinion — but the decision certainly can't rest with you alone."

"I understand," Jace said. "It's just that I feel what you told me yesterday was still too vague. Are you certain what kind of demon lord you're trying to summon?"

Mor'zul said, "You needn't worry about everything spiraling out of control. From our casting materials and the mana required for the summoning, we can estimate that its strength won't exceed expectations. We have ways to control it and put it to our use, Jace. Don't think that because you've killed a demon lord with your own hands, you can look down on the abilities of us demon researchers. And don't measure our understanding of demons by those warlocks at the Slaughtered Lamb. I admit they've done impressive work collecting orc incantations, but when it comes to demons… they're utterly clueless."

Jace said at once, "I don't mean any disrespect. The reason I managed to kill that demon had a lot to do with luck. But you mentioned controlling it — putting it to our use?"

Mor'zul said immediately, "Gaining demonic knowledge from a true demon lord — wouldn't that be far better than piecing together secondhand, rough knowledge from the orcs bit by bit? By then, Jace, you might not even care about the demon knowledge I have in my hands! Of course, this isn't to disparage the orcs. If not for the Horde's invasion, I wouldn't have gathered the summoning materials, and I wouldn't have obtained so many methods for binding and weakening demons. You should know — there are orcs among us."

Jace couldn't help but widen his eyes slightly. He hadn't expected that among the people he was talking about, there were actually orcs. "Could I see the summoning site?"

"Of course," Mor'zul said. "Follow me."

Jace exchanged a glance with Morophil and stood to follow Mor'zul out of the house.

The three of them went behind the house and entered a dense stretch of woods. Jace couldn't see anything resembling a path, but Mor'zul walked straight into the depths of the forest. The moonlight at night kept the place from being pitch-black, but the stone cliffs and bizarre rocks on either side still made the atmosphere somewhat eerie.

After walking for less than half an hour, Mor'zul led them to an open clearing deep in the back hills, surrounded by trees and low cliffs. Jace could see braziers that had been left behind on the ground, still containing the charred residue of something burned by Shadowflame. There were dried black bloodstains on the earth, and claw marks gouged by something.

But what surprised Jace even more was that the outline of a huge dark circle still lingered on the ground. He could tell that this circle was extremely complex, whether in its phrasing or its structure. Morophil had been staring at the circle observing it the whole time — she seemed quite taken aback as well.

Mor'zul said, "The back hills of Moonbrook are hidden enough. The Wizard's Sanctum rarely pays attention here. This is the most suitable hermitage I've found after moving several times, Jace. Our previous two attempts were both conducted here."

"What does this circle do?" Jace asked.

"A demon-sealing circle," Morophil said. "Extremely powerful — but it requires several people to maintain it."

"That's why I need many helpers," Mor'zul said. "And powerful helpers. The mana for the summoning ritual can draw on the power of the ley lines, the power of casting materials, the power of soul shards — but maintaining this sealing circle requires the casters' own mana. So the six of them were hand-picked by me."

"Six people maintaining this circle?" Jace asked. "How many people did you recruit for this summoning, exactly?"

"Including you, fifteen," Mor'zul said.

Jace had assumed he was only recruiting within the Kingdom of Stormwind. Now it seemed he'd very likely expanded his search to the entire Eastern Kingdoms. If there were orcs among these fifteen, heaven knew what other races were mixed in.

Mor'zul was no ordinary figure, it seemed.

Jace crouched down and touched the traces of the circle, then brought his fingers to his nose and immediately caught a sharp, acrid fel scent — though the power had mostly faded.

"What was this drawn with?"

"Demon blood," Mor'zul said, looking at Jace. "To be precise — the blood of Azgoth. So I owe you some thanks for this, Jace. A circle written in a pit lord's blood is far more effective than the one we wrote in Mo'arg demon blood before. Our progress was smoother than last time… but in the end, we still didn't fully succeed."

"Where did you get Azgoth's blood?" Jace asked in surprise.

Mor'zul said, "His blood covered the slope in front of Nethergarde Keep. You know what would happen if that blood were left there to freely contaminate the land… what it would do to Nethergarde Keep? So someone had to collect it and take it away. Still, it doesn't take much blood to draw a circle like this — Azgoth's blood is thicker than magma."

Jace immediately wiped the dried blood off his fingers on the grass beside him. He had to admit — compared to the small-time operations beneath the Slaughtered Lamb, these people were playing for real.

"So you see, Jace," Mor'zul said with a hint of pride. "You should trust our strength and our preparations. Even if Azgoth himself came, I'm confident I could keep him here — unable to escape… unable to resist!"

"But you still failed twice," Jace said.

Mor'zul said, "I admit it — but not because of our preparations. The mana for the summoning still isn't enough. We need more soul shards, more powerful summoners. So you must join us, Jace. As long as you serve as one of the three primary summoners executing the circle, it will definitely succeed. Your shadow talent… I saw it clearly last time we summoned a felhound. Now I've revealed our whole situation to you. Can you set aside your wariness and suspicion and join us? Once that demon is summoned, I'll make you one of the three people with priority to speak to him — everyone else will be behind you."

Jace asked, "Can I alone make up for the mana you're missing, Mor'zul? That's the crux of it."

Mor'zul said, "After calculations by me and several other warlocks with arcane training, this summoning site sits on a ley line — the mana it provides should be enough to support our summoning of this demon lord. The last time, we were actually very close to success."

"If the mana here is truly that abundant, wouldn't the Wizard's Sanctum have any interest in this place at all, Mor'zul?" Jace asked.

"After the Dark Portal, the Wizard's Sanctum has been too busy with too many things," Mor'zul said. "Nethergarde Keep is still busy clearing demons from the Blasted Lands, and they need to do their best to support them… Moonbrook is a place even King Varian can barely manage right now. So now is the perfect time to accomplish our undertaking, Jace. You're a member of the Wizard's Sanctum — you know I'm right."

Jace said, "The real reason the Wizard's Sanctum doesn't care about this place is that the ley lines of the entire Kingdom of Stormwind aren't as abundant as they used to be, Mor'zul. Karazhan was built on a powerful ley line nexus — it influenced the ley lines of more than half the kingdom. The Karazhan Cataclysm affected the stability of the ley lines across the entire region."

This wasn't something Jace had read in his past life — it was investigation records by the mages he'd come across when looking into Stormwind's mana sources. But that was information only visible to those bearing the Wizard's Sanctum's sigil. Mor'zul had likely investigated this summoning site using old, undisclosed materials.

Judging from the expression on Mor'zul's face, Jace had guessed right. He was stunned for a long while before saying, "Actually, after last time, you weren't the only one who brought this up. It seems that's the reason. Which means we should find a new place for the summoning."

"Perhaps outside the kingdom's borders," Jace said.

Mor'zul thought for a moment and said, "I know a place — north of Redridge."

When Mor'zul mentioned north of Redridge, Jace already knew almost exactly where he meant. "North of Redridge — isn't that Blackrock Mountain?"

Mor'zul said, "Not inside Blackrock Mountain itself, but to the west of that scorched plain. The orcs built a very secret altar among the mountains there, used for summoning demons. A Dark Iron dwarven warlock mentioned it to me before — but at the time the orcs still held that place. Now the fighting between dwarves and orcs has grown fierce. The new chieftain of the Blackrock clan doesn't pay much attention to that place — after all, the Shadow Council has declined — so no one has control over that altar anymore. It's time to put it to our use."

Yes — that Altar of Storms.

In the game, it was near that altar that Mor'zul stood, teaching warlocks how to summon dreadsteeds. It seemed Mor'zul had had his eye on that place for a long time.

He continued, "I plan to gather everyone for a meeting and make all the preparations — the incantation, the summoning site, the casting materials, the summoning process, and so on."

"I have other business a month from now," Jace said.

"Within the week," Mor'zul gripped Jace's shoulder. "Everyone is waiting for the next summoning, Jace. Even without you, we were planning a third attempt soon — let alone now that we have you."

"I hope there really aren't any orcs guarding that place," Jace said.

"If it were two years ago, I might have doubted it — but now, even if there are, there won't be many," Mor'zul said expressionlessly. "We can clear them all out then. If we can't handle a handful of orcs left outside Blackrock Spire, we needn't discuss summoning a demon lord at all, Jace. Once you see the people I've gathered, you won't have these doubts."
