Chapter 721: A Mixed Bag

Four days had passed since Jace's arrangement with Mosh'aru. He'd spent the whole time sitting in the alley out back, studying the book Master Malin had given him — The First Burning. He'd reached the section on the Fire Protection Barrier's incantation, though he wasn't entirely sure he'd actually understood what he'd read.

The thing was, the Fire Protection Barrier's incantation was a spell in Thalassian. He didn't yet know how to pronounce elven words and sentences according to the proper phonetic rules, and he hadn't gone to Malin to ask about the correct way to say it. Learning it in the short term was pretty much out of the question.

For him, any arcane incantation without someone around to guide him was like trying to scale a sheer cliff face. Chanting the spell while holding Song of the Blue Sky would probably work a lot better, but he was more afraid that, unfamiliar as he was with it, he might turn the spell into something like a spontaneous inferno explosion or a rapid self-immolation technique. Better not to even try.

That evening, as he was getting ready to head home, he glanced up at the sky — it looked overcast, like rain was on the way. He gazed down every road that led here from a distance, but there was no sign of anyone suspicious.

Who exactly was Mosh'aru planning to send for him? Or... a demon?

He swept the area with Detect Demon, but no demonic shadows were lurking around.

Just as he let his Detect Demon fade and started packing up the food parcels spread on the ground, preparing to head home, he suddenly heard someone calling his name. "Jace Sesso... do you hear my call?"

The voice appeared out of nowhere, as if echoing up from the abyss, and it put him on edge immediately. Jace felt a wave of void energy settle over his head — it was this magic that was producing the sound.

"Answer my call, Jace Sesso. Mosh'aru the Bloodcaller is at my side."

The voice was unfamiliar, but it reminded Jace of the way Sazeno said his name. Was this a messenger Mosh'aru had sent, searching for his location?

"I hear you, stranger," Jace replied, watching the passersby who might be passing through. "Come to me, Jace Sesso..."

The words were spoken in Demon Tongue. Jace said, "I can come, but where are you—"

The moment he answered, he suddenly felt as if the void had snuffed him out by the river — his whole body shrank into an indigo vortex he couldn't quite describe...

The murky, pitch-black darkness swept him up, spinning and deforming him within it, as if trying to dissolve him entirely. Jace struggled to break free of its grip, finally wrenching his hands and feet out of that swamp-like blackness, and suddenly his feet hit solid ground. He stumbled forward two steps.

Before him lay another kind of darkness — endless black earth and sheer cliffs. Nearby stood some red beast-hide banners caked in black ash. The air was thick with a choking burnt stench, and having just left the canal bank to plunge into a place like this, the sheer contrast made the smell even harder to bear.

Jace recognized this place. After all, it wasn't his first time in the Burning Steppes.

He turned around. Behind him stood a group of... people who could only be described as bizarrely dressed and strangely assembled, all staring at him.

Among the summoners there were humans, orcs, goblins, dwarves, gnomes — but unexpectedly, not a single elf.

Then again, after his trip to Quel'Thalas, Jace could understand. The quel'dorei probably looked down their noses at these fiends and freaks. Even if they wanted to summon demons, the bare minimum would be cooperating with the Kirin Tor or the Violet Hold — not teaming up with a ragtag outfit like this.

He called them a ragtag outfit because, while they were probably all demon researchers, Jace noticed that not everyone seemed particularly skilled with shadow magic.

One of the Dark Iron dwarves had a rod on his back that wasn't a staff at all, but a large-bore firearm. The dwarf standing next to him held a long pole that looked more like a pike with a pincer than a staff, because Jace couldn't sense any mana on it that its wielder could make use of.

The others with swords and scimitars hanging from their waists seemed quite normal by comparison. The most absurd was a human "warlock" who, in addition to a sword, was actually carrying a bow and had a quiver slung over his shoulder.

Then again, considering Jace hadn't brought a staff either, he fit right in. Out of the fifteen people here, only three humans, one orc, and one gnome — Mosh'aru included — had brought a staff or rod.

Less a team of demon researchers, more a demon-cult gang — fighters, mages, each with their own role...

Jace guessed they probably focused most of their energy on how to summon and control demons, and weren't particularly adept at curses or shadow magic. In that regard, they might even be weaker than the warlocks of the Slaughtered Lamb, or the bunch in Moonbrook.

Beside and behind them stood a host of demons — various imps, male and female succubi, felhounds, even a Mo'arg warrior that had been summoned over. That demon wasn't wearing the helmet and armor of a doomguard; it carried a long blade and didn't quite look like a doomguard, but it was formidable nonetheless.

Seeing so many demons, Jace almost thought he'd been lured here by a mixed Burning Legion squad. But looking closer, none of these demons bore fel markings, nor did they wear the fel-inscribed armor or robes that Legion members typically sported. They were indeed all wild demons that had been summoned.

"I'm guessing you've never been summoned by a ritual circle before, Jace Sesso." Mosh'aru handed the bead necklace back to him with a proud air. "When I say I'll assemble everyone within a week, I mean within a week — never a moment beyond. I hope that dispels some of your doubts about our little group."

Not far away, an orc warlock in a dark brown robe said in fluent Common, "For someone being summoned for the first time, your reaction was remarkably mild. You really are, as the legends say, a human with considerable shadow talent."

Though Jace hated to admit it, he was quite surprised these people had mastered summoning magic. He noticed the ritual circle drawn in some demon's purple blood at his feet was still glowing faintly. A female summoner beside him said, "Watch your step when you leave, Jace Sesso. Don't damage the circle we spent an entire day and night carefully crafting."

Jace nodded and stepped aside.

Before this, he'd had his doubts about what he'd actually gain from helping these warlocks perform such a dangerous summoning. But after seeing this circle, Jace felt he might get far more out of them than he'd originally expected.

In the future, it wouldn't just be about summoning demons — he might actually learn how to summon other people. It was just that this summoning magic seemed to require not only a complex ritual circle, but also three casters working in concert. He wondered whether the Slaughtered Lamb could even scrape together three people capable of performing a summoning ritual.

And maybe even the "candy" that warlocks in the game could summon to hand out to their party — healing stones, essentially an extra health potion.

Strictly speaking, that was also magic learned from demons, well within the scope of demon research.

But after thinking it over for a moment, he realized he hadn't brought the potions Ursula had prepared for him — and Molophil and Sazeno hadn't come along!
