Chapter 723: The Storm Altar

At this abandoned orc ruin, Jace got acquainted with the group of warlocks and studied incantations together with them.

Jace didn't meet much opposition to his proposed modifications to the incantations. He realized that almost everyone here had been more or less influenced by the demonology texts handed down by Dath'morar, so they held a considerable degree of reverence for Dath'morar himself. So when Jace brought out Dath'morar's demon scrolls to correct their incantations, they almost immediately agreed.

Realizing this, Jace felt a bit of relief that he hadn't told Mosh'aru at his house that Dath'morar had already been killed by him. Otherwise, wouldn't these warlocks chase him to the ends of the earth?

That evening, resting in a cave within the ruin in preparation for departure the next morning, Mosh'aru came over to Jace and handed him a scroll.

"What's this?" Jace asked.

Mosh'aru replied, "This is the incantation to activate the power of that altar. Once we reach the altar, you, I, and Master Ely — the one who summoned you to this camp — the three of us will be responsible for summoning the demon. Master Ely is an expert in summoning magic I found. His teacher was a member of the former Order of Tirisfal. You saw the summoning magic he just led, Jace."

"I saw it," Jace nodded.

When this Ely was summoning Molophil, he'd recited an extremely long incantation. Jace had originally wanted to memorize it outright with his fairly good memory, but as it got longer and longer, and he also had to follow along with the auxiliary incantation Mosh'aru had given him, he gave it up.

If he could get hold of the incantation Ely recited, and figure out how the circle outside the cave worked, learning the summoning ritual would be within reach.

Mosh'aru continued, "When Master Ely leads the summoning, we need to channel the altar's power into the summoning circle and help him complete the entire ritual process. This is much more complicated than the hills behind Moonbrook, so it requires even more concentration. Tomorrow we'll do a rehearsal here — not only to confirm you've mastered the incantation, but to make sure our coordination is perfect before we set out for that altar. However, you showed enough talent in this area when summoning Lady Molophil. I have great confidence in you."

"Where did this incantation come from?" Jace asked, holding up the scroll.

"Kagel Shadowfist's incantation. He was an attendant who followed the Shadow Council to Blackrock Mountain, Jace," Mosh'aru said. "He witnessed the construction of both altars and knows them far more deeply than we do."

Kagel Shadowfist — that would be the only orc in this group. Jace could tell from his skin that he was either a member of the Shadow Council or a Blackrock orc. Both orc groups had skin darkened by dark magic.

Mosh'aru said, "Tomorrow during the rehearsal, the three of us will handle the summoning ritual, six will maintain the binding circle's power, and the six outside will continuously use incantations to weaken the demon's power. Once the binding circle completes its total control over the demon, he'll be trapped on that altar, unable to escape — our prisoner."

"How long will this process take?" Jace asked.

"Can't say for sure," Mosh'aru said. "But we've already successfully bound a doomguard with this circle. That doomguard is still being held firmly. Gorsuch's succubus minion is guarding him. That binding took only five minutes. This time, the circle channels the entire altar's power, plus six people outside weakening the demon with magic. I don't think it'll take long."

A bound doomguard?

"Do you have the incantation to summon a doomguard?" Jace asked.

"No," Mosh'aru said. "That doomguard was found in the Black Swamp and ended up falling into our trap." Well, no difference either way — at least they'd gotten a chance to speak with an Eredruin demon. Hopefully this demon would be willing to answer the questions he wanted to ask.

If he wasn't willing to answer... Jace touched his chest through his cloak, feeling the Will Chain. Then he'd be forced to submit.

Jace said, "After this summoning ritual, I want to see that doomguard, Mosh'aru."

"No problem," Mosh'aru said.

"You might think I'm being overly cautious, but I still want to ask," Jace continued. "What if something goes wrong with the binding circle — for example, someone can't hold on, or other..."

"These six are the most patient and stable among us," Mosh'aru said. "That circle is led by Kagel. The remaining five are 'Short Rod' Gladys,科尔·托比亚斯, Hobe Brightforge of Ironforge, Regisfal, and Demetrius Klaus."

Jace noticed that except for Hobe Brightforge, all of them were human. "What about Mr. Albert Fizzlebang?"

Mosh'aru replied, "Of course he's on the outside. The others include my student Gorsuch — that goblin, Albert Fizzlebang's friend — another gnome named Sparker, the Dark Iron dwarf Fezias, Luthor Pickman — that Lordaeronian you talked to earlier who has a succubus — Martha Stein, a demon scholar expelled from Dalaran, and all of our demon minions. Those on the outside are not only responsible for weakening the demon but also handling emergencies. If someone maintaining the binding circle has a problem, they'll immediately step in as replacements. If other intruders outside try to cause trouble, the succubi lurking beyond the altar will be responsible for eliminating those uninvited guests."

Jace noticed they'd assigned the gnome and the goblin to the outside. It seemed even among these warlocks, having these two races in critical positions was considered a bit too much.

Mosh'aru continued, "If the demon still breaks free of our binding, there's nothing left but to perform a banishment. Master Ely will execute the banishment and send him back to the Twisting Nether."

"That simple?" Jace asked. "Even a demon lord, once summoned to this world, can be sent back with a banishment incantation?"

"Of course it's not as simple as banishing an imp, Jace," Mosh'aru said. "This is the reason the summoning circle exists. This is the reason we two exist. We've prepared the entire altar's power and so many casting materials not simply to summon him here. When Master Ely executes the banishment, we'll also channel the altar's power in the same way to provide mana support for the circle and for him personally."

"What if the banishment fails too?" Jace asked.

"If it truly comes to that, then we'll have to rely on our own strength to forcibly send him back to the Twisting Nether and execute the 'banishment,'" Mosh'aru said.

Jace said, "Then I hope everyone's prepared to fight to the death."

Mosh'aru nodded and said, "Everyone here has long since prepared to fight to the death against the demons they've summoned, Jace. A warlock being killed by a demon they summoned is far too common — it's a disaster even those ten-year-old acolytes in Northshire know about. How could we not know?"

After that, the fifteen-person group conducted rehearsals for several days. Mosh'aru and Ely used their staffs to sketch the rough outline of the circle in the charred black earth on the ground, having everyone stand in their assigned positions.

These two plus Jace stood in the innermost ring. The six assigned to maintain the binding circle stood in the ring outside them. The outermost ring was the remaining six warlocks, plus a host of demon minions — just as Mosh'aru had said.

And without any need for direction, everyone immediately knew their positions and what they should do.

They were indeed already very familiar with the entire process. By comparison, Jace felt like a transfer student, completely at a loss.

But Mosh'aru had also said he didn't need to understand what the others were doing — he just needed to complete his own task, which was the most important part of the summoning ritual... the summoning itself.

For his own part — memorizing the incantation, coordinating with Master Ely for the summoning — he had of course practiced quite proficiently. But without the Storm Altar beneath his feet, without soul stones and other casting materials having their power channeled, this summoning ritual could at best be considered a dress rehearsal — a performance with no props.

Although during practice Jace could feel the incantation taking effect, without being able to channel mana, there was naturally no communication with the Twisting Nether at all.

To truly know how it would perform, they'd still need to go to where the circle and altar were located.

On the third day, the group left the camp and headed northwest, setting out for the Storm Altar.

Following the foot of the Redridge Mountains for less than two days' journey, they entered a canyon piled with tattered orcish hide banners. There were orc habitation traces everywhere — it seemed an entire army had once been stationed here. But now, aside from the occasional smokeweb spider, each about the size of a wild bear, crawling out of the ruins and garbage from time to time, no other living things remained.

When the warlocks entered the canyon, even those savage, bloodthirsty beasts immediately hid away. They must have encountered demons and orc warlocks who used dark magic before, sensing the message concealed within this group.

After all, a few years ago, the Storm Altar had been the Blackrock orcs' primary summoning site.

Walking deeper into the mountains along the canyon, broken orc skeletons, picked completely clean, began to appear on the ground.

By midnight, Jace smelled a rather distinct shadow aura rushing out with the wind passing through the canyon. The orc Kagel said, "We're here."

Everyone quickened their pace. The shorter ones even ran, chasing the firelight that had already been lit ahead. Climbing over another high slope and crossing over, a magnificent dark altar appeared in the center of the valley before them.

Behind two massive orcish stone pillars, the hexagonal altar was surrounded by lava flowing from within Blackrock Mountain, connected to the steps on this side only by a bridge shaped like a beast's spine.

Around the altar, three giant hooded humanoids over ten meters tall, hands resting on their swords, stood surrounding it, overlooking the stone-carved summoning circle at the altar's center. Countless deformed demon bones, large and small, were piled at the feet of these statues, silently testifying to the altar's past glory.

"Even abandoned, the altar's great power can still be felt," Mosh'aru said, looking at the lava flowing below. "Rest tonight. Tomorrow we begin drawing the circle."
