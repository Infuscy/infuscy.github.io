## Chapter 737: The Letter

Though the fight had been harder than expected, the exhilaration among the Stonewatch soldiers made it clear that this assault had gone far more smoothly than they'd anticipated.

Because Jace had broken through the tower's defenses floor by floor, the soldiers involved in the assault had been able to hold the interior against the gnolls trying to force their way in from outside — and so none of them had lost their lives. The most seriously wounded was a warrior up above who'd been hit by a gnoll's Curse of Weakness and fallen two stories down; thanks to the attending priest's care, he had only just stabilized.

Jace helped the soldiers carry the injured back into the tower to rest. The busywork actually reminded him of the days he'd spent at the War of the Dark Portal, helping the Stromgarde soldiers haul supplies at Stonard.

He actually liked this kind of life — busy, doing what work he could manage.

The soldiers who'd charged to the top of the tower were now recounting what had happened up above to the soldiers whod been holding off the gnolls at the base. As they spoke, they kept glancing over in Jace's direction.

The terrifying corpse of the hellhound was still lying there. Watching the disbelief on their faces, Jace felt as if he'd been caught red-handed.

"Master Sesso." A priest came up beside him. "I saw you were injured in the tower — I don't know if you need…" Jace turned his head, and the priest glanced at his face, startled.

"Perhaps I was mistaken…"

"You weren't mistaken." Jace touched his face, still able to feel a faint, lingering ache.

He felt as though he'd never get used to the strange sensation left behind by healing through Life Drain — the wound restored perfectly, no blood, no opening, not even an itch in the skin and flesh, yet a dull, throbbing pain, like some kind of phantom-limb ache.

The priest gave a knowing "mm," then said softly, "That life-draining magic, isn't it? The dark magic the orcs use — I can sense that shadow power."

This Cathedral of Light monk, dressed in his plain gray robes, had pointed out his use of dark magic so calmly, without any hostility or accusation, that Jace actually found it a little unsettling.

Come to think of it, he ought to go to the Cathedral and meet those priests. After all, King Terenas II of Lordaeron had instructed him to, and keeping it on the back burner indefinitely wasn't a good idea.

Then again, if Archbishop Alonsus Faol never showed his face in Stormwind, that would just be a matter of bad timing.

"Since everything is well, Master Sesso." The priest glanced at the soldiers in the distance, rolled up his sleeves, let out a breath, and said, "Then I'll go help the others. There are many injured soldiers — if you feel any discomfort at all, don't hesitate to say so."

"Of course, thank you for your trouble, brother." Jace gave a slight nod.

Hearing this, the priest gave Jace a somewhat peculiar look, but still nodded his farewell and headed over to where the other wounded were resting and lying down.

When it came to caring for the injured, Jace could do little beyond hauling whatever supplies were needed. By now the wounded had all been settled, and many of the soldiers who'd been helping were sitting off to the side catching their breath. Jace raised his head and gazed up at the upper levels of the mage tower, carefully taking in the tower's dimensions.

The tower felt about fifteen meters tall. Without the exact specifications of the Wizard Sanctum, Jace couldn't judge it with any precision, since it sat on raised ground to begin with and was thicker than an ordinary watchtower, making it seem somewhat squat overall.

Standing at the base and looking up, it appeared considerably larger than it had when he'd observed it from above while riding a griffin.

If they removed the central staircase on the ground floor and replaced it with a spiral running up along the inner wall of the mage tower, while leaving the upper portion unchanged, then perhaps Kallrez really could be moved into the center.

Of course, that tower door, a little over two meters tall, would never let a doom lord squeeze through — even a doomguard would have a hard time.

So if they wanted to get Kallrez in here without damaging the tower structure, there was only one option: perform the summoning ceremony inside the mage tower and summon the demon lord here from the Stormwind Altar.

However, Jace didn't know whether the weakened ley-line energy beneath the tower at this point could support such a ceremony.

Then again, the difficulty of summoning Kallrez from the Stormwind Altar here would certainly be far less than summoning him from some unknown corner of the Twisting Nether to Azeroth, so the energy required wouldn't need to be nearly as great.

If the energy still wasn't enough, Jace considered summoning a voidwalker himself, using it as the mage tower's power source the way the gnolls had, then sacrificing the voidwalker to summon the doom lord…

Thinking this, he climbed the stairs again one step at a time. The central staircases were damaged to varying degrees along the way — they swayed a bit underfoot — but they didn't look like they were about to collapse immediately.

Reaching the top floor of Darrowshire Tower, Jace noticed a dark-red magic circle drawn in what appeared to be the blood of some beast or humanoid, set before a bookshelf. Its strokes connected to the sigil lines on the tower walls, linking to a metal fixture at the peak of the tower that held a small, dimmed magic stone — a pathetic thing like a shard of broken glass.

The sigil lines the gnolls had drawn were remarkably crude, like a bunch of schoolchildren scribbling all over a blackboard — but they'd at least done the job.

That small magic stone was barely the size of a drinking cup. Compared to the massive magic stone the elves had transported to the top of the Nethergarde Keep mage tower, it was truly pitiful.

But it was still able to draw power from the voidwalker through the magic circle and supply mana to the spellcasters inside the tower. That said, the tower was far too decrepit — before they could summon Kallrez here, it would need a proper renovation.

Even if they didn't go for anything as lavish and handsome as Antonidas's study, at least it should be clean and tidy overall, right? Having endured several battles and been occupied by both gnolls and orcs for extended periods, the mage tower reeked of a foul, rank stench.

He kicked at a broken tile nearby, raising a cloud of dust.

Still, no matter what, this tower was about to be his. So starting with a thorough cleaning, he'd take proper care of her.

That evening, returning to Stonewatch, Jace drank and celebrated with the soldiers. At the feast, he was seized by a group of veterans and tossed into the air again and again. The tale of his heroic charge into the tower ahead of everyone else, braving a hail of gnoll magic and arrows, was passed around by the drunkards in three or four different versions…

At first Jace tried to correct the more exaggerated parts — for instance, he hadn't used a great fire powerful enough to destroy everything to shatter the gnolls' formation, nor had he sealed off the entire gnoll army's assault with a single wall of ice, let alone used a mass teleportation spell to fling all the gnolls into the Dark Portal.

But in the end he gave up entirely, since he could barely keep up with the constant stream of soldiers coming to clink cups with him, let alone go out of his way to chat with anyone.

"…I said halt, monster, turn around, back to the mountains!

"Don't come to Elwynn, asking for trouble! If you won't stop,

"My sword will guide you, the Light will guide you!"

The soldiers sang songs from the Gnoll War era, and Jace hummed along to the simple tune, even though he had no idea what the lyrics meant.

At that moment, Colonel Tordman came over and said, "News of Stonewatch's victory will reach Stormwind soon, Master Sesso."

"Just call me Jace, Colonel," Jace said. "I feel I still fall well short of the true masters in the Wizard Sanctum. To me, shadow is not so different from the clubs in the gnolls' hands — I just know how to swing them in a way that cuts more easily."

"Even for clubs, the ones who cut the easiest are still called 'weapons masters.'" Tordman raised his cup to Jace. The two clugged and each took a long drink. He then added, "But since you insist, Jace, that's what I'll call you."

Jace looked at the soldiers, then asked, "I'm a little curious how they view my situation, Colonel."

"You mean the dark magic?" Tordman said. "Most people feel that having a warlock like this on our side is better than having him on the gnolls' side or the Horde's side. But to say that everyone has no reservations at all — that's impossible."

Jace nodded. That was already far better than he'd imagined.

Late that night, he returned to the room prepared for the Wizard Sanctum's mages at Stonewatch. He remembered staying next door the last time he'd been here.

Many soldiers were still celebrating in the great hall below. Listening to their singing and shouting, Jace sat down at the desk and laid out a sheet of paper.

He was going to write a letter to Vereesa Windrunner.

During the tower assault, Jace had been hit by Fear, and the vision that had surfaced in his mind had stayed with him to this day. He didn't know whether he was worrying about an uncertain future or simply missing her.

So even during the feast, he'd been eager to get back to his room to write the letter — after all, this was what they'd agreed upon. Thinking this, he wrote the first line, in Thalassian.

— Greetings, the esteemed Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas, Lady Vereesa Windrunner.

He looked at the line he'd slowly written out, agonizing over the grammar and the spelling of the runes for a while — but quickly realized that Vereesa would never pick him apart over something like that.

So he smiled, gave up adjusting it, and simply added in Common: "Or good evening, depending on when you receive this letter. For the time being, I have no Thalassian dictionary at hand, so I'm not sure how to phrase the greetings in formal letter format."

Next, Jace gave a brief account of what he'd been doing lately. Of course, regarding the matter of summoning demons with the warlocks, to prevent the letter's contents from being seen by others, he only hinted: "My research into the Eredun demon language is essentially complete, and the task you assigned me has borne results — more optimistic than I'd imagined. If you have the time, perhaps we could meet in person to discuss it in detail."

At the end he signed off, then added "looking forward to your reply" in Thalassian.

After folding the letter carefully and tucking it into his pack, Jace lay in bed thinking about Vereesa's possible response, and before long he'd fallen asleep.
