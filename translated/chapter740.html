Chapter 740: Know Your Own True Desire

Jace gave the hellhounds their orders: defend this tower to the death, and on no account leave the tower. Before setting out, watching the two mismatched-sized hellhounds patrolling earnestly between the stairs and the platforms, he actually got a real sense of an evil lair straight out of the game. Then he informed the Stonewatch soldiers of his departure and set off at once for the Stormwind Altar. After spending a night resting at Lakeshire and three days traveling north over the Redridge Mountains, he arrived at the valley once more. The orc camp had been tidied up by the warlocks to a far more presentable state than before — it looked as though the group was planning to settle here for a while. Coming to the cliff's edge and looking down at the altar surrounded by lava, Kallrez was still half-kneeling exactly as he'd been when Jace left. Molophil came over. "When you set out for here… I sensed your thoughts, master. I've been waiting for your arrival ever since. Being alone here is truly unbearable." Jace stared at the demon lord as he descended the steps and said offhandedly, "Flattery like that doesn't serve much purpose on me anymore, Molophil." Molophil leaned closer. "Then should I switch to a different persona? For instance, posing as an innocent maiden to seduce you, like Vereesa Windrunner does? Does master prefer that type? Actually, I could do that too." "Is that needling?" Jace asked. Molophil whispered close to his ear: "Did I needle you? Perhaps. But there's not a shred of hostility in it, master — nothing but my uncontrollable jealousy…" Reaching the platform of the Stormwind Altar, Kallrez finally raised his head. His shield-like pauldrons and broad chest plate scraped against each other with a grating metallic sound that, under the magic of the binding circle, echoed as though reverberating from the depths of some impossibly distant valley. After fighting those gnolls and the voidwalker inside the mage tower, facing a doom lord this time, Jace immediately felt an overwhelming pressure — even though the creature was firmly bound and under his enslavement, the sensation was that of something from an entirely different plane of existence. Because he'd faced Deathwing before, he realized he'd seriously underestimated this monster from the Twisting Nether. "What other foolish questions do you have for me, you self-proclaimed 'master' little rat?" As he spoke, Kallrez abruptly tried to lunge his head forward, only to be yanked back hard by the binding circle's power. The lava beneath the Stormwind Altar churned once more. The expression on the face of the female warlock Demitri Clauss, who was walking over from a distant stone pillar, could hardly be called composed either — after all, she was one of the people who'd participated in the binding circle before. Jace glanced at this warlock. The demon's oppressive aura was clearly affecting her too. "Have you managed to get anything out of him these days, Lady Clauss?" Demitri replied, "After Musul left, none of us has dared ask him any questions on our own initiative. Today it's simply my turn to keep watch." "What did Musul go?" Jace asked. "He went back to Moonbrook to fetch things — since he's planning to settle here for a while too, he's probably going to bring over his unfinished research." Demitri answered. Jace nodded, hoping this fellow still remembered the teleportation circle incantation he wanted and would bring it along — that would save him a trip to Moonbrook. "Are you even listening to me, Jace Sesso!" Kallrez growled. "I heard you," Jace said. "But I'm the master. I'll answer when I feel like it and not answer when I don't feel like it. No need to give you any reason." The demon lord swore furiously in Demonic and shook his body violently — but under the binding circle's control, the movement was so slight that Jace couldn't even tell whether the creature was trying to attack or simply take a step forward. It was nothing more than a shiver. Looking down at the binding circle beneath his feet, it was unclear whether there would even be enough energy to maintain this kind of restraint once they got to the mage tower. Jace took out the Eredun-language scroll the imp had written for him, scanned it, and found a word the doomguard had spoken in that underground chamber. "Tell me the meaning of this word, Kallrez." He pronounced it from memory: "Orulyte." The moment he heard the word, the demon lord laughed. "Did I mishear, or are you truly trying to insult me in our own language?" That doomguard had said this word meant "enslaved beast" — the name given by wandering Eredun to their kin who'd joined the Burning Legion, deeply insulting to these soldiers of the Legion. Judging from Kallrez's reaction, that doomguard had been telling the truth. "So you do know what it means." Jace said. "What exactly are you trying to say?" the demon lord asked in a low voice. "I want to learn your language." Jace said. "Next word." He picked a short word from Dasmora's scroll and copied it onto the ground with a charred stick. The demon lord swept his eyes over the scornfully and said in Demonic: "Hide." "Good." Jace glanced at the page in his hand. "Once I've gathered more Eredun, I'll have you continue translating for me." "What, did you find the report Dasmora intended to submit to me and want to know what it says?" Kallrez said, almost cockily. "Don't think I don't know what little game you're playing, Jace Sesso. There are barely any Eredun left in this world still using this language — Kazzak, Kaz'rogal, Archimonde… the rest I can count on my fingers. Dasmora is one too, because she thought one day she could become a being like me — but her ambition far outstripped her power! Well then, how does it feel to have your mind seen through, you little worm? Do you think you're clever?" Jace glanced at the demon lord's hands — they had only six fingers in total. He didn't answer Kallrez's taunt head-on, but asked: "Then what's your ambition, Kallrez? Since even an Eredun like Dasmora has her own designs." "My ambition is to serve the Great Lord Sargeras with all I am," Kallrez said. "You know more about demons than I gave you credit for, Jace Sesso. Now you're not satisfied with controlling me by brute-force magic and intend to forge a master-servant pact with me instead? You arrogant scum — you're even more presumptuous than that Dasmora slave!" "Tell me the truth." Jace demanded. "That's an order!" "I make no secret of my loyalty!" Kallrez roared. "Serving Lord Kil'jaeden, serving the Great Lord — that is my highest aspiration, whether you force it out of me with your magic or not…" Jace said, "Then I promise that one day I'll send you back to the Twisting Nether and return you to Kil'jaeden and Sargeras's side. In exchange… you must be loyal to me during this time. I command you to tell me, in full, the incantation for forging a pact on these terms, Kallrez." Kallrez snorted disdainfully, then began chanting in a low voice. "Selota, Embati, Kaindel, Gangengantis…" The incantation was long, because it included the pact's conditions — much like the incantations for summoning succubi and imps. Jace chanted along as he recorded the pronunciation in Demonic on a piece of paper. But he realized this wasn't Demonic — it was very likely Eredun as well — so he added corrections beside it to make his pronunciation as close to the original as possible. After cross-checking with the demon lord, he began attempting to chant it. Channeling the power of the Stormwind Altar, he found the spellcasting went remarkably smoothly, and after several attempts he established some faint connection with the demon lord. Of course, this incantation didn't need to summon a demon from the depths of space — it was only a magical communication with the demon before him — so it was naturally far less difficult, especially since this demon was already obeying him. But Jace felt that this incantation wasn't achieving the effect he wanted. The connection was severed very quickly — this was probably far from enough to establish a pact. He wasn't the only one who felt this way. Even Molophil said: "It's not working, master." "Why?" Jace asked. "She's right." Kallrez said in a low voice. "Even if I were ordered to attempt to forge a pact, it would be a waste of time — because this isn't your heart's true desire, Jace Sesso. Not only do you not want me to go back — you're afraid I'll go back. I can sense it without any mind-probing magic, you laughable little clown. If you want to play at clever schemes… your fear and worry will prevent the pact from taking effect. If you want to reach a pact, first sincerely yearn for me to return to the Burning Legion…" The demon lord burst out laughing. Jace caught his breath — though he hadn't held out much hope to begin with. "Not everyone knows what they truly want, master." Molophil said with a smile. "Demon lords are no exception. You commanded him to speak his inner thoughts, and all he could produce was what he thought he wanted." Kallrez shifted his gaze and fixed it on the succubus. "You think you understand everything, succubus — in reality you're just showing off your ignorance." "Go on, Molophil." Jace said. "Yes, master." Molophil raised her head and gazed up at the colossal demon several times her size with an almost arrogant composure. "Just now, when he spoke the names of those demon lords, I sensed that one of the names affected him considerably. I could feel those complicated emotions within it — hatred, jealousy…" "Kazzak." Jace said. "I felt it too." Though Kallrez had spoken all those names in roughly the same tone — after all, he'd spoken fast, rattling them off one after another — Jace had still sensed something off when he got to "Kazzak." Of course, Jace was a warlock skilled at reading emotions, but Molophil was even better than him, which was why she could see more that was amiss. "Command him to tell us why he detests Kazzak so much, master." Molophil's tone carried the triumph of someone who'd found the key to the puzzle. "Go ahead," Jace said. "Obey my command — tell us the reason you detest Kazzak." "Because Kazzak is an out-and-out coward!" This time Kallrez roared almost without hesitation. "Lord Kil'jaeden must have been deceived to make him the leader of all doom lords! The only reason he's powerful is that in every battle he hides at the very back of the Legion, shielded by countless magic circles and protective barriers, so he never gets hurt! While I charge to the front every time, answering my subordinates' summons — the merit I've earned is countless times greater than that empty-title bastard's!" "So have you ever considered that this is the reason you're trapped here?" Sazeno couldn't help but sneer. "Who's speaking!" Kallrez lowered his head. "I can't even sense the presence of another creature — so weak I can barely make out the voice…" "What?!" Sazeno angrily tried to shout out, only for Jace to shove him into the pack in one motion. Molophil glanced at the pack at Jace's waist, then looked back up at the doom lord. "So your ambition is to become king of the Eredun. Unfortunately, my master doesn't seem willing to let you have that either." She looked at Jace again. "What do we do, master?" "There's at least one thing I'm willing to do," Jace said. "And that's kill Kazzak — just like you, Kallrez. I think Kazzak's death would at least move you a tiny bit closer to that position, though I don't know whether you can compete with the other doom lords for Kil'jaeden's trust…" "You don't even know who Kazzak is." Kallrez cut him off bluntly. "The arrogance of mortals never ceases to astound me." "Maybe I know a little more than you think." Jace flexed his fingers. "Let's use this — it satisfies both our desires at once. You obey my orders, and I find a way to help you kill Kazzak. Truly perfect." "Speak the incantation containing this condition, Kallrez. That's an order." Kallrez seemed about to erupt, but his fury was suppressed by the enslavement magic's command, and he had to spit out the pact-forging incantation. This time, as Jace chanted once more, even Molophil, whose expression had been perpetually cocksure, grew grave. Halfway through the chant, he felt shadow light ripple beneath his feet from the magic circle — it reminded him of the sensation when he'd summoned the succubus, communicating with the ethereal entities of the Twisting Nether. When the incantation was complete, his perception of the demon before him changed in an instant. The intense oppressive force vanished entirely, even though the creature was no different in size or strength. Looking again at Demitri Clauss, the female warlock watching all this from not far away, her expression was still deeply unnatural. "Remember your promise, Jace Sesso!" Kallrez warned. "I remember." Jace said softly. "I'll think of a way to kill him as soon as possible. Don't be hasty, Lord Kallrez." "But master, you still haven't finished writing the report your mentor Marin assigned you." Molophil said. "Ah, that's right." Jace slapped his forehead. "I knew it!" Kallrez roared. "You little liar!" "But there's nothing you can do about it," Jace said. "You might as well accept the situation and put yourself in a better mood — after all, we still need to work together for a while."
