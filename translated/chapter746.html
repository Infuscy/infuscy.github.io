Chapter 746: Attempting to Unbind

That day, Mor'zul returned to the Altar of Storms with the pack he'd gathered from Moonbrook, sorted his things in his small tent, and Jace waited outside until he came out — one hand holding several scrolls, the other carrying a tome that was clearly of orcish make, reminding Jace of the damaged tome he'd obtained before that had recorded the Life Tap technique.

"This is a Shadow Council artifact, Jace. It records Gul'dan's summoning circles," Mor'zul said. "When the Horde's army entered Lordaeron, it was by relying on these circles that he was able to travel between the front lines and the rear, maintaining his rule over the Horde… until Orgrim Doomhammer smashed the Shadow Council. Many orc warlocks fled with these items, and only then were we able to obtain the knowledge within."

He handed the tome and scrolls to Jace and said, "The scrolls contain our transcribed contents. You can compare them against the orcish originals to check for errors and discrepancies — at least, I can't spot any. I can't give you the original, because its value is beyond what I can even estimate, but the transcribed scrolls I can give you."

"Have all the warlocks here already mastered this teleportation circle?" Jace asked.

"If you mean leading the summoning ritual, Ely and I are the most proficient. Kagel can also lead, as could the late Regisfal. 'Short Staff' is fine too, though he's relatively less practiced — for now we only dare let him summon demon minions. Of course, serving as an auxiliary in the ritual doesn't require any particular talent, just a basic understanding of Shadow power. Like how you succeeded in summoning your demon minion here on your very first try — though that also owed to the fact that Miss Morophil herself is a demon, making her easier to summon."

"I'll take them back and study them carefully," Jace said. "Also, I wanted to ask — do you have any spells that can temporarily make a minion stronger? Something that reinforces a demon's power, that sort of thing?"

"Curse of Recklessness?" Mor'zul said. "That's probably the simplest method."

"Nothing else?"

Mor'zul said, "There definitely are, but… I've heard that the orcs have many kinds of demon-empowering curses, and I've seen a doomguard use a spell to empower its felhound minion — but I couldn't remember the incantation it uttered that time either. The Ered'ruin language is nearly impossible to imitate."

Jace nodded and said, "We were planning to unbind Kallrez and have him demonstrate a spell, Mor'zul. Now that you're back, I think we can make the attempt."

"I understand," Mor'zul said. "Let me prepare some mana potions. We'll meet at the Altar of Storms."

Watching Mor'zul head back into his tent, Jace thought to himself that this warlock collective really was a thrown-together affair, and there wasn't much trust to speak of within it.

Clearly, the Demonic Empowerment curse that Demisette Cloyce had kept from Mor'zul was one example; Mor'zul's familiar magic mostly concerned summoning rituals and the Twisting Nether. Others probably had magic of their own they hadn't intended to share with him either.

Viewed that way, Demisette's curse really was quite valuable.

Though Jace found it a bit strange. After all, more than a decade after the Dark Portal, succubi were nothing particularly exotic to the warlocks here. Demisette Cloyce, while reasonably good-looking and decent of figure, still couldn't compare to the succubi summoned from the Twisting Nether. Why would she think that she could get a warlock like Jace to part with his ace just by offering her body?

And from what she'd said, it was clear this wasn't her first time pulling this sort of thing — she was an old hand at it. If the "Demonic Empowerment" curse had also been obtained from some other warlock through this same route…

Perhaps to many warlocks, no matter what form a succubus took, in the end she still couldn't be truly compared to a real human woman. Though Jace still found Vereesa far more attractive.

Just then, Morophil glanced over that way, as if she'd sensed what Jace was thinking.

That afternoon, the warlocks assembled at the Altar of Storms. Except for Demisette Cloyce, who was in Lakeshire, and Gorsuch, who was still bound, all the warlocks who had participated in the summoning ritual and hadn't died were present. Even "Short Staff" Gladys had recovered from the injuries sustained during the interruption of the summoning ritual and was back on his feet, standing atop the binding circle.

The warlocks placed the soul shards that needed to be transformed in front of Kallrez. Jace looked over the soul shards, raised his head, and said, "First, ground rules, Kallrez."

"A familiar procedure," Morophil murmured involuntarily.

"Speak," Kallrez said.

"First, you must not harm anyone," Jace said. "Unless they intend to harm me."

"Second, you are not to use any magic I haven't permitted. Third, you must not attempt to leave the Altar of Storms…"

"All pointless words," Kallrez said. "You hold my true name, you've enslaved my soul with a pact, and now you're putting on this show of laying down rules — you hypocritical coward."

He wasn't exactly wrong. Jace also wanted to put the warlocks around them at ease.

He ignored the doom lord's words and turned to the warlocks instead. "He understands my meaning. The ritual to dismantle the circle can begin."

Mor'zul nodded to Ely, and the group began chanting the incantation. The circle's light dimmed bit by bit. As the circle failed, the doom lord slowly rose to his feet, spreading his enormous wings.

When he stood fully upright, Jace couldn't help but take several steps back. The guy was just too big.

Roughly estimating, he had to be at least two or three stories tall — taller than most buildings in Stormwind. At this size, he wasn't much different from the statues in the Valley of Heroes.

If not for the Altar of Storms' giant sword-wielding figures standing beside him, making him look small by comparison, he would have seemed even more oppressive.

The warlocks were all a bit intimidated. The doom lord looked at their reactions and gave a cold laugh. Mor'zul shouted, "Don't be afraid — we can suppress him again at any time…"

"Don't waste time, Kallrez," Jace called out. "Transform these soul shards. Now."

"As you command, little one."

Kallrez extended his hand, and the countless soul shards on the ground large and small were immediately seized by a dark green fel glow into his enormous three-fingered palm.

At this moment, Jace kept silently reciting Kallrez's true name in his mind, ready at any instant to speak it and deal him a fatal blow.

He saw the demon suddenly clench his fist, a ball of fel fire engulfing it entirely. When he opened his hand again, a large pile of fel stones shimmering with dark green light scattered onto the ground, and the warlocks' imp minions all couldn't help but shriek with delight.
