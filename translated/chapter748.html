Chapter 748: Reasonable Conjecture

Now Jace found this a bit strange. Dasmora's scroll had mentioned the wailing — or howling — of souls in Demonic. Why repeat it again in Ered'ruin?

Was she trying to translate demonic into Ered'ruin, or the other way around?

Jace recalled how, when he'd translated Teron Gorefiend's orders, he'd run afoul of Teron Gorefiend himself over a single missed tense. So this time he didn't dare jump to conclusions, and instead turned to the next word.

"Uultfrikiaz." Jace had to exert considerable effort just to pronounce the syllable — he even doubted whether the imp could actually say the word.

"Maze," Kallrez said. "It comes from an allusion passed down from an ancient world you've never seen — speaking of a maze full of danger, and moreover a living maze. Perhaps it's a spell? Where did you hear this word, Jace Sesso?"

Jace didn't answer him. He was thinking — did this so-called maze refer to a specific place? Or was it some kind of metaphor, an image? Or perhaps a comparison to some "maze" on Azeroth?

This probably wasn't the right time to dwell on it.

He glanced at the paper in his hand again, picked out the longest word, and read: "Oleukadindabeerdri… Laloberg."

"Mountains?" As he said it, Kallrez fell into thought for a moment — he seemed to be getting a bit into the translator's role too. "Mountains, but with an ominous hue, terrifying, unapproachable. Jace Sesso, I gather that's the meaning." At last there was a bit of concrete location information.

Mountains with an ominous hue — Jace's very first thought was practically Grim Batol. Could Ulthalesh be hidden inside Grim Batol?

Jace seriously doubted it, because it had been eight hundred years since Guardian Scavell defeated the necromancer. Back then, Grim Batol had been nothing but barren hills, and the War of the Three Hammers that split the dwarves and drove the Wildhammer out of Ironforge was still over five hundred years away.

Moreover, the reason Grim Batol had become ominous and terrifying was because of the magic of the dark witch Modgud and her dark dagger. If that place had already become a land of ill omen because of Ulthalesh, the Wildhammer dwarves would never have chosen to establish their city there in the first place.

Most of the remaining short words were things of little significance. After getting the gist of the better part of the scroll over this period, he'd been able to guess the rest pretty accurately from context alone.

At noon, sitting on the stone platform overlooking the altar, Jace gazed at the giant figures surrounding the altar while turning over the sentences he'd copied from Dasmora's report in his mind, trying to connect all the clues he'd translated so far.

Based on the imp's and Kallrez's translations, plus what Jace had asked about that morning, the content of this report was roughly this:

"An ominous, unsettling mountain, a place where souls moan, traces of elves found, encountered foes wielding magic, trapped in a maze, blocked by a great dark-green wall, already very close to the weapon hidden by the mage — can feel it."

Blackrock Mountain also seemed to fit the description. The powerless moaning of souls — could that refer to the countless Dark Iron dwarves who'd died summoning Ragnaros? But this place faced the same problem: before the Firelord was summoned, Blackrock Mountain had just been a part of the Redridge Mountains, a region not much different from the Elwynn area. Plus it sat midway between Ironforge and Stormwind. Would Guardian Scavell really have hidden such a weapon in such a livable place?

Or had someone recently already found Ulthalesh and brought it to one of these places? No, that wasn't it.

At Feathermoon Stronghold, Shandris Feathermoon had said that Dasmora had disappeared at least a hundred and twenty years ago. Vereesa had also said that the Reliquary had never found any trace of Dasmora in the Eastern Kingdoms.

Jace felt his thinking had been boxed into the Eastern Kingdoms. Could Ulthalesh have been hidden on Kalimdor?

A Guardian's magical power was formidable — using a teleportation spell to send oneself to another continent was nothing unusual. And if he'd wanted to place Ulthalesh as far away from human habitation as possible, then Kalimdor would be perfectly normal.

The moment Kalimdor crossed his mind, Jace suddenly felt like he'd opened up his thinking. So where was that mountain?

Jace suddenly realized — Dasmora was a demon. A mountain she found unsettling and terrifying… could it be sacred, even blessed, to the enemies of demons?

The thought made his heart start pounding.

Because if that were the case, there was practically only one answer: Mount Hyjal.

Exactly. Ten thousand years ago, after defeating the Burning Legion, the night elves and Mount Hyjal had received the blessings of the dragons. The dragons planted the World Tree Nordrassil here, and from then on Mount Hyjal became forbidden ground to all evil. A doomguard fearing this mountain was only natural.

Viewed this way, the elven traces also made sense.

But what about the dark-green wall? Could it be alluding to the endless Ashenvale Forest? Quite possible. The Ashenvale where the night elves lived was like a dark-green maze…

Jace felt this matching might be overly simplistic, but Dasmora hadn't mentioned any proper nouns in the report — nothing like Hyjal or Ashenvale… He didn't know why she'd written so cryptically. Perhaps it was for the same reason she'd chosen to record things in the Ered'ruin language. For now, there was no way to verify it.

So where was the place where ghosts moaned in low voices?

The land of ghosts beneath Mount Hyjal, moaning feebly — Jace could only think of one place: Darkwhisper Gorge.

In the game, Darkwhisper Gorge was a place teeming with high-level demons. More than a decade from now, after the Burning Legion's invasion of Mount Hyjal failed, many demons would hide in this remote corner, waiting for their chance to strike.

But did Darkwhisper Gorge have this name before the demons arrived, or did it become the "Darkwhisper Gorge" precisely because demons lurked within?

Actually, it wouldn't be hard to figure this out — one could just consult some records from Quel'Thalas. The high elves had been traveling to the Mount Hyjal region for many years searching for artifacts lost by the Highborne, so they should have some knowledge of the geography around Mount Hyjal.

Even if they didn't know, the goblins of Everlook should know — after all, Everlook was in Winterspring, just north of Darkwhisper Gorge. A trip to Booty Bay to ask Mithershield should yield results.

As long as there was already a Darkwhisper Gorge there now, it would basically confirm his guess — because all the clues basically had corresponding matches, and they also largely fit Dasmora's previous movements.

So what was the green maze?

The answer might just be hidden in that very gorge.
