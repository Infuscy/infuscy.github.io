## Chapter 761: Dark Iron Traitors

The Thorium Brotherhood — of course Jace knew about the Thorium Brotherhood!

The most high-level blacksmithing recipes of the level-60 era, even the forging of the orange weapons, were all tied to the Thorium Brotherhood. No crafting-skip player could get around these Dark Iron dwarves.

Still, they weren't much of a power — just a small band of Dark Iron defectors. So despite their masterful craftsmanship and wealth of unique knowledge and techniques, they were chronically short-handed and still needed outside mercenaries to help them source materials and solve problems.

In the game, this played out as the need to donate mountains of materials to grind their reputation…

The name of this faction seemed to yank Jace straight back to his past life. He stared blankly for a moment, glancing out the tavern door at the little Trade District square, until a rough dwarven palm waved in front of his face.

"Hey, I'm talking to you, Jace — what came over you? Don't tell me you've heard of these folks?"

"I've heard the name." Jace snapped back to his senses, blinked a few times, and said offhandedly, "I thought they were Bronzebeard dwarves. So they're Dark Iron. But aren't they a bunch of smiths who specialize in forging? Why would they know anything about fire elementals?"

Grede speared a piece of dried wolf meat onto Jace's plate and said, "A smith who can't commune with fire will never become a great smith — and I'm a Wildhammer, I know that much. And the Dark Iron dwarves' obsession with fire makes the Bronzebeard metal-lovers look tame by comparison. I have no doubt the Thorium Brotherhood has some mighty powerful fire sages. Frankly, without a fire elemental envoy worth their salt, they couldn't produce anything worth talking about."

He cleared his throat and continued, "All that said — the Thorium Brotherhood… they're a strange bunch of Dark Iron who broke away from Shadowforge City because they refused to swear allegiance to Emperor Thaurissan. But they're Dark Iron to the bone. Jace, you asked, so I'll tell you — but my personal advice is I don't recommend dealing with them."

"I've heard of some Dark Iron dwarves. A lot of them escaped from Shadowforge City. They all claim they don't serve Shadowforge anymore and they refuse to follow those lunatics worshipping lava and flame — but they're still moody, scheming, and paranoid… that grating, sinister disposition might as well be tattooed on their black skin. It's nothing like the ancient Dark Iron clan at all — I'd half wonder whether their skin wasn't cursed by fire, but by shadow."

"Then let's leave it for now," Jace said. "My mentor only just reminded me about the fire elementals. If I decide to make contact with the Thorium Brotherhood, I'll let you know."

"Don't tell me," Grede waved a hand. "I've got nothing to say to those Dark Iron dwarves. Go find them yourselves — you're all so fond of dark magic, you'll have plenty to talk about."

The dwarf took a swig of ale, bit into a blood sausage, and muttered under his breath, "I'll say this — the Gilded Rose's blood sausage seems to have improved. They're actually trying for the Loch Modan style. I knew the people of Stormwind would eventually get tired of these so-called 'improved-for-local-taste' fakes and crave the real original flavor… It's still a bit off, but at least it's a breakthrough. Don't you think, Jace?"

"Yeah," Jace nodded.

The next day, Jace planned to head to Goldshire to find Erland Faolble about renovating the mage tower. Before that, of course, he needed to go to Andormas first to pick up the deed and other documents for Darrowshire Tower.

When he arrived at the door, the Wizard Sanctum's mage tower guards didn't even check his ring before letting him through. Walking among the mages in their blue and white robes already felt a bit like a graduate returning to visit his old school — even though his highest title was apprentice mage, not even close to a full-fledged mage.

Still, he wouldn't have minded having a proper mage's sigil. Of course, at this point he was too embarrassed to go ask Andormas for one.

He spent the walk observing the Sanctum's interior décor and style, wondering if he could use it as a reference for his own little tower.

He stared at the ancient runes and magic circles, trying to figure out whether they served a specific function here or were purely decorative. Naturally, given his academic record, this kind of analysis was mostly self-indulgent nonsense — only the anti-demon wards were obvious enough for him to spot.

He climbed to the top floor and entered the spiral staircase once more, glancing up at the portraits of famous mages. His gaze lingered a moment longer on the strikingly youthful, handsome face of Nielas Aran — though to be fair, the man's age in this painting could hardly be called "young." He just looked young compared to all those other white-bearded old men.

Then he noticed there was a portrait behind Aran's.

He didn't remember this painting from his last visit. Had it been hung up recently?

Jace looked up at the newcomer. He appeared even younger than Nielas Aran, with a gaunt face and soft features that made him look almost like a child from this angle. A messy head of untrimmed brown hair hung down over his ears, and he lifted his chin slightly, staring out of the canvas with an expressionless face.

Wait — was that supposed to be him?

Even as the thought crossed his mind, the man in the painting seemed a bit more handsome than him, a lot more confident too — and that bright purple robe…

He was fairly sure he'd never worn anything like that.

But the hair…

Then he saw the name rendered in archaic Common script beneath the painting — Jace Sesso.

Jace glanced down the corridor. No one passing by seemed to be looking this way. Everyone who came here was on business, hurrying past with no attention to spare for anyone else. Jace lowered his head and walked quickly toward the main hall.

In the main hall, Andormas didn't seem to be in. His assistant was tidying the desk.

"Sir," Jace said, approaching. "When will Master Andormas be available?"

The assistant looked up and said, "Master Sesso… you're here at last. Master Andormas instructed me that when you arrived, I was to help you complete the signing for Darrowshire Tower. He's away on business in Dalaran. Is that what you've come for?"

Off to Dalaran — probably to help the Kirin Tor deal with Kel'Thuzad.

Jace nodded. The assistant hauled an old leather case out from under the shelf, spoke a spell to undo the seal on it, and carefully drew out a thick scroll, spreading it across the desk.

Jace looked at the dense list of clauses, the wax seals of House Wrynn and the Wizard Sanctum hanging from it. He took the assistant's pen and signed his name at the very bottom.

"Just the imprint of your Court Mage sigil left to affix, Master," the assistant said with a smile. "Darrowshire Tower is yours now."
