## Chapter 762: Trip to Goldshire

At noon, Jace rode Alana into Goldshire. A young man in a long robe, wearing several rings and riding a handsome mare, was already enough to draw no small amount of attention entering this town.

He left Alana in the care of The Lion's Pride Inn and crossed the street to Erland's residence. When he reached the door, he could already clearly smell the cloying, greasy fragrance of a succubus.

He knocked and called out, "Is Mr. Erland Faolble at home?"

A clear clatter and clatter came from inside, followed by hushed voices. Not long after, the door was flung open with a bang, and the scent of demon spilled out — not just the succubus, but a whiff of fel stench too.

"Jace — I recognized your voice," Erland said, glancing toward the end of the hallway.

"It's way too obvious, Erland," Jace said. "Can't you get your succubus to tone it down a bit? Aren't you afraid of being arrested by the guards?"

"Goldshire's guards aren't all as sensitive to demons as you are… Jace." Erland gave a provocative smile. "Want to come in and join the fun?"

"I didn't come here to have fun."

"Then come in and sit for a bit."

Jace nodded and stepped inside. Erland walked into the back room, where his succubus sat naked on the bed, swinging her legs and staring at him.

"Jace Sesso — I still haven't forgotten you," she said softly.

"I'd forgotten your name," Jace said coldly.

"How heartbreaking…"

She seemed a bit shorter and smaller than when Morpheus had first arrived in Azeroth. Even with the small wings growing from her back, her pale-red skin, the mane on her legs, and the hooves all telling anyone she was a genuine demon and not some little girl — the sight of her sitting there bare, her private parts just exposed, still made Jace's skin crawl.

He remembered that two years ago, the spell to summon a succubus was still something most people in the Kingdom of Stormwind had no idea the purpose of. Galders and Jagin, in a state of complete befuddlement, had tested that spell over and over to figure out what it did — and it had ended in a serious accident.

Now, setting aside the Dark Iron dwarves who'd long known how to summon succubi, even humans had quite a few running around with their own succubi. Even a runt goblin warlock like Gaxxek had managed to get his hands on a succubus with shapeshifting abilities.

One could say that warlocks' mastery of dark magic on Azeroth's side had developed at breakneck speed since the Dark Portal. Jace wondered to himself whether he had anything to do with that.

Erland's succubus had one more unusual feature, though: her eyes didn't glow with pure arcane energy, but with an evil green fire.

Though it didn't burn very fiercely, and the brightness was somewhat overpowered by the oil lamp Erland had just lit, Jace could still recognize it — that was fel.

A succubus's power was closely tied to her master, especially one like Erland who was utterly unstinting in "channeling mana" into his succubus. It seemed Galders had been right — Erland had indeed been quite obsessed with fel magic lately.

Erland waved a hand at the succubus as he passed, signaling her to go back inside, and set down two small glasses, filling them. Jace observed him carefully — he still couldn't detect any signs of fel corruption on him.

"Jace Sesso, who's usually impossible to find, suddenly showing up at my door — this can't be nothing. Go on, we can have a drink while we talk," Erland said.

"I remember you said you were a member of the Stonemasons," Jace said.

Erland dropped into his chair, crossed one leg over the other, and said, "If Jagin hadn't died, I'd much rather think of myself as a Stonemasons tourist who came to visit The Slaughtered Lamb — not someone who actually planned to join you all. That arrogant bastard looked down on people like us with no real talent for dark magic. I've no idea where his confidence came from."

"Never mind the dead man. I heard you went to that place, Grim Batol, and rescued the Dragonqueen. Is that true… or did the king cook up that insane string of stories to persuade you to become Court Mage?"

"The real story might be even more insane than what you've heard." Jace took a sip of his drink. "Kul Tiras mead?"

"You can actually tell?" Erland said, surprised.

"Spend enough time with dwarves and there's some knowledge you pick up whether you want to or not," Jace said helplessly.

"Hah — listen, it gets even crazier." Erland rapped a finger on the table. "Some nobles are saying that Alleria Windrunner's younger sister is already with you. Those people see that Marshal Turalyon got to marry an elven noblewoman and they're so starved they're making things up…"

Jace leaned back in his chair and said, "Sylvanas Windrunner is indeed with me."

Erland's smile froze on his face. After a moment of silence he said, "Well… I should be very surprised. The man sitting in front of me is a hero who fought his way out of an orc nest, saved the red dragons, and saved the Alliance too — but honestly, not surprised at all, Jace Sesso. You give off this feeling that anything strange happening to you is perfectly normal."

"Galders was the same," Jace said. "When he heard about it, he didn't react much either — everything carried on as normal. That's actually what I'd hoped for, because that wasn't something I wanted to do either. I didn't want to finish it and become a completely different person, then have everyone around me treat me like a monster."

"You're definitely not a monster," Erland said. "But I'm not like Galdes. Galders acted so nonchalant because that guy always carries himself like that. Even if he met Anduin Wrynn or Medivh himself, he'd probably put on that same 'I look down on everybody' act. Chances are he's dying of admiration and jealousy inside, but he's got to maintain that 'Blackclaw Raven' vibe — you know what I mean?"

Jace couldn't help but laugh. "I know."

"Oh, right." Erland glanced sideways at him. "Could you talk to Lady Sylvanas and ask her to introduce an elven girl to me too? I'd like to experience that…"

"Fuck off," Jace said, his expression cooling. "Go find one in Dalaran yourself."

"Pity. I don't think my charm is enough to attract an elven girl." Erland stroked the stubble on his face. "Look at you — you've got a baby face. Elves generally go for fellows who look like you. But you're different now — you've grown too tall these past two years, Jace Sesso. You're getting further and further from that always-easy-to-bullshit look you used to have. If you went to Dalaran now, you probably wouldn't be able to seduce anyone like Sylvanas Windrunner either."

"Seduce?!" Jace knocked back the drink in his glass in one go. "Don't bring that up. I didn't come here just to drink — I have real business."

"Go on, then. What does my guild want from me?" Erland asked.

"I want you to help me renovate a mage tower," Jace said.

"Darrowshire Tower!" Erland said excitedly. "So that rumor is true, Jace Sesso! You're actually the master of Darrowshire Tower now? You might be the youngest mage-tower master since the founding of the Kingdom of Stormwind. That's a big job — I can't make that decision on my own. You'll have to talk to our boss, Edwin."

"Edwin VanCleef?" Jace asked.

Edwin VanCleef — in the game, he had once been the greatest enemy of the Kingdom of Stormwind. Compared to the orcs on the border, the troll empire of Zul'Gurub to the south, and the gnoll tribes roaming the forests and riverbanks, the threat he and his bandit guild posed was far more immediate and clear.

After the Stonemasons' falling out with Stormwind over pay, VanCleef led the guild's members out of Stormwind and founded the Defias Brotherhood, dealing a heavy blow to the rapidly developing post-war nation.

In the war between the Kingdom of Stormwind and the Defias Brotherhood, the entire western region had been turned into a wilderness no one dared approach. People fled in droves, vast tracts of farmland lay abandoned, reclaimed by wild boars and gnolls. Moonbrook had become a deserted ruin, and the mine that had once brought prosperity to Moonbrook became the Defias Brotherhood's nest.

"That's him." Erland glanced at Jace's expression and said, "What, you doubt the Stonemasons' craftsmanship? Let me be frank with you — Nethergarde Keep and the Nethergarde mage tower were our guild's work. That tower held up against the charge of a demon lord… Of course, without you it might have actually fallen, but you've seen that tower. You know its quality — you should have no illusions about what level that represents. Even the Kirin Tor was willing to hire us for that kind of project, so don't go questioning anything. As an aside, the post-battle repairs on that tower were done by our people too."

"I'm not questioning anything — otherwise I wouldn't have come to you," Jace said. "Have you been to that tower? Can you help me estimate the cost?"

"No." Erland shook his head. "But I heard that tower was occupied by orcs and gnolls for a long time, and fought over again and again by the kingdom — the fact it's still standing after all that is no small feat. Anyway, the cost for Dalaran to repair the Nethergarde mage tower was over six hundred gold. They've already paid three hundred and fifty…"

"Six hundred?!" Jace nearly shouted.

"Why are you yelling?" Erland started, then scratched his ear before saying, "I didn't say repairing your tower would cost the same. That six hundred gold included the danger of working while wading in that demon lord's corrosive blood. The mages were actually reasonable — they understood how damaging that blood was to our health."

"Has the back pay for rebuilding Stormwind still not been settled?" Jace asked casually.

"Not yet," Erland said. "But at least they've been paying steadily. I heard a batch of the brothers finally got theirs settled — the ones down at the harbor. At this rate, it'll probably take a few more years… Considering the young king's current finances, maybe longer. It's not bad now, Jace. Two years ago there was a stretch where the king genuinely couldn't produce a single copper, and Alliance funding couldn't keep up because of the internment camps up north — that was real chaos. I have to say, the Nethergarde project helped ease our difficulties for a while."

In fact, judging by how much livelier Stormwind and Goldshire had gotten over the past two years, the kingdom's financial situation was indeed improving bit by bit. If Onyxia hadn't suddenly appeared later to throw everything into disarray, maybe the relationship between the kingdom and the Stonemasons could have ridden out the storm.

Erland continued, "When it comes down to it, the reconstruction cost for Darrowshire Tower depends on your needs, Jace. So you should still go talk to Edwin and let him give you the details. I can help set up a meeting or something."

"Then I'll leave it to you," Jace said.

When he was riding back to Stormwind that afternoon, a man on horseback caught up to Alana on the road.

"Master Sesso." The man slowed his mount to match pace and said, "Denton Het, SI:7 agent. I've been looking for you these past two days."

"SI:7?" Jace sized the man up. Black hair, nothing remarkable about his face, wearing a brown coat — he looked no different from an ordinary hunter.

Denton Het — Jace had never heard that name either. He asked cautiously, "What do you want with me?"

"The king's secret orders," Denton Het said. "His Majesty says he's already spoken with you and hopes you can assist us in investigating the movements of the black dragons. Dwarves Preston's magic could also endanger Stormwind."

"Already?" Jace said. "I thought this investigation hadn't started yet."

"We already have some leads," Denton said. "Would Master Sesso have some time now? I can take you to SI:7 to get familiar with the location — and finish the task Lady Shaw assigned me, since I've been putting it off for a while…"

"I'm sorry, I genuinely didn't know you were looking for me," Jace said with a smile. "Of course."

By the time they got back to Stormwind, the sky had darkened. The man who claimed to be an SI:7 agent led Jace to the Stormwind barracks — this was indeed the location of SI:7's base in the game, so Jace let his guard down bit by bit.

They passed through the barracks to the back, to a villa sheltered by trees against the wall. Denton Het led the way while pointing out the directions. I had to say the place was really hidden — following him at a brisk pace there, Jace felt a bit dizzy.

The villa itself wasn't anything special, much like the standalone townhouses outside the Park belonging to the nobility. An old dog lay at the entrance, and there weren't even any guards.

After entering, there was a spacious hall with carpets on the floor and some decorative paintings on the walls. Beyond that, the overall style was quite plain.

But Jace quickly noticed one of the large hanging paintings — this wasn't an ordinary painting. It was a map of Azeroth.

Unlike the vague map Jace had seen at the harbor before, this one rendered Kalimdo quite completely, already very close to the game — especially northern Kalimdor, with Mount Hyjal, Winterspring, and Ashenvale all clearly marked…

"I heard you've been to Kalimdor, Master Sesso?" Denton saw Jace's interest in the painting and asked.

"I was there on elven business," Jace nodded. Then he noticed a forest south of Winterspring that wasn't painted very clearly and asked, "What's this place?"

"A ravine at the foot of Mount Hyjal," Denton explained. "It doesn't have a proper name, Master. Perhaps the elves know more about it, but ghostly whispers are often heard from that forest. That's not some local legend — it's a phenomenon our agents have recorded. So we've marked it as 'Whisper' and warned other agents not to approach it during operations. It's an unsettling place."

No wonder Sylvanas didn't know about it. That was actually a Common-language name!
