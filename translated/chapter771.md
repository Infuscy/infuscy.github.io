## Chapter 771: Time to Start Saving Again

"Ailumenar, Mar, Alan." Jace murmured the Thalassian blessing Vereesa had written beneath her signature.

The breeze off Lake Everstill had turned warm. He sat on the railing of the great bridge, letter in hand, gazing across the water toward Stonewatch perched on the far mountain. The sky was clear — not a wisp of mist on the lake. On an overcast day, you wouldn't make out the fort's silhouette at all. Today was the day he was supposed to meet Edwin VanCleef, leader of the Stonemasons, arranged through Erland's connections.

A few days earlier, Jace had stopped by the Altar of Storms to confirm that Karlez was still stable and that the warlocks were still guarding him. After that he'd come to Lakeshire to wait for the meeting with VanCleef.

Jace pulled out his pocket watch and glanced at it. Not yet time.

A gust swept across the lake. He tightened his grip on Vereesa's reply — and on the purple-and-gold flower tucked inside the letter — before the wind could whip them away.

When the breeze died down, he unfolded the letter again.

After learning about Shadowtongue Canyon from SI:7, he'd written to Quel'Thalas immediately, sending the letter with an elven mage from the Wizard Sanctum. He hadn't named the place outright — just told her the Kalimdor expedition was confirmed and that he'd found solid leads.

The "Sorrow" was its Thalassian name. In Common, the flower had a far more blunt, well-known name: Plaguebloom.

Before the Scourge destroyed Lordaeron, the flower was rare. It grew from necrotic plague — a terrible bloom, even more extreme than Grave Moss. No surprise you only ever found it around the cursed burial pits of the trolls.

He knew what Vereesa meant by sending it. She wanted him to be like this flower: born from darkness, standing against it.

At the same time, he could sense that she carried a quiet worry — a fear that the darkness might eventually consume him, or simply a fear of losing him.

The flower was probably a kind of talisman, too.

All Jace could do was try his damnedest not to betray her trust — not to betray anyone's trust in him. He stood and looked south along the bridge. A young man was approaching.

He was under thirty, about Mathias Shaw's age, powerfully built. Plain white shirt, loose gray linen trousers, a shock of black hair. His skin was reddened from long hours working outdoors.

Despite the look of a poor tradesman scraping by, the man carried a strange air — a touch of pride, as though he'd had a proper education. He had the look of a fallen noble.

The man noticed Jace's gaze as he approached. He glanced at the ring on Jace's hand, stepped forward, and extended his own.

"Edwin VanCleef. You must be Master Jace Sesso? A pleasure to finally meet you."

Jace shook his hand, feeling a grip like stone. "That's me, VanCleef. Erland briefed you on my situation?"

"Il'gathar Tower." Edwin nodded, looking out across the lake. "One of the four mage towers in the Kingdom of Stormwind. A distinguished structure. We'd be honored to restore it."

In the game, VanCleef had always worn that red Defias mask. His face was never shown.

So Jace couldn't help stealing another look. Honestly, his appearance was a little disappointing — especially compared to the other legendary figures, like Varian Wrynn or Arthas.

Then again, comparing anyone to Arthas — a man whose looks could make the young women of all Seven Kingdoms swoon — was deeply unfair. Among ordinary folks, VanCleef's lean, wiry features were passably handsome. He had the look of a ruthless fighter.

Maybe it was just the bias of someone who already knew the story.

"It's getting late, Master Sesso," Edwin said. "Even on horseback, if we don't leave now, we might not reach Stonewatch before dark."

"Then let's not waste time," Jace said.

VanCleef was a blunt, no-nonsense man. Jace had expected to at least buy him a meal at the Lakeshire inn, but the man clearly couldn't care less about any of that. He just wanted to see the tower.

The two rode hard toward Stonewatch. VanCleef barely said a word the whole way. He ate in the saddle. Occasionally he'd catch Jace looking at him and give a polite nod.

They didn't stop at Stonewatch. They followed the forest road straight to Il'gathar Tower, arriving just before full dark.

Both horses were blown. Jace tied them to the hitching post below the tower, glanced up, and remembered — the two felhounds were still patrolling inside. He needed to hide them before Edwin went in.

"Hold on," Jace said. "Let me check inside first. The gnolls held this tower for a while — there might still be traps and magic circles left behind. I haven't been back in some time. I need to make sure it's safe."

VanCleef nodded and waited. Jace stepped inside, looked up toward the broken windows where dim yellow light filtered through drifting dust motes, and let out a long breath.

Clicking felhound claws on wood echoed from above. Jace barked in Eredun: "Hide! Don't let anyone find you — go as deep as you can!"

The rapid clicking retreated upward, quickly fading to silence. Jace stepped back out. "We're good, VanCleef. Should be fine."

Edwin nodded and followed him in, glancing around as he climbed the stairs.

Jace laid out his requirements as they went: clear out the ground floor, build ladders into the side walls, reinforce the upper levels, make the stairs and walls somewhat magic-resistant, and — crucially — preserve the tower's existing arcane conduits perfectly.

VanCleef seemed to have anticipated most of Jace's requests. He didn't look surprised once. Then again, that was to be expected — the Stonemasons had built mage towers for the Kirin Tor. Jace's demands were perfectly reasonable by comparison.

On the top floor, Edwin inspected the damage, then looked up at the small arcane crystal set into the tower's crown.

"Stormwind's reconstruction is entering its final phase," he said. "I should be able to bring in plenty of experienced craftsmen for this job. To restore this tower to your exact specifications, Master Sesso… it will run you between two hundred and two hundred and fifty gold."

Hearing that number, Jace kept his expression steady so he wouldn't buckle at the knees in front of the leader of the Stonemasons.

It was cheaper than Nethergarde Keep — that tower had been blown through by a pit lord, after all — but it was still a staggering sum for him.

His savings were under sixty gold at this point. Five of those had already gone to the dwarven craftsmen renovating the house by the canal. Another chunk had to be set aside for travel expenses to Kalimdor.

He wondered if the Reliquary task Vereesa had taken came with a reward. Like last time in Dathmor — that had paid eighty gold all at once.

"Let me… think about it," Jace said.
