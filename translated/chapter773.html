Chapter 773: Not Like a First-Timer

Jace had never set foot in this part of the Dwarf District. To ordinary Stormwind citizens, it was practically a little Ironforge — a dwarf district within the Dwarf District. Not just him: most humans wouldn't dare come here at all, because many of the dwarves in this neighborhood barely spoke Common and lived by their own set of rules.

Standing at the intersection, he looked toward a wide opening carved into the rock, piled high with supply crates and building materials, where teams of dwarves and gnomes streamed in and out. Even without a sign, Jace could tell that was the tram entrance.

Aside from a few Stormwind guards in blue coats loitering outside, Jace was the only human here. He edged toward the entrance, craning his neck to figure out where to go or who to ask, when a childlike voice piped up behind him.

"The tram isn't open to visitors yet, sir!"

Jace turned. A female gnome, sweating profusely, was hauling a pair of iron tongs comically too large for her frame. "If you want to go to Ironforge, you'll have to take a boat or find a gryphon!"

"I heard that people on important missions or duties can ride the freight cars to Ironforge," Jace said.

"You're clearly not the first person to hear that," the gnome sighed. "So what's your important mission? Go talk to the senior supervisor, Mr. Elbig, over there."

Jace looked where she pointed. A cluster of gnomes was deep in conversation. Which one was Supervisor Elbig? He couldn't tell them apart at all.

But when he turned back, the female gnome had already vanished.

Nothing for it. He steeled himself and walked up to the group, clearing his throat. "Hello, gentlemen. I'm looking for Senior Supervisor Elbig. Is he here?"

"That's me." A gnome with a pale green beard stepped forward, gave Jace a once-over, and demanded irritably, "What do you want? Who sent you here to make trouble — the Church, or the wizard tower?"

"I'm a court mage advisor to King Varian." Jace held out his hand to show the signet. "This is my seal. I need to travel to Ironforge for a secret investigation…"

Strictly speaking, it wasn't even a lie.

The Dark Iron dwarves very likely held the key to the black dragons' secrets — or at least a thread of it. Jace going to meet a group of Dark Iron dwarves who were relatively friendly to the Alliance was well within the scope of the king's secret directive to investigate the black dragons, and part of his duties as a dark magic advisor.

"The king?" the supervisor murmured, turning the seal over. "Jace Sesso… that name sounds familiar."

"Grim Batol…" another gnome said. "He's the human mage who destroyed Grim Batol!"

"What?!"

"Jace Sesso — he's Jace Sesso!"

"You're Jace Sesso?!" The senior supervisor lifted his screwdriver and pointed it at Jace, eyes wide. "Stay away from the tram, you magical bomb! Get out of here! Don't even think about casting anything — did the Trade Cartel pay you to come sabotage this place?!"

Jace instinctively raised his hands. "I don't have the power to damage this tram, sir…"

At that moment, a crisp, clanging metallic noise rang out from the great tunnel mouth nearby, followed by a deep rumbling — like an old-fashioned train starting up, iron wheels turning, steam hissing — echoing up from somewhere impossibly deep, drowning out Jace's words.

That metal tunnel, those churning machinery sounds… they tugged his consciousness slightly out of this ancient, fantastical world, giving him the strange, fleeting illusion that he'd returned to his past life.

The gnome glanced in the direction Jace was staring, then looked back. "Isn't it a magnificent marvel, mage?" Jace nodded.

"I can see you're someone who appreciates engineering masterworks," the gnome supervisor said. "My apologies. I've always felt Stormwind shouldn't have let the goblins anywhere near trade — even just around the harbor. Those wretched green-skinned runts are jealous of our technology and would love to blow this whole place to the sky. And then, hearing your Grim Batol story… anyway, I don't want to hear any words connected to explosions right now — things like gunpowder, cannonballs, rockets, dragons, mages, goblins. They make me nervous!"

"But you really are Jace Sesso?"

"That's me," Jace said.

"A pleasure to meet you here, Sesso." Supervisor Elbig stepped forward and offered his hand. "Since it's a royal directive, your seal checks out, and I've got no reason to stop a hero from going where he needs to go — follow me."

The diminutive Elbig led the way with quick, busy little steps. Jace followed him slowly into the deep tram tunnel.

A frigid passageway under the earth, a cold metal floor, harsh lighting that stung the eyes, the shouts of dwarves and gnomes mixing with the rumble of machinery. Elbig talked nonstop as he went about how the tram had been built, and with his odd Common accent, Jace couldn't follow half of it.

According to Elbig, the tram wasn't a straight north–south line like the in-game model. For safety, it curved around Blackrock Mountain to the west, and a section of it ran beneath the floor of Baradin Bay.

In the game, the tram passes through a long aquarium-like stretch — look up through the giant glass ceiling and you'd see a rich underwater world, complete with a serpentine leviathan of boss-level strength drifting around.

Jace had always assumed that was the bottom of the Loch in Loch Modan.

The tram hall looked the same as in the game — a vast, factory-like underground chamber. Metal pillars held up the ceiling, two sets of tram rails ran through the middle, and benches were set out nearby for waiting passengers.

A tram was docked there at the moment. Calling it a "carriage" was generous — it was really just a metal platform fitted with a few support bars and protective railings. Dwarves and gnomes were busy loading cargo; it looked ready to depart soon.

"You'll ride this one to Ironforge, Jace Sesso." The supervisor pointed to one of the platforms, glanced at his pocket watch, and said, "Get on now — it leaves in less than half an hour. Miss it and you'll have to wait until evening!"

"Understood." Jace walked over and hopped onto the platform. He eyed the gap between the platform and the train, and had the distinct feeling someone was going to fall in and get their waist — or their legs — snapped in two.

He gripped a metal pillar and checked the distance to the nearby secured cargo crates. The supervisor squinted at him. "Feels like this isn't your first time riding our tram, Mr. Sesso."

"It really is my first time," Jace said with an awkward smile, glancing down into the pit beneath the platform. "I'm just a little worried about falling."
