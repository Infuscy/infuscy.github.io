## Chapter 774: Through the Depths

The tram was loaded with cargo crates. The lights overhead flickered, and a bell clanged — from somewhere, no one could tell.

The dwarven laborers backed away from the rails. Supervisor Elbig stepped back too. "A pleasant journey to Ironforge, Jace Sesso."

Jace nodded. The platform beneath his feet jolted, as though it had unhooked from something. A rapid series of clinks and clangs rang out from above, and a warning light that had been showing red turned white.

Elbig's gaze dropped for a moment, then rose again. He made a farewell gesture. Jace barely caught a glimpse of him before the platform beneath him lurched — as if something had snatched it forward, or flung it — and with a whoosh, it rocketed ahead.

Jace's vision went dark. It felt like his soul had been left behind, too far to chase, his consciousness scattered somewhere he couldn't place. All he could do, dazed, was cling to the tram's railing — one hand became two, two hands became a desperate bear hug.

The wind slammed into the back of his head and scoured his ears. His only thought at that moment was: why the hell hadn't these gnomes fitted a windshield to the front? Or just a solid metal plate to block it all off?!

It wasn't as if anyone needed to drive the thing!

The deafening roar of the tram grinding against the rails filled the air. It was nearly impossible to open his eyes and make out anything in the surroundings, but Jace was certain the speed was insane. Thank god his luggage was strapped to his belt and wouldn't come loose — though at the moment his packs, sword, and dagger were all whipping back and forth in the gale hard enough to drag his trousers down.

The intense vibration soon numbed Jace's legs and made it hard to stay upright, until a strong hand grabbed him from behind and hauled him behind a stack of crates. It was a dwarf.

This dwarf and two other gnomes lashed Jace firmly to the cargo with ropes. They were probably the cargo escorts. At some point they'd all put on goggles and heavy coats, dressed like a crew of pilots.

Given how terrifyingly fast this tram hurtled along, the outfit was perfectly reasonable.

If Jace didn't have the blessings of the red dragon's essence bolstering him, he'd probably have been blown clean off by now. Was that the reason the tram wasn't open to ordinary passengers yet?

He was in no state to think about it. One of the gnomes beside him handed Jace a pair of goggles, and he fitted them on. Everyone fell silent — there was no point talking, not when no words could possibly rise above the thunder of the tram on the rails.

Sometime later, after several successive jolts, the tram seemed to shift from a long descent into a steadier cruise. The roar became more even, and the punishing wind eased.

If it was going to be like this all the way to Ironforge… Jace didn't know whether he could take it. On the earlier stretch, even breathing had been a struggle.

He checked his luggage to make sure nothing had been lost in the chaos, then settled in between the cargo crates.

With the goggles on, he could finally take in the view — not that there was much to see. White lights streaking past one after another, an endless dark tunnel, shadow-everything blurring away behind him… and no sign of the aquarium-like underwater world he'd been hoping for.

Once the vibration and noise had numbed the passengers into a stupor, the monotony of the view made eyelids heavy. Still, Jace forced himself to stay as alert as he could.

He'd never been to Ironforge, and he didn't know whether the dwarves there had any special means of detecting demons, so he hadn't brought his imp. The succubus wasn't with him either — just to avoid trouble.

There were other people in the carriage, and he had far too many treasures on him that he couldn't afford to lose. He couldn't just drift off to sleep. The tram ran into the second night, and he held out the whole time without sleeping.

It wasn't the first time he'd forced himself to stay awake, but keeping alert on a vehicle this tedious and terrifying was orders of magnitude harder than in the novel, stimulating depths of Grim Batol.

Just when Jace felt his consciousness starting to crack, the speed seemed to ease.

The lights around him had turned yellow at some point, flickering and swaying. The cargo escorts stood up and began gathering their things.

Were they finally reaching Ironforge?

What a torturous journey…

The tram rolled into another great hall and ground to a halt with a screech of metal on metal, stopping dead as though something had seized it.

Jace felt a jolt run through him. The world went deathly quiet. The footsteps of people entering, the voices of dwarves and gnomes talking — everything sounded muffled and distant.

He made a silent resolution: he would never, ever ride this thing again.

Stepping up to the tram doors, Jace peered out into the tunnel. Bronzebeard dwarves were approaching from the other end — they looked no different from the dwarves in Stormwind's Dwarf District.

But outside… this was Ironforge.

Actually, for most early Alliance players, Ironforge was the more familiar true "capital city" — more so than Stormwind.

Because of its geography, the early placement of the Auction House, and other factors, Alliance players had clustered in Ironforge first, treating it as the true heart of the whole Alliance.

So in his past life, as a double agent, Jace had had a special attachment to this place.

Following the dwarves unloading cargo, he walked toward the outer gates and into an underground hall. The sight of it reminded him of the chamber in Grim Batol where the dragon eggs had been kept — no dragon eggs here, but the walls, the broad central hall, the structures on either side, were all strikingly similar.

He remembered that in the game, just outside the Ironforge tram entrance was Tinkertown, the gnome quarter — gears everywhere, metal walls, massive machinery — a completely different aesthetic from the space before him.

There were plenty of gnomes here too, but most of them seemed to be tram employees, not actual residents.

Jace recalled that it was only after Gnomeregan was destroyed by the troggs that the gnomes fled to Ironforge and transformed this area into a gnome quarter, much like the Dwarf District inside Stormwind.

Following the crowd, he turned into a narrow passage. The smell of iron rust faded bit by bit, and the scent of coke grew stronger and stronger. If Jace had caught that smell in Stormwind, his first thought would have been that something was on fire. But this was Ironforge!
