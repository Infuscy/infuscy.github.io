Chapter 775: Ironforge

He hadn't even left the passage yet when the noise hit him from every direction — the buzz of conversation, laughter… the clang of hammers on anvils, even the churning of lava echoing from somewhere deep and rolling closer.

Stepping out of the tunnel, across the dark street stood a towering stone wall carved with all manner of doorways and windows. Many of the doorways hung signs, most of them bearing the mark of an anvil or a hammer and chisel. Dwarves argued and chatted outside no end of them.

He looked up. Ancient stone arches, a dozen meters high, supported the cavern ceiling, carved with dense, enormous Dwarven runes — plain yet solemn.

Hanging above was the Bronzebeard banner — a gold-and-red-crested warhammer on a white field — and beside it, the white Lordaeron royal standard, representing Ironforge's support for the Alliance and the close bond between humans and dwarves. But seeing the Lordaeron emblem he knew so well displayed here gave Jace a faint sense of disharmony.

Like Grim Batol, Ironforge's streets were smooth and broad — but they were nothing like the emptiness of Grim Batol. After all, the old Wildhammer capital had been abandoned, and for all the Dragonmaw orcs' numbers, they couldn't restore the pulse of life to that dead city.

Here in Ironforge, countless dwarves came and went hauling all manner of goods. Jace dared only walk along the edge of the street, observing as he went, worried he might accidentally brush against something, bump into something, or knock over someone's precious property.

Because he noticed that some of the goods even radiated magical energy. They were all covered with cloth so outsiders couldn't see what was inside, but Jace could guess — probably some kind of magic-infused mineral or crystal.

It was getting late. Jace hadn't slept the whole way here, so he was thinking about finding somewhere to rest — an inn, something like that.

But he wandered the streets for a good while, his ears nearly ringing from the constant flood of Dwarvish babble, and still couldn't find a single sign that had anything to do with a bed or sleeping.

He'd figured that in a bustling city like this, with shops and markets everywhere, finding a place to stay would be easy. He hadn't expected to wander himself dizzy without any luck. Ironforge seemed to be a ring-shaped colossal city, much like Grim Batol — after walking a while, he felt as though he'd looped back to where he started. But his instincts told him Ironforge's scale couldn't possibly be small enough to circle in that little time… so he'd better find a guard and ask for directions.

In the game, Stormwind's soldiers were decked out in silver armor from head to toe, but here on Stormwind's streets, Jace had rarely seen guards dressed so intimidatingly — only the royal guards inside Stormwind Keep came close.

But Ironforge was a different story.

In the game, Ironforge guards wore heavy, stone-like plate, carrying massive square shields and battle axes as they patrolled the streets. Here, with the level of real-world detail turned all the way up, Jace felt like they were even more ridiculously equipped than in the game.

Layer upon layer of gray-white pauldrons, trimmed in gold and red, sat on their already massive shoulders and arms. On their heads sat helmets that looked like giant boulders sprouting two bull horns. From the gaps in the helmet poured great cascades of golden and brown beard. Two small eyes darted about in the narrow space between beard and helmet rim.

And beneath those enormous helmets and pauldrons, the chest guards and leg guards were no less extravagant — thick steel plates, iron rings, and chains, clanking and clattering with every step, like walking mechanical golems.

Jace had glimpsed Ironforge guards like this from a distance before the Dark Portal, but they'd been far away on the front line, crashing headfirst into a sea of pitch-black felguard. Jace's battle mages had been on the flanks, and those savage dwarven axemen hadn't been armored nearly so heavily, so he'd never had the chance to see them up close.

It was genuinely hard to imagine the scale of smithing and mining required to arm every guard in the city like this. Probably the hammering and forge-fire that filled the entire city, the rumble of magma rolling deep below — that was the confidence behind it all.

Those fully armored guards marched in formation at a steady cadence, boots striking the solid ground in lockstep, occasionally shouting in unison in Dwarvish. Jace glanced at them and decided he'd better not step in front of them to ask for directions.

He looked toward the street and spotted guards standing posted on the sidewalk from time to time, watching the road. He found the nearest one, stepped up, and cleared his throat, offering a careful greeting in Dwarvish: "Good evening, sir."

The guard looked up at him and unleashed a long string of incomprehensible Dwarvish. Jace coughed awkwardly and said in Common, "Sorry, I don't understand Dwarvish. I've come from Stormwind."

The dwarf guard's eyes were half-hidden behind his helmet rim. Jace couldn't even tell if the dwarf was looking at him. But after a moment, the dwarf said, in Common: "Human. Question. Speak."

Jace more or less caught his meaning, and pronounced the word in clear, standard Common: "Inn."

"Ah… inn." The dwarf repeated the Common word in an enlightened tone, then set his axe and shield down on the ground while rattling on in Dwarvish. He extended both gauntleted hands — one showing five fingers, the other showing seven.

"Seven? Or twenty-five?" Jace frowned. He had no idea what the guard was trying to convey.

Seeing Jace's blank look, the dwarf patted the stone stair railing beside him, held up five fingers, then strutted up a few steps of the staircase. He turned back, held up seven fingers on both hands, and pointed at the floor.

Jace looked up — and seemed to understand all at once.

Grede had told him Ironforge had many levels, but the game's stereotypical impression had always made him feel like all of Ironforge's districts were on a single floor…

So was this dwarf guard saying they were on the fifth level, and the inn was on the seventh? "The Little Ring," the guard suddenly said in Common.

"What?" Jace looked down. The guard was pointing at the ring on his hand. "I look at little ring."

He wanted to inspect Jace's ring. Jace held his hand out. The guard studied it, then looked up sharply. "Jace Sesso… Grim Batol?"

"That's me."

"Jace Sesso!" the guard repeated, then launched into a long torrent of Dwarvish that Jace couldn't understand at all, drawing the attention of dwarves nearby.

Jace was deeply convinced that he'd learned a ridiculous number of languages — he even knew a bit of Eredun, a tongue that barely a handful of beings in the whole Twisting Nether could speak. And yet the languages of the Alliance's own allies — whether elvish or Dwarvish — he was next to illiterate in all of them. Wasn't that a bit much?
