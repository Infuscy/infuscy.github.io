Chapter 796: Cold Danger

"How are we getting to Booty Bay?"

A stew with a faint smell of smoked meat was set on the table, and Grede snatched the spoon to serve himself a bowl without hesitation. He picked up Jace's bowl as he asked.

Jace, whose attention had already been drawn to the fragrant spotted sunfish on the table, had it pulled back by the question.

He'd originally planned to go by ship, but since Grede was right here, he figured he might as well ask: "Any chance we could ride a gryphon there? Gorjet?"

"I don't know," Grede said, passing the spoon to Vereesa on the other side. "I'm a little worried Gorjet won't fly back to Stormwind on his own."

"Hire a pilot?" Vereesa said, pinching a fish tail and examining the meat on it. "Have him fly us to Booty Bay, then ride Gorjet back."

Grede took a sip of stew, then counted on his fingers: "You, Jace, me, Morophil, the gryphon pilot — five of us. Gorjet may be big, but he's a gryphon, not a drake, little elf."

"I'm pretty heavy too — I count as a sixth person!"

A sharp, tiny sound came from below. Jace felt his scalp tighten. He glanced around to make sure no one had noticed the little brat's outburst and slapped his bag hard.

This little guy's guts were getting bigger and bigger — who knows if that fel healthstone he'd eaten hadn't fried his brain.

Vereesa glanced at the remaining fish. "Still, I heard from the Reliquary that the goblins of Booty Bay, hearing the whole Alliance is building gryphon roosts to improve transport and communication, have hired dwarves to build their own gryphon roost too."

"Really? You're sure?" Grede asked.

"Of course," Vereesa nodded. "The Reliquary used to rely on dragonhawk riders to ferry people to the Cape of Stranglethorn, but I heard they've cut that budget recently — probably because the dragonhawk riders demand far more magical supplies than hiring a gryphon pilot costs."

Grede rapped the table lightly. "If you're sure, then I'll make arrangements first thing in the morning to get Gorjet ready to go."

If they could ride a gryphon, the trip to Booty Bay would shrink from twelve or thirteen days to less than three, cutting out a lot of misery at sea.

"Then once we reach Kalimdor?" he continued. "Are we still heading to Gadgetzan?"

"Other than Steamwheedle Port, there's no other way to Kalimdor," Jace said. "I've already made inquiries. The Steamwheedle Cartel has wanted to build a second port in Kalimdor, but it's not going well. Everlook up north isn't a port. I think we can only head to Steamwheedle Port first, rest up a while, and see if the goblins have any expeditions or trade caravans heading north. I'm sure they must have a way to get from Gadgetzan to Everlook — zeppelins, for instance."

"Zeppelins?!" Grede asked, shocked. "You sure you want to ride those things?"

Vereesa looked up, swallowed the fish in her mouth, and muttered, "I want to ride one."

"You've got a death wish," Grede glared at her.

"For a dwarf, you really lack a bit of adventurous spirit," Jace said.

"I'm not with the Explorers' League! Don't pin the stuff Bronzebeards like to do on me." Grede wagged his finger. "Anyway, I'll be damned if I can figure out how something with no wings stays in the air."

"We'll cross that bridge when we come to it. I'm just guessing. But if there's no convenient way to travel, getting from Gadgetzan to our target — Darkwhisper Gorge — could mean an extremely grueling and lengthy journey. We'd still have to cross the Shimmering Flats, pass through Thousand Needles, and enter the Barrens."

Grede said quietly, "That wouldn't be so bad. We might run into Mosa and get her to come along."

"Is that a good idea, Grede?" Jace dipped his bread in the fish broth, glanced at the elf who was focused entirely on eating, and said, "Last time we went to Kalimdor it was to fight demons, and those demons threatened the tauren's homeland too. Now we're heading far north, away from the tauren's homeland — it has nothing to do with her, and she certainly has no use for money. I can't think of a reason to get her to help us. After we're done with this, I promise I'll help you find Mosa. We can even ask around for her in Gadgetzan first."

"I'm just saying," Grede coughed, caught off guard. "If those goblins really do run zeppelins between the northern and southern towns, then of course I can ride one too — I'm not that much of a coward! Just ask me in advance how often the zeppelins crash, so I can be mentally prepared."

"If I remember correctly, Everlook is in the mountains of Winterspring, Jace," Vereesa said. "The mages of Quel'Thalas know of the place. It's a rather dangerous mountain range, with powerful magical beasts — chillwind chimaeras — roaming about."

"What's a chimaera?" Grede asked.

Vereesa explained: "A large, dragon-like flying creature, but far more savage. They have two heads, breathe frost, and can even use simple magic. Of course, I've never seen a real chimaera — that's just from books."

"So it's a myth, then?" Grede tried. "Like Greatfather Winter?"

"Like Ragnaros the Firelord," Jace said.

"Uh." Grede coughed. "You hoping we run into trouble."

"I'm hoping that when we actually run into a chimaera, you won't freeze up in panic." Jace stared at him. "These things definitely exist. Remember Feathermoon Stronghold? The night elves mentioned hippogryphs flying from that island to the north being harassed by some two-headed giant monster along the way — that had to be a chimaera. Would a two-headed monster that can't fly harass flying hippogryphs?"

"Chimaera or no chimaera, Winterspring is a very dangerous place," Vereesa said. "By the time we reach Everlook and then head south into that valley, the journey in between will be a tough one too. I think it'll be much harder than our last trip — mountains, snowfields, unknown creatures... In any case, the travel journals in Quel'Thalas about that place are all harrowing, and those were left by the most brilliant scholars of the Silvermoon Council and the Magisters, Jace. They were all mages who were very good at traveling."

"Have they fought Deathwing?" Grede asked.

"Of course not," Vereesa said.

"Then that's settled," Grede said.

Despite the sound of a journey that would be extremely dangerous and unknown, it didn't dampen the three of their spirits in the slightest.

For Jace, the last trip to Kalimdor had been a rather good memory. The same went for Grede, of course. As for Vereesa, he couldn't be a hundred percent sure, but at least it was plain to see that she was looking forward to the coming adventure too, and eager to get back to Kalimdor as soon as possible.

By the end of the conversation, Grede had drunk more than the other two combined, and had boasted himself hoarse.

It was late by then. Jace glanced at his pocket watch — past midnight. He stepped outside the tavern and looked up at Grede, who was staring at the moon. "Want me to walk you back?"

"Absolutely no need." Grede waved a hand, head tilted back. "The moon is still two. I'm fine."

"There's only the Blue Child here. The White Lady is behind the wall, Grede," Vereesa reminded him.

"I know, dammit! That's why I said two!" Grede stared at the sky, turned in a full circle, looked around, then pointed at her. "Trust me, this isn't the drunkest I've been lately. Worst case I fall in the canal, and the great earthen don't need to breathe, so there you go..."

The dwarf muttered to himself all the way to the fountain statue in the small square. Jace and Vereesa watched him, terrified he'd tumble into the fountain pool. Only when he barely made his way around it and onto the road heading straight for Old Town did they relax.

"I still owe you an apology, Jace," Vereesa said. "I shouldn't have blamed you before. I should have known you must have had something important to do to leave Stormwind."

"You should say that while Grede's here," Jace said.

Vereesa flicked one of her long ears and tilted her head. "That won't do. It's too hard to say in front of him. I don't want him to think I'm that weak... but that's how it is, especially in front of you. Maybe I just missed you too much and got a little impatient."

"Where are you staying tonight?" Jace asked.

Vereesa pointed at the inn door behind her. "Right here. I booked my own room this time." With that, she lifted the corner of her mouth slightly and asked, "What are you thinking?"

"You tell me." Jace bumped her lightly. "Came all the way from Quel'Thalas to study Thalassian with a teacher, of course."

The elf bit her lower lip, took his hand, and pulled him back. "Then hurry up. Miss Vereesa Windrunner, who's come all this way, is going to teach you a few words nobody else would."
