Chapter 797: Smooth Sailing, No Matter What

The next day at noon, Jace arrived at the basement of the Slaughtered Lamb. Ursula saw him and came right out with it: "You're heading to Kalimdor, aren't you?"

"How'd you know?" Jace was a little surprised.

Ursula folded a blanket on the bench as she spoke. "The day before yesterday I heard about Vereesa at SI:7. At first it was just that the Ranger-General of Quel'Thalas had arrived in Stormwind. I was curious and asked who — they didn't mind telling me — so I figured you were planning to set out."

Jace nodded. He actually didn't want too many people knowing he was going to Kalimdor. And even if they did, he didn't want it connected to something like Uthales. So he was a little sensitive about information leaking beyond his control.

The alchemist noticed Jace's slightly worried look too. "Relax, SI:7 doesn't know why she's here — at least as far as I know. They only know it probably has something to do with you."

"Actually, I'd really like to meet her, Jace." Ursula leaned against the counter. "Meet my little buddy's girlfriend — that's not too much to ask, is it? And get a sense of what these 'Quel'dorei nobles' think of our sort."

"She's staying at the Gilded Rose." Jace gestured vaguely to the west. "How about the three of us grab lunch today? She admires your alchemy, Ursula — I've told you that before."

Ursula drew a long breath in and let it out slowly.

She stared at Jace for a moment, then waved a hand. "Never mind. I might not be as brave as I thought. These elves... you have to understand, I don't have anything against her personally, Jace. I just can't quite get used to their... everything. Their looks, their attitude, the way they talk. So forget it."

"Then let's get to business. What did you come to me for? Looking for me to prep some potions?" Ursula asked, then pointed at the cook and the shelves behind her. "Lately, what with the summoning rituals, we've burned through a lot of potions here — Elixirs of Wisdom, Shadow Oil, Shadow Power Potions, plus Protection Potions and Mana Potions. I need to stock up for the next round of attempts too. I don't have much on hand right now, but I can prep some for you. When are you leaving, Jace?"

Jace said, "I still have some from what you gave me last time. This time I mainly wanted to see if there's anything you need help with — I might be gone a while."

"Ha." Ursula smiled. "I thought you'd found a girlfriend and weren't going to help me out anymore."

"What kind of person do you think I am?" Jace sat on the bench and lay down sideways. "Go ahead. Kalimdor..."

"Wonderful." Ursula pulled out a small rope-bound booklet from the cupboard, flipped through a few pages. "Hippogryph horns — any chance you could get hold of some?"

"Might require us going to war with the elves over there, Ursula." Jace shook his head. "I don't think my team's got the strength for that."

Ursula nodded. "There's a plant that grows in Kalimdor called Bloodpetal. The goblins are especially interested in that flower. Could you bring back some samples for me to look at?"

"Honestly, it's out of the way," Jace said offhandedly.

"Out of the way?" Ursula frowned. "Do you know where Bloodpetal grows?"

Huh?

Jace looked up. Of course he knew where Bloodpetal grew. This aggressive plant grew in vast numbers in Un'Goro Crater in southern Kalimdor — a lowland rainforest nestled between the two deserts of Tanaris and Silithus.

In the very center of the rainforest stood a towering volcano, and the crater was home to a host of bizarre creatures — dinosaurs, for instance, in great numbers.

This trip they were heading to northern Kalimdor. They definitely wouldn't have time to go exploring Un'Goro Crater. Just finding the crater itself was an extremely difficult task — crossing the entire Tanaris desert alone would be more than enough to deal with for the few of them.

"I've heard of it," Jace shook his head. "The place it grows — that region called Un'Goro Crater — is far from where we're going."

"Un'Goro Crater." Ursula thoughtfully took out a pen and jotted it down, muttering, "You'd actually know that."

Jace thought: you heard about Bloodpetal from the goblins but didn't know it was in Un'Goro Crater — that's the stranger part. But he didn't say much.

After Ursula stopped writing, she went on: "Then, do you know about Furbolgs?"

"I've heard of them," Jace said.

"I don't know much either." Ursula closed the booklet on the table. "I heard these creatures live in the forest in tribal groups, and they've been in Kalimdor for a very, very long time. Logically, a tribe that's survived to this day... must have its own herbalism, medicine, and so on. If you get a chance to meet those creatures, could you ask them about any special alchemy recipes their tribe has?"

"I don't even know if I can talk to them," Jace said.

Ursula folded her arms. "You're the first one here to learn demonic, Jace. Don't tell me you can't find a way."

Jace said, "It depends on luck, Ursula. First, don't count on me actually finding any Furbolgs. Second, don't count on them being willing to teach me what they know. And third, don't count on what they know being any better than yours."

Ursula said, "I can accept that. But if you do bring back something unusual, there'll be a reward."

"Such as?" Jace asked.

"Depends on what you bring back," Ursula said.

"Then it's a deal." Jace stood up. "So, do you have any really powerful Distilled Elixirs of Wisdom? I'll buy."

Ursula sighed. "As far as I know, there are very few people who can make a Distilled Elixir of Wisdom. Selling you one for eight gold makes me feel like I'm the one losing out. And it takes me a long time to gather the materials for one. I've got one on hand that hasn't sold yet, and now you're about to snatch it away at a discount."

Jace smiled and counted out eight gold coins from his purse one by one onto the table. "Isn't having a steady customer a good thing?"

Ursula eyed those Stromgarde gold coins. "Then when you go to Booty Bay, run an ad for me. Say the alchemist of Stormwind's Arcane Star Alchemy Lab, Arkhana, brewed the elixir that was a key factor in you surviving Grim Batol..."

"Arkhana?!" Jace frowned. "Isn't that Thalassian? 'Arcane.' I thought you didn't like elves."

"What do you know?" Ursula said. "Precisely because I'm a half-elf and shouldn't like anything elven, a mysterious alchemist using an elven name won't be connected to me. Understand?"

"Fine, fine," Jace said. "I'll remember to spread the word."

"Mm." Ursula nodded in satisfaction. "That's more like it. Safe travels, Jace. Bring back good news."

"I will," Jace replied.

The sky was a bit cold, sullen clouds pressing overhead. The howling wind swept through the Valley of Heroes, scraping against the edges and angles of the great swords, longbows, and war hammers held by those colossal statues, producing a shrill, eerie sound.

The gryphons on the wall flapped their wings restlessly, as if itching to pick a fight with the wind. A few of the largest Alterac Gryphons were especially loud, drowning out even the gryphon keepers shouting at them to settle down.

Unusually, Gorjert was remarkably quiet today. The dwarves who usually watched him were quite surprised, and were chatting with a few human rider trainees about this curiosity nearby. They thought Grede's arrival had calmed Gorjert down, but Jace knew it wasn't that simple.

He glanced toward the corner of the wall. Molofir was hiding there, watching the silent gryphon — and the gryphon was staring right at where she seemed to be, as if he could keenly sense the demon's presence.

Not only could Gorjert detect her, he seemed to be able to foresee that he'd soon be carrying this demon. He kept making a displeased, slightly frightened "boo-boo" sound — less like a gryphon and more like an oversized cuckoo bird.

"He doesn't want to carry Molofir, does he?" Jace asked quietly, testing the waters beside Grede.

"It's fine, I've already talked him into it," the dwarf replied. "Honestly, I'm glad this lunatic's finally learned what fear is. It'll do him good."

Not far away, by the parapet facing outward, Vereesa and Amy had been chatting for a while. Then a dwarven gryphon keeper wearing goggles walked over and handed a thin scroll to Grede. "Give this to the gryphon keeper of Booty Bay, Thunderfist! They'll send Gorjert back then. Don't you worry!"

"Much obliged." Grede looked over and said, "We can leave anytime, sprite. How about you?" Vereesa faced the wind and gave a thumbs-up, then smiled and said goodbye to Amy before trotting over.

Amy walked up to Jace. "I'd really love to come with you to Kalimdor for the adventure, Jace. But it looks like this gryphon can't carry a fourth."

"Maybe by the time we reach Kalimdor, you'll have learned to teleport yourself there," Jace joked.

"There aren't many mages in Dalaran who can teleport that far. More of them can do it on the Quel'Thalas side." Amy watched Vereesa's retreating figure. "So she's really looking forward to this trip, Jace. She enjoys being with you. Don't make her sad."

"I know," Jace said.

"That day at the Blue Heron, I said a lot of stupid things I shouldn't have." Amy lowered her head. "I'm glad you were willing to listen. Other than you, I don't know who else I could talk to."

"Trust me, Amy." Jace said. "I know that feeling of having nowhere to turn. I know it all too well." Amy nodded in silence.

"Well then. Hope everything goes well for you in Dalaran," Jace said with a smile. "And even outside of Dalaran."

Amy smiled too. "Then I wish your Kalimdor journey smooth sailing — and smooth sailing after you get back too."
