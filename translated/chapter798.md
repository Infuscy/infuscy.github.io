## Chapter 798: Approaching from the Sky

From high in the sky, everything within sight centered on the valley where Karazhan stood seemed shrouded in a shadow.

This shadow didn't just reach Elwynn — it even touched the Black Morass and a small part of the Redridge Mountains, covering a range that might be four or five hundred kilometers across. The area affected by the shadow stretched far beyond what it appeared on the surface. The gryphon flew for nearly two days, deep into the rainforests of Stranglethorn Vale, before they finally felt its assault fade away entirely.

Grede followed the Stormwind gryphon keeper's advice to the letter — once they entered Stranglethorn, he flew over the sea near the coast and kept his distance from the shore.

Because Stranglethorn, including the coastal areas, was full of troll villages and cities, with bat riders patrolling from time to time.

After entering southern Stranglethorn, the calls of wild animals and the sea birds sweeping across the sky grew more frequent. At the edge of vision, between the dense forest, a grand yellow troll building stood tall behind layers of overlapping treetops. Cheers rang out from afar, still clearly audible even from such a distance.

If Jace wasn't mistaken, that was the rumored Gurubashi Arena — a place that threw its doors open to any creature willing to pay in money or blood.

Whether you were a troll from anywhere, or another humanoid race, or even something not quite humanoid, you could come here to test your strength or your purse.

All around the Gurubashi Arena were crumbling walls and collapsed troll structures. At the height of the Gurubashi Empire's glory, this had been an immensely prosperous troll metropolis.

But as the empire declined and its population shrank, the great cities outside Zul'Gurub were all abandoned. Now only this iconic arena was still in use by the trolls. Everything else — walls, houses, even the temples that had once held the trolls' divine faith — was completely forsaken.

Seeing the Gurubashi Arena meant Booty Bay wasn't far off.

In the evening, Gorjert soared over a towering cliff, meeting the briny sea breeze, and all of Booty Bay suddenly came into view.

No matter how many times he'd ridden a gryphon to this flight master in the game in his past life, it couldn't compare to witnessing the sight with his own eyes right here and now.

The bustling crescent-shaped town — shacks and houses packed together and jumbled up, roofs of all heights and styles, the streets between them where you crushed shoulders and jostled elbows. Unlike Stormwind, where most people were humans of a similar size, here you could clearly sense the different races of varying sizes and movements, yet coexisting harmoniously in one town.

Lift your gaze a little, and the vast, gemstone-blue seawater stretched endlessly. In the distance, the great mogul statue stood with its arms spread wide, facing away. Sails of every color — small and large — nearly motionless on the sea compared to the gryphon's speed — sailed in and out under flags of all hues.

These two utterly different districts met along a single arc, forming a starkly contrasting picture. Seen from high above, it felt completely different from approaching by sea.

"This gryphon can't stay the night here!"

A shout rang out from a distance. A dwarf riding a smaller gryphon quickly gained altitude from below and pulled up alongside Gorjert.

Perhaps because his voice was too sudden, or the smaller gryphon's approach was too fast, Gorjert was clearly startled and let out an unhappy growl, causing the smaller gryphon to wobble in midair before the dwarf rider managed to steady it back into position.

"Why not?" Grede asked. "We heard there's a gryphon roost here!"

"Booty Bay's gryphon roost is built above the Sailor's Home Inn, boy of Thunderfist!" the dwarf stared at Grede. "And your gryphon is clearly overweight. The Grand Magnate here won't allow an Alterac Gryphon to land. The one who told you there was a gryphon roost — didn't he warn you?"

"He did not," Grede muttered.

"Follow me, then!" The dwarf turned his gryphon's head and flew toward the distance. Grede had no choice but to follow.

"Does he know you?" Jace asked, curious.

"No, but he's from Aerie Peak. He can tell by my face," Grede said.

"Tell by your face? Even the family name?" Jace asked, surprised.

"There are certain telltale features — the nose and the eyes, Jace." Grede glanced back at him. "After you've spent enough time in Aerie Peak, you gradually learn to read them. It's not something I can explain to an outsider like you."

"It's not that hard to tell. That dwarf's of the Firebeard family," Vereesa said.

Grede turned slightly, startled. "How do you know?"

"Simple," Vereesa said. "That family's called Firebeard for a reason. Their beards are red as fire. Not hard to tell."

"But... you can't just decide someone's a Firebeard based on beard color alone." Grede gestured wildly. "Kurdran's beard is red, and Falstad's is even redder! But they're both of the Wildhammer clan."

"But only the Firebeards braid their mustaches that carefully. You can see—" Vereesa was just about to point when Grede shouted and cut her off, muttering, "All right, all right, you're right, you're not wrong!"

"I couldn't see anything on his mustache at all," Jace said.

"That's because you're not a ranger," Vereesa whispered in Jace's ear.

Gorjert followed the dwarf gryphon rider and landed on a hilltop. A few dwarves and goblins were there learning to ride. When this enormous creature suddenly landed, all the gryphons on the field grew agitated.

Grede calmed his mount in Dwarvish first. By now Jace could even make out that he was coaxing Gorjert not to bully the others.

His Dwarvish had improved enormously after that trip to the Thorium Brotherhood's outpost.

The upcoming sea voyage to Kalimdor would be a good opportunity to study both Dwarvish and the Kalimdor tongues. He absolutely couldn't let it go to waste.

The manager of this small training ground, a Wildhammer dwarf, seemed to be an old acquaintance of Grede's. The two recognized each other from quite a distance and started chatting away in a language Jace couldn't understand at all. They talked so long that it felt like they were about to head to a tavern for a long night of drinking before Grede finally pulled out the scroll from Stormwind and handed it over.

The manager dwarf looked at the scroll, then at Gorjert, nodded, and clapped Grede on the shoulder. "No problem at all. You can rest easy," he said in Dwarvish.

Hearing that, Jace relaxed too. He'd been worried the gryphon situation would hit another snag.

A path through this small valley led to Booty Bay. Through a tunnel shaded by palm trees, they soon came out onto a main road in town.

The three of them walked and chatted all the way to the road. Vereesa glanced down the street, and her previously relaxed expression turned cold in an instant.

Jace followed her gaze and saw three trolls — two males and a female — crouching on the steps by the roadside, staring tensely in their direction. Or, to be precise, staring at Vereesa.

Their upper bodies were bare, including the female troll, painted all over with dense beast totems and symbols whose meaning was unclear. Though they carried no weapons in hand, they had daggers, meat cleavers, and even axes strapped to their waists and legs of varying lengths.

Still, they never dared reach for those weapons, especially with the ranger before them meeting their gaze without flinching. Finally, the female troll said something, and all three stepped down the stairs at once, leaving warily one step at a time.

"Amani forest trolls," Vereesa said, looking at Jace. "Those totems mean they're under the dragonhawk loa Kajalai. Why would Zul'Aman elites be showing up here?"

Jace explained: "They may not belong to Zul'Aman anymore. During the Orc Wars, the Amani cooperated with the Horde. Some trolls who didn't want to join the orcs fled here. After all, other than here, there's no place on the entire continent for them to set foot."

"How do you know all that?" Grede asked. "Last time I was in Booty Bay, I never exchanged a single word with these trolls."

Jace said, "I'm a warlock. I have to deal with dark magic all the time. It's inevitable I'd pick up some information on this front."

"Friend or foe, these green-furred monsters have ruined a good day. Just the wild-cat stink coming off them makes me sick." With that, Vereesa grabbed her hood and shook it hard, as if trying to fan the surrounding air all away at once.

Jace cleared his throat guiltily. Vereesa glanced at him, as if she sensed something was off but couldn't quite put her finger on it.

Molofir smiled at him, then provocatively glanced at Vereesa beside him, her lips moving as if she wanted to speak but didn't. Jace shot her a look warning her to behave and not go looking for trouble.

By now, he felt his ability to mask his emotions was on the verge of collapse.

"I'm heading to the Reliquary's outpost to arrange passage to Kalimdor for us — save us some coin," Vereesa said, facing the two of them. "Are you two coming with me?"

"I'm going to the goblin alchemy lab to ask about the spectral goggles," Jace said.

"Then go ahead, sprite. I'm heading to the tavern to play axe-throwing first," Grede said, pointing at her. "But you're not allowed to come."

"Ha. I'm more worried about you amateurs dragging my level down," Vereesa said, then leaned in and kissed Jace lightly on the neck. "See you later, Jace."

With that, she glanced at Molofir beside her, and the succubus was staring right back at her. The two women's eyes met for a moment, then both looked away.
