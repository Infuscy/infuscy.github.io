Chapter 806: Rumors of a Female Druid

The voyage of the Pandaria in Motion hadn't gone as smoothly as expected. Based on the previous trip aboard the Clever Traveler, they should have reached Tidefury Cove in about forty days, then arrived at Steamwheedle Port in less than a week. But now it had been over fifty days, and the shores of Kalimdor were still nowhere in sight.

The cargo ship was no smaller than the Clever Traveler, and they hadn't run into any serious trouble along the way. Jace had actually thought they'd arrive sooner.

Still, after nearly two weeks of anxious nerves, on the morning of the fifty-eighth day, a dark brown stretch of forest and sea cliffs finally appeared at the edge of the horizon.

A crowd of passengers crowded along the deck railing, hollering and cheering at the sight of land, celebrating their successful arrival on the far shore. Jace had the feeling that if there were no railings, they'd have jumped straight into the water and swum for the coast.

Honestly, he felt the same urge. Short of clothes, short of food, the endless rocking — his whole body, from head to toe, felt like something was off. He really didn't want to spend one more day on this ship than he had to.

"Good morning."

A greeting in Thalassian came from behind him. Jace turned to see Wrensa walk over and flip up the hood of his cloak. "Have you been remembering to practice our language these past few days?"

During their time on the ship, Jace had naturally focused on Dwarvish and the Kalimdor tongue, but he hadn't let up on his study of Thalassian grammar and everyday phrases either.

By now, he could basically understand Thalassian when he heard it, without his brain defaulting to the NPC voice lines from his past life.

"Not bad," he replied in Thalassian, then switched back to Common. "But you should know, Wrensa, these two months on the ship have been boring enough as it is. Add in you quizzing me on Thalassian every chance you get, and it's more miserable than studying in the Mage Quarter."

Wrensa asked, "What did you just say?"

"Thalassian," Jace corrected himself immediately.

Wrensa said, "Respect for us elves… no, respect for the quel'dorei has to be carved into your heart. Ugh, damn it." Jace smiled. "It's not the first time you've slipped…"

"Have we reached Kalimdor?" Wrensa cut him off.

"We have." Jace pointed at the hazy coastline in the distance. "I'd estimate we'll reach Steamwheedle Port within a week."

"Finally, a bath." Wrensa sighed. "I feel like a pickled fish right now."

Jace instinctively licked his lips. The elf's eyes snapped wide, then quickly darted to the nearby passengers. She lowered her voice. "If you dare say anything here… I'll kill you."

The warlock nodded emphatically.

In truth, Jace's estimate had been a bit conservative. By the evening of the third day, they arrived at Steamwheedle Port.

The Pandaria in Motion seemed to have sailed a slightly more southerly route than the Clever Traveler, which meant they'd spent longer on the Great Sea — but it also meant they'd made landfall closer to the desert of Tanaris.

After a night's rest at the port, the next morning they headed out to the waystation outside the port, looking for a caravan headed to Gadgetzan they could hitch a ride with.

From a distance, they spotted the giant kodos squatting lazily by the road, dozing off. All of them were quite excited, feeling as though they'd been transported back to a year ago.

"Hey, hey, you two."

Grede punched Wrensa's arm and pointed at a kodo up ahead, shouting back to Jace at the rear. "Look over there, look at that kodo handler."

Jace trotted forward a couple of steps through the sand and followed the dwarf's gaze. To his surprise, sitting there was a tauren. Seeing a few foreigners running toward him, the tauren lifted his head warily and pricked up his ears.

He was an exceedingly old tauren, his mane turned entirely gray.

He was even taller than the tauren guard Jace had seen back in Booty Bay. Leaning against the kodo's belly, he held a massive long-handled greataxe in his hands. His head actually stood a bit higher than Jace, which somehow made the kodo's enormous frame seem a little less colossal.

"I'd like to ask…" Grede started in Common, then suddenly realized the fellow might not understand. "Go ahead, dwarf," the tauren rumbled, his voice thunderous enough to make one's chest tighten.

Grede nodded and asked, "Have you heard of Mosa Woodmane?" The tauren shook his head.

Grede frowned. Jace reassured him, "Mosa's clan was nearly wiped out not long ago, Grede. What makes you think she'd have spread her name across all the tauren tribes in a single year?"

Wrensa asked, "Have you heard of a druid appearing among the tauren?"

"A druid?" The tauren raised his eyelids and looked at the ranger. "A druid, yes. A few months ago I heard tell of a female druid appearing on the Barrens."

"That's got to be her," Grede said excitedly. "I knew she could save her people…"

"Save her people? You mean, us?" The old tauren shifted his head with a great effort, fixing his gaze squarely on the dwarf. A shower of sand tumbled from his head.

"A lot of folks think like you — that her arrival heralds the Horde reclaiming its ancient traditions… But I know many of our people who never abandoned communion with nature, outlander, and yet they changed nothing. So prophecy is just bullshit that hasn't happened yet. Her name is Mosa Woodmane? Woodmane… I know them. A stubborn bunch of forest walkers. That sounds exactly like them. But they betrayed us. They betrayed their oaths. Naively believed they could survive the centaurs without us. Hard to imagine what kind of clever daughter a family like that could raise."

"No… one… can save us."

The tauren's words came slow and heavy. At the end he let out a couple of chuckles that sounded more like a feeble cough.

His words marked him as an outcast of the Grimtotem. Mosa had once mentioned that her clan had worked alongside the Grimtotem.

Grede was clearly unhappy with what he'd said; anger had already flushed across his face. Wrensa thanked the tauren and tugged on Grede's cloak, signaling him not to get worked up.

The group moved off a ways. Grede said irritably, "What right does that guy have to talk like that? His own clan might be the ones who destroyed Mosa's family!"

"That fellow can't do much of anything anymore, Grede." Wrensa glanced back over there. "He's missing a leg. Did you not notice? Don't lower yourself to his level."

"Uh." Grede looked over again. "I, uh, didn't notice that."

"At the very least, he told us there's a female druid on the Barrens. That's a solid lead, Grede. That might just be Mosa," Jace said.
