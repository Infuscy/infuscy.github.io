Chapter 821: The Impenetrable Thicket

Jace knew Aegwynn had probably hidden herself somewhere here on Kalimdor. But if she'd gone to ground, why show herself?

The most likely reason he could think of: she wanted to see whether the three of them had been sent by the Order of Tirisfal. After realizing Medivh's fall, Aegwynn had once tried to talk her son back to his senses.

But obviously, a Medivh possessed by Sargeras was never going to listen to his mother — and by then, Medivh's power had already eclipsed Aegwynn's anyway.

After surviving her encounter with Medivh, the gravely wounded Aegwynn no longer had the strength to stop him herself. All she could do was warn the Alliance to be wary of Medivh's dark designs.

After the Alliance won the Orc Wars and Medivh was slain, she'd quietly teleported herself away to Kalimdor and begun her life in seclusion.

Aegwynn had selfishly stolen the Guardian's power, and for a long time she'd been hunted by the Order of Tirisfal. Even now that the Order was destroyed, there were plenty of mages who'd want to find her.

If she suspected he recognized her and had come here to seize the Guardian's power — that would be the end of everything.

After all, throughout history plenty of mages had claimed they were going after Aegwynn to reclaim the Order's treasures or recover her power. But either they never found her at all, or they simply vanished without a trace.

Aegwynn would have shown no mercy to anyone like that.

Even after being defeated by Medivh and left in a severely weakened state — one of the most powerful mortals to ever walk the world was still not someone the three of them could handle.

So right now, all he could do was play the clueless passerby. A traveler, an adventurer, a treasure hunter, a bounty hunter — he could even be a pirate or a bandit for all she knew — anything so long as it had nothing to do with Dalaran.

He wondered if she'd noticed the succubus and the imp.

In fact, now that the Dream's concealment had faded, the two demons had gone invisible again and hidden themselves at the appearance of an outsider. But Jace had never counted on their invisibility fooling a mage of Aegwynn's caliber.

Yet whatever the reason, the woman hadn't taken any interest in those two demons just then. Maybe she didn't want to expose her identity and invite trouble over something so small?

"Well, anyway, glad to meet fellow Alliance folks here." Grede waved a hand at everyone. "Let's all ease up on the hostility, hey?"

He walked up to the woman. "Just passing through yourself, miss? Though you don't much look like a traveler."

"I live here, Master Dwarf." The woman's tone was casual. "I'm a mage. I came to Kalimdor to study the ancient magic hidden here. Since you lot made it this far, you must know this continent is every bit as old as the lands beneath the Seven Kingdoms. I came after you to see if there was anything I could do to help. The marsh hides dangers that even a mage who's lived here many years wouldn't dare touch — the fact that you charged in anyway was reckless. That you made it out alive was pure luck."

"Reckless is right." Grede yawned. "We weren't planning to go in in the first place. Did you see a crashed zeppelin, down south…"

"No." the woman said. "Though it sounds like your journey's been nothing but trouble."

"No way."

The dwarf muttered and glanced south, only to realize the marsh's forest was already blocked off by mountains. From here, there was no longer any sign of the burning zeppelin's crash site. Without noticing, they'd already fled a long, long distance.

"Doesn't matter then." He waved a hand. "Since you live here, miss, you must know a safe place to rest?"

"Head west from here and you'll enter a canyon. Follow it north and you'll reach a quiet stretch of coast before dawn, Master Dwarf." The woman pointed north. "It's wide and flat, and nothing hostile lives there. But don't go any farther east, or wander about this forest — you'll lose your way fast and get surrounded by quilboar. And if by some miracle you make it through to the eastern shore, what's waiting for you there will be even more crazed murlocs."

As the woman spoke, Grede covered his face and sighed — probably upset that there'd be nowhere decent to stay for a while. Wenresa, meanwhile, curiously peered toward the forest to the east.

Jace noticed that even this small motion from the elf had put the woman on alert — she'd swept her gaze over her, almost imperceptibly. She was definitely not here out of kindness. She was here to stop the three of them from going any farther.

"Thank you for the warning, miss." Wenresa relaxed her guarded tone. "We just shook off a pack of dragon-kin. We really don't want any more trouble."

"Now that we have a clear route, we'd best leave soon." Jace looked at the other two. "Very well. Safe travels."

The woman smiled and nodded. Then Jace caught her gaze drop to his hand. No question — she'd seen his ring. That Stormwind court mage's ring.

Aegwynn's late husband, Nielas Aran, had been a member of the court mages. She would recognize that ring. Then she raised her eyes to Jace's, and the look in them was extraordinarily complicated.

Jace turned his head away. He knew that since the woman was willing to let them go, he shouldn't stir up any more trouble. After they entered the canyon to the west, Grede was so tired he could barely walk straight.

If not for the woman's warning about quilboar in the area, he probably would have collapsed right there on the roadside and refused to get up. Wenresa stayed silent the whole while. Jace could tell she'd also sensed something was off about that woman.

After walking for two or three hours, just as the woman had said, the salty tang of sea breeze was already drifting up ahead. Jace guessed they'd finally reached the Barrens' coast.

The moment Grede caught the scent of the sea, he dropped to the ground with a thud and started snoring. Jace and Wenresa knew there was no waking him up like that, so they had no choice but to drag him to a soft patch of ground shielded by low brush along the road.

Honestly, Jace was so exhausted he was about to lose it. If quilboar really did surround them, so be it — he'd fight.

He'd just leaned against the brush roots and closed his eyes to rest for a bit when sleep refused completely. The image of that woman was seared into his mind.

By now, Jace couldn't even be sure she was Aegwynn at all. "You also think something's off about that woman, Jace?" Wenresa asked. "Yeah." Jace nodded.

"Sazeno." He called the imp out. "Can you sense that mage's power?"

"Of course I can." Sazeno said. "But I don't think there's anything special about her power, Master. Did you sense something different? You definitely know something, don't you?"

Just then Molofier, who'd been covering their rear, caught up. She stepped out of the shadows and said, "No quilboar are following us, Master."

"Remember those quilboar we ran into on the stone pillars' cliffs last time, Jace?" Wenresa looked over. "I don't know much about quilboar, but from what I've seen, they mostly live around the hills out on the grasslands. They dig caves into those hills, pitch tents, build their own cities. That woman saying quilboar would be hiding in the forest sounds more like gnoll behavior."

"More like she made up an excuse to chase us off than actually wanted to help." Jace said. "That's exactly the feeling." Wenresa nodded.

"Molofier, did that female mage detect you?" Jace asked.

"She never looked at me once. In my experience, she shouldn't have spotted me. Why, Master — do you think someone of her ability could see through my invisibility? That's a bit too insulting." Molofier smiled.

Jace glanced at the succubus. It was rare for Molofier to say anything particularly arrogant — but the woman across from them was Aegwynn. Or was she? Was he reading too much into this?

Aegwynn wasn't the only mysterious recluse in Dustwallow Marsh. There was also an alchemist named Tabetha. No one knew how she'd come to Dustwallow Marsh, and no one knew her past.

Given the many similarities between Aegwynn and Tabetha, plenty of people believed Tabetha actually was Aegwynn — that the mysterious alchemist persona was just an identity Aegwynn had crafted to dodge trouble, her real purpose being to aid Jaina Proudmoore in the struggles against Kalimdor's many dark forces after Theramore was founded.

But in the end, no one could say for certain whether Tabetha was Aegwynn — especially after Aegwynn's death, when Tabetha remained in that little hut, making everything even more bewildering. The contradictions between game and story became harder still to reconcile.

Here and now though, Jace knew he couldn't just jump to conclusions.

In fact, that woman had been able to see through the Dream's location at a glance. There was almost no room for doubt about anything anymore — not to mention the way she'd looked at his ring.

Could it be that right now she was conserving her mana with the intention of resurrecting her son?

After Medivh's death, she'd always planned to bring him back, to pull her son's soul back into this world. She definitely wouldn't want anyone in the Alliance to know her plans.

After all, Medivh's reputation across the Alliance had shifted from the great Guardian to a demon summoner, a collaborator with the orcs — even their mastermind.

To want to resurrect him was to make an enemy of the entire Alliance.

Either way, Aegwynn would have no interest in what these three thought of Medivh.

Jace knew very well that once she learned he'd discovered her plan to resurrect Medivh, she'd immediately kill without hesitation — wipe the three of them off the face of Azeroth.

Was that the real reason she'd stopped Jace and the others from pushing deeper into the forest? Afraid that Jace's group would discover the traces of her attempt to resurrect Medivh? If so, that would explain quite a lot.

After being gravely wounded by Medivh in Karazhan, Aegwynn had been too injured to make another attempt to correct him. But after her son was killed, seeing Sargeras's soul banished to the Twisting Nether, she believed Medivh had finally broken free of the Dark Titan's curse.

If he could be resurrected, he would surely return to the right path and continue fulfilling his duty as a Guardian.

So she'd hidden herself here, avoiding the use of any mana unless absolutely necessary, cutting off all contact with the outside world, devoting herself entirely to gathering power for Medivh's resurrection.

Recalling his encounter with Aegwynn, Jace thought carefully about whether he'd let slip any detail that might have given away his recognition of her. After confirming there was nothing obvious, he barely managed to relax.

What surprised him more, actually, was that Aegwynn hadn't sensed the Flowing Ice Crystal he'd been carrying all along. Thinking this, Jace took out the small box at his waist.

Ah… right. This box was a magic box that shielded mana. He wondered — when the Kirin Tor crafted this box, had they also considered not wanting Aegwynn to detect it?

Either way, Aegwynn had certainly recognized his identity as a Stormwind court mage. She might already know exactly who he was.

After all, something as big as the Grim Batol incident — half a years' time had passed, she might have heard about it. Whether that counted as good news or bad, he couldn't tell.

The next evening, the group walked out of the canyon. Towering hills and the flat grasslands between them filled their vision. Sunlight scattered off the distant sea's surface in a glare that was almost harsh. All three of their moods lifted at once.

The mouth of the Southfury River — the one the goblin zeppelin captain had kept mentioning — was right here. Jace could even make out the hazy coastline on the far side of the water.

That was Durotar… or rather, it wasn't Durotar yet. That name came later, when the Horde's Warchief Thrall named the land in honor of his father, Durotan.

According to the goblins, they called this place the Eastern Barrens. It was home to quilboar, harpies, and all manner of beasts even more savage than those two.

It wasn't as smooth or fast as traveling by zeppelin, but at least they no longer had to live in constant fear of the blimp crashing and killing them all. That was how Jace comforted himself.

That evening, the group made camp beside a massive boulder by the sea. Jace and Grede took a stroll along the beach, keeping an eye out for anything edible they might pick up — shellfish and the like.

"Jace, you think we'll run into Mosa?" Grede asked, staring at the land across the water that had already faded beyond recognition. "I have no idea where the tauren around here live," Jace said.

"Should we go look? We're already here." Grede said. "Follow the river north, and if we don't find them, so be it. Those tauren have to drink fresh water, don't they? Seems like besides this river, everything around here is sea!"

Jace nodded. Honestly, he was quite keen to see Mosa again too, to find out what kind of druid she'd become.

Especially considering the things he'd told her the last time they'd parted.
