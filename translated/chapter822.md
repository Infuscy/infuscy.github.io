## Chapter 822: May the Wind Guide Your Path

They followed the Southfury River north for roughly seven or eight days. At first Grede kept looking around everywhere, searching for traces of the tauren, but after finding nothing for days on end, he slowly grew disheartened.

Wenresa, on the other hand, had been carefully observing both banks of the river the whole time, keeping an eye on the grass and the far side of rocks, hunting for anything that might be connected to the tauren.

Jace wasn't much good at tracking anything himself, so he'd told Molofier to stay hidden and watch their surroundings at all times, and spent the journey peacefully continuing his study of Dwarvish and the Kalimdor tongue.

Early one morning, the group had just woken up and were still in that dazed state of not knowing whether they should eat breakfast yet. Jace had just pulled out his Dwarvish runes booklet when Molofier suddenly walked over from the side and said, "I have a gift for you, Master."

"What gift?"

The moment she said that, Jace was reminded of cats that liked to bring their owners dead mice as gifts. He had no idea what sort of horrifying thing hers would be.

"Come and see and you'll know," Molofier said mysteriously.

Curious, the group followed her for a while until they reached a patch of tall river grass by the water. Molofier pushed aside a clump of weeds, and Jace saw a tauren lying face-down on the ground behind them.

"You killed him?!" Grede jumped.

The tauren lying there was quite large — a male, or rather, a man. The river grass around him had been pressed flat in a wide circle. Half his body was on land and half was in the water, making him look like a broken ship run aground on the riverbank.

His body still rose and fell slightly, just not very obviously — probably because of the tauren breathing rate. At a glance he'd looked dead, but for now it seemed he'd merely passed out.

Jace had assumed Molofier had captured Mosa, but then he thought — Mosa would definitely recognize a succubus. It shouldn't have come to blows at first sight, should it?

"Shh, he's not dead," Molofier said. "Last night I noticed him following us, so I tried to put him to sleep. I didn't expect tauren to be so resistant to sleep magic. I chased him for quite a distance before I finally got him… Master, isn't this the tauren you've been looking for all this time?"

Jace sighed and said, "He is, but knocking him out with dark magic like this — I'm afraid he's going to be furious when he wakes up."

Just then, the tauren on the ground coughed. Jace immediately waved at Molofier to signal her to hide. Molofier looked helpless as she melted into the shadows, her whole face radiating a "I'm the one who caught him, so why should I have to back off?" sort of displeasure.

"Any of you speak the tauren tongue?" Jace asked.

"I can't. I only know a little," Wenresa said. "Mosa taught me."

"Let me handle this." Grede cleared his throat. Jace cast a suspicious glance his way — could this guy have secretly found some way to learn the tauren language just to be with Mosa?

The tauren slowly opened his eyes, coughed violently twice, then kept shouting in the tauren tongue, "Demon! Demon!" Jace was of course very familiar with that word — after all, Mosa had repeated it many times when he'd first met her.

The tauren was just trying to get up when Grede hurried over and said something in the tauren tongue. The tauren blinked groggily, saw the little dwarf, and was struck dumb for a moment. Then he casually picked up a rock from beside him, raised it overhead, aimed it this way, and roared something in the tauren tongue.

He was quite young, and his mane was thick and bright. Jace had felt the effects of age on the tauren body back at Sunblaze Hold, so he could make a rough judgment.

"Don't tell him about the demon, Grede," Jace warned. "Just tell him he saw wrong."

"Of course, I'm not an idiot," the dwarf replied. Then he said something to the tauren, who looked somewhat hesitant. Wenresa frowned. "Are you sure you said that right?"

"What's wrong with it?" Grede asked, displeased.

"We're not demons," Wenresa said. "Ni, aluo, niqi, aermeng…"

"Ah, all right, I got it wrong." Grede sighed. "Let me organize my language. Wait, give me a bit of time, go slow — it'll definitely be fine."

He glanced up at the sky, then pulled out a little booklet from his bag and flipped through it, muttering as he went: "You saw wrong, how do you say 'you saw wrong' in the tauren tongue… maybe use illusion? How do you say 'illusion'?"

The tauren up ahead kept the rock raised overhead, staring at him the whole time. After a while, the atmosphere grew excruciatingly awkward.

"Ah, this is it." The dwarf nodded and began to pronounce the tauren words one by one. Jace rarely saw him be this serious about anything.

After a laborious exchange like this, with Grede repeating Mosa's name over and over in the middle of it, the tauren ahead actually did seem to lower his guard quite a bit. He slowly lowered his already-stiffened arm, waved a hand, then turned and started walking up onto the riverbank.

"He knows Mosa?" Jace asked.

"He doesn't just know Mosa — he heard we're Mosa's friends, and he's going to take us to meet their chief!" Grede excitedly slapped his chest. "See? Looks like that tauren at Hotsand Port was telling the truth. Maybe that tauren who wants to restore the druid traditions is Mosa!"

Hearing news of Mosa, the three of them were naturally thrilled. Jace told Molofier to follow from a distance so as not to startle the tauren, hid the demon-skin bag under his cloak, and followed the tauren's footsteps.

He'd originally expected the tauren hunter to lead them to a fairly impressive tauren village, but after walking for ages, what they finally arrived at was a temporary little camp hidden deep within a sparse grove.

There was only a single tent that looked like it had just been pitched, with four or five tauren sitting around a campfire. When they saw the outsiders approaching, they all stood up.

They were all powerfully built — quite different from the civilian-looking tauren at Sunblaze Hold. This was probably some kind of hunting party out in the field.

An especially tall tauren walked out of the tent. His fur was yellow tinged with red, and a pair of black horns curved forward, pointing at the people before him. After exchanging a few words in a deep, low voice with the tauren hunter, he looked at the three of them.

"I am the chief here."

This tauren could actually speak Common. Though he wasn't very fluent, it was already enough to surprise Jace. Grede had even gotten his notebook ready to translate, and now he'd come up empty.

"I am Baine. You may call me Baine." The tall tauren said. "If you say you are Mosa's friends, then please state your names."

Baine?

Could this tauren before him be Baine Bloodhoof, the son of the future tauren chieftain Cairne Bloodhoof?

Of course, there was no ruling out the possibility of duplicate names among tauren, but what were the odds of running into a tauren leader on the coast of the Barrens whose name happened to be Baine?

After all, Cairne Bloodhoof's tribe was rumored to live in this very region.

But Jace didn't dwell on it too much — if he acted too familiar, it would only arouse suspicion.

He gave his own name in reply, and after the other two had done the same, this self-proclaimed Baine said something to the tauren beside him, then said, "Mosa did mention you. A human, a dwarf, and an elf — and the names match too. It's just that… Mosa is gone."

"Gone?" Grede's eyes widened. "What do you mean, she's dead?!"

"No." Baine said. "Perhaps the Common I learned from the goblins isn't fluent enough. I only mean to say that a month ago, Mosa Woodmane left us and headed north. She said she had a way to find powerful allies — from another world. Those allies could help us fight the centaur."

"A month ago." Grede frowned. "So when we set out from over there, Mosa was still here?"

"No." Baine said slowly. "Mosa rarely returns to camp. She spends most of her time outside making contact with people from every tribe, searching for possibilities to fight the centaur. My father and I are both grateful for her efforts. It's only that the last time she came back, she mentioned this, then set out on the road again. She hasn't returned since. And she mentioned you specifically, Jace — I know your full name, Jace Sesso. She mentioned the guidance you gave her. Without you, she never could have found this way."

Grede and Wenresa looked at Jace in surprise. Jace thought: Mosa Woodmane, you damn woman, aren't you selling me out?

"Mosa didn't tell us about your conversation, Jace Sesso," Baine said. "But I know your words must have been very important to her."

"Thank you very much for the news, Baine." Jace bowed. "Knowing this is enough for us."

Baine slowly nodded, then said, "You may stay the night here, friends of Mosa. We still have a few days before we set out."

"I'd rather not." Grede looked at Jace. "We should get on the road as soon as…" Hearing that Mosa wasn't here, the dwarf clearly didn't want to stay a moment longer.

Jace nodded. "Thank you for your hospitality, but we still have a ways to go — we've already lost quite a bit of time."

"Then may the wind guide your path, travelers." Baine said this, and the other tauren nodded in salute as well.

Leaving the sparse grove, Grede asked as they walked, "Did Mosa go looking for elven reinforcements? How could those elves possibly help her come here and fight the centaur?"

"I don't know either," Jace said softly.

"Maybe she wants to ask the Keepers of the Grove for help," Wenresa said. "Like the ones we met outside Maraudon."

The Ashenvale held plenty of potential allies for the tauren — night elves, Keepers of the Grove — but none of them seemed like a force that would help the tauren go to war with the centaur.

Could it be the furbolg?

That felt even more far-fetched than the night elves.

"I'm curious what you said to Mosa, Jace," Wenresa asked in a low voice.

Jace said, "Just some things about the Keepers of the Grove. Maybe she just drew some inspiration from what I said. I don't think what I said was all that important."
