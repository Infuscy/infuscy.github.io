## Chapter 823: The Tauren's Potential Allies

In his past life, Jace had played both factions, but mostly the Horde.

So he'd always had a deep fondness for the road stretching from Durotar all the way across the Barrens.

Even though Thrall's new Horde hadn't arrived on Kalimdor yet — hadn't even been founded, in fact. After all, right now Thrall was just a strapping young fellow in Durnholde Keep. Jace recalled the look of Thrall he'd seen in that arena earlier and figured the guy probably still thought he was a green-skinned human.

Naturally, that also meant there were no proper roads built by the Horde here, no guide fires or signposts lining the route, no orcish watchposts or waystations set up either.

But just gazing at the distant rolling hills of the grassland, watching the herds of gazelles wandering about, was enough to bring back a great many memories of his past life.

Picking up quests at the Crossroads, hunting tallstriders and raptors, exploring the oases…

He looked up at the two moons overhead, then lowered his eyes to the campfire before him, glanced at Vereesa chatting and laughing with Grede by his side, and for a moment Jace didn't quite know what he was nostalgic for.

Was it his past life?

It seemed the two had just finished their previous topic. Vereesa stretched, slowly let out a sigh, looked over and smiled. "You've seemed like something's been on your mind ever since a while ago, Jace. Missing home after only two or three months on the road?"

"Why do you think I'm missing home?" Jace asked.

The ranger took out something like a potato the tauren had given them, narrowed her eyes and said, "The way you look reminds me of those noble recruits who'd just turned twenty when they joined the Farstriders. But after the excitement of finishing training and joining the patrol squads, within a few weeks they'd start missing Silvermoon or Sunstrider Isle. I was probably like that myself a few years back."

"I'm thinking about the route ahead." Jace took out the map he'd gotten from Gadgetzan, pointed to the mountains northwest of the Azshara coast on the map and said, "The zeppelin was supposed to cross over these mountains into Winterspring. I don't know if it's possible to make it through these mountains on foot to reach Darkwhisper Gorge. If this route doesn't work, we'd have to cut straight through Kaldorei territory and take the path at the foot of northern Mount Hyjal into Winterspring — but the problem is, that's furbolg territory."

He continued, "I have to admit, my alchemist friend asked me to find traces of furbolg on Kalimdor, so personally I wouldn't object to going to have a look. But of course, you also mentioned that Eldarath is near that coast. We could go there first and check things out."

"I think after that trip through the swamp… right now we're not in the best shape to head straight to Azshara looking for the relic my Reliquary contact asked me to find." Vereesa glanced at the quiver at her side. "I don't know what sort of danger we're about to face, so I have to find the best arrows and be prepared for any enemy."

"You're planning to borrow arrows from the Kaldorei?" Jace asked.

"For now, the only way to quickly find suitable high-quality arrows is this one. If we'd come to Kalimdor to hunt, the standards wouldn't need to be this high." Vereesa smiled. "Or we could go to the coast and see if there are any naga around. Legend has it that the sea witches' arrows are inherited from the ancient Kaldorei empire — they can bear any enchantment, no matter how powerful."

"You're not actually serious about that, are you, little elf?" Grede couldn't help but ask. "What do you think." Vereesa gave him a sidelong glare.

Jace said, "Actually, that's what I was thinking too, I mean… going to the Kaldorei. We can resupply other things there while we're at it. We drank some potions fighting the dragonkin earlier — those were all supposed to be saved for Winterspring, but we've already used up a fair bit on the way."

"Then." Vereesa pointed at the map. "We enter Ashenvale from here."

Jace nodded. He noticed that the spot Vereesa was pointing at was very close to where Thrall would later build Orgrimmar. "What do you think, Grede?" she asked the dwarf beside her.

"What do I think? Of course I'm willing — at least we can ask about Mosa's news!" Grede said.

At the mention of Mosa, everyone fell silent for a moment. After a while Vereesa said, "That tauren chief, Baine — did you listen carefully to what he said? He said Mosa wanted to find allies, and the allies were…"

"From another world," Grede imitated Baine's slow tone. "What does that mean?" Vereesa said.

"It might not mean anything at all," Grede said. "To the tauren, Ashenvale could be another world. Maybe Baine's Common isn't good enough and he meant to say another region, or another culture."

"I know you don't want Mosa's situation to be too complicated, Grede," Vereesa said. "But you have to admit — he might really have meant another world."

"It can't be the Twisting Nether, can it?" Grede laughed, then glanced at Jace, then looked back at Morophil perched on a dwarf tree in the distance, and awkwardly realized that nobody's expression had the slightest trace of joking.

He shifted his gaze to the little imp dancing around the fire. Sazeno seemed to sense someone staring at him and said, "I don't have a clue what you Azerothian savage beasts are thinking, so don't ask me!"

"Tauren aren't beasts, imp," Vereesa said, displeased. "Most of them are kind and trustworthy."

"Mosa is certainly one of them, and that Baine seems like a decent fellow too — but what about that Grimtotem tauren at Steamwheedle Port?" Grede asked, squinting.

The two of them then had another discussion about what sort of people the tauren really were. Jace watched them chewing on the tubers in their hands, tasted the slightly sweet juice that seeped out, and thought about this so-called "another world."

Actually, back at the tauren camp, the first thing that had come to mind when he heard "another world" was the orcs who'd come to Azeroth from Draenor. After all, those orcs were the most textbook "people from another world" you could find.

Of course, that was absolutely impossible on Kalimdor at this point in time, so Jace thought for a while and said, "She's a druid — well, half a druid. But now she's been trying to make contact with the inhabitants of Ashenvale, whether Kaldorei or keepers of the grove. I think it's reasonable to speculate that 'another world' might be the Dream — the Emerald Dream."

Vereesa rubbed the green gemstone earring of Ysera at her ear and said, "Now that you mention it, it is possible. After all, she was so confident she'd found a way to deal with the centaur… maybe a Kaldorei druid was willing to help her enter the world of the Dream."

"So what's actually in that dream?" Grede asked.

"I've only heard some very rough descriptions," Vereesa said. "It's said to be a lot like our world, except without us."

Jace opened his mouth, originally wanting to give a brief introduction based on his own knowledge, but after hearing Vereesa's words, he realized he couldn't really add much more.

"Without us — then what's there?" Grede counted on his fingers. "At least there are druids, right? And then…"

"Green dragons," Vereesa said. "The Dream Guardians."

Green dragons? Actually, Jace couldn't say he hadn't considered it at all.

It was just that he'd previously felt it was too far-fetched, so he'd had a hard time seriously considering the possibility — it had only ever flashed through his mind and been dismissed.

But eliminating the possibilities bit by bit like this with Vereesa and Grede, it seemed there weren't many options left. Had Mosa gone to Ashenvale intending to enter the Emerald Dream and seek the aid of the green dragons?

Jace said, "I think… Vere, these colossal beasts that stand apart from the conflicts of the mortal world probably wouldn't involve themselves in this kind of fight. From a green dragon's perspective, the centaur may be aggressive, but in the end they're still tribal populations that have lived on this grassland for thousands of years — not fundamentally different from the tauren."

"But not every single dragon is exactly the same, Jace," Vereesa said. "I don't know if you've heard the legend of the blue dragon prince — a pitiful fellow who tried to control all of Azeroth's magic and was destroyed by the backlash of the ley lines, leaving not even a bone behind. I'd heard that story since I was little, back at Falthrien Academy. So even though the blue dragons all guard magic, and even the temperamental blue Aspect has never thought to destroy the Well of Eternity, there are still individual blue dragons who covet power and do crazy things that upset the balance of the world — especially since he was Malygos's son."

"Though I don't know as much about dragons as you do," Grede said, gnawing on a piece of hard black bread as he spoke. "I know legends are mostly unreliable. For example, when I was little my father told me the Dragonqueen was a treacherous monster living in a black cave who breathed deadly flame at everyone who came near. Those people would be burned to cinders before they ever saw her true face. And most importantly, she was forty yards long. Even then I didn't believe it — how could there possibly be a forty-yard-long creature in this world, huh?"

After saying that he chewed a few more times and added, "Well, when the real Queen Alexstrasza's size was right in front of me, forty yards even seemed a little laughable. Damn, it nearly scared my eyeballs out. And what's even more insane is that she's as gentle as my mother."

"Mother of the Dream," he added.

"Not the mother of the Emerald Dream," the dwarf corrected himself, pointing at Vereesa. "Forget it, I'm not saying anything else." He coughed again.

Vereesa smiled and said, "The fall of Balacgos was a real event confirmed by the blue dragons' emissaries, Grede — not like the stories you dwarves pass around by word of mouth until they're unrecognizable. After all, you only live three hundred years."

Grede fired back: "Your people aren't any better. I flat-out don't believe someone who's lived three thousand years can still remember what their parents who died a thousand years ago looked like!"

Jace cleared his throat. "I don't believe it either."

Vereesa picked up the quiver beside her and swatted Jace with it. "Whose side are you on?"

"After all, none of us have lived three thousand years," Jace said.

"Exactly." Grede nodded.

Vereesa said helplessly, "All right, all right — what I mean is, standing apart from the conflicts of the mortal world is the dragons' general attitude, and I know that too. But that doesn't mean there won't occasionally be a maverick or two who breaks from their longstanding tradition, like the blue dragon legend I just mentioned. Since Mosa was so certain, what if she'd heard some clue, or received some other guidance? After all, the Kaldorei have always maintained their ten-thousand-year isolation, and the keepers of the grove are weak and few… who else has the ability to help the tauren? Or let me put it another way — what could make Mosa so convinced she'd definitely succeed?"

"So you mean Mosa might have found a green dragon willing to help her?" Grede poked the campfire with a branch and said. "Even if that's the case, there'd have to be a reason."

"If you put it that way, there's actually a ready-made reason right in front of us." Jace stared at the sparks flying out again and again and said. "The keepers of the grove have been intent on freeing Zaetar's soul trapped inside Maraudon. But to do that, they'd have to find a way to eliminate the centaur guarding Maraudon and the earth elemental princess inside. Given the numbers and power of the keepers of the grove, that's absolutely impossible for them. They'd either need to find a large number of mercenaries to attack that sacred tomb, or find a stronger force — their close allies, the green dragons. If there has to be a reason that could make a green dragon willing to help Mosa fight the centaur, it's probably this."

After hearing Jace's words, Vereesa and Grede both fell silent for a while. Around the campfire there was nothing but the crackling of the flames and the humming the little imp made as he danced back and forth.

Grede slapped his thigh. "Then when we go to Ashenvale, we can ask those elves along the way. After all, if you want to enter the Emerald Dream and speak with the green dragons, you'd definitely need their druids' help, right? A tauren coming to Ashenvale to learn the ways of the druid — I bet that's a pretty sensational event."

Both Jace and Vereesa nodded, though he didn't know whether they could still find any druids who were awake at this point.

Grede flung his cloak to the ground and plopped down on it, then hugged his already-spotless golden round shield over himself like a blanket and said, "Then it's settled, you two. Next stop, Ashenvale. I'm going to sleep with my precious baby."

Jace glanced at the big cloth bag wrapped in insect-carapace armor beside him. "This is your precious baby, then what about the armor General Shandris Feathermoon gave you?"

"They're all my babies, all of…" Grede kicked the bag with his foot and mumbled drowsily, "Good night, you three."

"Sleep well, sleep well," the imp said as he paced little steps around the fire. "Good night, Grede," Vereesa said with a smile.

"Sleep well," Jace said.

Grede suddenly raised his hand and shouted, "Good night, our esteemed night watchwoman Miss Morophil!"

Far away on a dwarf tree, the flames in the succubus's eyes brightened and then slowly dimmed until they were no longer visible. That was probably some kind of response.
