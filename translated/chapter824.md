## Chapter 824: The Red Mountains

They walked north for roughly another week. The group could feel the big trees and sparse woods growing more numerous around them, and wild animals appearing more frequently.

Occasionally lions or even raptors would follow at a distance behind them, but most were beasts driven to the end of their rope by hunger, desperate enough to risk a fight to the death. They'd run out of options for survival and had no choice at all.

Against beasts like that, leaving behind a bit of meat was enough to solve the problem. Following the Southfury River all the way, Wenresa caught plenty of fish, so this little party didn't want for food either.

The closer they got to the upper reaches of the Southfury River, the higher the terrain became as well. Jace followed Wenresa up a high slope and saw the ranger suddenly stop at the summit, her hand moving as if to reach for an arrow on her back, but hesitating and not actually drawing one.

Jace walked up as well and looked in the direction she was gazing. He could just make out several yellowish-brown figures on the other side of the waterfall. From this distance it was hard to see clearly.

"Quilboar," Wenresa said. "They've got weapons, Jace. Watch out for them throwing something this way, or shooting arrows. I don't want to waste arrows on them."

Just then Grede came up beside them and bellowed, "Hey, pig-heads!"

As he said this he raised his warhammer high, and a bolt of lightning suddenly descended from the sky and struck the hammer's head, making the whole hammer radiate with electrical light. He raised his warhammer and stone axe and struck them together with a crack, the burst of white light making Jace squint involuntarily.

But by the time his eyes readjusted to the sunlight, the quilboar down below had already been scared out of their wits and fled for their lives.

Grede shrugged and said, "The elements always love helping me scare these little things. Works every time, Jace. I reckon the storm elementals and I are kindred spirits."

Jace smiled and looked again in the direction the quilboar had fled — but suddenly felt something was off.

The plateau they'd just climbed up to was lined with towering red earthen mountains in the distance. These earthen mountains stood like great walls, and the canyons between them were as straight up and down as if cleaved by a blade, or like enormous gullies carved out by ancient rivers.

In these canyons, even with Jace's eyesight he could make out sheds and scaffolding built by quilboar. Here he could even see no small number of quilboar soldiers holding animal-skin banners, patrolling in formation across the open land in front of the canyon entrance with comical strides.

This was an exceptionally large quilboar tribe. Their banners were ones the three of them had already seen a few times while following the Southfury River earlier.

And the place they were occupying… Jace looked at the goblin-drawn map in his hand and was almost certain: this was the future Orgrim.

He couldn't help but take a deep breath. It was hard to imagine how Thrall, leading the orcs and trolls who'd crossed the sea to follow him, had managed to build Orgrim under the siege of this horde of quilboar.

"We should leave here, Jace." Wenresa waved him over. "Those quilboar seem to have run to their companions to call for reinforcements."

Jace nodded and took one last look at that canyon. He could almost picture the gates of Orgrim from those earthen mounds and high cliffs.

The three of them continued running toward the upper reaches of the Southfury River, cresting several more high slopes — but then a deep, rough horn sound suddenly came from the quilboar dwellings, followed immediately by a chorus of chaotic quilboar shrieks.

"Looks like they weren't scared off by your elemental power after all, Grede," Wenresa said helplessly. "Who the hell knew there were that many quilboar over there!" Grede roared from behind.

"They're catching up, Master," Molofier said, catching up with the party. "There are many soldiers behind us."

"Damn it."

Jace cursed under his breath. Before the words had even finished leaving his mouth, a thick fletched arrow suddenly struck the rock beside his foot and snapped, tumbling into the surging river. Immediately after, another came, batted away by Grede as he raised his shield.

"Long-nosed bastards!" Grede cursed in Dwarvish, looking toward the height the arrows had come from. Somehow a few quilboar heads had popped up on the hillside across the river too. Right now they were aiming the clumsy crossbows in their hands from above.

Jace silently recited the incantation for Fear. The crossbow-wielding quilboar wailed, slipped, tumbled off the cliff and broke his neck. His crossbow clattered and bounced all the way down, smashing against the rocks below.

"That's a Lordaeronian crossbow if I ever saw one — I'd never mistake it!" Grede glanced at it and said. "Who'd have thought you'd meet a fellow countryman all the way out here, right, Jace?"

"What do you want me to do about it? I didn't make the crossbow…"

Jace complained a line, settled his hood and continued following Wenresa forward. The elf nimbly leaped between slopes and rocks — it almost seemed like she ran faster on this terrain than on flat ground, while he could only scramble on all fours and barely keep up.

Grede wasn't much better than him either. Though he was from Aerie Peak, when it came to climbing mountains, this dwarf was probably better at going down than up.

But the farther they went, the narrower the cliff paths on both sides of the Southfury River canyon became, and the harder the road grew. Just then the little imp grabbed Jace's collar and said, "Master, watch out across the river — they still won't give up!"

Jace glanced across the river and saw that through the flying spray, a large group of quilboar really had followed them all the way, keeping up a mad sprint while howling. They seized every possible chance to loose arrows, hurl wooden spears, even throw rocks — though most of the projectiles ended up falling into the river.

It wasn't only their weapons that fell into the river. No small number of quilboar were shoved in by their companions too. But even so, those quilboar still chased like madmen.

The reason they were so crazy was that they'd definitely set their eyes on the magic items the group was carrying.

Though most of the good stuff was hidden away, Grede's shield and weapons were already flashy enough. Who knew what other treasures might be hidden in the bag on his back?

Jace forced himself to look ahead. At the lower end of the canyon's end he could already make out a blurry green — meaning that once they crossed these mountains, they'd reach Ashenvale, or at least not be far from it. These quilboar definitely wouldn't dare trespass on night elf territory. Once there, they'd have escaped with their lives.

But as they ran, Wenresa suddenly stumbled ahead with a grunt, nearly tumbling off the cliff. It was the ground beneath them — vines were growing wildly, trying to entangle the three of them.

"Up there! That way!" Grede swept aside the vines trying to surround him with his axe and pointed up high. "There's a quilboar shaman up there."

"It barely qualifies as a shaman, Grede." Wenresa angrily nocked an arrow, drew and loosed in one fluid motion. Before Jace could even make out the quilboar priest's clothing and movements, the arrow had punched through its heavy skull and pinned it to the earthen wall behind.

With the shaman's sudden death, the vines still writhing on the ground all wilted as if half-dead. But Wenresa looked ahead and cursed again. "The road ahead is covered in freshly grown vines… the path's blocked. What do we do?"

Jace stared at the vines climbing up from below the cliff and recited the Kalimdor tongue. When he thrust his hand out, a ball of flame suddenly ignited on the nearest vines, burning them through one by one. The ones blocking the road ahead couldn't bear the weight either and slid off the cliff's edge one after another.

These flames summoned from nowhere carried a visible ferocity as they spread in every direction. Even the quilboar were startled by the savage blaze into shedding much of their frenzy — a good number of them hesitated, stopping in their tracks and daring not advance further.

"Burn, burn, hah!" the imp shrieked excitedly.

Jace's own excitement was no less than his, and the response from the fire elementals was even more fervent. At this moment he could even hear the battle cries of the elements.

But in moments thick smoke billowed up, obscuring the road ahead until it couldn't be seen clearly at all.

Grede stared blankly ahead and said, "Hard to say whether this road is harder to walk now or easier." Jace tightened his hood. "All right, I didn't really think that through just now…"

"At least this kind of road is a bit more familiar to us, isn't it, dwarf?" Molofier smiled. "Though I hate to admit it, she's right," Wenresa shrugged, then turned and plunged into the smoke.
