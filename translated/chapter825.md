## Chapter 825: The Guardian of Ashenvale

As they pressed deeper north, the Southfury River transformed from the broad, calm river it had been on the Barrens into a torrent churning through the heart of the red mountain ravine.

After breaking free of the quilboar pursuit, the terrain didn't level out either — it grew even more rugged, and the group's pace slowed further and further.

Ahead, what looked like a lush forest seemed to stain the vision green, but after walking the whole day they still hadn't reached it by midnight.

That night the group had no choice but to stop and rest on a relatively wide spot on the cliff face. The entire first half of the day had been spent fleeing the quilboar chase, and after that they'd been constantly worried the quilboar would catch up.

Though for the three of them as they were now, a quilboar squad or two wasn't much of a threat, a quilboar army of dozens or hundreds guided by geomancers who could call on the power of nature and move earth elements was a lethal threat — especially fighting on the cliff paths the quilboar knew far better.

Grede was dozing against an earthen wall. Wenresa and Jace gathered some dead branches and chunks of wood from around the area and piled them here to make a fire — the nights on the Barrens were still a bit chilly.

Once the pile was ready, Jace casually lit the campfire.

After getting familiar with communicating with fire elementals, lighting a fire — a simple trick that only required channeling the free-floating fire elemental energy nearby — no longer required much dialogue with the fire elementals at all.

He only needed to sense the fire elementals in the air and try to make contact with his consciousness, and they would quickly respond to his will and gather for a brief moment.

This feeling was completely different from casting Scorch or Fireball. Reciting incantations to cast those mages' spells was like swinging a meteor hammer — lethal enough, but it always felt like he wasn't holding the lethal end, only pulling the rope in his hand, exerting his power indirectly.

Though in the end he could deal considerable damage, it always felt like there was some kind of delay, or that his strength couldn't be fully brought to bear — an irritating feeling.

Right now, Jace felt like he was holding a morning star. Swinging it was more direct; smashing with it was more satisfying.

Even now he was still savoring the thrill of commanding the flames to burn away those brambles that morning. He wondered how much power could be produced if he channeled shadow into them too.

But how would the fire elementals react then? Would they abandon him and stop answering his call?

Back in Ironforge, Jace had struck a pact with the fire elementals: so long as he didn't let shadow enslave him, the fire elementals wouldn't leave him. Casting shadow magic shouldn't count as being enslaved by shadow — he was the one commanding the shadow, after all.

"You're getting more and more skilled with your flames, Jace," Wenresa said from beside him. "Is this what those Dark Iron dwarves taught you?"

"Yes," Jace said. "Many Dark Iron dwarves are masters of fire. In Shadowforge City there's even a whole group of Flamekeepers — I'd guess it's probably like the Magisters of Quel'Thalas. Someday when I have the time I'd like to visit Shadowforge City. Maybe I could take it to the next level."

"You want to visit Shadowforge City?!" the elf asked in surprise.

"Hey!" Grede suddenly opened his eyes. "I only closed my eyes for a moment and you already got a fire going. Aren't you afraid those sentinels will spot it? Don't forget how you got caught by those sentinels back in Feralas, little elf!"

"Getting caught in Feralas had nothing to do with me," Wenresa glanced at Jace. "That's on him for not being able to beat that half-orc for so long that we were forced to reveal ourselves."

"Why is it my fault? Weren't the sentinels already watching us?" Jace shot back.

"They were just bluffing to make themselves seem like they knew the forest inside and out," Wenresa raised her brows. "That's definitely what it was."

Jace gave the ranger an annoyed look and poked the campfire with a branch. "So what if we get spotted here? We were planning to find the Kaldorei in these parts anyway. If they catch us, that saves us the effort and the time."

"Glad to oblige, invaders."

A ringing female voice sounded from the cliff top. Jace looked up and saw, beneath the night sky, a pair of golden-yellow eyes appear up high.

It was a black or dark blue nightsaber. Riding on its back was a heavily armored sentinel wearing a lavender cloak and a silver metal helmet.

Soon, more and more of these nightsabers appeared, those eyes gradually filling the cliff top.

"The ranger lord of Quel'Thalas has been caught by the sentinels again," Grede muttered under his breath.

Wenresa glanced with clear displeasure at the elves on the cliff, brushed off her cloak and stood up, speaking in a low voice to the dwarf: "This time I let these sentinels catch me on purpose, Grede. I did it to help you find Mosa's news faster — I surrendered willingly. If I'd wanted to evade them in the forest, there's no way they could have found me."

"Hah, I don't believe that for a second!" Grede muttered.

"She's wearing the green dragon's earring right now," Jace whispered. "She might actually be able to pull it off."

Wenresa gave his boot a light kick and said quietly, "I could do it even without that gem, you clueless fool."

"Hey, where's your demon?" Grede leaned in close, his voice so low Jace could barely hear it. "Shh." Jace's lips barely moved. "As long as you keep your mouth shut, she'll be fine."

"Ah, right." Grede nodded.

Jace glanced at the cliff face beside them. Molofier was bracing against it steadily with her hooves, lying flat against a half-hidden rock in the shadows.

Through the Soul Link, Jace could sense that her emotions hadn't fluctuated in the slightest — meaning she was completely unfazed by the sentinels surrounding them.

He remembered back in Feralas, when Molofier had sensed the sentinels closing in and tried to escape, she'd actually been a bit flustered. She probably hadn't been very confident she could slip through the encirclement either. In the end, not getting caught by the sentinels had involved a fair bit of luck.

By now, though it was hard to say whether the sentinels of Ashenvale or the front-line troops of Feralas were the higher caliber, Molofier's strength had grown to the point where she could easily handle the sentinels' reconnaissance.

Even Jace himself, using Detect Demons, could barely sense her shadow.

The empowerment of the sigils, combined with the power Jace had given her, had made her far stronger than the last time she'd come to Kalimdor — plain to see from how easily she'd hunted down those dragon-kin in the swamp.

At that moment, the lead sentinel on the cliff said, "Anything you have to say, you can tell my commander. I can't let you spend the night freely here. I'm sorry."

After saying this, she shouted something in the elven tongue. Grede asked anxiously, "What are they going to do with us?" The sentinels didn't answer. Jace said, "Don't worry, Grede. Just follow her." Grede asked, "They're not going to put us on trial, are they?"

"She didn't say that," Jace said. "She only mentioned taking us to… to…"

The night elf language, though very close to Thalassian, wasn't to the point where Jace could just understand it off the cuff, so he could only look to the ranger beside him.

"To a place called Silverwing," Wenresa pointed at a banner one of the sentinels up above was holding. "I'm guessing it has something to do with that banner."

It was a purple war banner, painted with a silver crow or goshawk emblem, surrounded by silver starlight symbols as decoration. And not just the banner — as the sentinels came up to Jace and the others, he noticed the soldiers' cloaks, pauldrons and more all bore similar insignia.

This name quickly made Jace think of a place in the game: Warsong Gulch.

Warsong Gulch was a small battleground in the game. The orcs and night elves occupying either side of the gulch fought over the forest at its center. The night elves were trying to protect their forest from being disturbed, while the orcs planned to log the area to provide timber for Orgrim and the entire Horde.

The reason it was called Warsong Gulch was that the orcs of the Warsong Clan had occupied the area to build a lumber mill. The night elves launched a counterattack to drive them out of Ashenvale, and the sentinel force responsible for this battle was named Silverwing.

Silverwing, Silverwing — it was almost certainly the same thing.

These night elves must be the Silverwing Sentinels. They were planning to take the group to Silverwing Outpost.

Following the sentinels out of the cliff area and turning west, they'd already crossed the mountains at the northern edge of the Barrens. On this side of the mountains were stretches and stretches of forest.

Looking north from here, one could see at a glance the deep, dense forest that nearly swallowed a person's gaze whole in the moonlight. Jace knew this was the boundless deep green he'd seen from afar before — the location of Ashenvale.

As they walked through the forest, the sky grew brighter and brighter. Grede was almost already asleep clinging to Jace's arm. He looked at the sentinel riding the nightsaber beside him and said, "Can I sleep on your big cat's back for a bit?"

The sentinel looked at him and said nothing. Grede couldn't help but ask again, "Exactly how much longer until we get to your place?" This sentinel gave a brief reply in the elven tongue: "Soon."

"Who knows what that means…" the dwarf grumbled.

Wenresa up ahead turned her head, about to translate for Grede — but her expression froze in that instant.

She gazed into the distance to the right of the column and didn't say anything for a long time. This piqued the curiosity of the sleepy-eyed Grede too, and he followed her gaze to look north. When his eyes turned there, he was also struck dumb at once.

Jace noticed the expressions of the two of them and glanced to the side as well — but this one glance was all it took. He couldn't turn his head back again.

In the golden light of dawn, north of the deep Ashenvale forest, a lone, magnificent peak lay beyond the boundless deep green and thick mist.

It was so tall and so sublime that the surrounding peaks clustered around it seemed like humble servants at the side of a giant, lowering their heads and daring not look up at its majesty.

What was even more breathtaking was that at the very summit of that peak, a colossal tree like a blooming flower was hidden among the clouds. When the mist parted slightly, it faintly revealed its form, as if placing a crown of branches and leaves upon the giant below — crowning the king of the entire world and sky.

Wenresa swallowed and said softly, "Nordrassil of Hyjal… Crown of the Sky."

The World Tree Nordrassil — this was the pillar supporting the immortality and health of the entire night elf race, and by now it had nearly become the foundation of all the flourishing life of northern Kalimdor.

The reason there was such an enormous environmental difference between the Barrens and Ashenvale despite only some hills and cliffs between them was largely because of the existence of the World Tree Nordrassil.

"Why in the hell is there a tree that big?!" Grede asked in a low voice. Wenresa glanced at the dwarf — clearly, she found it hard to answer that question too.

Regardless, after seeing Mount Hyjal, Grede was suddenly a lot more awake. On the surface, he showed almost no trace of sleepiness at all.
