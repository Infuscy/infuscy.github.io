Chapter 826: The Dragon Slayer's Renown

Gazing at the World Tree, they had unconsciously arrived at a low cliff.

Atop the cliff sat a massive fortress built of gray stone and wrapped in green. Following the sloping path up and around, they passed through a torii-like wooden gate — the same sort Jace had seen at Feathermoon Stronghold in Feralas — and emerged into a tranquil courtyard-like space.

He had to admit, Jace was thoroughly familiar with all of this. The last time he'd set foot here, he'd come as an orc hunter.

Though it differed from the game in the details — for one thing, there were none of the war machines here, no ballistae or stacks of shields and armor lying about. It was far cleaner than in the game. The layout of the fortress and ramparts was a bit different too. But by and large, it was about the same.

Looking south from here, Jace could see, through the mist, a slope on the far side of the canyon dotted with sparse trees. In the game, the trees there were far fewer — it was practically a bare cliff top. The orcs must have clear-cut it. Later the Warsong clan had built a fort up there to contest control of the forest with the elves on this side.

At this point in time, Silverwing Garrison was merely a base for the sentinels, holding back the quilboar, centaur, and harpy tribes to the south from invading Ashenvale. But any faction with a shred of sense wouldn't dream of invading here, so the whole fortress looked as though it had been abandoned for ages, nearly swallowed up by vines and trees.

The elves living here were quite relaxed too. At the moment, only a single sentinel stood guard.

When that sentinel spotted the saber cat riders, she gave a small bow, cast a glance at Jace's group, and hurried off through a small door at the base of the fortress.

That small door was probably the entrance to the fortress. It was hemmed in by tangles of tree vines, and ghostly blue lights flitted past it, climbing to the very tops of the enormous trees that stood alongside the fortress before vanishing among the canopy. The lights drew the eye so completely that the hidden entrance was nearly impossible to notice.

"Doesn't this place give you the creeps, Jace?" Grede muttered.

"Perhaps it's those blue lights. They say those are the spirits of dead Kaldorei." Wrensa gazed at the star-like things drifting back and forth in the forest beside the fortress. "Even in death... they protect the forests of Ashenvale this way. Protect their home from the intrusion of outsiders."

"How are those things supposed to fight?" Grede whispered. "Rush up like Arcane Missiles and blow the quilboars to bits?"

He let out a couple of dumb "heh hehs." A saber cat rider standing nearby, waiting for orders, shot him a withering look. Grede cleared his throat and wiped the grin off his face.

"I don't know either," Wrensa said softly.

Actually, Grede had pretty much hit the nail on the head.

These things, known in the game as "wisps," actually possessed terrifying energy. They killed the Kaldorei's enemies by detonating themselves.

A decade and more later, when Archimonde the Defiler was summoned to Azeroth, he led the Burning Legion's demon hordes alongside the Scourge into Mount Hyjal, seeking to devour the power of the World Tree. But the Ancients of the entire mountain — summoned by Archdruid Malfurion Stormrage — gathered and detonated, blasting Archimonde down to a skeleton.

Of course, Mount Hyjal was nearly burned to cinders in the process, and the World Tree was brought to the brink of death. But that alone showed just how far these ancient spirits were from being as fragile as they looked.

Just then, an elf in silver armor walked out through the gate. Seeing the three of them, she showed no particular reaction at first, only studying their weapons and clothing again and again.

Her gaze lingered on Jace's cloak, then she said in a cold tone, "State your names and your purpose. Ashenvale does not permit outsiders. Give me one reason not to punish you for crossing the border."

"Honored ma'am," Grede stepped forward. "I'm Grede, from Aerie Peak. I'm passing through Ashenvale, tracking down a friend."

"Aerie Peak," the night elf said. "I've never heard of it."

"What... you don't even know Aerie Peak?" Grede asked, stunned.

"But I know you." She turned to Wrensa. "You and your people have trespassed in our forests more than once, quel'dorei. My superiors don't wish me to take drastic action against you. If it were up to me, I'd have driven you out with blade and arrow long ago."

Wrensa frowned, displeased. Jace said, "Thus far we've shown no ill will, elf."

"And are you planning to demonstrate this 'ill will' of yours?" the night elf said. "Or am I simply not fluent enough in your language and have misunderstood your meaning."

Wrensa said coldly, "The superior you mentioned — Sentinel Commander Shandris Feathermoon — she received us with kindness at Feathermoon Stronghold in Feralas, treating us as friends of the sentinels. It's fine if you didn't know about that. But I'd appreciate it if you watched your tone."

The night elf asked, "You've met Sentinel Commander Shandris Feathermoon?"

"About a year ago. I don't know if Feathermoon Stronghold informed you of this. I am Wrensa Windrunner, Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas," Wrensa said.

Hearing Wrensa's name, the night elf's expression shifted slightly. She clearly understood what the Ranger-Lord of Quel'Thalas meant. Whatever their feelings toward their cousins across the sea, she knew the distinction in rank.

"I'm aware of the matter. The sentinel commander received three travelers from across the sea at Feathermoon Stronghold. They found something the Keepers of the Grove had been searching for in southern Kalimdor... and there was indeed a visitor from Quel'Thalas among them."

Having said that, she looked at Jace.

"As for me, I'm Jace Sesso," Jace said casually. "From Stormwind City."

After all, he hadn't expected these recluses hiding in the Ashenvale forest to know anything about him. The fact that they understood what a Ranger-Lord was had already exceeded his expectations.

"You're Jace Sesso?!" Silver light flared in the elf's eyes. She fixed her gaze on Jace. "You're really Jace Sesso?"

At the sound of Jace's name, the sentinels around them grew unsettled, murmuring amongst themselves.

"Your name's clearly made its way here by some means, Jace." Grede bumped Jace with his shoulder. "Since they know who you are, things should be a lot easier, right?"

"Of course we know." The night elf said, "Word of Deathwing's fall reached here months ago. The Dragonqueen Alexstrasza's messenger brought the news to Astranaar and Mount Hyjal. Nearly every inhabitant refused to believe it! And what's even more unbelievable... that man has come to Ashenvale?"

"The hero who destroyed the Demon Soul is said to carry the blue dragon's sword, Azuresong," another sentinel beside her said, wearing a dark helmet. "But I don't see him carrying that sword, Captain."

"Right here." Jace threw open his cloak, gripped the sapphire pommel, and drew Azuresong from its battered leather scabbard. A brilliant arcane blue light flooded the dim courtyard in an instant. A delicate chiming mingled with the low, chant-like hum of the nearby wisps.

The sentinels each drew a sharp breath, exclaiming in the elven tongue. Jace could tell that not all of them recognized the sword, but without exception they were stunned by the radiance it unleashed.

And Azuresong — which, restrained by Jace's will, rarely blazed this bright — now seemed to have finally thrown off those wretched shackles. It didn't drop the ball for a second, pouring out arcane power freely in its master's hand, showing off everything it had.

Now even the saber cats began to growl restlessly.

"I offer my deepest apologies for my offense, the three of you." The night elf lowered her face, lit by the sword's glow. "In recent years, more and more outsiders have come to Kalimdor. Goblins, humans, dwarves, quel'dorei. Not a few of them intend to push deep into Ashenvale and commit theft — or worse — in our lands. It's made us ever more sensitive and vigilant."

"Welcome to Silverwing Garrison... You must be weary from the road, especially the dwarf." She stepped aside. "Please enter the fortress and rest. The food here may not be lavish, but I promise it'll fill your bellies. If you have any other needs, you need only tell me."

"Quickly, inform Commander Sula Swiftarrow!" she shouted inside in the elven tongue.

"We can help you carry more arrows, Wen," Jace whispered. "Especially Grede. I reckon he's got plenty of room for more."

"Shh." Wrensa hushed him, but the corners of her mouth were already twitching upward.
