## Chapter 827: Silverwing Garrison

Silverwing Garrison was cold and quiet. The night elves had arranged a private room for each of Jace's party to rest in. Sitting on the chilly stone bed and cushions, drowsiness crept up on him before he knew it.

Jace hadn't even finished putting his things away before he heard the snoring already coming from Grede's room.

From outside came the occasional footsteps of night elf sentinels passing by, the distant sound of a horn whose meaning he couldn't guess, and the birdsong that rose from the forest from time to time. Filtered through God-only-knew-how-many-years of fortress stone, these sounds that barely made it inside only amplified the sense of security.

When he woke, night had already fallen. Silver moonlight poured in through the high windows, and Jace felt as though he were in a prison.

Of course, the stone and wood antique furnishings around him, carved with exquisite runes that spoke of history and culture, and the blankets that felt like fine silk — all of it made clear to Jace that he was certainly not being treated as a prisoner.

Compared to when they'd first arrived at Silverwing Garrison, the outside was much livelier now. From time to time he could hear whispered conversations and faint laughter. The night elves preferred the night — that much was beyond doubt.

Stepping outside, he could still hear Grede's intermittent snoring. He went to Wrensa's room and knocked, but there was no answer. She must have been up for a while already.

Gazing down the elven corridor of silver brick, the moonlight-like lanterns drifting at the corners, Jace found it impossible to shake the memory of his past life's battles in Warsong Gulch. It was just too similar — especially the elven moon blades on the walls and the purple Silverwing banners.

Walking through here, he always felt like a rogue was about to jump out from somewhere and sap him.

Rounding the corner, two night elf sentinels stood at the corridor exit, talking about something. Jace couldn't quite make out their conversation from here, but every so often he caught his own name.

Sensing his footsteps, both sentinels looked over in unison, then fell silent with practiced ease and nodded in greeting. "Greetings, honored Jace Sesso," one of them asked in the elven tongue. "Is there anything we can help with?"

Though everyday Thalassian conversation was no longer much of a challenge for Jace, understanding the night elves' speech immediately was like someone who'd barely learned a few lines of American English suddenly being dropped into a thick British accent. It took him a moment to realize what the elf's second sentence had asked.

"I'm sorry," Jace also said in the elven tongue. "I'm not very good at your language. If you speak Common, I'd prefer to converse in Common."

The two sentinels exchanged a glance, but the one who'd spoken smiled and switched to somewhat halting Common. "As you wish then, Jace Sesso. I apologize for my earlier offense — I assumed you'd know our language."

Jace swallowed, his expression complicated. Looks like he needed to pick up the pace on his elven studies. A hero who'd supposedly slain Deathwing alongside the Dragonqueen, and he couldn't even speak the elven tongue — that really didn't sit right. He felt like he was letting the whole "hero" side down, especially as a spellcaster.

"I was wondering where Wrensa Windrunner went," he asked.

"Wrensa Windrunner should be in the courtyard," the sentinel said, glancing at the pocket watch in Jace's hand. "She's been there for quite a while."

"Thank you." Jace nodded and used a phrase in the elven tongue as a farewell: "Blessings upon you."

Both sentinels smiled and nodded in return. The other one added in elven, "Blessings upon you and your family."

Leaving the sentinels behind, he headed down the long passage toward the fortress entrance. Along the way, every sentinel took the initiative to salute him, nod, and address him with considerable respect.

There wasn't a single male in all of Silverwing Garrison — even more absolute than Feathermoon Stronghold. The Sentinel Corps didn't recruit men. But in a fortress this large, there wasn't a single male elf, not even a druid serving as an advisor?

Jace knew that at this time the druids were all deep in slumber. After the War of the Ancients, the Dreamer Ysera had linked the World Tree to the Emerald Dream, channeling dream power through the World Tree into the real world to repair the devastation wrought by the explosion of the Well of Eternity.

The druids had forged a pact with Ysera, entering the Dream through slumber to lend their power to the Dream and the Green Dragonflight — to maintain the health of the World Tree, and in turn sustain the blessing of immortality the World Tree granted the night elves.

Once this slumber began, it often lasted centuries. They wouldn't wake unless the matter was truly urgent. But at this very moment, was there truly not a single druid awake in all of night elf society?

If he were merely passing through, he probably wouldn't have given it a second thought. But he'd once urged Mosa Woodmane to come to Ashenvale to seek out the Keepers of the Grove and learn the druidic ways from the night elves. What if Mosa couldn't find any druids to teach her? Or — what if she too had fallen into the slumber?

Did tauren druids also need to honor the pact between the druids and the Green Dragonflight, the way the night elf druids did? Would she too have to fall into slumber in exchange for the teachings of the Green Dragonflight and the Keepers of the Grove?

Stepping out of the fortress and into the courtyard, Jace heard the crisp clang of metal on metal.

Wrensa was sparring with the sentinel officer who'd greeted them yesterday, wielding the sentinels' long blades. Both of them were fast. The sentinel was slightly taller, her attacks sharp and crisp, but Wrensa was far more agile, and in the hard exchanges there was no sign she was outmatched in strength.

After a few rapid volleys, Wrensa's blade came to rest on the sentinel's collarbone, forcing the other woman to raise her weapon in defeat.

The watching sentinels applauded and marveled from time to time. It seemed that, whatever the night elves thought of their cousins across the sea and their reckless use of arcane magic, they held genuine admiration for the quel'dorei's refined combat techniques.

Wrensa, as one of the finest rangers of Quel'Thalas, had earned the sentinels' respect not only through her reputation but through her own prowess in battle.

Then the two exchanged weapons. Wrensa tried one of the sentinels' moon blades, swinging it under the officer's guidance, then attempted an attack.

The sight reminded Jace of the demon hunters. As he watched, he couldn't help glancing north, letting his gaze fall once more into the shadow of Mount Hyjal under the moonlight.

At this very moment, Illidan Stormrage was imprisoned in the ancient prison within those mountains. The Watcher, Maiev Shadowsong, was his jailer. Thinking that this legendary hero was practically within eyeshot — the distance between them almost visible to the naked eye — Jace found the whole thing deeply surreal.

But clearly she wasn't accustomed to this ancient Kaldorei weapon either. Getting the balance and the rhythm of the swing was difficult. After a session of practice, she was already breathing hard.

The two of them had clearly been at it for a while. Wrensa set down the moon blade, looked up, and spotted Jace standing there. She rose on her toes and waved.

Jace nodded and walked over. Wrensa said, "How long have you been standing there?"

"Not long enough," Jace said.

Wrensa smiled. "If only we could stay here longer. In Quel'Thalas it's hard to find so many worthy opponents to train against."

"You're too humble," the officer sighed. "Even among the sentinels, I've rarely seen a warrior with such skill. I assumed the descendants of those mages who crossed the Great Sea had long forgotten the importance of flesh and strength. To possess such power at your age — though I haven't met other Farstriders, I can imagine the sort of training you must endure."

She turned to Jace, as if suddenly remembering something. "Ah, right. I'm Ephalan, captain of the garrison here at Silverwing. Yesterday was rather rushed, so I didn't even introduce myself."

"Captain Ephalan." Jace nodded, then looked at Wrensa. "Wen, did you ask Captain Ephalan about Mosa?"

Wrensa nodded. The sentinel captain said, "I'm sorry, Jace Sesso. I'm only responsible for the defense of Silverwing Garrison, so I'm not familiar with matters elsewhere. But I've already dispatched a sentinel to inform Commander Sula Swiftarrow. She travels often between Silverwing Garrison and Astranaar. If a tauren came here, she would certainly know."

Sula Swiftarrow — even though she'd spoken in the elven tongue, Jace could guess the meaning of the surname and connect it to the figure from the game.

Sula Swiftarrow was the leader of the Silverwing Sentinels. From the time the Horde arrived in Kalimdor and began raiding Ashenvale, through the Scourge and Burning Legion's assault on Mount Hyjal, all the way to the Warsong clan's construction of lumber mills in Ashenvale that sparked nearly a decade of conflict between the two sides — she had been the leader of the Silverwing Sentinels throughout, coordinating the entire Sentinel Corps against any outside invader.

Jace nodded. Ephalan continued, "You must have just woken up and be hungry. The great hall of Silverwing Garrison is preparing a late-night meal. Please, come and taste the hunters' catch from yesterday."

"We'll be right there," Wrensa nodded.

"Then I'll leave you two to it." Ephalan saluted and headed for the fortress entrance. Jace watched her back. "Does she know that we two are... a couple?"

"She guessed," Wrensa said.

"So a Kaldorei isn't even surprised that we're together?" Jace said, taken aback.

Wrensa said, "She told me that there were Kaldorei who'd fallen in love with green dragons in Ashenvale. Even with furbolgs. To her, a human isn't an unacceptable match."

Jace raised his eyebrows, surprised. "It seems these supposedly conservative ancient folk are a good deal more open-minded than your people in Quel'Thalas."

"What's that supposed to mean?" Wrensa said, annoyed. "The opposition to the two of us back in Quel'Thalas wasn't all that severe. Otherwise you wouldn't have received such a warm welcome and courteous treatment there. Besides, what's so terrible about falling in love with a human? It's ten times better than falling in love with an Amani troll."

Having said that, Wrensa shot Jace a quick look. Jace's mouth went dry and he swallowed stiffly. "Why are you looking at me like that?"

"Hmm? Can't I look at you? Come on, Jace, I'm starving." Wrensa set off up the slope with a light step, leaving Jace standing there alone.

She couldn't have found out through some channel, could she?

The feud between Quel'Thalas and the Amani Empire had stretched across thousands of years. If she really knew what he'd done back in Booty Bay, no amount of "it was a moment of impulse" would paper over it.

Jace could only hope he was being paranoid and letting his imagination run wild.
