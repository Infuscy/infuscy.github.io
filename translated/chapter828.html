Chapter 828: Missing in Ashenvale

Arriving at the great hall of Silverwing Garrison, Jace found many sentinels and officers already gathered, making the place feel as though a feast were underway.

Before he'd even walked through the door, Jace could already hear Grede's laughter. He'd gotten up at some point without a thought for finding Jace or Wrensa, probably following his nose here on his own.

At the moment, he was regaling a crowd of elves with the tale of how he'd fought the orcs that had come through the Dark Portal to Grim Batol, determined to describe every axe swing, every hammer blow, and every expression on every orc's face in the finest detail.

And the female sentinels listened, rapt. After all, across their centuries — sometimes millennia — of life, these sentinels had fought no end of bizarre opponents: centaurs, berserk mountain giants, fearsome elementals, poachers and mercenaries and thieves from across the sea, even dragons... green dragons lost in the Nightmare, or black dragons looking to make trouble in Ashenvale. But orcs were a completely new enemy they'd never encountered.

Seeing Jace and Wrensa enter, the sentinels all turned respectful gazes toward them. One sentinel very close by even lowered her head in a bow and said something Jace didn't catch. Wrensa, hearing the sentinel's words, paled slightly and quickly lowered her head to reply in the elven tongue.

Jace whispered, "What did she say?"

"She called us..." Wrensa frowned, hesitated, then whispered, "'Saviors who ended death.' That's an even grander meaning than what Noralar Alkhanos conveys by 'savior.' Perhaps the differences between our languages might cause some ambiguity, but I don't think so. I don't understand why they hold us in such high regard."

"Over here! You two!" Grede noticed them entering and called out loudly. "Come try these meat skewers! Fresh and tender... I'd bet my beard you won't find shrimp this fresh in all of Loch Modan!"

"Coming, coming." Wrensa sat down beside Grede, took the skewer the dwarf offered, and looked at Jace as she continued, "If, back in Lordaeron, the cold indifference of the human nobles made me extremely uncomfortable, then here in Ashenvale, the respect these sentinels show us makes me uncomfortable too. Frankly, I never expected to receive such treatment here. I'd thought I might need to use this green gem pendant to establish my identity, since the Kaldorei all revere the Dreamer..."

Jace had felt it too, actually. From Stormwind City and Lordaeron to Ironforge, to Quel'Thalas, to Ashenvale, and even among the dragons.

The more powerful and ancient a being was, the more keenly they grasped what Deathwing's fall meant to this world.

"The Kaldorei witnessed Deathwing's power firsthand during the War of the Ancients, Wen," Jace tried to explain. "Many of them are even still alive today. I'm also surprised they value our deeds so highly, but it's not beyond understanding. I'm just not used to it myself. But strictly speaking, Deathwing wasn't actually killed by us. He died of his own madness. Yet in these sentinels' mouths, it's as if we personally hacked the Father of Black Dragons to death."

"But you were the key to all of it," Wrensa said, holding Jace's hand. "Without you, Deathwing wouldn't have died. So stop overthinking it. At the very least, when we get back to Quel'Thalas, don't you dare be so modest with those nobles. You'd better tell those self-important bastards that you stabbed Deathwing to death with your own sword, stroke by stroke. No one can call you out on it anyway."

"No problem." Jace smiled, picked up a skewer, and bit off a piece of white meat, chewing it.

He found the meat loose and soft. Even with plenty of pickled seasoning, he could still taste a faint earthy gaminess underneath. Whatever this was, it was definitely not shrimp.

"You sure this is shrimp, Grede?" Jace said. "This tastes awfully like..."

"Spider legs." Wrensa nodded. "I still remember that meal the young guard at Raven Hill treated us to. Remember, Jace? That was the first time the two of us went out together, so that meal left a deep impression on me. We ate spider..."

"Sp...ider?"

Grede had frozen. The little skin visible between his whiskers had turned a shade paler.

He covered his mouth, retched, and bolted from the great hall. The sentinels all stared at his reaction, baffled, with no idea what had happened.

"What's wrong with him?" Wrensa asked.

"I'm guessing it's because the Hinterlands has a lot of troll priests who worship the spider god," Jace said, popping another piece into his mouth, then glancing back at the door. "When I was in the Hinterlands with him, we ran into a batch of giant spiders too. I heard a lot of dwarves had died horribly to those spiders and their priests."

Wrensa nodded. "I know Sha'dra's priests."

"You don't care about that?" Jace asked. "They're your enemies too."

"Why should I care?" Wrensa said, sliding the last piece of meat and a slice of roasted fruit off the skewer and into her mouth all at once, muttering as she chewed, "I'd honestly prefer it to be the flesh of Sha'dra's children. Eggs would do too. I have zero problem eating giant spider eggs on a skewer. Actually, I'm kind of looking forward to it. Thinking that way, I even look forward to swallowing the flesh of those bastards who are always trying to burn our homeland, slathered in sauce. I mean trolls. And orcs. Orcs too."

"Eating troll flesh..." Jace's eyes widened in shock.

Wrensa glanced at him. "Hah, don't worry, I'm kidding. We're not trolls. We have no interest in eating those guys' flesh. But spider meat really isn't that bad."

Just then the dwarf came back, panting. Jace brought over a plate of red fish slices and pickled vegetables. "Eat this instead." Grede blinked. "This isn't some... spider fish, is it?"

"What the hell is a spider fish? This is salmon. We have it back home too," Jace said. "You can't tell?"

"All right, I'm afraid I'll get it wrong again," Grede sighed and sat down.

Just then, the low growls of saber cats came from outside — another company of sentinel cavalry returning. Unlike human cavalry, whose hooves thundered when they approached, even a whole company of saber cats made almost no sound when they arrived.

Before long, a night elf in silver armor with pale gold trim appeared at the entrance. She handed her helmet to a subordinate, looked over, and immediately spotted the three of them. The sentinels all rose to their feet and saluted the newly arrived rider.

"Jace Sesso, Grede Thunderfist, Wrensa Windrunner — Silverwing Garrison welcomes you. I am Sula Swiftarrow, warden of Silverwing Garrison. Months ago, I heard word of your deeds at Grim Batol in Astranaar. Your bravery saved Kalimdor, and it saved Azeroth." The sentinel leader came before them and said, "Please, sit down at your ease. Every Kaldorei will thank you for what you've done, as I do — they simply haven't had the chance. Now that you're here, I will do everything in my power to host you."

"You're too kind, ma'am!" Grede asked. "But do you have bear meat or boar meat or anything like that?"

"Of course." Sula smiled. "If you're willing to travel to Astranaar, there'll be more sumptuous fare awaiting you there. Silverwing Garrison's stores are ample, but they can't compare to what's available there. And I know the High Priestess has been wanting to meet you as well. She mentioned it before."

The High Priestess — did that mean he might get to meet Tyrande Whisperwind?

Jace hadn't imagined he'd have such an opportunity coming to northern Kalimdor. "How long does it take to reach Astranaar?" he asked.

"About ten days round trip, Jace Sesso," Sula answered.

The three of them exchanged glances. Jace said, "I'm sorry — it's not that we don't want to meet the High Priestess. But we have urgent business and need to press on as quickly as possible. If we have the chance in the future, we'll visit Astranaar."

"Then I look forward to your return," Sula Swiftarrow said. "As for your mission... if the matter is urgent, the Silverwing Sentinels can dispatch saber cat riders to escort you on your way. Where are you headed?"

"Winterspring," Wrensa said.

Sula Swiftarrow said, "The road to Winterspring runs through furbolg territory, Wrensa Windrunner. To cross it, you'll likely need to earn the furbolgs' trust — otherwise the journey will be harder by an order of magnitude. But I imagine that won't be a problem. After all, the furbolgs know what kind of creature a black dragon is. When the time comes, we can explain the matter to them. They'll certainly be willing to let you pass."

"That's wonderful. Thank you so much," Jace said.

"It's the least we can do — it barely compares to everything you've brought to Ashenvale and Mount Hyjal," Sula said with a smile.

Jace and Wrensa both smiled awkwardly and nodded. Grede, though, jumped in: "There's one more important thing! I want to know — has a tauren come here to study druidism?"

"A tauren?" Sula Swiftarrow shook her head. "I haven't heard word of any tauren coming to Ashenvale." All three of them were taken aback.
