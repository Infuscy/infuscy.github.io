Chapter 829: Onward to the North

After the meal, the three of them returned to the corridor outside their quarters.

Making sure there were no sentinels around, Grede couldn't help asking, "Is it possible that that tauren chief was lying to us? Mosa never left the Ragefire River — at least, never left that grassland. For all we know she's still in the Stonetalon Mountains. You remember that place, right? There's still quite a few tauren living there."

"Why would they lie?" Wrensa asked.

"I don't know." Grede shrugged. "Don't trust us, or they're just compulsive liars. They might be Grimtotem for all we know."

Wrensa shook her head. "Grimtotem tauren paint themselves with strange totems, and many of them are red. I can more or less recognize them by now, Grede. Don't you remember? That tauren the goblins hired in Steamwheedle Port was just like that."

"But that doesn't mean tauren from other totems are all good people, does it?" Grede leaned against the wall in thought. "Or maybe they thought we meant Mosa harm. Didn't you hear what those elves said? Lately there've been a lot of mercenaries and hunters from our side coming to Kalimdor. Maybe those tauren thought we'd been hired by the centaurs to assassinate Mosa."

"Then why didn't they just kill us on the spot?" Jace asked.

"They figured they couldn't beat us. They wanted to trick us into Ashenvale and let the elves do the dirty work for them." Grede said it with certainty. Wrensa frowned. "I think you might be..."

"I didn't get the sense that the tauren was lying," Jace said, thinking back. "Unless tauren have some special technique for hiding their lies that can fool both me and Morophil, they were probably telling the truth."

"You can tell when people are lying?" Grede asked. "Some kind of dark magic? Mind reading?"

"Not exactly mind reading. But unless they're exceptionally good at lying, I can pick up on it through emotional sensing." Jace tilted his head slightly. "That said, I'll admit I wasn't focusing on reading those tauren's emotions. They didn't seem like they were trying to hide anything."

"Let me test you, then," Grede said. "I've got ten gold coins in my pocket. True or false?"

Jace let out a quiet sigh. Wrensa frowned. "Right now, the person we can't find is your Mosa Woodmane, Grede. You've got the mood to play stupid games with us?"

Grede waved a hand. "Why wouldn't I? If those tauren really were lying to us and Mosa's still in the south, I'd actually be a lot more relaxed."

"Why's that?" Jace asked.

Grede said, "That means Mosa's most dangerous enemy right now is still the centaurs, right? Look at this place, Ashenvale. The moment you step into this godforsaken forest, there's no telling what kind of danger you'll run into."

Jace fell silent for a moment, then realized he had no comeback.

He said, "When we parted on that beach, I told Mosa... she could take that horn to Ashenvale and seek out the Keepers of the Grove to learn the druidic ways. She found Zaltar's spirit — those Keepers would surely be willing to repay her. She'd always resented the Kaldorei for refusing to help the tauren. I'm wondering — could it be that she didn't want anything to do with the Kaldorei, and went straight to the Keepers of the Grove on her own?"

Wrensa said, "That's a fair point. She doesn't like the Kaldorei. And Mosa can talk to trees, transform into a great cat. She's fully capable of evading Kaldorei sentinels on her own — getting the forest to tip her off, using nature magic to conceal herself in the woods."

"Is it that easy to slip past the Silverwing Sentinels' patrols?" Grede asked.

"At least it's not that hard," Wrensa stared at the dwarf. "After all, she's not dragging two dead weights behind her. And don't forget — she's carrying the long horn of Remulos. That's a pretty powerful artifact."

"Fine, fine, you've always got a reason," Grede said, defeated.

"If she's with the Keepers of the Grove, I don't think that's something we need to worry about," Jace said. "It just means you might have a hard time seeing her, Grede."

"What can you do?" Grede said. "She has her own path and her own pursuits. I just hope she's all right. We'll have a chance to meet again someday."

Wrensa patted the dwarf's shoulder. Grede smiled at her, then asked, "So, when do we set out?"

Jace said, "Wait till morning. Get some more sleep and rest up properly. By now we've flipped our days and nights around following these elves."

Grede clapped his hands. "Being told to go to sleep — now that's a luxury."

The next morning, Sula Swiftarrow brought the three of them to the warehouse beside Silverwing Garrison. She pointed to an officer directing sentinels as they moved supplies at the warehouse entrance. "That's Sentinel-Leader Aetil Moonfire, the quartermaster of Silverwing Garrison. You can go to her to pick from the warehouse's stores — arrows or other supplies. You can also head to the other side to grab some emergency provisions for the road. I've already spoken with her about you three. I'm going to check whether the saber cats are ready."

"Oh, and bring some antidotes too. Mount Hyjal and Nordrassil nurture more than just the Kaldorei — they also make the beasts living around them very powerful, including the giant venomous spiders that lurk in the dense forest. Even the saber cats, if bitten without an antidote, can be in mortal danger. The sentinels will be bringing some along as well, for this journey north."

"Thank you, Captain Sula. You've really helped us enormously," Jace said.

He wasn't being polite. If they had to walk from here to Winterspring, it would be a long, grueling trek through forest and valley. Having saber cat riders to help would make things a lot easier.

That stretch of forest and valley — that was Felwood Forest from the game.

Felwood Forest literally meant "forest corrupted by fel energy." It had originally been part of Ashenvale, but after the demons invaded Mount Hyjal they had corrupted the area. At this point in time, it was still an ordinary forest. Aside from being home to a lot of furbolgs, it was no different from the rest of Ashenvale.

In the game, many of the furbolgs had gone mad and attacked outsiders on sight without a word, precisely because most of the areas they inhabited had been heavily corrupted by fel energy.

Right now, the furbolgs should still be a fairly peaceful bunch. They should be easy to talk to. I wonder what kind of strange alchemy recipes I could get from them to bring back to Ursula?

Grede watched the night elf leave, then turned and whispered, "Giant venomous spiders? Scarier than the ones in the Hinterlands?"

"Why are you so afraid of spiders?" Wrensa said, disgusted. "Haven't you fought trolls plenty of times?"

"Afraid of spiders?" Grede said, offended. "Ask Jace Sesso — did I show even a shred of fear when we went to that Sha'dra priest's lair? It's just that I've seen too many dwarves eaten by spiders. It makes me sick to think about it. I just don't want to run into them again, that's all."

They got three quivers — sixty or seventy arrows in total — from the quartermaster Aetil Moonfire. The night elf arrows seemed to weigh a bit more than the ones from Quel'Thalas. Jace suspected the slightly whitish-silver arrowheads might be made of mithril.

He wasn't a blacksmith and couldn't say for sure. Grede spent all his time around dwarves, but he hadn't picked up much knowledge about metals either, so he couldn't make a definitive judgment.

In any case, even Jace could tell these were arrows imbued with enchantment — better even than the arrows Wrensa had brought from Quel'Thalas.

Of course, Wrensa flatly refused to admit that.

Taking the quivers, along with Silverwing Garrison's field rations, six jars of healing salve, and five rolls of bandages, they set out.

Standing at the cliff beside the fortress entrance, Jace, Wrensa, and Grede looked at the big and small packs each of them was carrying and still felt terribly embarrassed.

But embarrassment was one thing. Taking what they needed was another.
